All the Wrong Moves: Gloria

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 Failure's Excuse Phoenix, Arizona December, 2005 "The Avatar" William Gwynn sits between two of his friends, George "Gus" Killibrew and Walter "Igloo" Kulowiyi. Gus holds a video tape in his hands, and has it pressed to the opening in the VCR. "Igloo, I think you're going to have to tell this one, I'm out of the picture at this point," says George. "Agreed, are you ready?" Igloo replies. "No, no, wait," William Gwynn orders, "stay that hand! I've got some explaining to do before we go into the horrors of my young life. First of all, I was born in Scotland to an extremely conservative, Christian family and I was home-schooled for sixteen years. Sixteen years! In fact, I was so isolated that I had never even MET a girl of my age. So, obviously, it was quite a shock when my parents decided that they had to move to this place called 'Arizona'. In my geography studies, I knew that Arizona was a really hot, desert place in the United States. So, I wasn't thrilled. Anyway - " The hand 'slips', the video slides into the VCR, and the scene of three friends changes to two adults and a teenager. NO MOZART FOR YOU! Somewhere Over the Atlantic, January 20, 1996 The teenager is undoubtedly a much younger, much better kept William Gwynn. His hair is short, and he has a pile of books on the seat next to him, completely enthralled in the lives of others. His mother watches him, and has an epiphany. "You know, William," she says, "since your father and I are getting new jobs in Phoenix, we probably won't be able to home-school you anymore." William looks up at her, and blinks in response. "You'll probably have to go to a public school for the rest of your schooling, and then college." "But Mom," he says, "I've already passed the high school equivalancy test. I can go to college this coming year." "I think it'd be good for you to go to public school, baby," his mother says, "you need to get to know some people your own age before you go to college. You'd be an outcast." "Mother!" he complains. "I do know people my own age. What about George?" "George is a thousand miles behind us right now," his mother says, "and he was a bad influence anyway. Besides, don't you think you should start meeting some girls?" William simply gapes, as though his mother had just done blasphemy. "Honestly, I'm the only girl you know," she continues. "That's not true!" William says, "George's - " "George's little sisters don't count," she snaps. "There's no room for argument, you will be going to public school, and I don't want to hear another word about it." William, seething and sad, puts a bookmark in his book, slams it down, and lays down in his seat, his face in his pillow. He turns on his CD player and listens to his Mozart. "You're done listening to that crap, too. No one your age listens to that." She replaces his CD with Pearl Jam, and William turns it off immediately and throws his CD player back in his bookbag. He storms back to the bathrooms in the plane. "Don't you think you're being a little hard on him?" his father asks. "Don't you agree with me?" she asks, turning an angry eye to him. "Yes," he says, "but you can't dictate his tastes. I think that classical music is fine for him to listen to, and books are great to be read. He is a wonderful son. If you try to take away everything at once, he's going to be very unhappy." Slick, Sharp, Spiffy Phoenix, Arizona January 29, 1996 It was William Gwynn's first day at school. The last week had been preparation, and it had been hell. William felt like one of those birds that they held in captivity for years and years do to a health condition, only to set it free years later. Maybe that bird likes to be fed and sheltered; maybe that bird doesn't want to go. When William explained this to his mother, she said: "Lynyrd Skynyrd didn't write 'Caged Bird', now did he? No, he wrote Freebird." "Mom, Lynyrd Skynyrd is not a real person," he said. "I don't like this place. Can we go home?" "This is home," she explained. So here he was, with a backpack slung over his shoulder, waiting for a bus to come pick him up. He had snuck several of his own books into the bag, along with some of his albums, in case he got bored. His appearance was slightly skewed from the common 1996 teenage male. He wasn't wearing curduroy, or denim. He was wearing a polo shirt and khakis. Here was a kid not overcome by Pearl Jam and Cobain. Here was a kid who was smuggling books into school, not cocaine. William Gwynn's short hair was, for the first time in his life, spiked and gelled in place. Even William Gwynn knew how ridiculous he looked. His mother had said that he looked "spiffy". His father agreed that he looked "sharp". All William wanted was a plane ticket back to Scotland. When the bus pulled up, William got in and took a seat by himself. Hardly any of the children noticed that he was new. They were far too tired, and all sleeping. William got out his CD player, placed in some Mozart, and got out his book for the day: Walden by Henry David Thoreau. He hoped that this book would help him gain insight into the American mind, as it was written by an American. Unfortunately, as William would find out later, "Civil Disobedience" would do little in the Year of the Macarena. William Gwynn was completely prepped for the regularities of school life. That is, how to open his locker, what combination he has, what locker number he has. He knows where his classes are and all the different ways to get to them. He knows all his teachers names, and the proper way to address them. This was all taught by his father. By his mother, he had been taught the proper way to waltz and square dance, though William was thoroughly confused as to why these skills would be needed. He hopes that these American children don't waltz down the halls or anything silly like that. William does not put up with nonsense, and being thrust into a situation of nonsense is the last thing that William wants. Two Hours Later William Gwynn has already been to one class, and he hates it. His math teacher is a complete idiot. He spent the entire class teaching about how to solve an Algebra problem, when there was a much easier method. Gwynn had the answer in two minutes. The teacher took the entire class. He is now in his second hour class, Spanish. William had done some studying in Spanish, but very little. These Americans had done two years of studying and knew next to nothing. The class ends twenty minutes early because the teacher is "tired". And so the rest of the class begins to chatter. Several minutes later, some of his classmates realize that he is just writing scribbling an angry letter to his mother in his notebook. "What's your name?" a dark-haired girl asks. "William," he says. "Yours?" he asks, being polite. "Gloria," she responds, with a smile. "Where are you from? Sorry, I noticed your accent … " "Scotland." "Scotland?" she asks, shocked. "I was thinking like … Canada or something. Well, why did you come to Phoenix?" William considers that for a moment. "My parents got new jobs here," he says, "how long have you been here?" "All my life," she says. "I sort of like it here. I mean, it's never really cold. It's sort of dull sometimes, out in the middle of a desert, you know." The bell rings, interrupting William's first conversation with a girl his same age. Little did William know, but she was just recently separated from a boyfriend of several months. It had been a bitter fight. "I guess it's time to go," William says, "I'll see you tomorrow." "Tomorrow it is," she says, with a smile. "Goodbye, William." "Bye Gloria," he responds, leaving the class. At Lunch At lunch of William Gwynn's first day, there is a fight. It's between a girl and a boy, or at least William thinks. An Eskimo boy is getting his butt kicked by a very long-haired, denim-wearing girl. William can't help but laugh. The girl snaps around. "What are you laughing at?" she demands, in a very deep voice. "It's just … he's getting beat-up by a girl," William responds, "not that that's rare or anything … " The girl attacks William. She punches him in the face, and William's glasses fall off and break on the floor. William grew up learning to "turn the other cheek", but this time he's pissed off. He doesn't care if he's hitting a girl. All it took was three swings of his arm, and the girl was on her back, knocked out cold. Kung Fu will do that to you. The boy gets up, and thanks William. "Thank you," the Eskimo says, "my name is Igloo, at least that's what they call me. He called me something else, that's why we fought." "She, you mean?" William asks. "No, he," the Eskimo answers. "Oh. You honestly thought - well I guess with the long hair and all - but, don't you know about the whole grunge movement?" William shakes his head. "Oh, well, I'll have to teach you then." "That would help," William says. "My mother is trying to, but she doesn't know much." The Eskimo finds that funny for some reason. "What?" "Your mother can't help you be modern," Igloo says. Several lunch tables away, the girls are chattering. "Isn't the new boy so hot?" one of them asks. "If we get those nerdy clothes off of him, he'll be great. Looks like our own personal Cobain already broke his glasses." "Back off," Gloria says, "that one's mine … " December, 2005 The two friends of William Gwynn have just realized just how poorly that clip made them look. "I didn't know your mother thought I was a bad influence," Killibrew says. "And why did we have to show that part where I get beat up?" Igloo demands. "I thought it was funny," Killibrew grins. "You two think that made YOU look bad?" William says. "Look at me! I'm Captain Nerdo!" "What do you think 'Own Personal Cobain' thinks of the pictures he has of him that age?" Killibrew says. "He's probably some hotshot, clean-cut banker now. Cobain killed himself, kid cleaned himself up." "Yeah, well," Igloo says, "we're going to fast forward a bit. Explain what happened in the next few months, William." William blinks. "Yeah, so, anyway," he begins, "that Gloria girl and I sort of, what's the term the kids use now?" "Hit it off?" "Had sex?" "The first would be preferable," William growls. "And she asked me to go with her to that big American dance for Juniors and Seniors." "The Prom!" "Yeah," William says, "so I said yes. Why not? She was pretty, and my mother would have killed me if I didn't go anyway. So here's the story of that night … " The Big Night Prom Night in Phoenix, Arizona May 11, 1996 William Gwynn arrived with Gloria several minutes after the event began. There were a great number of people already there, and William was immediately uncomfortable. Crowds do that to William, he likes the idea of space. This idea would be tested several times throughout the night. As he watched the shadowy figures when the lights illuminated their bodies, he found that he would be in for a tough night indeed. Not only would the waltz and the square dancing that he had learned be completely useless, but he would most certainly embarass the hell out of himself trying to learn this new style. As William listens to this music, he's found that his greatest fear has been realized. These Americans do not dance to Mozart. They dance to … yes, rap … and techno … and sometimes rock. The music made William nauseous. Fortunately, Gloria and her friends ran off to find a table. Unfortunately, they came back. "So tell me Gloria," William Gwynn began, "how are people supposed to square-dance to this? There's no fiddle." She laughed. "William," she began, "you are the funniest boy I have ever met." He wasn't joking, but he decided not to admit that. She seized him, and William made his first attempt. He didn't do particularly poorly. Actually, he did. But it wasn't catastrophic. Well, it was. But it could have been worse. Maybe. At least she pretended not to notice for a bit. As he put it, he was "just getting warmed up". Just then, William notices someone in obvious distress. "Oh Heavens!" William mutters. "He's seizing! I read all about that in my medicine independent study!" "William!" Gloria protests, but he's already gone. He's checking the kid's pulse. "What the hell is your problem?" the kid suddenly asks. "Shh! Seizing people can't talk," William says. "I'm not seizing! Yo! Get your hands off of me," the kid yells. "What'd you go and do, Gloria? Date a queer?" "Hey!" William retorts. "I'm not the one strange enough to have a tuxedo with a peace symbol on the cuff." Even Gloria shakes her head, she pulls William away. "William!" she says, "that was a tattoo. Now come on. He was just dancing. Like we should be doing?" But William was a little angry. "That guy called me queer," he growls. "Okay, umm," she says, "let's settle you down. Take a break? How about we go vote for King and Queen?" "You don't vote for Kings and Queens," William retorts, "that's the whole point of a monarchy." She laughs agian. "You are funny," she says, "you're lucky you've got that for oyu." And now William is thoroughly confused. "These Americans," he mutters, beneath his breath. Minutes later, when they did get back on the floor. William Gwynn attempted to do what that guy was doing when William checked for his pulse, but William charlie-horsed. Gloria shook her head, slightly annoyed. "William," she said, "you've just got all the wrong moves." The rest of the night was just disappointment after disappointment, mistake after mistake. William couldn't have performed worse, he concluded. His rating was atrocious. He knew that his days with Gloria were not just limited, but obsolete. He knew now that there had to be other focuses, if that's what he wanted. Six months ago, he would have been perfectly satisfied with nothing, but now an animal has been awoken. Now he has an emotional craving for love, for woman. He doesn't understand, but he knows now that it will dictate the rest of his life. There's no turning back now. December, 2005 The grizzled face of "The Avatar" William Gwynn encompasses the sight, it shakes from side to side, almost amazed at what his life once was. There was a time when he was just a regular guy, at least as regular as an immigrant in a foreign country could be. He was the new guy, or, as those five years his predecessor might have said: the new kid on the block. The other two have left, leaving William Gwynn to brood on those distant times. He has a paper in his hand, holding a headline declaring AWC wrestler "The British Bomber" dead, several weeks before. "She told me I had all the wrong moves," William Gwynn begins, "if only she could see what I've done. A matter of years later, and I've grown to a standard that I sometimes find it hard to compete with. Here I am, a former Legacy Champion. I'm a former PRIDE Champion. They called me the PRIDE of cW. But now all I'm thinking about is fighting the dead man, The British Bomber. I mean that literally. I don't know if they plan on making me wrestle a casket or what, but I'll do whatever it takes to get to round two. I don't care if it takes me ten years to win one of these tournaments. All I want is some advancement. Last year I was defeated in round one. This year I would love the opportunity to get to round two. "It's the great thing about America," William Gwynn says, "it's something I've learned with blood, sweat and tears. It's not always easy, but there's always that chance to succeed. It's the grit that gets you through. Some of us have it, some of us don't. I plan on proving to myself, to British Bomber, to Core Wrestling, to my GTT5 competition, and to the World that I have it. I have that grit. I am a competitor, a force to be reckoned with. Maybe then the fear will set in. Maybe then I'll be respected as a Dark Horse or even just recognized. For that I would put it all on the line, and that is what I am going to do against British Bomber. I wonder what he plans to do, what evil plan he might be conspiring. Will he cheat? Will he run? Will he play a game of lies and deceit? Will grit and honor not overcome these vices?" He sighs. "I have been down that road," he mutters, "and it is not a happy one to travel, but to advance, I daresay it is British Bomber's only hope. Will he sink so low? And even then, can he overcome me? Can he overcome a former Legacy Champion? Can he overcome the MAN in Core Wrestling? I advance, he falters. It is the way of the world. The strong survive, the weak fall prey to them, despite all their cunning, all their trickery, they will slip, they will falter. Again, the way of the world, survival of the fittest. British Bomber, I am the fittest. You can play Lazarus all you like, rising from the dead. But I am just as driven against the undead as I am the living. I am just as strong, and you are just as weak. Listen!" He begins to whisper. "Do you hear the wind? Can you hear it howl?" He smiles. "It beckons," he whispers, "it's calling you back. It says its unnatural. It says that it is futile to fight against Gwynn. It tells you that you have no hope. Everyone tells you that. Everyone lets you know how worthless, how pathetic your odds are. They scream it, and yet you are deaf. Do the dead not hear? Did your ears rot while you served your time beneath the soil, the grass?" He snorts. "You're dead, Bomber. I will not be haunted. I advance. And I will put you back to rest, back where you belong. Good night." Fade.
