Restoration

17 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-17 “CAGH~!” I give a really strong, loud, mucus-ridden cough trying to release the phlegm at the back of my throat to no avail. With my throat aching intensely, these coughs are absolutely killing me. It’s just typical that I have to catch a cold half-way through the biggest tournament of my career. “CAAGH~!” I pull the covers up over my shoulders to try and counteract the symptoms of my virus. At least there are some things around me to compensate the way I’m feeling, the central heating being on full blast with the electric blanket and hot water bottle making my back sweat. It’s probably overkill, but whatever. These coughs aren’t really the source of half the pain running through me, though. The coughs are merely the catalyst sending my body convulsing and moving all other parts of my body. As each part of my body moves more and more pain streaks through my body like an evil lightning bolt, or that bloke from Star Wars, you know the evil guy who could like stick his fingers out and KAPOW~! ‘Lightning for you, Master Luke! Mwahahahaha!’ As you can probably tell, my last match hurt a tad. There’s a knock at the door. I wasn’t expecting visitors today… I wonder who it could be… “Alo?” I call, the door slowly creaks open and from the shadows of the gap between the door and the wall is a little smile, a pretty little smile, a warm smile. The smile disappears for a moment and the door pushes open, with the source of the smile walking slowly into the room, backwards. Her eyes flutter my way, those beautiful crystal orbs glistening, with a special smile just below it. The smile is extra special, because the smile is for me. “Hello there!” Wendy says enthusiastically, her grin never faltering. “Alo there Wendy,” I reply softly with my gaze fixated on hers. “Your little friend wanted to see you.” She says. “He has a special prize for you for your win the other day.” Wendy turns around holding a tray. Sitting on that tray is a small plastic yellow duck. His name is Ducky and he is my friend. Because the tray is being so high up I can’t see what my special prize is… I wonder what it could be! Wendy slowly lowers the tray onto my lap, Ducky now looking straight up into my face as if to say ‘alo’ and before me is the wonderful prize for defeating my friend Jay the other day. “A mug of Lemsip…?!” I exclaim, “Aww thank you little mate! You shouldn’t have.” It was a godsend, the mucus had reached an all time high and it needed something. I pick up the hot mug by the handle, trying desperately not to burn my finger on the side of the cup and slowly tilt it towards my mouth taking in a sip of that glorious lemony goodness. Liquid bliss for just those few moments as it passes through my lips and into my mouth. Man that was hot, but good, wonderful even. I move the steaming hot mug across the bed and lay it down on the bedside cabinet, trying not to knock over the flash-looking blue lamp. “Aww…!” I give an audible moan for turning my body to my side and moving the mug. That was not smart, the effects of the match still shooting through me. “Careful Dave…” Wendy says, obviously concerned about my well-being, “that was a really tough match, Dave, you need to rest up.” “Tell me about it!” “Well I’ll leave you be, you get some rest. I’ll take Ducky to the living room so he can watch some TV.” She walks to the bed and leans over to pick up Ducky, he’d go himself, but he’s got a bit of a handicap. He can’t actually move. As she leans forward her golden streaks of hair dangle down, a waterfall of beauty in front of my face. She lifts up Ducky and places him on her palm, then leans towards me and kisses me softly on the forehead. “Now you get some rest,” she whispers softly with her special smile appearing once more then turns and walks out of the room, closing the door quietly behind her. I attempt to turn my body once more for another dosage of Lemsip for me, but the pain is too much, I just can’t move that far over, so I lie back flat on my back. My word Jay hit me hard in that match… That’s not to say I didn’t hit him hard as well… we gave our all, every last effort, every last piece of energy left in our bodies, every last move in our repertoire, every last piece of skill we could muster, everything; all for the chance to continue to reach our dream. I remember as Jay clasped his arm around my neck and hoisted me into the air, using his other arm to clutch my legs. I remember wriggling, shuffling upside-down, trying desperately to escape from the move, but it wasn’t to happen. He pulled me down with a Fisherman Buster, my neck crumpling under the extreme force of the move. As I imagine the whole event unfolding once more in my head, I feel my neck once more as it folds under the pressure and impact of the devastating move. My neck burns up again and I let out a groan. I took a lot of punishment in that match. We both did. I don’t think I’ve been in anything like it in my entire career. It was a squash match, only for both of us. We were both going to be obliterated in the squared circle and there was nothing that was going to stop that. Not if I had anything to do with it, and not if Jay did either. I remember Jay kicking me repeatedly from side to side into my ribs and back, I remember the thud reverberating through my ribs and up my spine, I remember trying desperately to stop it from happening, but failing to block kick after kick after kick, the relentless pounding against my body that I just couldn’t take. I still feel the great soreness from my back and ribs from those kicks, remembering the turn of events just makes it ever the more excruciating. The critics after the match said that it was a comprehensive victory, but I still wonder how I got out of there in one piece. I felt like a beaten man after that match, in more ways than one. I was beaten to a pulp, I was beat up more than I’ve ever been beat up before, but somehow, some way I managed to pull down deep inside and gain the victory. I remember gaining momentum in the match, I remember Jay taking my arm and as he was about to wrench it, twisting the arm and spinning around his body, locking in a full nelson. I remember him sitting out to reverse it, but me leaning back placing a boot on each shoulder, and nearly gaining a pin fall. I had wrestled better than I had ever wrestled before, and it was for good reason. Jay is a great wrestler, someone I have always admired, one of the greatest wrestlers I’ve ever seen. It seemed strange that after all the times I’d watched in awe as he defeated every opponent that came his way that I would ever have to compete with him, but I did. I had always proclaimed myself as the greatest technical wrestler in the universe, but nobody ever took that seriously. I had never had the chance to prove to the world that I am an exceptional technical wrestler and in that match I think I showed everyone what I had. I remember us both being completely shattered. 45 minutes of sheer determination, defiance the only thing keeping us from falling flat on the floor. I remember Jay’s massive arms around my waist as he was about to hoist me into a German suplex, but me grabbing his hands, prizing them open with all my strength, spinning around behind him and falling back, rolling him into a bridged pin as the referee makes the count. 1… The count seemed to go on forever. I was begging for it to hurry, I was begging for it to just be over. 2… Jay struggled to escape the move, he struggled to gain a little bit of leverage to exit the move, I was desperate for the referee to just end it. Jay was so close to kicking out. And then it happened. The referee’s hand slapped the mat that one last time, the slap echoing through my ears, it was the sweetest sound I think I have ever heard. It feels so bad that I had to defeat him. My friend… One of my closest friends was trying to write the wrongs of GTT2 when he was cheated from what should have been his. He came back, you couldn’t have written it better in any novel, it was going to be Jay Phoenix in one last go. One last attempt at bringing back what was rightfully his and I had to take that away from him. It eats me up inside, it hurts even more than the physical pain from the match. I have taken away the dream of one of my closest friends. But he is in the same boat that I am. As much as this is his last chance at gaining the greatest trophy in all of wrestling, it is probably also mine. I remember the taunts before the match. I remember the people laughing at me on the train, and the kid at the train station calling me a joke. I remember the state I had gotten myself into, the drink, the destruction to all the lovely things Primetime Central had placed in my apartment as a gift for doing so well in the tournament. I remember thinking it was over. I remember declaring that I was never going to wrestle again, not against Jay. I never wanted to humiliate myself again. It seems silly now, but I would have never thought in a million years that I could have won that match. Just little old me, just little EDH, the comedy character that everybody took in for a laugh and a joke, a bit of comic relief for them to brighten up their day. There’s no way that somebody like me should have won that match, no way. But it’s funny how significance can be found from insignificant things. All you need is a little faith, a little faith from all the people around you can change you into something awesome. I remember just before the match, I was trying to preoccupy myself to keep my mind off of things, but I was leaving it all a little too late. I stood there in the hall-way with a plastic watering can, pouring some life-saving water into my plant. I remember Wendy turning to me “It’s just a plant, Dave. It’ll be there for you when you get back.” I remember putting the can down and walking out to the car to drive to the arena, leaving the plant there on its own. Oh no…! I pull myself out of bed, the pain shooting through me, but this time I just don’t care. I rush out of the room and into the corridor leading to the front door. I can’t believe I left it for so long, after the match I just forgot about it. There it stood, withered and weary. Its lush green leaves now dull crisped and brown. The plant was drooped over, completely lifeless, devoid of the liquid it needed so much. How could I have forgotten? I grab the watering can that still sits on the floor beside it, the water from before still present. Nothing had been near it for days. I pulled the can up and quickly poured water into the soil. “Sorry, little mate!” I cried pouring more of the water into it until the right amount had been given. “Dave…? What are you doing out of bed?” Wendy asked as she moved swiftly into the corridor. “I… look…! The plant… it’s dying…!” I scream to her desperately trying to get the words out. I just can’t believe it. This poor little thing is going to die and it’s all going to be my fault. “Oh no… I had completely forgotten about that. I’m sorry Dave.” She said softly. “It’s okay Wendy it’s not your fault. How could I have been so stupid? I took this plant in, I took it in as my own and I was going to bring it up to be a beautiful flower, but now it’s going to die. Now it’s going to just rot and die, and it’s all my fault.” “Don’t say that… there’s still time. If you water it regularly and give it extra care and attention, you might still save it yet.” She’s right. I might still save it yet. The fate of this plant is in my hands, with the aid of my watering can this plant may still live. I just need to give it my all, I need to have faith. This plant is going to live. I’ll make damned sure of it.
