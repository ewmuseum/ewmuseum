The Patriarch: Staring At The Sun

9 January 2006

Date: 2006-01-09 Cross the street from your storefront cemetery I’m here again, in the crypt that my father calls a home. The air is static with a lingering cold, an unconquerable cold that defies all clothing and reason. Sparsely decorated halls are the mark of a man who has few things to be proud of. Ettore Calvino doesn’t hang his military medals up on a wall in his own little Slaughter Of Innocents Hall of Fame. He knows better. I pass through the dining room on my way upstairs. The room is sterile, chased of any life that it once had, by a man who fights off a ripe loneliness in other families’ dining rooms. He’s probably never eaten a meal in this room before because eating alone is solitary at its core. It’s a telltale sign that you’re going to die alone. My father hasn’t yet accepted that he will die without a legacy; if I were to bear his name, I would be washing my hands in a bloody sink. And not the English euphemism "bloody," but the primal, carnivorous, red life force. Hear me hailing from inside and realize Up the creaking wooden stairs I go. Invading the fortress of solitude is slightly amusing; very few people have ever entered this upstairs study. A young Chandler Tsonda would’ve been forbidden to play in father’s study. In the silence upstairs, a door ajar is the only sign of humanity. Then again, maybe there’s none left in this house because he’s sucked all of it out with his tender phone calls to me on my birthdays. "Chandler, I hear that you’re eighteen now." His voice was lower then I remembered. Was there a hint of happiness? "I’m a man now, yes." That wasn’t entirely true. "I wanted to say congratulations. I’ll be sending a check in the mail." "I’ve wanted to say things all my life to you, but it all boils down to this: I despise you." A disaffected youth’s words still shoot through my head. I am the conscience clear In pain or ecstacy I traverse the forbidden city, meandering among meaningless legal documents and Christmas cards. Unlike the rest of the house, this room does not possess the immaculate barrier; this room has a soul to it. The spirit is warm and good-natured, but it’s his and I know that I must ignore it. The bulky desk takes up most of the room. I peek inside the top right drawer, clawing around for something familiar. There is no picture of him and my mother; there isn’t one of my baby pictures. There are only old driver’s licenses and unclaimed three dollar scratch ticket wins. And we were all weaned my dear Upon the same fatigue "Getting nostalgic?" He’s accusing already; having dozens of secrets stored somewhere in this room, my father’s question is blunter then intended, I think. "I was looking for you." We haven’t talked since my blow-up and for good reason: there’s nothing more to say. He doesn’t know that I’ve come to say my goodbyes and go focus on the things that need to be focused on. With my mind already racing with thoughts of Jimmy Bong and GTT5, I can’t be fending off my father at every corner as well. "This office…what’s it for?" "You know, just the odd paperwork. I like to have a place where I can collect my thoughts." That’s bullshit and he doesn’t know that I know it. Either my people-reading skills are from my mother or he’s fading in relative old age. "Fine, I don’t need to know the big Mafioso secrets anyway." There’s a reason to baiting him one final time: I want to walk out in a huff. If a true compromise is marked by the unhappiness of both parties, then the finale of this soap opera is bound to be a big compromise. "The house looks like shit." You’re staring at the sun Oh my own voice Cannot save me now "I’m never around." "Well, hey, there’s a common theme to your house and your parenting skills." "Please don’t." He’s pleading now. It’s sad the effect that I still have on him, after his betrayal. That’s why I’ll never have kids; they’re an undeniable weakness and I seek unimaginable strength. "I won’t. I'm giving up on this failed experiment." He’s not pleading anymore. What’s wrong? This should be his moment of prayer, begging me to stay. And nothing? Just silence? I had him pegged as more of a fighter. Standing in the sea It’s just One more breath And then I’ll go down "For the record, the failed experiment is me coming to see you. I have a tournament to win back home." Maybe it’s not the truth, since the fact is that the tournament is drawing closer by the day and I’m not totally focused. And if I happen to lose? The dagger of irony sticks in deep since this little detour will be to blame for my unprepared state. "I saw. Jimmy Bong is your opponent. Good luck then." "Yeah, you too." And then it’s done. Without any of your sappy movie melodrama, I collect the few things I brought with me and leave. * * * * Your mouth is open wide And now it’s time to get down to business. It’s only a couple of days until I have to face off against Jimmy Bong and considering the magnitude of this losing streak I’m on, beating Bong is just about the only thing that can stop me from opening fire with an AK-47 into a daycare center. "Channy!" The lover is inside As the old saying goes, home is wherever the reliable poontang is. It’s not ten seconds into my triumphant that she, dubbed by her month, is upon me. "Oh my God did I miss you!" January is the clingy girl. I swear it’s not a power trip that I like to start the year with the groupie chick. She tries to kiss me and she tastes like her breakfast. I have to pull away as her tongue, with shards of banana still attached. She has clearly been eating the cereal with sliced bananas. I fucking hate the sliced bananas. "Listen, I had a long ass day." I put on the best charade I can. After all, if I don’t make it to the first of February with this one, then it’s not even a fling. It’s just several dozen one-night stands in a row. She’s been staying at my apartment since almost New Year’s. That’s just how it ends up working. It’s better for me if someone is watching my shit and it’s better for her if she has consistent access to my love wand. No joke, she’s horny as fuck; I’m pretty sure it has nothing to do with me, although she was a fan before she was mine. So, just in case you thought I was tooting my own horn…I’m just along for the ride here. And all the tumult’s done Collided with the sign She buys the excuses and lets me get some sleep. In my dream, Jimmy Bong kills my father. I’m not sure what the symbolic meaning of this is, but I wake up feeling like I haven’t slept a lick. I look in the mirror and I look like death, sans warmed over. January is already up…I don’t need to tell you what she’s eating, but her morning affections assault me all at once. I’m not sure I’ll make it to February alive with the banana kisses and the pet names. You’re staring at the sun You’re standing in the sea Your body’s over me Bong is probably hot on the trail right now. He’s the lovable goofball with a ridiculous back story that the fans soak up at every chance. And what’s more disconcerting is that he doesn’t seem remotely concerned about the match. On a sliding scale of craziness, he’s crossed into the bounds of "what the fuck I don’t even know how to respond to what you do because it’s so goddamn crazy…I mean GODDAMN you are just too crazy." Yeah, it’s crazy. I know. But before I can get crackin’, I’m grabbed by ADD (no, not Meanstreak’s old finisher, the debilitating and/or made-up disease afflicting half of the United States or no one, depending on who you ask). That chair near the TV looks mighty exciting and Sportscenter is on. It’s not like I’m a writer on a deadline; I can spend the entire afternoon looking up everything there is to know about Jimmy Bong. By midnight tonight, I’ll have my eyes on the prize. Note the truth because The dirt is temporary The apartment is empty now, with January out to do errands. I stretch lazily on the chair, finding that my hamstring is tighter then usual. Wrestling is looming again on my mind, which usually signals that it’s time to go back to sleep. I’m crawling in dormancy; I need a break from it. * * * * More to mine than fact face Name and monetary Back at home, Ettore Calvino is longing for a smoke. At first, the smoking was a slight indulgence, but now it’s a ruthless persuasion. No matter the amount of awful things his son does to him, he will always feel something towards Chandler Tsonda. He doesn’t know what emotions there are left to feel. His son is as foreign a concept as calculus to him. Sorting through the miscellaneous scatter on the desk, Calvino finds his pack of smokes, but tosses them aside. Finding a more urgent matter, he goes diving into the venerable paper stacks, leaving a trail of dust and mess everywhere his hands don’t meet their aim. Beat the skins and let the Loose lips kiss you clean He finds solace in the form of an otherwise unremarkable packet of white papers. Legal mumbo jumbo litters the first few pages, with notarized signatures scrawled indolently. Calvino’s eyes scan the meaningless litter of words, obviously looking for a recognizable turn of phrase. And there it is, as clear as the day it was written. Quietly pour out like light Like light, like answering the sun "So, sir, if you acknowledge that all the changes within are correct as of today, December 4th, 1993, you can sign on the line." Ettore was still shaking with anger and pride and too many things to mention over the phone conversation with his son. Having talked with Chandler, Ettore had decided a change was needed in his last will and testament. "I’m just taking in the finality of it all, one moment please." Regret was swirling in his mind, but he had to make a decision, one that he wouldn’t look back on. He knew that after today’s events, that he would never be a father in Chandler’s eyes. A bead of sweat inched down his broad forehead towards hair that was still full of black shine. His lawyer was waiting. He moved swiftly and deliberately with the pen, with a confident signature that arched to his want, twirling away the fate of his earthly possessions…when the time came, at least. Ettore’s eyes were beginning to wet. It was a bittersweet day, changing the fate of his son like that. As much as what he was doing hurt him to do, it was the only way to do things. He had already fulfilled his legal responsibility to the child. With this day, there was no longer any link between them. Your mouth is open wide You're trying hard to breathe The lawyer saw Calvino’s emotion and tried to sympathize, patting the man on the back, but Calvino responded fiercely, abruptly jerking his arm away. "Please, leave me alone." Calvino’s tone was inflexible and vindictive, much like his son’s would be in the very same room thirteen years later. How could this lawyer help but judge him by these actions, though? The man had stuck by him, despite Ettore’s insistence on being generally difficult and unhelpful. But there was no remedy for the maladies that ailed Ettore Calvino. Love had pledged him to thirteen red and white stripes; neither heart nor soul nor body could have ever stayed in Hanoi to reciprocate the feelings of a teenage girl. And in turn, he could never have been the father to his son. The water's at your neck Your body's over me Ettore’s eyes are dry as he reads over the main clause of the will: -Of my earthly possessions, monetary and otherwise, I bequeath entirely to my son Chandler Tsonda. * * * * Be what you will And then throw down your life Oh it's a damned fine game And we can play all night I can’t find the emotion in my today to figure out what it takes to beat Jimmy Bong, because all day today and for the past two weeks have been spent destroying myself. I’ve rid myself of my father in my life and for once in my life there’s a weight off my shoulders. I’m a man and a wrestler and I don’t know if it’s in that order any more. Tomorrow, I’ll make decisions about being a wrestler, but today I’m done being a man. There is no need for him to reminisce on what the medal means, only to feel it between his fingers one more time. Without another thought on the matter, he shoves the medal back into the desk, back among family secrets and lies of great depth. * * * * The water's at your neck Your body's over me A young man climbed over the hill, wiping profuse perspiration on his pants. His black hair clamped onto his forehead, causing him to swipe at it as it sloppily fell from out underneath his helmet. After a couple more steps, he plucked the helmet from off his head. In the jungle heat, it was impossible to continue on with standard military equipment. He looks back over his shoulder at the valley. In the shady hills leading up the mountains outside Hanoi, he had found an oasis. However, duty called. He could not spend another day among the kind people; he had to make it to headquarters. Someone had to convince the generals that this battle was futile. One day, he would come back and live among these people, but until then…he turned towards Hanoi, spying the city’s outer limits. It was savagely beautiful. He grasped in his right hand both Vietnamese and American flags.
