The Last Battle

27 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-09-27 ‘All life does is end.’ it says, it’s footsteps being heard clacking upon the ground and echoing through the chamber. ‘Life isn’t defined by success, nor by rapture, honour or kindness. What is obvious to me, and has been for years, is that instead it is defined by sin and suffering and sorrow.’ His eyes open and sight floods him, drowning the sounds that he took in moments before. Desecrated and debased bodies hung from hooks in the ceiling where those with faces still attached glare at him lifelessly and emptily, frozen in agony, some dripping with blood and others long since bled dry. ‘…By chaos.’ The red light fills the room with a sickening closeness and the stench is unbearable. Deszaras looks around disgustedly at the various knives and saws and sharp, pointy objects that glint at him contemptuously. One of the bodies swings gently, its rusted hook-chain groaning as it rocks and the sound rasps painfully against Dez’s mind. ‘How a man measures his life,’ continues the daemon, ‘during his solitude is what really counts in the world, since these hours are the ones that make the mind what it is. Those that deny themselves time alone or time for thought also deny themselves catharsis and understanding. Most people that we have encountered try to drown out their thoughts with debauchery or conversation or drugs or passion… and this is why they are not true men. This is why we prevail more times than not. We understand. They hide.’ Ignoring the grim seen of destroyed carcasses around him, Deszaras glares at the beast. ‘You have nothing more than lies to give, filth.’ ‘No,’ it bites back, a sonic boom through the hall of death, ‘dreams don’t lie.’ ‘But they can confuse.’ Dhar walks from the darkness, his footfalls hurting Deszaras’ ears once more, pushing past hanging corpses and squeaking trolleys with body parts upon them as he goes. His Kabuki mask creaks and cracks into shape as its smile splits open, wider than any human orifice is capable of and his forked tongue protrudes from the mouth-hole to sample the damp air. ‘Passion lies. Debauchery distracts. Drugs are filth and language is a disease. We live in a world of warriors, and barely a single one understands life or battle. War should be treated with fear and respect. Not childish lust and dreams of becoming king. These people must be taught that to strive to be a king, first one should strive to be a man.’ Dhar jumps up onto a table in front of Deszaras, lifting a severed arm as it sits down and waves it at him playfully. ‘Just look at this Kae piece of shit. He’s a fucking joke, yet he is convinced that he will walk into battle with us and walk out holding his head high and holding his dick in both hands.’ ‘We can only hope, then, that he sees the err of his arrogant ways.’ Dhar’s smirk dies away abruptly. ‘Hope is the first step on the road to disappointment, you sarcastic fuck. Battle should be revered, not scoffed at or used as an ego boost.’ ‘A true warrior fights for honour. A true warrior fights every battle with the same knowledge in his heart. That every battle is your last. It is the one that will steal your life. To treat combat any less seriously would be certain death.’ he says. ‘A real warrior fights every battle as if it was his last.’ The daemon chuckles theatrically and cocks his head, looking around him at the twisted ruins of flesh… at his handiwork. ‘I’m going to slaughter Kae.’ it states coldly. ‘Then expect me to do things to the remains in front of his relatives.’ As he finishes, he smirks once more as, in unison, the corpses all begin to moan and shudder and scream in pain, rattling their chains or noisily slapping their wet, bloody flesh upon stone or wood. All of a sudden, his hearing is swept away in a tide of confusion and agony as he feels like he’s drowning in the ocean, struggling to survive. ‘Don’t you see?’ continues Dhar as if nothing had transpired. ‘I am what the PTC needs. We are what the PTC needs.’ Deszaras confusedly looks around, seeing that he has returned to the slaughterhouse. ‘As it is in life, the older generation and antiquated inhabitants resent the new blood because they fear change. In the world children are becoming worse and worse. Each new wave of life brings more violence, ignorance, haughtiness, intolerance, hostility and hatred into a world already overflowing with such things.’ ‘And this is a good thing to you?’ replies Dez, trying his best to sound surprised… but he just can’t manage it anymore when Dhar is concerned. ‘Of course it is.’ says Dhar corrosively. ‘All it is, is human nature finally punching and tearing its way out of its cowardly shell after aeons. It’s what makes our species so superior to everything else in existence. We kill and we hate. They are the only things that we do well, and every new generation that enters the world becomes better and better at those things, having more potential to cause chaos and pain. Why should the world of PTC be any different? What makes them special? Why should they escape what the rest of the world cannot? It’s a new playground for us, Deszaras,’ the daemon snarls vaingloriously. ‘And we’re the new bully.’ ‘One that got his arse handed to him by a girl on our last venture here.’ bites the Resident Evil in riposte. ‘Do you forget that we have been here before? This is nothing new. I fear that there is nothing new left for us to experience.’ His sentence ends, and then his mind crashes, and he’s back under water. Though, this time, he isn’t simply thrashing around… something else is instead. He is dead still, strangling the thing. ‘Fucking wimp!’ screams the beast angrily. ‘Are you so determined to deny yourself that which is yours simply because you’re too afraid of being content?’ Enraged, Dhar overturns the table with a roar of vehemence. ‘Why the fuck does everybody talk about the PTC as if it were some fucking killing floor where the big vicious dogs rip little pups to bloody shreds. Well fuck that! Just like when you were a child, you listened to people call you useless, worthless and insignificant and you fucking believed them. You made it true with your brainless acceptance created through fright of trying to make things better and failing. Through fear of fighting for fear of defeat.’ The thing speedily approaches Deszaras, its mask set in a scowl. ‘The defeats that you have let yourself take, for fear of receiving worse ones, will last lifetimes over you. …And unfortunately, you’re only allowed to have one. You destroyed yours. And you still refuse to try to fix what is broken inside you.’ The air is ripped from his lungs and searing pain shoots through his head as once more he returns to the liquid as it fills his lungs and ears and mouth, his eyes burning as he looks at his hand on the throat of the man before him, his other hand drawn back in a fist behind his ear. ‘Pathetic.’ Dhar’s voice, or the frequent nightmarish reverie begins to hurt Dez’s head. ‘Pathetic little man.’ It raises its bloodstained hands to Deszaras’ face. ‘Look. Today, even I am more human than you are.’ ‘This isn’t about humanity and never has been. And you call me stupid?’ ‘Often.’ The rain hits his head and the balled fist that stabs forward and back with a judder as it hits its target repeatedly. Incensed and growling, eyes widened dementedly, he knows that they’re grabbing him and shouting at him. He knows he should stop. But he can’t. He can’t feel or hear them. He can’t hear a thing… until Dhar’s satirical whispering assaults his calm again. ‘In the nineteen-twenties and thirties,’ it says, ‘the German people became civilised and tamed, culturally obsessed with fine details in science and society. Their self-image of audacity and courage was both soothing and subjugation. They were required to behave as if they were stalwart, even when they were not. This is why they were so easily manipulated by the Third Reich. The leader of their republic was devoutly religious, was plastered all over magazines as man of the year… shit, he was even a choir boy.’ Deszaras doesn’t move, transfixed upon the victim beneath him whose identity he cannot even remember. The man’s blood drips from his fist. ‘…An actual choir boy, mind you.’ States the daemon sarcastically. ‘The German nation as a whole had lost its mettle, making them ripe for said manipulation. They thought they had freedom, and they thought they were safe from tyranny.’ It laughs. ‘Freedom needs to feel fear in order to work. You were a victim when you had no mind of your own – when you were a child. That’s how I know now that one must instil fear to gain control. But the German people of that time didn't want to lose their pretentious pall of courage. So when they were confronted with a situation that demanded individual fortitude, in the form of a government gone wrong, the German people simply pretended that the situation didn’t exist. And in that simple self-deception lay the ruin of an entire nation and the coming of one of the most memorable wars in history.’ Deszaras, frozen in his rage and confusion, still cannot move, though he tries. Still the hateful thing speaks. ‘We are an excellent speaker, Deszaras. So was Hitler. He knew he ruled a nation of cowards, and knew he had to spend the money to make the new war something that cowards could fight and win. He decorated his troops with regalia to make them proud of themselves, further trapping them in their façade of bravery. Hitler mimicked the parade regalia of ancient Rome to remind the Germans of the defeat of the legions at the Tuetenberg Forest. Talismans were added from orthodox religions and the occult to fill the soldiers with delusions of mystical strengths and an afterlife if they fell in battle. Finally, knowing that it takes courage to kill an enemy face to face, Hitler spent vast sums of money on weapons like planes, submarines, long range artillery, the world's first cruise missile and the world's first guided missile, weapons that could be used to kill at a distance, so that those doing the killing need not have to face the reality of what they were doing. The German people were lured into war not because they were as debonair or as tenacious as they pretended to be, but because they were cowards who wanted to be seen as brave, and found that shooting long range weapons at people they could not see took less nerve than standing up to Hitler. Sent into battle by that false image of courage, the Germans were dependent on their weapons to do the fighting for them. When the weapons stopped working, the Germans lost the war. Just like us. Our Covenant has been useless thus far in helping us win our war on life. Maybe, as you think, we need a new war. But maybe we just need new weapons with which to fight. Maybe we need to develop the courage to fight our enemy in the trenches. Maybe we need to treat every single battle as if it was our last.’ ‘I…’ Deszaras tries to speak, but he can’t find words in him anymore. He can’t remember why he is destroying this man’s face… nor even where he is, how he got here, why he’s here or what day it is. He feels so empty that he is scared of himself. ‘Look at you. A fucking blind man could see you for what you really are.’ The daemon’s eyes blaze with intent. ‘What kind of a man are you, Deszaras? What kind of a fucking partner would you make? What kind of a father would you make for this nameless, faceless little brat of yours? Is it any wonder that bitch kept you in the dark about this shit?’ ‘Shut up.’ ‘You were a worthless son.’ ‘Shut up.’ ‘You were a worthless brother.’ ‘I…’ ‘And you are a worthless man. Now you hide within yourself to try to avoid the shame of being a bad father as well.’ The statement makes him think too much, makes him question himself. Aren’t these things true? The suspicion eats at him like cancer. ‘Think of all you’ve missed out on because of your consternation. You missed seeing your child for the first time and holding it close. You missed the joy that it would bring. You’ve missed so much of its life… you’ve missed so much of your own. Of your sisters’. Of your family’s. Your mind.’ This, he thinks, must be how dying begins. ‘And this is how your world ends, Deszaras. Your world of honour… because there is none in reality. The only way that the world keeps on spinning is because of lies. Manipulation. That’s why we surround ourselves with hapless, expendable allies. Go back to your verminous hiding place, recreant.’ His shocked, hurt and confused glare darts towards his bloodied fist and then to the stunned faces of the onlookers and the frowning, wearied looks from the security guards as the sickening stench of blood fills the air around him. The noise rushes in all at once, where before he didn’t even think of listening to the world around him, since his soul had been dwelling elsewhere for a while. Screams, groans, shouting, gasps… and an unusually loud dripping sound. He looks down, only to see the man’s blood trickle off his cheek and tap against the floor, yet the sound seems like it’s magnified in his ear drums. Boom. Boom. Boom. ‘Do not fear any more,’ the daemon’s whisper reappears, cutting the air like the peel of a bell and hurting Dez’s head once more. ‘You will know torment for a while longer yet.’ He looks around him once more, and now it all comes rushing back. The Headstrong signing session… the guy had approached, mouthing off, Dez performed his usual smart-arse routine. The man threw something at him and Deszaras… Deszaras just hit him. And hit him. Again. And again. And again. Security had tried to stop him, but that simply wasn’t going to happen. ‘Now do you remember, child?’ asks Dhar. ‘Do you remember why we hate everyone so? Homo Sapiens… “The thinking species”. Even those that named our kind had a sense of humour. …Then again, maybe it was just blind arrogance. Or maybe they simply underestimated everything else on Earth. All things die under Heaven, boy. All things. We are nothing more than a periapt to depict how things should be. The patron saint of understanding.’ ‘…You talk shit, Dhar.’ Retorts Deszaras harshly. ‘And lots of it.’ He pushes the security guards away from him and begins to stagger off. ‘If all of this… this anger, this reaction and this illusion has been for the sole purpose of forcing me to destroy a parochial piece of shit, then you needn’t have bothered.’ he grates, the heat of fury flooding his veins. ‘That fucker Kae has had his beating coming for a long, long time. It’s past due. But then, our beating’s way past due as well.’ He spits on the man lying on the ground with malice. ‘Horace Walpole once said “life is a comedy for those who think and a tragedy for those who feel. Living through both at the same time is none too comfortable. My life has been a story about hope. Failed hope. For it is such a thing that… well… the more you become familiar with hope, the less beautiful it becomes. Dhar snarls. ‘In this world, for you, there is no beauty left.’ ‘We’ll see.’ replies Deszaras as he walks away from the carnage as if nothing had happened at all. ‘Hope is not the only thing that seems beautiful. There is still danger left to explore. Pain. Hate and love. My world is not a place where people win… but a place where everybody loses. My world is a place of aimlessness.’ Then, for some reason, he dives into the pool of reverie once more. A memory. He remembers the sounds first; the creaking of the stairs under his footfalls and the whine of the old hinges on the door as he gingerly pushed it open with his small, eight-year-old hands. Then the sights flood in. Whiskey bottles, some empty and some full scattered all over the table, the floor covered in glass shards and remnants of over-turned and thrown picture frames or ornaments. He looked around bewilderedly as a record player spun on, crackling, the music having ended a long time ago. He shuffled through the mess, magazines, newspapers, broken things and stains and lifted the needle from the record, as if draining the room from the only life it had, forcing silence upon its soul. Each second here is another lesson in the education of desolation. He reached past a full ashtray to lift the picture that had fallen, or had been thrown down, and looked upon the seemingly happy faces of his parents on their wedding day. As he set the photograph down he looked around at the devastation and thought about how when a ship sinks, all of the rats aboard it float to the top. Now, he thinks dipping back into the pond of reality, it’s time to sink ships. He thinks about how easy it would be to go home and dive straight into a bottle of Jagermeister or Wild Turkey or… or any of the other poisons with which he infects himself regularly. …Too regularly. He thinks and realises that it would be too easy to stop. Then the icy hand of fear grips him by the throat yet again, and he pauses. His eyes open . The bottle in his hand is empty.
