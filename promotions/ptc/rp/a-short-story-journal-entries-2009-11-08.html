A Short Story: Journal Entries

8 November 2009

Date: 2009-11-08 NOW Camaraderie. That wasn't the word Jason Snow used when he told me that he wasn't going to choose anyone from PRIME as his second round opponent, but the sentiment was there. Truth be told, Devin Shakur was the only guy in the tournament I actually got along with. I guess it helps that he's probably the only guy that I haven't beaten in PRIME. We haven't actually had a match, so there's no real way to say one way or the other which one of us would win. Still, I have a tendency to brag about the wins I have over people. The people I've beaten don't like that very much. It was one of the reasons I asked Snow to pick me as his opponent. I figured I'd dangle that tasty nugget in front of him like a carrot and see if he'd bite. I wanted to use the momentum I had to get past the odds on favorite to win our bracket, then worry about the rest later. Just my luck, Snow caught a case of team spirit and chose to face Lance Marshall instead. It was no secret that members of the PRIME and Sin City rosters do not get along, almost getting blanked by the Sinners does not sit well with a PRIMEate. We're kind of like a family in that way; we may not get along, but if you pick on one you better believe the rest of us will be close behind. Of course, it turns out a new member of Sin City's roster had the same idea. James von Doom is also a seeded contestant in GTT, and therefore had his choice of opponent for the second round. Of course, instead of choosing a member of the PRIME roster to try and eliminate; he chose to have us take out one of our own. It was a good strategy, one I would have used myself if given the chance. Of course, I wasn't given that chance. Instead, I have the pleasure of facing off against the other man that wasn't chosen by a seed. I get to face off against the monster known as Hessian. Again. THEN He stood next to the bed, a genuine smile on his face as he stared into the eyes of the woman that he loves. They had been married for almost a year now. They became a family about an hour ago. Arriana Violette Gambillini was in her mother's loving arms, swaddled in a hospital provided blanket in order to keep her warm. He had been distant these last few days, his mind filled with worry over the abduction of his Goddaughter, Adelina DeAngelis. His attempt at mending the bridges that had collapsed when Michael's envy had blinded him from the fact that family always came before the family came to an abrupt end when someone kidnapped her. He had just been with her less than an hour before they snatched her in broad daylight, it was the moment he realized that he could not protect everyone all the time. Salvatore Fennucci, the man Tony entrusted the family business to, had promised to use every resource available to track down the people responsible. It had been two weeks since she was abducted, and the police had no clues that could tie anyone to the crime. Everything seemed to be planned to perfection, right down to the tire marks left behind at the scene leading to a brand of tires very popular in the upper east coast. Still, none of that made him as uneasy about the situation than the unexpected visit from Cynthia's father earlier in the day. "I'm glad you took the time to see me," Daniel Berkman stood at a window in Gamble's living room, he had thought about what he was going to say the whole ride over, and the words still struggled to come out. Instead, he was left with this awful small talk that Tony could probably see right through. He really wished he could have Cassel here with him, but Jake was right when he told him that this needed to be discussed on a more personal level between the two men. He tentatively agreed. "Cynthia told me you've been on a pretty hectic schedule lately." "I've been close to yanking my hair out a few times." Tony poured his father in law a mixture of scotch and soda – heavy on the scotch. "But I'm not the one out there kissing hands and shaking babies." The ice breaker did exactly what it was supposed to do, as Daniel released a chuckle as he reached for his drink. "I wish I could say I've been following the race, but I've been running around from city to city these last few days and have barely been able to tell Cynthia that I love her before I'm rushing out the door again." "Things have been going really well," Daniel welcomed the warmth of the scotch as it filled the back of his throat before he swallowed. "Kind of why I'm here, actually." "I don't know if I'd be much help," Tony pointed to his face with open palms. "Not many people like this face." "It would probably help more than you know," Daniel stifled a laugh. "Thing is, that's not really what I needed to talk to you about." "Please," Tony motioned toward the couch as he made his way to the recliner. "I'm not really sure how to bring this up without sounding like an asshole." Tony's eyes widened a bit by the comment, his eyebrows peaked with more than interest. "We both know I had my objections to you marrying my daughter, but I'd like to think we've grown past that." Tony offered a nod in silent acknowledgement, but there were no other words exchanged on the subject before Daniel decided to continue. "As busy as I have been, I didn't find out about DeAngelis' daughter until a few days after it happened." There was something in his voice that made Tony shift in his seat. He wasn't sure whether it was the tone or the way he worded it, but something was off and he didn't like it. Things took a turn for the worst when Daniel pulled an envelope out of his inner coat pocket and tossed it on the table in front of Tony. "Someone sent me those." Tony's hand trembled just enough that it made him look in Berkman's direction. It didn't look like he had noticed, but Berkman hadn't been promoted to his position for being a bad detective. Tony took a hold of the envelope and nearly lost his breath when he saw the first photo. "They say you're responsible for her disappearance." Tony closed his eyes briefly and allowed a deep sigh to resonate from his lungs. "Is it true?" He knew why his father in law would ask the question, he knew why he needed, to ask it, but it still managed to hurt him more than he would allow to be seen. "She's like a daughter to me, Dan." Tony leaned forward in his seat and placed his drink on a coaster that Cynthia said would keep their glasses from sliding across the smooth mirror surface of the coffee table. "You know why I have to ask." Tony nodded curtly, his lips twisted into as much of a frown as he could muster. He rested his elbows on the armrests of the recliner as his fingers interlocked a few inches above his lap, then said something he hoped he would never regret. "All my leads have turned into dead ends. Have your people been able to turn up any leads?" "You're looking at them." The bureau had him pegged as a suspect in Adelina's abduction, and photographic evidence to back up the claims. He was the last person she was seen with, and a coffee shop full of witnesses would be able to take the stand and say that he tried his best to avoid eye contact with them. He had tried to avoid the cavalcade of autograph seekers that would have hounded him for his signature for hours, but only found the welcoming arms of tabloid headlines that awaited him if this information was leaked. "You can't be serious?" "Unfortunately, I am," Berkman leaned forward and placed his empty glass on the table, not as worried about the fact that there was no coaster to protect the table's surface. "So the fact that these pictures magically appeared doesn't strike you as odd?" "Of course it does. Why do you think I'm the one here asking you questions?" the look on Daniel's face as his eyes narrowed and his brow furrowed were evidence that he was at least a little offended by the remark. Still, he grabbed a couple of the photos off of the table and holds them up in front of him. "These pictures give my agents a very good reason to drag your ass in and grill you like some common street punk. But something just doesn't sit right about this, and I wanted to make sure that your history with DeAngelis doesn't cloud anyone's judgment." "So the fact that having your son in law be the main suspect in a kidnapping while you're trying to win an election has nothing to do with this?" The slight hesitation said more than his answer, but Daniel offered one anyway. "Not as much as you'd think." "So then," Tony leaned back in the seat. Despite the casualness of his demeanor, the awkwardness of the situation they found themselves in still weighed heavily on his mind. "What do we do now, because we both know someone is trying to screw over both of us with this." "You think that's what this is all about?" "Not all of it," Tony shook his head slightly. "But there's a reason those photos were sent to you and not the local authorities, and I doubt it was common courtesy." "They want to see what I do with the evidence against you." "Seems like they set me up in order to set you up." "So what do we do now?" "Well, we're eventually going to need to find out who is behind this, but right now..." his voice trailed off as he turned his attention toward the view from his window. "You're going to have to take me in." "What?" "It's the only way to keep this from spiraling out of control." "Cynthia would never forgive me," Daniel said, as he ran his fingers through his hair and let out a deep sigh. "There has to be another way." "There is no other way," Tony Gamble - the voice of reason. "If you do this now, you come off as the man that isn't going to take it easy on anyone. That alone could bump you up in the polls, and it severs any control these people would have over you." "I don't know..." his voice trailed off as a lump stuck in his throat "It has to be done," Gamble walked over and placed his hands on the back of his father in law. "You let me worry about Cynthia." He wasn't sure how he was going to react once the baby was born, but a tear streaked a trail down his cheek and puddled in the corner of his scarred lip as he looked into the light brown eyes of his little girl. It would be rather difficult to deny that what little heart people thought he had, had melted away the moment he heard her first scream. It was a high pitched shriek that seemed to last forever, but actually lasted for less than twenty seconds. "She has your eyes," he told his wife as he traced a finger along the cheek of his newborn child. Cynthia had recovered well, but she could only manage a faint smile as the medications she had taken to ease her pain had yet to wear off completely. Nine hours of labor after forty weeks of what Tony assured her were dropkicks had taken their toll on the woman, but there was no way she was going to release her hold on the bundle of joy she had wrapped up in her arms. "I love you," he whispered as he leaned forward and kissed Cynthia gently on her forehead. "I need to talk to your dad real quick, but I'll be right back. Okay?" There was something going on that she couldn't put her finger on, but Tony and her father had acted strange the moment she waddled into the room and told them her water had broke. She had no clue as to what it was, so she just smiled out of the side of her mouth and nodded her head slightly. "Can you get me some ice chips on the way back?" she added, as the weight of the world appeared to hang from her eyelids. "Yeah, sure," he replied as the door closed shut behind him. He hesitated a moment, unsure whether he should check to make sure she heard, then shook his head and continued on down to the waiting area. There was so much going on all at once, and as much as he wanted to help Berkman out with the issue with the photographs; there was no way he was going to leave Cynthia alone with the baby anytime soon. He hoped Daniel would understand that. "How is she doing?" “She's doing fine. You can go in and see her if you'd like.” It was Cynthia's mother that had asked the question. He remembered the first day he met his wife's parents, quick to notice the way Cynthia resembled her mother more than her father, but had her father's demeanor and instinct. He had to remind himself that Evelyn Berkman was his mother in law, because she was quite attractive for a woman her age. Time had been good to her, along with the countless hours she spent in the gym, and Tony was sure he was not the only person to ever notice that. “Hey Dan,” Tony grabbed him by the arm as he started to walk with his wife. “Can I talk to you real quick?” “Yeah, sure.” Daniel Berkman turned to his wife and nodded for her to go on ahead. “I'll be right there, honey.” “Thanks,” Tony watched Evelyn power walk in the direction of Cynthia's room, her maternal instinct had kicked in and she wanted to be there to comfort her daughter in what she knew to be a very stressful moment. “Look, Tony,” Daniel began, but Tony was quick to cut him off. “Hold on,” Tony took another glance down the hall before he continued. “She's gone,” Daniel assured him. “Just had to make sure.” “I understand.” “About what I said earlier-” “Don't worry about it,” Daniel interrupted him this time, then added a pat on the shoulder to ease the tension that had been building. “You don't think I'm going to arrest my son in law on the birth of his first child, do you?” “But the photos?” “We'll figure something out.” “Thank you,” Tony reached for Daniel's hand, but he only took it to pull Gamble in closer. “What's family for?” “Speaking of family,” Tony pulled away from the embrace the two shared, and swallowed nervously. “I gave Cynthia my word that I was leaving the DeAngelis family, and I have.” “But?” “But they can get places your agents cannot. They haven't been able to come up with anything.” “I remember you mentioning that earlier.” “Yeah,” Tony remembered he had let that bit of information slip during the discussion the two had back at his house. “But the pictures open up a whole new can of worms. I'm gonna call my connection and see if he can find something out about that.” “You do what you think you need to do,” Daniel patted him once more on the shoulder. “I'm going to go meet my granddaughter.” “I'm gonna make that call real quick,” Tony said as Berkman started down the hall. “I'll meet you back in the room. Tell Cynthia I'm getting her ice chips.” “Alright.” Tony watched Berkman hurry off to his daughter's side. All he had to do now was find a pay phone and make the call. Hopefully Sal wasn't too busy. Salvatore Fennucci had been friends with Antonio Gambillini since Tony was knee high to a grasshopper, and the two had been through enough over the years for Sal to know what needed to be done whenever Tony set his mind to something. Fennucci was the man Tony entrusted the family to when Michael DeAngelis went 'missing', and it was for good reason - the man brought results. Salvatore walked through the empty warehouse in even strides, a man on either side of him. Frankie Carino paced him on the left, a baseball bat rustled against his blue Adidas track suit with every step he took. Michael Bastelli was on the opposite side, a slick smile that appeared to be drawn on with permanent marker played on the corner of his lips. "Over here, boss." The three men turned their heads in the direction of the voice that called out to them. Nicolae Palmetta stood there with a nub of an unlit cigar jammed between his lips. He wiped his hands on his blood stained white tee shirt, then waved the trio over to where he was. The layers upon layers of dust scattered across the floor kicked up under their feet, the only proof that anyone had even been there. "How's it going, Nico?" Sal nodded as he passed the portly fellow that scratched at the scruff of his beard. "Could be better." "Fuck happened to this guy," Bastelli pointed at the naked man seated in the center of the room. The man's chin rested against his chest as blood trickled down and pooled in his lap. Frankie the Felon grabbed the man by his hair and lifted his head to take a look at him. He released his hold on the tangled mess of hair and watched his head flop around like a bobblehead doll. "Looks like someone stuck his face in a fuckin meat grinder," Frankie chuckled, possibly the only one amused by his failed attempt at humor. "You should be one to talk." "Fuck you, Mike," Frankie said as he turned to where Bastelli stood. Michael's hands were stuffed in his pockets as he shrugged his shoulders nonchalantly. The fact that Michael's face was as rough as a piece of leather was not lost on anyone, the scarred skin on the right side of his face was something he was self conscience about regardless of how long it had been since it happened. Bastelli still had nightmares of the car bomb that nearly took his life, and while he was glad to still be alive he couldn't shake the fact that his face had been burned pretty badly by the blast. "How bout bof'a you guys shut the fuck up." Bastelli and Carino turned their attention to the man that looked as if he worked in a slaughterhouse. Nico pulled the unlit cigar from his lips and spit to his left. The frustration evident on his face. After the last few hours being wasted trying to get the sorry excuse of a man to give up any information he might have, the last thing he wanted to do was listen to these two fools act like an old married couple. "Come on, Nico," Mike smiled as he extended his arms in feign innocence. "We're just fuckin around, you know." "You sound like a coupl'a old ladies," Sal ended his silence. He stood in front of the yet to be identified man, his arms crossed behind his back as he contemplated the next course of action. "Sorry, boss." Frankie lowered his head and took a few steps away from the man that seemed to struggle to take a breath. "Sorry?" Fennucci followed Frankie with his eyes, which had narrowed when his brow furrowed in anger. "Do you think sorry is the last word this spook wants to hear before he dies? You two have been running your damn mouths since we walked in here." Frank and Mike did everything they could to avoid eye contact with Fennucci. It was clear that they understood their position in this hierarchy, and it was nowhere near the top. Nico just shook his head as he stuck the saliva moistened end of the cigar back between the grip of his lips. "Nico," Sal turned to face his enforcer turned interrogator. "Why don't you go get cleaned up while we let these two take out the trash." "Sounds good ta me." he flicked his fingers under his chin - in the direction of the duo - before he turned and headed out of the room. "Get rid of this son of a bitch," Sal waved nonchalantly toward the man. "Make sure you send them niggers a message. No one muscles in on our neighborhood without paying the price." “Gotcha, boss.” “I know just where to leave him too.” “Good, make it as messy as possible.” Sal nodded toward the two men just as the phone in his inside coat pocket started to vibrate. He pulled it out and glanced at the caller ID on the outside display of the phone before he gave Frankie and Michael one final nod and flipped open the phone. “Hello?” He smiled as he walked out of the room. The sound the man made echoed through the entire building, but Sal didn't even glance back. “Nah, I'm not busy at all.” “She's so beautiful.” “She has her father's smile,” Cynthia said as Tony walked into the room. Evelyn Berkman had Arriana Gambillini cradled in her arms, unsure whether her daughter had just cracked a joke at her husband's expense or was serious about the comment. She managed a curt smile, but could not hide the fact her cheeks flushed a light shade of crimson. “Did you get my ice chips?” “Yeah,” Tony lifted a small red pitcher in the air and shook it. “Though I would have stayed outside if I knew you were trying out for comedy night at the Apollo.” Cynthia lightly brushed her fingers against the side of Tony's face as he leaned in to give her a kiss. “But you have such a beautiful smile.” “Sounds like someone's medication hasn't worn off yet.” It wasn't the first time he had heard her say that, but it was the first time she had said it in the presence of company. The fact that it was her family did not help to ease the awkwardness of the situation. “I'm fine.” “Maybe Tony's right,” her mother interjected before the situation escalated. “Daniel and I should probably head home anyway. It's been a long day and my clothes smell like outside.” “Not to mention, everything happened so suddenly. We haven't been able to call any of the family to let them know the baby was born.” “Just make sure they don't come down here to visit,” Cynthia eased herself higher on the bed, a bit of a struggle as she had yet to recover her full strength. “Tell them we'll have everyone over once we get settled back in the house.” “And by settled, she means once we're able to clean up the mess she left on the kitchen floor.” “My water broke!” “I'm kidding,” Tony shook his head as Evelyn handed him his daughter. “We'll call you once we're settled.” “You get some rest, sweetie,” Daniel leaned in and gave his daughter a peck on the forehead, his hand on a section of the pillow above her head for support. “Call if you need anything,” it was her mother's turn to give her a kiss, this one on the cheek. “Doesn't matter what time it is either, okay?” “I will,” was Cynthia's response as Tony nodded his head in her direction. He had Arriana tucked close to his chest with one arm as if he was a running back for the Giants when he shook Daniel's hand. The sight brought a smile to Cynthia's face. There was no secret that the two men in her life had trouble adjusting to the fact that they were going to be related when they first met, the awkwardness that she noticed when Tony went to call them did not seem to be there, so the simple show of respect that made her smile nearly made her eyes tear up. She knew the best choice he ever made was leaving the family behind; he had one of his own to take care of, after all. “So where we headed, boss?” Salvatore Fennucci stared out the window, from his seat on the rear passenger side of the Lincoln Continental he had equipped with bulletproof glass, as the car made its way down the street. “We need to make a quick stop by the office,” Sal never turned his attention away from the scene that streaked past. “Tony faxed over some pictures he wants me to take a look at.” “Pictures'a what?” “Him and Adelina.” “No shit?” “Yeah, apparently someone took these pictures the day she went missing. Sent them to the feds.” “And he ain't locked up?” That last comment caught his attention. Maybe it was the high pitched squeal of a man being tortured that had distracted him, but Sal had paid no mind to the fact that Tony had these photos and was not being hounded by a federal agent desperate for a promotion. He never even bothered to ask him where he got the copies of the photos. “That's one lucky son of a bitch.” “He sure is,” Sal nodded even though he knew Nico wouldn't see it. “So what'a we spose ta do with 'em?” “You remember Charlie Fillino?” “Guy that doctored the photo of Senator Schumer for us, right?” The car pulled to a stop as the traffic light turned red. “That's the guy,” Sal watched a couple of kids playing stickball down the street. If only life was still that easy. “Tony said the feds are on a witch hunt, thinks he saw a reflection in one of the photos no one is paying any attention to.” “So ya wanna take 'em down to Charlie see if he sees anything fishy?” “Right.” “Seems easy enough.” If only everything could be so simple. Now I'm not going to lie; I was a little scared when I was told that I was going to have to go up against Hessian. It didn't last long, but for a brief moment I was worried. That may sound funny coming from me, but it is the truth. Who wouldn't be worried though, right? I mean, the guy is PTC's Elite champion. Not to mention that he must have a real big chip on his shoulder after losing to little old me. I overheard a couple of the ring crew talking about how distraught he was over just losing his wife, that his mind wasn't all there. I laughed. I mean, who uses the word distraught in real life. I laughed at the fact that he was making up excuses as to why I beat him, too. Just not as much. It didn't dawn on me that he had no problem beating Troy Douglas when he found out she was fucking someone else until a few weeks ago, but it was easy not to notice when everyone and their mother has beaten Douglas. Still, it was hard to believe that he wasn't going to be looking for a bit of revenge. He'd probably use the word retribution, but I'm a simple man with a simple plan. That's what happens when you don't finish high school, I guess. It could also have to do with the fact that he is making up for the fact he's as dense as a board. Whether or not that plays into our match is still up in the air, but we both know he is the one with something to prove here. I know I can beat him, so I'm not going to paint myself as the underdog, but it is hard not to look at this as a David versus Goliath match up. The real question is... Which one of us is David, and which of us is Goliath?
