5 October 2008 Doubt is a Deadly Weapon (IG50 Promo #1 of 5) Doubt is a Deadly Weapon - (IG50 Promo #2 of 5) Doubt is a deadly weapon. It slices through self-confidence, like a scythe to the abdomen. It tears through your guts, through your bone and your sinew, your flesh, your muscle and your skin. I stare at the punching bag. Staring is the only thing I can do now. My arms feel like they’ve been anchored to the floor, my pores spewing sweat down my body, my chest rising and falling like a piston, but that’s not the thing keeping me from hitting that bag. “Come on man,” Jimmy Hahn, my trainer, says to me, a hint of desperation in his voice, “you want to beat these chumps? You’ve got to work for it! Do you want this?” Do I? A few days ago that would have been an easy question, but when your body is much more deteriorated than you thought, your anxiety at stepping into a ring of youthful, trained atheletes grows by the second. Can I do this? Can I get my body in a good enough shape to even keep up with these guys? The game has evolved. Do I possess the skills to compete with these people on a technical level? Are they too fast now? Can they overpower me? Humiliation can be felt many hours before it actually occurs, days even. Why did I do this? What possessed me into entering a wrestling contest, with a lack of fitness, with no idea of my opponents, and a wrestling style that went out of fashion in the eighties? “Dave, listen to me, okay?” Hahn says, clutching me by the arm and pulling me over to the bench. We sit down and he passes me a bottle of water. “I can help you get in shape, I can help you shake the ring rust before your match, but what I can’t do is get you ready mentally.” “I’m sorry, Jim.” “Don’t be sorry, man! There ain’t nothing to be sorry about! You get in there and train your ass off, you hear me? But you’ve got to want this, man. You’ve got to get your head straight if you’re going to do this. Messing around with your ‘feel-sorry-for-me’ attitude ain’t going to get you nowhere, you hear me? You’re gunna have to work for this.” “I hear you, Jim. I just don’t know what I’m getting myself into.” “Fuck them, man! It don’t matter if they’re a bunch of kids from ballet class, or a bunch of trained killers, man. That don’t change this situation. All you can do is work as hard as you can and give yourself the best chance at this, you understand?” “I understand, Jim.” “Well I don’t think you do, Dave. The fact that you’re looking at the floor with that ‘pity-me’ look slapped across your face tells me you ain’t been listening to a word I’ve been saying. Now I suggest you take the rest of the night off, go and get a bite to eat, and think about what I’ve said to you today. You hear me?” “Okay, Jim.” Jim gets up and starts walking out of the gym. How can I waste this guy’s time like this? I’m sure he’s got better things to do, like training the regular wrestlers, than mess with the likes of me. I should thank him. “Jim?” I call out, but he’s gone. He’s out of the gym - it’s too late. -----=====I=====----- I need to clear my head, but it’s hard to cope with the possibility of humiliation with what I’ve gone through. I’m not talking about the people who think I’m nuts for talking to my plastic duck. They can insult me that way all day and it makes no difference to me, but there are things that get to me. Some things have scarred me mentally, and I don’t think I’ve ever recovered from it. I remember that day. I was elated. As I folded him over and hooked the leg, I knew there was no escape. The referee made the pin. 1… 2… 3…The match was over. I had defeated the tournament favourite, the undefeated champion - the one that they thought couldn’t be beaten. But I had no idea what that result would cause. I had opened a pandora’s box that would destroy my wrestling career. A tirade of abuse followed. So-called respected professional wrestlers spoke out about the travesty that had taken place. There were cheating allegations, there were phone calls, there were people on the streets spitting at me for what I had done. All I did was wrestle. I knew that this was a near-impossible task. I knew that all I could do was train as hard as I’d ever trained before, to put my heart and soul into completing the greatest accomplishment of my wrestling career – Winning the GTT. I went in with no doubts clouding my performance, with no expectations, but with the burning desire to do something exceptional. I was lucky. 9 times out of 10, Deville would have seen the reversal coming, he would have reacted and the result could have been different. He just made a mistake. He underestimated me. He never thought that I would pull off something like that, so he ignored the threat of the reversal. I pinned him. I did nothing wrong. I did not cheat. I had not bribed the referee into a fast count. But that didn’t stop people from making that assumption. And so with training for the final in full swing, I had to cope with death threats, with graffiti on my car, with people banging on the windows of my gym, and the multitude of insults thrown at me everywhere I went. I would never be the same again. ---- 5 October 2008 I can't edit the title, but that title should read RP #2 of 5, not #1. Sorry!
