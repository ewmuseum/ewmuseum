Butterflies and Hurricanes: The Redemption of Cereal Killer - Part Five: "You've got to change the world, and use this chance to be heard"

15 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-05-15 Cereal Killer had spent the past few days worrying and fretting. He’d been bothered and bugged, jittery and jumpy, stressed and shaky, vexed and… and… he’d definitely been vexed. That’s the kind of thing that can happen to a man when life grabs him by the throat and takes time to find the precise spot in the nuts that will hurt the most. Some people will say that the anticipation of the following kick is worse than the kick itself, but those people must have been women, eunuchs or stupid people, because by God, the kick’s the kicker… so to speak. It wasn’t that CK had actually been kicked in the nuts; it was rather that his worst enemy, the man who’d spent several years settling in as the very definition of terror, had reappeared in his life, and seemed to be working as some sort of evil puppet master behind the scenes. It was something Frank had seen all too often; The Ripper tormenting some new kid, turning him one way and then the other, before disposing of him when it ceased to be entertaining. As if that wasn’t enough, The Ripper had predicted that Frank’s girlfriend would be history before his next opponent after Tsunami Oni. Cereal killer’s only silver lining to the cloud was that The Ripper must have thought that he would beat Oni. It seemed ridiculous at the time to think that he could beat such a seasoned veteran, but news had slowly filtered to CK that Tsunami Oni was not the force he used to be. An injury had stopped his ascent to the top of the tree; a break to his leg that would prevent him from competing properly in the tournament. Cereal Killer thanked his lucky stars, before he remembered who he was still dealing with. The Ripper; a man full of more coldly calculated evil that a frozen malevolent calculator. “Why does it always rain on me?” asked CK, as he rolled over and lay down on his blue sofa. Time passed in an instant for Frank, until words lulled him from his sleep. “Hey babe, how’s it going?” The colloquialism seemed so out of place after getting used to the curt tone of The Ripper. It was, of course, Valerie. She stood by the front door of the apartment, the keys in her hands making the usual noises of metal on metal. “How’s it going? Well, everyone’s trying to get me down, and I have bills coming out of my ass. How do you really think that it’s going?” “Don’t snap with me, Frank!” said Valerie. CK stopped himself for a minute. He was going to prove The Ripper wrong. He was going to defeat Tsunami Oni, but then he wouldn’t let some crazy block get in the way of his relationship with Valerie. He wouldn’t let something stupid stop his life and the love he felt for her. “I’m sorry, Valerie.” He could prove The Ripper wrong. He could defeat Oni and keep his relationship together, couldn’t he? “Well, you should be. I’m trying to help you, Frank, but you keep on pushing me away. A lesser woman might have left by now.” “I won’t let you leave.” “What’re you going to do, tie me to the bed?” asked Valerie, a lascivious smile on her face. “Only if you ask nicely,” laughed CK, standing up from the sofa to look right into her eyes. He could do this… he could do this… --------------------------------------------------- A couple of hours and a couple of orgasms later, Frank was feeling a whole lot better about himself. He lay on the sofa, which had formed an impromptu bed for himself and Valerie, and he thought that this was the time he’d smoke a cigarette, if he didn’t find them abhorrent and irritating to his lungs. “So, honey, why’s it such a problem if you don’t win the tournament then? I mean, if you lose in the final then surely one of the other federations would pick up your contract?” “It’s not that easy,” said Frank. “XUW has a vicious no-compete clause, and I signed my name to it when I started working for them. I can’t compete for six months after I leave their company, even if they release me. It’s harsh, but that’s the way of the business.” “Well, my friend got headhunted by some agency and they paid her wages while they waited for her contract to run out. Can’t you just do that?” asked Valerie. “What did she do for a living?” “She was an accountant.” “It’s really not quite the same. Fame is fleeting here, and if I made it to the final I’d be famous for a month or so… but six months on all I’d be good for is indy shows. I can’t afford to go back to that kind of stuff. I’ve got to win the tournament, there’s nothing else left for me.” Valerie nodded, understanding his predicament. Cereal Killer laid back and thought of all he’d been through, and all that remained to come. He could sit back and bask in the love of a good woman, and the certainty within his own mind that he could overcome anyone in the federation if he put his all into it. He could do all that, but the words of The Ripper kept on coming back to him. His idyll would soon be shattered. His cosy life would be ended, turned on its head with a Valerie shape hole torn in it. Those were the words of The Ripper. Well, screw him. Cereal Killer was going to have his cake and eat it. Or so he thought. So he thought as he drifted into sleep, with Valerie by his side. A sleep that was free of monsters or manipulative influences. A sleep that was free of men who were controlling his every action, and overseeing how he developed. A sleep that was free of The Ripper; the man who had sculpted every decision CK took, and whose predictions would come true far too soon for the man asleep on the blue sofa. Far too soon.
