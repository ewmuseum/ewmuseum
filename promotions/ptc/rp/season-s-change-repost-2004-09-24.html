Season's Change (repost)

24 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-24 A crisp September afternoon, the first days of autumn now upon us. A purple 1969 Dodge Charger rambunctiously enters the twisting laneway off of a gravel sideroad. We see several shots, from several angles, of the muscle car dashing up the drive. Each time the car flashes past, we can hear the radio blaring “Snowbird” by Anne Murray. No, really. After way too much Anne Murray, the car eases to a halt in a clearing, several yards away from a large cottage. This cottage belongs to NGW American Champ and GTT4 Tournament Entrant “Hollywood North” Dallas Reeves. This cottage is in the Muskoka region of Central Ontario; cottage country. Sliding out of the driver-side window is none other than Dallas Reeves. He’s wearing blue jeans and a brown corduroy jacket. His long curly blond hair is held together in a pony tail. He’s holding a cd in his hand, which he then flings into the leafy undergrowth of the forest, adjacent to the cottage. Exiting the Charger through the passenger door is the large, lumbering form of Reeves’ bodyguard, “Beastor” Brandt. Back in his heyday, the Beastor was a terror in the mid-South wrestling promotions. Known as much for his brutality in the ring as his wild black mane and beard, he literally tore his way through the ranks. During one particularly violent match, Brandt took the usefulness of an opponent’s ear and eye. But the Beastor’s newfound financial status was not well-handled. Soon the Beastor would find himself arrested, the outcome of a cocaine and cough syrup bender going horribly astray. Beastor smartened up after his community service, and for the good of his own health and public safety, exiled himself to the wastelands of northern Quebec. Earlier this year, Dallas Reeves brought him back into society, one that he still doesn’t quite fit into, evidenced by him now madly dashing into the leafy undergrowth to retrieve an Anne Murray cd. From the back seat steps a very slight man in comparison to the Beastor’s 6’8” 334 lbs frame. This younger man, in his early twenties, has a look of relief on his face, as well as the aspirations of a mustache. He straightens out his t-shirt depicting Corey Haim and Corey Feldman, which is pulled tightly over a black hooded sweatshirt. He follows after Reeves, who is now entering the cottage. This man is Frankie “the Cameraman” Calrissian. He pauses, and looks back to the Beastor. Frankie: Leave it be, Beastor! I’ll burn you another one! Frankie then continues on into the cottage, leaving a dejected-looking Beastor to tear himself away from the pursuit of his beloved cd. Inside the cottage, the rustic interior is filled with 1950s era furniture, which is not uncommon in the Muskoka region. The heart of Ontario’s cottage country is basically full of people’s old furniture. Everything old and since replaced is sent to the cottage. Reeves hangs up his corduroy jacket on the halltree, and kicks off his black loafers. Frankie is walking closely behind him. Frankie: That’s perfect though, isn’t it? Just get Beastor a discman and then we won’t have to listen to any more Anne Murray! Dallas: That’s a good idea, Frankie. Reeves veers left, past the spacious living room, complete with cackling fireplace and Don Cherry Rock’Em Sock’Em video playing on the big screen, and enters the kitchen. He heads straight for the fridge, and pulls it open. He hands a beer to Frankie, and pops the cap on his own frosty Moosehead bottle. Dallas: Why don’t you just let Beastor borrow yours? Frankie looks as if he was just spit on. Frankie: But you gave me that so I could listen to my Nickelback cds! Reeves stops sipping from his beer and faces Frankie. Dallas: You should just be happy that I let you listen to Nickelback at all. Reeves continues on into the living room, seating himself in the leather recliner. Dallas: So it’s settled. Go burn him a new cd and fetch your discman. Frankie: Fine then. But you better let me listen to “How You Re-“ Dallas: Let’s talkin’, more walkin’. Frankie disappears down the hallway and into a room, presumably with a computer in it. In his own pathetic form of protest for giving up his discman, Frankie begins to blare Nickelback’s “How You Remind Me”. Reeves rolls his eyes, and yells at Frankie. Dallas: Frankie, what’s rule number one about listening to Nickelback at my cottage? Frankie does not respond, but he does close the door. So in fact, I guess he did respond, only non-verbally. Reeves smiles. Dallas: Rule number one. Close the door. Frankie Calrissian was a cameraman at NGW, dutifully filming his fair share of wrestling promos. But Frankie had his goals, and they included becoming a wrestler himself. The brass laughed at him though, they laughed at his 5’11” 178 lb frame. Then came Frankie’s big shot. Dallas Reeves quit the NGW, leaving his Extreme Title in the hands of young Frankie. Now, Frankie was a wrestler. And a champion to boot. However, things did not go as well as he had hoped. He was utterly destroyed in his first defense, and then went on to lose six more bouts in succession. Down on himself after getting booted back to cameraman status, Frankie was destitute. That is, until back through the NGW gates walked Dallas Reeves again. Reeves soon took on Calrissian as his own personal cameraman. In exchange, Reeves agreed to mentor Frankie on the ways of wrestling. With Dallas as his manager, Frankie shaped up and is now the current and 5 time NGW Untamed Champ. But Frankie is a long way from Medicine Hat, Alberta now. The main door opens up as Beastor sullenly enters, shuffling his feet. Reeves looks over, and tries to pick up his spirits. Dallas: Don’t you worry, Beastor. Frankie is fixing it right now. The Beastor flashes a toothy grin, immediately placated, and sits on the floor cross-legged, watching the hockey.From the same hallway that Frankie disappeared down steps a distinguished-looking gentleman. Wearing a purple velvet smoking jacket and black silk trousers, Jack Carrington seats himself on the couch, kicking his rather expensive looking slippers up onto the coffee table. He greets Reeves with a nod of his silver head, then rubs his black mustache with his fingers before pulling a pipe out of his pocket. Reeves just looks at him, eyes wide. Dallas: Well, how are you this evening, Mr. Hefner? Are there any bunnies in the grotto? Carrington flashes a fake smile at Reeves, and sparks his pipe, which is filled with pipe tobacco. Sorry, potheads. “Alberta” Jack B. Carrington has been quite the manager for Dallas Reeves. Since taking him on, Dallas has won the NGW Extreme Title and became the longest-running American Champ in NGW history. An old friend of Reeves’ pro wrestler-turned wrestling trainer uncle, Dash Hansen, Carrington has proven his dedication time again, be it through his words of wisdom or fists of not-quite-fury, but you could tell that a few years ago they were fists of fury. The 53 yr old himself is a former wrestler-turned businessman, and since retiring from wrestling has done fairly well for himself. Now merely a figurehead at his own Carrington Enterprises, J.B. spends his days returned to the sport he loved, now as a manager. Dallas: Say, J.B…did you hear anything further about who my Round Two adversary was going to be? And don’t act like you don’t care, ‘cause I know ya do. And don’t act like you didn’t hear anything, ‘cause I know ya did. So which PTC superstar am I facing? Please tell me it’s Robb Hardy. Oh! Or Scott W.! Carrington puffs on his pipe, his attention turned towards all the hockey hi-light action played on the Don Cherry dvd. A billow of smoke escapes his lips. Carrington: Nah, none a those two twits…check tha fax yaself. Carrington is from Calgary, Alberta, and therefore speaks with a bit of a western drawl that sounds, oddly enough, like Texan. Carrington motions to the corner of the room, next to a bookcase and the large bay window over-looking Lake Joseph. Reeves bounds to his feet and retrieves the fax pages, reading over them with a great deal of excitement. Reeves: Kenny Mercury, eh? From the UWF, eh? I recognize him. Frankie: Well ya should! Frankie returns to the living room, smiling, and now wearing his favourite red visor, the one that reads “178 lbs of Pure Fury”. He is cradling something in his arms, and walks over to Beastor. Frankie hands him a discman and a cd case, containing Anne Murray. Not actually containing Anne Murray, but her songs. Frankie pops the Don Cherry dvd out of the player, to the lament of Carrington and to the groans of the never-speaking Beastor. Frankie puts another dvd on, and the face of Kenny Mercury graces the television. His flowing blond hair immediately is noticed by Frankie. Frankie: Look at the similarities between you two…a) you both have long blond hair b) he’s 6’1” 245, you’re 6’2” 246, which is pretty close- Carrington: So what, Frankeh? Dallas is twenny-se’en, an’ Mercureh is thi’ty-fie. Frankie looks defensive. Frankie: Yeah, well…they kinda look alike. That’s all I’m sayin’. It’s weird- Carrington: Sho’, but not weird enough ta tu’n off Don Cherreh. Frankie: Okay, fine, sorry. Dallas: Uh, maybe I should be watching that Kenny Mercury tape…he is my next opponent, after all. Carrington pats Reeves on the shoulder, re-assuringly. He then winks. Carrington: Afteh Don Cherreh. Frankie switches the dvds again, and sits on the opposite end of the couch as Carrington. The picture is coming in rather fuzzy, and Carrington kicks Frankie gently in the leg. Carrington: Fix it. Dallas: Well, I best be makin’ my phone calls. Frankie: Beastor, can you fix it please? Dallas: I’ll be in the other room if anybody needs me. Reeves exits off to the kitchen to use the phone. Beastor gets off the floor and in behind the 48’ screen. He starts messing around with the wires, as Frankie directs him. Frankie: Worse…worse…better…almost!…THERE! Perf-…no, ya lost it again. Way worse. Bad Beastor. Beastor lets out a frustrated groan, as the setting switches back to the kitchen. Reeves is leaving a message. Dallas: Okay, John, gimme a shout when ya get this message. I’m at the cottage, but gotta head to NGW HQ tomorrow morning. Lates. Reeves presses the hang-up button with his fingers, then catches himself from hanging the phone back on the wall. Quickly his fingers dash across the keypad. The phone rings a few times, but no one picks up until the answering message clicks on. Machine: Hi, you’ve reached Jessika’s cell phone. I can’t take your call right now, because I am at a photo shoot in Cuba. If this is Dallas, I’ll call ya soon, babe. Miss ya. –click- Reeves smiles to himself; the way Jessika always jokes about her modelling career is one of the things he likes most about her. Dallas: Hey babe, it’s me. I’m at the cottage until tomorrow. Call me here or lose me forever. He hangs the phone up, grinning some more. Ah, those silly little things that couples say to one another. Reeves’ moment of happy thought is interrupted by a commotion, coming from the living room. Frankie: Waaaahahaha! Beastor’s electrocuting himself! Reeves scampers into the living room to an odd sight. Behind the television, Beastor lets out an uncomfortable groan, and a flicker of light comes from his direction. He then begins to tremble, a frenzied look on his face. Frankie starts freaking out. Frankie: Waaaahahaha! Dallas merely rolls his eyes. Dallas: *Again*, Beastor? I mean really…Frankie, get the stick. Frankie, on the verge of happy tears, runs off to the broom closet, returning with a long wooden stick. He pokes Beastor several times, until he is free from the wires. Beastor looks stunned, much like usual, but then clicks in a moment later, smiling his goofy grin. Frankie wipes the tears from his eyes. Frankie: Man, I love it when he does that! Reeves gives Frankie a stern look, then turns to Carrington, who himself is laughing. Dallas: Don’t encourage them, J.B. They’re chromosomes are dysfunctional enough as it is. Carrington: It’s still kinda funneh. So what did Misteh Hitchin have to say? Dallas returns to his leather recliner, kicking the foot rest completely out. Dallas: Johnny? Couldn’t get a hold of him. Carrington: Are ya still nuh-vous ‘bout talkin’ to ‘im again? Ah mean, he did just recentleh beat ya in tha ring. Dallas cringes visibly, still a little sore after losing a huge match against his best friend. Dallas: Thanks J.B., I had almost forgotten. And no, I’m not still “nuh-vous”. I’m done with that whole”apprehension” thing. We’re going golfing when he feels better. Still no word from Jessika neither. That shoot she’s on now must be keeping her pretty busy. Carrington: Ya did meet ‘er at a Playboy Mansion parteh, Dallas. Dallas flashes his manager an icy gaze. Dallas: And? Carrington: Girls like that, Dallas…ne’ermind. Ah’ve said enough as ‘tis. If ya’ll excuse meh, Ah’ll retiya ta ma quartehs. Dallas: Good. Night. Carrington snuffs his pipe and stands, heading down the hallway. He then turns back to the living room. Carrington: Bizzeh coupla days, Dallas. New Yawk ta-marrah, Regina on Satta-deh. Dallas looks to him with a confused, dreaded face. Dallas: Re…gi…na? Why!? What did I do!? Carrington: Ya’ve got an audience with “Pepsi” Joe Trudeau again. Dallas: J.B., that man is a maniac! Carrington: An’ a helluva traineh. Sleep well. Carrington exits the room as the look of a pissy child takes over Reeves’ face. Reeves: God damn…god damn that “Pepsi” Joe Trudeau. Reclining back again, Reeves clasps his hands together behind his head, remembering the times he has already spent under the “unique” training regimen of Trudeau. Frankie has now recovered from his laughing fit, only to have it replaced by a moment of pure fear. Frankie: Pepsi Joe? Nooo! Beastor grunts distastefully, his face blackened from electrocution, just like in old Bugs Bunny cartoons. Trudeau and himself have a long-standing feud. See, “Pepsi” Joe is another former wrestler friend. He was, for a time, Carrington’s tag partner. But that, that was a long time ago. Nowadays, the sixty year old lives on his ranch south of Regina, Saskatchewan, occasionally training new talent. In fact, it was his eye and ear that the Beastor took, which unsurprisingly lead to their long-standing feud. Dallas: I know, Beastor. I don’t like it either. But J.B. knows what’s best for me…I suppose. He hasn’t steered me wrong yet. Frankie: I don’t have to go too, do I? I’m not fighting in the GTT4, so I see no reason for me to have to spend a few days in Regina with that lunatic. Dallas: It’s up to you, Frankie. But it wouldn’t hurt. Frankie: Well yeah, Dallas, it probably will! Dallas: Frankie, as your manager- Frankie: Stop. I already know what you’re going to say. And before I hear it, I’m going to bed. Frankie hops up off the couch and darts down the hallway to his room. Reeves looks over at Beastor, but he has fallen asleep sitting up, much like a gorilla. Dallas: That’s a little unsettling. Reeves walks over to the bay window, and stares out. Thanks to the moonlight, Dallas catches a glimpse of the steam rising off the lake. He then whispers to himself. Dallas: Getting colder out there. The season has already changed, next thing will be the leaves turning colours. Autumn is upon us. Time for a change. It’s gettin’ colder out there. The mercury seems to be dropping. Reeves smiles to himself after the last line. He’s proud to have made it on to the second round of the GTT4 tournament. He also feels a certain sense of privilege to be facing off against the pride of the UWF, Kenny Mercury. Pulling himself away from the window, Reeves places a blanket from the couch over the head of the seated and sleeping Beastor. Dallas switches off the light in the living room, heading to his own room with thoughts of his trip to New York tomorrow, to NGW headquarters. Then his thoughts turn to “Pepsi” Joe Trudeau. Dallas knows that he is in elite company in the GTT, and he is aware of how talented Kenny Mercury is. He has every ounce of trust and faith in “Alberta” Jack Carrington, and is thereby taking his visit to Regina with a grain of salt. If Carrington didn’t truly feel that Trudeau could help Reeves out, he wouldn’t send him there. Dallas: Mercury’s dropping…I just got it. He shuts the door, and the scene plunges into blackness. The words “Dallas Reeves vs Kenny Mercury” appear in gold lettering on the screen, then fade to black. This promo has been released through Dudesy Productions. MMIV.
