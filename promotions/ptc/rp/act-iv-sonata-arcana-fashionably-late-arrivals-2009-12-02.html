Act IV: Sonata Arcana; Fashionably Late Arrivals

2 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-12-02 October 1st, 8:18 AM EST Resurgence of Undersea Phenomenon By Naomi Oaks Associated Press Writer Bermuda (AP) -- The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) has experienced a rash of unexplained undersea readings along the South American coast in recent days. The organization picked up similar sounds in the summer of 1997 in the same area, off the southwest coast of the continent, and analysis has proven the frequency to be similar. But as interesting a conclusion as that may be, the story doesn’t end there. Scientists have shown particular interest in the recent series of readings found via the administration’s hydrophone array. After extensive study and investigation, they have come to a conclusion that may shake the scientific community: the origin of the sounds is an animal. Given the distance the sound would have to travel to be picked up by the organization’s equipment, originally designed by the U.S. Navy to detect submarines, the size of the animal would have to be immense. Easily the largest on Earth, many times larger than the blue whale, the current holder of that record. The scientific community is now wondering why the creature has reemerged and what it may be… * * * * Few could accuse the Sect of Black Wisdom mastermind of being guided by emotion since the founding of his infernal brainchild, a cut-throat organization that every knowledgeable religious group in the world trembled at the mention of. He had always directed his minions through his faith and by the words of unholy texts, leading many to wonder if he truly had any humanity left. Then one of his followers inquired about how he felt about the attempt on his life in Germany two days prior. Consumed with rage, Shanahan drew a knife and nearly decapitated him. Only a few bundles of sinew kept his head attached to the body. Suffice to say, Marion Sterling was rather upset about him staining her carpet. Ethan Knight only laughed. At one time, blood made him queasy. Now, as a hardened killer, even the most gut-wrenching of slaughter roused a snicker from the Sect‘s newest right-hand man. With Tully gone rogue, it had been Knight that had ended his life and taken his place. “Clean that stain,” growled Shanahan to a pair of shivering disciples, indicating the place the unfortunate had fallen. “I want no memory of that blathering cretin to remain.” They had gathered to plan for the near future. But it was clear the orchestrator was in no mindset to discuss his schemes tonight; he seemed rattled by the attempt on his life, as if expecting the day would never arrive. The word was that he saw it as a reminder he was mortal, vulnerable, and that brought the flaws of his humanity back into the forefront of his thinking. Perhaps it had been too long since he saw any true challenge outside of the ring. “And remove the gag. For once I’m actually interested in what our insolent Japanese friend has to say.” Tatsuya Mizakawa had been fighting against his chains for weeks. It was a futile gesture but Shanahan had come to admire the ferocity of his spirit. But then he supposed it spoke equally well of his fervor that he’d managed to become a feared ringleader amongst the ruthless Yakuza gangs. The first words from his mouth were a string of curses in his native tongue. None of them were learned in the language, but it didn’t take a skilled linguist to suppose that a bound man with hate burning in his eyes wasn’t praising you as he shouted in the words of his homeland. Knight’s mood wasn’t too divergent from his leader. Not amused, he silenced the Yakuza with a swift haymaker that sent him plunging to the ground, head cracking off the exposed floorboards of the parlor. “Give him time to put his thoughts in order,” the assassin said, reseating himself. Shanahan peered out the spacious front window into the forest beyond. “Suffice to say, things had been running according to plan until two days ago. On that day, someone made shambles of Brother Krauser’s estate in an effort to put me in a casket. On top of that, Ridley and the Crying Seraph failed to apprehend Major Stryker, who is more savvy than even I give him credit for. Stryker is far too cunning and resourceful to allow to live at this juncture in our designs; I’ve tried to evade him through the years, but we’re at too critical of a stage to allow his white knight tactics to ruin what we’ve worked to achieve.” Spinning to face the Yakuza, whose mouth had taken a blood-red hue after Knight’s punch, his stone-cold countenance settled on the man from the land of the Rising Sun. “Your organization has been a real thorn in my side, Mizakawa-san. Had you simply handed over the holy stone when we requested it, there would never have been this much bloodshed.” Knight had drawn his favorite blade from its sheath and was sharpening it. He laughed. “Bloodshed was inevitable. I mean, we’re trying to murder the world and all. He just means less early losses for the yellow-bellied islanders.” Shanahan paused, shifting his attention to his grinning lieutenant. “You need to put it in terms those slant-eyed monkeys can fathom is all,” Ethan replied in parodied self-defense. “I was interpreting, giving the bastard added clarity.” Sterling pulled herself up, straightening her emerald-green dress. “A storm is brewing. I hear thunder.” The skies were clear, blue as the open sea. His minions strained to hear the distant report but Shanahan only shook his head. “Are you being poetic, Lady Sterling?” “No, literal. I heard thunder. Curious because it doesn’t appear that…” The Sect’s lord cut her off with an abbreviated curse and a furious shift in posture, turning to study the wilderness bordering the estate. Those curt movements were all it took for the assembled to know something was drastically amiss. “That’s not thunder,” Bruce growled, glaring out the window. Frustrated, he sent a bulky forearm crashing into Mizakawa, and the chained Japanese collapsed to the linoleum. “It bodes far worse than any storm could right now.” He caught motion, diverging from multiple sides. Donned in camo-green, the flash of gunmetal as the sun struck their places of movement. “Get Ridley on the radio. Now.” * * * * Slade kicked aside the casings spewed from his gargantuan heavy gun. Marching forward, he sidestepped the wide-eyed corpses of Shanahan’s perimeter sentries. That part had been too easy; they were cultists, novices to combat and untrained in war. Even before his weapon had sent them splaying to the earth in a blood-choked heap, he realized it may have been the first time they’d ever been handed firearms. A morsel of guilt had touched him momentarily, until he remembered these motherfuckers were out to turn his precious world into a chaos-ridden cinder. “Mind your engagement parameters,” came Major Stryker’s voice over the comm. “We’re going in hot with one primary target and two targets of opportunity. Lethal force is not authorized. I repeat, lethal force is not authorized! We need these maniacs alive!” Slade paused, the edge of his boot probing the minced remains of his victims. Not much was left of either after he‘d laid the hammer down with his custom. “Uh…what about their friends?” “Cleared to engage all tangos that aren’t Bruce Shanahan, Tatsuya Mizakawa, or Doctor Rourke. And take special care with Marion Sterling as well, we can‘t implicate her at this point. Could cause a mess.” “Hallelujah!” It was Millen, his shotgun pumped emphatically as he sang his praise. “And before you say another word, Russ, you could have woken the dead with that volley. How many?” “Two down, sir.” “Only two? Judging by that roar, I’d expected the entire house to be razed down around us. Your ammunition consumption leaves something to be desired, sergeant. Overkill is rarely a necessity on the field where we pawns play. It’s part of the art, the ballet of warfare.” “Who the fuck??” exclaimed Webb. “I don’t know if we’ve had the privilege. Captain Luther Ridley, commander of the Crying Seraph. It’s a pleasure to meet you in combat again, Corporal Webb.” “Luther, there are three squads this time,” chimed in Stryker, attempting to rattle his self-imposed rival. “Not just us. Bravo and Charlie are also preparing to engage…” “Incorrect, major,” the other corrected. “Charlie has already been neutralized by my team in the center of the woods. Not far from their LZ. And as we speak, they have a lock on Bravo. You‘re coming in alone, minus stragglers that manage to flee my Seraph.” Marcus knelt, grinding a white-knuckled fist into the mud as a transmission came over the comm. It was chaos, a hellish cacophony of death cries, abrupt orders, rushed demands of surrender, and frenzied bursts of fire. Ridley was right. Alpha team was alone. “I look forward to our duel, major. I know you have the heart of a warrior. Demonstrate it to me. Show me the skills I‘ve heard so many stories about!” His voice fell hush, almost pleading. “Set me free. Ridley out.” There was a silence. No one had to say a word; it was a requisite moment of mourning. It was almost ten seconds before Marcus spoke again, his military bearing returned. “All you have. Do it for your fallen brothers this day. End the Sect of Black Wisdom and their reign of terror! Hoo-rah!” “HOO-RAH!” * * * * They had come with the power of thunder and the swiftness of lightning. Before Shanahan could even react to the thunderous wail of what he imagined was a colossus of an automatic weapon, sniper fire peppered the parlor and den. He hit the ground. Beside him came one of his many faceless apostles, though he did so with a bloody wound just below his hairline and eyes like glass. Looking around, it took him a moment to register the shooter had taken three of his guards in under two seconds.No, no, no no, Shots echoed throughout the previously serene domicile. Sterling, the beauty garbed in the green she loved, had retreated behind a bar where shattered glass now fell all around and on top of her. Mizakawa rested on his knees amidst the death around him, dust settled on his long, greased black hair. His breathing was steady, a man who was experiencing a gunfight long after his first. “Where is he?!” roared Shanahan. “Where the hell is Luth…!” “GET THE FUCK ON THE FLOOR, BRUCE! AND THE REST OF YOU, NOW!!” It had happened that quickly. His men, completely outclassed and unprepared for the deluge that was the Blackhelm team’s surgical infiltration, had been overrun. And now Shanahan was at the mercy of his enemy. For the first time in so long, and he knew that this time, he wouldn’t be released. Even if it meant a ’malfunction’, or a ’misfire’ while he was in custody. Marcus Stryker, the prototype of the ‘sheriff in a white hat’ philosophy, would see him dead before he ever saw the sun again. “It’s over. Everything you’ve worked for ends here. And everyone who has died by your hand will be avenged. I swear it on the graves of all my brothers-in-arms, you murdering slime ball.” Shanahan, defiant as ever, pulled himself up. His visage was blank, but his eyes were as sharp as ever. “Your timing is impeccable.” “I’m glad you’re prepared to give this up…” Someone cleared their throat. It wasn’t him, and it wasn’t any of his. He was covered, but was the only one in the room. He turned to face what he’d feared. Luther Ridley, spearheading a formation of his hell sent killers. Beside them was a familiar face. Familiar, if not twisted by the unhallowed words of Shanahan’s gospel. “Marcus. It’s been a long time. I’ve seen many things, felt the tug of so many evils. I fear when I stared into the abyss, it stared back. And now…” Malcolm Brooks was a broken man. It saddened Stryker. “…I’m a hollow man. And with it, I’m subservient to Their will.” Stryker faintly recalled Malcolm Brooks. A hardboiled former detective with the LAPD, he’d left the force soon after the massacre in Los Angeles that had started the Yakuza-Sect war, an inhuman genocide Shanahan had devised to scare his Japanese rival into returning something he considered valuable. He’d hoped to avoid the culmination of that play for power. “Malcolm. Jesus, what they done to you?” “There was no resisting it, Marcus. I tried. I thought I could. Maury thought I could. But we both underestimated the dominating presence of the Old Ones. The human spirit is no match for what they bring crashing down on your soul, on your mind.” Rourke moved among Ridley’s mercenaries, stammering out nonsense in whatever devilish tongue he’d learned in his own brainwashing. Brooks sighed as the other brushed by him. “Rourke, kindly cease your babbling. Another word and you’ll be sharing a plot with him.” Shanahan indicated the stain on the carpet. For the first time since the attack, he felt the return of dominance. “Interesting little reunion isn’t it? Detective Brooks, Doctor Rourke, Mizakawa-san, the entirety of Major Stryker’s squad…now if only Maury were here.” “I feel suddenly unwelcome.” Drawing himself up, Shanahan’s ambitious prodigy straightened and ran the dull edge of his knife down his sleeve. “Ethan Knight.” It was a reunion Marcus could do without. His detest for the cult’s second-tier and personal ‘Angel of Death’ ran nearly as deep as it did for Shanahan, and such was evident as he spat the name with a hateful scowl. “Damn you, you soulless little rat.” “It appears we have a dark history. I confess that I’m embarrassed to say I don’t know exactly what has you so bent out of shape.” “Don’t feign ignorance, you son of a bitch. You killed one of my best men. Jago Sensaki. I’ll have your head for this. As a matter of fact…” He leveled his weapon on Knight, a red dot spiraling around his central forehead. “…I have full clearance to take you. Guess you’re not important enough to warrant a ‘do not kill’ order.” Knight’s scowl spoke volumes of what ran in his psyche. A man who craved prominence, told he carried the weight of any other mindless follower of the Sect of Black Wisdom. “Sir. Um…you might want to get a look at this.” Millen stirred uneasily, craning his neck to catch whatever had caught his eye on their flank. It was a black Cadillac. Then two of them. And finally a third appeared around the bend. “This can’t be happening,” snarled Shanahan, his attention momentarily deviating from his armed Blackhelm opponents. His loyalists swarmed the first floor, drawing whatever weapons they could find. “Willis!” Stryker roared into his mouthpiece. “Bogeys incoming from the south! What have we got?” “Three Cadillacs, major. Passengers appear to be Asian, most garbed in dark suits. And from the sheer volume of metal I see being passed around in those cars, I’d say they’re armed to the teeth.” “You’ve gotta be fucking kidding me,” muttered Slade into the comm from his position on the outside deck. He turned, adjusting the heavy weight slung across his chest and shoulder. “Can this get any…” “Don’t jinx it, you buffoon,” Vanessa snapped into his ear. Shanahan’s underlings sprang into action. Ridley gestured and his Seraph fanned out downstairs, but his eyes never fell from Stryker. A whistle from Willis as he audibly reloaded. “Semi-autos, fully-autos, fucking samurai swords…Christ, Marcus, this is looking like a crazy-ass kung fu movie.” Stryker wasn‘t sure if his sniper was unnerved or jubilant. Adrenaline could be a strange thing that way. “Let the Seraph and Bruce’s crew of knife-wielding maniacs handle melee. The Yakuza aren’t our problem. If they get close or target you, drop them. Priorities are to capture Shanahan, Mizakawa, Rourke, in that order. Sterling and Brooks have no bearing on our mission, do not engage them unless necessary. “Roger that,” came the collective chorus. In the commotion, Ethan Knight had vanished. He was like a shadow, easily a match for any of Ridley’s death-heralds. And he had a particular goal in mind, which completely unsettled the major… “Webb is down! It was the kid, Knight!” Such was the unforgiving way of war. There was no time to pause and grab a snack, or even to crawl and get a bearing of what happened around you. These times were about reaction and adaptation, taking what was offered and letting years of honed instinct guide you. The deepest of martial philosophies that Marcus knew from day one he needed to embrace. As the thunder of Slade’s special drowned out everything else, bullets tearing into the disembarking Yakuza, he heard the words of his rival into his ear. “You’re mine. Give me my redemption.” They barely had time to register. He saw Luther come at him with a long blade, like an ornamental Marine sword honed for actual battle, swinging and jabbing with the practiced strokes of a classic swordsman. In his other hand he clutched the same nickel-plated revolver as he’d seen in the cabin, the one he‘d thought was set to end his life. He wanted to get to Webb, to sling the kid over his shoulder and get him to safety before Ethan Knight could claim another kill of his squad. But he couldn’t as long as the captain of the Crying Seraph so fervently pursued the duel he longed for. “Fight back, Marcus!” Ridley implored, probing forward with the edge of his blade. Marcus had taken up a wooden chair and used it to push back his foe. “Give me the battle I need!” He was frustrated, and his strokes began to lose their grace, their poise falling well short of the standards any master would have set for him. “Slade here!” The roar of his heavy weapon, the death-rattle of many that their crazed quarry would call symphony. “We’re about to be overrun! Pulling back under cover! No-so-friendly cover, but we‘ll take what we can get down here!” The steady whine of the Yakuza’s weapons faded as Slade’s transmission cut out. Chaotic times made for the most unexpected of bedfellows. Another swing from Ridley, the wind of his stroke caressing Stryker’s ear. It was too close for his liking. He lashed out, hitting Luther in the shoulder with one broken chair leg. His manic opponent, frustrated at his inability to pass the major’s defenses, drew up his revolver and pulled back the hammer. He fired one shot into the chair, and surged backward through the cloud of smoke. “Next time,” he whispered with an angry hiss. “I want the honor of no distractions. Farewell, Marcus.” Just like that, in the tradition of the ‘demon-ninja’ comparison Brooks had made so long ago, he was gone. “Primary target has fled!” Vanessa Wilder reported. “All secondary targets gone except Mizakawa. Moving in to capture!” “Vanessa, get him! Willis, call up the chopper, we’re getting the hell out of Dodge!” “Roger, preparing for extraction. Everyone to the top floor and secure, clear out the LZ as much as possible. Chopper should spray enough metal to not require us to fully clear the area first. Let the Yakuza and the remnants of the Seraph and Sect kill each other on the bottom floors.” In the hell of war there was only a haze. Stryker didn’t know what all had happened. Webb was down, Ridley had escaped, and his main targets had slipped through the cracks. The source of so much pain and suffering in the world was still at large, and that pained him more than the Yakuza‘s capture could make up for. “Shanahan and Rourke got away, but we have Mizakawa. That oughta count for something, right major? “Yeah. It should count for a real shit-storm when Bruce gets all of his mental faculties back. I pray for us all when he retreats back to Wyatt Manor with the full forces of Hell at his disposal again.” “So what now?” “We pull out. And when we get back home, I need to have a sit-down with Maury. I want to grease that psychotic dirt bag and need to know why the hell they won’t authorize it.” Plus he was already hesitant to tell Dawson about the transformation of Malcolm Brooks, a man they’d hoped to use as an informant when they sent him in. He was lost, just like the enigma that was Rourke. They’d acquired the crime boss, but raised far too many new questions. Made matters far more complicated. * * * * November 12th, 6:35 PM EST Mental Illness Explodes Worldwide By Vernon Hilliard Associated Press Writer New York, New York (AP) -- Mental health professionals around the globe are overwhelmed by an epidemic of psychological instability and schizophrenia, an event of which there appears no rational explanation. Mental health institutions are reporting a monumental escalation in inmate violence. Psychological clinics and hospitals indicate such an influx of mentally ill patients that staff is completely unable to deal with the flood of admissions. There appears to be no common ground or cause in those afflicted. Males and females are equally stricken, as are others of all ages, races, nationalities, and religions. Symptoms have ranged from acute nightmares to powerful hallucinations, asserted to be manifestations ranging from demonic persecution to visions of global destruction. Reports have come in of ordinary and mentally fit people committing violent suicide, their methods as diverse as self-inflicted gunshot wounds and leaping from high windows, claiming to be ‘saving themselves from extermination’. Most curiously is the report that many of these so-called paranoid schizophrenics have descended into spells of intense babbling in ‘alien tongues’, strings of nonsense monosyllables that match no human language. Interestingly, however, is the fact that when compared, the babbling appears to match a similar and flowing pattern of speech… * * * * “I knew you would betray me. I’m just surprised you were so sloppy.” “Who is this?! Goddammit, clear the channel!” “Is that any way to greet your other employer? Have your Roman masters become so desperate that they‘ve finally licensed assassination?” “…Bruce? How did you get this frequency?” “A little bird told me, albeit a bird now deceased. So tell me…why so brazen in your treachery, Maury? Surely you knew I’d be monitoring you better than that.” “I have no idea what you’re talking about.” “You putrid little swine! You know full well what I’m referring to. Someone pointed Major Stryker and his team to Sterling’s estate today while we were encamped. And surely it was no coincidence that a rather irate army of the Yakuza stormed the house in search of their boss. You should thank your impotent messiah that Ridley and the Seraph were there, else I’d assuredly be haunting you from the afterlife right now.” “I sent nobody after you. Did it ever occur to you that you have a rather plentiful number of enemies that may want to splatter your brains over a wall somewhere? You‘re on the shit-list of every religion and every humanitarian organization in the civilized world. I‘m surprised the Muslims haven‘t declared Jihad on you yet.” “They have. And I’ve killed almost as many of those sniveling jackals as any Army Rangers regiment.” A sigh. “You may be a maniac, but I didn’t send Stryker or the Yakuza to you.” “A part of says you speak the truth. You wouldn’t dare seek my demise knowing the consequences on this world that they’ve saddled you with protecting. Unfortunately, the hour is late. And there’s no turning back. This is your darkest hour, Maury.” “My God. Bruce…what the hell have you done?” “The next phase of our agenda. But damn you for forcing me into it so early, Maury.” “Damn you, Bruce! I told you that I didn’t…!” Static. * * * * The figure in the red mask watched the flames push skyward, reaching to envelop the heavens. Beneath the mask, he smiled, feeling the heat wash over his mask. Even as it singed his skin, the fiendish grin didn’t dissipate. The grandeur of this moment would drown out any mortal pain. This was a statement. This was the trumpet announcing the end times. And next would be the funeral dirge for the human race. Shrugging deeper into his trench coat, the figure walked away through the horrified throng that had gathered, hazarding a final gaze at the hastily assembled news teams. They’d barely set up their equipment before the reporters began screaming into their microphones, tears welling in the eyes of the pious as monks and priests fled. Their shrine was about to become a necropolis. “The Vatican has just been bombed by terrorists! I repeat…the Vatican has just been bombed by terrorists!!"
