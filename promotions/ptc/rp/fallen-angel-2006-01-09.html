Fallen Angel

9 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-09 "What do you stand for in this world? Better yet, what do we stand for? It echoed as more of an accusation than a query to the man publically known as Masquerade, and deservedly so. As of late he'd felt empty, his once sharp and convicted mind wandering from idea to idea like an A.D.D.-ridden schoolboy. Charisma and eloquent speeches could only carry a man so far when every facet of his faith was called into question; with leadership capabilities having waned considerably throughout his enduring depression and his most trusted officers often skeptical of his executive competence, self-examination couldn't take a backseat to his present designs anymore. Some things had to be faced immediately, or all could be for naught. At this critical stage, such dissension and denial could break the delicate balance they needed to maintain. "Nothing has changed…Kendall." The alias was still an awkward one but necessary. Every piece of the illusion must be maintained, even in private, until the goal was achieved. "Nothing at all. And it appears that as much as I offer you that reassurance, it does nothing to quell your uncertainty. Have you come to doubt me so much? Do you believe we have fallen so far from grace?" A subway train rumbled far down the tracks in the inky black depths of the tunnel, a now-common sound that did nothing to shatter the tranquility the duo shared in the barren terminal. It drowned out the grunt that Kendall offered as a first response. "I have never considered it a fall, 'Chaney'. I believe it to be more of an expulsion. We've been cast from grace, not fallen." Had the masked man not been so accustomed to the manner of the other then he'd have taken it as an affront, but then it was simply a part of Kendall's persona to be so cold and direct. "And at this rate, we cannot win." Resting on the grimy bench he'd occupied for nearly fifteen minutes now, Masquerade stared down the tunnel into the yawning darkness, every element of his reaction shrouded beneath the mask. "Greed and self-righteousness nearly destroyed us all. Never again will I allow us to be driven by vengeance and ambitions not dictated by our faith. Our beliefs will never again be counteracted by Earthly desire." "Can we all trust you this time? Can we commit ourselves under your guidance knowing that we will not be led astray like before, used as crutches for a man trying to ascend to a throne not meant for him? A man battling his own internal demons by embracing his own vices and nothing more?" Kendall could have delivered the most potent haymaker he could muster the strength for and still not have equaled the force his words had just had on his elder. "When I defeated Shadow nearly three weeks ago…I did not detect so much spite when you offered your opinion after the match. Why the change of heart, friend? What can I attribute this newfound 'integrity' to?" Disappointment was evident in the voice of the masked man, as serene as it may have been, at least to the one who had taken on the moniker of Kendall. They had been through many a conflict together, overcoming wars of the soul and agencies that sought to have them confined for the rest of their natural lives, if not outright executed. The other sighed as he ran a hand through jet-black hair that was uncharacteristically unkempt. He was not sorrowful, not in this endeavor that was greater than both of them. Honesty was the only thing that could set them free, the only trait that could prevent them from being diverted down that path to failure. They had all come too far to meet with self-destruction. "Because I was shocked you pulled it off, old friend. Even I didn't believe you had the capacity to defeat an adversary of that caliber, not in such an afflicted state. And I hoped that such an eminent victory would give you the vigor to recapture your spirit and break free of the manacles you've allowed to imprison you." A leatherbound tome had found its way into his hand from a pocket, the faded numerals emblazoned across the cover contorting his smirk into a glower. "But it didn't happen. The effort I saw you exert against that filthy Calix dog showed no signs of the fire I once knew, not in your movements or words. None of the passion that I could feel when you delivered speeches and sermons. I saw a corpse in the ring with Kaden Calix, a dead man whose body had yet to realize it was soulless." He spat the final words before hefting the book down onto the lap of the other. Kendall rarely showed any monicum of emotion and this was no exception. But Masquerade could just feel the feelings welled up in his partner; the other was seething with a combination of resentment and shame, fitting emotions directed at the man who had once given all he shepherded a sense of enlightenment and truth. The book he carried and had dropped onto his mentor's lap was his greatest treasure for so long; many of its pages had been shredded in frustration recently, the product of his comrade's battle with complete philosophical reversal. It was that moment when he finally realized the disgrace he had brought crashing down on all who had once relied on his wisdom. Even his most trusted had become skeptical of their beliefs, and it had come to pass under his watch. Thumbing through the fragmented first chapters, he listened as Kendall vented his disappointment. "We've struck accords with despicable men, self-righteous zealots that used us for their personal gain. We once swore an oath to shed their blood whatever the cost, instead we signed back-alley treaties and treated them as brothers. The only red that flowed in our presence was the ink from their pens they used to confine us and shackle our capabilities. You abandoned us to fulfill hopes and dreams borne of of flesh. And now, regardless of rituals and prayers, still I'm cheated out of seeing the resurrection of the man I once would have followed to the grave." His eyes drifted to the old book he had tossed into the lap of Masquerade. A coy smile brightened his expression though his words were as icy as the last. "At the least…I suppose these men you've brought into the fold still view you as a savior." The shrill sound of grinding brakes announced the arrival of the impending subway down the blackened corridor. Masquerade paused as his finger touched one of the raised letters printed on the cover. He knew the road back would be long and arduous, but never so riddled with doubt. "I am the key to their deliverance. And I am still yours, unless you wish to challenge that right here and now. Otherwise I suggest you make yourself presentable for our visit to an old friend." The words were issued so matter-of-factly that it would be hard to believe the same man had been so forlorn only moments prior. "Well….your old friend. I never met the man." "Inconsequential. I truly doubt he'll have eyes or ears for any but me at this juncture of our relationship anyhow. We've failed one another before, and I fear I'll have to break his heart one final time. Only then shall I know if I can have atoned for my transgressions." "And if you have?" The train screeched to a halt before them, the terminal flooding with humanity as the doors opened. The crowd dispersed as Chaney and Kendall headed for the doors to keep their appointment. Even in a teeming metropolis like this, men donning pallid masks in daylight were avoided by any passerby with self-preservation in mind. "If I have…then we make the world a better place. Just as we've always intended. And those men you've come to despise so much will be crucial to our victory." "What of McFarlane? Or do you forget that this all goes for nothing if you fail against that vengeful little maiden?" The other was a purist in regards to the wrestling side of their objectives, the consummate in-ring guru who preached focus and technique before all else. He would never allow the masked one to enter the ring with Dawn McFarlane until he had memorized every component of her style. Especially not with her reputed mindset. Shadow's fall had sparked something powerful in the girl, or so the rumors said. "She wants your head, Chaney. Surely you realize how serious this is? You will lose if you treat this as an afterthought." "The girl is interjecting herself into a spiritual war," commented Masquerade. They had taken their seats in an isolated corner of the car, far out of ear-shot from the nearest passengers, and his tone was as scathing as he cared to allow under any circumstance. "I cannnot allow petty affairs of the heart to stand between us and retribution. Her revenge will have to wait." Kendall opened his mouth in rebuke, only to be silenced by a simple gesture from the other. The lights in the dreary subway car flickered as a momentary silence passed between the tandem. "I've watched tapes. I don't go into battle blind. I simply acknowledge that I will win or lose this war on account of what happens outside the ring moreso than inside." Kendall seemed almost aghast at his associate's wanton dismissal. "You….you're not serious? She's dangerous…" He trailed off and sighed, observing the glassy vacancy through the eyeholes of the mask. He simply failed to understand the rationale behind his partner's choice to largely ignore Dawn McFarlane and realized that addressing the issue further would just be a futile effort. No appeal, regardless of how fervent, would convert his venerable elder over to his logic. "Yes, I've dismissed the girl." He hadn't needed to speak on it after all; perhaps the other truly could read him that well. "Partially. My battle is a spiritual one, completely immaterial. Before this reckoning, I shall reclaim the strength I once held. And when that happens…Dawn McFarlane will not and cannot stop me. The affection that was once my ruin will now become Dawn McFarlane's." The laugh emanating from beneath the mask was reserved but no less maniacal. Kendall folded his hands and gazed out the window at the tunnel lights streaming by in a luminous haze. "Trust me on this one. I have made all the arrangements. Dawn McFarlane will begging for defeat when all comes full-circle." * * * * On the outside, Maury Dawson was the picture of serenity. The regard he held for the other six individuals seated in a circle with him was of suspicion and impatience at best, disdain at worst; some he had found to be tolerable, if not enjoyable company, while a couple others evoked little but ire and enmity in their presence. A nagging sense of deja vu had pervaded his thoughts as he dealt with them all in recent days, a sense like memories striving to be freed. He had met Beckett just prior to Chaney defeating Shadow, a shady computer hacker who he was sure had a rather tumultuous past he had yet to acknowledge, and the others had been 'recruited' in the days since Calix's subsequent defeat. None failed to arouse that same inkling in his mind and it bothered him. Reading Dawson from the surface told the tale of a composed and placid businessman. Going deeper would reveal a lost soul full of questions and doubts, repressed feelings and dire regrets. They'd been brought into this room in what was almost a forced march, herded inside a conference suite in a hotel on the outskirts of some redneck town by a handful of brawny, silent-type fellows. Their "guides'"smiles were fragile, likely as fake as a Rolex hanging inside a dirty derelict's coat on a Detroit street corner. But then, if his own notions mirrored those of his new brethren, this rag-tag alliance was equally fragile. He knew nothing of the others beyond names, with the exception of Beckett. A television was the only other furniture in the room, an old Zenith model that looked imported from the 80's. Impatience was flourishing amongst the congregated before the screen inexplicably flickered to life, the VCR beneath whirring as the tape began to play. Doubtless it was controlled from a source outside the room. The figure in the chair presented himself with just the appearance that Maury expected. A clean suit, the silver and red striped tie as the only inclusion noteably unexpected. And, of course, that pallid and impassive mask that was so reminscient of Michael Myers in the Halloween series, his trademark that told of an inner pain veiled by outer apathy. At least, such was Maury Dawson's interpretation. The scene was the essence of simplicity, lacking in any fancy backdrop or elaborate props. Chaney looked ahead, his tone cold yet reassuring as he delivered his message; never had Maury believed the two could coexist and so plausibly retain their effects until he met his enigmatic benefactor. "You all been chosen as much for your frailties as for any skill or aptitude you may have. Every man seated around the table where you each now find yourself is a result of the same tragic cast: one who wanted to do good for the world and those they loved, and found themselves embroiled in corruption and schemes that betrayed all they believed in. Men who discovered too much about their backers and soon found themselves deceived and denounced by the same agency. Men living double-lives, the consequences paid for by those they loved when those savage truths were revealed." Maury could sense the discomfort in the room, a feeling stirring tangibly from every one of his newfound colleagues. It was a bittersweet experience, the relief that he wasn't the only one to have made mistakes while unnerving that others sharing that same room may have perpetuated deeds many times more wicked. Fists tightened on the table, he could feel his hands grow moist as sweat began to form. "You are a collection of martyrs and pariahs, once friendless and hopeless. You all have wandered through a state of Limbo, some have even seen Hell itself in a proverbial sense. Friends, I'm here to bring you back into the light. I've come to give you Eden. You all know each other, only by different aliases. Like me, every one of you has donned a mask in your past dealings, veiling your identities through shadows and vast networks. I am just like you. I am you, an embodiment of every individual in that room as you were before you felt the cruel hand of fate clench around your throat. As such, I shall be your voice and your guide as we claim our redemption, together as one." The figure in the video gently reclined, the squeak of the chair breaking the quiet of the hushed assembled. "Through your endeavors, you have learned that life is not the idyllic odyssey through paradise that so many would have you believe. It is often hard, dark and bleak. The road I offer you all is of the same nature: a hard path riddled with delicate judgements and questionable decisions, all which may eventually lead to the salvation you have all clambored for since your sins came back to haunt you. None of you is unaccustomed to the burden of a heavy conscience, not in your more 'unsavory' lines of work you've all undergone in the past." That was the one link they all shared. Dawson shuddered as he could only garner guesses at the sort of immorality gracing this room. The sensation of eyes resting on him too was not lost on one as perceptive as he. "The dossier you all now have before you holds, among other information, a revelation offered with the intention of keeping the one in question honest. And just as you now hold one of this person's most valued secrets, and possibly incriminating evidence along with it, someone else in that room is in possession of a similar nature about yourself. It are these frailties and dark secrets that bind us. My friends…if God can forgive you, then why can you not forgive each other? Why can you not forgive yourselves?" Even Dawson couldn't help but question the cryptic, almost hypocritical statement. But, like his colleagues, found himself mesmerized by the enigma on the tape. "All the necessary arrangements have been made. You need only follow the directions given to you in your individual folders, many of which involve your cooperation. You are all acting as my agents until such time as our mission is complete. Do not fear; all will be answered in due time. I bid you good luck and again offer my thanks. Farewell, friends. Our redemption is close at hand." "Pawns." Without even glancing up, Dawson knew the source of the venomous statement, already visualizing the twisted look on the face of Christian Beckett. "Being used, brainwashed into carrying out God-knows-what. We're feeding the machinations of a madman. A treacherous anti-Christ…" Dawson had grown sick of Beckett's blasphemy in the past several days and chose to just shut it out. It would make whatever relations they had to maintain all the more tolerable. Chaney may have been eccentric, perhaps even a bit callous in his affairs, but the man was no villain. Maury soon found himself alone. A silent lot, they had cleared out rather quickly and quietly, none offering so much as a greeting and never uttering a solitary word. He was almost afraid to open his dossier, opting to wait until he was free of this dingy hotel and back inside the manor on Avery Shields' estate. Whistling as he stood up to leave, he couldn't help but shake his head. Strange bedfellows indeed. * * * * He wept freely in his closed chamber, fumbling with the only tool he knew that could offer him refuge. A devout man of the cloth, even Father Rockwell had finally experienced the failure of prayer in stopping the hideous events that were beginning to unfold. One trembling hand still rested on the haggard cover of his Bible as even now the shadows crawled with obscene shapes, the air saturated with the promiscuous melodies of some unhallowed abyss. Hell was real, and it was coming for him. "For the greater good to prosper, sometimes an act of evil must be offered. I pray my present sin shall someday be considered the act of a saint. For my selfishness, I ask forgiveness in the holy name of our lord God, His son Jesus Christ and the Holy Ghost." A single tear spilled from his eye as he offered the prayer, caressing his cheek as it slid down past the corner of his lip. His fears had been made manifest and now madness consumed him. It was self-sacrifice or a straitjacket, God help him. Surely his sin would be forgiven if it helped others. "Amen." As his finger lightly brushed over the trigger of the revolver, he prayed that death would offer the solace he needed. His free hand drifted over his desk, grazed the letter and accompanying parcel he had received only hours ago in the mail. A single shot pierced the silence of the cathedral, and with it quelled the horrors of a dying old man who had seen the face of evil. The gun fell from his limp fingers, clattering atop the desk before coming to rest beside the worn Holy Book he had held for over thirty years. Glassy, lifeless eyes stared forward at the sinister mask that had come with the letter, its significance a long-time secret between the sender and its new bearer. There were men who knew, and could prevent the potential cataclysm that would follow, but it would potentially be a long time before it was unearthed by those knowledgable persons. Father Rockwell offered a silent hope that it would be soon enough, the last thing thought that passed through his mind before the bullet silenced his prayers forever.
