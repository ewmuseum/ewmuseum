Size Matters

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 "And then, we can go get those publicity shots taken. I don't know how you forgot the last two appointments. Not after I reminded you a dozen times and even put a black 'X' on your forehead so you'd remember." Coach glances up from the stove at his young protégée. She's sat at the kitchen table which is groaning under the weight of breakfast. Stacks of fried food vie for space with a wide selection of sugar encrusted cereals. Doreen Pepperpot, GTT5 entrant, steadily and systematically works her way round them all. Tucking into a plate of pancakes, she mumbles, "Hmm, maybe I'd be a bit more inclined to have my photo taken if I didn't have permanent marker smeared all over my face, don'tcha think? Normally, you put the 'X' on the back of your hand. And besides, I think it's a stupid idea. No one really needs to have pictures of me do they? Can't we spend the money on something else?" He flips an egg in the pan, "Come on sweetie, you're a fabulous wrestler, and once everyone knows it, your merchandise will fly of the shelves!" "Coach!" "Call me Dad." "No I bloody won't! You're not my…" Doreen stops herself mid-sentence. There's no point arguing like this. She knows Coach means well, it's just that sometimes, it can be so hard putting up with him when he gets like this. "I'm sorry. Look, when I call you Coach it's like, a term of respect or something? I'm just trying to acknowledge all that you've done for me." Coach smiles and slides the fried egg from the spatula onto Doreen's plate. "You're such a good kid. I meant what I said though; you're going to get those shots taken." Doreen's shoulders drop and she lets out a sigh. In a small voice, she says, "This isn't going to work. I'm never going to be the person you want." "What's that sweetie? Do you want more sausages? Protein's important." She shakes her head no. Dragging her fork in circles through the pool of grease on her plate, she feels a wave of guilt wash over her. What would her mother think of all this? Before her thoughts can turn maudlin, the stereo in the dining room attached to the kitchen bursts into sound with Ashlee Simpson's latest musical massacre. "Aw fuck!" She turns to confront her sister as she pirouettes through the doorway. Sharifa, younger than Doreen by 4 years and the bane of her life, takes a bow and gracefully perches on a stool at the table. "It’s way past nine, why aren't you in school?" Sticking her tongue out at Doreen and then smiling sweetly at Coach, she replies, "Why should I? You've missed weeks and weeks. Only fair that I get to ditch too." Doreen looks imploringly at Coach, "Tell her she has to go to school, please? I really don't want her hanging around all day. She does my head in. And anyway, if she doesn't go, she'll miss all her extra curricular stuff." "Now Doreen, be nice to your sister. She doesn't have all the advantages you have." The girls look at him in amazement, Doreen more advantaged than Sharifa? "It's going to be a lot harder for Sharifa when she grows up. She'll never have your strength and body mass. What good will ballet, athletics and gymnastics be in the ring? Bigger is so much better, and by God Doreen, you are the biggest around." "Yeah, you're Moby Doreen: the whale who thinks it's a wrestler!" Doreen grabs Sharifa by the neck and pulls her to the ground to pin her there with one hand. With the other, she angrily brushes away a tear. "I'll never forgive you for telling the papers that nickname. It was bad enough when it was just at school… but you just had to make sure the whole bloody world knew! I HATE YOU!!" Aware that she's moments away from doing something she shouldn't, she tears herself away from her sister with surprising agility and slams her way past Coach to her room. Coach's face creases in confusion, "Moby Doreen? I was thinking more 'The Big Showess', but it does have a ring to it. Can't understand why she's upset though. All good wrestlers need a catchy name. Do you think it might be hormones? I’m not sure I signed up for hormones" Crying alone in her bedroom, Doreen doesn't hear Coach's last comment, but if she did, it would cause her to scream with frustration. All she's ever wanted was to make everyone else happy. All her life she's been the dutiful daughter, the sister who's always on hand to help with homework, the pupil who is actually interested in what her teachers have to teach. But these last few months, it's all started to come apart. What began as a simple deception to please a friend, her only friend, has grown far too big for even her to handle. Like the driver of a runaway team of horses, she can only hang on tightly, eyes wide with fear and wait for the ride to end. Never normally the type to bemoan her fate, Doreen whispers her first "Why me?" of many. But she isn't given too much time to dwell on her situation. The doorbell rings and Doreen gently pulls the curtain open a crack, just enough to peep out, but not enough to announce she's there. Seeing the little red Honda parked by the kerb, her heart leaps in her chest. She recognizes the car and that it's here again can mean only one thing. They know… or at least suspect. All she has to do is open the curtain a little more, let her presence be known, and all this could end. Such a simple action couldn't be a sin could it? Sure, she knows the consequences… but could she really be blamed for all that would happen if she just… just moved this curtain a teeny tiny bit? Frozen with indecision for too long, Doreen watches helplessly as the woman turns and walks back to the Honda, taking her chance for salvation with her. She sits in the car for a few minutes more, making a note in the folder propped open on the steering wheel. The woman in the nasty beige suit starts the engine and pulls away, out of Doreen's life for another day. Unable to decide if this bodes good or bad for her, Doreen cries her second 'why me?' into the empty room. Back in the kitchen, Coach and Sharifa emerge from under the table. "That was close. She's calling earlier and earlier in the day now. We may have to start moving around hotels for a while. I hope you don't mind pumpkin, but it's imperative that we get Doreen through this." Sharifa, too young to really care what's going on as long as everything continues to her benefit, just shrugs and sticks her iPod on. Things have never been better. A few hours later, Doreen's mood drops further into despair with each outfit presented to her. All her life she's known she was different to other kids. All her life she's had to put up with what that meant. But never has she had to face those differences so bluntly. There are few mirrors at her home and definitely none so brightly lit and… life sized as the one she is now standing in front of. If she looks at the various parts of her body separately, it never seems so bad. Her arms are big. Sure. But they are muscular and in proportion. Her shoulders are broad, but they need to be. How else could she support a neck like that? She knew just how unusual it was for a girl to have such a neck and not be a body builder… her classmates had taunted her often enough about it. When she was younger, they hadn't dared say anything to her, scared that she would hurt them. But it didn't take long for the smarter ones to realize that despite her size, Doreen wouldn't be able to hurt them, it simply wasn't in her character to do so. And thus the nicknames began. "Frankenstein" due to her immense sloping forehead. "Great White Whale" because of not just her size, but the skin that burns when the sun barely peeks from behind the clouds. "Crusher" followed her around for a while after she accidentally sat on a first grader, but was soon changed to "Blubber Butt". "It" as in Cousin It from the Adam's Family when she grew her hair long to hide as much of herself as she could. Looking in the mirror now, Doreen identifies with all these names and more. There’s the nose that is too small for her face and the eyes set just slightly too close together to ever be considered attractive. The only thin thing about her is her lips, which make her look kind of mean. Her skin is pocked with acne scars and there’s a permanent crop of blackheads across her nose that no product in the beauty isle can banish. Her eyebrows are wild looking, but she’s never seen the point in plucking them, she would still be as ugly. Her one redeeming feature is her naturally straight, pearly white teeth. Something never noticed as on the rare occasions she smiles, Doreen covers her mouth with her hand, never wanting to draw attention to herself. And yet here she was. Slathered in oily make-up and kitted out in a leotard, waiting to have photographs taken. Waiting for those photos to be sent out in a press pack to all the wrestling magazines. Waiting to turn on the TV and see herself as a human interest piece on the local 6 o' clock news. Waiting for the ridicule; the laughter. Waiting for the 'you go girl' letters from women so big they can’t walk and so bitterly slam their mobility scooters through the crowds at the mall. "See, she's big and she made something of herself. She's not scared to be out there, to be seen for what she is. Big is beautiful" But how little they know. There was nothing in the world that Doreen is more scared of than the click of a camera shutter. Except perhaps the knowledge that in less than a week, she would be in a wrestling ring facing a formidable opponent, wearing a skin tight suit as dozens of TV cameras follow her every move, zoom in on every wobble and illuminate every flaw. Unable to bear the thought any longer, she runs to the bathroom to throw up noisily in the white porcelain bowl. Her whole body shakes and tears flow freely down her face. She crawls back to the dressing room when done and curls up as small as she can in the only corner where she can't see her reflection cruelly looking back at her. It isn't long until Coach comes in. "Sweetie, you ready? This guy charges by the hour." Seeing his charge shuddering and hugging her knees to her chest, slumped on the floor, he softens his tone. "Sweetheart, what's happened? Are you Ok?" Sniveling, she gulps, "No! I can't do this… not even for you. Please don't make me do this!" "The photographs? Jeez Doreen, I swear I never knew it would be such a problem for you. I can't understand it. You're such a fine specimen of a wrestler." "That's just it coach. You're looking at me like I'm someone I'm not. I'm not a wrestler. I'm a 16 year old fat girl with an incurable growth hormone problem. I don't want to be compared favorably to Kane and The Big Show. I just want to be left alone. I want to be an anonymous kid again." "Shh! You're supposed to pass for 20, remember. Otherwise I don’t think we’d have been able to enter you in the tournament!" Sighing, Doreen lowers her head to rest on her knees. "You're not listening to me, are you? I don’t want to have my photograph taken. It's one of the hardest things you could ever ask of me. I'm not a proper wrestler. I don't need a press pack or a new costume to wear in the ring. I'm a joke. I should never have entered GTT5, and somewhere deep down, you know that too." Coach can feel his heart close to breaking point as he stares down at the one person that means something to him in this world. Can it be that he's the one causing her such distress? How can something like this happen? Mystified, he asks, "Doreen, what do you mean you're not a wrestler? Hasn't that been our dream for years?" Knowing where the conversation could go, and not wanting it to go there, Doreen bites back what she’d like to say, that it's been Coaches dream for someone else, not her, boring Doreen, and instead meekly whispers, "Yes Coach." "That’s my girl! You know, I reckon it’s just stage fright or something. You’ve never been in a tournament as big as this." Without thinking, she replies, "Coach, I’ve never been in any tournament before. Remember? That was… someone else." Perplexed, he stares at Doreen, "Are you sure? I could have sworn…" Pain grips him tightly through his chest. Shaking his head and squeezing his eyes tightly shut, he banishes the memories bubbling up to the surface. Horrified at the anguish etched so clearly on his face, Doreen can only stare at him as begins to flail at his own head, muttering all the time. Is this her doing? Is she responsible for this? Doreen can’t help but feel guilty. Coach may be the adult around here, but she’s the only one in true touch with reality. All she’s wanted is to protect him from pain. Is that such a bad thing? How can she cause such hurt? It all started so simply. When Coach first began to substitute her for someone else in his mind, she let it slide thinking, well, if it comforts him, then what’s the harm? It was just one more eccentricity. But the more he misidentified her, the more she colluded until she was so wrapped up in his delusional matrix that she could see no way of untangling it. Soon, he started imposing his delusions on reality and although there were people she could call on for help, she couldn’t bear to betray coach to them. She knew how fragile Coach’s mind was at the best of times, and grief had twisted his vulnerable psyche even more. She vowed to stand by him no matter what… even though that would mean going along with his plan; his grand scheme for Doreen to enter GTT5, to win, and to do him proud. Looking at his wide grinning face, smiling for the first time since the funeral, how could she have refused? And so she let their daily sessions escalate from exercise to serious training. She helped him forge a note to get her out of school for the last month. She wrote e-mails filled with false reports to her mother. She bribed her sister with the years of allowance she had saved. Always an honest girl, she cringed inwardly as each layer of the lie grew. But it was all for the greater good. Wasn’t it? This was giving Coach time to get strong enough to deal with his grief. It was giving him time for his sub-conscious to come to terms with the accident. Wasn’t that better than running to his Community Psychiatric Team? Better than opening the door to the nurse with the little red Honda? How could she do that knowing they’d simply take him away again? Lock him up and pump him full of anti-psychotics and anti-depressants, again. Knowing that it would chip away at soul, eat at his personality, mute his feelings and blunt his understanding. And so Doreen taps him on the shoulder, eager to distract him from the turmoil of feelings sloshing around beneath the surface. "Coach! Talk to me. Come on, let’s talk about the match. Tell me about Butterfly Hamada!" He lets his hands slide to side, no longer clawing at his skin. A tentative smile briefly plays across his face. "Butterfly Hamada, eh?" "Yes, tell me, do I have a chance against her? She’s been wrestling since the day she could walk; she has it in her blood. What do I have to do to make sure she fails?" Sitting up straighter, more focused now, he grins, "Why Doreen, all you have to do to make her fail, is to make sure you win. Play to your strengths, but always be wary of hers and react accordingly." Doreen snorts derisively, "Strengths? What you mean the fact that I’m just plain bigger than everyone else? That’s hardly an achievement on my part. I can’t help the way I was made." Coach slides his hand gently under her chin and tilts it up gently, looking her straight in the eye, "Doreen Pepperpot. I’ve known you all your life, correct?" "Yes." "I’ve watched you grow into a fine moral young woman with strength of character that could put a saint to shame. I’ve seen how your mind works, how you take note of everything around you and use it to the best advantage of others. Use those skills for yourself this time. Analyze and re-analyze the situation in the ring continuously. If Hamada makes a mistake, you’ll know. Use it against her." Doreen nods. He may be unhinged, but when it comes to wrestling, there’s no one more astute. "Use your size to your advantage. All you have to do is take her down to the floor and the match is yours. She’s small and quick. She’ll try to tire you out and keep it all in the air. Don’t let her. We’ve practiced for this for years. When you step into that ring, all that training will guide your instincts. Don’t let Hamada’s reputation chip away at your confidence. She may be hailed as the hottest new thing around, but that’s only because they don’t know you yet. And whatever happens, I’ll be there for you. Feel better now?" She smiles tentatively. Maybe everything won’t turn out as bad as she fears after all. If Coach can be strong for her when so unwell, then surely she can be strong for him. If she can just get through this round, can pin down the Butterfly, she can buy herself enough time to figure a way out of this mess. A way to buy back the lies and fix her friend.
