Montgomery/Salos/Medina Interfed Triple Threat

19 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Montgomery RP For the sake of a little variety, our scene opens up in the back passanger area of a Hummer-style limousine that would make Donald Trump jealous. Sprawled across the back seat is UWF World Heavyweight Champion and Executive Vice President of Talent Relations, Mike Montgomery. The man who sent out an open challenge to the entire PTC Community for a match. The car pulls up to the curb. Montgomery looks out the side window to see that the vehicle has stopped in front of a McDonalds in Chicago. He watches the driver climb out, walk around to the side of the limo facing the curb, and open the back door. Cramming himself into the seat beside Montgomery is the massive three hundred and twenty pound frame of seven foot two bodyguard Gavin Frost, Montgomery's #2 man and placed in charge of the Corporate Intelligence division of Asylum Industries. In his right hand is a black attache case which he rests across his lap. "Mr. Frost," Montgomery says in greeting, "a pleasure as always. What do you have for me?" "An answer," comes Frost's reply, "two of them actually." Montgomery raises an eyebrow, a smile creeping across his face, "Really? I must say I'm surprised. What can you tell me about my opponents?" The limo pulls back into traffic as Frost pushes a pair of buttons on the top of the attache case, causing it's clasps to snap open. Inside is a stack of two folders, beside which sits a pair of video tapes. Frost withdraws the top folder, "Salos, J." handwritten across the folder's ID tab. "First is Jacob Robert Salos," Frost reads as he opens the folder, "born July 2nd, 1978 in Los Angeles California. He's a recent signee with Core Wrestling, previous experience with the DWO and GW. We're thinking he hasn't been around that long...but if he's former DWO, that's something you've got to be prepared for. They're well known for their talent." "True, true," Montgomery says, nodding, "that's what makes this interesting though. Well now we know Jacob Salos the man, what have you got on Salos, the wrestler?" "Plenty," Frost flips to the next page in the file, "as far as his promos are concerned...he no-showed the first round of GTT 4 and hasn't done anything with Core yet so we haven't got anything on his interview style." "Nothing at all?" Montgomery asks. Reaching across to a TV/VCR setup built into the back of the front seats, Frost slides a tape in and hits play. "No, not nothing," Frost explains as the tape starts, "this is from Core's Pay Per View of ten days ago when Mr. Salos 'signed' his contract." The two men sit in silence, staring intently as the tape plays through the 8 minute segment. When the tape goes black, Montgomery leans forward, fingers steepled in front of him as he reflects on what he's just seen. "Hmm...interesting," Montgomery muses, "Very interesting. Confident, clearly self-assured...perhaps a bit naive though. Little respect for authority...I like him though. A great deal of talent, simply looks like he needs to learn about how to properly deal with authority." "You may be right," Frost comments, "but he may have the level of talent that allows him to buck against authority to the extent he did there. Shall I continue with the briefing?" Montgomery considers it for a moment, chewing on his lip for an instant, "No, not yet anyway." Montgomery pulls a car-phone from the armrest, quickly dialing a number and holding the receiver to his ear, "Yes, Deacon Blaise please...Deacon? Hi, it's Mike, how's everything?...Uh huh, uh huh...great...listen, how's your schedule for the next few days?...Really? That's perfect. Pack your stuff then. I'm going to need you for some consulting...yeah...I can be there in about twenty minutes...great, I'll see you then." Hanging up the phone, Montgomery turns on the direct line to the limo's driver, "Driver, Blaise's gym please." Blaise's gym is where Montgomery works out on a regular basis and a gym that is also one of the many subsidiary holdings of Asylum Industires. Deacon Blaise, the gym's manager, is also Montgomery's personal trainer. "Right away sir." Turning back toward Frost, Montgomery explains, "I'd like to bring Deacon in before we start talking style and mechanics. What do we have on my other opponent?" Sliding the Salos folder into the bottom of the briefcase, Frost pulls out the second folder which is labeled "Medina, J." Opening the folder, he begins to read, "Jonathan Medina, nick name 'Blaze.' Born October 23, 1977, also hailing from Los Angeles California. Previous employers include the ASWA, IWO, IWZ, XWF, as well as various 'indy' organizations. He was the IWZ Intercontinental Champion, #1 contender to the IWZ world title, as well as #1 contender to the XWF tag team titles with a man by the name of Ronin BlackKnight. "With XUW, he's scored a run as the organization's 'Submission Champion.' He'll probably be the bigger threat of the two, he's sporting a seven and two record." Montgomery strokes his chin, muttering, "quite the winning percentage there." "Indeed it is," Frost continues, "he was trained by his uncle, known in wrestling circles as BoneCrusher. His style consists of a mix of technical, daredevil, and submission. I'll leave the details for when we pick up Mr. Blaise, however, I am obligated to note that he has been criticized for working too stiff. He's got the skill, but that stiffness may be a problem." "Indeed," Montgomery nods, "I'd prefer not to lose my world title to injury a second time. Please, continue." "Medina spent the majority of his childhood with his father, a heroin addict. Father dead at seventeen, at which point Medina signed up with the Marines where he served from 95 to 99. After which he made his debut with the ASWA while training with his uncle. He changed his name to Beret and teamed with Snyper to form a group known as the 'Lethal Enforcers.' Determined, but not sufficiently experienced, they didn't achieve much success before the ASWA closes in 2001. After that, our friend becomes Jonny Blaze and his partner changes his name to Nick Fury." "You're kidding," Montgomery states, stifling a laugh. "You're the one who tagged with a teddy bear, remember?" "Mr. Frost, if you ever mention that again..." "Moving on," Frost says quickly, "the two never caught on as a team. Medina finally signs with the IWZ in 2002, alternating between them and the Indies for about a year and a half. He then also signed on with the XWF, primarily as the tag partner for Ronin BlackKnight. At this point Medina is alternating between the IWZ, the XWF, and the Indies. After losing two tag title matches, Medina returns full time to the IWZ. His IWZ time ends with the company's last two matches. Medina goes from there to the XUW where he makes his debut at their last Stablewars Pay Per View." Montgomery sits back in his seat, closing his eyes for a few minutes. Taking a deep breath, he doesn't say anything for a few moments, allowing the vast amount of information that Frost has provided to sink in. He almost lurches forward when the limo pulls to a stop. The two men hear the driver's door open and slam shut. Looking out the window, Montgomery realizes that they've arrived at Blaise's Gym. The passenger door opens, admitting the six foot five inch frame of Deacon Blaise. Brushing his long, black hair to the side, Blaise slides into a seat opposite Frost and Montgomery. There is a barely perceptible bounce as the driver loads Blaise's bags into the trunk. The car is moving again seconds later. "Airport," Montgomery says into the intercom. The driver's response is barely perceptable as Montgomery continues, "Deacon, glad you could join us. Here's the deal, I've got a Primetimecentral Interfed Triple Threat match. Opponents are Jake Salos of Core Wrestling and Jonathan Medina of XUW." "And you want me to go over the files you undoubtedly have on them, look over their styles and moves of choice, then determine how best to counter their arsenal, right?" Blaise asks. "Basically," comes the answer, "you up for it?" Blaise nods, "Show me what you have." "Well, we're at the good part actually," Frost tells him, "unlike Mr. Salos, we actually do have promos from Mr. Medina. The first," he draws a tape from the attache case, "is his first round taping from GTT 4." The three men watch the twenty minute tape in silence. The tape cuts from the black to static, before finally popping out of the machine. No one says a word for several minutes afterward. "Well..." Blaise doesn't say anything. "Indeed," Montgomery says, "you have to respect a man with that kind of never say die attitude. I must admit to being rather honored that this was one of the men to answer my challenge. What else have you got for me Mr. Frost?" Frost withdraws the final tape and pushes it into the VCR, "As it happens, Medina sent this promo out along with his response to your challenge, Mike." "Really?" Montgomery asks, "I like him already. Put it on Mr. Frost, let's see what Mr. Medina has to say about me. I'm rather intrigued." The three watch the final tape, hanging on every word, studying every second of footage. Nobody notices the limo coming to a stop at a private airfield. All three men imperceptibly jump as the passenger door opens. Frost closes up the attache case just before the three exit the limo and head for a nearby Lear Jet. The three men quietly board and sit quietly as the pilot goes through the pre-flight checklist. The whine of the jet's engines prevents any attempt at conversation as the plane rockets down the runway. Finally, cruising at thirty thousand feet, Montgomery turns toward the other two men, "Alright gentlemen, let's break it down. My usual psychological warfare won't work here, so it'll have to be a straight up physical contest. What do I have to look forward to, Mr. Frost? Let's start with Salos." Frost nods, placing the attache case on a table in between the group. Flipping the clasps open, he lifts the lid and removes Salos' file, "Okay, Salos is six foot two, two hundred forty five pounds. Technical wrestler primarily...his set up move's a Fisherman's Suplex DDT known as the 'Saloplex.' After that, the finisher of choice is the 'Devastation Driver' where he sets up for a Scoop Slam...then piledrive's his opponent to the mat." Montgomery nods, taking it all in, "Thoughts Deacon?" Deacon takes a moment, pondering the question, "A couple. Your safest bet is probably going to be a his Saloplex. Use your momentum and you can probably flip right through the move and land on your feet. If he manages to get to the Devastation Driver...that's when your life becomes a lot more difficult. If you can wriggle out of the scoop slam, take the opportunity, otherwise it's game over and Salos wins." Montgomery stands up, arching his back. He walks over to the mini-bar fridge near the front of the cabin and pulls out a can of coke. He offers cans to both Frost and Blaise, however the two both decline. Montgomery pulls the tab on his can as he returns to his seat. He takes a long swig from it before nodding to Frost to continue the briefing. Frost slides the "Salos" folder back into his attache case and removes the one marked "Medina." "Now Medina's a bit more up your alley when it comes to style, sir," Frost begins, "also a technical wrestler, Medina does incorporate some of the more high-flying cruiserweight style moves into his arsenal. Medina's 6 foot 4, two hundred twenty pound frame gives him quite a few options, most notably the Trail Blazer. According to our sources, this might be one of stiffest missle dropkicks around. His finisher of choice is the 'Funky Cold Medina,' a jumping Emerald Fusion Two. As I mentionned, he's the XUW Submissions champ, so he's also got an extensive arsenal of submission moves, most notably what he calls the 'Silent Assault' which is a spinning head-scissors take down into a Figure Four Choke." "Chokes are illegal in this match," Montgomery mentions. "That's true. However, that doesn't mean he won't use it. I'd recommend being ready for it no matter what." Montgomery nods in agreement, "Fair enough. Deacon?" Throughout this entire conversation, Blaise has been relaxing in his seat, eyes closed, seemingly oblivious to everything going on around him. When he hears his name, Blaise's eyes snap open and he sits up. "Well, countering the Trail Blazer is easy," he begins, "duck. If that kick is as stiff as Mr. Frost's people think it is, you've got to avoid that at all costs. The Silent Assault...the standard powerbomb counter for the headscissors should do the trick. You may be able to go straight into the 'Power Play' from there as well. The Funky Cold Medina's going to be much trickier though. The best I can recommend...if you time it right...is a tornado DDT. However, and I can't stress this enough, the timing will have to be absolutely perfect. If not, you could do some serious damage to your head and neck." "I'll take that under advisement," Montgomery comments, "any other thoughts?" "Just this," Blaise answers, "both men require a vertical base to be even remotely effective. Use your Eternal Struggle ankle lock as much as possible and you take that vertical base away. If you can do that, the match is yours." "Thank you gentlemen." Montgomery tells Frost and Blaise, "I must say though, it will almost be a shame to win this." Deacon Blaise arches an eyebrow, "How so?" "Well, a man like Jonathan Medina is someone that's very hard to find nowadays. In this business you so often get the standard 'I'm better than you, no one can stop me, you're nothing compared to me' riff-raff that I must say it's rather refreshing to find a man who shows as healthy a respect for his opponent as Medina does. I wonder if maybe he should be the first..." "The first?" "Yes, I'm wondering if he should be the first to fall before my Destiny Manifest...or will he feel the Lightning Strike...perhaps he'll be the butt of the Hangman's Joke...or suffer his own Fall From Grace...the possibilities are indeed endless. Either way, Medina and I, in a sense, are on equal ground. His Submissions championship...my two Shoot Wrestling Titles...but I think I do have one significant advantage over both of my opponents." "And what would that be?" Frost asks. "Simple. Experience. Neither man has held a world heavyweight title. All told I've held about six. I've seen it all and I've done it all. Medina's been in the business for a long time, but unlike him, I'm the Main Event. It's all about me, and they'll just have to deal with that." The three men return to studying tapes as the scene fades out.
