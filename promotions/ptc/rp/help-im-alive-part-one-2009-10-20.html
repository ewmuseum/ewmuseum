Help; I’m Alive (Part One)

20 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-20 Ever hear about the guy who took the plunge off of a skyscraper? Wind nearly stripping him naked, unfortunate bastard plummets past every floor as bored office workers daydreaming out the windows pop wide-eyed and spurt sips of coffee out their noses. The floors tick down to the ground, 15, 14, 13, 12, and he keeps repeating the mantra; “so far, so good… so far, so good… so far, so good… so far, so good…“. But the fall doesn’t matter. It’s how you land. I’m not that good with words, so I kinda stole that from some French movie I never sat through to the end of. Was black and white and had subtitles so has to be pretty poignant, right? I 06:52, The Morning After Karina Wolfenden’s claggy, panda eyes peeled themselves apart as a column of low morning sunlight toppled upon her. Behind unravelling, tarry lashes, timid eyes squinted against the warm glare intruding through the kitchen’s window. A numb ache paralysed her right flank where her hip bone had chilled against the ceramic tiles. Her cheek had been spared a similar fate only because of a nauseating pillow of blonde, black and purple hair woven with a thick porridge of her own vomit. Like an eighties relic of a computer booting up, Karina’s body awoke muscle by muscle, nerve by nerve. A dull throbbing deep in her stomach drew her into a foetal ball, triggering a an iron maiden of sharp pains all over her body, from mottled brown and purple forehead to cracking, split-nail toe. “Ugh,” resonated deep within her aching skeleton. Her tongue slowly crept from an arid, acidic mouth to probe her split lip, where taste buds were stung by the stomach-churning cocktail of ferrous, congealed blood and tangy citrus sick. Her souring face collapsed inward as her gullet recoiled, ready to expel another toxic stomachfull before it could further pollute her lame body. Bare feet wading through a quicksand of half-digested junk food and liquor, Karina’s dirty fingernails scratched down the blackboard tiles of the kitchen floor, scrambling towards the sink. Her raw-knuckled right hand clung to the tap so that her gaping, puss-encrusted elbow could lever her up to expel a putrid, garbage-juice brown cocktail from her stomach. The caramel whey to the lumpy curds that she awoke in. “Auntie Karina!” cheeped gleefully behind her. An excited pitter-patter of slipper soles slapped against the kitchen tiles as Karina’s clammy grip on the tap faltered and she crumpled down against the cabinets. Trawling the tatty remnants of her sleeve across her face, a weary Wolfenden did her ill-conceived best to appear presentable on the special day. The flutter of the young girl’s eager footsteps subsided at the sight of Karina’s crimson, lilac and black facemask. “Did you be sick?” she accused, with an innocent hint of a lecture. Struggling against bruise-constricted facial muscles and the stinging of lacerated skin, Karina broke a horrific, bloody-toothed smile across her face. The excitement on the young girl‘s face morphed into one of fear, her few years not rendering her judgemental enough to contort her features in disgust. “Erin!” Karina slurred, dulled senses amplifying her voice. “Promised you I wouldn’t miss your birthday…” II 20:53, The Night Before Ripples of applause whooshed around within Karina Wolfenden’s ear as she hung in the air like a frenzied, multicoloured gyroscope. Instinct alone assuring that she wasn’t about to pogo off a brittle spring of her own vertebrae, Karina exploded out of her rapidly spinning ball with a standing, twisting 450 splash. Just like she did every match. An energetic wave of appreciation was emitted from the packed-in crowd, crashing against the ring apron to spray Karina with a stuffy, salty mist of adulation. Towards the beacon of her flushed opponent the K-Wolf rushed, inverting her lithe frame in the blink of an alert eye. Gloved palms tightly embraced the canvas before tearing themselves away from the brief affair to propel Karina up into a cartwheel corner kick. Just like she did every single match. The dense, muggy air that encapsulated the ring buoyed Wolfenden as she again spun frenetically around her body’s own axis, racking up degrees of rotation like an arcade score. 90. 180. 270. 360. 540. 630. New High Score. Just like she did every single match. Like an Olympic high-diver, Karina shuffled on tip-toes to her mark before soaring up into a mesmerising ball of psychedelic, swirling colour. If one flap of a butterfly’s wings could cascade into a hurricane, Karina Wolfenden just brought about the inevitable destruction of planet Earth. She landed the standing corkscrew moonsault with laser-guided precision. Of course she did. Just like she did single fucking match Lather. Rinse. Repeat. It was then, after clocking up enough air miles to need a formal reintroduction to terra firma, it dawned on the K-Wolf like a glorious beam of the obvious over her jaded horizon. She was bored out of her fucking mind, playing Russian roulette with a cylinder of empty chambers. With a sharp rip of velcro, the gloves came off; literally. Swift, Matador steps danced her around the wounded bull of her opponent, stabbing fierce, unchecked jabs into his face. The anger brimming within him vented through his nostrils rather than his fists. Groggy eyes traced Karina’s sneaker as it took off from the canvas on a graceful arc of flight, before ploughing into his cheekbone with shattering ferocity. She urged, begged, dared, goaded, seduced him to hit her back. But unlike Karina Wolfenden, he was not comfortably wrapped up for the next 4 years in a warm, cosy guaranteed contract. He silently took to the ring with an extra $250 padding out his wallet and the scant hope that a good impression will reward him with a phone call. He was Glenn Hibbert, 32 year old Staples warehouse worker, father of two, and thanks to the fierce lash of an un-cushioned spinning backfist, the possessor of one less tooth than the normal adult human. Just when she wanted something stiff, he went limp on her, spilling out through the ropes and across the frigid concrete. Disengaging the last of her fraying failsafes, Karina locked on to her jelly-legged, punch-drunk opponent, closed her eyes, then launched her body through the torpedo-bay ring ropes. Reaching terminal velocity, lids were peeled back from burning emerald eyes, and rapidly approaching steel barricade bars were branded onto her retina. III 23:45, The Night Before “Down a notch, Starbuck,” a low, gravely growl from Christopher Sheffield urged. “Already got one bump on the head tonight.” Known to the wrestling world as Alias, the Original Pulp Hero, Sheffield was used to blowing out the regularly lit fuses of his temperamental partner, but tonight he was on full bomb-disposal duty. Yearning thirst for a fight unquenched, the prickly Karina reacted violently to every stimulus, and there were many of those to be found on Doherty’s. It was a bar packed to dry-rotting timbers with character; so far as to say even the health inspector left with a side-order of hepatitis to go with his meaty backhander. “This isn’t a frigging spectator sport,” she hissed under the claustrophobic weight of the bar’s attention. In all fairness to the Doherty’s territorial patrons, the Original Pulp Hero had a tendency to gravitate attention towards him. Ripped arms were decorated with two sleeves of Aztec tattoos, war wounds adorned every inch of flesh and a scar slashed down his left eye, draining it of colour. It was the anaemic, dead eye which had drawn the attention of one particular patron. Whiskey lubricated his tongue and inhibitions had been pissed a good foot wide of the mouldy urinal. “You oughta keep an eye on that one,” he mocked to hoots from the hyenas who’d joined his pack. Stroking his grey-speckled, unkempt beard, Shane Buckley winked towards the irritable K-Wolf. Spoiling for a fight, Karina burst up from her table and broke the tackle of the Pulp Hero. Tensions cascaded through rickety furniture, tipping two dozen inebriated souls up to their feet to pen in Wolfenden and Sheffield. Chests puffed, chins pointed and lips pursed, but not a fist did fly. “Afraid yah won’t find a fight here, sweetheart,” Buckley snidely grinned back, then cocked his head to Karina. “Or you, lassie.” With Alias peeling her back from the violent attraction to Shane, a gnashing K-Wolf glared around to see not a single clenched fist or brandished bottle. Not even a twirled pool cue. Neutered in all but tongue. Engaging Buckley in a killing-spree of a death stare, Karina seethed. “Gonna make me beg?” IV 23:47, The Night Before The bristly haired, naval tattoo decorated arm of Jack Sweeny barred the path of his spent-fused drinking buddy. “Easy,” soothing words urged, lips barely parting to crease his weather-worn face. “Easy,” Sweeny repeated with a sharper edge, responding to another angered jolt. Wiping a stout’s foamy head from his thick, wiry moustache, Jack had his barring arm snapped at with an aggressive bark of, “woiy?”. The explanation would require him to polish off the last third of his pint. “You don’t know who that is?” the storyteller in Jack enquired. “Course you don’t. Why would you? Who she is, that isn’t important. Who she used to share her fluffy little bed with… that is.” Dispensing ethanol-soaked wisdom from a creaky bar stool, Jack Sweeny’s soft words polished his dank, grimy surroundings. His pitted nose grew redder with every pint of stodgy, cloudy stout which passed his lips. “That would be a Mr. Deville, Barney,” Jack explained, words hanging in the dusty air with intrigue. “The Angel of Crimson let it be known a Halo were to be put upon that pretty little hot head of hers. Don’t matter that he ended up calling the little thing words too foul for even this dive, or that his black heart hasn’t beaten in a fair while, nobody took that Halo off and so nobody’s touching a fair hair on her head. You know Frank Kilcullen? His youngest gets pushed around in a wheelchair because of that unassuming little thing over there. I‘ve seen Frank break a full house of fingers and thumbs for splashing a raindrop from a puddle onto his Mother‘s shoes, but he‘s been bottling all that rage up so that it rots his very soul rather than go after that girl right there.” “’im?” whisky soaked cynicism snarled. “Greasy bastard’s dead as a doornail.” “Oh, so you saw a body?” Sweeny interrogated, a grumbling shuffle in his seat expressing his unease at the slut on the deceased. “You been taking sly coroner’s examinations after we stagger out of here every night without telling me now, Barney? Have the pleasure of an invite to an open-casket service that I didn’t?” Flushed jowls vibrated with a conceding scoff. “No, I didn’t think so,” Jack triumphed. “So until someone digs up his bones and drums a jaunty little farewell song on his headstone, then she’s untouchable. Only souls who can take it off are six feet under, and standing over there running her pretty little mouth off.” Jack clunked his empty pint glass on the nobbled bar. “Your round, young Barney,” he decreed. “Now, if you’ll excuse me for a swift minute, I’ve some watering to do.” V 06:59, The Morning After “I don’t want her in our fucking house, Max!” hissed Alice Wolfenden who, as 7am chimed from the hallway’s Grandfather clock, was far from enamoured with her sister-in-law. “I told you I didn’t want your fuck-up of a sister to have a key, because I knew this would happen… again.” Despite pale blue eyes that appeared all the more frosty bordered by bangs of raven hair, there was a fire raging within Alice which threatened to melt the accusing fake nail which she jabbed towards her husband. “She’s here for Erin,” was Max’s hushed appeal. “In her own way, this is actua-” “No,” ferociously slammed the door on the unfortunate fingers of any argument. “Just… no. I don’t want our daughter finding her fucking GODmother choking on her own vomit on our kitchen floor.” As Max went to rest a calming hand on his wife’s shoulder, she shuffled away and huddled up with a tightening tug on her silk robe’s belt. “You know I’m right here…” mumbled the tender-headed Karina, who was still sat propped-up against the kitchen cabinets. Every micro-reaction of Alice Wolfenden’s body language told Karina that she wished it wasn’t the case. “Oh, and let’s not pretend I was anywhere near your first choice as a Godmother.” “Max,” Alice snapped with wincing sharpness. “She doesn’t talk back to me in our house.” “What was I? About 7th choice?” Karina kept pushing. “Yeah… I was about as wanted as the pregnancy.” Every poisonous syllable was barbed to wound her domineering sister-in-law, but, stood in the kitchen doorway, warring parties oblivious to her, was the victim of their heated verbal crossfire. Karina sank as a bruised and scratched hand reached out towards the slamming door. “Erin…” VI 23:50, The Night Before “Oi,” the rumbling voice of Barney Reid belched from deep within his fermenting stomach. “Real big runnin’ yeh mouth when yeh know ain’t a soul here gonna lay a finger on yeh.” A waft of yeasty breath engulfed Karina’s rage contorted face, testing Sheffield’s restraints. “Yeh got the word o’that wop bastard Deville protectio-” As if a steam iron had been run down them, Karina Wolfenden’s snarling features unfurled. Her bottom lip hung, quivering, words shaking off of it. “Who did you just say..?” “Angelo fucking Deville,” Barney boomed with contempt, as if he‘d dared utter the unmentionable, the unforgivable. “So don’t you be all spoilin’ for a fight knowin’ yeh got his word keeping’ this fist from doin’ somethin’ my Ma won’t be proud of, may God rest her soul.” Gagging for an adrenaline fix, for something, anything to crack the cotton wool cocoon of monotony from around her, the last thing Karina Wolfenden wanted was another smothering, protective blanket. “He’s dead,” she coldly declared, frostbite weakening Sheffield’s grip and cracking the protective Halo. “I don’t need a frigging thing of his. Now, no more excus-” Karina saw the surly slugger launch his clobbering right fist, but even free of Alias’ shackles she didn’t parry it. Instead, she closed her bloodshot eyes, smiled, and ate it up with relish. VII 07:02, The Morning After “Cunt.” In an age of casual swearing, the serrated edges of certain words have been filed down and blunted. But the venom with which Alice Wolfenden cussed her sister-in-law. Exclamation in the form of a vicious slap sharpened the insult with steel. An apology clung to the tongue of the silently devastated K-Wolf, unable to shake it loose. Eyes withered, vision collapsing for the first time down her dishevelled, blood-and-vomit-stained body, which Karina then knew would be the last memory Erin would have of her. “Get,” shrieked Alice as she let loose with a despairing flurry of slaps. “Out. Of. My. House. NOW!” Unable to offer any defence for his challenging sister, Max shuffled back, head hung away from Karina. Two clawfuls of vomit-clumped hair dragged Karina up. For once, she abandoned her Vox Nihili motto of Pugna, Ergo Sum / I Fight, Therefore I Am and offered no resistance as she was tossed limply out onto the back porch. “I pity any child you end up bringing into this world,” Alice snarled, motherly instincts fuming out of every pore. “Don’t ever come near my daughter again.” A tear from Karina, polluted with mascara and blood, left a black stain as a memory of her. VIII 23:56, The Night Before Crimson blotches swelled like Rorschach patterns in the ale-soaked bar towel which mummified the right half of Shane Buckley’s face. Over a splintered, nail-studded landscape of broken barstools he crawled, erupting a bloody belch from Barney Reid when his knee pressed into the unconscious patron’s swollen, welted stomach. To a dawn chorus of groans and spluttering, Shane’s fallen comrades began to stir as he yanked at the cord of the bar’s phone, bringing it down upon the back of his towel-padded head. With the one finger he could properly extend, Buckley stabbed a number from his hazy memory and limply dropped an ear against the receiver. “Uncle Frank,” he croaked, congealing blood sticking in his throat. His one visible eye winced as a gravely roar turned the receiver into a distorting megaphone. “Oh, you’re gonna thank me for wakin’ yah... it‘s off.” Credits: (1) “Help; I’m Alive” is track one of Metric’s 2009 album Fantasies.
