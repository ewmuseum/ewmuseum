Questions Beget Answers

17 December 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-12-17 (Roleplay versus AgentDash) Once again, this is Doctor Daniel Rourke, chief psychologist at Weirsburg Institution For the Criminally Insane, filing my audio report. The date is December 13th and the current time is 9:18 p.m. Things are quiet tonight. It's a rare thing when you can hear the sounds of the highway for more than a passing moment in this hellish place; the screams of the east wing's drug-crazed derelicts and the other sleepless maniacs tend to rend apart the silence twenty-four hours a day. But things are somber at this hour, and even the most disturbed of the population are too scared to so much as close their eyes in the darkness. We had several requests to leave the lights on, and in order to preserve some modicum of order in light of what's happened, we obliged. There isn't a coherent inmate slumbering in darkness at this hour, and I can't blame them. I may follow their example when my shift ends. The mood in this facility has been particularly gloomy and, dare I say, foreboding as of late. Tragedy struck early this afternoon in B-block. You could call what happened several things, but that's the term I'm using. Byron Sharp, our security chief during that shift, used the term 'disaster' when I arrived on the scene. Jameer Croft, a longtime sufferer of schizophrenia, somehow obtained an axe from a Fire Emergency station and savagely murdered three others from B-block. Normally I'd write this off as a tragedy caused by a particularly violent strain of the disorder, but the accounts given from witnesses…well, things don't add up in line with his sickness. Nothing is adding up around here anymore for fuck's sake. From what's been gathered, that morning he slipped into a nearly catatonic episode where he refused to respond to external stimuli, staring forward as if into something no one could see but his himself. He was given the appropriate medications that should have pulled him right from that state back to normalcy, but to no avail. As the members of his block were being escorted down the block's western corridor, he abruptly became like a man possessed, exhibiting a show of strength that none of the staff could foresee and fending off even armed security in a deranged frenzy. 'Freakish' was what some called it, stating that it was if he was drew the strength from an outside source. Preposterous of course, but I've still searching for answers as to how a timid 165 pound inmate could throw multiple 220-plus pound guards halfway across the room. At this point he shattered the plastic casing of a Fire Emergency station on the wall and seized an axe located within. From there he turned and brutally attacked two of his fellow inmates, killing both and wounding another before finally being subdued. The scene was horrific and gruesome and I fear that members of my staff present may need counseling of their own in the aftermath; I won't be stingy about approving them for the necessary mental stress recovery programs given what they had to see and what the involved parties endured. Some of the details I'm still investigating, particularly the chilling theme that I gathered from Byron and his team regarding Jameer's speech patterns during the murder. He described it as sometihng like an alien language, all guttural mutterings and shrill shrieks with no discernible words amongst his cries as he mutilated his victims. Nothing like this falls in line with his case file on any level. Croft had no ties to anything so sinister as to suggest such a knowledge, not to mention provoke such a violent outburst. I've ordered a full investigation into this matter, primarily to unearth any links he might have had to any cults or similar organizations. As I stated in a report dated three days ago, an epidemic of heightened paranoia has been sweeping this institution. At first it was confined to the ward shared by our inmates afflicted by schizophrenia, particularly the paranoid subtypes. But now it's consumed the entire population, and in light of the horrors of this afternoon's butchery and the surrounding circumstances, I can see no way of calming the mood other than ordering an increase in sedative medications, particularly for those currently on the more severe antipsychotics and Geodon. As a followup, I must say that I'm greatly alarmed by the reports I've been given. It's bad enough that even the violent antisocials are so spooked as to actually cooperate and allow us to aid in alleviating their fears (something I never believed I'd see from Tyson Campbell), but things have taken an even more drastic turn. Now even my staff is reporting sightings of the apparitions that have the inmate population gripped in such terror. I'm not keen on buying into tales of hauntings or any of that mumbo-jumbo superstitious junk, but I will say that something is horribly wrong in this building. Stokes and Murphy reported something eerie in the basement while on a routine errand together, and I loathe to chalk up two of my more reliable staff members' account to a low-scale mass hysteria incident. That was bad enough, but the repeated reports of muffled chants and bodiless whispers from those who aren't incarcerated here doesn't bode well for morale. With morale low, my staff can't carry out their duties to their potential. And with a lapse in performance, that may open a proverbial Pandora's box and allow even more incidents of this kind to occur. Given all of these factors, it must be noted that one particular resident of this sanatorium isn't the least bit perturbed by these happenings. And if you've listened to any of my past recordings, you can probably place a bet on his identity. Inmate number 296715, Bruce Douglas Shanahan, the mastermind of the Sect of Black Wisdom pagan cult in upstate Vermont. I can't shake the feeling in my gut that his outward indifference to the mood in this institution is a veil of some kind. As such I've arranged a special session with him, hoping to delve further into his psyche. A man cannot, in a lawful universe, hold sway over the sort of phenomena and paranoia that has descended on the Weirsburg Institution. I cannot and wil not entertain such a foolish notion. But perhaps there are answers to be found in that brilliant mind, as sinister and conniving as it may be…" The staffing situation has complicated significantly. I've received three resignations in the past two weeks and at the moment there are difficulties in… Click. "Doctor Rourke? The inmate is ready for you now." Rourke turned off the recorder and swiveled around to face the male nurse standing stiffly in the doorway. On edge, just as his other colleagues were in recent days. "Very well," he replied while shrugging on his white coat, a pen secured to the breast pocket as he whisked a notepad from the desktop and into his hand. He followed his colleague down a veritable maze of hallways before reaching the bare sitting room now confining a mildly sedated Shanahan, eyes closed and head tilted down as if dozing. The patient, his reputation the catalyst behind many wild stories generated by the staff as of late, seemed comfortable even in the straitjacket he'd come to know so well since his incarceration, fitting as it was. He had yet to show any indicators of violence during his stay but was handled with the utmost caution; intelligence and cunning aside, his strength and training would quickly fell even the most capable security employed by Weirsburg. Rourke had barely crossed the threshold before the fear-inducing inmate lifted his head, eyes leveled on the doctor. His upper lip twisted upward into a warped sort of half-smile before his gaze turned to the male nurse. Rourke could almost feel his associate go rigid and his muscles become taut under that wary regard, soulless eyes both blank and wicked at the same time. "Thank you for the drugs, Robert. Now be a dear and go back to your station before fetch me some slippers. My feet are cold." Rourke glanced at the aide and nodded in his direction. The other moved even before the doctor issued his decree. "Leave us please, Hugney." He scurried from the room, eyes set to the floor untiil the moment the door closed behind him. Rourke wasn't the least bit hesitant to believe it was due moreso to Shanahan's shadowy presence than his own order. Ever since the incident involving the latter's harassment of Rodney Sanders, few of that paygrade wanted anything to do with the macabre cult priest and his grim prophecies. Rourke, however, seemed immune to the proddings of his patient and felt no such apprehension. No fear existed, but a bond of respect had developed between the pair. It appeared to run both ways. Rourke pursed his lips as he looked upon the bound maniac. Perhaps it was best to get straight to the point, but not right to the heart directly. "I spoke to Jared McKenzie today. He was an officer present at your arrest several weeks ago in Vermont." Tilting his head back and flashing a wide grin toward the flickering overhead light, Shanahan clicked his tongue. "Unfortunately I have no recollection of said officer, Daniel. It's hard to discern one from so many, especially when a mob of men in uniform are trying to shrug off their fear by flashing authority and firearms in a frenzy. I can only hope their badges and guns helped to calm their nerves." 'Their fear'. He'd gathered the purpose of this discussion that quickly, and the smirk he directed at Rourke reflected it. "He told me what he saw, and what his colleagues saw. Why no one would come forward to speak on it. And given the insanity of it, I can completely understand their silence. I want answers straight from the horse's mouth before I mention any of this in a report." "Go on, doctor." Rourke was venturing into territory he'd hoped never to cross into. He'd actually begun to almost give credence to these theories and reports, alarming as it was for a man in his position. "He still spoke little of it, but…." A sigh, his throat drying even as he struggled to form the words, entertaining the notion that so many dependable professionals witnessed something so hair-raising. "….he told me of shapes moving about in the woods. Not the shapes of man or beast, not of any Earthly kind he told me. Something else. He said he could feel a tangible evil in the air, and a stench like would be carried on the winds of Hell itself." The other scoffed. "Absurd. Hell doesn't exist, Daniel. How many times must I tell you that?" "No games. Not with this. I want you to tell me what the men who raided your ceremony saw." His eyes narrowed, his struggle for the truth finally coming to fruition. He could feel it finally happening, though whether he would like the result was a risk worth taking. "What did you conjure in those woods, Bruce?" "You wouldn't understand, doctor." The other's eyes closed as he breathed in deeply, exhaling as his arms stirred within the jacket. He moved slowly and silently, a man in comtemplation. "Though you may not put faith in the cross like Stryker or his dogs, and thus chalk up peculiarities to divine intervention, you are conditioned to believe that all in this world can be explained by logic. Even were I to explain to you the rituals of our organization, and the effects they brought into this world, you would deem it all a grand delusion. You're seeking a reason to keep me here as long as you can." His smile was baleful, reserved ire hidden behind a mask of contentment. He communicated so much from behind that grin, gave it so many different meanings with so many subtle gestures. "I'm your prize study. I've had detectives and officials view me in the same light. You're not the first psychologist to see me as a meal ticket and you likely won't be the last." Denying it was a pointless effort for the doctor. Dawson had accused him of it and Stryker had even voiced that same observation. There were truly mysteries to be plumbed inside the mind of the zealot, ones that Rourke intended to reveal. "Try me," he stated after a moment of quiet. "Are you going to tell me there are truly demons of some kind lurking in that forest? Because I won't believe that." "Of course not. And I'll say no such thing. There are no demons in those woods." "Do the words 'Dark Young' mean anything to you, Bruce?" Shanahan actually took on a look of astonishment at the mention. His poise wasn't broken but he was surprised at his overseer's fervor for research in the subject. "You truly are a persistent one. Tell me, Daniel, where did you pick up the term?" "It doesn't matter. Is that what they saw?" "And here I thought you believed it all to be hogwash, doctor. Before I respond, tell me…what do you know of these 'Dark Young'?" "Little." He couldn't help but feel that the man in the straitjacket was baiting him, prying to see how far he had already gone. He wanted to know how long Rourke had stared into the abyss, and thus if the abyss had stared back. "They're linked to some fertility god of yours. Legends say they appear when summoned as servants of the god itself, and they look like mobile, deformed trees. Reeking of the grave." Bruce Shanahan laughed. "You've been a good pupil, Daniel. The legends do say such. That said, what do you think?" "I think you were attempting to communicate with your god. The local police, I've been told, are well-aware of both your activities and many of your specific beliefs. I think they saw movement in the woods and chalked it up to superstition. One or two men panicked, mob mentality spread like wildife, and soon every tree branch wavering in the wind became the limb of some devil lumbeirng in the woods. It's common behavior in out-of-the-way regions like that. A part of rustic society." "The omens pile up at your feet and your mind is so infected by the world's wretched dogma that you refuse to heed them." His patient was obviously disappointed, a sneer washing away that chilling eternal smile. "The dead could rise from their graves and hordes of locust could sweep across the Earth and still you'd find some grand and groundless explanation to quell your fears. You're no different than the others, Daniel. You hide behind science like the Vatican hides behind long-dead ideals in a book of lies." His eyes narrowed and fell on Rourke. How he despised those eyes, those soulless orbs of sentient shadow. "It's all reaching a climax, doctor. Every so often the sunken city rises, its barnacle-encrusted steeples reaching toward the sun and its slumbering denizens are released from their watery necropolis to commune with a world that has relegated them to hushed legend and myth. And when that happens….His influence is wide-spreading, His power unprecedented when set against eons of imprisonment. He is allowed a period of sway, offering temptation and revelation to all who would someday fall before him. A glimpse at their near future, a prelude to the extinction of order and paltry human law. His followers are imbued with the power we need to prevail in the face of the heretics of this world. The faithful are rewarded for their work." He trailed off with a thin smile, a sick mind basking in the ideals of unholy doctrine. His finger began to trace the outline of a tattoo imprinted on his lower neck, some arcane symbol that Rourke couldn't begin to guess the significance of. "Horace Tully is feeling that surge of strength. The boons of his zeal will bring victory, in my name and Theirs'. The last skirmishes before the final battle." Bruce Shanahan faced charges levied by agents of the Church, ones they now fought to bring to federal authority. They would see him imprisoned for life or even executed for his crimes, and were he to somehow escape legal persecution, the shadows would hide their deputies of righteous vengeance; if prison didn't contain his evil, it would be snuffed out by assassins. But Shanahan found no ill omen in what surrounded him, not even with death and oppression staring him in the eye. His gospel was his refuge in the storm and faith his salvation. Rourke had seen hundreds of madmen pass through this asylum, but none with the staunch conviction of the man before him. "Horace Tully competes in my stead in the 6th annual GTT tournament. As we speak, during this period of revival, his conviction is being recognized. He's a vassal for Their might. As he does so, my place is here, watching and waiting as events unfold. You see…I'm merely the scribe, charged with overseeing the events of this soon-to-be historic period and record them. To plan and be a witness is my only role in this epic plot. We are both pawns, Daniel, you and I together." Rourke shivered at the statement. He wasn't sure if it was the implication of what the other said or the chill in the air. It had seemed warmer when he'd first sat down. "This sunken city…youc laim it shall be rising again soon?" A nod. "For a brief period, it shall be so." "And how will I know if you tell the truth? When this city rises above the waves as you claim it will, how will I know? Can you prove that you're not feeding me garbage?" "You didn't hear me, doctor. When the city breathes again, He will be granted a period of sway that is otherwise limited. Trust me…" He paused and glanced around the room. There were cries resonating down the hallway in the adjacent block and the one known as Violence Jack acknowledged them with a quick glance toward the door. Rourke wasn't blind as to what the man was getting at. "…you will know. You will see its effects firsthand. Some are more prone than others." Shanahan was either mad far beyond rehabilitation or the herald of a truth that could destroy the civilized world if it became commonly accepted. Rourke still believed it was the former and, for a man so rooted in the sciences, it would take something awing to change that now. But something abnormal was still happening in the Weirsburg asylum. And Shanahan's answers had only raised more questions. Questions that Daniel Rourke wished he could ignore. * * * * The door closed behind Ethan Knight as he entered Wyatt Manor. He wasn't the least bit surprised to discover he wasn't alone in that corridor, his associates in the Sect leadership lying silently in wait. The aroma of Sterling's perfume had entered his nostrils the moment he cracked the door and, given the orders of Father Shanahan prior to his capture, that would also indicate the presence of Brother Tully. The duo were nigh inseparable as long as conditions remained as they were, pooling their resources to maintain dominion over the discipleship. "Clinton Sage knew precious little, or so we're led to believe," remarked Tully. "We apologize for your trip, if indeed that was the case. I assume you learned nothing?" Ethan removed his jacket in silence. He unsheathed his weapon and placed it on the floor before drawing himself up to meet eyes with his associate, the acting head of the Sect of Black Wisdom. They were almost as brothers now. "Correct. However he knew more than he was willing to give up. By the look in his eyes when I pulled the trigger, it would require an act of God for him to reveal anything to us. I wasn't willing to wait around for that sort of divine intervention." "You executed him?" There was strain in Sterling's soft voice as she voiced the question. "His torso looked like Swiss cheese. And the back of his head looked like a slice of bread after someone had jammed a couple of their fingers through it. Why do you ask?" Sterling look unnerved, confused even, as her tongue danced lightly over her full-red lips. Tully's composure was unaltered, a face still as smooth as blank paper. "Because. Your method? Your gun I assume?" "Three shots. Point-blank range. No man on Earth could survive it." He glowered at the sidearm resting on the floor, a tangible resentment for the instrument as if it were alive. Ethan Knight was ever solemn when it came to the business of murder. "As usual I'd have preferred the intimacy of a blade, but orders are orders." He reacked into his pocket, coming up with three spent casings that he deposited with a clatter at the feet of his murderous cohorts, inches from the weapon itself. Neither Tully nor Sterling truly doubted Ethan's penchant for truth but things had come to light that brought more questions than answers. "You hit the mark?" inquired Sterling. "You're positive?" Knight looked almost winded by the accusation, as if the words were a shot to the gut. Tully assumed it was his a blow to his pride that the "Angel of Death's" killing edge was under review. "Completely. I was inches away. This isn't fucking Pulp Fiction, Lady Sterling. There was no miracle, and God didn't come down and stop the bullets from rending holes in Clinton Sage. It didn't take a medical doctor to tell the man was dead. Is there a point to this?" Tully tossed a tape recorder at Knight, the latter swiftly snatching it out of mid-air. A tape was already loaded. The homicidal youth looked first at Tully and then at Marion Sterling. She tapped her chin daintily with one long, green-lacquered fingernail. "You may want to play that," she said delicately. "If Clinton Sage is as dead as you claim, that shouldn't exist." A chill crept down his spine. He'd punched three bullets into the man and watched him die. It was impossible to believe that he lived, simply unthinkable unless… "The…meteors?" he stammered out, rattled and shocked by the notion. He'd never taken such a thing into account, estimated their capabilities to even shield one from death. "Is…could this be true?" The duo nodded. A clock chimed the hour as Marion opened her mouth to speak. "Father Shanahan's knowledge about them is very limited. He says they were a gift from the cosmos gone stray, fallen into the hands of a society that they were not meant for. But now, with the power the Sect has established, the time has come to claim them for our own. We'll need to learn what they can do, but for now, nothing is clear. Though it appears resurrection isn't a stretch if we're in the mood to assume." Running a hand through his blond hair, Knight tossed the recorder back at Tully. He was in no mood to hear his failure clarified, unexpected circumstances or not. "I'll take your word for it for now. I assume he's still to be sent to the grave?" Horace's nod was his only response. No more needed said for the Angel of Death to begin formulating his further death machinations. "Very well," growled Knight, sheathing a long-bladed knife alongside the empty holster. "Focus on Dash and let me handle this." He turned toward Tully with one hand resting on the hilt of the knife. He had wanted to use one of his favored blades in the Sage situation but had been ordered to use the gun, much to his chagrin. Shanahan wouldn't say why and his logic simply wasn't questioned. "Remember, Brother Tully: you offer up plans, and I offer up death. Such has always been the way of things. Fulfill your end of the bargain and consider mine carried out." Tully eyed the blade and acceded to his colleague. "I'll defeat Dash in the name of the Old Ones and Father Shanahan. You make sure that Sage is eliminated, but this time not until you discover what you need to. Prolong his demise as long as necessary." Ethan sighed, aggravated. He didn't take his failings well, even if this one resulted in no more than an unfortunate delay. "The prophecy is at hand. Why the need for some rocks we know nothing of anyway?" "The meteors are instrumental in this plan," explained Tully. Even he wasn't sure why, but it was his simple duty to see Shanahan's schemes carried out as long as he planned and waited safely in the asylum. "It was suspected that Sage knew next to nothing about their abilities, but it appears he knows their location. Or he should. Find him and finish the job. You can listen to that tape during your travels." Ethan agreed by way of a nod and blinked at the portrait of Great Cthulhu mounted high in the foyer. "That said, one other thing still needs taken care of. What of Blackhelm and the worms they employ? I'm shocked that Marcus and Maury haven't stormed the estate with an army yet." "That's taken care of." Tully had already turned and begun his stroll back into the main chamber, the gears already turning in his head as they awaited the rise of R'lyeh. No mistakes could be made now. Not when they were so close to their goal. "Leave that to our friend Stryker. He will carry out his duty, oblivious to it as he may be. He's so predictable."
