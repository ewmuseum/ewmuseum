Eternal: The End (Bracket Finals RP 1)

2 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-02 Note: This is a continuation of the series that have been written so far in the GTT4 tournament. ETERNAL: THE END <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> Within the own confinements of the stained plastered walls a sin had been committed. A wrong that had defined the very true name of evil. For a moment, time stopped as if it had been stabbed in the back, with crimson red blood flowing profusely down in streams. That night, the pact of humanly remorse had been thrown out the window, split apart by jagged edges of glass ripping ruggedly into the nature of the dark side of the inner soul. So the deed had been done. In a way that would have made common mortals cringe and tremble, the act of insanity had driven an immortal to commit a murder never imagined in his very mind. With the cold winter winds gushing through his blood in pure hatred, he drove his anger in a straight arrow-like direction, crippling the target within a split second. In the medium of a bullet, splitting through the air like a dagger running through a chest, it conquered the air despite its desperate protest, and in the end succeeded. On that night, the life of one immortal had ended. Fallen short of the eternity that the heavens had promised him, betrayed by the treachery of his own blood. And before he died, within the ear-splitting sound of the shot, he muttered words that were unheard by both immortals and mortals alike. It was as if they were never spoken, ceasing to exist in the records of life. In truth, even though he had gripped the trigger firmly with his index finger, he knew that he was never going to pull it. He knew deep in his heart that he was never going to kill his own son, despite the sins he committed that could never be forgiven. And as he lay dying on the wooden floorboards with the life of him seeping slowly through his skin in crimson red, he smiled to himself, knowing that the day he had been waiting for all his life had finally beckoned close. Soon, he would be standing next to his wife, holding her hand. The very woman who had waited generations for her husband to join her side in the land of the dead. The woman whom he had accused his son of killing. His wish had finally come true. Dying before their time had always been feared by mortals and immortals, but to him, it couldn’t have come at a better time. Simply, he had been begging for it. To him, it was the end. But not before spluttering out his parting words, with red flowing down his lips and onto his neck, as he desperately tried to let the world see everything through his own eyes. “Kasper Sky, my son…” “Fear not, your time will come. And when that happens, your redemption will be complete…” “Your time will come…” “The hour is near.” <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> Her footsteps slammed against the concrete pavement in quick succession, forcing itself to quicken in desperation. With beats of sweat slipping down her forehead, her pale face was drawn and weary, a strain of urgency glazing through her crystal blue eyes. She knew that she shouldn’t have told Kasper of their father’s intention of meeting him, realizing too late that she had in fact signed a death certificate for one of them. She hoped that she wasn’t too late, as she rushed through the streets, looking for the house address that she had given Kasper, with the instruction that father was waiting there for him. It was a mistake, those words repeating itself over and over again in her head, spinning her mind, manipulating it into overdrive. The sound of her steps faltered, as her lips trembled in fear, her eyes watching the movements of a man struggling out of a house trying to keep himself on his feet. Her eyes darted to the run-down house even though she already knew, but she just had to make sure. Her right hand automatically clasped her mouth in subconscious emotional instinct, stifling her scream. Kasper staggered out to the street, blood dripping from his hands. His face was strained, worn out from the fifteen minutes of madness that he had just endured back in the house. He was flustered, affected by the sin that he had committed. He turned his head and took a step back, surprised at the appearance of his sister, Krystal, in his line of sight. He shot a desperate look, and turned away, not wanting to continue staring at his sister in the eye. Guilt was now slowly enveloping his body, causing his blood to curdle and chills running down his spine in rapid movements. “What happened?!” Krystal questioned in a harsh tone, panting hard from the painful run that she had to endure to reach the location. After seeing Kasper, she mustered up enough energy to take her across the fifty meters separating the two immortal siblings. Despite her question, she dreaded the answer. She tried preparing herself for what words that would be spat out from Kasper’s mouth, but she received silence in return. “Tell me what happened in that house!” she screamed, unable to contain her anger and urgency. She threw out a right hand, and grabbed Kasper’s white jacket in a vice-like grip, wrenching him closer to her. Kasper looked down, not wanting to see the hurt in his sister’s eyes. His body slumped, the aura of confidence from within him slowly fading away. “I’m sorry,” he muttered, finally managing to find his words that he seemed to have lost just a couple of seconds ago. His reaction drew a pale response from Krystal; her hand went limp as she pulled away from his jacket, like a magnet being repelled by a similar end. Tears welled up in the drawers of her eyes, gleaming slightly in the dim street lightings surrounding them on that empty cold night. “You killed your own father, didn’t you…” she whispered, her phrase was more of a statement rather than a question. A wild thought crossed her mind as she stood there staring at her brother, a bewildered expression extruding from her face. She had the sudden urge to kill him. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> The art of pretence is a valuable tool. I can never stress enough the importance of being able to manipulate self emotions and expressions to influence others of your guilt and conscience. To many, I am portrayed as a dysfunctional mind, not able to build upon my choices with proven facts to support them. They worry about my state of mind and the level of my understanding of the past. They think I am not able to remember my past. That the jigsaw of eternal memories had not yet been completed, that my life is but a blind search for redemption. To them, I am an object of pity. A victim of my own sins and actions, burying me deeper within the realms of my own self inflicted guilt. Deep inside, they blame me for it. I can see through that no matter how hard they try to hide their emotions, hoping that I will look past it in the hopes that I will resolve my mind before the end turns near. They think I am weak, self-destructing from within. But really… Never mind, you’ll find out with time. I guess I will just have to play along for now, revolving myself in circles to suit the portrait being painted of me. I know, it can’t last forever. But who says I need forever? I will be the first to admit that the end is near. But not now… now is the time for vengeance. The zone in which the sins that I had committed in the past have to be redeemed by more sins in the present and the future. You see, it is nearing completion. The task is almost done. For in the end, everything will be beautiful. Just not now. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> “He deserved it.” The small voice whispered those words from behind Krystal, startling her slightly, before burning her deep as her anger quickly rising, disengaging her from reasoning. Her eyes flashed as her body tensed, spinning herself to face the person standing behind her, her fingers clenched tight in a fiery ball of knuckles. And as quick as her anger rose, her expression was replaced by that of shock. Athena was standing in front of her, a firm line on her lips which quickly pushed the thought of a malicious joke out of Krystal’s mind. “What do you mean?!” Krystal spoke, in a harsh tone that surprised even herself. “Your father deserved his death,” Athena continued, a flash of hate in her eyes reverberated through Krystal’s mind, confusing her further as to why her once best friend about ten years ago had now turned her back on her. “Why? What did he do to you for you to speak this way?!” Krystal spat out her words, her lips trembling, unable to control her anger. “His past sins propelled him to his own end.” “What sins?!” By this time, Krystal had already taken a step forward in Athena’s direction, thrusting her body with every word she spoke. She was nearing breaking point, as her evil side cried out to her mind to put a stop to this girl’s manner of speech. “He killed my father.” Athena whispered those words menacingly, her eyes gleaming in blind infuriation. Her face had turned red by this time, trying hard to not allow those tears welled up in her eyes to flow down her cheeks like a streaming line of water droplets. Taken aback, Krystal’s right hand covered her mouth, shock registering in her mind. As a chilling silence enveloped with Krystal at a lost of words, she stared in Athena’s eyes trying to not believe at what she just said. “You lie!” Krystal snarled, baring her teeth in apparent anger and confusion drawn and mixed into one. Athena just shook her head in disbelief, overriding Krystal’s refusal to believe her words. “Ask your brother, he saw the whole thing happen. He told me when he jumped into the boat ten years ago in Athens, just before your father shot him in the chest.” Up until then, Krystal had entirely forgotten of Kasper’s presence in the group of three, engaging herself fully in the Athena’s accusations at her now dead father, who was killed by her own brother. Krystal turned her head as her eyes flared at Kasper, daring him to speak his words. “Is that what you told her?” Krystal enquired, with a questioning look in her expression. “You told Athena that my father killed her father?!” she went on, her tone of voice rising in desperate fury. Kasper looked past his sister, ignoring her words. He stared at Athena, an icy look in his oceanic blue eyes. “He did not kill your father,” he spoke simply, clear and concise. “What?” Athena took a step forward as she questioned, her facial expression giving away the look of bewilderment. “You mean that I’ve been living in your lies all these years?” Kasper kept his silence, a firm line on his lips reflecting his conscience. “What happened in Athens ten years ago then?” Athena spoke in a tremble, her soul urging her to find an answer to her father’s death. He turned his head and looked at his sister. “Tell her what exactly happened, Kasper Sky.” Krystal replied to Kasper’s glance, her body shaking slightly in resentment. “I killed him.” A moment of pause passed. And then two. After Kasper spoke those words, a tear slid slowly down Athena’s pale right cheek, as she collapsed on her knees in pure shock. The effect reverberated through her mind, freezing it of thought, corrupting it with evil flowing freely now in her mind, roaming unopposed in search of retribution. And for the second time that night, another had a sudden urge to kill him. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> Invincible. Unable to be overcome nor defeated. That’s what people are starting to speak of Shadow. Just like my father, she seems to have it in her mind that she simply cannot be defeated. And just like him, I will see to her end. For you see, everyone thinks that they will not lose. That’s the nature of mortals and immortals alike. Hell Itself thought he wouldn’t lose, as with Tony Stecchino, Larry the Legionnaire, as well as the self-proclaimed drug lord by the name of Token Weed. Even my own father thought that. We all know what happened to them by now. They lost. But the list just won’t end there. For there are many others who had instilled in their minds as ones who will never be defeated. After beating Tsunami Oni and Jonathan Sail, you proclaimed Shadow as the saviour. Someone who will step up and remove me from my grandeur. You think that she has what it takes to overcome my splendour and overthrow me from my lordship. You think that she will defeat me. You believe that I will indeed lose. I guess you must have severely underestimated my abilities. The ability to survive and overcome. The ability to defeat all those that stand in my way. The ability to win. You detest that, just like you hate every single bit of my body and soul. You think that I am evil in unimaginable ways, even more than the evil you make yourself out to be. And that just drives you forward to usurp my throne of glory, thinking to yourself that if you try hard enough, you can propel me to my end. Hoping in your wildest imaginations that Shadow will step forth and prove her worshippers right. You are willing to place her among the ranks of Gods just so that you can defeat me. You want to see to my end before I kill you. You want me to die before you die. I’m afraid that you’re living under false pretences. You know that isn’t ever going to happen. You know that I just don’t lose. Once again, you don’t need to speak for me to know what’s going through your mind. You don’t believe me, and I don’t blame you for that. And just to make things more interesting for you, I shall come forward and put forth my challenge to Shadow. Shadow thinks she can defeat me, then she can step up and do it. She can try all she want, in every imaginable way possible. Don’t worry, this is just the beginning. Try and defeat me if you can. Then I will finally show you the end. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> TO BE CONTINUED
