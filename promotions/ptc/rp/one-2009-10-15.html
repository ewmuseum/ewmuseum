15 October 2009

Date: 2009-10-15 The colour was the first thing that caught the eye, but the lasting memory was of the size. This beast was absolutely gargantuan, dwarfing all others of its ilk and leaving a permanent impression on all those who set eyes on it. It was a remnant from past times, a testament to history and a source of wonder to some. This great blue whale had withstood the hardships of time well and it was not unrealistic to think that it could survive for another 10, 20 or even 30 years. As it boomed the Pacific Coast Highway, it was a sight to behold, the early morning sun reflecting off every inch of the highly polished body, making the car shimmer and glow like an ethereal being. Single-mindedly it chewed up the miles, heading for a destination as yet unknown, the empty road brought to life by the growl of its engine, dust and debris being scattered in its wake. Looking beyond it, the Pacific Ocean sparkled like a perfectly cut sapphire, the vista too perfect to be imitated by man. The vehicle had such a presence that it wouldn’t have been difficult to imagine that it had it’s own spirit, a soul which could power it without the need for trivial considerations such as fuel or a driver. However, reality being what it is, this couldn’t be the case, and as such there was a tank full of gas and a man firmly ensconced at the helm. Ensconced being the apt word, as this man was not so much an addition to the entity as he was an integral part. He had cared for the vehicle, maintained it, repaired it and treated it with the respect it rightfully commanded. As such, he had a bond with it, knowing its idiosyncrasies and quirks, steering and controlling it by feel and sense. This man goes by the name of Albert Jefferson, though he prefers to be called AJ. As the years have mounted up, so have his skills in his chosen field. Although you’d never hear it from his lips, he is a wizard with anything automotive, having a spookily innate understanding of the relationships between each and every part of a vehicle, able to determine faults from the sounds the car makes, diagnosing problems without even opening the hood. It is this aptitude that explains the name others use when talking about him – Gandalf. If he wanted, he could double or even triple his income without any difficulty, such is the level of his skill and notoriety, yet he prefers not to, instead living a comfortable, if not extravagant life. Now well into his 29th year, he is happily content, living in a tidy apartment with his long-time fiancée and their pet Labrador. Marriage is something he thinks of occasionally, but in reality, is not a pressing concern. In his mind, the contract of matrimony is an arrangement that nobody benefits from in the long run and therefore is an unnecessary complication, a hindrance. Marriage would just be a rubber stamp on something that he and his partner know is lasting and real, an expensive day out for both their families. The journey he is making today isn’t of particular note, merely the fulfilment of a promise he made several weeks previously. Normally, he wouldn’t have made such a concord, being a man who likes to keep himself to himself, an introverted sort not entirely comfortable in social situations and most happy when alone in his workshop. Still, his fiancée had encouraged him to broaden his horizons and when it became apparent that this engagement wouldn’t be an awkward drinks reception or a forced family gathering, he had decided to accept. Indeed, he was actually looking forward to the day ahead, the opportunity to demonstrate his expertise in front of a group of his peers, doubtless gaining some praise and awe from some, was just too much to pass on, even to a person as humble and unassuming as him. Obviously he wouldn’t go out of his way in efforts to elicit acclaim, but he certainly wouldn’t go out of his way to deflect it. After all, everyone needs an ego boost sometimes, no matter how small it may appear to those on the outside. The smile on his face was still one of contentment, with only the merest smidge of self-satisfaction present. The collision was shocking. The first impact was to the front fender on his side, the metal compressing like a soft drink can being crushed, the vehicle simultaneously going into a spin, the steering wheel becoming a mere mockery, all semblance of human control gone in an instant. Seen from above, the cars appeared to be performing a kind of sickening waltz, one second intimately close, the next apart, seemingly ignorant to each other’s presence. Inside, the chaos was amplified. Loose items flew threw the air with abandon, everyday objects transformed into flying missiles. The safety belt worn by Albert Jefferson was strained almost to its limits, his body hurtling forward towards the steering wheel, another potential killer. Already, his mind was attempting to process the reality of what had happened in the milliseconds since impact, every part of him unknowingly attempting to adjust, to move in order to protect itself as much as possible from the impending trauma. It was equivalent to rearranging deckchairs on the Titanic. The crack of the flimsy wooden rail breaking was lost among the screech of tires and the sound of breaking glass, the vehicle threatening to take flight for the merest of instants before the inexorable pull of gravity reasserted itself and metal met earth. In this battle, for superiority there was only ever going to be one winner and with every jolt and bounce, the car seemed so much like an overmatched boxer being pummelled even after it’s clear the bout is over. All frames of reference inside the metal carcass had disappeared, the world tilting and warping so randomly and frequently that it was not even worth attempting to designate a direction as up or down, left or right. The eruptions of blood on Albert’s face, head, arms and torso were so frequent and explosive that within a few seconds there seemed to be more of the sticky, red liquid than anything else in the vehicle. The crack of his ribs as they impacted on the steering column was drowned out by the crunch of metal as the car tumbled down the embankment towards its final resting place. Ending as quickly as it had begun, a modicum of calm returned to the stretch of highway. Albert Jefferson was again at one with his vehicle, both of them lying in a battered, crumpled heap at the bottom of the hill, nothing more than shells of what they had been a mere minute earlier. Briefly, there was silence, the sound of the waves lapping against the shore and the cawing of sea birds the only noises disturbing the peace. Then the screams started. Heard on their own, they could have been the howls of a dog gone rabid, deafening and primal, with all traces of human inflection absent. However, with the complete vista available for perusal, they took on a much more terrifying quality. Albert Jefferson’s face was contorted into a mask which showed on it a gamut of emotions. Terror, fear, agony and, most of all, desperation, were etched on his face. His eyes spoke the words that his mouth couldn’t. Help me. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ As the gurney was wheeled into the emergency room, the patient lying prone on it could barely be seen, such was the throng of doctors, nurses and paramedics surrounding it. Information was being exchanged and assimilated at a rapid rate, pens were moving over clipboards, eyes were roving over the horror in front of them and an action plan was being mentally formed by those that would be entrusted with the survival of the patient. One such person, Donna Simmons, was not a woman given over easily to emotion. In her field, emotion was a luxury that could only lead to problems, for her, her colleagues and their patients. So calm and collected was she, no matter what the situation, that she had earned the moniker ‘The Ice Queen’ within mere months of joining the staff. It was a skill which she had developed over her years in the profession and had played no small part in her ascent to her current position as chief resident. This morning though, she almost lost her breakfast. The patient was in his late twenties, a handsome looking man in an unassuming, understated sort of way. This much was clear even through the plethora of bruises and contusions that covered almost every inch of his body. Indeed, his looks shone through despite the fact that his jaw and nose were severely broken and several teeth had been knocked out. What was worse were the burns that had turned one side of his upper body into a mass of charred meat, the nerve endings destroyed by the intense heat they had endured. Add to this the sight of his broken femur, the splintered ends grotesquely piercing through the skin, and her momentary nausea was understandable. Still, her training was only on a brief trip away and quickly returned, enabling her to do the necessary, coordinating her team and making sure that they were all aware of their roles in this gargantuan effort. One mistake, one single error by any one of them could be the deciding factor in the question of Albert Jefferson’s continuing mortality. The first objective, obviously was to stabilise him, ensure that he didn’t go into shock, stop his body from reacting in the only way it knew how to when facing such trauma. If they couldn’t stop it his body would just shut down. Permanently. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ The private room is dark, save for the dim green glow of the heart monitor that is keeping it’s watch, the electrical signals coursing up and down the wiring creating the trace on the screen, the only evidence that the patient in the bed is actually still in the land of the living, so still is he lying. It is a minor miracle that he is in this area of the hospital though, so dire was his prognosis less than half a day before. If the hospital had been in Vegas, people would have been putting their mortgages on him ending up 4 floors below in the morgue. Somehow, though, he was still here. The religious among us would call it a small miracle, those with a more scientific bent would be the first to hail modern medicine. Albert Jefferson’s mind, anaesthetised as it was, believed the likely truth to lie somewhere in between these two extremes. For while his mind had become disconnected from his body, with little to no awareness of what was happening to him in that brightly-lit emergency room, it was still operating, on a level he had never before experienced. A series of images didn’t flash before his eyes as he and his vehicle tumbled down the embankment, there wasn’t a abrupt and short-lived recollection of a gamut of past events, from childhood right through to that specific morning. Instead, he went through a slow and measured train of thought, time a seemingly alien concept, pondering what was to come. These thoughts continue now in his subconscious brain, the incident seemingly triggering some new machinery in his head, unknown regions of his brain opening up and allowing him to be fully self-aware. It could be described as an out-of-body experience but that would be too clichéd, too easy, when in fact what AJ is feeling cannot be pigeon-holed in any shape or form. In this state of thought, one motif echoes over and over again, a metronomic sound always striving to force itself to the forefront. This theme centres on the disappointment of unfulfilled potential, in all aspects of his life. The prospect of imminent death ignited the fuse that would lead to the realisation that Albert Louis Jefferson, for all his skill and ability, for all his attractiveness and impressive genetics, had achieved nothing of note in his time on the planet. Even disregarding the modern accoutrements of achievement, it was obvious that he hadn’t even fulfilled the most basic, primordial reason for his existence, namely to reproduce. Of course, this urge, this desire, is hardwired into all of us and always has been, yet in the contemporary world it is often suppressed, as was true in Albert’s case and indeed, as will be true for him in the future. For although his subconscious mind had a great epiphany as he lay in the burning wreckage of his pride and joy, his conscious mind was untouched by this realisation. Therefore, no matter what happens to him from here on out, the chances are that Albert will continue on much the same path as before, content with comfortable mediocrity in every way, never striving to leave an indelible marker that will stand the testament of time. When he passes, it’ll be like he never existed. Not so Josh Marquez. At a first glance, he and the eternally unfortunate Mr Jefferson are uncannily similar. They are around the same age, from a distance look similar, both live on the West Coast and both have roots from countries other than the land of Uncle Sam. Dig a little deeper though, and it turns out that they are like calcium carbonate and gruyere. Chalk and cheese, that is. For while Albert is content with his lot, living securely, doing his job every day, clocking off at the same time, eating the same food, having sex the same way, Josh is doing the opposite. He is always going for more, always looking to mix things up, wanting to make a mark that the world will remember. He has a son, a future heir, a boy who will grow, he hopes, into a man who will be proud of his father and will want to follow in his footsteps. He’s not getting any younger though. Life is short, and a wrestling career is even shorter. This is Josh Marquez’s first ever GTT and he’s under no illusions that it could well be his last. If he’s going to do something, it has to be now and if he’s going to do it, he’s aware that he’ll have to be better than he ever has been before. There are many things he can’t control but hell, let the chips fall where they may, no matter what happens, he’ll be going out there to exceed all expectations and leave his imprint on the minds of all who see him. He isn’t Albert Jefferson.
