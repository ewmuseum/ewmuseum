Internal Passion: The Decision

9 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-09 The cool morning air was refreshing to the citizens that were already up and about. The sky was clear and it looked to be a magnificent October day. Soon the town would be awake and active; about as active as a small town could be. One man who was already up and about was John Covel. After watching the Cardinals defeat the Padres the night previous, John’s passion for baseball had swelled up inside him again. Ever since the incident in June of 2003 that put John out of baseball for good, he’s had a burning desire to get back into it. He set out to curve the "craving" as he had many times before. He’d head to the park with his radar gun and bucket of balls. He’d throw every single one of those balls and then return home. He stepped from his 2006 silver Grand Am and walked to the trunk to retrieve the equipment. AS he slammed the trunk shut, another car pulled up and another man stepped out. "Hey, Tim. What’s going on?" John asked. Tim shrugged as he reached into the backseat of his car and emerged with a bat and glove. He showed them to John as they both shared a chuckle. They closed up their cars and began making their way towards the batting cage. "Nothing much, man. Thought I’d bring my bat and glove. Mix things up a bit in our normal routine." He replied. John nodded in agreement as they approached the cage. John set his glove in the bucket of balls and grabbed the door with his free hand. He pushed it open and stepped in, ducking under the protective netting that hung after the door. Tim followed suit and dropped his gear by the plate at his feet. "How are things going for you, lately?" John asked. Tim shrugged as the two of them moved toward the metal stand at the side of the cage. John set a case down and opened it, revealing a high tech radar gun. He pulled it from its container and set it upon the stand, securing it into place. "Did you get a new radar gun?" Tim inquired. John smiled and nodded. He reached back into the case and emerged with a small black box and two speakers. He set them back to the side of the cage and began running their cords to the gun itself. After plugging a few things in, John stood and pressed a button on the side of the gun, bringing it to life. "It talks. It verbally tells you the speed, and then records it into the external hard drive." He states. Tim looks sort of impressed by this. Tim heads back to the plate and picks up the bat, swinging it back and forth. John heads the other way with the case in his hand. As he approaches the mound, he sets the case down and retrieves a small video camera from it. He checks to make sure it’s battery is charged before pulling a stand from the box as well. He quickly sets the stand up and attaches the camera to it. John punches a button on the camera and a red light appears on the front as he heads to retrieve his glove and bucket of balls. "Did you see that Cards/Padres game last night?" Tim asked. John looked at Tim with a sarcastic grin before letting out a chuckle. He didn’t look at Tim as he dug his foot into the limestone mound. "Why do you think we’re out here?" John replied in question. Tim threw up his hands and laughed himself, seeing the obvious. He took a few more casual practice swings off to the side as John picked up a ball and tossed it around. He set himself and took a deep breath before kicking his leg and firing. The ball starts high and breaks downward, crossing the plate at dead center, pushing the protective netting back to the chain link wall. "Ninety-Four miles per hour." Said the computer voice. Tim looked at John with a glare of disgust and then broke into a chuckle. John flipped him off and grabbed another ball. He set himself and fired. "Ninety-six miles per hour." States the digital voice. John flashed a smile back at Tim and snatched another ball. He set himself and fires again. This time the ball stayed on a direct trajectory right down the center of the plate. It hit the chain link with a "clink" and fell to the ground. "One hundred miles per hour." Tim shakes his head and steps up to the plate, taking a few practice swings. John picks up another ball and twirls it in his hand a bit as Tim digs in. Tim nods to say he’s ready and John fires towards him. The ball breaks sharply down and outside, bringing an embarrassing swing and miss from Tim. "Hey! No sliders! Fastballs." Tim yelled. John laughs a bit and reaches for another ball. Tim steps back up to the plate and looks down to John as he sets himself. "Tim, I think I’m going to try and get back into baseball." John says as he winds up and fires. The ball whizzed by Tim as he dropped the bat to his side and looked to John with a gaze of disbelief. John looked back down and nodded as he blindly reached for another ball. "Yeah right." Tim said as he stepped back up to the plate. John fired his next pitch and Tim swung, barely catching a piece of it. "I’m for real this time." John replied. Tim shook his head, still not believing his friend. He took another few practice swings and stepped back up to the plate as John fired his next pitch. Tim elected to let it fly past, right down the center of the plate again. "One hundred-two miles per hour." "You’ve said that a lot of times before, John." Tim said as he swung the bat again. John shrugged his shoulders and grabbed for another ball. He took a deep breath and dug his foot into the limestone by the pitching rubber before looking down to the plate. He fired his pitch that sailed low and skipped through the dirt. "I know I have. But the passion inside of me is really burning now. I’m going to give the office a call." John stated. Tim backed away from the plate and dropped the bat, walking up towards John. John didn’t stop firing pitches off as his friend approached. "Even if you are serious about this, would they even let you back in?" Tim asked, John threw another fastball and turned to walk back to the mound. He raised his head and looked to his friend. "Why wouldn’t they?" John inquired. Tim scoffed and began laughing as John fired off another pitch. He walked back to the top of the mound and looked at Tim as his answer came. "You assaulted an umpire with a bat." Tim said. John looked on, his face not changing. He picked up another ball and threw a breaking ball that started high and to the right, finishing low and to the left. He took his glove off and wiped his sweaty hand on the fire department shirt he wore. "They banned you for life, John." Tim said forcefully. John dropped the glove he held into the bucket of balls and looked to Tim. "They considered letting Pete Rose back in. Why not me?" John asked. Tim simply shrugged his shoulders and looked to the ground. "I’ll support you one hundred percent if you try to do it. I just don’t see it happening, John." Tim stated. John nodded and reached for his glove again. He slid it over his hand and retrieved another ball. He tossed it around for a second and then bent over, setting himself. He kicked his leg and threw the ball. It hit the fencing with great force and fell to the ground. "One hundred-one miles per hour." "I appreciate your opinion, Tim. But I’ve had the passion and dream to play in the major leagues since I was a little kid. How do you think I felt when I fell just one day short of accomplishing that dream? One day. I’ve still got the same skills I’ve always had. I think it’s for real this time." John said. Tim nodded to agree with his friend. He watched as John threw another pitch and walked back to the mound. "Then do it. I wish you the best of luck. And if they have a hearing for it, I’ll be there." Tim said. John looked to Tim with an appreciative glare and nodded. He took a deep breath and dropped his glove to the ground. John had been trying casually to get his foot back into the door of professional baseball. Deep down inside he knew he could still aspire there. But even deeper was a part of him left wondering if Tim was right. Could they ever forgive him for assaulting an umpire in the minors? Most people would say "no," but in the right place, maybe the right people would say yes.
