"When you wish upon a fallen star"

10 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-10 * * * * * Burnt photographs about Spartan fluttered in the air, and small funnel clouds of ash swirled their way throughout the shrine. The room was filled with the beating winds coming off of the helicopter’s rotors – which sat perhaps only a dozen feet from the broken window. Clinton stood there in a dumbfounded amazement, watching as two of the FBI agents carefully escorted the meteorite – his meteorite – to the helicopter. Ducking slightly as they passed under the twirling rotors, a large section of the helicopter’s fuselage slid backwards and revealed the innards of the flying machine. Two more agents, dressed in the identical black suits, took hold of the meteorite and examined it for a minute. One of them traced a finger across the meteorite’s glassy exterior, and then pointed towards the Clinton. Standing next to the pointing agent was his partner, who nodded. The two agents who had taken the meteorite out of the crater each hopped up into the belly of the flying beast, and the panel once again slid shut. Clinton still marveled at how silent the helicopter was. He had never heard anything, or rather couldn’t hear anything, like it before. It was painted black, sleek and angular, and had black tinted glass in the cockpit. Considering how large it was compared against regular helicopters, this one produced only a fraction of the noise. In the place of where a normal counter-rotational tail rotor would be, there was simply a vertical aileron and two horizontal ones. The helicopter had the tail of an airplane. The FBI Agent standing next to him finally introduced himself with a forced handshake and a single nod of his head, "My name is Agent Hamlin. Considering we’ve already seen the police and medical reports that were filed this morning as a result of the meteorite striking your home, we’re going to need your signature on a piece of paper." Clinton Sage continued to watch the dark bird of prey sitting on his lawn, the rotors still churning the air and the engine revved enough for it to be ready to flee at any given moment. Agent Hamlin reached into his pocket and unfolded a few pieces of paper, and produced a pen from the inner pocket of his jacket, holding the items towards Clinton. "Now I must warn you. These are confidentiality agreements, so if what you’re about to be told gets repeated to anyone – and I mean anyone – these papers give us the right to your assets. You will basically forfeit your fortune, and any property you might own to the United States Government." Clinton scoffed in the direction of Agent Hamlin, shielding his eyes from the ash-laden, spiraling winds that filled his now useless shrine of Spartan, "Now why would I sign anything like that?" "Because if what our records on you show is true, you’re not one who likes to draw attention towards himself. You like to be in control, and right now we just can’t allow that. The events that took place this morning need to be kept quiet." "So how does this benefit me?" Clinton asked, taking the papers from Agent Hamlin’s hand, then snatching the pen out of his other one. "Because in order for you to understand what happened, Mr. Sage, you need to sign the papers." Agent Hamlin stared at him. Everything about the FBI agent peaked Clinton’s interest, because it all seemed so calculated and prepared. It reminded him of how the masked Spartan wrestled in the ring, always one move ahead of what was being thrown at him. This connection between the two men gave way churned the dormant frustration inside of Clinton Sage, and soon the hatred for Spartan started to spill over unto Agent Hamlin. But the curiosity quelled the anger, and thoughts of the miraculous morning he had encountered filled Clinton’s mind. The wounds on his hands had healed themselves. Then the self-inflicted wounds on his calf, and finally culminating with the shattered kneecap that Officer Hanes had rewarded him with. He had been unconscious as the skin of his palms recreated itself to perfection, but he was completely lucid and awake as the leg injuries healed themselves. Clinton couldn’t grasp how, and wanted to know. Needed to know. It seemed to amaze Officer Hanes as well, as it should. And here was the chance to get answers, albeit at the risk of losing everything he had. Pressing the paperwork against the shoulder of the FBI Agent as a faux table, Clinton Sage signed his name on the Confidentiality Agreement, and jabbed the pen down forcefully when dotting the "i" in his first name. "Cute," wincing at the mild blow, Agent Hamlin soon smiled, "Now that the United States Government has your full cooperation, I’m going to need you to tell me exactly what went through your mind when you had the meteorite in your hands." Suddenly, things became clearer for Clinton Sage. It all made some semblance of sense. * * * * * What the fuck just happened?, Reggie thought to himself. He had tried to keep his calm, but once Clinton had touched on his beautiful little girl, Amy, he snapped. And Sage’s kneecap shattered as a result. But once again, the injuries healed itself right before his very eyes, and Clinton Sage was left without any sign of physical violence. It was impossible. He had stared at Clinton Sage after smashing the bastard’s knee, who sat wide-eyed with excitement as his knee reformed. Reggie couldn’t stop thinking about how dark Clinton’s eyes were and how they seemed to be all pupil, with no iris. Clinton’s eyes were equally as dark as his heart. If God gave people color in their eyes as a window into the soul hiding behind them, it would certainly explain why Clinton’s were so black, and why his Amy’s were so brilliant blue. He pulled into the nursery and turned off the Honda’s engine. His heart was still racing from the outburst of violence towards Sage, and the unexplainable healing that had occurred almost immediately afterwards. Reggie knew he needed a drink, but even during the early afternoon, he knew it would be a long line of beer bottles before he had calmed to a level rational enough to weed through what had just occurred. Instead of alcohol, he knew of the next best thing. He got out of the Honda, and was prepared to pull his 2 year old daughter from the nursery a few hours early. He just wanted to be around someone that he loved and cherished, a part of him that would never be darkened the way Clinton’s life had been. When Reggie popped his head into the nursery, his daughter’s eyes widened with delight and he could see their brilliant blue from across the room. * * * * * Agent Hamlin led Clinton away from the helicopter, back through the shrine, and into the hallway leading back through the mansion. He picked the bloody knife and the hammer up off of the ground as they left the room, and kept them in his hands as they made their journey. "Every 32 years, Earth passes through a section of space that is heavy with comets, meteors, and asteroids. The remnants of when a gigantic asteroid smashed it’s way through the Kuiper belt millions of years ago, it left waves through space. Kind of the way a boat leaves a wake as it crosses a lake, or the ocean." Clinton remained by Agent Hamlin’s side, assimilating the knowledge. "One such meteor, named VK716 or "Vekoma", has left a trail of meteorites and shards during it’s passage through space. The bits of this meteor, for some reason or another, carry a special power. The reason I asked you what went through your mind while you were holding the meteorite is because of one simple fact: they seem to grant wishes." Clinton stopped, and gave a faux smirk. He believed the story, but wanted the FBI Agent to think he was skeptical. It all made sense to Clinton. Glorious, sense. "So you’re telling me that I’m Pinocchio, and you’re Jiminy Cricket? That I can simply wish upon a fallen star and the wish will be granted?" Agent Hamlin almost smiled at the reference, but kept his composure, "What I’m telling you, Mr. Sage, is that I hope you controlled your thoughts when holding the meteorite." "If you want to know what went through my mind, I’ll flat out tell you. All you need to do is ask." Clinton turned and stared directly into Agent Hamlin’s eyes. They remained penetrating and invasive, but suddenly eager to know what Clinton had to offer. "Go on." "When I picked up the rock the first time, it burned my hands. I’m guessing it was the residual heat from its trip through the atmosphere. If you want to, you can ask Officer Hanes about the condition in which he found me, and he’ll back up my claim. I had passed out from the pain of the wounds it inflicted on my hands. That fat little shit of a police officer annoyed me when I came to, and I simply grabbed the meteorite and was completely enraged because – and I don’t know if you realize who I am, or what I do – the biggest moment of my career is only two days away." "I’m aware that you’re a professional wrestler." "Good," Clinton nodded, and managed to fake a smile. He wanted the Agent to feel like they were building a rapport, when in fact the only thing Clinton was building was a mountain of lies, "Well I’m set to wrestle in a tournament, and my first round opponent is none other than the previous year’s winner, Rich Rollins. I was so angry at that cop, and the notion that the biggest match of my career was going to be cancelled that I simply picked up the meteorite and chucked it out of the window, wishing that it had never harmed me." Agent Hamlin continued to stare, looking for any signs of deceit. Clinton could tell the agent found nothing, and continued, "Then suddenly my hands start to burn like they did before, and I pass out once again. I wake up, and everything is back to normal." "So, you basically wished away the burns on your hands. Am I correct on that?" "Yes, sir." It sickened Clinton to have to resort to respecting authority. "And the kitchen knife, which has traces of blood on it?" More lies poured out of Clinton, "Still frustrated and probably a little delirious at the situation, I tried to stab at the meteorite. The knife stopped dead, and my hand slid down past the handle and onto the blade. Except this time, the wounds almost immediately healed themselves. So, instead of trying that route again, I went and fetched the hammer." "Why try to break open the meteorite, Mr. Sage?" Agent Hamlin asked, still going over Clinton’s story in his mind. He bit his lower lip as he weighed the explanation. "Curiosity, I guess." Clinton said with a shrug. Right now he wished he could smile, knowing the deception he was conveying was clearly working. But now it was time to steer clear of his lies, and try to get some more facts out of the FBI agent, "So, how many more of these meteorites are out there?" "We tracked five entering the atmosphere. Yours was the first we’ve picked up that had its powers depleted." Clinton was shocked, "What do you mean, it can only be used once?" "That’s correct. We’ve picked up two more, which were still radiating the heat you spoke of. Once the meteorites power is expended, they grow cold and glassy, exactly like the one we’ve taken from you." Clinton tapped away at his chin, lost in thought. Would it be possible to find the meteorites before the FBI found them? The thought was instantly appealing. He might need to up any sort of monetary bribe that Officer Hanes might try to take, in hopes of getting some inside information. "It is the highest priority for us right now to locate the final two, and keep them out of the wrong hands. In an odd twist of fate, Mr. Sage, the last time Earth passed through the tail of Vekoma, the meteorites were used for horrible, horrible crimes. And they were successful until one man in particular managed to kill off the cult." Suddenly, coupled with the emphasis that Agent Hamlin had put onto the word ‘cult’, Clinton’s stomach sank. In his mind’s eye, Clinton remembered that when he uncovered the tombs of Harlan Raines’ victims – all members of the cult both Clinton and Harlan had been born into, The Eyes of Fear – he found about a dozen or so shiny rocks of various sizes. The Eyes of Fear had used the meteorites decades earlier to pull off some of the most heinous acts of terrorism that Pennsylvania and New York had ever seen. "I can see that you’ve made the connection, Mr. Sage. Now, what do you think are the odds that you, being the last surviving member of that cult, would just happen to come across the same fragments of the meteor, like your parents and their horrible friends did three decades ago?" Agent Hamlin cocked his head to the side like an inquisitive parrot, but the curiosity was feigned and the intent was mockery. Clinton was still numb with the knowledge, "Almost sounds like someone wanted it to happen, Agent Hamlin. Like someone wished for it." "Exactly, Mr. Sage. Now you know why we’re so intent on finding these meteorites. We can’t allow them to fall into the wrong hands like they did when ‘The Eyes of Fear’ stumbled across them 30 years ago. Luckily enough, you used the power to simply heal your body before a career-defining match. It’s my guess that once your match is over with, your body will return to normal. Wounds will stay. But first, let me tell you a little story about a friend of mine." Clinton blinked in astonishment, "A story? You just told me the most fascinating story I’ve ever heard, you idiot!" He winced at the slip of character. It was hard being sincere and interested when all Clinton wanted to do was find the meteorites and paint his own masterpiece of havoc. "Well then, perhaps I’ll just call it a warning." Hamlin paused, but continued to stare into Clinton’s eyes. He continued to pry, trying to find some sort of deceit, but Clinton knew that this story was more than likely his final attempt at uncovering any lies Clinton had told, "I once knew a very bad man, you see, and for the first and only time in my life, I put my own personal compassion ahead of the government’s desire to collect all of these meteor fragments. I allowed the man to place his hand on a fragment, and make a wish." Clinton hadn’t realized he was perked up, curious. He tried to downplay his curiosity, but could sense that the Agent was aware of his interest. Hamlin continued, "This man was deceitful from the day he left the womb, and caused much pain with his lies throughout his life. After losing nearly everything, and spending time in prison, he sought only one thing: repentance. When I told him about the Vekoma meteorites, he asked me if he could place his hand upon one and make his wish: to lead an honest life." At this point in the story, Agent Hamlin loosened his necktie and unbuttoned his collar. He produced a small dagger, which hung from a chain around his neck. The handle was gold and reflective, while the blade was apparently made of rubber. It was no more than 3 inches in length, from the end of the handle to the tip of the blade. Sliding the rubber away from the handle, Agent Hamlin revealed the blade of the dagger: black, pocked, and radiating mild heat. The blade was made from a fragment of the Vekoma meteorite. Hamlin continued with the story, "And he was granted that wish, as a result of one of these little daggers. They are the Vekoma Daggers, and every agent on my team wears one around his neck. These are here for the sole purpose of undoing what someone wished for." "Are you going to use that on me?" "No, you’re not worth the power this thing holds. But if you’re not telling me the truth, I’m going to bring the my friend around, and he’ll let me know exactly what you wished for," "So he wished for an honest life, and now he works alongside you, huh? From the gutter to the Bureau?" Clinton said in a mocking tone. The sincere act was continuing to falter. Hamlin smiled, and waved his hand in disagreement, "No, you’ve misunderstood my wording. When he wished for an honest life, the meteorite gave him the inability to tell lies, and the ability to detect dishonesty in others." "A lie detector? That’s not so bad." "I disagree," Agent Hamlin started, but paused for a moment. He appeared to be thinking about his friend, "Sometimes you just don’t want to know how often people lie to you. And it can drive you a little crazy, knowing the truth about everything." Agent Hamlin sheathed the dagger once more, and tucked it back into his shirt. He started to walk back towards the waiting helicopter, leaving Clinton in the main lobby of his mansion, standing silent under a massive crystal chandelier. Amazed and astonished. Clinton looked down at the bloody knife, and smiled. The FBI agent continued to walk away from him, but yelled back a final warning, "We’ll be watching, Mr. Sage. I’d be on your best behavior, unless you’re in the market for losing your home and the fortune you’ve cashed in because of what you were born into." Clinton winced at the last sentence. They had done their homework. "And you don’t want me to bring my friend around. He may excel at the ability to discern truth from fiction, but he severely lacks the tact and reserved manner that I’ve entertained you with." Agent Hamlin showed no emotion with the threat, and finally disappeared around the corner towards the helicopter. * * * * * With Amy strapped into the toddler’s chair in the backseat, Reggie Hanes drove home. He remained amazed at how easily it was to forget about the rest of the world when he was taking care of his only daughter. Her mother had died during childbirth, leaving Reggie to care for an infant daughter. He had taken out loans and expended all the favors that he possibly could during the first year of her life, taking off of work for the entire duration. But once she hit a certain age and he had run out of cash, he needed to return to work. Being a cop didn’t pay much, but considering the years put into the force, his pay was making the Hanes family break even. The court case against Amy’s mother’s death was still pending, but it didn’t look like much money would come out of that, not that Reggie wanted it. How could he eliminate his own personal losses, via the loss of his wife? But each time he dropped off Amy at the nursery, he frowned a little. It was hard giving up the only thing in his life that he truly loved. But to counter that, every day when he picked her up and she recognized him with those brilliant blue eyes and the most beautiful smile he had ever seen, he was filled with joy. Passing through the neighborhood, Reggie’s mood continued to languish. Houses with peeling paint and missing pieces of siding were staples of the community. The beating that his Honda received from the horribly paved and patched road only rattled his confidence into a pile of sadness. " Have a little bitch of a daughter that you know you can’t send to a college that she deserves?" Reggie winced when he thought of Clinton’s insults towards Amy. It was ridiculous to get angry at such a comment, considering that Clinton didn’t even know if Amy existed. Reggie knew it was absurd, but he couldn’t control himself. And when he pulled up to their small, two-bedroom house, the driveway was littered with filth from a neighbor’s wind-tossed trashcans. With Amy mumbling something unintelligible to anyone over the age of two, Reggie Hanes stared at his own home and felt disgusted that his daughter had to grow up in something so shitty. It sickened him. Because Clinton was right. He heard Clinton’s offer in his head once more, "…everybody’s got a price. Name it and it’s yours…" Reggie sighed, and would call Sage after he put Amy to bed. It sickened him to think that his daughter would hear him accept the offer to become Clinton Sage’s whipping boy. She deserved better than this. And right now, Clinton’s offer was the only way to guarantee that. * * * * * Clinton had just heard the oddest and most far-fetched story of his life, yet he believed it. He had to believe it, because not more than half a day earlier, his hands healed themselves. His knee reformed itself after being shattered. What Agent Hamlin told was the truth. It had to be. But what Clinton told Agent Hamlin was pure fabrication, except for his disdain towards Officer Hanes. He had looked an FBI agent directly in the eye, and lied. The pleasure he received from getting away with such a feat was beyond comprehension. Gooseflesh ran down from the nape of his neck to the small of his spine in pure excitement. The FBI now thinks Clinton Sage wished away injuries so he could be prepared for a career-defining run through the GTT6 tournament. In reality, at the moment he was holding the meteorite out of pure frustration and contempt, Clinton Sage wished that all his pain, anger, feelings, and rage would befall the man that had hidden under the mask of Spartan. The injuries weren’t healing themselves, but in fact, they were more than likely being transferred to that bastard who hid under the mask during GTT5! Clinton’s lips peeled back in a sinister smile, and he weighed the possibilities of his situation. He could simply keep hurting himself, and try to scout out hospital reports around the country for similar injuries. But the FBI believes that after the first round of GTT6, Clinton should return to normal. It was preventing him from doing anything substantial, like what occurred earlier when Officer Hanes had crushed his kneecap with a hammer. He would have to be careful, very careful. But right now, the only thing he wanted to do was to test his theory, and send a message to the man who had defeated him prior to GTT5 a year earlier. Clinton made his way back towards the gutted shrine, and picked up the kitchen knife he had used to slice his leg open earlier this morning. He took off his shirt, and started to carve gigantic letters into his own chest. Clenching his jaw as the steel ripped apart his flesh, Clinton continued until all four letters were finished. Oh, how the fun begins. Letter after bloody letter. S A G E By the time the ‘E’ had been carved deep into his left pectoral, the ‘S’ had already healed and disappeared from the right. Clinton knew that it had transferred itself onto the chest of Spartan, whoever and wherever that masked bastard was in the world. Clinton smiled at the thought, Now that prick knows who he’s up against. The irony of the situation dawned on him. For a year, it had been Clinton seeking out the identity of Spartan, wondering who could have possibly given him such a thorough beating inside of the wrestling ring, and who had given him such enlightenment towards the world. Now, it was Spartan who was wondering how these injuries were manifesting themselves, no doubt giving him equal doses of agony and confusion. By the blade of a knife, Clinton had revealed himself. He had no doubt that Spartan would understand what it meant, and come calling in return. Pleading. * * * * *
