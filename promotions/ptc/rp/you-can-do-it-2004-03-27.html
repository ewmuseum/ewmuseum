You Can Do It!

27 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-03-27 Five years old without a care in the world, how I wished I could go back to those days. Even though my childhood was filled with neglect and elders who only cared for themselves, those were always things that could be tossed aside for the moment, because I was a kid. To me none of those things mattered, what mattered to me then was fun, something that had been long since forgotten after having to remember what it feels like to be stabbed in the back. And even now almost a year later it still hurts, but that’s where the Cruiserweight Tournament that I recently entered myself into came into play. Perhaps even at twenty two years of age I could rediscover what it was like to be five again, when all that mattered was being the best at the things you could do. If I could win this thing, perhaps all the heartache I’ve suffered over the past few years wouldn’t matter anymore. All that would matter then would be bragging rights, something I hadn’t entitled myself to in quite awhile. It wasn’t often that thought about being number one. To me just having a contract in the business was rewarding enough. Nothing would be worse than having to go back to the days of training camp. But there wasn’t any need to worry about that, even if Rush Mason managed to close RPW as he threatened to do. As long as I didn’t fail in this tournament, I was sure that there would be plenty of other places to take me. Of course if I lost in the first round to a manager and some chick who apparently snaps at any given moment, then well I might as well give up. “If I lose this round, I’ll never forgive myself.” I whispered to myself as I kept my eyes on my adopted son, Evan, who currently held what I envied the most, a carefree life. Even when he struggled to balance himself on the child sized bike I had bought him for Christmas there was no frustration shown. I almost wished that I could be him going into this match, just so that even if I lost I’d be glad I gave it my all. “Tessa, I need your help!” He shouted towards me, showing no shame for asking help from the people he depended most on. It was something he had learned from me, before I became too independent for my own good. I wondered if Bob himself would have the same nerve to ask the man he managed, Ric Austin, for some pointers. It probably would be a wise thing to do, but if Bob were as stubborn as I had become after losing to Chris Storm, than he’d rush in assuming that he knows what he’s getting himself into and perhaps make a fool of himself. Then again, I had no problem with that. If he makes a fool of himself, that’s just one person less I need to worry about defeating in order to go onto the second round where I’d face either a fellow RPW colleague in Karina Wolfenden, or the man who’d have to have a load of talent to take her down, Limp. “No you don’t, you can do it on your own! I saw you, you were doing just fine!” Though I felt bad for leaving him on his own to learn, it was unfortunately the only way he was going to learn. I knew this better than anyone, considering it was I who refused to let go of Chris, but it wasn’t till I let him go that my true potential came out. Now I had a chance to further prove my abilities once more, provided I made it past my first round opponents of Bob The Temp and Dawn McFarlane. If not, well then I’d end up failing myself and further proving Chris right that perhaps I’m really not a wrestler, just a girl who can bounce around the ring. Instead of insisting that he couldn’t, Evan decided to listen to the words that came from the person who looked up to the most, me. Like the little engine that could, Evan insisted that he too could ride his bike if he believed in himself, but instead he ended up proving us both wrong as the bike tilted to the side and he came crashing down with it. It was almost as if were a message were being sent to me, that maybe this time things wouldn’t go the way I hoped. “I should have known that would happen.” I sighed to myself as I ran over to the fallen Evan, helping him up to his feet as he bit on his bottom lip, doing what he could to be a big kid and not embarrass himself with tears. It was yet another trait he had inherited from me, not through blood and genes but through watching me both inside and out of the ring and suck up all that has happened within the last year or so which is another reason why winning this round would be nothing but bittersweet for me. I had been working quite hard, but had yet for something this big to go my way. This time I didn’t want to walk out, fighting off the tears the way Evan was right now. “Look at that, you see what you pressured the poor kid into doing.” I turned to notice a woman with coal colored hair, glaring over at me as if she were trying to hint that I was a horrible mother just for trying to encourage independence in my child. “You’re parenting skills are almost as bad as your wrestling skills.” She was obviously a wrestling fan, perhaps one that favored the old school type wrestling that Chris Storm favored as well. “Listen, I know I made a mistake, but it happens.” I responded in a cool and collected manner, despite wanting to spew more than a few unkind words towards the woman for her unnecessary comments. “Of course it does, but hey maybe one day you’ll learn that the wrestling business is not the business for you, nor is parenting.” She walked off with a shrug, suggesting that I was too stubborn to realize just that. Of course she obviously wanted to avoid the words she knew would come next from me as well, which is probably another reason for departure. Perhaps she was right though. With my recent decline of success inside of the ring I could understand where she was coming from. But that wasn’t going to stop me. There was no was in hell I was going to back out now, even if I was facing two people who I knew almost nothing about other than where they came from. Evan watched the woman walk off with an expression that clearly questioned what the big deal was. So he fell off his bike? “Tessa you’re not a bad mom, or a bad wrestler.” He said as he disregarded the scrapes on both his knees, just to make sure I was bothered b the comments. “You’re the best and I can’t wait to see you win the first round of that thing.” That was all the encouragement I needed. Who cared if no one else thought I’d do it? With support like that all I needed to do is go out there and give it my best, and if I lost that at least I knew I’d have Evan there to remind me that I gave it my best, something I often forgot when losing.
