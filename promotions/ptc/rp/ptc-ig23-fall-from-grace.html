IG23 - Fall from grace

17 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Fall from grace One of the perks about renting a penthouse apartment in one of the tallest buildings in the city was the unobstructed view that it afforded. The bustling streets so far below were forgotten, the ant-like bodies of stockbrokers and bankers that pressed together at rush hour were nothing but a dim and distant memory as day turned into night and a modicum of stillness swept over everything like a summer fog. The reds and yellows of daylight that coated the city in harsh shades, like an aging hooker with platinum hair and too much makeup on, were gone now, replaced by the muted tones of silver that only a full moon on a cloudless night could bring. No bright and contrasting lights, no sunlight to expose all the cracks in the façade of the city; moonlight, and the blessed shadows that it brought, could hide a multitude of sins and turn the depravity of the city into something almost poetic. Standing on the balcony of the apartment, the breeze chilling me to the bone as it swept my hair in waves around my body, I allowed the night to wrap me in its arms and looked into its single eye, the moon, and basked in its non-judgemental gaze. For if in darkness even a whore could reveal a hint of her former beauty and decrepit and crumbling stone could give a glimpse of its former majesty then surely a man who had once held the world in his hands could pretend – for a moment – that he had never fallen so far. “Once upon a time …” The words escaped my lips almost before I realised that I was thinking them. How easy life would be if it was nothing more than a fairy-tale, a story written by someone far removed for the reality that their fingers created with quick and thoughtless keystrokes. Someone sitting in their apartment could craft a whole world of possibilities, as they looked out over a sleeping city, and they would never need to worry about any consequences. “… happily ever after.” Like all fairytales nothing could possibly ever go wrong. Finish the chapter and go to sleep, knowing that if the worst comes to the very worst they could hit the delete button and re-write their hero’s fall from grace. The real world, of course, was so different to that. So painfully different. The wind gusted slightly stronger, breaking me abruptly free from my reverie and I chuckled as I realised how maudlin, how pathetic, my thoughts had become. It was probably the closest that I had come to calling out to a Creator – to God – in my whole life. A cosmic writer telling the tale of someone else, crafting their every word, their every action, and the fairytale was complete. As easy as that would be I knew that it wasn’t the truth. I knew that, for me, there was no-one pulling the strings, no-one writing my tale … or at least, if there was, he was doing a bad job of it. “You know, you could just jump and save us all from having to share this misery of yours.” I didn’t even flinch as I heard the voice from behind me. I could have checked the door to the apartment but I knew that I would still have found it locked from the inside, just as I knew that there was no-one else inside it with me. I could have turned in surprise, incredulous that I was hearing someone that couldn’t be there. I could have done many things when the woman’s voice mocked me, but I didn’t. I did only one. I ignored her. … or at least I tried. “Though knowing your luck you would probably just break every bone in your body” continued the unseen female. “Too inept to even die properly!” Another gust of wind brought a large strand of hair whipping in front of my face, blinding and choking me until I managed to reach up and pull it away. My fingers caught on the thin material that covered my face, from brow to throat, and I groaned ever so slightly as I felt the slight irritation against my skin. “That mask still giving you problems?” If anyone else had been listening they may have thought that they heard a note of concern in the question, but they would have been wrong. On both parts; the concept of concern was unknown to the woman and it wasn’t a question. If it was she may have waited a fraction of a second before continuing. “Well, that is what you get for screwing everything up like this!” There was no mistaking the emotion in her voice this time, pure, unbridled venom. “You had to go and lose it didn’t you?!” With a sigh I fixed the mask back in place, choosing to ignore the fact that she was right. On both counts. The mask that I wore was just that, a mask. Just a few short weeks ago something more than that had covered me, had been part of me. … but in a moment of sheer hubris I had put it on the line, I had put everything that was important to me on the line. All because I wanted to prove that my words were true. That I was the best talent that the WWA had to offer, with or without the World title around my waist. So I put my mask on the line against the WWA World Title, knowing that I would do what I had always done before – walk away with the win. … I didn’t. I lost the match, I lost the mask … I lost part of me. No-one had ever grasped the fact that I didn’t wear a mask to hide who I was, I didn’t wear a mask as a part of a gimmick. I was the mask, the mask was me. Reflexively as I thought about it my hands went up and traced the lines of the ‘thing’ that covered my face now. Seamless and colorless, it looked more like a birth skein than a mask. Minimal features were evident through it and even less of my thoughts, my emotions, could be seen or guessed. To anyone looking at me from the outside I was as cold and impassive as always. I, however, knew the truth, I knew the turmoil that was broiling inside my body and mind. … and so did she. One match, one loss, and everything had changed so much. I had been on top of the World, on top of my game and – literally – on top of the WWA itself. Then one moment of bravado wrecked it all. What had it been now, three – no four – matches in a row that I had lost and each time that another one took place I saw the smirks and sneers on the faces of my opponents. For over a year I had dominated the WWA, right up to the moment that I faced the whole damn roster to win the World title … but now, only a short while later, and I was relegated to appearing on house shows and opening matches. If I was featured at all. With the loss of the title and the loss of the mask came the loss of my usefulness, it seemed, and even though I pretended to be seething about the slight that was done to me inside I was more honest. I couldn’t blame them. I wasn’t the same man I had been before because I had lost something else too. My confidence. I may still be called Ember, the so-called masked enigma, but I wasn’t even a shadow of my former self. “You know,” the woman’s voice scathed me, “I can’t even be bothered trying to talk you into jumping, there is no point – you aren’t worth it!” A shiver ran down my spine and I smiled slightly as I realised that she was gone. The woman who seemed to haunt my dreams and come and go with the ease of an actual ghost, someone who claimed to know me from a different time, a different life, and who I couldn’t even confirm was more than a figment of my own mind, was gone as if she never existed. “Maybe she doesn’t,” I thought to myself as I walked back into my darkened apartment. A glint of white caught my eye and I moved to the door, bending down to pick up a couple of envelopes that had been pushed under it. Slipping a finger under the seal I ripped the first one open and scanned the contents before dropping it to the floor, the words still buzzing in my brain. Notice of eviction. Like I said, renting a penthouse apartment had many perks, but those perks didn’t come cheap and when the big money had stopped coming in the bills had mounted up. I realised that I probably hadn’t paid the rent for a month or so and considered getting in touch with the letting agency to sort it out but quickly dismissed that as pointless. It would be just another fight that I would lose. Turning back to face inside the room I stared out at the balcony and remembered the words of the woman who haunted me. “Just jump.” My feet moved closer to the open door without my mind realising what they were doing. It really would be quite easy to just let go, to just allow the night to take me in her arms and carry me away. Holding onto the railing that was the only barrier between me and the city street so far below I felt my knuckles tighten and my teeth clench. I had fallen from grace, the World champion stripped off gold, the masked man stripped of himself. No-one to care, no-one to even notice if he was gone. No-one … I realised … except the only one who mattered. Myself. Ripping open the letter, expecting another bill, another demand, and my eyes widened slightly as I read the words. An open invitation to a tournament. An open invitation … perhaps … to salvation, rather than the infinity that the ultimate fall promised me. Leaning over the balcony railings I breathed in the night, I soaked in the darkness, and I basked in the glow of the moonlight as I let the invitation fall from my hand and gently drift down and out of sight. I may have fallen far, I may even have hit rock bottom, but that only meant one thing. The only way was up, and that climb would begin in the Infinite Gauntlet.
