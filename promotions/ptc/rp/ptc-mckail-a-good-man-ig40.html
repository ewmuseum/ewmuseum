"A Good Man" Jacob McKail || IG 40 “THE SCOURGE” FUSE Uproar || 12th October 2007 Your heart skips a beat as the action unfolds before your eyes via the backstage monitor afforded to FUSE workers and staff alike. It’s the main event and you can feel the atmosphere capsulated within the arena even from here. Other workers and FUSE technical staff gather around you. They feel it too. They suspect something’s about to happen; something big, something momentous. But you know what the forthcoming series of events are going to be. You helped devise the plan set in motion weeks ago and you’ll play an integral part in it‘s execution. The people all around you will very much dislike the inevitable scenario that‘s about to unfold. They will be disappointed with you. They will be angry at you. They will feel betrayed by you. You tell yourself that you don’t care and their opinions are meaningless, but for the first time in an age, you also feel a twinge of guilt creep down your throat and lodge itself deep in your gut. You realise that you’re lying to yourself. It doesn’t feel good. The questions pose themselves immediately and you can’t help but entertain them, even though you know you’ll undoubtedly wish you hadn’t. Is what you’re about to do what you really want? Are you ready to be universally hated? Are you really going to be able to live with yourself after this? The answers to the question elude you completely and you try to block them out and concentrate on the backstage monitor the best you can. You tell yourself it’s too late for you to back out now and you’ll deal with the consequences of your actions later, but you know you’re over-simplifying things. You realise you can’t even imagine what the consequences will be, never mind pre-constructing effective methods in which to manage them. You see carnage envelope the arena as the Affliction and the Highwaymen erupt in the middle of the ring, completely disrupting the ill-fated Lane Stevens and Jason Cruise match without thought to consequence. You bear witness to the Affliction gain the upper hand and feel the piercing gaze of around you, glaring in your direction expectantly. They want you to go help the Highwaymen and stand against the Affliction like you did all of those months ago, in the old days, in your glory days. They don’t realise it’s been a hell of a few months. They don’t realise you’ve changed, that you‘re different somehow. And that’s all part of the plan. You head for the entrance hatchway with purpose and those backstage with you clap and cheer and you hear: “Go get ‘em, McKail!” echo throughout. If only they knew the truth. You part the curtains and pick up your speed. It’s not long before the crowd notices you and their reaction is deafening. This time you can only hear: “RRRRRAAAAAAAAAAAAAHHHHHHHHH!!!” They’ve erupted into ecstasy. They think you’re here to save the day. To them you’re a hero. But you don’t let yourself carried away. You keep your mind on the plan and accept that soon you will be widely considered to be the villain. You tell yourself that you’re a good man and that sometimes good men are forced to do unspeakable things. You’re trying to justify what you’re about to do and it isnt working. But you quickly remind yourself that nobody is forcing you to do this, even if the reasons behind this course of events still remain unclear, even to you. You get to the ring and you turn on your ‘friend’ Jonathan Rhine and his Highwaymen. You publicly align yourself with Clinton Sage and the Affliction. You let them all know that you are the dreaded Scourge that's been plaguing FUSE all this time and you have been all along. “GUILTY CONSCIENCE” Some Nameless Local Dive Bar || The Bronx, New York || 21st November 2007 Jacob McKail wandered into the same damned rat-infested bar he’d been frequenting for the last three days and pretended to ignore the fact that all eyes darted toward his general direction and the muttering which promptly followed. Same thing happened yesterday and the day before, but as of yet nobody had just come out and affronted him, neither verbally nor physically. It was only a matter of time before they did, it was as inevitable as night following day and then he would be forced to find himself a new watering hole so that the cycle could begin anew. Hell, he hadn't even suspected so many people even watched FUSE, but somehow the story followed him wherever he chose to frequent and the burning question was always asked: Why, McKail? Why did you join the Affliction, McKail? Why did you betray your friends, McKail? How could you possibly betray your fans, McKail? Those were just the nice ones. The rest didn’t even bother to ask questions, they simply lunged in with a punch or a kick or spit in his face from a far. The reasons behind his change of heart still remained a mystery to that day, despite the aggressive interrogations he’d since received from Jonathan Rhine and Ethan Frost, the latter beating him unconscious dead centre of the ring only a few nights previously. His nose was still immensely painful, still the large doses of alcohol he was about to consume would undoubtedly help in that aspect. McKail made sure not to look anyone in the eye and directed himself to the far end of the bar, the section least populated by the other patrons. The bartender was leaning against the counter at the other talking to a few regulars as McKail mounted the barstool warily and upon recognising his overeager body mannerisms, promptly excused himself from his conversation and pushed himself up into a standing position. The man was in his late forties, maybe even early fifties. His grey hair already took root and grew down to shoulder level, but appeared to be well trimmed and despite the sorry state of his drinking establishment, his clothes always seemed to be impeccably clean. “How’s the nose today, kid? He questioned, placing both hands on the stained and chipped counter before him and leaning forward ever so slightly. His name was Ike, or that’s how he introduced himself. Whether that was his birth name or just something others called him was beyond McKail and truth be told, he didn’t really want to know. Like most bartenders he’d had suffered in his time, Ike was friendly and talkative to the point McKail got angry. The worst thing about it was he didn’t even seem to notice, which pissed McKail off even more. McKail shot Ike a rather distasteful look. “Less talk, more beer…” Ike shook his head and reached for a beer glass. “Manners don’t cost a thing, kid. Remember that.” McKail sighed, frustrated. “Manners are for people who care about what other people think of them.” He paused and glared at the bartender incredulously. “Whiskey first, brother.” Ike rolled his eyes, placed the beer glass to one side and fetched a tumbler instead. He tossed in a handful of ice and poured the whiskey over the top. It was the good stuff, just the kind McKail liked. “And I take it you don’t much care what people think of you?” McKail eyed him warily, before snatching the tumbler out his hand. Ike didn’t even complain, he just scooped the beer glass again and began to fill it. McKail drank the whiskey in one long gulp and although the fiery liquid burnt his mouth, he recovered quickly. “Of course not.” “That explains a lot!” The unfamiliar voice from across the bar exclaimed. McKail didn’t react immediately, he grabbed his beer out of Ike’s hand and took a mouthful first, but Ike sighed. “Come on, John. Leave the man be.” Across the bar John ignored him. “Considerin’ what you did an all.” Something raged inside McKail’s gut upon hearing the strangers words and threatened to surge up through his throat and out to the surface, but with great effort he held it in. He finished his beer and signalled to Ike for another. Ike gently scooped up McKail’s glass a third time and went about pouring him another, whilst shooting John a cautionary look. He obviously recognised just how much of an explosive situation he had unfolding before him. He’d probably been in the business for years and could see trouble brewing a mile away, though the by look of his drinking establishment's shattered and worn interior, he couldn’t been incredibly successful at preventing it. “I mean, you did stab both your friends and your fans in the back,” John continued, taking a grateful mouthful of his own beer. “That’s some cold shit, right there.” McKail maintained his flimsy façade of ignorance, but the truth was, every single word spewing from this idiot’s mouth was cutting him deeply. But that’s not say he didn’t deserve it. He had this coming; this guilt, this self-doubt, this agony… “What the hell are you getting at, John?” Ike questioned, finally handing McKail his beer. “He can’t much care about what other people think of him, can he?” John continued. “He betrayed them all knowing he’d become the most hated man in the place.” He paused and took another swig of his beer. “Like I said, some pretty cold shit…” McKail wanted to get off his barstool and beat the crap out of the fucking clown shoes for being a lying sack of shit that needed to be taught a lesson, but in the end he did nothing. The idiot wasn’t lying at all; his conclusion was shockingly close to the bone and that’s upset McKail the most. He drowned himself in another beer and told himself, for the hundredth time that day, that he was a good man. “DISAPPOINTMENT” Some Crappy Motel Room || 11th October 2007 Caroline is the woman you love, she’s the sister of your late wife and she’s known you were The Scourge all along. Like every one else with a vested interest in you, there is only one question swimming through her head: why? Problem is, this isn’t the same as lying to other people, nor is it the same as ignoring other people. This is your life outside of the wrestling industry. This is the woman you think you love. This is the woman who you think you can spend the rest of your life with. You don’t lie to her. You don’t ignore her. You tell her what you’re about to do. You tell her that tomorrow night you’re going to turn on your friends. She asks whether all of this has anything to do with your alcoholism. You bite your tongue and prevent yourself from telling her that you don’t have a booze problem and then you tell her this has nothing to do with booze. You can see in her face that she wants to believe you. You see her in her face that she doesn’t believe you. You see disappointment spreads throughout her face like a virus and you feel your heart sink right down to the bottom of your gut. She pleads with you not to go through with it, but you resist. You tell her that this is something that you have to do. You tell her that you’re not going to change. You tell her that deep down you’re still a good man, the man she fell in love with. She tells you she knows, but you can’t tell whether she’s lying or not. You fall asleep with her in your arms and you wonder whether it’s for the very last time. “THE RIGHT THING” Some Nameless Local Dive Bar || The Bronx, New York || 21st November 2007 They sat in silence for the longest time. Ike didn’t know what the hell to say and he certainly didn’t even attempt to test the water. McKail could tell that the bartender was having a difficult time trying to read him, trying to determine just how well the ‘big bad wrestler’ took the other patron’s comments. Just how McKail preferred it. The criticizing patron, however, simply continued his beer as though nothing had happened, not even bothering to fruther hammer home his point. In light of the man’s landslide moral victory, McKail found his actions, or lack thereof, completely bizarre but did not dare question it. The silence was eventually broken, by two individual’s sporting expensive leather jackets and over the top sunglasses who burst into the drinking establishment with purpose. McKail had lived and grown up in the Bronx long enough to know when trouble arrived and right about now that same old clench in his gut was telling him all he needed to know. He also noted the fear swipe across Ike’s face and from then on, the story pretty much told itself. The two of them glared around the place, casing the joint for both potential victims and problematic have a go heroes alike. They cockily strode forward, daring anyone to establish eye contact with them, but they did not remove their sunglasses for reasons beyond McKail’s admittedly limited knowledge. He disliked them upon first sight. He knew what was coming and it didn't sit well at all, besides like he always said, the only people who wear sunglasses inside are assholes and the blind. Ike nodded a clearly frightened greeting at them. “Afternoon boys, what can I get ya?” The lead asshole was smaller than the other and had a perfectly bald head. He cracked a menacing smile and pointed toward the cash register. “Don’t get cute.” Russian’s, McKail easily determined from their accents and judging by the way Ike was scrambling for all the cash in his register, they were obviously criminally connected. The smaller asshole snatched the money out of his hands and immediately began counting it. Upon completion he glared at Ike and then at bigger man flanking him. “You fucking kidding me?” He asked. “Where's the rest?” Ike backed away. “The taking’s have been bad, man, I--” The smaller man ignored his excuses and instead nodded at the bigger asshole standing behind him, who promptly stormed forward and grabbed the hapless bartender by the throat. The other bar patrons scattered, scampering out of the place like rats leaving a sinking ship. McKail stayed to finish his beer. “We don’t want excuses!” The smaller asshole declared, his anger flaring up and dying down again in a heartbeat. “I think you need to be taught a lesson.” The larger man nodded and smiled; he’d just been given permission to seriously hurt someone and it seemed he particularly enjoyed the concept. Ike managed to turn his head toward McKail and mouth a very weak and quiet ‘help me’, which prompted the little asshole to momentarily turn his attention toward McKail. “I recognise you, don’t I?” He glanced back at his bigger friend. “Yes, yes, you big bad American wrestler, yes?” He paused for a response, but he didn’t get one. “You’re thinking of helping your friend here, yes? We all know wrestling’s fake! My friend Boris is not. He will rip your arms off if you even try something!” Doubtful, McKail thought to himself. Neither of these Russian morons carried weapon, at least none that he could determine, and despite the big man’s size, McKail could tell just by his stance the mammoth relied on brute strength and intimidation alone. It would be so easy to help Ike. It would be so easy to teach these Russian bastards a lesson. It would be so easy to be a hero again. McKail stood from his barstool and silently headed toward the exit. Ike gurgled in horror and pain at the sight of this and the smaller Russian laughed manically to himself. “That’s right big bad wrestler! You leave! We‘ll look after your friend just fine!” He was just a normal man. Not a superhero, not a cop and certainly not a gangland soldier wanting to earn his stripes. He didn’t need this shit. He didn’t need this heat. Anyone else would - and did - do the same. Or at least that’s what he told himself. At the back of his mind, lay the truth of the matter. Despite his previous fervent protests and grandiose self-delusions, he finally realised that wasn’t really all that good of a man at all and he didn’t know how in the hell he was supposed to live with himself. He left.
