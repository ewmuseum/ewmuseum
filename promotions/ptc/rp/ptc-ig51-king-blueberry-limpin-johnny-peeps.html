Limpin Johnny Peeps Now “God, I hate this thing. It’s like a thousand little hands choking me.” Jared Sykes, the man who’d once wrestled as ‘King Blueberry’, had two fingers wedged between the collar of his shirt and his neck, trying to loosen his tie and give himself a bit more room to breathe. It was rare that someone in his profession had the need to don a suit, but this event had nothing to do with actions in or outside of the wrestling ring. “But you look so handsome,” Mary-Ellen Lothrop, his mother, always had an air of je ne said quoi about her, and her response carried with it some of the playful sarcasm that had helped make her son moderately famous. “Besides, it’s only for one afternoon.” Jared’s fidgeting was relentless. “Well I need to at least loosen it a little, otherwise we’ll be burying two people today.” For thirty years he’d been on the planet, but never once did it surprise the elder Sykes when she’d need to chide her son for his behavior. “Can we please try not to make a scene today, if nothing else than for your grandmother’s sake? And stop playing with your tie, you’re knocking it out of place.” Jared sighed, conceding to his mother and straightening his tie. “Yeah, well,” he muttered in hushed words, under his breath, “not like he’s going to be critiquing my outfit.” * * * * * Then Family gatherings at the Sykes/Lothrop house usually tended to be ridiculous engagements, though there occasions when things were not intended to be amusing. This was one of those evenings. “So I got some news today,” Mary-Ellen began, addressing the other four people in the living room. On the couch sat Jared’s sister, Stefanie, and her boyfriend James. Next to them rested Mary-Ellen’s second husband, and the children’s stepfather, Bruce. Jared had occupied himself by sprawling on the floor, and had been wrestling with one of the family’s dogs when his mother entered the room. She sat down in an olive colored recliner, eyes set on the family. Stefanie was the first to address her. “Is it something bad?” “Kind of. Your uncle John has been diagnosed with prostate cancer. I found out this morning when your aunt called me.” The last few months had been stressful on Mary-Ellen, as a rift had emerged between her and her siblings, so it came as no surprise when she referred to the family so distantly. “He’s going to be starting treatment, and they’re not quite sure how advanced it is yet.” Jared sat up, cradling the dog on his lap as it licked at his face. His sister continued to press with questions. “What does this mean? Is he, like, going to have radiation or something? Is he really sick?” “Well,” Bruce spoke up, attempting to offer some information on the subject. With over 35 years of experience as an operating room nurse his was the most qualified opinion the family had at the moment, “it’s going to be a little tough. Some of the treatment methods that they use can cause erectile dysfunction, and he might become incontinent.” This time it was Jared’s turn to ask the questions, and the result would not be favorable. “So what happens to Limpin’ Johnny Peeps when the treatment is over?” The rest of the family tried to gloss over what was just said, pretending to ignore it. “Do we know if he’s going to be okay?” Stefanie asked before her brother got the chance to speak again. “Depends on how far along it is. If they got it soon enough, he could have a full recovery, but if not…” * * * * * Now It was less than ten minutes into the funeral services when from the corner of her eye Mary-Ellen saw her son stand up, step into the aisle along the wall of the church, and quietly excuse himself. By the time she managed to turn fully he was already on his way through the doors into the vestibule. Concerned, she stood, made a small gesture to indicate she would address the situation, and quickly followed suit. When she caught up to Jared he was standing in the vestibule by himself, his back to the inner doors of the church. His hands were both over his face, and he was shaking. “Jared?” she asked, gently putting a hand on his shoulder to comfort him, “I know it’s hard. Is everything okay?” The sound of laughter caught her a bit off-guard, and she briefly pulled her hand away as Jared turned around, his face red and his eyes tearing. He certainly hadn’t been crying. “I couldn’t… I couldn’t stay,” he gasped, in between bouts of laughter he was struggling desperately to control, “I looked up at the crucifix, and my mind started wandering, and…” “And what?” her tone was stern. “All I could think of was what it would be like if one of the cables that held it up snapped. I imagined the top drooping, falling forward, and then in my head I saw Jesus being shot across the room.” Mary-Ellen’s eyes went wide. Jared continued. “Like he’d been shot out of a giant crossbow. The Crossbow O’ Christ. Ha! We could call it a Christbow!” She tried not to laugh, tried to convince herself that she didn’t want to laugh, but that didn’t stop her from clamping a hand over her mouth just in case one should escape. “You’re terrible,” she chided, still trying to suppress a smile. The doors behind them flew open, and into the vestibule stumbled Stefanie, practically tripping over her own feet. She wore a look of concern on her face, and her eyes quickly darted between both brother and mother. “Oh my God, brother Jay,” she always referred to him as ‘brother Jay’, “is everything okay?” The sudden realization of the scene hit her like a truck. “Why are you both smiling?” “I’m not smiling,” Mary-Ellen insisted, “and your brother was being incorrigible.” “I totally was not. All I said, Stef, was that wouldn’t it be funny if the Jesus came flying off like a big crossbow.” Stefanie gasped. “This is a funeral! That’s fucking terrible!” “Stefanie!” Mary-Ellen tried to reprimand her daughter, slapping her in the shoulder. “Stef, you can’t say ‘fucking’ in church!” Jared blurted out. “Jared!” now it was his turn to get hit. “Jesus will strike you down with his laser eyes!” he continued, seemingly unabated by the slap. Stefanie stood in stunned silence for a moment, before shaking her head and turning back towards the main chamber of the church. “This family is crazy,” she said, gently opening the door and stepping back inside the church. * * * * * Later The gathering after the funeral had all the traditional accoutrements. There were flowers, there were cards, there were collages of photographs to remember the deceased. There was also food to give the grieving some energy, and drinks to give them strength. A hall had been booked for the occasion, with the food having been catered. Tables had been set up and decorated, enough for all of the mourners to gather and grieve together; mourners who happened to included the Sykes/Lothrop family. “You ever think about how you want your own funeral to happen?” Stefanie was feeling a bit less reserved, courtesy of a few vodka drinks. “Like, would you want everybody you know to go, or would you keep it small? Would you want to be buried or cremated, that kind of thing.” she continued, taking a sip from her most recent beverage. Jared had been sitting slouched in his chair and was looking a lot more comfortable than he had prior to the ceremony. His tie was removed, his jacket was off, the top button of his shirt had been undone, and his sleeves had been rolled to just below the elbow. Upon hearing his sister’s question he promptly sat up, elbows on the table and smile on his face. This was a surefire sign of imminent doom. “Well, actually, I’ve given it some thought,” he said, taking a drink of his water. The fear on the face of his mother was palpable. “I’m afraid to ask,” she said, stabbing half-interested at her salad. “There are a few things I have in mind,” he continued, “but I’m not really sure which one I’d want to have people do. So, the first one…” “The first one?” his mother interrupted, “How many of these ideas do you have?” “About six, but a few are kind of variations on a theme.” “I’m going to need another drink.” “I’ll get it for you,” a cousin who happened to be passing the table offered, “what’re you having?” “Scotch and water, please,” she turned her attention back to her son, and her words were less than enthusiastic, “So what would these be?” “Well the first one is kind of awesome, but it would require dying around early summer,” contrary to his mother, Jared was very enthusiastic about these ideas of his, “I’d be cremated, and then my ashes would be given to a pyrotechnician.” His sister sounded exasperated, “What?!” “Yeah, a pyrotechnician. I’d have him load my ashes into fireworks, and then boom! I’d be blown up in the sky on the 4th of July. There’s another one I have like that, but instead of fireworks I’m mixed into gunpowder and used to shoot cannonballs into the side of a pirate ship.” The folks at the table sat and stared in stunned silence, but that didn’t seem to bother The Artist Formerly Known as King Blueberry one bit, as he kept right on ranting and raving. “Not too keen on the explosions? Okay, not a problem,” he took another sip of his water, adjusted his sleeves (which had slipped down his forearms a bit), and continued, “So I also thought it would be cool to be embalmed, and then propped up on a jetski. We fill the jetski’s tank full of gas, strap me to the seat, and then jack up the engine as high as it can go without flooding. I’m set free from the end of a pier to ride off into the sunset, until either the tank runs out of gas, I capsize, or the sharks get me, whichever comes first.” His sister’s boyfriend, James, started to laugh, but whether it was out of nerves he did not let on. “You’re so weird,” his sister said, staring at him with eyes wide. “If the jetski is too much, we could just rig me up on some rocket-powered roller-skates, build a ramp next to the Grand Canyon, and…” “Stop! Enough, enough, enough,” his mother, for the sake of everyone at the table as well as a few passers-by who happened to overhear the conversation, tried desperately to bring this conversation to a halt. “I think you’re starting to get a little morbid, and under the circumstances…” “But I haven’t even told you the best ones!” he protested. “Like about how I think it’d be awesome to be cremated and mixed with instant pudding, then fed to my friends with whipped cream and mint.” “What the fuck,” his sister whispered, somewhat flabbergasted. “But the best one,” whether he was immune to their pleas or he just didn’t care, Jared would not shut up, “the best one is what I like to call the Human Piñata.” Facepalms, ahoy! “So we strip me of all internal organs and bones, and then let my skin dry so it becomes brittle. Then, my body is filled with delicious candies, and sewn shut.” James and Stefanie exchanged a quick nod, and both stood up from the table. This did nto deter Jared in the least. “Then, we coat my body with papier-mâché, and allow it to dry. Once everything has dried we raise me up into a tree, and suspend me by ropes.” His mother and stepfather soon followed, leaving Jared at the table with a pair of cousins he didn’t recognize. “All of my loved ones are given sticks, and they’d use those sticks to smash my lifeless body, until I explode in a shower of delicious treats,” he paused, finishing his glass of water, “it’d be pretty frickin’ amazing, huh?” They said nothing, but looked around awkwardly before standing up and walking away, leaving Jared alone by himself at the table.
