A distinct lack of clarity

18 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-18 If we’re keeping score then it’s 1-0 to the bad guys, courtesy of a particularly devious bomb that was wrapped in nails and concealed in a bag. The blast eventually took out the entire police station in Basra, Iraq, and so it came as no surprise to onlookers that it took me with it, via the lovely method of a couple of nails going straight through my skull. It also took out another twelve people, so I suppose you could call it 13-0. Though Shanty Jones survived, so maybe it’s 13-1. We’re on the comeback trail. *** "Jah! You have taken one step on ze path of glory zat I trod last year!" Despite Shanty’s spectacular lack of enthusiasm for his company, Doctor Curiosity had come to visit him again. "I don’t want ye here," said Jones, his accent still betraying his Welsh roots. "I don’t even know how you got in here in the first place." "Vhat? You need a manager, clearly!" "I thought you were going to open an orphanage for potential future wrestlers? So go off and do that and leave me alone. I don’t know if your brother mentioned it to you, but I’m not exactly a people person." "Ah, ze orphanage. It vas going to be a resounding success, until I found out zat all wrestlers vith tragically murdered parents are terrible in ze ring! Ah, I lost thousands building zat place!" "I’m really not interested. Now leave." "Ah, my dear Shan. Can I call you Shan?" "No." Despite the negativity from Jones, the Doctor smiled. "Vell, Shan, perhaps you should reflect on exactly where the money for this hotel room is coming from?" "I’ll go and pack my clothes then. I was doing this as a favour to Paul, and nothing more." "Bah, you don’t vant to listen to Eegor!" Back in prison, Shanty Jones had grown to become friends with Paul Goodman, the brother and former assistant of Doctor Curiosity. He had been jailed for the murder of the good doctor’s wife, but once locked up he had grown in stature and become a leading figure in the many nefarious schemes that will inevitably occur when you incarcerate a few hundred criminals together. Shanty, in prison for a series of minor assaults, had relished the chance to work with someone like Paul, though it was as much for the chance to be left alone as anything else. Talking to other people, or being subjected to their points of view, was not something that ranked high on Shanty Jones’s list of priorities. "Look, Chris, I’m…" "Do not call me by zat name!" yelled Curiosity. "Look, whoever, I’m not interested in what you’re selling, and I’m not interested in doing anything for you. Paul gave me a chance to do a few things for him and I got rewarded into the bargain. Where I come from you pay people back the same way they treated you. So I’m honouring what I promised Paul, but I’m fucked if I’m going to have you talk your shit at me all the time. Now get out of this hotel room before I snap you in half, no matter who your brother is." The whole tirade was spoken without ever raising his voice beyond its normal timbre. "So, you’re playing hard to get, eh?" said the wily doctor. "No more warnings," purred Shanty as he rose from his couch and walked towards the winner of GTT4. The difference in height was not immense, Shanty only standing one inch taller than the doctor at six foot two, but the difference in muscle mass and demeanour would have been enough to pick them out of a line-up from a good mile away. Curiosity quickly backed off, and waving his hands as if to calm the other man down, he moved to the doorway. "So now’s not a good time. Sure, zis is fine, I vill come back when you’re not feeling so up tight. You’ve got another match to prepare for, you know?" The doctor closed the door behind him and Shanty Jones was left alone with his thoughts. *** Of course, it wasn’t actually another match that Shanty had to prepare for; it was his first of the tournament. The match in round one had never come to pass, with Belial Kahn absent from the arena. Shanty had turned up and was waiting there, exercising and keeping his pulse rate up, but after the obligatory wait in the ring of three minutes, the match was forfeit and Shanty was progressing through to round two on a bye. In a way he’d been happy, but in another disappointed. The nerves that accompanied his first match of the tournament were eased by the realisation that he would be fine for another week, or at least as long as it took for the wounds to heal enough to allow the competitors a second go at it. He’d not had the chance to prove himself though, and start himself on the road to a sort of recovery. Jobs weren’t always easy to come by for those convicted and jailed for violent offences, but even the biggest wrestling companies in the world had shown time and time again that they were willing to overlook a murky past if the ‘asset’ it came with was worth it. Shanty was also very interested in seeing how his own personal style would work out against the best that PTC had to offer. He’d not had much in the way of formal wrestling training, though Paul Goodman had shown him a few amateur moves back in prison. No, the main basis of Shanty’s offence would be based on striking and roughneck tactics learned in his army training and in hundreds of fights across bars in three different countries. There was a definite potential for him to end up on the wrong end of a true stretching, but those who had met Shanty would somehow doubt it. He had a certain feeling about him, almost like an aura or something, and it would take one hell of an effort to have him down on the mat, either to submit or be pinned. Not that I believe in that stuff anyway. *** But what do I believe in? My name is Steven Atkins and I’ve never been what you’d call a God fearing man. I’ve never been religious at all, which is something I found in common with Shanty Jones. One of the few things actually, since we were never really alike. So, without any belief in pretty much anything outside of what I could see or feel, it came as something of a surprise to me to find myself still here after I died. Of course, ‘here’ is somewhere else entirely, and I’ve genuinely got no idea what’s going on, but I still have a consciousness, even though they lay what was left of my body to rest many months ago. So I stay here in the darkness, feeling no sensations other than that of recollection. It’s not just my own life that I can recollect though, for I can see back to the past of Shanty Jones. I don’t know why we’re linked like this, as other than having served in the army together, there was no great bond between us. At least not one any stronger than that I felt with everyone else in my unit. I feel instead like I’m here to bear witness to something, to see Shanty Jones fulfil his destiny and no longer waste his potential. I’ve seen how Shanty grew up, and with precious few gaps have seen him grow up to where he is now. The images of the past come into my brain… well, not my actual brain as that is decomposing somewhere in the ground, but what can I call it? My brain housed my thoughts for so many years that I’ve become set in my ways. I was attached to it, you might say. I know Shanty is important, and I know that I have to watch until this adventure is over. I don’t know why I used that word though, ‘adventure’. For some reason I really wanted to say… I wanted to say, ‘charade’. *** "Mister Jones isn’t accepting calls right now," said the receptionist at the desk of the hotel. "Vhat? He vill take one from me!" said Doctor Curiosity, on the other end of the phone. "He said not to be disturbed." "Jah! But zis is an emergency!" "Who is this calling him?" "It is Doctor Curiosity, his manager!" "Ah," said the receptionist, with a tone of finality. "What do you mean, ‘ah’?" "I mean that Mister Jones left specific instructions that if you were to call that we were to inform you that he is not accepting calls, but especially not taking them from you." "Tell him zat I have a message from my brother!" said Curiosity, happy that he’d thought of a reason why Shanty might genuinely want to talk to him. "I’m afraid that he told us you’d say that." "Curses!" said Curiosity. "Vell, he may have von zis round, but I vill catch up to him somevhere. He von’t be able to avoid me all the way up until his next match, and vhen I find him he VILL listen to me! I’m ze reigning champion, not him! Vhen you hear ze vord ‘Doctor’, you automatically tzink of ‘Curiosity’… or sometimes Nurse… but mostly Curiosity! Vhen you hear ze vord ‘Shanty’, vhat do you think? Not ‘Jones’, zat’s for sure! Maybe you tzink of seafaring songs, but not of some stupid convict who’s not even fit to lace my boots! Are you listening to me? Are you? Hello? Are you impersonating a dial tone?" *** Shanty Jones sat on his couch in his hotel room. He’d have to leave it at some point before his match, but for the moment he was content to just sit there and mull things over. His life was not taking a course that was familiar or expected, but it was a ride that he had to take. He was thinking… he was… for… for some reason it’s masked from me. I don’t know why, but he was just sitting there, pondering on the past, and something he had to do in the future. It wasn’t the match, but it was a confrontation. I can’t see any more of it. My gift, if you will call it that, is thoroughly out of my hands. Sometimes I just can’t see past what someone might want to be kept hidden. I don’t understand, as I’ve seen some pretty messed up things in the past of Shanty, but this one… I just don’t know, but I do have the feeling that it was important. *** I won’t be at the arena for GTT5, but I’ll know what happens. I won’t be in the crowd, but I’ll feel the excitement as Damien Ryan and Shanty Jones enter the ring. I won’t be on the edge of my seat as one or the other goes for the first pinfall, or hits a high impact move, but I’ll see it all. It doesn’t end here.
