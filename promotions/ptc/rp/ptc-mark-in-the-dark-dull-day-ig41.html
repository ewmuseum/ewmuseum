IG41 - Mark in the dark - The moments that make up a dull day.

12 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
When I wake up, it is atop of a mattress no thicker than an industrial strength piece of cardboard. My heartbeat and pace of breaths have increased rapidly since emerging from my not so peaceful slumber and I find myself drenched in sweat. Upon examining my attire and that of my "roommate's" -- both of us in matching hot-pink jumpsuits with our own exclusive 5 digit numbers printed across the shoulders in sleeves -- I am quick to realize that I'm confined to a prison, jail, or holding facility of some sort. "Whoa, Rob Van Winkle." The roomie says as he removes the toothbrush from his mouth, revealing a face full of foamy Colgate. I'm too busy securing my head on my shoulders amidst the throbbing pain to ask him what the fuck he's talking about, and frankly even if I weren't preoccupied I still wouldn't care. "Do you have any Tylenol?" I ask him, and despite the two of us being the only people in the entire fucking room – I mean, tent – this douchebag doesn't think I'm talking to him. He lifts his head from the stainless steel sink, peering into the stainless steel mirror as he swishes the water inside his mouth. Out of the corner of his eye, he catches me staring at him through wide eyes. He spits. "What's up?" "Fucking aspirin." I say and shove myself off of the top bunk and onto the dirt floor below. At this point, I don't care about 'why' nor 'how' I came to this facility. I'm even less concerned, once catching a glimpse of myself in the unbreakable mirror, about why I look so haggard. Despite all of the bruises and gaping wounds decorating my body, the only pressing need is for relief of this head-splitting migraine. He turns and looks at me stupidly as I rub my face through waking and it's almost as if he ignores the demand. "What's your name?" he asks me, rinsing his toothbrush under water then tapping it dry on the sinks rim. "What day is today?" I reply. He thinks for a moment. The fucking tool must have been in here for a while. "Tuesday." "Nah, too fagoty. Call me Friday." I shove my way to the sink, nudging him off to the side rather forcefully. I catch him sneering out of the corner of the metal frame and if my reflection were made of glass I'd have shattered it and lunged a shard in his neck by now. Unfortunately, jails take precautions for those kinds of incidents. The water flows from the sink in a semi-constant stream with a tint of brown, hacking up inconsistent temperatures. A little splish splash around my face to prevent myself from dry-heaving, but all I need is a fucking aspirin. "Is it safe to assume that you couldn't help me get rid of a headache?" "Like, drugs?" I stare at him staring back through crooked eyes, looking like the fucking idiot he is. "Yes. Like, drugs." "Nah, bro. Gotta go to the infirmary for that shit." Even aspirin? "Even aspirin." Fuck being incarcerated. I'm not a child; I want my blood-thinners. I start to stomp outward in hopes of finding this infirmary, but my heavy steps are a quick reminder of how sudden movements can be very painful. The moment my eyes close as I attempt to gather myself, the flap of the tent parts, allowing the sun to creep in and paint my eyelids. I pry them open, allowing myself a moment to adjust to the sudden abundance of light, before I can finally make out the large man standing in the tan garb of an corrections officer. "Mark Grim?" I turn around to see if the man is speaking to my roommate, only he still contains the same dumbfounded expression on his face when I asked him for Tylenol. This guy has no fucking clue. "Are you Mark Grim?" The guard asks me. I don't think so. "I guess so." "You've got a visitor." "Alright, can I go get something for a headache, real quick?" Apparently, it's common courtesy to treat people in pink jumpsuits like insignificant shits, as if the embarrassing attire isn't enough. Even your fellow inmates regard each other within an intricate hierarchy that I will never understand. Throughout all of the incidents and battle lines, it's really nothing more than a high school, only here everyone is a fucking bully. The guard disappears out the flap, allowing my pupils to open up a bit in the welcomed darkness. God intends to piss on me with his sunshine. All it serves to do is aide my migraine into a pulsating state, paced awkwardly with the beat of my heart. I stumble out the tent in search of relief and not my visitor, only once I'm outside I find that the asshole in tan waits for me, positioned as if he is meaning to reflect the sun off of his badge and into my face. My head is just about ready to explode. Behind my back, the roomie calls out "It's peeping hot out there, bro. I'd grab some shoes if I were you." Who knows what the fuck he's talking about; I ignore him. So I'm following the guard as he guides me through the facility, unintentionally providing me with the grand tour – there are military tents contained by large razor-wire fences and watchtowers running alongside, the inhabitants dressed in the same pink jumpsuit as me. They all manage to keep themselves busy throw card games, exercise, and the community television set-up in the recreation tent located in the center of the compound. Each of them with their exclusive reason for rehabilitation and their exclusive identification numbers. The funny thing is, I bet that each of them knows exactly why they are here. Leading me through a set of sliding glass doors, watched over by two men holding assault rifles, the guard absolves me of the relentless sun outside and delivers me to the sweet haven of circulated air. "Through there." The guard points, ordering me through a long corridor with large metal panels lining the walls, at the end of it a set of metal doors. "Room eight." Warily, I stumble through the scanning mechanism to no alarm and then push my way through. Again, I find myself in a long corridor, only this time the far wall is lined with a series of doors and their accompanied number hanging over the frame. I make my way to number eight and enter. The door automatically locks upon latching shut behind me, leaving me trapped before a wall of bulletproof glass and a telephone receiver. A portly man walks through the door on the other side, though it is impossible to hear anything aside from my own breathing – my own splitting headache. He is dressed in a light blue polyester suit that is bursting at the seams and probably purchased during a time it actually fit him. Despite his snazzy appearance, he comes across as a bumbling fool. Sliding a palm through his greased hair, he takes a few deep breathes before finally lifting the receiver on his side to his ear. He nods at me, and I do the same. "Yes?" "Alright, Mark, we might be in a bind but I think we can run with this-" I interrupt him and ask him the pressing question, "Who the fuck are you?" Only I find it answered before the sentence can be finished. Suddenly, details of the past week return to me in a flood of memories. Not everything, but the thick of it all – not including why I'm here. "Are you dicking me, Mark? Because we can't afford to be dicking around." his lo-fi voice echoes out of the receiver. "I'm sorry, Uncle Pappy." "You fucked up, Mark. You fucked up big. But I think we'll be able to work with what we've got." The ring-leader stands before me a disappointed yet hopeful man. Despite it all, I still ask him if he has any aspirin to spare. He says, "Not on me." Funny thing; I still don't know why the fuck I'm inside here. INTERMISSION I first met Uncle Pappy after I murdered his drug-addled chimpanzee in the middle of a circus ring. This was after his drug-addled ostrich collapsed and died due to "heart-complications." Most people would expect the owner of an exotic animal to be exceptionally upset upon seeing his property murdered, but not Pappy. Judging by the amount of empty, blood-stained cages he had in the backstage area of the carnival, Pappy expected casualties amongst his "pets." To say that Uncle Pappy was 'exceptionally upset' would be a lie. To say that Uncle Pappy was 'exceptionally thrilled' would be an understatement. After the incident, he immediately took down my contact information – as vague as it is – and in less than a day he'd already contacted me about a business plan. I could see it in his eyes; every time he looked over me I could feel his impressions as envious, exploitative. He'd never say it out loud, but deep down he knew that I'd be his meal ticket. What came next was the daunting task of convincing my "parents" what's right for me. And by 'me,' I mean 'Pappy.' "You wanna take 'im where?" Father asked from the opposite side of the kitchen, on the opposite side of the breakfast table. The way everyone was positioned, it was like a house divided. The front lines were drawn and the sides had been chosen very clearly. On one side, my mother, father, and sister all sat with their cereal spoons suspended just before their faces. It was as if each of them was caught off of their guard in mid-bite. On the other side are Uncle Pappy and I – my future guardian with the grin of a Cheshire cat as he explained to my parents that there really was nothing they could do about the events to transpire. "Michigan." Pappy replies to my father, his hands holding each other politely behind his back. My father looks away, visibly upset with what he's hearing. "No no no. He's coming to the mines. With me." Apparently, my father had his retirement all worked out. If it were up to him, I'd start going with him to work every night at about 10 pm and work straight through until Noon the next day. We'd work together for a couple of months, him working to familiarize me with all of his old routines and responsibilities until he was finally able to fade away, collecting his pension. Of course, pension never pays enough. Even with a union contract, my father would never make enough scratch to support his family's eccentric habits without working. Which is where my inherited life of labor came into factor. Pension, plus the same amount of pay he'd get if he'd continue working, would work out for a pretty healthy living situation. But Uncle Pappy had to come and fuck that up for my father. "You're not taking my boy to some ass-backward boondock to do no prairie dancin'. No sir-ee." "Mr. Grim, I assure you that your boy will be in the safest of hands and in no way will he be partaking in these ass-backward, boondock prarie dances. You have my word." "Yer gonna grease 'im up and make i'm scrap with other leotard-wearin' fags!" Dad snaps back at the ring-leader, who takes a moment to readjust his slicked hair under the top-hat. "Most people refer to it commonly as professional wrestling, and if I may--" "Nothin' 'feshonal about dressin' like a fag for the whole world, Mister." My father's quick interruption incited only the slighted chuckle from Uncle Pappy. "—if I may, sir, your son is extremely talented. Why, he even came out runner-up in a local open invitation just last week. And might I say that everyone was extremely impressed with the display he put on." "Last week?" Father turned from Uncle Pappy and now his gaze fell squarely upon me. "That why you ain't show up to the mines at all? To go dick around with a coupl'a other homos in yer pink little outfits?!" His face grew red. He was still angry with me for not showing up, but was never able to properly express his anger as to why. There were still some liberties that I'd continue to take that he'd never understand. Apparently, the son I was could be different in comparison to the son I am. So I shrug at him. "I had fun at the time." I said, and return to my place at Pappy's side. "The fact of the matter is this, Mr. and Mrs. Grim: your boy is very talented. He fared very well against some very revered opposition in this business, and he only needs one more vic--" "Daddy, that's the man I was telling you about, from the fair." Rissa not-so-nonchalantly whispered into father's ear. "That's the man with the drug monkey." My sister's words and Pappy's hairline fraction-of-a-smile helped my father draw a conclusion asto what Pappy's intentions were. His lips pursed, facial expression growing quite sour, and he stood angrily from his chair. Vindictive finger-pointing was soon abundant. "I know what you're tryin'ta pull here, and it ain't gon work. Ya hear me? Ya ain't takin' the boy to no god damn wrestle show." "Sir, I don't think you understand--" "You ain't takin' him." "Oh, I know I ain't taking him. Because, you see, this 'boy' isn't a boy. He's almost a grown man, right?" Pappy leaned back to ruffle my hair, still looking my father in the eye. "I'm sure you know that, though. It's written all over his birth certificate. You do know how old Mark is, right Mr. Grim?" The way Pappy said that, it was almost as if he were challenging my father. "Ye-yeah." Dad stuttered. "He's 'bout twenty-two." "Twenty-Two years old. And such a lucky twenty-two year old man, wouldn't you say?" Pappy's hand has moved to my shoulder and he now stood to my side, holding me as if I were on display to my family. "He's been through so much in his short-time on this earth. You see, your daughter there told me all about Mark's sickness. And how he didn't leave the hospital for over seven years. Truly is a shame." Pappy's eyes stayed locked on my fathers while he spoke, further piercing into the shady details of what he had planned. He knew he was being tested by the stranger, the worry on my mother's face was more than evident. But they'd already come this far, and weren't about to let some carny ruin their perfect plan. "The boy don't have no choice. He stays." And then Uncle Pappy's face grew sour, and he immediately spun on his heels to head out the front door. "It's too bad you feel this way, Mr. Grim. I don't want to complicate things anymore for your family than I already have. However, I think we can both agree that we don't need to get the authorities involved." Upon finishing his sentence, Pappy turns around, once again peering into my father's eyes with an intensity only the two understood. "We both know that you raised yourself a smart boy." Pappy smiles. "You'll only hurt yourself to rob him of this opportunity." Pappy's thin smile grew wide, showcasing the tobacco stains across the face of his teeth. The silence taking place after his last word would linger for a few moments as my father pondered over what little options he had. Slowly, surely, his head lowers in a defeated fashion, and Pappy turned on his heels towards the outdoors. "Gather your necessities, Mark. We're going to Michigan." I went to follow Pappy out the door, deciding that everything I need could be boughten in an airport gift-shop, only father called out to us. "This ain't gonna work. You hear me? Someone's gonna find out, and it'll all be over. You'll ruin it for the rest of us." As we walked out the door, me leaving my family behind in the wake of the success I'll find in Michigan, Uncle Pappy replied to my father. "I'll take my chances." On the car ride to the airport, I asked Pappy "Why Michigan?" "Because we've got a meeting with some very important people. Officials of a large organization that have big plans in store for you, son. And after that, you're going to fight a couple of big names in the Infinite Gauntlet." I felt insignificant in the grand scheme of what Pappy had in mind, which is probably what he intended. "I'm going to lose, aren't I?" I asked him. "Yes, Mark, you are. But that's the whole idea."
