Standing In The Darkness

20 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Primetime Central presents: GTT6! Title: Standing In The Darkness Featuring: Saij Date: 20th Oct 2006 Location: Western Territorial Federation Gymasium/Offices Standing in the darkness Waiting for the light The smell of pure adrenaline burning in the night Random blinding flashes Aiming at the stage Intro tape begins to roll Igniting sonic rage - Dream Theater, "Six Degrees Of Inner Turbulence – The Test That Stumped Them All" -~-~- Every night it was the same. "Saij… Saij… Saij…" They chanted his name as he waited in the darkness. Every night without fail, they were there. Every night, since that fateful day, they were there. They loved him. Adored him. Plagued him. "Saij… Saij… Saij…" Kicking at white sheets as sheets of white light kicked on. "Saij! Saij! Saij!" The heat and smell of nonexistent pyrotechnics. "Saij! Saij! Saij!" Music blasting loudly over the din, silent to reality. "SAIJ! SAIJ! SAIJ!" Blinded by illusion, he stepped forward. There they were, on all sides. He could see them. They couldn’t see him back—they weren’t real. None of it was real. But it didn’t matter to him. It was like he was home again. To fight again. Live again. "SAIJ! SAIJ! SAIJ!" Struggle. Sweat. Strike. Win. Lose. Bleed. It was all the same. All that mattered was just being there. Just being. Yet he wasn’t there at all. This was his torture. His mind teased and cackled gleefully at him with images and echoes of the past. There was no hope of escape. He was reminded of that which he had lost; that which had been taken from him. Every night it was the same. Every night it was the same. Every night it was the same. And it was slowly killing him. -~-~-"Ladies and gentlemen, the next match is scheduled for one fall and a time limit of fifteen minutes. Introducing first…" The shoddy PA system screeched ear-piercing waves of feedback, reverberating off the gymnasium walls. The few dozen people who even bothered to shell out the ten bucks for admittance became even less pleased with a show they were already less than pleased with. They came to see quality wrestling and quality wrestlers—that was what the Western Territorial Federation posters all over town had promised, anyway. The card had so far consisted of: A thirty-second bout between a paraplegic and a narcoleptic that ended in a draw, when both men tumbled over the top rope to the outside and were unable to get back in the ring before the referee’s ten-count (but the paraplegic made an effort, God bless him); A shoddy contest between an anorexic who thought he was a power wrestler, and a morbidly obese man who thought he was a cruiserweight; A highly-publicized bra and panties match between two female wrestlers who didn’t quite turn out to be female after all; And, the brightest spot of the night thus far—a fight between a chicken and a midget. The midget, not having eaten anything in days due to his piss-poor pay, was disqualified after relieving the unfortunate fowl of its head with one decisive chomp. Crappy wrestling in a crappy gym with a crappy sound system so the crappy audience couldn’t even hear the names of the crappy wrestlers. It was par for the sub-par course. And it was just as well, because the fans never seemed to recognize the name of the next wrestler whenever he was introduced… "And his opponent, weighing in at 220 pounds… from East Lansing, Michigan… Scott Jeffries!" A tall young man walked out into the gym, down a makeshift ramp thrown together from two folded cafeteria tables. The surface was slick, and he had to watch his step to make sure his feet didn’t slip out and fly up from underneath his ass. Forty-odd people remained in their seats, treating his entrance with cold indifference. Oh, if only they had realized who he was. If only they could have seen underneath his thick stubble and the unkempt mop of dark brown growing ever longer on his head. Or perhaps if his skin wasn’t so pallid, his face so wan; maybe they couldn’t see past the dark bags puffing just below his lifeless eyes. Or maybe if they had basic cable. Maybe then they might have recognized him. They might have recognized the XUW Hall-of-Famer, the former PTC Global Champion. They might have recognized Saij. Instead, the man once known as the Master of the Huzzah didn’t look much like a master of anything. A cursory glance showed he was a mere shadow of his former self: gone was his trademark goofy grin; he seemed to have lost the bounce in his step as well; he certainly had lost some muscle since he was last seen in XUW; Saij was a wreck. A broken-down jalopy of yesteryear. He looked like a junkie addicted to some bad crack. He climbed into the ring with his opponent, a middle-aged nobody who probably had a drinking habit to fund. Mr. Nobody. Joe Average. Whatever. The match was nothing special. They went through the motions. Saij didn’t even pull anything out from his arsenal of high-flying maneuvers that helped him earn his fame throughout the world. A hip toss here, a suplex there, throw in a headlock for good measure. The fans didn’t care—if you could call them "fans" anyway. XUW fans would have been appalled at the outcome: Mr. Nobody hit Saij with a DDT that a blind man could see coming a mile away, and won after the ref made a less-than-enthusiastic three-count. The ref raised Joe Average’s hand in victory. A few people clapped. Saij simply got up from the ground, dusted himself off, and trudged heavily out of the gym. Another night in the ring, done. He’d gotten his fix. Unfulfilling as it was, it was all that mattered. He just wanted to compete. -~-~- "Here’s yer take for the week. Now get outta here, you and yer damn kid." Ralph Woodson’s office was dank and rank, Saij thought to himself. He liked to rhyme words—doing so reminded him of happier, care-free times. But his observation was nonetheless astute: Woodson’s office was dimly-lit, the only illumination coming from a shaded lamp dangling from the ceiling. The air was foul, smelling of whiskey and cigars. Cheap ones. The Western Territorial Federation wasn’t exactly a booming business. Woodson’s fat fist thrust an envelope stuffed with bills into Saij’s hand. Saij began counting, as the pitter-patter of little feet came running up to him. "Dada!" It was Leanne, Saij’s daughter. She was nearly one and a half now, and had all but mastered the art of running. She ran just about everywhere she went, in fact. Keeping up with her was a task. "Hello sweetheart," Saij muttered without looking down as Leanne clung to her father’s leg. "Dada wan’ play?" Leanne asked, tugging at his pants leg. "Not now, Leanne," Saij replied with a frown. He looked up at Woodson, who had taken a seat at his desk and was leafing through a copy of The Daily Racing Form. His view of Saij was obscured, but he’d been in the indy wrestling business long enough to know when someone was glaring angrily at him. "What’s the matter?" he grunted without even looking. "This isn’t the amount we agreed on," said Saij. "It’s not even half of it!" "Yeah? So what?" "‘So what?’ You’re kidding me, right? We made an agreement, and I held up my end of the deal. You should farkin’ hold up yours." Woodson remained silent and still for a moment. Then, he began to laugh. "Ha… haha… hahaha!" he guffawed. "Hold up my end of the deal, huh? Oh, look at Mister Former Famous Superstar, griping about his payday! Crying because he’s not making thousands of dollars a week! Hey, tough shit. You get what you get and no more. Consider the rest of the money I didn’t give you payment for babysitting that little brat of yours." Saij’s fist clenched tightly at his side. He very much wanted to punch Ralph Woodson’s lights out right then and there—not only for bilking him in his pay, or for insulting his daughter, but also for refusing to advertise his name and draw a bigger crowd, and for telling him to job to Mr. Nobody or else he’d be back on the unemployment line. It was almost too much to take. He wanted to strike back. But he couldn’t. None of the other major world-level federations would take him. They would have loved for the chance to have Saij on their rosters, but no federation owner was willing to take on a superstar who insisted on bringing his child backstage with him to every show. They saw what had happened in XUW—Leanne had nearly been seriously injured, even killed, in a backstage brawl involving her father. She had been kidnapped by another wrestler, who did it just to get under Saij’s skin. Having Leanne backstage was a liability no fed was willing to take on; so, Saij had to search through the indy feds to find a job. Any job. As long as he could just wrestle, and keep Leanne with him, he would do anything. Even put over Joe Average in a run-down gymnasium with a few dozen complacent patrons who had no idea who he was, for a place run by a guy who looked like a walrus in a cheap suit. So he couldn’t strike back, and risk losing his weekly wrestling fix. He needed his fix. It wasn’t as if he really needed the money anyway—he’d accumulated enough during his tenure in XUW to get by for a good long while. Plus, he was still getting paid the remainder of the salary on his contract after he’d been told he wasn’t welcome anymore. He just couldn’t sit around and do nothing, not with the dreams that haunted him every night. "Fine," Saij mumbled with a tone of defeat. He reached down and scooped Leanne up into his arms. As he turned around, Woodson’s gruff voice came once more: "By the way, you’ll probably be opening next week’s show against Fat Freddy The Flyer. Be sure and put him over too, would ya? Just let him sit on you or something. Gwahahaha!" It was becoming increasingly difficult not to punch that man’s fucking lights out. -~-~- Saij sighed heavily as he stepped out of Woodson’s office. "Well baby girl," he said quietly to his daughter, "looks like Daddy isn’t going to be winning for a while." "Tuff shit!" Leanne replied with a giggle. Damnit! Saij thought to himself. Now he’s got my daughter saying curse words! Fark, this place sucks. But at least I’ve got a place to wrestle, that’ll actually let me keep Leanne with me. I guess it could be worse. But it sure could be better. At that moment, his cell phone started vibrating in his pocket. He jumped. "GAH!" Saij shrieked. "What the fark was that!? There’s a poltergeist in my pants! Crap, and I’m allergic to ghosts! NOOOOO!" He ran aimlessly around in circles for a moment, as Leanne laughed and clapped gleefully. A casual onlooker might have guessed Saij was putting on an act to entertain his daughter. The reality was that he was just a moron, often at completely inappropriate times. It might have just been his own way of coping with what was slowly becoming a rather miserable life, but nobody really knew for sure. Eventually he stopped, reached slowly into his pocket, and procured the vibrating phone. "Oh, it’s just this," he said. "Holy fark, nobody ever calls me anymore… I totally forgot I still had this thing." He flipped open the phone. "Hello?" "Hello, Mr. Jeffries? This is Northwest Passage International Travel Agency. We’re calling to inform you that the time of your flight to Singapore has been changed to…" "Whoa, wait a second," Saij interrupted. "Did you say… ‘Singapore?’" "Yes sir." "But, like, I’m not flying anywhere… especially not to Singapore. I never even booked a flight! What the fark are you even talking about!?" "Are you certain, Mr. Jeffries? The flight has been booked for weeks. Let me check the records…" Saij scratched his head and gave Leanne a confused glance. Singapore? What the hell was going on? "Okay… yes, the flight was booked three weeks ago by your company, Xtreme Ultimo Wrestling." "Huh?" Saij’s mouth nearly hit the floor. "XUW? That’s not possible. I don’t compete there anymore. I guess technically I’m still on the payroll until I get the rest of my salary, but still…" "Well, according to the information we’ve been given, the flight was booked specifically for you." "That doesn’t make any sense. Why is my name on a flight to Singapore that I didn’t even book? How’d you even get my number, anyway? Ugh, never mind. Look, let me call you back in a few minutes while I sort things out. I’m very confused right now… not that being confused is anything new for me." "Very well then, sir." The travel agent provided Saij with a phone number so he could call back, and then hung up. Saij rubbed his scraggly chin for a moment, pondering what to do next. Truthfully, he already knew—but he really didn’t want to do it. He had to get in touch with XUW co-commissioner Derick Jackson, the man who effectively ended Saij’s contract with his home federation. He was the only one who knew what was going on. After taking a deep breath to calm himself, Saij made the call. He half-hoped that DJ wouldn’t pick up. Of course, he did. "Hello?" For a moment Saij didn’t know what to say. Memories came flooding back to him all in an instant: how he and Robert Anderson had tussled backstage and knocked into a pile of crates that fell over and came within inches of crushing Leanne; how DJ subsequently called him into his office and ended his association with XUW; how angry Saij was, how betrayed he felt, and the bitter taste that had been left in his mouth. There was really only one thing he could say at that point. "Uh, hey there DJ." The other end of the line was silent for a moment. "Hello, Saij." Another moment passed. Neither of them said anything. Finally… "What can I do for you?" At least he’s not being hostile, Saij thought. "Well," he said, "I was wondering if you could clear something up for me. I just got a call from some travel agency, saying that XUW had booked a flight for me to Singapore. You know anything about that?" Saij could have sworn he heard a gasp, followed by a low muttering of "shit." Otherwise, there was nothing on the other end of the line but dead air. Finally, Saij heard DJ draw his breath and let out a long sigh. "Alright look, I’ll level with you. We booked that flight because you’ve been entered into GTT6. Not as a free agent, mind you. As a member of XUW." "…" This absolutely floored Saij. GTT6? XUW? There was no way it was possible, not after the way he'd been unceremoniously booted out of his home federation. It had to be some kind of sick joke. "You’re kidding me. You’re farkin’ kidding me, DJ. There’s no way I could possibly be… fark, I didn’t know GTT6 was even happening. You’d better not be messing with me DJ, because this isn’t funny." "No, it’s not funny, and I’m serious. Our relationship with PTC has become stagnant in recent months, and they insisted that you compete in this year’s tournament as a former Global Champion. Naturally I thought that was out of the question." "Yeah, because you think me and my kid are liabilities instead of human beings," Saij interjected angrily. "Please Saij, we’ve discussed that enough," DJ took a firmer tone. "Let me finish. Anyway, I tried to tell them that you weren’t competing in XUW anymore, but they knew you were still under contract and… well, details aside, the long and short of it is that I told them you were going to compete, and made all the travel arrangements." Something wasn’t right about that, and Saij knew it. "Then why the fark didn’t you tell me?" There was another silent pause. Saij was getting sick of those silent pauses. "Saij, when we parted ways, we parted ways for good. I wasn’t about to have you representing XUW in the most prestigious wrestling tournament in the industry. So I…" "You just plain didn’t tell me about it, which would have meant I’d have missed my flight and the tournament," Saij finished. He was seething. "That’s just great, DJ. It’s bad enough that I’ve been black-listed from every decent federation on the planet because of you, but then you try and tarnish my reputation even more by signing me up for GTT6 and putting the blame on me when I no-showed?" "It was the best thing to do for XUW. How would it look if you competed for us, and maybe even won GTT6 after we’d let you go?" "I don’t farkin’ care how it would look!" Saij shouted into his phone. "It’s not even about that! It’s about the fact that I can’t even wrestle in a decent fed anymore! I’ve gone from place to place and they all say the same thing: ‘Sorry, but if you’re gonna keep your kid around here all the time, we can’t let you work with us.’ Do you know what that’s done to me, DJ!? Do you have any… FUCKING… IDEA!?" "Saij…" "I need to wrestle, DJ. I… need… to wrestle. I need it so badly that I can’t even sleep at night, because I keep having dreams about when I was in XUW, stepping out into a packed arena, with thousands of fans cheering for me. It’s driving me crazy, every damn night, seeing what I can’t have anymore because of you. I need to wrestle so badly, that I’ve taken this shitty job working for some shitty fed and jobbing to guys twice as old as me with about half the talent! And now you’re telling me I had an invitation to GTT6, but you had no intention of ever letting me know!? Jesus Christ DJ, I’m beginning to think you’d rather I just roll over and die!" "Saij, just stop and think for a second. Don’t you realize that if you would just leave your daughter in someone else’s care, those problems would all go away?" Saij clutched Leanne closer to him at that suggestion. "FARK NO!" he shouted again. "I’m not leaving her, not even for a minute! Most other guys in the business can stay on the road and not see their families most of the year, but not me! I’m gonna be with my daughter every minute! I’m gonna raise her, and I’m gonna watch her grow up! And I’ll be damned if anyone else is gonna take her from me!" "For God’s sake, just let your parents take care of her while you’re gone, or SOMETHING…" "NO! Now look, I’m done talking with you. I know everything I need to know now. I’m getting on that plane, I’m gonna compete in GTT6, and when I win, I’m gonna shove it right in your farkin’ face. I may be competing as an XUW wrestler, but believe me… I’m not fighting for XUW. I’m fighting for myself. Screw you, and screw XUW. Goodbye." With that, Saij definitively snapped his phone shut. He nearly smashed it against the wall in anger, but caught himself just at the last second. "Unbelievable." Saij just stood, staring blankly into the distance. He didn’t know what to think of it all. The federation he once loved, once proudly represented, had stabbed him in the back. DJ was trying to keep him out of GTT6—something he needed to do. It probably would have been his final taste of quality competition… of real competition. Not some cheap farce that had him taking a dive for some no-name schmuck. Soon, it hit him. He was going to compete in GTT6. He was going to compete in GTT6! Finally, he was going to get a real fix—he was going to walk into a real arena, with real lights, real fans, and real world-class competitors. It was going to be his final huzzah, before descending back into a lifetime of anonymous inadequacy. "Fark, we’d better back our bags," Saij said suddenly to Leanne. "We’re going to Singapore!" Saij started down the hall… then he stopped. "Actually, there’s one more thing Daddy has to do." -~-~- The door to Ralph Woodson’s office swung open. He looked down from his paper, and saw Saij marching right up to his desk. "You again? I thought I told ya to—" POW! Saij threw a right hook into Woodson’s face, causing him to fall backwards out of his chair and onto the floor, knocking him out cold! "Hey, ya farkin’ bastard! I QUIT!" He turned on his heel and started to walk out of the room, but then he stopped and spun around once more. "And your fed sucks! For cryin’ out loud, it’s initials are WTF! WTF! What the fark is wrong with you, man!? Good lord, you’re a horrible businessman! I bid you good day, sir!" With a nod, Saij left the room and closed the door behind him, leaving behind an unconscious Ralph Woodson. -~-~- Derick Jackson sat at his office desk with a somber expression. "I have no other choice," he said. He pressed a button on his phone, calling for his secretary. "Yes, Mr. Jackson?" she asked over the line. DJ sighed. He had really hoped that Saij wouldn’t find out about GTT6… not just because of the ramifications for XUW, but because deep down, he knew that Saij was going to take Leanne along with him. He was worried about the little girl’s well-being. With a heavy heart, he decided there was nothing else he could do. It was time for action. "Get me… get me child services." website code by: Pete.
