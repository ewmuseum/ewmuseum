The Singing Woman

26 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-26 Easton woke up out of his trance and into the world. He ran a hand over his eyes and sat up, swinging his legs off the bed and planting the balls of his feet on the floor. He hadn't gotten the best sleep in the world, but one couldn't always win. Standing up, Easton did his best to stretch to tense feeling out of his limbs. With a mighty yawn, he let his arms go slack and walked across the bedroom, pushing open the door and leaning against the doorframe. He couldn't say exactly what had brought him to New York City; it had been dictated by some strange impulse that Easton didn't completely understand. He figured that the salvia vision had some part to play in the decision. Once he'd rolled into town, however, it had struck him that unlike Baltimore, he truly had no idea where to look. He had been lucky in Baltimore, but he could never rely on luck to get him anywhere. So upon reaching the city, he'd managed to focus on more pressing matters. Right now, his mind was fixated on breakfast. He was glad he had had the presence of mind to buy some cereal. He shuffled towards the little kitchenette and lifted his box of Raisin Bran off of the counter. He glanced around curiously for a second, then something hit him. Well, fuck. Trust Easton to buy cereal and forget milk and a bowl. And spoon! Grunting, he ripped the box open and tore into the bag, sticking a hand inside and freeing a handful of Raisin Bran. In broader terms, however, Easton had been thinking about GTT6. Josh had been easy; either the kid's mind was totally not on the match or he hadn't been expecting someone of Easton's caliber at all. After the fact, Easton had taken to thinking of the match as a warm-up. It had been the first time Easton had stepped into the ring in about a month. He was proud of his own performance; the fireman's carry into triangle choke maneuver he'd pulled out was a great innovation on his part. It was easy to dismiss on the first round of a tournament, but he knew the whole thing was like a chase. There were favorites—Kimbusa, Doc Curiosity, Nova—and the tournament was a chase to see who would defeat them first. The way Easton thought of it, actually winning the tournament was secondary. The real thrill was when you got to take down one of the face cards. For example, at this point it didn't quite matter if Clinton Sage was eliminated. Everybody would still talk about how he defeated Rich Rollins, they'd still carry him on their shoulders and chant his name. Easton sighed a bit. Unfortunately for him, beating Josh didn't quite carry the same thrill, the same prestige. Neither did beating Sammy Brown; when was the last time that he had run through a tournament without taking a single loss? When was the last time that Sammy Brown held the PTC Global Championship? Sammy Brown was, as unfair as it was and as much as Sammy himself would probably try and deny it, nobody. So how did you go about getting yourself that same sort of prestige when faced with lackluster opponents? Well, you beat the guy, and you beat him well. You don't leave a question in anybody's mind. You don't win by fluke, you don't win by chance, you win because you dominated the match. With that philosophy in mind, it was less than chance Easton found himself stepping through the doors into the gym half an hour later. If he couldn't have people be amazed because "Easton Hall? He beat Seymour Almasy!", he was determined to make them say "Easton Hall? He's a damn good wrestler." It wasn't long before Easton, shirtless and dressed in a pair of lightweight black shorts and sneakers, found himself sweating profusely. His hands were raised up as he pounded the speedbag, rolling his fists one over the other. His green eyes locked on the flopping red tonsil, but he let his fists do the damage. It was a bit of a rest, standing in one place, but his arms were still getting a workout. "Come to me…" Easton blinked for a second. This time, the speedbag thumped into his hand rather than the other way around. He had heard a woman's voice, a slow crooning, out of nowhere. He glanced around. There were women over near the treadmills and a couple around the weights, but none of them looked like they were singers. At least, they didn't look like they could have… "Light on my… shoulder…" …that voice. That breathy, seductive siren's call. He couldn't tell exactly where it was coming from, but it felt as if it were pulling him by the hand towards the door of the gym. He resisted for the moment, touching his temple. Whatever it was, it didn't matter. He needed to train. He took a deep breath and exhaled out of his nose, hoping that these bad humors would tumble out. Shaking his head, he turned back to the speed bag and lifted his hands, ready to go again. "Come to me…" Easton hauled back with his right hand and let a monstrous punch fly into the speed bag, stalking away from it. There was no way he could concentrate like this. It infuriated him – nobody or somebody, he had to get ready to face Sammy Brown – but what could he do? For now, he'd have to give it up and hope that this wouldn't plague him tomorrow. He threw a shirt on and zipped up his duffle bag. "Burn your starry crown…" Dammit! This was getting to be too much. He slung the strap of his bag over his shoulder and marched out the door. He noticed after he had started walking that he was going just where the song trailed in from. Irritated, he stopped and tried to find another exit, but there wasn't any. He gave a grunt and started towards the doors again. He wasn't sure just what was fucking with him now. Maybe it was overwork, too much stress. Maybe he just needed to lie down. He had to admit, he'd almost been obsessing over this match with Sammy Brown. Besides, he vaguely recalled these lyrics. Maybe it was just him hallucinating or something. Well, he knew which drugs he wouldn't be taking when he got back to his hotel room. "My dark angel…" The whisper made Easton look up; he couldn't shake the annoyance of it, no matter how much he tried to convince himself that his mind was playing tricks on him. His eyes fixed on his rented red coupe. Someone was at the front window, trying to jam a hanger into the window. "Hey!" "I've tried… I can't get over…" Easton took a few steps forward, balling his fists. "Hey!" he called again. The man looked up at him and gave a start. Easton blinked. Who was that? He thought he recognized him. "'Cause when I think I'm climbing…" As the voice continued to croon, the man abandoned the hanger and bolted. Tugged into motion by some otherworldly yanking at his consciousness, Easton dropped his bag and darted after him. He had to figure out just who that was. "I'm really so far – dooooown…" The man zipped into an alleyway like a bumblebee and Easton played the hummingbird, zooming after him. Without regard for his own safety, Easton's prey darted across the busy street. Easton tried the same stunt, but a car passed right in front of him. Surprised, Easton threw up his hands and backed up, losing his balance and falling hard on the pavement. "Time takes a while…" Easton's lower back struck the curb and he lay there for a split second, pain tearing its way up his back. He pressed his forearm on the sidewalk and lifted himself out of the road, lying on his face for a bit as he tried to recover. He lost the punk. He couldn’t believe it. Slowly, he got to his feet. "To break ya…" Curiously, Easton thought he heard the voice from somewhere else, a different direction from before. It had led him this far, so he trusted it, against his better judgement; he picked himself up off the side walk and began to jog along this new course. At the same time, the desirable, womanly voice seemed to soothe his pain, massaging his back gently with every new note. "And now…" Easton picked up speed as the road curved a bit, taking a more obtuse one set him in an odd diagonal direction from where he was before. Suddenly, he saw the punk spilling out of an alleyway, dusting himself off. The man looked about to make sure his pursuant was no longer around. Easton glanced to his left and then to his right, making sure a vehicle wouldn't disturb him this time. "Only fire…" Easton jogged across the street, feet padding across black asphalt before reaching the raised concrete sidewalk on the other side. The punk had started walking up the street, hands shoved into pockets. Easton's eyebrows furrowed. "Can wake ya…" "Hey, you!" Easton yelled. The man who had been trying to get into Easton's car turned about. After seeing Easton marching towards him, he whipped back around and started sprinting down the sidewalk. Gritting his teeth, Easton bolted after him. Sometimes, you knew exactly why you did things. This guy obviously didn't want to get caught by Easton; he was a wiry sort and Easton certainly looked to have muscle on him, especially with his lower biceps and forearms exposed. It'd be to the guy's disadvantage to meet Easton one-on-one if Easton decided he was going to get violent. Sometimes, though, you didn't. Easton's car was in tact – at least, he didn't think anything had been broken and his car certainly hadn't been gotten in to – and the man hadn't had a weapon on him or anything to threaten violence to elicit a violent response in return. Easton had thought he recognized the guy, but was that cause for a chase across New York City? Why had Easton picked up the phone a month ago, answered Adam Dick's seemingly random phone call and agreed to this tournament? He had been living a pretty good life, training wrestlers, being an independent. Why had he, all of a sudden, decided he needed to make a name for himself at two in the morning? Why was he even booked up against Sammy Brown? Was it fate? Or… fates? "Ohhhh weeping willow…" A billboard perched atop a distant building advertised grapes as the fruit of fortune. What seemed like a million miles away, John Edwards was cold reading some sap who looked at him dumbstruck. In every town in the Bible belt, preachers told their lay people that their fate was hell if they continued to sin like they had been doing. It seemed like everyone in the world knew their fate beforehand except for Easton. What Easton did know was that by some cosmic logic, his fate included catching that guy. "Coughing up my heart…" Impatiently, Easton brushed his way through a sparse crowd; he managed to keep his eyes on the back of the guy's head as he made it through. The punk grabbed a nearby trashcan and threw it on the ground behind him to try and put something between them. Easton lowered his head, making his form a bit more aerodynamic. The punk swept forward, running as fast as he could away from Easton. The swift-footed breaker of necks planted his foot on the trashcan and threw himself forward, soaring through the air at his quarry. With a yelp, the would-be car thief was tackled to the ground. Reflexively, the man kicked away from Easton and scrambled to his feet. The Canadian grappler raced to his feet as well and started after the man, catching him by the hand. The punk whirled around and lashed out at Easton, striking him in the temple with a hastily-formed fist. Easton dropped against the wall for a second, shaking his head and trying to clear the black spots where his eyes were suddenly blind. "Ask god to see my shadow…" Easton glanced up and saw that the man had disappeared. He frowned, taking a few steps forward. Fuck. He'd come so far just for a cheap shot to blind him long enough that he lost the trail. He scratched his head, fingers dragging through long hair. "And kept us apaaaaaaaaaaaart…" Again, the voice drifted to him from somewhere else. Easton glanced to his side, down a little nook where the buildings didn't meet. The punk was picking his way through this alley, trying frantically to get away. Easton planted his hand on the wall and began after him, grunting. He tried to pick up speed, but the littered obstacles made it incredibly hard. Still, he was making better time than his quarry. That was really what mattered. Surprise formed desire formed obsession. That was how Easton had found things went. At first, he had been surprised that to find that the key to success was control. Control over yourself, primarily; by controlling yourself, you could control everything around you. Naturally, he wanted to succeed, so he desired control. He'd been led on a mad chase for it against Axis and Kael Stanton. He became obsessed with it, maniacal in a quiet way, and that had brought him to his eventual destructor – the mystic. Easton had been surprised at his betrayal at the man's hands. After losing that match, after losing his shot at another golden belt around his waist, the northern-born crippler desired revenge. Even after he had knocked the man down, had delivered his final words, he wanted to do more. He wanted to… he almost wanted to kill him. He was obsessed. He'd been surprised when the phone rang at two in the morning – two in the goddamn morning! – and he'd been offered a spot in GTT6. Once he'd secured his place, he had naturally desired to win. And now that he'd had his first victory… "Through my mind's every riot… or revelation…" The punk picked up a heavy object and hurled it at Easton. With a surprised gasp, Easton pressed himself flat against the wall just enough so that the object – a grayish cinderblock, Easton noticed as it tumbled through the air – missed him. Easton snarled and started again, resolving to catch this man and beat him into the earth, familiar or not. The punk managed to balance himself on a trash can and he jumped up, grabbing the fire escape ladder and pulling it down. He started to climb up it and was halfway there as Easton bounded over the last pile of trash. The Canadian leapt up onto the ladder and grabbed at the punk's ankle, gripping it hard in his right hand. This guy wasn't getting away, not now. "You've just now gone… as I arrive… at every station…" With a mighty effort, the punk managed to free his leg and scale up the ladder, heading onto the fire escape itself. Easton grunted, flying up it after him as if he were simply swimming through air rather than pulling himself on the rungs. He pulled himself onto the fire escape and started up after the man, gripping the railing as he ran. His feet pounded the steel relentlessly, always half a beat ahead of the punk's; their combined footsteps created a hypnotizing jangling like the rattling of a brass rattlesnake's tail. "Ohhhh, come to me…" They flew up the fire escape in tandem, the punk in the lead, until they reached the top. The would-be carjack lifted open a window and practically dove in headfirst, body disappearing into the building. Easton followed him up quickly and made his way through the window a bit more carefully, stepping in and pushing off with his inside foot. The apartment the two sprang into was not one that Easton recognized. A man sat in the corner, looking at the two intruders bewilderedly; a newspaper was balanced on his knees, but he obviously wasn't reading any longer. The punk grasped the doorknob and wrenched it, almost ripping the door off its hinges in his haste. He leapt out the door and was running down the hall in an instant. Easton tore after him, soles blazing a path through the thin rug and out into the tiled hallway. "Either early or late…" The punk stood at the end of the hall, shaking a doorknob furiously. He needed it to open, but it wouldn't. Finally, with both hands gripped on it, it twisted in his hands. He looked up and saw the short, angry dragon-marked warrior barrelling towards him, fist lifted high into the air. What came after obsession? What came after all that thrill of chasing, after the singular feeling of being kept up at night because something was eating away at your heart? When Easton found the control he cherished, what then? Contentment? Disappointment? Disgust? If Easton killed this mystic, would he grieve then? Not for the fortune teller, of course, but for the loss of something that had bound his life together. How would he move on? Nothing approaching complacency was in Easton as his fist rocketed into the punk's mouth, sending his head whipping backwards into the wall. "You little fuck!" Easton yelled, grabbing the man by his black shirt and throwing him sideways into the door. "I've learned this by habit…" The door swung open as the man crashed into it. The punk's head hit the doorknob on his way down and he crumpled awkwardly, landing on the ground in the doorway. Easton breathed hard, but reaching this goal of his permitted him to let some air slowly seep through his teeth. Easton noticed something in the air. A slow, mournful tune was playing through the room, coming out of it. He looked up from the punk on the ground to the scene in the room. It was an ordinarily furnished room with a wide space just in front of the door; Easton noticed the stereo sitting on a cabinet on the nearest inside wall. An odd geometric figure was painted in black – or scorched? – into the ground. On the immediate center of it, a young blonde woman in a soft blue babydoll tee that hung down over her hips and almost covered flower-stitched panties was dancing barefoot, the balls of her feet never moving off the broad lines. The Unicursal Hexagram. Kelly. "Now I know how to wait…" Easton's eyes wanted to jump out of his head and sit in his hands. That voice. It was from here. It was her. How had her voice reached him so far away? How could she sing… that well? When had she gotten to the city? His jaw sat open as he tried to remember how to speak so he could call out to her. She hadn't noticed him yet; her eyes were closed as she danced and sang. "Come to me…" Easton's world crashed into reality in a sequence that was put in place by Hollywood. A large, black shape came out of a room to the right. Easton noticed a snarl on the man's face, a snarl that didn't mean anything like normal anger. Easton's eyebrows shot up, but still he couldn't speak. If a cat had his tongue, it had buried itself deep in his throat and taken up residence. He could barely breathe. "I cut your thread!" came a booming voice that Easton recognized almost instantly. At the moment of recognition, the man's face seemed to erupt in flames. Both images were the same. The mystic. He had a gun in his hand. The shock of it hit Easton hard enough that he knew to duck backwards. Before the scene of the room passed out of sight, he saw the black-robed seer grabbing Kelly, wrapping a big arm around her and clasping his hand over her mouth. She had kicked her feet fleetingly, helpless, just before the gunshots sounded. Easton wasn't always sure why he did what he did. Some days, he went with Fruity Loops rather than Captain Crunch. He found himself resenting a granola bar he'd bought on an impulse at certain occasions. At the top of a bridge with a bungee cord strapped around his waist, he would suddenly remember he had an intense fear of falling. If those events were written into his fate, he had no idea what purpose they served. What he knew, however, was that he learned something from every one of these encounters. Don't buy Fruity Loops. Don't eat granola bars. Don't bungee jump. What he could learn from chasing a man through New York and almost getting shot in the head – what part this episode played in his fate – Easton almost didn't want to know. But it had surprised him. Lyrics: "Come to Me" by Mark Lanegan
