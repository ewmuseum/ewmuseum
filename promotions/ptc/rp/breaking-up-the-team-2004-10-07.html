Breaking Up the Team

7 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-07 Is there ever a point when enough finally becomes too much? Does putting in enough effort ever turn into too much effort? At what point does eagerness turn into overeagerness? Is there even a way to recognize this? How does it affect those around you? Most people would agree that a bit of zealousness is a good thing, but overzealousness generally tends to annoy those same people after a while. What to do then? How best to deal with the situation? How do you confront someone who appears too eager? Is there even such a thing as “too eager?” For some, yes. But not for the Latch Key Kid. Frank’s Coffeehouse is bustling with the usual activity that we (and Frank) have grown used to. The day is in full swing, the coffee is brewing, the students are studying and the poets are snapping. Frank is making the rounds, talking up the customers, and in general, keeping an eye on the place like a casino’s eye in the sky. The only thing missing from this scene that belongs here, at least of late, is… “The Latch Key Kid!” One of the students yells out as the door opens and in walks, of course, the Latch Key Kid. The student quickly turns back towards his group at their table, and buries his nose in a textbook, in an effort to make himself appear less star-struck than his outburst would indicate. It doesn’t work, as his study partners point and laugh at him. Frank looks up, and excuses himself from the group he is talking to. He hurries over to the door, and greets Latch with a smile and a handshake. “Hey, Brad. Good to see you. How was the trip?” Ah, the trip. Latch recently traveled to London, England, for a match at XUW’s latest pay-per-view event, Road Rage 4. The event itself was in Minnesota, but through some cruel twist of fate, the London Street Fight match that Latch had demanded was actually moved to London. It was a jolly good battle, through the streets of the city, but in the end, Latch pinned Henrik the King right there on London Bridge. And now he’s back. “Well, overall, I can’t complain. At least, not about the match. I’m still miffed that Mr. Webster did that to us, but I guess I should learn to be careful about what I say to him from now on. At any rate, I picked up the win, so that’s a positive. On the other hand, I had to go to London, and it was too quick a trip.” “Well, it’s good to see you back. You look well, considering what you went through.” “Thanks, Frank. I feel okay, save for a few aching bones. And jetlag. That jetlag is a real pain in the rear.” Frank scratches his chin. “In that case, what will it be today, Brad? The usual?” Frank heads towards the counter and walks behind it. Latch follows him. “You bet, Frank,” Latch says, as he picks up an apron and slips it over his head. Frank turns to him and gets a startled look on his face. “And just what do you think you’re doing?” Frank asks, half-playful and half-serious. “I’m punching in, boss. You did ask if it would be the usual today, right?” “I meant, in terms of coffee. Your usual latte, or something different. Thought you might want to mix things up a little bit. I didn’t mean get to work.” “Oh, I realize I’m not due in for another few hours, but I figured there was no better way to start my day than by watching the world come to life in a coffeehouse. So, here I am.” Frank furrows his brow. “Brad, I can’t let you do this all day. You had a tough match in London, you’ve got jetlag, and you still have the tournament to worry about. Here,” he says, while pouring a mug of latte, “take this coffee, have a seat, and relax. You’ve earned it.” “Frank,” Latch says, taking the mug, “I’ll relax when the work is done. There’s stuff to do around here. Coffee to brew, tables to clear, people to greet.” “That’s what my employees…err, team members, are for, Brad. So go on, relax.” “But I am a team member, Frank. Remember? You yourself said so.” “Brad…it was supposed to be a one-time thing, to teach you a lesson and help you learn something about yourself. I didn’t realize you would take to it so well and so quickly. I didn’t mean -” Latch’s face turns sullen, and he sits down on a stool behind the counter. He takes a sip of latte, holding the mug in both hands. “So what you’re saying is you don’t want me to help out around here anymore? I didn’t do a good enough job to keep my spot here?” “You did a great job, Brad, don’t worry. It’s just that, well, I’ve already got people working here. You know I can’t afford to pay you. And I know you said you’ll work for free, and I appreciate that, but the other team members, Brad, they need work to do, and there’s enough for three of them, but four starts to take away from the stuff that’s left for them.” “Let me get this straight. Too many people working here is a bad thing?” “I guess that’s what I’m trying to say, yeah.” Latch frowns again. “I see. So, uhm, what you’re saying is that I should go sit in the corner and enjoy my latte and not bother you or the other team members?” This time, it’s Frank who frowns. “No, Brad, I’m not telling you to go sit in the corner.” Latch looks up and smiles. “I’m telling you to go sit at your regular table. That’s hardly in the corner.” Latch looks sorrowfully at Frank. “So you don’t want me here at all?” “It’s not that I don’t want you here, Brad. It’s just, I don’t need you here. There’s plenty of help right now. There’s really no reason for you to bust your butt to help me out when the other emp…team members are quite capable of handling it.” Latch looks at Frank, nods his head slowly, then picks up his mug and walks away from the counter. He sits down at his usual table, though it feels foreign to him as it’s been so long since he actually sat here. He stares at the mug sitting in front of him, then puts his head in his hands. This isn’t a new feeling for the Latch Key Kid. He’s had teams fall apart around him in the past. Most recently, the Lunchroom Patrol, one of the most dominant tag teams in XUW, disbanded when Doughboy walked out over a dispute with the commissioner. Doughboy was right to do what he did, of course, given the circumstances; but that didn’t make it any easier for Latch to deal with. The team that he had been part of for nearly two years suddenly no longer existed. He knew there would be a period of adjustment. It might be painful at first, but he would have to learn how to once again be successful as a singles wrestler. It was partly for this reason that he entered the GTT4. If he could be successful against some of the best wrestlers anywhere, surely he could be successful in XUW, against wrestlers he had faced many times before (albeit mostly in tag team matches). After a first-round loss, Latch began to doubt himself. Was he good enough to compete here? Did he belong? What would he be able to do if given a second chance? His questions were answered when he was offered a play-in, back into the tournament. He made good on that play-in, living up to his own lofty expectations, and proving to everybody that he could hold his own here. Maybe he belonged here after all. Maybe he was good enough to make some noise in this tournament. Maybe, just maybe… “Eric Russo, huh?” While Latch was lost in thought, Frank had taken a seat across the table from him. “‘Superstar’ Eric Russo, that is. What are you thinking about, Brad?” “Doughboy, Frank. I still haven’t heard from him. I don’t know where he is, how he is, what he’s doing…this whole situation with Nicole really messed him up, and I can’t say I blame him.” “You weren’t thinking about Matt, Brad. I’m smarter than that. Sure, maybe he crossed your mind, but that’s not what you were focusing on. So come on, let’s have it.” “Well, not directly, I wasn’t, but same thing, anyway. I was thinking about the whole team concept, Frank. The Lunchroom Patrol fell apart, and suddenly the world seemed upside down. I figured I could use this tournament to ease that transition, and it really has helped. And I figured I could further ease that transition by hanging out here and doing the odd job to help out. And that helped, too.” “Working here instead of lounging around here helped you?” “Yep. See, I was part of a team again. You welcomed me, the other team members welcomed me, and the customers welcomed me. Well, once they stopped getting so upset at my ineffective use of the cash register. But the point is, you did show me the value of teamwork, yet at the same time, the importance of individualism. A team, Frank. People working together to accomplish a single goal. That’s what Doughboy and I did as the Lunchroom Patrol. Our goal was to win the XUW tag team championship, and after we did that, our goal was to defend those belts like nobody else had. After we did that, there was really nowhere left to go. Two people, working as one, accomplishing a goal.” “And this is -” “This is related to working here because you have so many people working here. Nancy, Betsy, Bill…they have one goal in mind, Frank. And that’s to run this coffee shop the way it should be run; friendly, professionally, with the customers in mind. You’ve got a great thing going here, Frank. They’re luck to be a part of it. And for a while, so was I. Being part of a team again was great. It was a great experience, a great time, and a great feeling. To know that I was working with other people to accomplish a common goal, I really couldn’t ask for anything more from this. Thanks for giving me the opportunity. But as seems to be the case with me and teams lately, this one fell apart as well.” “I’m glad you liked it, Brad. It sounds like you got a lot out of it. And I’m sorry you feel that this team fell apart. It didn’t, Brad. You made it stronger when you were part of it, don’t get me wrong. But it’s already strong enough. And it just wouldn’t be fair if I kept you on.” “How do you know what would be fair to the others? Maybe they want me to stay on the team.” Frank shakes his head. “I’m not talking about being fair to the others, Brad. I’m talking about being fair to you.” Now Latch shakes his head. “I’m not sure what you mean, Frank. How could booting me off the team be fair to me?” “Well now you’re on your own again, right?” “Yes. And that’s the problem here. But you seem to think there’s some great trick -” “There’s no trick to this, Brad, not to any of it. You remember why I brought you on in the first place?” “Yes. You tricked me into working here with the premise of having to run some errands.” “Okay. So maybe there was a trick in the beginning. But it was for your benefit. You realized by working here that you really can do something different if you put your mind to it. You learned something about yourself, you learned to function as an individual, and you learned the value of each individual to a team. Don’t you think it was worth it?” “I suppose it was. It just seems like you teased me with the job here. Just as I was getting used to it, you pulled it away. It’s just stunned me, I guess. I suppose you have your reasons for doing what you did, and now it’s up to me to figure out for myself what those reasons are.” Frank smiles in a fatherly manner. “Brad, you know I always have my reasons for doing what I do. But don’t you think it’s possible for you to do great things now that you’re no longer part of a team?” “How do you mean, Frank?” “Okay. Well, you said you’ve been part of two teams that have recently fallen apart. First the Lunchroom Patrol, and now working here. To help you get over the Lunchroom Patrol falling apart, you entered the GTT4, and now you’re in the third round, and you may not be finished yet. I’d say that’s pretty successful so far, wouldn’t you?” Latch nods and Frank smiles again. “Good. And now that you think this team, let’s call it Team Frank, has fallen apart, it’s up to you to figure out how to get past it. There’s something out there that can help you, Brad, but I can’t lead you to it.” “So you do think it’s possible to go on to success as a singles wrestler after such a long tag career?” Latch asks with a puzzled look. “Of course. Take a look at your opponent, “Superstar” Eric Russo.” “Russo? What’s he got to do with this?” Frank sighs and shakes his head. “You know he’s in LOW, right?” Latch nods in agreement. “Okay. Then you should also know that he enjoyed a few tag team title reigns, as part of both the Shadow Riders and the Metallo Zords.” Latch nods again. “And you know what he’s doing now, don’t you?” Latch smiles. “Of course I do. He’s facing me in the GTT4.” Frank shakes his head again. “Not exactly what I meant. He’s the current LOW World Champion.” “So he’s the best wrestler in LOW at the moment. How does that affect me?” “Well for one, you’re wrestling him. For two, he’s the current LOW World Champion. And before that, he was one-half of the LOW tag team champions. He enjoyed a great deal of tag team success, and now he’s enjoying singles success. He did it, Brad. He rose to the top after a lengthy tag career.” “And if he did it, then why can’t -” Frank claps his hands as if he’s just discovered the cure for some vile disease. “You can, Brad! That’s exactly my point. If Eric Russo can do it, you can do it. Don’t you see? It’s possible. It really is. You just have to make it happen.” “And how do you suggest I do that?” Latch asks. “Well,” Frank replies, “one way you won’t make it happen is by moping about not being able to sling lattes anymore. I think you know what you have to do, Brad.” Frank stands up and pats Latch on the shoulder. He picks up his mug and heads back towards the counter. Latch stares down at the mug in front of him, and then nods. He mutters under his breath. “Yep. I know exactly what I have to do.”
