Square One

24 September 2004

Date: 2004-09-24 It was a light and balmy night. It was the kind of night where you'd just love to sit out on the balcony and sip a nice glass of vintage champagne with the your sweetheart, feeding one another strawberries and watching the fireworks explode over the gently undulating ocean. Sadly there was no ocean in sight, but the mountains in which the Curious Laboratory was set made for a beautiful vista all the same. A shame then that the owner of the same laboratory was not interested in such a thing. He was, after all, a mad scientist, and mad scientists need nights of the dark and stormy variety. I mean, who ever heard of anything as preposterous as creating despicable evil on a night where the forecast is bright and breezy? "Eegor, zis just vill not do!" The doctor did his best to make up for the weather by storming through his laboratory. The blackboard was still dusty, and yes, the scientific ephemera still lay all over the desk, bubbling and smoking for all it was worth. "Dark and stormy, Eegor! Vhy does it never rain here?" The assistant to the good doctor sighed. Not this again. "I vait and I vait and I vait, and it's all for nothing," yelled the doctor, pacing past Eegor, who was modeling a huge pair of false teeth in his quest to debase himself and become a true deformed assistant. "Mmmm mmhhhmmm hhhmmmm mhmhhmhmm," replied Eegor. "Eegor, vhile I appreciate ze effort, remove zoze ridiculus things from your mouth before I am forced to his you in ze face vith a large metal pole." The assistant acquiesced, and turned back to the doctor. "Eegor, you are as useless as zis veather! I vant storms! I vant vind and rain! I vant to hear the fury of nature unleashed like a primordial beast!" "Then why did we live in the Mojave desert?" "Silence, you insolent fool! It vas a good price, and zere aren't too many laboratories for sale, vhen you actually look for zem," mumbled the doctor. "Maybe we could move to Florida?" "Move? Move? Move?" "Yes," said Eegor, his voice growing timid. "I have a better idea, you buffoon! I shall use ze Curious Weather Altering Device and bring ze dark and stormy night to me! Zat is far easier." "But master, last time you used that, you created Hurricane Ivan!" "A petty thing, Eegor, you are not seeing ze bigger picture here!" "Which is what?" "I hate movink house! No, Eegor, ze simplest thing is for me to use ze machine. Now, vhere did I leave it?" The doctor surveyed the room. To his left was a pile of bills, unopened and unpaid. To his right was the workbench, but to tell the truth it was mostly ornamental. The doctor had never even worked out what the beaker that spewed clouds of smoke was for, but it didn't seem to do much harm, and gave the place a certain degree of atmosphere. In the middle of the floor rested the remains of the Curious Mallet Version 3.0, and in one corner, hiding beneath a big white sheet, rested something large. The Curious Weather Altering Device. Doctor Curiosity walked over to the device, and quickly pulled off the cover, showering himself in dust and fresh cobwebs. The device itself was a thing of beauty, if you like big ugly machines with flashing lights, shiny levers and big red buttons. For Doctor Curiosity it was the stuff of wet dreams. "Right, I'm sure I remember how to use zis thing…" muttered Curiosity as he flicked a switch, causing the machine to start humming, whirring and clicking. The device towered over and around the good doctor, standing a good eight feet tall in total, and being almost cubic in dimensions. The doctor shrugged, and pressed a button at random. There was a crunching noise from the device, and a huge bolt of lightning flew from outside the laboratory, through the window, and slammed in to Eegor from behind. His squeal was drowned out under the sound of gears crunching within the machinery. Doctor Curiosity looked out of the other window in front of him and kicked the machine, before realising it was made out of metal, and rubbing his sore toe. He turned around and spied Eegor on the floor, smoke rising from a scorch mark on his back. "No laying around on ze job Eegor! If I am to get zis to work I vill need assistance!" The doctor sniffed the scorched air. "Jah! Bring me some toast! Good Eegor!" The hapless assistant groaned in inimitable fashion and slowly pulled himself to his feet. His entire body was shaking, and his hair stood on end in a grotesque parody of his master's. The doctor hit another button… in fact he hit the same one, twice. The lightning shot through the window and slammed in to Eegor's side, spinning him around to let the second bolt catch him in the chest. He slumped to the floor, flitting in and out of consciousness. "Damn it, I can't see any rain at all! Zis is useless! Eegor, I need zat toast now, or maybe a nice bit of…" The good doctor turned to find Eegor prostrate on the stone floor once again. "How many times have I told you, no sleeping on ze job! I don't care if you've been up for forty eight hours cleaning ze laboratory and running my errands, zere are standards you need to maintain!" "But master…" "No excuses, Eegor! I have to perfect my Curious Weather Altering Device, tzink of a vay to defeat my next opponent, whoever it may be, and eat some toast! I cannot do zat without your help! Now move it!" The brother of the good doctor struggled back up once again, and tottered towards the huge workbench. "You're facing Jade," he called out over his shoulder. "Jade? Vhat kind of a name is Jade? I miss ze good old days vhen men had proper names, not zis kind of von vord nonsense." "It's not a..." "...good name," interrupted the good doctor, "I know zis! Vhy not call himself Amethyst or Topaz? Jah! Topaz ze terrifying wrestler man!" "Doctor, I'm trying to tell you, Jade is a.." "Great wrestler? Pah, no man vould allow themselves to..." It was Eegor's turn to interrupt. "Listen to me! Jade's not a man! You're wrestling a woman!" There was the merest flicker of emotion that crossed the face of Doctor Curiosity. It would have been invisible to most people, but Eegor could see it, and he knew exactly what it meant. ----------------------------- To explain the full gamut of feelings that Doctor Curiosity held towards the female half of the human race, you would have had to have known him since he was a child, the youngest of three. Growing up, the children of the Goodman family were about as normal as can be expected in this day and age. They were all born within a six year period, the eldest, Paul, coming two years before his sister, Leona, and a full six years before Chris, the afterthought. There were all the usual squabbles that children had, over cookies, chocolate bars and hair-pulling, but underneath it all there was a bond of love that existed, at least between the two brothers. Yet, resentment was soon to grow within the Goodman household. Leona and Chris fought solidly throughout her early teenage years, Leona in particular reveling in being physically stronger than the little brother she used to torment. Paul would try and calm the pair down, but with both parents always busy working, it often seemed that he was the only voice of sanity between the warring siblings. At the tender age of sixteen, Paul was not always around to play peacemaker, having recently discovered the wonder of girls, and illicit drinking. What little affection for the children was shown by their parents tended to get directed towards Chris, who had a “Straight ‘A’ Student” written all over him (once, memorably, by Leona, in bright blue ink). The lenience that was bestowed upon Chris Goodman, the man who would grow up to become Doctor Curiosity, did nothing but make Leona hate him even more. With teenage hormones going in to overdrive, Leona tried everything she could to make his life a misery, but he seemed to be able to shrug off her attacks, and dive back in to his numbers. Time passed for the Goodmans, and the children grew farther apart from one another, punctuated only by a degree of togetherness when their Mother, Eleanor Goodman, passed away from a sudden heart attack. The doctors blamed it on the stress of her lifestyle, working long hours in the office, trying to get ahead of the competition... but for what? An early grave was her sole reward for her efforts. The three children, now aged between twenty and fourteen, took it very differently. For Paul, the eldest, the young man who would in the future be called 'Eegor', he thought it was a slap in the face directly from God. His mother had been distant to him, but she had worked hard, and if trying to provide for your family gave you that, then what was the point in working at all? From that point onwards, Paul Goodman, a promising young wrestler at college, would skip the odd training session, miss a few classes here and there, and take a first class journey towards mediocrity and apathy. Leona, by now a quite bitter eighteen year old girl, didn't care. Truth be told she was mostly happy about it, though a seed of guilt did lurk just below the surface. Whenever she had contact with her mother she had tried to antagonise her, almost goading her towards stress and the inevitable failure of her heart. Leona had been ignored for years, but now with a parent lost, sympathy flooded her way from all angles. Her mock grief definitely became her, and she started to get more interest, from both friends of the family, and the local boys. From being shunned to becoming a figure of sympathy, she took whatever was offered to her, starting with the advances of boys, and moving on through them, casting each off like her old clothes. Out of this emerged a very different Leona than before. She was popular, she was taking all manner of drugs, and what's more, she still hated her little brother. Chris didn't take the death well at all. Always an insular boy, he turned back again to his three mainstays in his fourteen year old life; solitude, mathematics, and masturbation. He studied harder than ever before, throwing himself in to the world of learning with a reckless abandon that would come back to him later in life, though in a different field from his school work. A life full of study is too one-dimensional though, and deprived of the attention he was accustomed to receiving from his dear departed mother, Chris Goodman grew to be more surly than before. The usual teasing he endured at school with a previously good grace started causing him to lash out at the bullies. He went from being an easy target to being an unpredictable one. Teenage boys being what they are, this didn't stop the ridicule and punishment, it just made it funnier to the older children to see his reactions and temper tantrums. In the background, Leona laughed and pointed at him, her influence spreading among the boys to taunt her brother ever more. With an elder brother away at college, his sister alternately tormenting and ignoring him, and his father even busier than before, Chris Goodman just had to turn in on himself. He became like a hermit, shying away from social contact with anyone, and especially girls. While the rest of his classmates were trying to fumble their way to first base and beyond, Chris Goodman was thinking up fantastic new ways to extract revenge on them all, especially his sister. ---------------- "A voman you say?" Eegor nodded, his body still giving off steam from the lightning strikes. "Vell... I had better get zis blasted contraption vorking zen! Get me zat toast, and I vill fix my machine so zat it strikes Jade vith lightning any time she dares come close to me! She von't even get to lay von of her whorish hands upon my body!" A combination of disgust and lust ran through the doctor as he yelled out his intentions. In truth, he was very worried. Once Eegor had explained that his opponent was a woman, his brain had filled in the blanks and remembered who this person was, and how dangerous she could be to his plans. The doctor looked at his Curious Weather Altering Device and frowned. Even this might not be enough. "Eegor, put zat toast on hold. I tzink zis device needs to be bigger!" Eegor shrugged for what seemed like the millionth time. It was hard to work for Doctor Curiosity, as he seemed to think that everyone else should be able to read his mind and fetch him whatever he was thinking of. "Vell get to it, fetch me ze Curious Wrench!" ------------------- A few years passed from the death of Mrs Goodman, and the family started to move back towards an even keel. Paul graduated from college, though with marks lower than were expected, and he lost his place on the wrestling team. He moved back home and re-established contact with his family, making friends once more with both of his siblings. The time and distance apart had mellowed any feelings of ill-will, and both Leona and Chris accepted him back with open arms. Leona herself had finally straightened herself out and was working locally, having turned down the chance to go to college for a job in a nearby clothing store. She served the great and the good, whilst dreaming of a career as a designer. With the drugs firmly in her past, and her days of forcing other boys to humiliate her brother over, she held no ill-feeling towards Chris, and in fact had tried to mend her relationship with him. Chris was having none of it. All he could remember was that his sister had tormented him. Every time she apologised for something, a new crime sprung up in his mind, remembering the time she had made the other boys stick his head down the toilet, or strip him naked in a classroom. It wasn't just that he hated her for her past exploits, though he certainly did. Instead it was that with all the torment he'd been through, he still believed this was just the next ploy. She was going to get him to be friends with her and then unleash something so monumentally foul that he would never recover from it. Experience had taught him to trust nobody other than himself, and even though he was friendly with his elder brother, he wouldn't give anyone a chance to betray him further. Chris continued to excel in the field of mathematics, and also in the physical sciences. He started to develop a love for the work of Albert Einstein, and studied closely both the work and the life of the famous scientist. it was this devotion to him that enabled Chris to go to college a year ahead of schedule, having taken all the necessary examinations and passed them with flying colours. The bitter, shy, closed book that was Chris Goodman went to the University of California at Berkeley. Nothing he found there helped him, other than his education. He still resisted the overtures to reconciliation that Leona made towards him, and in the end she stopped trying. Chris had reached the end of the road with regards to life in America, and life with the family he had remaining. He cursed his Mother for leaving him with them, and he left for England. Which was where he met Collette. -------------- Eegor brought Doctor Curiosity his Curious Wrench. Unlike most of his ridiculous contraptions, this was actually just a genuine wrench, apart from the fact that it extended our further to give even more leverage, and that it had flashing lights taped to it. The doctor was nothing if not thorough in his theme of flashing lights. Curiosity froze when it was handed to him. He looked at the Curious Weather Alteration Device, and back to Eegor. The device… and then Eegor. Device. Eegor. “How in ze hell are ve going to fit zis next to ze wrestling ring?” Eegor continued his fixation with shrugging and groaning by pulling off both at once. Curiosity did not seem impressed. “Even if I vas to fix zis machine and make it bigger and better, zey might suspect foul play if a lightning bolt vere to crash through ze ceiling and kill Jade. I don’t… I don’t know precisely vhat I should do.” For the first time, the doctor was at a loss for something to do regarding an upcoming match. He could always be counted on for ingenuity, but something was holding him back this time. Facing Jade was a blow to his confidence. He mind was frozen, not through a normal lack of inspiration, but by something else entirely. Fear. Every time he had encountered a woman, she ended up betraying him. Every time he reached out for a woman, she stabbed him in the back. Every time he thought he’d outwitted a woman, she defeated him. The doctor stood still, tapping his wrench in his hand, emotionally crippled and incapable of movement. ---------------- The bitterness of Chris Goodman when he touched down in Manchester, England, was off the charts. Nobody he had ever met during his studious life seemed to want to help him at all. It was if there was a constant struggle between him and the rest of the world, particularly the half without the Y chromosome. Whenever he tried to open up, to rid himself of his solitude and find a girlfriend, or even a companion, he would get rejected. He completed his second degree, becoming something of an expert on radio astronomy, and tried to continue living his work, but as with most human beings, he needed something more. He started his Doctorate, but his focus was beginning to waver. There was only so long he could redouble his efforts at study. Eventually he’d have to come out the other side, and when that happened, he’d need something more than a stack of papers and a certificate that proved he’d spent the last seven years alone. He tried again and again, but for every dating agency he joined, there were no matches, and every woman he managed to wrangle a date, there was no interest. Bitterness grew even sharper in Chris Goodman’s mouth, and he was well on the way to being the sad, lonely man who sits in bars alone, mumbling to himself and unnerving anyone within a thirty foot radius. Then, on what we might call another fateful night in the like of Doctor Curiosity, the young Chris Goodman met a lady by the name of Collette. She was an English girl, around the same age as Chris, and when he first set eyes on her, his heart broke. She was painfully beautiful to his eyes; from her battered shoes all the way up past her sweater that had seen better days, all the way up to her glasses, which were held together with some form of tape. Despite it all, you could tell that she was a good looking lady, the kind who would ‘scrub up well’, to use the vernacular. One last chance, he thought. One last chance for womankind. If this was to be rejection number eight thousand, then it would close the book for him, and he would return home to America the same broken man. “You look, well, I don’t know what to say,” were his stuttered first words to her. She smiled though, and accepted his offer of a drink. The whirlwind romance that followed began to melt even the hardest, coldest recesses of Chris Goodman’s heart. He could never fully give himself over to her, not right away, and when he left for America, he left alone, but as a changed man. Upon arriving back in San Diego, at the family home, he was greeted with friendship and love from his brother and sister. They had missed him, and were both elated and surprised to see him come back with a smile on his face. Seeing how things could be, Chris called Collette up, and offered to pay for her flight to come and visit him. She accepted, and once there, she never left. Everything seemed to be coming up roses for Chris as his time went by with Collette, and his relationship with all of his family had never been as strong as the day when he walked down the aisle with his new bride. The relationship with Leona was mended, for who could hold a grudge over such a long time, and Paul, his brother, was just delighted, his guilt at having failed his younger sibling assuaged by Chris’s happiness. The one last chance had turned out to be the best thing he’d ever done. The final chip placed on the right number in roulette; the last dollar spent on the winning lottery ticket. Of course, fate had other plans for Doctor Curiosity, and would unleash them in the cruelest possible way. Her betrayals, when they came, were all that was needed to break Chris Goodman, permanently. ------------------ ”Jade… Jade… Jade… vhat can I use against Jade?” The Curious Doctor was mobile again, pacing up and down the length of his Curious Laboratory. “Vhat is better zan Jade? Vhat is better zan…” Curiosity snapped his fingers together. They made no noise, so he tried again, and it was a success. Eegor turned round from trying to untangle his hair, and looked across at the beaming face of his brother and master. “Diamonds! Diamonds are much better zan Jade, and far more expensive.” Eegor went back to his regularly scheduled sighing. This was typical of the good doctor. When faced with an insurmountable problem, he’d work gradually sideways in logical steps until he found some straws at which to clutch. It’s called ‘thinking outside the box’, or ‘being a jackass’, dependent on your point of view. “Eegor, it’s so simple! Ve shall get ourselves an enormous stack of diamonds! Jade shall see ze diamonds and be reminded of her relative lack of vorth, and be humiliated. In ze confusion surrounding her being made a fool of, ve shall sneak up behind her and smack her vith a big stick! JAH! It’s so devious, so fiendish… so curiously delightful! It’s foolproof! Now…. Vhere can I find myself enough diamonds to confuse ze voman?” The doctor rubbed his chin, and as he did so, someone on the other side of the door straightened their coat, and checked themselves in a mirror. “I have it! Ze British Crown Jewels! Zey have enormous diamonds, ze like of vhich have never been seen elsevhere! I shall steal zem! Jah!” Eegor looked around again… and as he did, someone on the other side of the door set themselves, ready to open a door, and a very open, very sore wound. “Doctor, the British Crown Jewels are kept under extremely tight security… and they’re in England. How the hell are you going to get there and back before your match against Jade?” “Jah… jah… zat vould be a problem. No matter, Eegor, ve shall go to ze local mall and see vhat diamonds zey have for sale zere! Get your coat, my ridiculous assistant, for ve have vork to do!” The doctor turned on his heel, his lab coat flapping out behind him as he turned towards the door. His expression changed as he saw the door open unexpectedly; and then again when his eyes registered the sight before him. She was wearing a tight-fitting business suit in charcoal grey, with her hair drawn back from her face in a severe ponytail. Her glance alone made both Eegor and Doctor Curiosity flinch, as she cast her eyes over them, twin orbs filled with something between pity and hatred. It was Collette. With a laugh, and a voice like the sound of broken glass falling to the floor, she spoke. “You look, well, I don’t know what to say…”
