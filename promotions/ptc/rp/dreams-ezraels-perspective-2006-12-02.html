Dreams: Ezraél’s Perspective

2 December 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-12-02 I cried the day my son was born. My tears served as a vessel for my faith; I led a tormented youth, full of the same doubts and reservations we all face when confronted with the Great Beyond, but the moment I held that boy in my arms, my selfish life evaporated like the dew outside my doorstep. A selfless Ezraél was birthed from my wife’s womb, the twin to guide my precious Juda down the path of the righteous, and I prayed to God this world would show him half the love I held for him in my heart. If only my wife had survived to see her son grow into the holy pillar he became. We all have reasons for our tears, even as they simultaneously sting and cleanse. Show me a pious man, a man led by his heart like a horse to his bit, and I will show you my son. I was blessed with many years as his acting guardian, raising the boy to lead our village in the ways of the Light long after I was no longer able to hold the mantle; they were happy years, full of memories I’ll cherish on my lonely walk through eternity. I thank my Maker a thousand times over he granted me those times, before the darkness came and swept the world away. I never saw the Evil in that poor Stranger’s face. When he washed upon our shores, weather-worn and sick, riding the tide on the backs of splinters he had fastened with crude coils of rope, I did what any laudable chaplain should; I fed him, kept him warm, kept him safe. My son Juda, naive and inexperienced despite his heavy heart, worried that this strange man’s disease would spread throughout the island. He never questioned my resolve, at least not directly, but I could always read his heart better than I could his head. I told him our future was in the hands of an entity far more benevolent than we, and what was destined to happen, would. I wonder who turned out to be the more naive, Juda, or myself? Benevolence can mask malevolence far too easily, it seems. The Stranger’s fever ravaged the poor traveler; I sensed it then, the unnatural nature of his affliction, but I did not act upon it. Who are we to question the wisdom of fate, lest we be condemned to the halls of the wicked? The man came to our village for a reason, however sinister it may have been, but mere mortals such as I know not the intentions of the Great Creator. I used my knowledge and my faith to care for the man as best I could, but in the end, death took him still. He spoke few words in his delirium, but I will never forget the things he said before the cold certainty of oblivion swallowed him whole. "The Army of the Damned cometh," said the Stranger, "and with it rides the pale face of a new King." He spoke the language of our village. How this is possible I will never know. The villagers mourned the Stranger’s passing. We gathered in the temple to pray for this poor man’s soul as it walked the long road to Providence, unguided and alone. We didn’t even know his name. I led the people through a renewing hymn used by our village to lead the lost along the path of the Light, offered the strength of my torch to see the Stranger’s journey to a peaceful end, and asked the Great Creator to welcome him into the warm embrace of the everlasting. And the people said, "Amen." It was Juda who informed me of the disturbance. "Father," he came to me, "There is an evil eye hovering over this island, and it may have already taken innocent lives. Hamád Belize has not shown his face in the Council for days." I did not worry at this. Hamád was a very strong-willed man of faith, reminding me very much of myself at his age, and I knew that whatever his reasons or his whereabouts, they must have been perfectly justified. Then Juda began to worry me. "It’s that Stranger, father." His eyes held fear; it was not a usual fixture on his delicate features. "The dead sailor… his grave, it lies empty." There comes a time in every Age when men are called to answer to powers that operate far beyond the scope of their comprehension; be it evil or glorious exaltation, it is in these times that man proves his mettle. This was our time. I do not know how or why, but some foul hand from the Great Beyond had taken this poor sailor as its puppet, pulling strings never meant to guide mortal men and altering the fabric of our very way of life. It saddened me to see my fellow countrymen take up arms against their own; even though we knew what we hunted was a blasphemy against God and all His works, we never imagined how far the strings of the One Evil fell. We learned a simple lesson, and it was a lesson that cost us dearly. I was convinced that this madness could have a peaceful resolution, and to that end I led my son and a group of frightened farmers out to the home of Hamád. I did not know what to expect as we turned our torches against the darkness of our fellow villager’s hut, searching for the answer to this macabre mystery, but what I do know is that nothing in my dreams, not in my wildest nightmares, could have possibly prepared me against Him. What we found was a horror wrapped inside a tragedy; wallowing in the blood of one of my closest friends, the reanimated Stranger had been waiting for us. Body parts littered the floor like discarded tools, mixing blood and sand in such a way that the coagulated remains made my sandals stick to the packed earth. The funk of rotting flesh overwhelmed me; it was clear that the Stranger had been taking his time, performing deplorable acts on this poor family that I dare not describe. Juda spilled his supper across the floor. He wasn’t the only member of our party made sick by this discovery. The demonic Stranger snarled, its eyes flashing dull contempt for the light of our torches. I raised my staff against this evil, commanding the spirit of the damned to leave its decomposing host, but the Stranger merely laughed. The sound that ripped itself from the creature’s throat was not human, nor had it ever been. "You cannot banish that which does not die, mortal." The creature’s voice bellowed in the tiny cottage, amplified by a contempt so pure that my arms pimpled. "I’ve roasted the limbs of your ancestors over the spitfires of Hell, and I’m here to tell you, they tasted sweeter than life." Consumed with emotion I lashed out and struck the demon across its face, knocking loose a string of gory giblets from the creature’s twisted mouth. In an instant my companions (likewise enraged by the grizzly scene and its diabolical occupant) had descended upon the Stranger, ripping the foul beast apart at the seams, using equipment never intended to tear more than patches of soil to shear dead flesh from living bone. The creature cried all the while, but it wasn’t the mournful sound of defeat. It laughed, gurgling defiantly even as its throat split open like a banana peel. It was then I felt the sting; turning to face what I assumed was a new assailant, I was wholly unprepared for the image which lay at my feet. Haley Belize, the three-year-old angel of the Belize clan, had crawled across the floor on legs that had been nibbled down to wet stumps and embedded her gummy teeth into my leg. Innocent eyes long glazed by the touch of death glittered in the torchlight, smothered by a spiteful expression no earthly child should possess. I cried out, horrified, remembering in that moment the look on her father’s face the day she had been born, of holding her at her first Harvest, watching her run through the wheat as an innocent, not this obscene thing clawing at my calves. Juda did what I could not, sending the demon child into the darkness as darkness itself overcame me. I remember very little of my final days, stretched out as they were in an endless parade of delusions and dark dreams. I could feel Juda by my side at night, holding my hand as I wept for the loss of my kinsmen. I could see them all, Hamád and Haley, even the mysterious Stranger, lurking at the edges of my fading vision yet dissipating like mist whenever directly encountered by the eye. They spoke to me in my sleep, blaming me for their deaths and condemning me to their fate; in truth, I knew my suffering was to come at a far greater price than theirs. It was He I feared, not these moaning apparitions. I knew my time had come when a new visitor entered my midnight delirium. Shrouded in white silk, the woman who appeared at the foot of my bed had haunted my dreams for twenty years. Unfortunately for me, she appeared on this most inauspicious occasion not in a dream, but in the flesh wrought of Hell. My wife smiled, even though the crevice of her womb bled on my sandstone floor. "Do not fear me," said my demonic angel. "I have come to offer you a truce." I told it that I did not make deals with devils and deceivers. "I am neither." Crawling across my fevered body, my beautiful princess bride placed a tender hand on my brow. "Come, for the Master is waiting." Her hand twisted, joined by its twin as her thumbs found my eyes and invaded the privacy of my skull. I screamed, damning the vile harridan back to the pits that spawned her, praying to be taken by my God and not this loathsome creature. Her fingers dug, scratching bone, squashing my eyes like gelatin against the curvature of their sockets. My body was too weak to fight; she whispered sweet promises into my ear even as the fingers turned to claws, piercing my brain, puncturing thought and killing my final attempt to resist. She promised me the eternity we had always planned to spend together. As the claws invaded my mind, I submitted myself to Him. Lord forgive me. < - > < - > < - > Lord forgive me. I awoke from the dream, ripping a layer of covers off my chest so thick I could have wrung them out like a wet rag. The voice of my ancestor, the eternally suffering Ezraél Jarib, echoed in my head like a broken record spinning out of control, threatening to destroy what fragile fabric remained of my mind and soul. I clutched my heart; I don’t know why, really. It hasn’t been beating for months. Pulling myself up on shaky legs, I looked at my clock-radio and sighed. 3:00 It was always the same. Every goddamn time. I began to regret what I had done, delving into the history of my bloodline. Why should I need to know what happened to my ancestors? They were dead and gone, the only trait they possessed that I could possibly envy. Even though I ask myself these rhetorical questions on a daily basis, I still know the answer. I did it because He wanted me to remember. It’s always as simple as that. I did it because I wanted Jason Snow to know exactly who it was he was dealing with. To know the name of the man who will destroy him. I wanted him to know Iblis. Know him the way I do. For Snow’s sake, I hope he’s the lucky one. I really do.
