IG 73 - Open Invitation - Where are they now? RP's here

17 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Jacob McKail | Underground 5 Months Ago The Bronx, New York With a lit cigarette lodged between his fingers, Jacob McKail drew the bottle of Jack Daniels to his lips and gratefully embraced a generous mouthful of the comforting bourbon. Trying to ignore the fierce stench of piss, he cast his other hand outward and leveraged himself against the filthy stone underside of the bridge, as his body began to rebel against him. He managed to gulp down the booze before being thrown into a fit of coughing and wheezing so hard and so long that everywhere from his chest to his throat cried out in agony. This god-damn life is killing me, he silently grumbled, hammering his chest as hard as he possibly could with a clenched fist. McKail arched his head to one side at the sound and vibration of heavy footsteps and carefully eyed the bulbous figure approaching. It was the man he knew as The Organiser; McKail hadn't bothered to remember his real name. The Organiser chewed the end of a large cheap cigar, his eyes firmly enraptured by the sizeable wad of cash in his hand that he was counting. “You're up, Champ,” he announced, only bothering to shoot McKail a glance as he turned and made his way back over to the already lively crowd. Champ? Yeah, right… McKail nodded and took a final drag of his cigarette before flicking it away haphazardly in the distance. He set his half bottle of Jack Daniels down gently on the floor, before hauling his hooded sweater over his head and throwing it right down on the floor next to his booze. Bare chested, McKail shivered as a bitter winter breeze cut right through him. On some level, he was glad of it - the cold reminded him that he was still alive and it was just about the only thing he could actually fee any more. Through the throng crowd McKail caught sight of his first first opponent for the evening glaring at him with violent intent, bouncing up and down on his toes, cranking his neck and rotating his left arm, warming up for the fight ahead. The cocky prick was grinning like he already had it won; he saw weakness in McKail and he intended to exploit it for all its worth. Good, McKail thought. That should make this a whole lot easier... McKail entered the makeshift fighting circle, created by jeering drunkards wanting to win some easy money and see some bloody violence while they were at it. He intended to oblige them – at least on the latter. This was his life now; fighting for money under some god-awful bridge in the bad part of town, like some kind of bum. There was no honour to be had. There was no glory to be gained. There was only survival - McKail wasn't even sure he wanted that. All he really wanted these days was to make enough money to keep him in booze and smokes for the foreseeable future - nothing more, nothing less. After being blacklisted by the wrestling industry, turning to fighting on the streets for money seemed the most logical career path. Violence was the only damned thing he was ever good at. What the hell else was he going to do? Get a job at Denny's? Fuck. That. *** 7 Months Ago Salt Lake City, Utah The Lightening Kid slammed his locker door shut with some fury, trying to both make a point and to get attention from the other boys in the locker room. The little shit had been in McKail’s face all week about his ‘unprofessional’ behaviour; he’d been talking shit about McKail the rest of the locker room and had even tried to put a complaint in with the head booker. Problem was, this was only a shitty little Indy fed. They were just happy to have a former wrestling superstar on their card. It gave their company that little bit more credibility and it basically gave McKail free reign. He could do whatever the hell he wanted and pretty much everyone knew it. Except The Lightening Kid of course. The Kid glanced around the locker room, hoping that somebody, anybody, would take him on so he could get up on his high horse again and bitch and whine some more. The other boys knew better. They knew having McKail on the card was good for business. McKail decided to incite the little shit some more; he reached over to his travel bag and hauled out a sizeable bottle of Jack Daniels, before unscrewing the lid and downing a quarter of the bottle right then and there. “Of fuck this,” The Kid whined, pointing at McKail and his bourbon. “I gotta fight this asshole tonight!” “So?” One of the other guys questioned. McKail couldn’t quite see who, but he guessed Zebra. For some goddamn reason Zebra looked up to him; saw him as a mentor. But McKail hadn’t taught the kid shit, except of course how to be entirely unprofessional. “The more drunk he gets, the better chance you have of kicking his ass. Why complain?” The Kid rolled his eyes. “Like I need this dick to be drunk to kick his ass! What if he pisses himself in the ring?” “He’s had a little Jack to take the edge off,” Zebra told him. “He ain’t a hobo!” The Kid shook his head and kicked the metal lockers. “This is so damn unprofessional!” McKail stood and headed for the can, making sure to slap the Kid on the back as hard as he could along the way. The Kid flew face-first into the lockers, much to the delight of Zebra and the other boys. McKail grinned. “See you in the ring, tough guy.” *** 5 Months Ago The Bronx, New York The punch connected hard with the side of McKail’s head and the impact caused ringing in his right ear as well as a temporary loss of balance. McKail stumbled backwards into the crowd, who jeered and cursed for all their worth. “You useless fucker!” “Get out there and kill him!” “Stay down you piece of shit!“ “You can’t win this, so don’t even try.” “You oughta be ashamed of yourself!” The crowd pushed him back into the makeshift circle and McKail’s first opponent for the evening, the man who’d been introduced as ‘Lefty’ grinned like a lion stalking its prey. He lunged forward fists first and McKail parried the blow and got the hell out of harms way. Best course of action at the moment was to fight defensively until the ringing in his ear had stopped and he was sure his balance was back to normal. When it came down to it, these are the moments he loved; this was who he was. The thrill of the fight; people cheering him and jeering him. Blood, sweat and tears. The difference between whatever the hell this was and wrestling was the distinct lack of honour and no hope for glory. If he were to make a name for himself on these streets, it would remain on these streets and forgotten within weeks, if not days Lefty attacked again. This time throwing a weak punch as a diversion, following up with a knee to the gut. McKail was just about to avoid it, but was caught off balance when Left capitalized with a kick to the gut. Lefty’s boot only grazed his gut however and McKail was able to strike Lefty in the dead center of his thigh. Lefty’s face contorted in agony and immediately limped away, holding the afflicted appendage and cursing profusely. How the hell did it come to this? McKail silently questioned, checking to see if Lefty’s boot hadn’t left any damage to his gut. How did I fall so far from grace? He already knew the answer – he’d thought of little else since. *** 7 months ago Salt Lake City, Utah The crowd roared as McKail made his way down the aisle and climbed into the ring; they continued to roar even when the referee made his final checks and the bell had rung. He grinned waved at the crowd, noticing The Lightening Kid break from his corner and draw closer. McKail obliged and met him in the center of the ring. The Kid’s face was placid, but his eyes betrayed him; there was anger there, fierce and undeniable. Naturally, McKail decided to make it worse. Whilst it was a good tactic to have your opponent fight angry, that didn’t figure into McKail’s reasoning all that much: he was just a prick and he enjoyed it. * “Hear that, boy?” McKail asked him. “That ain’t for you.” “Like I give a shit,” The Lightening Kid lied. “You’re so goddamn unprofessional it’s ridiculous. It’s no damned wonder that JUST canned your ass!” “Whatever you say, kid.” “Seriously though,” The Kid began, “didn’t you ever wonder why all the big wrestling organizations let you go? You think it was because they all had it in for you.” “You don’t know what you’re talking about, kid.” “The hell I don’t,” The Kid replied. “I was your biggest damn fan, you asshole. I’ve followed your career for about as long as I can remember.” “They say you should never meet your heroes, kid,” McKail told him. “It never ends well.” “No shit,” The Kid replied. “You oughta be ashamed of yourself, man. You went all that way, so close to the top and you threw it all away. Now you’re here, in some crappy Indie fed about to get your ass kicked by a kid half your age.” “Doubtful,” McKail responded. He decided he’d had enough of this bullshit. The rest of that bottle of Jack Daniels he had out in the locker room was calling to him and he was damned if he wasn’t going to obey and succumb to its temptation. “You done flappin’ your gums?” “I know you’re fucked up, Jacob,” The Kid said. “I know all about what’s happened to you and I’m here to tell you that’s no fucking excuse!” “What the fuck would you know about anything?” McKail asked him. “You’re just another punk ass kid who doesn’t know his ass from a toboggan.” “I may not know much, but I know you had a wife,” The Kid replied. “Don’t go there, kid,” McKail warned, already beginning to feel his blood boil. “Or you’ll do what, old man?” The Kid asked. He didn’t wait for any kind of answer. “Have I touched a nerve? Have I finally found something you do care about? How does she feel about what a pitiful state of man you’ve become?” The fans were beginning to get restless. It’d been far too long for this to be something as trivial as trash talk. But neither The Kid nor McKail cared much about that right now. I told you not to go there, kid. McKail grit his teeth and desperately attempted to wrestle his anger under control. With considerable effort he was successful, but he was unsure how long he could keep it down. “She must be ashamed of you, man,” The Kid said. “How could she not be?” McKail glared at him coldly. It was the calm before the storm; there was no stopping it now. “Mary died.” “No wonder, I—“ McKail hit him hard and fast, spearing him to the ground. The crowd roared and The Kid gasped for air. If the fans came for great technical wrestling ability today, they were going to be disappointed. McKail wasted no time in plying the Kid with punches. The crowd was still cheering him, but after thirty seconds their cheers dulled considerably. McKail hadn’t stopped punching The Kid and didn’t look like he was going to stop any time soon. The Kid’s face had become nothing more than a bloodied pulp and McKail’s knuckles had started to bleed. After realising this wasn’t going to stop, the referee tried to tackle McKail off the kid, but he didn’t carry with him enough weight to even budge the much bigger man. Yet, he continued to try and McKail away, just as a dozen or so more officials ran down the aisles to offer assistance. McKail was too far gone in his rage to notice them or even register the kind of damage he was afflicting on the Kid, not to mention the future implications this mauling may have. It took all the officials and even some of the locker room to eventually break McKail away from the Kid and ultimately restrain him. It wasn’t long after that, the wrestling industry decided his actions were not befitting of a professional wrestler and consequently they blacklisted him. McKail didn’t blame them. In truth, he was too engulfed in his own shame to even register their decision. ** Everything the Lightening Kid had said to him was true and he damned well knew it. *** 5 Months Ago The Bronx, New York Lefty hit the floor hard; his lifeless face bounced off the concrete floor, bloodied and broken. McKail had won and the majority of the crowd cheered and chanted his name. But it meant nothing. There was no honour in what he was doing. He’d won them a couple of bucks and that was all. His victory didn’t mean any more to them than that. The Organiser grinned from ear to ear and waddled over to him, pressing a wad of notes into his hands. “You did great, man!” He beamed. “You up for another one?” “Yeah,” McKail agreed, wiping blood from the corner of his mouth. However, as the cheering crowd began to settle another noise hummed in the distance, getting ever closer. Goddamn it! “Is that what I think it is?” The Organiser questioned, his voice laced with fear. “The cops?” McKail said. “I reckon so.” “Oh shit!” The Organiser exclaimed. “I gotta get outta here! C’mon man!” With that, The Organiser made a break for the other end of the bridge. It was the fastest McKail had ever seen him run. The rest of the crowd caught wind soon after, realising the sirens were indeed closing in on their direction. They bundled past him, begging McKail to join them. But he wasn’t interested. He was sick of this life, but left to his own devices he’d carry on doing the same damned thing tomorrow; getting himself all liquored in the hopes he’d forget enough to be able to make it to the next day. McKail had never faced his demons he always ran from them. Everything from the death of his wife to failing as a professional wrestler haunted him and instead of dealing with it, he’d always preferred to forget about it. But that was getting harder to do and this downward spiral he was on was going to come crashing down any day unless he did something about it. Getting himself arrested seemed to be a good idea as any.
