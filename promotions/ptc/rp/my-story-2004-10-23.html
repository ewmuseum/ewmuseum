My Story

23 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-23 When I was younger I didn’t exactly have the best of lives. I grew up in a rough place on the outskirts of New York, the harsh gangs, the racism, and the prejudice that filled up my life, that convinced me to work as a professional wrestler, all so that I could make people know not to mess with me. And the story that convinced me to be a wrestler? Well, here it is… You see, I always had this soft spot for chicks. I was always making like Sir Galahad, opening doors for them and complimenting even the homely ones, and I beat out a lot of guys better looking than me and they never could figure out why. But it wasn’t just a line with me, I guess I’m a sucker – I’ve been taken a few times, like ‘loaning’ money to chicks who came on with a sob story – But I’ll always believe the best about a girl until I’m proved wrong, which is my own hang up. That explains the way I acted that night the gang and me were hanging around the drugstore and this black chick came in to buy some cigarettes. Me, I just saw a nice-looking chick with really beautiful eyes, all black and inky-soft. I guess I’m a little funny that way, because black people just don’t get me upset like they did the gang I hung around with. I mean, I can see a black guy and a white chick together, and it sure don’t bother me, while most white guys couldn’t stand to see that when I was young. Like the gang I hung around with, the minute she walked in, they got all tensed up because black anyone, chick or otherwise, just didn’t happen to come around much, if at all, where I lived. I guess she worked downtown and got off late and just stopped in on her way to the bus stop. I think she told me that later, I don’t remember too well now. So she gets her cigarettes and starts for the door, when a couple of guys block her way. Now the gang I hung with were a pretty good bunch of guys – a lot of heart and only a couple of wise apples in the group – but see, nothing much had been happening, and they were bored so they start picking on the chick, calling her what I now know are pathetic names such as Black Beauty and some other choice things. They were really getting rude, and I was feeling sorry for the girl. She kept her eyes down and just said, “Let me by, please,” real soft-like. The guys started pushing her around, not enough to hurt her but enough to scare her plenty. She just gripped her purse with both hands and tensed all over like she was trying to keep from running, which was pretty smart. Running is just an invitation to be chased, and if she got caught it wouldn’t be in a lighted drugstore. The old guy who ran the drugstore had disappeared. He was scared silly of the gang. I don’t know why. We never did anything to him. When one of the guys grabbed hold of her and really got crude, I got fed up. I went over and said, “Let her go,” like I meant it. They all looked at me for a while, like they were trying to make up their minds whether or not to jump me. We didn’t usually go around beating each other up, but it has happened. They finally decided not to. My big brother, he had a pretty big rep as a tough guy in our neighbourhood. He was in jail then, that’s why he didn’t come to see me. It was his rep and not mine that stopped them, because I wasn’t ever known as being a tough guy. So they turned her loose and went back to reading comics, and I followed the girl outside. She was looking up and down the street, kind of desperate-like, and I knew she’d missed her bus. I said, “Hey, uh, girl, if you’ve missed your bus I can give you a ride home.” She just kept her eyes down. Finally she said something – but brother, I’m not going to repeat it. I saw there and then she thought I had evil intentions. I didn’t blame her. Hell, if I’d had to take what she just did, I’d be sore and suspicious too. I said, “Look, I don’t want a pick-up or anything…” She gave me a funny look so I added quick, “Not that you’re not real cute or anything – I mean, you’ll have to stay here another hour to catch the next bus and I’ll be leaving, and I don’t know what those other guys might do.” She saw the logic in that, because it was getting dark. Not too many cops came around that area; it was kind of a deserted street. You know how cops were; there was a million over on the ribbon, making sure the nice kids didn’t kill each other or run each other down, while we could cut each other’s throats and they didn’t give a damn. Finally she said she’d let me drive her home. I had my old ford parked in the drugstore parking lot. It was really my brother’s car but he said I could drive it any time he got busted, which is often. He was a pretty good guy, but if you had a rep for fighting, somebody would always be trying to take you on. The last time that happened, my brother busted a bottle over the guy’s head and got charged with assault with a dangerous weapon. He never used weapons before, but he had finally got fed up with the whole routine. It wasn’t his first offence, so they sat on him kind of hard. Anyway, we get into my ford, and I can see the poor kid is still scared – she sits hugging the door on her side like she’s going to jump out any second. I got a couple of good looks at her; she was real slender, looked like she’d sort of sway in the wind, and her hair was down to her shoulders and it must have been straightened. She had on a yellow dress and yellow shoes and she had her straw purse sitting on her lap. She held on to it with one hand and the door handle with the other. She really was cute. I started talking to her about just everything. Would her old lady chew her out because she was late? My old lady would. Man, they never liked anything you did, did they? But still, sometimes you couldn’t get along without them. Did she go to school? I did but, boy, it was really a hell of a place to spend all day, I wanted to drop out but the old lady said she’d kill me if I did. I kept talking because that’s what I do with animals when they’re hurt or scared, and pretty soon they get over being scared. I’ve got a hang up with animals, too. I could tell she was beginning to calm down a little, at least she let go of the door handler. I even got her to smile once. I forget what I’d been saying. And then I said, “I’m sorry about what happened to you back there,” and suddenly she started to cry. Man, that got me so shook. Nothing got me shook like chicks crying. Well, I didn’t what to say to her then. I finally decided on “Hey, don’t cry,” which never does any good. She kept on sobbing and now and then I’d catch a word or two. I got the idea she was fed up with getting walked all over by white people. I could see that. I got fed up with getting walked over by ‘the fuzz’, teachers, my old man, and the upper-class kids at school. So I could see that what she was saying was fair. This chick, she tells me about her problems. And she uses some pretty bad language but nothing I hadn’t heard before. I finally pulled the car over to the curb and reached into my pocket. She sat up and straight and got all uptight. “What we stopping for?” she says, and I said, “I thought I had a handkerchief, but I guess I don’t.” I pulled back out on the street. She looked at me for a minute – I kept staring straight ahead but I could tell she was watching me – and she said, “Thank you.” I drove her home. She lived way out on the north said where most of the black people lived back then. It was a pretty lousy neighbourhood, but it was about as bad as mine. As I pulled up in front of her house, I could see a bunch of kids hanging around on her porch and in yard. “Well here you are,” I said, a little nervous. For somebody who’d been practising in her mind how to get the door open, she was pretty slow about getting out. That’s how it seemed to me, anyway. I think she was tired out from crying so much. Then, out of nowhere, there were all these black kids around my car. Some big guy opened the girl’s door and pulled her out and said, “What’s the matter, Connie? What happened?” You could tell she’d been crying. Then they opened my door and dragged me out. It seemed like there were a hundred faces staring at me. I guess it was just really about a dozen, but it sure seemed like a hundred. I just stood there, backed up against the car. Talk about scared – man, was I scared. To top it off, the chick had started crying again so she couldn’t talk. [A pause as Odium stares into the distance of the close up room, unable to identify where he is, just sitting on the floor. And when he started talking again, it was as if he was living the whole thing over again.] The big guy came around to my side of the car. “You hurt her, white boy?” “No,” I said, and it didn’t sound very loud so I cleared my throat and said, “No, I didn’t,” so loud that it sounded like I was shouting. It was real quiet; you could hear somebody’s TV from down the street and a dog barking a street away and Connie’s soft sobbing. I could even hear my heart pounding in my ears. Then the big guy said, really quietly, “What if we don’t believe you?” And I got so scared I was about to cry and said, “Ask her, huh, just ask her!” The guy called across the car, “Connie, what you want me to do with this white boy?” And real soft – her voice was so soft, just like her eyes- she said, “Kill the white bastard.” And sure enough, they almost did. <><><><><><><><><><><<><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> There was a long silence. Odium had forgotten that he was alone, imagining if people were watching him. Then he took a long breath. “That’s how I got here. I must’ve been a dumb kid like the old man said so. Dave Hurst is my next obstacle, this story keeps me focussed, inspired to do well, and you can be damn sure that I will remember this in our match.” “Because as long as I have a drop of blood left in my body, as long as I am able to sweat, as long as I have air to breathe… … I. Will. Not. Back. Down.”
