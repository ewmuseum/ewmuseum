PTC — Roleplay

Guilt and Gullibility

30 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“What was that? You want to know what’s in my bag?” [Absently swirling his finger around the rim of a full glass of warm beer, Vincent McArthur cracks a small smile as he nods to the man sitting two seats to his left. The room was poorly illuminated and reeked of half a dozen different brands of bottom-shelf cologne mixed into one foul cloud of noxious musk that could choke a dead horse. Most late-night pubs had that smell; it came with the territory, especially on the south side where folks tended to bathe themselves in commercial perfume with more regularity than actual tap water. Tonight however, Vincent was oblivious to the seedy scents of his surroundings, his attention instead focused on the mysterious stranger sitting alone at the bar top.] Vincent: I’m sorry, mister. I don’t mean to be nosey, but people don’t usually swagger in here with a Giorgio Armani attache case under their arm. You a lawyer or something? [The stranger responds with a glare that suddenly makes Vincent feel uneasy. Dressed in a finely-pressed blue business shirt with the sleeves casually rolled up to his elbows, the stranger certainly looks like he just stepped out of a GQ Magazine, yet somehow managed to land in the middle of this suburban slum without so much as scuffing the polish on his Italian leather shoes.] [Turning his attention back to the rows of bottles set up behind the bar, the stranger puts his own glass to his lips before responding.] Stranger: First of all, I don’t swagger. [He takes a healthy drink, then puts the glass to rest beside the black bound briefcase before inclining his head back toward Vincent.] Stranger: Secondly, it isn’t polite to interrupt a man in the middle of minding his own business. Vincent: Like I said, I’m sorry to bother you… Stranger: But it just so happens that the contents of this briefcase belong to you. [Vincent’s sputtering apology dies instantly on his lips, replaced by a look that manages to be both incredulous and amused. Fighting back the urge to laugh, Vincent instead shakes his head respectfully.] Vincent: You’ve got me all wrong, man. I’m not a beggar, and I’m not looking for trouble. Stranger: Open it. Vincent: Are you serious? Stranger: As cancer. Open it. [The stranger’s suddenly piercing gaze seems to swell with an inner pleasure that once again makes Vincent feel uncomfortable, but the feeling quickly passes. Why should he be afraid of some arrogant drunk? The man was obviously on some sort of medication - legal or otherwise - and it was best to simply play along.] [Vincent shoots a quick glance at the stout little bartender on the other side of the room, but he was too busy flirting with a local hooker to take much notice. Inhaling sharply, he grudgingly reaches for the case.] Vincent: All right, then. You convinced me. [Stretching down the length of the polished bar top, Vincent slides the black case his way to rest next to his lukewarm beer. The stranger watches his movements carefully, the corners of his mouth turned up into the first whisperings of a wry grin. Vincent ignores his penetrating stare as he unlatches the two clasps holding the case shut, swinging it wide open…] […and slams it shut almost immediately. Stuffed to the brim with stacks of unmarked hundred-dollar bills, Vincent suddenly shies away from the black briefcase as if it had grown teeth.] Vincent: Are you insane? You could get yourself killed running around with a stash like that! Stranger: That’s the idea. Vincent: …are you on medication, man? [The stranger sighs deeply, absently running one hand through his manicured mane of thick brown curls. Trying to create himself an exit, Vincent starts to drink down his beer while inching away from the oiled perfection of the black briefcase and its curious contents. Blood money or drug money, Vincent didn’t care either way; that money was not for him.] Stranger: Oh, but it is. [Vincent stops dead, his heart pounding in his ears like a primal war drum ready to explode.] Stranger: Oh, it was all over your face. “This guy is nuts,” that’s what you were thinking, right? That someone either died or overdosed to get this kind of cash, something along those lines? [Vincent tries to form an intelligible response, but all he can manage to do is breathe on himself.] Stranger: Seven hundred sixty-three thousand dollars, my friend. And it’s all yours… for a price. [For a price.] [Words that Vincent had expected, certainly, but they still sounded dangerous. Especially now, coming from this mysterious man who was either filthy rich or completely insane. Or both.] [Either way, Vincent finally manages to find his voice once more.] Vincent: At what price? [The stranger leans in close, his eyes locking with Vincent’s in a seductive dance of lunacy.] Stranger: You see that hooker over there? The one the bartender seems to be getting to know quite well? Vincent: Yeah, so? [The stranger grins.] Stranger: I want you to kill her. ~<>~<>~<>~ Two hours earlier… [Chicago.] [The Windy Sin City.] [Somewhere between the far-off slamming of a screen door and the piercing wail of an ambulance siren, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins swears he can hear the verse of a nursery rhyme from his childhood drifting across the placid evening breeze.] Not last night but the night before, Twenty-four robbers came knockin’ at my door. As I ran out, they ran in Then I hit them on the head with a rolling pin. [Carried along on the distant voices of faceless children, the sing-song tune builds in volume in The Renegade’s head until he subconsciously begins to hum the melody himself. Tightening the knot keeping his red bandana snug against his scalp, Rollins wonders not for the first time today whether it was worth getting up at all.] [Anything to break the tedium, he constantly tells himself.] [Sitting on the top stair leading to a rickety back porch lost in the middle of Chicago’s south-side slums, The Renegade absently caresses the tender skin around his jaw and cheekbone. Four days ago he had been sitting ringside at PRIME’s ReVolution at Madison Square Garden, trying to gauge the competition leading into PTC’s Infinite Gauntlet when all hell broke loose. Rollins was intimately introduced to the business end of a catering table - that is to say, any side that hurts and still doesn’t break - courtesy of “The Specialist” Tony Rolo. The scene had been playing on repeat in Rollins’ mind ever since, and despite having a vivid imagination, there was only one conclusion The Renegade could come up with: ] [PTC has officially gone to war.] [With itself.] Voice: So, the rebel with a cause finds himself on my back porch once again, huh? How lovely. [Startled, The Renegade jerks himself out of his morbid daydream and stands erect, turning on his heels to face the newcomer. Leaning in the open doorway with his arms casually crossed at his chest, Mexico’s best kept secret shoots Rollins a mischievous smirk.] Renegade: It’s been longer than I’d have liked, Julian. For that I’m sorry. [Julian Sanchez , perhaps better known as El Poeta (translation: The Poet) in most Mexican wrestling circuits, merely shrugs. His accent isn’t as thick as it used to be; years of American television had done wonders for his phonetics and pronunciation.] Sanchez: Your priorities have been different in the last couple of years, Rollins. It’s not like the old days, not like when we were twenty years old and full of ambition. Hell, can you see yourself moving to Mexico at the drop of a hat like you did when you came looking for a partner? Renegade: I wasn’t looking for some training dummy, Julian. I was looking for you. Sanchez: Yeah, but we sure crash tested the hell out of one another, didn’t we? [Despite himself, The Renegade can’t help but chuckle. Memories invade his head like a flash flood, filling his mind with sensations of years past… and then they vanish as if they never were. A lifetime ago under the Mexican sun, they were nothing more than fond memories etched into the dark recesses of a troubled man’s brain.] Sanchez: Why don’t we have a seat and talk like civilized people? [Motioning for Rollins to follow, Julian Sanchez walks to the far side of his porch and drops down into the remains of a dirty old couch, spilling an army of dust motes into the fading Chicago sunset as his weight rests down on the moth-eaten mattresses. The Renegade obliges, depositing himself onto a simple green-and-white thatched plastic folding chair opposite Julian.] [A handful of heartbeats pass in complete silence, neither man quite sure what to say. Finally, The Renegade speaks his mind.] Renegade: I’m gunnin’ for the PTC Global title, Julian. Sanchez: I know. I saw your little adventure at MSG a few days ago on television. That took some balls to show up at a PRIME event. Renegade: Why? Because they’re better than I am? Because I can’t… Sanchez: Did I say that? You should do more listening and less talking, signore. It’s not one of your stronger qualities. [Rich Rollins frowns, but he knows Julian has a point. It’s almost the uncanny cosmic joke of Rollins’ entire life: the more talking he did, the more toes he stepped on. It was both a gift and a curse in a business such as professional wrestling, but it was impossible to control. Most times he preferred to just let his lips run and hang on for the ride, but now was not one of those times.] [Julian clears his throat.] Sanchez: I meant that PRIME isn’t the bush leagues, Rich. You aren’t used to the rate at which they operate, plain and simple. On top of that, it’s not like you’re invisible to those people, either… Angelo Deville punked you on national television, for Christ‘s sake! They’re out for blood, and they won’t stop until they get it. Renegade: Then I’ll be happy to disappoint, to say the least. I was at ReVolution for one sole purpose, Julian… the PTC Global Championship. It’s the only thing I have left to accomplish in this business, the last mountain to climb… Sanchez: … and true challenge to face, yadda yadda yadda. Do you realize you sound like a broken record, Rollins? You sound like any and every rookie who ever laced up the boots and stepped into the squared circle, and that’s what you need to focus on. You’ve got so much talent that it scares me, but the only thing holding you back from exploding in the ring like a true wrestling poet is your inability to lift your attitude, signore. Renegade: There’s only room for one wrestling poet in this business, Julian. I’m just trying to get out of his shadow. [Peering deeply into The Renegade’s eyes, Julian snorts in amusement.] Sanchez: I cast no shadow, signore. The shadow you refer to is merely the rock you refuse to crawl out from under. [For the first time in recent memory, Rich Rollins is forced to look away from another’s gaze. It was usually the opposite; most people found his constant, penetrating stare to be discomforting , but for once it is he who shies away.] [Shame isn’t the right word, but it’s the first one that comes to mind.] Renegade: I have to go, Julian. Sanchez: I know. You’re always leaving, Richard. Always leaving, and never finishing what you’ve started. [His voice dropping to a tone that rings with an aggressive self-loathing, The Renegade looks at Julian Sanchez once more.] Renegade: Not this time. This time, I finish the whole damn thing. I promise you that. ~<>~<>~<>~ One hour and thirty-seven minutes later… “You… you want me to what?!” [Hiding his emotions behind a mask of cold indifference, the stranger sizes up Vincent with a brief nod of his head as he takes another drink. Meeting Vincent’s incredulous stare with one of calculated assurance, the stranger continues on as if nothing had transpired.] Stranger: Do you come to this pub often, Vincent? Vincent: How did you know my name…? Stranger: You looked like a Vincent. The question was if you consider yourself a regular. Vincent: Well, you can find me here most every Saturday… I suppose that’s regular enough. [The stranger nods once, acknowledging the hooker at the end of the bar with his eyes.] Stranger: You see that tramp, Vincent? Vincent: Yeah. Stranger: That disease-ridden hussy, you see her? Vincent: I said I saw her, okay? Stranger: Well pay attention, because I’ve got seven hundred sixty-three thousand dollars that says you could save a man’s life tonight. [His mind desperately trying to keep up with the words spilling from the stranger’s mouth, Vincent noticeably tries to regain his composure as he too draws deeply from the beer in front of him. His ears listening but his eyes elsewhere, he probes.] Vincent: How… how does killing that woman save anybody? Stranger: Hooker, Vincent. Hooker, please… if that’s a woman by anybody’s standards we’re all fucked, and not in the proper sense. You see, I happen to know for a fact that her name is Gwen Webber, but most of her “customers” know here simply as Diamond. And when a diamond sleeps in the rough as long as she has… well, let’s just say that the air downstairs isn’t exactly fresh, if you catch my meaning. Vincent: So what, she has AIDS or something? Stranger: To say the least. And your good friend the bartender over there is about to sink his teeth into more than he’s probably willing to chew. [Vincent shakes his head violently.] Vincent: No. No, I won’t do it. Stranger: Seven hundred sixty-three thousand dollars. Vincent: Absolutely not. Stranger: One less tramp bitch infecting our streets, Vincent. Vincent: I… why are you doing this? Stranger: Because somebody has to, Vincent. Somebody always has to be the bad guy. [Burying his head in his hands, Vincent slumps over on the bar top. A sudden sense of guilt ambushes his brain, and try as he might he can’t force himself to look over at the bartender and his cheap date. ] [The stranger moves closer, his voice nothing more than a whisper in Vincent’s ear.] Stranger: You have the chance to save lives, Vincent. For every customer that tramp services, it’s another hundred dead men walking. Do you want to have those men on your conscience? Do you think they’d look poorly upon you if they knew that this satin-and-lace prostitute handed them their own death wish… Renegade: Why Steven Caldera… I didn’t think you’d actually show up. [The “stranger” almost falls off his barstool, jerking around to face the newcomer with a livid countenance that shoots daggers at Rich “The Renegade” Rollins.] [Strolling up to the bar and plopping himself down in the space between Vincent (who is now watching Rollins like a hawk) and the owner of Global Championship Wrestling, The Renegade doesn’t skip a beat as he fondles the black leather briefcase and unclasps the straps.] Renegade: I figured you’d just write a check, but I’m not complainin’. [Rollins opens the case wide, and a wry grin soon follows.] Renegade: Everything looks in order. (he notices the strange expression on Vincent‘s face) I’m sorry bub, do I know you? Vincent: That’s your money? Renegade: (he laughs) Yeah, it is. [All Vincent can do is stare blankly. His focus drifts between the satchel of cash and Steven Caldera, the mysterious stranger, who’s face has turned a decidedly deep red.] Caldera: God damnit, Rollins! I was in the middle of a conversation! Vincent: You… you’re a sick bastard. Jesus Christ, what was that all about? Renegade: What was what all about? [Vincent’s eyes threaten to well up, but he retains control over his emotions long enough to collect his thoughts. Still chatting at the other end of the bar, the bartender and his prostitute continue their idle conversation.] She has no idea how close she came, Vincent muses to himself… and immediately feels the beginning pangs of a guilt he had never known. Vincent: Mr. Caldera there made me an offer I almost couldn’t refuse. Caldera: Almost? [Vincent shoots him a look that would flay the hide off of an elephant.] Vincent: He told me the money was mine. Renegade: If? [Vincent releases a breath he hadn’t been aware he had been holding.] Vincent: If I killed that girl at the end of the bar. [The Renegade’s eyes furrow as he takes a look at the woman for himself.] [And immediately begins to laugh.] Renegade: Who, Shelly? She’s the bartender’s daughter. Vincent: He told me she was a disease-ridden prostitute, and that killing her would save the lives of every man she would have slept with. He also said her name was Gwen something-or-other. [Mindlessly twiddling his thumbs on the bar top, Steven Caldera meets Rollins’ quizzical stare with a smug grin.] Caldera: I was fucking with you, Vincent. Rollins here is usually fashionably late, so I decided to amuse myself. Besides, you asked me what was inside the bag. [Pushing his half-finished drink aside, Caldera doesn’t even offer so much as an apologetic nod to Vincent as his attention turns full force on Rich Rollins.] Caldera: Sorry for the inconvenience Vincent, but I have some business to attend to. [Catching the curt dismissal, Vincent McArthur collects his beer and excuses himself, heading down the bar to sulk in his new wave of guilt.] Renegade: That wasn’t very funny, Caldera. That guy is in here every week, and scaring off the regulars isn’t a good way to make friends. [Caldera offers a shrug, nothing more.] Caldera: He was acting like an asshole. I was just pressing his buttons. (he straightens up) Regardless, I hope we have a deal. I didn’t drag myself into this cesspool for nothing, did I? [Propping one elbow against the bar, The Renegade casually leans over the black briefcase like a vulture over its prey. His eyes grow distant; indeed, Rich Rollins’ entire demeanor changes like a roaring tide as a sinister tone begins to find its way into his voice.] Renegade: All I want is John Edwards. Nothing more, nothing less. Caldera: Moraless? Renegade: (he nods) He was the only man who managed to pin me down in the UWF, and considering our history all the way back to GCW, I have a score to settle. Period. Caldera: So you’ll compete in the Infinity Gauntlet, then? [The Renegade snorts, a sound that Caldera had learned to loathe over the years. It usually meant Rollins was about to say something condescending, and the look in The Renegade’s eyes don’t suggest this time will be different. Motioning to the briefcase, Rollins responds.] Renegade: Seven hundred sixty-three thousand dollars, to the cent. Right? Caldera: Correct. Renegade: Might as well be blood money. Caldera: Why do you say that? Renegade: Because for this kind of cash, the other PTC hopefuls won’t even know what hit ‘em. As soon as I finish with Edwards, the next conversation we’ll be having will involve setting the date for my PTC Global Title shot. [A slow smile snakes across Caldera’s lips. Pulling himself to his feet, the owner of Global Championship Wrestling extends his Armani-swathed arm toward The Renegade, his palm outstretched.] Caldera: That’s all I needed to hear. It’s time we showed these bastards what GCW is all about. [Rollins regards his bosses’ hand as he would a coiled snake, his nose twitching in irritation.] Renegade: I’m not your friend, Caldera. I’m just an employee. [Caldera’s smile quickly drops, as does his hand. Dusting his lapel with an indignant flourish, Steven Caldera turns his back to Rich Rollins, heading for the dimly lit corridor that passes as an entrance.] Caldera: Don’t disappoint me, Rollins. You may find yourself working for the UWF by year’s end. [The Renegade suppresses a sardonic grunt as Steven Caldera exits the pub with little fanfare.] Renegade: So what else is new? [Finally alone, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins sighs deeply. Gently touching the oiled leather of the briefcase, his eyes flicker with a careful consideration that isn’t anything new to his unshaven features. After what could have been no more than two minutes, he finally grabs the case. Renegade: Fuck it. [Giving the briefcase a good shove, he sends it sliding down the bar top. Reaching the end of the bar, it just so happens to tip over the remainder of Vincent McArthur’s beer.] Vincent: What the…? Renegade: Keep the change, kid. [And with that Rich Rollins dismisses himself from the bar, leaving a room full of wide-eyed winos and drunks in his wake.] “We are what we do repeatedly. Excellence is therefore not an act, but a habit.” -Aristotle ~Fin~
