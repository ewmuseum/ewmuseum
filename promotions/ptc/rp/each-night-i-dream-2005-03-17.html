Each Night I Dream

17 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-03-17 Blackness, all around, its monotony broken only by a sharp jab of pain lancing through his mind. An ethereal, yet raspy and echoed voice slices through the pain and the air like a supernatural phenomenon. Like a ghost screaming through reality’s bulwarks. ‘Desire is a state of madness,’ proclaims the apparition with disdain. ‘It functions in a space devoid of logic.’ There is a faint sense of familiarity through the haze and dizziness. Eyes try to open like ancient and rusty doors, but they fail and fall closed once more, catching only the tiniest glimpse of a blurred black and white figure. But then vision, and the voice, trail off and fade into more blackness. The sound of a door opening compels those rusty eyes to try to open once more, and again they behold a blurred figure, dressed in black with a face as white as death, and a voice to match. ‘It operates without analysis or explanation,’ it continues. ‘It is beyond language and beyond reason. Somewhere inside the void from where it originates lurks a nefarious feeling of pleasure, or the promise of it, that distorts reasoning. In this place any superstition, no matter how strong or weak can become powerful enough to overthrow the familiar parameters of duty and sanity.’ Then vision becomes encysted in darkness once again. ‘It is the worst pain imaginable, having desire be denied.’ The ghostly voice reappears through the dark, disturbing the safety that it betrays. ‘But pain is our teacher. It is our saviour. To feel it slither through us, snaking all the way into our souls, is to know beauty. But what is mere beauty, without the conjuring of desire to ferment it?’ A hand touches his face, warm and soothing, and then slides off. A door closes soon after somewhere nearby. He whimpers, like a beast caught in a bone-crushing trap. Still he tries with all his being to wake, but still he cannot overcome the obstacle of unconsciousness, and the ghostly voice that haunts him. ‘You understand how torturous our world is, and thus how ingenious, or perhaps devious, champions of truth must become in order to fight.’ As devious as himself, perhaps? ‘Inside you are two different powers vying for control. A force for desire and a force for rage. Both lurk, waiting to burrow like hunger-fuelled maggots out of your head at even the slightest encouragement…’ But could it be, he wonders, that it is possible, somehow, to balance the two forces? Is it possible to be simultaneously possessed by both powers, and therefore fully by neither? Could it be, he wonders, that there could be jealous conflict between rival daemons? A war in one’s very soul. Thus the daemons would mutually disable one another, allowing their intended victim to squirm free to liberation and insusceptibility. Could it be? ~ Though the winds edge it somewhat whetted by the river, still it cuts like razors, screaming as it slices. The clammy dampness of the draught bites at him as he walks scrupulously forward watching for any sign of danger. Pausing in hopelessness at the task ahead, he peers downward gazing into the grey and empty waiting void, and tries to cast his thoughts away from the nebulous nature of reality and destiny. ‘Everyone’s destiny is death,’ he whispers into the nothingness below the rope bridge upon which he gingerly treads. The bridge, is a bystander to this and has known too many spells of turbulence and blizzard weather. Its cast iron girders always held firm in the face of such elements and steadfast in the darkness. It remains a silent and towering witness to acts of nature and deeds of men alike. But the bridge cares not for the feelings of man or nature. Simply, it stays as it is, concerned only with its purpose. The bridge cares not about life, or about destiny. It is what it is and no more. But Deszaras cannot walk any further. The feeling of hopelessness dawns around him, a realisation that seemed to flood his mind with unwanted light. He feels as though he should let the gusting wind take him away with its breeze as though he were litter, used and empty and discarded, to land only where nature allows him to rest. Looking forward, he sees the bridge disappearing into the mist, a never-ending obstacle of will – an impossible challenge, yet he has come far too far to turn back. So, frivolously and vacantly he walks on toward his unknown fate beyond the mist and beyond the seemingly endless bridge until he is forced to stop once more. Not through sorrow this time, but through obstruction. Before him stands Dhar, looking down at his feet upon the bridge, the wind rustling his hair and coat, making them flap dementedly. Sluggishly, Dhar’s head rises to stare at Deszaras. ‘Desire, Deszaras. Desire will be the end of you.’ ‘What are you talking about?’ he asks despondently. ‘Have I not warned you already? Have I not told you before of the consequences of your actions and thoughts? I have no idea what the actual medical term is for whatever type of crazy you have, but it’s very self-destructive and self-absorbed. You made me through one act of thwarted desire. Just one, powerful though such as it was. Yet still you tempt fate once more. Still you poke and prod where you should not. Your passions for harming yourself are magnificent.’ ‘My self is my own, to do with as I please.’ ‘And this is what you want to happen? You want another me inside you? I was made to fix you, Deszaras, yet every time I try to make you better you fight me more and more and dig yourself in deeper. You walk across the path of every landmine you can think of… and why? How could a man possibly hate himself this much?’ ‘Everyone gets what they deserve, in the end.’ Dhar scoffs. ‘Is that what this is for you? The end?’ ‘The day dies at night. It always has and always will.’ ‘This is a different day.’ Suddenly, the wind gets much stronger, shaking the rope bridge. Deszaras grabs hold of it to steady himself, but Dhar remains as he is, silent. ‘Most days you sit, waiting for death. Once in your life you prayed for a miracle. Then you got one. And what did you make of it? What did you create out of your second chance? Nothing! You’re just a useless sack of shit!’ Thunder roars into life from the dark clouds above, and rain begins to fall. The wet patters off his mask, making vicious sound. ‘Whether we win the fight or not, Deszaras, is not for us to decide. For man, there is only fighting. The outcome is up to the higher powers that control the war. All we can do is fight to the end, to the death and until our souls run dry. We may never win, of course, but at least that way we can ensure that we never, ever lose.’ ‘But if it really looks that bleak, why do we keep fighting?’ Dez must shout, now, to be heard over the sky’s banshee-call. Dhar laughs. ‘Why not, Deszaras?’ Maybe we’ll win. Maybe some of us will survive to tell the tail. Maybe we’ll restore some honour to the world.’ ‘That’s a lot of maybes.’ sighs Deszaras. ‘Maybe it is,’ replies Dhar as he turns around slowly and walks into the mist, disappearing into the nullity beyond. Then an almighty crack out of the stratosphere and a bolt of lighting forks into existence, stabbing through the sky and hitting Deszaras. Suddenly, he feels as if he is chained down under water, and no matter how hard he swims, he cannot move to reach the surface. Help me… His skin feels like it’s being eaten, a thousand tiny bites at a time. I can’t move… He screams within his mind, but no one can hear him, and the bridge rocks uncontrollably. I can’t wake up… His lungs burst inside him. Then, at last, he explodes awake, gasping for air. A scream tears its way out of his throat. ~ They march down the street, signs held high and their sanctimony spewing freely from their mouths as naturally as breathing would be to anyone else. Their footsteps clacking in unison, a military of hatred and opinion. Kiri was reluctant to come here, to witness this abomination, but Deszaras was intent on studying the pious madness of a multitude possessed by ideal to the point of derangement where injuries or deaths would be as nothing. There were lessons to be learned here about passion, obsession and possession. About derangement of the senses and of the soul. Arms folded, he surveys the scene through his darkened eyes. Humanity has endured its long existence in a dangerous world through a handful of various different reasons. One of those reasons, and perhaps the strongest of them all, is the tendency to form tribes. Sharing beliefs and goals is one of the best ways to ensure survival, having zealots to a cause watch each other’s backs. And most tribes in humanity’s history have been guilty of mistaking ideas for something else. Kiri scowls, though still she does not manage to discredit or hide her beauty. ‘What are we doing here, Dez? These things are always so… so…’ ‘Hideous?’ he says, as much finishing her statement as wondering what she meant. Still he lurks in the darkness, watching the sickening parade of religious nuts, feeling a sickening tickle in his throat. ‘Is religion so weak that they must do this any and every chance they get? Must they take every single opportunity to try to convince themselves of how pious they are? And, more than this, why do the laity take such great pleasure in stomping on everyone’s happiness? Why do they enjoy telling people that they will go to hell for liking things, or thinking things, or watching, playing listening or doing certain things? Must the word of their God be so… dull? …So low and wretched?’ He turns to look at Kiri for a short while, before returning his glower to the mass. ‘Or perhaps it’s simply jealousy. Jealousy of people who live their lives how they want to. Jealousy that some don’t need the crutch of religion to help them walk through life. These people are so weak, in will and mind, that they use religion as an excuse not to experience new things in case it hurts them somehow, or in case they feel vulnerable. They fail to enjoy themselves, therefore hiding behind religion gives these people all the excuse they need. And, through this, they want everyone else to be just like them, so as not to feel alone. So maybe it isn’t through some malicious act of misguided posturing that they do this… but through some pitiful form of reaching out and asking for help.’ ‘I think,’ she says, ‘that there’s been enough learned here today. Let’s go, hmm? You’ve got a big event to prepare for.’ Deszaras backs away, yet still stares at the mob of protesters. Here they march, protesting an album of a band for its lewd cover and offensive lyrics. They call this band the work of the devil. Yet they still don’t understand, while it’s a source of fame for them, it’s also great advertising for the product or piece of art that they protest against. For every single comment they make on it, there becomes a dozen people with whom it sparks interest. No publicity is bad publicity, as they say. And still he wonders. How could the human race become so pathetic? ‘Pathetic… just like you.’ he whispers to himself. And, just then, he thinks he can hear distant laughter…
