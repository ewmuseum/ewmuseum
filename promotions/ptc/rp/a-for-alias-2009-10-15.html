Date: 2009-10-15 “What do you ask the Original Pulp Hero?” That was the question on the mind of Russel David, as he softly said it aloud. Now, for a man with David’s reputation and pedigree when it came to wrestling journalism, you’d think this would be an easy question to answer. David would simply approach the current FWO Tag Team Champion, and recent ACW Scorpion Fighting Champion, who had recently entered GTT7… and ask him how he was, how his match went. It was easy as that, right? Rumor had it that Alias wasn’t one for small talk though, and he even once taped a reporters hands behind his back and booted him in the ass out of his locker room, for not asking a decent question in the first five minutes of an interview. If that was to be believed, he was a worse interview then Robert De Niro. David slipped his pen and paper back into his lightly weathered leather jacket, and sighed as he ran a hand through his short-cut and neatly kept curly hair. He peered over his black-rimmed glasses, down the hall of the All-State Arena toward the makeshift EMT set-up. That was were Russel David was told, would be the best place to find Alias, and if he were lucky (or unlucky depending on her mood) Karina Wolfenden. David was also told that Alias was always his most talkative after a match, something about how when Pulp Hero punched people in the head it got his own mind racing with all sorts of ideas on things. That’s at least what they said, so Russel hoped it was the truth. Especially with some of the interesting bits of information that David had found in researching for this interview. He took a moment to plug an iTalk Voice Recorder into his iPod. The iPod, was it seemed, about four generations old as it didn’t have any fancy shine or shape to it… or even a video screen. But that’s how David liked it. He wanted this interview to go as smoothly as possible, and scratching on his notepad through out it would just prove to be a distraction. Better to record it now, and transcribe it later. Still, how to start things off… David, his PRESS pass snuggly hanging from his neck could now see Alias further down the hall, he was full mended with a fresh row of stitches across his forehead. His blood had been hastily wiped away with, probably only, a wet clothe. He looked tired, yet happy. The gold belt lying beside him probably played into that last part. It was then, that Russel David almost walked into a man who was also making his way through the backstage area, ushering with him his young son. David quickly apologized, I mean in this sea of people backstage, who knew who you’d run into. He didn’t want to make anyone, or anyone connected to anyone, angry with him. As he looked to walk away though, he noticed the young kid who couldn’t be older then seven, staring past David as if in a trance as he looked at Alias. “Who is that?”, said the child softly, in awe and wonder. David looked from the kid, to the kid’s father and shared a grin with him. Russel David knelt down and looked over at Christopher Sheffield, with the child. “That is Alias.” David replied with a slight grin, over top of the measured reverence in his voice. “Oooooh,” the child was impressed as he nodded his messily brown head of hair, and then he turned to Russel David, this seemingly knowledgeable fellow who had decided to talk to him about Alias, and asked, “Why Alias?” Russel David looked from Alias, to then child, and thought for a moment. Looking back at Alias he replied, “Good question.” Sometimes it took a perspective closer to the ground floor to find the right question to ask. Russel David smiled at this fact, and then stood back up, he nodded to the boy’s father and then walked towards the Original Pulp Hero. Alias was still fully collecting his breath, as he doused a small amount of water over top of his head, before wiping away the rest of the dried and flaked blood from his face. With the sweat on his body finally settling in with the rest of his dried blood and bruises… he felt like he needed a damned shower. Karina was already way ahead of him, on that fact, actually. Sheffield wanted to stay back and answer a few questions for this eWtorch interviewer first, after all. Slowly standing on stiff and aching legs, the Original Pulp Hero held out a hand as Russel David approached. Alias didn’t even need to be cued on the man’s arrival, he had noticed him coming down the hall, stopping to talk to a kid… and the Pulp Hero never forgot a face. The bloody knuckles surrounded by an array of scars with there own stories stuck out to shake Russel David’s hand. With an assured shake, and an extra squeeze to keep the reporter on his toes, Alias sat back down. David pulled a chair over from were the EMT had sat to stitch up Alias. David switched on his recorder, and set it on the weathered wooden bench beside were Alias sat, before asking his first question. “Chris Sheffield, it’s a pleasure to speak to you again. I want to start from the start. Why Alias?” Alias looked at Russel for a moment, and though lost in thought for a moment, he grinned a devil-ish grin as he looked down for a moment. “Hrrrmm, funny you’d call that the start.” A for Alias “I get bored easily. Tree gonna fall down. Down, down, down. Down to the bottom.” He smiled because even with the strange world he lived in, he had the chance to see Brian today… even though he was never sure when the crazy old man would come or go. Alias always looked forward to seeing Brian, because if not him, Christopher Sheffield wouldn’t be who he was today. “Trigger, when you pull it. Go home with a bullet. No birds in the birdcage. Flew off in his old age.” Alias smiled… because he didn’t see the car coming towards him, and for him. A brown-haired man who looked oddly familiar, behind the wheel. “Down, down, down. Moon gonna fall down.” And like that, the world cut to static. Thump, thump, thump. Cracked vision welcomed Alias when he awoke within the cold expanse of an unrecognizable warehouse interior. Alias didn’t feel like himself anymore. “Gaddamn it all, whoever just hit me… better get ready to hit me again, or they’ll have a problem on their hands.” Then again even if he didn’t feel like himself anymore, with the split lip oozing a slight silky crimson, a black-ish green welt stiffly holding over his only working eye, and a bruise over what was luckily a granite cheek, he knew that if the evening continued on down the road it had started on… he’d at least be leaving behind an easily recognizable corpse. This fact made the Original Pulp Hero chuckle a little bit, but the chuckle quickly twisted into a pained gasp. The belted contraption around his waist, which was hidden under a thick layer of gauze and tape, forced breathing to be pained, let alone laughing. It certainly didn’t help that his hands were tied tight behind him to the back of the chair in which he sat, forcing him to slouch awkwardly forward. Christopher Sheffield felt disoriented, and could only growl to himself out loud, wondering what time it was. “7:14 in the morning. Hopefully you don’t need to be anywhere… Alias. We’re going to be here for a while.” Replied a man from the dim shadows to the side of Alias, as the Pulp Hero arched his head as best he could and squinted through his good eye, trying to size him up as best he could. Such a thing was an automatic response for Alias, even if he was meeting a friend of a friend he would size them up and look to figure out how exactly he’d take them apart in a fight. The figure in the shadows seemed to be around the same height as he was… though he was thinner, and thought for a moment, what to say to the Original Pulp Hero. “Please to meet you, hope you guess my name…” the figure said, with a hushed smile slowly twisting across his lips, as he walked closer to Alias. It was this moment that the blood chilled within the fiery old salt that many knew as Christopher Sheffield, since it was his name. It could be said that every bad intention that Alias had ever had, had come roaring back at him like a pack of big bad dogs, embodied by this one man. Alias shook his head, and then answered the man, because it was either that, or he was going to throw up because he was looking at the man whose identity he’d taken as his own. “You... are Christopher Sheffield. Son of a bitch, coulda sworn you died.”
