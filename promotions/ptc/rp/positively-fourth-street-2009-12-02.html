Positively Fourth Street

2 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-12-02 Sunday, November 8, 2009 Working for Sin City Championship Wrestling was a legitimate health concern, both physically and mentally. Tonight had been no different. A hallway fistfight with a close friend and finding himself in the role of reluctant babysitter for an old nemesis had each taken its toll on him, so it was no surprise that Jared Sykes wanted to get as far away from the arena as fast as possible. By the time he stepped out into the mild Houston evening the only thought on his mind was the drive back to his hotel. Unfortunately, and as a result of arriving late to the show, his car was parked furthest from the door. Had he been paying attention to that door instead of fighting to get his gear into the back seat of his rental car he might have noticed the pale scarecrow of a man who stepped through it. Mr. Hawke quickly surveyed the surroundings before a slight nod signaled the rest of his party. If Jared had seen them he might have worked faster to get the car to cooperate. The thin girl with the unnaturally black hair and the green eyes wasn't one to worry about. Neither was the shorter redhead with the mismatched eyes, at least not if you didn’t deliberately try and upset her. The one to fear was the tallest of the three, the woman with the heart-shaped face and the eyes of gray. She was the only one left behind as Amy Campbell grabbed Quinn Gregory by the wrist and headed his way. “Dammit, bag.” Jared leaned into the backseat of his rental car, struggling to get a piece of luggage in place. A wheel on his travel case had turned itself sideways and lodged between the seats, preventing him from closing the door. “Stupid wheels are supposed to make this easier.” “Holy crap, just man up and kick it!” The redhead offered the advice just as she thrust a sneaker against the bag, dislodging it (the bag - she would have kicked Jared if that strike had hurt one of her precious DC sneakers). “See? Doofus.” She looked at the dented luggage, then to Sykes and added, “Hope nothing breakable was in there.” “Uh, I don’t think so.” He prodded the bag gently with his hand, eliciting a muffled jingling from inside. “Okay, I take that back.” He leaned back into the corner formed by the open door, hoping it would make him look cooler. It didn’t. “And what’s with getting all kick-happy today, Amy? You tell Quinn how you almost took my head off with one?” He nodded in the direction of the raven-haired teenager and smiled. “I think you’re rubbing off on her. Kid stole my damn soda.” “I dunno. I think Alex may have passed on the 'Be mean to Jared Sykes' gene...” “That's awful, Ames. But thanks, Jared. For... uh,” Quinn had chimed in, then skirted eyes over toward where her mother slowly approached. “Thanks again for looking out for me.” “Yeah,” Amy added, “About that. I appreciated it, and you should know that I don't actually expect that kind of thing out of friends...” “Don’t stress it.” He shifted his position against the car and rested an arm on the roof. “Besides, I’ve managed to more or less convince myself that it’s because I was the best man for the job, and not because I was the only schmuck you could find…” His voice trailed off, his cheeks flushed, and for a moment Jared felt that the mild Houston air had somehow grown colder. “Oh, hey, Alex.” “Jared.” It was close to a greeting, and Alexandra Pierce's head tipped just slightly as she neared. “Forgive me for interrupting, but we should be going.” “Right.” Given the circumstances, agreeing with her seemed to be the best option. In truth he would have regardless. “Probably for the best, what with all the crazy, and the chaos, and… well, whatever it was that went down. Should get going myself. Busy night for everyone, I guess.” “Indeed it was.” Pierce straightened slightly, turning her smile down to her daughter – though that distance wasn't much of one anymore. “Actually, could you... could you two excuse us for a moment? I would like to... to thank him properly for looking after Quinn while Amy was occupied.” She didn't have to turn to see Amy's eyes wide, her mouth opening to offer some objections. “I'm not going to hurt him, Amy,” she said. “I promise, I won't lay a finger on him.” “But—” “Please?” Alexandra Pierce rarely asked for permission or forgiveness, and the relentless tug of the teen on Campbell's arm proved too much for even Amy Campbell to overcome. Jared wasn’t quite sure he believed her. After all, it had been almost a year since the last time he was in a position to slight Alexandra Pierce. The encounter had sent him to the hospital and put his best friend in a coma. “Something tells me this isn’t about my babysitting rate, huh? I mean, normally it’s ten dollars an hour, but I figure in your case I can knock a few bucks off.” He smiled, hoping his joke would help ease him into a situation he dreaded. This, much like every other time he’d tried to be funny around Alex, bombed and bombed hard. Jared’s gaze fell to the ground, and with it fell his hope. “We’re, umm, we’re going to have that conversation now, aren’t we?” “Perhaps.” She leaned against the car, but only because of the exhaustion of her ordeal. “I am given to understand that you and I had this talk this evening during the broadcast, however.” “I mean, I thought I did, but… I guess not, huh?” He rubbed his eyes. The throbbing in his head was getting stronger, though he doubted very much it had anything to do with his injuries. “I’ll be honest. I’d be really surprised if this one goes as well as the one with Bizzaro World Alex.” “I don't have a problem with Amy having friends,” Pierce said. “I do not have a problem with my daughter having friends, either. I want to make this clear, straight off the bat. This is not a 'stay the hell away from my girlfriend' discussion.” Jared let his hand drop to his side. It took all he had not to let his jaw fall with it. “Okay, I’m confused. Like, really confused.” He turned his gaze in her direction but did not make eye contact. “If not that, then… Yeah, I got nothin’.” “I love her. I love them both dearly. You understand that, right?” Her brows lifted, and he could swear there were the beginnings of tears in her eyes. “No matter what I do here, no matter what happens outside the ring, they have always supported me, no matter what I have done.” “Yeah, I... I get that, but—” “You don't.” The smile was small, sad. “Not really, you don't. But I wouldn't really expect you to. The problem with you, Jared, is that, beneath all the nervous twitches, you are fundamentally nice.” “A lot of people have pointed out a lot of things that they think are wrong with me, Alex. Seriously, I stopped counting years ago.” His own smile was warmer. “I don’t think anyone’s ever put that at the top of their list, though. At least not that I remember.” He shifted his position against the car, now turning his whole body to face her. “This might actually rank up there with the weirder questions I’ve had to ask, but… How exactly is that a bad thing?” “If you spend time around Quinn and Amy, they will absorb your kindness, and they will lose their tolerance for me. Because I am not a nice person, Jared. I am trying to be, but... it is a struggle for me sometimes.” She stepped closer, her voice dropping slightly. “The worst part is, you won't try to. You won't think about it, or plan to do it. But I fear you will cost me my relationship with them. That I will have to compete with an unreasonably high standard, and I will ultimately fail. Should that happen, I will blame myself, certainly – I will be miserable. But I will also blame you.” His brow furrowed. His tone went flat. “Well, that’s not creepy at all.” Jared shifted again, resting an arm atop the roof and bracing his head with his hand. One could chart a mathematical correlation between the amounts of fidgeting done during an exchange to his current level of anxiety. “So, I don’t really know how to respond to that. I mean, on the one hand you’re right. It’s not really a ‘stay away from Amy and Quinn’ thing. On the other hand…” He pushed away from the car, pacing a small circle nearby. Threats, however veiled, were not to be taken lightly. Not when they came from Alexandra Pierce of all people. That was a mistake he would never make again. “So it’s risk versus reward,” he continued, still pacing. “I seem to remember that it hurts a lot when you get mad at somebody, and I’ve got a weird feeling that this would be a lot worse, which… yeah, last time was pretty bad.” Her silence was all the assurance he needed. “So if I’m hearing this right, and I very well might not be, then it’s on me to figure out whether it’s a chance I really feel like taking.” “In a manner of speaking.” “So, is it?” The smile that snaked across her lips was almost assuredly false. “That is left for you to decide. You are perfectly capable of making them both very happy, in ways I would not dare try. I just... do not want to lose either of them.” There might have been a sigh there, soft enough that Jared could barely hear it. “I know I can be... harsh. But I do not have anything else in this world except for those two women, and I will not trade that away.” “I have no desire – none at all, to take either of them away from you intentionally, accidentally, or…” He paused, momentarily weighing his options. “I guess there really isn’t a third option here, huh?” He shook his head. “Besides, word on the street is I’m like the last person anybody should be hanging around with anyway. I’m not exactly high on the list of folks people should be picking up traits from.” “Just... all I ask is that you be careful. I do not want to be put in this position.” She smiled again; this time, it was more real, smoother – but he couldn't tell if it was actually genuine. “I should get some rest.” “Yeah, I hear ya’.” Again it seemed best to agree with her, if only to help expedite an escape. “I need to go drown my head in a bucket of ice. Plus it, umm… it looks like I have a little bit of thinking ahead of me.” He sighed, and once again tried to rub the tension from his forehead. “Have a good night then, Alex.” “You as well.” She ducked her head and stepped away, stopping to look back over her shoulder. “And Jared?” The SCCW Gateway Champion glanced up again, brows lifting. “Yeah?” “Thank you. For keeping an eye on Quinn tonight. I do appreciate it.” “Sure.” He wanted to say “any time”. It would have been the truth. Regardless of popular opinion and the way she operated Jared did not hate Alexandra Pierce, though he had every reason to. He did fear her, and that was enough. Jared watched as Alex walked back to reconvene with her girlfriend and daughter. He watched as the three of them piled into the car driven by Mr. Hawke. It was only when he saw the taillights fade did he push himself away from the car, turning around and closing the door with a light kick. “Fuck my life.” Tuesday, November 10, 2009 It was an oft-overlooked benefit of working for a shipping company that one could get access to addresses. Truth be told it was one of the reasons that the job had appealed to George O’Donnell when he applied for it. Of all the information within his reach there was only one name and one address that he ever had any interest in. The aged brick building with a clear view of the Charles River had been in the family of Stanislav Kovalchuk (Stan or Stash to his friends, but never Stanley) since the 1950’s. His family had immigrated to the United States from Poland after the war and made their home on the outskirts of Cambridge, Massachusetts. When Jozef Kovalchuk passed away in 1971 he left the property to his only son to maintain. It was in the lobby of this building that George stood, patiently waiting for his 2pm appointment. The sound of a chime and the shuffling of worn shoes on tile drew his attention to the door. Stan was a stocky man, his body hardened and worn from years of construction. Over time his hair had migrated south from his scalp, and now exploded forth from his ears like alabaster fireworks. Though he had the rent from his building to live on, his father had extolled the virtues of an honest days work, and his face bore the liver spots of a lifetime toiling in the sun. “Can I help you?” “Hi.” George took a step forward, offering his right hand. “I’m George. I’m here to see you about the apartment you have for rent.” “Ah, yes. So sorry for my delay. A year now I wait to rent apartment, you would think I at least show up on time.” Stan’s smile only served to increase the number of wrinkles on his face. His handshake was firm, strong; the way his father had taught him a man shakes hands. “My granddaughter, I promised I would visit her this morning. You wait long?” George found himself straining to try and decipher the man’s dialect. His grasp of English was strong enough, but his accent was painfully thick in places, especially when it came to hard consonants. “Oh, no. Not at all,” George reassured him. “I only just got here myself. I was admiring the photos.” “My family.” Stan slowly drew his left hand past the picture-covered wall. “They are great people, and I am very proud of them. I keep these to remind me. Every man should know where he comes from. My father.” The photo he pointed to had yellowed with time, but still held the honor of the proud young soldier it portrayed. “He teach me how to be. He was great man. Very great man.” George imagined his own father coming home late from work to find his home destroyed. The fire would still be burning, consuming everything in its path. The Franklin fire department would fight valiantly to try and save the home, but ultimately they would fail. In the end, John O’Donnell would find the lifeless bodies of his wife and youngest son. He would bury them in a small, sad service in the Tennessee rain, and try to pick up the pieces of an otherwise broken life. George thought of all this, and felt nothing. “Yeah.” George nodded. Courtesy told him he should. “My dad’s pretty great, too.” Stan playfully slapped George on the back, the force of the blow almost knocking the younger man over. “Come. I show you apartment, and then you tell me about him.” The elevator ride to the sixth and final floor took less than a minute, but George still found it hard to control his anticipation. He had done so much, come so far, and now he was that much closer. As he watched the old Polish man turn the key in the lock, George could barely contain himself. He’d long imagined the things that took place on the other side of this door, and yet now he was here. “It sticks sometimes.” Stan smiled as he pressed against the door, which eventually gave under his weight and swung open. He moved out of the way and gestured into the vacant room. George was all too happy to step inside. “Wow,” was all that George could say. Stan seemed to find the reaction amusing. “Is nothing special,” he said, chuckling. “Is living room here. Around corner and down hall is kitchen and two bedroom. The big one has view of river.” As Stan continued his sales pitch George simply walked to the center of the living room and let his eyes wander. There were dents in the carpet from the legs of furniture. The walls still had a few tiny pinholes where decorations had been hung. Even the Batman light switch had yet to be replaced. It was beyond anything he could have imagined. “Bathroom is nothing special,” Stan continued. He started towards the hall, expecting George to follow. “Come, I show you.” “I’ll take it.” “Hrm?” “I’ll take it,” George repeated, sliding his hands into the pockets of the light jacket he wore. “If it’s still available, that is.” “Oh, is still available.” Stan raised a bushy white eyebrow. “But you have not seen everything yet.” “It’s okay. I know the person who used to live here.” George smiled slowly. It was becoming harder to contain his glee. “You do?” “It’s how I knew to come here.” “Excellent!” This pleased Stan greatly. He was a student of the old school. Word of mouth was as good as any written reference. “He was a good man, a little, you know, in the head.” As he spoke he extended a finger and drew a slow circle in the air around his ear. “But he was good man. He live here for ten years. Always pay me on time. He even pay extra to go up to the roof, but when he move out I go up there and all I find is golf balls.” Stan threw his hands up in exasperation to punctuate his story. George’s laugh got stuck in his throat, coming out more as a half-snort. “How is Jerry?” Stan continued, tilting his head gently to the side. To George he looked not unlike an old sheepdog. “Jared.” “Hrm?” “His name is Jared. Jared Sykes.” “Ah, of course!” Stan slapped himself in the forehead with the palm of his hand. “My mind is not what is used to be. Do you see him often?” “Once every few weeks.” George neglected to add the part about it being relegated to television shows, as Jared was wholly unaware of his existence. “He’s doing quite well for himself, for the most part. Some things are still up in the air, but by and large I’m very proud of what he’s accomplished.” “Very good, very good. He was always good to me. Never cause any problems, only the golf balls.” Stan playfully jabbed George in the arm with his elbow. “If you see him tell him I finally fix elevator, eh?” “I’ll tell him you said that.” Again George grinned, and this time his eyes widened with it. “I hope to meet up with him in the near future.” Saturday, November 14, 2009 He’d been waiting patiently by the door ever since he got the call to expect company, but decided to hold off on opening it until he heard the doorbell. At least this way he wouldn’t appear too overzealous. “Merv’!” Jared’s smile was wide, genuine. It had been some time since he’d seen his oldest and best friend, due in no small part to the brutal schedule Jared was keeping as of late. “Hey.” As always Mervin’s response was subdued. “Can I…?” “Yeah, totally.” Jared stepped aside and held the door open. Mervin stepped through and into the hallway, sliding his hands into the pockets of his khakis. “It’s been way too long, dude.” “Has been a while, huh?” “Seriously. I mean I totally get it, though. My schedule’s been a fuckin’ nightmare, and I know you’re tied up with that… whatever it is.” Jared followed Mervin into the living room and leaned against the threshold. “Hey, did you guys ever get funding for it?” “Nah, we’re still looking.” Mervin’s eyes moved listlessly about the room. “We thought we had a group ready to back us, but they pulled out before we could finalize the deal. Something about not thinking we’d be first to market.” “Wow, that kinda sucks.” “Eh, whatever. Ollie knows a guy, but we’re trying not to call in any favors. I mean, we will if we have to, but I don’t think we’re quite desperate enough. Not yet, anyway.” “That’s awesome. I’m sure you’ll be fine. You’re more or less the smartest dude ever, you know?” Jared smiled, but was a bit surprised when Mervin didn’t. More surprising was that his friend hadn’t yet made himself comfortable. “Everything okay, dude?” Jared asked, pushing away from the wall and stepping into the living room. “You seem kinda… I dunno… out of it.” “Yeah, no, everything’s fine.” The reply was less than convincing. “You sure?” “Yep.” Jared raised an eyebrow in concern, but opted not to press the issue. “Okay. Can I get you anything to drink? Want me to hang up your coat?” “No, it’s okay. I’m not staying very long.” Mervin unzipped his jacket and took a seat on the edge of the couch. His tone gave Jared pause, sending his threat advisory level spiking to ‘yellow’. “Alright, that doesn’t creep me out or anything.” Jared moved further into the living room, nervously taking a seat in one of the armchairs opposite the sofa. “Seriously, what’s going on? You’re making me kinda nervous.” “There’s something that I want to talk to you about. I feel like I need to get this off my chest.” “Ohhhhkay.” Jared said it slowly, not quite sure about what was coming next. Yellow became orange. “So… what’s up?” “It’s…” Mervin cocked his head to the side and tried to compose his thoughts. “You’d be my hero right now if you just said it.” Jared’s pulse quickened. He could sense a serious conversation on the horizon. He didn’t handle those particularly well. “It’s complicated, and I want to make sure that I don’t screw it up.” Mervin put his hands on his knees, mindlessly rubbing them. It was a nervous habit he exhibited, but Jared had only seen it in a handful of situations, and none of those ended well. “I need to make sure I find the right words.” Jared’s concern maxed-out; redlined. He rubbed his fingers against his palms, trying to wick away the sweat he could feel pooling there. “Merv’, you’re killing me here. Whatever it is, just say it. You’ve known me forever. Whatever’s on your mind you can just say.” Mervin nodded, and let his head drop. After a tense moment he raised his eyes and sighed. “You… You have no idea how hard it is to be your friend sometimes, Jared.” Mervin swallowed hard and shifted nervously on the couch. Mindlessly he picked at a piece of lint on his pants. “You really don’t.” Jared fell back into his chair, stunned; it was as if the breath had been driven from his lungs. “I don’t… but…” He tried to steady himself, to make register and digest what Mervin had said, but the shock was so sudden. “This isn’t easy for me to say. I want you to know that.” The world was starting to come back into focus. He was at home. He was in his living room. With only a few words his best friend decimated his sense of self-worth. These were the only things Jared Sykes was sure of. “Not so awesome on this side of things either.” It probably sounded a little more sarcastic than he’d wanted, but Jared found governing his emotions to be quite difficult. “Where did this come from?” “I’ve been thinking about it for a while, actually.” Mervin ran a hand through his hair. “For a few months now at least, though to be honest it first crossed my mind about a year ago when I got out of the hospital.” Jared couldn’t bring himself to look his friend in the eye. “Oh,” was all he could offer. “It kind of put things in perspective, you know? Like, I started thinking – really thinking – about everything, and I realized that you didn’t have any idea the number of things I have to go through in order to make this friendship work.” Jared didn’t respond, so Mervin continued. “And it is work, Jared. If I’m being honest with you, sometimes it feels like it’s more work than it’s worth.” Still Jared said nothing. “Can I ask you something?” Mervin pressed, leaning forward in his seat. “Do you even realize that I more or less put my life on hold so that I could follow you around on this little wrestling escapade?” “No, I… I guess not.” “Like, okay, sure. Once upon a time everything was great. It was fun, and cool, and exciting, but the more I thought about it the more I realized that it hadn’t been that way in a long time, but I felt like I had to be there, because it’s what you thought you needed.” Jared closed his eyes. He tried to convince himself that this wasn’t happening, that he wasn’t having this conversation with the only person who knew him - really knew him. It was a futile gesture. His fantasy was shattered the minute Mervin started speaking again. “For the last six months, six months that I’ve been able to focus on me and what I want to do with my life, it’s been… I’m happy. I’m glad to be able to finally get to take my life in the direction I want it to go, you know?” “I didn’t…” Jared’s thought was cut short as Mervin waved a dismissive hand. “I put on a blueberry suit because you needed somebody to be there. I guess I was just too used to the role of sidekick to say anything but yes. I stayed because I figured that’s what you wanted me to do, and it ended up almost costing me my life, Jared.” He cleared his throat and shook his head. “I’ve been wanting to say this for a while now.” “So why wait?” Still Jared’s eyes lingered on the floor. Keeping them low helped hide the tears that had started to well. “Why not just hit me with it then and get it over with?” “Because back then I thought you still had my back.” “I do!” “You don’t. Not after the things I’ve heard about. No, not just heard about, seen.” Mervin could feel his anger rising, and it was evident in his voice. Even Jared, though he’d blocked out most everything else, could sense it. “I’ve given up ten years of my life to follow you around, but apparently that loyalty means dick to you, since suddenly you’re all buddy-buddy with one of the very people responsible for putting me a fucking coma.” “Merv’…” “Don’t. Don’t even try. Do you want to know something?” Seething, Mervin didn’t give him a chance to answer. “You know that little stretch you had a few years ago? The one where you were overseas for a while, and then locked yourself up in crazytown for a few months?” “Please…” “Do you have any idea how much easier my life was then? Do you?! Fifteen years, Jared. Fifteen! And you’re going to just toss that aside because all of a sudden Amy fucking Campbell gives you the time of day?” Jon Rhine had said it. Spacely had said it. It was no different from anything else the rest of his friends had said to him, but when the same sentiment came from his closest friend it was a little more than Jared could take. He brushed away a rogue tear. “So I’m not allowed to give somebody a second chance, right? You and I are both so perfect that we get to sit and cast judgment on everyone else? You know what I’ve done in my life. You know I’m in no position to judge anybody.” “That’s not the point!” “Then what is?” “Jared, the last time you tried to get friendly with these people they put us both in the hospital, and a fucking surgeon had to drill into my skull to stop a bleed in my brain. My fucking brain, Jared.” Mervin pushed back the mop of hair that hung over his brow, revealing a circular scar and a small rounded hump barely visible at the hairline. Jared visibly winced. “You remember this, don’t you?” Mervin gestured to the bump with his free hand. “It’s the plug they had stick in my head because, oh hey, it turns out that your skull just doesn’t magically grow back when someone cuts into it.” “You think I forgot all that? Do you have any idea what I had to go through, knowing what happened to you because of my mistake?! Here, I’ll show you…” Jared was quickly on his feet, storming out of the living room and into the nearby hallway. From his seat on the couch Mervin could hear the sound of a door and a light rattling. When Jared returned to the room his hands were full of orange plastic bottles, each one bearing a sticker with his name. “For the depression,” Jared said, throwing a few of the bottles at his friend. “For the anxiety.” Another batch followed. “For the insomnia that all the other drugs gave me.” Jared’s hands were not empty, but he’d thrown enough to make his point. Mervin glanced at the plastic cylinders that surrounded him and shook his head. “And that’s just what I have down here,” Jared continued, letting the rest of the bottles he held fall to the floor at his feet. “You want to see the rest? I have a whole goddamned collection!” “I can’t believe you’re actually trying to win this argument! Do you have any idea how retarded you sound? That you’re actually defending these people is insane!” He all but leapt from the couch, knocking a few prescription bottles to the floor. “I should have expected this.” “Expected what? I feel like my character’s been called into question here. How did you think I’d react?” “Like a goddamn child, so I guess I was pretty accurate.” “Merv’, come on. Since when am I suddenly guilty by association?” “If you don’t get it, then I can’t explain it to you.” He was already in the hallway, his hand on the doorknob. “I think it’s probably better if I just leave.” “Can’t we at least try to figure this out? You owe me that much.” “Owe you? I owe you?! Jared, after everything that’s gone on in the last year, and the way you’ve handled it – or rather, fucked it all up – I don’t owe you anything! I can’t tell you who to be friends with. That’s out of my hands. Only person I can control that for is me.” “What does that mean?” “It means leave me the fuck alone.” He slammed the door behind him, hard enough to shake the photos on the wall. Jared stood alone in his living room. He let his eyes fall to the clear orange containers that littered his carpet, hoping that if he stared at them long enough a pattern would emerge. It was a failed and futile gesture, designed to occupy his mind with something – anything – beyond the events of the last few minutes. Fights with Spacely, fights with Jon, he’d been able to rationalize those. No one was innocent. Everyone was a hypocrite. He had nothing to explain; no reason or need to justify his decisions to anyone but himself. The consequences would be his and his alone to face. One friendship at the sake of another. * - The title is from a Bob Dylan song. Thanks, Bob Dylan! ** - Thanks to Katie and Joe for letting me play with their toys!
