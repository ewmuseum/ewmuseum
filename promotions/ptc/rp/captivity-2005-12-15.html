Captivity

15 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-15 He couldn’t actually see them, but outside he knew the cars passed. If he strained very hard, he could almost hear them lumbering beasts on four wheels speeding by, occasionally a horn sounded louder than the engines piercing the nearly absolute silence. The only light came from the window, placed just a bit too high to actually see out of, today, it had been left open. As to what today was, he had no idea. The days had run together in an endless cycle. The film of filth on those windows was so thick it became impossible to distinguish natural from artificial light. Days? Weeks? Possibly a month? It was impossible to tell, life had become one endless waking nightmare. His arms had long hence ceased to hurt. He knew they still twisted over his head, he knew his entire weight was supported by pair of cuffs shackling his wrists. They just stopped hurting, maybe numb from the cold, maybe just asleep. He couldn’t tell anymore, perhaps most likely their pain had simply merged with the other nagging aches throughout his body. Forming one giant mass of torture, nearly overloading his mind. He’d really started to worry when he realized he stopped feeling the hunger pains. His last meal was now a dim memory, for all he knew it’d been months since he last ate. Of course that would have to mean he was dead, and this was indeed hell as his captor had called it. Although hell wasn’t supposed to be this cold, at least he thought it was cold. He vaguely remembered shivering, at first. That had stopped too, most likely he wasn’t dead. Yet, he was defiantly dying. There wasn’t really any question to it now. He was going to die, in this place, alone and naked. Starved, when they finally found his body he’d be nothing but a walking skeleton. Maybe dehydration would kill him first, or possibly the cold. It didn’t really matter, dead was dead, no matter how you died. At least that’s how he figured it. The captor hadn’t spoken since he referred to the place as hell. To be perfectly honest, Stevens wasn’t even sure the guy was still around. He should have been able to hear the man’s breathing, but all was quiet save for the muffled street noise. That really didn’t matter either, whether or not the co called Angel was here, Stevens would still be dying alone. Just like he’d lived. Silently he prayed for death. The same simple little prayer he’d mouthed what had to have been a hundred times before. He just wanted it all too end, the pain, the loneliness, the desperation. At first he’d yearned for escape, prayed to be rescued. He’d held high hopes that someone would come for him. Maybe the captor would just be another kidnapper, looking for some ransom money. But as time past Stevens came to realize this was not the case. No one was coming to save him, and the Angel wasn’t interested in money, just suffering. Of course it was all a fitting punishment he supposed. After everything he’d put his loved ones through, he probably deserved this. Suffering more than he ever imaged he could suffer, starving, in agony. Every waking moment a nightmare worse than the one before. The moments he was awake, it was everything he could do to simply keep his eyes open. As bad as things were here, there were still better than the hell he’d made for himself. The Thanksgiving nightmare had repeated itself. Every time he drifted off to sleep, he was there again. Surrounded by everyone he loved, it was so great, so perfect, until it wasn’t anymore. Even awake he could smell the charred flesh, feel the ragged nails clawing at his skin. If there’d been anything in his stomach he’d probably lose it. The memory of the dream was too strong to keep anything down. A thundering click sounded throughout the room. The sound of a door opening, yet no light spilled through. If the silence had not been so nearly perfect, he never would have heard it. But in this vast empty space it filled up everything, like the sound of a bomb, he thought his ears may bleed from the deafening sound. Shuffling footsteps approached, straining his eyes he searched for his captor but found nothing. It was too dark, how his captor could move about was a mystery. Screeching chair legs drug against the floor. Like nails on a chalkboard, the sound caused his spine to shudder. He wanted to call out, beg the noise to stop, offer anything for his release. Yet he knew it would do no good. For the first time in the life of Michael Stevens he finally understood what it meant to be completely powerless. As the racket faded away he could hear a the crumpling sound of a paper bag. The little clink of objects being placed on metal table. Little sounds, ones you’d never normally notice had become more important to him than he ever thought possible. Each familiar noise made all this more real, in some dark little corner of his brain he’d tried to rationalize all this as the worst nightmare ever. That maybe just maybe if he tried hard enough he’d open his eyes and be in some dingy little motel room, hung over, and praying for death, only in the good way. Not like this, not the way now he actually meant it. Without warning came another sound, like an engine starting. Then he began to fall, realizing quickly he was being lowered from his position he tried to brace for impact, but his legs crumbled under his own weight. He landed in a heap a heavy weight of chain striking him moment after. He felt something hot begin to cover his arms, it must be blood the motion having torn the skin away from his already ragged wrists. He moaned softly at this new pain, sharper and brighten than the dull ache he’d felt before. He curled up into a ball, expecting the worst. He couldn’t remember having been beaten, but then again sometimes it was difficult to even remember his name these days. Yet, the rain of blows he expected never came, it was something even worse. Nothing but a low throaty chuckle filling the room. A sort of mocking laughter that drove a cold spear of terror into his very soul. "Wake up Michael." He dared not speak. Silently cursing himself for the moan that had already escaped him, he tried to even his breathing, silence the rapid beating of his heart. Maybe if he played dead, acted like the fall had knocked him unconscious, the captor would lose interest and leave. He might be able to escape then. There was silence as the laughter died away. The moments stretched, he tried to count them, using his heartbeat to gauge seconds. After by his count five minutes had past he again heard the sound of the chair legs scraping against the floor. As much as the sound hurt he tried not to shy away from it. He must remain perfectly still to maintain the illusion. Not that he really expected it to work, but it was the best he could come up with at the moment. Clicking footsteps approached, involuntarily his muscles tensed. The kicks would come now, he was sure of it. He hadn’t fooled his "guardian Angel" for a second, and now he was going to be punished for trying to do so. A hair’s breadth away the footsteps stopped, and he could feel more than see the man standing over him. He waited for the first kick. "You don’t fool me, you know that right? Listen Mike, I don’t have all day, get up." Still too afraid to speak he remained silent. Surely the captor couldn’t see any better than he could. If that were the case he couldn’t have possibly seen Stevens tense up. If he just stayed quiet his plan might work. If nothing else made if the captor bent over to check on him he could choke him with the handcuff chain. He was too weak too stand, but he thought maybe just maybe the adrenaline would kick up enough to escape. "Mike, either acknowledge me, or I’m going to be forced to hurt you. I don’t want to do that Mike. I’m here to help you, protect you, guide you. You have to understand that, now respond." He really had no choice, either he stayed silent and suffered, or he spoke and bought himself some time. At this point he had started to feel like maybe time would be the only thing he really needed. With the right amount of time he’d be able to get back to his feet, and run. He tried to speak, the words at first dying in this throat, making him cough and gag. Everything was so dried out he couldn’t speak, he just didn’t have the strength, but at least his effort appeared to have been noticed by the captor. "Good, I see you’ve finally decided to try and speak. Now, hold out your hands, and I shall help you rise. I can’t very well carry on a conversation with you on the floor like this." The man’s tone was so even, so smooth. Like the purr of a cat ready to pounce on it’s prey. While there was a threat behind the words the outward signs were so calm, so pleasing. He couldn’t help but obey, almost as if the man’s words held the power to hypnotize. He lifted both hands to the air, hoping against all hope the captor would at least be gentle. Of course that would have been too much to ask. Roughly he felt hands grip the chain connecting the cuffs. He was lifted up to his feet by his wrists, the pain nearly unbearable. Biting his tongue to keep himself from screaming he leaned into the captor. He still couldn’t support his own weight, his legs having grown weak from a too long period of non-use. The two took a shuffling step forward the captor groaning just a bit. Obviously he wasn’t used to supporting so much dead weight. Quickly after another step or two Stevens found himself tossed aside into a chair. Stretching out his hands just a bit he felt the cool edge of a table before him. Slowly he allowed his head to sink down on top of his arms. The effort of walking had just been too much, he was too tired. He’d only close his eyes for a minute, just enough time to regain his strength, al he needed was a short rest. "THUD!" The metal of the table vibrated against his skull, like a million hammers pounding on his brain. Jerking his head up quickly he almost lost consciousness. He moved too fast, the room spun around him. Sick from exhaustion, hunger, dehydration, pain, the bile rose to the back of his throat. He couldn’t be sick not now, he thought the acid from his stomach may boil him alive. That couldn’t happen now, not when he could see this chance for escape. "Did I tell you to go to sleep Mike? I want you awake from this, and really owe it to me. You know that don’t you Mike? I saved your life, so you owe me. Every breath you take is nothing from a gift from me, a sign of my grand generosity. When I pulled you off the street away from those cars, I became your master. Your life became mine…" How this hell could be considered a gift Stevens couldn’t understand. But then again nothing had made sense for a very long time. He barely even remembered throwing himself in front of the car, it was like something had happened more than a lifetime ago. Still he knew he must say something, or at least try to. "I….*cough*….why?" It was the best he could manage. Because I care about you Mike. I told you, when we first met on the street. I’m your guardian Angel, it’s my job to set you back on the path. You’ve strayed too far for too long. I’m here to correct that." He should’ve recognized the sound immediately, sulfur scrapping against something rough, like sandpaper. It registered a second before the sun exploded in front of him. So completely had been the darkness that the tiniest spark of a match head could be blinding. Piercing, white hot fire stabbed at his eyes and he turned away. After a moment the room filled with a familiar aroma. A good cigar, something you’d have to pay an obscene amount for, just to get them across the border. Cracking open one eye he looked towards his Angel. The only thing he could really see was the glowing hot cherry at the end of the cigar. As the man puffed, it glowed brighter casting a shadowed red light over his face. It was still impossible to make out his features, but the table before him back into focus a bit. Laid out between them on the steel table were three objects. First a bottle of whiskey, and last what looked like a police issue side arm, between them a small white Styrofoam container. Oh how he longed to reach for the bottle of whiskey. Logically he knew it would dehydrate him even more than he already was, but damned if they idea of drinking himself into a coma didn’t seem good. Just the thought of a few hours of undisturbed drunk sleep sounded better than any escape plan. Just a few hours to hide from all the pain. "It’s your choice Mike. Consider it another gift from your most generous of benefactors. Choose one item from the table, and it’s yours. Remember though, choose carefully whatever you touch first is yours, and you don’t get a second chance." He’d begun to pick up the smell of something like food from the container. Loudly his stomach rumbled, and he again felt the hunger pains. With the gun he could probably try to escape, but there was a good chance he’d be over powered before he could do anything. And really what good was the food to him, it wouldn’t fill him up, and would just make him all the more thirsty. Again he considered the whiskey bottle, he dreamed of that perfect, blissful quiet sleep. With shaking hands he slowly reached forward. Decision made, he would get some rest after he took the bottle. Afraid that it might be snatched away some cruel joke, he shot his hands forward unexpectedly. Snatching the bottle from the table and cradling it against his chest. He wouldn’t leave the room, but at least he’d find some escape. It was a lot better odds than the gun offered. Glancing up he saw the face behind the cigar looking grim. Slowly the Angel shook his head. Clucking his tongue against the roof of his mouth, the Angel stood. "Oh Mike, I’d hoped all this might have finally taught you the lesson. I suppose it hasn’t." Searing pain against his cheek as the Angel struck him. The impact sent him tumbling from the chair to the floor. The whisky bottle flew from his hands shattering on impact. "NOOOOO." Less a yell more of a weeping cry of defeat as Stevens saw what was to be his salvation destroyed. Lowering his head to the ground he bitterly wept. The one thing in the world that could have made this hell bearable, and it was stolen away from him. "You’re pathetic Michael. Crying like a child, not over spilt milk, but over spilt poison. Every drink of that kills you a little more, and you know that don’t you? I figured it out, you just don’t care. You think you’re worthless, and you want to die. I see that now." Not caring to look up he heard the sound of the other chair tumbling over, a small metallic sound as the gun was lifted from the table. Thudding footsteps coming over closer, and the unbearable heat of the cigar scalding him. He felt it then, the cold steal muzzle of the pistol pressed against his temple. "This is your life now Mike, and what you’ve got to realize is that you’re no longer in control. You’ve fucked it up too badly. And maybe once upon a time in your own little fairy world you could have made it better, but that time is past. I fucking own you Michael Stevens. If you want to kill yourself, at least do it fucking right. No more of this killing yourself slowly with liquor. If you’re going to die it’s going to be by your own fucking hand, and it’s going to be quick." The gun dropped the ground beside Stevens, and he saw the Angel rise. Turning away he stomped away towards the doorway through which he’d entered. After a moment he turned, his face just barely visible as an angry orange splotch against the darkness. "No pussy way out this time Stevens, no car to throw yourself in front of. You fucking want to die, grow some goddamn balls and do it right." The click sounded again followed quickly by a door slamming. Alone in the dark, Stevens felt the gun beside his head, now the only way out. He wept. ~To Be Continued~
