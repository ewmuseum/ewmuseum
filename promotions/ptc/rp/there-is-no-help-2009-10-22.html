There Is No Help

22 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-22 30-year old men should not have life-changing experiences. It’s difficult to understand how the human brain makes the subtle transformation into insanity, and Chris Griffey was an odd example of an ordinary family man whose mind slowly, yet noticeably, plunged into darkness. But what can be done? A professional wrestler of only a year, who had given up a promising career to devote himself to his wife and move to the other side of the country with her, Chris had a firm grip on his morals—and his mind. But if someone such as this, someone who relies on himself to make righteous decisions, someone not extraordinary but rather strong-willed, can all but visibly see his mind deteriorate and remain helpless, then what chance do the rest of us have? So I ask once again, what can be done? For Chris Griffey—but perhaps not the rest of you—he had accepted what he was becoming and knew there would be no help… ************* “Mommm!” The little boy shouted from the couch to the other room. He heard her voice from what he thought was the kitchen. It was barely audible, and he strained to hear what she was saying, still giving most of his attention to the video game. “Sarah, I’m just worried. He hasn’t been the same since,” her voice trailed off as if walking away from the living room. The boy clenched the controller in anger, clearly upset with something on the TV screen, and threw it down on the couch. He sat there for a moment and then went over to the window. The mid-afternoon sun was covered by a dreary haze. Looking left to right over the rolling landscape, he thought how big the world seemed, and it still scared him. The blueberry bushes that covered the Connecticut farm had already turned a dark purple and were skeletons of their normal selves. He could see the Mexican migrant workers hard at work pruning what was left of them before November came when the men would return home. At the bottom of the hill leading up to the house, the last of autumn’s apples dotted the trees. Maybe he would walk down there and grab one; the Jonagolds were his favorite, as big as his head. “MOM!” He shouted again still looking out the window, and when she didn’t answer, “Mom, mommmm, mommm!” He could still barely hear her. She sounded upset, but he didn’t know why, and it made him nervous. He didn’t like it when his mother was upset, he was still too young to fully understand these things. “How can I not worry?” He heard her voice get a little louder and pulled himself away from the window, creeping toward the door. “I saw application forms next to his bed. It doesn’t make sense, he stopped years ago. It’s something to do with Brian. Something happened out there; I know it.” He didn’t know what she was talking about, but he knew he didn’t like it. “MOM!” He yelled, popping into the kitchen. She jumped, nearly dropping the phone, and held her hand up to her chest. “Sarah, let me call you back.” She clicked off the phone. “What is it, Jason?” She sounded like someone who had not had much sleep. “Have you seen Charlie?” He said getting upset. “I let him out this morning; he’s been gone all day.” “Oh, honey, I’m sure he’s around somewhere. He’s probably out with the guys down the hill.” “But he always comes back when I eat lunch! He’s gone!” Jason cried while his mother simply sighed and tried to comfort him. “Don’t cry, sweetie, I’m sure the guys probably fed him earlier. He’s probably playing down in the creek or something; he never goes far.” She sounded concerned, but Jason didn’t think she cared. He knew something was wrong, but he didn’t know what to do. He became angry and stomped over to the closet and swung open the door so that it slammed against the wall. “Jason, stop it! I’ll tell your father if you keep it up.” He ignored her and grabbed a small parka from the clutter on the floor. “I’m going to go look for him.” He said, still pouting. “Where’s daddy? Maybe he’s with dad.” “I think I saw him go over to the barn earlier. Be careful, you know I don’t like you wandering around the farm by yourself.” She picked up a pair of gloves off the counter and walked over to Jason, helping him button up his jacket. “Just go over to the barn, I don’t want you going anywhere else without me or your father.” “I can do it myself! I’m almost nine!” He grabbed the gloves from her and ran out the side door toward the large brown barn that loomed in the distance. She quickly followed to the door calling after him. “Be back before dinner time! You know how your father is!” He pretended not to hear her and took off running down the back road toward the barn. After about fifty yards, where the road bent and the house was no longer in sight, he slowed down to a brisk walk, breathing heavy. Blowing the steam out of his mouth, Jason held his fingers up to his mouth and smoked a fake cigarette. He had seen the Mexican men smoking all his life and had always wondered why their cigarettes made more steam than his. Some day he would get his own cigarettes and blow as much steam as he could; when he was a man, he told himself. As he passed the large garage where the men kept the tractors and farm equipment, he saw Carlos working on one of the sawing machines. He had on a big sweatshirt with dark stains all along the front and a dark hat that almost covered his eyes. He had a wrench in his right hand and didn’t see Jason coming toward him until he was only about ten feet away. “Carlos!” Jason shouted, smiling. Carlos looked up in surprise. “Amigo!” Carlos said, putting down the wrench and smiling. “Que pasa?” “Ohh, nothing. Have you seen Charlie?” “Charlie?” He paused, thinking. “Oh, no. I don’t think so. You can’t find him?” “Mom said maybe he was down with the guys.” “Maybe. I don’t know.” Carlos shrugged. “I don’t see him.” He walked over to the edge of the driveway where it dropped off down to the berry fields. “Maybe ask your father, no?” “That’s where I’m going,” Jason said looking toward the barn about fifty yards away. “Let me know if you see him.” “Oh si, I will!” He said smiling and then turned back to the sawing machine. Jason turned away and began walking toward the barn again. The large sliding door was wide open so he assumed his father was definitely in there. Reaching the door he peered inside, but all he could see were tools dangling from the large walls. No one was there. He walked inside and looked over toward the walk-in freezer and saw that the door was cracked. Maybe dad’s in there, he thought, but he knew he wasn’t allowed inside. He crept over to the door. “Daddy!” He shouted from outside the door, but all he could hear was the loud buzzing of the freezer fans. He tried to look through the crack, but the inside was too steamy, and he could feel the snot in his nose freezing up after a few seconds. He didn’t know whether he should go in, but he was too worried about Charlie to care about getting in trouble. He pulled the door open with all his strength and propped up a plank of wood in case he had to make a quick getaway. As he walked inside, he tried to cover his ears from the biting cold and thought he could hear a faint thumping sound. A light was coming from behind a curtain in the corner, and he thought he saw the rough outline of a figure that must be his father. Moving closer he could see dark streaks lined the other side of the curtain; the thumping was coming from behind it. “Dad,” he piped up nervously. The figure stopped what it was doing and the thumping stopped. “Daddy?” Jason said a little bit louder. He saw a hand grab the edge of the curtain and turned in fear toward the door, the rings of the curtain sliding harshly along the rail behind him. The light clicked off, and he glanced back just in time to see the large figure speeding toward him. ************* Blood dripped down Chris’ fingers, the crimson streams drying almost instantly in the cold freezer. His right hand, nearly stiff from the sub-freezing temperatures, gripped a hunting knife determined to inflict more damage on an already mutilated carcass. The frenzied rage in his face had been replaced by a hollow—and quite morbid—look of defeat seemingly unbeknownst to him. Back and forth, in and out, the blade went, striking without any sort of placement. His breathing, once heavy, had transformed into sporadic gasps as the chilling air pierced his lungs. more, more, more, you fuck, no more, bastard. Madness was setting in with no control. “Please, senor, I don—” The man began, but Brian dug the hook deeper into the man’s cheek and began to pull it down slowly. The man screamed in agony as the sharp point tore through his flesh. Chris dropped the knife, his hands now a deep purple, barely able to make a fist. He looked down at the blood on his knuckles and grinned yet still fearful of his mind’s churning, horrific thoughts. They would never stop. Not all experiences are for the better, and it had only taken mere months after shocking trauma for him to notice a change. It crept in uneasily, nightmares plunging him back into the day his world changed. They wouldn’t go away, slowly racking his brain into chaos and then dying down as his sanity drifted away. A state of apathy—no, not a state; it seemed permanent—left him estranged from his wife; his son. It was so unfamiliar, and it made him uncomfortable, he thought, but not scared, not yet. “...I swear, I can help, I swear—AH!” The man screamed as Brian ripped the hook out through his mouth. He sliced the front of the man’s shirt open, lacerating the man’s chest and then ripped his shirt off as the man riled in pain. The more he tried to forget, suppress, destroy that memory, the deeper it affected him. His veil of false stability was quickly being shed, and Chris knew this growing insanity was now taking hold of Bailey and would soon eventually grab onto Jason; sooner than he thought. Chris shifted uneasily, a distant shout grabbing his attention. He looked up at the white curtain which hid his dark secret. “Dad,” Jason’s voice rang out inside the freezer. Chris stripped the white apron he had on and wiped the rest of the blood under the flap of his shirt. “Daddy?” Jason said again. Grabbing the side of the curtain, he squinted into the darkness just in time to see his son turning toward the door. Clicking off the light he quickly ran up behind him and grabbed the sleeve of his parka, jerking him back around. “What did I tell you about coming in here!” Chris manhandled the young boy out of the room and slammed the freezer door behind him, the latch sending a resounding SNAP throughout the barn. “When will you learn to stop disobeying me, God damnit!” “But daddy—“ Jason began but was caught off as Chris’ hand struck him on the side of the face. “Don’t talk back to me, boy! I don’t wanna hear any of your damn excuses!” Chris’ face was bright red, partly from the cold but mostly from anger. Jason was crying hysterically, clutching his face where his dad had hit him. “Shut that snivelling mouth of yours and get on back to the house! We’re gonna have a serious talk later.” Jason still stood crying as if he had not heard him. “Go!” Chris shouted again, and the boy fled back to the house. Trying to get his pulse back to normal, he sighed heavily, watching his son disappear out of the barn. He walked over to some wooden shelves near the large freezer and grabbed a giant padlock, which he then snapped onto the door’s latch. Taking the key, he shoved it in his jeans’ pocket and walked back over to the shelves, staring at a small wooden box. He closed his eyes. “Oh for fuck’s sake!” Brian shook his head and plucked the wrench out of the man’s chest. Without any hesitation, he swung back and clocked him right between the eyes with the head of the tool, knocking him to the floor. The man’s eyes rolled to the back of his head as blood filled the sockets. “Jesus Christ!” Chris cried as he covered his face. His knees buckled and he fell to the floor. Brian took the wrench and threw it at him. “ARGH!” Chris screamed as he grabbed the box and slammed it down, chopping the shelf in half and splintering the box wide open. Panting heavily, he gazed down at the small metal tool, a few droplets of dried blood still caked to the head of it. He dropped down to his knees, his mouth trembling, and began to cry. ************* “I don’t know what to do. You know he promised me ten years ago after we had married that he’d never go back.” Bailey was back on the phone, now on the verge of tears. “Brian Arson. I could tell right away that man was bad, but Chris and he had such a close friendship, and I didn’t want to say anything that might hurt us.” She began to cry. “Sarah, I’m so scared for Chris. I know something happened out there in California a couple months ago.” She continued to bawl uncontrollably to her friend who tried to comfort her to no avail. Despite her emotions, she began to prepare dinner, albeit clumsily, knocking a bottle of salad dressing onto the kitchen floor. “God damnit!” She cried, attempting to cover the receiver. “Sarah, I’m sorry for bringing you into all this, I just don’t have many close friends to talk to anymore.” She grabbed a handful of paper towels and began to clean the mess, the phone slipping between her ear and shoulder. Frustration was setting in, “I’m gonna have to call you back later, or maybe tomorrow, I’m sorry.” She scrubbed the floor furiously. “I love you too. Bye.” As she knelt behind the kitchen counter, the side door opened and then slammed shut, causing her to jump. Jason sprinted past her into the hallway and ran up the stairs. Bailey could hear the door to his room close and then lock behind him. “Jason?” She got up and walked to the bottom of the staircase. She called up to him, “Honey, what’s the matter!” “Leave me alone!” He shouted back down, his voice faintly muffled from behind the door. “Jason—“ “I said leave me alone!” Bailey swallowed hard and walked back into the kitchen, stopping at the window just above the sink. She could see Chris coming from the direction of the barn and went over to the side door and stepped out onto the patio. Her tear-filled eyes quickly began boiling with anger. “What the hell did you do to him!” Chris looked up, staring at her, but did not respond. She could barely see him in the feigning sunlight but could tell by the way he was walking that something was wrong. It frightened her, but she remained there to confront him. He finally reached the patio, an envelope in his hand. “Tell me why our son’s locked himself in his room!” She glared at him, but he brushed her aside and went into the kitchen. She quickly followed. “Chris—“ “I’ve told that boy countless times that certain parts of the farm are off limits!” He shouted, whipping around to face her. “I don’t have to explain myself to you. This is my farm! He follows MY rules!” Startled, Bailey stepped back carefully. She glanced down at the envelope, hoping to change the subject for a moment, and recognized the PTC emblem in the top left corner. “What the hell is that?” Her worst fears coming true. “This?” He lifted the envelope in front of her face. “This... is none of your business. I didn’t come in here to be interrogated.” He threw the envelope down onto the kitchen table and went over to wash his hands in the sink. “What on God’s earth is wrong with that damn boy anyway? I swear he doesn’t listen to a word I say.” “Well, for Christ’s sakes, Chris, he’s only eight years old. You know how much he loves that dog! How would you feel—“ “Dog?” He turned toward her. “What are you talking about?” “You mean to tell me that he didn’t even get to explain himself!” She was furious. “I don’t know what the hell you’re talking about.” “It’s Charlie! He couldn’t find him, and all he wanted was your help for once!” “He was looking for Charlie?” Chris pretended to be interested but then shed any remote thought of sympathy. “That damn dog’s a pain in the ass anyway.” “Chris! What the hell’s matter with you? I swear—What the?” She spotted the flap of his shirt. “Is that—Is that blood? Oh, Chris, what did you do to yourself?” He looked down at the bottom of his shirt. The blood he had wiped on it was seeping through and a large stain had appeared. “It’s nothing.” He desperately tried to scrub it away with a dish towel without any luck. “I cut myself working on one of the tractors. I’m fine.” He walked back over to the table and grabbed the envelope; this time removing the letter from the inside and re-reading it. “Please don’t tell me...” Bailey began but didn’t finish. He held up a hand motioning her to stop talking, and she sobbed silently in the background. When he finished reading, Chris folded it back up with a little grin and stuffed it back into the envelope. “What’re you gonna do about Charlie, Chris?” She asked again, but he pretended to not listen. “I’m gonna be leaving for a little while, Bailey,” he said calmly. “I have a flight to California tomorrow morning, and I don’t know how long I’ll be. There’s some certain business I need to take care of,” he said, not entirely telling the truth. “But you were just out there a few months ago! Please tell me you’re not gonna be wrestling again! Chris—“ “Shut your mouth! That’s not why I’m going out there.” Again not entirely the truth. He would be wrestling, sure, but that was not why he was going to California. He had other plans. “Please, Chris, Jason needs you—“ “That dog’s probably gone by now! I’m not gonna waste my time with it. Now, I’m leaving tomorrow, and that’s final so save your blabbering. The guys are gonna be here to help you with anything you need and make sure Jason doesn’t go wandering around where he’s not supposed to!” He shifted uneasily, “I don’t need him getting locked in that freezer for one reason or another but hopefully that shouldn’t be a problem. Now I’m going upstairs to pack, and I hope dinner’s gonna be ready by the time I’m done!” Bailey tried to calm herself down, and Chris turned from the table toward the hallway. As he got to the door, he heard something in the distance and turned around. Bailey had heard it too, and they both turned toward the window. From beyond the apple trees, the loud barking was unmistakable. Chris turned toward his wife. “Well whaddaya fuckin’ know...”
