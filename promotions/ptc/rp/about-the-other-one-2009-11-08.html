Date: 2009-11-08 FOUR MONTHS AGO "I can't believe I let you talk me into this. Dressing as a fruit? Working with a guy named Coral? What the hell." "I can't believe you caved so damned easy, Gabe." Gabriel Simon had retired from professional wrestling in early 2001. He was young, incredibly young by the standards of the sport, but he'd made a promise to himself at the start of his career: if Jamie, his wife, ever became pregnant he would give it all up, walk away, and focus on being a father. He'd been orphaned as a result of a car accident at the age of five. He was determined to make sure his children knew their father. In eight years a lot had changed. He'd watched his first and only daughter grow up. He'd left the wrestling business for a desk job in information technology. He'd moved out of the city and into a suburb known for the quality of their school systems. More recently he'd been given the opportunity to make sure that little Emma Simon would never need to worry about money for college. "Well it's not like you played fair, Jared," Gabe said as he descended the last step into Jared Sykes' basement. "And I'll never know how you got Jamie on board." The chair that Jared rested in once belonged to a man named Eugene Robinson. During his time in Minnesota State Wrestling, a member federation of the National Wrestling Council, Jared had broken into Mr. Robinson's office and stolen it. It was, sadly, among the more notable moments of Jared's career in the Council. He spun in the chair once before planting his feet and smiling. "Well, to be honest, when I went to talk to her I kind of thought she was just going to slam the door on my face. I still don't think she likes me very much." Gabe smiled. "That's because she remembers everything, Jared. Everything." "Dude, I was fifteen when that happened, and I've been trying to tell you ever since that it was all only a rumor." Jared leaned back in the chair and folded his arms behind his head. "Plus I'm the one who should be holding a grudge, seeing as you're the one who broke my arm over it. A promising career in the majors all downhill, and all because Jared will never throw a fastball again. It's tragic, really." "Oh, shut up." Gabriel met Jared's sarcasm with a smile. "You've been using that line since high school, and it's still not funny." "Scouted by the Red Sox. About ready to sign a nice little contract and get shipped off to Pawtucket, but noooooo." Jared never was one to simply let a joke die gracefully, but he laughed as he dodged a flying DVD case that Gabriel threw at his head. "Alright, fine. I'll stop." "Thank God." "Just like you stopped my dreams of playing in front of a sold out crowd at Fenway Pa..." It was only when Gabe reached for a resin bust of Hal Jordan, the Green Lantern, and tested the weight did Jared break off his teasing. It wasn't so much that he feared getting hit by the small statue as much as it was the thought of having to replace that particular piece. It was numbered out of five hundred, after all. Jared raised his hands in surrender. "Okay, okay," he said, trying very hard not to laugh. "This time I mean it." "Awesome." Gabe set the bust back down on the shelf where he'd found it. "Then I guess Green Lantern will live to fly another day." "Okay, see, if you're going to be making shitty jokes now then all bets are off." Gabe paced past the chair where Jared sat, moving closer to a stack of sealed cases along the far basement wall. Though this side of the cellar was finished and furnished it seemed to be serving more as storage than actual livable space. "Jesus, Jared. Do you keep everything?" "No, not everything." Jared adjusted the height of the chair. "Only the really cool stuff." "I'm not sure I would call this really cool," Gabe replied, kneeling next to a large black and silver case marked with the word 'Earl'. "Tell me you don't still have the lawn elf." "Lawn gnome, actually," he corrected. "And yeah, I do. It was part of the schtick for a long time, G'. What did you think I'd do with him?" "Oh, I don't know. Put him on your lawn, maybe?" "Couldn't. I don't think he'd survive. It turns out that porcelain doesn't really hold up when you use it to bash people in the head over and over, so he's more or less held together by superglue and faerie magic at this point. I'm pretty he'd disintegrate the first time it rained. That's why he's in the case." "So why not just get rid of it all together and clear up some space? Seriously, Jared, you could be on that 'Obsessed' show. Get rid of some of this." "Nah, I'm not a hoarder." Jared rose from his seat and stepped slowly to join Gabe in the historical section of the basement. "It's just, I don't know... I'm not stockpiling it just to have. I could probably sell it all on eBay if I really wanted to. I'm sort of sentimental about some things. I've been wrestling in some capacity since before I could drive a car. It's kind of who I am. Hell, it's kind of who you are too, otherwise you wouldn't even be here right now." Gabriel nodded. Of all their many differences (and were they ever numerous) this was one of the few places where they really understood each other. "Everything down here," Jared continued, nodding towards the stack of boxes and crates. "Everything here is part of that. I can't give it up. It'd be like cutting off an arm or a leg." His voice trailed off. This was not the kind of conversation that he had ever been any good at having. "See? I told you. I get sentimental. Maybe even a little sappy." An awkward silence hung in the air. Truth be told Gabriel wasn't very adept at these conversations either, and while he'd never admit to it (knowing full well it would be held over his head forever) part of him respected his smaller friend all the more for being so open. When Gabriel opened his mouth to respond Jared rushed to cut him off in an attempt to prevent the situation from getting any weirder. He figured if it was strong enough to help keep the house up then it would offer him the same support against whatever half-cocked explanation he was about to receive. "No, not at all." Jared's tone was slightly argumentative, and more than a little defensive. "I don't know what it is. I don't like-her-like-her, if that's what you mean. It's just... It's complicated." Trying to explain away or rationalize his interactions with Desade was never high on his list of favorites. He wasn't even sure he understood it, which made trying to justify it all the harder. "I don't really know if I can explain it, Gabe. It's probably part fear, part respect, and part something else. It doesn't even make sense to me all the time. He closed his eyes, still hopeful that he could draw some answers out of his peculiar counterpart. "Okay than. What about the other one?" "Who? Campbell?" "Sure. ONE WEEK AGO Though his was the only car in the driveway George O'Donnell made sure to tuck the silver case deep into the bowels of his backpack before he exited the vehicle and stepped out onto the gravel. Both of his parents were still at work, and Mike would be at driver's ed until the early evening. There wouldn't be anyone else at home for at least a few hours. He slung the bag over his shoulder and headed for the backyard. There he would find the family's barn, and within it his private sanctuary. As he approached the door he felt a knot forming in his stomach. Something didn't quite feel right. The door was closed, but the latch looked out of place. He'd sworn he replaced that the last time he came out here. Part of his routine involved taking great pains to insure that everything looked the same when he left as it did when he entered. It's probably nothing, he thought. We've had some wind storms, and some heavy rain, and this is an old door, so it was probably just the weather. All that changed when he lifted the handle and stepped inside. "No," he whispered, letting go of the bag and allowing it to slide free from his shoulder. His room was empty. His personal space had been violated. Gone were the photos and the magazine clippings. No longer did any of his artwork adorn the walls. His collection of shoeboxes had been removed. The worst affront of all was that his mask, a replica of the mask worn by King Blueberry, had been removed. Moreover the hook on which it once hung had been pulled clean from the wall. "No!" All that remained in the space were a rickety table and a small stool. He charged out through the doorway, grabbing his bag as he ran. Long, awkward strides took him to the back door of his house. George's hands shook. He found it hard to find the right key let alone get it into the lock. Unable to properly focus he tried every item on his ring, growing ever frustrated as the kitchen door would not yield. Finally, after a fair amount of tugging and cursing, the lock disengaged and he burst into the house. His mask lay on the counter next to a note that confirmed his fears. George, Your father and I stayed home to clean out the shed, and we're a little concerned with what we found. We're happy to get you whatever help you need. We'll talk when we get home. -Mom George felt his extremities go numb. His world, his fantasies, his plan, they all crumbled around him. Naked and alone he slumped to the floor. Then he remembered his backpack, and the small metal case it contained. ONE WEEK AGO The first thing Mike O'Donnell observed when the gold sedan pulled to a stop was that his house was dark save for a faint light coming from the kitchen. He noticed that none of the outside lights were on, and then his eyes came to rest on his mother's car parked in the driveway only a few feet away from his brother's car. "Oh, is that your mom's car, Mike?" the woman behind the wheel asked. "Yeah, but I don't know if she's home. I wonder if something happened." Mike's attention was caught by a shadow moving through the light coming from the kitchen. He squinted to try and make out the shape, but it was gone before his eyes could focus. "Thanks for the ride, Mrs. Caruso," he said, gathering up his backpack and reaching for the door. "I'll see ya' later, Ronnie." "See ya'," came the response from the passenger seat. Mike exited the car and slung his backpack over his shoulder. He had just closed the door when the passenger-side window descended and Ronnie leaned out of the car. "Oh, hey, are you gonna log into Warcraft tonight?" "Yeah, I might. Depends on whether my mom gives me sh...," he wanted to say 'shit', but with Ronnie's mother within earshot it wouldn't have been the best idea. "I mean, she, uhh, she might give me a hard time." "Damn." "Yeah, we'll see. If she's not home then maybe I can sneak a few minutes." "Alright cool." Ronnie ducked back into the car, and nodded as he rolled the window back up. The Caruso's waited until Mike opened the front door of his house before they pulled away. He watched the taillights slowly disappear into the darkness as he closed the front door, latching it shut. "Moooooom?" he called out into the darkened living room. "I thought you were going to pick me up from driver's ed tonight. I had to get a ride home with Ronnie." He let his backpack slide from his shoulder, and casually tossed onto the floor by the television. Not receiving a reply, he stepped over to the stairs and flicked the nearby light switch. "Hey, mom?" he called out again, this time up towards the second floor. "Are you home?" One again his question went unanswered. "What the fuck," he muttered. He knew that if she heard him cursing in the house that there would be hell to pay, but so far it seemed that he was home alone. Then again, he did see that shadow move through the kitchen. "This is so gay," he whispered in frustration, stepping through the living room and towards the hall. From where he stood he could see a soft light from the kitchen bleeding into the hallway, and he stepped closer to investigate. "Hey, George, is that you? Seriously guys, what the hell." He was starting to get concerned. He could understand his mother perhaps being out with his dad, even though it wasn't like her to forget to pick him up. George was something else though. If his car was in the driveway than that sure as hell meant he was home, and probably watching the Discovery Channel. George didn't have much of a life. From around the corner he could see his mother seated at the kitchen table, leaning across the tabletop with her head resting on her arm. "Hey, mom," he said, bounding into the room. "I thought you were going to pick me up." When she didn't reply he approached the table slowly, an overwhelming sense of concern building in him. He found it suddenly hard to swallow, but he moved forward nonetheless. He extended a trembling hand, recoiling at the frigid touch of her skin, and then he saw the blood. He staggered backwards and tripped over a nearby chair. His legs tangled as he toppled to the floor. The urge to vomit came on fast, and there was nothing he could do to prevent it. When he tried to stand his legs betrayed him, and he fell to the floor once again. Mike's eyes fell on a figure in the corner of the room, shrouded in darkness save for a faint glint of silver. "G-George!" he cried out, straining his lungs. "Mom!" Mike started to cry; deep, soundless sobs that wracked his body and burned his eyes. George O'Donnell answered his brother with what sounded like sniffling. "She... she saw," the elder O'Donnell said, his voice hushed and eerily calm. "She went and she saw it and then that meant that she knew." "George, no. No." "It wasn't supposed to be this way, Mike. It wasn't supposed to be this way." George shifted in the darkness, rising from his crouch. In the soft light of the kitchen Mike saw the revolver and felt his hair stand on end. "I didn't want this. I didn't. You have to believe me, Mike. Tell me you believe me. Tell me!" George, now on his feet, strode slowly towards his brother. Terrified, Mike crawled backwards, desperately trying to get to his feet. His hand found a nearby table, and he used what little strength had not been sapped away to try and balance himself on his feet. "TELL ME!" George bellowed. Even if he wanted to answer his brother Mike found it impossible to think let alone speak.
