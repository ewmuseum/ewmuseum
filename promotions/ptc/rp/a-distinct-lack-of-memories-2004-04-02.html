A distinct lack of memories

2 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-04-02 A Distinct Lack of Memories It’s a nice neighbourhood, with low crime rates and a sense of community spirit. There’s little to no crime, and that’s probably why X Factor decided to live there. As many have found out to their cost, X Factor is a fantastic thief, which mostly comes down to having been brought up in Liverpool, England. In the centre of a wide arcing semicircle of tall white houses sits something of a park. Within the park it a pristine white bench, and on it sits Ian Fetzer, the man hoping to make an impact as ‘The Fool’. He looks at his surroundings, smiles ruefully, and looks down at the action figure sitting in his right hand. It’s a crude toy, with joints at just the shoulders and hips, and a head that doesn’t even turn. The paint is chipped and there are a few dents in it, but it’s definitely a figure of the man holding it, right down to the harlequin pants and green t-shirt. The clothes are the same as they ever were, but if the action figure was a better facsimile of his past, then you’d be able to see the difference between then and now. It’s all in the eyes. The fool of old was a focused man, one who was willing to be funny and who would entertain the masses, but who could throw it all aside and become a fierce competitor, striking out with lightning speed to defeat his opponents. He had a veneer of frivolity, but under it all was something hard, a determination to not just have fun, but to also be the best. The Fool smiles at his action figure one more time, and you can see that the only thing remaining is the veneer. There’s nothing underneath it all, and in that way he is hollow, trying to be supported on a vacuum, and failing each and every time he tries. The man who approaches him, X Factor, he knows that for The Fool to even make it past the first round, he will need something to fall back on, something to grab and make his own when the veneer cracks. Factor: Hey there Ian. Fool: Hey there. Silence descends over them again. Both men know that what they really want seems to be out of their grasp, but they don’t know how to approach this at all. Factor: Any more ideas? Fool: Well, I was thinking of trying to train a tiger to unicycle. Factor: Any relating to the tournament? Fool: Oh… none. Factor: We’ll think of something. Ian plays with his action figure for a few seconds, before looking up at X Factor, who is still standing there. Fool: That Justin Walker fellow seems pretty focused. Factor: Yeah. The small talk is excruciating, meaningless platitudes churned out by two men afraid of the future holding nothing for them but failure. Fool: That Robb Hardy guy, he seems… Factor: Seems what? Fool: Scary? Is he a superhero or something? Factor: I don’t know. Fool: I think he might be some sort of superhero gangster type. Factor: Really? Fool: Hard to tell. Factor: I see. More silence. They’ve seen what Justin Walker and Robb Hardy can do, and they know that if he was going all out, Fetzer could take them both on. Yet, in his current state, The Fool’s best chance of progressing looks to be trying to pay off his opponents. X Factor would be more likely to suggest a gangland shooting, but against someone as streetwise as Robb Hardy, it would probably fail anyway. Admitting defeat, X Factor slumps down on the bench next to Ian, and buries his head in his hands. As he does so, the action figure in The Fool’s hand starts to move. Of course, this is The Fool’s own doing, but he seems oblivious to it all the same. X Factor looks over at it, and nudges Ian. Fool: Oh, sorry Mister Action Figure, what was that? The toy does a little dance on Ian’s leg. Fool: That’s a fantastic idea! Factor: What did you come up with? Fool: It wasn’t me, it was Mister Action Figure! A roll of the eyes from the deeply cynical X Factor. Factor: Okay, what did he suggest? Fool: Well, while I can’t seem to remember how to wrestle in my old style, and whilst I lack the necessary steely resolve to just ‘gut it out’ and persevere to succeed on the talent I currently have, neither of my opponents wrestle in my old style or have any of my old mannerisms. Factor: Meaning? Fool: Meaning that I can’t be my old self, not for the moment anyway, but perhaps with a little bit of training and study of people that I am up against, I can take on enough of what makes them tick and add it to what I still have, such as my resilience, and it might be enough to carry me through to the next round, where we can worry more about me getting back my memory and becoming a fantastic wrestler again. X Factor just sits there, his jaw wide open. He looks down at the action figure, and then at the smiling face of The Fool. Factor: Ian, sometimes I… well, it’s just that you… Fool: It’s a bad idea? Factor: No, it’s a fantastic idea! Fool: I know! Mister Action Figure is so smart! X Factor wants to tell Ian that the action figure can’t talk, and that it means there must be some shred of himself left on the inside, but with the smile on The Fool’s face it would be like kicking a puppy. Mind you, Factor’s kicked a few puppies in his time, so it’d be like kicking his own puppy, and that’s something not even he would do. Fool: So, my opponents are Justin Walker and Robb Hardy. I wonder what I should… The Fool’s train of thought is interrupted as an armoured car speeds into the little park and slides to a halt just a little in front of them. The Fool’s eyes widen as out jump ten men, all armed with guns and knives and other nasty implements of death. Factor: Run! Run for your life! The men, all dressed in camouflage gear, turn their attentions to someone walking to the side of X Factor and The Fool. It’s a woman pushing a pram! Oh Lord, the humanity of it all, they’re going to kill her! Fool: We can’t let this happen… hey, stop running away, Factor!!! The cowardly Englishman flees the scene, leaving The Fool to run, dance and skip his way to the woman, dodging the knives and bullets flying his way. As he does so, one bullet finds the mark though, nailing him right in the chest! The Fool falls backwards, as the camouflage guys come in… but then The Fool flips to his feet, grabs the lady and her baby-filled pram and runs to safety! Lady: Amazing! You saved our lives, but how are you not dead? Fool: Well, as luck would have it, I placed a metal cigarette case in my chest pocket just this morning! He pulls it out, and it does indeed have a bullet stuck in it. Lady: Wow, that’s unbelievably uncanny! Fool: I know… I don’t even smoke! His eyes narrow. Fool: Hang on, you look like that woman I once knew! Margot, can it be you? Can it be that you didn’t die in that horrific garden shears accident, and that we cremated the wrong body? My God, Margot I’ve missed you, let’s go and make love until the morning! Lady: Ummm, nope, you’ve got me confused with someone else. Fool: Drat. Lady: So, thanks for saving our lives, but we’re off now. They wander off, just as X Factor comes out from hiding behind a phone booth. Factor: That was somewhat strange and coincidental. Fool: That she looked like Margot? Factor: The whole bloody thing! Fool: Well, it’s certainly given me a taste of the high life that Robb hardy lives. Factor: I’ll stick to losing rather than go through that again. Fool: No, don’t you see? I’ve never felt so alive, at least not that I can remember! I can see why he does what he does, it’s the adrenaline buzz, it’s living on the edge and doing whatever it takes to get the job done! I feel revitalised, I feel like I could take someone on, and maybe even beat them! X Factor shrugs. Factor: Well, I guess if it’s working for you. Fool: It is! Now I just have to see if I can add in a bit of whatever motivates Justin Walker, and I’ll be set for the match! Ian’s friend goes into a thoughtful looking pose, and then his eyes brighten up as if a light bulb has magically appeared over his head. Factor: You say your friend Margot died? Fool: Yes, in a terrible gardening accident, I really miss her. Factor: Were you close? Fool: Fairly close, but she left me in the end, just like everyone else always leaves me on my own. I’m a curse, a joke, a millstone hung around the neck of anyone and everyone I come across. Factor: Oh come on Ian, it’s not that bad. Fool: We’re both former World Champions! Ever since we’ve been hanging out together you’ve become as bad as I am. It’s just all too much, too hard for me to cope with. Factor: Don’t give up. Fool: Oh, I won’t give up. I’m going to rise above it all, stronger than ever, making a place in history for myself as I crush my opponents, as I revel in their bones snapping and their sinews being forced beyond breaking point, tearing apart at the seams until their anguished cried sound louder than a thousand gunshots, shots they wish could bring their pitiful existence to an end. Factor: Woah. Fool: Tales will be told of this day, stories will be written and told to children as a warning. It was the day that Ian Fetzer threw off the shackles of boredom, stopped taking the utter crap that others threw at him and became the unstoppable force. It was the day when Justin Walker and Robb hardy signed their own death warrants by daring to stand up to a man who doesn’t care about their past, only that their futures will be wracked with pain and defeat. The Fool exhales deeply, his eyes burning with some sort of inner passion and desire to cause pain. Next to him, X Factor is still looking shocked. Fool: Well, that was good. I think I’ve got all I need for now. Factor: What? Fool: I’ve experienced what Justin Walker and Robb Hardy have to offer, and I feel stronger for it. It’s an unusual feeling, different somehow. Factor: Yeah, you’re scaring me instead of making me laugh. Fool: Well, it’s going to have to do for the moment. Factor: Then next week? Fool: Next week we try to find out how to get my memory back, and we try properly. Factor: That, my friend, is a deal. The two men walk off, back in the direction of X Factor’s house, presuming that the nasty men with guns and knives have run off. Ian Fetzer, The Fool, is still a veneer, but one that’s got some patches on, and it slightly tougher than it looks. It won’t carry him through the whole tournament, but it might be enough to get him past this round…
