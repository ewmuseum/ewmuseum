GOP: The College Years! (Part 4)

2 December 2006
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-12-02 It was 9 AM on a Sunday morning, and the Campus Republican office had a nice crowd assembled inside. Usually, 9 AM on a Sunday morning meant sleeping in for usually hung-over college students, but this morning was a little different. In front of a dual cassette CD player stereo, Jamey Bossy held up a tape. Gordon Powell and Maggie Meltzer stood right next to him. "At 5 o'clock this morning, history was made. KUT aired an episode of Campus Talk, the FCC mandated show about community happenings, which they use to highlight a student organization. Several student organizations have been featured in the past, like the Campus Democrats, the Mexican-American Student Union, the Filipino Student Organization, The Gay and Lesbian Alliance… even the Transgender Student Union. But never the Campus Republicans… until today. Bright and early, our own Gordon Powell was featured on the show!" A small cheer went up from the crowd. Gordon smiled and bowed. Jamey continued his speech. "Now I know most of you don't get up that early in the morning, so I taped the appearance. Let's take a listen!" Jamey put the tape in the deck and pressed play. The speech was grainy like you'd expect something taped of radio would be. "Hello everyone, you're listening to Campus Talk on KUT with [name withheld], and my guest this morning is Gordon Powell." "It's a pleasure to be here, Jane." As the rest of the Republicans listened to the re-broadcast, Jamey and Gordon started to walk off towards Jamey's office. "So, how did you end up faring with your O-chem paper?" "Oh, well, I gave the shredded remains of my paper to Dr. Harley, and I had Maggie and Marissa come over to to his office as eyewitness accounts. He let me redo the paper, but he was only going to count it half-credit." "What? That's a load of horseshit!" "Well, he said late's late, and Joe had a bunch of people corroborate for him that I never handed in the paper." Jamey just shook his head. "I mean, Joe was his best TA, but he had eyewitnesses and a shredded paper. He had to compromise somehow. I mean, I still got a B in the class. A B's not the end of the world." "No, it's not in context, but you got the full amount of credit that you could have gotten on that paper, right?" "Yeah." "And if that dirty egg sucking dog didn't mess with your work, you'd have gotten an A easily, right?" "Well… yeah." "See, that's the kind of thing that boils me up inside. Liberal bias on this campus." "Liberal… bias?" "What, you've never heard of that before?" Gordon shook his head. "Well, in various places, like college campuses, there's a distinct liberal bias. They all slant to the left or the Democratic Party. Most of them are pretty reasonable, like Dr. Harley seems to be. I mean, I still think it's horseshit that he didn't count everything, but it's still better than what some professors here will do." "You mean there are professors on campus who'll do worse?" "Yeah. There are professors on campus that I've heard horror stories about, that they mess with people's grades and ostracize them in class. It's horrible." "Oh." Gordon was silent for a moment, still somewhat in shock of what he was just told. "Umm… do you ever try to fight back?" "Fight back?" "Yeah, like try to get bad grades appealed and such." "Well, I think there is a method for fighting what you think is an unfair grade. I've never had to do it, but then again, I've never really been in that position. People know who I am, but whenever I'm in class with a real bastard of a professor, I just fall back." Gordon started to realize why it took himself to enter the Republicans for them to be an effective group. The next day was the beginning of a new semester at UT. Gordon and Maggie walked to their first classes hand in hand, although both of them had different schedules on Mondays. Maggie took a sociology elective, while Gordon took History of Europe 101. When they got to the hall where Maggie's class was, they exchanged a quick kiss. "All right sugar, we'll meet up for dinner tonight, and we can talk about our first day of classes this term." "Yeah babe. I'm sure we'll have lots to talk about. Love ya." "I love you too." Gordon walked to his class with a smile on his face and in his heart. In terms of school subjects, chemistry was his first love, but history was a mistress. (Just in case you couldn't tell from this series, he didn't get out much :p) He looked forward to his first University level history class. Regular history in grade school and high school was so mundane, so basic. He thirsted for a challenge, for the delving into the deeper annals of history that the course promised to him. Gordon sat in the lecture hall, up near the front, and watched as all the other people came filing in. Finally, the professor came in, a stately man, graying black hair slicked back. Gordon could sense an air of arrogance about him as he strode into the room. "Good morning class. My name is Dr. Babel, and this is European History I. I hope you all came prepared to learn, because there is no one in this University who knows about history better than I do. We're going to dive in headfirst. I don't believe in long introductions because we have a lot to learn. If you want to get to know me, come during office hours." Yep, Gordon thought. He's an arrogant prick. "All right, the first and best place to start off with any understanding of European history is the conversion of Emperor Constantine in the Fourth Century. It was this moment that would set the tone for the history of Europe up until the Reformation, a sort of conversion by sword, do as I say, not as I do, just because God said so mentality that European monarchs had over the years. It's the same kind of mentality that Christian sects and the Catholic church would rather follow to this day." Gordon's face contorted. Christianity is a religion of peace, at least that's what his pastor always preached on Sunday. This was wrong, dead wrong. Even though Gordon thought Catholics to be, by and large, the most illogical of all Christians, he knew that John Paul II was a good man and certainly didn't want to return to the days of the Crusades. Throughout the rest of class, Dr. Babel continued to preach on about the Roman Empire post-conversion with a definite anti-Christian slant. At several times during the lecture, Gordon felt like raising his hand to try and dispute some of the things that the professor was saying, but he kept the advice Jamey gave in the back of his mind, and thought it better not to. After the class was over, Gordon plodded out of the lecture hall amazed at what he was just hearing. He never heard his high school history teachers speak with such a bent before. As classes got out, Gordon met Maggie at the Taco Bell to discuss their first day of their second semester. They sat at their regular booth and chowed down on their usuals. Maggie took a bite out of her MexiMelt and raised her head to look at her boyfriend. "So sugar, how was your first day of class?" "It was okay, I guess. I mean, I kinda like that we're actually going to get to synthesize real polymers in my polymer science class." "Dangit, I'm so jealous. I knew I should have taken that class." Gordon chuckled a bit, but then his face went a little sour again. "But my history class… man, that guy went off the whole class. He kept talking about Constantine and how Chrsitianity was evil like it was historical fact." "Oh, who's teaching that class?" "Dr. Babel." "Oh, him! I heard some things about him. Like how one time, he yelled at a Jewish kid for wearing a yarmulke to class." "Really? Did you hear that from Jamey?" "Naw, from my roommate's older sister who goes here." "Oh… well, maybe he'll settle down a bit as the term goes on." "Maybe. I don't know, she said she dropped the class after the second week because she got pregnant and had to go home." "Oh wow… maybe I'll just ride the term out then." The next class was that Wednesday. Gordon went to class and sat up front again. Dr. Babel walked in after everyone was seated and started in on his lecture for the day. "You know, I don't mean to take the course off track, but this Republican witch hunt against President Clinton is outrageous. He's done nothing legally wrong…" For fifteen minutes, Babel went on about how the GOP was wronging Clinton, and about how the Leader of the Free World shouldn't be trifled with such charges. Gordon was aghast. Not only was he slanting material against what he believed, he wasn't talking about anything remotely close to Europe or history. Every class day for the rest of this week and the next two, Babel taught history littered with his own opinions. He'd expound on his own politics, and then afterwards, tell his class "You can thank me later by voting Democrat." By the end of week three, the spew coming out of Babel's mouth had finally gotten to Gordon enough that he got up the gumption to confront the professor as he was leaving class. He walked right up to the table in the front of the class where Babel was gathering his things to go. "Excuse me, Professor Babel?" "Yes?" "I'd like to talk to you about your lecture today…" "I'm sorry, I'm very busy today, too busy to talk to anyone, let alone a student such as yourself, someone who makes faces every time I say something. It's not curteous to do that, especially when someone is teaching you valuable life lessons along with historical truths. Now if you'll excuse me, I have a train to catch. If you want to talk to me, visit me during office hours." With that, Babel walked out of the lecture hall. Gordon's face got so red that he resembled a beet with eyes, a nose and a mouth. He stormed out of class and made a bee-line to the quad. He didn't care if he ran anyone over or knocked anything down. He charged with the tenacity of a bull to the center square, looked up to the sky and screamed out. Everyone around him looked at him as if he had three heads. Finally, he felt a tug on his shoulder. Gordon turned around to find Maggie there. "Goodness gracious, sugar! What are you doing?" Gordon was too flabbergasted to answer. "C'mon sugar, let's go get something to eat. WE can talk about it there." Maggie dragged Gordon to Antonio's Pizzeria. Over a thin-crust pepperoni pie, Gordon explained all his feelings about Dr. Babel to Maggie, and how it made him feel. "…and it's just brutal. I mean, he's indoctrinating people to believe his far left views are what's right. I really feel like just saying something to him during class." "And why don't you, sugar? I mean, you stood up to Brent and all them Democrats." "Yeah, but none of the Democrats have tenure. I don't want to get a bad grade in this class just because I'm speaking up." "Oh nonsense. What happened to the Gordon who shoved a megaphone in Brent's face and yelled at him because it was the right thing to do? What happened to the Gordon who got up and spoke at that rally in spite of the fact that a jealous TA was going to shred his paper because it was the right thing to do?" "Well… 'cuz Jamey said laying back and not getting in the professor's faces was the right thing to do in this case." Maggie's eyes grew wide. She took an ice cube out of her Coke and flung it at Gordon's head. "Ah! What the hell was that for?" "For listening to Jameson Bossy in matters of standing up for yourself and what's right, that's what. I mean, I like Jamey and all, and he's a great guy, but he's got no balls, sugar. None. You should know that. I mean, if you want to spend the next twelve weeks listening to his garbage unabated, then follow Jamey's lead. But if you want to make a difference, if you want to stand up for what you think is right, then stand up to him… during class. Especially if he's going to blow you off like that like you told me. Make an example of him." Gordon rubbed his chin. "Yeah… I will! I love you baby." "Love you too." Maggie smiled at him as she took another sip out of her Coke through her straw. The next class day, Gordon sat in his normal seat as still as a statue. A look of dead seriousness came over his face as he awaited the arrival of his newfound nemesis to class. Like clockwork, after the last student came in, Babel walked in, placed his bag down and started spouting off. "All this terrorism happening in Israel, and you know why? Because we continue to back the Israeli state even though we know that that land was wrongfully taken away from the Palestinians. Classic Western arrogance, thinking we could repay the Jews for the Holocaust by giving them land…" Gordon raised his hand, the first movement he made since sitting down. He was ignored. "…And now, we support that blunder. I say we make a move to fix that. I say we move to expel the Jews out of Israel and give the land back to the Palestinians. If we give them what they want, the terrorism will surely stop." "Excuse me…" Gordon's first statement in interruption was greeted with a pause and a nasty look from Babel. That was all as he went back on his tirade. "As I was say…" "EXCUSE ME!" "Do you dare interrupt me?" "Yes, I do." Babel scowled. "Oh really then? What insight do you have to add that you had to interrupt my speech." "What you're advocating is appeasement. You want to fix this problem in the Middle East by giving in to all the demands of the PLO and Hamas and every other extremist group, thinking they'll go away. Well you know who else tried that strategy in the late 30s?" "Of course I do! I teach Euro…" Gordon cut him off. "Neville Chamberlain. He gave Hitler everything Hitler wanted, and it wasn't enough. Hitler didn't just want Sudeten, he wanted all of Europe, and all the Jews either subjugated or dead. That's what. That's what these terrorist groups want too. They don't just want their land back, they want jihad on…" "That's enough! If you want to continue this, speak to me after class." "But…" "I said speak to me after class, or else I will give you an automatic F on your next quiz." Gordon remained silent for the rest of the class. After it was over, he approached Babel. "Dr. Babel, should we stay here, or…" "No, I'm actually not interested in continuing the debate. I just wanted to tell you something." Babel approached Gordon, menacing look on his face. His pointed finger inched closer and closer to Gordon's chest, but he stopped it short of physical contact. "You… are the first person ever to stand up to me in class. I can respect that. It's always good to have a differing viewpoint." Gordon recoiled in shock, albeit a pleasant one. "Just don't interrupt me during class again. Okay?" "Okay." "Good… remember, your research paper on the Crusades is due next class." "I know." Gordon left class with a sense of satisfaction, like he accomplished something great. He reached out to another mind, diametrically opposed to everything he believes in, and came to an understanding. He rushed home to look over his Crusades report. As he opened the Word file where it was saved, he realized that how he wrote it was all wrong. It may have been factually correct, but he wrote it with the words Jamey told him in the back of his mind. Because of that, in his rereading, he found it to be completely sterile. Without a moment's hesitation, he began to reword the entire report. He went from having an paper that just described what happened during the Crusades with a few block quotes from historians to making a stunning, cogent argument in favor of the Crusades being fought. Everything lined up perfectly in Gordon's mind. The facts he pulled from all his sources fell in together and supported his argument. He had written the perfect paper. He also stayed up all night doing it. As his roommate Jerry was waking up, Gordon was putting the finishing touches on his rewrite. "Dude, were you up all night writing that thing?" "Yeah… I didn't even keep track of time." "Like, no shit. All that pecking on your keyboard, I thought I was having fucking dreams about woodpeckers and shit." "I'm sorry… but could you do me a favor? Could you read this for me?" "Yeah… later though, when I actually fucking wake up." Gordon rushed to class the next day with his paper in tow. Everyone he showed it to, Jamey, Jerry, Maggie, thought it was well-written and a definite A. They also thought it a bit weird that he got so excited over a term paper, but they came to accept Gordon's eccentricities when it came to school. The first thing that Dr. Babel requested when he got into class were the term papers. Gordon hopped out of his seat and handed his to the professor like he was a jackrabbit dashing across the plain from a hungry coyote. "You seem awfully excited to hand this paper in." "How can you tell?" "Well, usually, when I ask for papers, people just hand them into the front and give them to me in one shot." Gordon nodded and then sheepishly went back to his seat. The next week for him was a blur. Gordon was eager in anticipation to see what he got on his term paper. He couldn't stop talking about it to anyone, including Maggie. "I can't wait to get this paper back," Gordon said as he ate Sunday brunch at the local Perkins. "I know sugar, you keep talking about it. You told me last week when you handed it in. You told me the other day when we went to get pizza. You even mentioned it while we were making love last night, which actually perturbed me a bit." "I'm sorry babe, but I mean… I worked so hard on that paper. I mean, I just hope that he enjoys the differing viewpoint and respects it enough to see where we're coming from." "I know sugar, I know. But I mean, you're making too big a deal out of it. Just wait until it comes." Gordon went back to class on Monday, jittering like a young child who had just been given espresso. It had been almost a week since he handed in his paper, and Dr. Babel had a reputation for being a pretty quick grader. The last student took his seat, and like clockwork, Babel walked into the class. "I have your papers graded, and I have to say, I'm rather impressed with the turnout. There were a few bad eggs, but for the most part, you guys seem to get it. You'll get them after class though." Gordon got so anxious that he felt like he was going to burst. The one hour lecture felt like it took a week, and each word that Dr. Babel said went in one ear and out the other. Nothing mattered to Gordon except for this paper. He anticipated several comments on it, almost like a political debate through the medium of critical writing, a political debate that would garner him an A and the respect of his adversary. Finally, it happened. "All right, class is dismissed. Please gather your papers. They're sorted in piles by last name." Gordon shot out of his seat and went right to the "P" pile. He rifled through the papers and finally found his. He clutched it tightly to his chest and looked at the front page. No comments. Nothing. He walked out of class and went through the pages, one by one. No red ink, no comments, no nothing until the last page, at the bottom, below the last entry in his bibliography. It was his grade, written in big, red ink. It was an F.
