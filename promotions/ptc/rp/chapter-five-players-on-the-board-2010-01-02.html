Date: 2010-01-02 There was one major problem with eyewitness testimony, so notorious that even the hack writers of TV crime shows knew to treat it as an inviolate truth: that, despite what its very nature would seem to imply, eyewitness testimony was notoriously unreliable. There was the not terribly subtle issue of biases or prejudices, distance from the crime, the witness’s vision, lighting conditions…any one of a million variables to consider. For those who would argue that, hey, that may apply to witnesses but what about the victims? It would seem obvious that the folks who’ve actually been affected by crime would be able to tell you all about the people who had victimized them. Once again, though: no. Victims of fraud or other white collar crime often won’t be good witnesses due to a sense of frustration or shame. If we move on to the area of violent crimes…well, let’s just say that it’s asking a lot of someone to stay calm and collected when they’re being beaten, raped or assaulted. There’s the body’s natural reaction to trauma, the feelings of shock and disbelief…not to mention that someone is doing their damndest to try and hurt you. Which was precisely the difficult situation Alanna Marshall found herself in at this time. It wouldn’t stay that way forever, certainly not. Once the effects of the attack had worn off and faded away, Alanna would be able to remember that day with crystal clarity. She’d be able to see it with pinpoint accuracy in her mind as though she were watching a movie. Be able to zoom in on every detail. Be able to tell the police that The Voice had blue eyes and wild, dirty black hair. Be able to tell the cops that The Voice stank of whiskey and cigarettes, how the heat from his breath had felt on her face as he’d whispered in her ear, how his body had pressed against hers as he’d threatened her, made her believe that he would kill her right then and there and nobody would ever now… But that would take weeks. Weeks for Alanna to come to terms with the trauma and the fear. Weeks for the effects of her surgery to wear off, for her to be strong enough to even consider coming down to the police station to make a statement. Weeks in which the memories would linger at the back of her mind, where images would flash into her brain in jagged bursts of terror before disappearing again. Weeks in which Alanna Marshall often wondered if she would ever be okay again. Weeks that, it turned out, the Marshalls didn’t have. She’d been told it would hurt. She’d been fully briefed by the doctors, told just how invasive the surgery would have to be and what kind of after effects it would have on the human body. Hell, it wasn’t as though Alanna Marshall was any stranger to pain in the first place. Ever since she and Lance had gotten into professional wrestling together, she’d been the target of physical attacks more times than she cared to count. Hell, she’d sometimes had folks who targeted her specifically, not particularly giving a damn to whom she was married. Right now, though, was different. Right now was pain on a level different than anything she had ever felt before. Right now felt as though every nerve, every synapse, every cell in her body was on fire. Moving just the slightest fraction of an inch sent pain searing through her body, limiting her range of movement. Not that she particularly wanted to move right now anyway. Now with some lunatic holding a scalpel to her neck. “My, but it’s such a very pleasant evening, don’t you think?” The Voice rasped. “Now, I think it’s time that you and I had a little chat…” Screw that, Alanna thought. “help,” she croaked weakly. “somebody…” The blade of the scalpel pressed tighter against her neck. “Uh uh, I really wouldn’t. You wouldn’t want to push yourself so hard, not so soon after such a difficult operation. Why, I even hear that one of the nurses was so clumsy as to give you too much sedative. That’s the sort of thing someone could lose their job over,” the Voice said. He finished with a giggle, pleased at his little joke. Alanna quickly understood. “you…” “Oh yes,” The Voice slurred. “It’s good to see that you’re not as much of a brain dead simpleton as so many of the women in your “business” are.” The Voice grabbed Alanna’s right breast in his free hand, twisting roughly. Alanna couldn’t help but wince in pain, biting off a scream at the base of her throat. She winced as her flesh was roughly kneaded beneath The Voice’s calloused fingers. “I imagine this was probably part of the attraction for that idiot that you married,” rasped The Voice, “knowing that at least his wife was all as nature intended. Probably helped him feel better about his misshapen, steroid bloated form to know that at least his wife was genuinely beautiful.” “fuckyou…” The Voice’s free hand left Alanna’s breast long enough to backhand her across the face. “How typical. All of you noble and holier than thou types…put you under some real trouble and suddenly you sink right back into the dirt with the rest of us. Too stupid to realize the stink of hypocrisy comes off you in waves.” “howdidyou…” The Voice laughed. “You’re actually going to ask me that? Some idiot manages to get a bomb on a plane in Detroit and you can’t figure out how I got past security. I snatched your son away from everyone, did it in broad daylight…and still your tiny little brain can’t comprehend how I managed to be here, holding your life in my hands.” The pupils in Alanna’s eyes widened. “you took…” “That’s right, bitch. I’m the one who took your son away from you. I’m the one who did whatever I wanted to him for an entire week. I’m the one who listened to the mewling little brat as he screamed and whined and cried for Mommy and Daddy, how he wailed every time I hit him, how he screamed in fear every time I touched him. Her brain screamed, forcing her stomach to settle itself as much as it could. She was not about to let this bastard have the satisfaction of seeing what he was doing to her. Instead, she tried screaming again as loud as her weary lungs and shredded throat would allow. “help…somebody help…” “I really wouldn’t bother,” said The Voice. “Listen closely and you’ll hear something interesting going on further down the ward.” Now that her attention was called to it, Alanna could hear the sounds of screaming and raised voice coming from farther away, occasionally punctuated with the clang of something being thrown and hitting the floor. “The gentleman five cubicles down,” answered The Voice, “was brought in as the victim of a rather nasty car crash. The man in the other car was killed instantly. Which is a bit of a shame as the man we have is currently in the process of doing some rather unwanted detoxing. Not sure from what, mind you, but the volume he’s been able to achieve certainly indicates that it’s something interesting.” The voice paused for a moment, a slight smile creeping across his face. “Well, it’ll definitely be interesting once the PCP I slipped into his water takes effect.” Don’t panic, girl. You’re not helpless. You’re not. Someone’s going to have to come and check on you eventually. If you can just keep him talking long enough… “whatdoyouwant?” “Oh dear,” snapped The Voice, “you’re proving to be such a disappointment, Mrs. Marshall. After everything that’s happened so far, you really expect me to just stand here and tell you all of my evil plans. You’re either the stupidest bitch alive…and I’d like to imagine dear old Lancey boy has at least some standards…or you’re trying to play me. News flash: you’re not very good at it.” “wehaven’tdone...” “You offend me by existing,” barked The Voice, “parading around on television and in magazines. Playing the great All-American hero couple, letting the idiots running around free out there believe that true love and happiness actually exist. That there are good people in the world. We can’t allow that.” “we? youmeanAlex?” The Voice laughed, just softly enough to keep from raising any undue attention. “You still don’t get it, do you, you stupid little bitch? Your horizons really are so limited and so narrow that you think poor little Alexandra Pierce is the be-all and end-all of evil? “ The contempt dripped from his words and pooled in little puddles at his feet. “The people I work for,” The Voice continued, “they know Alexandra Pierce for who she really is. Just a little piece of street garbage, polished to a high sheen but still stinking to high heaven underneath. You see Alexandra Pierce as scary and evil and untouchable. We see the truth of her: a social experiment, to be thrown away when no longer of use.” “you’refullofshit” “And there’s that Marshall bravado I keep hearing about,” smiled The Voice. “It’s just a shame that it took this long to come out. So, what now? You’re going to tell me to go to hell? Go on and on about how your big, strong husband is going to find me and kill me? Maybe even try and hurt me yourself, as fucked up as you are right now?” If Alanna Marshall’s mouth wasn’t bone-dry at this moment, a rather large glob of spit would have landed smack dab in the middle of The Voice’s face. As it was, the gesture was made…but the results were empty. “That’s it,” sneered The Voice. “That’s the best you could do? I do my very best to teach your husband and your family the truth of how things are and that’s the best you can do?” “whattruth?” “That no hero will come riding to your rescue,” said the Voice, his expression slowly from disappointment into rage. “That I can come and hurt you anytime I like and there is not a damn thing you can do about it. That the hero does not always win. That the bad guys are smarter and sneakier and dirtier and that is why they win.” “don’tthinkwealready…” “I don’t think you have the slightest clue,” snapped The Voice. “But you will. Oh, believe me, you will.” The Voice then reached into his pocket and pulled out a syringe, filled full with a liquid that Alanna couldn’t identify. Without warning, he jabbed the needle point into Alanna’s arm, plunging down and withdrawing the syringe as quickly as possible. The Voice smiled as she winced in pain. “whatdid..” “You see that nice little machine next to you,” snarled The Voice, “the one that pumps out a little bit of morphine into your system every time you press the button? Well, I just did something like that…only multiply that by…oh, I dunno? One hundred? A thousand, maybe? I know it’s something with a few zeros at the end.” The Voice found himself getting happier as Alanna Marshall’s eyes widened in fear, her hands scrabbling feebly at the bed beneath her. Everything was still going according to plan. Not only would his employers be happy about that but he was just having the most fun he’d had in…well, forever. “There isn’t enough in that syringe to kill you…necessarily. Not if the doctors and nurses out there are in any way competent…although, having seen them at work today, I do have my doubts.” Alanna Marshall’s movements were already starting to slow. It wouldn’t be long now. “But that doesn’t mean we…well, I really…can’t have a little fun.” He kept the smile on his face as he slowly crept on to the bed, pinning her down with the weight of his body. The Voice traced a slow, lazy path down Alanna’s prone form with the scalpel. “I figure you’re an incredible fuck. You’re doing twice the work, after all, given how bad steroids shrink his balls and his dick. I wonder what it is, though, that really gets Lance off. What really makes that little pop gun of his fire a shot or two.” The scalpel made its way back to Alanna’s neck as he continued. “I think I’ve got it, though. I think I know what you do that really gets Lance all hot.” “I bet you’re a screamer. Just like Zach.” That did it. Even with the excess morphine flowing through her system, Alanna Marshall wasn’t about to take that. She clawed at The Voice’s arms with all the strength she could manage. It made The Voice chuckle. It was almost cute, the way she was trying to fight for her life and all. “I can see how that might be fun. But we’ve gotta try and keep it down. After all, we don’t wanna wake the neighbors, do we?” Before Alanna could react, The Voice had whipped out the pillow from underneath her head and placed it on top of her. “Say goodnight, Alanna.” It took only a few short moments before Alanna Marshall once again fell unconscious. Once The Voice was sure that she was no longer fighting back, he placed the pillow back underneath her head. He then slowly slid off of the bed, making sure to make as little noise as possible. Getting rid of the evidence didn’t really matter. Possibly being interrupted…now, that was the tricky bit. He’d made arrangements to make sure that wouldn’t happen but you could never account for all eventualities. A quick glimpse outside the cubicle let The Voice know that he had nothing to worry about. The strung out patient from earlier was still causing problems. As a result, most everyone was clustered at the opposite end of the hallway, trying to calm him down. No one would notice him quietly leaving the ICU, making his way towards a bathroom located three halls over. Once inside the bathroom, he quickly slipped into the oversized handicapped stall and locked the door behind him. Shimmying loose one of the ceiling tiles, he reached inside the ceiling and pulled out a small shopping bag containing a change of clothes. Climbing back down to the floor with the bag in his hand, he made sure to place the bag in the corner. One quick change of clothes later saw The Voice clad in a nondescript pair of jeans, a rather generic look pair of sneakers and whatever the Hot Topic t-shirt du jour was. The exit was just a few hallways away; no one would look twice at a visitor making his way out of the hospital. The Voice then took a pack of matches from his pocket, striking one and throwing the lit match inside the shopping bag, now filled with his previous medical disguise. It wasn’t about destroying the evidence…but a little extra fun was always worth it. As the fire burned slowly, The Voice made his way calmly out of the bathroom and, a few moments later, the hospital. If she was lucky enough to make it back from the overdose he’d administered, Alanna wouldn’t be able to tell the police anything. Her current physical condition, combined with the amount of drugs in her system, would make anything she had to say extremely unreliable. That had been part of the reason he’d only ended up threatening to rape her, to confuse her and make her doubt her memories. Well, that and the fact that she wasn’t really his type. The Voice chuckled slightly to himself as he slid behind the wheel of the rented Ford sedan and made his way out of the parking lot. This was all turning out to be so easy, he thought. Even the ultimate end, the one that his employers had informed him had scared so many other potentials…he was even looking forward to that. Because everything he did, every single act that he undertook, was all geared towards the same goal. Lance Marshall wasn’t getting it now. It might take Lance a little while longer or a lot longer, The Voice didn’t really care. Because in the end, he’d know. Lance would know the truth. “No matter what, Lance..I win.”
