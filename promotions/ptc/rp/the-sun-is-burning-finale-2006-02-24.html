The Sun is Burning: Finale

24 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-02-24 Now the sun has come to earth Shrouded in a mushroom cloud of death Death comes in a blinding flash Of hellish heat and leaves a smear of ash And the sun has come to earth ~<>~<>~<>~ Their eyes were dancing as they danced with death. Hefting the loaded gun and pressing it into the soft tissue of Seymour Almasy’s neck, a bead of sweat trickled down Rich Rollins’ brow as he cocked the hammer with cold certainty. "I just want you to know," he all but whispered, "I’ve never killed a man." Seymour’s face was a blank void; it was the kind of place where sense and reason went to die. "You’ve already killed me," he said as he pushed his own pistol deeper into the folds of Rollins’ flesh. "Now finish the job." Rollins pulled the trigger. ~<>~<>~<>~ Six hours earlier… "Ma’am? Are you going to be all right?" The mortician placed a tender hand on the small of Cynthia O’Doyle’s back as she looked about two seconds from fainting. No, she was not going to be all right. As she looked down into half a face, the shattered remains of the man whom she had dedicated her life to, Cynthia O’Doyle began to weep. The listless eyes of Paul O’Doyle seemed to share her grief, if only in a grotesque parody of the mournful expression that had died with him. Her own husband, a suicide. "Can you identify the body, Mrs. O’Doyle?" What the mortician lacked in tact he made up for in sympathetic sincerity. "We don’t need a statement or anything, just a simple yes or no." She spun on him then, jerking away from his roving hand. "What the fuck do you think?! You found him in his office didn’t you?" She was on the verge of hysteria and the margin between sensibility and insanity was getting slimmer by the second. "Yes, that’s Paul. Okay? Can I please just leave?" "Of course," the mortician assured her, gesturing towards the tiny autopsy room’s exit. "I’m sorry for your loss, Mrs. O’Doyle." Technically Miss O’Doyle now, but it was a thought that only made Cynthia’s teeth clench tighter between dry sobs. Turning sharply she headed out the door, dabbing uselessly at her ruined makeup with the back of one hand. A flock of third-year medical students led by an ostentatious orderly parted like the Red Sea as she barreled through them with little regard for their studies. One look at the woman’s face was enough to give her the room to drive a Buick down that hallway; the students offered no resistance whatsoever. They knew the look of somebody who had just been given bad news, and they respected it. Had she done the right thing - the ethical thing - by telling her husband the truth? After seeing the self-inflicted hole in the side of his head, she wasn’t sure. She couldn’t deny the fact that waiting until Rollins was in his presence to make that call was intentional. In Cynthia’s mind it was the easiest way to round up all the loose ends, to force everybody to see the situation for what it was and come to some sort of an understanding. Maybe she wanted her husband to take a few swings at Rollins so that at least somebody was sticking up for her. When Rollins had more than insinuated that she was lying, pieces of her heart had broken off and died. It was her first child, and she did love Rich Rollins even if it was blindly obvious that he didn’t love her. All she wanted was for somebody, anybody, to tell her it was okay. Tell her that she wasn’t alone. She never expected Paul to react so violently, and she didn’t do this just to strike back at Rich. Maybe someday she would be able to look herself in the mirror and say that with a straight face. Ten minutes later she was walking through the automatic doors and out underneath the Mercy Hospital loading awning, collecting more than a few sympathetic stares from those who bustled back and forth. She wanted to scream at them all, curse them for how lucky they were to have loved ones who actually loved them and could still blink and talk and fuck. She didn’t have a partner of any kind, not anymore. Cynthia was about to hail a cab when a voice spoke out aimed directly at her: "Mrs. O’Doyle, over here!" She jerked in surprise, not recognizing the voice. Looking out and over two lanes of loading traffic, a man was sticking out of his window waving his arm like a lunatic, desperate to get her attention. He drove an older and unremarkable Saturn coupe the burnt shit brown color of a paper lunch sack, but his demeanor was friendly and inviting. Wiping a final wayward tear from her cheek, Cynthia O’Doyle walked across the lanes to meet the stranger. "Do I know you?" she asked. "No," he said with a heavy sigh, "but I’m a friend of Rich Rollins. He wanted me to swing by and take you to his place ’cause he figured you weren’t in the mental state to drive yourself, and it’d be a shame to make you pay cab fare. Is that okay with you?" Cynthia bit her lip, looking back at the line of taxis that were being emptied and restocked with passengers like a conveyor belt whisking away little yellow boxes to wherever. Turning back to her unexpected chauffer, she hesitated. "Rich sent you? Why wouldn’t he call me first?" The man shrugged. "His phone must be dead. Anyways, I’ve got to be back at work in like half an hour, so if you don’t mind…" he paused, looking into her reluctant, mascara-smeared face. "Or then again, I can just give you money for a cab if you’re not comfortable with that. Here," he said, reaching for his wallet. "No no, I’ll come," she quickly decided as she walked around to the passenger’s side. "I’m sorry, I just lost my husband. I really don’t know what the hell is going on anymore." Sliding into the vehicle and closing the door behind her, Cynthia closed her eyes as she set her purse in her lap. They sat there for a second as Cynthia tried to regain her bearings while the man gave her the time to do so. Finally she looked at him, offering a weak smile. "Okay. It was nice to meet you, but I didn’t catch your name…?" "Jason," Frank Jacobs lied, extending his hand with a grin. "We go way back, Rollins and me." ~<>~<>~<>~ The Love Box warehouse, south Chicago… "Oh Christ, that feels good," Rollins said to the ceiling as he sunk deeper into his newly-installed 94" J-480 Jacuzzi, the veritable luxury yacht of home spa equipment. He had ordered the machine as a gift to himself after defeating Angelo Deville, but the model had been on backorder for months. Fortunately for him, the manager at his local bath and spa dealer was a wrestling fan, so a few extra bills provided enough incentive to get his model installed a month ahead of time. And not a moment too soon, he mused as he let forty-eight aqua jets soothe the tension in his tightly-woven frame. The hot tub sat near a back wall in the main storage hanger of his warehouse, separated from the odd assortment of boxes and crates by no more than fifteen feet of open concrete. The filter pumps thrummed without competition in the massive space; when he closed his eyes, Rollins could almost convince himself that he was soaking on the balcony of a downtown penthouse fifty stories above the street as the vibrations echoed against the industrial steel canyons of the skyscrapers in his mind. He could do it too, if he wanted it. In the end Rollins preferred his privacy, but the fantasy was still a seductive one when you were chin deep in one hundred and four degrees of bubbly bliss. "She was my wife, damn you." The echo of Paul’s memory cut through his peace with the invasive persistence of a searing lump of charcoal in his trunks, forcing his eyes to flutter open in shock. He knew it was all in his head, but the biting words were still fresh in his memory. He could still hear the agonized inflection on the man’s voice, could still see the scandalized disbelief in his eyes. The good doctor had been a friend to him, and Rollins had stuck a hunting knife in his heart for every encounter he and Cynthia had enjoyed. The damnable truth was just that, though; they had enjoyed it, every breathless night and sticky morning the entire tumultuous affair had invited upon them. But their lust had come at a great price, and it was a price that Rollins would never be able to repay. "Her womb was not yours to violate, and you’ve taken that from me." What was supposed to be Paul’s first child was instead going to be Rich Rollins’ biggest mistake. "I want you out of my sight." It was then that Richard Andrew Rollins, reclusive millionaire, GCW Hall of Famer and PTC Global Champion, realized how ugly his selfishness had made him. "I hope I’m not interrupting happy hour," a very real and very unwelcome voice suddenly blurted, startling the Renegade to the point of swallowing a mouthful of hot chlorinated water. Gasping and coughing his way back to reality, the Renegade tried to rub the chemicals out of his eyes as he turned around to see who would dare invade his sanctuary. Coming around a stack of empty crates, Lt. Detective Noel Newton shot Rollins a sly wink as he sauntered closer to the tub. "I hope you’re decent enough to have company," he quipped as he came up beside the bubbling spa, running a hand along the smooth surface of the jet controls. "You’d have to be invited to be considered company, asshole. What makes you think I have anything to say to you?" Rollins turned back around and closed his eyes, already trying to tune out the unwelcome interruption. "Don’t you have some old lady purses to retrieve or something? I mean, that’s what you cops do when you’re not circle jerking each other around the office, right?" Newton feigned an interest in the spa controls so as not to take offense. "This is a neat toy you got here. Looks new. I almost bought one like it for myself last September, although-…" "Cut the bullshit, Newton." Rollins still refused to look at him. "What do you want?" He stopped fussing with the controls long enough to lean over Rollins’ relaxed form. "I want to tell you a story." "A story of…?" "A story of how I found Frank Jacobs," he replied with histrionic flair, "and how he kidnapped your little girlfriend." Not surprisingly Rich Rollins was suddenly all ears. "Wait, what?" The butt of the pistol connected with the top of Rollins’ skull before he had any idea it was coming. Releasing a surprised groan he sank below the surface of the water as Newton re-holstered his sidearm, swinging around the edge of the Jacuzzi with a purpose. Struggling for something to hold on to, the Renegade lashed out for the side of the tub, his head spinning and his eyes burning from the high levels of chlorine and mineral purifiers. Just as he breeched the surface of the water, Rich Rollins’ entire world descended into darkness as Detective Newton slid the industrial-grade foam and polyester cover over the top of the entire hot tub, trapping the Renegade inside his own conceited contraption. Newton moved fast, clicking all of the restraining straps into place before Rollins could shove his way out. The Renegade thrashed violently under the cover, pounding the vinyl undercarriage with his fists while the water splashed between the crack separating top from base. Just to be on the safe side, Newton hopped on top of the entire apparatus, sitting down in the very center to make sure that Rollins had no way of escape. "I hope I have your attention now, you arrogant prick," Newton spat. He slammed his fist on the cover a few times, mocking his captive’s struggle. "Unless you want to suffocate on the chemical fumes, I’d seriously suggest that you shut the fuck up and listen to what I have to say. You hear me in there?" Rollins could hear him, all right. It was a regular sweltering wonderland of bromine vapor and asphyxiating steam inside little more than five inches of air under that cover, but Rollins could hear him just fine. "You better pray I have a seizure before this lid comes off," he wheezed, trying to keep his nose and lips above the surface. "I want to tell you a story, Rollins." Newton’s voice took on a distant, almost surreal quality as Rollins’ ears submerged again and again. With the interior tub light turned off the Renegade had to resist the tingly grip of claustrophobic terror that suddenly threatened to seize his muscles. Still, he listened. "You see," the detective began, "I always wanted children. Unfortunately I never found that perfect woman, you know? I was engaged once, but I caught the bitch sleeping around with a friend of mine. Gee, I wonder where I’ve heard that one before?" What Newton didn’t know was that Rollins had also slept with Seymour Almasy’s ex-wife Laura not two months prior. The Renegade didn’t keep skeletons in his closet; he hid a whole fucking cemetery. "I can’t imagine why she’d cheat on you," Rollins gurgled sarcastically, his anger only slightly muffled. "You’re such a charming guy." Newton ignored him. "So instead, I found what happiness I could through my older sister’s first and only child, a little girl. I lived vicariously through them, you know? I took care of that kid whenever I could, because if I couldn’t be a daddy I wanted to be the best damn uncle the world had ever seen. And I was, damnit!" He slammed a fist on the cover again, trying to rein in his emotions, but it was hard. "I guess it wasn’t enough to save her from you, though." Having been clenching his eyes closed to keep them from stinging, Rollins’ lids instantly shot open. "I don’t know you, Newton! We never met before a few weeks ago! How the hell-…" "Her name was Jessica Jacobs, Rollins." His heart suddenly sank to the bottom of the tub. "You know, the girl you plowed into doing sixty on the freeway? The girl you put into a coma? The girl who fucking died because you got blitzed, then decided to go for a little joy ride?" Newton’s voice was starting to shake now, and this revelation had frozen Rollins to his marrow despite the tub’s temperature. "She was my niece, Rich." Clenching his teeth, Rollins fumed at his own ignorance. How could he not have known? It explained why Newton had requested his mother’s corpse case when by all rights it wasn’t anything any self-respecting Chicago detective should have given two shits about. It explained why Seymour Almasy’s address had been swapped with Frank Jacob’s, because Rollins knew there had to be a rat. Only somebody with the proper access could have modified that much information, and it looks like the rat just came out of his hole. But more importantly, it meant one thing. "Rollins, I’d like you to meet somebody." Footsteps. "He’s been wanting to have a chat with you for some time now." They were coming closer. "I’m proud to finally introduce to you my brother-in-law…" And they stopped. "… Frank Jacobs." Silence. Rollins waded quietly, waiting for the man who he knew was standing outside his spa to say something. Finally: "Let him out, Noel." Frank’s voice was soft and tense, almost as if he were just as scared of what lay inside the tub as Rollins was of what lay on the outside. "I want to see his face." The suffocating fumes had started to get to the Renegade. His breath had been shallow by necessity, and after just a few minutes of wallowing in his own makeshift boiler his arms and legs felt like globs of jell-o held together with popsickle sticks. When the exterior straps had all been unsnapped and the imprisoning cover was ripped away, the sudden influx of light nearly rendered Rollins blind. The Renegade slowly rose from his tub, looking more like a wet cat than a two hundred-fifty pound professional wrestler. Detective Newton stood a good five paces back, his pistol trained expertly on his heart, but he was merely a blur in Rollins’ peripheral vision as he locked irises with the large bearded man standing directly in front of him. Frank Jacobs stood with his arms draped loosely at his sides, his frame packing less meat and more fat than Rollins had remembered. The only other time he had seen Frank face to face was at an autograph signing prior to his round three match with Pete the PG Tips Chimp, right before Frank had pulled out a gun in an attempt to smear the Renegade’s brain matter across the concrete. The hazy adrenaline-fueled memory had since been distorted in Rollins’ mind, turning this ordinary man that now stood in front of him into a monster. But Rollins didn’t kid himself. The man who stood in front of him was still a monster with or without an impressive physique. Frank stood for a long while just staring at Rollins, his jaw clenching and unclenching with the anticipatory release of this one moment. Playing his part, Rollins simply stood his ground, dressed in nothing but an old pair of red swimming trunks, feeding the man’s fantasy by being Frank’s own monster caught in his own trap. Newton stood all the while, his finger held ready over the trigger. "I want you to apologize," Frank finally said. Waves of complicated emotions rolled between his eyes. "I want you to tell me you’re sorry you murdered my daughter." Rollins shook his head. "Fuck you. I didn’t murder anybody. It was an accident." Frank took two steps closer, putting his head within striking distance. If Rollins really thought he could have pulled it off without getting shot he would have jumped the psychopath right then and there, but Frank beat him to the draw. Rearing back with all his might, Frank clocked Rollins square across the jaw, sending the Renegade backwards into the tub. Quickly regaining his posture the Renegade took his position once again, glaring defiantly at the man who haunted his dreams. "I want you to apologize for taking her from me," Frank reiterated, trying unsuccessfully to keep his emotions in check. "Apologize!" It was then that Rollins heard the click of the detective’s gun as Newton said: "I’d word my response more carefully this time, champ." Looking the devil in the eye, the Renegade slowly nodded. "I’m sorry that I killed her," Rollins seethed, "instead of you." Frank’s eyes quivered as he came close enough for Rollins to smell the whiskey on his breath. "Boy, you’re going to regret that." ~<>~<>~<>~ The Paddy Wagon, Rollins thought without a single shred of humor, how fucking cute. The name on the side of the van was quickly forgotten however as the good detective threw open the rear doors. When he saw a tearful Cynthia O’Doyle, her face bruised and her body bound and gagged, Rich Rollins tried to knock Newton’s head off despite the handcuffs binding his own wrists. Newton simply backhanded him, knocking Rollins unceremoniously to the pavement without the use of his hands to break his fall. "Don’t be such a renegade, Renegade." Newton spat on his back. "The nineties are over." Rollins hissed around his own gag as both Frank and the detective hoisted him head-first into the van, bowling over Cynthia in the process. The first thing Rollins noticed was the stink, and he knew exactly where it came from. The Renegade almost choked on his own vomit behind the gag as he looked down upon the half-covered face of his dead mother’s rotting corpse, her eyes long since sunken into the back of her bony skull. "Your mother wanted me to say hello for you," said Frank as he grabbed the rear doors, "but I figured she’d make a bigger impression in person." And with that the doors slammed shut. ~<>~<>~<> Resthaven Cemetery… Under the black veil of the midnight hour, the black van and its black-hearted occupants crunched along the gravel access road and into the heart of the cemetery. Rollins and Cynthia sat cuddled together in the cargo hold, handcuffed at the wrists and trying desperately to keep as far away from the bouncing corpse of Rollins’ mother as possible. Cynthia’s pupils were the size of saucers as she hyperventilated through the oil-stained gag between her teeth, communicating without the use of words. The message was simple, and it made Rollins’ eyes well up. I forgive you. With his head still swimming from the chlorine vapors he had inhaled, Rollins felt the onset of a nasty migraine kicking in. The lump on the back of his skull where Newton had knocked him with his own brass knuckles only intensified the dull throb, but it was little more than an annoying footnote at this point. There were far more important matters at hand. From the front seat came Newton’s taunting tone, a snide mimicry of anything human. "You lovebirds are going to love the plot we found for you," he snickered. "Isn’t that right Frank?" Frank didn’t answer. His lips moved in what Newton figured was a soundless prayer, a promise to his daughter that he had made the moment her life had ended. Instead of verbally torturing their captives, Frank concentrated instead on the path ahead while Newton turned and watched the headstones roll by outside his window. Two minutes later the vehicle came to a stop, but their location was no mystery to Rollins as Newton came around and swung open the rear doors. Surrounded in yellow police ticker-tape, the empty hole where Margaret Rollins used to rest was waiting for its new residents. "All right champ, out you go," Newton declared as he snagged the Renegade by his hair and underarm, hauling him face first onto the ground beside his mother’s grave. Cynthia began shrieking as Rollins was taken away from her, but Newton backhanded her across the mouth to shut her up. "You ready for this, Frank?" Slamming the driver’s side door, Frank came around the side of the van with a can of kerosene in his hand. "You have no idea." At the sight of Frank’s flammable cargo Rollins began to squirm, cursing through his gag. Amused, Newton drew his firearm as he knelt down beside him. "What’s the matter, Rollins? You don’t like feeling helpless, do you? Helpless like my niece Jessica when you put her in a hospital with a plastic tube down her throat to keep her from swallowing her own tongue?" Newton hit Rollins in the side of his face with the pistol, knocking the Renegade on his side. Newton’s voice began to rise as his anger slowly consumed him. "Not such a walk in the fucking park, is it? You’re a piece of shit, Renegade. All your lawyers and attorneys and cocksucking Steven Calderas can’t do a fucking thing for you now, Rich. Oh, did you want to say something?" Reaching out for Rollins’ gag, Newton untied the knot. Spitting the hateful cloth to the ground, the Renegade lashed out with his legs as he snarled in Newton’s face, but the detective simply laughed at his pitiful attempts for revenge. Rollins’ eyes were wild with madness. "Let her go, Newton! Let the woman go and just kill me, okay? Fucking let her go!" "You hear that?" Newton offered over his shoulder to Frank, who was climbing into the back of the van. "Our bad ass here just grew a heart of gold. He wants to negotiate; how touching." "You bastard!" Rollins roared. That’s when he noticed what Frank was doing in the back of the van. He was dousing everything with kerosene, Cynthia and his mother’s corpse included. "No! No! No! No!" They weren’t even words anymore. It was simply the only strangled sound Rollins was capable of producing. "Stop moaning like a woman," Newton hissed as he pressed the muzzle into Rollins’ temple, wrapping a forearm around his neck to keep him still. "It’s just not your style." Frank emptied the entire five gallon container throughout the cargo hold of the van, standing on the small of Cynthia’s back to keep her in place. She wriggled and squirmed and screamed as the liquid soaked into her hair, onto her clothes and into her skin, but Frank wasn’t about to let her go. When he finished distributing his payload Frank tossed the container aside, turning back to face Rollins who was held securely by Newton. When Frank pulled out a matchbook and struck one of the sticks, Rollins’ pupils shrank with horror. "You want to apologize now, or are we still too busy trying to be a bad motherfucker?" Frank used the single match to light the entire pack, hovering it over the lip of the van’s kerosene-soaked hold. "I’m sorry! I’m sorry!" Rollins sobbed, throwing any iota of machismo bullshit into the wind. "If I could bring her back I would, but I can’t, Frank! Please, I’m so sorry…" With a sad frown, Frank shook his head. "So am I," he said. He dropped the matchbook anyway. The unimaginable trauma of watching as the woman who carried his unborn child roast alive alongside his mother’s corpse was something that snapped an irreparable synapse in Rich Rollins’ brain. Frank immediately kicked the rear doors closed as the fire consumed everything in the blink of an eye, shutting out the horrific image of Cynthia as the flames began to lick her flesh. Seconds after the doors clicked shut, the side windows exploded outward, emitting the frantic cries of a woman caught in an overwhelming inferno as the van became a makeshift furnace. The acrid funk of bubbling flesh filled the air, replacing the musty smell of hallowed ground with something more on the level of a sweltering meat factory in the middle of July. Rollins was incoherent. His screams rivaled Cynthia’s as his heart died along with her. Newton just laughed as the funeral pyre continued to burn. "TURN AROUND, ASSHOLE!" Before Rollins was aware it was happening, Frank Jacobs fell to the ground as a gunshot split the sky. Jacobs cried out in pain, clutching a knee that had been shattered by a mystery bullet. Rollins hardly noticed. All he could see was the sizzling van as its newly-painted exterior cracked and peeled until the "Paddy Wagon" became an unreadable mess. Cynthia had stopped screaming, but Rollins didn’t know if it was by mercy or misfortune. The new voice spoke again: "Let him out of the handcuffs, or so help me God…" Newton eased up his hold on the Renegade’s neck; moments later another gunshot almost deafened the hysterical Global Champion. Slumping to the ground, Rich Rollins twisted over in shock as he felt somebody grab his wrists and unlock his cuffs. He saw Newton laying beside his mother’s grave, his knee likewise blown apart, screaming at the bloody mess that used to be his right hand. Then he looked up. It was a hallucination. It had to be. There was no possible way that Seymour Almasy stood in front of Rollins, saving his life with a gun in one hand and clenched vengeance in the other. They stood but three paces apart, staring at one another as the blaze that had once been a beautiful woman continued to roar, silhouetting their shapes against the unfeeling stone of the surrounding graves. Almasy was shaking; his gun hand rattled uncertainly as he thrust it at Rollins, seething with unbridled scorn. So he finally found out, Rollins thought bitterly. The Renegade spread his arms. "You picked a real bad time to talk about this, Seymour." His face was matted down by chlorine-soaked hair and bitter tears, but Rollins still managed to look invincible as he gazed back through the sight of Almasy’s weapon. "That van," he choked, "that woman, she was carrying my baby." Under most other circumstances, Seymour would have been apologetic. Sympathetic. But not today. The gun remained trained between the eyes of the Renegade as Seymour practically seethed with a combination of hatred and sorrow. "Not so nice," he whispered, bitterly, "when people fuck with what shouldn’t be fucked with, is it?" He tried to remain strong, but Rollins’ equilibrium finally crumbled. "You’ve been the hurdle that I could never jump for years, Seymour! I wanted to be you when I came to the UWF. I wanted to be the celebrity that you were, reach the top of the mountain and hold my ground like you did! I only went after the Global title because you held it, for fuck’s sake!" While it was touching, Seymour didn’t waiver. "That has nothing to do with you fucking my wife, Rollins." And there it was. The Renegade had hoped that Almasy would have found out much sooner, but running over the obvious with a highlighter was not in his job description. The Renegade’s eyes hardened. "I knew we’d cross paths sooner or later in this tournament, Almasy. I knew it. What better way to get inside your head than to get inside your ex-wife?" Seymour snorted, a completely out of place expression for the man considered by most to be a champion of good, of the sort of pure motivations and feelings that wrestling so often lacked. "So this is about the tournament, Rich? This is about a fucking wrestling match? Fuck, if you would have told me you were going to sleep with Laura, I would have dropped out of the goddamned thing! Do you have any idea what’s happened to me since this piece of shit tournament started, Rollins? ANY FUCKING IDEA?!" The Final Fantasy glared at the Renegade, but really, neither man was his nickname. This was Seymour Almasy and Rich Rollins, in the middle of a fucking cemetery, with a two now-corpses burning in a van and two men shot in the kneecaps looking on, wondering just what the fuck had just happened. Rollins’ breath sounded like a hissing snake as it slowly escaped through his teeth. He stabbed a finger at the burning wreckage, tears welling up in his eyes. "Yeah Jason, I think I have a pretty good fucking idea." "Don’t call me that," Seymour warned. "Or what, you’ll fucking shoot me?!" Rollins’ voice rose to near hysterical heights. "With the gun I gave her, no less!" *THWACK!* That was the sound of Seymour Almasy, hero to thousands of children everywhere, PISTOL-WHIPPING Richard Rollins. The force behind the blow wasn’t anywhere near what he’d like to channel, but the action itself felt better than anything he’d done in ages. "YOU. FUCKING. ASSHOLE!" The gun remained leveled at Rollins’ head, but he moved it over, lining up with one of Rich’s eyes. "You see the blood on the barrel of this gun, you fucking cocksucker? That’s Laura’s. THAT’S MY EX-WIFE’S BLOOD FROM WHEN SHE SHOT HERSELF IN THE CHEST! You know something, you fuck? I had this gun in my mouth for thirty minutes today, trying to work up the courage to blow my own fucking head off. But now? Now I’m fucking gleeful I didn’t. Because you? You are the lowest fucking form of life I have ever met. You may be THE ONLY PERSON walking this Earth who DESERVES to have his pregnant lover burned alive!" Still moaning on the ground, Frank Jacobs shot a sour grimace at Almasy. "He deserves a lot worse than that for killing my-…" "SHUT UP," Seymour screamed at him. The sickeningly slick lubrication of blood met Rollins’ fingertips as he caressed the side of his head, having now been knocked around all evening by just about everybody in Chicago. Struggling to his knees, the Renegade spat a crimson mouthful at Almasy’s feet. "I didn’t intend for her to kill herself, Seymour," he said as he looked into the Final Fantasy’s eyes. "I told her that you cared more about your career than you ever did about her. I told her to use that gun on you." Seymour’s eyes went kaleidoscopic, flashing from sorrow to anger to hatred and back again. His shoulders slumped forward in utter defeat, the gun lowering for the first time since he’d come face to face with the man who wanted to outdo him so, the man who wanted to replace him atop Primetime Central. "Richard, I wish she had. Because if she had, we wouldn’t be here having this conversation. Shit, everything’s gone now. For both of us. If what you told me is true, your girlfriend really just got set on fire. My ex-wife shot herself to death. You’ve got two fucking nut jobs after you for God only knows what reason, though if it’s anything like what you did to me, I’m sure it’s not pleasant."
