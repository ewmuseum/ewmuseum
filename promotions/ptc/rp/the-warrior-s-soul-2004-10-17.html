The Warrior's Soul

17 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-17 The draw was irresistible. The thirst for blood unquenchable. The urges for mayhem and destruction not to be denied. The euphoric rush of adrenaline that signaled the coming combat swept through my body, sweet ambrosia to the practiced taste buds of a seasoned warrior. This is my life, my true calling in this world, the battle magnifique. The sirens of sacrifice were singing their sultry sermons from on top their mountain peak, safe and sound from the grand melee that fast approached below. Their voices touching upon cords deep within my very being, pulling upon my emotions like a harper would strum upon his cords with graceful ease. I hated them and loved them all at once, I wanted only to please their every desire, and at the same to deny them their sport and stand defiant. All across the arena floor, in every direction my eyes could sweep they stood prepared, warriors of all sizes, shapes, and skills. An entire nation of gladiators had gathered here, lulled into service by the same song, a song that played upon their emotions as much as mine. Softly whispering promises of grandeur into every listening ear, inside their heads, planting visions of triumph to stir the soul. The sands of the floor ground beneath by booted heel, the reality, tension hanging morbidly between us all, thicker than a knife could cut. This tension required something far greater than a knife to penetrate; this called for a sword. A sword or an axe, or a whip, or a pike, or in some cases nothing but raw fists and feet. The weapon isn’t what mattered now, for a weapon is only a tool, the tools of our trade. The trade of combat, blood sport… Thousands of spectators packed themselves in neat little rows all around the amphitheater, cheering for their heroes and booing their villains, all waiting for the catastrophic dance that would end half the lives on the field. The field of battle that was not a war, this was not for land or money, this was for pride. The pride of victory. Hundreds of combatants stood upon the same sand I ground beneath by heel, breathed the same electrified air I breathed, looked around at the same sights that I saw. This was an event unlike any other, the greatest of warriors had gathered to defend their reputations. And the most talented of hopefuls had come to steal it away. For me to describe the sights I saw would never do the event justice, only those that stood and watched from the stands above, or readied their weapons from the ground below can understand. This was the first night of the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament, the first glorious night of many that would lead to determining the greatest warrior in the realms today. And as much as I enjoy saying that I intend that to be me, so do the hundreds of others that have come. They all want what I want, and the only way to get it is to fight. The siren song changed. A cheer went up through the spectators. The time for preparation was quickly drawing to a close; the powers on high grew tired of waiting. Sweeping my gaze across those around me I spied the man whom I was destined to destroy or die in trying. I knew little more than his name, uttered quietly upon careful tongues. Lori Equinox. As much as people knew about him there was much more they didn’t, rumor and speculation had built him into a monster, macabre in his decadent displays of morbidity. From my perspective he was only a man with a sword, and not a very big sword at that. There was no room for rumor and speculation in the mind of a gladiator; there was only the cold testing of steel. And it was his steel I would test this night. As my gaze swept further, I wondered how many others I would test before my time was done. I recognized some, but not many. Seymour Almasy, Angello Deville, Emperor Riley, Karina Wolfenden, and Alex Grey to name a few. They all held the same apprehensious looks as I did right then. They all waited for the signal to come, for the damnable sirens to decide it was time. “I hate this.” A nearby voice grumbled into my ear, hushed so not to draw too much attention. “Savor these moments, for they may never come again.” I responded with as much poetic brevity as I could muster. Problem was I hated this just as much, but my years in the arena had tempered my tongue far beyond the need to express such emotions. “Who’s your first round draw?” The same voice asked. The voice was husky and deep, suiting the monster of a man who it belonged to. I recognized it before ever turning my head to pass regard. Lestatt Knight was a creature I’ve come to know better than some family members. Some people say you can only truly know somebody after you’ve fought them. Well I’ve fought Lestatt, more than once, and the things I knew about him I didn’t want to. “Equinox.” I replied curtly, turning my attention away from the man who at any other point in time would be attempting to choke the life from my body. “You?” “El.” He said just as briskly. We didn’t want to talk to each other, we never did, but in a see of anonymous faces you find that one you recognize and cling to it. Funny how human nature works like that. “Which one’s Equinox?” He asked. Armor clinked, a familiar clink, and an obsidian blade buried its tip in the sand, a familiar blade. Lestatt cloaked himself in darkness, claimed it gave him power. From his sword to his breastplate to his hair, all of it was black, glimmering in the high sun. Raising a hand I gestured in the general direction of my opponent. “The one that thinks his mean stare is going to intimidate me.” I spoke. “The altar boy with red hair?” He queried. I only nodded. What a pair we must have struck just then. Knight in his black glamour and me in my silver battle garb, prepared for a fight and for once we were not the ones fighting. I chose to arm myself lightly, custom job on a half plate suit turned it into little more than a breastplate and leg guards, even my bracers had been cropped to give my hands a wider arc of movement. “How do you like your chances.” Lestatt pressed the conversation. It was now that I took the opportunity to end this false pretense, by turning to regard the black adorned figure of mayhem I sneered as only Johnny Lexicon can. “I’ll deal with Equinox, you just worry about you and yours. If we meet later on in this thing, I will kill you.” I put as much critical emphasis on every word as is humanly possible. And he grinned. Oh how that grin infuriated me, but he did not reply. Instead he gathered his obsidian helm and settled it onto his head. “The tune changes.” He said simply. And indeed the sirens had begun a new song; their voices blared and invaded my ears, each syllable burying itself deep into my mind until the only thing I could focus on was the blood pounding through my veins. All other emotions ceased to exist, my family was gone, my very life was but a dream, the only thing true was Lori Equinox, the only thing real was the sword in my hand. As hard as I tried to fight that hypnotizing serenade, I gave myself to it just as much. Lori was coming for me and I for him, none of the other fighters were there, they weren’t people anymore, they were only obstacles that kept me from him. They were burdens that weighted me from flying, and I hated them for it. I loathed them from the very pit of my stomach for no other reason than that they were in my way; they hindered my appeasing of the sirens. I yelled something then, words formed by my own tongue but not deciphered by my brain. For my brain had one single all consuming directive, destroy Lori Equinox, kill him, mutilate him, and advance. And the distance closed, I saw the flash of silver overhead and reacted without thought, parried his blade without effort and attacked without premeditation. I didn’t know how I was fighting just then, but I just was. It was as if an all-consuming blood lust had captured my skill with the sword and wielded it for me. I understood the movements as I made them, remembered them from the training halls and from previous competitions, but I did not perform them. I mean I did perform them, it was my body, but it wasn’t. It was theirs. The sirens. It was the fan’s body, it was what they wanted me to do, they wanted me to dodge left when I had the chance and I did. They wanted me to strike hard when Equinox was open and I did. In those glorious, bloody moments I wasn’t Johnny Lexicon. I was the embodiment of battle, the warrior. I watched the fight without acting, I stood back and watched a silver clad Johnny Lexicon parry an onslaught of precise attacks, I watched him cry out for more, begging Equinox to give it harder, faster, stronger. And I was mortified. Horrified by what I had become but unable to stop it, or perhaps I was unwilling. And slowly I start to lose even this, my inner voice, my monologue was fading, becoming one with the siren’s sweet call. I have always preached about calm in the face of adversity, never one to panic or lose my head. But I was losing it now; I was so losing it. But before I lost it entirely it was over, I no longer watched my body perform of it’s own accord, I felt the hot drench of Equinox’s blood wash over my trembling hands and spatter against my cheeks, I could taste the bittersweet victory when licking my smiling lips. I was in control once again, but at the same time I was still consumed. Consumed now not by the sirens, by the bloodlust that had urged me into performing before thousands of fans. Consumed now by my own hatred of what I’d done, angry at myself for nearly losing this inner voice and allowing the battle to take over my identity. And I took out my anger on that who had caused my bloodlust to rage, the one who had made it so easy for the siren’s to persuade my blade. I struck at a fallen Equinox again, and again, I raged against my own slowness in hitting him a third and fourth time. His blood was no longer blood in those furious moments of unrelented anger, his blood was my spoils, and to the victor it went. And when it was over, when I stood hunched over a body twisted in dead agony, his vitae creating rivers of anguish down my armor I was again myself. I looked around to view similar carnage on all sides; there was no wasted time and hardly any drawn out battles. The fights had been fast and they had been furious, and now the victors stood in mostly the same bewildered state I find myself in. Dazed and confused, possibly sickened by their own aggression and ferocity. The siren’s song slowly subsided, taking with it their mesmeric hold on the combatants that remained breathing. Their play upon our primal emotions ceased and we could finally feel the full force of what we had accomplished that day. I longed for it, just for a split second, I felt used and abandoned, like somebody had taken a kidney without so much as a thanks. I wanted it back, I wanted it all, the brutal levity of watching my actions without taking part, the horrified state of transcendence, but most of all I wanted the triumphant swell in my chest. Because my chest was not swollen then, no my chest had sunk along with my stomach. I want to be rid of this foul arena now and to never look back. But I know that I cannot, because tomorrow the call would come again. The sirens would find me no matter where I hid, they would stroke my warrior’s ego lovingly and beg me to return for another fight, and another after that. I could no more resist that calling than I could resist the urge to eat or sleep. It was a part of my survival; it was not a desire but a necessity of my existence. And I knew without even questioning myself that I would be back to play this scene once again, to give the fans what they wanted, to give the sirens what they wanted, to give my thirst for blood another dousing. And even as I rose to stand up to my full height, to drop my shoulders back and hold my head high in false triumph. I wanted to do this again. I hated it, I hated myself for wanting it, and I hated the people for wanting me to do it. But I loved it just the same. Tis a bittersweet relationship held between the war… and the warrior.
