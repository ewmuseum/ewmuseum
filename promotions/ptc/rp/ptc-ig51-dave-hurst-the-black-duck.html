The Black Duck - iG51 Roleplay When a fighter’s life could not advance, He happened upon a friend by chance, A wildfowl partner, a friend for all time, From then the career took its climb; But all the while, the friend omits- To tell the fighter important bits- From life’s past - And wherest he came. He daren’t not tell, or bear the shame. For there is another in his life, Another that made him struggle and strife. One he hoped he’d never see – A curse upon his family’s tree. For when the friend’s birth was due, Not one animal was born, but two, The abomination, and thing of bad luck, The birth of the one… Black… Duck. -----=====I=====----- The Black Duck “It’s okay. It’s okay… calm down little mate. Dave’s here. It’s all okay.” Another nightmare, that’s the fourth night in a row now, something seems to be really bothering him. I wonder what it could be. He usually sleeps really easily, even when he floats on milk before bed. Ducky looks up from the bed, his eyes wide open with a shocked expression on his plastic face. I wish I could do whatever it is I need to do to help him sleep. I’ve tried having him float on camomile tea, I’ve dissolved a sleeping pill in his bowl, but nothing seems to help the little mate sleep. “There there, Ducky…” I stroke his head softly, gliding my finger over the plastic. Seemingly nothing I do will calm him. It’s like there’s something in the air that’s bothering him; as if there is an inevitable event that he has no control over. He knows something is going to happen soon. He’s very good for sensing these kinds of situations. I just wish I knew what it was. I pick my plastic companion out of the bed and take him to the couch. As he settles onto the arm, I switch the television set on and put on the Discovery Channel. He loves watching those wildlife documentaries. Well I might as well make a cuppa anyway. I get up and walk over to the kitchen, flick the switch for the electric kettle and head back into the living room. That’s weird, where did Ducky go? I notice the bed sheets rustle in the bedroom at the end of the hallway. He must have gone back to bed. Heading into the bedroom I notice that the sheets are shivering. Ducky is wide awake, the same shocked expression on his face. Something must have spooked him. “Come on little mate,” I say, pulling him back into the living room. “It was nothing, probably just the trees outside.” As I approach the television set, he won’t look at the TV screen. He’s petrified of it. “What is it, mate?” I ask, but all he can do is bury his head in my palm. I walk him back into the bedroom, place him into his bed and pull the sheets gently over him. And the cycle continues. He’ll be up again in about an hour I’m sure. This is the worst thing that could happen with my Infinity Gauntlet championship coming up: a lack of sleep. How I’m supposed to train properly for this match when I’ve barely slept in four days I don’t know. I’m hardly going to enter this thing in the best of condition. Hopefully this phase will end soon and Ducky will go back to sleeping easily again. I walk back through the living room to grab my tea. I go to switch the television set off. Whoa! The lights just went out. I bet we just blew a fuse. It’s pitch-black in here - I should have eaten my carrots. Where’s the flaming torch? There’s a flash of lightning outside, followed by a loud crack of thunder. Great – a storm will really help Ducky get some sleep… At least the lightning might help me find my way to the counter to grab the torch. Another lightning flash and this time I leap across the room to the countertops. *CLANG* “Agh!” I yell, but the quietest yell I could muster. I whisper-yell if any such thing could exist. Of course I had to stub my toe on the flaming trash can. I knew it was a stupid idea to put that flaming thing in the entranceway, but there was nowhere else it seemed to want to go. I hope my toe isn’t too badly injured. That’s the worst thing that could happen with my Infinity Gauntlet championship coming up: a broken toe. How am I supposed to compete when I can barely stand? Let’s hope it’s just bruised, eh? Lightning flashes again followed by the loud bang of thunder. That one must’ve been close by. I hobble over to the counter and grab the torch from the drawer, head to the basement door and…who’s that at the door? Someone is wrapping on the front door to the house. They seem to be in a bit of a panic, I hope they’re okay. I limp to the front door and open it. Nothing… Just a gust of wind followed by a bunch of leaves swirling behind it. That’s odd, I’m sure I heard something. I close the door and walk back over to the basement door. There’s another knock at the door. What is going on? I bet it’s some kid playing tricks on us. It is close to Halloween after all. I swing the door open violently. “Oi! You little sh-” Oh… Looking down, a small black plastic duck sits on the doorstep. I guess I didn’t notice it before in the dark. I bend down to pick the duck up and suddenly freeze mid-motion. There’s something not right about this duck. I can feel some bad energy around me, like a small, sharp electric shock pulsing through my veins. A force seems to be telling me to get away from the duck, as if an invisible spring was pulling me back from the thing. I step back and slam the door shut behind me. That was weird. Hopefully whoever put it there will take it back. How bizarre. I go to open the basement door again. Oh! The lights have gone back on, and the television set started up again. It must have been the lightning. I sit down on the couch to gather my bearings. I wonder if that duck is gone now. It is a bit strange with Ducky getting agitated so much lately. Maybe this duck has something to do with what’s been going on? Nah, it’s probably just some prank from one of the kids in the neighbourhood. I switch the television set off and the lights and shuffle back to Ducky’s bedroom. Aww, bless him, he’s fallen asleep. He looks so peaceful when he sleeps, lying on his side, a content grin across his beak. Hopefully the nightmares have stopped now. -----=====I=====----- “Come on Ducky, time to wake up.” I say to the lazy oaf. It’s 9am, I should be heading off to the gym for some weight training now. I slide the covers back and Ducky lies there, still sound asleep. “Come on little mate,” I lightly tap the plastic duck on the side – Nothing. “Ducky…?” I hold the duck and move him around, but still no motion from the duck, not even a stir. Something is seriously wrong. What should I do? I have to resuscitate him! I grab Ducky, turn him upside-down, and breathe into his squeaker hole. I turn him back around, put one hand over the other and press my palms into his back to squeeze the air out. “One… two… three… four… five… six!” Is it supposed to be six? I don’t know! Like it matters when reviving a plastic duck anyway! I flip the duck back over and breathe into his squeaker hole again, then flip him back for another palm press. “One… two… three… four… five… six… seven… eight! Come on Ducky!” Eight didn’t work! I flip Ducky back over again and breathe once more into the squeaker, then turn him back. “One… two… three… four!” He’s up! Ducky is alive! He tilts forward a little bit confused as to what’s going on. He nods to me as if to say that he doesn’t know what happened, but he’s thankful of whatever I did. “That’s okay Ducky,” I tell him, “thank God that you’re okay.” Wow, what a relief. Something is wrong with Ducky, but how am I supposed to find out? -----=====I=====----- This is the worst that could happen with my Infinity Gauntlet championship coming up: sitting outside the veterinarian’s office waiting for a diagnosis on a plastic duck. I hope that Ducky is okay, though. The door beside me opens up and the vet peers around it, he doesn’t have Ducky with him for some reason. “Mr. Hurst,” he says, “I have something important to tell you.” This sounds ominous. The vet sits down on the seat next to me with a very serious look upon his face. He looks right at me with a concerned face, almost dejected. “Well, what is it, doc?” I ask the vet. “Mister Hurst, there isn’t an easy way to say this, so I’m just going to go ahead and say it. I’m afraid that Ducky has developed a tumour.” “What…?” Is this some kind of sick joke? “He has beak cancer, Mister Hurst.” The what, now? This is a wind up, surely. How can it be possible for a plastic duck to develop a tumour? Surely you have to actually be living to be able to develop these things. “But… he’s just a plastic duck.” I inform the veterinarian, as if he didn’t realise that the thing wasn’t breathing or anything. “You must be finding this hard to take in right now, and that’s understandable.” “But, he’s a bath toy. He’s not a real duck. How is this possible?” “I’m afraid this is quite common among ducks, particularly at the age that he is now.” “I don’t think you understand. He’s not a real duck!” I yell. This guy is making me so angry. How could you say such a thing? He can’t possibly have this illness, so why all the drama? “Do you doubt my judgment as an experienced veterinarian Mr Hurst?” Oh and now he’s pissy. “No... I’m just say-“ “I’ve been doing this job for thirty-five years, Mr Hurst!” the guy spits, “I have a PhD in Animal Sciences, and certainly know the difference between a live and a plastic duck! I am telling you, Mr Hurst, that this is a sick duck!” “You don’t need to yell,” I tell the guy. He takes a big intake of air, probably trying to keep his composure, but I’ve had enough. I’m going to take my duck and go home. “Come on Ducky,” I call into the room as I approach my plastic companion. He doesn’t respond. “Come on... Ducky...” I ask him again - still nothing. Oh no, not again. “His vital signs are showing no heart beat!” yells the doctor as he rushes over to the defibrillator. He clasps the two pads and a dangerous humming sound emanates from them. He hovers the two pads close to each other to prepare, and then pushes the pads against the sides of the duck. “Clear!” he yells, as if someone is supposed to hear him, before pressing the pads into the duck and sending an electric shock through the toy. I don’t know what this guy thinks he’s doing, but I hope it works. “Clear!” he yells once more before pressing the pads into Ducky again. “No, no, no, no, no! Come on kid! Don’t give up on me now!” He presses the pads into the animal once more and sends another shock through him. Ducky is alive! He gets up from the bed suddenly, black pad-shaped burn marks across his sides. He still doesn’t have vital signs (did he ever?) but he’s with us again. But almost as soon as Ducky gets up, the lights in the room start flickering. The defibrillator machine starts to murmur as if it’s struggling under the weight of energy. The lights suddenly turn out and the room is left in darkness. I grab Ducky quickly, and clasp him in my arms. He still feels warm from the electrocutions. That... feeling... comes over me again. The force is stopping me from moving towards the door. It’s that Black Duck again! Ducky wriggles in my arm, attempting to escape, but I won’t let him. He struggles even more and eventually breaks free of my arms. “No Ducky! No!” I call out to him. He rolls across the floor to the door. A light emerges from the hallway illuminating the evil Black Duck sitting in the doorway. The lights in the room start to pulse on and off. The pulsations and light intensities become more and more extreme, as if there is an ongoing battle through the national grid. I’m startled as next to me sparks fly from some electrical equipment. A crack like thunder sounds from the life support machine. The heart monitor lights up duck-shaped patterns from the graph. The murmuring from the defibrillator becomes louder and louder, building up to a crescendo of noise and light. The ducks stand there in the hallway; their eyes locked together in the most intense staring contest ever. As chaos erupts around them they sit opposite one another, completely still. And then, as if the storm has passed, the sound dies down. The lights flicker slower and slower, the sparks stop flying, the lights to the room come back on. Ducky and the Black Duck still sit opposite one another, staring into each other’s eyes. Finally, the Black Duck relents. He rolls across the hallway and out of sight. The sounds subside. The heart monitor returns to normal operations. Everything is calm and still. -----=====I=====----- I don’t think I’ll even know the extent of what happened in the veterinarian’s office that day. When I asked Ducky what happened that night he simply said that he told his brother to go home. I never knew that Ducky had an evil twin. I guess it’s not something that comes up in general conversation. “Oh, speaking of evil twins...” No. It doesn’t really happen. I’m not mad at Ducky for not telling me these things. Sometimes when you worry so much about what your friend will think of you, you’re too scared to tell them anything. I am thankful though that Ducky managed to scare the evil duck away. We came back to the veterinarian’s office a few days later for Ducky’s regular check-up and it turned out that Ducky had made a miraculous recovery. Funny that! I expected nothing less of the luckiest duck in the world. The Black Duck never returned. The other day the lights started flickering, but on later inspection the train was passing through town that day. We breathed a sigh of relief, I can tell you. You know, after all that happened. I don’t think the training has worried me at all. I was so agitated before that my training was being interrupted. Now, I think I’ve learned my lesson from it all. Worrying so much about my training that I forgot the things that really mattered: We still have our health and Ducky is safe.
