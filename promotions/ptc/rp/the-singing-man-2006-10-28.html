The Singing Man

28 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-28 Easton woke up out of his trance and into the world. His knees were drawn up in front of him and he cradled his forehead between them. His arms were behind his back, harsh metal bracelets slipped around his wrists. His side throbbed with pain. His brain bounced around inside his skull, pounding incessantly, hurting sharply each time the gray matter hit the stiff bone. His eyes watered, stinging sourly. He kept his eyes on the blue-gray tiles of the floor. He sat for a long while, trying to summon up what had happened, and at the same time he seemed to want to keep it down. He tried to ignore the motion of people about him and their words; he could barely understand them, but he didn't want to hear them at all. He pulled his arms up a bit; gritting his teeth, he felt harsh metal cutting into his back. He shifted his feet a bit and grunted. He didn't want the world, not now. Closing his eyes, Easton tried to will himself back into his trance, but to no avail. Not even a sliver of sleep came to him. Easton didn't know what it was, exactly—the air had sort of a hostile feeling; maybe that was it?—but he did know that it distressed him. He didn't want to be forced into this strange reality where he was curled up like a disobedient fetus. He wanted escape. Slowly, he made himself lift his head, feeling his shaggy hair spilling over his face and catching a bit in his mouth. He didn't bother spitting it out. He felt weary, like he had walked through a desert for sixty spans of the moon—how else could one count time in a wasteland?—and had nothing to drink. That was the feeling. He was about to die. A smile drifted onto his face, a curious thing like a flattened crescent. His eyes drifted shut and he pressed his shoulders back against what he assumed was the wall behind him. His breathing shallowed, but his chest seemed to swell larger and larger every time he inhaled, puffing out like a poisonous floater. "Elusive dreamland…" The voice reached out to him, a man's voice that seemed to channel everything that existed in realms other than this. It grabbed him by the temples and dunked him into darkness. He saw a pair of eyes, womanly eyes. He felt his hands materializing, cradling a smooth jaw; his thumbs ran over cheeks. He felt soft hands tangling in his hair. His greens seemed to intertwine with her hazels. He felt a strange feeling inside, a swirling feeling that was something more than base desire. His lips quivered. "Oh, god, Easton!" she cried, pulling him towards her. He felt her pressing against him, her head laying on his shoulder, arms wrapped around him, hand laying across the back of his neck. He slid his hand upwards, through her long black locks. He sighed, just feeling her against him for the moment. When was the last time that they had failed to kiss? Surely, it was years ago, before things had ever gotten serious between them. Before he had ever called her his wife. Before he had begun to be away from home for so long, to leave her cloistered like a nun like a big house could satisfy her on its own, without a husband to fill it with her. "I've missed you so much," she breathed, bringing him closer. She didn't look at him, though, didn't open her mouth to meet his. How long had it been since they had seen the stars together, since they'd exchanged quiet vows of love? How long since they had exchanged rings, since he'd slipped the plain gold band engraved only with her name about his ring finger? Easton didn't even remember where his ring was. "It's so good to have you home." Easton wasn't home, though, and he couldn't come home. He had never stayed, not for months. He would come and see her, reassure her that he was alright, that they would be alright, and then he left again. It was a crime, but what could he do? Something always had him away, some hunt that he couldn't give up. One day, she would be the world to him again. One day, he would give everything else up to stay with her, to hold her in his arms and never let go. How long had it been since he had cried? "Behind a velvet curtain…" Easton's eyes slid open and he found himself tipping over, landing hard on the ground. He groaned, extending his legs and looking up at the ceiling. His stomach roiled, sending waves of discomfort through him. How could anything be so bad, so unbearable? A pair of legs moved in front of him; they wore black slacks with black dress shoes over what must be feet. Something unintelligible was said. Easton opened his mouth to ask, but it quickly fell slack. He felt rough hands grabbing him under his arms and yanking him up off the tiles. His head tilted back dangerously. His legs dangled on the ground. With a shuddering gasp, his eyes dragged closed once again. "Lysergic vision…" Where was she going? Splashing through water, Easton tried to keep up with the little girl. Wearing a red polka-dotted dress and a wide-brimmed, straw hat with a red ribbon tied about it, the girl seemed to dance in this dark place, the only sign of color for miles. Easton flagged, putting his hand up against the wall to keep himself steady. How could she keep this up? "It's not too much farther!" she said, leaping up with some divine grace. Her small feet landed on the edge of a dumpster and she turned around, apparently undisturbed by the smell or the innate repugnance of it. Easton nodded, gulping in air as he tried to turn his trudge into a run. She waved him on and he seemed to gain strength, pulling his hand away from the wall. Suddenly, Easton stepped into an ocean and found himself falling to earth. Easton moaned as his face struck the asphalt: giving a spasmodic quiver, he half-curled on the ground, lying on his side. He looked up at her, blood trickling from his nose. His jaw shook as he tried to make his mouth work. "Wh-where are we going, Fortuna?" he choked. "To visit Sammy Brown!" she answered happily. Easton blinked. The name was so familiar. "S-Sammy… Sammy Brown?" "Of just a mortal man…" "What does Sammy Brown have to do with this?" A bald man had Easton's jaw in his hands and was shaking him relentlessly. "Easy, Stan," a voice from behind Easton cautioned. "Who's Sammy Brown!?" the bald man—Stan?—growled. "A somebody," Easton mumbled. Sometimes, the smallest things could get you in trouble. Thinking of something so dear to him in a time such as this—even when he couldn't understand what was going on—was one of them. How could he help it if he wanted to win GTT6 so badly it hurt, though? How could he help it that every one in six thoughts was of his next match, was of if he could defeat this enigma known as… Sammy Brown. "Utopic pastures…" Easton sat back, closing his eyes as he felt the needle dive into his skin again. He sighed and then closed his lips again, trying to concentrate. The black-robed mystic at his side continued to jab his skin, dyeing it. Easton looked up at the man, noticing his wrinkled features for the thousandth time and the way that his jet black hair contrasted it. It was a testament to the man's endurance, Easton decided. Neither man spoke; this work wasn't meant for speaking. This needed silence, meditation. The seer needed it to know exactly what he was doing, to make every pinprick count. It would be hard to take away the ink once it was in, nearly impossible, and the image had to be perfect. Forever, his arms would be painted. Forever, he would be linked with these images. In retrospect, that day was the last time he had really trusted the mystic and the last time that the mystic had really trusted him. The markings were the only thing he had gotten from the mystic's tutelage that he was in the least bit proud about. They were about him, they were his essence. Around his arms wrapped green-and-gold dragons, one for each, the images of destruction which Easton would be so quickly identified with. As a wrestler, any hint of a destructive force would be the first thing people associated with you. Easton accepted that, styled himself after it. On his wrists, the dragons each carried an eight-spoked wheel in their fanged mouths. Dharmachakra, the symbol for Buddhism's Eightfold Path. Easton had never been a Buddhist, but it was a powerful image. It spoke of immortality in some form, even if it was not the mortal one. Easton knew that no one would ever call him "the Wheel," but that was alright. They didn't need to recognize what he did. They could marvel at his dragons if they wanted, wait with glee for more destruction. Even destroyers died. "The edge of paradise…" Easton opened his eyes and mumbled something that even he didn't understand. The bald man—Easton now saw he wore an open blazer and a black shirt underneath—made a surprised sound and snatched up a piece of paper. "Do you know this woman?" Stan growled, pushing a picture in front of Easton. Easton squinted, trying to think. She was a blonde, quite pretty, but Easton had to shake his head. Stan grunted. "What about her?" Stan asked, lifting up another image. This one showed a blonde woman lying naked on the floor, her face a mask of blood. Easton thought he saw a hole just above her temple, but he didn't look to hard. Pressing his lips together to prevent himself from spitting in disgust, he shook his head again. "Stan, he's not even fully conscious," the man who had spoken to the bald man earlier said from far away. Easton couldn't see him, but somehow the fact that he was trying to talk Stan down was comforting. Easton's eyes started to slide closed. "Maybe you don't recognize her face," Stan continued, slamming both photos down on the table that was in front of Easton, "But maybe you'll remember this: she was wearing a mask. It was of another woman. Ring any bells?" Easton's eyes shot open and he looked up at Stan. A wicked smile crossed the bald man's face, but he had no idea. Could he? Could he know about the vision? "K-Kelly?" Easton stammered. Stan blinked. "What? No, you fucking idiot. Her name was Rita Sa—" "No, the mask," Easton cut in. It was quite an effort to talk. "Was it… Kelly?" Stan leaned forward, his face right in front of Easton's. "Kelly who?" Stan seethed. His breath stank of chili peppers. "I… I don't know…" Easton groaned. "But…" "Blue sparkling falls laugh…" "Not far now!" Fortuna giggled, dancing in a puddle. The golden ringlets of her hair bounced joyously under her hat. Joy was hard to find in this gray world where the sun ran too fast and the moon too slow; Easton was thankful for her being here. Picking himself up off the ground once again, Easton began after her. The girl was far too smart for her own good, but he had no choice. Why exactly that was, Easton didn't know, but refusing had always seemed like a very bad idea. Soon enough, Fortuna led him to a place where the street curiously ended in a steep ledge with a parapet marking the end. She climbed up—why did she have to climb now?—onto the parapet and leaned over, looking below. "Over here! Hurry!" Easton did, his limping steps increasing to an odd gait that would be very hard for any tracker to identify. Soon enough, he was leaning over the parapet, too. He wasn't brave enough to climb up over it like Fortuna, but that didn't matter. He could see fine. A white chalk outline was clear on the street below, in the comical position that people always seemed to die in. "Who's that?" Easton asked. He wanted to rephrase the question, but he decided to hold his tongue. Fortuna shrugged. "I'm not sure," she told him. "Then why did you take me all the way over here?" "Well, okay," Fortuna sighed. "I have some idea. It's either you or Sammy Brown." "Me or Sammy?" "Echo! Echo!" Fortuna bounced, clapping her hands and giggling. Easton couldn't help but smile. Soon, though, it was gone. "But what do you mean? One of us are gonna die?" "Maybe," Fortuna said, biting her lower lip. "So why did you say that we were going to visit Sammy Brown if it's me or him?" Fortuna gave him an odd look. "Haven't you ever heard of optimism?" Easton nodded slowly. Fortuna always surprised him with her vocabulary. "With steaming jungle rains…" The door opening again brought Easton to his senses and he looked up, seeing Stan walking back into the room. Stan looked down at Easton and grunted. "The only record we have on you is an incident where you were found by Baltimore police," Stan said, throwing a manila folder on the table. "Sitting on the ground, asleep, apparently crying yourself to sleep. You appeared to have been banging your head on the wall of a building. Now, that's not really much to go by." Stan placed his fingers on the file. "But you are a Canadian citizen. That means that if you don't start talking, Easton, we can deport you. At the very least, you won't be murdering any more American citizens." Easton blinked and lifted his hips up, touching at his backside. He quickly remembered that he didn't even have his wallet on him at all. He opened his mouth, but Stan held up his hand to stop him. "A duffle bag was turned in," Stan explained. "Someone witnessed you chasing a guy down after he tried to steal your car, decided to keep your bag for you. When you didn’t come back, they turned it into the police. Took a look and voila! Your picture on your license." Easton let his head drop a bit, sighing. Stan gripped his hair and yanked his head back, glaring at him. "Rita Sammael!" Stan yelled at him. "Heiress to a media fortune! She is dead, you little puke! I'm going to get a confession out of you if it's the last thing I do!" Easton looked at him lazily, unable to make anything of it. He hadn't heard the name before, didn't know anything about it. It was making Stan angry, though. He gave a growl and lifted his hand, balling his fingers into a fist. Easton couldn't defend himself. He knew he couldn't. Fear of being struck welled up in his gut; being a fighter didn't mean you automatically began to accept being beaten. "No!" the man who was talking Stan down cried, leaping up and grabbing Stan's wrist. Relief flooded through Easton's system. "I want to dream on a pillow of clouds…" Easton plummeted. The sky didn't hold him up like he wanted it to. He didn't flail as he fell, but neither did he fall headfirst. He dropped like a body wrapped in a coffin, completely parallel to the earth below. With some effort, he managed to roll and face the ground. At least he wanted to see how close he was. His fear of falling somehow didn't manifest itself, and his fear of death seemed almost nonexistent. Below him stood a man in black robes with the hood back and an inferno licking the air from his collar upwards in place of a head. He stood in the midst of a Unicursal Hexagram, turning about as if to look at everything at once. His feet didn't touch the ground; he walked on the stomach of a fair-skinned naked woman with blonde hair. She didn't move, even though the man's weight had to be enormous on that one spot. Who was she? Easton tried to pull up a name he had heard. Rachel? Rena? Rochelle? Rita? Ramona? It had to be the woman he had seen in his vision, the woman with the plastic mask. The woman… with the lance… through her head. This woman had no hole, though, none that he could see. He struck the earth with a crash. Before he bounced back upwards, he took note of the face of the woman. There was no mask. It was her. Kelly. "Cast aside this neon shroud!" "He did it!" Easton cried out, head slumped on his shoulder. Iron-hard hands gripped his shoulders and shook him. "Who!" Stan bellowed. "Who!? Spit it out!" "He did it!" Easton stammered again, eyes roving about wildly. "He killed her! He did it!" "Who!? Who killed her, damn you!? Who killed Rita Sammael?" Stan's voice drifted away slowly, still demanding answers. "Make a bed in the riverside leaves…" Easton couldn't see. It didn't bother him, though; he couldn't feel anything, either, so it made a bit of sense. "Dee tried it," a voice said. It was a familiar voice, a voice that Easton connected with fire and black robes. "After he discontinued his relations with Kelley. He plunged headlong into the Abyss, never to be seen again. "Crowley succeeded, actually, in visiting the City of the Pyramids. Common knowledge that he did, but it's more of a secret that he tried to beseech Babalon into crossing back with him. A fool, an utter fool, but talented. Of course, that was what ultimately led to his downfall, the curse of Babalon. "Parsons and Hubbard tried to do it, too. The Babalon Working—you've heard of it?" "Vaguely, yes." Easton was startled by another voice, a female. It wasn't a voice he recognized. Easton didn't know how to feel about it; relief because it wasn't Kelly or anxiety for whoever did own the voice? "A bit more intelligent an attempt, but a disaster. Hubbard went insane, of course, after they failed. Parsons, for his part, was already insane. There was nothing left but to kill him, which came in due time. A genius, but completely mad." "Mm." "I will succeed, of course. I've perfected it. I can summon her! Where all the rest have failed, I will succeed. I will bring her to the shores of the mortal world and then… and then the world will be at my knees. I simply must have the perfect vessel." "Me." The voice had a sound of contentment to it, a smug self-worth. "Yes. You. You are the… perfect… vessel." Derision was thick in the man's voice, giving away the lie, at least to Easton. But if this woman wasn't the vessel, who was? And why was she here? "And ride a sunbeam up through the trees!" "In there!" came Stan's rough voice. Easton was shoved backwards into a dank room. A barred gate was drawn over the way he had come in. He looked around and sighed, sitting on a thin cot. He glanced up to see the bald man glaring at him. "You'll come out when we get some answers." Easton grunted as Stan marched away. What kind of answers did Stan want? What the hell was the man's problem? He was coming closer to full consciousness, but he couldn't completely comprehend everything. He supposed that was why he couldn't defend himself adequately, why he was now being held in a jail cell. "I want to dream on a pillow of clouds…" The song was coming from somewhere near. Easton could feel it. He sat up on his cot and touched his forehead. It seemed to coalesce into something familiar. "Cast aside this neon shroud!" He looked up. In the cell directly across from him was a brown-haired man, hunched over his knees. That was who was singing. He could hear it clearly now, pinpoint it. He leaned forwards, squinting. "Make a bed in the riverside leaves…" It was him! It was that punk that he'd chased down! Easton felt anger swelling up in his chest. His fingers curled into fists reflexively. Suddenly, though, he recognized something else about him. Something more familiar. He knew the guy from somewhere else; he just remembered that that was the reason he'd chased him so long. And the voice… he recognized the voice, too. "And ride a moonbeam up through the trees!" "Brian?" The singer slowly lifted his head and gave Easton a somber, defeated look. His face was streaked with tears. He mouthed a word to Easton before his face screwed up and he had to wipe his eyes with his forearm. It was quite a distance and Easton was very bad at reading lips. Still, Easton knew exactly what he had wanted to say. Kelly. Lyrics: "River of Soul" by the Obsessed
