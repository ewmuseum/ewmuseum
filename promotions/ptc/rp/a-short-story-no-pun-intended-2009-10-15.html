A Short Story: No Pun Intended

15 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-15 Confidence. Many people claim to have confidence seeping from their pores. They like to think that if they say it enough, it might actually come true. Unfortunately, that never really works. Well, unless you happen to be me, because I actually have the talent it takes to step into a ring and out-wrestle someone. I know I don't look like much. Most people look at me and see the short guy with a smile on his face, that easy win they have been longing for. I'm the class clown everyone laughs at, the butt of everyone's jokes. I try to tell people that things aren't always what they seem, that they need to realize that the fool they see outside of the ring is not the man that steps between the ropes. They never listen. They pass it off as the false bravado of a fool trying to make them lose focus. It turns out to be their undoing, because I actually want them to pay closer attention to the real me...the man they are not ready for. It hardly ever happens though. It didn't work for Hessian, and it more than likely played on the mind of Angelo Deville until the day he died. Hell, Jason Snow tapped so hard the first time I beat him; I thought he was a new PRIME referee. I'm name dropping. I know that may sound petty when you take into account that I'm not facing any one of those men, but how can I not take pride in knowing that I've taken down some of the biggest names in this venture we often call a sport. I'm proud of my accomplishments. What I've done not only brings a smile to the normal side of my face, but it reminds me not to make the same mistake those men made when they faced me. I know better than to underestimate someone that I have never faced before, and I can honestly say that being prepared for anything and everything has been the main reason for my success. I work hard in preparing myself for the unexpected. So when I beat someone that people say I should beat, I am not surprised. When I beat someone I should not, I am even less surprised. I win because I expect to win. Hell, I am win. The Pursuit September 18, 2009 A young woman walked down Fifth Avenue at a steady pace, a shopping bag from the Disney store tucked neatly under her arm as she followed the flow of pedestrians headed in the same direction. Hundreds of things filled her mind, yet she still managed to sidestep before she collided with a woman that decided to stop short in front of her. That was the normal hustle and bustle of an afternoon out in New York's famous shopping district. There was no way for her to know the man and woman that followed not too far behind had been there since she left her home, but they trailed her nonetheless. “She walks pretty fast,” the man commented, his pace quickened as they struggled to keep up with the young woman that spent three nights a week doing cardio and Pilates in her home gym. “You sure she's wearing heels?” “Stop being a damn baby,” was his partners reply as she brushed away a stray lock of tawny brown hair that had fallen in front of her eyes. “We need to catch up to her before she gets back to the damn car, or this isn't going to work.” “We should have grabbed her in the parking lot when she got down here,” the man defended himself, though he knew why the plan had not been set up that way. She needed to have a paper trail, make it look like a random snatch job so her family would not be able to put two and two together and figure out what really happened to their daughter. “Yeah,” the woman said with a slight giggle, her pearly white teeth slightly visible between a pair of lips that have a light shade of Coral named Peach Colada layered on them. “I can just picture the shit storm that would have started if we strayed off of Alex's plan.” “Always worried about what Alex is going to do,” the man said, slightly winded as he struggled to keep up with the young woman as well as his counterpart. “We could have picked her up when she got out of her car, then you could have put on one of your wigs and spent the day buying me something for Christmas.” “Buying you something for Christmas?” “Yeah,” he replied, almost confident that it would have been the perfect plan. “That way it would look like she ran away with some guy that might be her boyfriend.” “Let's just make sure we don't lose her,” she brushed a stray strand of hair away from her eyes as she spoke. Her gait slowed slightly as she lifted her hand and pointed toward the Starbucks the target had entered. “Hold up.” “Dammit, Juli,” The young man bent at the waist and placed his hands on his sides while he attempted to catch his breath. “What did I tell you about using real names, you idiot?” The woman now known as Juli turns to face the young man. While she does not look exactly like the woman who shares the shortened name used, there are certain features that are dead give aways that she is indeed Sin City's Mexican Minx – her piercing hazel eyes being the most apparent. Which means the young man with the brim of his baseball cap pulled down to right above his brow must be her partner in crime – Sin City's only two time LiveWire champion – Jadian Bridden. The bushy mustache, dark sunglasses, and long sideburns that made him look like a throwback to the seventies, accompanied the hat to complete the most awesome disguise in the history of the World. “Yeah, whatever, babe. Do you really think any of these people will even remember a damn thing when this shit hits the news?” “Doesn't matter,” she replied. “Then why did you call Alex by her real name?” “Because the name Alex relates more to a man, that's why.” Julissa shook her head, her eyes set on the door of the popular coffee shop. “I still don't get all of this cloak and dagger shit,” he shrugged his shoulders nonchalantly as he spoke. “Yeah, well, I would have brought Roddy if he didn't stick out like a politician in a whorehouse.” Jay stared at her, a bit puzzled by the comparison. That was when he noticed the man seated across from the target. “Hey, isn't that...” Juli turned and saw the man. More importantly, she saw the scar that started at the corner of his lip and ran up his cheek a few inches. “...Tony Gamble.” She finished his sentence, her finger already pressed against the button that was set to reach Alexandra Pierce on speed dial. It only took a few rings for her to answer, and before she could say anything Julissa was already speaking. “Things just got a bit complicated.” “Dammit,” Jay said aloud. “I think he saw me looking at him.” “I can't take you anywhere,” she shook her head as she started to walk away from the window. “I'll call you right back.” “What the hell did I do?” “Just come on,” she was pissed off. The only thing good about that, was the fact that the sex was a lot rougher and ten times as great as usual. Tony Gamble sat with his hands cupped around the Venti White Chocolate Mocha he ordered a few minutes before she walked through the door and stood in line. It was strange the way they met lately, and he couldn't even think of the stories the tabloids would spread if they saw him with an unknown woman that was young enough to be his daughter. Still, he felt partially responsible for the disappearance of her father. Even at this young age she knew better than to ask too many questions, even if it hurt her to know that she may never see him again, so the million dollar question was never brought up. “Hey, Uncle Tony,” the light hearted smile that greeted him, as she wrapped her arm around his shoulders and squeezed him tight in a hug, brought a full smile to his own face. “Hey, sweetie,” he patted her on the shoulder, then watched as she took the seat across from his and placed an Enrobed Espresso Brownie on the table in front of her. “How's school?” Adelina Amour DeAngelis shrugged her shoulders slightly as she licked a bit of the rich chocolate off of her thumb and index finger. “It's alright, I guess.” “Just alright?” he grilled her a bit, though he knew he could never be a father figure to a young woman that had grown up in the environment she had. Her father made a point to spend more time with the family instead of his family, and in doing so had isolated himself from his wife and children. “It's school,” he was sure a million other students shared her feelings, but she wasn't just any student; she was family. “So it's nothing worth talking about...I get it.” He took a sip from his drink, then ran his tongue across his lips to lick off the excess. “Just make sure you keep your grades up, okay?” “Yeah, yeah,” she replied just before she took a bite of her brownie. “I got a Tall Strawberry Frappuccino for Lina!” the girl behind the counter with her light brown hair pulled back in a ponytail that dangled over her green visor yelled out. Tony couldn't help but notice the quarter sized hole in her ear, reminded him of an old National Geographic magazine he had looked at when he was growing up. Funny how kids acted like this was something they just came up with. “Hmm,” Adelina chewed on her dessert, but still didn't keep from talking with her mouth full. “That's mine...be right back.” Tony nodded, then took another sip from his drink as he glanced out the window. Some guy with a baseball cap pulled down against his sunglasses stared at him through the window, so Tony turned quickly and hoped the guy didn't recognize him. He didn't notice the man did the same thing. “So, how's Cindy?” Adelina asked as she plopped herself down in her seat. “What?” Tony was still a bit shaken about being seen with DeAngelis' daughter. Though he considered her to be family, they were far from blood relatives. It took him a while before he realized what she had asked. “Oh, yeah...she's doing great.” “Any new news on the baby?” “Not too much longer,” he answered quickly, then took another glance out the window. He relaxed when he noticed the man was no longer there. “Are you excited?” Lina took a sip from her drink, the smile on her face when she asked about the baby nearly melted his heart. “Of course I am,” he answered her. Truth be told, he couldn't wait to become a father. He had enjoyed the time he and his wife, Cynthia, had spent painting the nursery and going out shopping for items they needed to fill the nursery had brought them even closer. “Don't I look excited?” She laughed out loud when he pointed to the side of his face that was disfigured into a permanent grin. Neither one of them cared that it drew the attention of half of the coffee shop. Well, it didn't bother Tony at first, but as his eyes shifted around the room to find a hundred eyes on the two of them he hoped everyone just thought they were lost in their own world. “You're such a goofball, sometimes.” “Cindy tells me that all the time,” the genuine smile that spread from cheek to cheek held her attention for a bit. Then she asked something that he wasn't expecting. “So, how have the family barbecues been?” She stuck the straw between her lips. “Few and far between,” Tony paused slightly. “Ever since her father decided to run for mayor, he's hardly been around.” “Well,” she pulled the straw from her lips and swallowed what was still in her mouth. “He doesn't like you anyway. So this is good, right?” “I guess.” He shrugged. The song 'Unbreakable' by Fireflight began to play, a ringtone of her new favorite song that she downloaded a few days ago. She pulled it from her purse and glanced at the caller ID. “Crap, it's my mom.” She pressed a button then stuck the phone against her ear. “Hello?” Tony took another glance out the window as she spoke on the phone. Whoever the man was, he had not returned. Still, he couldn't help but feel that something felt wrong. He wasn't sure what it was, but something bothered him about the man he saw. “Alright,” Adelina said to her mother. “I'll be home in a bit.” Tony turned back to see her stuff the phone back in her purse. “Everything alright?” “Yeah,” she said with a nod. “My mom's just tripping out.” “I don't blame her,” Tony said. “You've been pushing her out of your life lately.” “Don't start lecturing me now,” she grabbed her bags and stood up. “I get enough of that at home.” “I'm sorry,” Tony shook his head as he reached over and grabbed a hold of her hand. “Look, I deposited some more money into your account.” “I told you,” she pulled her hand away and tucked the bag from the Disney Store under her arm. “You don't have to do that.” “I know I don't have to,” he replied. “I...I just want to make sure you're not struggling.” “We have enough to get by,” she looked away. “My dad made sure of that.” “Alright,” he stood up and stepped closer to her. “But if you need anything. Anything. You call me, okay?” “I will.” “Alright.” he hugged her tight, then gave her a peck on the forehead. “You be careful heading home.” “Now you're really starting to act like my mom.” “I think I look better in a dress though.” Tony grinned. Okay, he really grinned. She just shook her head as she walked away. “You're such a goofball.” Goofball. He had no way of knowing that may be the last word he would ever hear her say, that the man that stared at him through the window was not there for him. Still, as he watched her walk away, he had this pain in his gut that made him feel a bit uneasy. He thought he just had a bad case of gas. Coffee did that to him sometimes. The Generous Contribution September 19, 2009 Daniel Berkman stands tall and dominant behind a wooden podium, a very confident smile curving the ends of his lips slightly as he basks in the glory of political favor. He thrusts his arms up toward the heavens, a steady roar from the small crowd echoing in his ears as they cheer their approval. It is only a few hundred people, but it is a start. These few hundred will increase to a thousand, then multiply into tens of thousands before it is all said and done. At least that is how they planned for it to turn out when Daniel decided to take his long time friend and confidant, Jake Cassel up on his challenge. Jake Cassel has a rather large grin on his face as he listens to his associate, his friend, speak to the masses and demand not only their attention, but their admiration as well. It was part of the charm that convinced Jake to persuade his Monday night poker partner to run for office in the first place. He was not flashy, not even in the custom tailored suit he was dressed in right now. It was a dark gray pant and jacket combo that he put together with the help of his wife. The gray and burgundy silk tie coordinated perfectly with the dark red button up shirt he had bought at the same time. It was the same with his decision to run. It was a remark made in passing, in jest, that sprouted wings and took flight before either one of them really had time to laugh it off and pretend nothing was ever said. Much like his wardrobe choice, it was natural and took little to no effort. That, in itself, was reason enough to believe that this could actually work. The roar of the crowd begins to swell as Berkman's arms slowly lower and widen – a gesture of welcoming people into his arms – before he turns and steps away from the podium. Jake nods, satisfied that Daniel's words are beginning to soak into the minds of the voters that are tired of the current mayor getting rich off of their taxes. Apart from Mayor Bloomberg and Comptroller Thompson, who happen to represent the two major political parties, Berkman had the highest support rate in the Metropolitan area. He was a decorated veteran, had rose through the ranks in the Federal Bureau of Investigation, and had the support of FBI Director Robert Mueller. Everything was falling into place like pieces of a jigsaw puzzle, and it all started with pocket two's. “So how did I do?” Daniel Berkman's question causes Cassel's lips to separate and frame slightly yellowed teeth, turning his grin to a wide and welcoming smile when he stretches out his hand to give the mayoral candidate a congratulatory hand shake. “You had them eating out of the palm of your hand, Danny,” Jake replies as he motions with his free hand for Berkman to follow him to the limousine that is waiting over by the curb. “To be honest with you,” Berkman wears his own toothy grin as he follows Cassel and his security detail past the throng of supportive voters. “I felt like a roasted pig standing up there in this suit. Why can't I go up there in a nice shirt and a pair of slacks?” “That's fine if you just want to have a nice run for mayor,” Jake's reply is not meant to deter his friend, but help him realize the seriousness of the opportunity they seem to be getting ready to kick down the door on. “But if you want to win the whole damn thing you need to look like you've already won.” “Yeah, I think I like the sound of that better,” Berkman gives his associate a quick wink as his lips twist into a confident smirk. Gibril Evans is the first member of the security detail to reach the limousine, taking a hold of the handle and pulling the door open seconds before Daniel and Jake arrive, he manages a curt nod to the man he has already pegged as the next New York mayor. He slams the door shut and motions toward the two black Chevrolet Tahoes that are already running in front and behind the white stretched limo, before he opens the door and eases his way into the front seat next to the driver. Berkman and Cassel are sitting across from each other in the back of the plush limousine, and – judging from the drinks they each have in their hand – the mini bar is open. Berkman has a manila folder open on his lap as he glances over a sheet of paper that he pulled out of it when they sat down. Jake studies him from the opposite seat, curious as to his thoughts on what could be the newest contributor to the campaign. A few 'hmms' and 'uh huhs' escape the lips of his counterpart, but nothing for him to get a grasp on what he thinks of the offer. “Have you done a background check on these people?” Daniel finally asks, before he brings the glass of whiskey and soda to his lips and takes a quick drink. The burning sensation as the liquor coats the inside of his throat causes him to squint his eyes in a wince, but it doesn't last long. The man likes his whiskey. “I haven't received a full report,” Jake leans forward as he takes a drink from his glass, also a whiskey and soda concoction. He pulls another file from behind him and hands it to Berkman. “But you can see for yourself...they appear to be on the up and up.” “It's the fact that they appear to be on the up and up that bothers me, Jake.” Berkman closes the first folder and places the new one on top of it. He flips it open and scans the first few lines. “I have enough things to worry about already, so having someone looking too squeaky clean is going to raise more than an eyebrow. I want to know that these people are not going to be a problem.” “It's a substantial amount of money, Danny.” “It's a ridiculous amount,” Berkman corrects his friend. “That is why I am having a hard time believing that they don't expect something in return.” “Doesn't everyone?” Jake asks with a wry smile playing on liquor moistened lips. He finishes off his drink, then places the glass beside a few others. “Sadly, yes.” Daniel does not look as amused about the situation as Jake does. So when he starts to bite on the side of his lip, Jake notices the shift in emotion. “We need this money, Daniel,” Cassel interlocks his fingers as his elbows rest on his knees. “But if you have a problem with it, then we'll just tell them no.” “We can't just tell them no, Jake.” Daniel shakes his head, leaning back in his seat as he stares out the window. “It would be rude and we could lose potential voters. Just do me a favor and make sure you do an extensive background check. Use our people in Jersey to do some snooping if you have to, but make sure that this doesn't come back and bite us in the ass.” “Very well,” Jake eases back into his seat, the cool leather is refreshing against his back when his sweat moistened shirt is sandwiched between his flesh and the hide of a cow. He takes the folders Berkman hands to him and places them on the seat beside him. The words 'Pierotti Foundation' are written on the top one in bold black marker. The Important Conversation September 20, 2009 “I take it all went as planned?” Jadian Bridden is the first to sit up in his seat, the cold and calculating voice of the Director startling him as he did not hear her enter the room. She does not walk as much as she glides across the room, her snow white pant suit free from any blemish as she crosses her arms behind her back. “Well,” he stands up, glancing over at Julissa as he speaks. “I'm not sure if Gamble saw me.” “Everything went well,” Julissa blows on her nails, the fresh layer of nail polish still wet as she gives Jay a wink. “Perfect,” the woman known as Desade in Sin City Championship Wrestling stands in front of the open door of the room, her eyes set on the young woman that appears to be unconscious on the full size mattress thrown on the floor. The restraints around her hands and ankles are cutting into her flesh slightly, a sure sign that she has been struggling to get free. “Wait a minute,” Jadian switches his attention between the two women. “You knew he was going to be there?” “There is little I do not foresee, Jadian,” Pierce does not turn to face him. “So you wanted him to see me?” “Not really,” Julissa interjects. “But it works out, because now he knows that this wasn't random.” “Mr. Gamble will go to extreme measures to find out who has abducted his goddaughter,” Pierce finally turns, the slight smile that dances across her lips enough to make a grown man turn away in fear. “Perhaps even turn to an old friend for assistance.” “Okay,” Jadian shakes his head, his palms facing the heavens as he shrugs his shoulders. “I'm completely lost.” “Yes, well, certain things are not meant for your ears.” “Jay,” Juli walks up and runs a freshly painted nail along his cheek. “You think you can give us a minute.” “Oh, so I'm good enough to snatch some girl off the street, but I'm not -” “There will be no need to leave, Jadian.” Julissa turns to face the Director. “Are you sure?” Jadian seems to be just as surprised when Desade nods. “I have other plans to tend to,” Pierce makes her way to the couple. “Just be certain the package is sent to Mr. Berkman.” “Care of the Pierotti Foundation?” Julissa's lips spread to a thin grin. “Yes.”
