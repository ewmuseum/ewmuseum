Love and mallets, love and mallets

16 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-16 It was nearly a dark and stormy night. Sadly, once more the vagaries of the weather had ruined the ambience, and instead it was a dark and moderately windy night, with a forty percent chance of scattered showers as the night progressed. None of this worried Doctor Curiosity as he wandered around his Curious Laboratory. On his mind were just two things; the creation of the Curious Mallet Version 3.0, and the upcoming first round match of GTT4. He walked past the old blackboard, untouched in nearly a year, the names of opponents from tournaments past faded and lost to the passing of time. He walked past the workbench, the huge slab of dark oak that was about thirty feet in length and six feet wide, with every inch covered in some form of scientific ephemera. Here was a beaker with bubbling green liquid, and there was a test tube spewing clouds of smoke that floated down to obscure the view of almost everything else on the desk. This was truly the lair of a mad scientist. It should be, for Doctor Curiosity had spent enough money kitting it out with the highest in quality Mad Scientist Furnishings. He walked on, past the alcove where the Army of Geriatrics had once been hidden and over to the small green door in the corner of the expansive room. His feet flipped and flopped over the stone floor, and his mostly white lab coat flapped behind him as he reached for the handle, pulling it open and letting loose a bellow in a heavily accented voice. "Eegor, bring forth ze Curious Mallet!" Doctor Curiosity had been spending a lot of time creating his latest in-ring innovation, and this was the third incarnation of it. The first two had proved to be useful, but lacked in the extra killer touch that would be needed in a tournament such as GTT4. Curiosity had thrown himself into the development of the weapon with all of his soul, partly because he wanted to win and progress on in the tournament, but also because of the time of year. The autumnal months always made Dr Curiosity work particularly hard, to take his mind off the Anniversary. Before Curiosity could dwell on anything, Eegor appeared holding an extremely large wooden mallet. He was really struggling under the weight of it, the mallet being around five feet tall. There was a big button on the one side of it, but the biggest thing in the room at that time was the smile of Doctor Curiosity. It was going to be tricky to sneak it in to the ring, but this little beauty was going to win him all the glory, and put George English down for the count. The doctor laughed to himself as he recalled how far he had come since he invented the Curious Mallet Version 1.0. -------------------------------------------------- The wrestling ring was set up in the Curious Laboratory, and inside stood Eegor, in a hideous patchwork wrestling singlet, which showed off his deformity of the week rather too well. In the interests of not wishing to induce vomiting, let's just say that the deformity would go unnoticed in baggy pants as opposed to tight ones. Dr Curiosity walked over to the ring, carrying a regular sized mallet, that is to say one that can be comfortably held in a single hand. The two wooden faces of the mallet were unscarred, as if it had never been used before. The good doctor entered the ring with a wide smile on his face and held the mallet in front of him. A look of consternation briefly crossed his face. "Good grief, Eegor! Could you not have vorn something less disgusting?" The assistant shrugged, and Curiosity raised the mallet in front of his face. "You see zis invention Eegor, it vill be vhat gives me victory! I'll not need to even break a sweat. You can distract the referee, and vhen my opponent attacks me, I just press zis little button and zey vill be knocked out! Now, attack me!" Eegor lumbered slowly forwards, and the doctor pressed a button on the handle of the mallet. The front face flipped open and a big boxing glove on a spring flew out and smashed right in to Eegor's face, knocking him off his feet! "Jah! It vorks, it vorks!" yelled the doctor, unaccustomed to seeing his inventions have a genuine practical use. The man in the lab coat smiled again. He wasn't used to being happy at this time of year, not so close to the Anniversary… … The Anniversary. ---------------------------------------------------- To understand what we have in the present, and where we can go in the future, we must first understand the past, and what lessons it has taught us. For Doctor Curiosity, starting his new life in the wrestling ring, it was initially a time to take stock of what he had left in his life, and what he had lost. As time passed, he came to terms with a lot of this, but every year, around the Anniversary, it became harder. Back in the beginning it had all been so much simpler. He was Chris Goodman, just a regular guy who studied hard. He'd been bullied in his school days, but over time he'd filled out a little and learnt how to take punishment and come back with enough ferocity to no longer mark him down as an easy target. Fast forwards through high school and on to late in his university career. He'd taken an affinity for science and turned it in to a series of good grades, a scholarship and a possible future career path. Astronomy was his field of choice, and he went abroad to take a Master's degree and do his Doctorate. Abroad in his case was to go to England, where he studied Astronomy in great detail, and became Doctor Chris Goodman. Upon finishing his research assignment and gaining that distinction it was time for Chris to return home, to his native California, for Doctor Curiosity is not from Munich, as he claims, but instead from San Diego. One thing struck him before he left, and that was a young woman by the name of Collette. In the last few weeks before Chris left the British Isles, he romanced this lady, taking her for dinner at expensive restaurants and making her life a joy. It was no surprise then that she was to follow him to the United States and the relationship to blossom. In the fullness of time it became more than just lust, and the two fell deeply in love. Indeed, Collette would marry Doctor Chris Goodman two years later on a cool September afternoon, making him the happiest man alive. With a book deal on the horizon, capitalising on a worldwide interest in popular science, everything looked good for Chris Goodman. He had a beautiful wife, was trying to start a family, and his career was in great shape. He was a man in love with life. Until it all fell apart. ------------------------------------------------------------ Eegor was in the wrestling ring again. This time he had decided that the tight singlet wasn't really necessary, and so in its place he wore loose trousers and a shirt that gave plenty of space for a balloon down the back of it. Some people said that his hunchback deformity was passé these days, but in his mind it was 'old school', a classic along the lines of Dior's 'little black dress'. So Eegor waited inside the Curious Ring for the good doctor to come and show him the improvements he had made to the Curious Mallet. Apparently Doctor Curiosity had thought that although the mallet had worked the first time round, it could do with improvements. Eegor remembered him clearly saying that though he liked the instrument, he didn't truly love it, for should the opponent learn how to duck, it would be rendered useless. In a tournament setting he might only have one shot at using his fiendish apparatus, and as such it had to be enough to guarantee victory for him. Truth be told, Doctor Curiosity knew nothing at all about his first round opponent, George English, but he wouldn't be overlooked. Other people had made that mistake against the good doctor many times over, and every single time they did so they found themselves on the wrong end of a curiously effective beating. Eventually Doctor Curiosity made his presence felt, walking through the small wooden door on to the cold, hard stones of the floor. In his hands rested a large mallet, a good three feet in height, with a large enough head to fit in a plethora of boxing gloves on springs. The doctor was not smiling on that day, though, as the happiness that came from creating a new invention wasn't enough to fill the void that had been left behind by the loss of everything he had held dear. Still, he entered the ring and held up the mallet. At the other end of the ring, Eegor steeled himself for what would surely be another painful session with the good doctor. He didn't relish this kind of treatment, but he knew what Curiosity was feeling, and was determined to help him through it. It's often that way with brothers. "You vill do ze same as before, Eegor, and attack me in ze usual manner. Zis time ze Curious Mallet Version 2.0 shall defeat you even if you try to duck, for it is eqvipped vith motion sensors and heat-seeking capabilities, jah!" Eegor sighed, and looked closely at the mallet. Something was amiss. "Come on Eegor, don't be so useless… attack me!" "I don't think that…" began the assistant to the mad scientist. "It is not your place to be doing ze thinking, Eegor. Zat is vhy I am ze doctor and you are ze deformed freak." "But master…" "Nein! Attack me, svine!" Eegor shrugged his hunched shoulders. Sometimes the doctor had to learn things for himself. The doctor's face finally started to grin as Eegor moved towards him at what could only be described as a slow waddle. Chances were that the Curious Mallet wouldn't need any heat seeking capabilities to find the target. "Prepare to be destroyed, fiend," yelled Doctor Curiosity as his finger searched out the button to fire the loaded boxing glove towards Eegor. It's worth saying at this point, in Doctor Curiosity's defence, that when you're not paying very close attention, one side of a mallet is much like the other. As such, the face of the mallet flipped open, and the good doctor had about half a second to appreciate how big the boxing glove looked before it ploughed in to his face at a fantastic rate of knots. The mallet flew right out of his hand as his head snapped back at an alarming rate, leaving the doctor comatose on the floor. Should he have been pointing the mallet in the correct direction it would surely have scored a direct hit on the unfortunate opponent. Eegor picked up the doctor, whose nose looked a little out of joint, and took him from the ring. He doubted that Curiosity would see this as much of a success, not when he was already so upset about a hundred and one other things. -------------------------------------------------------- Things started to go wrong for Chris Goodman when his book deal fell through. His work in the field of black holes and the origins of the Universe had been much like that of Stephen Hawking, when he published "A Brief History of Time". Chris was unfortunate enough to submit his research work and postulations for publication shortly before Hawking's book went on sale, and so there was suddenly no market for the work of an intelligent, if unspectacular American scientist. To say Chris was unhappy about this would be to make a colossal understatement. He had worked for years on this, under funding from both the government and his publisher. With the work no longer relevant, both withdrew their monetary assistance, and the budding author was temporarily unemployed. Many people could have taken this in their stride, but things were going badly at home for Chris too. Upset at work and at home, Doctor Goodman started to transfer the blame away, on to anyone that would provide an efficient scapegoat. The first on the hit-list was Stephen Hawking himself, branded a charlatan and a plagiarist, popular only because the disabled were more marketable. His disintegration from a respected scientist to a lunatic was accelerated when Collette dropped the biggest bombshell he could have imagined. She was thinking of leaving him, and wanted them to go to counselling. The trouble was that they had been trying for a baby for months, for years in fact, and nothing had happened, nothing at all. The tests had come back now, and it was clear for all the world to see, there would be no Chris Goodman Junior. A low sperm count was no fault of his own, and yet it had come between Chris and his wife. Perhaps using IVF treatment might have salvaged the marriage, but with the book deal dead in the water, there was no way for them to afford it and try to find out. Fate had again dealt him a bad hand, giving him an insurmountable object to fight against, a foe that through no fault of his own could not be beaten. Chris felt so powerless to do anything, so impotent against these invisible enemies, and so he lashed out against anyone that came near him. Collette was the first to find this out, causing the men and women of their street to talk in hushed voices over the black eye she sported proudly, in the final weeks of the relationship. She claimed she had walked in to a door, as many women do, but this was not someone who was being intimidated and cowed by her abusive husband. Collette was made of stronger stuff than that, and her defiance after that had the opposite effect on the doctor. He lost his will to go on, and turned his fury inside. He stopped working on a new book, and he stopped trying to please Collette in any way. He became so insular it was if he had looked up the word 'hermit' in a dictionary and decided that was the best way to go about life. The bitterness inside him was focused, drawn in on itself time and time again, until it hardened inside him, like a diamond. He gave it one last try on that most fateful of nights. The location was to be a fairground, a local one that opened from time to time during the Summer, and the early part of the Fall. It was their Anniversary again, celebrating a mere two years together, two years that looked certain to be the sum total of their involvement together as man and wife. Collette was on the verge of moving out and filing for divorce. The proud, strong man she had married had wilted and died in the face of a crisis, and she had simply had enough. Not that she was an angel herself, sometimes comforting her own pain in the arms of other men, disrespecting her marriage vows. For better or for worse. The night was drawing in as they went on the Ferris Wheel. Chris Goodman looked longingly in to his wife’s eyes as they rose upwards. Had the book deal gone sour, had he lost everything he owned, he would still have been fine, finding everything he would ever need in those eyes. His heart sang with symphonies of love, and he thought that maybe there was a last chance, if they both took some time off and tried their best to mend the pain and the words that had come between them. “Chris, I’ve filed for divorce. You’ll be getting the papers soon. I just… I just can’t go on like this… with you acting like this.” His heart broke at that moment. As melodramatic as it might seem, that was the exact instant that Doctor Curiosity was born. His dreams shattered, his still-beating heart ripped asunder by the pretty woman in the floral dress sitting opposite him, Doctor Chris Goodman turned the rage inwards one last time, and found something else. All his love was gone. The love for his wife, for his work, he was bereft of it all. Nothing lightened the dark places in his life any more. He was empty, and needed something to fill the void. It came in the form of hatred, bitterness and anger, and it came in spades. The Ferris Wheel crested the rise and for a moment they were on top of the world. To a casual observer they might still be married in their hearts, might still be filled with anything other than disdain and hatred, and even in her case, pity. Doctor Curiosity felt no pity. He pulled her to her feet and looked in to her eyes for one final time. The wheel lurched, and with Collette off-balance, Curiosity pushed, and she fell. Catching a metal strut on the way down smashed the back of her skull right in, and she was lifeless well before she landed on the damp ground, her blood mixing in with the spilt cola and lemonade of a thousand children. The doctor looked down, but could not find any emotion to name, no feeling to assign a place next to the memory. When remembering that night when he had killed his wife, killed his only love, Doctor Curiosity could only say that he felt nothing at all. ------------------------------------------------------- Eegor dragged the Curious Mallet Version 3.0 towards the Curious Ring in the Curious Laboratory, and cursed his master… his brother… for the startling lack of originality with regards to his naming scheme. The doctor was already in the ring, his smile masking the pain that he still felt inside. “Come on you stupid oaf, ze Curious Mallet is not zat heavy!” Eegor dragged it along behind him, very aware of that fact that it was indeed that heavy, and then some. He had no idea what the doctor had finally ended up putting inside the mallet, but it felt like it might be about three tons of robotics and gas cylinders. Eventually he reached Doctor Curiosity inside the ring and passed the mallet over to him. “Jah! Zis vill be ze greatest tzing ever!” Eegor walked to the other side of the ring and waited for the inevitable pain. The doctor had painted a large arrow on the side of the mallet this time, so there would be no easy escape from his role as the human punching bag. Eegor was sometimes saddened by the punishment he seemed doomed to take forever, but he knew that it had to happen. He knew that there was a very good reason for the way he acted, and that he might always be in debt to his brother, in ways that mere money could never repay. So Eegor stood there, his teeth gritted, his will resolute, as his brother prepared to inflict yet more pain upon him. “Here ve go!” Doctor Curiosity pressed the button, and two hydraulic arms shot out of the end of the mallet. They had laser pointers attached to the tops of the gloves, and they homed in quickly on Eegor. Both gloves moved in for the kill, swinging in hooks so deadly that even Eegor couldn’t just stand there and take it. He ducked, and both fists went sailing over his head, narrowly missing one another too. Their momentum took them round in an arc, continuing at pace and swinging round to complete a full circle, and then another one. Between them, Doctor Curiosity was getting crushed, the arms swinging round him in ever decreasing circles. “Help me Eegor, ze arms have minds of zeir own!” The doctor’s assistant rushed to his aid and quickly pressed the button marked for ‘Emergency Arm Release’. They continued whipping around, but soon lost contact with the doctor and, in time honoured tradition, defenestrated themselves in to the courtyard. Doctor Curiosity heard the sound of breaking glass and his heart sank. It looked like the Curious Mallet might never actually make it to the ring after all. Next to him sat his brother, showing his fraternal love by rescuing the man intent on harming him once more. Eegor slouched on the mat, still unsatisfied. It seemed that nothing could repay the debt that he owed to his brother. No amount of love could seem to bridge the gap that might bring them back together and rid him of the need for the outlandish costume and subservience. Eegor had seen them on the Ferris Wheel. He had been there that night when Doctor Curiosity had been born, and the last light of love vanished from that man’s heart. Eegor had seen the married couple go up on the fairground ride, and he had seen them come back down, forever apart. What he didn’t see was Collette falling to her death, because outside of the mind of Doctor Curiosity, it never happened. On the Ferris Wheel, Doctor Curiosity had been unable to push his wife over the edge. He had been unable to conquer his love completely, and that hurt him even more than if he had been able to commit the murder. This broke Chris Goodman for the last time, and Doctor Curiosity was indeed born from that loss… but not only from that loss, for there was one more left in store for that Anniversary night. Upon leaving the final fairground ride of the night, the remnants of Chris Goodman watched his former wife, the love of his life, leave him. He watched all the way as she walked towards the edge of the crowd and found her solace in the arms of the man whom she had taken as a lover. Paul Goodman. Eegor. With that final betrayal, cuckolded by his own kin, Chris Goodman was washed away for good, with any trace of love he felt for either friends or family. Paul, his brother, had chanced a backwards glance over the shoulder of his lover, and had seen Chris fall to his knees, weeping like a child, and it was only then he realized the magnitude of what he had done. He didn’t really love Collette, it had only ever been a chance to overshadow his brother, something he had never been able to do. In the end, it was a hollow victory, and as he saw his brother smashed and broken, he too walked away, and promised that he would do anything he ever could to mend what he had destroyed. Sitting in the middle of the Curious Ring, in the Curious Laboratory, Eegor reflected on how far they had come since that fateful night. His service to his brother, his repayment for his deeds would probably never be completed, but there was a certain sense of achievement to what he had done in the meantime. He had filled Doctor Curiosity with a sense of purpose, and a new goal in life. Having no desire to turn back to conventional science, Eegor had persuaded Doctor Curiosity to try and use his brain to create new devices… and where better to use them than in the world of professional wrestling? With this new goal, Doctor Curiosity had fallen in love again. This time it would not be with the fickle minds and souls of human beings, so adept at breaking hearts and ruining lives. No, this time the second love would be the creation of inventions that would give him an edge in the ring and make him unique in the sphere he would inhabit. The first love would be seeing them in action, and watching as other men suffered like he had done so on his Anniversary. The new love was in seeing others fail, their dreams of glory smashed by the most unlikely of opponents. This was what now fuelled the good doctor. He was in love with wrestling, and in love with winning. So, Eegor mused, it wasn’t the most noble of arts, and he wasn’t pushing the doctor in what would be considered the ‘right’ direction… but what he saw in his brother’s eyes when he defeated his opponents, much like he would defeat George English, was worth it. He wasn’t just happy. Much like his brother, he loved it.
