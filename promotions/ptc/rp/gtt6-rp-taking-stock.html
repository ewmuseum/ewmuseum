Taking Stock: The Flaws of Being Human

Primetime Central presents: GTT6! Title: Taking Stock: The flaws of being Human Featuring: Hunter Jones Date: 14th Oct 2006 Location: Studio/Past The Past Monday, December 19th 2003. 11:31 AM. "It's torn." He couldn't move, breathe, or even bring about a thought. At least not at the moment. His entire mind was wiped clean, and for the first time in the past several hours the awful pain in his right leg had subsided. The brief shock that had washed over the body of "Hardball" Hunter Jones, however, didn't last long. "What?" He said, having heard it the first time, but not quite believing what he was told. "Your right quad is torn, and it's severe," said the Doctor, whose name had been long forgotten by now. "There's about a 1 in 16 chance you'll ever wrestle again." *** Monday, December 19th 2003, 3:03 AM. Chuck Bush had navigated his rig over the icy roads of the Colorado Rockies many times, and often joked that he could in fact "Do it in his sleep". Cradling a bag of cheetos with his right arm, and the wheel with his left, he took a very sharp curve, throwing on a smug smirk to no one in particular about completing it. "Dayum Straight." he bellowed, sipping a beverage through his road thermos, its rubber straw sticking out from the top. Talk Radio blared, and by the time of morning, you can guess it was of abysmal quality. As two insomniacs began tackling the issue of local vandalism, Chuck tossed his bag of fatty snacks into the passenger seat, and rubbed his hands together. This was it. The moment he'd been waiting for, the sharpest turn on this particular stretch of road. Rather than slow down to take it, he increased his speed, attempting to break his own record. Placing his hands on the wheel he muttered to himself some words of encouragement. "WHOOOOO-HAH! I'M COMIN' FOR YA', YOU SUMABITCH!" There was nothing he could do. He took the turn, tongue displayed in a Jordan-Esque fashion as he prepared for his slam dunk. By the time he noticed the jet black Honda it was too late. Slamming on the breaks, he barreled into the rental, which had been sitting crumpled against the side of the mountain for an undisclosed amount of time. A Patch of black ice had been its initial downfall, and Chuck Bush's careless hobby it's second. When the rig finally squealed to a halt the passenger side of the Honda was thoroughly laid to waste. The car had flipped completely due to the speed of the 18-Wheeler, and Chuck's face turned a ghostly pale as he threw open the door to the cab, surveying the scene he'd just caused. "Uhh.. Ohh Shit.." Were the only words he could muster, as he stared at the wreckage. As Chuck scrambled back into the cab to call for an ambulance, the occupant of the now totaled Honda began to pull himself through the driver’s side door, which had come off the frame. Blood obscured his features, various cuts and lacerations were clearly visible on any patch of unclothed skin. He didn't appear to have any broken bones, but had to throw all of this weight onto the car to keep himself standing. This sight alone was enough to cause Chuck to drop his walkie talkie, so he was totally unprepared for what came next. The Laughing. Maniacal shrieks begin to emanate from the man, as he used all his strength to turn around and face the rig. It wasn't the laugh of a sober man, nor the laugh of a man who'd consumed one too many drinks. It was the laugh of a man whose veins had recently been pumped full of opiates, to the point that a severe car accident seemed like an everyday occurrence. Using the sleeve of his leather jacket to wipe the blood from his eyes, he pointed a finger at Chuck Bush, who sat unmoving behind the wheel of his truck. "Holy Shit, man!" he yelled, taking a break to turn his neck and survey all the damage. "LOOK AT THIS! HAHA! FUCK, what were you thinking back there?" It wasn't long before his body took control, and he fell to the pavement unconsious, in a growing pool of his own blood. He didn't come to until the paramedics were loading him into the back of an ambulance. His morphine high had worn off, and he was aware of the pain for the first time. The last thing Hunter Jones saw before he slipped back into blackness was the shaken figure of Chuck Bush, giving his account to a Colorado state trooper. The last thing he heard, were words that both he and Chuck would never be able to purge from their minds. "There was nothing I could do.." *** The Present Saturday, October 7th 2006. 8:53 PM. "Sounds Messy." The voice belonged to Ian Jackson, host of the #2 Morning Radio Show in the greater Los Angeles area. The Power Hour was simply an outlet for those stuck in rush hour traffic to call in and debate about sports on their way to work. This morning's guest? You guessed it. Former wrestling star and current FUSE Wrestling Color Man, "Hardball" Hunter Jones. "From what I can remember, Ian, it was more than messy." He'd only received the phone call requesting he be a guest the previous morning. With GTT6 on the horizon, apparently the stations demographic wanted their wrestling fix; and since this was the #2 show, Hunter is what they delivered. So he'd woken up at 5 AM, and toyed with the bus system until it carried him from his run down apartment in South Central, to the high rise studio in downtown Los Angeles. "So," continued Ian, staring across the studio at Hardball "You were at the top of the circuit, and you suffered a severely torn quad due to some black ice and a redneck Trucker? Gotta Love those Southerners!" "I wouldn't blame it all on him," returned Jones, his glare fixated on the table in front of him rather than his gracious host. It was the first time he'd actually recalled his story to any media, and it made him feel weird. "The fact of the matter is, I had a BIG problem. It started in early 03', when a friend of mine's sister got her hands on a little morphine. She's a nurse over in Inglewood, and thought it’d be fun to try. I guess she was right, because I started using weekly. Then three times a week, then every night before every match. Under normal circumstances I'd have seen the black ice that caused the accident, but I was flying. I didn't have a care in the world, and that's why I blame the accident just as much on me as I do on him." "Whoa! That's a drag, man, total drag. Did you keep using afterwards?" "No," replied Hunter "I suppose you can say the accident was the last event on a long list that flushed my life down the toilet.. I suppose you can say I forced myself to clean up my act.." *** The Past Saturday, April 2nd 2004. 6:31 AM. Nestled into one of the more unsavory areas in South Central Los Angeles sat the newly rented home of Hunter Jones. He'd only taken up residence there a few months prior, having instead used his savings on hotel rooms. After the accident he'd been to see a specialist, who'd confirmed the tear. He'd stressed to Hunter the odds of getting back into the ring would strengthen if he worked his ass off, something he intended to do. Kicking drugs was a lot easier than he thought it'd be. After really sitting down and thinking about all he'd lost, His wife, his daughter, his beautiful home in Malibu. All of that, including 80% of his fortune had been lost in the divorce, a punishment Hunter Supposed, for shooting up in the mens room before the hearing. It wasn't much. The door was swollen, probably due to a rare-yet-severe Southern California thunderstorm. It stuck occasionally, but nothing a good shoulder tackle couldn't fix. The windows were dingy and unkempt, the counters were chipped, and the entire living room was enveloped by brown shag carpeting, though Hunter suspected the original shade was much lighter. None of this bothered him, of course. The Apartment was used only for sleep, he spent the rest of the day outside. He jogged a lot, for hours on end. Worked out at the gym two blocks away, it was no 24 Hour Fitness, but it had equipment, and as long as you were considered a 'regular', membership wasn't needed. He was immersed in his new routine. Wake Up. Eat. Leave House. Work Out Until Dark. Eat. Return to House. Sleep. He'd considered trying to regain custody of his daughter, but having no money for a lawyer, and going from drug-addicted-wrestler to unemployed-broke-social-reject probably wouldn't bode well in court, so he tried to put it in the back of his mind. Losing his family, and his life he realized, was a lot easier to deal with on drugs. However, he had a new addiction. Getting back his wife and daughter, and getting back into the ring. Each of those induced a rush much more powerful than any opiate high. Bar none. *** The Present Saturday, October 7th 2006. 9:03 AM. "Rough, man, Rough. So how long did you keep up with that?" "Not Long," replied Hunter. He'd really hit his stride with the interview, and all the previous butterflies vanished. "A few months maybe, before my savings started to run out. I was down to the bare minimums as it was, but with no income I was forced to look for work. I went for a meeting with my former employer, XoW, the company that would become FUSE years later, and asked if they could set me up with something. Turns out they were looking for a new color man and the position just fit me like a glove." "Excellent, so you're not still living in the dregs?" "Eh, Sort of," he said "I still rent out the same shack in South Central for when I'm in Town, but that's not often. I dropped the strict training when I got back on the road, still did my thing but not nearly as much. I didn't need to, in my head. I'd gotten visitation with my kid whenever I was in town, now that I had a steady job, and I suppose I was content with the life I'd re-built." "And When did you find out wrestling was a possibility again?" "Hmm," he said, thinking out loud. "It was about a month ago. I hadn't visited a Doctor about it in a year and a half, having given up on it, mostly. FUSE Was back in Miami at the time, and I'd heard of an excellent sports doctor. Considering company insurance covered it, I got there really early and decided to check him out.." *** The Past Wednesday, September 10th 2006. 10:01 AM. "Mr. Jones, Do you believe in Miracles?" It was obviously a rhetorical question, and deep inside Hunter Jones was giddy at the words. He, however, hadn't gotten far in life admitting he used adjectives like giddy to describe his emotions, so he kept his casual demeanor going. "Not really." The Doctor walked closer, in his hands some recent X-Ray photos that had been taken an hour ago. The Doctor's face was one of intense shock, even extreme happiness, as he dropped the photos next to Hunter. "Maybe you should start." he said, as Hardball scooped up the images. "What am I looking at exactly?" "What you're looking at is a nearly healthy quad muscle. I studied that, and several other images and I've come to the conclusion that your muscle tissue is healing at an alarming rate." "A-a-are you serious?" stuttered Jones, the information laid before him taking a while to register. "I'm one of the best sports Physicians in the country, Hunter. That's a near healthy muscle, certainly healed enough to compete with. I could clear you for competition right now if you'd like, to prove how serious I am." The moment he'd been waiting three years for had arrived. He couldn't open his mouth to say 'YES' fast enough, and was about to scream it at anyone who'd listen before it hit him. Getting over this initially was hell.. Could it happen again? Suddenly it all came rushing back. The hours of intense physical therapy. The months of walking assisted with a cane. Hunter was smart enough to know an injured quad could easily re-tear.. and he had a good life now. He'd made peace with his ex-wife, even if she'd run off with someone else by now. He had visitation of his daughter. He had a steady job, that paid more than enough to suit his needs. Why risk it? Because it's your passion, dickhead. "Go ahead and sign the slip, Doc," he replied, his mental battle subsiding momentarily. "I've got some things to think about." *** The Present Saturday, October 7th 2006. 9:15 AM. "So What Happened, did you ever get a serious explanation?" "Sort'a," Hunter said, feeling completely at home now. "Something about the initial burst of exercise over a three month period followed by a year or so of rest helped heal the muscle faster. Not a very common thing, but I suppose taking the desk job worked in my favor." "WOW, I bet. So what's this about inhibitions you were having? Those still bothering you?" "No. Not at all," he said, matter-of-factly. "I had a little help with that, though.." *** The Past Wednesday, September 12th 2006. 12:21 PM. "Do you have an appointment?" The Security guard stared down from his magazine to address him, in a very bothered way that indicated stragglers attempted to infiltrate the premises quite often. Hunter Jones stared up at him, with a half smirk on his features. "No." "Then you can't see Mr. Lar-" "Just tell him it's Hardball," said Jones, his half smirk evolving into a full one, knowing he was about to wipe the grin off of Joe Security. "He'll know who I am." Glaring, the Security Guard turned and headed back to his booth. He returned seconds later, and said nothing, but the large gate enclosing the property began to swing open. With a curt nod that screamed 'You just got fucking pwned'', Hunter began his hike up the property, not quite remembering how large it was due to the length of time between visits. Michael "Lars" Larson was a former wrestling champion turned millionaire promoter. Why would he make that jump you ask? Years in the ring had deteriorated his neck in a bad way. He'd be warned to stop, but he'd kept it up, and up, and up until he was as he is now, unable to walk without intense pain. The saddest part? Mike Larson is only 39 years old. "Hunter, you old bastard! I can't believe it's you!" It hurt him to see a man he considered a mentor like this. Probably the reason he stopped visiting in the first place. Even with a cane firmly in hand, he could see Lars wince as he attempted to walk out and greet his old student. Hunter quickly picked up the pace to avert him from walking more than possible, and followed him back into the large mansion. "Have a seat, my man, Have a seat!" bellowed Lars, who appeared happier than he had in ages. "Yolanda! Drinks Please!". Falling back into a leather couch, Jones contemplated how he'd word his insecurities. He'd been able to get over so much in the past three years, but he knew he'd need the advice of one man if he were to solve this particular problem. "So tell me," said Lars, accepting a beer from his housekeeper. "What the hell brings you here?" "That should be obvious," said Hardball, taking the second beer from the tray. "What I come to you for everytime. I need advice". Letting out a deep laugh, Lars took a drink of his beverage. "Lemme Guess, you wanna talk about the Business? You wanna ask me how I deal with retirement?" "Sort'a," replied Hunter. "I suppose I've got a personal question to ask, that you may not like to answer." "Pft, Has that ever stopped you from asking before?" He had a point. "What if, by some stretch of the imagination, you visited a Doctor and he told you that you could start wrestling again?" said Jones, taking a long drink. "Would you do it? Return to a sport that's done.. done this to you..?" Hardball expected some apprehension.. "..In a second," replied Lars, right after Jones finished his sentence. He had no doubts in his voice. "I'd do it in a second. I'd take all the money I've got, all my possessions, and trade them for just one more match." "B-b-But Why?" were the only words Hunter could deliver. He was thoroughly shocked that someone who'd gotten a far more raw deal that he would be so sure of such a serious thing. "What? Why? Has sitting behind that announce table warped you, Brother? Because we're wrestlers. It's what we do." The words may have seemed simple to some, but to Hunter Jones they were very deep, and made a lot of sense. Seeing a near crippled man say that, without a shadow of a doubt, he'd give up everything for One. More. Match.. "That's all I needed to know." said Jones, a relived note in his voice. *** The Present Saturday, October 7th 2006. 9:27 AM. ".. It's about that time of the hour, folks! I'd like to thank our special Guest Hunter Jones for joining us this morning, but I've got one final question before we close." "Sure." "You. The Illustrious Face Eater. GTT. Your Thoughts." "I, unlike a lot of men in my profession, have no problems with Adam Dick. I've seen his work in AWC, I know what he's capable of. I'm not in this tournament to gain fame and fortune like most people, though. I've had all that. I know this is going to sound cliché, but I'm in this for myself. I've got something to prove to ME, and no one else. If I win this whole damn thing, it’d be a damn good way to prove I've still got it, and if I'm ousted from the first round, I've got other consolations to look forward to.." *** Wednesday, October 11th, 2006. 10:52 PM "Daddy!" As he wrapped his arms about his 9 year old daughter for the first time in weeks, every doubt he may have had for dropping his name into the GTT hat vanished. He wasn't concerned with re-injuring his quad, he wasn't concerned about losing his match, He was only concerned with spending as much time as he possibly could with this little lady, because he knew their next visit wouldn't be until after the tournament. He'd love to take her somewhere, but the nasty 'supervised' clause to his visitation squandered all that away. He was a clean man, but the legal system was a game, and he wasn't currently holding all the cards. As he and his daughter walked up the steps of the Malibu estate he realized she was another reason he had to succeed. She was growing up so fast, and not seeing her tore at his soul. Doing well in GTT6 would lead to lucrative contracts, advertisements, the works. Then he'd be able to afford the best lawyer in the country to fight on his side. For too long, the words of Chuck Bush rang in his head, though their meaning had slightly changed to fit the situation. "There was nothing I could do.." "Now there is," he muttered to himself. "Now there's something I can do." With renewed enthusiasm he followed his daughter into the house that he and his wife used to populate. She stood in the corner and smiled at him, not a forced smile, but a genuine one. An 'I Miss you, Even though I act like an Insufferable bitch at times..' smile. Hunter returned it, knowing deep inside he was ready to fight. For himself, for his daughter, and for the family he desperately wanted back. -Fin- website code by: Pete.
