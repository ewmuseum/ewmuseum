Darth von Doom versus Brian Spaes

Character: Brian Spaes Matchup: Darth von Doom Title: Visitation - One Word Count: 995 === A grimy loft in Brighton Beach, Brooklyn, New York inhabited by Brian Spaes. Layers of teal floral print wallpaper peeled off the walls around him. Several holes in the ceiling allowed the rain to drip and hit the floor. Considering the dilapidated state of the loft, Brian kept it Spartan and spotless, just his bed, a dresser and a bed. A stack of library books act as impromptu nightstand. Spaes looked up at the puddles intently. His construction work was dying down for the year, so he found himself with a great amount of spare time. Instead of the exhausting work seven days a week, going to his NA and parole meetings, and then hitting the gym before passing out, his schedule was not wide open. There were only so many times he could go to the gym, or bug his sponsor. Brian Spaes was going nuts. The worst thing for an addict is spare time. Not having anything to do presents them with the opportunity to fall back upon bad habits. And now he was facing a winter full of it. *buzz buzz* *buzz buzz* He rolled over and picked up his cell phone. “ig round robin. nxt monday. 5 matches.” Brian shot up. A smirk started to grow across his weathered face. He stroked his salt and pepper colored beard and he got out of bed. He wouldn’t need to worry about having too much spare time on his hands. Brian grabbed a hair tie and put his long grayed hair into a ponytail. His face brightened drastically, showing some youth in the weathered 31 year old. Spaes now needed to prepare. He grabbed his backpack and moved towards the door. Brian Spaes. New holder of the Infinite Gauntlet. *buzz buzz* *buzz buzz* He looked at the phone again. “brian. visitation tonite w jack. 6. pick up at dr. goulds. –d” “Shit.” His mind couldn’t be on it too much longer. === Dr. Alisa Gould’s office was in stark contrast to how one would typically imagine a Chelsea brownstone. The interior had the design much like an IKEA catalogue, functional, comfortable with an European and futurist touch; the office over looked the street, and with the hustle and bustle of Manhattan. Dr. Gould sat in her blue fabric backed chair and looked alternatively between her yellow legal pad and a red elevated futon. She was in her early forties and considered a prodigy in her field, EMDR therapy to treat childhood trauma. She wore a black “Twiggy” dress and her auburn hair in a pixie style. She spoke with a slight midwestern accent. Jack was a peculiar looking child, a visual cacophony of ethnicities. One could look at him and see Hispanic, Indian, Caucasian, and Native American tendencies. His hair was short and jet black with a part down the left side. The 11 year old’s purple eyes shone brilliantly, especially in contrast with white office. His attire completely conflicted with the couch, a purple polo shirt and plaid cargo shorts. The look on his face was pained as they were engaged in this session. “What’s the image coming to mind Jack? What is it of your father that pains you?” “I walked in on him smoking his pipe. He looked horrible. He didn’t bath for weeks and his skin was this gross oily mess. And the holes…” Alisa peered over on his eyes, darting back and forth in their socket. The violet eyes looked erratic and frantic. She took her Mont Blanc pen and put it over the bridge of his nose. She brought the pen back and forth across his eyes. “Follow the pen in rhythm Jack. Slowly.” “He’s yelling at me, telling me to leave. He’s cursing and throwing the spoons at me. He’s like really strung out. Cursing me.” “Are you at a “one” or a “zero?” “Zero. One of the spoons hit me in the head and I started crying. I’m at a one!” “Okay lets go to your positive cognition...” === Brian stood outside of the office. He looked rather out of place; his dirty jeans, work boots and grey hooded sweatshirt made him look like he was in the wrong borough. That’s because he was. Model types walked down the street in their tight designer threads. Artists wore drab colors in order to cause a “statement.” And there was Brian Spaes, with a boot on the guardrail, waiting for Jack. After a few minutes, Jack left the office. Brian moved away from the rail and looked at him. Jack peered upwards. “You got here quick.” “Yeah, Denise sent me a text. I was surprised I gotta see you tonight.” “You thought the wrestling would keep us away from you.” Brian nodded. “She said that this will probably be one of the last times they’ll let you see me.” “Probably.” The pair walked down the block towards the subway system. Brian’s body held a lot of apprehension where as Jack was cathartically at peace after the EMDR session. “So I’m in some sort of a round robin for a title. Five other guys.” “And that’s going to end well. You’re going to get sick again…” Brian stopped. Jack continued but turned around. “I just got done with therapy dealing with you on drugs. Dad. I’ve seen you insane. I am sorting through it. These other guys have to be as crazy as you are.” “You have no idea, I’ve seen the videos of them,” Brian shook his head, “I don’t think any of them are right in the head.” “Promise me you won’t get sick again Dad. I know you’re finally right in your head, but we’re all scared. Tonight’s gonna be the last time I will see you for a while, but I need to know that this all won’t kill you. I need to know my Dad will be safe. “ All Brian could do for a moment was nod his head. “I promise you.”
