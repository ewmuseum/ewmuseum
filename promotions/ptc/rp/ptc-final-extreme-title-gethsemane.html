Gethsemane As he sits, his girth spreads and spills over, testing the buttons of his crisp white short-sleeved shirt. A third chin is pushed out though his stiff collar, which looks as if its choking him, aided by a blue and grey check tie. Body constricted by his dress code, his face takes an unhealthy beet-red hue, highlighted by a glistening of perspiration. The unhealthy aura is heightened as the souring musk of old smoke oozes from his pores, and there’s a feeling that his steaming black coffee might have something harder than caffeine to keep him perky. His name is Frank Corrigan, and as he leans forward, it appears - through straining shirt buttons - that someone once shot him in the stomach. The bad mood that must’ve put him in appears to have stuck to this very day. “So,” he concludes, voice fierce and gruff like a chainsaw being started up. “What says you?” His family name betrays his Irish ancestry, but his appearance had already given the game away. A Celtic auburn hint to his dry-brush hair comes out in the light, and his complexion is mottled, where a peppering of youthful freckles faded and spread. Sitting across the wonky desk, likely symbolic rather than functional, is Karina Wolfenden. As the exhaled air from his question creeps across towards her, eyes begin to water at the repulsive concoction of smoke, coffee, liquor, and mouthwash. Dishevelled hair, dry, cracking lips, and bags under her eyes make it clear that the K-Wolf isn’t exactly feeing tip-top. As the scent of Corrigan violates her nostrils, she has to swallow hard in retaliation to the sickly taste leaking up into her arid mouth. “I say it’s the single stupidest thing I’ve ever heard,” she snaps back, then pauses for just a second to add a little emphasis. “And I’ve been in wrestling for 4 years.” _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ Sadly, the stupidest thing I’ve ever heard is tied in with one of the stupidest things I’ve ever done… _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ It was a full-body hangover, not an extremity spared of the queasy lethargy. Her furry tongue was Velcro’d to the pit of her mouth, while dehydration had almost plastered her eyelids shut with sleep. “Unghhhhhh,” went her weak groan, fuzzy head buzzing as she screws her face up. Blindly reaching out, a bitten-nail hand hunts down a glass of water on her bedside table. After a couple of grabs snag only air, she drops her hand down to search along the surface of the table… only for her arm to limply drop. “Uhng?” a curious cave-girl grunt ponders. Stretching down, her fingers touch cold concrete, sending a shiver up her arm that hastens the laboured awakening process. Going to drop her face back into a nest of pillows, there’s instead a yelp as he nose is sandwiched between her head and a wafer-then mattress that’s more akin to a gym-mat. The metallic clunk of a heavy lock turning then splits her skull in two. “Rise and shine, Princess.” Slowly crawling out of slumber and towards the light, Karina lets out a yawn that pulses through her entire body, and feels as if it wants to burst out through her temples to escape. A merest hint of light feels like it’s burning her eyes out of their sockets. She feels prickly. “Seems you’re not as lively as you were last night,” the same gravely voice remarks. With every throb of her head, a little bit of the past 12 hours drops back into place, like a game of Tetris. Only without the catchy tune. She remembers alcohol, vast quantities thereof. She remembers yelling above the deafening volume of the music. She remembers… “Oh shiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiit…” she remembers. In the doorway stands Corrigan, grey suit-jacket just about able to button-up across his stomach. A steaming polystyrene cup of black coffee in his hand, and hipflask protruding just out of his jacket pocket, he watches Wolfenden recall the events, teeth mauling a stick of gum. “Yeah, I heard that’s what he said when you broke his wrist,” Frank divulges, confirming the K-Wolf’s worst fears. “Amongst other things, most of them not to complimentary of you, I‘d guess.” It’s not so much the primal wail he let out as the damage was done that gives her a feeling colder than the concrete floor her bare feet swing down on to, more the badge that he produced when the doormen arrived. Peeling her hands off her face, running them through messy, multi-coloured hair Wolfenden looks across the cell to the suited man. “Anyone think of asking how his hand got close enough to me that I could break his wrist?” Karina shoots back, despite knowing that it was, for all intents and purposes, rhetorical. A young woman who knew how to look after herself inflicting some kind of injury on Marc Hellier hadn’t exactly surprised many of his fellow officers. While they’d be chatting about the previous day’s family softball picnic, or how their children were doing in school, Marc’s conversational input was more of the hedonistic variety. His colleagues would have had a problem with it if they didn’t all envy him when they went back home to a frazzled wife and demanding kids. They didn’t, however, envy him when his hands wandered onto the sleek hind-quarters of a young woman whose already short fuse had been slowly seeped in alcohol to make it burn all that quicker. “Yeah, I guess not,” Wolfenden scoffs, before rising to stretch her streamlined six foot frame, trying to purge some of the sluggishness out through her fingertips. “Just you police sticking together, again.” After knocking back the gritty remains at the bottom of his coffee, Corrigan crumples the polystyrene and tosses it into the corner of her cell. “I think you‘re a little mistaken, Miss Wolfenden,” Frank says, before breaking into enough of a smile to show his teeth, stained mustard-yellow.. “I’m not with the police.” _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ I’m under no illusions that the majority of wrestlers who’ve met me, hate me. I seem to have that effect on people. Not really caring about anything they do, being about as reliable as a watch with a dead battery, and having the boredom threshold of a hyper-active goldfish doesn’t really help matters either. So, I’m basically using that to justify what I’m about to do. It’s not like I’ll lose many friends over it. That, and who’s gonna find out? _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ Still donning the side-slashed dress and shorts combo she’d been wearing the night before, a blatant homage to Resident Evil’s Alice, a hung-over Karina Wolfenden sits slumped in a hard, plastic chair. Across the table from her, looking out at the view across the park, is Frank Corrigan. Peeking back over his shoulder, he catches sight of Karina craning her neck to get a look at the outside world, then responds by dropping the blinds down. Folding a stick of gum into his mouth, Frank finally turns to acknowledge the K-Wolf, dropping a stack of files onto his side of the desk, almost sending his coffee flying. “You’ll be pleased to hear that I had a little chat over breakfast with Officer Hellier and his superiors,” reveals Corrigan, resting his ample exterior on the top of the radiator. “And they’ve agreed that no action should be taken through official channels over what was, let’s face it, a little misunderstanding.” “As in nobody understood that he wasn’t house-trained?” the ever-jaded Wolfenden fires back, unrepentant even when exonerated. “Now, now, Miss Wolfenden,” Corrigan gently reprimands, wagging a finger like he was a headmaster ticking off a sulking pupil. “When I said I had a little chat, I meant it took me half an hour to worm you out of it. That’s half an hour earlier that I had to get here, which was half an hour less I could spend with my wife on our anniversary.” “So this was all out of the kindness of your own heart, I assume?” Wolfenden prods, cynicism dripping off her tongue. “I’m from the school of one good turn deserves another,” Corrigan replies, pushing his girth off the radiator. Returning to the desk, Frank shuffles the sack of folders into order, grabbing the top one. “What can you tell me about Adam Dick?” Corrigan then asks, retrieving a blown-up publicity shot from his folder. “You might also know him as the Illustrious Face-Eater.” Holding the photo up to the K-Wolf, Karina is met with the masked visage of Facey sticking his tongue out at her, while shooing out duelling gangsta-hands. “He’s in idiot,” is her blunt opinion of PTC’s resident insano-stoner. “He also blew up,” Corrigan dryly states, “and a Mr Danny Ferguson was arrested for his murder… except it turns out it was really a robot - codenamed a ‘Faceybot’ - that exploded.” Making his disgruntled point by dropping the folder down a good 4 feet onto the desk, he swipes the next one off the top of the pile. “What can you tell me about Angelo Deville?” is then asked of Wolfenden as Corrigan places the Cadillac of Wrestling’s >=) over Adam Dick’s moronic face. “He’s a c-u-n-t,” is her stark assessment. “Cats also hiss at him a lot.” “Am I right in believing that you once had, how shall I put this, a relationship with Mr Deville?” Corrigan presses, not reacting to her crude language. “At any point did you not suspect that wrestling alone couldn’t support his lifestyle? Were you aware of any outside income?” “Dunno,” Karina shrugs, Angelo not exactly being a topic of conversation she’s fond of. “Try looking for an E-Bay member called angelodeville1337.” “You see, Miss Wolfenden,” Corrigan tuts as he whips the photos back away from Karina, “I don’t think you’re ‘getting’ the whole one good turn deserves another philosophy.” “And I don’t think you’re getting to the point,” the hung-over K-Wolf bites back. “Then I’ll dispense with the ‘background’, Miss Wolfenden,” Frank Corrigan doesn’t seem too discouraged in stating. Symbolically, he swipes the files off the desk, starting on a fresh canvas. “For too long, wrestling as been a law unto itself,” he seethes. “You have guys faking their own deaths, ex-convicts among the biggest names in the industry without anyone blinking an eye, secretive cults, people concealing their true identities, blatant drug abuse, suspect pharmaceutical companies taking over promotions, and enough rumours of supernatural activity to keep any Fox Mulder wannabes occupied for the next 5 years.” “Blame the writers,” Karina mumbles, hushed. “And what door is always slammed in our face when we try to get answers?” Corrigan’s rant continues, frustration brewing in the gravel-quarry his voice resonates from. “The door with ‘it’s just TV’ written on it.” He keeps his gaze resting upon Karina, wanting to press his point home. “Bottom line is, Miss Wolfenden, that the line between fantasy and reality has become too blurred,” he claims, hands pressing down on the edge of the table. “But now, fate would have it, a star in one of the biggest promotions in the world is sat down not 6 feet away from me, and finds herself in my debt.” “There isn’t a chance you have Lindsay Troy sitting right behind me, is there?” Karina asks in futile hope. “That would be a no,” Corrigan dismisses, tiring just a little of her understandably grouchy attitude. Feeling his eyes again linger on her, and with the temperature of the room not ideal for her dress, a self-conscious K-Wolf crosses her arms across her chest, tucking her shoulders in. “But, all you have to do to repay the kind deed I did for you this morning is to be my eyes and ears,” Corrigan finally divulges, backing away from his side of the table to grab a chair. “It’s time for a little regulation, and you’ll be the woman who helps me achieve it.” “Super,” Kari remarks, sarcasm oozing from the word. “As if everyone else there didn’t hate me enough. Oh, but FYI, you do realise that I’m not exactly part of ‘the inner circle’ anymore, right? I’m pretty much Ann Coulter at an anti-war/gay-pride mash-up.” “Then you’ll just have to socialise a little more then, won’t you,” Corrigan dismisses her negatives with, stamping the chair’s leg’s down to add an exclamation point to his words. “I also think it would be in your interests, therefore mine too, if you become a little more active in the wrestling world… broaden your horizons, open a few more doors.” “I dunno, “ the K-Wolf wearily sighs, “sounds like… effort?” “A scary prospect, I’m sure,” Frank dryly replies. “But, turns out my son happens to like the insult to real sport that you portray, something he’ll hopefully grow out of, and it turns out you have an ideal chance to make quite the name for yourself. Not to mention, help our cause a little, right off the bat.” A printed web-page advertising a PTC Extreme Title match is placed neatly in front of her on the desk, her own name on the invitees list highlighted in blue. Finally he sits down opposite her, leaning forward over the print-out. “So,” he concludes, voice fierce and gruff like a chainsaw being started up. “What says you?” “I say it’s the single stupidest thing I’ve ever heard,” she snaps back, then pauses or just a second to add a little emphasis. “And I’ve been in wrestling for 4 years.” “So that’s a yes, then?” Frank imposes, knowing she‘ll take the self-preservation option. “That’s a yes.” _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ Oh, shut up. We stab each other in the backs every day, it’s the nature of the business. Or are you the guy who’ll still be talking to all the people he worked with now in 25 years time? We’ll be forgotten when we’re gone, and we’ll do our best to forget it. _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ As he emerges from the makeshift interview room, Frank Corrigan displays no sense of victory, seeing no need to celebrate a business-like formality. “Give her ten minutes, “ he instructs the officer standing sentry outside as he squeezes back into his jacket, almost un-tucking his shirt in doing so, “then release her.” Thunderous footsteps lead him away from the room, until a voice calls out and halts his bull-charge. “So, did it work?” Striding over to him is a man in his twenties, a day’s stubble adorning his chin, but more noticeably, a cast on his right wrist. With his left hand, he shakes that of Corrigan. “Like a charm,” Frank replies. “Good work, officer.”
