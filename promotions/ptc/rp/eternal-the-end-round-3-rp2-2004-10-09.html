Date: 2004-10-09 Note: I’ve cut my chapter system, but this is still a continuation of the series that I’ve started since Round 1. Just a note to readers and judges. Eternal: The End <<<<<<<<>>>>>>>> Imagination. Some might argue that it is what keeps us going forward, chasing our dreams. Imagining of what may become of our future. Driving us towards a successful winding road that leads to our very end. But what if we had imagined of what had been our past? Think about it. What if one day you look back at your past and realize that everything that you thought had happened, didn’t actually take place? That it was all a figment of your own imagination, and that the very one mistake that you made was believing your own delusional mind. And then we come to further questions. Do we really want to know the truth? Will our mind be able to accept the factual events of our history, back to the very years of childhood? Are we capable to accept the fact that everything had been a lie? That all along, we had searched for something without actually knowing the foundations of our lives. It had always been about choices, making decisions based on our emotional strengths. But sometimes, we don’t have the liberty of making choices. Some decisions are simply made for you. That whether you like it or not, you just have to embrace the truth in one shock-like moment. That is where I stand now, at the crossroads between fact and imagination, with no clear time to actually choose my way. It had always been about time, but until now I forever had plenty of it with more to spare. Like I said just then, some decisions are just not there for you to make a choice. This time I had been shoved in a direction that I formerly would not even look at. My hand had been forced into gracing the very road in which I fear. Yes, I fear. You heard that right, and this is the first. My primary fear had finally been unveiled, revealed before my very eyes. My past. <<<<<<<<>>>>>>>> Location: Unknown Timeline: About ten years ago It was an unusual day at the only jetty on the island, with a crowd gathering slowly but surely around the latest boat that arrived there. To most, they had it in their minds earlier that it just contained tourists, wanting to view the beautiful scenery of the place. The residents were proud of that, but at the same time, they weren’t overly joyous at the amounts of strange people coming in and out of the island daily, tainting the environment with their inconsiderate behavior. But some of them noticed something strange on this particular boat. The deck was painted an amazing blood red, painted by someone lying half-conscious with a girl crying over him. “Kasper, wake up…” Athena pleaded in a soft voice, the tears flowing down to her mouth causing the words to blur slightly. Kasper mumbled something, but it wasn’t understandable to her ears. He winced, trying to move his shoulder. Athena noticed that blood was still pouring out slowly out of the wound on his chest, even though it had been like half an hour ago that he was shot. Most others would have died by now due to a lost of blood. Athena looked around at the gathering crowd around her, with whispers floating around between them. She stared at them desperately, not knowing what she should do right now. “What happened?” Kasper muttered softly, his words just slightly audible. He had great difficulty in trying to get the words out of his mouth, his mind in a weakening state that he couldn’t remember what had happened just before. Athena looked down at him, relieved that he was still able to speak. “Nothing Kasper, you’ll be fine. I promise.” Athena replied, urging him to stay strong and not to lose himself. She grabbed his hand, and held it tight. She couldn’t see the paleness of his hand when she did that, as both of their hands were covered in red. She could Kasper trying to keep himself awake, but he was slowly drifting off to a state of unconsciousness. “Someone call the ambulance!” Athena screamed at the crowd, as she collapsed on Kasper’s broken body, sobbing. As he slowly was reduced to an unconscious state, the last he heard was the blaring siren of the ambulance. Yet to him at that time, it was just a noise in the large never-ending black background. Three years later “How’s your friend’s condition?” Jonathan spoke in a concerned voice, peering at Athena’s face. She remained calm, as she stared across the ocean, her legs crossed on the fine-grained sand. It was a beautiful day at the beach, one of the various tourist attractions on this small island. But yet Athena’s mind was troubled beyond all those on that piece of land, wandering from time to time at what had happened three years ago. “As usual. He hasn’t gained consciousness for three years already.” She replied shortly, her face grim, not wanting to even look at Jonathan. He looked away, knowing that he had touched a nerve. Athena had always been this way ever since he met her on this very same beach about two years back. The same forlorn look, sitting in her usual spot every evening, thinking of her past. She was joyous at times, but her sadness overshadowed that. Jonathan sighed, and looked down, running his right hand through the sand. “The doctors still don’t know how long he will be in a coma. Some of them said that it was due to his strained mind, of its apparent lack of ability in trying to grasp at what had happened. He lost a lot of blood on that day, but his body condition now is fine. It’s only the mental stability that’s worrying the doctors.” Athena covered her face with her hands, closing her eyes and darkened her world. Three years had been a long wait, and along with the mental trauma at came with it, she was at the edge of a nervous breakdown. A sound interrupted her thoughts, as she opened her eyes and slipped her left hand into her pocket. Her mobile phone was ringing, surprising her slightly. She hadn’t been receiving too many calls lately, and never at this hour. “Hello?” she spoke in a hesitant tone, putting the phone to her ear. There were a few minutes of silence as the person on the other line spoke. Athena nodded her head from time to time, and she beamed as she canceled the call. “Kasper is awake.” Athena looked at Jonathan with a slight glint in her eyes. <<<<<<<<>>>>>>>> Present Day/Time “For the next few years after that, you remembered nothing of your past. Not even the bit where you got shot at.” Athena said in a forlorn voice, glancing at Kasper. He had been keeping quiet for last couple of minutes, listening to Athena telling him about what had happened after the bullet pierced through his chest. And for a moment, he couldn’t believe his ears, so much so that he forgot all about asking Krystal about the man that shot him. Athena had interrupted the conversation in a sudden fashion, trying to take Kasper’s mind off wanting the answer to the identity of the man. And by the looks of it, it had worked. “What happened after that?” Kasper spoke with an urgency, trying to feed everything into his head, wanting to recover more memories of his past. Athena hesitated, looking at Krystal, before continuing. “You forgot all about what had happened in Athens, and also your memories before Athens seemed to have disappeared. You know nothing of your past, and then you started getting memories.” “With the amount of frustration available in your mind, you started believing those memories, thinking that you found me on the streets of Athens, crying in the rain.” Athena whispered in a sad voice, clasping her hands together. “That never happened, Kasper.” Kasper looked down at the ground, his face relaxing. His world had just caved in on him, his world of lies and imaginations. “Only one memory remained, her sister. You sought to find your sister again, but to no avail. I couldn’t give you any form of information, with the doctors advising that you were on the verge of a mental breakdown.” Athena murmured, trying to clear Kasper’s mind. “And that was when I turned to wrestling,” Kasper declared with a sigh, suddenly able to fit that piece in the big picture of the puzzle. “I subconsciously remembered my wrestling training with Christopher, and went on taking out my frustrations in the wrestling ring.” Athena winced at the sound of the name of her father, clearly still distraught at what had happened back then, even though that was roughly ten years ago. “You knew who my sister was,” Kasper looked up at Athena, finally realizing it. “You knew it all along, even when I was fervently searching for her, up to the point where I thought that all hope had been lost.” “Yes.” Athena’s reply was short, but it was enough for Kasper to flinch. He finally understood now. He knew now why Athena never really wanted to talk to him about his sister. “What happened before Athens?” he questioned, turning to look at Krystal. “Are you telling me that everything that I had believed about my childhood and my life had all been a lie?” He dreaded the answer to his question, but he forced himself to look at Krystal in her eyes. “You’ll find out with time, Kasper. And I can promise you that it will be soon.” Krystal whispered, her voice clear and sure. <<<<<<<<>>>>>>>> My past. That was why I turned to wrestling. I realized that now. I used a form of art that Christopher taught me, and used it to satisfy my frustrations. I wrestled to try and forget my past, and look towards the future. But I guess sooner or later the past will always catch up to you. Isn’t it ironic that I killed the one person who taught me how to wrestle? Well, it is to me. Christopher taught me wrestling, and gave me a medium to relieve my mind of my past and its strains, and I gave him back something in return. I gave him Death as a gift. A token of my appreciation. A show of respect, an undying love for my wrestling mentor. To you, it is something that I should regret. You think I should feel guilty, and carry the weight of my sins on my eternal shoulders. That I have to feel sorry for the murder of someone who gave me the latter part of my life. The very same moves that I had used to overcome every opponent including those whom I had defeated in the GTT4, had been taught and perfected by the man who died by my very hands. You say I should burn in hell for my sins. That it is just wrong for me to use the moves of the person that I killed, in order to beat someone like Larry the Legionnaire. You think I should step down from wrestling and let those who have pure souls to advance. You think I should lose. I think that you are wrong. I say that I will never stand in defeat, that I will overcome all those who stand in my way by using the very same moves taught by the person that I killed. I say that I will defeat Larry the Legionnaire in clear conscience, with a wide smile on my face with the sins on my shoulders swallowed by my soul. You really think that I’m going to feel guilt and conscience for the murder of a wrestling mentor ten years ago? Then you clearly don’t know me well enough. With my damning soul, I will destroy Larry the Legionnaire. Hate me for it if you wish, and then see if I actually care. You want to see the constraints of my splendor destroyed, wanting to experience what it is like to view my end. But what you will see in return will the rising of my grandeur, with every urge of my damning soul. You want me to lose because you hate me. You curse me for what I have done. I guess your wish just isn’t possible. I’m sorry to disappoint you, but you will be joining Christopher before you see me drown in my own conquest. You see, I don’t plan on losing to someone like Larry the Legionnaire. That just isn’t a possibility. Far from it. His destruction will be my beginning. This world’s death will be my end. <<<<<<<<>>>>>>>> The sun had set beyond the hills, enveloping the world in total darkness except for the dim moonlight trying to illuminate the eternal surroundings. But yet, Kasper didn’t seem to notice. Standing in front of the door of where his mother passed away, he looked at it from the ground up, not wanting to walk in. His world where he built his past upon, was slowly disintegrating. Dissolving itself into various lies of his life, concealed by his very own mind. “Why can’t you tell me what happened in the past? What is there to hide?” Kasper went on, urging Krystal to reveal the truth, a part of him wanting the jigsaw to be complete. “Everything is there to be hidden. You have to find the answers yourself, Kasper.” “But why?” “Your mind betrayed your past. Everything you believed had been a lie. Every single thing.” She continued cautiously, threading the fine line of truth. “What about mother? Was she a lie as well? That she didn’t die when I was ten, crying by her bedside?” Kasper spoke in a harsh voice. Everything about that particular memory seemed so real, that he had been caught up in his emotions every time he thought about it. “Mother did die…” Krystal replied. Kasper heaved a sigh of relief, thinking that at least he had been right about something. “But not the way you thought it had happened,” she whispered, continuing her sentence. He jerked his head up; his face grew pale with shock. “What do you mean? How did she die?” he shouted, taking a step closer to Krystal, his tone frightening both Athena and Krystal. “Answers are never the result of simple questions.” Krystal looked away, starting to walk in the opposite direction from where Kasper was standing. Athena hesitantly followed. She then stopped, dead in her tracks. Just a dark silhouette in the shadows of the night. “She was murdered, Kasper.” She turned her head slightly, glancing at Kasper from the corner of her eye, the blue in it gleaming wickedly. “Murdered?” he repeated incredulously. “By who? It was the man that was with you that day in Athens, wasn’t it? He murdered mother didn’t he?” he screamed, his mind apparently not being able to take that piece of fact from Krystal. Krystal kept quiet, still observing Kasper from the corner of her eye. He couldn’t see her expression from where he was standing, but could imagine her calmness as opposed to his hysterical behavior. “Who was that man? Answer me.” Kasper whispered just loud enough for Krystal to hear. He had subsided, waiting intently for her answer to his question. And then she said one word. A word that caused his knees to go weak as he kneeled on the grass, looking up to the heavens. One word that stayed in Kasper’s mind for those few seconds, like a thunderbolt running through his whole body. Freezing him from everything else, causing him to ignore everything else around him. “Father.” TO BE CONTINUED
