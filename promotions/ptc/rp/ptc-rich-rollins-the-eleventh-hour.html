PTC — Roleplay

The Eleventh Hour

6 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Corner of Jefferson and Malamuke, North Chicago Monday, June 6th 9:37pm Two hours and twenty-three minutes until Infinite Gauntlet Event [Between the intrusive blue-and-red glare of the police strobes and its accompanying screech, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins could almost forget about the uncomfortable clutch of the handcuffs around his wrists. Or the smell. The backseat of a police cab always smelled like dried urine with a healthy helping of regurgitated liquor thrown in just for texture.] [Rollins wouldn’t mind the frenzied mob of officers and reporters either if the handcuffs didn’t chafe so goddamn much.] [Cramped alone in the back of a police cruiser, Rollins tries to forget his discomfort as another sudden outburst of flashbulbs invades his peace only to be swatted away by ticker-tape patrolmen and newly recruited deputies. Everyone looks so stunningly animated; indeed, this was more excitement than this quiet little suburban neighborhood had probably seen in years.] [Every picket fence freshly painted. Every lawn kept immaculately manicured. Every hundred yards housed with another bright red fire hydrant. Everything the very definition of perfection according to the Gospel of Martha Stuart.] [Aside, of course, from the mangled body of Vincent McArthur enshrined in brilliant white chalk.] It’s the little details, The Renegade absently muses to himself. [Sitting as a disturbing centerpiece in the middle of the small neighborhood intersection, Vincent McArthur’s corpse does nothing but blankly watch the fading Chicago sunset as a sea of frantic activity swarms all over his final resting place. Unaware of the legion of official eyes bearing down upon it, McArthur’s remains merely continue their unannounced transition into oblivion with cold indifference. The cold indifference of the dead.] [Tearing his eyes away from the body, Rich Rollins wonders just how this unfortunate scenario is going to affect his ability to compete in the Infinite Gauntlet.] [He glances at the dashboard clock on the other side of the security screen.] 9:38 Fuck. ~<>~<>~<>~ The Gaslight Tavern, South Chicago Sunday, June 5th 11:13pm Twenty-four hours and forty-seven minutes until Infinite Gauntlet Event “… you won WHAT?!” [Hardly able to contain himself, Vincent McArthur frantically tries to organize his own thoughts as they spill out of his mouth.] Vincent: I didn’t win anything, Bianca. He gave it to me, no strings attached. Hank’ll fuckin’ tell you, he was there! Hank, tell her I’m not full of shit. [Cleaning out the sudsy interior of a beer mug, the hefty bartender slowly shakes his head.] Hank: It was the damndest thing I’ve ever seen, no question. Seven hundred and sixt-... Vincent: -sixty-three thousand dollars, to the penny. He slid it over to me in this huge Mafioso briefcase, smooth as silk. Didn’t even break stride as he got up and walked out, serious as the weather. Bianca: Jesus, did he say anything to you? Vincent: (chuckles) Yeah, “Keep the change, kid.” The guy must have lost a screw or two, if you know what I’m saying… but by no means am I complaining! [A delighted giggle erupts from Bianca’s cherry red lips before she can restrain it.] Bianca: So what did you do with it? Did you put it all in the bank? Vincent: Fuck no I didn’t, are you crazy? How am I supposed to explain three quarters of a million dollars appearing on my account come tax season? Do I tell the IRS it was an anonymous gift from some relative in Switzerland? Get real. [Suddenly realizing that he was nearly shouting, Vincent casually glances over his shoulder and peers at the handful of bottomed-out customers spread throughout the dim tavern. When it becomes apparent that the only interested sets of ears in their vicinity belong to Bianca and Hank, Vincent turns his attention back to the wonderfully curvy brown-eyed girl sitting next to him.] Vincent: You know what this means, don’t you? Bianca: (smiling) A summer in Tahiti? Vincent: How about a honeymoon in Tahiti? [Those big brown eyes instantly begin to mist over as a genuine excitement fills her breathless reply.] Bianca: Really…? You’ll finally set a date? Vincent: No sweetheart, we’ll set a date. We don’t have to worry about anything, not ever. Hank: (chuckling) Kids… [The jovial old bartender finds himself something to do as the couple-to-be share a tender moment in one another’s embrace. Alone in their own little corner of reality, Vincent and the future Bianca McArthur stare hopelessly into the other’s puppy dog gaze, neither of them daring to shatter the precious moment.] Vincent: I gotta take a piss. [Well, almost anyway.] [Giving her fiancé a playful slap on the ass, Bianca giggles like a schoolgirl.] Bianca: Your porcelain chariot awaits, dear. [Politely excusing himself, Vincent leaves Bianca at the bar and makes his way to the rear of the near-empty room. A handful of odd customers - mostly bombed-out hipsters and the occasional regular - scatter the stuffy beer hall like toys in a child’s playroom, everyone else perfectly content to mind their own business. Himself completely sick with glee, Vincent tosses a long haired drunk near the restroom entrance a huge grin as he pushes the men’s room door open.] [More well-kept than the average tavern restroom, the pungent odor of lemon cleaning solution assaults Vincent’s nostrils as he makes his way to the first of three toilet stalls, closes the door, plops down the seat, and begins to do his business.] [With his knees nearly touching the stall door, Vincent can’t help but notice the single message scrawled into the metal surface: ] I love Jesus [Then, written below in a different handwriting: ] I love him long time [Despite himself, Vincent emits a stunted chuckle as he sadly shakes his head. Four minutes later, he buttons his blue jeans back up and flushes the toilet, ready to leave the stall and its lewd graffiti for the next resident to enjoy.] Vincent: Wa-what the fuck?! [His eyes widening in surprise, Vincent almost teeters back into the stall door as the unmistakable costumed figure of a man comes into his view.] Vincent: Who the… what the fuck are you supposed to be? [A silly question, considering it was incredibly obvious.] [Standing before Vincent, swathed in the traditional black robes of the Law, is a Judge.] [His heart now firmly lodged between his ears, Vincent blinks repeatedly, trying to shoo away what was undoubtedly a drunken hallucination. Anyone could imagine a six-foot tall man dressed in thin vinyl; indeed, the man blocking Vincent’s escape seems almost comical, his face nothing more than a blank plastic novelty store Halloween mask. Tightly wound in laddering white curls dangling past his padded shoulders, the Judge’s wig screams with a hidden Wal-Mart tag.] [And it would have all been so funny too, if Vincent had been drinking.] Judge: You blatantly broke a code, Vincent. A morality code. [The menacing baritone voice sends a shiver straight down to Vincent’s soles. His jaw works itself in systematic circles as his brain frantically tries to catch up, desperate to filter fiction from reality.] Judge: Did you tell her, Vincent? Did you tell her what you almost agreed to do for that money? [Without warning, Vincent’s heart plummets into his heels.] [Lord no, he never told Bianca. Some steroid-induced wrestler named Rollins got drunk and then generous, end of story.] [But why didn’t he tell her the rest?] Judge: What do you have to be ashamed of, Vincent? Only the Guilty feel ashamed. [What was he ashamed of?] Judge: You would have murdered an innocent woman for a sack full of cash, Vincent. [No.] Judge: Lying to yourself is the worst kind of betrayal, Vincent. [No.] Judge: Vincent. Vincent: NO! [Finally coaxing his brain into forward motion, Vincent sucks in a great gasp of air as a sharp snap from the Judge gets his attention. Folding his gloved fist back into the folds of his robe, the Judge continues in a lulling monotone that both soothes and scares him. Through a stark oasis of flimsy plastic where a face should be, the unruffled voice beyond rings with that much more finality.] Judge: Does the address 1-1-8-7 Malamuke ring any bells, Vincent? [And once more, his heart does hoola-hoops in his stomach.] [Bianca’s address.] Judge: I thought so. If you’d like to keep your moral anominity, I suggest that you pack up your new bankroll and take it with you to your better half’s home at precisely 8:30 tomorrow evening. Consider this a subpoena, Vincent… you will be Judged, or found Guilty of the of the basic humanitarian Laws you would so quickly throw away. [Whetting his lips in a last ditch effort to form something of coherence, Vincent sputters a response.] Vincent: How will I find you? [The blank face answers.] Judge: The Law will find you. [And with that, the room plunges into complete blackness, the silence broken only by Vincent’s stuttered breathing.] [After standing in place for what seems like an eternity, Vincent McArthur bolts out of the restroom’s empty void. Startled, Bianca rises to her feet as her sweat-soaked husband-to-be rushes into the room. Without so much as acknowledging Hank’s quizzical look, Vincent grabs Bianca’s arm and ushers her towards the exit.] Bianca: Honey, where are we going? Vincent: Tahiti. ~<>~<>~<>~ Corner of Jefferson and Malamuke, North Chicago Monday, June 6th 9:45pm Two hours and fifteen minutes until Infinite Gauntlet Event [It had taken the initial squad a good ten minutes, but they had finally managed to collect the majority of loose hundred-dollar bills around the crime scene. Since the reporters had gone out Live! to their dedicated viewers, a local mob had gathered around the police barricade, desperate to pick up the scraps that the cops hadn’t already pocketed.] [Through his own little private window, Rich Rollins watches the world descend into complete anarchy.] SLAM~! Bianca: You murderer! You fucking corward sonofab- [Somehow breaking away from her official entourage, Bianca slams her fists into Rollins bulletproof window three more times, spewing obscenities more colorful than anything even The Renegade could summon up. A burly pair of officers finally peel her away, kicking and screaming.] [The Renegade doesn’t even flinch.] [For the briefest of seconds the bustling mass of humanity parts, allowing a glimpse of one man Rich Rollins definitely doesn’t want to see.] [Steven Caldera.] Never one to miss a good spotlight, he thinks dryly. Officer: Did you say somethin’? [Startled, Rollins glances into the front seat of the police cab, surprised to see that a uniformed officer now sits behind the wheel. He hadn’t even noticed the door open.] Renegade: Excuse me? Officer: Just now, you were talkin’ to yourself. You crazy sonofabitch. [Confused, Rollins’ eyes furrow into a deep frown as he peers closer at the man sitting behind the wheel. Grinning like a mad dog, the olive-skinned Italian sitting before him throws a little wink through the rearview mirror.] Officer: Betcha didn’t expect to see a goomba like me, didja? Renegade: …well I’ll be damned. Ciccio, is that you? [Sticking the keys into the ignition, the “officer” bellows out a scratchy laugh as he kicks the cruiser into drive, waving off at a deputy standing guard duty at the ticker-tape line.] Ciccio: The one and only. Consider this a favor, Rollins. The boss wanted me to tell you that. [Dispensing with the pleasantries, The Renegade cuts through the ego-stroking bullshit.] Renegade: How the hell did you get here so fast? Ciccio: We picked up the call on a police scanner, and Don Dante sent me as soon as he got word. We’ve got this end of town in our back pocket, kid… there are more dirty cops on our payroll than we have members of the Family. And we got a big Family. [Rollins snorts.] [The abrasive glare of the police strobes slowly begin to fade into the evening sky as they pull away from the crime scene, winding out of the picturesque residential suburbia and back into the industrial abyss that is Chicago. His eyes still adjusting to the twilight, Rollins watches the back of Ciccio’s head as the Italian muscle maneuvers into the ebb and flow of downtown traffic.] [He glances at the clock.] 9:53 Ciccio: So, what the hell did that guy do to piss you off, Rollins? I mean Jesus, he was-… Renegade: I didn’t kill him, Ciccio. I’m being set up. [With a matter-of-fact scowl sitting squarely on his unshaven features, The Renegade absently stares out at the passing city, his mind decidedly elsewhere.] Renegade: I received a letter. Ciccio: A letter? Renegade: It was taped up in a white envelope on my door last night, and gave me the intersection address. Told me to be there at exactly 9:00, and that it was “worth seven hundred and sixty-three thousand dollars.” Some screwball set me up, and they’re damn sure gonna regret it. [Keeping his eyes on the road, Ciccio merely shrugs.] Ciccio: Tell it to the boss, Rollins. He wants to have a little chat with you. [With no option other than to be led like a horse by the bit, Rich Rollins shifts uncomfortably in the backseat of the cruiser, his fingers going numb from the handcuff’s merciless grip.] ~<>~<>~<>~ 1187 Malamuke, North Chicago Monday, June 6th 8:52pm Three hours and eight minutes until Infinite Gauntlet Event “The Law will find you.” [Haunting words.] [Words that no matter how hard he tries, Vincent McArthur can’t erase them from his mind.] [Sitting alone in his fiancé’s living room, Vincent tosses a surreptitious glance at the VCR clock and curses under his breath. The black briefcase, its payload of unmarked bills nothing short of a poor man’s wet dream, sits next to him on the couch. He had sent Bianca to the supermarket for a random assortment of things he didn’t really need, but that was just fine by him.] [No matter what happened, he did not want her to be there.] [The television began telling him who the next American Idol should be, but Vincent couldn’t care less.] Ding dong... [His heart skips a beat as Bianca’s corny doorbell begins to play its merry little tune, heralding the arrival of… who? Terrified of the answer, Vincent listens to the cheap rendition of “Ding Dong the Witch is Dead” until it plays itself out, leaving nothing but an oak door between him and his uncertainty.] [Clutching the briefcase and climbing to his feet, Vincent forces his legs to cooperate as he makes his way, step by step, towards the ominous frame of the front door. His pulse racing, he reaches for the knob… and pauses.] Ding dong... Vincent: Damnit! [He grabs the knob and gives it a violent jerk, swinging the door wide open.] [As the overly-bright bell tone of “Ding Dong the Witch is Dead” echoes throughout the house again, Vincent is left standing in the front hallway staring outside at an empty porch.] Vincent: Mother fucker! [Engulfed in a sudden wave of equal parts shame and fury, Vincent hefts the briefcase as he stalks out onto the front porch, his vision blurred with adrenaline. Not a soul is around to be seen.] [His frustration getting the better of him, Vincent screams into the fading Chicago sunlight.] Vincent: What the hell do you want with me, huh? Answer me! [Stepping off the front porch, Vincent tears off on a wide curve through the front lawn and out into the street, his arms flailing in confusion.] Vincent: I’m right here, you arrogant prick! Let’s get this over with, and let me get on with my life. [The voice drifts from somewhere behind Vincent, cold and calculated.] Judge: You were smart not to run. There is no place the Law wouldn’t find you. [Spinning around so fast he almost trips himself, Vincent initially shies away as the faceless visage of the costumed Judge standing ten feet away nearly gives him a heart attack. Looking in all directions at once, a good portion of Vincent’s rational mind doesn’t even want to know where the figure came from, because ultimately it doesn’t matter.] [He was already there.] Vincent: I suppose you’ll be taking this, then? [With an angry grunt, Vincent tosses the briefcase at the Judge’s feet.] [The Judge hunches over as he looks down at the case, then back at Vincent.] Judge: Do you think you can bribe my Judgment with this… pity money? No, Vincent McArthur… your Fate lies within my capable hands, not the width of your wallet. On your knees. [Scoffing, Vincent frowns.] Vincent: Excuse me? [Before he has the chance to scream, a small silenced pistol hidden away in the folds of the Judge’s robes destroys the cartilage in his right kneecap, sending him crashing to the cracked pavement.] [Amidst Vincent’s mortified shrieks, the Judge slowly begins to walk forward.] Judge: Vincent Charles McArthur, you have been found morally Guilty by a qualified judiciary official who is your unquestionable superior. Me. [The Judge keeps talking, and Vincent keeps screaming.] Judge: In accordance with the Law, you are found Guilty of deceit… [He raises the pistol.] Judge: …of lust… [The warm muzzle rests softly against Vincent’s forehead, who has now become deathly silent.] Judge: …of greed… [The hammer cocks back.] Judge: …and of emotional infidelity. Your future spouse doesn’t deserve a husband who’s mind wanders between the legs of every blonde harlot this side of Lake Michigan. You’ve heard your Charges, how do you plead? [His eyes nothing more than pupils the size of saucers, Vincent lets out a ragged breath as a single tear rolls down his dirty cheek.] Vincent: Not guilt-… BANG~! [The bullet tears a quick and bitter path through the back of Vincent’s skull, locking his eyes in that final cold embrace of death.] [A small curl of white smoke snakes out of the barrel’s silencer, twisting through the overlapping folds of the Judge’s robes. The emotionless mask looks upon the writhing remains of Vincent McArthur, his body twitching in spasmodic throes as the brain begins to shut down.] Renegade: What the hell…? [Slamming the door on his baby blue ‘67 Shelby Mustang three houses down, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins stares dumbfounded at the backside of the Judge, unaware of Vincent’s bleeding body.] Renegade: Who the hell are you supposed to be, Benjamin Franklin? [Without turning to face the new arrival, the Judge simply bends down and collects the black briefcase, the silenced pistol disappearing back into the cloth from whence it came. Slowly unclasping the dual latches, the Judge speaks.] Judge: Your Judgment is not for me to decide, Rich Rollins. That is up to Fate, as it should be. Renegade: What…? [Without warning, the Judge slings the briefcase high into the air, spilling seven hundred sixty-three thousand dollars worth of unmarked bills into the breeze. The loose paper scatters like angry bees, billowing out in a thick cloud of flapping currency.] [Three seconds later, all that remains in the middle of the street are hundreds of grinning Benjamins and one very dead Vincent McArthur.] [Sirens.] [Breaking into a dead sprint, The Renegade charges into the swirling mess of money and blood, his mind racing with questions that offer no answers. The Judge was completely gone.] [No more than ten feet from Vincent’s final resting place, an open sewer grate tells the whole tale.] Sneaky little bastard, Rollins muses. [A heavy weight settles on The Renegade’s chest as he finally recognizes Vincent’s body, now covered in a mountain of cash that sticks to his corpse like rice paper on a wet plate. As the distant sirens become more and more not-so-distant, everything begins to click into place.] [A setup.] Fuck. [Before he has the chance to dive down into the open sewer grate in pursuit, the first police cruiser comes screeching around the corner, its lights finding only Rich Rollins and the body of Vincent McArthur.] [Slowly raising his arms out to his sides, The Renegade faces the inevitable with a stern scowl permanently etched into the rough features of his utterly frustrated face.] ~<>~<>~<>~ The Dante Exchange Building, Downtown Chicago Monday, June 6th 10:59pm One hour and one minute until Infinite Gauntlet Event “If everything happens for a reason, this better be a damn good reason.” [His wrists finally free of the biting grip of the handcuffs, Rich Rollins absently rubs the tender skin where the shackles had rubbed him raw. Sitting back in an extravagantly tailored black leather chair, The Renegade meets his host’s curious gaze with sheer abhorrence.] Renegade: I’m tellin’ you, the guy was all dressed up as a judge. He ditched the scene almost immediately as the police arrived, fingering yours truly for a crime I most certainly did not commit. How the hell am I supposed to sort through all of this? [The words vibrate harshly in the spacious interior of the ornate antechamber that serves as the lofty private office of one of the most powerful Italian Families’ in Chicago.] [Sitting behind an elaborate black desk, his fingers interlocked patiently in his lap, Alexandro Dante maintains a considerate amount of control as he listens to Rollins’ deadly honest question. A well-groomed man of his mid thirties, Don Dante’s broad frame is encased in an expensive Armani business suit, the collar unbuttoned at the top in a gesture that suggests a casual mood.] [But he was never in a casual mood. Especially now, when the business at hand was most certainly personal.] [Brushing The Renegade’s comment aside with an overbearing flourish, Don Dante replies.] Dante: We’ve already posted your bail, Rollins. Our legal staff officially put your case on the top of their list as of ten minutes ago, so rest easy. This opponent of yours won’t be able to wiggle away from us twice. Renegade: No, you’ve got it all wrong… this guy wasn’t the Uber Judge. He may have been dressed up like some fluffy English official, but I think it was intended as a message. This guy could be trying to frame us both for all I know. [His brow puckering in consideration, the Don leans back in his chair as he studies Rollins’ face with fastidious interest.] Dante: Your other opponent, perhaps? This Infinite Gauntlet of yours… you are one of two challengers, correct? Renegade: Yes, Kyle “Velocity” Lamen is the second challenger. Dante: Do you believe him capable of sabotaging the match by framing both his opponents for murder? Renegade: Honestly? (he chuckles) I don’t think he has the balls. Dante: Indeed. [Rapping his fingertips against the polished Japanese wood of his desk, the Don sighs.] Dante: So where do we go from here? [Leaning in as if he were about to divulge a dark secret, The Renegade simmers with barely-restrained rage.] Renegade: I get my legal forms turned into the PTC offices, then get my sorry ass to the Infinite Gauntlet. If someone wants me to fail so badly, they better be doin’ it in the ring. I promise you that. Dante: How long until the deadline? [The Renegade glances at the banded watch on his left wrist.] 11:04 Renegade: Fifty-six minutes and counting. How the hell am I supposed to make it to the PTC offices in less than an hour? [Don Dante allows an amused chuckle to escape his chest as he rises to his feet.] Dante: Don’t worry, there’s a PTC kiosk outside of the bank across the street. Damn things have popped up like weeds since this Gauntlet started. Have you seen the television spots? [The Renegade shakes his head negatively.] Dante: Don’t worry, you haven’t missed much. [Gesturing for Rollins to stand, the Don casually saunters around the girth of his desk and clasps The Renegade on one shoulder, who takes the hint and follows suit.] Dante: We’ll get your papers in on time, and then my people will fly you personally to the PTC venue. How does that sound? Renegade: Thank you, Don Dante. [He dismisses the gratitude with a wave of his hand.] Dante: You are a good investment, Rich Rollins. Besides, you need to win back the money I lost on you in the first round of this damned Gauntlet. [Dante tosses Rollins a playful wink as he escorts The Renegade towards the intricately-carved rosewood doors leading out of the antechamber and into his secretaries’ offices.] [Holding one door open, Don Dante nods.] Dante: Il sole brilla sempre il più luminoso nella scia della tempesta. [“The sun always shines brightest in the wake of the storm.”] [And with that, Don Alexandro Dante leaves Rich Rollins alone to do what he does best.] [Win.] ~Fin~
