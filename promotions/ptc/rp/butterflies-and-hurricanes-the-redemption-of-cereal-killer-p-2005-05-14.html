Butterflies and Hurricanes: The Redemption of Cereal Killer - Part Four: "Best, you've got to be the best"

14 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-05-14 They’d been some funny days for the man known as Cereal Killer. One the one hand he was flying high in the world of professional wrestling, advancing for a second time to make it to the quarter finals of the second annual PTC Cruiserweight Tournament. On the other hand he’d recently been reacquainted with Jack “The Ripper” Nobbledy, a man who had not only been involved in Cereal Killer’s previous traumas, but had really been the main cause of them. CK sat in his apartment on the sofa, a pile of mail ripped open next to him, the brown and white envelopes sat on the blue cushions, a stark contrast that the XUW entrant to the tournament was doing his best to ignore. “Did you read them?” asked Valerie, his girlfriend. “No.” “Why not?” she said, moving from the kitchen to the living room and looking at him with her good eye. Cereal Killer shrugged. His mood swings were getting more pronounced, and with the return to his life of The Ripper, he was ignoring Valerie a little. It wasn’t something he could help, it was just that when that certain lethargy kicked in, he couldn’t speak to her. As a result she had been coming around a little less often, but she was making an effort. “I asked you a question, Frank,” said Valerie. Closing his eyes briefly, he squeezed his fingers to the bridge of his nose. He knew that he owed Valerie some answers, or at the very least some attention. “I’m sorry, babe. I didn’t read the mail… I just couldn’t face it. All I ever get are bills and bad news.” Valerie bit her lip. Sadly, Cereal Killer was right on the money. “I know, but you can’t hide from reality.” “I’ve been trying to for years.” “But you can’t, in the end. There are things you have to face. Bills are terrible, but you’ve got to pay them, or they turn off your electric and evict you.” “Okay, I’ll pay the bills then. I’m still getting that paycheck from XUW, so we’ll get along. We always do.” “Ah,” said Valerie, her face stern. “There’s a problem.” “Problem?” “Yeah.” Both Frank and Valerie knew that he only still got paid because of a clerical error. It had been years since Cereal Killer competed in the federation, after sustaining some major injuries whilst trying to make an ‘Immediate Impact’. Unsurprisingly, the string of strange characters to be in charge of XUW had failed to take him off the roster due to either gross incompetence, being too preoccupied with drinking themselves to death, or, currently, being an eight year old boy. “What’s the problem?” asked CK. “Well, it’s just that you’re competing at a very high level here, and with this there’s a certain degree of publicity that goes with it.” “Meaning?” “Meaning that XUW has written to you, saying that they’ve noticed the oversight and want to terminate your contract.” “What?” exclaimed CK. “It’s in the letter; you’re sitting right next to it.” Frank grabbed the pile of mail and quickly rifled through it, soon finding the trademark letterhead. His lips moved in a blur as the words sank in to his brain. “Is there anything we can do?” he asked. “Read to the bottom.” “How can they do this? I’m actually winning matches and… oh.” “You get there?” “Yeah.” “What do you think?” she asked. “I think it’s a bit fucking harsh, to be honest.” “You can do it though, I know you can,” implored Valerie CK was lost in a world of doubt. All through his career, all through his life in fact, he’d never really given his all for something. He’d always been crippled with a fear of failure so great that it prevented him from even attempting to succeed beyond a very basic level. His mind always told him that if he didn’t try with all his might, then falling short wasn’t because he failed, it was because he didn’t really want it in the first place. It had held him back all through his life, all up until the one time he went for it, and tried to take down the most vicious team in XUW history. That was the night he got injured, and left the federation. “Frank, you can do it.” He looked back down at the letter, and the final, threatening words. ‘Termination of your contract is conditional upon your failing to win the PTC Cruiserweight Tournament.’ It was worded in an odd way, the evident consequences of drawing up paperwork when working for a small child, but the message was clear. To keep on being paid, Cereal Killer was going to have to win the entire tournament. ----------------------------------------------------- The truth be told, the XUW office had far less to do with that letter than either Frank or Valerie would have imagined. The owner of the federation, Webster Junior, had different things to worry about, such as getting the biggest PPV of the year staged without a hitch, and tidying his room. Instead, unseen forces moved around the corridors of sports entertainment power. A little intimidation here, a small bribe there, and before long there were actions in motion to threaten the livelihood of Cereal Killer. The orders were sent out and just as CK’s worst fears were being realised, the complicated plans of the man he feared the most were just beginning to pan out. ----------------------------------------------------- “Tsunami Oni… Tsunami Oni… Tsunami Oni…” Cereal Killer’s words bounced off the walls and came back to him, still sounding as strong as ever. One of the most famous sons of the well respected Universal Wrestling Federation, Oni would always be a dangerous man to face in the ring. CK threw a tennis ball at the floor, bouncing it up and off the wall before it returned to his grasp. “Tsunami Oni… there’s no freaking way I can beat Tsunami Oni.” He clenched the ball even tighter in his hand, and threw it down again. Two ricochets and it was back in his hand. Cereal Killer went for a third throw, but words cut him off. “It’s surprisingly easy if you know how.” The sound, like a dagger of ice being slowly drawn up his back, made Frank turn, but he knew, just like he always knew, who he’d see. “How did you get in here?” asked CK The Ripper laughed, but the sound bore no relation to anything Frank knew as humour. “Won’t you at least knock?” “It is not in the interests of The Ripper to pay heed to the niceties of your etiquette.” “What are you here for now? Have you come to tell me how Valerie is never coming back? Have you come here to gloat that I’m going to lose, or will it be some kind of motivational speech about how I could use the darkness to motivate me?” CK was shocked by the force of his own words. He was normally deferent to The Ripper, intimidated either by his physical presence or his general air of menace. Now though… now his blood was running with fire again. The enthusiasm was coming back, and with it, the voices. “I don’t need you, Jack! I’m the Demonstration of Domination! I’m the Paragon of Perfection! I’m the period between 1920 and 1933 during which the 18th Amendment forbidding the manufacture and sale of alcoholic beverages was in force in the United States” The Ripper thought about this for a second. “The Definition of Prohibition?” “You heard me!” snapped Cereal Killer. “The Ripper does not care for your names, nor is The Ripper interested in why they make no sense at all. All that matters is that you need to know how to defeat your enemy, and The Ripper has that information.” “You know Tsunami Oni?” asked CK, hopefully. “The Ripper has defeated him.” Cereal Killer had heard of the event, PTC Survival, but much of the happenings had passed into mythology. Nevertheless, The Ripper was speaking the truth. He had indeed caught Oni in mid-air and driven him to the mat, pinning him and winning the match. “The tactics would hardly be the same,” protested CK. “The tactics are immaterial. The mind is what matters. The mind is where this battle will be won and lost. Your mind, foolish one, is a mess. To emerge victorious, you will need strength; mental strength greater than that you possess now.” “And I’ll get that if I listen to you?” “No.” “No? Then why the hell are you here?” demanded CK, exasperated. “The truth that will see you continue lies not in any words spoken between now and the time the bell rings to signal a victor. You will not understand it yet, nor will you be able to find anyone who can tell you what it is. Rest assured though, you must seek it nonetheless.” The Ripper turned to leave the room, pausing only by the door as CK opened his mouth once more. “Will I need Valerie’s help?” Without seeing his mouth, Cereal Killer knew that The Ripper must have been smiling.” “Her time with you draws to a close. By the time you have defeated Tsunami Oni and move to fight another time, she will be gone. Gone forever.”
