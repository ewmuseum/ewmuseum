"Shedding Skin"

16 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-16 * * * * * “Nobody cares about the good guys, anymore. Unless you have some shade of gray or are rotten to the core…the fans just don’t seem to care about you.” It’s been nearly a decade since I’ve heard those words. Daniel Harmon, the man whom introduced me and trained me in professional wrestling, hit me with that brutally honest fact. I hear his haggard voice repeat that very phrase each time I debut in a new region of the country. Like tonight. In a moment, the curtain will be parted and I will make my debut once again. I can hear the fans cheering, a slow roar of applause and catcalls, mainly for wrestlers that are popular and well known in this circle of competitors. When I step through the curtain to face Itagaki Freeze, I’m fairly certain the reaction I’ll receive will be somewhat subdued and unsure. It’s only a matter of seconds away, and I’ve felt this knot in my stomach tighten like it did last time. They will watch me walk down the aisle towards the squared-circle, wondering who this average looking wrestler is. I stand no taller than 6’ and weigh in at roughly 220lbs. Intimidating isn’t a word often associated with my wrestling persona, or my appearance for that matter. I will not be the biggest competitor in this tournament, nor will I be the smallest…but without any hype coming into the tournament, I will be only one thing to the fans: Unknown. My name is Jonathan Sail. For the last decade I have wrestled in small towns and even smaller wrestling promotions, because during that time, I never craved the fame and fortune that our sport brings to the men and women whom call themselves professional wrestlers. “Nobody cares about the good guys, anymore.” Danny, as I called him, had been in the wrestling business for nearly two decades prior to our first meeting. When I left the Marine Corps shortly after my 22nd birthday, I ran into Danny at a local bar hiding behind a pint of Guinness, his pock-marked cheeks slightly flushed with the redness alcohol can bring to it. We immediately became friends, and from then on out, you could say my life started down a different path. Over the next 24 months, I trained with Danny occasionally. I never really put 100% of my effort into the training, but at the same time, showing enough initiative to keep Danny content ‘wasting his time with a rookie like me’. For the last 10 years, I have tried to prove that he wasn’t wasting his time. But, much like my training…I never did put 100% of my effort and skill behind it. Until now. I can feel the intensity coursing through my blood, the admirable feeling of stepping away from something you once feared. * * * * * For nearly 6 years, he has been known by only one name: Legion. Sitting on the park bench, an afternoon breeze occasionally tugging gently at his ponytail, a casual passer-by would hardly give Legion a second look. Their stray glance would simply reveal Legion to be a broad shouldered, black haired man. A scar starts above his left eyebrow, jumps across Legion’s brilliant aqua colored eye, finally resuming it’s jagged path down towards the center of his left cheek. Other than the scar, which is pale in color, faintly standing out against his Florida tan, the man known only as Legion shows no other distinguishing attributes such as tattoos or birthmarks. A few strands of his dark hair are dyed red, but only stand out when the sun shines directly on them. Resting on the bench beside Legion is a paperback booklet, “Bird-watching for Amateurs: A Beginners Guide.” Atop the book is a pair of binoculars. Legion sat peacefully, enjoying the beautiful afternoon by slowly encircling an apple with a small knife, removing the crimson skin from the pale innards of the fruit. With two well-placed slices of the knife, Legion removed and ate a portion of the apple directly from the edge of the blade. He could hear the birds singing in the surrounding trees, but right now the only thing on his mind was finishing his delicious snack. As he readied another slice of the apple for consumption, Legion felt a small series of vibrations from within the pocket of his pants. Setting down the apple and knife onto the bench, he quickly produced a small cellular phone. He momentarily glanced at the number on the screen, promptly flipping it open. “I’m assuming the applicant failed his interview.” Legion said, still partially chewing the slice of apple. “A sound assumption,” a heavy baritone voice replied, “The job is yours, if you still want it.” “Fair enough.” “Watch out for the Hurricane, I hear Charlie is supposed to pack quite the punch.” ”The skies haven’t even begun to change, although the birds seem to be aware of it’s impending arrival. They seem agitated, yet calm.” ”Touching up on your bird-watching?” “I’m always learning, Victor.” Legion flipped the phone closed and glanced around the large park. The trees were barely starting dance with the breeze, although in a few days it would take the strength of their roots to keep them firmly in place. Many of the birds noted in Legion’s book could be heard singing their endless songs, but that was the least of his interests. Quickly tossing the remainder of the apple into the wastebasket, Legion quickly stood up and reached for the duffle bag resting next to the bench. * * * * * The Marines took my hair before my first day of boot camp, and since then, I have kept it cleanly shaven. It’s a reminder of the tough times I went through during training, and then the duty that followed. Besides, it seems as though the ‘bald’ look is in…so I tell myself I’m just keeping up with times. The curtain still hasn’t parted yet, and I’m sure the match before mine is over with. The cheers have died down in preparation for my match. I know when I slowly walk down the aisle way, I’m sure there will be a few people who cheer for me simply because they are wrestling fans, in general. They will see an average looking wrestler, wearing navy blue tights, making his debut in a new promotion. It’s exciting to the fans who cherish this sport for what it is…a competition. These are the fans that give me that feeling of pride in what I do, those adrenaline rushes. You know the one, the rush a person gets when they are doing something they love, and others respect and admire them for it. I can feel the goosebumps tickling my back at the thought. At nearly 33 years of age, I can imagine that I’m one of the older competitors in this large tournament…but it doesn’t bother me. I’ve had nearly a decade to practice and hone my skill inside the ring, even though I will be the first to admit I probably have never wrestled against anybody of this tournament’s caliber during my years in the small ‘Indy’ promotions I used to call home. ”Unless you have some shade of gray or are rotten to the core…the fans just don’t seem to care about you.” A decade is a long time, especially when you love the competition like I do. 10 years is made even longer when you attempt to keep the name Jonathan Sail off the professional wrestling radar, something I found nearly as trying as the wrestling itself. Every so often, I remember noticing a wrestling journalist hiding in the small crowds surrounding the ring throughout the country. My name appeared in ‘Wrestling Illustrated’ in 1995 as a ‘A Potential Superstar to Watch out for’, and as soon as I heard about that particular article…I immediately stopped wrestling for a year, once again dropping off the radar once more. Danny resented me for that, but he gave in. He always gave in, except for two things in particular. * * * * * Throughout her life, it has often been said that Natalie Duncan could be mistaken for an angel. Her face held a natural glow, a natural ambience that even on overcast days could bring a smile to the face of any person walking the planet. Her blonde hair usually fell softly behind her head, fastened behind her in a ponytail, only a few select strands falling across her smiling face. Natalie’s light-brown eyes were often mistaken for tan, giving her a unique stare that seemed to capture the attention of those who made eye contact with her. Over the years, Natalie’s mother always said that on sunny days, at the right angle, the sun would create a halo hovering above Natalie’s head. “If anyone tells you that it’s only the sunlight reflecting off of your beautiful hair,“ Mrs. Duncan would say, “You just smile, shrug your shoulders, and reply with ‘Maybe’ and be on your way.” Sitting on a nearby park bench, Mrs. Duncan watched little Natalie play in the large sandbox that surrounded the swing-set and slides. Dressed in a pink dress, Natalie’s hands quickly molded the sand into what appeared to be a large castle. After a few minutes, Natalie looked up at her mother and smiled with pure childlike innocence, before once again plowing her little hands back into the rough sand. ”Baby, watch your nails…the manicure was a nice gift from your father.” Mrs. Duncan smiled. ”Mommy…my birthday castle needs a birthday moat!” Natalie informed her mother. A few minutes had passed by, and Mrs. Duncan looked up to see if her daughters birthday castle was finished after nearly a half-hour of construction, “Natalie…what are you doing?” She stared at her daughter, who was poking at what would be considered the spire of the castle. Her little fingers jabbed at the sand one too many times, and the spire quickly crumbled down, into the moat. ”Baby…why are you ruining your birthday castle?” ”There’s a red dot on it, Mommy.” Natalie said with a curious smile, her head turning to make contact with her mothers. Her eyes almost matched the color of the sand she was playing in. “It’s like a princess’ ruby, shining in the sun!” “A red dot, sweetie? Have you been staring at the sun? I told you not to do that, sweetie. You’ll need glasses before too long!” Mrs. Duncan sternly said, suddenly pausing when she realized that the sun did create a reflection off of Natalie’s hair. Across the park, a flock of birds erupted from the trees. The first bullet ripped through Natalie Duncan’s chest, killing her instantly. She fell onto what was left of her spire-less sand castle, dead even before the castle stopped crumbling around her lifeless body. Mrs. Duncan suffered only about a half-second of the worst grief she had ever endured before her chest was punctured with an identical bullet, and an identical result. * * * * * Danny always resented two things about me: 1) I always wore sunglasses to cover my eyes. I was born without any pigment in my irises, much like the deficiency an Albino suffers from, except instead of effecting my whole body, it’s centralized in my eyes. They are a pale, milky color. Grayish, but closer to white than any color you’ll find in the crayon box. 2) I wanted to be the good guy in the world of wrestling. I strived to never break the rules like other wrestlers, never using outside objects to achieve victory. Even as a teacher, Danny taught me how to get away with ‘tricks’, as he called them, while the referee’s back was turned…but I just ignored his advice. I wanted to make it on my own accolades, not the ignorance of the referee, or winning a match I probably shouldn’t have by simply using a chair to beat my opponent…when my own talent wouldn’t do. I wasn’t going to be that guy, I feared him. To take the talent you’ve been given, and toss it away for an easy victory. There are few things on this planet that put an honest scare in me…but ignoring my own abilities is one of them. So I chose to be pure white, in a world of wrestling constructed with the bland hues and contrasting grays of a newspaper. “Nobody cares about the good guys, anymore. Unless you have some shade of gray or are rotten to the core…the fans just don’t seem to care about you.” Danny said, his face usually full of delight, but after he mentioned this fact, the delight was replaced with a sour resentment. I remember saying to him, “Never. I’m going all out, one hundred percent good-of-heart.” “You will fail, and miserably. I love you to death, Jon…but in this age of wrestling, you need to be a rebel.” He said. I told him, ”Pardon the pun, but I chose the ship I will be sailing on. If it’s destined to sink, then so be it. But at least I will have tried.” Danny quickly replied with a backhand across my face. ”Personal vindication doesn’t equal wrestling success, Jonathan! Besides…each time you get close to the spotlight shining on you, you just run from it!” Those words pierce as sharply as a dagger, it’s hilt quivering as it sticks out from between my ribs. That was years ago, long before I stopped running from my fears. The time is now, and I’m looking straight at the curtains…on the other side, fans have cheered for wrestlers that are younger, more popular, and possibly more talented than I am. But the fact of the matter is this: Jonathan Sail has stopped running. I’m here, and I’m going to prove everyone wrong…I will prove Danny wrong, may his soul rest in peace. My defiance starts right here, with this tournament. Once that curtain opens, I will start down the path I should have taken a decade ago. I will walk the path that everyone knew I should be following, and not fear the consequences like I have in years past. It may not happen over night, or even in this tournament. But after I step through the ropes and into the ring with Itagaki Freeze, victorious or not, I promise you this: The wrestling world will know exactly who I am. * * * * * Arriving back at his hotel room only an hour after the assassinations in the park, Legion quickly inserted the key-card into the lock and entered his room. As he threw his duffel back onto the small dinner table, Legion could hear the metallic banging of the collapsible rifle pieces bouncing off each other. He put the small paperback bird-watching booklet atop the bag, along with the binoculars. As if compelled by some unseen force, Legion promptly made his way towards the bathroom at an accelerated pace. He slammed the door shut, and locked the door. It was as if he was hiding from someone, or preventing his empty room to witness what was about to happen. Instead, he stood with both palms on the countertop for a moment, staring at himself in the mirror. He studied his reflection with a vivid intent. Without removing his stare from the mirror, Legion removed the knife he had used earlier to carve the apple from it’s sheath. He began preparing himself for the mild pain he was about to endure. With one small slice at the base of his hairline, Legion peeled back the adhesive tape that held the black wig atop his head. Next was the edge of the knife digging into the scar on his face, carefully pulling the thin line of rubbery makeup from his cheek. He repeated the process for the upper half of the mock scar. The knife soon made it’s way toward his wrist, where so many people have taken their lives in the same fashion. But Legion wasn’t about to venture down that path of absurdity and weakness, instead, he carefully slit the thin latex coating and peeled back the mock skin. Hidden beneath the thin layer of makeup was his sole tattoo: a small boat with a large sail caught in the wind. Finally, resting the knife on the marble counter top, Legion raised his hands to his eyes, making two swift motions with his nimble fingers. The blue contact lenses fell into the sink, one after the other. Without his disguise, Legion stood in his bathroom and stared at the mirror once again. He could only see one thing in particular, and was unable to rip his gaze away: the milky, colorless stare of his own eyes. Staring for a few moments, Legion wondered exactly whom the reflection really was. * * * * * I am Jonathan Sail.
