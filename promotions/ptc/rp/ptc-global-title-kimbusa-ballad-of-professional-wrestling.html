11 February 2007 Ballad of Professional Wrestling, Part One Preface This is a story about professional wrestling. This narrative follows the events transpiring in the lives of three men in one day, the day of the PTC Global Title Match featuring Kimbusa, Angelo Deville, Dr. Curiosity and Clinton Sage. But while the match itself is relevant to the story, the true matter at hand lies somewhere deeper. First comes the account of the day of The Driver, no more than a nameless chauffeur in the employ of Caldera Enterprises, Incorporated. Second comes the tale of Steven Caldera, wrestling and media mogul and manager of the Global Champion. Finally comes the story of Kimbusa himself. Each man’s story carries with it a different tenor, a different slant, but all are inexorably linked. For though their motives may differ, and though they all are wildly different men, they are bound by a single entity; professional wrestling. Despite their differences, all three men are led down the same road, as they contemplate their current roles in the world of professional wrestling. Ultimately, this one day will bring all three men to a crossroads, all ending at the Global Title match. The Driver’s Day 6:00 AM The alarm rang at six. It was one of those awful, jarring alarms that sounded with a grinding persistence that seems to ruin a day the moment it begins. Every time he heard that sound The Driver wondered why he hadn’t replaced it with a nice clock radio or something. The Driver sat up and slammed his leathery hand on the off button. That terrible grating sound never failed to put him in a bad mood. Cursing under his breath, The Driver rose from bed and tried in vain to shake the sleepiness from his eyes. He had good days and he had bad days; on the good days he was able to snap out of his slumber with relative ease and prepare himself for another thankless day in the employ of Caldera Enterprises, Incorporated. This was going to be a bad day. 7:03 AM Three minutes behind schedule. Shit. Normally, Steven Caldera’s underlings were able to work in a state of comfortable anonymity. But The Driver wasn’t a normal underling; hell, he didn’t really consider himself an underling at all. In fact, The Driver wasn’t a driver at all; at least, not normally. He was a minority partner of CEi and Caldera’s first boss in the wrestling business. But The Driver had been surpassed by Caldera’s unrelenting ambition and business prowess, and so he had accepted a lower job in Caldera’s corporation. The Driver floated about, squandering what was once a promising career as he lingered in the shadow of the imposing Steven Caldera. But Caldera never forgot those who gave him his start, and in Caldera Enterprises, Incorporated, no good deed goes unpunished. As such, Caldera had singled him out, and he was subject to the full attention and scrutiny of the company’s President. And now, for some bizarre reason, Caldera had stuck him with the job of chauffeuring a 500 pound professional wrestler about town. What did I ever do to deserve this? Pulling the limousine out of its garage, The Driver sped towards the offices of Caldera Enterprises, Incorporated. It was a blustery, bitingly cold Columbus morning. Six inches of snow had fallen during his brief, unsatisfying slumber, and The Driver shivered in his seat as he desperately waited for the engine to heat up. He fiddled with the radio dial for a few moments as he pulled out onto the street, but the sound of over-exuberant morning radio jocks blathering into the small cabin of the limo was too much to handle. Grumbling he turned the radio off and sped down the road with nothing but the sound of the tires crunching on the salt on the road to keep him company. 7:36 AM Thankfully the traffic hadn’t been bad. He’d made it downtown relatively quickly, though he was still a few minutes late. It would be going too far to suggest that The Driver feared Caldera; hell, he sometimes found himself wishing Caldera would banish him from the company so that he might take his earnings and spend the rest of his life on some tropical resort surrounded by topless women. Then he could sit back sipping one of those fruity drinks with the little umbrellas in them, spend his days being fed grapes on the beach by some sexy South Pacific chick and laugh to himself while thinking of Caldera cooped up in his cramped office, rotting away as a slow creeping death approached- Damnit, his mind was wandering again... He still wasn’t awake. And how the hell did he just go from Mai Thais and topless women to slow creeping death? Man, this place was having an effect on him. 7:42 AM After parking the limo in the garage underneath CEi’s downtown headquarters, The Driver passed through security and took the private back elevator up to the top floor. The elevator was playing some wretched easy listening muzak that quickly led The Driver to begin seriously considering suicide. The morning was turning into a particularly miserable one. And he hadn’t even spoken to Caldera yet. He didn’t have all the details of his assignment for the morning, but he knew that something big was brewing. Caldera had been watching Kimbusa like a hawk, and The Driver had been his eyes and ears. The Driver had turned up very little he thought useful, save the fact that Kimbusa was a decidedly disturbing human being. The Driver didn’t fear Caldera, but he sure as hell feared Kimbusa. Maybe the most terrifying thing about him was how similar he was to Caldera. Kimbusa had seemingly adapted some of Caldera’s cool, Michael Corleone collectedness. But where Caldera used it as a form of intimidation, you could tell that Kimbusa genuinely needed to focus to control his emotions. The Driver had quickly gained a sense of Kimbusa as a rumbling volcano, constantly building pressure until the inevitable eruption. The Driver hoped to hell he’d be relieved from his duties before that happened. But there was a definite sense of anxiety surrounding the morning, and he got the feeling that whatever Caldera had been planning was about to come to a head; and it might end badly for all of them. The elevator doors opened into a modest white reception room. It was subtly decorated and welcoming; to the inexperienced visitor it might appear to be a pleasant and cozy little pass-through on the way to what would surely be a comfortable office where Caldera was waiting. But The Driver knew better. Caldera was the ultimate micromanager; he oversaw even the most minute of details when his office was constructed. This welcoming little nook served to lull Caldera’s visitors into a sense of security before they were blindsided by the majesty of Caldera’s office. Led out of the unassuming white room they entered two massive wooden doors and entered a wonderland of excess; massive ceilings adorned by priceless chandeliers, tall windows opening out over the edge of Caldera’s thirtieth-floor office that created a distinct sense of vertigo, majestic paintings spanning entire walls that seemed to stare accusingly down at the doorway. The first time you walk into that room, you’re blindsided by it, instantly losing your equilibrium. Once that happened, Caldera had you right where he wanted you. Fortunately for The Driver, he’d quickly adapted. He now saw the office for just what it was; an elaborate throne for a man obsessed with power. The reception area was empty; Caldera’s secretary, Janice, didn’t arrive until 8. The lights were still off, and only a few beams of the rising sun were trickling through the windows. The dim light wasn’t doing much to help The Driver’s lethargy; he knew he would need to stay alert today, but his mind was lagging far behind his body this morning. He knocked hard on the large wooden doors at the back of the room and pushed his way into the office. Instantly his eyes were assaulted by the light; the sun had risen behind the building and was pouring in through the enormous windows. Caldera’s desk was perched on an elevated platform in front of these windows, and he was surrounded by brilliant golden light. The Driver rubbed the sting out of his eyes as Caldera’s silhouetted head rose to attention. He was wearing a wrinkled, day-old suit, and his normally impeccably groomed features were rough and unkempt. Caldera had a small bedroom adjacent to the office behind a hidden door, but it didn’t look like he’d made use of it in the last 48 hours. Caldera motioned The Driver to wait, and maneuvering out of the light The Driver saw that he was on the phone. The acoustics of the room were designed so that Caldera’s voice boomed from his sunlit throne, but he was speaking with a hushed and hurried tone. At first The Driver thought nothing of it, and looked with longing eyes at the wonderfully comfortable leather couch set back in the corner of the office. But he caught the word “Kimbusa” from Caldera’s whispers, and The Driver began straining to hear. “…but I’m just not sure he can handle it,” Caldera was whispering. His piercing blue eyes glanced over his shoulder and found The Driver inching closer, and quickly Caldera spun in his chair and turned his back. The Driver strained harder yet to hear, a bit surprised at his curiosity. He’d slowly lost interest in the business over his years with Caldera Enterprises, Incorporated. Hell, at this point, it was just a paycheck, even if it was an insultingly small one. But for some reason, on this exceedingly crappy day, something woke within him… a desire to be a somebody again. Maybe he had simply had enough of the menial tasks handed down from above, maybe his pride had been sufficiently wounded as to reawaken his ambitions. But whatever it was, wheels began turning in The Driver’s head. He decided he wouldn’t just be a passive player in the day’s events. He was going to change things. Yeah, Caldera was definitely hiding something. The Driver casually moved over towards a low coffee table near Caldera’s desk, picking up a four-month old copy of GCW Magazine to glance at. Calming his breathing until he was almost completely still, The Driver began to resolve Caldera’s words again. “…son of a bitch. I’m in charge here, and I’m not going to let this situation get out of hand… I know what I’ve been saying to them, but you don’t think I’d show even the slightest weakness, do you? As far as they’re concerned, he’s still the most dominant man in the sport today. But as far as I’m concerned, he’s an asset, and one I’m not willing to see fail… No, that’s not what I’m saying. I’m just saying that these guys are good, damn good, and it’s a lot to ask for anyone to overcome…. Well, good as he is, I just don’t see this thing having a happy ending for any of us… Look, I’ve got something to take care of, I’ll call you back later.” Caldera slammed the phone down and spun in his chair again. He let loose a heavy sigh and slumped down, exhausted. The Driver’s morning got a little better seeing that Steven Caldera was even more miserable than he. “Corey, come in,” Caldera said, motioning towards the small visitor’s chair set before the desk. The Driver stepped up and took a seat, suddenly finding himself quite alert. He’d received quite a rush from eavesdropping on Caldera’s conversation; it had confirmed for him that something big was going down, and if he kept his eyes and ears open, he might find an opportunity to deal himself back in the game. “So, what’s so urgent?” The Driver asked, doing his best to sound professional. Despite his newfound enthusiasm for the day, he couldn’t help but feel a little self-conscious. His voice sounded weak and timorous; it had been so damn long since he’d played the game… Caldera didn’t seem to notice; his eyes were fixed on some distant point on his desk, and he was clearly struggling to stay awake. “I don’t know if you’ve heard by now or not… I don’t even know how much you really pay attention to wrestling any more,” Caldera said, displaying a wry smirk. “But something kinda big is going on around here. Kimbusa’s defending his Global Title; the match is tonight, and I need you to make sure he’s there on time and ready to fight.” Caldera slowly slid the top drawer of his desk open and produced a small slip of paper which he handed to The Driver. “That’s the address where he’s been training… he’ll be there.” The Driver nodded. “What’s the problem, then?” he queried, trying his hardest not to sound too interested. Caldera shot back a quizzical look. “Problem?” “Well, yeah,” The Driver replied nonchalantly. “You’re a mess, you haven’t slept, you seem… stressed out. What’s the problem?” Rolling his eyes, Caldera slumped back as the leather crackled on the chair. He nervously tapped his fingers against his chest, seemingly lost in thought. Finally, after a lengthy hesitation, Caldera spoke up. “It’s just a really big match, and we really, really want to win this one.” The Driver nodded. Looking down at the address, The Driver instantly recognized it. It was that of Caldera’s family farm, which had been left to him by his grandfather years ago and he’d held onto for nostalgic reasons. The Driver had been there before back in Caldera’s early days; Caldera used to enjoy relaxing there, but now he hardly left the office. Standing and taking his leave, The Driver moved back out into the elevator and pushed the Garage button. “The Girl from Ipanema” was playing in decidedly trite opposition to the sudden mood of decisiveness that had come over him. Looking up at the ceiling of the elevator, The Driver threw a hard fist and drove a massive dent into the speaker. But the mockingly soft jazz played on. 10:59 AM The Driver hadn’t rushed to the farm. He stopped off at a local café for coffee and crepes while he considered his best play. He’d read Caldera fairly quickly. And, despite what he led on, he had heard about the match; Kimbusa was up against Dr. Curiosity, Clinton Sage and Angelo Deville. Angelo Deville. That’s exactly why Caldera wanted to win this one. There was no doubt in The Driver’s mind that Caldera was afraid; afraid of failure. Caldera had always been obsessed with victory, but he’d never seemed so overwhelmed. Kimbusa was a hell of a wrestler, that much was certain, but it seemed that the general feeling about town that morning was that Kimbusa didn’t have what it takes to top three men the caliber of his opponents. For his part, The Driver didn’t know what they were so worried about. He’d stood next to Kimbusa and seen how imposing he is in person. But he guessed that on television Kimbusa just looked like a goofy fat guy in a thong. Ah, television. It had been so long since The Driver stood under the bright lights and on the sweaty, bloody canvas in front of thousands of screaming fans. He’d once been a very successful promoter in the Ohio Valley region; there was a brief time where he would have been mobbed on the Columbus streets with fans. But fans in the wrestling business have a short memory, and The Driver was left in peace to sip his coffee and ponder his lost career. It happens to everyone, eventually, he reasoned. Wrestlers tended to have a short life span, even if some of them held on too long. Most never really made it; the lucky few get to be stars for a few years before the endless beatings took a toll on their body. Some lingered well past their prime, unable to move on, unable to let go of the thrill. The Driver had vowed to himself long ago never to become one of them, not to outstay his welcome. So he’d retired when Caldera took the torch and ran with it to a higher level. But his retirement didn’t bring with it the dignity he expected. Instead he was relegated to driving a car for the man he introduced to the world only seven years ago. In truth, The Driver found himself admitting, he didn’t like how the business had evolved. He was a true old-schooler; he grew up in the business in a time before national and worldwide promotions, when the regional promoter was king of his land. But men like Caldera had greater visions, and like some tyrant of old conquered all the lands and took them for himself, eventually expanding into a worldwide empire. The Driver maintained some respect for Caldera in that he’d never lost his vision of what professional wrestling was supposed to be; but still, Caldera had contributed to what The Driver estimated to be the downfall of the industry. Wiping his mouth and leaving two crisp ten dollar bills on the table, The Driver exited the café and stepped out into the blistering cold. The sun provided some small reprieve, but the wind was working hard to penetrate his heavy overcoat. The Driver scrambled to his limo and slid inside, nimbly evading the patches of ice on the sidewalk. For a sixty year old man, he still moved pretty well. The engine struggled but finally turned over, and The Driver sped towards the farm. As he navigated the slick city streets he found his mind wandering back to the good old days. He remembered a time when people wrestled for the sake of wrestling; in that respect, Caldera had certainly lost his way. This Global Title match was the perfect example of misplaced priorities; there was a time when it simply would have been anticipated because it featured four of the absolute best wrestlers in the business. But now business and politics had infiltrated his beloved business. Caldera viewed the Global Title not as an expression of phenomenal person achievement, but rather as an asset, a tool for obtaining profit. Actually, Caldera viewed The Driver and Kimbusa as assets, too. Caldera wasn’t worried Kimbusa was going to lose the match; he was worried Kimbusa was going to lose a valuable piece of golden property. Certainly, Deville, Curiosity and Sage were formidable opponents. But even if Kimbusa were to walk into the ring that night, destroy all three men in a display of utter dominance, what would that really do for Caldera? Eventually, Kimbusa would lose the Global Title. It’s the way of all things in wrestling. And on that day all Caldera’s boasting and posturing would come back and bite him squarely on the ass. Rather than sitting back and appreciating a world-class athlete at the peak of his career, Caldera was seeking to drain every penny from the situation and then cast it aside the moment the run was over. And that’s why The Driver had lost his interest in the business, because there were a million Steven Calderas out there. It seemed to him from his lowly vantage point that nobody really cared about the true thrill of the fight; it was all about the buildup and the aftermath. But today, he was going to make things right. He knew he couldn’t change the business overnight. But he wasn’t so washed up that he’d forgotten how to work the system. Yeah, he could make the system work for him all right; if people only cared about bragging about the victors and taunting the losers, The Driver would make sure Caldera would be the one bragging tomorrow. If he made sure Kimbusa won that night, if he showed Caldera that he was still useful, he could work his way back to prominence. And then, slowly but surely, he could set things right with the world. 12:09 PM The Driver arrived at the farm an hour outside Columbus. The drive had been rejuvenating; that first moment’s reflection had set loose a deluge of memories, and soon he was recalling moments under the bright lights he hadn’t thought about in years. For a time he had forgotten all about his epic fall from the top and felt like a king again. That is, until he stepped out of the limousine and promptly slipped on a slick patch of ice, landing on his head and blacking out. 12:16 PM The Driver awoke and felt a chill. He tried to rise but felt a throbbing pain in his head that drove him back down. Squinting through stinging eyes he was unable to resolve his surroundings; an extremely bright white light was relentlessly shining down on him. “I’m dead.” The Driver slowly forced his eyes open, certain he’d passed to the afterlife. Suddenly the light was eclipsed and a looming shadow engulfed him. Standing above him was a large, glowing man of ample proportions. “Buddha? Man, was I barking up the wrong tree…” Kimbusa was not amused. “You slipped on the ice,” was all he grunted before turning away. The Driver groaned as he sat up. You’ve got to be kidding me, he thought to himself, embarrassed at his lack of grace. Still, Kimbusa was the only one who had seen him, and if he’d pegged Kimbusa correctly, the guy was nothing more than a mindless wrecking machine. Kimbusa moved about the room with a quiet poise, surprisingly quiet and unobtrusive for a man of his stature. The Driver had spent some time driving Kimbusa around his adopted hometown of Tokyo, and it never ceased to put a shiver down his spine the way Kimbusa expertly navigated the shadows at night. The Driver had heard rumors of Kimbusa’s connections to the Japanese mafia, but Caldera had refused to go into details. In truth, this had always bothered The Driver about Kimbusa; sure, he was a mean looking piece of humanity, but he wasn’t a true wrestler. He was a gangster, a thug, a man who despite his size once needed to carry a firearm to get his way. Kimbusa wasn’t a real wrestler, he wasn’t a real man; he was just a wandering ghost who was most at home in the shadows. Kimbusa himself seemed to be a bit of a shadow; the guy was 500 pounds and yet you could never really get a fix on him. Or maybe he’d just really messed up his head. Kimbusa was standing in the light across the room, staring out the window. He stood absolutely still, eyes closed, silently breathing in some sort of odd meditation. The Driver stood and watched him silently for a moment. When it became clear that Kimbusa wasn’t going to move, The Driver cleared his throat. “Um, we really should be going. Caldera wanted to make sure you got to the arena in time.” After a pause, Kimbusa turned and, ignoring The Driver entirely, headed for the door. There a small black duffel bag was waiting, and Kimbusa snatched it off the ground and headed for the limo. OK then, off we go. 1:28 PM The limo pulled up on the tarmac at Columbus International Airport two full minutes ahead of schedule. The ride had been spent in deep contemplation; Kimbusa was rarely in the mood for the conversation, though The Driver did make a few astute observations. It seemed that Kimbusa himself seemed to be a bit troubled by something; perhaps even this monster was capable of experiencing fear. No doubt Kimbusa had no desire to lose the Global Title so soon after capturing it for the first time, and the thought of facing those three men at once was turning out to be quite daunting. The Driver had begun to formulate his plan on the way, but had given pause to consider what Kimbusa’s reaction would be. Ultimately he convinced himself that Kimbusa would likely be grateful, even if he’d never admit it. Still, The Driver had thought to himself, there could be worse fates than having a big angry sumo as an ally… While the details of his plan were still a work in progress, The Driver was definitely beginning to see a great potential for gain. Get back in Caldera’s good graces, befriend the PTC Global Champ, play an integral role in one of the biggest matches of the year… yep, this could turn out very well. Despite his fall from the limelight, The Driver still had friends in the business. Despite his determination to avoid being one of those old, washed-up and lingering wrestlers, he still knew plenty of guys who were perfectly willing to hang on. So he’d begun to mull his possibilities, and finally decided that he’d have to call Dero. The Driver exited the limo and opened the door for Kimbusa. Kimbusa slid out and immediately headed for the Caldera Enterprises jet that was fueling up in preparation of the final leg of his journey. But before Kimbusa could board the plane, The Driver made his move. Caldera was nowhere in sight; now was the time. “Kimbusa, wait up,” The Driver called after him. Kimbusa paused, turning slowly to face him. The Driver hesitated, feeling suddenly timid under the weight of Kimbusa’s impatient glare. Finally he choked down his trepidation and continued on. “Look, I’ve been hearing… things. Things about tonight’s match. I just wanted to let you know that I could be of some service to you, if you’re interested.” Kimbusa’s blank expression did not change. “I’ll take that to mean you’re interested. Don’t worry about tonight; I have a feeling things will transpire to swing the evening in your favor. Just don’t sweat it; I’ll take care of everything. And after the match is over, perhaps you and I could sit down and talk about the future.” The Driver concluded his sentence just as Steven Caldera stepped out through the front door of the jet. Caldera raised his right arm and tapped his wristwatch anxiously. It was time to go. Kimbusa said nothing; he simply moved forward again and headed for the jet. The Driver followed a few paces behind him. His initial estimates had proven to be accurate; there really wasn’t much going on upstairs. Kimbusa was exactly what everyone said he was; a mindless, five hundred pound tool. Caldera had used Kimbusa for his purposes for quite some time; now it was The Driver’s turn to implement it. Ascending the white steel steps into Caldera’s Gulfstream, Kimbusa and The Driver prepared for a night that would resolve both their destinies. 5:22 PM The sun was just setting when the plane landed at its destination. Kimbusa and Caldera had spent much of the flight in a rear compartment talking over something or another, leaving The Driver to sit alone and ponder the specifics of his plan. He’d come up with something he regarded as quite ingenious; he surprised himself at how quickly the creative juices had begun flowing again. Despite his self-promise never to wear out his welcome in the wrestling business, the wrestling business needed him. The business needed men like him, old-school guys who remembered the good old days when mindless thugs like Kimbusa weren’t allowed in a wrestling ring, and legitimate tough guys ruled the squared circle. He’d begun to reconsider his opinion on wrestlers sticking around too long; after all, he was getting on in years and yet he was still proving to be more than capable at the art of promoting. He’d dropped Caldera and Kimbusa off at the arena, giving them no indication that he had any interest in the event. He promptly left and hurried to a nearby restaurant, where he was meeting his friend. Big Daddy Dero, as he was known back in the day, was once a big star. But then a young kid named Steven Caldera had come along and made Dero look like an amateur, and before long Dero’s wrestling days were over. But Dero was one of those legitimate tough guys, so he moved on from wrestling into the next most logical pursuit; bounty hunting. Fortunately for The Driver, Dero just happened to be free that day. Dero was waiting for him inside a little family run diner. The place was pretty full, though the tables adjacent to Dero’s booth were conspicuously empty; no doubt the young families inside were slightly wary of this seven foot tall man with the leather jacket, the tattoos creeping up his neck and the mangy mullet dangling from his grizzled head. The Driver walked toward him and laughed. “Damn, Dero, it’s been too long.” Dero had his face buried in a menu, a plume of smoke rising from the cigarette pressed between his lips. Dero dropped the menu and looked up, peering at The Driver from above the rims of his aviator sunglasses. “Shit, Corey… take a seat, brother. Get something to eat.” The Driver sat down across from Dero and idly glanced through the menu. Neither man spoke for a while; for some reason, the two being side by side again made both men feel energized, like they were back on the road again. For those few years a lifetime ago, they had been the kings of the Midwest. Now they were relegated to unsavory and unsatisfying jobs and, without having seen it coming, had grown old. But for a fleeting moment, their minds both wandered back to those glorious days on top of the wrestling world, and they felt young again. Dero finally broke the silence, suddenly finding himself tired of the nostalgic moment. “OK, now that we’ve got that out of the way, what’s this job you’ve got for me?” Dero extinguished his unfiltered Marlboro Red and immediately lit another. The Driver tried to act casual. “Just a few… pro wrestlers I need you to take care of.” Dero looked up. He let out an incredulous chuff. “I don’t do that shit no more, you know that,” he muttered. “Can we get some fuckin’ service over here?” The Driver was not to be deterred. “It’s not like that. No in-ring work required. Call it a bounty, if you will. It’s right up your alley. I just need three guys softened up before a match tonight.” “Tonight?” Dero sneered. “Come on, man, I thought we were just gonna go out boozin’. There ain’t no time for that tonight.” “Sure there is,” The Driver urged. “Look, the arena’s right down the street. I can get you in. Then you just find these three guys and give them the old once-over, just like the old days. Come on, you’re telling me you haven’t once thought about those days and wished you had them back?” Dero scoffed. “I’m not like you, Core. I don’t fret over the demise of our beloved art form. The days of real wrestling are over, brother, and it’s best we just accept that. Yeah, sure, I think about it. But I don’t watch this crap that’s on TV any more. I don’t watch this crap your boss comes up with every week. It’s not wrestling. It’s big business. It’s damn capitalist America bullshit. Nothin’ but melodrama and tits n’ ass. I’ve got nothin’ against tits n’ ass, mind you, I’m just saying… there ain’t a real man left in the business.” “Then help me fix it, Big Daddy,” The Driver pleaded. “Come on. You help me do this, you help me prove I’ve still got what it takes to make it in this business, and I can get back in the game and start turning things around. I can show them that they’ve lost their way. I can make it so that real men can get back into the ring, and send all these catchphrase spouting, gimmicky pussies back to Hollywood.” A waitress finally walked up, looking rather ragged and intimidated. “Can I get you fellas something?” she asked politely, doing her best not to make eye contact with Dero. “Six eggs, over easy, bacon, grits,” Dero rattled out. The waitress scrambled to write his order down and quickly left. Dero looked across the table at his old friend and smiled. “Ass kickin’ food.” 6:05 PM The Driver had led Dero back to the arena, and using his credentials (as Kimbusa’s chauffer) got them inside. They headed down a busy hallway; the press was everywhere, along with countless ring hands preparing for the night’s big match. The Driver couldn’t help but feel angry by the whole situation; years ago, he and Dero entering the arena would have been a huge deal. But now he had to drop Steven fucking Caldera’s name to get in, and he and Dero navigated the halls utterly unrecognized. Fate had truly dealt them both a cruel hand. But tonight, he was holding aces. They found a quiet room somewhere in the interior of the arena and The Driver produced a small file folder from inside his jacket. “These are the guys,” he said, handing it to Dero. Dero opened the folder and flipped through the pictures. “Seriously? You’ve got to be fuckin’ kidding me. They really let this kind of garbage in the ring these days? These three fruits wouldn’t have lasted five minutes with me back in the day.” “You should see the guy we’re helping…” The Driver muttered. “You gonna be ok?” Reaching his leathery right hand into his jacket, Dero produced a pair of rusty brass knuckles and slipped them fondly over his fingers. “Yeah, bro. I’m gonna be just fine.” 6:23 PM The Driver finally tracked down Caldera in a private office he’d had set up in the locker area. Kimbusa hadn’t been seen by anyone; he was undoubtedly preparing for his match in whatever twisted way such a man prepares. The Driver’s hands were sweating; he trusted Dero absolutely, but if they were caught it could mean game over for good. Fortunately for him, Caldera was even more nervous than he was. That much he had mastered while with the Yakuza; he could hide in the shadows, cold and lifeless as a stone, waiting for his prey to move in range. After a few moments, The Driver spoke up. “Um, we really should be going. Caldera wanted to make sure you got to the arena on time.” Kimbusa was in no mood for conversation. He stepped across the room, picked up his duffel bag, and left the farmhouse. It was time to leave for his date with destiny. Tonight, he would become either a wrestler… or a coward. 1:28 PM The limo arrived at the airport and The Driver let Kimbusa out. Kimbusa had spent the ride in quiet contemplation; he was barely cognizant of the outside world, lost entirely in his own somber thoughts. But as he approached the plane, The Driver shouted out for him. Scarcely sure he’d heard him, Kimbusa turned. “Kimbusa, wait up,” The Driver called after him. “Look, I’ve been hearing… things. Things about tonight’s match. I just wanted to let you know that I could be of some service to you, if you’re interested.” Kimbusa was most certainly not interested. “I’ll take that to mean you’re interested. Don’t worry about tonight; I have a feeling things will transpire to swing the evening in your favor. Just don’t sweat it; I’ll take care of everything. And after the match is over, perhaps you and I could sit down and talk about the future.” Kimbusa’s face remained blank as he turned and headed for the plane. He looked up and saw Caldera tapping his watch impatiently; it was time to go. Did The Driver really just offer to help him? What could an aging former wrestler really have to offer him? For that matter, how could he really think that such an offer was becoming a man of the business? As a former wrestler, The Driver should know better; wrestling wasn’t about politicking or conniving maneuvering. It was about man to man combat, a test of wills in a pure environment. To suggest that he accept some outside influence was quite insulting; not to Kimbusa, but to the wrestling business itself. As Kimbusa entered the plane, he looked at Caldera and was again disgusted; he was traveling with two men who had lost sight of the true nature of pro wrestling. Kimbusa himself didn’t claim to know everything, for he was still but a novice. But Caldera and his Driver were two men who were clinging to the edges, seeking to distort it that they might hold on a bit longer. Caldera beckoned Kimbusa into a rear cabin. Reluctantly, Kimbusa followed, and Caldera closed the door behind them. Kimbusa faded again, staring out the tiny airplane window without taking a moment to observe his surroundings. But Caldera broke his trance. Everything all right?” Caldera asked proddingly. Kimbusa considered ignoring him, but he knew Caldera wouldn’t take the hint. “I would much prefer to be left alone.” Caldera came back with a chuckle. “Come on, Kimbusa, I just need to know that you’re prepared.” Kimbusa inhaled deeply, his disdain for the situation growing by the moment. “You needn’t fear for your investment. You will get your money’s worth.” Because that’s all it was to Caldera; an investment. Caldera himself was trying to live through Kimbusa’s accomplishments, but he had it all wrong. Kimbusa cared not for the Global Title, for the glory attached to it, or for creating a great spectacle. No, he was going to fight for the right to continue wrestling. Kimbusa did take some small consolation in the amusing notion that Caldera thought he could actually win. 5:22 PM The plane touched down and before he knew it, Caldera had whisked him away to the arena. The Driver promptly disappeared; Kimbusa began to wonder what he might be up to. He decided his meditations would have to wait; he’d need to keep his eyes open to ensure The Driver did nothing to compromise the match. If the match were in any way tainted, that would reflect on Kimbusa; and he needed this match to happen more than anything, regardless of the result. Kimbusa slipped away from Caldera the first chance he got; he needed time to think. The more he dwelled on the notion, the more concerned he grew that something was going to happen. He considered what The Driver had said to him, and before long it occurred to him that The Driver might not be acting alone. Caldera… At once Kimbusa felt quite insulted. It had hardly taken any thought at all to see through his transparent plan; one way or another, Caldera had set this thing in motion, whatever it was. Certainly they were arranging something to give Kimbusa an edge in the match, and that would severely compromise his goals. He quickly made up his mind to do something about it. He hated the notion of this night turning out to be anything other than a pure contest of skill, but soon he began to see a way to end the game for good. This was an opportunity to be sure; an opportunity to prove himself to these two misguided men and rid himself of the nuisance. He did his best to avoid attention; despite his size, he could quite easily avoid being seen when necessary. He lurked back towards the entrance, and after some time he saw The Driver enter the building. Behind him was a very tall man wearing a leather jacket and smoking a cigarette. He looked quite uncouth and dirty; he was clearly a rather unsavory character (quite an indictment coming from a former hitman). The Driver led the tall man through the hallways, and Kimbusa followed them at a safe distance. Finally they disappeared into an isolated room, and Kimbusa pressed his ear against the door. “Seriously? You’ve got to be fuckin’ kidding me,” he heard an unfamiliar voice say. “They really let this kind of garbage in the ring these days? These three fruits wouldn’t have lasted five minutes with me back in the day.” “You should see the guy we’re helping…” came the Driver’s voice. “You gonna be ok?” “Yeah, bro. I’m gonna be just fine.” Footsteps approached the door, and Kimbusa retreated back into the hallways. The tall man moved down the corridor and Kimbusa followed. The tall man was flipping through some photos, and even from his distance Kimbusa recognized the pictures of his opponents. It was fairly clear what was about to happen. Kimbusa slipped away from his pursuit and cut across towards the locker rooms. He moved swiftly and quietly; the art of silent pursuit was one that still came second nature to him, a fact that still troubled him. Though many would consider his skills quite useful, Kimbusa regarded them as a grim reminder of his true roots. Perhaps that was another trait of wrestling he admired; despite the corruption of the business, in the ring there was no subtlety, no treachery. Just man against man. Kimbusa waited for the tall man around a corner. There was a door nearby and he peered inside; it appeared to be empty. This was the spot. The tall man rounded the corner and immediately Kimbusa went to work. He struck the tall man in the face with a powerful palm thrust, then grabbed him by the greasy hair and flung him into the room. Kimbusa nimbly slid inside and locked the door. The tall man sprung to his feet, ready to attack; he had a pair of rusted brass knuckles on his right hand, cocked and ready to fire. But he hesitated when he recognized the man he was faced with. “Hey, you’re that-“ Kimbusa’s fierce glare cut him short. “What is your purpose here?” he demanded. The tall man smirked. “What, you don’t know? I’m just tenderizing these three meatbags for ya. So what the fuck was with the punch to the face? I’d kick your ass if they weren’t paying me to save it.” Kimbusa sneered. “Why do you do this? Why do you rob me of the chance to beat these three men?” The tall man looked confused. “What the fuck are you talkin’ about? I’m just givin’ you a little head start’s all. Besides, what they tell me, you can’t handle this yourself, you could use a little helping hand. So why don’t you just move your fat ass out of my way so I can get to work?” Kimbusa paused. He’d hoped it wouldn’t come to this. With a kick as fast as lightning, Kimbusa drove his foot into the underside of the tall man’s face. To his surprise, the tall man didn’t fall; he staggered back before charging forward hard. Kimbusa quickly sidestepped him and grabbed him by the head, driving him into the heavy metal door with all his power. The tall man instantly dropped to the floor, bleeding from the head. Kimbusa sighed and looked down at the unconscious man on the floor. They had been responsible for this. His job wasn’t done yet. 6:23 PM Kimbusa stood in a darkened room down the hall from Caldera’s office. He waited patiently for Caldera to exit, still unsure of what he would do when he got his hands on him. He had his Global Title belt with him now, and he’d dressed in his wrestling gear; he was not going to be late for his date with destiny. For a moment, Kimbusa found himself contemplating the wisdom of competing that night. What would it really prove? He was the most dangerous man in the wrestling world, there could be no disputing that. He didn’t need the confirmation of a bunch of American wrestling idiots to reaffirm that to himself. Nobody had seen him; he could walk out the door right now and preserve his image. NO. No. He would stand and fight. This night was not about winning and losing, it was not about the Global Title belt he held in his hands. It was about doing right for the sport he loved so much; it was about honoring it by competing no matter what. After some time, he watched as The Driver left the room. A pair of men came running by outside, blowing past Kimbusa and running by The Driver. The Driver quickly picked up his pace and followed, and Kimbusa knew it was time. He stepped out from the darkened room and crossed unseen to Caldera’s office. He moved inside but was dismayed to find that Caldera had gone. He looked at the clock on the wall; almost match time. Kimbusa opened one of Caldera’s desk drawers and produced a yellow pad of paper. Grabbing a nearby Sharpie, he wrote I know it was you. He left it there for Caldera to see; he wasn’t about to let Caldera enjoy his night without knowing what awaited him. Kimbusa slipped back out of the door and headed back for his darkened room. There he waited for some time, until Caldera returned back. Caldera entered, but emerged again before Kimbusa could make his move. Caldera walked right past the room and Kimbusa moved to give chase, but then he watched at The Driver entered Caldera’s office moments later. Again the door swung open shortly thereafter, and this time The Driver stepped out and moved down the hallway, not far behind Caldera. It was time to finish this. Conclusion Kimbusa emerged from his darkened room and followed The Driver. The Driver rounded a corner, and immediately bumped into someone moving swiftly in the other direction. The two men fell to the ground, as The Driver began apologizing frantically. Steven Caldera looked at The Driver with dismay as he collected himself. “What are you doing?” he demanded. The Driver picked himself up, confused. “What are you doing?” Caldera sneered at The Driver. “Was that Dero I just saw in the other room? How the hell did he get in here?” The Driver shrugged frantically; the plan had all gone to hell, and he needed a way out. “I have no idea what you’re talking about!” Caldera began to raise his voice in anger. The Driver had clearly failed at his task, and it was time to pull the plug. “Don’t give me that crap! He had photos of Kimbusa’s opponents! I want to know what the hell is going on!” Before The Driver could answer, a massive figure turned the corner. Both men fell silent as a tremendous shadow was cast over them. It was as if death itself was looking them in the eye, and at once their collective guilty conscience was on display. Kimbusa looked down at them both; bickering over their failed plan, unable to admit to each other their shared agenda, now caught in their failed scheme. The disgust that Kimbusa felt was nauseating; he felt as if he might gag on the stench of their treachery. “I know what went on here tonight. And for it I should strike you both down,” he said, feeling the fiery red glow enter his eyes. It was that same fire that burned every night on the mean streets of Tokyo, the fire that dictated his actions for so long, the fire that drove him to heinous acts of violence. But with a sudden realization, Kimbusa felt the rage subside. It was the realization that without lifting a finger in violence to the two men, he had surpassed them. “I will not hurt you now; I save my energy for the ring. But when I walk to the ring, I walk away from you, Caldera,” he said with a subtle venom. Caldera’s mouth opened to respond, but he found no words. “If I ever see you again, I will tell them what you tried to do here tonight, and I imagine you would look quite ridiculous,” Kimbusa sneered. “And as for me, I know what you are now. I know what you both are. You are fakes, you are frauds. You cling to this vision of what professional wrestling is by crafting elaborate schemes of violence; but your violence that takes place beyond the ring belittles the sport, belittles you. And you mock it by attempting to change it, either by innovation or regression. But you are both wrong. I go now to do you cannot do. I go now to fight not for your irrelevant plots, not for your flippant dismissal of the business. And I fight not to win. I fight now because to be a wrestler is simply to show up… and fight.” Kimbusa tossed the PTC Global Title belt over his shoulder for what would likely be the last time and walked away. The result of the match no longer mattered. He had done what he came to do. He had preserved the purity of the sport, he had conquered his last lingering doubts. He had shown up. He had proven what he came to prove. He was a wrestler.
