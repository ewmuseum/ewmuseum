Road To Triumph – Part IV: The Final Sacrifice

13 December 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-12-13 Road To Triumph – Part IV: The Final Sacrifice The barrel of the weapon shakes frantically in all directions. The woman wielding the weapon, Nurse Putrid, is unsettled and anxious. She hyperventilates wildly, out of control of her actions in her heightened emotions. I don’t know what to do. This is it. This woman would rather kill me than let me run off with another woman. She is bound to press the trigger any second now. “Nurse… calm down…” I say trying desperately to evade a shooting. “Everything will be okay… You just need to do this one thing for me… just put the gun down, and we can walk away from this, no harm, no foul… you can do this for me… right…?” “Shut up!” she says in hysteria. “Shut up! Shut up! Shut up, I will not be deceived by you! Not any more, you evil fuck! You were just going to run away with Wendy again, weren’t you? Well, what about me, huh? What about nurse? I’m just stuck here, on my own, without you, in the dark dingy basement with that slimy, miserable excuse for an evil doctor! Well I won’t have it! You’re mine, Dave! And there ain’t nothing you can do about it!” “But you can’t have me, Nurse. You can’t just go and take someone. It doesn’t work like that, the feelings that you have for someone has to be mutual. You have to love each other, not one or the other.” The nurse bows her head and lowers the gun to her waist. She cries uncontrollably with her hand gripping her hair and tugging on it in frustration. It’s time to stop her. It’s time to go and take the pistol from her hand, but I have to be really, really careful. I start to inch forward, step by step, with my hands outward in innocence. “It’s okay Nurse. There are plenty more fish in the sea… there are enough people out there that really do like very much to be whipped to shreds trust me… just not me.” “But it’s you I want, Dave. Not anybody else, nobody.” In heartbreak, and trying to clutch on to what little she has left, she raises the gun to me once more, aiming it directly at my head. “I’m sorry Dave,” she says, and her finger wraps around the trigger, she shuffles her hand for comfort, and readies to squeeze the trigger. BANG! -----=====|=====----- In the last moments before the trigger was pulled, the heartbroken nurse held the gun to her temple. A lightning bolt of remorse shoots through my body, sending a shiver down my spine. Blinking a small pitiful tear from my eye, I feel a sudden overwhelming feeling of guilt. “What have I done…?” This woman has taken her life, because she’d much rather be dead than live her life without me; all because of me; it’s all my fault. Wendy walks across to where I stand and wraps an arm across my back and grips my shoulder in consolation, pulling me to her. “It’s okay Dave. It’s not your fault. She needed some professional help, she wasn’t well.” “I wish I could think that way, Wendy. I should have done a better job of calming her down. I should have done a better job of stopping her from pulling the trigger. I should have…” “Shush…” Wendy cuts me off, trying to calm me down. “Dave, there’s nothing more you could have done. You did all you could, and that’s all that anyone could ask for.” I did do all I could. I tried as best I could to make that woman realise that there was plenty more opportunity to find love. And forcing it on someone was not the way to go. “You’re right,” I say solemnly, looking Wendy in the eyes, “Now we need to get out of here before the crazy doctor returns.” I get up and walk across the room to the counter beside the huge rocket. I grab Ducky, open the zip on my jacket and place him gently inside. We had done it, together, the three of us had managed to escape the claws of the evil doctor and now it was time to get out of here. Breathing a sigh of relief I turn around, ready to walk away from this dingy, stinky dungeon. “Not so fast…” says Wendy, her voice stern and deliberate, and somewhat sinister. Slowly, with a degree of care, I turn around. I have no idea why Wendy would talk like this, and then as if my bewilderment couldn’t get any greater, my eyes meet hers, and then I find out that once again… …I have a gun pointed at my head… -----=====|=====----- “What…? Wendy…? Wh-what are you doing…?” I ask her, bemused at any reason that she might have for pointing a gun at my head and not wanting to get out of this place. Wendy just stands there, silent, with her face completely and utterly serious. Her grip on the pistol getting tighter and tighter by the moment, she fully intends to blow my brains out. “What am I doing, honey?” she says, a slight chuckle escaping her lips as she starts to reveal her real intentions, and all of a sudden she seems incredibly amused by what she has to say. “I’m taking the one opportunity I have been waiting for since the very first moment we met when I worked for Furiously Addictive Wrestling.” “What…? What do you mean?” “Ha! Ha, ha, ha! I don’t believe it… you mean you can’t work it out? You really are a retard, aren’t you?” This was not like Wendy. This was not like Wendy at all. The sweet innocent news reporter I have known for the last three years suddenly disintegrates into a scheming, manipulative bitch. And it’s more and more apparent with each word escaping her lips. “You had the hots for me all the time we worked in FAW, you couldn’t help but accidentally pop up where I was and then deny wanting me to interview you. You couldn’t help but play bashful and silent, while I twisted and tormented you the whole damn time.” I don’t believe this… how could she possibly say all these things? What is she trying to say? “And then there was the opportunity of a lifetime… Exceptional Dave Hurst, the man who sees me as the woman of his dreams, about to make his way through a wrestling tournament and earn himself a small fortune, not just in prize earnings, but in lucrative sponsorship deals, further business ventures, movie roles, the whole works.” “So… this… this was all a scheme?” A smile of amusement crosses Wendy’s face as she knows her wicked plan is suddenly being realised by me, and it hurts. It hurts more than every one of those lashings from the nurse, it hurts more than that horrendous smashing over the head with the steel chair. How could she be saying these things? I thought she loved me… “It was more than just a scheme, honey. This was the perfect plan. I would step back into your life, we would hit it off, I would play the innocent news reporter you always knew and loved, and I would be set to earn millions.” How could she do such a carefully planned and callous act? Does the last two months we spent together mean nothing to her? Is there not a small, tiny little nook in a corner of her heart that is in any way guilty of what she has done to me? “So that’s it? You never for one moment had any lasting feelings for me? None, whatsoever? What about all the time we spent together? What about how you cried for me in the church when I was missing and possessed by the devil? What about when I lied in my bed, sick and broken and devoid of any motivation for my match with Odium, and you looked after me, and helped me get back on the right track? What about when you saved me from the boss of the underground gambling group, Jasper Achilles?” “All for the Benjamins, sweetie…” Wendy retorts in a razor sharp manner, the malice of her words slicing and dicing away at my heart and my feelings, “I know you find it hard to believe, that I would go to such lengths to ensure that you can continue in this tournament, but you don’t see what that investment means to me. With millions of dollars I can spend the rest of my life without worry, without boundaries or problems or strife. I don’t have to work my ass off and still be unsure whether the pay check will be enough for me to keep my apartment, or my cars, or my things. With millions I could use the money for my career to skyrocket, I could move up the social ladder and live the high life, like I always wanted to.” How could she do this to me, just for a large sum of money? I can’t believe the woman that I thought I’d spend the rest of my life with would just turn her back on everything that we had just for money. “And you’d be prepared to wreck the life of a poor innocent person, who loves you with all his heart, to do it?” “Fuck yeah! In a moment, buddy! Especially a puny dumb fuck like you! I mean look at you… you’re just an overgrown kid with mental health problems. You carry a duck with you that you believe is real. I mean how ridiculous is that? How could you possibly… in a million years… think that I could like you in any way, let alone love you? You’re pathetic.” My heart feels like it has imploded, it feels like she has reached through my chest to my heart and yanked it about in all directions until the only remains are a crushed flat whimpering mush. I can’t believe she would do such a thing. Has she no remorse? Trying desperately to come to terms with what has just happened, my eyes water up and tears start to dribble down each of my cheeks leaving a wet sliver in its wake. It’s incredible to think that all of the times that we had spent together in the last few months, all the great happy and triumphant moments, all of the happy joyful moments we had ahead of us, the plans that we had, the things that we were going to do together, that we seemed destined to do together, all stolen from me, stolen from me by one sudden moment of pure unadulterated greed. But wait a second… if she wants to earn money from my winnings, why doesn’t she want me to escape to compete in my match? If I lose I gain nothing… “I have one more question, Wendy. If you want to earn so much money from me defeating Craig Goat and Doctor Curiosity, why are you making sure I stay captive, instead of letting me escape and win my match?” “Good question, Einstein. As with any great scheme and every great plan, there is always a backup plan. Well… firstly, I’ve watched the tapes of each and every opponent in this tournament, I’m a wrestling columnist; it’s my job. I know each and every person in this tournament and exactly what they are capable of, and I’m afraid to say buddy… but you just don’t cut it.” Wendy plays with her pistol, letting it dance around her fingers like a cheap impression of a gunslinger from a western movie. She wanders towards me, shaking the pistol at me taunting me with each time the pistol aims in my direction. “You’re good. I even thought you might be the best, but I’m afraid that you aren’t quite good enough for Doctor Curiosity. He has a great wrestling skill and ability matched with an incredulous strategy, and he will bend and play and even break the rules in order to gain a victory. And THAT… will be what clinches the match.” Wendy pauses, basking in the glory of her great observational skills, an arrogant, smug, conniving little bitch. “You still haven’t answered my question.” I inform her. She was probably going to continue, but at least it stops her gloating in silence. Plus I just want to get this over with, I want answers… now. I’m not going to be tormented with a lack of information any longer. “Well, I was given an opportunity for an excellent plan B, and I took it. Last round, when I paid back Jasper Achilles the money owed to him. Do you really think that Achilles would leave it at that? Gamblers aren’t interested in breaking even they want to make money… lots of money, as do I… so we came to a great agreement. He leaves me be and gives me a large sum of money for my effort, and I make sure that you don’t show up to the GTT final. Do you realise how much money Jasper is set to gain for this? It would increase the amount of money he would have earned for the semi-final tenfold.” I didn’t think it was possible, but my heart sinks even lower. My throat fills up with soreness, my tears stream out in floods, I fall to my knees, push my hands to my face obscuring it from her. This is it. Everything I ever wanted is gone. The love of my life, gone in a flicker thanks to her mischievous, treacherous ways; all my hopes of anything in my career, achieving the greatest prize I will ever achieve in my entire life, gone thanks to the barrier between me and success materialised into a small pistol, held by the woman who at one point made me feel like the happiest man alive, and now makes me feel like the worst. -----=====|=====----- A click sounds from behind Wendy Sanchez, the door unlocks. The door swings open and walking into the basement oblivious of everything that has recently unfolded is the evil doctor. He enters the room, seeing Wendy wielding the pistol, he realises that she has finally made me fully aware of the truth. He bellows a loud deep evil chuckle, trying his best to aggravate my intense depression. “Ah Vendy, I see you have zis fully under control now. I trust you are going to give me ze money, jah?” “Of course I will, Chris. It will be sent to your account first thing tomorrow morning.” As the doctor walks further into the room, he sets eyes on something that shocks him and makes him step back gasping at what appears on the floor in front of him. Laying there with a pool of blood seeping from beneath her head and a round, red-rimmed bullet hole as clear as day showing on the side of her head, is his beloved wife, Nurse Putrid. “Vhat the…?” he drops to his knees by her side, reaches over and cups under her head, bringing her head up and her hair out of the pool of blood. He looks into her eyes, taking in the last moments he can of the overwhelming beauty that he sees in her. She may not have loved this man, but he certainly adored her. A small tear drips in his eye and falls from his face, making a small splash over her round, motionless cheek. He sniffles, and then all the feelings he has is suddenly transformed from sadness and sorrow into a blazing explosion of vengeance. His face turns from that of a depressed man that has lost his love, into a raging, vindictive man ready to wreak havoc on whoever was responsible. “Who… did zis… to my vife…” controlling his anger to not scream it at the top of his lungs, he waits patiently for an answer, like a bull in his pen ready to be let loose into the bullring, and me the matador is all dressed in red… “He did it,” the conspiring bitch says bluntly. The doctor looks up at me, with his eyes squinted, his lips pursed and his chest raising and falling like the sea in a raging storm. And then suddenly, he leaps to his feet, rushing over to Wendy, and before she could do anything he snatches the pistol from her hands, placing it into his own… and swinging it in my direction. Without any hesitation whatsoever, he squeezes hard on the handle, and pulls his finger back on the trigger. BANG! -----=====|=====----- It is strange how things never turn out how you expected; how when all things seem inevitable, how when all odds are against you, somehow, some way, something happens and fate changes. When that gun went off, and the ear-splitting crack seemed to repeat over and over again, the visions of the bullet rushing through the air and colliding into my chest repeating in my head over and over again, the look of relief and joy on the faces of those two people as I fell to the floor clutching my chest with the overwhelming pain of the bullet entering me haunting me, I thought that I was dead. But I did not die that night. That night, when the bullet hit me, it was headed directly for my heart. There was not any way that that bullet could enter my heart and not render me dead. But something stopped that. As the bullet tore the fabric of my jacket on its way to my chest, it collided with an object and deflected away from my heart. The result was a glancing blow that only left me with a large gash on the side, under my arm. The whole situation, heightened by a bullet that I thought was just killing me, caused me to faint. And now I sit on the bench of the changing room at the GTT arena, awaiting my time to step out and compete in the very last match in this competition, the end of this road to triumph. I should be trying to prepare myself for the match. I have Duck & Run by 3 Doors Down playing through the PA system by request, it’s the one track that I always play before I ever compete in matches to get me in the mood. But now I’m not in the mood, my mind is not on the match, my mind has wandered to the object that has given me the opportunity of being here tonight. The object that had deflected the bullet that was sure to kill me, decimating it in the process. The object that is a small, plastic yellow duck… Yes, that’s right. Ducky took the bullet. He took it for me, to save me from my impending death, the faithful sidekick taking that one last sacrifice to give me the opportunity to be still standing here today. My great, brave little trooper… I break down into tears, these past few days have been emotional, I have lost a great deal in the process, but the one thing that has stuck by me through all the good times and all the bad, the one thing that has never turned on me when things are going badly, who never dumped me for a sum of money like that whore of a wrestling reporter. The one thing that would do anything for me, would put his own life on the line to save me, his greatest friend, is dead. The chorus of the music track runs through my head, making me ever more emotional. We used to listen to this music together, me and Ducky, as I was about to step out for each match, with him by my side. Now that he’s not here, now that he’s not by my side, I feel a void. I feel like I’m missing a vital organ, like a part of me that I can’t live without has been taken from me. But I cannot think this way… In a matter of moments I’m about to step out of this dressing room, and into the ring, to compete for the greatest prize in all of wrestling. To be looked at and considered the greatest wrestler in the world, if only for that one moment my arms are raised in victory. Ducky wouldn’t have wanted me to sit moping around. He wouldn’t have wanted me to step out, go to the ring and be too broken up by the death of my greatest friend that I couldn’t compete. He would have wanted me to give it all I had. So that’s what I’m going to do. The end of the road is here, I’m in the driver’s seat. Now all I have to do is drive over that finish line. The time is now, and I will be triumphant. I stand up, jump up and down on the spot getting myself warmed up and ready, loosened and prepared. There is a knock at the door, a loud wrapping alarming me that the time is now. “Dave?” the young voice of one of the ring crew shouts through the door over the loud music from the PA system. “It’s time.” I clasp the handle to the door and pull the door open. As I start to step through the door I pause, the music reaching a crescendo, the chorus ringing through my ears sounding ever the more appropriate. I won’t duck and run… I won’t duck and run… I won’t duck and run… no, no no. This world can turn me down, But I won’t turn away, And I won’t duck and run, ‘Cause I’m not built that way, Everything is gone, There is nothing there to fear, This world cannot bring me down, No ‘cause I’m already here. THE END
