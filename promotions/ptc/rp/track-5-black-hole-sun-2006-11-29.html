Date: 2006-11-29 For a recap of the series and character sketches, check my bio. *** *** *** September 16, 2006 *** *** *** Anthony "Anthony! What the hell is going on!?" Maria’s shrill voice was nothing more than static in the black chaos of Anthony Dugaze’s mind. He hurried to jam six shells into his revolver - four made it in. It wasn’t the most "gangster" of weapons, but it would have to do for now. "Anthony Dugaze!" The police officer’s voice was sharp from the other side of the door. "Come on now, son - lets not make this any worse than it already is. We have a warrant." "Get the kids down to the basement!" Anthony barked at his wife. He was pressed flat against the wall next to the front door, his heart beating to break the sound barrier in his chest. "They’re asleep!" "Get the fucking kids!" The officer’s voice came through the door again: "Just come on out of there, Anthony." "Fuck off!" He heard the officer try the knob again, and once more, he found it locked. Anthony Dugaze was no punk - if he had to go down, he was going down fucking kicking and screaming. His hands were trembling; his mind - a blender. I’m no fucking coward! He told himself. Men in our thing don’t go out like fucking chumps! From her apartment on the other side of town, Angela Alissandro must have called the fucking pigs. Anthony pictured the cunt crawling across her kitchen floor toward the telephone, blood streaming from her busted mouth down her chest - the child in her womb scattered to nothing. That’s probably how the police found her, screeching and wailing with her left tit exposed from the damage Anthony had done to her clothes in the heat of the moment. Somehow, he couldn’t help but think it looked worse than it really was. And there was no time for regrets now anyway. "Anthony!" Maria’s half-shriek busted through Anthony’s wall and pulled him back into the moment. "I said get the fucking kids," he hissed. "Don’t make me say it again." She didn’t make him say it again. After one last look of desperate confusion, Maria hurried up the stairs to wake their four children (if they weren’t awake already) and usher them quickly back down and into the basement. He watched her go before inching closer to the door. It would only be a moment before the police inevitably started kicking it down, he supposed. He knew they would succeed; he also knew that in the end, they would take him - but that first fucker through the door was a dead man. "There’s no where to go, Dugaze," the officer was standing right outside the door. Anthony could just live the revolver to a height with him and blow his brains all over the front fucking lawn. "We have the place surrounded." Of course they had the place surrounded. Anthony had seen enough of his friends taken down (and enough movies) to know how warrants were carried out when the suspect was considered dangerous. Somehow that thought made Anthony feel a bit of pride - he was considered dangerous. And then the image of his house being surrounded lead his thoughts to his garage. Were there officers already in there? Or were they standing outside the garage door? Either way, Anthony left the front door of his house and hurried toward the den and through the exit to his garage. He hoped to find a police officer there - hoped to find him and kill him. But when he busted through the door and into his garage, he found only his black Sedan, and no one to kill. They’re on the other side of the door, thought Anthony as a sadistic smile crept across his face. Immediately, he ran down the steps to the garage floor, past the scattered tools and kids’ toys, and hopped into his Sedan. He knew he was going to have to act fast because as soon as the officers outside heard the engine start, they’d be all over him. Anthony searched his person for the keys and jammed them clumsily into the ignition. As soon as the car started, he jerked it into drive and his foot slammed the gas pedal right down to the fucking mat. The car crashed through the closed garage door like a bullet firing out the barrel of a gun, sending a police officer that had been standing outside crashing violently over the hood, cracking his head off the windshield along the way. "Fucking motherfuckers!" Anthony screamed from a state of half insanity. He heard gunshots behind him as he screeched away; he heard bullets ricochet off the black metal of the car’s exterior. One smashed through the back window and pierced a perfect, tiny hole through the front. Through it all, Anthony Dugaze laughed hysterically, his foot choking the life out of the gas pedal on the floor, jammed down so tight against the mat that the engine screamed in response. He laughed so hard that his throat was soon stripped raw and he felt hot tears running rivers down his cheeks. He laughed like a madman; he was a madman. He cut the wheel sharply to avoid a police car and took out half of the picket fence around his lawn as the Sedan jumped the curb to the street. He’d babied the car for years, and now in the last twenty seconds, it had all gone to waste. Not that it mattered - Anthony figured he was about to die anyway, and the Sedan wouldn’t be much use to him in hell. He sped through his neighborhood streets toward the highway. It wasn’t long before he heard sirens in the distance and saw the flashing red and blue lights whipping against the black canvas of the cold November sky. They were far behind him, he realized - very far. He could cut down a back street and perhaps even lose them long enough to double back and head toward the south highway. That was the first time it occurred to Anthony that he might actually make it. Up until that point, he had accepted that these were the last moments of his life. But if he could outrun them just long enough to find Johnny Silver… Johnny would make everything better. Johnny would hide him out long enough to make Angela, that cunt, disappear. Maybe… just maybe, he could beat this out yet. And then some old bitch in a little white tempo ruined everything. She didn’t even glance to her left as she pulled out of her driveway, and Anthony’s Sedan barreled into her driver’s side door. Metal crunched and shrieked and then the biggest fucking marshmellow Anthony had ever seen exploded out of his steering wheel and cracked him in the face like a God damn sledge hammer. As hot as his tears were, the blood from his busted nose was hotter. Fuck, he thought he cursed, but in reality the word was lost somewhere between his brain and his mouth. With all the adrenaline drained from his body, Anthony couldn’t even muster the energy to pull his face out of the fucking airbag. He heard footsteps outside the car. Shouting voices. "Anthony Dugaze," he heard a male officer outside of the car, huffing his breaths. "You’re under arrest." *** *** *** September 21, 2006 *** *** *** Emily To get her mind off Jamie French and his pretty, sparkling eyes, Emily pulled something out of Raven’s bag of tricks - she cleaned. She started in the kitchen; washed every dish and utensil, scrubbed the floor, and organized (and then reorganized) each of the cupboards. She even found a plastic rose that would look nice on the table until she got a chance to get out and pick up some real flowers. Then she moved on to the living room where she arranged the bookshelves and DVD case, vacuumed twice over, and dusted every inch of varnished furniture until she could see her reflection in it. The bedrooms came next and then both bathrooms. Finally, her restless compulsion lead her to the attic where she had a thought to bust dust bunnies and reorganize old boxes. But when she got there, she found her sister. Raven’s clothing, her jewelry, her beloved stuffed animals were all gathered in the far corner attic, illuminated by the soft setting sunlight that poured through the lone window. The clothing was unfolded, piled in open boxes and on top of closed ones. Her dresses were strewn over still more boxes against the wall; pink, purple, and red pastel colors. Her wedding dress hung from a hook beneath the window, abandoned with care. Emily felt her heart bulging in her throat, and for the briefest of moments, she thought she smelled vanilla. Her footsteps creaked as she crossed the floor and closed in on her sister’s possessions. She could feel her hands trembling. There was a pearl necklace draped over one of the dresses; dust from years of neglect had gathered on it. Emily picked it up with her trembling hand and stared at it for a moment, trying to recall an instance where she might have seen her sister wearing it. She couldn’t. With almost absent motion, Emily rubbed the pearls softly against her cheek. Her sister’s pearls. They evoked memories from her youth; Raven had always been more a mother to Emily and Danielle than their real mother had ever been. When they cried, it was Raven who comforted them. When they broke the rules, it was Raven who scolded them. They went to Raven for advice. And all the while they could never understand why she was so hopelessly in love with the brooding, angry young man, Jason Snow. Emily slipped the pearls around her neck and closed her eyes. "I’m telling you, Emily, boys are more trouble than they’re worth," Raven says while gently rocking Haley, just a year old, to sleep in a small, cozy rocking chair next to the crib. Emily is eleven years old and her eyes are fixed, much as they always seem to be, on the child in her sister’s arms. Haley has been fighting sleep all night, but now she’s finally losing the battle, wrapped comfortably in the arms of a mother she’ll have for only these first two years of her life. Raven looks hardly old enough to be a mother at all. She’s a difficult girl to put an age on. Some moments, like this one, she looks forever sixteen. But there are other moments she looks like she might be thirty. Her complaints about "boys" seem light-hearted enough, but Emily can see that they’re deeper than that. It will be much later that Emily will find out she’s right. She does not know, at eleven, that Jason is in prison for an aggravated assault. Raven thinks she hides her pain will, but Emily is her sister - she sees through Raven’s face like glass. "They’re messy, they can’t do anything on their own, and they stink!" She goes on, her eyes turned toward the window on the far end of the room next to a giant mural of Mickey Mouse. "And they never get their hair cut when you tell them to!" Emily wonders why Raven bothers with Jason at all. Other than Danielle, the entire family seems to either hate him or be afraid of him. Emily’s not sure if she hates him, but he scares her more than anyone she’s ever met in her life. "…And you’re always waiting for them…" Raven sighs, her big, beautiful eyes filled with so much pain that Emily wants to cry. "So why do you wait?" Emily asks, biting her lower lip. "Why don’t you just… you know…" "I’ve tried that before," her sister sighs again in her cartoon-like high-pitched voice. She shakes her head and for a fraction of her moment, she forgets to mask her pain. "But in the end, you always find out that you don’t have a choice. You need them just as much as they need you…" Emily set the pearls back down. He still loves her, she thought sadly of Jason. He’s still in love with her… The realization had set on her like the slow fading light through the trees. His dead wife’s belongings were piled - abandoned - in a corner of the attic, and judging by the dust, she doubted he’d ever mustered the courage to ever return to them. In her mind, she agonized over the image of Jason bringing them up a box at a time - the man she spent her entire youth being terrified of… the man her sister had loved so much for reasons that Emily could never understand. And now she understood. He would have preferred to have her in the passenger seat, but all the books said that kids were supposed to sit in the back until they were much older, and much larger, than Haley. "Maybe sometime next week," Jason replied. "I’m going to be pretty busy over the next few days." "Why?" "Because I’m going to be a wrestler again," he told her, and then he checked his rearview mirror to gauge her reaction. He had a meeting set up with the suits at PRIME to talk about his return to the ring. It was a bitter after-thought that he’d be kicking up a large portion of his contract to Johnny Silver and Justin Hattoni - even worse, he had to send a bit Dante Gasperoni’s way as restitution for the broken fingers incident. "You were a wrestler before?" It often surprised Jason how little his daughter knew about his former life, but then, she was only five, and so much had happened before she was born and in the time that she was a baby. "A long time ago," he replied, looking into her forever-innocent blue eyes in the rearview mirror. Three years was not really that long, he supposed, and he’d still be younger than most of PRIME’s roster upon his return, but that all felt like another life. "Well, can Emily take me?" "Emily can’t drive." "I’ll bet she could if she tried," Haley replied, turning to her window. There’s so much of your mother in you, he thought. Getting back into the wrestling ring had been a constant in Jason’s mind lately. He’d often get so lost in memory that he could hear the crowd - the nights they loved him and the nights they didn’t. Haley had missed all of that. There were only faded photographs and old magazine articles to prove her father’s short-lived glory. But not for long, he thought confidently. He had no doubt he could still do it. In fact, he thought it was likely he’d be better in this second installment of his wrestling career. He was a man in his prime now, rather than a kid who should have been in college. "Hazen’s mom is nice," said Haley. Jason looked to the rearview mirror again. Hazen’s mom had spent a fair amount of time on his mind as well. Alyssa… sweet, kind - definitely attractive. And he thought she had been sending him signals too, but then, Jason had never been good at picking up on things like that. He’d only really been in one relationship in his life, with Raven, and that one had come so naturally that there was no need for "signals." "You like her?" "Yeah," she replied. When Jason checked the rearview mirror this time, he found himself staring directly into his daughter’s stunning blue eyes. "My mom was nice too, right?" These moments always felt like a bone lodged in his throat. "You know she was," his voice cracked. "I’ve told you that." "Sometimes I forget," she replied. He knew it wasn’t true. Jason often felt guilty for not speaking with Haley more about her mother, but those conversations were like hooks in his heart, and often they brought him back to the original agony he’d felt the moment he heard the news. Still, he knew that Haley deserved more from him; Raven was remembered most for her maternal instincts. From the time he met her, when she was just fourteen, she had always had a way with children. He thought now that perhaps that had been what had initially attracted him to her. Maybe it was a male instinct. Jason had asked Haley once, a year ago, if she could remember her mother at all. "A little," Haley had replied, tucked into her bed and thinking hard. "I remember her arms. I remember the way she smelled. I think of her when I see flowers." Fucking flowers, Jason thought now, yet again, with a subtle shake of his head. They were tied so strongly to the memory of his wife that he couldn’t bare to look at them anymore. "Hazen says his dad visits sometimes," Haley went on. And then she added, almost under her breath: "I wish my mom could." They drove in silence the rest of the way home. *** *** *** September 27, 2006 *** *** *** Derek Anthony Dugaze sat stone-faced, sweating beneath the FBI’s hot-light in the county prison’s second interview room - sweating beneath the weight of his situation… sweating beneath the weight of his crime, maybe, but Derek doubted that. Dugaze was a statue, sitting with his cuffed hands folded on the desk in front of him while Derek circled the room. He had the eyes of a killer. Derek had no doubt that he had murdered the unborn child in Angela Alissandro’s womb; he wondered if there was a shred of remorse in the man. "The girl’s been in the hospital for a week," Derek informed him. Dugaze said nothing. "The baby’s dead, but you know that," he smirked, circling back in front of the killer now. "You knew that the day you killed it. What you might not know is that under state precedent, your aggravated assault charge is probably going to get bumped up to murder. I’m not sure if the city police told you that." Anthony Dugaze’s dead brown eyes snapped to life - upward, for just a moment, to meet Derek’s, and then they died again, focusing on the door. That got your attention, Derek thought, you sick fuck… "And then, of course, the old lady in the car wreck? It’s touch and go with her right now, but if I had to guess, I’d say she’s not going to make it. And then the cop you hit…" Derek leaned in so close he could smell the prick’s breath. "You’re looking at twenty years, you slimy fucker - minimum. My guess? You don’t ever get out. You’re going to fucking die in here, Dugaze." Anthony Dugaze’s face remained a stone, but Derek plainly saw the color drain from it. Sociopath that he was, the man was still human. Derek wondered if he might be getting ready to lash out; maybe he was thinking of pulling another stunt like the one he pulled on the night of his arrest. "…Or some place like it," Derek went on. He reached inside his suit-jacket, pulled out a folded piece of paper, and held it up on display. "See what I’ve got here? This is a court order. A transfer order, more specifically. All typed up and waiting for a judge’s signature. And believe me, no judge in his right mind is going to deny an FBI request to transfer a prisoner that we deem highly dangerous to those around him. You ever hear of the Tank, Dugaze?" "What do you want?" the statue moved its lips and spoke. "That’s good," leered Derek, placing the order (which was actually an e-mail from his wife that he’d printed that morning) back in his jacket. "I feel like we may actually be able to have a conversation now." "I asked you a fucking question." "See? This is a solid rapport." Derek smiled at the prick sarcastically. "We want John Marione. More than him, we want Justin Hattoni." "Why the fuck would I know about those guys?" "Don’t fuck with me, Dugaze," Derek leaned on the table again. "You’ve been under surveillance for years. You know it - and we know you know it." "Fuck what you know," Dugaze spat. "I’ve talked to Hattoni maybe five times in my fucking life. I ain’t that high up." "That’s too bad," Derek shrugged. "You give me Hattoni, and things can go a lot smoother for you. But then again, I’m in a generous mood today - give me Johnny ‘Silver’ Marione, alone, and I’ll see what I can do." "I don’t know anything about that," the stubborn prick replied, such a transparent lie that Derek wanted to jump over the desk and throttle him. But all he said was: "Alright then," and then he straightened himself. "If you change your mind, I’m not hard to get in touch with." That should have ended it. Derek should have walked out the door right then and had Paul Cyr type up a real transfer order for a judge to sign, sending Dugaze’s sociopathic ass straight to the Tank, where he fucking belonged. He should have called it a day, but instead he lingered a moment too long, studying the prick’s face for a sign of weakness - a sign that he might crack, finally giving Derek the "in" to the Hattoni family he’d been after for so long. But for all of his faults, Anthony Dugaze was as loyal as stone. There was no weakness in him - not the kind of weakness Derek was looking for anyway. "Funny," Anthony spoke again, smirking, just before Derek opened the door. "Where I come from, you people are the ones robbing cars and ‘busting caps.’ Now here you are in a fucking suit trying to play cop. The FBI put a new affirmative action plan in place?" Derek stopped cold and his eyes turned to ice. "What the fuck did you just say to me?" For his entire career, Derek Saunders had been breaking down the boundaries and stereotypes that came along with the color of his skin. He always had to work twice as hard as the other agents around him to get to his current position. He was one of only two black men in his entire department. He’d worked much too hard to take shit off homicidal fucks like Anthony Dugaze. "Just saying," the piece of shit went on as Derek turned to get a look at him over his shoulder. "It’s good, you know? But I mean, you’ve got to admit, it’s a fucking trip to see a moolie in a suit." Derek turned around slowly. Would anyone really care if he just snapped this fucker’s neck? He could do it so easily - he’d been trained to do it, in fact. And the world would be a better place to be rid of Anthony Dugaze - a man that kicked his mistress’ pregnant stomach until the child growing inside her died. Derek had no doubt that it was only the latest in a long string of vile acts… Maybe he didn’t have to kill the son of a bitch. Maybe just one shot to the face - a broken nose, maybe… But all Derek did was tighten his jaw and clench his fists. "I strongly advise you to say nothing more," his voice cracked. "My lips are sealed," Dugaze smirked. "Have fun in the Tank, you piece of shit."
