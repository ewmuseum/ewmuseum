Or Die Trying….(A Back Alley Brawler Noir)

18 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-18 [name withheld] "Fuckin’ blood…jus’ great." He did it. He stepped in the ring with the former WWA World champion, Kash, a true legend in his home federation…and won. The odds were against him, the dirt sheets had labeled him the loser he already knew he was….and he won. And despite his knee wishing to terminate the bodily partnership between the two, he walked in and actually managed to WALK out. At that moment though, he wish he hadn’t. Praying to the BronxBar’s Porcelain God, or the toilet for the uneducated, the Brawler had somehow managed to find himself in an even more desperate situation then collapsing on the floor of O’Malley’s, unable to rise to his towering 6’6 stature for some time. The Brawler was on his knees, the grout lines of the tiled floor digging into his Dickies work pants, watching on as blood forced its way through his lungs and out his mouth. The shit-stained toilet reminded him of what Hell wished it smelled like, the dingy toilet water now a crimson soup courtesy of The Brawler’s failing innards. Yes he came out victorious, but Kash had taken more out of the Brawler then he had left to give. If the Back Alley Brawler had to describe his health as a bank account, it would have been a negative 100-grand balance. The worst part was he couldn’t even remember getting to the bathroom at all. The shocked roar of the crowd after his surprise 3 count on Kash still rang in his ears, but after hitting the curtain, the past day and a half was a blur that Joe Balboa wasn’t sure he wanted to remember. The Brawler paint-brushed the callous mitt known as his "hand" across his mouth; a juicy cocktail of blood and saliva quickly crusting on his backhand. The Brawler did his best to shake off the wailing pain behind his rib cage, looking over with winced eyes and noticing a GTT program, the ONLY item of any substance in the dilapidated bathroom. The native New Yorker reached his hand out and seized the program in hand, showing no real interest in the slick Global Title Tourney logo or any of the round 2 matches. Even in his disoriented state, he had enough sense to look for his own name….and there it was…. ….Next to Seymour Almasy’s. "What asshole on a cloud up there keeps fuckin’ wit me wit dis bulls-" His comment was quickly interrupted by more blood erupting from his mouth, Brawler managing to deposit it in the toilet just in time. His patience had expired twenty minutes ago in regards to his condition, the Brawler knowing what had to be done if he expected to be able to put up any fight. Digging in his back pocket, the Brawler unearthed an orange bottle with at least 100 tablets of Percocet. He eyed the bottle the way a pedophile eyes a playground during recess, knowing he was nothing without the "magic pills". As downhill as his battle with Kash was, it would have been hopeless without the aid of his army of painkillers. The Brawler had already broken the childproof seal on the bottle for easy access, the cap launching to the floor with a flick of his thumb. His need for the pills only intensified when he thought of what he would be facing in a few short days. Normally the Brawler never made it a point to sweat warriors the size of Seymour Almasy. But nobody his size had his heart. That heart worried the Brawler. Joe Balboa’s attempts to understand Seymour’s success always equated to pounding a square peg into a circle hole. He could remember multiple times when the Final Fantasy should have been fertilizer for a cemetery somewhere, yet somehow always managed to come out on top and ever stronger. It scared Joe. It scared him because Joe used to have what Seymour had, drive just because. No reason, no cause, just because it was natural instinct with no trigger. What could the Brawler do to a man who had experienced EVERY manner of physical pain possible, with all the T-shirt’s to show for it? The answer came to Joe as he pieced what was left of his broken will back together. The clarity rivaled Alaskan water as he poured 10 Percocets in his free hand. His lazy eyes had found a spark of hope with his little white partners now by his side. "I cain’t lose…not now….not eva’. Almasy might have da heart. But I’m jus’ gonna havta rip it out an’ eat it. I kno you guys’ll help ol’ Brawla out." The ends justified the means. Who gives a fuck about a prescription of up to 4 a day with everything the Brawler had to stand up to. Could Carmen afford not to have Christmas presents this year because daddy didn’t go the distance to see another check would be waiting in the mail box for her? Could the Brawler live down the fact that he was beaten by Seymour Almasy, knowing full well that just a pill or two more could have turned his degrading loss into a surprise victory? How long could he let his body mistreat him before he took the initiative to silence it? Percocet was the answer and the answer was shoved into his mouth all at once. The Back Alley Brawler chewed on all ten tablets, until they formed a bitter paste in his mouth. The Bronx Barbarian would have preferred to guzzle the tainted toilet water then force down the Percocet paste, but it was the cure for what ailed him… The cancerous tumor that was the need to win. Finally he swallowed, getting up on one knee as he placed an elbow on the toilet for support. He looked across from where he situated, finally noticing a cracked full length mirror across from the toilet. "Now what muhfucka wants ta’ look at himself takin’ a shit?" The crack was dead in the center, probably from a patron who didn’t like what they saw, namely their reflection. The cracks created a "spider’s eye view" of the beholder, a kaleidoscope of Brawler reflections all of his decrepit shape. Anger slowly boiled inside as he saw what he had become, every reflection equally displaying a weathered black male with Salvation Army worthy clothes, airline luggage under his eyes, and a crusted dribble of the aforementioned Percocet paste and blood. No matter which reflection he gazed at, it was the same fucking loser in every one. "Yea, I hate all yous muhfuckas too." The brace on his knee made an ugly creak as he rose to his feet, the Percocet doing the work it was meant to do as the pain subsided, at least for now. Turning his back to the mirror he stepped to his right and went to hand sink, which brandished an unhealthy amount of rust in the bowl. He turned the cold water knob, the hot water knob ripped off in a brute manner apparently with only a stripped screw to show for it, letting the water rush into his open hands and splashing it on his face. It was the icy sting he needed to get the ball rolling. Maybe it was a sign, cold water always serving as a reminder to the Back Alley Brawler of what defeat felt like. But with the magic pills and the Bronx behind him, Seymour would just have to settle for the laundry list of other accolades he had achieved. GTT5 was just not his destiny. Cleansed of the crust around his mouth and at the ready for the world, the Brawler picked up his extremely subtle "I’m A Lesbian" trucker hat and placed it on his head. "Now fo’ da breakfast uv champyuns….Rum & Coke." The Brawler made his way to the exit, but not before looking back to that same broken mirror once more. Despite the reflection changing, the image still haunted him of what he saw earlier. It was one of the few times the Brawler looked like as much of a loser as he felt. And that sight scared him. Only the death of his daughter could scare him more than the image that had now been drilled into his head. He turned his head and opened the bathroom door, stepping out into the world of the BronxBar and leaving his demons behind in the bathroom. By no means were the demons gone, merely waiting until the next time his body failed him. And they would be there to remind him of the price of failure. 1 Hour Later Watching the world pass me by / God gave me arms, but I won’t reach for the sky / Days like these make me want to die / Because I realize life is one big lie / The Brawler did not look like it, but he paid close attention to the words of the ailing folk singer on the stage. An ancient man with sickly warts over his face, he sat on his wooden stool and lightly strummed his guitar, musing in rhyme stories of sadness. For as much as the Brawler hated country music, he always loved the stories they had to tell, this song absolutely no different. It almost made Joe think that that only reason the folk singer remained in existence was to remind anyone within earshot that the world really is shit, despite what TV or those silly folk known as "optimists" would lead you to believe. "How do I beat dis’ muhfucka. It’s gonna take more den mah magic pills, I need ta fuckin’ crush dis fool." For an hour since emerging from the bathroom, the Brawler had been tossing back glasses of Rum & Coke in a corner booth of BronxBar, with the intention of erecting a game plan. So far, he had only managed to reach a new level of drunk known as "shit-stormed", but he had yet to let that set in yet. His empty glasses formed a chorus line, with his last full glass about ready for it’s solo. It was just then that it hit him. The stench of sulfer, feces, and mustard rolled into one potent cocktail. There was plenty of cigarette smoke about the bar, but nothing this nerve-wrenching. No cigarette company had the good sense to make a cancer stick THAT foul, as if eventual death wasn’t enough. "I was hoping I could have a seat." The Brawler looked up, his face construed with disgust as he laid eyes on a man draped in an all black coat, his face and skin as pale as the proverbial corpse. He was the source of the stench, but it wasn’t even that which kept the focus of the Brawler. It was his blood-shot eyes of crimson, forming twisted webs about his pupils. Not since the Vercelli Mob set up operations one apartment complex away from his family’s home, had he been this uneasy about an entity in his life. "I gotta be honest witcha bro’. I don’t kno whetha I need ta tell ya to take a bath, knock yous da fuck out, or buy you a drink. Good news fa you tho. I’ve been havin’ quite da shitty week and I need a laugh, so sit down. What ya want? The man merely smiled, raising up his coat so he could have a seat in the booth as well. "You wanted ta talk, talk." The Brawler punctuated his statement with a swig of his drink, letting it burn his insides as he smiled in delight. The mysterious man smirked, looking lost for words as to what to say next. Totally out of his nature, the Back Alley Brawler waited patiently for this man to speak, glancing over occasionally at the relic of a folk singer on stage. "What if I told you that I can’t stand your guts? That I’ve tried to kill you before you could even walk? And what about the fact that I have a full proof plan for you to end your life surrounding your tournament situation…Mr. Brawler." He failed to make the native New Yorker laugh, but he got his attention. Joe downed what was left of his Rum & Coke, looking to him with every intention of beating him to a bloody pulp within the next 30 seconds. "Take a numba, bro. By the way, befo’ I kick ya ass, what’s ya beef wit me eh?" The man smiled, leaning back in his chair with his smirk giving away to a piercing look of utter contempt for the warrior he shared a booth with. "You were born. You must have no clue who I am?" "Not a damn one, Sherlock." "One second." He looked over to the generic potted plant sitting on the end of the booth. Despite being exposed to the contaminated bar air, the plant’s leaves were still a regal shade of green and standing proudly. The stranger pulled back the sleeve on his coat, the blue and red veins clearly visible in his arms as they ran up to his elbow. Extending a single index finger, he touched the top leaf of the plant. What happened next froze the Brawler in the vinyl upholstered seat under him. The stranger’s touch took away the bright green that the bar was unable to take from it for years and years of abuse. The beauty began to slowly shrivel away; first the color, followed by the leaves shriveling to a crisp until the plant crumpled together like a balled up piece of scrap paper. The stranger’s touch had just killed a plant, which left the Brawler to create his own conclusion as to who this stranger was. He could feel something evil, other-worldly radiating from this stranger and it was the death of the plant that lead to the ultimate conclusion. "Yous….yous…" "Say it with the respect it deserves, Rocky. "H…H….how do you kno’ mah mi…" "Why wouldn’t I know. I know what they know. They give me the information and lists, I carry it out." "Dey…dey who?" The stranger gave an innocent point the sky above, followed by another to the pit below. The votes were in from the Brawler’s mental jury and they all ready "guilty". "Yous Death." "Bingo." Death leaned up on the table, getting comfortable as he locked eyes with the Back Alley Brawler, almost as disgusted with the New Yorker as Joe was with his stench. Joe was sure this was some sick joke, perhaps his mind playing tricks on him, but this was not really happening. If he could just wake up, he promised he’d never take another taste of alcohol… ….for a few days. But this was nothing like the unsettling image in the bathroom that disappeared when the Brawler decided not to look anymore. This was a living, breathing nightmare right before him that would not be ignored. "You know what my beef with you is Joe?" He had no answer for the being responsible for seeing souls to their final destination. What could he have possibly done to upset a being of his stature? "They won’t let me kill you. The past few years have been depressing as far as people that have been put on my death list. See, unlike what the movies would let you believe, I’m given orders on who dies and where they go after. Shit, just a few weeks ago, I had to take this Latin dude. He was a wrestler like yourself…." Eddie Guerrero. The Brawler could remember tossing back a bottle of Jack and wondering how a man who fought so hard to defeat his demons could be taken away in his prime. Men like that deserved everlasting life, not an untimely end. It wasn’t to the Brawler and apparently, Death felt the same way. "This guy destroyed his life with drugs and alcohol, much like yourself. Lost his job, almost lost his family, he could have easily fell to the wayside like so many other wrestlers I’ve had to pick up. But something strange happened….he rose above it all. He cleaned up, he got his family back, he got to the top. So you have to understand my anger when I’m asked to take a man who went through HELL to set his life right while some grubby fucker like yourself can’t even SPELL "rehab" let alone check yourself into it!" The Brawler couldn’t hear the folk singer’s musical tales nor the random conversations that bounced around the bar. His focus was squarely on the angered deity before him, his fury swelling up like the impending eruption of a volcano. Death’s pale skin threatened to show color with how worked up he was getting. "Why people like you? Why is it that guys like you, who don’t add ANYTHING to this world, and don’t even attempt to change, get to lead full lives? Why am I always taking those who bother to redeem themselves, or soldiers who give a shit about their country, or the clergy who truly believe in the word of our Father, or innocents who were minding their damn business before some ingrate takes a wild shot and kills them off?" The words cut deep and precise like a scalpel, but were the truth. "You were the product of a crooked cop and a whore. You coveted a dream more than your own damn family. You’ve escaped responsibility with bottles of alcohol and now, drugs. You’ve committed so much wrong against man….and in the name of your daughter at that. Everyday, I pray the name "Joe Balboa" is on my list and you somehow always dodge that bullet. Despite the drugs, the matches, and the stress….you keep breathing. But I have plans to fix that, sir." "Ww…wha da fuck do you mean?" "I know you Joe. You’re too headstrong to back down from GTT. And it seems like God and Satan seem to only want people for shock value the past few years. How else could you explain Richard Pryor? I’m going to tell you this information now….if you win the Global Title Tournament, you’re going to die. Between all the stress, the pills you’re popping, and the punishment you’re absorbing; your celebration will be cut short by an untimely death." The Brawler wanted to be angry, but he instead looked to Death in utter confusion. This was one situation that a solid right hand to the face couldn’t solve. Death had just told him the future and it was an outcome the proud New Yorker couldn’t stand to hear. "Why is you tellin’ me dis?" "Simple. It sets up my patented "damned if you do, damned if you don’t scenario". If you continue Joe, you know you’ll win. But you’ll win how I told you….and the only real winner is me when I come for your soul. However, with the information you now know…you could quit. But that’s when it gets REAL interesting. Could you live with that Joe? Knowing you could have been Global Champion, but giving up at least that moment of glory because you feared death? It’s the kind of thing that makes a man think of…" Death placed his hands on the table and slammed something on it, which made a metallic clang. Moving his hand off it, it revealed a dangerously kitchen knife. The gleam caught the Brawler’s worn eyes as he gritted his teeth bitterly, immediately getting where Death was going with the symbol. "….suicide." Death politely clapped for the finishing of his thought by Joe. It made him tingle all over with the fact that no matter which way Joe turned, his death would finally come. No more miraculous survivals from a hardcore match. No blackouts from an all-night alcohol binge, waking up and wondering why he had yet to see Hell after draining a bar of Wild Turkey.’ This ultimatum left no room for excuses and loopholes. Either the Back Alley Brawler’s pride or fear would do him in. "Exactly. The choice is all yours Joe, and I’ll leave you to it. But I’ll get my way, and when I do….I have all kinds of sick tortures that await your filthy soul. I’ll be awaiting your choice…either way….you’ll still be a never-was like you’ve always been. Just either with or without a title." He couldn’t take anymore. The Brawler smacked the emptied glasses of Rum & Coke from the table as they slammed into a sea of glass shards on the floor. Living or not, no one disrespected the Joe Balboa and Death would be no different. He defied him for this long and tonight, he would do it again, this time to his face, "FUCK YOU YA PALE MUHFUCKA! I’M DA BACK ALLEY BRAWLA AND I DON’T CHUMP OUT TA NOBODY! I DON’T GIVE A FUCK WHAT GOD OR WHOEVER WANTS, I’MA WIN DIS BITCH REGARDLESS! IF I GOTTA DIE, I’MA DIE IN VICTORY AND NOT SUM LOUSY BUM LIKE EVERYBODY THINKS I AM! FUCK YOU YA HEA’ ME?! FU-" "JOE! JOE!!, CHILL OUT!" Joe whipped his head over to his right, Louie (the bartender of BronxBar) standing over him with a distraught look on his face. Concern oozed from his eyes as the Brawler sweated bullets, worked up over the losing ultimatum laid out to him by Death. "Joe, what is up with you? I’m about ready to cut you off man. I mean, you’ve got the respect of the Bronx and everything, but you’ve been talking to yourself all night?" "Talkin’ to mahself? Do you not see dis muhfucka across fr…." Louie wouldn’t have been able to see Death because he wasn’t there. As quickly as he joined the Brawler for the talk, the entity was gone. There was not a trace of the pale figure, but one. His stench. The sulfurous odor remained. angering the Brawler to no end. It was no hallucination, he was there. It was all too vivid, too real….no amount of drink on the planet could make him imagine what had just happened. It was then that Louie pointed his stubby index finger over to the plant at the end of the table, still withered and dead from Death’s infamous touch. "What did you do to that plant, Joe? That thing has been in the bar for years! Are you sure yo-" "I’M FINE!" The Brawler pushed Louie aside, not enough to hurt him if he wanted to, just enough to let him know he didn’t want to be bothered. He unearthed a 20 dollar bill from his pocket and threw it unceremoniously on the table, making a beeline straight to the exit with the weight of the universe on his mind. As if the challenge of Seymour Almasy wasn’t enough, the thought of impending death was not settling either. The proud New Yorker ignored the other patrons, eyeing him suspiciously due to also catching his apparent "one man show" in the booth despite the Brawler’s inner protests that Death was there. It was just when he was about to exit when he saw Death once more, this time sitting in an empty booth. No one else seemed to notice his grotesque smell and gothic presence but Joe, and maybe he wanted it that way. Maybe it was just the side effects of the magic pills with Rum & Coke as a chaser. Or maybe fate had it in for Joe, it had to get a draw like Seymour Almasy for the second round. Whatever it was, the Brawler and Death shared a gaze of intense hatred that filled the 10-15 feet of space between the two. Hallucination or not, the Brawler could not afford to let the weight of the world crush him or worse, penetrate his psyche. Or maybe, it already had. He would have time to wonder in a cheap motel bed, the Brawler hastily exiting and hitting the Bronx streets. The Brawler found solace in one fact however. Win, lose, or draw; the Bronx Barbarian would probably wish for death all the same. And when it was all over, would anyone write a country song about him? He knew it would have made for a great story, if not a tragic one.
