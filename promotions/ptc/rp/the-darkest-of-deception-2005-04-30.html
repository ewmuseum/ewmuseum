The Darkest of Deception

30 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-04-30 And so it begins. The inevitable war destined to tear us apart, and bring about our salvation all in one swoop, has been initiated to restore the world to its rightful place. It may take time, but the outcome of this conflict will be felt the world over. It's a day that has wrought me with both fear and rejoicing since it was ordained. I weep for what must be, yet I know that when I bask in the light its end provides, all will be worth it. There will be no need of redemption. I'll be cleansed in the blood of the disbelievers, and my loyalty will be all I need to prove my rightful standing among Them. In time, when the heavens are painted black and the killing fields littered with the dead who stood against us, there won't be a man left alive refusing to kneel before the might of Great Cthulhu. What follows in the coming weeks will herald in the dawn of a magnificent new age, a prophecized dynasty decreed thousands of years ago by the essence of the cosmos Himself. We are merely the messengers and the trumpeters, and in time, we shall be the funeral procession for the human race that dares oppose. Your true Book of Revelations is about to penned and brought to life. This is the first trumpet. May its song bring glory to the Old Ones, and curdle the blood of our enemies. * * * * An icy breeze whistled dismally through the evergreen wood, the silhouette of Wyatt Manor cast against a waning moon and the curious scarlet aurora penetrating the horizon. This night the birds were silent and even the symphony of the ever-present whippoorwills was absent. Such a foreboding ambience seemed to hang forever over the property encompassing the isolated mansion in the mountains, lands etched in evil for decades with the depraved crimes of the past inhabitants. The debauched Wyatt clan, a hermit family found to be novice sorcerers and practicing cannibals, had left their vile mark on the region even years after their collective death. And the individual who had taken up residence in their old home was of no greater virtue, perhaps in fact even more corrupt and vicious. In the eyes of locals this part of the mountains was eternally cursed by God. But that was nothing but ridiculous superstition to Samuel Grant as he crouched among the dense thickets overlooked by the feared edifice. He may have been hired out by an organization covertly operating under the Vatican's regulations, but he himself had no faith in Biblical prophecy or any other pious doctrine. The man was a soldier, a mercenary of the highest order and the coldest of hearts. Adhering to some ancient set of commandments would simply hinder his style. And this wasn't a business where one could afford such liabilities. A cloud of smoke wafted from behind his squatted position, pierced by his steely gaze as it drifted ahead of him. He suppressed a sigh, not taking his eyes from the target. "Make sure you put that out," he grumbled to his unseen cohort, tobacco spit oozing from the side of his own mouth. "Hate to piss off Smokey the Bear by burning his goddamn forest down." The cigarette was dashed into his field of view amidst a grumble, embers cast off from the burning tip to scatter on the ground. Grant abhorred smoking but found it perfectly acceptable to slip in a wedge of snuff most of his waking hours. At least that way the cancer would be purely self-induced; he'd be damned if his ailings in old age were caused by some other ignorant son of a bitch. "Nothing yet. If I couldn't see a light or two glowing near the back, you'd think it was long abandoned. That house has the personality of a tomb, and it's as inactive as one." Surveillance had yet to dredge up so much as a guard or lookout. It may have been a crazy religious group, but this one was known to fortify its grounds with an occasional patroller to ward off intruders, and Lieutenant Grant wasn't about to take chances. A photographer had once told his co-workers he intended on getting some shots of what went on in Wyatt Manor, lurking in the shadows at night so the madman and his legion of followers would be none the wiser. He'd never been seen again. "It's fitting. Those bastards inside its walls don't have souls as far as I'm concerned." He'd been briefed and made well-aware of the deviant acts of the men who claimed that accursed estate as their own. As hard and skeptical a man as Samuel Grant was, even he had bad premonitions in his sleep the night before. Something about this place, an infernal and decidedly unearthly aura reeking with evil, almost made him reconsider his convictions in the Good Book. * * * * Hunted. It's no great secret that I'm a marked man now, given both my fitting renown and the dark deeds I've perpetrated. Upstarts instinctually seek to forge their own names by conquering the established. There's no cloak-and-dagger mentality to these pups, they simply stare you in the eye and declare they're taking what they feel is fated to them; I almost feel remorse as I listen to their screams, sorrowful and pleading. I was in their place once, and not long ago either by any means. One has to mature quickly when you exist in this dark world, else you may find a half-dozen knives jammed into your spine. This new predator hasn't made his intentions clear as anything disparate from the norm. He's really the least of my problems anyhow, another military man who puts unhealthy stock in high-tech gadgets and war tactics. There are bigger fish in this pond now, and in due time, they shall be drawn out. It's just their money-grubbing pets that need disposed of first. Weakness has pervaded the discipleship. Not of this covenant, not by any means. Cthulhu knows that the garden soil is already rife with the blood of the Sect's failures. No, instead I fear that the majority of the brotherhood has degenerated in faith and ambition. Maurice Thorne hit the pitfalls of a growing union and his ideals took a backseat to shady business dealings. Howard Ignalls, one of my mentors as much as he whom I serve, simply became too complacent for one of his stature in the brotherhood. In an organization such as our own, indecision and impotence cannot be tolerated. By the same token, I won't allow this treason to stand. I started it, and I'll help finish it. * * * * "I hate these types, Jim," he remarked before saturating the dirt with another stream of tobacco juice. "Preaching all their contrived bullshit to a mass of impressionable kids, reading out of books written by whackos thinking that it's all some kind of truth. The world's going to Hell in a handbasket. And I can't say we're really delaying the collapse at all." He heard his comrade skulking through the brush to his rear before settling down at his side, binoculars poised to survey the foreboding mansion. James Nelson shared Grant's views but wasn't one to openly echo his philosophies. Right now he was strictly concerned with avoiding a mob of torch-wielding, survival horror-rejects potentially calling for his blood. He was also aware of the assumed bloody exploits of their targets. "It's not our job to care, Sam," he muttered through squinted eyes, taking in all of the looming structure through the binoculars. "At least it ain't mine. I'm here to do my job and get paid. This damn Shanahan can chant out of any oddball books he wants and it's none of my business, but when I'm given a check and told to get rid of him for the sake of the world, then that's that." Grant enriched the soil with more tobacco juice as he scanned the walls and outlying property with his naked eyes. "Suit yourself. I think I'll take particular joy in seeing him go belly-up, along with all this psychotic drivel he spits." Nelson lowered the binoculars, handing over the radio on his belt to his squad commander. He was satisfied with the dead stillness surrounding the house and a few dull lightbulbs on ther verge of dying out didn't signal anything that would halt their advance. They never had in the past, not for this team. "Bravo team already reported in," he stated. "Banks says the rear is quiet and just as still, like an old grave. Odd thing was he also said there was something in the air, almost like a….how the hell did he put it?…..'a lingering evil ready to swallow us whole'. What do you make of it?" Grant raised an eyebrow at the comment, pondering over it for but a moment. He'd served with the man for over a year and had never heard anything remotely like that out of him. He related as much, but at the same time he couldn't help but feel a similar sense of menace hanging around that house that no locals would tread near. "Paranoia I guess, I dunno. That's not like the sergeant at all. He doesn't open himself to that sort of crazy mumbo-jumbo. Gather the others and we'll move in. I'm ready to flush out this sick fuck." His reply came in the form of a nod, Nelson sweeping back and staying with the shadows while Grant brought the radio to his mouth. The trees stirred as a breeze blew out of the west, chilling the damp night air and carrying a low whistle that almost conveyed the timbre of a voice on the winds. Grant trembled unwittingly, but couldn't place the reason. He wasn't cold. "The area appears to be secure. Now moving into position to neutralize the targets. You and your boys meet us outside the rear courtyard. Copy, Banks?" The man on the other end responded with a monotone "Yes, copy that." before dropping the walkie-talkie into the grass with a breathless choke. The knife ripped open his throat and his limp body was hauled into the shadowy forest by unseen hands. There was a deep ravine nearby that would be his resting place. * * * * They're near. The assassins have finally come, and I'm assured that this time their orders originate from Drake, Diedrich, Meier, and the other spineless rats we call brothers. I guess that the saying holds true here. With friends and family like that, who needs enemies? No matter. They will be punished for their crimes. We'll see to it. I'll never forget Ramsey Meier's shit-eating smile the day he nearly poisoned Brother Tully and was exposed for it, only to 'make amends' with another of his overzealous bribes. He'll die slowly, that one, right alongside that uncouth little rat, Daniel Colby. The time is almost is now. Already I can see their eyes, both hateful and fearful alike, cursing the day of my birth and the choice of their God not to strike me down as I still lay in the cradle. The same God that took my parents, stole away my life and childhood, transformed into an outcast, the very essence of hate and malice. They don't know what lurks in my heart. They could never comprehend the disdain I hold for all they obey and stand for. Damn them. And damn their wretched God for putting me in this position. * * * * The courtyard was a veritable jungle and museum combined into one, where aging statues and twisted relics were obscured by a sea of thorny vines and plant-life that Grant couldn't recall ever seeing in any survival guide or training exercise. Half-broken cobblestones formed a snaking web, many almost overgrown witht hose same alien shrubs and ropy vines. A lofty canopy hung as a ceiling over the grove, shielding out any rays of outside light and illuminating the internal fauna with only the dimmest of lights, emitted from melting candles and incandescent bulbs atop high arches and metal poles dispersed among the garden. The walls surrounded the yard, an upper balcony running the circumference with closed doors leading to the second floor. It all held a sense of eccentric beauty and elegant creepiness, depending where you stood. The first of the monstrous idols that really caught his eye, placed at the center of the grotesque garden among a myriad of half-buried, striated jewels that seemed to designate it as the most revered effigy of the lot, shared features with several things the lieutenant could distinguish. It was almost equal parts octopus, human, and serpentine or even dragon-esque, hunched down on two legs with a mass of tentacle-like feelers protruding from where the mouth should be and small wings folded over the back. Grant was far from an initiate in this voodoo trash and had no idea what the thing depicted was meant to be, and ditto for the other deformed statues exhibited around the peculiar garden. All of them seemed plucked directly from a nightmare, a horde of monsters spawned deep in the subconscious of the most blackened and radically disturbed mind. Surely their likenesses had been sourced of no one sane. The final man trailing in, Higgins, brushed a hand over the mouth of another of the statues, rows of crude and serrated teeth lining both the inner and outer jaws. It was like something ripped from a bad horror movie, but this one didn't inspire unintended laughter. Even touching it raised a shiver from the man with the rifle and he quickly withdrew his gloved hand, deviating his eyes from the thing. "I think it suits you actually, Higgins," chuckled another of the party. "A little eye-liner and you'd make a perfect pair." ""Fuck you, Collins." He narrowed his eyes as the reserved laughter spread, strutting slowly toward the near wall and out of earshot. There hadn't been so much as a moving shadow since they walked into this walled-off square. They' expected at least a couple men meandering the interior, discussing whatever the hell this little devil-cult believed in. Nelson shook his head fervently at the stillness. Not even an owl hooted in that shadow-veiled wood. "Something isn't right, lieutenant. Nothing, nothin' at all. Not so much as a bird calling." His opinions were echoed from the rest of the team. There had been an inexplicable tension ever since they re-grouped in the back and had been unable to raise Banks and his team; radio communication was lost often, especially in this elevation and with this older equipment, but you could almost smell the misfortune hanging in the air. "For the love of God, Sam, try calling Banks again!" Grant went to the radio, but not before the cracking of limbs and tearing of brittle leaves got his shocked attention. A mangled shape plunged down through the canopy, hanging from a rope by its ankles. Cold, glassy eyes stared forward, mirroring the fear of all five men before it. There were strangled gasps, men falling to their knees as they crossed themselves and whispered the Lord's Prayer between gags. Others stood firm, albeit panicked gulps, tightening their grips on the weapons they prayed would get them through this. Obviously, they had already failed some, evident by the fact others joined the first amidst cracking branches and crumbling leaves overhead. Laughing, peals of insane glee, split the night that had been tranquil only moments before. "Jesus….. "Holy Mary, Mother of God……" "This can't be…..God, no……" A door creaked open on the second floor, robes spilling onto the stone cobblestones. Eyes flew to the opening door, broken from their shock and momentary bout of mourning, but Lieutenant Samuel Grant's remained transfixed forward, watching the blood stream from the open throat of Corporal Armstead. It wasn't so much his own pain at the loss as his not wanting to meet the eyes of the man he knew now strided arrogantly atop those stairs. Nonetheless, he turned to gaze hatefully at the figure glaring ghoulishly from the balcony, others like him already beginning to flood the upper tier with knives, bows and the rare firearm in hand. He collapsed to one knee, another feeling of surprise jolting him to the core. He knew death was knocking at the door. He only wondered how long he could hold the Reaper at bay. * * * * All is in place, and surely they have already met my welcoming committee. They're men they should know quite well and be fond of my this juncture. They know them as do I. They're all here because of me. In a sense, anyway. It was all a ruse. And now that they have all shown their hand, and their true aspirations, what needs to be done is obvious. And it starts with the men they sent on their command. The same poor, oblivious fools I indirectly charged with this mission, and condemned to their deaths for doing so. They were my pawns, and they will continue to be even in death if I hold any sway in that dominion. Their living counterparts also dance under my strings, those proud heathens operating across the country and even in Rome itself to a degree. Up to now I've only gotten my feet wet, but very soon, I'll plunge directly into the pool. I'll hold all the cards, and when I have that unbeatable hand, I'll call. In a sense, it's sad. This didn't need to happen really, did it? Did so many need to meet their end in order for this to happen? Even brothers who struggle to meet the same goals as ourselves aren't exempt from this selective bloodshed. But then, such is the consequence. That is Their edict, and I am in no position to defy it. It's the most bittersweet sensation a man can feel, even a man whose soul is broken. I am Ethan Knight. And even I do not know if I can bear the consequences of my actions this time. * * * * "Welcome to the jungle!" Ethan Knight's jubilant cry was ghastly, almost as much so as the display he had orchestrated and not gestured to like some eccentric artist. Banks already lay a ravine, never to be found more than likely, but those under his command hung from that great tree like ghoulish ornaments decorating the lawn. Grant's stance was one of fury and betrayal. He had never fully trusted the fork-tongued boy, but allowing himself to walk into a trap only served to amplify the rage. He couldn't stand giving Knight, or any holding membership in this blasphemous little 'family', the satisfaction of ringing in his downfall. It had all been a scheme produced of Ethan Knight's own devious mind, and a ploy with untold, evil machinations. He intended to speak his mind, unleashing all his rage in a final tirade before succumbing to the hail of knives, arrows and gunfire that would follow from the twisted apostles lining the walls, or even get off one quick, lucky shot that would splatter the boy's brains, and sickening grin, all over the backwall. But that opportunity didn't present itself, not before he heard the creak of another door and the sly smile it produced on the face of that punk-ass kid. From a near door shambled the man they had been sent to murder, his entrance ushering the bows and praises of his dark congregation. "This is most disconcerting," iterated the Sect's maniacal mastermind, his expression both morbid and arresting as he came into sight. At his side, Ethan Knight's grin reflected his mentor's dementia. "Tell me…." His eyes wandered to the olive drab fatigues donned by Grant and his men, spying the silver bar on his lapel. "…..lieutenant. Exactly who hired you for this glorious little campaign? I implore you to entertain me before I rid this mortal coil of your existence." Grant's eyes scanned the balcony and the mob of robed servitors poised at the side of the madman called Shanahan, many already pouring down to surround and disarm the soldiers. Pride screamed out a need to somehow strike the man down alongside him, but he was well aware that he'd fall before he came within five feet of that criminally insane lunatic or his blond pupil. His eyes swept over the dogs lapping at the man's heels, and fell on the beady-eyed blonde man. Rage still consumed, ways to possible see him dead before he met his own demise. "Why don't you ask your lapdog? I'm sure he can shed as much light on the situation as I can, you fucking whacko." The madman's eyes burned with unholy fire. That wasn't the response he was looking for, and frankly he wasn't in the mood for this insolence out of some embittered grunt. He glanced at his nearest follower, a silent gesture that produced a boot into the soldier's ribs and hopefully a semblance of future respect out of Grant. It wasn't apparent, to the chagrin of Shanahan. "But….the accord….." stammered the lieutenant. He thought one rib may have been cracked, breath rushing in through pained gasps. "Why even…..feign this fool…..allegience….." "You're a soldier, not a bureaucrat, Lieutenant Grant," said Knight. He felt a pretense of deja vu at the man's proposal. "And you surely have no knowledge of proper strategy, else you wouldn't be in this mess, would you? Don't overstep your bounds, old man. Besides, I didn't orchestrate all of it. Father Shanahan's input was a vital part of this whole little web. And now you're a fly caught in the web. So how does it feel, staring into the eye of the spider that's thinking over how best to suck the juices from you?" The worst part wasn't so much the boy's response as much as the feeling that those terrible statues filling the garden were now staring at him. It was impossible, of course, but he could have sworn particularly that the eyes of that abominable half-spider thing had rolled from their stone sockets to set on him. The feeling of accompanying dread served to extinguish the rage brewing inside, replacing it with an implacable fear the likes of which he had never felt in his life. What the hell did lurk around this damned estate? "You're nothing more than a glorified domestic terrorist!" Grant could barely restrain himself as he barked the accusation at the maniac wandering idly about the balcony, not even ofering the decency to stare his prey in the eyes. "You spread fear and terrorize any who oppose you as bad as any Al-Qaeda radical!" "Terrorize is such a strong word, lieutenant. I prefer "enlighten". And, if you please, don't group me with those hate-driven vermin again. Oh, what is that?" Another gesture and the nearest robed apostle of the cult had snatched up Grant, wrestling him down as two others moved forward to seize his struggling arms and legs. A patch, only a couple inches big, had caught the eye of the wicked mastermind as he studied the grizzled soldier. It was one he thought he knew well, and his presumption was confirmed when his follower held up the piece of cloth for all to see. Shanahan's head lifted back, eyes resting for a moment on the stars. Not a sound escaped him for a long moment. "Tsk tsk tsk. Since when did the Blackhelm Coalition whore themselves out to the ones they vowed to root out and destroy? Did the Church cut back on your funds so badly, lieutenant? They had no more foolish crusades for you dogs to embark on, no slippers to fetch?" Grant gritted his teeth so firmly at the accusation that it was a surprise some didn't snap off. He was positive Knight had told the man that part too, but it wasn't necessarily true; he sported the organization's patch below his left breast and it was far from an ambiguous emblem. At the same time, Knight held in a smug laugh. Captain Stryker had expressed similar discomfort at the plan Knight had hatched when their truce had been finalized. If Stryker hadn't been one to feel that the security of many was alleviated by the loss of a few, it would have had never been agreed upon. He still smiled though when he recalled Marcus wretching his guts out when he thought the young lion had left. "I've had enough of these incompetent cretins," uttered the Bringer of the Black Gospel, his tone like a man nonchalantly ordering a tuna sandwich. "Kill them. But do it quickly. I'd prefer there to be no screams tonight, and besides, I can afford a soldier a swift and dignified death." His men, obedient to a fault, moved in like a wave on Grant and his hapless command. There were brief shouts and the sound of fists meeting skulls, but they were momentary. Death came upon them like an assassin in the night. "A rather difficult endeavor to get here undetected," observed Ethan Knight, himself not too concerned with the crude dealing of death only yards away. "I'd hoped for better out of the Coalition's 'elite' operatives." He only watched the regiment downed with a sense of morbid fascination, half-hypnotic yet casually at the same time. Charles Manson had nothing on the one known to so many as Violence Jack. "Of course. All of Brayston act as our sentinels. A town of the walking dead has its uses, no matter how mundane they may appear." Something had bothered the boy ever since he arrived back at the manor the day before. It wasn't difficult to tell; Shanahan had been the man's lifeline for nearly three years now and knew him beter than the young lion knew himself. With a sigh, Ethan dug into his pocket and spoke without even meeting the eyes of his superior. "I was targeted by an assassin." He passed a slip of paper into the hand of VJ, crumpled and the ink smeared. It was scrawled with the name of the contractor, taken directly from inside the pendant worn around the throat of The One Once Called Carmen. He knew beforehand that the elder man wouldn't take it into much account, and he was correct. "Heh, Drake and Diedrich. It seems then that revealing your presence here wouldn't be tremendously erroneous, or counterproductive. The fact you sold them out won't alleviate the price they put on your head." He hadn't spoken about the 'other' incident. He preferred the details of that little encounter remain in the closet for numerous reason, one of which was his own embarassment. He had a hard time stomaching the idea that Colby and Boller had sent such inept goons to kill him. "It would still disperse their attention, and resources, if they still thought we were fragmented," his apprentice scrutinized. "If they wage open war believing us to still be allies, they'll focus exclusively on us as a whole, cutting the head from the snake and killing the entire body. However, under the assumption that we're now two separate entities vying for control of the Sect, they'll split their assets accordingly to deal with two snakes and not one. We divide and conquer while now allowing the same to be done to us." Bruce Shanahan smiled, almost the smile of a father but still marred with that wickedness that so defined him. The boy's thought process was always impressive. Between he and Brother Tully, he again questioned he would be his eventual successor, if indeed it was necessary. Ethan only hoped he would make the choice easy. * * * * Father Shanahan is the way, and the light at the end of the tunnel. My mission was to uncover any and all who endeavored to usurp his leadership and potentially send the organization plunging to its doom. Their own lust for power must not undermine the common goal, and this organization must flourish at all costs. Even if our methods may appear "excessive". A pity, at least to some. I don't particularly fancy being the quarry on this hunt. Therefore I've decided not to be. There can be only one leader of this pack and it shall be me baying beneath the moon when our goals are collectively achieved. Or should I say, us. The Sect of Black Wisdom will not be denied. But, with that out of the way for the time being, there comes the issue of this weekend. And my travels to meet the one called Archibald MacGregor. Three times in the past, he has endeavored to adopt a sacrifice to persevere in the face of his enemies. One time in PCW against Skylar G., the 'secret weapon' of the Celestials he served, another in a battle against the Japanese sumo called Kimbusa in the third Global Title Tournament, and another last year against a certain convict, an unknown they called Nathan Hollinger who disappeared shortly after his bout with The Deville. Alas, he was defeated in all three instances, but I don't intend on allowing such to happen this time, not after the honorable deaths of the Blackhelm men. One man's meat is another's poison, as they say. Adopting a strategy that failed my mentor three times, combined with that presumed ill-omen of being the only remaining man left from the late stages of last year's competition, one would think it a suicide run. I beg to differ. It all sounds so good to me. But then again, they're already calling me a madman. So why fight the inevitable? It was an amusing little side objective to pursue, I thought. So sue me. Archibald, I know little of you, just as I did McMann. But that didn't stop me from crushing that insect underfoot, and you will follow suit accordingly. Green was a successful champion and Kimbusa also a renowned figure. But Hollinger…..well, I don't know what happened to that faceless heathen and I could care less. Soon you'll be joining him wherever he may be. I'm coming for you. And, my friend, you will not stop my march to victory.
