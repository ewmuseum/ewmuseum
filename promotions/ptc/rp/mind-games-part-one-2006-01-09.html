Mind Games: Part One

9 January 2006

Date: 2006-01-09 24th October 2005. WWA Meltdown. Salt Lake City, Utah. October 24th is the date that I will always remember upon looking back on my esteemed wrestling career. The date strikes cord with me now, especially, with my third round match in the GTT5 just around the corner. The date, of course, was the day when Steven Murphy brought chaos back down upon the WWA so intense I remember being mildly impressed. Murphy marked his return, by viciously mowing down fellow WWA superstar Armaan for, apparently, no reason whatsoever. But I know why he did it. I know why he continued the torment. Mind games. Truth be told, by doing what he did, Murphy gained the upper hand on the entire affair right from the very beginning. I will not make the same mistake. If he wants to play mind games with me, well that’s just fine and dandy. I’ve had experience with mind games before, both in and out of the ring, so I’m more than prepared. This time it will not be as easy as for Murphy as it was before. This time he will meet with resistance and he will lose. This time his unlucky victim will be no victim at all; I will be his superior. Hell, I’m everyone‘s superior. Now. A Bank. Manhattan, New York. The weather in Manhattan was unyieldingly bitter, so bad in fact, I had to return to my apartment to climb into a much warmer coat; my previous garment far too thin to deal with the cold and my current veneer not a great deal better. It’s not very often I make the journey to Manhattan. Generally, I have no need to come here, but today is different. Today I have business at the bank that I cannot put off any longer. Nothing drastic or overly dramatic, but I need to go to the bank in order to take decisive action. My mobile phone company decided to pull a scam on me and now that I’ve finally come to realise it, they decided to claim ignorance and refused to help me out of the jam. So I decided to cut them off at the pass, as it were, instruct the bank to block all transactions from the Phone Company and get a new phone. The contract option is a bad deal anyhow, I don’t use the damn thing nearly often enough to get any kind of value for money. I could’ve done this over the phone, but the truth is, I sometimes like to get away from the grim surrounding my place of residence. Manhattan, at the time, seemed as good a place as any. Sounds a little stupid now that I re-hash the idea through my head, but there you go. I stood, leant against the delicate mahogany wall panelling, waiting for the account manager to finally get around to see me. I watched him at his desk intently, witnessing the elderly married couple in the throws of an argument, first with each other and then with the account manager and then finally with each other again. I took a quick glance at my watch. Eleven-twenty. They were already running twenty minutes over and by the looks of the commotion, their meeting wasn’t going to end any time soon. Just then, I sensed someone approach me from the rear. I couldn’t tell whether they were intentionally trying to be stealthy or whether I just heard them approach too late, whilst concentrating upon the mildly amusing scene unfolding before me. I actually had to fight the urge to whirl around and grab the mystery ‘assailant’ by the throat. The knowledge of this both pleased and disturbed me at the same time. If I were in any other scenario, in any other part of the city, that’s precisely what I would’ve done. The persons hand lingered inches away from my right shoulder for a moment or two, almost testing whether I could sense its presence, but when it finally decided that I wasn’t going to react, an outstretched index finger tapped me roughly. I raised an eyebrow and turned my head slightly toward the perpetrator, forcing him to sidestep into full view. It was a man. In his early to late thirties. Clean-shaven. Styled hair. Wearing a rather expensive-looking business suit. Not what I expected, but, upon further examination, I realised that I hadn’t really expected anything at all. "Yeah?" I said, not trying to mask the annoyance, etched deeply in my voice. "Can I go before you?" The man asked, a pleasant smile hung upon his face like a picture upon a wall. My eyes widened at the question and it took me a couple of seconds to actually realise the guy was being serious. "No." I turned my head back to the account manager’s table. The elderly couple had stopped bickering now, the account manager had calmed them down a lot now. God knows what compromise he had to make to do so though. The man in the expensive business suit stepped into my eye line again. "Come on, man," he responded. "I have to be back at work in half an hour!" I rolled my eyes around the back of my head. Yeah, likely story. "Not going to happen." I could see the anger in his eyes now. I couldn’t hold back a smile, amused at this whole situation. "Are you laughing at me?" "No," I told him, "it was really more of a smirk!" The man shook his head in disbelief. "That’s it! I’m going before you, whether you like it or not!" The man then turned his back on me, blocking my view of the account manager table, truly believing that’d get to him before I did. I couldn’t help but chuckle again. "I think not," I told him. I grabbed the back of his collar and casually threw him to one side. Not realising my own strength, I watched the man hit floor and slide across it’s smooth surface with delicate grace. The throw was nothing spectacular; there was no real force behind it really and so the man was up and to his feet almost immediately. I watched the anger engulf all of his facial features now, as he stormed back over toward me. What the hell did he hope to gain from this? It was obvious to everyone with eyes and a sense of reality that I had at least thirty to fifty pounds on the guy - and all of that muscle. Of course, everybody within bank saw this fool fly across the floor and they were all watching on intently now. Although, I did hear one of the bank tellers call for security and moments later an elderly man appeared from an ‘employees only’ door from the far side of the room. I don’t particularly want to fight this ass clown, but if I did just what in the hell did this old-timer expect to do to stop us? This situation was getting more and more comical with each passing minute. I made a mental note to venture into Manhattan more often, just as the security officer managed to wedge himself in between the two of us. "What’s going on here boys?" The security officer questioned, eyeing us both up intently. "This asshole is trying to push in the queue," I told him bluntly, still keeping one eye on the account manager’s desk. "I’m the asshole…?" the man asked, almost in disbelief. "Glad you agree," I told him. I was making the situation worse than it had to be with my wise-ass comments, but I couldn’t help it. I was enjoying myself too much. The guy who’d tried to push in heard the comments and angrily took his suit coat off. Then he undid his tie. Keeping back the laughter was now really an effort on my part. "Now, now," the security officer began, "there’s no need for fisticuffs." Fisticuffs? When was the last time this guy had a fight? 1894? Still completely amused by the events unfolding before me, I didn’t even notice them enter the bank until they began to shout. There were four of them. All wearing masks. All carrying some sort of powerful firearm. The bank was being robbed. I sighed, heartily, wondering just why in the hell this shit always seems to happen to me. "Don’t think this gets you off," the guy who’d try to push in front of me declared. "After this is over, me and you are fighting!" Priceless. 24th October 2005. WWA Meltdown. Salt Lake City, Utah. I parked my rental car in the underbelly of the WWA Meltdown arena; a parking space expressly for arena staff and also the wrestlers. I remember being distinctly pleased with myself because the previous week I’d defeated the long-time undefeated Television Champion, Freddie Arson, with minimal effort and picked up the title and maintained my unbeaten record within the WWA. A mob of fans had already congregated at the entrance of the parking area and had thrown various dangerous objects at me. At the time, I thought they were upset at someone like me just waltzing into their organisation and taking the Television off one of their established stars. I was soon to discover there was more to it than that. That night, I had a match scheduled against another future WWA superstar, a guy who went by the name Vex, in an advancing round of the WWA Extreme Title tournament - the previous champion had left the organisation abruptly in some scandal or another that I didn’t particularly care about. Anyhow, I grabbed my bag from the boot of the car - it was a squeeze getting the thing in there let me tell you. The boot of the rental car seemed to be about the same size as a matchbox. Then I locked the thing behind me, as I made my way into the arena. Walking through the backstage corridors of the arena, the scurrying WWA production staff seemed a lot quieter than usual. Most of the time a couple of them would nod or say ‘hi’ or something. Granted every time they did, I ignored them equally, but that never stopped them before. I guess alarm bells should’ve rung right there and then, but I’m sorry to say they didn’t and I entered my locker room, ignorant of the series of events that were about to unfold. Now. A Bank. Manhattan, New York. "EVERYONE GET YOUR FUCKING HANDS UP NOW!" The apparent leader of the robbers screamed. Most had already obliged before he’d even said anything, but those who’d saw the guns late (like the asshole, the security guard and myself) or who were in too much shock to even move, needed to be told what to do. Reluctantly, I obliged. The asshole who’d tried to push in the queue eyed me up wearily and leant closer to the security guard. "I bet this is his fault," he nodded over in my direction for effect. "I bet he’s behind all of this!" I grinned and shook my head. "Asshole." "If he’s working with them," the security guard responded, "then why in the hell does he have his hands up!" The asshole developed a confused expression. "Shouldn’t you be doing something about this?" The asshole changed the subject. "I can’t," the security guard told him, "I left my gun in the john." The bank robber closest to us overheard our conversation and came bounding over toward us like an angry bull. He didn’t think twice-about what to do, he jammed his sawn-off shotgun into the asshole’s gut. The asshole folded over and collapsed upon the floor. "Fantastic," I said, chuckling in delight. The robber angrily made his way over to me, bypassing the security guard completely. I’d have thought they would’ve spotted him immediately, but there ya go. "DO YOU WANT THE FUCKING SAME, DICKHEAD?" The robber screamed at me. "You could try," I told him, "but I wouldn’t recommend it." "Oh yeah?" The robber questioned, promptly lunging the butt of his shotgun in my direction. I, of course, parried the blow, sending his momentum to the left, adequately putting him in the correct position for the knockout blow. My right fist connected with the robbers jaw sweetly, sending him sprawling backward on the shiny surface of the bank floor. He didn’t move. The security guard glared at me and I just shrugged. "I warned him." "I’ll get the gun," the old guy told me, "you get behind me. We can take ‘em." I shook my head. "I don’t think so." "But you just took out that guy," he said. "Lets take ‘em all out. We’ll be heroes!" "It’s not worth it, gramps," I told him, "they’ll be out of our hair in a couple of minutes…" "WHAT THE FUCK?" Another robber bound over toward me. I could see the anger in his eyes, despite his stupid-looking mask. Why do these guys always have to shout anyway? "WHAT THE HELL HAPPENED HERE?"
