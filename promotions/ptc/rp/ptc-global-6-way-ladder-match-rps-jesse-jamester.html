27 April 2004 The Long Road of Championism (Ch.1) Prologue. It’s true in the wrestling world that gold will make a difference upon how you act and are seen in others eyes. Beyond the logical nonsense of rambling that people did backstage before events, a wrestler had a lot on his plate to display as a champion. When you saw the World Champion of a company, take Global Wrestling for instances, you thought of that person as the top contender of them all. It wasn’t as if the man would go to the ring and be shot down by some rookie as easy as 1-2-3, no he would fight and show why he was the champion. That’s why wrestling was found to be unreal in the fans eyes. It wasn’t normal for one person to always win, and when they did it became boring to watch. So companies began to throw in some stories and told their members to do this and that, because why? It drew ratings. The technology of today has made wrestling a whole knew profession, something many would watch for the entertainment, instead of the athletic performances. Although it was watered down by the entertainment, the wrestling of today had not lost all of it’s roots. Some of the most EXTRAORDINARY men still wrestled with their hearts. One of those men was Jesse Jamester, ‘the kid’ as he was formerly referred to. He proved it when he won the Global Championship, a match that didn’t have a determined winner or some whacked out story leading into the big match. It was simply four of the top best wrestlers going at it, and Jesse proved he was the best when he pinned Apocalypse after hitting the Jwister. Yet deep down, he ached, not because of the match, or because of the pressure that was now on his shoulders, for those two things he could handle. Yet this aching pain was deeper than both of those things together. It was deeper than his personal problems of being a on-and-off alcoholic, and even deeper than the blood in his body could travel. This aching came from the fans… Whether they were the most loyal in the world or not, he felt their position on the matter of Jesse’s next title defense. Not a single one of them thought he’d win in the six-way ladder match. With names like Josh, Hessian, Blake Wolfson, Iron Lung, and Todd Dream, the odds were definitely stacked. Yet was this not the man who proved he could beat Chris Storm, the proudest technical wrestler of this generation? Or how about the 7’ monster Apocalypse who dominated in power and size over Jesse? And though not the biggest, strongest, or technical wrestler, he also defeated the woman who proved to show the wrestling world she could hang with men in the squared circle, Patti Anderson. So what was all this bull shit of doubt about? Maybe it was a lack of respect, or just motivation on Jesse’s part to feed the match to the public. However one thing would definitely not be forgotten when in his mind… That determination he used to win the title. That’s all he needed… so he thought. Story begins. It’d been a beautiful day in Calgary, Alberta, Canada, one of the finest in months if my memory served me correct. The sunlight burst on my face like fresh water hitting dirt as I stepped out of my black dodge truck that had four by four drive. Walking across the newly paved street road I entered a gym that had a title ‘The Canadian Dungeon’ which was famous for it’s big time lifters who took immense amounts of pills to get ahead. However, I wasn’t there to lift, I was going to do an autograph signing for some local fans. Being my hometown I was a large asset to the communities overall view to the United States and the public eye of Canada. As I entered the locker room I set my stuff in my locker and locked it as I went back out to the table that’d been set up for me to sign autographs. Large piles of pictures with my face on them were in boxes under the table and a pile on the table with a box of permanent black markers. I sat down and as the doors opened the fans poured in. From the place I entered the building, you could never imagine that so many people were waiting outside. The first person was a little teenage boy with brown hair that went down past his ears and was growing longer. I leaned over and shook the kids hand, he was about thirteen and looked to be in the brightest of moods as his eyes sparked open when he saw me. ’Hello, who do I have the pleasure to be talking to?’ The boy was shy at first, maybe instinct held him back from speaking, but within moments his mouth opened and he finally mumbled his name, ‘Aaron.’ ’It’s nice to meet you Aaron. You want to become a professional wrestler?’ Nodding his head yes, the boy was sure of it, as a smile of agreement overcame his frail face. I signed an autograph and then told him I’d take a picture. Stepping to the side of the table the kid handed a camera to one of the assistances at the gym. Taking a good two snaps the kid was happy and as he shook my hand he looked into my eyes with the most blissful face I’d ever seen. ’You’re my hero Jesse,’ he whispered before he turned and left. My eyes fixed a gaze on the small boy as he walked out of the gym where he met his father outside. He didn’t know it but those words had made my day, and possibly made me forget everything that had gone wrong in my life up to this point. I could tell Aaron was happy because as most children do, he pestered his dad showing him all the things he got and explaining the whole experience in detail. I sat back down and the next person came up to get an autograph. It lasted all day, I’d been there for near an hour and forty-five minutes of meeting people so I stood up and told one of the assistants I’d be taking a fifteen-minute break after these few autographs. Sitting back down I smiled as four teenager boys walked up, all four being over the age fifteen and definitely showing a passion for wrestling as their clothes displayed such. Leaning over I shook their hands, and smiled as they one by one said their names ’Jake, Dave, Mike, Alan,’ as I signed their autographs for them. ’You’re going to annihilate those other six man, I’m sure of it!’ stated Jake as he shook Jesse’s hand. ’Yeah, no way will those five punks beat you,’ followed Mike who shook his hand following Jake. The other two agreed, and I just smiled, because honestly I didn’t know if what they said was true. A ladder match with six guy in it was like trying to fight someone who was invisible. You didn’t know where they were at, and so anything from a cheap shot to an unexpected strike could take you out. Nodding to the four as they left I stood up, my legs were getting really tiring from sitting down for so long, and so I stood up and stretched them as the assistants told the people I was taking a break. Though disappointed I knew many wouldn’t leave, because something like this came by only a small percentage of times in somebody’s life. I knew the feeling, because I use to be one of those fans. I’d be walking through that line just hoping to see my idol as I peaked closer and closer to the table where I’d eventually meet him. It was an adrenaline rush, you could never really tell what was going to happen until you met him, and then it ended so quickly and you relived the moment in your head as if you recorded it in your head to remember. I went outside to get a breather, for being in that musty gym got to you. I was bored, even though the sky was beautiful and the streets were active as they usually were in Calgary, I was bored of just standing there staring at the ground. Seeing the newspaper machine near the telephone pole I decided to grab something to read for I hadn’t been caught up on the latest news of Canada for a few months now. Being an active wrestler in the states had it’s perks, but as I was Canadian by naturalization, I missed the stunning scenery that was across the Canadian-United States border. The main news wasn’t all to great but then I saw the sports and the Canadian Football teams. I was a big football fan, and being that the Rams were my favorite U.S. team I always compared them to the Canadian teams. Of course though, Canadian football was much rougher, being in the blistering cold and tackling people onto ice would do that to players though. Turning the page I looked to see the stats but my paper flew away and one of the four kids I saw had it caught under his shoe. As I walked over to get it I heard my name being mentioned in the conversation and quickly hid behind the telephone pole trying to look as casual as possible. ’That guys a joke… Jesse has no chance against Blake Wolfson and Hessian, he’s going to be slaughtered like a pig and left to hang by his neck when they’re done with him,’ chortled the kid Mike, ’Just look at him. He’s all big headed about winning and thinks he’s going to be able to take down the monster Hessian after he was man handled by Apocalypse last month. Heh, yeah right!’ The four of them snorted in laughter without knowing I was there behind the pole. In my own little world now my heart was being torn apart for it was these four teenagers who I worked my ass off to be the best for and now they were talking behind my back with such disrespectful things to say. Even though I half expected some to do such, I didn’t expect four kids who shook my hand and said I was talented to flip the words they said moments before in a completely negative way. ’It’s all Blake in this one guys. He’s got the XUW behind him and it makes sense that he’ll win since he’s got the talent to. The fans will be saying, Jesse Jamester who after this match,’ snickered Dave as he pounded fists with Alan for props on his smack talk. ’If I were Jamester I’d go bury the title so they couldn’t take it from him when they climbed the ladder, but then again, he’ll probably no-show anyway because he’s afraid of the best, Blake Wolfson! Can only be one champion and it’s definitely not going to be Jamester after Sunday Night,’ Alan claimed thinking he was a tough man while trying to impersonate Blake Wolfson. I couldn’t hear it anymore. Ten minutes ago I had the impression that these kids had idolized me or believed in me to win the match, and now they were saying Blake Wolfson was going to demolish me. Falling apart piece by piece inside I turned from my side as I had been laying up against the pole to hear them and walked the opposite direction of the four. Not caring that I’d told the people inside I’d be back in fifteen minutes, I just wanted to get away, this wasn’t the same as what I expected. I thought being the champion meant getting respect, but now I knew different… No matter how good you were the respect you earned from some would never be fulfilled in all. Inside me, as I walked down the sidewalk my body began to ache, not only in my gut or in my chest, but in my arms and in my neck and all the way up in the back of my head. It wasn’t pain or a headache, it was like a mental void I could not get rid of with pills or a glass of water. It was a puzzle I’d have to unscramble… But with the time constraints, and the desire to prove everyone that I wasn’t a one hit wonder, I questioned whether I could do it. Could I defeat the odds? Could I beat myself in a game of chess? Or would the aching only get worst, and would the power of me inside begin to crumble? This was a test… the ultimate test… and now I had to face it… … ---- 28 April 2004 The Long Road of Championism (Ch.2) Prologue. As depressed as one could get over a lifetime, Jesse had become that and more in less than a day. He’d not slept, nor eaten, or even moved since he heard the comments made from the four boys outside the gym he’d been signing autographs at. Sure Jesse knew that people would say things behind his back, some of these people who’d never even talked to him before, but these were kids who’d just given Jesse praise for his talent and claimed he would win the match. Lies like those go deep, but how deep? Could they get him to think deep enough to get him wondering of his career choice, or maybe his childhood? It wasn’t a positive thing on neither an athlete nor a normal human being to have their emotions played with like a yo-yo. People who did such put others at risk for it was those type of games that caused teenagers of today’s youth to go into their schools and shoot their fellow peers and teachers. However this was a grown man, and it’d be awkward to think he couldn’t deal with some punk kids words after all he’s been through. Just look at the history of Jesse Jamester, he’d survived some of the worst matches in the world. Some of those being Japanese death matches, Taipei death matches, scaffold matches, cage matches, and matches that were so sickening that they were rated R for content in the United States. Though the question still remained, what had dug so deep into Jesse that it caused him to go paralyzed in the intellect? Story Begins. In Jesse’s past many things were seen as disturbing or unusual compared to what he now had as a life. Now that past would be confronted in the worst of ways and Jesse was reliving them in his mind. Long ago, way back when Jesse was thirteen he had been living with his foster parents. Those being his stepmother and stepfather who he’d been with near four years since his parents untimely deaths or disappearances, neither subject he having any knowledge of. One night a world of anguish would suppress Jesse into dust, and that would be the explanation we looked for in this mood of recession. Reliving the Past It was another normal night, their sat Jesse’s stepfather in the living room watching the Sunday night fight. He was all set up, his six pack to the right, an ashtray on his left and his cigarettes in his shirt pocket. Puffing away his life with each cigarette he didn’t have a care in the world as he argued with the Television over the participants fighting style. It wasn’t anything special of a match, but the old man had wanted to watch it. Sitting in his chair he was having a good time, and beside him on the couch was his son Jesse. Without a doubt he was interested in the match as his eyes concentrated on the screen for the brutality was too entertaining to give up just to go to bed. Yet his mother came out and she was livid, it was near ten o’clock at night and he was still awake. In a tone of fatigue and irritation she told Jesse to get to bed. He obeyed, knowing it was late and he had school the next day. As he closed his bedroom door he laid down and tried to sleep but the quarrelling from the living room was too loud and so he sat up and leaned against his bed post listening to it. ’You have work tomorrow honey, why don’t you get some sleep?’ asked his mother politely. ’I’ll get to it when this match is over,’ he says not really paying attention to her and just using his normal phrase for a response. As she goes through the kitchen to go back to bed her eyes stumble upon the sink full of dirty dishes. Already tired and upset over her husband letting Jesse stay up late she curses under her breathe as she rinses off the dirty dishes. Slamming the cupboards she does with a slap of the wrist just to annoy her lazy husband who did nothing all night. ’HEY! Stop that, I’m watching the match!’ he barked from his beloved chair. Her eyes flared towards the echo of his voice. She couldn’t see him but knew he was there for his hoarse voice and stench of what he called breath was unbearable to not notice. She didn’t obey though; she kept on slamming the cupboards one by one until he finally rose from his feet to see what she was doing. ’I thought I said STOP THAT WOMAN!’ shouted Jesse’s father as he trotted into the kitchen with feet of concrete, ’I told you to stop it, I paid thirty dollars for that boxing match and I’m damn well going to see it without you out here slamming my cupboards!’ She stared at him before uttering words through the slit she had open between her upper and bottom lips, ’YOU’RE CUPBOARDS!? I clean this house! I cook! I do everything! Yet you’re lazy no good for nothing self can sit here and tell me this is your house?’ ’Yeah because I pay the god damn mortgage and make sure you have money to spend when you buy groceries! Who the hell pays one hundred and fifty dollars on groceries, huh?’ muttered the father as he stepped up to her with his beer in his right hand and his other hand tensed up because of the arguing as it gripped the cupboard she was about to slam. ’I don’t see you arguing over what I cook you! It isn’t like you don’t eat it! If you didn’t I sure wouldn’t buy it for you, because you don’t do anything for me to deserve a meal. All you do is drink, eat, sleep, and repeat it all over again! Ha, father my ass!’ she retorted gritting her teeth before slamming the cupboard shut crushing his right hand and causing him to spill his beer. As she smiles in comfort knowing he deserved it he holds his hand close to his chest shaking it to ease the pain before back handing her with the hand that held the beer. ’What are you smiling about now huh?!’ he yelled in her face as tears began to swell at the corner slits of her eyes. He knew he’d hurt her and he loved the power it brought him. Bringing his hand back again he did it once more but this time she moved and he hit her under the jaw creating a red mark. Backing away she went down the hallway and then ran into her bedroom. Jesse’s father followed with thuds for steps, each one louder as it came closer to his door and then once it passed it began to fade a bit, but still was heard. ’Get the hell out of there! That’s MY room! This is MY house! I want you out!’ he grunted as he hit the door with his fist as if wanting to break it down but knowing that’d cause damage he didn’t want to fix. Inside she wimpered and moaned in pain as she looked into the mirror, her nose had begun to bleed, and her lip was fat from the first backhand he delivered. Wiping the tears away with a hankie she had in her top drawer she went to the phone to call the police. Hearing the phone dial Jesse’s father became enraged, but was also worried as he knew she would call. ’Don’t do this! You don’t want to bring them into this now do you? I would have you out of this house so quick you wouldn’t know what hit you, you hear me!’ he bellowed in worry but with a tone that scared her stiff, forcing her to put the phone down. Crying even more she made her way to the door and opened it, only to be hurled down onto the bed, ’YOU EVER, EVER, at any time call those police, so help me god I will do something I’ve never done in my life. You hear me?’ She couldn’t answer, for the fear in her eyes didn’t allow her to move. She was a one legged man without a crutch, no matter where she got up she’d end up falling back down. Nodding her head that she understood, he left her alone and walked out of the room. ’I’m going out…’ was all he said before slamming the front door shut and leaving the house. The truck revved up minutes later and he roared out of the driveway in a frenzy, drunk, but not drunk enough to not know how to drive. Rolling off her bed, Jesse’s mother was terrified still, even after he’d left she was frightened of what happened. Jesse’s door opened and he came tip-toeing out to see if it was all clear. Pacing as he walked to his mothers room he took in the images of the hallway. The door being a bit beat up where his father had hit it with his fist, and the floor littered by the mud his father had on his shoes, it looked quite horrendous. Finally reaching the room Jesse stared at his mother on the floor in a ball, her arms wrapped around her knees as she rocked herself back and forth while crying. Jesse walked past her and grabbed the phone, picking it up and dialing 9-1-1 but she stopped him two-thirds the way through. ’Don’t… you… do… that… I don’t… want… hi-m… to have rea-son… to hit me more,’ she murmured, hiccupping and losing breathe in between some of the words. Jesse sat down next to her on the floor cradling her with a hug as she was too fragile to get up and move without falling down at the time. Seizing the hankie he wiped her tears away, ’don’t worry mom, he’s gone now… don’t worry.’ That alone was a cure for her, knowing her abusive husband had left the house, but still her body was weak and she was tired. It was only eleven o’clock at night and the house was a wreck. ’Jesse… dear… don-‘t ever… (sniffle) tell any-one… about what- happened… here tonight, okay?’ she managed to peep through her sniffles and weeps. Jesse nodded, he knew it was wrong, he knew how it’d all been so very sick to hear, and though he wanted to he cared for his mothers wishes. The worst thing he could do was tell the truth he thought, but the truth was what was right, and so he was bewildered by it all. AlThe only thing that made sense was that he had to keep quite, and so he promised to do so. Holding her he sat their all night until she fell asleep. Back to Reality Deep in the basement of the house where no lights but the sunlight that creaked through the panel boards above appeared, sat the mythical man known as Jesse Jamester. His depression was now uncovered, his step-father was an abusive man, his mother was beaten a lot, and he’d been the ventriloquist puppet his father used to get away with it all when in trouble. The guilt in his past had lead him down many roads and when his heart had been chipped away a bit too many times, it began to deteriorate at a rapid pace as it did the day before when he heard the four kids talk about him behind his back. Lying had been a way he had to accept when young, it kept things in order and was what Jesse had to do if he wanted to live in a house with two parents. As much as he despised it he did what his mother told him, but once he left his parents house he went away from that life. Lying was now an objective in Jesse’s mind that would never take place again. He wanted nothing to do with that path, and so buried it with his memories. Except, those memories now were coming back to haunt him. The kids were just a piece of it, but now he was starting to see everything dreadful in his life come back through memories he recently took part in. Sitting in his rocking chair, he rocked back and forth gazing at the wall, not sure of what to do, nor caring to try and doing anything. He just sat their with a puzzled look on his face that gave no clue what he was thinking about. Would the demons Jesse had stored away for so long begin to escape his body and let him get on with his wrestling? Or would they eat away at his insides until he no longer seemed human on either the inside or outside? A test of his darkest weakness was about to be brought out in him and he had nothing to answer it with besides fear… The end may be nearer than many think… … … ---- 28 April 2004 The Long Road of Championism (Ch.3) Prologue. Demons were something every person had, whether they came by singles or by groups, everybody had them, and at one point in life had or would face them. That’s what one man thought would never happen though, and he is now facing a demon so large that his beloved treasures of life are being sucked dry from his mind and being replaced with the sinful acts of his past. We learned this all when Jesse relived a bit of his past in his mind, the happenings on one given night where Jesse’s stepmother was beaten by his stepfather and the family covered it up by lying and not telling anyone. If that didn’t get to your conscious what would? Jesse being a man with a lot of dark secrets he definitely felt this with an impact. Bottled secrets brewed inside him and with each memory came more hate filling his heart. What will Jesse do to snap out of this trans and get into the flow of wanting to wrestle? Or will he not? Is it even possible for a man in such a sick state of mind to do such a thing, or does that call for a miracle? One way or another fears will be confronted, secrets will be revealed, and hope will be possible if all goes right. Story begins. Still rocking back and forth Jesse was planted firmly in the chair still gazing off into his own little world. The creaking of the chair had begun to slow down and Jesse’s trans was wearing off. He wasn’t out of it yet, but he was building a platform to step out of it, and that was better than nothing. Above the panel floorboards a scraping noise is heard, a door opening perhaps. Then footsteps confirmed that someone had entered the house. The boards creaked above Jesse’s head and as the person walked by dust had polluted the air from between the cracks of the floorboards. The sunlight that reached the cellar floor blinked off then on as the person above walked by. Moments later a trap door was opened and then a man climbed down inside to see Jesse sitting their stuck stiff as if a corpse. The man reached over and touches Jesse instantly awaking him from the trance. Turning his head Jesse stared at the figure and as it came from the dark and into the light Jesse’s best friend Ryan Bladez showed a face of foreboding and gratitude. Besides the hand he touched Jesse with Ryan left Jesse where he was, for even when somebody was out of shock the person could act differently or be light headed for a bit. Ryan sat down on the steps and stared at Jesse, who stared at the floor in front of him blinking his eyes every few seconds. ’How’d you find me?’ Jesse asked out of nowhere, as if he wasn’t sure where he was. ’We were suppose to go to the gym today to train for your match and you never showed, so after checking your girlfriends hotel, I came here… You know I know you best Jess, please tell me you’re okay?’ Ryan inquired as he leaned forward to get a better look of Jesse. He couldn’t answer right away though for the answer he searched for was far from that of what he had in mind. So he just nodded his head in a ‘yes’ fashion and kept staring at the floor. ’I know you’re lying but I don’t care. I just want to make sure you’re okay enough to wrestle… because if not, you’re not going into that ring with those other five guys,’ insisted Ryan as if he were Jesse’s mother. Yet it didn’t phase Jesse, he didn’t really care. He was just thinking hastily and with each thought he had a quick answer for. Ryan reached over and slapped Jesse in the side of the face, taking him completely out of his little state of confusion. ’I’m fine, really I am,’ responded Jesse looking over at Ryan and staring at him as he did at the floor, ’I’ve just been thinking… and I came to this conclusion that I’ll never be like my father.’ Ryan looked at him awkwardly, no doubt Jesse wouldn’t be like his father he thought and showed with his facial expressions, he was someone totally different. ’Why would you be thinking about your father? ’No… not that father, my stepfather,’ Jesse affirmed to clear up all lose ends for Ryan, ’about the past and what he did… I never want to be like him Ryan… If I ever am, I want you to ki-‘ Though before Jesse could finish Ryan hushed him, ’I’ll do nothing the sort brother, we’re blood, and I don’t kill family, even if I’m asked to in the mists of crisis, you’ll die peacefully and without me as your last image. However you’re not dying today Jess, so shut up about it.’ Ryan was a big guy and his voice was deep, so when he argued he put up a convincing stern voice that would make him sound more serious than he was. Grabbing Jesse he picked him up under the arm and helped him un the stairs. Walking through the kitchen, down the hallway, and finally into the living room Ryan seated Jesse and went to get him a drink. When he came back Jesse was cracking his neck. Ryan didn’t bother to ask, he just gave Jesse the water and let him guzzle it down. Sitting down opposite of Jesse, Ryan showed his massive body to be something that could barely fit into Jesse’s house. The chair slowly sank in as his large backside eased into it. ’You definitely need to get new furniture man,’ chuckled Ryan as he leaned back easily and waited for Jesse to speak. Eyes wondering again Jesse was seeing things, and then all of a sudden his eyes went completely white. The room froze as if crystallized and then a man came out of Jesse’s chest like a spirit, sitting on the armrest of where Ryan had his arm. It was the same man Jesse had spoken to the day of his first Global Title Match, his father, his real father. The man now had white hair receding down from his forehead all the way back over his head not showing any baldness but that which was in the front. His eyes the same navy blue as Jesse and the facial structure quite similar except for the age difference. This was of course without doubt Jesse Fristik Senior. ’Jesse… I am glad to be here to speak to you son… I know you’re wondering what is going on, for you’re probably livid about now, but don’t worry I’ll explain,’ Jesse Senior stated before going on to explain, ’Jesse, you’ve been seeing things that happened a long time ago, and it’s bothering you quite a bit, maybe more so than what you wish it to, but trust me, it will all mean something down the road. You’re a gifted person Jess, and I want you to use that to your advantage. Forget stressing over these little things, and get all your secrets out… Otherwise you’ll be like me.’ Jesse couldn’t talk, he wasn’t transfixed or anything, he was just stunned in awe as he saw a hologram type figure in front of him, not like a ghost but as if he were looking into the mirror of the future. ’Boy, you’ve got a long time to go before life is shortened for you, and so I want you to remember this when I leave. If you choose your paths and not let the people around you influence your choices then one day you’ll be told what all this stuff has to do with you. For now, I’m just going to leave you with knowing you’ll be happy in life… I’m always watching after you son,’ he exclaimed before vanishing into thin air. The room became normal within a snap of the fingers and Jesse stared over at Ryan who was sitting there waiting for some sort of reply about the furniture. ’Hey, you okay? Ryan interrogated in a casual voice but trying to find reason in Jesse. Jesse could only shake his head yes as he thought it all out. Inside his head much became clear, he realized everything he’d been doing was wrong, he hadn’t even prepared for the match as he usually did, and that he was more worried about what was going on in his head than what his opponents may do to him in the ring. Beyond all that, Jesse wasn’t focused because of all the stress he put upon himself. This wasn’t the Jesse Jamester who’d beaten Apocalypse, Chris Storm, and Patti Anderson to gain his championship, no it was someone totally different. Inside he could feel the difference, it was as if freedom had overtaken his soul and he didn’t care anymore, for what he had in his head now would only help him maintain his balance as a person. For once, Jesse felt… … Normal. As well ready to take on any challenge, whether it was the Mercenary Hessian, the American youth Josh, Iron Lung, Blake Wolfson, or Todd Dream… Jesse was confident in himself one hundred percent, as a true champion should be… … … …
