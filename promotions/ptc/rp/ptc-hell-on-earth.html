[IG72] Chikyuu no ue no Jigoku ("Hell On Earth")

2 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
March 1st, 2010; Honolulu, Hawaii Annabelle and I were in Hawaii for Sin City Championship Wrestling's "Defiance" show, which we viewed as a much needed vacation from some elements of our hectic lives. Annie had been working diligently on her music for the last four months, and had just finished the soundtrack for one of the games that she worked on as a lead composer. She wanted a break, I told her about the show in Hawaii, and she became extremely eager to go. She convinced me that this would be more of a vacation time than a work-related trip, and I was inclined to agree. After all, how many times do you get to be fly out to Hawaii and not have to pay for it yourself? We got to the airport in Seattle and immediately did we have trouble. My mask, which I used for my role as the diabolical Baron von Blackberry, was nearly confiscated by airport security. It took some major convincing of these people that I was not a crazed terrorist plotting to take over the airplane by laughing maniacally at it. Yes, apparently, laughing maniacally at airplanes was severely frowned upon. Who knew? In the end, we got on the plane and we flew to the airport in Hawaii, where I was once again detained temporarily... this time because I had a lab coat in my luggage. As if I were conducting science while on the flight and thus, I must have created an explosive device made out of ordinary household objects. Of course, I could not have done such a thing. See, I don't know when it happened, but prior to my flight to Hawaii, I must have contracted some kind of stomach bug or something. I spent the trip to the airport feeling carsick, and then I spent most of the flight feeling airsick. Especially when we hit the patches of turbulence, which I swore up and down happened every fifteen minutes. About midway through the flight, I had determined what I had for breakfast, lunch, and dinner for the past week. I was absolutely convinced that I couldn't throw up any more, and then I was proven wrong when I was forced to put more of my past lunches into the vomit bags a couple more times on the second half of the trip. I bet I was quite the memorable passenger. I was freed from my predicament by none other than Mitchell Quinlan. He did not do it out of the goodness of his heart. Rather, the airport security folks took one look at him and realized that he was far more suspicious - and also far more Canadian - than I was. So, obviously, he had to be a terrorist. Or a Canadian, which, judging from prior experiences with people from that country, probably wasn't that much different. Besides, have you MET that guy? Seriously. I'd probably detain him, too. * . * After we picked up the ill-fated rental car, I drove Annabelle and myself to the building where they were holding a fan festival to prepare the fans who'd made the trip to Hawaii to attend the show for Defiance. Annabelle was the one driving, and this was for three reasons. The first and most important reason was that I had to put on my Baron von Blackberry outfit on the way to the festival. Just because I had grown used to being this completely asinine character didn't mean that I wanted people to know it was me. The second reason had to do with my stomach bug, as I was feeling ill and I couldn't imagine the car ride helping to improve my condition any. The third reason was simply because Annabelle would be more willing to stop for directions if we got lost than I was. We arrived at the convention center, with me in my ridiculous lab coat and my even more obnoxious blackberry mask. Despite feeling like I'd been punched in the gut all day, it was a strange experience to be at one of these fan fests shortly before a massive pay-per-view. I'd somewhat done this twice before, but never to the extent that I was to participate in this event. The geniuses in marketing had decided to construct a Fruitsylvanian laboratory for me to wander around in and conduct "science". I would be giving my press conference interview from this laboratory, and I would be doing it completely in-character, at my request. Largely, this would involve me taking serious questions about my conditioning and my abilities to withstand the Dead Man's Hand's twisted machinations, and turning them into bouts of insane babbling off the top of my head. "The Dead Man's Hand are a bunch of snivelling little revolutionaries trying to unseat my position of power upon my frozen porcelain throne of despair in Fruitsylvania! They shall be reined in by none other than I, the great and perfect Baron von Blackberry, and they shall be collectively castrated with the Crushing Cudgel of Castration!" Like that, for example. The reporters, a large number of them basically a bunch of people who went on the internet and found ways to slander the names of perfectly good wrestlers just to bring in more website traffic, could only stare at me in disbelief. Not a single one of them knew that I was Coral Avalon. After all, I spoke with a weird accent and I wore a lab coat and mask, not to mention that my wrestling style as Blackberry was very different. I was also quite pleased that they didn't know who I was, because these guys were the last bunches of idiots I wanted to have knowing who I was. "So, you're going up against the Dead Man's Hand?" one of these guys asked me. He wore glasses, he had a pocket protector, he looked like he was a toothpick masquerading as a man, and he looked to be the type to be easily flustered. He would pay for daring to ask me such a silly question. "No, I'm going up against Los Wombatos. For too long have these ecologically misplaced miscreants have thwarted my every diabolical scheme, shaking the very foundations of every plan I have wrought for the conquest of the globe! Soon, they will know the very name by which they will fear and respect, and they will tremble in terror becaus-- WHAT DO YOU THINK, FOOL!" I shouted. I think I might have made this guy wet himself, "Do you think the Baron would simply drop a bombshell such as his uncanny assault on all things Dead Man's Hand idly?! Fool! I simply stated that they deserved to be castrated with an instrument meant solely for making castration as painful as humanly possible. INCLUDING THE WOMEN!" "Uhhh... n-no... of course not..." the nerdish fellow said, before he muttered something to the effect of "freak" under his breath. He thought he said it low enough that I couldn't hear him, but he thought wrong. Nevertheless, I decided to spare him any further humiliation. He deserved that much. Another of these reporters asked another question, one of the Sin City reporters that tried to keep things well within kayfabe. I liked these reporters. "Mr. Blackberry... What will your strategy be against Jadian Bridden and Mitchell Quinlan?" "Hah. Strategy is the refuge of the weak." I said, lying through my teeth, "When the Baron conquered the Fruitsylvanians, he did so by instituting a strategy developed ON THE FLY by simply making his robot army do all the work! It was a brilliant testament to my military genius! AHAHAHAHAHA!" Stares. That's usually what I got when I jumped upon the crazy train. Finally, another reporter spoke up, "What about your challenge for the Infinite Gauntlet against Eegor? Many have accused you to be a ripoff of Doctor Curiosity, so is there a personal grudge between you and his... assistant?" "Absolutely ridiculous." I said, "He is a mere doctor, whereas I am the mighty and flawless Baron von Blackberry, who rules the scenic, YET DIABOLICAL nation of Fruitsylvania with an iron, yet fruity fist! ......Also, I am a ruthless dictator. He is a mad scientist. There is a difference." Seriously, where did people get this idea, anyway? "But you talk about science all the time." the same reporter said. "I do NOT talk about science all the time!" I responded, "I also talk about torture. Do you not pay attention to my weekly seminars?" I don't really hold seminars with Blackberry, so I don't blame this guy for shaking his head "no". That didn't stop the Blackberry within me to go off on him, "Bah! This is why you are a failure as a journalist! And also at life. Return home to what spawned you and beg to be sent back within its womb! I have no time for such... such... introspective twaddle!" "Introspective whattle?!" the same reporter asked in shock. I'd like to think that I understood why the smart mark reporters *hated* people like me. The interview progressed without too much further incident. By the end of it, I had to find my way to the bathroom to empty what I presumed were the remnants of a pizza I had maybe two weeks ago when Jared Sykes vowed to ruin my diet as much as possible. When I finished my prayers at the porcelain altar, I heard a knock against the stall door. "What is it?" I asked. I couldn't even be bothered to use the Blackberry voice. "This is Nigel." a stuffy British voice said to me. Nigel Kensington was one of my bosses, and if you could ever point anyone out as being "stereotypically British", Nigel would be that guy. He wasn't a bad guy, except that I had this low-lying level of contempt for him for shoving this mask in my face and saying that this was who I'd be from now on, "Are you alright?" "Just peachy, I always wanted to throw up in the men's bathroom of a Hawaiian convention center." I said, with every word dripping in pure, untainted sarcasm. Even from my position, I could tell that Nigel wasn't exactly going anywhere. If only because even now, I could sense the everpresent smell of his scones. Okay, I couldn't actually smell that. "Mr. Avalon, am I correct to assume that you came here ill?" Nigel asked me. I responded in the positive. It wasn't like I could realistically lie to the man, he was practically right behind me, listening to me try to find something new to throw up. "I'll be over it by the pay-per-view, if you're worried about that." I told him. Nigel wasn't the sort of guy who thought about business a lot. As I understood it, he was actually an English professor and he actually got to go around with people calling him "Doctor" all the time. Maybe I should go to college some day, so I could be called "Dr. Coral Avalon". Or, I guess, "Dr. Baron von Blackberry". "I worry that you will overwork yourself leading up to the pay-per-view and worsen your condition. Take the rest of the day off, if you would. You have done the press conference, I doubt there is any sense in detaining you here any longer." he said. I swore to myself that the man was what most Englishmen wished they could be, but I could only gurgle some kind of response that said "okay" before the contents of the Chinese from two weeks ago found their way into the porcelain throne. * . * "You're starting to look like one of those aliens from Avatar. Are you okay?" Annabelle always knew exactly what to say to make me feel better about my health. Truth be told, I was far from okay. We were in the car, driving on Interstate H-1, and I swore that we were in the middle of a damn earthquake with the way the ground was moving like waves. Considering that I made my living doing flips and somersaults like a monkey, that just didn't make for a stellar time in Hawaii. "Just peachy." I said. To most people, that's sarcasm. Between Annie and I, it was a code that meant "I'm not doing okay at all". "Do you need me to find a hospital?" Annie asked. "No." I said without hesitation, "Just get to the hotel. I just... need to lie down in something that's not in motion." I tried not to look at anything that moved. I focused down on my feet. I wanted to believe that if I focused on my feet long enough, I would feel better about the fact that I was in a moving vehicle. It didn't work out quite as much as I would have liked, but at least I didn't feel like puking. At least, not until the moving vehicle began to bump FAR more than it should have. "What the hell was that?" was my first response, and while I might have heard Annabelle say something at the time, I couldn't exactly hear her over the loud scraping sounds that pierce my ears. Much to her credit, Annabelle didn't completely panic and slowed the vehicle down before pulling it off to the side of the road. My stomach, unlike Annabelle, decided that it simply must panic. My first action once we had pulled over was to break open my door and puke out the other half of that Chinese we had the other day. I didn't remember having the shrimp and rice, but lo and behold, there it was. Annabelle got out of the car, the moving vehicles and the prevailing wind serving to blow her flowery purple dress around. I got out, too, but I was so disoriented that it was more like I tumbled out of the car. I braced myself against the asphalt and took some care not to fall into my own puke. It was from my unbalanced position that I caught the reason for our problem. The right rear tire of the car had literally slipped off the rim, which would explain the loud scraping sounds. It was actually a small miracle that Annabelle had maintained control of the car well enough to stop it on the side of the road. "...Wow." was the only thing I could say in response. I started to get up while Annabelle squatted down next to the problem area of the car. I could see her frown as I got to my feet, "It's cracked." A tire slipping off the rim was one thing, but if the rim itself was damaged, there wasn't a whole hell of a lot we could do. Annabelle looked up at me, although I wasn't exactly the one with all of the answers. In the years I'd been doing this, I never had a rental car break down before. Since I had the cell phone, I started to go through the list of people I had to call. I began by attempting to call Thad Denver, otherwise known as Spacely. Unfortunately for me, after two attempts and nary a response, I had to rule him out. Thad would later tell me that he misplaced his cell phone, a claim I was slightly incredulous about when he claimed it. I next attempted to call Jared, but as it turned out, he was signing autographs at the time and couldn't leave, lest he get yelled at by everyone in management and shuffled so far down the card that he'd be jerking curtains with von Doom. I was about out of options and very tempted to call Amy Campbell, who didn't exactly know that I was Baron von Blackberry and whose number I only got because Jared gave it to me "in case of a super emergency", before it occurred to me that I should be calling the rental car company. That turned out a tedious fifteen minute conversation where they blamed me for being on the side of the road and I blamed them for giving me a defective vehicle. It was a fun time, and by "fun time", I mean that I might have been serious about looking for a rope after all. After getting them to at least bring me a replacement car, I hung up and turned to Annabelle, "I'm gonna find out which god I pissed off to get a day like today, and I'm going to punch him in the nose." I thought my trip to Hawaii couldn't possibly get any worse. And in hindsight, I really needed to stop tempting the gods to prove me wrong. * . * March 7th, 2010; Honolulu, Hawaii I'd be lying if I told you that Defiance was a bittersweet affair. I had vanquished Jadian Bridden and secured about five pounds of additional luggage to haul to Japan, which was all well and good. I could show it to Inoue Doi, whom I would be spending the majority of my time with when I reached the Land of the Rising Sun, and she could use it to beat me half to death. Except that someone I would consider to be one of the extremely short list of friends I had in this promotion had been brutalized almost beyond repair: Thad Denver. So, while I would normally be one to celebrate a newly won championship - or by God, dance the rare jig - I instead found myself looming around a hospital. The only nice part was that it wasn't me that was the patient... for once. I couldn't even begin to *describe* how much I hated hospitals. The air was often oppressive, filled with the smell of medicines and something akin to blood. Doctors in white coats shuffled about, conversing amongst themselves about their patients and ignoring the patients themselves. I saw a man in a Hawaiian shirt who was sunburned so badly that his skin was starting to flake in that unsightly kind of way. Over to my right was a particularly unpleasant-looking wheelchair-bound woman wearing a neck brace and cursing at anyone who ever remotely came near her. A man with a perpetual hacking cough sat no less than two feet away from me. I began to wonder if there were enough antibiotic wipes for me to survive this trip to the hospital without getting sick. Again. I looked at all of these people and I wondered just how many of my friends had ended up in a similar situation, with me powerless to help them. I had called Annabelle after the show to tell her that I was going to the hospital to see about Thad. She had only met him once before, the day before tonight's show. When she heard what happened, she denied that she was sobbing even though that was exactly what she was doing, and told me to take all the time I needed. I was here first, having left the arena pretty much as soon as they handed me a championship belt. I literally drove away in my rental car with my mask still on my head, the championship belt in the passenger seat like it was already my newest "son", and I don't even think Alexandra Pierce even got to the gorilla position to make her entrance before I had left the Stan Sheriff Center. Before I walked into the hospital, I took off my lab coat and my mask, leaving me with the shirt I wore underneath, a pair of black slacks, and a pair of black loafers that I simply slipped on in place of my wrestling boots as soon as I possibly could. So, I actually looked pretty normal, if a bit sweaty. It didn't surprise me that I was the first here. Jared had the biggest match of his life later in the evening and Thad's girlfriend had also found herself in the hospital to treat some pretty ugly-looking gashes on her neck, courtesy of Terrence Kingsley. I had a single interaction with Kingsley once before, and the encounter had unnerved me. It didn't surprise me that he would prove capable of doing what he did, and yet, I was further unnerved by how easily he was willing to inflict harm on Mackenzie. It made me think of what awful thing could happen to Annabelle if she continued to travel with me, but I simply couldn't send her away until after the upcoming show in Tokyo and our customary visit to Chiba, Inoue's city of residence. A doctor in green scrubs approached me. He was a black man, vaguely reminding me of Allen Brown if he still had his hair. He looked younger, too. "You're the one who came to see Mr. Denver and Ms. Malone, correct?" he asked me. "I am. How are they, doc?" I asked, after a short pause. "Mackenzie was lucky. One of the cuts was just half an inch away from ripping open her jugular. She'd have bled to death long before she even got here. The head injury, though, is a bit more severe. We don't know how bad the concussion is, but... we need to keep her a while for observation." the doctor told me, shaking his head at disbelief. I think he was more disbelieving that a man could do the things he did to Mackenzie and not be arrested for it. "And Thad?" "Mr. Denver... has yet to regain consciousness. We believe that he is concussed pretty badly, and we've had to stitch him up pretty good. Also, his shoulder was dislocated. I can't say I can allow you to see him, though. He was beaten rather severely, and he's not completely out of the woods yet." the doctor said, before he looked at me with narrowed eyes, "Isn't wrestling supposed to be fake?" "Yeah." I said, sullenly, and so low that I was surprised that any sound came out of me at all. There were times that people in this profession went too far over the line. If this were six years ago, I'd have been the first to jump on the vengeance train and come after Terrence Kingsley with everything that I had in my body. Before I had Annabelle in my life. Before I had grown more mature. Before I had recognized my limits. Six years ago, the only thing Terrence Kingsley would have seen was me relentlessly finding ways to make his life into misery. "Thanks, doc," I said. I pulled out my cell phone and checked the time, and sighed when I noticed that Jared was probably just making his entrance in the Stan Sheriff arena. I made a mental note to call him later, "I'll let them know about Thad." Walking away, I didn't even want to think about how much more this trip could unravel. Truth be told, I didn't think I needed to tempt fate any more.
