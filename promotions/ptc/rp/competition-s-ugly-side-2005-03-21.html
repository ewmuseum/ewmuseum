Competition's Ugly Side

21 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-03-21 Competition is a healthy thing. It forces us to grow stronger, to find new ways to survive, to search for innovation. Competition demands that we strive to better ourselves, in order to remain at or near the top of the ladder. And if we fail to meet those demands, we fail ourselves, and the next step in the process is collapse. Maybe not total collapse, and maybe not sudden collapse, but eventually, collapse. There is no other option in the dog-eat-dog society that exists today. But while competition is generally a good thing, it has its drawbacks like most things in the world. Too much competition can sometimes do more harm than good. --------------------------------- As Frank walked up to the his coffeehouse to open for the day, he glanced over at the lemonade stand on the corner and frowned. He was dismayed by the long line of people standing in front of it. In fact, the line spilled over the edge of the grass and into the parking lot. The parking lot that was reserved for Frank's customers, not little Timmy's lemonade stand. Sure, it was a bit funny in the beginning, maybe even a little cute. But lately, things had gotten out of control. Frank unlocked the front door, stepped inside, and flipped on the lights. He walked behind the counter and immediately began to brew three fresh pots of coffee, each pot a different blend. Gotta keep the customers satisfied, he always said; some of them like the Guatemalan, some like the Kenyan, some prefer his own special house blend. And if one pot was out, forget it; you try telling a coffee addict that their preferred blend is out, and after you do, stand back, because here it comes. He looked out the window at the lemonade stand, and frowned again as customer after customer dropped a quarter in the jar in exchange for a cup of lemonade. Those were his customers, not some little snot ten-year old's. What had originally been a symbiotic relationship had slowly evolved into more of a parasitic relationship. Whereas in the beginning, Timmy's customers had mainly been the children of Frank's customers, who had been discarded, or more politely "sent off to play," while their parents grabbed a cup of coffee, lately those same parents had begun opting for lemonade instead. Frank couldn't figure it out; maybe some of them did it for health reasons, maybe some did it to spend that time with their kids, but in the end, it really didn't matter to Frank. He didn't care why more of his customer had begun to stop at the lemonade stand; all he cared about is that they did. His mind began to wander, and he silently cursed the day the lemonade stand had been built. It was his own dumb luck, though, and he knew that the only person he had to blame for the lemonade stand was one of his best friends. And then, his frown turned to a smile as he thought about – --------------------------------- "Latch!" Frank yelled out, as the Latch Key Kid walked into the coffeehouse. "Where've you been? I haven't seen you around here lately." Latch took a seat in front of the counter and shook Frank's hand. "Oh, I've been here and there. It seems like I've been everywhere, man. A few weeks ago, I was down in Orlando for XUW's latest pay-per-view. I had a world title defense against Saij, actually." Frank nodded. "Very good. How did that go? I haven't heard anything about it, and I always assume that no news is good news." Latch nodded in reply. Earlier in the night, Saij announced that it would be his final match; he was retiring immediately after the match. The Latch Key Kid was honored to be Saij's final opponent, and the match was a classic show of respect between the two fan favorites. "You would assume correctly, Frank. It was a heck of a match; neither of us wanted to give in and be the one to have to lose. In the end, though, Saij tapped out when I locked him in the Reading Rainbow." Saij had never tapped out before, and as such, Latch was stunned to have won the match in such a manner. "It was also Saij's final match, Frank. He stunned the crowd and everybody else with his retirement announcement earlier in the night." Frank reached over the counter and patted Latch's right shoulder. "Well, it sounds like it worked out well for you. It's good to see you pick up a win over someone like Saij. So what now? I assume you're gonna take some time off to rest and relax?" Latch shook his head. There was never much time to relax when you were both the XUW World Champion and the PTC Global Champion. In fact, no sooner had he defeated Saij in his first XUW title defense, when he found out about his upcoming PTC Global title defense, also his first. He was the first person to hold both of those titles at the same time, and while he viewed it as enormous accomplishment, he almost wished he didn't have to worry about defending both titles at the same time. He had gotten himself into a classic catch-22 position. "Eh, you know what they say, Frank. No rest for the weary." Frank scratched his head. "I thought it was no rest for the wicked." "Maybe that," Latch shrugged, "but it doesn't really matter. There's no rest for me. Not with my PTC Global title defense coming up. Especially since I'll be defending my belt against three other wrestlers; Mal Somers from WWA, Nick Worrell from Core and Emilio Rage from PRIME." Frank shook his head. "Well, it looks like you've really got your work cut out for you this time, Brad. I don't envy you with your problems." Latch sighed. "Thanks, Frank. But I'll be okay, I'm sure. This is just one more match in a long string of difficult matches. Besides," Latch tilted his head to the window, "from the looks of things, I'm not the only one with problems right now." “Yeah, well,” Frank replied with a half-hearted smile, “what can you do? I mean, the kid’s ten years old, for crying out loud.” “That’s just it, Frank,” Latch said, “I wish I could do something. After all, I can’t help but think that this problem is all because of me.” --------------------------------- Ultimately, Latch was right. It was his fault. He opened it after Frank fired him from a job at the coffeehouse that he was never officially given. Rather than go away completely, he opened a lemonade stand on the corner in front of the shop, partly out of protest and partly because he still wanted to be around Frank. Frank, however, was none too pleased with it; as it turned out, neither were the goons who were harassing Frank for money. It had been a confusing situation to deal with; first it appeared that the goons really were harassing Frank and other local business owners for money, and then Frank revealed to the Latch Key Kid that the goons were actually working for him, only for Latch to realize that Frank had been lying to protect Latch. In the end, Latch and Frank drove the goons away, and life around the coffeehouse had returned to normal; well, as normal as things ever got around Frank’s. The goons were gone, but the lemonade stand remained. In fact, Timmy, the ten-year old kid to whom Latch sold the stand for a measly dime, had taken the pitifully-constructed stand and turned it into a thing of beauty and amazing profit. He had even branched out around town, and his fifth location was due to open soon. But perhaps the biggest reason why the Latch Key Kid felt guilty about causing problems for Frank was that his own name was displayed prominently on the stand’s sign; despite the turnover in ownership, Timmy kept the original name: Latch’s Lemonade Stand. It had been flattering at first, as Latch felt honored that Timmy chose to keep his name on the stand. He never explicitly gave permission for his name to be used, but then again, he never explicitly said his name couldn’t be used. Besides, Timmy was just a kid, and there was no harm in a kid using a wrestler’s name to capitalize on his fame and success. Now, however, it had gotten a bit out of hand. Latch had become worried that having his name on the stand might come back to bite him in the butt in the future. What if a batch of lemonade soured and made people ill? Who would they turn to to receive recompense? Ten-year old Timmy, or the man whose name was splattered all over the stand? --------------------------------- “No, listen to me, Frank,” Latch protested. “Of course it’s my fault, and you know that. I appreciate you trying to take the burden off of my conscience, but it’s not working.” Frank was his friend, and what had started out as a simple prank had spiraled into a veritable juggernaut that threatened to drive even the beloved Frank out of business. Latch hated the thought that he had unwittingly stabbed his friend in the back. “Even after I sold the stand to Timmy, Frank, I still thought it was a good idea at first, as it made for healthy competition. You know, competition leads to exploration leads to innovation leads to success leads to great products. But look what’s happened here.” Frank set another mug of latte on the counter in front of Latch, and tried to calm him down. He knew the Latch Key Kid had more important things to worry about than a silly lemonade stand, yet he also knew he was being his usual stubborn self. “Look? All I see, Brad, is a ten-year old learning on the fly how to run a business. And he seems to be doing a pretty good job of it. It’s unfortunate for me in a sense, but that’s okay. I’ve been through this kind of thing before, and I’m still standing. Once the popularity of that lemonade stand dies down, those customers will be right back in here,” Frank spread his arms wide and beamed, “where they belong.” “I sure hope so, Frank,” Latch sighed. “I’d hate to be thought of as the person who drove your business away. I’m not so sure that stand is such a good idea anymore. Now that there are so many people involved, not to mention five locations, things have become too cluttered and it’s more difficult to succeed. Competition is generally a healthy thing, Frank, but too much of it can be unhealthy for a person. Take, for example, my upcoming PTC Global title defense, against three other wrestlers.” Latch had come to realize that this was mostly his fault, as he was the one who opened the lemonade stand to begin with. Without that, none of this would be happening. He brought this upon Frank. He also brought his current PTC fate upon himself, by being as bold as to win the GTD title, and then being thrown into a four-way title defense. “My goodness!” Frank exclaimed. “That’s right! So what are you doing rambling on about some silly little lemonade stand for? You should be focusing on those three challengers, not on that kid selling lemonade on the corner.” It figured that Brad would be thinking about other people over himself, Frank thought. He was a great friend in that respect, but at the same time, it could hurt Latch as the match drew near. And now he was sitting here, casually sipping a latte and talking about some child prodigy businessman? Nope, not this time. “I’m not going to stand for it, Brad. You’re done here.” “I’m sorry, what?” Latch replied. “What do you mean, done? Done where?” “Here. Sitting here, talking about Timmy and how he’s crowding in on my business. You’ve got bigger things to worry about, and I can’t let you lose sight of that. You may not think it’s fair to me that Timmy is out there peddling that lemonade like it’s going out of style, but it’s also not fair to you that I let you sit in here and talk about it all day.” Latch glanced at the clock behind the counter. “Half an hour by no means constitutes all day, Frank,” Latch said with a grin. “No, half an hour doesn’t, you’re right. But that’s likely to turn into an hour, which can easily turn into three, and before you know it, you’ve wasted half the day worrying about nonsense like this. Now, come on, finish up your latte,” Frank snapped his fingers while Latch drank down the rest of the coffee, and set the mug down, “and get out there and worry about the important stuff. You can do it, Brad, I know you can. Go take care of those three guys, and then we’ll figure out how to handle Timmy.” Frank pushed Latch up, and the XUW and PTC champion smiled and turned and headed for the door. He pushed the door open and stepped outside, and held it open for a few loyal customers who couldn’t be swayed by lemonade; that, and Latch figured these teenagers were probably too hip to drink lemonade on a street corner, anyway. He walked toward his car, and shook his head. He definitely had some work to do this time.
