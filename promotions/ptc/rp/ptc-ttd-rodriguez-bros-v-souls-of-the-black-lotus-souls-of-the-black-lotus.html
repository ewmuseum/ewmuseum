TTD - Rodriguez Bros. -v- Souls of the Black Lotus

21 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
GW’s – Souls of the Black Lotus Tag Title Division Week 3 RP Title: The Key To Winning – Continuity – .::What value can we give to the virtue of continuity? How can one give value to that which is of seemingly endless use and importance? How truly important can continuity be to a team? Continuity is the essence of teamwork, the very being of a team. So how can continuity help a team so much? Continuity is the virtue by which a team’s strength can be measured. A way in which to measure the full character of a team. A virtue by which an entire team’s strengths and weaknesses can be measured simultaneously. A virtue which is the backbone of a team and team success. A virtue that when absent can lead only to disaster and a sheer team breakdown. So how can continuity be measured? Not by wins or losses. But instead, the ability to pull through hard times. The ability to persevere against all odds. The ability to bounce back after a big loss or after a long slump. The true value of continuity cannot be measured but can be seen in all of the great teams of history. The Edmonton Oilers dynasty years, the Cincinnati Reds; “The Big Red Monster” and most of all the golden days of baseball and the New York Yankees. “FUCK!!” The words echoed throughout the long dark corridors, Ichiro looked backwards at his brother. Jushin was walking slowly with his head hung low, he knew why and he felt the same way. It was never easy to cope with a loss, but he knew that the key to them winning was for them to bounce back. He replayed the match through his mind, they had made a few sloppy errors and it had cost them. “Do not trouble yourself Jushin,” started Ichiro. “We are not out of this tournament yet, not by a long shot. Our next opponents are in the lead, but with a win over them, we can make it to the finals.” “We shouldn’t have lost out there tonight! We shouldn’t have to win this next match! We blew it, Ichiro!” Jushin yelled back. Jushin stopped and threw his back against the wall. He sank to a seated position in the shadows of the long hallway. He had a towel over his head, but that did not stop him from burying his head into his hands. He slumped over and leaned his head back. He was pissed off and Ichiro knew it, but there was nothing he could do. That was Jushin, always to quick to give up, but always ready once inspired. Ichiro knew what he had to do, Jushin would be ready to kick ass soon enough, he just needed the right inspiration. Ichiro sat down beside his little brother and sat in silence for several minutes. He had done this many times before and he was pretty sure he had it about as close to mastered as it would ever get. He sat in silence merely to comfort his brother and give Jushin time to settle down. Jushin hung his head against the wall in shame, he was pissed off. He felt like he had let his brother down, he couldn’t help the feeling it came naturally. Needless to say he was pissed off, but the feeling of having his older brother nearby was a strong sense of comfort for him. “How do we come back from a loss like that!?” he screamed, more at himself than at his brother. “Relax Jushin, we are brothers. Do you not have faith in our friendship? We have pulled through tougher things than this together,” said Ichiro. “Like what!?” Jushin’s voice was sarcastic and still filled with rage. “Like the death of our father, like the tones and tones of fights we got into as kids, like the tournaments in the temple when it seemed like everyone was against us and everyone wanted us to lose,” said Ichiro. “Or how about the time when there was a huge bounty on my head and together we took out a small militia!” “You know what brother, you are right!” exclaimed Jushin. “I have no reason to doubt our skills, we have never been held down for long, and I will be damned if I sit here and allow it to start now! Hell, I say we go out here for our next match and we give the Rodriguez Brothers hell. Sure they are brothers, but we will truly prove who is the most dominant family in the wrestling business today! We will go to the finals, and when we get there, we will win the PTC Tag Titles!” Jushin felt suddenly relieved, he didn’t know how his brother did it, but somehow he always got him thinking optimistically. Ichiro just had that effect on him. There was just something about his big brothers presence that made him relax. Jushin began thinking of all the hard times he and his brother had fought through together. The memories came back fast, but one began to replay in his head. He saw the Shao-Lin Temple where he and Ichiro had grown up. He saw himself sitting in a small locker room, pouting and crying in the corner. He heard loud screams coming from inside, the memory began to take shape and he remembered what it was. This was at one of the battle competitions that they had in the temple. Ichiro walked through the door and Jushin began to piece together where he was. Jushin had just lost his match and would need to win all his next fights by significant point values to win the tournament. He was crying, but as Ichiro sat down beside him, his spirits instantly began to life. Ichiro put his arm around his little brother and began to speak, softly and soothingly. “Jushin, what’s wrong man?” asked Ichiro. “I am sucking it up. I have lost almost all my matches so far, and I can’t even do a freaking roundhouse kick and land it, I’m getting my ass kicked out there!” said Jushin. “Don’t worry bro, you still have a shot at it. You can bounce back from this, you’ve come through before, come on, how would Master Tanaka feel if you just gave up?! Get out there and kick some ass, I have faith. You need to win your next matches but you can do it, you can bounce back and you can win this tournament,” Ichiro’s voice was soft and assuring. “You really think so?” asked Jushin, still doubting himself but his spirits rising. “I know so, you are the toughest kid your age I know. The match I just saw you lose, the kid fighting, that wasn’t my little brother that is someone who is letting himself blow this thing, now I know you know better than that! So, don’t get down, kick some ass out there!” Ichiro said as he stood up to leave. He began to walk away and left Jushin sitting there by himself. Jushin looked around and picked himself up off the bench, from outside in the dojo area he could hear his match being called onto the mat. He wiped the tears from his eyes and stepped up. He tightened his belt and stepped outside. Jushin let a smile spread across his face for the first time that night. He remembered how well his brother’s inspirational speech had worked then, he had bounced back from those losses and eventually went on to win that tournament. Ichiro noticed the smile and knew that his job was done, Ichiro stood to his feet. He then reached down and helped his little brother to his feet before they made their way down the hallway and back home. Ichiro sat perched atop his apartment complex. He always enjoyed meditating from this spot, it was nearly midnight and the moon was at its full peak in the sky. He smiled the city of Dallas looked so peaceful from here, he began to relax slightly. He let his mind and body separate, as he sat cross-legged atop the roof ledge. His muscles began to seemingly disappear as his meditation began. He was now separated from his body, it was always weird at first. To see yourself sitting cross-legged on a rooftop from above, but he did not let it bother him for long. He soon began to float above the clouds and across the city. His mind began to refocus from what it was seeing in the real world to the true focus of his meditation. His upcoming match in the PTC Tag Title Tournament. It was hard to describe his meditation, as nothing was ever truly what it seemed to be. It was as if dreaming, everything appeared in symbols. However, he had begun to realize what the symbols meant after his many years, he and Jushin were the easiest. Represented by seemingly general balls of light, Jushin was black and himself, he was white. His opponents were usually symbolized by the association his mind made with them. This time he saw two more balls of light. He understood why instantly, his opponents were brothers, just like he and Jushin. So that is how his mind associated them and chose to show them, however they were matching red balls of light. Ichiro watched in awe as a story was told between the two balls of light, the meditation session often took only half an hour, but it seemed like hours were spent as he watched the story unfold in his mind. It was like a silent version of Fantasia. Watching the balls of light go about their separate stories before intertwining together and facing off for one epic battle. But the result was quite assuring to Ichiro. As he walked off of the roof he felt an unbreakable sense of confidence. A great confidence in himself and in his brother, a confidence that it was destiny for them to succeed. Together he and Jushin had bounced back from great pains to make great gains, and they would do so again. They would make it to the finals of the PTC Tournament and they would win. It was part of the destiny of the Souls of the Black Lotus. “Great pains can often lead to great gains, if you have the character.” – Anonymously donated.
