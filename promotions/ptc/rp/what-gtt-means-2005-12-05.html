What GTT Means

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 What does GTT mean? What does being part of it mean to those involved? To those who watch the tournament from a distance? What is it that makes GTT so special? What makes it the talk of the town? What is about the tournament that drives people to come out in droves to be a part of the giant single elimination tournament? Why am I here? What is it that makes Buddy Kingfisher want to be part of this whole circus? Why do I care about what the GTT means? What was the defining reason that got me to sign my name to the application that would give me a shot at competing in what maybe considered the most difficult, honored, and loved tournaments in this entire sport? I'm on a mission. An important mission. A mission that is one of the most important in the history of my career. The GTT has roled around and the time has come for me to step up or put aside any hopes of ever making the upper tier of Primetime Central wrestlers. There are great wrestlers, entertainers, and what not in every single company that graces our fine sport. There are tons of guys who can go hold for hold with anyone in the business. There are plenty of other guys like myself out there who can entertain the fans so much that they go home happy every single night with a smile on their faces. There are, however, very few people who can truly call themselves a legend in this sport. Primetime Central has one of the biggest, if not the biggest, collection of legends in this entire business. You've got Seymour Almasy, Angelo Deville, Rich Rollins, Xavier Kannon, and the list goes on an on. Men who've seen it all, said it all, done it all, and lived it all. They all have the common ground of being able to make a huge impact when it comes to stepping it up against the top competition that PTC has to offer. Angelo Deville has won the GTT before. Rich Rollins is the current Global Champion. Xavier Kannon once upon a time held the top prize that PTC had to offer. Seymour Almasy is Mr. PTC with his tournament performances and multiple title wins with the wrestling giant. They have all accomplished the huge moments in their careers that are needed to be considered one of the very best that this entire industry has to offer. This year is my shot. GTT is my chance to finally step up my game and prove that I too belong in the same breath as those men and others like them. Winning the whole tournament is the goal but it isn't the only route to stardom in PTC. Sure if I win then it absolutely cements the fact and it can never be taken away from me. Still a great showing can go a long way in proving my point. The point that I belong in the ring with each and everyone of them as one of the best. Most people outside of Core Wrestling and their fans have more than likely never heard of Buddy Kingfisher. I'm not some household name that everyone automatically talks about when they mention the wrestling business. Unless Core Wrestlings flagship show Pride is on your weekly dose of wrestling fixes then you more than likely have never heard of the big, fat, red head who isn't exactly the smartest guy in the world. If you've never watched Pride then you've never seen The Partys Over executed, never seen me hold up the Classic Championship. You wouldn't have any clue that I'm the Core Legacy Champion with not one but TWO victories over Core legend and future Hall of Famer Munson. Outside of my legion of Core Wrestling fans Buddy Kingfisher is just another one of those long list of names in the field of 128 of this years GTT that makes people go…"Who?". Does it bother me that I'm not a household name? Do I care that I'm not as popular, loved, respected, and considered one of the greatest in this business like they are? Sure I do. Everyone wants to be respected for what they do. Everyone wishes they can walk out to the ring each and every week and be the favorite. Is it the most important thing in the world to me? At this point yes it is. You might wonder why. You might ask yourself why a guy as young as me who hasn't been in the wrestling business, at least not the big time part of the business, for very long would consider this so important already. The truth is that even at this young age I'm not some fool. The time at the top for a wrestler in this sport isn't exactly the longest tenured job in the world. I'm on the top of the world in Core right now and its great but I know soon enough I will no longer be there. Someone else will have that top spot. GTT is one of the very few chances in a fast closing window of oppurtunity that I've got to try and step through and take my spot on the pedastal. To most everyone in this sport Buddy Kingfisher is a flash in the pan and someone to forget about after the first round or two of this tournament. Fodder for some other much more important wrestler in this thing that is just dying to add another notch to their huge belt of accomplishments. Buddy Kingfisher at this point is nothing more than the red head idiot who everyone is going to forget about as quickly as this first round is over. So it is with all this on my mind that I have to walk into the first round to face off with another man who is an unknown. A guy who hasn't got any respect in this sport outside of his home company either. A man who is facing the same mountain that I am as he walks into GTT wanting to prove all those same people wrong that I do. He has all the same hopes, dreams, aspirations, and desires to make it far into the GTT so that he can also be a man who people look to as a threat. Lots of people think that guys like Angelo Deville and Rich Rollins have it tough cause they have to face one another in the first round with one of them having to go home right away. That is fine and dandy for most to believe but the fact is that guys like me have it far more tough cause if a guy like Rollins or Deville lose to one another then they haven't lost anything really. They just lost to another big name in PTC. If Buddy Kingfisher loses to Evan Jackson it is just another nobody losing to another nobody. There is no lasting impression for someone who gets beat by some one who has never accomplished anything in PTC. For both Evan and I this is a must win situation. Getting to the next round and a shot at someone like Deville, Almasy, or some other huge name for a shot at instant stardom. If you lose you really haven't lost anything. If you win then you've cemented yourself as someone to be worried about. That is what GTT is going to be for me. A chance to walk in as the dark horse and walk out a legend. The shot at knocking off a person or two that I'm not suppose to be able to hang with and cement myself as a major player in the history of this sport. This one shot to face off with some of the greatest talent in the world that I might not ever get another shot to face. A guy like Craig Miles who getting a shot at in GTT is probably the only time you'd ever see a Buddy Kingfisher vs Craig Miles match up. Classic matches, instant stardom, legends being born right before the eyes of those who love this business, and dream matches that might not be seen anywhere else are all the things that make the annual GTT one of the greatest tournaments ever assembled. It is what attracts people to enter the tournament, to watch the tournament, and to honor the tournament as one of the most important and exciting times of the entire year. The time to walk down that aisle and begin to take my place in the halls of Primetime Central history. It begins with Evan Jackson in the first round of GTT5. It ends with… Only time and a first round victory will be able to tell. +++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++
