The Needle In The Haystack

28 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-09-28 In The Dead Of Night God’s wrath could be a funny thing for the cynics in the world. For reasons beyond human understanding, Katrina had blown into the coast and devastated everything. Entire towns had been removed from the map, families had been separated and thousands had died. Still, the worst toll was the emotional drain. People could never completely recover from being reminded of their insignificance in the grand scheme of the world. Now, as the world slowly recovered from the reality destroying events another threatened the world once again. Where Katrina had been unexpected, Rita was well known. They were already tracking it out in the Gulf of Mexico. There had been no order to evacuate yet, but it was simply a matter of time before the order was issued. Families had been rushed from Louisiana to the open arms of the Lone Star State, but now they faced another evacuation. Depending on the tracking you followed, the storm could be even more destructive than the first. The oil industry in Port Arthur was under severe threat, along with all the coastal communities and the nation’s fourth most populous city. Rita was a bitch to say the least. As they stepped off the plane he was struck once again. The heat had not relented in Texas, where the seasons weren’t ever four distinct ones but a long summer followed by a mild winter that lasted but a few months. The three-digit temperature was accompanied by humidity from the nearby sea that made your clothes sticky and uncomfortable. At least the sun not being out kept the temperature in the nineties, but the humidity was even worse as night winds carried more sea air to the city. Jake stepped down the short series of steps, feeling the weight of gravity and the world on his body as his booted feet echoed inside the hangar. He waited for his two friends to join him in disembarking from the plane. They were all worn out and beaten down. Dirty clothes, streaks of dried sweat and a general malaise washed over them all. Jake was hoping everything would be okay. Part of him had already given up on his family, and it wanted the proof to fly off into a rage without guilt. That animalistic part wanted the freedom to attack without thought…only rabid purpose of destruction. Hawk was the most depressed of them all. He had agreed to take over the business, and help out in the search. Everything had worn on him heavily, and old bones did not support such burdens well. He was cranky, becoming short and annoyed at the slightest words or gestures. He had seen there was no blood on the boat, but he expected them to be dead anyway. They might find one or two of them, but losing half of his extended family was not a victory as far as he was concerned. He considered himself a failure. Sensational Stephen Reed was in the best mood. Unlike the other two men he accompanied, he faced a certainty that could not be avoided. Where they saw despair, he saw hope because there was still a chance everyone was safe and secure. Stephen thought the path they walked was a little easier now that his own fate was sealed. Without a family of his own, he relied on his family by choice to remember him, to be protected by him and now to be rescued by him. The two white pills he had taken just before descent were beginning to have an effect, and he enjoyed the side effects. The three men walked slowly over to where the rental car had been left. They climbed inside the black Dodge Caravan, with Hawk taking the driver’s seat. He made sure everyone was ready to go, and then put the car in gear and took off using as much power as the engine could muster. Surprisingly, the engine was quite powerful bringing them up to above speed limit speeds quickly. They had some addresses to try, but being armed in the middle of the night pounding on residential doors would quickly invite the police. After a lot of talk, Hawk and SSR were able to convince Jake to sleep for four or five hours before the picked up in the morning. If they were behind bars, even temporarily, then their family was lost. They pulled into the hotel parking lot, and slowly climbed out of the car. The weapons they brought were stowed away in the cases. Check-in didn’t take long, and they all made their way to the rooms. Purposely, they had gotten rooms right next to each other. After walking inside they were asleep within minutes. Beginning Of The Day The ringing sounded like an explosion in his head. He threw the comforter off his body, exposing unready eyes to the bright Texas sunlight streaming through the windows. The ringing and sunlight only added to the headache he just now realized was trying to implode and explode his skull simultaneously. Jake tried to grab the phone, but his uncoordinated movements just knocked the phone off the hook. He pulled the cord like a rope until he had retrieved the handset. Jake put it up to his ear, listening to the automated wake-up call announcement. Jake dropped the phone down, missing the receiver as he threw the blankets completely off his body. Slowly, he stumbled to the bathroom. Jake turned on the shower, and stepped right inside. The cold water was a startling wake-up call, and he shivered as his body rebelled against the cold. Jake grabbed the cheap hotel soap from the dish, and ripped off the wax paper covering it. He tossed the paper over the top of the rod and then went through the process of cleaning himself up. Soap was followed by shampoo to clean out his long hair. He was finally human again as he stepped out and wrapped the long towel around his waist. Hix grabbed another towel off the rack as he stepped out of the bathroom. As he returned to the bedroom he heard the beeping from the phone, and put it onto the receiver like he should have done earlier. Jake toweled off all the water, and then slipped into a pair of jeans and muscle shirt with no logo. The long black socks and cowboy boots went on next, and he was ready for the day. Jake dialed Hawk’s room. “Hello?” “How’re you doing old man?” Jake teased his friend. Hawk laughed, “I may be old, but last time I checked when I went out with a beautiful woman I didn’t just drop her at her door step like you did.” “Oh, touché.” Jake liked the give and take with his friend. “You ready for the day?” “Just slipping my shoes on now. Stephen called me earlier. He went to grab us some breakfast taquitos for the road, and we’ll be ready to move.” Hawk pulled his tennis shoes on while he spoke with Jake. Jake began packing his things into his bag, “Good, then let’s get out of here.” “Meet you downstairs in five.” Jake finished packing his gear, and then headed down to the lobby. Stephen and Hawk were waiting on him. Stephen tossed a Whataburger bag to him. Jake caught it and kept walking. They followed him out into the parking lot. “You eat, I’ll drive.” Stephen announced as he stepped around to the driver’s side of the car. “Coke is in the cup holder for you.” Jake loaded his bags into the back of the mini-van. He climbed in and began devouring his taquitos and hash browns, washing them down with Coke. Stephen took them through the streets and highways of Houston. The first possible address was the Methodist Church on the West side of town. Many of the buses had moved there as a sort of weigh station before final assignment to the good families who had opened up their homes to evacuees. The trip only took twenty minutes, and they pulled into the crowded parking lot where all the refugees had parked their escape vehicles. They parked at the back of the parking lot, where there was a single open spot. Jake had finished his breakfast, and carried the trash with him as he kept sipping on his Coke. There was plenty of activity, even in the early morning hours. They walked inside, and after a little asking around they were directed to one of the Deacons who was in charge of organizing the evacuees housing. They all waited patiently for their opportunity to speak with James Porter. “Gentlemen, how can I assist you?” James asked as he led them into a small office. Jake took the lead, “We’re looking for some family members we lost in the evacuation. We understand they may have come through your church.” “What were their names?” Porter asked. “Well, that may be tricky.” Jake leaned forward, giving the appearance of secrecy as he spoke. “There were problems on the road. We think they were hit by looters, and they hurt one really badly. They lost their wallets, so they aren’t using their real names. They’re on the run in all sorts of ways, and I just want to protect them.” The Deacon looked them over carefully. He wasn’t quite sure, “What will you do?” “When all of this is over they will have to make amends for what they have done. Even if they were attacked, it wasn’t worth hurting the young man like they did. Restitution has to be made, and if they have to answer for what they did to the authorities I’ll stand beside them.” Jake answered smoothly. James Porter thought about it for a little while, “What were their names?” “Black.” “We haven’t had anyone named Black we’ve assigned.” James checked the list to be sure, but all the names were burned into his brain at the moment. “Do you have a picture?” Jake pulled his wallet out from his back pocket. He put down two pictures in front of the Deacon. “These are our two brothers.” “I remember them.” James nodded. “These were big boys, much like yourself. They were here with two young women. What was their name…what was their name?” ‘You’ve seen them?” Hawk was on the edge of his seat now. His melancholy was broken by the glimmer of hope, “They were all okay?” James tapped Xavier’s picture, “He didn’t look so good physically, but he was stronger than anyone else mentally. I saw it in his eyes, he was determined to make it through. They were all shaken up, more so than a lot of our evacuees. I’m trying to remember their name.” Hawk and Stephen began whispering to each other. James picked up a radio, “Michael, this is James are you there?” A moment later a voice came back through, “I’m here.” “Do you remember those four evacuees, and two of them were big strapping men. They looked like football players?” James asked. “Yeah.” “What were their names?” “I sent them off this morning.” Michael said as he flipped over his notes. “That was the Boudreaux family. We sent them off to the Johnson family on Cherry Lane.” “Thank you Michael.” James set the radio down. “I have their address here. I can give it to you, and you can pick them up at their home. I’ll let them know you’re coming.” Jake stood up, “Thank you Deacon.” They all stood, “Reuniting a family during all of this is part of the joy in doing God’s work.” Jake shook the Deacon’s hand. “We appreciate it. How are things treating you here sir?” “We’re doing what we can. No one was ready for this, but we have a generous community.” James smiled. “Sometimes our worst disasters bring out the best in each of us.” Jake reached into his pocket. He pulled out the folded bills in the money clip, and handed them off to the Deacon. “I hope this can help. We expected to be searching on the road forever, so this is just a little more than I thought I would have to spend on hotels. We’ll send another donation when we get back. Keep doing God’s work Deacon.” The Deacon stared down at the money surprised, “Thank you son.” Hawk nodded, “The least we can do.” They headed out, one step closer to finding the family. Cherry Lane Street names usually had some sort of idea or history. Whatever reason the street had been named Cherry Lane was long since lost in the history of the city. The upper-middle class neighborhood was perfectly maintained, and there was plenty of peer pressure if a lawn ever got too long in the blade or the gardens weren’t perfectly maintained. Jake had purposely sat in the back of the van. The tinted windows concealed him from the world around. He should have felt good about finding his family. He should have been happy, ecstatic even. He couldn’t find any joy at the moment because there was a nagging feeling inside his gut that something was terribly wrong. The feelings had been kept to himself. He didn’t want to worry his friends unnecessarily. Even as he stepped through the events of the last few days in his mind, he felt he hadn’t seen everything. There were pieces of the puzzle missing, and he couldn’t put them together. Sid had sent what was essentially a death squad to kill his family in Louisiana, but that attempt had failed. He couldn’t believe that the lone attempt would be the end of Sid’s pursuit of his brother. “Do me a favor Stephen.” Jake piped up from the backseat. Stephen looked in his rearview mirror, “What?” “Drive by the house.” Jake said. “Just do a straight drive by and turn down one of the other streets.” Hawk turned toward his friend. He was still feeling on the top of the world. He wanted to see everything through rose-colored glasses, and he didn’t want anyone to taint that view he was holding onto at the moment. With weary eyes he asked the question he didn’t want the answer to, “You think something’s wrong here Jake?” “Let’s just say this has seemed far too easy for my taste.” Jake’s stare didn’t waiver. “If something is wrong, I want to know before we’re out in the open.” Hawk turned back around. Jake could be paranoid. Plenty of people felt defeat right before they achieved a long sought after goal they never thought they would reach. Jake might be falling into that old trap, but he doubted Jake would have succumbed that easily. He was usually rock solid, and he hadn’t shown anything like dangerous self-destruction. He was on track, just like he had been at the Baton Rouge airport. Stephen turned down the street, and Jake kept his eyes peeled for anything strange. He was looking at every corner, every nook and cranny and even in the backs of cars as they passed bye. Hawk pointed to a house on the left, “That’s where they’re staying.” Jake didn’t look where the finger was pointing. He was still watching the street, and he saw what he thought he needed to see. Only a brief moment when the curtain was pulled aside gave him all the glimpse he needed. Sid had men watching the family. They could be there to keep an eye until reinforcements arrived, or they could be waiting on Jake to show up so they could all be taken at once. No matter, he wasn’t going to let anything ruin it for his family now. “Take a right up here and let me out. Circle around, and park about three houses in front of their place. Stay there for a little bit, make sure anyone looking can see you.” Jake ordered as he pulled one of the bags to the floor in front of him. “What do we do then?” Stephen asked, as he turned right and stopped. He looked at Jake in the rearview mirror, “What are you going to do?” Jake pulled the Beretta out from the bag and checked it over quickly. The pistol went into the small of his back, “Go say hi to our family, and I’m going to make sure no one interrupts the reunion.” “Okay.” Hawk shrugged, knowing Jake was set in his idea. Jake hopped out of the mini-van and began walking down the sidewalk. He was calming himself, staying loose as he covered each yard in the manicured neighborhood. SSR made it around and parked exactly where he wanted them to park right out in the open. They were visiting with each other, killing time while Jake continued covering the distance to the house. Jake was within two homes of the house he had spotted someone watching traffic from. He stepped up onto the thick, soft carpet of grass for the neighbors. He stayed near the structure as he moved down the length of the two houses to the one he suspected might be occupied by someone other than a good neighbor. Jake nodded at SSR and Hawk. They stepped out of the van, and began walking to the house where the family was supposed to be staying. Jake saw the curtains open once again, but he couldn’t see who might be there concealed behind the designer curtains. Jake waited for the curtains to close, and then bending low he moved over to the wall near the front door. Jake waited, in the blind spot for anyone coming out of the house. As SSR and Hawk walked up the concrete path to the front door Jake heard the deadbolt slide open. He waited; ready to take the action he knew must be taken. They knocked on the front door, and a young girl opened it. She and his friends spoke for a moment, and then she disappeared. A scream came from the house, and Alex came running out, leaping into Hawk’s arms. Jake’s heart soared for a moment, and then he heard the front door open. He waited for a three count before turning the corner to confront whatever was there. The eyes were brown and sharp. A normal sized man, he had the look of someone working for Sid with fanatical devotion in his eyes and a weapon in his right hand. The sharp eyes slacked in confusion, as the man obviously didn’t expect to see Jake standing before him. Jake grabbed the man’s wrist and violently twisted it over. The move sent the weapon flying out of his hand. Jake slipped behind the man and wrapped one arm around his throat while his other arm came around to cover his mouth. The young man struggled, but he was completely overmatched by the huge man pulling him back to the door. Across the street, Jake caught a brief glimpse of his family as they all rushed out of the house to embrace their friends. The family was reunited again, and in that brief moment all of the worry left Jake’s stressed body. He closed the front door with his foot, and then snapped the man’s neck as though it were a dry twig. Jake pulled out the pistol and began a systematic search of the home. He checked downstairs, and found no one. He moved upstairs slowly, not letting any footfalls give away his approach. Jake went through each room, not finding the truth until he checked the master bathroom. Piled into the bathtub and the shower were the four bodies of the family who truly owned the home. They had been murdered, cut apart and thrown into the two receptacles. Piled on top were air fresheners to keep the smell away. The man had been alone. Jake put the pistol back into the small of his back. He walked back down the stairs to the ground floor, and opened the front door where his family was still out front visiting. Jake walked across the street, time seeming to stand still as Xavier and Pitt broke to approach him. Word could not express the depths of emotions between the brothers. They embraced, not worrying about what anyone might think of them. They were finally together again, and that was all that mattered. Jake broke away as tears fell down his face, “Everyone’s okay?” “We made it.” Xavier squeezed his arm reassuringly. Pitt nodded, “We’re all together again.” “We need to leave, now. As soon as possible.” Jake said sternly. “Why?” Xavier looked over at the girls, “We just got to see each other. Let’s have breakfast.” “I just killed one of Sid’s men across the street. More men will be coming. We can’t wait for them to get here. Grab any bags you need, and let’s go now. I have the plane waiting at the airport.” Jake’s explanation got Xavier and Pitt’s attention. Jake walked by the men and picked his sister up in a huge hug, “I worried everyday.” Alex was crying as her brother held her, “I missed you. I missed you so much.” “Everything is okay now. I promise.” Jake let her down, and then hugged Bella. “Good to see you too.” “You look well.” Bella kissed his cheek. “I am. Now everyone go get your things and we will leave. Men are coming.” Jake looked each of the girls in the eye, and they understood. All four ran inside to get their bags. Stephen came over, “What is it?” “Sid’s man was across the way. He won’t be here for nothing. I want us back in the air as soon as possible.” Jake explained. Hawk smiled, “Back to house. We’ll be safe on our own territory.” “We’re never safe.” Jake corrected him. “But we’ll be better protected there.” Jake walked over to the mini-van, “Help them, I’ll stand watch.” Jake leaned against the vehicle, and swept eyes up and down the street. No one was around, and he hoped it would stay that way. No more than three minutes passed when everyone came out with their baggage. Hawk and SSR lent them a hand, and they proceeded to load the mini-van. As they were loading, a black S.U.V. turned down the street. Jake stepped out away from the car. He locked his gaze on the driver, and he knew without hesitation that the men inside the vehicle were ready to do harm. He stepped into the middle of the street and stood his ground. The car stopped one hundred feet away, and Jake locked his eyes onto the driver’s eyes. They were talking inside, but Jake’s fierceness did not waiver at all. He heard the frantic loading behind him, “Get in the car. I’ll handle this.” More sounds of scraping and the engine fired up. Jake moved his arm behind his back, clutching the pistol in case he needed to draw it. He wanted to have it out here, he wanted to send the message to Sid and most of all he wanted to have more blood on his hands this morning. Come on you pussies…keep coming forward…I’m just one man, aren’t I? Jake could taste the anticipation in the air. He wanted this more than anything else he had ever wanted. The message needed to be delivered once and for all. He would no longer run, and he would not let his family be taken advantage of anymore by thugs and bullies. The driver reversed, backing into one of the driveways and taking off away from the scene. The turned the corner, screeching tires as they fled from the confrontation. “Pussies.” Jake spat on the ground and turned to his family. Xavier, Pitt, Stephen, Hawk, Bella and even Alex were all standing there holding onto various weapons. They were all ready to defend themselves, and Jake realized they would have seen the entire group armed and ready when they had stopped. “What, you didn’t think it was just your angry grimace that did it, did you?” Pitt asked with a wicked little grin. Jake felt his cheeks flush in embarrassment. Xavier leaned on his brother’s shoulder, “Well, his face is scary enough as it is. With the grimace too, he could have scared them off on his own.” “I was scared.” SSR added. Hawk laughed, “So macho.” They all broke out into a much needed laugh, all at Jake’s expense.
