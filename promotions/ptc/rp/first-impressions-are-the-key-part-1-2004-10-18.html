First Impressions are the key..(Part 1)

18 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-18 “Elia,” “Yes?” “Will you marry me?” The words that every woman would love to hear. And at this very second, those words were asked. As I looked down from the nice water fall, I could see the sparkle in Thomas’s eyes. Just like a kids who got the bike that he wanted at Christmas, he eyes danced from side to side. His smile connected from his left to his right ear as my hand was held, feeling the sweat pour off the two of our hands. “Ye…” What was I to say? I wasn’t sure of anything as I felt the tears work up. Not sad tears, no. They were the kind that you got when you have your first baby. They were the kind that you got when your live was changed by something that you only dream about. I shook my head as the rain from the clouds above began to pour down. “Elia Katrina Smith,” Thomas said, looking up at my crying and wet face, staring down towards the love of my life. “Please say yes so that I can spend the rest of my life with you forever.” I knew the answer. I knew the words that I was to say but they just wouldn’t come out. They were so clear. So right for me to place out but I couldn’t get them to. I looked down at his sparkling screaming face, ready to hear me say that one word… 12 hours before… “May I see that dress?” Walking into one of Dallas’s primer dress shops, I had my eyes set on one thing. A satin red dress that was placed on the female manikin in the clear company window. It looked so beautiful for the occasion tonight. Thomas was taking me out for dinner to try and get my mind off both the wrestling ring, which has stressed me more then anything and the death of my wonderful father. And I got to say, I needed it. The store wasn’t busy. A fresh smell like the kind in a new car you get on a car lot show room, made me sniff my nose in the air. Looking around, I could tell the lady wasn’t to sure of me. Her eyes told the whole story. “That dress…?” Her words were even more unsure. Like a mother that just found out that her husband of eleven years wanted a divorce. What dress did she think I wanted? Turning towards the window rank, the lady and I passed by the discount clothing that they had in the store. There were several dresses that I liked. One, a faded blue into a light green dress with the slit on the side going up. That would so turn on Thomas I knew. But the one I wanted. Something deep inside of me told me that it would do more then get Thomas and every other man in this world a hard on if I walked into the room. “Are you sure you want this dress miss? I mean, we have a lot of other dresses in the store that would surely meet what you’d need.” The lady said as she turned the manikin around. Were we talking the same English here? Was I maybe giving off signals about something that I needed? And how the hell did she know what I needed? I shook my head in the no matter. Taking a look at the satin dress, feeling the effect it had on me in my hand. “How much is it?” Asking the lady as I crossed my hands at my chest. What ever Thomas was going to do tonight, it was well worth it. And besides, I was only going to be wearing it for a few hours. If he was a good boy I was going to be what makes his night tonight. The lady looked on the arm of the dress and grabbed the piece of paper that was on the dress. “My,” her eyes got bigger as she looked up at me. “It’s one hundred and thirty dollars.” Great. I had just enough. It was somehow less then what I was expecting. The lady turned the manikin around, back to facing the window. “I’ll take it..” Her eyes went wide. Even a kid could tell something was wrong. It was as if she knew something I didn’t. I reached into my torn up jeans and grabbed the cash that Thomas had gave me. But it wasn’t going to be that easy. The lady walked away from me. Walking towards the discount rake, pulling off a nice looking purple and black dress that shined like a diamond. It was nice but it wasn’t what I wanted. “Are you sure this isn’t what you want? I mean, that dress over there is just so expensive for you I can tell. This one is only Forty Dollars.” “Ok. What in the hell did she mean by that?” I said to myself, scratching my head for a moment in disgust. I didn’t want that dang dress. I wanted so much to scream at this woman. God, it was like a was trying to teach a six year old about trigonometry and they keep asking stupid questions. “LISTEN!” Screaming at the top of my lungs. Making an old woman turn around to figure out what was going on. “I WANT THAT DRESS…NOW!” Well that was it. I couldn’t take the nice approach to this. But after all the screaming, a man came over. Wearing a nice white button up t-shirt with a straight fine pair of black dress pants. He was clean shaved and his hair, slick back, just like a manager’s hair was. “Is there a problem here?” The man asking, looking right at his helper in the store. Why was he asking her? I’m the one with the problem. But as I stood there, the woman took a look down at my legs. Staring at the torn up and stained jeans that I decided to wear to the store. I swallowed a little before responding. “Yes there is a problem. I have money that can by that dress and this woman wants me to buy this dress.” I was angry. The day that was promised wasn’t starting out the way I wanted or the way Thomas said it would. I reached into my pocket and pulled out the two hundred dollars that Thomas handed me. Like lotto winners, the woman’s face and jaw dropped, surprised to see the money that I had. “See. I have the money. NOW I WANT THAT DRESS!” The customers in the store were starting to really make noise now. The man, guessing the manager, took his hand, trying to calm me down. It was over. My day was ruined and it was all the fault of a woman who judged me without even knowing who I was. I pressed my hair out of my face as the woman ducked her head. “I’m sorry…” The woman’s seemed sincere. Like she was fixing to cry. “I just thought…” “You just thought that because I’m wearing these torn up jeans and I look trashy and all that I wouldn’t have the money.” My eyes were starting to water with anger. I wasn’t about to buy a dress anymore. I was disrespected. I stuffed the money back into my pocket before leaning closer to the woman. “YOU CAN TAKE YOUR DRESS AND SHOVE IT!” I turned around, screaming those words as I began to walk towards the door. Grabbing the front door, the cold breeze hit my face, cooling the anger that I had in my body and mind. Why do people always judge others on their clothing and body features? Why does this world figure the poor don’t have rights to? I kept walking away down the street towards anywhere but here… ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- * The first part is actually the ending of this three RP series.
