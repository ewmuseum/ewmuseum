Aspiring for Approval III - Hard Times

9 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-09 There isn’t anything fancy about the surroundings I’m in. If it weren’t for the smallish banner hung limply over the ring, you’d probably think this was some sort of backwater federation with nothing to offer. But this was GTT and I was right in the middle of a match with the one guy I didn’t want to be. As about ten people watched from outside the ring, the contest just continued. GTT wasn’t meant to be a tournament of glitz and glamour and the powers that arranged it had made it as such. There were no fancy lights, no attractive women walking around, no explosive pyrotechnics. There wasn’t even any music for the competitors to enter the ring. It was gritty, it was cold and harsh, it was brutal and severe. And I revelled in it. Nothing had given me greater joy to see the anguish on some of the pretty boys as they wandered into the arena which would hold the GTT. Their despair at the lack of facilities and the bare bones attitude of the staff had turned many off as I noticed people walking away and out the building without even wrestling. That was fine by me, it just made the job easier. Wrestling was never meant to be about the glamour and over the top antics, even if it is today. It’s the guts and glory, the grim determination as two hulking masses trade blows one on one in a simple ring that was set up by those two wrestlers. That’s the way things should be, that’s the way I remember the good old territory days. They seemed far away now, but this, this event was a nice remembrance tour for me. One such person who was extremely pleased with the surroundings (in other words, not at all) was Adam Dick. The one man who had tormented me for so long. The one man who right now was looking…nervous and agitated. It was perfect, seeing him squirm and frustrate himself, envisioning him laid out on the mat in just over an hour’s time. Flash forward to that match. Sweat pouring down my head, grappling with the much smaller and lighter Adam Dick, I finally took control and lifted him high above the ring. Taking him down with my patented finisher I heard something rip and tear. I grumbled and assumed it was something of Dick’s. No such luck this evening. No such luck at all. *** ‘FUCK!’ I slammed my fist into the locker and slumped heavily on the bench, feeling the throbbing pain of my knee. My stupid left knee again. That bloody prick had known about it all this time and it had to be THIS day of all days to do it in. The pain was excruciating, going right from my lower calf and upwards to the knee. The only reason I hadn’t left the arena was because I had to wait. I had to wait for Jessica unfortunately. She had been on edge ever since our last conversation, she’d gone down to some motel just outside of town and it was only on the day before my match that she returned, a little bit calmer but still brooding. No words had been spoken between us and nothing could be done about it. I just had to gut it out until she realised her mistake. She tries to tell me about the important things in life and then remarks casually how insignificant wrestling is? I don’t know where it came from, but it sure as hell wasn’t from a girlfriend who I thought understood me. Maybe she didn’t anymore but there was nothing I could do right now. A few other wrestlers passed me by and nodded, some looking sympathetic, others grinning malevolently as they saw a prize kill in their sights. To each of those I took stead of their faces… …because I would remember them when I crushed them underfoot. I felt the swing door of the locker room whoosh behind me and then the slow clicking of heels could be heard behind me approaching. I tried to move but the pain was too much. Soon though I didn’t have to as Jess stood in front of me. Her face looked concerned, but one that wasn’t very amused by the situation. ‘What have you done now?’ ‘It’s nothing, trust me. I just need a bit of help to get to the doctor. I can’t exactly drive right now.’ Tilting her head, Jess gazed deep into my eyes and figured what she wanted to. I didn’t care what she thought at the time, because I had bigger fish to fry. I had higher anticipations…and the possibility that I might not get there. No, I would get there. There should be no doubt in my mind about this because I was going to get there. Nothing will stop my progress. Not an injury, not someone in my way and definitely not Adam Dick. It was his fault that all this has happened, it’s all his fault that I’m sitting here with a potential crippling injury. His fault. His fault. ‘Fine. Where to?’ ‘They recommended this specialist for while I was in town. Said he’d sort things out.’ ‘Ok then. Shall we go?’ She fidgeted awkwardly, looking around like she really didn’t want to be there, like something else was on her mind. She was looking lost amidst another world, something that despite her efforts to get into, could not. ‘Sure.’ ‘Let me…’ But I’d already struggled to my feet. I wasn’t about to let anyone see me showing any sort of weakness in me. I was going to tough it out, just like the gladiators would and grit it till the bitter end. A wounded animal certainly is a more dangerous one, but a predator being able to smell blood is worse. *** A clinical mind is something that can pinpoint exactly what you need to do and what necessary sacrifices should be made in order to reach that point. A philosophical mind will, on the other hand, consider hazy and perhaps inconsequential things, but a lot of the time they might just figure out a quicker way to the path than the clinical mind. This is really one of those times. ‘So you’ll see Mr. Murphy, as we look at the left knee…’ The specialist droned on and on about scar tissue and cartilage and other long-winded words that served only to confuse and befuddle a patient into doing whatever the hell he wanted. My brain switched off at this, tired of hearing bullshit from a doctor and simply waited until he finished. ‘…with that in mind I would recommend you rest up for at least a month and refrain from any physical activity, such as wrestling, for a minimum of two months.’ ‘And what are the alternatives?’ ‘What do you mean Mr. Murphy?’ ‘What are the alternatives? Because that blatantly isn’t going to happen.’ The specialist sighed to himself, taking a tug of his beard to think on it. Jessica meanwhile who was sitting to my left looked utterly bewildered by the entire sentence. Her jaw lay a little slack and she sputtered somewhat as the doctor gave it serious thought. ‘Jack, you can’t…you shouldn’t….’ ‘Quiet Jess. This is none of your concern.’ ‘None of my concern? I’m your girlfriend?’ ‘And you’re in a world that is separate to wrestling and my abilities. I will deal with that world, and I will deal with it alone.’ Shaking her head she fell back into the chair, crossing her arms and hunching up, trying not to look at me. ‘Well…I suppose I can prescribe some painkillers Mr. Murphy…but you must be fully aware of the consequences.’ ‘Consequences?’ ‘Yes, serious consequences. If your knee is to get injured while on this course that you have taken, you may never walk again. What is worse is that whilst I focused on your knee in the scan, it would be remiss of me not to tell you that your entire body is deteriorating at a good pace. I give you at tops two more years in this business, if you rest now. If you don’t…we could be talking months, or even weeks. Do you understand?’ There was no hesitation. ‘Where do I sign your responsibility away?’ He frowned and stared steadily at me but my gaze would not be broken. Coughing and spluttering for a moment, the doctor then turned to his notepad, writing the prescription down. Tearing it off he thrust it in my direction. ‘I wish you the best of luck Mr. Murphy in your endeavours. I hope that I will not be seeing you again anytime soon.’ ‘You won’t. I promise you that.’ ‘Hmm.’ I pushed myself to my feet and went for the door, seeing Jessica follow slowly behind me, holding back a tear or two. She didn’t understand, otherwise she wouldn’t be crying. How much this meant to me. How much I needed this. *** The drive back to the hotel from the hospital was quiet and reflective. Jessica kept her eyes on the road and did not once move from that vision. A couple of times I tried to put on the radio, but she quickly quashed that and so instead we would sit in silence, just like nervous lovers who aren’t even sure what they’re doing. It certainly felt like it. So we sat. And waited for the argument that would brew. We both knew it would come, we just wouldn’t do it in the car. So we waited. *** Of course once we got home, then it started. ‘I simply cannot believe that you think that what you’re doing is right.’ Jessica was in the middle of hysterics. I would recap the argument up until this point but it wasn’t really worth explaining. Settle in any sort of hissy fit by a woman over something that isn’t important to them but they argue anyway and you’ve got the gist. Currently, she was at the point where she was always on the verge of crying, but never quite doing it, just threatening. Her hands on her hips, her eyes flashing laser beams at me through spite, she waited for an answer to continue her tirade. Oh and there would be plenty of a tirade. ‘Can you at least see it from my point of view? This is my life Jessica, my life.’ She shook her head in disbelief. ‘But it isn’t Jack, don’t you see that? Look around you in this house. We have a comfortable, smooth life without any of the duress of the travels that you go through. You have the life that most people dream of. You’ve got the house, you’ve got the car, you’ve got the woman…and you definitely have the possibility of the family. What more could you possibly want?’ ‘Because you don’t understand what this means to me. You’ve never understood how much it means to be out there in front of the crowd, basking in the glory of the cheers or the jeers. Irrespective of whether or not they like you, you still feed off the energy. It’s a moment of pure adrenaline and a feeling of achievement. It’s something that can only be paralleled by sports, or musicians.’ I paused, taking in her gaze which was softened slightly but still determined. There was no turning back for her, just like my own path. ‘This is my life Jessica, one which you may never understand but something that you’re going to have to accept. There is nothing that is more important to me than wrestling in this tournament right now. Nothing and nothing can change that. Can’t you understand how I have toiled for this? Can’t you understand that I have given my life to get to this point?’ Tears fell to the wooden floor, creating a slow dull thud as they happened. Her head hung low and silent, unable to respond. In the briefest of moments she looked up and took stock of my resolute stance, then broke out into a full waterfall. She didn’t say a word, just fled up the stairs, leaving droplets of water all the way down the stairs as she went. Fuck that. I don’t need all of this. I don’t need people telling me what is and isn’t the right choice for my life, it is my life. So what if he told me that I could be paralysed, doesn’t she realise who he’s telling? Doesn’t she have any faith in me? I have been wrestling for well over fifteen years now and to think that she feels that I’m in trouble? I’m at the peak of my condition. I sat down in the living room and looked at the prescription drugs the doctor had given me. I’d already taken some but there wasn’t nearly enough of a buzz to keep me relaxed Luckily, I had the other painkillers in my bag and took them out. That’ll do to keep me relaxed. I soon found out that that would be a huge mistake…
