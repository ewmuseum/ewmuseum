The Sun is Burning: Part I

1 February 2006

Date: 2006-02-01 May those that love us, love us. And those that don't love us, May God turn their hearts. And if He doesn't turn their hearts, May He turn their ankles So we will know them by their limping. -An Irish Prayer ~<>~<>~<>~ The clouds were nothing short of a self-indulgent escape for Rich Rollins as he watched their painted parade smother the ceiling, moving across the canvas of his mind. With his boots kicked up along the curves of his therapist’s jungle camo-colored couch, the Renegade completely and utterly surrendered to his imagination. He was there, floating, playing grab ass with the entire 49er’s cheerleading team in those clouds. It was like sex without the uncomfortable inevitability of having to sleep on the wet spot, and the only talking he was forced to do existed in the comforts of fantasy. "I like a man with muscle," one of the cheerleaders breathed with sultry implication, slipping the baby blue strap of her bra down a shoulder of silk. "Want me to help work out the only one that matters?" Slipping an arm around the cheerleader’s bare stomach, the face of Rollins’ current mistress appeared with a white rose woven into the curls behind her left ear. Her eyes blazed like an emerald furnace as a mane of champagne hair washed over a face so mischievous it made the blood in Rollins’ veins run cold. Whispering into the other woman’s earlobe, Cynthia O’Doyle giggled. "Wanna go to Cloud Nine?" she taunted him. That’s when the Renegade’s therapist clapped his hands, forcing Rollins out of a fantasyland where he was pleasuring Dr. Paul O‘Doyle’s wife. He almost felt guilty. Almost. Dr. O’Doyle narrowed thoughtful eyes on Rollins. "You still with me there, champ?" The Renegade smiled with a guilty kind of satisfaction. "Only in body, doc. I could live in this office." It was true. Commissioned out of the abandoned husk of an old fifth-story dance studio situated in the towering skyscrapers of Chicago’s Miracle Mile, the office of Dr. Paul O’Doyle was remarkable for its ability to soothe the raging psyche. Sprawled across three stair-step levels of cheery lemon carpet, the room was organized into half a dozen sitting circles, each section of furniture upholstered in a different theme. One corner was fashioned in the style of a country cottage deck, complete with straw rocking chairs and a swinging stand-alone hammock. Others were spotted with polka dots or thick hippie shag throw rugs, each section paying homage to somebody’s childhood fantasy. Rollins preferred the camouflage. Rugged yet tasteful, just like his women. "So," the good doctor began, "I know you’ve had a rough couple of weeks. Is there anything you’d like to get off your chest?" I’ve gotten more ass from your wife in a week than you have in six months, was what almost said, but common sense stepped in before Rollins could crucify himself. Instead he shrugged, watching the cheerleaders beckon him back into the cloud-covered ceiling. "I fucking hate pikers." Direct and to the point, the Renegade let it go at that as Paul folded his arms. "You’re referring to Paddy O’Shea, your next opponent in GTT5?" He didn’t miss much, that Dr. O’Doyle. As an avid wrestling fan himself, Paul had been a fan of Rollins’ work for years. It was the primary reason he had offered to take on Rollins as a patient at Steven Caldera’s request, and it was the reason his wife was in an extracurricular relationship with the self-proclaimed anarchist. Nothing turned a woman on more than fame and a wedding ring, and it didn’t take much to figure out who possessed which. Paul chewed on his lower lip as he leaned back in his own camo recliner. "What is it about gypsies you dislike so much? Have you ever met one?" The Renegade rolled his eyes, trying to not to let his innate sarcasm out of its cage. "I already beat that bog-trotting spudfucker last round, so yeah, you might say we’ve been acquainted." Well, he tried anyway. "He wants to make himself look like some sort of political martyr, as if the British actually gave a shit about some smelly gypsy and his band of merry Micks." "You realize that it’s more a matter of religious freedom than a political agenda, right?" His brow creased in a fit of insolent dissent as Rollins turned to look his therapist in the eye. "No. People only kill people in the name of God when it serves a greater purpose." Dr. O’Doyle frowned. "What greater purpose is there?" Rollins chuckled, regarding Dr. O’Doyle as if he were a kindergartener and the Renegade was his woefully belligerent substitute teacher. "Death and taxes, doc. The only certainties we have in this world always revolve around cold hard cash, and the people who wish they had more of it." Dr. O’Doyle was about to come back with a response when out of nowhere his cell phone started screaming Grand Funk Railroad’s "Some Kind of Wonderful." Offering Rollins an apologetic look he whipped his Cingular out of a breast pocket and flicked it open. The Renegade couldn’t say the interruption bothered him. His cheerleaders were at it again, chanting pep rally-style as they proceeded to disrobe one another. A deep frown crossed the good doctor’s face as the voice on the other end squawked in his ear. "Yeah, I have him right here." Rollins was still too preoccupied to eavesdrop. "You want him now? I… sure, we’ll reschedule, but I… no, it’s fine. Really. He’ll want to know, I’m sure." Handing his cell phone over to the Renegade, Dr. O’Doyle looked helpless as Rollins suddenly caught the sense of urgency. "What is it?" Rollins asked. "It’s Chicago PD," Paul replied. "They want to talk to you about a grave robbery." "Who’s grave?" But Rollins already knew. O’Doyle winced. "Your mother’s." ~<>~<>~<>~ For the first time in recent memory the 52nd Precinct was an open door to Rich Rollins as he stormed through the revolving glass entryway, stalking up to the receptionist’s desk with an obvious impatience painted along his brow line like an open sore. He had been in the building before, numerous times; this occasion however marked his first journey into the depths of Chicago’s Finest where he wasn’t cuffed at the wrists. A slender brunette sporting a broach instead of a badge looked up from her spreadsheet, assessing Rollins’ evident agitation with a quick glance. Adopting an air of refined authority, the woman nodded in the Renegade’s direction. "Can we help you, sir?" She was used to handling sobbing mothers and fuming felons. She’d never met the Renegade. After two minutes of screaming over the sound of a crying baby so the woman could get the message, four heavily armed security guards were escorting Rich Rollins into a private office while the receptionist retrieved her overturned keyboard from the floor. A young detective with all the menace of a newborn Dachshund was patiently awaiting Rollins’ arrival, flipping through a manila folder thicker than his wrist as the Renegade shrugged off his unwelcome escort. Looking up from the dossier, Lt. Detective Noel Newton stood as Rollins approached his desk, extending a friendly hand and a smile while security took their leave. Rollins ignored the camaraderie. "Where are my mother’s remains?" Newton’s face remained cordial. Rollins’ tone was anything but. "We received a report this morning from Resthaven Cemetery," the detective began, motioning for the Renegade to have a seat opposite him. "Won’t you please sit down, Mr. Rollins?" "Are there pictures in that little black book of yours?" Rollins stabbed a finger at Newton’s file. "We’ve found information that-…" Newton began, but Rollins was already moving. The detective tried to swat the Renegade’s roving hands away from the folder, but Rollins was not only far larger than Noel Newton; he wasn’t in the mood to run in circles, having questions asked of him when all he wanted were a few answers. "Those are police property, damnit!" But it was too late. Forms and reports flew all over Detective Newton’s desk as Rollins manhandled the dossier out of his grasp, bringing it closer to his face. Nearly ripping off the cover page, his face froze. There, sitting front and center atop a pile of pictures exposing his mother’s exhumed and empty grave, was a GTT5 promotional poster with Rich Rollins’ face smeared all over it. His own handwriting graced the photo, signed three weeks ago just before his match with Pete the PG Tips Chimp: To Jessica, the best fan a Renegade could ever hope for. Yours truly, -Rich "The Renegade" Rollins It the was the last autograph he had ever signed. Immediately thereafter, the photo’s owner had pulled a gun on Rollins and attempted to blow a hole through his head. "I knew it. I fucking knew it, that sonofabitch-…" Detective Newton tried taking a step towards Rollins, but the Renegade shoved the autographed poster in his face before he had the chance to snatch the dossier back. "Who was this? Who tried to shoot me at that arena? Who the fuck dug my whore mother out of the ground, and where the fuck can I find him?" The Renegade’s intensity pushed Detective Newton right back into his swivel chair, teeth gnashing to keep from biting his tongue in anger. "His name is Frank Jacobs," he seethed through clenched teeth. "You put his daughter in a coma when you smashed into her SUV on the freeway two months ago. Now get the fuck off of my desk." Slamming the dossier back on the desktop, Rich Rollins pulled up a chair and took a seat. "Start talkin’, chief." Regaining his composure, Detective Newton straightened his tie as he pulled back into his work space, bristling with indignation. "We found that poster on the grave of Jessica Jacobs, which was near your mother’s plot," he began, trying to regain his poise and dignity. "It rained less than forty-eight hours ago, which means that whoever placed that picture on her resting place was likely the same man who dug up Margaret Rollins. Your own testimony from the scene of the shooting three weeks ago identified that picture, and it wasn’t hard to put two and two together." "But you’re telling me that you have no idea where my mother’s body is." It wasn’t a question, even though the blank expression on Detective Newton’s face would have been answer enough. "For all you know the guy’s a necropheliac and my mother’s remains are being penetrated as we speak, that’s what you’re saying?" The detective made a sour face. "Being difficult and accusatory isn’t going to help this case, Rich. I know this is a lot to deal with, especially with the stress of wearing that PTC Universal title and all-…" "Global title," Rollins corrected. "And my being the hottest thing since the electric stove ain’t got nothin’ to do with this." Leaning back in his creaking swivel chair, Detective Newton raised his hands in a submissive gesture. "That’s all I have for you, Rich. We wanted you to be aware that this man is capable of just about anything, and we’re doing everything in our power to help restore your mother’s remains in one piece. If Frank contacts you, please let us know." Taking that as his cue, the Renegade stood, snatching his autographed poster along the way. "You can’t take that, that’s pol-…" "Oh you’ll know if he contacts me, Noel." The Renegade placed a deliberate emphasis on the detective’s first name, just to piss him off. "I’ll be the one standin’ over my mother’s grave as I bury them both six feet under, where they belong." Moments later Detective Newton was alone once again, shaking his head as he watched the Renegade’s silhouette disappear through his office window. ~<>~<>~<>~ Meanwhile… "Before history is written down in books, it is written in courage. Like Americans before us, we will show that courage and we will finish well. We will lead freedom's advance. We will compete and excel in the global economy. We will renew the defining moral commitments of this land. And so we move forward - optimistic about our country, faithful to its cause, and confident of victories to come." As President George W. Bush concluded what was no doubt destined to become one of the most forgettable State of the Union addresses in American history, Frank Jacobs slapped himself in the face to keep from falling asleep at the wheel. A pothole tried to eat the passenger’s side tire, nearly jerking his newly-bought ‘96 Chevy Astro into the next lane. A honking horn brought him back to full attention as the driver to his immediate left flipped him the bird. "Thank you, God bless you, and may God bless America." God is dead, Frank nearly screamed at his radio as he watched the driver speed away. The gas gage began flirting around the big E, forcing Frank to veer off onto the nearest exit lane. Three minutes later he had his precious new conversion van gassed up and ready to go as he strolled into the station proper, pulling out his wallet to pay for his pump. An emaciated college-something with bad skin and a horrible overbite gazed back from behind the counter, his dull doe-like expression giving Frank pause before he spoke. "You got any air fresheners?" Frank asked. The kid moved like a slug, only somehow even less motivated as he reached underneath the counter and produced a green scented pine tree. Frank sighed. He’d have to stop by Wal-Mart before too long; the stench in his van was starting to make him nauseous. "That’ll be twenty-three fifty-seven," the stoned slug informed him. Frank paid for his gas and the tiny scented pine tree and left, eager to get home where even his dog at least displayed some semblance of personality. As he pulled back onto the highway, Frank Jacobs tossed the little pine tree into the open cargo space of the van. If Margaret Rollins’ corpse could have talked, she might have expressed her gratitude. "Stop stinking up my van, you smelly bitch," Frank mumbled to nobody in particular as he drove on into the night. ~<>~<>~<>~ "Rich? Rich are you in there? We need to talk." It was Cynthia. She sounded flustered, but Rollins was in no mood to have a serious conversation with the women who he fucked for pleasure. Had he been in love with the girl it would have been a different story, but as it stood he had more pressing matters to attend to. Her obsessive infatuation with his personifying everything her husband was not was starting to grow stale; it was time to end the fairy tale. Maybe another night or three, Rollins quickly reconsidered. After all, their secret relationship was giving him a killer rate for Dr. O’Doyle’s services. Why screw with a sweet deal? "Not now Cynthia," Rollins bellowed as she pushed into his bedroom. "I’ve got a lot on my mind right now, and I don’t need to deal with any bullshit drama, not tonight." Cynthia wasn’t about to be shoved aside, not right now. While Rollins sat at his desk mulling over the autographed picture of himself he stole from Detective Newton, Cynthia made a slow walk to the piled mattresses he called a bed and plopped down. "This isn’t bullshit drama, Rich. We really need to talk." "For the love of God, woman!" the Renegade turned, shooting her the most intimidating scowl he could conceivably manage. "I just found out my dead mother has been dug up and stolen by a man who tried to kill me. I ran over a girl and put her into a coma only to die and send her grieving father into full-fledged sociopath mode with my face in his fuckin’ sights. Meanwhile I have the biggest tournament in the history of tournaments to contend with, which means a runt Irish piker is lookin’ to make a name for himself at my expense. I’m furious. I’m fuckin’ tired. What in the hell could you possibly want?" All things considered, Rollins figured this day couldn’t possibly get any worse. Looking straight into Rich Rollins’ impenetrable eyes, Cynthia O’Doyle’s lip quivered. "Richard, I’m pregnant." And he would have been wrong. To be continued…
