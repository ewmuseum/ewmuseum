PTC — Roleplay

Fire

8 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
~<>~<>~<>~ A Prologue… The sharp aroma of sulfur stung the interior lining of Rich Rollins’ nostrils, sending his thoughts into a dizzying tailspin that absolutely refused to give him a moment’s peace. Stripped down to nothing more than an old pair of dirty blue jeans and his bare feet, he began to seriously reconsider his options as he watched the man in front of him take his first agonizing steps onto the searing surface of smoking coals. Having been culled like so many branded cattle, all Rollins and the dozen or so shoeless men behind him could do was watch and wince in sympathy for the man as he began his frantic jitter-hopping dance across the flames, the fervent look of a man possessed etched like a billboard of desperation across his face. Rollins wanted to cry out, wanted to lend some support to his fellow captive, but the man had already gone too far across. That, and the fact that a mouthful of kerosene made it difficult to say much of anything. Besides, it was a moot point anyhow. The Judges wouldn’t allow it. Standing in uniformed rows along the entire length of the narrow coal pit, hundreds of men dressed in the formal dark robes of the Judge watched on in silence, each face the same blank mask of mystery that The Renegade had by now grown so accustomed to. They observed the man’s frenzied flight across the inferno with an intensely scrutinizing interest that sent a chill down Rollins’ spine. He wasn’t the only one; kept in rigid formation, the men behind him shifted nervously as they looked on in trepidation, seeing their own bleak fate in the footfall of every fiery stride. There had been more in the beginning. The handful of men who remained were slowly being weeded out, a harsh reality that had not been lost on Rollins. One by one the athletes fell - that was their one similarity, they all were professionals of some sport or another - and one by one the talent pool became all the more shallow. There had been a bright young man from the University of Chicago who’s future in the NBA had been all but assured, but after three rounds of bare-knuckle boxing with a Samoan quarterback from Tampa Bay, he no longer had much of a future. The only dribbling the kid could do now was through a straw. Rollins thought he might have cracked a rib during one of his “trials,” as the Judges preferred calling them, when he and a burley Russian soccer player were forced into a frenetic game of dodgeball, using thirty-pound ball bearings instead of the usual padded variety. The Russian proved that his strength wasn’t confined to just his legs, however. The straight shot to the side had knocked the wind out of The Renegade, but he’d be damned if he was going to let the injury show. He knew better. The last athlete to give in to the pain had quickly been dealt with, and the shocking visual lesson had taught Rollins the value of keeping his grievances to himself. The man’s final excruciating moments as he roasted on a bonfire of those lost before him still rang in Rollins’ ears. The man’s journey across the coals was nearly complete when he finally surrendered to the blisters on his protesting feet with a shrill moan. The Renegade recoiled slightly as a Judge along the side produced a long black whip and cracked it harshly, cutting the flesh of the man’s bare backside. He cried out, his concentration completely broken, and fell face-first into the final five feet of smoldering fire. An involuntary twitch ran up and down Rollins’ legs as two Judges removed the man’s convulsing form from the strip. He whet his lips, trying to clear his mind as the infuriatingly calm Judge to his right gave him the signal, and it was time. Taking a deep breath, Rich Rollins took his first step onto the blazing embers. ~<>~<>~<>~ Act I: Introducing the Weapon He had been taken in the night while he slept. “You can sleep when you’re dead,” were the last words Rich Rollins had heard before hanging up the phone, his boss Steven Caldera obviously not buying his excuses for missing GCW’s most recent house show. “I’d better see you in New York next week, otherwise your Infinite Gauntlet stint becomes a run as a free agent.” As it would turn out, it was the last conversation he’d hold in the outside world before being whisked away like an immigrant without a green card. How ironic. Rollins remembered very little of the kidnapping itself. One minute he was dozing off in the comforts of his Boston hotel room, and the next thing he knew he was bouncing around the back of an empty moving truck with a musty burlap sack tied snug around his neck. His head had hurt, he remembered that quite clearly; the lingering grogginess of sleep refused to wear off, a tell-tale sign that he had been drugged. His thoughts had been sluggish, and his body felt a familiar kind of numbness that screamed of medical-issue sedatives. There were people in the back of the truck with him, of that he was sure. Despite the thick layers of duct tape that restrained his arms behind his back, Rollins was able to feel the presence of other like-minded captives, but nobody said anything. Everyone had been either too drugged out or plain terrified to form anything of coherence. The journey had been tedious underneath the constrictive shroud of the burlap sack, but eventually they jerked to a halt and the rear door clanked open, admitting an unknown number of their assailants as they were manhandled like the bagged vegetables they had unwittingly become. A muted query came from a bound sack somewhere to Rollins’ left, and it was answered with the none-too-gentle thud of a fist. Rollins cringed. “You’ll speak when spoken to,” was the only response given. They were somewhere in the midst of the Massachusetts countryside, of that Rollins was certain. The smell of grass and manure was strong enough to choke the dead, even filtered through the rough cloth covering his face. With no other choice than to be carried along, The Renegade accepted the situation and dealt with it, trying to keep calm. As the sedatives began to wear off, he had to fight harder to keep his racing heartbeat in check. The unwelcome expedition continued down into what Rollins could only guess was a storage cellar, but the stairs seemed to go much deeper than the average below-ground shelter. The figure who had Rollins over his shoulder never broke stride except to heft him more comfortably, and The Renegade could feel the power in his arms. Even had he wanted to, Rollins was unsure of how he could have possibly defended himself against an enemy that strong. All he would have managed to do is break his own neck tumbling down the stairs had he attempted anything in retaliation, and the prospect of being left down here to die didn’t sit well in his stomach. Instead, he listened. And waited. ~<>~<>~<>~ When the sack was finally ripped off of The Renegade’s head, the cool rush of fresh air would have knocked him over had he not already been on his knees. Trying to adjust his eyes to the sudden soft light source, Rollins blinked repeatedly, forcing himself to bring everything into focus. He was inside what looked to be the remains of an old military hanger, or some similar cavernous structure that someone had wanted to bury deep under the earth. The walls were made out of some sort of granite rock, intricately molded in enormous snaking columns that contained some sort of writing on them, but at his distance Rollins couldn’t be sure of exactly what. Seated all around him were men just like himself, wide-eyed and confused, a horde of unasked questions begging to be answered on every frightened face. The chamber danced in an eerie firelight that emanated from a large bronze brazier that sat in the very center, flanked on either side by rising rows of candles that didn’t so much as flicker despite the drafty breeze that was somehow pumped into the place. Raised on an ornate platform overlooking the bonfire, a podium presided atop the entire scene, the crowning touch to Rollins’ living nightmare. But it wasn’t any of these things that held The Renegade’s attention. Standing in ordered rows around the brazier leading up a small staircase to the platform itself were the robed and masked figures of the Judges, curly white wigs and faceless masks adorning every last head. Rollins quickly swallowed his sudden anger as the memory of his encounter with a similarly-dressed man only weeks ago back in Chicago invaded his thoughts, threatening to conjure up an ill-fated encounter that was most assuredly not in his best interests, all things considered. It was the stuff Stanley Kubric films were made of, only without the benefit of a nude Nicole Kidman. When the initial shock of their situation finally began to wear off, a single Judge who lorded over the podium like a truly malevolent dictator raised his voice to the captives, somehow amplified in the cavernous chamber. “You have been Chosen,” he intoned with an edge of finality that rang throughout the assemblage, “Each one of you is a specimen of physical dominance and human achievement, masters of your chosen domain. But this is not your domain… this is the House of Judgment.” Rollins exchanged a few bewildered looks with those kneeling nearest to him as the Judge went on. “You all share a bond of competitive brotherhood, a bond that will either strengthen your resolve or break your bodies in the coming Trials.” Behind the mask, The Renegade was sure the lunatic was grinning. “But you also share another trait, one that is not so becoming of men with ambitions such as yours. Each and every one of you has been found Guilty of purely selfish endeavors, growing fat and spoiled in the cushy lofts you have built around yourselves, carved out of society’s pockets with abundant talents that you’ve used to rape this nation of its will to achieve. Its desire to raise standards instead of openly accepting mediocrity. For this, you are to be Judged.” “Is this guy for real?” the man next to Rollins whispered, his tone incredulous. “This is bullshit.” Before Rollins could come back with a response, the sharp snap of a whip sliced through the air, tearing into the disgruntled man’s cheek and thus ending any sort of retort. Startled, all the captives shied away from the abrupt outburst, refusing to stare directly at the black-robed Judge who had produced the scathing lash. A clearly defined red gash remained on the man’s face, the first of many reminders that there were no individual rights in this hellishly intense incarnation of a concentration camp, if it could be called anything at all. Moving on as if nothing at all had occurred, the Judge at the podium continued. “Look around you. Know the faces of those you will learn to see as brothers, and accept the reality that there can be no brotherhood through intentions born of greed. Know the faces of your brothers, and learn to recognize him as an enemy.” But The Renegade already knew the face of his enemy. He wore no face at all and had billowing white curls of hair that he would have sold his eternal soul for a chance to get his hands around. “Those of you who prove yourselves worthy of Judgment will be rewarded with your freedom,” the Judge finished, his prophetic rhetoric finally coming to an end. “If you do not, you will be similarly rewarded… with your extermination. The Law has no middle ground.” Slamming one black-gloved fist into the podium, the Judge nodded once. “Court is adjourned.” And in unison, the cult repeated: “Court is adjourned.” As Rollins and the rest of the captives began to file off into the interconnected series of corridors and workrooms, his mind was already racing with a plot to give these psychotic bastards exactly what they had asked for. A little taste of judgment, shoved right down their own righteous throats until they choked. It was then that he finally read the scrawled messages on the stone pillars. They were cries for help. ~<>~<>~<>~ Act II: Loading the Weapon “Does anyone else feel like Arnold Schwartzenegger in The Running Man, or is it just me?” A few scattered chuckles met Dave Olson’s lighthearted attempt at a joke, but even then it sounded flat. A young cyclist who had until yesterday been training for the next Olympic summer games responded in kind. “You only wish you were Schwartzenegger,“ he quipped, the barest hint of a southern accent riding his tone. “I could probably see you in Junior, at best.” The much larger Dave Olson, a promising NCAA wrestling hopeful, shot the smaller cyclist a sour glare. And that’s how they passed the time, trying to keep themselves occupied with mindless small talk so that nobody would have to think about the stark reality that stared each one of them in the face. Confined to an uncomfortably cramped chamber that offered little more than leaking water pipes and a few wall-mounted kerosene lamps, the captives littered the cold stone slab of a floor like broken toys strewn throughout a child’s playpen. Some of them spoke to those nearest them out of nervous habit; others simply contented themselves with staring at the uneven surfaces of their makeshift prison, preferring to keep their own company. Rich Rollins was one of the later. “I know you,” Dave Olson said, tossing The Renegade a smug look from his position next to the cyclist. “You’re one of those professional wrestlers, aren’t you?” The condescension was blatant, and the scowl that sat itself plainly across Rollins’ face let his addresser know in no uncertain terms that he was about to bark up the wrong tree. Olson merely nodded. “Yeah, you’re The Renegade. That guy from… what’s it called? Primetime Championship Wrestling, right?” Rollins tried to bite his tongue, but his tongue moved faster than his teeth. “PCW is dead, asshole. And I have a feeling that there are good many more zeros at the end of my salary than anything the NCAA would ever pony up for an amateur.” That hit the button. Adopting an air of flimsy indifference, Olson shrugged. “Not everyone can be legit. ‘Least when I put someone down I know it’s because I was better, not more over than the other guy.” “For someone with such an obvious distaste for professional wrestling, you sure got the lingo down pat,” The Renegade spat back, his irritation boiling well beyond the tolerance level. “Keep this shit up and we can find out who the real professional is.” “Seriously guys,” a new voice chimed in, drawing the attention of every set of eyes in the room, “if we’re done trying to discover who has the bigger dick, can we move on to something a bit more productive?” The newcomer was a stocky man in his early thirties, a front line defender if Rollins had ever seen one. Exhaling in annoyance, The Renegade looked the man dead in the eye. “And what would you suggest?” His answer was simple. “That we get the fuck out of here.” A chorus of mixed emotions filled the dank chamber, but the man cut through it with a purpose. “Look,” he continued, “I’ve got kids, folks. I was taken from my home while my wife slept not four inches away, and the thought of these twisted fucks rummaging around my house with my family unprotected doesn’t exactly put me in the mood to listen to all this senseless bickering.” “Amen to that,” agreed the Russian, who not four hours later would unintentionally crush one of Rollins’ ribs. “So what the hell are we supposed to do, let them whip the hell out of us until they tire out and let us go?” Olson responded in a tone that was more hostile than he had intended. “Unless you have some deep damn pockets full of guns and ammo, I don’t-…” His heated comeback was cut short as the single steel-framed door in the room swung wide open, admitting the faceless shapes of three costumed Judges. All banter died instantly; the mere presence of the Judges had a startling effect on their sense of self-preservation, killing off any unwanted heroics with one expressionless glance. They held no obvious weapons, but it didn’t take much of an imagination to guess that something unpleasant was likely tucked into any one of their ceremonial death robes. His voice commanding obedience, the lead Judge spoke. “Steven Hendrickson and David Olson,” the blank mask crooned, “You will come with us. Your Trial awaits.” Like bird dogs catching the scent of their quail, both escorting Judges peeled off into the chamber and picked out the men in question without difficulty, their intentions very clear. Hendrickson, a man who as of yesterday was getting ready to move overseas to play rugby in a privately-funded European league, offered no resistance as he was led out the door and into the open arms of whatever fate had in store. Every pair of eyes in the room moved in unison as both he and Olson accepted the impromptu escort, and when the steel door slammed again the general atmosphere had dropped to near-catatonic levels of depression. Curled up in a far corner, someone began to weep. There were no warnings. Every so often the Judges would come and list off names, and those men were taken away to Trial. Sometimes there were only two chosen, and sometimes it was three or four. The intervals were so inconsistent that everyone began to keep to themselves, dreading that at any moment their name would be called and off they’d go. Many men were taken. Only a handful returned. Even then, those who stumbled back in began to display the scars of their “judgment” in the form of blemishes and burns that marred more than just their arms and legs. You could see it in their eyes; even when they won, they lost. Time did not exist as a rational and consistent entity any longer. The only way to gauge its passage was by counting the random visits and the resulting decrease in able-bodied captives. The intervals in between the sporadic collections stretched on like individual lifetimes, as it usually signaled the final fleeting hours of someone’s existence. Rollins began to envy those who were chosen. The ones who never came back. They were the lucky ones. Even as he heard his own name get called and watched the muscular frame of his Russian counterpart drag himself to his feet, he was almost resentful of those who had managed to escape this surreal nightmare in their own way. Almost. ~<>~<>~<>~ The juice of the plump tomato ran down The Renegade’s chin in rivulets of seedy pulp, quenching both thirst and hunger in one glorious gulp. He tore into the delicious vegetable - he still considered them vegetables, no matter what anyone said - with a possessive vigor that under any other circumstances would have caused those around him to stare. Fortunately for him, everyone around him was doing the exact same thing. A large trough of cold spring water and a basket of freshly picked fruit and vegetables had been delivered during the most recent “culling,” as the captives began calling the visits. Apparently their malevolent captors kept a well-tended garden somewhere aboveground, to which The Renegade was most thankful. While they intended to test the mettle of each and every man holed up in this dank little detention center, the Judges weren’t about to let anyone starve to death. The Trials seemed to do a much better job of population control, anyway. As he licked the remainder of the tomato’s mashed insides off of his fingers, Rich Rollins began to wonder just how long he had been down here. A day, perhaps? Maybe two? It was impossible to tell. The steady throbbing in his side, the result of his Trial competition against the burley Russian, kept a steady reminder that the longer this madness went on the more likely the possibility that the only way Rollins was making it out of here was in a box. The thought put a decidedly irate crease on his forehead. Having been the only one to return from his Trial, Dave Olson spit out the core of an apple and wiped his mouth with the back of one hand. His other arm remained cradled tenderly against his side, an injury that he had not been very interested in divulging the details of. He looked at his fellow captives for a moment, a thoughtful contemplation resting behind the exhaustion in his eyes that danced in the soft kerosene candlelight. They had started with just a shade over thirty men, but now the surviving landscape had been whittled down to no more than a dozen. “I’m not going to wait around for my number to get punched,” Olson finally declared to no one in particular. Turning their attention away from the messy remains of their meals, everyone regarded him with something akin to sympathy. Rollins preferred to call it pity. “There has to be something we can do,” agreed Rollins’ Russian friend. “I’d rather die trying to fight my way back to civilization than to be hounded into the grave by some freaks with a fetish for cheesy Halloween costumes,” cried Olson, slowly rising to his feet. “This is insanity. We’re fucking athletes for Christ’s sake! If we worked together, there’s no reason why we couldn’t put up a decent fight.” “Maybe that’s what they’ve wanted all along,” said Jason Hedley, the only man of their number who wasn’t in and of himself an athletic competitor. He had been a referee for the NFL for seven years, but was fired under allegations of false officiating due to suspected bribery. Regardless, his words put a thought-provoking wrench in the cogs of the captives’ proposed game plan. “Think about it,” he continued, trying to push aside his innately timid nature in favor of being heard, “What have the Judges been doing since we arrived?” He paused. “They’ve been testing our will to survive. They’re trying to breed out the weakest links in order to uncover the deadliest kind of athlete… a ruthless, unstoppable monster.” The words sank home, sending a ripple of determined conviction throughout the chamber. “Damn straight,” Rollins finally spoke, breaking the heavy silence. “I think it’s time we showed them what ruthlessness really is.” As The Renegade stood and walked over to the opposite wall, Dave Olson grinned. “And what does the pro wrestler have in mind, exactly?” he asked, a noticeable respect having washed away his previous animosity. Picking up one of the round kerosene candles from its small cubby, The Renegade shook the small glass ball holding the precious flammable liquid. “I hope you guys can breathe through your nose,” he answered. ~<>~<>~<>~ Act III: Firing the Weapon The searing heat of the smoldering coals hit Rollins’ bare feet without mercy, instantly sending him into a panic. He had done his best to mentally steel himself for those first few excruciating footfalls, but no amount of confident inner dialogue could have possibly prepare him for the sweltering shock of the real thing. He knew it was simply a case of mind over matter; fakirs and gypsies had been walking across coals for thousands of years, their feet coming away no more blistered than a day of leisurely sun tanning. Some people could simply ignore the pain and tune out the discomfort, building barriers in their mind that somehow protected their soles from any real harm. Unfortunately, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins was not one of those lucky few. With a payload of acrid kerosene tucked inside his cheeks, The Renegade buried his toes into the smoking embers one step after another, keeping his eyes focused on the end of the strip. His mouth watered in protest, dripping alcohol-soaked saliva down the back of his throat as if to mock his every agonizing stride. All the while the Judges watched on, any one of them ready to whip Rollins into fiery oblivion at the slightest hint of hesitation. It wasn’t until he was within jumping distance to his goal that The Renegade gave them the opportunity. He immediately stopped dead in his tracks, planting his screaming soles into the hot ashes despite his better judgment. The Judges themselves began shooting one another surreptitious glances, obviously not expecting such an action. While Rollins stood in place, the bottoms of his feet smoldering quietly in the intensity of the inferno, the Judge nearest to him finally spoke up. “Commendable, Richard Rollins. Now finish your Trial.” In response, The Renegade spat his flammable cargo of kerosene into the Judge’s expressionless face. And just like, the mutiny began. Rollins had anticipated the first belting lash of the whips, and turned around just in time to catch one around his forearm. Ignoring the sharp sting as the leather cord wrapped itself around his waiting appendage, The Renegade responded with a violent jerk that yanked the offending Judge off of his feet, sending him head first into the waiting coals. The black robes caught aflame instantaneously, and the resulting shriek was hardly muffled by the emotionless fabric of the mask. Burrowing his right foot even deeper into the embers, Rollins kicked outward, sending coal and snowy ash alike in a wide arc straight into the Judge’s ranks. The heated rocks caught the kerosene-spattered face of the Judge in front of him, turning his mask into an uncontrollable conflagration that spread across his shoulders and onto the costumed cultists around him. Finally submitting to the desires of his raging soles, The Renegade barreled off of the strip and into the rows of screaming Judges. Back at the waiting line, the remaining captives had likewise released their mouthfuls of bitter fluid, knocking over any costumed figure who was too slow to react, their fists fueled with righteous rage. Whips began to crack, but most of the captives had managed to beat a leather lash of their own from some of the downed Judges. Without any semblance of mercy in their step, the survivors began responding with a frightening bloodlust that completely took the Judges off guard. “Hit the brazier!” Rollins cried, bowling through a panicked ocean of black cloth. The screams of those who were fighting for survival against the angry onslaught of fire filled the cavernous space like Death’s own orchestra, nearly drowning out everything else. But The Renegade didn‘t care. “Hit the goddamned bonfire!” A savage swipe whistled in the air next to Dave Olson’s ear, nearly succeeding in removing the left half of his face, but a well-placed crack of his own whip sent the offending Judge backwards into the roaring furnace of the coals. Turning at the sound of Rollins’ voice, he whistled sharply at his fellow athletes. “Take out the base, boys!” he bellowed triumphantly, hitting a dead sprint toward the roaring bonfire on the other side of the cavern. Those who heard him quickly followed suit. Those who didn’t began to feel the omnipotent fury of the Judges, their retaliation not the least bit tender. The burley Russian feel to his knees, suffering one last crack to the face before falling over into the advancing wave of black-robed cultists. Jason Hedley - the referee who swore he never took a bribe - loosed a final defiant cry of freedom before his face was forcibly buried into the black coals. The young cyclist who found it easier to deal with his fear through witty invective enjoyed his final moments of insubordination before he was finally silenced by the onrushing horde of his enemy. The man who merely wanted to see his family one last time, caught in the crossfire and hurled unceremoniously into the flames. The casualties quickly began piling up, but the remaining captives were too focused to be denied. Standing shoulder to shoulder with his NCAA rival, Rich Rollins cracked his whip one last time, snagging the cord around one of the three crisscrossed supports that held the blazing bonfire aloft. Two Judges who remained atop the ceremonial platform above nearly knocked one another off the side in their frantic attempt to reach safety, but it was too late; hooking his own whip around the same support, Dave Olson shot The Renegade an affirmative nod. And they pulled. Both fleeing Judges lost their balance as the platform teetered dangerously, sending them off the side to smack with the cold stone base below. The snagged support wrenched free of its mooring, knocking the remaining two legs off-kilter. Like a movie playing itself out in slow motion, the bronze brazier slipped free of its rig at an awkward angle, spilling flaming bits of log that sparked angrily as they hit the floor. With a final resounding clang the brazier crashed into the ground, landing squarely on a small fuel supply tank that was attached underneath. “Implausible!” screamed one of the Judges. The ensuing explosion rocked the entire cavern, reverberating so powerfully that many of the old stone pillars used to support the arced ceiling overhead buckled under the intense pressure. The desperate pleas for help that had been written on those pillars had finally been answered. The House of Judgment had fallen. ~<>~<>~<>~ Throwing open the large steel door that provided the final barrier between darkness and daylight, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins and Dave Olson coughed the soot from their lungs as they stumbled onto the surface. A cool evening breeze greeted their ashen faces, bringing with it an immense relief that brought both men to their knees. They had survived. “I take back what I said,” wheezed The Renegade, trying to collect his senses. “And what was that?” Olson replied as he leaned against the shanty stone structure that served as the above-ground entrance into their own personal hell. “The NCAA,” Rollins replied with a smirk, “I think you deserve a raise.” They laughed. And oh, what a sweet sound it was. ~Fin~ ... ~<>~<>~<>~ A Completely Unrelated Epilogue… Standing on the sidewalk outside of the much-famed central offices of PTC, Rich Rollins’ upper lip twitched involuntarily as he stared at the empty structure attached to the outside of the building like a theater box office. Where it had once been housed indoors, the powers-that-be must have moved it outside for reasons that Rollins really didn’t care for. Whether or not it garnered more business to be right under people’s noses, The Renegade was sure that he hated the damn thing. For looking right back at him, seemingly mocking his very existence, was the PTC kiosk. “What’s the matter?” Dave Olson asked as he noticed the strange look on The Renegade’ face. Rollins quivered. “It reminds me of somebody,” he replied. “Who?” His expression dire, The Renegade looked his fellow wrestler in the eye. “A guy who likes to dress up like a judge,” he answered. Looking back toward the little kiosk, Dave Olson frowned as he cocked his head ever so slightly. “Burn it,” he decided. And so they did. And it was good. ~Fin Finale~
