Distracted: Birdwatching

20 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-20 Could he have even had a hand in something that beautiful? That was the question that swirled around inside Train’s head. The very notion impressed him as nearly impossible, the doubt sucking it down and consuming it like a whirlpool of polluted water. Train sat in the bucket leather seat of the rented Lincoln Navigator, his head resting heavily against the head rest, dreadlocks crushed between the back of his head and the leather as the thoughts on his brain attempted to weigh down his entire body. The day shined outside the truck. It shined even brighter in the eyes of the children as they charged around the playground that stood just a short ways off from the passenger side of the truck. They were running and jumping with the reckless abandon of true youth, though their abandon was countered by the chain link fence that stood just a few inches in from the sidewalk. It was obviously a security/safety feature installed by someone who worked at the beige and sizeable, one story school that stood in the background behind the flurry of activity. The fence stood as a blockade to keep them in and everyone else out even as the men and women with whistles swinging from their necks stood watchfully, evenly distributed through the crowd of children like sentries, on guard for any problem. It must be nice. Train sat transfixed, watching them, wondering when he’d grown of that stage of simplicity where the world is black and white and you’re protected from what lies in wait for you to stumble into the dark. Train sat wondering how long it had been since he’d forgotten there was that kind of innocense in the world. There were so many of them. Little minds and hearts unaware that evil really existed. Train snorted as the questions pursued him. Sitting here it was impossible to defend himself. The innocense of the children shattered his self image and laid him bare and plain for himself to see. He existed in the darkness. He was akin with the evil that existed there. He’d done evil things. That realization only added to the questions that clawed at him. If you do evil... does that make you evil? Fuck. God damn it. Now just a few days ago he’d been fine with all this. Just a few days ago the mask he hid his nature behind had been right where it was supposed to be and there were none of these bullshit existential inquiries to plague him. “So....how the fuck did I get here?” Train asked himself silently. Of course, he didn’t know why he bothered asking himself that. It’s not like he didn’t know the answer. It wasn’t the kids...it was the kid. All the way over here she’d sat in a little frame attached to the dash board with a cheap looking suction cup he’d picked up at the airport. It was the second time he’d been in New York in as many weeks all because he needed to see more than the picture. It had been a few days since, what seemed like a chance encounter with an old flame began look a little more like fate. The picture of the little girl almost literally fallen into Train’s hands. Since that night time seemed not to really pass at many points, the whimsical gaze from the little girl’s light hazel eyes almost stopping it cold. Even with a few missing teeth here smile beamed like a lighthouse, calling him towards the light. Her light olive complexion spoke of a background that was likely a mix of several different heritages. PC society would say she was “bi-racial”, but when she grew up the boys would just call her exotic. Train suddenly realized he’d have to keep up his wrestling skills for a long while as it became obvious, if she was his, a few teenage boys would likely have an ass kicking coming there way. He just shook his head and grinned a little. Brianne... she was a beautiful little girl. She looked a lot like her mother. She warmed his soul. She chilled him to the bone. She bounded around the school yard with her little friends, that same beacon like smile probably making the normally mature sun a little jealous. Train didn’t think kids still played ring around the rosey, but Brianne was determined to bring it back, leading the trend with her boundless 5-year old energy. It was fun to watch. He could have been here to do so long before this. The P.I. he’d hired came highly recommended from his lawyer. It only took him a few hours to find Gabby’s address and find out what school her little girl went too. The fear had paralyzed him for a while. And it was the fear that, unfortunately, brought him right back to the original question... Could she really be his? And that question spawned a million more... If she really is his daughter...should he be pissed at her mother, Gabrielle, for not telling him? Or should he really just be relieved? Hell, it ain’t like he was mister responsible back in those days. wIw was a small promotion, but it was popular and provided a good brush with true superstardom. Money... Weed... Liquor... Pussy.... It was all readily available. Just part of the circle of life back then. Thinking back on it he could half see it from her prospective. What would have been the point of telling him? Guilt drizzled down on him like rain from the sky, but the fact was, not knowing? It was a relief. At least not knowing he didn’t have any ugly memories to look back on. No ugly conversations that cut into her with questions of whether the baby was really his...or real at all. No chance that an offer of money to ensure the death of the little girl he watched now was accepted. And God forbid he’d had a little too much to drink and smoke one night and gone Ray Caruth on the situation. It was perhaps that thought that scared Train the most. It’s not as if he hadn’t cared about Gabrielle. In truth women had passed through his sheets like a turnstyle in the subway in those days, but none of them meant anything. Except Gabrielle. Gabrielle meant..... something. Yeah...something.... But it’s not like the smoke and the drink hadn’t lead to the pain and suffering of others before. The fact that he cared? It didn’t change a thing. Train had spent a lot of years insinuating it to people, but was never stupid enough to state in plain in public... He watched men die... women too... And he watched because he’d ended their lives... sometimes by executive order... sometimes personally. Some cats had always tried to make the street game seem so normal and even noble, but Train was never one to bullshit anyone... including himself. He wasn’t some knight in shining armor for his community. He fed on it’s pain and despair, turning it into cash that he gladly stuck in his pocket. And those that tried to muscle in or oppose him? There were a lot of things laying on the floor of Lake Michigan. Whole or in pieces... Train and put more than his fair share on the bottom. As a young man Train never questioned whether he’d be able to carry the load of what he’d done to people on his shoulders with no problem. Too young and stupid to know no one walks away untouched. He’d accomplished a helluva lot and had a helluva lot for some punk kid from the south side of Chicago. But that didn’t stop the cold sweats in the middle of the night. The bloody faces of his victims staring at him from the darkness in his dreams, they’re eyes glowing the same red as the blood that covered them, their gaze cutting into him like a knife. And they all said the same thing as the growled from the darkness...ominous words that, somehow, in his heart, he knew were true... “We’ll see you again....” they said. “We’re waiting for you.” Train was no coward...but they scared the shit out of him. That’s when he knew he needed out. But there wasn’t really an out. At least not for most. But he was Train...he was the fucking boss...he did what the fuck he wanted. Scared? Yes Suddenly some kind of bitch? Fuck no. That’s when the God he was pretty sure existed hit with one of his few moments of mercy. That’s when wIw came calling. Train had made a short run in Asia back when he was about 21 at the behest of the man who had trained him for the ring. It had all gone pretty well until Train’s past came looking for him back home. He wanted out, but Pacino stated it plane... The game always pulls you back in. But, someway or another, the powers that be had gotten hold of the tapes from that tour and came calling for him. That was that. Sure there were objections from cats who though somehow he’d suddenly lost his mind and was turning snitch and/or bitch, but he had enough friends to watch his back, taking over in his stead. Train got the freedom he needed to make a new life. But, deep down, despite what he hoped for on the surface... A new job don’t make you a new person. Train knew he’d always be the man he was. But in the wrestling game at least violence was rewarded with something other than a warrant. Train made sure he was highly decorated. Popularity, fame, money... it all came by using his natural ability to hurt people. But just because it was legitimate didn’t make it a true change. He was the same man, it was just a different game. Now... here was this little girl. Could she seriously come from him? Can darkness turn into light? While it probably should have played a major role, biology didn’t really occupy any space in the equation Train’s psyche was attempting to solve. But then the glimmer of an answer. She was so sweet... so innocent... right up until that little punk messed with her. Now at this age little boys are curious. When the little snot snuck up behind Brianne and flipped up the back of her little school skirt he didn’t mean any real harm. But Brianne looked like she did when she responded. Everywhere Train went the announcers loved to point out how deadly his right hand could be. It wasn’t any kind of exact match, but she had a hell of a left cross. It’s all fun and games until you get socked in the eye. “OHHHHH!!!!” Train yelled, swinging up straight in his seat, shooting his fists into the air as if she’d caught a Hail Mary in the last second of the 4th quarter to score the game winning touch down. Now if he was going to be somebody’s parent that response was highly immature and inappropriate. He’d have to grow and mature...to realize his responsibilities when it came to teaching a child the proper decorum. But for now he didn’t give half a shit. Aside from Bernie Mac in “The Kings of Comedy” that was the funniest thing he’d ever seen. A gale of laughter bubbled up from the inside and exploded outward with the force of a WMD. Chip off the old block. Now.... was that good or bad? Before he could contemplate the question to any real depth his concentration was broken. The cell begins to buzz and lightly flop against the leather on the passenger seat, making just enough noise to snap his real focus. Train leaned over and wrapped his large hand around the small piece of chrome like plastic as it rumbled like an earthquake, shaking him awake from what had been like a fairly pleasant dream. Righting himself in the driver’s seat he flicked his thumb and the phone unfolded in his hands. Train didn’t know why he’d bothered to look at the caller ID, it’s not like he hadn’t known who was calling. There were more than a few things on Train’s plate as of late and there were too many people trying to feed themselves off the scraps he’d leave behind to expect any peace just because a wallet sized picture had turned his entire world upside down in a heartbeat. This was a distraction. Train knew that. Train didn’t care, but he knew others would One was calling to remind him. “Talk to me.” Train said, pressing the phone to his ear, fully expecting the conniption that was in store. “Talk to you?” inquired the somewhat high pitched voice of an older man. The tone was light and on even keel. Something Train already knew wouldn’t last long. “Actually, I think I’d much rather ask you a question there my friend... Namely.... Where the fuck are you?!” Train had already pulled the phone away from his ear, fully expecting the calm demeanor his agent Jerry had started the conversation with was just the flood gate and would likely burst soon. Jerry was.....excitable. It didn’t take much to set him off on a tangent. It was his most annoying trait. Train would likely have fired or beat his ass a long time ago if he hadn’t come to the understanding that it was also one of his best traits as well. Train hadn’t sat in on any of the negotiations for the endorsements or wrestling contracts Jerry had landed for him, but he figured the cats sitting on the other side of the boardroom tables had gone ahead and payed out the big money just get him to shut the hell up. Train just shook his head, grimaced and waited for the first moment he could get a word in edgewise. “Are you kidding me with this Train? Huh? Are you kidding.” Jerry continued, the yelling tailing off, but his tone of exasperation still front and center. “Kidding you about what Jerry?” asked Train half-heartedly, putting the phone back to his face to ensure he’s heard, but still focusing his attention to the little girl on the playground. Train knew full well what Jerry was talking about, but figured it would do the man’s heart good to vent a little as he had no intentions of offering any explanations or apologies to ease his anxieties. Train had taken this sojourn to New York unbeknownst to anyone, not needing the headaches of anyone attempting to talk him out of it. Irresponsible, but necessary to keep himself from being dragged down by a pack of rabid questions that nipped at his heels, their ravenous designs on consuming him growing stronger the harder he tried to run from them. “About... about what?” Jerry begins, still agitated to the utmost. “About what he asks me..... Ok, ok, ok... About what?! Ok, I’ll tell you what Tony. How about all the work I’ve been doing this week and you just making like a ghost and disappearing?! How about that, huh?! How about that?” “I had some shit I had to take care of Jerry.” Train states plainly, but now only 1/4 paying attention now, as apposed to the half heated attention he was giving the conversation earlier. Apparently one of the sentries had spotted the little girl’s attempt to defend her honor. Or maybe it was the boy laying on the ground and hold his eye like a little girl that caught their attention... either way it was obvious she was the one catching the flack for this. The old woman in the flowered dress stood hunched over, hovering above Brianne. Her wrinkled finger was waving on overdrive, the words coming out of her mouth likely some kind of report to the little girl on just how wrong she was to smack the little punk like he owed her money. Dumb bitch. What kinda bullshit is that?! “What the hell you yellin’ at her for?!” Train rumbled, for a moment completely oblivious to the fact someone on the other end of the phone was trying to have a conversation. “Yell at that little pervert running around flippin’ up dresses!” “What?!” Jerry retorted to the seemingly random comment. “Huh?!” Train responded, the yelling into his ear bring him back into his own present. “Jerry, stop yelling in my damn ear man! And nothing.... I wasn’t talkin’ to you, don’t worry about it.” “You’re supposed to be in Chicago just like we talked about... working out... but instead... you’re yelling about flipping dress... And I’m not supposed to worry? Mmhmmm... Are you drunk? Huh? You been smoking that ganja again? Is that it?” “Check yo’self Jerry.” Train states plainly, turning his full attention back to the conversation that has quickly taken a turn he doesn’t appreciate. He states it in a cold tone that even Jerry can’t bull over, stabbing through the line with the frosty violence of an icicle. The verbal wound quickly reminds Jerry, who is by no means ignorant with his client’s past, just who he’s talking to. He’s stepped a bit too boldly...best to take a step back. “Don’t get shit twisted Jerry. You’re my agent, not God damn momma. We straight?” “Yeah....” Jerry stammers nervously, reigning himself in. “Yeah, we’re straight. But...but... C’mon Tony, you’re killing me!” he says, unable to control it. Train, understanding the man’s nature, just pulls the phone away a bit and shakes his head, tolerating the outburst like that of the drunk uncle no one wants to talk about at Thanksgiving dinner. Just the way it goes. “Do you know how much crap I went through this week to set this up for you? I mean I rented out the gym for the entire week. I lined up enough sparring partners to last you to the middle of next month! But more than all that Train I got you the biggest leg up of anybody in the next round of this tournament. I even got on the phone and tracked down some of this Deville guy’s old trainers! Hahaha... The guys that taught him everything he knows?! I flew ‘em in direct from Ontario! You believe that?” “No,” Train replied, starting to throw his attention back to the situation on the play ground and the fall out from The Smackdown in the Sandbox. “Naw, I don’t believe that shit Jerry. Why the fuck would this dude’s boys from back in the day be trying to help me today? Why would I trust them?” “Trust me Tony,” Jerry says obviously fully prepared to face his doubts. “There’s no love lost between these guys. From the stories they told be this bastard put them through hell. They wanna see the son of a bitch go down damn near as much as we do.” “Uhuh.....” Train softly replies, hearing and considering ever word, but still drawn to the plight of a young lady, pushed too far. “This guy is no joke Tony,” Jerry says, unknowingly vying for his attention. “I mean nobody who’s gotten this far in the GTT4 is, but especially not this guy. From what I hear the guy is a true to life psycho. Hell, I even heard the son of a bitch even worships the devil man.” “Worships the Devil?” “That’s what I heard man. You’re gonna need every bit of help you can get in this. Hell, the bastard even has Satan on his side.” “He worships him, huh? Haha... Well, I done sent muthafuckas to his doorstep. I’d say I have the edge there.” “I’m telling you, you need to be prepared for this guy Tony. I been checkin’ out the numbers in Vegas? He’s the odds on favorite to take this thing. And Tony...look... don’t go getting pissed at me, cuz I just got to say this. I can not... emphasize to you enough...just how much we can not afford to let this thing slip through our fingers.” “What makes you think you’d have to say that Jerry?” “Hell, maybe I don’t, but I’m saying it anyway. Do you know who I just got off the phone with? Of course you don’t so let me tell you who I just got of the phone with. You know Timberland, right? The boot company? Of course you do, you wearing them damn near every time you step into my office. Well, you know what they told me? Then told me that they’re looking for a new spokesman and they’re looking at you awfully damn hard. The know the whole rap, hip hop culture thing is huge and it’s half their business. They want somebody rough and tumble with an edge and they think that’s you Tony. This is huge Tony. They want to make you the only wrestler in history with his own line of footwear. Boots, sneakers, the works! And the best part?! You wanna know the best part? Of course you wanna know the best part... 2.5.... Million over the next 2 years!!! This is huge man! They figure your street cred and name will get even bigger if you knock out the favorite to win the biggest wrestling tournament in the world and they’re right. They told me flat out... You move on to the bracket finals the contract is yours. Add to that if you win this thing nobody in WMW is gonna be able to argue you don’t deserve a crack at the Great Lakes Championship? Please... it’s a done deal. You gotta get this done Tony. End of story.” Train had to admit...that was a hell of a story Jerry had. Beyond entertaining. 2.5 million. That wasn’t chump change. And a crack at the GLC? Proof positive the cats he’d left back in GW hadn’t payed enough attention. Train had a feeling when he first jumped into the wrestling game the street game, where it was your life rather than your record on the line, would set him apart from the competition. The street was fire and it purified his mettle. It was a damn good thing because this was just what he needed. More on the line...more pressure. Money. Pride. The shit could have almost been overwhelming... If not for the fact that, one, he was who he was... and, two, he was still only half focused on the challenges of the future. The other half still firmly engrossed on the challenges of the present. The scuffle on the play ground had drawn in a couple more teachers who all seemed intent on reading the little girl the 5-year old version of the riot act. Most kids would breakdown under the pressure. Brianne? Just folded her arms scrunched her face into what was likely meant to be an intimidating scowl, but only managed to make her look cuter than she had been before. It didn’t scare anyone, but it did get her point across. The teachers finally figured out she wasn’t listening and gave up on talking. The all pointed the way back towards the building, and likely, the principal’s office, determining she needed to be exiled from the play ground. She went...but in complete defiance... skipping the whole way. Her little legs flapping away, she reminded him of a hummingbird. She was a handful. Rebel to the core. “Tony?” Jerry asked, trying to get some sign that his pep talk hadn’t fallen on deaf ears. “Tony, you there or what?” “I’m here.” Train says, watching the little girl brush through the doors of the school... there and gone like a breathe of fresh air. Or a hit. Somehow, someway, in the span of a few days Train had become a junkie. He had to have his fix. He’d gotten it. Time to move on. “Put the trainers and the sparing guys on a flight to NYC and I’ll get at you with the name of a good gym to train at. Ain’t shit slippin’ through my fingers anytime soon. You can believe that shit.” “And that’s the type of shit I like to hear” Jerry responds, his smile beaming all the way through the phone. “But, ummmm... Tony... What’s up here? What are you doing in New York?” “Bird watching.”
