Partings and Photographs (Kyle Lamen - Round 17)

24 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Some take pictures to remember. I take pictures to forget. Somewhere in my apartment, I’ve got a large cardboard box, and it’s full of pictures and photo albums. And by “full of” I mean that there are about three or four albums, and a dozen mismatched pictures. I never, ever look at them. Why would I want to? I mean, I really don’t have to. Everything that I want to remember, I remember. And I remember them naturally, without having to refer to a photo, or a hastily-written notation with a place name and a date or time. For me, the pictures are mile-markers on the journey. When someone wants to know where I’ve been, and what I’ve done, I direct them to the picture books, if they’re really that interested. And, to be fair, not many people are. Sarah, my old assistant/crush, she wanted to see them. Not at first, but after a while when the initial mistrust and dislike wore off. She liked them, but said that she wondered why I wasn’t in very many of the pictures. Come on. Who wants to look at pictures of themselves? In my opinion, you’d have to be painfully narcissistic to want to stare longingly at yourself all the time. And while I have thoughts, and many of them, narcissism isn’t one of them. Sarah never really understood me. That’s why one of the pictures at the bottom of the box, sitting by itself, is of her. …aaaaannnnyways… So, as I was saying, I have four picture books. One for my family, and high school or so. One for my wrestling career, and the people I’ve met through it. One for my trip to Japan, and the years I’ve spent there. One for my trips elsewhere. India, England, Australia, China. And the assorted pictures…well, they’re from the girls of my past. There aren’t many. Like I said, a dozen. Okay, more like half a dozen. All are failed relationships. All are over and done with. Pictures of girls that I don’t see anymore, written on the back are phone numbers I don’t call, or dates that no longer hold any real relevance. So, when the time came to end the relationships, I took a picture, or I found a picture, and I kept a picture. And then, that picture was the signpost. The mile marker that seemed to signify a certain exit, a certain place to get off this road and start another. This relationship ends here. * * * * * We stood at the terminal in Detroit. Yeah, I’m the type of guy who’ll catch a flight halfway across the country to have a four hour goodbye conversation. Miho looked great, because she always looked great. Miniskirt or sweatpants. Halter or burlap sack. Anything she wore was perfect. She had the body for sexy clothes, never looking lumpy or out-of-place in polyester, vinyl, or silk. But more than that, she had the face for anything, because of her almond eyes that are so damned expressive, and because of the way she could smile at almost anything and mean it. I’m drifting here, I’m sorry. She’d said she had to go back to Osaka, because there was someone she had to be with back there. She never said anything different, from the moment she showed back up into my life a few weeks ago. She’s not the lying type. She’s got her man somewhere else now, and I’m the lost flame that she wanted to give a final farewell to. I can’t ACCEPT it, but I can understand it. But the two-plus weeks we’ve had back in the States…it’s just like the half-year we had in Japan. Well, except without all the physical stuff. Some harmless kissing…yes. The amazing sex…well, I guess we left that back in 2004, in Japan. Still, it’s just like heaven. We talk all the time. She makes me laugh at stupid comments. She slurps miso soup at the restaurant I own with gusto. She leaves straight, eight-inch long black hairs all over the couch. She has the most impeccable dental hygiene in the modern world. Let me just hold up here for a second. This is not an exaggeration. This woman brushes in the morning, before bed, and after every single time something enters her mouth. She flosses thrice daily. She wears a tiny, pink retainer. This is what happens when you’re a celebrity (or so I hear) where your youthful looks are everything. This is what it takes to be an idol over there in Japan. Anyways, she looks terrific, and when she looks at me, I feel like there’d be a chance that I could have a life with her. Which…naturally, brings me crashing back to the awful reality. I can’t have a life with anyone. Pretty soon, I’ll have a headache that won’t go away…then worse. The loss of hearing, of sight. The potential for aneurysms. Even though I’m committed to not treating it, not setting myself up for the chemo, the radiotherapy, and the surgery that will all lead to false hope and an inability to really LIVE…I know it will come at some point. I spend part of every night, before I fall asleep, thinking about her, and about how goddamn unfair it is. Not that I can’t be with her for the next sixty years…because hell, it’d be unfair for me to actually BE with her. Unfair to everyone else. No, it’s unfair because I’ve had the chance to be with someone so ideal, and I have to have it taken from me, along with my life. …give me a second here, okay? Yeah. So we stand next to the Chili’s in the Detroit Northwest terminal. There’s still a little smattering of buffalo sauce that’s caught on the side of my chin. She wipes it away. I pull out my disposable camera, the one I bought down at the gas station for WAY TOO MUCH money, and I ask her to stand next to the giant habanero pepper. And the seven foot vegetable stands next to the five-six Japanese model, and they make for a cute little dichotomy. She says that she has to go, but she doesn’t want to. I tell her that I love her, and need her to stay, at least for a day or a week or a lifetime. The only true part is the lifetime. She says that the most important person in the world is waiting for her back in Japan. I get so angry, so upset, that I feel like I’m going to just break in half in the center of this airport terminal, that I’m going to crush a disposable camera in my fist, and be carted off to jail screaming and spouting obscenities. She says goodbye. I say so long. And then, like almost everything about my life that’s been good and strong and worthwhile, she’s gone. * * * * * The ride home from the airport isn’t quite so bad. I’ve got my iPod going, and the music I select isn’t any of the mopey, emo, sad bastard stuff that I could play. It’s energetic, and upbeat, for the most part. Transplants. Alkaline Trio. Zeppelin. Maybe a little Poison. Who knows? I feel myself straying into monster ballad territory, and I know that once I go there, I’ll be listening to Cheap Trick’s ‘The Flame’, which is just about Miho’s favorite song. I get thirty seconds into it, I pull the earbuds out and stuff the iPod into my gear bag. This is some weak shit. There’s one thing that heartens me, and the more I think about it, the more I realize what an awful, terrible thought has entered me. I’m going to take this out on someone. I’m going to take all that anger, and hurt, and frustration that threatened to break me in half in that airport terminal, and I’m going to let it loose on two people. Ariana Al-Doleh and Alcaeus. For the first time in my wrestling career, I’ve got something that someone else wants to take from me. The Infinite Gauntlet Championship. I’ve lost my girl, I’m losing my life, I can’t find any damned traction in PRIME. Not only do I have all this pent-up feeling just looking for a violent, pathetic outlet, I’ve got what’s basically the last thing I have left to lose. My championship. Let me say that again. MY. CHAMPIONSHIP. Do you remember the last time I had the chance to defend a title? Yeah, me neither. But I’m sure I have a picture of that day and that belt somewhere in an album at home. I’m squeezing the armrest of the chair so damn hard. The bald guy in the seat behind me, if he wasn’t put out enough by the blue hair and the athlete’s build, has GOT to be freaking out a little now. This thought is almost enough to make me laugh, and move on. So, I take out the iPod, I put it back on ‘The Flame’. And I chart a course across the greatest 80’s monster ballads of all time. ‘Heaven.’ ‘I Remember You.’ I even throw in Saves The Day’s ‘At Your Funeral’ and Something Corporate’s 9-minute emoepic ‘Konstantine’ for good measure. I think they called it flagellation, when you punish yourself. I think monks did it for purity. Well, my mentor, Jason Core, taught me that the pain that doesn’t kill you makes you stronger. Trust me, if this doesn’t kill me, I’m gonna be pretty damn powerful. And I know it won’t. There’s too much else that could. Thanks to Miho Kurosaki, the woman who didn’t love me enough to stay… …I’m going to be strong enough to keep hold of my championship, for at least another week.
