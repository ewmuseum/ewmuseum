The Choices We Make, The Pain We Live With

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 Meeting At The Mansion On Turtle Creek Walls of pink granite greeted him as he walked into the restraint lobby once again. Around the doorway directly down the hall he saw the white granite, stylized in a very classical manner to outline where he should go. As he stepped through the doorway onto the black and white checkered floor he looked around the room. The same granite walls, light oak wood and handcrafted black iron railing presented the room just as the owners had long ago imagined it should. He was in a little opulent palace in the middle of Dallas, Texas. The Mansion on Turtle Creek was widely known as a great restaurant, but it was actually part of a much larger hotel. They did quite a bit of business in corporate retreats and meetings, but this time he was not coming for a few days of team building and five year strategy sessions. Fate had not been so kind to him now, forcing a meeting with someone he respected. Jake let his hair drape down to his shoulders, looking casual as possible to give no outward sign. He moved over to the right, grabbing the cold, solid railing as he proceeded up the wrapping staircase to the second floor. He was given a brief look by the maitre d, but he was not an infrequent guest of the restaurant. That, and the expensive Armani suit wrapped around his huge frame, did not place him as too far out of place. At the top of the stairs, he hesitated. There was no fear in this meeting, but apprehension came from places deeper than fear. Everything was swirling around him, and he had come to realize that the actions he required of himself and those around him carried a heavy price. Jake walked through the doorway and into the upper dining area. The room assaulted the senses in such a sublime manner. Rich aromas of the five star cuisine along with the rich warmth of the immaculately carved wood maintained by nightly polishing came at him like old friends. The serene setting of sophistication was filled with barracudas of all sorts, the businessmen putting on their facades to make it through the day. Just like me. Across the room, the man Jake had known for almost twenty years was sitting alone at a table. Red wine was almost gone from the crystal glass, and he seemed very relaxed. The meeting was not set-up as a high-powered affair, and so perhaps Enzo Hiroku believed he could finally relax while at the restaurant. Jake came up, and stretched his massive paw out to the smaller man. Enzo stood, all five foot four inches of a slim body going slightly to round in the belly. They shook, with the small businessman trying to crush his hand, and exercise in futility they had always enjoyed. Enzo slapped his shoulder, "You look good Jake." "Thanks Enzo. Have to stay fit in my line of work." Jake let go and looked his friend up and down. "You look like you’ve been enjoying the food here in America a little too much." Enzo let out a hearty laugh; already enjoying the company of his old friend he only communicated with through email anymore. They sat down, "I have already begun enjoying the wine cellar, but I left the selection of the meal wine to you of course." "I am honored." Jake nodded. He took the fanned out napkin from the table. A simple flick of the wrist unfurled the staff’s handiwork as he dropped it into his lap. "Of course, the meeting is not all fun and games. I actually had some interesting business information to discuss with you." Enzo’s thick eyebrows raised up, "Really? You know I always enjoy a bit of ‘interesting’ information. What is it everyone calls you now, the Big Dog? Are you becoming the same animal in our world, trading favors for favors?" "Maybe, but that is for later." Jake begged off. He wasn’t ready for that yet. He wanted to give his friend a chance, remind him of everything worth fighting for in life. He looked over the menu, trying to distract himself from what he must do. "You know, just about everything here is good." "Yes, I have been here many times with clients. I enjoy the lobster tacos." "They are good." Jake pushed his hair out of his face and looked across the table. He saw him there, sipping his wine as though nothing were happening, and he felt the anger rise inside of himself. He held it at bay, but the waiting was now over. "You ever do any work with Matsudo Industrial?" In the shadowy world in which Enzo traveled, there were very few signs of distress. He was expected to keep himself under tight control to ensure his opponents and adversaries never knew what he was thinking. As he raised the wine glass to his mouth, Jake noticed that he was holding the stem with his entire hand instead of the thumb and forefinger as he had so far that evening. "No, the name does not mean much. Where are they based out of?" Enzo asked. Jake closed his menu, setting it down on the table. "No one seems to know. They’re a shadow company." "Probably someone’s tax shelter!" Enzo’s laugh was forced. He pulled the menu slightly higher, looking for a shield behind the plastic covered paper. Jake leaned back, "Assuming so, they have hidden themselves quite well. I have not been able to get much information on them, but luckily I found an employee willing to speak with me." "An employee?" Enzo stayed behind the menu, feeling the cold trickle as a bead of sweat rolled down his back. This was no meeting of friends. "Yes." Jake lifted his water glass, taking a deep sip before continuing. "Apparently, not every employee is happy with…management. They don’t feel their vision for the company is as sure as they once were led to believe." "Well, one must believe in the vision of the company’s founder, or be an honorable man and step aside. There are far too many men who have chosen to go behind their company to do things such as talk to outsiders. Very foolish thing for a man to do, as it always leads to their ruin in the community no matter what happens." Enzo said. He could feel more cold trickles now. "Sometimes there are higher purposes Enzo. Men are guided by what their hearts tell them, and if you ignore the truth in your heart then you are worth nothing. How many times did we sit and talk about doing better things in this world?" Jake’s smile was reflective and mournful. "We did what we could I suppose through our charity work. Sometimes it never seems like enough though, does it? We look for something greater than ourselves. We just have to find the right organization to be a part of to make a difference." Enzo set his own menu down hitting the silverware as he did. The noise seemed quite loud in his mind, "That’s always the difficulty, is it not? You must find an organization you can trust. Too often, men fall for the fallacies of the masses instead of seeking the simple truth." "Oh Enzo, how philosophical you have become." Jake nodded his head at the man. He rubbed his hands together then held them out apart, "I am no great philosopher my friend. Hell, truth be told I’m not always an articulate man. I mean, I beat people up for a living, so generally I let my fists do the talking. All I know is there is right and there is wrong." "A scholar of the law I see!" Enzo laughed as he finished his wine. When he set the glass down, he scraped the top of the fork. He looked down at what he had just done, unbelieving. "No, the law was instituted by men. I believe in the idea of right and wrong. I mean, if a man attacks you and you fight back and hurt him, what happens? You both get arrested, and even if he started it, he’ll sue you for some fast cash. Is that right?" Jake rubbed the side of his face, "no, but that is the rule of law. Corrupted by the guardians we expected to uphold the law as our equal truth. No, I believe in what is right and wrong. If a man attacks your family, then you bury him. That’s what I believe in." Enzo swallowed, nothing but air and saliva went down his throat. He stared at the man locking his powerful, unflinching blue eyes onto him. "That is certainly…and older belief system." "Sometimes old ways are still the best." Jake leaned forward, resting a single arm on the table, "When it comes to family, men must do whatever it takes to save the ones they love." Enzo looked down at the floor, "Yes, whatever your family needs. I seem to be feeling the effects of the wine I drank. I apologize, but could we meet another time?" "Enzo, I need to talk about Matsudo Industrial. You and I both know they’re a shell corporation for Sid to work in Japan. We know that, because you made all the arrangements." Jake sighed, "I couldn’t believe it, but the paper trial couldn’t be completely hidden. I have someone who told me everything, and when I go back to Japan I am going to take the final book. I have to get the Book Of Ascension to end this, and I cannot let anything stop me." "You picked the wrong side Jake, the losing side." Enzo was still smiling, putting on a show for anyone who might casually glance over at them even as his voice took on an icy tone. "You should turn your brother over, and then come with us." "How did he get you Enzo? How did Sid turn you against everything and everyone you loved?" Mournfully, Jake stroked his goatee for a moment, "I remember being more than business acquaintances, and we were friends. You changed." Enzo picked his napkin up from his lap and folded it before setting it on the table. "We do what we must for our families." "Your daughter could be rescued Enzo, taken back and put into safe hiding. You don’t have to be beholden to them. Help me, help us, and we’ll do everything we can to save her." Enzo let out a small snarl of repulsion, "You cannot guarantee me anything, and you’re as castrated here as your God. Everything you believe is simply a lie, propaganda for the masses." "If you really believe that, then you’re already lost." Jake felt another piece of himself die a little within his soul. "Enzo, you don’t have to help them. I fly back tomorrow, and we’re going to take the book. Help us, and redeem yourself. Help us, and we’ll save your daughter too." Enzo stood, his face drawn in a sad frown. As his lip quivered, he spoke stiffly, formally. "A pleasure as always Mr. Hix. I look forward to our next meeting." He went to walk by, and Jake grabbed his wrist. He looked at the man, "For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?" Enzo pulled his wrist back, and kept going. Jake stared down at the table, the darkness in the man’s soul had sent chills up his spine when he touched him. He had given the man his chance, left the door open, but if he did not choose to help then there were the other options. The fire inside him began burning again. The Altar The sun had long since gone down. Inside the small chapel, no lights could be seen at all save for the one small beam of light from the streetlamp creeping across the floor to the huge crucifixion scene carved in marble as a high-relief sculpture. Serene, no sound seemed to be coming from the room at all as the man listened. Finally, there was a small clicking. He pushed the door open, and looked around the dark room. He could see no one, but then again they could be right before him and he might not notice. Veston closed his eyes, counting to thirty in his head so his eyes would adjust to the darkness. He opened his eyes again, and saw a small bit of movement down in the front row of pews. Walking down, he could hear the whispered prayers of the man kneeling down onto the hard stone floor. Veston slid into a pew several rows behind, waiting to be acknowledged. Finding Jake like this was not unusual. He found his…friend…was that the right word? Friend? Co-worker? Whatever they had become, he knew the man was beginning to struggle with the decisions he was expected to make, the decisions he had to make. Sid Hix had become something without shape or form, a dark cloud descending over the world. Of all of the men who could have been chosen to fight against the rising tide, a most unlikely man had been chosen. A drunk, a man once addicted to pain pills, a fornicator, a reformed Christian as he described himself and perhaps most disturbing the brother of the Dark messiah’s physical form. All of these things, and yet he had been chosen. Veston could not argue with His choice. He simply accepted the great span of wisdom that he could never fully understand. Veston realized in his musings that there was no noise anymore. "Jake?" "I said I wished to be left alone." Jake said gruffly. "Kings and conquerors may order men about in such a way, but you are neither." Veston replied with a sheepish grin. He would goad him into speaking with him. "I don’t claim to be anything, but I did ask for a simple favor. I guess I can’t even get that anymore." Jake re-wrapped the rosary beads around his hand. "Now is not the time to be alone." Veston leaned on the back of the pew before him, "What is bothering you Jake?" "You mean you don’t know holy man?" Jake stayed there, staring up at the huge wooden cross hanging from the ceiling. Veston rested the side of his head on the rail, "I have my suspicions, but why not ask? Reduces the amount of time spent guessing." "Look, I just need some time alone Veston." "You haven’t done anything wrong Jake. Everything you’ve done has been the right choice for your family, for the world and even for yourself." Veston tried to reassure him. Jake kept staring up at the wall, "I just don’t understand it anymore Veston. Do you know what we have given to this? I’ve buried more friends and family in the last year than in my entire life before. I’ve seen, I’ve seen things…I never wanted…horrors that still burn inside me like brands that never quit searing." "You have weathered these storms and come through them. You have been blessed…" Jake turned, like a bull set free from the stall. He grabbed the edge of the pew, ripping it free from the bolts holding it to the floor. He tossed the pew aside easily, his face a mask of rage as he yelled, his cries echoing off the walls of the empty church. "BLESSED!" Jake came right at Veston, "You call this blessed!?! They raped my family Veston! They took everything we had, and they shattered it forever. Do you think my sister will ever recover from that? Do you think as much as the images I saw have hurt me, do you think she will ever not smell the breath of the men who violated her and her husband? I know she will remember it every day of her life, I know she still sees her husband’s dead body lying beside her every single day! Don’t talk to me about being blessed!" Jake kicked his leg out, sending a loud crack resonating through the church as he cracked another pew. "At least she didn’t have to see what they did to her children. I saw what they did to them Veston! I saw the flesh ripped in half…ripped in half on a four year old, beautiful little girl! There was nothing blessed about what happened." Jake lashed out with another kick, splitting the pew in half. He threw each piece aside as he marched through the pews to Veston. He stopped, a single pew standing between them. He breathed in and out, energy seemingly spent momentarily as his eyes glazed over. Even in the darkness, Veston could see in his eyes he had gone somewhere else. "And the…with a cross made…they did to John what…" Jake stumbled backwards. "I’m just looking for a little quiet in my world Veston, a small space of peace." Veston stood up. He came out from the pews and walked down the middle aisle, avoiding the debris on the floor. He looked down at Jake with great pity, "There is no peace at the moment Jake. I’m sorry, but that is the truth." "Yeah, fuck you." Jake turned back and looked up at the cross, "Fuck you." "He’ll forgive that." "I don’t care about being forgiven." Jake walked back to where he had knelt down. He dropped to his knees again, bringing his hands together with the rosary there for him to rely on. "If you don’t care, then why do you pray to him?" Jake laughed, "You think I’m looking for forgiveness? I’m not looking for forgiveness; I’m looking for understanding. I keep trying to understand what is happening. Why he allows this to happen if he loves us. The eternal question is what I want answered, I want an answer to why he lets all these horrible things happen to us if he loves us." Veston came right beside Jake. He kneeled down beside him, "Free will works in each movement of our lives and the lives around us. Free will means we must endure the poor choices by other creations with no regard for the gifts they have been provided. God allows this happen because he loves us. Free will allows existence to be varied and unique. Free will allows exploration of the world around us." Veston looked up at the cross, "God’s gift is a two edged sword, but the alternative is not living as he envisioned. We accept all aspects of the world: good and bad, black and white, happy and sad. We must embrace all of them, for God has created everything for us. Sometimes, other people make choices that influence what happens to us. If God controlled everything we would be nothing but robots." Jake’s hand squeezed down on the rosary, the steel construction of his chain and beads tore into his flesh. "Just leave me alone Veston. None of your words can help me. Tonight, I told an old friend I knew he betrayed my family and me. This was a man who helped me start my first business, helped me invest my wrestling money. I told him he had to join us, or else…" "Or else what?" Veston seemed suddenly apprehensive. "You see, this is a man who knows we are coming for the final book. He knows about our finances, our friends and so much more. If he remains a threat, everything could be lost. So then the question becomes what should I do about him? Will God forgive me for what I have to do?" Jake squeezed on the rosary so tightly it snapped, falling off his hand down to the floor. Several of the beads, now loosed from their prison, rolled around on the tile. Veston kept staring at him, "What have you done Jake?" The phone on Jake’s hip let out a short series of rings, and then stopped. "I gave him a choice. Come with us, and we would save him and try to rescue his daughter. If he didn’t do that, he was our enemy. He was simply another agent of Sid’s, but I had to do the honorable thing. I left him open doors." Jake turned slowly; his yes were hard and filled with emotion. Veston understood, "What doors did you leave open?" "Free will." "That page you got…" "He could either call me, or he could perish. Either by his hand or someone else’s, he had very few choices. When he got home this evening, he made a phone call to arrange a meeting with Sid’s closest advisors. He was prepared to sell us out to the Dark Messiah." Jake looked back up at the cross, "So tell me Holy Man, if what I did was to protect God’s kingdom, to save the world and fight against Sid is it a sin? If it isn’t a sin like you and Robert keep saying, then why do I feel such a gaping, dark hole in my soul?" Veston stood, his legs now unsteady. "You killed him?" "That is a matter of perspective." "No, you killed him. You just want to know it was right?" Veston considered it for a moment. The delicate balance between what was right and wrong was even more difficult in these circumstances. "What you did was necessary, and as God’s agent it is forgiven." "Simple as that is it?" Jake looked up to the cross, seeing his brother John’s body there, even though it couldn’t have been. "How much blood will we sacrifice on his altar Veston? How much are we expected to give to appease him? Is it that simple? Have we simply not given enough? Veston turned away from Jake. He began walking away, crunching debris beneath his boots as he left, "Nothing is ever simple Jake." He kneeled on the floor, waiting to be alone again. Here he was, alone with God. The worst part of the entire relationship was that he knew in the deepest part of his heart he loved God. That was no surprise; it was always those you loved who you could also hate the most. Leaving He pulled the jacket around his body. The chill of winter had finally settled into the Metroplex. He felt the whipping wind rush by as he left the church. He walked down, seeing a familiar figure on the corner. "Marvin, you look a little riled up." Jake said as he approached. On his porch, Marvin was wrapped in an insulated jumpsuit with a scarf, ski cap and even a blanket draped over his shoulders. He looked normal, except all the signs he put up on his porch proclaiming the conspiracy theories about how the world really ran. Marvin was local color for the neighborhood, and since he was namely harmless most people ignored the crazy redneck with white hair and beady eyes. "Man done got his self blowed up." Marvin said. Jake pulled out a pair of leather gloves. He slapped them into the palm of his hand, "Really?" "Yes sir, yes sir." Marvin pointed to his police scanner, "I heard it all. Some foreigner, started up his Merrrrrrrrrcedes Benzzzzzzz…and BOOOOOOMMMMM!!!!" Jake nodded, "Happens to the best I guess. What was his name?" Marvin had to think hard, "Something sounded all funny. Urzo, Curzo or…" "Enzo." Marvin looked hard at the man in front of him pulling gloves on, "Well how in the bejesus did you know? "Lucky guess." Jake walked away. Marvin could be too paranoid for anyone’s good, "And it was on the news." Marvin began laughing, "Oh, you son of a bitch! Messin’ with old man, such a son of a bitch!" Yes I am.
