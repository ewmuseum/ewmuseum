Know Thine Enemy: Part II: Lugubrious Games

9 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-09 He kept to the shadows, following the tall man through the deserted wharf. The night was silent, filled with nothing but that awful sound that darkness makes. Nothing. Something about the man made him uneasy, he had no affiliations with the police force, yet he had been lurking in the shadows at every one of the mysterious crime scenes. He tried to breathe softly, slowing his steps, but Special Agent Bannan Knecht was out of shape, and horribly out of his element. He heard the sound of distant thunder, and it made him flinch. He was following a hunch, something he’d stopped doing years ago. The bright lights of the harbor glittered off the water, a picture worth a thousand words, if he had eyes to see that beauty. Time had turned him bitter, twisting his youthful optimism into jaded cynicism. Knecht kept to the same shadows his quarry had moved through, moving slowly, his weight thrown forward so his feet made no sound on the uneven ground. His breathing was shallow; he could have been sleeping for all the noise he made. He made no move to quicken his pursuit, he knew precisely where his quarry was headed. He hesitated behind a pile of crates, his eyes still fixed on the silhouette ahead. His hand crept to the butt of the pistol beneath his coat, his FBI standard issue Glock, the touch comforting. The air reeked of sawdust and rotten fish, the territory was familiar. These were the docks that DeNucci ran, fronting his smuggling and drug trade with his fishery industry. Clever misdirection. He kept his ass clean, Knecht gave him that. They couldn’t even nail him on tax fraud; he was paid up, honest. The stench in the air made his stomach churn, or perhaps that was the utter absurdity of this little stakeout. Jackson hesitated for only a second, adjusting the collar of his jacket, turning it up against the brisk wind. He knocked on the door, head cocked to the side, listening for noises from within. He wasn’t disappointed. Almost immediately, he heard the sound of footsteps and then the porthole in the middle of the door opened. “Whaddya want?” Jackson smiled, his eyes hidden behind that familiar pair of dark sunglasses. Tonight, he looked the part, dressed nattily in a dark pinstripe suit with expensive Italian leather wingtips on his massive feet. He’d topped this off with his usual leather duster. Last, but not least, his wild hair had been tamed, slicked back into a tight ponytail. He met the gaze of the goon behind the door, and spoke, his voice ringing with authority. “I’m here to see Mr. Signorello.” A chuckle issued forth from beyond the heavy steel door, followed by words laced with sarcasm. “Yeah? You got an appointment?” Laughter followed this rhetorical question; raucous cackling that would have infuriated a lesser man. Jackson simply stood there, that smile still fixed on his lips. Slowly, he tilted his head from side to side, cracking his neck, the sound echoing between the warehouses like gunshots. “No. Do I need one?” “What do you think you are, a fuckin’ comedian? You a wise-guy or somethin’?” “Nothing so garish, you imbecile. I have a gift for Mr. Signorello, and an offer he cannot possibly refuse. Do tell him that someone is here to see him.” Absolute silence answered his declaration, darkness the only thing visible beyond the open window. With a sigh of resignation, he reached inside his jacket, ignoring the sound of a gun being readied beyond the door. With one hand, he held up his gun… with the other, he held a small black box. He set both on the ground slowly, side by side and backed away, shouting now for his voice to carry. “A gesture of goodwill, I come in peace. My offering is there. Take it. I will wait here if you wish.” He leaned against a barrel filled with rainwater, casually lighting a cigarette. He watched the door from behind the dark glasses, his movements deliberately relaxed. He simply inhaled the smoke, exhaling it equally as slowly. They would bite. After a moment, he heard the latches thrown back, and watched as the door creaked open. His gun and the box were snatched inside, the door slamming closed with a sound that reverberated all along the dock. He heard the shout of outrage, muffled by the steel barrier, and a few seconds later, he felt the cold press of steel against the back of his head. He suppressed a laugh as he turned his head slightly to look at the goon who was pointing a gun against his head. His voice held a tone of mockery when he spoke, an insouciant smile playing at the corners of his lips. “Ah, yes… I suppose Mr. Signorello will see me now?” His reward was a thump to the back of the head that left him seeing stars. He was half dragged, half escorted inside, and led to a room straight out of any gangster movie. Two thugs stood behind the large desk, arms folded, pieces clearly visible. He felt himself pushed into a chair. He welcomed the respite, taking a brief span to collect his thoughts, and to quell the dizziness that threatened to overcome him. He looked up, meeting the gaze of the corpulent man behind the desk. Salvatore Signorello. The loanshark and money launderer for DeNucci’s family. The repugnant behemoth spoke, his voice filled with the same outrage he’d heard in that one shout. “You want to tell me why the fuck you gave me a pair of eyeballs?” Jackson sat there calmly, cool as the other side of the pillow, his eyes hidden behind the glasses, the ultimate form of disrespect. “Well, you see, it is rather difficult to get a horse’s head at this time of the night, and I’m afraid the florist was fresh out of black roses. I had to make do.” Signorello inclined his head slightly, and Jackson suddenly found himself being choked from behind by a rather beefy arm. “You have the nerve, the motherfuckin’ audacity to come into my house and insult me? Who the fuck do you think you are? Just who do you think you are?” Jackson remained still, for about a split second, time enough for his face to begin to redden, and then he reached behind himself, grabbing his attacker by the back of the neck. He shifted his weight forward, and flipped the man over the chair; slamming him into the floor, head first. There was a sound rather like the splitting of a ripe melon, and then the room fell silent. Jackson straightened up, smoothing his suit before returning to the hardbacked chair. He turned the chair around, and leaned his arms across the back, waiting for someone else to make a move. Signorello blanched, the color bleeding out of his face, leaving it pasty white. “Who are you?” Jackson replied, his tone mild, belying his words and the threat they carried. “I can be your best friend, or your worst nightmare. That, Mr. Signorello, is entirely up to you. Although, I must say, we haven’t quite begun on the right foot here. I don’t take kindly to being manhandled by your goons.” The fat Italian scowled, irritation creeping into his beady eyes. “And I don’t take kindly to being threatened.” “Oh? Did I threaten you?” Signorello looked down at the black box that stood alone on the green blotter that covered his desk. Recognition flooded his features, turning his grimace into a watery frown. “These are Joey’s? Ain’t they?” Jackson nodded. Signorello looked down into the box for a long time, and then with a roar of anger, threw the box at Jackson. It careened off the side of his sunglasses, knocking them askew, effectively knocking all the amusement from Jackson’s features. He stood, and shoved the chair aside, storming over to the desk. He leaned over, putting his hands on the green desktop, his face inches from that of the loanshark. “Now I’m threatening you, there is a distinction. My question is this, asshole… which way would you like to die? This is my offer, will you choose life or death?” Signorello laughed, right up until the point of Jackson withdrawing another gun from beneath his jacket, this one equipped with a silencer. He shot both bodyguards in one fluid motion. They slid to the floor soundlessly, startling Signorello in mid gleeful chortle. He looked up into Jackson’s eyes, and was unable to look away when Jackson removed the dark glasses, actually giving him a look at what lay beyond. His eyes were a deep shade of blue, that wasn’t remarkable; it was the utter lack of life, and compassion in those eyes that drove the fear home to Signorello. His jowls quivered as he looked at the barrel of the gun, which was pointed at his head. “Wh-what do you want?” “Oh, I want lots of things, who doesn’t? Love, a good fuck now and again, to rule the world. Grandiose dreams. The question, rather, is this… what are you willing to give me to spare your life?” “Money! I have money! Guns, cocaine… take it all. Take it… please, just don’t…” the words faded to a sob, making Jackson shake his head in disgust. He withdrew a mirror from inside his jacket, and he held it out for Signorello to look into. “Look at yourself, you disgusting pig, just look. You sicken me. You think you can buy your life with money? With drugs and firearms? What makes you think I care about material goods? Why don’t you try redeeming yourself another way.” Brian Davis stood at the edge of the scorched grass, smelling the stench of old ashes. The only things that remained here now, beyond the twisted fence were the broken bits of discarded metal. The coaster and the Scrambler, both mutilated beyond repair. He remembered seeing pictures of this carnival when he was a kid, but he’d never been. The place screamed of desolation, almost wailing in his ears like a manic-depressive choir, screaming out of tune. The wind blew across the barren wasteland, bringing the taste of ashes into his mouth. It was like visiting ground zero after a nuclear holocaust. Five homeless men had perished here, five John Does… more unclaimed bodies for the crematorium. Ashes to ashes, dust to dust. He knew the place well, only because it was where he had first met Jackson, the man who called himself Darkness. He wondered then, as he raked a hand through his graying hair, just how far he would go now that he’d fallen. He’d become on of those cops in the movies, the dirty ones. He’d bought and paid for a murder, and gotten more than he bargained for. Joey Capone had been the third. Frankie Fererra had been the first. Then Vinnie Astolfi. All parts of DeNucci’s family. The boulder was cascading down the hill now, picking up speed, and he felt like he was trapped behind it, about to be crushed. He hadn’t asked for this. It was time to call off the hellhound he had unleashed. He withdrew his cell phone and punched in a familiar number… He was sweating now, beneath the expensive suit as he crouched atop Signorello. The man was hogtied naked on the floor with an apple in his mouth, already bruised and battered. His legs were both bent at a horrible angle, and by the looks of him he was close to passing out. This was the best part of what he liked to call his lugubrious game. The torture. The screams. The music of breaking bones. A symphony, one best suited for background music during a dinner in the bowels of Hell. One perhaps with Hannibal Lechter, Jeffrey Dahmer and Adolf Hitler in attendance. Jackson held out the gun, smiling in elated satisfaction, and that was when his cell phone began to ring. With a muttered epithet, he withdrew it, flipping it open. “Jackson.” Davis’ voice sounded in his ear without any preamble. “We have to talk.” “Now is rather inconvenient, didn’t I ask you not to call me at this number?” He swiped at his forehead with the back of his gun hand, sluicing away some of the sweat. “No, we have to talk now. I’m sick of cleaning up your messes. This has to stop, now.” Jackson looked down at the bleeding, naked man on the floor, and let loose a chuckle. “You have piss poor timing, has anyone ever told you that, Detective Davis?” “I mean it, you have to-” Jackson cut him off with a rush of words. “That’s where you’re wrong, Davis. I don’t HAVE to do anything. What are you going to do if I don’t stop? Turn me in? Throw me in the cuffs and haul me off to the hoosegow?” “If I have to, yes. This has gone far enough.” “Good luck convicting a man who doesn’t exist, Davis. Stay close to your scanner; I’ll be in touch. Toodles.” He snapped the phone closed and returned it to his inside pocket before turning his attention back to Signorello. He unscrewed the silencer, letting the fat man watch. Tears rolled down his doughy cheeks, making his eyes look sad and wet, like those of the dog he’d had as a child. He’d loved that dog. He flipped Signorello over, and straddled him, sitting on the fat man’s stomach. “Last chance, fucker. Do you want to live?” The fat man shook his head, tears flowing freely now. He knew no matter what he said the answer would be wrong. He’d accepted his fate and was going gently into that good night. Jackson’s upper lip curled up in distaste, and he pressed the gun against Signorello’s chest, shooting him pointblank on the left side. The sound was loud, reverberating off the walls, echoing in the still night air. He didn’t hear the footsteps as the echo was still fading, but he did hear the click of the hammer being drawn back. “Freeze. FBI! Drop your weapon!” He laid down the gun atop the dead man and stood slowly before turning to look at the man who stood behind him. Agent Knecht. “Ah, well. I was wondering when you’d come in out of the cold. Don’t look so surprised, Agent, I knew you were following me. Frankly, I just didn’t care. Did you enjoy the show?” “Shut up. You have the right to remain silent. You have the right-” Jackson held up his hands, shaking his head. “Save it. See that window?” He inclined his head, indicating the huge bank of windows that covered the rear wall. Knecht nodded. “Good. See the little red dot?” Knecht looked down, at the red laser dot that was creeping up his leg, the gun trembling slightly in his hand. “Want to see who can get the first shot in?” Knecht shook his head, attempting to not lose his cool as the red dot settled on his crotch. He decided to bluff his way out of the situation. “I have backup on its way. They’ll be here any minute. They’ll find your sniper.” Jackson nodded. “Ah, yes. Your backup. Well then, we still have a few more minutes in which I can kill you.” Jackson lowered one hand, reaching inside his jacket. He withdrew a small silver object. Knecht snapped at him, the gun wobbling slightly as sweat beaded his upper lip. “Hey! Hands where I can see them!” Jackson didn’t reply, instead he depressed the tiny button on the knife, and the blade shot out, slamming into the single bulb that illuminated the room. The room was plunged into darkness, and Knecht found himself falling as something solid slammed into the back of his head. He landed atop the dead man, feeling something crunching beneath his feet as he went down. He saw stars in the dark, and then everything went black as a wet rag was pressed against his mouth. Chloroform. Jackson paused in the doorway, illuminated by the light of the moon. He held up the laser pointer, and snickered before dropping it back into his pocket. “Fucking amateurs.” He slipped into the night, mingling with the shadows, already gone when the police did arrive to arrest Special Agent Knecht for the murders. He was caught redhanded, quite literally, with money and baggies of heroin in his pockets, and broken mirror glass beneath his feet, covered in the dead man’s blood. His gun, seemed to be missing, but the unregistered Walther that had killed the man was resting conveniently in his shoulder holster. The perfect crime. The perfect frame-up. Smart, and nuts. The perfect combination… a deadly combination.
