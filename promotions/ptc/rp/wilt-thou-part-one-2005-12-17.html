Wilt Thou?: Part One

17 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-17 Seymour Almasy had been to many gyms in his career. He’d been to gigantic workout centers like a New York Sports Club or Work Out World. He’d been to a Gold’s Gym before, as well as many local establishments. Never, though, had he been to anything quite like Fasullo’s Gym. Because Fasullo’s Gym was what one could quite effectively call…a shithole. Actually, no, that really wasn’t an apt name. Holes with piles of feces in them would likely be offended by the comparison. Bare white concrete walls matched a hard concrete floor, stained with sweat, blood, and various other bodily fluids. The only thing resembling ventilation was a tiny, barred window high on one wall. Lighting could best be described as piss poor, with one of the light tubes annoyingly flashing on and off every five seconds. Why was he here? In truth, he didn’t know. Seymour enjoyed the rather odd hobby of simply getting on public transportation and riding it to the end of the line. Today’s journey had taken him from Manhattan all the way to the Bronx before the driver had called "last stop". It was about then, standing in the middle of an area that he didn’t know, that Seymour realized he’d forgotten to get in his daily workout. So, after a few minutes of wandering, he found Fasullo’s Gym, opened the door, and that brings us to now. Okay, so the place was worse than a shithole. But fuck, Seymour Almasy had come to work out, and work out he would. "Hey," Seymour heard, from somewhere around him. "The fuck ya doin’ hea?" The Final Fantasy whirled to face the man, lying on a mat after a set of sit-ups. He wore a threadbare wife beater, covered with various unpleasant stains. Missing a few teeth, he wasn’t exactly someone that anyone would like to look at for very long. Bravely, Seymour managed to keep one eye on the man while responding. "I’m here to work out." Silence reigned for a few seconds. And then, the man simply laughed and went back to his sit-ups. "Hmph. You’re not very polite, you know." Seymour’s comment got little more than a snort. Deciding to ignore the man, Seymour began his search of the gym, looking for an exercise machine. One problem. There weren’t any. There were no treadmills, or exercise bikes in this gym. Well, there WAS an exercise bike, but it was broken and lying on its side in the corner. So much for any cardiovascular workout. He shrugged, though, and found a sparse mat in a corner, devoid of any weights or benches. There, looking as if it hadn’t been used in quite some time, was an old jump rope.. Picking the rope up, he shook it thoroughly, to remove the dust, and began using the children’s toy. One. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Nine. Ten. And then back to one again. As he jumped, he couldn’t help but look around at the equipment and the clientele of this particular gym. It was sparsely populated, but still, the benches were being utilized. A burly Hispanic man was seated on one, doing dumbbell curls. Another bench had two tattooed men, one doing bench presses and the other spotting. Another set. The sound of rope hitting mat soon became a constant in the small gym. Plink-plink-plink went the rope, and the sound began to draw attention. One pair of eyes leveled itself at Seymour. And then another. And another. And another. Self-consciously, he stopped his jumping of rope, and gazed back at the seemingly hostile masses. For the first time, he REALLY looked, and thus understood the pointed stares. He was different from these people. Different in a way that went beyond simple skin color. After all, this gym was a melting pot, even without him. The differences were everywhere, and they were irrevocable. Take, for example, the watch that Seymour wore on his wrist. It wasn’t a Rolex. Not even close, for that matter. But it still likely cost more than most of the men in this gym would earn in two weeks. Or his hands. Seymour’s hands were soft, pristine, delicate. Sure, in the ring, he could throw chops and strikes with the best of them. But compared to these men, his hands were as soft as a baby’s bottom. After all, they’d never been used to build things. To tear them apart. To make his keep in this world. "Hey," one said, firmly, "you’re that Seymour guy, aint’cha?" The Final Fantasy nodded, slowly. He would not apologize for who he was. What he was. To anyone, period. "Yes, I am. Why do you ask?" Snorts rose throughout the room. Seymour frowned at that, as he rather felt like he had become one colossal inside joke that he couldn’t understand. "The fuck you doin’ here?" another asked, reiterating the earlier question. "Doncha got anotha gym to go to?" "I was in the neighborhood," Seymour replied, growing a bit annoyed at the situation. "Besides, I live here." "You live hea?" still another man asked, looking at Seymour through what appeared to be angered, narrowed eyes. "Yes, I do," came the reply. "I’m living here, in New York City." More laughter. The regulars exchanged looks at one another, and simultaneously rose. Seymour tensed, as if preparing for attack, but the men merely congregated in the corner opposite Seymour, wondering just what the Hell to do with the white-haired wrestler. "Dis guy can’t be fa’ real," one said, shaking his head in disbelief. "He ain’t from hea." "Naw," another replied, "I think he means it. He don’t understand. Look at ‘im. How could he?" They broke their huddle, standing together in what appeared to Seymour to be a pack. He remained tense, on-edge, just in case worse came to worse. He was thus much relieved when one spoke. "Where do ya live, isactly?" came the question. "Staten? Da Bronx? Manhattan? Where?" "Manhattan," Seymour replied. "That’s where my apartment is. Or was…just sold it, and am looking for someplace new to live." Of course, the others in the room didn’t hear anything after Manhattan. To someone unfamiliar with the city…there was no difference between Manhattan and the Bronx. They were just parts of NYC. Sections. Manhattan had its rough parts, sure. But it was, more importantly, where the rich lived. The crème de la crème. It was the part of the city you always saw on the TV shows, in the magazine. The image that New York City wanted to present to the world. You never saw the Bronx. You never saw the homeless people, or the thousands of schmucks living in dilapidated apartments. Fuck, the only thing people connected the Bronx with was Yankee Stadium. Not this gym. Not these people. Not this life. "If you wanna stay, you can stay. Ain’t that we got a beef wit’ you or nutin, it’s just dat I cain’t imagine you’ll be welcome in da back." "The back?" The group let out a collective sigh. Well, there was no going back now. Better to just let things run their course. "Dere’s a wrestling aca-acad--school in da back, behind tha door ova there. Best equipment in da gym. But the fella in charge is sorta--" "Thanks for the heads-up." Seymour had already turned, headed for the door at a brisk pace. The door opened, Seymour stepped through, and the door closed. Silence reigned for a few seconds. And then, the men scattered, and went back to their workouts. The sounds of clinking barbells, hard breathing, and screams of effort were once again audible in the gym. Everything was back to the way it should be. ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- "C’mon kid! You know you can do betta than that!" In spite of his gruff voice, Carl Williams was a proud man as he watched two young men competing in the middle of a run-down wrestling ring. Wrestling was Carl’s life. It had been since the day he turned 18, and for 30 years, he’d lived the business on the New York independent scene until the constant punishment he took forced him into retirement. Retired or not, though, wrestling had given a lot to Carl. Sure, the pay was crappy, but it taught him respect. Discipline. Values for life that had helped him find a good job to allow him to keep wrestling. Values that kept him out of a life of crime and helped him move into a nice apartment near the gym he’d worked out his whole life in. Being a respectable sort, he wanted to give back to the community and business that he so loved. And so, the Wrestling School of the Bronx was started up. Funded by donations, the non-profit school taught kids the art of the sport, and gave them a productive alternative to the gangs and misery that the inner city could all too often bring to those with too much time and too little money. Carl smiled fondly at the memories of his career, only to be brought sharply back to reality by a well-executed body slam by one of his two students, followed up by an elbow drop. These two were among his best and brightest this term. "Hey," a voice suddenly called out, catching Carl off-guard. "Mind if I join you guys?" Action in the ring stopped as the two young wrestlers looked up at the sound of the voice. Standing before them was a man that, as wrestling fans, they knew. But it didn’t make sense. Why the heck was Seymour Almasy here in their training room? "No way! You’re Seymour Almasy!" From his vantage point, Almasy nodded, as Carl slowly spun to look at the five foot nothing, one hundred nothing pound man who had become one of PTC’s best known and best loved stars. "Can I help you," he asked, hands on hips, staring down Almasy with an even, weathered gaze. Whether it was meant to be hostile or not, Seymour felt definite negativity in Carl’s stare. "Don’t mind me," Seymour said, waving a hand. "The other guys in the gym just told me there was a ring back here. If you don’t mind, when you guys are done, would it be okay if I hop in real quick?" Before Carl could answer, one of the students cut him off. "Mr. Williams, could he come in and train with us?" Williams scowled in response, an expression not lost on Seymour. The looks on the faces of his students, however, caused Carl to acquiesce. "Eh, sure. Go ahead." Nodding, Seymour sized up the ring, and the two men standing in it. They were both young, not a day over sixteen, he judged. Both about six feet tall, probably about two-hundred pounds each, and well put together. "Come on, Seymour," one taunted. "You afraid of a couple of rookies?" The Final Fantasy shook his head, and rolled in under the bottom rope. "Get ‘em, Shaun," the other called, and the student met Seymour coming in, hooking him for a hip toss. He was astounded, however, to see Seymour land on his feet out of the move, sweep out a leg, and connect with a somersault senton across Shaun’s chest. "Whoa," Shaun gasped, getting his air back from the move. "He’s awesome! Kyle, you go get him!" Kyle barreled forward into the rising Seymour, catching him with a clothesline that prompted a nod of approval from his trainer. The youngster went to follow up, but Seymour kipped-up, wrapping his legs around Kyle’s head to execute a modified hurricanrana that sent the student flying head over heels. Both men got up quickly, but this time Kyle took the advantage, snapping Seymour over with a well-executed arm drag. Almasy hit as he usually did, but he suddenly let out a yelp of pain. "Kyle, you dumbass," Shaun said, looking at their positioning. "You took him over on that nail! You can’t keep doin’ that to people!" "Sorry bout that Seymour," Kyle mumbled, releasing the hold he’d maintained on Almasy’s arm. "Didn’t mean to hurt you." Seymour sat up, rubbing the spot where the apparent nailhead had dug into his back. "It’s okay, don’t worry about it," he replied, looking at the young man appreciatively. "That was a nice arm drag." "Thanks!" Kyle responded, a little bit loudly in his excitement. "You were AWESOME against Winters! And that thing you just did against Shaun, how you landed on your feet like that…wow!" "It took a lot of practice," he admitted, sheepishly. "And lots of times being hip tossed and landing flat on my back, too." Both young men turned to their instructor, knowing smirks on their faces. "Mr. Williams, you still think Joe’s gonna beat Seymour?" Ah, Seymour thought. That explained the older man’s hostility. Obviously the trainer was rooting for the Back Alley Brawler to be victorious. Almasy looked at the trainer carefully, and wondered if it was possible that he knew Joe Balboa. "Damn right I do," Carl replied, looking up at Seymour, seeing the slightly hurt look there. "Nothin’ against you," he told the Final Fantasy, "but Joe’s fightin’ for a cause." He shouldn’t have been insulted, but damned if Seymour didn’t feel a bit annoyed at that comment. "And I don’t," he replied, his own gaze starting to become heated as he looked back at the instructor. "Shit, man…I didn’t mean…meh, there’s no easy way to put it. Lemme ask you a question. Why do you wrestle?" "To be the best," was Seymour’s unflinching answer. "To win tournaments, titles, to prove myself every time I step in the ring." Carl nodded. "Yeah, that’s about what I thought. Want to know why I wrestled?" Seymour nodded. "I wrestled for some extra money in my pocket. I wrestled to stay off of these streets, cause I saw what happened to my friends who didn’t. And you know what? I was one of the lucky ones. I got a job. I made enough so that my broken-down ass don’t have to get in that ring no more. Joe’s not that lucky. You know what Joe is, kid?" Almasy shook his head no. The intense look on the older man’s face was beginning to unnerve him, and despite himself, he took a step back. "Joe Balboa’s a broken down forty-one year old man who, really, has no business being in a wrestling ring at this point in his life. But he’s got child support to pay, for a daughter that he loves. So in spite of the fact that he‘s a broken down sonuvabitch with a busted up knee, he steps his ass in the ring night in and night out and fights so he can support his daughter. And I’ll tell ya what else. He hates your fuckin’ guts." "How is that possible," Seymour replied, incredulously. "He doesn’t even know me!" "It don’t matta," came the reply. "You’re everything that he hates in this business. Anotha punk kid who managed to make good right away. I saw that look on your face when you took that fall. You ain’t never been in a ring like that one, have ya?" Seymour could do nothing but shake his head no, and look around at the squared circle he stood in. It was the same dimensions as the ones he stepped in for GCW and PTC, but far rattier. Far more worn. Far less visually appearing. "Of course not. You don’t know what it MEANS to work for $15 bucks a show! To drive eight hours round trip to make that fifteen bucks! To bust your ass for TWENTY-ONE FUCKIN’ YEARS BEFORE YOU GET YOUR BIG BREAK! TWENTY-ONE FUCKIN’ YEARS IN THIS SPORT BEFORE JOE MADE TV! And there are some who don’t get that far. Thirty years in a ring. THIRTY YEARS, AND I AIN’T NEVER WRESTLED IN FRONT OF MORE THAN FIFTY PEOPLE!" "Mr. Williams, calm down man," Shaun told his mentor, growing increasing unnerved at the passion Carl was displaying. "DAMNIT SHAUN, I’VE BEEN CALM FOR THIRTY YEARS! I’ve been on shows with Joe! I’ve seen him work, and he’s nothing fancy, nothing pretty, but he will BEAT YO’ ASS! And that’s what’s gonna happen in GTT! Sure, Seymour can wrestle. But Brawler’s gonna fight him. And Brawler’s gonna beat him, on the behalf of every SOB like me who never got his shot! On behalf of every kid from the Bronx who wants to get outta here but can’t do it!" Kyle and Shaun looked at one another, and then back to their mentor. They couldn’t believe he was acting this way. They’d never seen Carl so emotional, so seemingly enraged. Kyle looked then to the third man in the ring with them, who seemed just as dumbfounded "Seymour, I’m sorry," he said, eyes wide. "He’s usually not like thi--" "DON’T APOLOGIZE TO THAT PIECE OF CRAP! Goddamnit all, he’s never paid a due in his life! He don’t know what I’ve been through! What Joe’s been through!" Enough was enough. "I don’t know why you hate me," Seymour said. "I can’t control the fact that I got a break. But damnit, I bust my ass every time I step in the ring. I give it my all for the fans, and I give it my all for this sport. What right do you have to insult me just because you think my reasons for wrestling are less noble or important than yours?" Something broke in the older man then. He discarded his baseball cap to the ground, revealing closely-cropped gray hair. In spite of the pain, he rolled into the squared circle, rising to his full height to glower down at Almasy. "Shaun, Kyle, get out of the ring. I may be a broken-down fifty year old, but I’m gonna do the one thing I’ve wanted to do for thirty years. I’m gonna get me a piece of one of dem young punks that’s where I shoulda been…" Without warning, he charged, throwing a wild looping punch at Seymour. Almasy side-stepped, and the fifty year old somewhat laughably spilled to the ring floor, clutching at his leg in the process. "MAH KNEE! DAMNIT, MAH KNEE!" Seymour sighed, sadly, as he went out to the ring apron. It was sad what time could do to a person. It could make someone jaded, so very jaded as this man appeared to be. He jumped up to springboard off the top rope, only to find that the rope couldn’t hold his weight. It broke, and he spilled forward. With a demon’s gleam in his eye, the goldbricking Carl took advantage, pushing himself up to his feet and throwing a single right-handed uppercut. *CRACKWHAM!* The unmistakable, disgusting sound of fist meeting face rang throughout the room, as Seymour’s body changed directions in mid air, falling to land flat on its back by the ropes, knocked completely and utterly out with a single shot. Almasy bounced once, twice, before suddenly lying still, much to the delight of the man now rubbing his knuckles with smug satisfaction. "DAMN RIGHT! THAT’S WHAT YOU GET FOR MESSIN’ WIT ME!" Shaun and Kyle, however, were somewhat less pleased. "Carl, what the fuck was that faking shit?" Shaun asked, his usual tone of respect for his elder gone in his shock. "Lesson one of this business, Shaun," he said, calmly, scooping up the unconscious form of Seymour Almasy over his shoulder. "Neva undaestimate a man from the Bronx. Let’s get this piece of shit outta here." Calmly, indeed, with a grin on his face, Carl Williams walked for the rear exit of the gym, carrying an unconscious former PTC Global Champion on his shoulder. Shaun and Kyle, for their parts, merely shook their heads. Adults could be so difficult, sometimes… ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- "Ugggh…" Seymour’s eyes opened, slowly, and he blinked a few times to try and figure out where he was. Last he remembered, he was in a gym, in an impromptu match, and then… "Must have gotten knocked out. Wonderful." Yeah, the news of this was going to get out. Probably make the front page of the dirt sheets. He could see it now. "FORMER PTC GLOBAL CHAMPION KNOCKED OUT BY NO-NAME NEVER-WAS." "Fucking rope. If that damn thing wouldn’t have broken, I’d have handed that guy his rear end on a silver platter." Maybe, maybe not. But really, when it came right down to it, Seymour should have known better. They’d told him the ropes were crappy. Like a true idiot, Seymour had tried to prove that he was invincible, and he’d gotten his lights dimmed for it. One punch had taken him out. One right hand thrown with enough force to drop a wildebeest. Nothing flashy. Nothing elegant. It was the sort of finisher that fit its user. That fit a man like the Back Alley Brawler, who called it the One Hitter Quitter. Before, he’d underestimated the Brawler. Seen a man who had been on two or three pay-per-views in his entire career. Held only one title. Toiled in arenas Seymour knew he would retch at the sight of. But that was a simplistic view. It overlooked a lot of things. Like, for example, the fact that the Brawler was a 6’8", 330 pound mountain of angry black man. He didn’t take chances in the ring. He didn’t know what a wristlock was, and quite frankly, he didn’t give a fuck. Or the fact that Joe Balboa NEEDED this. Seymour was motivated, but the Brawler was MOTIVATED. When comparing cementing one’s legacy to making child support payments and providing for a family, there was no comparison. Seymour was fighting for immortality. Brawler was fighting for the right to continue existing. And with a whole community behind him, he would be an especially tough man to put down for three, with the whole world watching and so very much at stake. For now though, he couldn’t worry about the Back Alley Brawler and everything he brought to the table. He needed to get the fuck out of a, well, back alley in the middle of the Bronx, and he had no desire to wait for a bus or hire a taxi. Half-hoping it hadn’t fallen out over the course of the day’s ordeal, he reached into his pocket and pulled out his cell phone. "Tessa," he said, into the phone, the voice-recognition acknowledging the name and beginning to dial the number of Seymour’s new girlfriend and fellow GTT5 competitor, Tessa Windsor. *Riiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiing!* *Riiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiing!* *Riiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiing!* And then, answering machine. "Damn," Seymour said, "she must be out shopping and forgot her cell phone." From the tone of Seymour’s voice, this wasn’t a unique occurrence. Hey, the girl liked her shopping, nothing he could do about that. So, who else was there to call? Jabbing the red button with his pointer finger to disconnect the call, he raised the phone to his lips and said another name. "Cid." Ah yes, Cid Wilson, Seymour’s grandfather and friend. Ever since the Final Fantasy’s youth, Cid had always been there for Seymour with a warm meal, a kind word, and good advice. *Riiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiing!* *Riiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiing!* *Riiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiing!* Answering machine. "FUUUUUUUCK!" This time, the swear came from the depths of his soul. He was stuck here, in the middle of absolute nowhere, at least for as long as it would take to call a cab. And considering the hustle and bustle of the holiday season in New York, Yevon only knew if any were available. Groaning audibly, he began cycling through the memory banks of the phone, looking to see if he actually knew anyone else in this city who could help him. And then, there it was. The name that could help him. "I can’t do this," he said, weakly. He hadn’t spoken to her in a few months. But he had no other choice. "Aerith." *Riiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiing!* *Riiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiing!* *Riiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiing!* *Click!* "Hello, this is Laura Winters. May I ask who’s calling?" …fucking FIGURED she’d actually be there to pick up. (To be continued.)
