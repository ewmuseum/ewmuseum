Glimpse Of The Future?

1 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-01 He opened his eyes. The light from the blinds slanted across the room, throwing prison bar shadows across his face. For a moment he was content to do nothing. he was between sleep and wakefulness in that happy state where memory did not exist. But all at once he began to remember and the feeling of dread, which he had carried inside him like a poison fetus for the past three days, once again seized him, radiating outwards from his heart in an sickly wave. She was dead. He kicked the covers off his body and sat up on his elbows. The clock on the wall said quarter past ten. He had overslept. Overslept for what? he thought. Rachel was gone and there was really no reason for him to get up. Every weekday morning for the past twenty years he had rose at seven to make her a cup of tea and to cook her breakfast. She was (she'd been he corrected himself) a busy woman, selling advertising space for a radio station in the city. It was a job she'd hated, but like most people she'd long ago come to the conclusion that no-one had a job they loved. Tolerated, yes. Sometimes liked, maybe. But loved? Love was for chocolate boxes and racy romance novels. For your family and your wife. But never for your work – work was work. On weekends the pattern would be reversed and it was Rachel who would get up and cook him breakfast. He used to lie, savoring the luxury of having the bed to himself, and move his feet to her side. The bedclothes would still be warm and he would feel his body heat clinging to the sheets like a comforting memory. Now he was frightened to move. Rachel's side would be cold, and somehow, if he were to feel that cold, it would be the most terrible thing of all. The final, irrefutable proof of her absence. He sat up fully and swung his legs out of bed. Although it was winter outside, the room was warm, the central heating firing earlier that morning. The carpet felt good beneath his feet, almost sensuous, and he felt a momentary stab of shame that he could derive pleasure from material comfort. Surely he was a bad person to be thinking such thoughts? The temptation to crawl back into bed was almost overwhelming. Somehow it was easier to face things when he was cocooned under the covers. He could lie on his side and bring his knees up. Pull the sheets over his head and close his eyes. It was just like a womb he realized. A king-sized, orthopaedically sprung womb. He let out a sigh and got to his feet. Life goes on. It was almost a cliché - the brave husband, trying to keep himself busy, soldiering on – but it was really the only way to deal with things. He remembered his own mother after his father had died. A week after the funeral he had dropped in to visit and found her in the kitchen. The room was littered with baking trays and half-filled bowls. Wooden spoons stood upright in thick dollops of cake mix. Flour, sugar and the odd, solitary raisin sprinkled the worktops, while the greasy smear of butter glistened on the cupboard doors. He had stood there and watched her mother, sympathizing, thinking he knew what she was going through. Her mother was looking haggard and the muscles in her forearm stood out, knotted and stark as she gripped the spoon and mixed flour and an egg together. "Got to keep busy, luv," she'd said. "There's a Church fete on Saturday and the vicar's asked me to do some baking. Best to keep busy." And he nodded and offered to help. Thinking he understood, when he understood nothing. Well he understood now. It was keep busy or brood; devour herself in a grizzly act of auto-cannibalism. He put on his shirt on and shoes and went down the stairs. He went into the kitchen and switched on the blender. A cup of protein was what he needed – that uniquely musclehead solution to all life's problems. The kitchen was scrupulously clean. The worktops were gleaming, the floor polished and shining in the thin morning light. It was another indication of Rachel’s absence, he thought. When she'd been alive the kitchen had been the busiest room in the house. Rachel had liked to cook, although her culinary skills never went beyond pasta and the odd, greasy stir-fry, and she'd inevitably leave the room in disarray. Whenever she'd finished one of her ‘masterpieces' it would be he who would be left to clean up, a fact that had irked him at the time. He would give anything to have her with him now, making a mess for him to tidy. But the kitchen was neat, almost desolate and he suddenly felt lonely, a lone traveler in a Formica desert. He made his protein shake and took a sip. It was tasteless. He got some rags from beneath the sink and took the plastic bucket from the cupboard. He'd busy himself by cleaning the windows he decided. It was a job he'd always hated, but which he now looked forward to – a pathetic penance, atonement for still being alive. "Angel…" A soft voice whispered through his mind and he quickly whipped around, his hand shaking slightly from the startle. "Who’s there!? Rachel is that you?" He quivered out the name to feel a soft cold wind rush up his spine. "Baby, it’s okay…no need to worry." A faint mist appeared in front of Angel and molded into the form of his fiancée Rachel. CRASH~! Loosing the feeling throughout his body Angel dropped his cup and it shattered against the tile floor. Quickly the noise of the broken glass echoed throughout the darkened house and all became clearer and brighter. Snow glittered through the windows and the misty figure of Rachel became clear. "Baby, what’s the matter?" Rachel questioned. "Huh…what!?" Angel shook his head and quickly glanced around the room. "I think you were sleep walking again sweety. Come on let’s head back to bed, you don’t have that long till your flight." "Flight?" Angel questioned once more. "For the GTT5, ya know? What’s wrong hun, you look like you seen a ghost or somethin." "Um…nothing I just can’t sleep. I think I am gonna hit the gym for a little. Try and calm myself. Rubbing her head and smiling Rachel replied, "Okay sweety, don’t be too long." … .. . "What the heck was that all about?" Angel shook his head while exiting the house. ~Fade To Black~
