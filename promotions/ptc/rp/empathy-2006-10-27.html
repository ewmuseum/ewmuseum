Empathy

27 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-27 * * * * * Reggie Hanes, now out of his police uniform and dressed in jeans and a flannel shirt, slowly pulled up to the luxurious mansion that belonged to Clinton Sage. Earlier in the morning, he had apparently witnessed a miracle that defied any description or rationale he could muster. Curiosity had been constantly jabbing at him all morning. Clinton Sage had severely burned his hands, and was treated for those burns by a medical staff. Then, minutes later, the flesh was back to normal as if it had never been touched. Being the only person to witness this miracle – it almost troubled Reggie to actually label it as such – he felt compelled to find out exactly what had happened, to try and come up with an explanation that would stop his mind from racing around the impossible in endless loops. Sitting in his beat up Honda Civic, hours after Clinton’s burnt skin repaired itself, Officer Reggie Hanes stared in disgust at the mansion itself. 13 bedrooms of luxury and arrogance, built upon a foundation of greed. Two years ago, the land had been a small neighborhood of middle class families – seven or eight, he couldn’t remember exactly – until Clinton Sage had arrived. He purchased the houses and land for upwards of triple the land value, and razed the happy homes to the ground once the families had accepted his money. Over the course of three months, the mansion was brought in via a fleet of massive helicopters. Purchased as a state of the art sectional mansion, it was put together much like the building blocks kids play with during childhood, fitting together piece by piece. The surrounding community had whined and complained, but by the time the mansion stood complete, alone amongst 15 acres of trees and grasslands, Clinton Sage had built his home and there was nothing more they could do about it. A happy neighborhood had become a tyrant’s own personal playground. Finally killing the engine of the Honda, Reggie stepped out of the car and into the arms of the chilly, autumn breeze. The trees that lined the driveway danced and whistled in the gusts, and the fallen leaves would occasionally dance across the lawn in funnels of crimson, orange, and brown. The driveway circled around a small garden, at its center an American flag with tattered and frayed edges flapping in the wind. Even though he was clearly out of shape and had plenty of his own fast-food induced insulation, the cold air stung Reggie’s face and hands. As quickly as his well-rounded frame would allow, he ascended the three steps leading up to the front door of the Sage mansion, and rang the doorbell. He buried his hands into his jacket pockets, and waited. Between the gusts of wind, Reggie could hear the far off chuffing of horses, which were housed on a few acres of the property behind the mansion. Clinton Sage had a passion for breeding thoroughbreds, specifically black stallions. He owned three of the prized beauties, along with an albino stallion as well. A minute passed by with no response, and Reggie rang the doorbell once more. He knew Clinton had to be home, especially after the events that occurred earlier in the morning. The embers in the so-called shrine were probably still cooling. While he waited, Reggie couldn’t help but think to himself. Why was he here, freezing his ass off? This was stupid. But he just had to know what happened that morning. How that happened. Not a man of any strong religious conviction, the sudden healing of Clinton’s hands had perplexed more than enlightened to the true presence of God. He could just accept it as a miracle, get back into his Honda, and try to get the bills paid. Stepping down off of the front porch, Reggie looked in the direction of the shrine. He couldn’t see it from his vantage point, but it was only around the far corner of the mansion. Once again digging his hands into his pockets, pushing his head down and shoulders up to keep the chilly air out, he made his way towards the tattered and destroyed shrine, hoping to find Clinton Sage. Rounding the corner, he could spot the shards of glass from the broken window that had been blown out upon impact. The gusting wind would occasionally waft the scent of burnt wood in his direction. Passing by the various rooms of the mansion, Reggie looked into each one hoping to see Clinton, but to no avail. The asshole didn’t answer the doorbell, and didn’t appear to be home. Reggie figured to himself that it wouldn’t be any trouble if a police officer, even though he was off duty, snooped around the scene. Just to get some final details. Finally arriving at the scorched section of the mansion, the walls blackened and the aroma became pungent. The scent of smoldering wood and singed metal overwhelmed the fresh autumn air. The massive window had been cleared of the shards that were still hanging after the impact. They crunched and popped under his boots and heavyset frame. Peeking in through the window, Reggie Hanes was shocked to see Clinton Sage sitting perfectly still, staring out through the blown-out window, past Reggie himself, in a trance-like state. Clinton had pulled a leather chair from the nearby study into the eviscerated shrine, and was sitting comfortably with his chin resting atop clasped hands. Sitting on the blackened floorboards between Clinton’s feet was the meteorite. On the floor beside the space rock was a hammer, a kitchen knife, and a lot of blood. * * * * * Upon opening his eyes for the first time and staring at Isis, Nathan Hollinger had thought it was all a horrible dream, a nightmare. As quickly as that thought came and went, the dull and throbbing pain in his hands raced it’s way up his arms and across his shoulders, up the back of his neck and right into his brain. This was real, not a dream, but still a nightmare. He winced, and looked around the hospital room. Sunlight poured in through the Venetian blinds, and beyond them, life went on like normal in Denver, Colorado. Sitting beside him on the bed, still in her bloodstained wedding dress, was Isis. Her blue eyes were surrounded by pink, and it was obvious she had been crying. "This is the second time in my life where I’ve opened my eyes, and my hands have been covered in blood," Nathan said, pausing for a moment to analyze the situation and try to come up with a reason for what had happened after they exchanged vows, "And both times I’m left wondering what happened, and how did it happen?" First on an airplane, now on his wedding day. Isis ran a palm down the side of his face, her lips pursed together trying to keep the situation in check by fighting back the sobs, "Don’t worry about that now, sweetheart. What is important is that you’re alive. Let the doctors try to figure it out." He sighed. She matched it, and they sat in silence as the machines monitoring his health whirred and beeped. Nathan Hollinger bit his lip and shook his head in disbelief. Part of him had no desire to look down at his hands and see the tattered, shredded, and burnt skin. But an even greater part of him wanted to see it, just to reassure himself that it happened. Nathan looked down, and saw bandages. White gauze wrapped each finger and palm, with some orange discoloration bleeding through. Between the layers, there were wooden splints. He was unable to move any of his fingers, as they were restrained by the splints and medical tape. As he inspected the job the doctors and nurses had done bandaging him up, Isis took notice and informed him of what had happened, "You’re missing chunks of skin down to the bone, and right now they’re keeping you from moving your hands because of the skin grafts that will be put into place. I really hope you don’t like your left thigh, because that’s where they’re going to be taking the skin from." Nathan didn’t say anything, instead he frowned. "I know, I envisioned our honeymoon to be in the Caribbean, not in the Intensive Care Unit." The corner of Nathan’s mouth curled into a smile, but he could only muster barely a grin. It would take time for him to smile again, because the weight of the situation seemed to crush any possible enjoyment he could think of. His wedding day, ruined by these impossible and mysterious wounds. His wife, covered in his own blood and left screaming at the alter, on what should have been the happiest day of her life. Their lives.. It was supposed to be the second chance at a normal life for two people who had been through so much drama and disdain from the rest of the world, and yet, not even God could grant them that simple gift. Nathan tilted his head back against the pillow and closed his eyes, thinking of days that should have been, "I’m sorry." "Don’t be, this isn’t your fault." "I’m not apologizing for what happened, but for what we won’t be able to get to. Where there was supposed to be banana drinks and umbrellas, there will be bandages and IV-drips. White sand and bikini’s are now white snow and hospital gowns." Nathan opened his eyes and turned his attention towards Isis, who sat there with her chin resting against the railing along the edge of the bed. She smiled, "I can sneak my bikini in here, I think." "I love you, babe. I’m really sorry." He could feel the pressing nature of the situation. Sorrow and disappointment overwhelmed the pain in his hands, or at least he thought it did, it might have been the drugs, but Nathan Hollinger couldn’t help the feeling that he was responsible. As they sat there in silence, he couldn’t help but feeling something else as well. It was a mysterious feeling, something that he shouldn’t even be remotely considering because of the situation. Nestled deep, buried underneath the feelings of remorse and agony, there was a seed of something else slowly growing, and it scared Nathan because there was no reason for him to be experiencing this particular emotion. Ecstasy. * * * * * Flakes of ash fluttered through the air like snow, and Reggie Hanes stood for a moment and waited for Clinton Sage to acknowledge his presence. He quickly glanced over Clinton for the source of the blood, but there were no wounds that he could see. Although there seemed to be a small smear of blood, possibly an older stain, on the left leg of Sage’s pants. The blood must have been from the wounds on his hands, before they healed. Although, even from the distance between them, no more than 20 feet, it looked like the tip of the kitchen knife had blood on it, but it could have just been a reflection. "Mr. Sage?" Reggie finally yelled in through the broken window, hoping to rouse Clinton from his trance. Clinton slowly raised his head and looked towards Reggie, who felt unnerved by the odd stare the man gave him. It seemed inhuman and cold, utterly soulless. On a normal day, staring into the eyes of Clinton Sage was enough to make any man reconsider his own definition of cruel, but today was particularly different. Sage appeared lost in thought, even as he motioned for Reggie to climb through the broken window and back into the flame-ravaged shrine. As he carefully climbed over the broken glass and hopped down onto the gritty floor, Clinton Sage stared down at the meteorite with awestruck eyes. Making his way closer, Reggie noticed there were indeed a few drops of blood on the sharpened edge of the kitchen knife, but not enough to cause any sort of alarm. The blood that was on the floor seemed darker than usual, although Reggie just assumed it was from the discolored floor beneath the small pool. It was congealed and perhaps two hours old. The hammer had no signs of malicious use. The meteorite, black and glassy, glimmered in the sunlight that poured in through the gaping hole in the roof. It appeared to Reggie as if Clinton had tried to smash the meteorite with the hammer, after perhaps digging at it with the knife for a little while. The rock showed no signs of abuse. No pebbles, no dust, and no physical markings on its smooth surface. Clinton seemed to snap out of his reverie, and stood up from the chair. He looked down at Reggie with those cold, black eyes in a stare that matched any chill Reggie would ever get from a blustery winter night. Clinton Sage finally smiled, and took a step over the meteorite, towards the him, "What seems to be the trouble, Officer?" "I was just, uh, following up on what happened this morning." Reggie said, almost ashamed at how stupid he sounded. Clinton seemed to mock that Reggie wasn’t in uniform, but rather in regular street clothes. "Nothing happened this morning, Officer Hanes." Reggie Hanes chuckled in disbelief, "Are you crazy? What happened was a miracle." Clinton folded his arms behind his back and didn’t immediately respond. Standing almost a foot taller than Reggie, Clinton Sage was an intimidating physical presence in any part of the world. Standing 6’6", weighing 250lbs, maybe more. Where Reggie was short and stubby, Clinton was tall and chiseled. A strong, sharp brow often cloaked the darkest eyes that Reggie had ever seen on a human being in a constant shadow. Slightly sunken cheeks hung above a strong and angular jaw, and when coupled with the dark-brown-possibly-black eyes, gave Clinton Sage a resemblance of a reptilian predator. Even though Reggie had been a police officer for a decade, the silence unnerved him. The wind circling through the room certainly provided a chill whenever Clinton’s personality lacked it. "No miracles ever occur in my life, Officer Hanes. Do miracles occur in your life?" Clinton asked, studying Reggie with those oily, black eyes. He continued, "From what I see, it looks like you work too hard and eat too much. If you managed to walk out of here and lose five pounds in the next week, I’d consider that more of a miracle than what happened this morning." Reggie knew he was off-duty, and just took the insult in stride. There was more truth in it than he’d like to admit. "What happened this morning was only the beginning, Officer Hanes. I’ve learned quite a bit since I kicked you all out of here a few hours ago. And let me tell you, it’s quite exciting." "The beginning of what?" Reggie wondered, aloud. High overhead, a large object passed between the sun and the mansion, sending the blackened room into a momentary darkness. Reggie looked up and saw nothing, having just missed whatever passed overhead. Probably a jetliner, high above, or a bird circling around the wound in the Sage mansion. "Before you learn anything, Officer Hanes, there is something I want to learn from you. How much is it going to cost me to buy your services?" "Buy my services? If you’re looking for a maid, then you’ve come–" Reggie stopped when Clinton held up an open hand, then waving it side to side. Clinton stared, unblinking, "Don’t bullshit with me. Like I said earlier, you aren’t exactly the poster boy for the police. So how much is it going to cost me to retain your services whenever I need them?" "I’m not for sale." Clinton Sage laughed, starting to circle around Reggie. With each step, he could hear the crumpling of the burnt photos that had once filled the room. Wherever the wrestler Spartan was, he was now being walked all over by Clinton Sage, perhaps for the first time, although unbeknownst to him. "Like one of my wrestling counterparts used to say, everybody’s got a price. Name it and it’s yours, and I’ll tell you everything. I can see it written all over your chubby little face, Officer, that you’re more growing more and more intrigued. I can tell by the shitty car you drove here in that you’re in need of money. I guess the police department doesn’t exactly pay handsomely, now does it? So…name your price." Reggie felt disgusted at the offer, "I’m not for sale. I should arrest you on general principal." Clinton leaned in, hovering over Reggie’s left shoulder and whispered into his ear. Even though Reggie couldn’t see the smile across Clinton Sage’s face as he spoke, he could feel it, "Then why did you really come here, Officer Hanes? Just to trespass on private property?" Clinton circled around to the front of Reggie, knelt down, and picked up the hammer. He turned it in his hands, and then in one sudden and quick motion, slammed the hammer down atop the meteorite with all the force that his massive body could muster. Not a pebble was jarred loose. Clinton stood once again, and then turned towards Reggie and held the hammer out for him to grab, "Ok, now it’s your turn. Except I don’t want you to hit the meteorite, I want you to smash me across the kneecap." "You want me to what?" Reggie gasped in disbelief. "Take the fucking hammer, and smash my knee with it. I know you want to, I can see the envy towards me in your eyes, you little shit. SO FUCKING HIT ME!" Clinton screamed down towards Reggie, thrusting the hammer into his hand. Reggie Hanes stood there, eyes wide and amazed, but silent. "Late car payments? Can’t get the mortgage paid on time?" Clinton fired accusations towards Reggie, with a sharp jab of the finger into his sternum accompanying each one, "Child support? College loans getting to be too much? Can’t pay up a bookie? Come on Reggie, I know there’s something you need money for! Want to put a child through college?" Reggie flinched, only slightly, but it was enough for the wrestler to notice. Clinton’s shark-like black eyes narrowed, and he smelled the blood in the water, "Oh, so that’s it. Have a little bitch of a daughter that you know you can’t send to a college that she deserves? SMASH MY KNEE AND TAKE THE MONEY, OFFICER HANES! Go ahead and do it!" Reggie gripped the handle tightly, but held back. This was ridiculous. Absolutely ridiculous! "I’m right, aren’t I? It’s a daughter. Is she as fat as you are? I hear they don’t allow fat kids into Harvard, Officer. The community colleges around here have been known for rapes, though, so I’m sure that she’ll be fine at one of those. Although we are in rural farm country, and they’ll probably mistake her for a farm animal and–" Reggie Hanes had been pushed too far. He dropped to his knees and brought the hammer down across the right kneecap of Clinton Sage with all the force and energy his 240lb body could muster. He could feel the kneecap shatter into as many pieces as the broken window that littered the lawn outside of the disturbing shrine. Clinton Sage screamed out with a mix of agony and laughter, falling backwards into the leather chair as he clutched his mangled knee. * * * * * Isis Maguire left this world kissing her husband with closed eyes. When she opened them, her cheeks were covered in his blood and her name had become Isis Hollinger. Since the moment he fell to the ground screaming at the alter, and during the entire ambulance ride from the church to the hospital, she had never left his side. Years ago, when the world accused him of murder, nobody stayed with him. Nathan had no idea who he was, or what he had been in the years beforehand, and she was deathly afraid that history was repeating itself. Amnesia had set in once, and she prayed it didn’t come around again to claim the memories they enjoyed together. Once more, he was at the center of something mysterious, and she couldn’t bear to leave him. She loved him with all her heart, and was afraid of losing what they had created over the last year. She had finally found someone that looked past her battles with alcoholism and immaturity, and into her heart. Their troubled and tattered threads of life were to be sewn together into something new, and beautiful. It wasn’t supposed to start with blood and screaming. But through the last few hours, amid the strange wounds appearing on his hands and emergency surgery that followed, Isis Hollinger found some strength and resolve that she hadn’t been aware of. Perhaps it was the love for Nathan that became the foundation upon which this strength was erected, or maybe it was simply such an emotional scare that she had finally wandered deep enough inside of herself to locate it. They were going to get through this. She would give anything, or do anything, for Nathan. And Isis was fully confident and aware that Nathan would do the same for her, no matter the cost. Seeing him lying in the hospital, bandaged up and sedated, nearly ripped her to pieces. Had it been months earlier, when she lacked the emotional strength that Nathan had opened up inside of her, she would have turned towards the bottle and drowned away her miseries with shot after shot of Vodka. Turn life’s horrible moments into a blurred memory, or perhaps drink enough to wash them completely away. But not any more. Nathan Hollinger had given her strength and love. Isis smiled at the thought. She leaned in and brushed his brown hair back from his forehead, and whispered into his ear, "I love you." He opened his eyes and turned his head towards her, trying to manage a smile, but she could tell that there weren’t going to be any smiles on his face anytime soon, "There is something I need to tell you." His need for a declaration startled her slightly, but she nodded and waited for what he had to say. Isis watched as his light brown eyes darted around the room, seemingly searching for words to describe what he needed to convey. She wondered if he had started to feel the dozen or so small cuts on his left leg that had appeared out of nowhere, just like the wounds on his palms. "I feel weird." "You should, you’re on drugs." He didn’t chuckle, "No, not the drugs. I mean…I’m sitting here, completely destroyed by the idea that our wedding day is ruined. Our new life together…postponed and starting off on the wrong foot. The grief is just unbearable, absolutely unbearable. But there is something else, and it’s troubling." Isis noticed that he couldn’t match her stare. During their previous year getting to know each other, Nathan never had trouble looking her in the eyes and telling her something. A knot formed in her stomach and started to tighten as he continued. "I feel emotions that I shouldn’t be feeling. Like right now, for some reason or another, I feel this urge and overwhelming glee of manipulation. I almost want to burst out in laughter at the notion of taking advantage of someone else’s misfortune. It makes absolutely no sense, because considering our situation, I shouldn’t be feeling anything but grief and sorrow. Our day was ruined…yet I’m almost ecstatic at the notion that I’m somehow manipulating someone." The knot grew tighter, almost suffocating her when she saw a small smile flicker across his face for a moment. His stare seemed to grow hollow and cold. When he finally turned and looked at her in the eyes, she could see nothing but a growing fear in his eyes. And nothing ever scared Nathan, it was one of his strongest traits. "This makes absolutely no sense. First the hands, and then these feelings that I shouldn’t be having. And it’s not stopping, either. I can feel anger starting to creep in. It feels like I need to have some sort of emotional release, but for what? I just feel like screaming at the top of my lungs and verbally berating someone until they break down and cry. But the shame isn’t there, only pure enjoyment." Isis was stunned, and didn’t know what to say. He wondered, "What’s happening to me?" "I think it might be the drugs confusing you, or maybe screwing with your system. You’ve been through so much trauma today…there could be an explanation for all of thi–" Suddenly Nathan rocketed up into a sitting position and screamed at the top of his lungs. Even with his bandaged hands, he clawed and prodded at his right knee. After letting out the blood-chilling scream, Nathan clamped his jaw shut with such force that one of his front teeth shattered, falling onto his lap. Blood trickled out of the corner of his mouth, perhaps from biting down on his tongue. Nathan’s eyes rolled back into his head, and he convulsed in pain for a few seconds before passing out. The machines that monitored his heart rate started to beep furiously, and only a few moments later a fleet of nurses and doctors came rushing into the hospital room. Isis Hollinger had screamed at the church, but what just occurred shocked her into silence. First his hands, then cuts on the leg, and now what appeared to be an injury to his knee. * * * * * Clinton Sage watched as Officer Reggie Hanes sped away from his mansion, following the winding driveway all the way to the edge of his property. The car barely managed to keep traction on the road, sometimes coming dangerously close to the grassy edge of the driveway, and the row of trees that lined each side. Clinton loved the fact that Officer Hanes had been so startled and surprised – not only by the unexplainable healing of his hands this morning, but also by the outburst of rage directed towards Clinton in the form of a shattered kneecap – that Hanes simply stood above a agonized Clinton for a minute, staring down as the pieces of Clinton’s knee reformed themselves before his very eyes. Shock and disbelief had overwhelmed the portly officer, and he bolted for the exit. Clinton knew that Hanes had come to his mansion looking for answers, but wasn’t going to find any. The best way, in Clinton’s mind, to deal with that problem was to simply offer the policeman a bribe. Although Hanes hadn’t taken him up on his offer, the seed had been planted. The little shit obviously had a daughter he loved so dearly that he would be willing to destroy his own moral foundation in an attempt to keep hers on the right track. Clinton needed the police officer, although he wasn’t going to let Hanes know it. If he were to find the bastard who was hidden under the mask of Spartan, it would involve doing some acts that either bent the law, or broke it entirely. The amazement and horror in the eyes of Reggie Hanes gave a small amount of resolve to Clinton. The cop would come around again, and when he did, he would have a price. And then, finally, Clinton Sage would end this yearlong misery. Clinton turned from the driveway, reentered the house, and made his way across the vast mansion towards the shrine, and the meteorite. How could something as small as a basketball give so much power? As he stormed down through the hallway, towards the shrine, he knew there was undoubtedly some connection between the rock and the ability to sustain life-altering injuries and heal almost immediately. Almost rounding the turn, a sudden and extremely strong gust of wind ripped it’s way through the mansion. One of the few pictures that managed to survive the impact of the meteorite fell off the wall, and when Clinton turned the corner towards the shrine, he saw three men standing around the meteorite, talking to each other. "Hey, get the fuck away from that!" Clinton yelled at the top of his lungs as he started to run towards the shrine. Someone would never know that only 15 minutes prior, his kneecap was in shambles. As Clinton crossed the threshold into the shrine, a man clad in a black suit stopped him in his tracks. The strong gust of wind ended up not being wind at all, but the propwash from a helicopter that was sitting in his backyard, the rotors still humming along at a high-speed. Clinton was amazed that engine noise was nearly silent, nor did the rotors make any of the usual chopping sounds as they cut through the air. Two other men in identical black suits picked up the meteorite, and began carrying it towards the helicopter. The man who stopped Clinton held out an identification card that read "FBI" and leaned in close, yelling into Clinton’s ear over the roaring winds, "Mr. Sage, I think you and I need to have a little talk." The agent held the hammer and bloody knife in his free hand, directing Clinton Sage towards the helicopter with the other. "I heard you experienced a little miracle this morning. I think I can provide you with a few answers, and a warning." * * * * *
