Insanity Prelude: Time of Troubles Ay, iyaiyai, Ay, iyaiyai A-a-a iyaiyai, Where's my samurai I've been searching for a man All across Japan Just to find, to find my samurai Someone who is strong But still a little shy Yes I need, I need my samurai Thursday, September 21st 6:00 PM McFarlane Estate In his basement room at the McFarlane Estate, Seymour Almasy was preparing himself for the trials to come in his own, admittedly unique, way. Running could be so boring, after all. At times, other cardiovascular exercises could be employed. Enter Dance Dance Revolution. Yup, Seymour’s got a pad and everything. “Ay, ay, ay, I'm your little butterfly,” the Final Fantasy sang, aloud. ‘Butterfly’ tended to be one of those songs he couldn’t do in public anymore, as he tended to sing along to it, and generally looked like an idiot while he did so. “Green, black and blue make the colors in the sky…” Feet flew across the dance pad with practiced grace. By now, he could do this particular song in his sleep. A good thing, really, as a few hours of DDRing had left the Final Fantasy someone tired. He executed the final few steps, and then hopped over to the PS2, turning the game off before even seeing his score. The score didn’t matter. The workout did. Wiping a few strands of sweaty hair from his face, Seymour plopped down in his leather computer chair. His heart still pounded in his chest as his came down from the adrenaline high of exertion, hand working its way to the mouth to commence his usual post-workout ritual: surfing the internet for Final Fantasy hentai. As he went to click on the bookmark reading “HOT XXX TOON ACTION”, though, he accidentally clicked on the one above it, reading “Wrestling Tattler - The Latest Dirt From The Sport Of Kings”. Almasy frowned momentarily, but then shrugged. “Couldn’t hurt,” he reasoned, “to check out what’s going on. God knows the wrestling world’s a fucked up place.” The page loaded, and Seymour expected to see a story about Karina Wolfenden taking a lover in Zurich. Or maybe another rumor as to his own homosexuality, confirmed by some random fan girl whose account of Seymour’s supposed tryst was essentially fan fiction. What he did NOT expect to see, however, was a grainy picture that looked like it had been taken by a camera phone. What he did NOT expect to see was his sworn enemy, Brandon Youngblood, and his leader, Shadow, at a table somewhere, drinking and chatting together. Seymour’s mouth, already dry from his workout, seemed practically barren as it hung wide open, frozen in astonished shock. Frightened eyes scanned the news story below it, giving the eyewitness account of what the two competitors had spoken of. At first, he laughed; a low laugh in the back of his throat at how absurd this was. But laughter gave way to nervousness. Nervousness begat fear. And fear begat pacing, as Seymour rose from his chair, wondering what the Hell it all meant. “The FUCK,” he all but screamed, before clamping his hands over his mouth. He was in the middle of what could now very easily be hostile territory. He looked back over his shoulder, to the door, to see if anyone was coming. No one was. “Least this explains why that fuck came for me in the Gauntlet…” In truth, he’d half-expected Youngblood there. Brandon seemed so intent on ruining his life in retaliation for what Seymour considered strictly business, after all. What he HADN’T expected was the arrival of the man known as Nephilim. Under the outfit, Seymour knew, was G. Ian Childress III, one of the big wigs in GCW. Why a man as important to the company as Childress was would risk his physical well-being in the ring was a question Seymour couldn’t answer. One of seemingly dozens of questions that were now playing havoc on his paranoid mind. “Is this it,” he moaned, suddenly, slumping back into the chair from his pacing fit. “Is the hammer going to drop?” He had, in truth, always been weary of the group. Vivica hated him due to his often pointed comments towards the man she formerly loved. Virgil hated him, quite legitimately, for Almasy nearly killing him with fire in Global Wrestling. Dawn hated him for a variety of legitimate reasons. Only Shadow really tolerated him…but that was what had mattered, especially as she had seized greater control of the Dies Irae power structure. Lia Radilu Vlaszy’s word was law for the Day of Wrath, and it had been good enough to keep her experiment safe. But now… “This some sort of test? There’s no way she’d want me to know she met with Brandon…but if she wanted me gone, she wouldn’t even need to leave the place she lives. She could get Virgil and Dawn, and…” Seymour shuddered violently at the thought. Just how much had he irritated the vampire? Living with them as he did, Seymour was more acutely aware than anyone that Shadow’s fangs were no parlor trick, that her life was no lie. After all, she’d told him the stories. Made him shiver with her accounts of killing those sent to kill her, easy as pie. “Stupid humans,” she’d called them, and he’d always wanted to object, because, by Yevon, he WAS a human. “They’re always involved in something,” he mumbled to himself, words tripping over each other. “Their careers are tied into who they are and what they do. I wrestle and come home. My job’s over. Their jobs are never over…” They talked to one another in whispers, and left in the middle of the night. He heard them come back in at all hours, feet causing the floorboards above his head to creak. Seymour never came back to the Estate with unexplained injuries. He never came back with blood dripping from his lips, a pleased smile on his satiated face. Fuck, even Vivica was involved in their fucked up shit. But he wasn’t. He stayed in the basement and played video games while they were out doing secret important supernatural things. “This is not fucking good,” Seymour whispered, in what could be the understatement of the evening. “I’m stuck here underground. If they decide they want to get rid of me right now, they could come down here and do it in a heartbeat. Fuck that. I’ve got to get out of here!” Almost immediately, he rose from the chair, trying to ascertain just how much stuff he could get out of his room. He doubted he could pack the entire room up, as not only would it be difficult, but it would be suspicious as well. Sadly, he realized that the game systems would likely have to stay. He plucked the memory cards from them anyway. Being in possession of a rather sizeable sum of money, he could always buy new systems and games if he needed to, but his save files were priceless. Jamming them into his pockets, he wheeled around to grab his duffel bag. This he threw on the bed, and grabbed a suitcase. “Still mostly clean,” he mused, picking through the various outfits he took on the road with him. Every so often, he dumped a worn one, before replacing it with clean clothes from the nearby drawer. Never before had his heart pounded when putting clothes in a suitcase. But never before had he been on such an urgent timetable when doing so. Seymour had a tendency to over dramatize the worst possible scenario, and currently he was half-convinced that he was going to become vampire chow at any moment. No sooner was the suitcase slammed and zipped shut then Seymour had fished his cell phone out of his pocket, and made a quick call to his favorite taxicab company. “I need a cab to take me from the McFarlane Estate to the airport immediatel--oh, don’t be afraid. It’s not THAT scary of a place, is it….? Okay, okay, what if I pay you double? Excellent. How long? Yeah, ten minutes is fine. See you then.” Closing the phone, and jamming it back in his pocket, Seymour sighed. “Geez, everyone’s so afraid of this place. You’d think a vampire lived here or something…” He chuckled nervously at his own poor joke. The McFarlane Estate’s location was known to most in Toronto, but most kept their distance. Rumors surrounded Dawn McFarlane’s home for good reason, considering who lived in it. Well, then. It was settled. Seymour was going to get out while he still could. As he sat on his bed, getting ready to leave behind his computer, Xbox 360, PS2, and GameCube, the fear that he felt was joined by undeniable anger. “The fuck did I ever do to them,” Seymour wondered aloud. “Since I joined, I’ve done what they told me to. Youngblood damn near killed me for it a few weeks ago, but I’m still here, still standing. She’s mad at me for joining Infinite Gauntlet? When the fuck did she become my mother? Who says I have to tell her shit?” Ah, another fun part of the Wrestling Tattler piece. She seemed to be mad at him for his excursions outside GCW. GTT6, for one. IG, for another. “Besides, I’m Seymour. I ALWAYS do this shit. Not like it surprised anyone else. I’m sure Vivica didn’t run up to her like a five year old and ask if she could compete for the IG title. Why am *I* the only bitch around here?” In some ways, it was a simple answer. He was too concerned with other matters, in Shadow’s mind. Not concerned enough with Youngblood. Too focused on himself. “Damn good thing I DO look out for myself,” he justified, smiling wryly, “not like they do. Dawn gets another shot at the title after she loses to Knight, but Lia gets pissed when I declare that I want to take the title back for us? Shit, maybe I should start sleeping with her too. Apparently, it comes with fringe benefits…fuck.” Once again, his eyes glance back at the door to make sure no one is coming. Tiptoeing over to it, he opened the door, and looked out, straight ahead and to both sides. No one. With a sigh of relief, he closed the door. It’s one thing to trash-talk a Brandon Youngblood. Youngblood did very many nasty things to Seymour, including smash bottles of perfectly good Bacardi over his head. Violent as he could be, though, Brandon Youngblood couldn’t drain Seymour of all life force by biting his neck. Couldn’t make him enjoy his own demise. The more he thought about it, the more insidious vampires truly were. “And to think,” he managed, weakly, “I used to wish they were real. Vampires. Fairies. The whole nine yards. Turns out they are…and it fucking blows. Why can‘t this shit happen to me three years ago, when I‘m practically begging for evil to vanquish? Why does it happen now when I don‘t give a crap about good or evil? Why does it happen now when all I want to do is become Global Champion and win GTT6?” *BEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEP* The sound of the horn nearly made the half-terrified Final Fantasy soil himself, before he realized what it probably meant. “….cabbie’s here,” came from his mouth in a small, meek voice. Throwing the duffel bag over one shoulder, and dragging his suitcase behind him, he darted out into the basement as quickly as possible. Thank God, as far as he was concerned, that the basement had a way into the garage. Emerging in it, and opening the door, he walked out into the brisk Toronto air. Night had fallen, and Seymour had to admit that the Estate was rather pretty at night. Darkly beautiful…a phrase which could describe the women he lived with. “No more of that,” he told himself, shaking his head as he walked over to the yellow cab. “Can’t care how pretty they are. Especially not when they’re after you…” Such thoughts were enough to sober him. After all, he knew, knew in the pit of his soul, that if he had Shadow defeated, Dawn would think nothing of costing him his victory. And that was even if they WERE on semi-friendly terms. She’d probably try to snap his neck into several pieces under the current situation. “NEVER honk like that again!” a half-enraged, half-terrified Seymour screamed at the poor cabbie. “They could hear you!” “The--,” the cabbie asked, before cutting himself off. Really, he didn’t want to know any more than the rumors said. “To the airport, right?” “Yeah,” Seymour said, throwing his baggage in the bag, before following it in. “What terminal,” the cabbie asked, and the desperation of Seymour’s situation kicked in for the Final Fantasy. He didn’t KNOW what terminal, or what airline, or even where he was going. He figured he could work that out when he got there, and more importantly, that it didn’t matter. Anywhere was safer than the basement of Shadow’s lair. “We’ll cross that bridge when we come to it,” Seymour said, a wry smile on his face as he closed the door. “For now, though, let’s get the Hell outta here.” ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- I’ve fallen asleep. I can tell this because I’m not in the backseat of the cab anymore. I’m on a beach, a nice, sandy beach. Just off the sands is the house that I shared with Laura. I am here because my subconscious has found a new way to torture me. She stands before me as if alive, holding me in her arms as I sob. As I apologize to her for everything, again and again. Just as I always do every time I have this dream. Just as I always will. And, just as she always does, she brushes my hair from my eyes. “You have nothing to apologize for,” I hear her say, her hand lovingly patting my head, as I only sob more. I miss this more than anything. More than being a champion. Far more than winning tournaments. More than my grandfather even, God bless his soul. “I want to see you again,” I say, in between sobs at what I’ve become, sobs at the person I’ve turned out to be. Only here do I seem to realize how vain I am. How angry. How much hate I truly harbor. Only here do I feel like a shell of a man. But Laura cares not. She simply holds me closer. The less I feel I deserve, the more caring she becomes. Laura nods at my statement, and whispers the same four words in my ear that always send my heart racing and crashing simultaneously. “But I’m right here…” “It’s not the same,” I respond, sadly, “and you know it. This is a dream. A fabrication…” She has the audacity to laugh, as she continues to run a hand through my hair. “You know it’s more than just a dream, Seymour,” she tells me, and I’m all too aware of that fact. I feel everything too clearly. When I wake up, I remember every last detail of our conversations. “Big match coming up, huh?” Her words snap me from my thoughts; make me look into her beautiful, beautiful eyes. “Yeah,” I say, clearly not enthusiastically. This only makes her chuckle. “Mr. Titlewhore doesn’t want another belt,” she teases, and I shake my head. “It’s not that. It’s everything surrounding it.” Thankfully, she presses me no further. She simply holds me there, in an imaginary moment I wish could be eternal. Here, there is no strife. No pain. No misery. There is but Laura, myself, and bliss. In spite of myself, I press her again. “When will I see you again,” I ask, desperate for an answer other than the usual, desperate for something, anything to change. “Soon,” she says, the single word damning me to return to the conscious world. ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ 9:00 PM Pearson International Airport Standing in Toronto's huge airport, Seymour couldn’t help but feel slightly fortunate. “I can go anywhere I want,” he said, in near child-like awe. If he wanted to take the 9:45 to Bangkok, he could. The 10:13 to Rio? Of course. 11:05 to Tokyo? Hai. The only thing stopping him was ticket availability. If they really wanted to find him, Seymour knew, they could. Dies Irae had connections in multiple countries. From his sparse conversation with Ambrosi, Seymour knew the vampire had Spanish and Asian connections. Who knew what else he had? Lia was well versed in a large chunk of Eastern Europe. So it seemed most of the world was out. Besides, most of those places had less protective laws than America and Canada. Far better chance of him ending up in a pine box in Bratislava than in Bangor. Continuing to peruse the list of departing flights, he pondered a flight to Los Angeles. “The match is there,” he reasoned. “May as well get some extra training in. Yevon knows I’ll need it.” Lost in his fear that she could strike at him outside the ring was the fact that he would have to face her in it for the richest prize in Primetime Central. Under most circumstances, a shot at the PTC Global Title was something to be treasured. After all, he was undefeated in such contests, and a third reign with it would push his legacy beyond challenge. But his opponent changed the entire equation Six times he’d faced Shadow in combat. Six times she’d defeated him. Six. NO ONE in the world possessed a record like that against him. It was inconceivable to Seymour that anyone could so thoroughly and completely own him in a sport that he had risen to the top of. “I just don’t get it. She’s good, sure, but so is every other top name I’ve fought and beaten. And even when I do lose, I usually come back and win a rematch. Or at least ONE of a series. She’s completely blanked me, and I don’t know why…” A vibration at his hip cut off his train of thought, and his hand automatically dipped into his pocket to retrieve his phone. Without thinking, he flipped it open and jammed it to his ear. “Seymour Almasy,” he said, monotone. “What’s up?” “Seymour,” the other man on the line said, in a Japanese accent. “How are things?” It didn’t take Almasy more than a few moments to recognize the voice as that of Ichiro Roppongi. THAT realization caused him to sigh. He’d almost forgotten entirely about Roppongi’s insane offer to him in a Seattle bar, an offer to become a member of a super-secretive pleasure club, for lack of a better term. “I’d be lying if I told you they were well.” Short, to the point, and entirely accurate. “Ah, apologies, my friend. Perhaps I can help improve them. In three days, Club Plaisance is having a meeting. Do you still have the robe and mask that my…associate brought to your Seattle hotel room?” Seymour couldn’t help but chuckle a bit at his euphemism. The woman was a whore, plain and simple. A high-class, well-dressed whore, but a whore nonetheless. And, as he had ever since his relationships with Laura and Tessa Winsdor had ended, he had indulged. Oh, had he indulged… “I do,” Seymour replied, snapping his attention back to the matter at hand. “I’ve kept it in the bottom of my suitcase since I got it.” “Good,” Roppongi stated. “You’ll need it to get in. Now, listen to me. In three days time, I will call you again. Be sure that you are someplace private, and I will give you instructions from there. Do not bother calling this number. I am calling you from a pay phone.” “Got it,” Almasy replied. “I’ll see you in three days.” “Indeed. Farewell.” One click later, the call was over, leaving Seymour scanning the departing flights once more. This time, though, he had a definite purpose. He would return to the city where it all began. To a site of triumph and defeat, love and hatred, life and death. Under most circumstances, Seymour would have avoided the place like the plague. But to the frightened, angry, and desperate Seymour, the 12:05 American Airlines flight to New York was damn near salvation.
