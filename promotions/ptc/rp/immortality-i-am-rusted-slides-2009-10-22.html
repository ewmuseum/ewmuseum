Immortality: I Am Rusted Slides

22 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-22 Leaves had begun their change, a few already falling to the earth, as they turned shimmering shades of gold, orange, and red. Autumn was the most vibrant of deaths. The demise of Fountain Park had been far less spectacular. -Scotty- Scott Ward tugged at the flesh of his lip, searching for the gash he knew was there, while blood pooled against his gums, turning his teeth pink. Finally, he found the split and pushed it apart with his fingernail to check its depth. “That’s pretty fucking nasty.” With his lip busted open and stretched halfway across the room, his words were barely English. “You think maybe next time you hit me, you could take that lame class ring off first? “Sit down, and shuddup.” Two mammoth hands clasped Scott’s shoulders and shoved him down into the chair beneath his ass. He looked up at the hulking figure standing over him and glared. Leon’s muscle was enjoying this far too much. “Sure thing.” “I’ve bailed you out for the last time, Scotty. Paid a lot of money to cover some bad bets you’ve made with the wrong people.” “C’mon, man… that’s what guys do for their ol’ highschool buddies. Help ‘em out.” “At this point, I don’t care if you were my fuckin brother, Scotty.” Leon rubbed his brow, gritting his teeth. “Egging mailboxes and setting tables on fire in Chem Lab only go so far. You’re pretty heavy in debt to me.” “Don’t suppose I can treat ya to a New York Strip and call it even?” “No.” The wealthy man crossed Scott’s dining room and pulled a chair away from the table, taking a seat opposite of his old classmate. “Not a chance.” Leon Gibbs had been a superstar in the making. As a junior, he’d set high school records for the Fighting Falcons, quarterbacking the team to the State Semi-finals. Major collegiate football programs had begun to offer up scholarships… Ohio State, Cincy, Pitt, even Notre Dame… but Gibbs’ much anticipated senior year had changed all that. Knocking up the principal’s underage daughter was a mistake he could have recovered from, but missing the blitzing linebacker that left his throwing shoulder a shredded mess caused all those scholarships to disappear overnight. For a few weeks, everyone around town talked about the star player with sympathy, but soon, as the Falcons advanced deep into the playoffs under the surprising leadership of a sophomore quarterback, Leon Gibbs became nothing more than that guy who knocked up Principal Bell’s daughter. And so, he moved on to other things. “Shit, Leon… I can fix this.” Scott shifted in his seat. “I know I can fix this.” “I’m all ears.” -The Park- A Couple Days Later His eyes locked on the swings, swaying in the Ohio breeze. Two of the black rubber seats marinated in mud puddles, hanging on their chains from one end. Of the four left unbroken, three had fallen victim to wiseass teenagers, raised too high for any child to reach, or tied in knots around each other. The fountains were just empty wells. Gone were the goldfish, replaced by fallen leaves of similar colors, so that all that remained of the park’s namesake were broken bricks, loosely holding their form. What had once been volleyball courts, soft sand and new nets, now were mangled pits. Poles still stood, but no netting stretched between, and the sand had been infected with shin-high spurts of grass and broken bottles from the cheapest beer college money could buy. The slide stood on rusted legs, a pile of shriveled brown leaves covering its slope. Thirty or so years ago, Matt had sworn it was fifty feet tall, conquering his fears as he scaled its heights. Three decades, and perspective, had changed all that, and standing at the slide’s side, he chuckled at the reality of the mountain from his youth, no taller than he was. At one time, the park had been a favorite spot for locals. Mothers watched their children on the seesaws and gossiped about the latest divorce, while fathers would cast a line in the nearby pond. Teenagers owned the basketball courts and took in the awful sounds of wannabe rock bands that played ‘gigs’ at the pavilion in the middle of the park. Now, there were no more concerts, and the pond stunk of piss and neglect. Matt stared at the ruins of youthful mischief, and it occurred to him that time touched everything, but some things it kicked in the gut. It had been nearly two years since he had retired from the squared circle. After a career of success he could have only ever dreamed of, he’d stepped away quietly… no fanfare… no parades or goodbye speeches. One night, he’d simply lost his match, dropped his title, walked out the backdoor and flown home to his wife and kids. It was the right move, made almost too late to matter. An unhealthy dedication to his job… ‘addiction’, as his wife called it… had nearly broken his marriage. In that regard, Matt was no different than hundreds who had come before, and hundreds more that would come after. But unlike so many of those, he had reached the apex, and stepped down before falling. Feeling there was nothing left to accomplish on the career front, Matt had turned his energy towards enjoying the company of his wife, and the responsibility of fatherhood. Both had been humbling, but rewarding, and in two years since retiring, he hadn’t once thought about returning to the ring. Until he’d stepped foot in the park and stood by that rickety mountain of metal from his childhood. -Scotty- “Tell me how you fix this, Scotty.” With his playing career disintegrating, Gibbs had put his athletic skills to use in the only other way he knew how. Leon turned his attention toward Vegas, accumulating a small fortune in sports betting. Soon, he found himself bored with the practice, and the once NFL bound Quarterback, took on larger aspirations, morphing into a small-time bookie. His Falcon fame not completely lost, Leon weaseled his way into the ear of some local players, talked them into shaving a few measly points from a game or two, and just like that, Gibbs became a legend with the underground betting community. An investigation had nearly brought his circus to a halt in 2006, but the Feds had swung and missed, and Gibbs had continued to grow his business, slowly but surely. Now, sitting across from Scott, he was a man worth quite a bit of dirty money. Less than he had been six months ago, thanks to some foolish loyalty. “I can help ya get back the money you forked over to cover my ass.” Scotty leaned forward, cracking his knuckles as he bounced his leg under the table. “How so?” “You remember my big brother, right?” -The Park- A Couple Days Later “Mary said I’d probably find you here.” Scott crossed the unkempt lawn, crunching a pine cone under his feet. As he reached the old bike path that ran through the middle of the park, his toe caught on a chunk of cracked asphalt and he nearly fell face-first into a tangle of weeds. “Shit, what the hell brought ya to this place?” “I dunno. Just decided to go for a walk, and ended up here.” Matt looked around, then pointed up the bike path toward the far end of the park. “Remember when they used to launch the fireworks down there on the Fourth? They’d bring in all those carnival rides for the kids, set up the funnel cake and corndog stands. Everyone just gorged themselves till they were sick.” “Yeah. That was a long time ago.” “I don’t think they’ve launched the fireworks down here in close to fifteen years.” “Haven’t done much of anything here in close to fifteen years.” "Was it sixth grade? The summer you snuck down to the park one night to make-out with your girlfriend? Mom went tearing through the house looking for you, and when she couldn't find ya, she headed this way. I had to rush down and give you the heads up so you could sneak home the back way. She ended up down here screaming and yelling, and when she got back to the house, you were sitting on the couch watching TV." "Man, that was crazy. I still can't believe we were able to convince her I had been in the bathroom the whole time she was looking for me." Scott shook his head, recalling all the girlfriends he'd brought to the park. "Guess that's just another one I owe ya for." “This used to be the coolest place in town. Everything worth a damn that this city did went down here.” Matt kicked his foot against the seesaw, sending a pile of leaves fluttering to the ground. “Now it’s just an afterthought. I dunno what it is, but being a father, it just kinda kills me that my kids will never know this place for what it really was.” “Times change.” “No kiddin’. Used to shoot hoops every Thursday after school on the courts over there. Only basketball my kids will ever play is on the Wii. They’ll never know this place as anything more than a run-down old dump.” “Just like a ghost town.” “I can sit ‘em down and tell them all the stories I want about the good ol’ days, but it won’t matter. All they’ll see is broken-down playground equipment.” Matt looked around, trying to picture crowds stuffing their faces with funnel cakes, powdered sugar sprinkling their shirts. “Jesus, you’re a real downer this afternoon, bro.” “Sorry, bud.” “Hey,” Scott zipped up his jacket. He wasn’t sure if a breeze had come through, but he suddenly felt cold. “You ever think about going back?” “No. Not until today, anyways.” “Least you knew what I was talkin’ about.” “You’re not hard to figure out, Scotty.” The words, if for just a moment, gave Scott pause. “Ya know what, I think you should do it, bro. I know Mary may not be crazy about it, but I’m sure she’d understand. Might roll her eyes and sigh, but when push comes to shove, she’ll support ya. You ought to give it one last go ‘round. Like the good ol’ days.” “Maybe. Its crossed my mind, I’m just not sure it’s a good idea.” “I think it’d be a real good idea.” Scott leaned against the pole under one of the basketball hoops, flakes of rust scraping off as he shoved his hands into his pockets. “Honestly, you should do it. You should join GTT.” -Scotty- Scott shoved a cold rag against his lip, closing his eyes as the towel began to numb the stinging. “Christ, that feels good.” “So how does your brother help me recoup my losses?” “One athlete helping another.” His older brother had always been there, no matter what sort of jam Scott found himself in, but the last time Matt had bailed him out… was the last time. Scotty had laughed when his brother used those words, never believing them to be true. Then, a few months later, when he’d approached his older sibling for a loan of a couple hundred bucks, Matt had turned him down. Since, Scott’s gambling habits had racked up increasing debt, but he hadn’t asked for another dime. No matter the mess, Scott was thankful for the dozens of times his brother had bailed him out, and he had to respect the fact that Matt was done taking care of him. The two had thrown their fair share of punches growing up, locked each other outta the house a couple times, but they’d always been close. Friends as much as siblings. Which is why it churned Scott’s intestines and filled his busted mouth with the familiar taste of vomit when he spoke. “Matt had a pretty fuckin’ stellar career in professional wrestling. Retired a couple years ago to do the family thing, spend time with the wife and kids.” “Yeah, and a retired professional wrestler doesn’t mean much to me.” Leon tapped his thumbs across the surface of his cell phone, replying to text about a college game that weekend between two MAC schools. It wasn’t important, but is more interesting than the pile of nothing Scotty seemed to be giving him. “I can get him back in the ring, Leon.” “How’s that?” “He’s my brother for God’s sake, I mean, I grew up with the guy. All he needs is a push.” Leon tucked his cell phone away, sliding it into his pocket. Leaning forward, he folded his arms and cocked an eyebrow towards his old high school friend. “You’re sure you can get him outta retirement?” “Definitely.” Leon rolled his tongue around his mouth, mulling over the possibility. Finally, he spoke, and when he did, his words were disturbingly calm. “Then here’s the deal, Scotty… you get him back in the ring, some place big, back in the spotlight, and when I see fit, I’ll pull the trigger.” “Ok.” “And when I pull the trigger,” Leon lifted the shoulder that had let him down in high school and pointed a crooked finger straight at Scott, “your brother better be ready to take a dive.”
