The Path that Leads to Nowhere

22 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-22 Cold and Broken One In the distance, the sound of screeching tires moving back and forth across a paved road rang through the night sky. There was a slight breeze from the north, rustling the tall grass and weeds in the endless sight of Midwestern plains, and the moon, just bright enough to illuminate the middle of nowhere landscape. At this particular spot you would find a solitary road sign, indicating to anyone on its path that they were heading north on U.S. Highway 81, with no other significant information to those who chose to traverse its way. The vehicle that made no effort at keeping its presence to nature, or the small town that illuminated the horizon to the North, finally came to a halt, but not without first sliding a good distance at a ninety degree angle from the direction in which it came. At a temporary resting place, the vehicle sat, its lights still turned off and the engine still running. Hushed voices could be heard from within, scheming, as the black cargo van rocked about. With a couple of slams and an order from an older sounding man, "Do it already!" the sliding door on the passenger side of the van came flying open, and two large men tossed another of equal size to the pavement. Still this man lay, bound with what could be made out to be a mental patient's straight jacket, but still breathing. "Let's go!" Another order from inside the van was taken, and the back tires began to squeal as the engine revved, and the sliding door was slammed shut. As quickly as the van came to a stop, it was back in motion heading in the direction from which it came. With a final glance, one could even make out a Kansas license plate attached to the rear of the vehicle as the lights were turned on just as it drove out of sight. As the sounds of an engine being pushed to its limit and tires gripping pavement faded into the night, all that remained were the rustles of wind caressing the grass and weeds across the fields that nearly camouflaged the highway from sight. However, a new sound emerged, that of a man breathing, clearly in pain. As he tried to bring himself to his feet, he couldn't help but struggle as the straight jacket prevented him from gaining full use of his arms in his attempts to get to his feet. "What the fuck?" He questioned himself out loud as he continued to find a better way to get upright. As if the record were broken, this went on for several minutes, and what would seem like hours to the man, clearly shaken up as the subtle moonlight reflected off a puddle of blood on the pavement, coming from a more than noticeable cut above his right eye. The chilly night air became more evident as the man's breath could be seen with every breath, as well as steam coming from the blood resting on the cool pavement. With the help of a couple already unbuckled straps on the straight jacket, the battered man finally freed himself from its bindings, and tossed the restraint into the ditch. Mere moments later, after several minutes of struggling, he was finally up on two feet, unsteady, but still able to keep his own balance. This would not be the case for long as he quickly reached out and grabbed the pole to the lonely road sign to steady himself again. Obviously he was still shaken up from the bump to the head he had taken, amongst other things that might have happened before he appeared from within the black van that was probably twenty or more miles away by now. Continuing to use the road sign for leverage, the man turned around, using it to lean against, resting his shoulder blades on either side of the pole. "Where am I?" One of many questions running through his head at this point, he chose this one to ask aloud, as if the rustling grass or half full moon would reply. Unable to move without dizziness or pain, he stayed put for the time being, and decided his next step would be to search his own pockets of the tattered blue jeans he was wearing accompanied with a plain white t-shirt and nothing more than a pair of plain white socks on his feet. In his back right pocket, he found a wallet, and quickly looked through it, finding nothing more than a note written on a piece of torn legal pad that read, "Take 5 Daily." He quickly tossed the note aside, having no idea what it meant, which wasn't a big shock, as he couldn't remember anything prior to the point in which a less than gingerly landing on the pavement woke him up. "Who am I?" Asking aloud again, with extra emphasis on 'who' he was. With nothing more in his back pockets, he turned his attention to the front two, finding nothing in the right one, though; his left hand did pull something from the left pocket. After removing his left hand from the pocket, he was able to examine more closely the item that was occupying it, this item happened to bring to mind the note he has just tossed aside, as he looked at an unmarked prescription bottle full of pills. The blank white label on the bottle did have the same note, written in the same handwriting as the note he found in his wallet. It was right then that the man, who was doing nothing more than trying to make sense of the situation, couldn't help but show his emotions. Tears started to run down both sides of his face as he frantically looked around for signs of life, and with the lights of the small town on the horizon to the north, the man had no choice but to struggle with the pain, and find any strength that he didn't remember he had to make it as far as he needed to find help. Taking a deep breath, he pushed himself back upright, standing alone on two feet. Steadying himself there, he took another look at the bottle of pills, and let out a heavy sigh, opening the bottle, grabbing one of the small white pills inside, and put it in his mouth. There it sat for a few seconds as he worked up enough saliva to aid in swallowing the pill. "What in the Hell is going on?" He asked aloud once more as he took a step and stopped, still unsure of himself. Able to keep his balance, he did his best to ignore the pain he felt, and began walking. Headed in a direction he was unsure of, knowing nothing about who he was and where he came from, and certainly having no concept of what was the come, he had to follow the path life threw him right then and there... a path leading to nowhere. Two The steady tones of music most would call 'elevator music' played inside of the small Mom and Pop convenience store at a gas station just on the outside of Geneva, Nebraska. One individual occupied the space behind the counter, a late sixty-something man, wearing a red polo with a shoddy embroidered logo of the store on the right side of the shirt. On the left side was a pinned name tag reading, "Charles Pennington - 5 Years of Service with a Smile." It was well after midnight, in a town that most people wouldn't find even if they were lost, and Charles didn't seem to mind too much. His nametag certainly didn't lie, even without any customers and a store that looked like you could eat off every surface, he stood behind the counter with a smile plastered on his face. This was no ordinary smile, in fact, to take a picture of it; one would assume the photo had been tampered with to make this man smile like he was. This was all about to change... A loud chime echoed inside of the store as a rather large man stepped inside. His dirty blond hair slightly discolored up front from blood that had obviously come from a nasty cut above his right eye. The clothing he wore was much of the same, blood stained and tattered, and all of it would not have been complete without a slight limp from an injured leg. It was about five seconds after this man came into view that Charles' smile disappeared, and his hand moved towards a silent alarm button installed just underneath the cash register on that side of the counter. Looking up at the only security camera installed with a worried look, he gained his composure and turned back to the unknown customer, regaining the smile that earned him such a prestigious nametag. "Are you doing alright there young man?" He asked, hoping for the best, but not ruling out any possibilities as several ran through his mind. A smirk appeared, and then disappeared as quickly as it came, on the man's face as he approached the counter. The short walk from the door to the counter was accompanied with pain, no matter how well he tried to hide it. Finally reaching the counter, the man placed his dirty, bloodied hands on the counter to help take some of the pressure from his injured right leg, and he took a deep breath. "I'll be honest with you..." He paused to look at the old man's nametag. "Charles, I'm not doing so well." The man finished his statement, still trying to catch his breath from a long painful walk. "Do you want me to call for help?" Charles asked back as he reached for the phone behind him. Reaching his right hand out, the man painfully grabbed onto Charles' shirt, keeping him from picking up the phone. "I'm not quite sure what all has happened to me tonight, and I think it would be better for the both of us if you tried to help me figure a couple things out before you go calling anyone." With a handful of shirt, the man turned Charles back to him, and the sure of himself smile that was glued to his face just five minutes ago was gone, and replaced with a look of uncertainty. Glancing through his bifocals at the man's hand, Charles glanced back and forth between it and the man holding onto him until the point was taken, and the grip was released. Placing his hand back beside the button to arm the alarm, the old man tried to keep calm. "I'll help you with what I can young man, but I'm no medicine man." Trying to stay relaxed, and hopefully relieve some tension, Charles replied with sarcasm. Another smirk formed on the man's face again, only this time it stuck. "I just want to know; do I look familiar to you at all?" The man asked with a look of hope. Headlights shine through the windows at the front of the store, drawing Charles' attention away from the man for just a second, but not distracting him enough to not turn his attention back to him. "I'm sorry son, but I'm afraid I've never seen you in here before; or anywhere else around town for that matter." Bracing himself for something bad to happen he answered the question. Instead of getting upset like Charles had expected, the man let out a very disappointed sounding sigh, and hung his head before taking two steps back away from the counter. "Could you at least tell me where I'm at?" Staring at the ground, he asked. Before Charles could reply, being the nice guy he is, the door to the store opened once more, and the loud chime signaling that someone has walked in echoed again. The man, just on instinct, turns around to look at who was joining him and Charles inside. It wasn't good news for him, though; as in stepped a couple of police officers looking to grab a quick snack while they filled their cruiser with gas. "Shit!" Exclaimed the dazed and confused man, and without hesitation he took off as fast as his battered and bruised body would allow him to go. There seemed to be a moment of shock with the first police man who walked through the door, the younger looking of the two that had just come in, as he let the man 'run' right by him, but the same was not true for the second police officer. "Stop right there!" He yelled already in pursuit of the fleeing man. "Get him Dad!" The younger of the two officers yelled at the other, but quickly corrected his self. "I mean Sheriff Williams!" Out from behind the counter came Charles, as fast as he could, to try and help with the situation as he approached the younger cop who stayed back instead of chasing after the man when he fled. "Luke, you need to tell Doug that man wasn't causing any harm." He stated with concern in his voice. "He's just lost, very confused, and I imagine once your father catches him, even more injured than he was when he came in ten minutes ago." Charles finished explaining. The young cop, Luke Williams, then turned his attention away from the foot chase, and scorned Charles. "Listen Charles, this is a small town, and we all know everyone knows everybody else." Luke admits. "But this is police business, so let the professionals handle it, and we'll get your statement in a little bit." The arrogant cop explained. Charles, slightly offended and very angry, turned his back to the cop and went back about his business. He walked back behind the counter and continued to do the job he was being paid to do. "By the way…" He announced. "Your total on pump three comes to thirty-six fifty." With a smile on his face, and a slight attitude, he informed Luke. "Yeah, yeah! You'll get it!" With an annoyed tone, Luke replied to Charles, as if to just get Charles to leave him be. Annoyed and even more angry now, Charles started to wipe down the counter tops of his station, but his attention hadn't turned away from Luke, the disrespectful, young, arrogant police officer. Charles continued to stare daggers into the back of his head while Luke stood there, waiting for his father, Sheriff Williams, to return with a fleeing suspect who, in reality, had done nothing wrong. A commotion had come from outside that caused Luke to leave the store, with this, brought an immediate reaction from Charles. "Idiot." Three Warm breaths of anger could be seen rolling from the mouths of Doug and Luke Williams, the father son duo of police officers for Fillmore County's Geneva Police Department. Their car was parked on the side of the road, and the two of them stood in front, the headlights to their cruiser provided a mediocre spotlight to this two man show. Both front doors to the car were open, along with the passenger side rear door; the only thing missing here was a suspect, a man nobody knew, not even that man himself. Finger pointing, foot stomping, and head shaking, among other things, aided this heated argument in the middle of the night, and it just so happened that a familiar Highway 81 sign just south of Geneva could be seen off to the side of the road, barely lit from the car's headlights. The moon, which was earlier the only light provided for a show that if that highway sign could talk, would give the two cops something to think about, was all but out of sight over the horizon. The tirades of both cops ceased for but a moment as Luke's facial expression went from one full of anger, to that of total confusion. "How did he know that the door in the back was just going to open up like that?" He questioned in a manner that seemed as if he already knew the answer to his own question. A dumbfounded look struck Doug's face as if he had witnessed the most idiotic event he could ever see in his life. He was speechless, his mouth gaped open, and with nothing more than heavy breaths escaping his chapped lips as could be seen hitting the cool night air. With nothing to say, at least off the top of his head, he simply shook his head, rolled his eyes, and turned his back to his son. "Well?" Luke questioned his father, now hoping to get a response. Walking out of the light the car's headlights provided towards the driver's door, Luke followed Doug like a lost puppy. The Sheriff placed his right hand on the open driver's door and turned around to face his son. The flabbergasted look gone, a smile now advertised Doug's feelings, though, not a smile of enjoyment. "Because you said they would, you waste of tax payer's money!" Doug proclaimed with a touch of enthusiasm in his voice. This time Luke was the one with a surprised look on his face. "I did no such..." He tried to explain, but his father was not about to let him lie about something he witnessed firsthand just minutes earlier. Doug removed his right hand from the top of the driver's door, and placed if firmly on his son's left shoulder, and with a handful of uniform, pushed him to the hood of the car. "Since you obviously don't remember, let me refresh your memory!" Doug yelled, the echo of his voice could be heard out in the fields, as the slight breeze started to pick up into heavier gusts of wind. "We hadn't even gotten out of park yet, at the gas station, before you opened your stupid mouth!" His mouth seething, Doug's face inched closer and closer to Luke's, and his son looked like he was about to wet himself, but still continued to believe he had done nothing wrong. "You must be thinking of something else." Luke declared, despite a hint of uncertainty in his voice. Luke's inability to own up to his mistake, however, did not sit well with his father, his superior on the force, and Doug's left hand came flying from his side, hitting Luke on his left shoulder, before another handful of uniform was latched onto. "I've raised a complete moron!" Doug bellowed, a heavy sigh quickly followed, before his tone of voice turned from anger to disappointment. "And I've let you become little more than a danger to this town." He admitted with a heavy sigh. "What are you trying to say Dad?" Luke questioned hesitantly. The disappointment in Officer Doug Williams' voice quickly went away after Luke's query. "I am your superior, and you will address me as such!" He howled. "You may call me Officer Williams, or Sheriff, or Sir!" Doug continued to exclaim. Tears started to form in Luke's eyes now, and he desperately searched for anything to make the situation better. "What do you want me to do, Sir?" He asked, sucking back deep breaths to avoid breaking down and crying. Taking deep breaths in and out of his nose, like a bull ready to charge, Doug snarled. "You can go home..." He stated before picking his son off the hood of the patrol cruiser, and tossing him to the pavement, nearly at the exact spot that the unknown man who is now on the loose was tossed earlier. "You're fired!" The sheriff roared as he crawled into the driver's seat of the patrol vehicle, closed his door behind him, and sped off, jerking the wheel once left, and quickly back to the right, causing both passenger doors that were still open to be slammed shut. Four An all too familiar sound of melancholy melodies continued to play inside of the small convenience store, and there, behind the counter, with his plastic smile, sat Charles, making the best of his late shift duties. As far as he was concerned, everything was per the norm again, and despite a less than ordinary night, he had convinced himself it was back to the same old, same old. Little did the happy-go-lucky man realize that, before the police officers even left the property just an hour ago, a slip of the mouth allowed a man who showed up in town, not on his own free will, just a few hours ago, battered and beaten, to be out on his own again. Unfortunately, Charles Pennington was not aware of any of this, and therefore, was unable to brace himself for what happened next. The phone, which sat on the counter top behind where he was sitting, rang. Charles turned a full hundred and eighty degrees to pick it up, his back to the entrance of the store. "Hello?" He answered, and stood silent, waiting for a reply from the other end. "Fran, this is certainly a surprise!" He exclaimed. "What are you doing up this early?" Charles looked at the clock as he asked the question. Once again standing in silence as he listened to Fran on the other end explain herself, Charles started to toy with the phone cord. "There's nothing to worry about." He reassured the caller. "Sheriff Williams and his good for nothing son chased him out of here a little over an hour ago." Charles continued to explain. "Actually, the kid wasn't really doing anything wrong, he just seemed very confused, like something had happened to him; and he kept popping pills from a bottle he has in his pocket." He finished, and then pondered a moment about the bottle of pills the man from earlier had been carrying around. Another familiar sound rang inside of the store, that of someone walking through the entrance, and Charles, none the wiser, barely turned his head to acknowledge the individual who stepped through the door. "Sorry to bother you..." The John Doe from earlier began to speak as he walked towards the counter, his limp gone, and seemingly pain free from the spill he took earlier. "I'll be right with you, Sir." Charles said to him, still unaware of who had stepped into his store. "Frances, I've got a customer, I'm going to let you go." He said to the woman on the other end. "You have nothing to worry about, trust me; and I love you." He finished, before hanging up the phone, and turned around. After realizing who was standing at the counter for the second time that early morning, Charles immediately felt his heart jump into his throat. "You..." The man placed both hands on top of the counter, and used it as leverage to hop over, rather than using the opening just six feet away, reassuring himself, as well as Charles, that he felt much better than before. "Charles, there's no other way." The man stated, a look of emotional pain filling his eyes. "I can't let you push that little button, I can't let you call those idiot cops, and I can't let you call that wife of yours back." He proclaimed, assuming the woman Charles was just speaking with was indeed his wife. Charles reached his arms out to plead with the man, and started to back away, though, with nowhere to go but into a corner. Sweat began to form on his forehead as if were the dead of summer, and over a hundred degrees in the middle of the day outside. "Please, don't do anything you are going to regret son." Charles pleaded, clinching his left hand tightly into a fist, and releasing it before he shook it about. "I don't know what else to do!" The man shrieked. "Have you ever woke up, bleeding, lying face down on a highway in the middle of the night, and not had any fucking clue who you were, where you can from, or where you were at that exact moment?" He questioned rhetorically. "The only thing I can even imagine right now is for you to open this cash register, let me empty it, and hopefully that will get me somewhere that's more familiar!" He finished, before he pulled the unmarked prescription bottle from his front pocket again, and tossed yet another pill into his mouth, and swallowed it. For the second time that evening, a man whose biggest worries have always been what to eat for lunch had feared for his life. This time it was more evident that before, and he began to shake, the sweat on his forehead so thick, it started to roll down his face, forming a waterfall from the tip of his nose. "Please..." He pleaded again, but was interrupted. "JUST FUCKING OPEN IT!" The John Doe screamed, obviously scared as well as in a hurry. The point was taken, though, and after pushing a couple of buttons on the register, its drawer popped open, and Charles back away, closer to the corner, his eyes locked on the phone that was within arm’s reach. "There." Charles said. His attention turned to the open register, the man all but forget about Charles being there, and started grabbing bills from the drawer, emptying it of all paper cash, but managed to catch Charles reaching for the phone just in time. He slammed the register shit, and as Charles latched onto the receiver of the phone, Mr. Doe lunged forward, pushing the elderly man to the ground, but not after his head had hit a shelf on the way down, the phone went with him. "I didn't want to do that Charles, but you left me no choice!" He explained at the top of his voice. It was not looking good for Charles at this point, though, as he clinched his chest with his right hand, his left looked as if had gone numb. "Aspirin. I need… aspirin." Charles said, as he used his right arm to motion under the counter where, luckily, a bottle of aspirin sat. The John Doe, however, had a different outlook on what was transpiring, and quickly fled. He hopped the counter again, his jean pockets full of cash, and ran as fast as he could through the front door, and was out like a flash, the only thing left behind was the tone that rang through the store of someone coming or going. Excruciating pain was the only thing that could describe what Charles felt, as he sat there on the floor, behind the counter, out of reach of aspirin, and in too much pain to reach for the phone to call for help. But, by some stroke of luck, sympathy was felt that night, and over the top of the counter, from the floor, Charles saw the man that's caused the biggest ruckus in Fillmore County in some years. "Help!" Charles implored. John Doe would not say another word to Charles, however, but instead, he walked behind the counter, and opened the bottle of aspirin that was on a shelf below the cash register before dumping them on the floor next to Charles. While the gas station attendant struggled to get one of the pills into his mouth, the man that caused all of this pain and anguish would become Charles' savior, dialing 911. "I'm at the gas station on the south end of town, and I'm having a heart attack." He said to the operator before simply setting the phone receiver on the floor next to Charles, standing up, and walking back out of the store. Five On the horizon, the black sky had turned shades of purple and orange unimaginable unless seen firsthand, and with nothing to obstruct the view from the highway for miles, Mr. John Doe had a front row seat to the most beautiful sunrise he had ever seen... because it was the only one he had ever remembered seeing. With the light from the sun peaking in from the distance, the way in which this sad and confused you man was traveling became more visible. The pain he felt in his leg from earlier, that had not been present since a few pills ago, had started to come back, and he noticed the limp again. He reached into his pocket, and dug through the twenty dollar bills that occupied the same space as his bottle of pills, still unsure of what they did; he pulled the bottle out, popped the lid off, and took his sixth pill in as many hours, disobeying the note of 5 per day. Off in the distance, from the south, a vehicle approached, one that the man was unaware of, and it wasn't until it was a couple hundred feet away that he heard the break squealing as the beat up, brown, Oldsmobile station wagon, came to a complete stop in the road next to him. An older lady in the driver's seat rolled the passenger window down, and leaning over the console slightly, peaked up at the wounded man. "My goodness young man, are you alright?" She asked with concern. The events of that morning had taken its toll on the man, however, and he was barely able to muster a head shake as he rested his left hand on the roof of the car. The older lady unlocked the doors, and used her right hand to wave at him, motioning for him to get into the passenger seat. Crawling into the vehicle, he gingerly rustled about, finding a comfortable position. "Thank you." He said at a level that was nearly a whisper, his voice was scratchy, parched from hours of running with nothing to wet his whistle. "Couple of guys attacked me, and I woke up in the middle of the rode about twenty miles back, and I have no idea what's going on." He explained, stretching the truth ever so slightly on what has actually happened over the last few hours. "We should get you to a hospital!" She exclaimed pushing down on the accelerator with a foot made of concrete. "It just so happens I'm headed to one right now." The older lady admitted As if he had gotten bit on the rear by something, the Fillmore County John Doe came out of his seat. "No hospitals!" He yelled, not realizing the volume of his voice. "I just..." He continued. "I just don't think I need to go to a hospital right now." The man admitted. "A little rest, and a bus station, and I can be on my way." He said turning his attention out the passenger side window as he watched the monotony of plains grass go by as the car got up to speed. Without hesitation, the old lady replied, as chipper as ever. "You would know what's best for yourself." She agreed. "I just hope you don't mind finding a way to the bus station in Lincoln on your own, my next and last stop is Lincoln Regional." She continued to share personal information. "You see, my husband was attacked earlier this morning by some crazed lunatic that just showed up out of nowhere, he had a heart attack, and somehow managed to call 911 on his own." John Doe continued to look out the window, only half paying attention to the old lady now as she continued to have no issues sharing too much. "That's nice." He replied as he started to nod off. She stopped for a moment, the smile on her face had disappeared, and she turned her attention back to the road she was driving on. However, her face didn't remain without a smile for long. "How rude is it of me to just ramble on about my personal life when you don't even know me?" She admitted with a question as her cheeks started to turn a rosy red color from embarrassment. "My name is Frances Pennington, what's yours?"
