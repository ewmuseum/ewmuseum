Like Father Like Son

9 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-09 A large shadow crept into the rectangle of light, filling it with a darkness that drew two startled sets of eyes like moths to a flame. For a moment, those within the sandhouse could see only a black silhouette superimposed on the doorway, the sharp contrast washing out the features and making the newcomer impossible to distinguish. The figure started at seeing the two of them, then lurched angrily forward, his steps hurried and purposeful, his hands balled into fists. "Father!" the woman cried, her anxiety turning the salute into a squawk. With a furtive, warning glance for the man at her side, she rushed toward the newcomer, now plainly visible. He gave her a tight smile and caught her easily when she leapt into his arms, but his stride only slowed for the second it took him to give her a quick squeeze and set her down. As much as he liked seeing his daughter, from what he had been told over the phone, this wasn't an occasion to be wallowing in affection. He had received the call not half an hour before, from a hysterical woman it took him a while to realize was his daughter. After several minutes of gentle soothing, he had her calmed down enough that she could relate her woes to him and plead for his help. He knew what the problem was even before she told him, though. It was the same problem every time. One he should have long since rid himself -- and her as well, he supposed -- of. Glaring death at the problem, he continued his march to the front of the sandhill, where the problem stood dry-washing his hands. Breathing heavily from anger as much as exertion, he muttered at the sheepish yet somehow defiant look on his son-in-law's face. The younger man shifted uncomfortably under that withering stare, finally peaking around the man's wide frame to flash a questioning look at his wife. From out of nowhere the back of the father's hand smashed into the son-in-law's cheek, shattering the stillness and all hope of understanding with that echoing clap, like a gunshot within the enclosed space. The son-in-law fell to his knees, wincing and flinching, rubbing at his already reddening face. "Father!" the daughter shouted, scandalized, but she was pointedly ignored. "What the hell is wrong with you?" the father asked in a dry tone that was far too quiet for his mood, looming over the simpering imbecile who had somehow avoided his radar long enough to woo his daughter. "W-what?" Stuttering, the imbecile ducked his head like a turtle. "I th-thought that--" "You didn't think!" the father roared over him, barely restraining himself from administering another slap. He wasn't a huge fan of physical violence, but some people were simply too stubborn and dense to have it hammered home any other way. While he wanted nothing more than to just choke the life out of the man cowering before him, he gave him the benefit of the doubt for the time being. "If you had bothered to think, I'd still be at home right now, enjoying the last of the good weather! And my daughter would be safe!" "B--" "Silence!" Violence returned, in the form of a cautionary kick to the shin. "You think I want to hear your excuses? Do you really think I want to know why you thought it was a good idea to capture Angelo- fucking-Deville and present him like a fucking diamond to my little girl? I'd rather peel your skin off and make you dance in your bones!" He fumed silently for a time, allowing a moment for his threat to take seed in the man's imagination. Behind him, Lisa cleared her throat timidly. "Why the hell Lisa stays with you I'll never figure out!" "Jack meant well, father, truly he d--" Once more her outburst fell on deaf ears. "Where's the body?" Still cringing, expecting another blow, Jack pointed towards the car haphazardly parked just a few meters away. Following his finger, the father saw the body, sprawled out on the gravel floor with a thick puddle of blood surrounding his head like an aura. Mumbling under his breath, he stalked over to the corpse and inspected it. When the other two came up behind him and peered around his bulk, he didn't notice. What he did notice was that despite half of his face being obliterated, the dead man lying on the ground was indeed Angelo Deville. Relief that his daughter survived the encounter warred with rage that she had been in the situation in the first place. Deville was a compassionless killer, a man to be avoided at all costs. If you ever did encounter him, you made your words sweet and honeyed, and hoped they were to his taste. Rumours abounded that he couldn't even be killed, that others had tried and wound up dead for their efforts. He didn't believe such fanciful boasts -- no man could come back from death - - yet he knew the truth of everything else from countless trusted sources. "I'm amazed you were able to kill him," the father said, and it was the simple truth. His eyes narrowed when he heard feet shifting behind him. Without turning, he sighed and demanded to know what had really happened. When the story was finally relayed to him in full, that Angelo had begged to be shot in the head and then forced the trigger down himself, he made his daughter repeat it for confirmation. Even then he didn't believe. Deville was crazy, sure, but there was no way he believed the rumours about himself. "I just wanted to collect the reward," Jack put in whiningly. "I thought bringing his body in would make me a hero. I thought you and Lisa would finally see me as a success, a winner . . ." "One hell of a trophy to show off," the father muttered, prodding Deville with his foot as if he needed the constant reassurance he was truly dead. They were in a small semi-circle around the body now, all regarding it with sober, staring eyes. "What are your plans for the body?" "I . . ." Jack had been about to say that he hadn't planned that far ahead, but even his dull wits realized that would only encourage another outburst. "I thought I'd just turn it in for the reward . . . Don't they want the body?" The father shook his head ruefully. The poor sap had absolutely no idea how things worked, and no sense to set him on the right path. "Normally just a memento, something to prove the deed is done. What the hell would they want with the body?" Jack shrugged, digging his toe into the gravel. "You could always have it stuffed and mount it on your wall," a new voice offered facetiously, startling them. A fourth person had joined the group, hands clasped nonchalantly behind his back as he peered down at the body with the same morbid interest that enslaved the others. Decked out in an imperious black suit lined with gold, his blonde hair slicked straight back from a sharp, perpetually smirking face, he poked the corpse with his foot as well. "Maybe harvest the organs and donate them to science . . . or, since you need the money so badly, to the highest bidder." Lisa, her father, and Jack snapped their heads towards the newcomer, their eyes bugging out when they came to rest on another Angelo Deville, calmly regarding his own dead body as if staring at a strange insect. Their awe-struck gazes flickered from the living to the dead and back again, their jaws unhinging and going slack as their tortured minds registered what they were being fed. Two stormy blue eyes, like a baleful ocean they were stranded in, crashed into each in turn. "Maybe you could even bury it." The half-formed grin was suddenly gone, replaced by a fearsome face forged in the fires of hell. "Maybe show the poor man a little respect now that he's dead." A shrill shriek pierced the air as the last of Lisa's reserve fled. Her father moved protectively before her, swelling up with bravado that never went anywhere near his eyes. Deville watched coolly, curiously, as Jack fumbled around in his jacket, snatching at the gun he had used to kill Angelo less than an hour before. Panic presented itself as a snarl as Jack produced the pistol and thrust it at Deville's forehead without really aiming, then fired. The shot reverberated through the concave, making Lisa abandon her own noise to cover her ears, making her father wince. Blood sprayed the two men as Deville's head exploded, then the freshly dead body crumpled down atop the old. Darkness flashed in the light of the doorway. "Attaboy," The Deville boomed, striding over to the trio of doomed confusion and his two dead bodies. "Glad to see you've found your nerve at l--" Three more ear-splitting crackles as Jack fired wildly at Angelo, missing him the first two times but catching him just above the nose with the third. Deville sagged to the stones, dead. "Really quite remarkable," Deville continued, another body emerging through the doorway and taking great care in stepping over the newest corpse. "I'm not sure your perception is quite what it should be, though. Nor your wit." He spoke conversationally, as if the four of them were out at a company picnic instead of presiding over his many murders. Again the gun fired. Again The Deville died. "Don't you get it?" The barest hint of exasperation was finally creeping into Deville's voice as he came through the box of light once again, garbed in exactly the same suit, hair combed exactly the same way, the same unmistakable air of malice surrounding him. "I can't be killed. All you're doing is wasting time and bodies. And irritating me. I know I make this look easy, but believe me, it isn't. If you put me through it one more time--" This time the roar of the gun had to compete with the father's shouted protest. "What the fuck is wrong with you?" he shouted at Jack, whose hand was trembling so badly his gun might have been a milkshake. He started toward him with a harsh "Give me that thing!", intending to take the gun away, but when Deville suddenly materialized behind Jack, his features contorted in a mask of inhuman rage, the father stopped, vainly raising his hands in warning. "I'll kill you," The Deville whispered in Jack's ear, finishing the promise that was so crudely interrupted a moment before. Jack jumped, screamed, and tried to turn around, but The Deville reached up and calmly took his head in both hands. Jack's neck snapped with a horrid pop that made the earlier gunshots sound like stepped-on bubblewrap. Staring blankly backwards, Jack slid from Deville's hands to a heap on the floor. "Shut her up," Angelo ordered the fat man as soon as Lisa resumed her screeching. While he waited for the man to do so, Deville reached down and wrestled the gun from Jack's hand, turning it over to get a better look at it before tucking it inside his jacket. It was an inferior work of craftsmanship, and the weight was severely off, but it had killed him before, so he felt a strange fascination for it. Lisa's screams died to an incoherent burbling and mumbling. The only thing holding her back from rushing to Jack's corpse was her father's hands on her shoulders, holding her close. Deville mused that it was a little bit late for him to try keeping her out of trouble now. Where were those restraining hands when she began tying herself to the fool at his feet? "So," Deville said tritely, folding his arms across his chest. He let it lie at that, quirking an expectant eyebrow that managed to demand more than words ever could. The fat man stepped forward, visibly shaking, his leisure suit straining to contain his jiggling girth. Face beet red and slick with sweat, mouth writhing soundlessly, stubby arms flourishing dramatically, he couldn't force himself to make a sound. A hundred useless gestures preceded his first stupefied word. "Uh . . . Mister Deville? Right. Mister Deville." He cleared his throat behind a meaty fist. "Ah, thank you very much . . . for taking that . . . lunatic . . . out of our life. My, uh, daughter and I owe you a great deal." Lisa squeaked indignantly, as if pinched, and opened her mouth to scold her father, but he shot her a warning look that held none of the pleading it had for Deville. When her eyes flared, the pleading returned, and finally she acquiesced, if grudgingly. She pursed her lips tightly and levelled a chilling stare on her father that promised there would be more on this later. He didn't look so sure. Deville nodded benevolently, accepting the false praise with good grace. "My pleasure," he said with utmost sincerity. "Any time." The father feigned a smile and a nod, Deville's civility giving him confidence. "Naturally, as far as we're concerned, none of this ever happened. We'll never speak of it again." Hoping to sever the relationship then and there, he stuck his hand out for Angelo to shake. Deville regarded the hand with a sigh. "Unfortunately, it's not that simple." The man paled and held his eyes shut for a couple of seconds, yanking his hand back as if burned. The slight was like a cool, refreshing breeze compared to The Deville's next words. "I made a promise" -- he gestured towards Lisa -- "to take her life." The man gave his daughter a desperate yet comforting look, then turned back to Angelo, the realization that he would not leave alive finally burning brightly in his eyes. "Surely there is something . . . we must be able to work this out another way." "I'm afraid not." "Very well then," the fat man heaved, once more turning his head to his daughter. "Stay back, love. Daddy knows martial arts." Deville almost laughed out loud, but somehow he managed to restrain his mirth. Not that the fat man helped him any. Turning toward Deville with his chin held high, the valiant martial artist closed the short space between them as if in slow motion, then turned sideways and crouched down a foot, eyeing Deville sideways as if measuring him. He grabbed each pantleg at the thigh and tugged them up a bit, then loudly exhaled all the bad air and reached for Deville's wrist. Staring dumbfounded, Deville checked off a long list of ways he could kill or incapacitate the buffoon as he went through his overly deliberate and entirely ineffectual ritual. Did the man think real life was scripted? That Deville would wait for him to make his labourious move? It was ridiculous. Had the man presented himself as any sort of threat to Deville, things would have been a lot different. In their business, you didn't sit and chat with your enemies, nor allow them an opportunity to hurt you. You certainly didn't just stand there while they approached you to attack. Normally, at least. With Deville, it wasn't really an issue, because he knew the man was as impotent as he was pathetic. "Ju-do . . ." the man yelled, holding the vowel sounds much longer than necessary. Spinning ever so slowly and theatrically away from Deville, he pulled his arm up over his own shoulder, then thrust his body backward, straight into Deville's midsection. ". . . FLIP!" With all his might, every last shred of energy he could muster, the fat man pulled down on Deville's arm, trying to flip him. For his part, Deville let his weight sag, then hooked his leg around one of the mighty trunks supporting the other's extreme mass. There was a grunt, followed by a much quieter "FLIP!", followed by another grunt and a final, weak, "flip?", then the man released Deville's hand, panting. He looked up at his daughter's tear-stained face and shrugged. "Alright," Deville snapped without heart, shoving the man in the back so he no longer had to smell his acrid stench. "I'll let her live, but only on one condition." Spinning, relief turning his face a new shade of red, the father scooted over to Deville on his knees, clutching at the hem of his jacket. "Anything! Name it!" "You see this sand here?" Deville motioned toward the massive mountain of sand that dominated the back half of the dome. The man nodded frantically that he saw it. "Good. You're going to bury all of these bodies in there, so that if anyone comes nosing around to see what's going on, the evidence won't be immediately apparent." The man barely concealed a worried look at his daughter. He wasn't quite sure he had heard correctly. There was no way Deville wanted to try hiding the bodies in the sand. It was the most ludicrous idea he had ever heard. Burying the bodies there would in no way hide them. It might take a few days, or a week, but the subterfuge would not last. Not that that mattered to the father at this point; if Angelo had ordered him to build a towering sandcastle he wouldn't have balked. "Somehow," Deville quipped, grinning wryly, "I don't like the idea of digging my own grave." "Can't say I blame you," the man grumbled. Deciding he'd better get down to business before the erratic Deville changed his mind again, he asked if there was a shovel nearby. The blank, guileless stare Deville gave him was enough to set him hurrying up the side of the hill, digging in with his hands as he went along. Deville offered Lisa a reassuring smile. She glared at him through her eyelashes. Hmmm, came a stirring in the back of Deville's mind, a voice he had never dared to hope was actually gone from his life. It was like the hiss of a wily, temperamental snake living in Deville's head. He imagined he could feel the flickering of the tongue against the front of his skull. I like this. There's hope for you yet, my boy. I'm so glad you approve of what I do with my body. My life. Ooooh, the sarcasm. Ooooh, it burns. A smirk sounded in his head like a snapped twig. Seriously, though, this is good. I like this. You're on the right track to fill my shoes. Deville wanted to strike him down with words, to tell him they weren't so alike as he thought, but the lecture died before it even coalesced as a firm thought. He would just have to ignore Satan again, and trust him to hold mostly to his word. Trusting the Devil. Christ, what was wrong with him? The fat man had dug himself a large furrow in the side of the hill, and he was now waist-deep into his hole. He bent down, disappearing, and then sand started flying up over the side. Most of it slid back down, but he was making progress, slow if steady. He had already dug deeper than Deville thought he'd be able to. Deciding it was time, Deville pulled out the gun that had shot him in the head so many times earlier that day to no avail. He pointed it at where he figured the man's fat head would pop up, then winked at Lisa. Her eyes turned into saucers as she dropped to her knees, opening her mouth to scream. Frankly, Deville didn't understand how she was surprised, or why her father had agreed to play along in the first place. Burying the bodies in the sand? What the hell was wrong with them? This situation was going to take an intense cleanup, not a quick-fix. And even beyond that, they had to have known from the outset that Deville couldn't let them live. It would be the most important rule in their business, had it not been so obvious and instinctual that no one had ever bothered naming it one. If there was one custom to follow above all others, it was this one. Never leave witnesses. Period. Even a harmless doorguard left alive could spoil the perfect crime. Lisa's piercing scream popped her father's head up over the rim of his hole like a mole, the worst instinct in the history of the world to flaunt itself in Darwin's face. The rest of his body followed, like a tantalized cobra coming up from its basket. Deville squeezed the trigger once, slamming a slug into the man's ample abdomen. The father looked down at the blood pouring through his fingers, then up at his daughter. When his other hand reached out as if to shield her, Deville shot him in the chest, purposely missing the heart. With an outburst of rage and pain the man disappeared into the freshly dug grave. Angelo flinched as a shoe whizzed by his head, then another, followed by the tiny woman herself crashing into him. Putting his gun away calmly and grabbing a handful of her voluminous hair, he dragged her kicking and screaming up the tomb of sand. She gnashed at the hand holding her by the hair, then managed to land a solid blow on Deville's nose. No blood came, but The Deville cursed, then drove his foot straight down through Lisa's kneecap, bending her leg the wrong way. When her bloodcurdling scream sounded right in Deville's ear, his fist smashed impulsively into her jaw, shattering it. The screams degenerated into screeching whines, like a quiet siren. "Leave her alone, you fuck." Choked coughing followed the adamant statement, then a loud oomph as Deville tossed the man's daughter down on top of him. She hugged him and clutched at him, worrying about hurting his wounds while trying to ignore the searing pain of her own. They were both crying bitterly, angrily, fearfully, but those sobs were like laughter in the face of the sounds they made when the first wave of sand rained down on top of them. Quickly, almost fervently, Deville kicked pile after pile of sand down onto them, destroying the ledge and burying them alive. They protested, of course, but Deville didn't pay it any more mind than to know it was going on. He imagined they thought him abominably cruel, doing this to them, yet before they had stood idly by, hoping he was dead. Wishing he was dead when hope abandoned them. Neither of them ever pulled the trigger, but they may as well have. Perhaps he was going a little bit too far with his method of disposal, but Deville knew that lessons could be learned even going into death, and he figured the best way his point could be made was through torture. Not the passé, garden-variety torture produced by unimaginative minds with ropes and knifes and manacles. That was the fast food variety of torture as far as Deville was concerned. Real torture was done to the mind, giving false hope to the doomed and then snatching it back just as quickly, only to restore it once more. Driving it to the brink and then pulling it back, then driving it there again, letting them know they're going to die, but offering enough hope that it could turn around at any time, so they could never be sure. Letting their mind play with the possibilities. Letting it magnify and prolong their terror. Their demise. Slumping down to his knees in the sand, utterly drained, Deville could hear the woman's terrified moans wafting up to him, gnawing at his bones. He really was exhausted, though he had done his best to conceal the fact. Dying and coming back once took enough out of him that he could drop into a deep sleep the moment he returned; doing it as much as he had in such a short period made it so he could barely think or stand. Well done, The Devil hissed, pausing momentarily so he could savour the wretchedness of the crying. Angelo could feel him grinning inside his skull. Very well done. Deville concentrated on reducing the voice to nothing, on pretending it didn't exist. He wished he could do the same with the woman's wailing. She deserved it -- she did! -- but still, it was wearing at Angelo. He could feel it tugging at his soul. He imagined the horror she was faced with, buried alive with her father, both of them too injured to dig their way out or find help. He envisioned the numbing terror streaking through her, making her want to crawl out of her skin, or slit her own throat just to end the misery. You know, The Devil's voice perked up teasingly, you keep this sort of thing up and I might just name you my official protégé. He knew that misery all too well. Had he really done that to her without batting an eye? Was he truly that evil? Birds of a feather, you and I. Maybe Satan was right. "Help me!" The muffled cry, as full of desperation as it was resignation, beseeched Deville like a manacle around his heart. Screw you, he told The Devil, deciding he would dig the girl up and shoot her in the dead. What would it hurt him to have her suffer less? When he tried to stand up, though, his body would not cooperate. He tried to flex his legs, to push himself to his feet, but it was like trying to lift a mountain. Fear like he'd never known before seized him by the throat. He couldn't move. His body was numb. He couldn't even feel it there. He could still see, could still hear and smell, and somehow, he could even feel the sand crunching beneath his knees, but it was like he was a powerless passenger in someone else's body. Well well well, The Devil chuckled mischievously, testing out his new arms and legs as he stood. Isn't this nice. The Deville, reduced to a voice in the back of his own mind, unleashed the unholy scream of the damned. You think that was evil? The Devil asked derisively, his laughter rising in both intensity and pitch. Angelo could hear the haunting, echoing cackling crystal clear, not only in his head, but coming in through his ears, mocking him with his own voice. The sardonic sound made it plain that he, Satan, thought it a very pale interpretation of the word. It more than implied that he could do better. "Watch," the mouth of Angelo Deville whispered thunderously. "Learn."
