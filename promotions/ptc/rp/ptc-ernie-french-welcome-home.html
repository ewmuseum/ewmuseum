[IG28: Ernie French] Welcome Home.

18 February 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
AIDS. It will kill you. Killed me. In more than one way. My Career My Marriage My Libido My Spirit And now it’s killing me. * * * Pack up my shit and leave. That’s exactly what my last boss told me when he found out. You’d think, in this mother-culture that we live in these days that he might have had some compassion. Seen it from my perspective. Perhaps given me a backstage role or something. But no, not in this business. Not in the world of professional wrestling. What a disgrace. Just before I left I spat in his face. Freaked him out. At least now, I thought to myself, At least now I’ll be able to go home and see my wife. See my kid. The plane journey was far from comfortable. Economy seating was not something I had grown accustomed to over the last 12 month. But money was tight now, at least until I could find myself another gig somewhere else. At least now, I thought to myself, At least when I get home I’ll be able to go home and see my wife. See my kid. Collecting my luggage I grew excited. A chill ran up my spine as I headed towards the airports exit. Grabbed a cab. Tell the foreigner my address and sit back. Relax. Close my eyes for a minute. * * * The thick accent of the cab driver wakes me. “’Scuse me, Mister. Mister. Thirty Two Eighty Nine Mister.” Who was I kidding? I didn’t have thirty two dollars. I didn’t even pay for the flight home, thReat did. A gesture of pity, I presumed. Luckily, I already had a plan. Naturally, this was not my home address. I told him I would just drop my luggage and get some cash from the house. Walk down the side path and through the gate in to the back yard. Toss my suitcase over the fence and follow it over. Almost too easy. I thought to myself. And it’s then. Right then. Standing in the middle of my perfectly kept lawn that I realised losing my job wasn’t His idea of a joke. This. This was the joke. Another man in my house. Drinking my iced tea with my wife. Luggage through the patio window followed very shortly by a large, angry professional wrestler. Screaming. Grab him by the eye balls and make myself a bowling ball. Feel them pop under the pressure. Drop him to the floor and stomp on his face. Crush his nose and shatter his jaw. Trample on his windpipe and kick the remaining air out of his lungs. He didn’t scream once, but she wouldn’t shut the fuck up. I turn and see her screaming, still screaming. Crying. Begging. I grab her face and shove her across the room. She flies across the dining table and falls to the ground in a heap. Turn the table over and kick her in the temple. Grab her by the hair and drag her to her feet. Break her nose. With my face. Bite her neck and tear out her jugular. Bite the other side and toss her in to the side of the refrigerator. Keep her on her feet by hammering her with body shots until my arms burn. A trail of blood smears down the white refrigerator as she slumps to the ground. She’s still alive. In fact it takes her another two hours to bleed out. In the meantime, I go to see my daughter. My lovely, lovely daughter. * * * It took her a while to recognise me. I can understand that. I was paler, thinner, had some odd marks all over my body. Takes me a while to coax her in to my arms. On to my lap. Oh how I’ve missed her. Oh how I will miss her.
