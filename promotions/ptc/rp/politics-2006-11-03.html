Politics!

3 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-03 We interrupt you on this trip down Gordon Powell's Memory Lane to bring you this paid political announcement Picture this. You're watching television, your favorite show. It cuts to commercial and you see one of those stupid political ads. They run all the time. Usually with one candidate smearing the other one in ugly fashion. It's like a car wreck. You know it's bad, and you hate the sight, but you can't look away. Well, this one's very similar to those you see during election season, like the ones you see on TV at this very point of the year. Yet, it's not the same. Your screen goes from the split-second of blackness between your show break and the commercial's beginning, to a bright field of red with the Republican elephant stamped right in the middle, where it looms like the Eye of Sauron lording over Middle Earth. Your interest is piqued. What have these Republican spinsters sensationally concocted this time to help their failing numbers? Then, the camera starts to zoom out and you see the elephant and the red field really is a close up, an extreme close up of a mask, a mask that's covering a face, a face that's connected to a chiseled body in wrestling attire. You realize that the Grand Old Party has gone off the deep end, or that this isn't a commercial for a politician. "Hello, my name is Gordon Oliver Powell…" The former New Era of Wrestling World Heavyweight Champion, on your television. You're left to wonder what Republican candidate he's endorsing in your district. Politicians these days, you think. They'd stoop to anything to pop a spike in the voting. "…and I'm here on your television because our nation is in crisis." More Republican melodrama. You yawn as you've seen this kind of thing several times before just this past hour. "The Democratic party would have you believe that the foreign policy of our righteous Republican government is failing, that domestically, things are falling apart because the Republicans either have failed to act or are acting improperly." Well, you think, they kinda are, although you know you have it easier than some. You're watching this commercial on a flat-screen plasma television. "Well, if you believe any of what the Democrats have been saying about our fair Republican candidates this season, you have been misled. It's not your fault. The devil comes in many disguises, and he preaches with a forked tongue, with promises of family and loved ones coming home from Iraq as soon as inaugurations take place in January, something that even if they somehow gained every seat in Congress they would never be able to do or intend to do. They play on your emotions, both on this medium and in other places, like on the Internet…" Your head is spinning at the spin, yet you're intrigued at the direction he's taking this. "…and sadly, in the wrestling ring as well." What? you think to yourself, wondering how he's going to tie in ring experiences to whatever candidate he's endorsing. "For the longest time, wrestling, the wrestlers and the crowds, have been the staunchest supporters of the conservative way of thinking, the right way of thinking." You wonder if it's a good thing that wrestling fan rednecks are your party's base. "But now, those things are changing. Liberal thought is creeping into the hearts and minds of wrestling fans, propping up anti-heroes and scoundrels as the men and women they want you to cheer. They present the most morally bankrupt competitors as the ones who will save the day, and they paint men and women like myself as the villains, and why?" Because you're a stodgy douchebag, you're thinking. "Because they have the mindsets of rebellious teenagers, thinking they can run the country with their touchy-feely emotional initiatives that would rather coddle criminals than punish them and raise our children in an immoral lifestyle while pushing God out of the picture altogether. The biggest offender of these is Joshua Kosidlo." Wait a second… you've heard that name before too. He's Powell's opponent in the GTT6 tournament. You rub your eyes as if to believe what you're seeing; a wrestling promo presented as a political commercial. You're disgusted. You feel duped… …yet you can't change the channel. "Mr. Kosildo has shown his blatant disrespect for anything that has to do with living a spiritually healthy and ethical lifestyle. He's a noted drug abuser, destroying his God-given temple for the temporal fix of feeling good, spitting in the face of our War on Drugs because he's a rebel, because he has no respect for authority. Sure, he claims he's clean, but how many times have we seen someone relapse?" This is so good… it has to be fattening. Now you know how your wife feels whenever she's watching soap operas. "And if he was abusing narcotics like cocaine in the past, what's to say he hasn't dabbled in steroid use? Do you really want someone like that representing the wrestling industry in the biggest tournament of them all?" Maybe you do, as you think to yourself how much Powell is making his opponent seem like the one to root for. Republicans are stupid though, right? "If you stand up for truth, justice, freedom and the American way, then I implore you. Come to the arena and cheer for me, Gordon Oliver Powell, the Phantom Republican. Together, we can right the wrong of this derelict being in this tournament. Together, we can avenge fellow conservative bastion Hoyt Williams. Together, we can take back America, one wrestling venue at a time." As the screen fades back to the Republican party elephant overlapping the GTT6 logo, you hear the voice again. "I'm The Phantom Republican, and I approve this message." You sit back in your chair, in a slight daze. Then, suddenly, you snap out of it. You realize you just watched a wrestling promo disguised as a political commercial. And you feel ten times dumber for having been sucked in. At the same time his commercial was running, Gordon Oliver Powell was at a Republican party rally in Altoona, Pennsylvania. Most people know Powell having two passions in his life; wrestling and campaigning. Tonight, his party needed him badly. Rick Santorum, the Republican incumbent in the United States Senate, was behind in the polls and dangerously close to losing his seat to Robert Casey, Jr., son of charismatic late former Governor Bob Casey. Powell had been speaking for the last half-hour. Looking down at his cards, he saw he was at the end of his speech, the part that he hoped would galvanize everyone in attendance to spread the word about Senator Santorum and the GOP in general. "Finally, my brothers and sisters, the Republican Party is about unity, loyalty. You notice what I called you just now, and I truly mean that from the bottom of my heart. I consider every single one of you in this building, and everyone in this country who is a registered Republican, my brother and my sister." Powell looks around the crowded auditorium and he sees his speech is taking hold. Women in the front row are sniffling and reaching for their hankies. Men were nodding and beginning to crack smiles. "The reason why I can do that is because we, the Republican Party, are a family, in my view. In the landscape of politics in this country, there is no equal to us. We stand united and we stand strong. When we act, we act for the common good, a goal that will strive to make America better. We're not a fractured party. When we act, we act as one and we act for positive reinforcement." Powell felt the energy, and was about to unleash more. "Compare us to the Democratic Party, which is, at best, a group of acquaintances who all want to take someone down, destroy them, and in turn, destroy the fabric of our country. All they want to do is take down our President, take down our leaders… take down good, honest men like my brother Rick Santorum. And they don't work together. They don't think of themselves as family. They each have their own agenda, and they're working so many different schemes to take us down, but they can't. They can never take us, the Republican Party, down, and do you know why?" He looked around again, seeing many of the crowd mouthing the answer he was about to give. "Because we're a family. United we stand, and in the long run, we shall never fall. However, tonight, on this eve of the election, we are in danger of falling in the short term. We are in danger of our state falling prey to the lies, the deceit, the subterfuge of the Democratic Party. Our brother, Rick Santorum, needs our help. He is being dragged through the mud, and it's totally unfair. So we must act, and what would we do if our brother came in, covered in mud and in pain? We would fix him a bowl of hot soup! We'd draw him a hot bath! We would tend to his bandages, pulling together to make our brother stronger than ever." The pitch of the room was so energized that Powell could feel it start to boil over. He had them in his hand, and now, he had to strike. "Tonight, I ask you, we must help our brother Rick become stronger than ever. We must go out and spread the word among all our brothers and sister who've been corrupted by the lies and the deceit of the Democrats. We must reach out to these Democrats and let them know that they can join a family if they wanted to let the lies out of their hearts! We must do all we can to make sure our family member is taken care of! And why? Because we stick together! Because we love Rick Santorum like a mother and father love their children, like a child loves his parents, like he loves his brothers and sisters! "And since family is the most important building block to a strong nation, we must unconditionally make sure that our family prevails on November 7th! Thank you and God bless!" The hall erupted in applause. The front row stood up and everyone followed. Powell took a bow and went backstage. Senator Santorum hugged him and thanked him for the kind words. Other various party chiefs shook his hand. Among the glad-handing and congratulations, Powell felt a hand tapping on his shoulder. He turned around to see his personal assistant Myra with a tense look on her face. "Gordon… Gordon…" "Myra, can this wait? I'm busy." "It's… it's about your mother. She was in an accident." Gordon's eyes became wide as he excused himself from the congratulationary Republicans he was schmoozing with. "Is… is she alright?" "Oh yeah. It was a fender-bender, but she got a little whiplash. She was driving along route 30 to go see your father…" "My father still lives in Boca, which is certainly not on route 30. She was going to see her husband… what, number six?" The emotion left Powell's voice and he became suddenly cold. "Oh, I'm sorry." "Yeah, I'm sorry too. Listen… just let the hospital know that I'll call tomorrow." "But… she wanted you to call her today and then go visit her." "Yeah, well, I wanted her and dad to stay together. She can suffer a bit like I suffered." Myra's mouth went agape. "She's your mother! Your family!" "I know… which is why she should start acting like she's my mother, my family." Gordon walked away to go back to the schmoozing. Myra stood there, shocked and dumbfounded, wondering if her boss actually knew what the meaning of the word "unconditionally" really was.
