A Strange Form of Control

21 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-21 The sun shone down upon the man toiling in what seemed to be a large ditch. The head of his shovel slammed down into the wet soil, and the principle of leverage allowed him to push down and remove a large chunk of earth. He paused to wipe the sweat from his brow with a white handkerchief, but it was already wet through and provided little relief. The man’s previously clean vest had steadily progressed from white to grey, with flecks of mud turning the lower portions brown. The man was Paul Goodman, known to the wrestling world as Eegor, the assistant to Doctor Curiosity, and he was working for his brother. A building such as the Curious Laboratory can not kept in shape without a great deal of work, and the large, gothic abode loomed above Eegor in precisely the wrong direction, reflecting the rays of the sun down on to his back, instead of giving him the precious shade he craved. The so-called ditch that Eegor was working in was actually the moat around the laboratory. The laboratory that it surrounded had once been a small castle, though nobody had ever found out why exactly a medieval style castle had been built out in the Mojave Desert. In any case, the moat had been drained, after a team of surveyors had discovered that the base of the moat was leaking water towards the foundations of the laboratory, and the area around it. The moat needed to be resurfaced with a less porous material, and Doctor Curiosity had decided to save money by delegating all of the work down to his brother, Eegor. “How is ze vork coming along?” yelled the doctor, looking over the edge of the moat. Curiosity was wearing a panama hat, complimenting his usual choice of white lab coat and tweed trousers. His only other concession to the heat was a pair of sandals, through which white sports socks poked. Oh, and the enormous parasol he had put up, to provide shade from the oppressive heat. Then there was the small air conditioning unit, running overtime to wash cool air over the doctor, and the fridge, complete with a selection of chilled wines and other beverages. Not to mention the small generator being used to power those appliances, the exhaust fumes from which were being pumped down a small pipe, and deposited into the moat area. Eegor coughed as the fumes blew past him again. “It’s going fine, master. It might be easier if I had some digging equipment other than this shovel, or perhaps someone else to help me…” “Vell, isn’t it just a shame zat zere is nobody around to help you!” laughed the doctor, refusing to take the bait of offering to help. “In zis vorld, Eegor, zere are zose who must toil and struggle, and zose who are allowed to sit back and enjoy ze luxuries. If you are von of ze vons who has to vork, zen zere is usually a reason. Perhaps you lack ze genius of ze other man, or perhaps you owe him a considerable debt.” The doctor paused. “Perhaps both, Eegor. Perhaps both…” The assistant turned his eyes away from his brother, unable to look directly at him. He had been, of course, referring to the time a few years in the past, when Eegor had slept with the doctor’s wife. The marriage had always been crumbling, but the final blows to the doctor’s hope of salvaging it were struck by his own brother. Out of remorse, guilt, and perhaps a little fear of vengeance, Paul Goodman had taken on the guise of ‘Eegor’, and had become subservient to his brother as a way of atoning for his mistakes. Sometimes Eegor was worried that he might be forced to carry out this role for the rest of his life, but deep down he did not resent it. His younger brother had always been prone to mood swings and eccentric behaviour, and in this position he could always keep a close eye on him. Plus, the perks of being close to the world of wrestling were considerable. To be able to live in the kind of environment he had grown up in as a collegiate athlete was something Eegor had trouble resisting the lure of. “Enough standing zere and reflecting, as if you vere thinking of ze past! Get back to vork you lazy scoundrel, before I bring out ze Curious Hitting Device, and get to ze hitting!” The man in the vest picked up his shovel again, and slammed it back into the ground. It was going to be a long day. “I am going to find out how ze prisoner… I mean how ze guest is doing,” said Curiosity, and left Eegor to his labours. ------------------------------------------ Collette awoke to the same sound that she had fallen asleep to. Her eyes slowly fluttered open, shrugging off the effects of a night of fitful sleep, and swam into focus on the television perched a couple of feet in front of her face. A television sat there, playing a loop over and over again of Doctor Curiosity dropping to the mat and pinning Sebastian Moraco. Collette grimaced, and even snarled at the image she was powerless to stop. The doctor had triumphed once more, but this was proof that he could do it without her. He was not going to need her help to win this tournament if he continued on with his current winning ways. The sound of a key turning in the lock to her room made Collette try to look around. She found that this was impossible, and her mind flickered back to the night before Curiosity’s bracket final match. The doctor had threatened her with a blowtorch, and had waved it mere inches from her face. She remembered the heat well, becoming oppressive, and enough to bring blood to her cheeks, yet stopping just short of truly burning her. She had seen his eyes, and realised that he was playing this game for more than she had previously thought. For him, the tournament was not just a game, and nor were her attempts to try and manipulate him into making her money. His eyes had been cold, and it made her think that there might be something inside of him that was dead. Some basic human emotion, some compassion that would stop a normal man from committing violence was missing from Doctor Curiosity. He had come within an inch of scarring her for life, and it had given her the fright of her life. The doctor was not the foolish, funny man she thought he had been. He was something tougher, something harder than his appearance indicated. He hadn’t actually done anything to harm Collette, other than shackling her to the wall and fastening her head into a metal device that did not allow her to turn her point of view away from the television, but still she was fearful that the next visit would end in violence and pain. The key turned, and the doctor entered. “Hey honey, I’m home!” called out the voice of the curious man. Collette gritted her teeth and remained silent. “Vell vell vell, vhat have ve here? You’re still vatching my great victory against Sebastian Moraco? You can’t stand to do anything else zan marvel in how I did it all vithout your scheming, manipulative soul? Vell honey, zat’s all very vell, but I must turn zis tzing off now.” The doctor flicked a switch, powering down the television. With a surprisingly deft touch, he then walked over to Collette and released her head from the device that was holding it in place. The contraption looked like some sort of ancient torture device, all black metal and jagged edges, but it was in fact a design all of the doctor’s own. He almost caressed her hair as he swept it safely out of the path of the locking mechanisms and released her neck from the braces. Within seconds she was free, apart from the chains that attached her arms to the walls. She looked up at him, her make-up smudged around her eyes from when she’d been unable to hold back the tears. The dead of night in the Curious Laboratory was not a reassuring place to be, especially with only a ten second clip of a wrestling match to keep you company. “So, what now?” she asked. Doctor Curiosity looked at her long and hard. Despite his actions prior to the match against Moraco, he was not an overtly malicious man. Sometimes, though, the pressure just got too much for him to bear. Collette had hurt him in the past more than anyone else in the world, and her return had really thrown him. To find out then that her coming back was just an act, just something she was trying to do to extort money out of him, it had upset the doctor. He had tried to play along with her, indulging in a spot of ‘cat and mouse’ with his ex-wife, but in the end he had to become something else, and turn it into a game of ‘cat, mouse and enormous carnivorous beast’. The calculating side of Doctor Curiosity, the side of him that prepared meticulously for each and every match, and made sure that he knew all there was to know about each opponent, was trying to force Collette out of the equation entirely. “Collette, my love. Ve’ve been through some very strange times, but I have to tell you zis… It ends now. You vill take your divorce papers back vith you, and zis shall be over. No more harm vill come to you, and ve need never seen von another again. Do you understand me?” Collette nodded her head. She understood, but she didn’t agree with what he was saying. She needed the money that would come her way by means of being married to the winner of the prestigious GTT4. She had debts to pay, and some of the people who wanted money from her were not the kind willing to wait around and accept excuses. For Collette, milking the money out of Doctor Curiosity was not so much a bonus as a necessity. Fortunately for her, necessity is known as being the mother of invention, and Collette was not without a few tricks of her own. “For me to go quietly, and for me to leave here and accept the divorce with no problems, you’re going to have to give me a lot,” she said, with more confidence than she genuinely felt. “Are you trying to demand things of me?” laughed the doctor. “I really do not tzink zat you are in any position to bargain vith me.” “Well, you think wrong then. I want more than half of your possessions, including this stupid laboratory. Shall we say seventy five percent, and maybe some of your future earnings? You look like you might be getting richer in the next week or two. If you win the whole wrestling tournament, you’ll be good for endorsements… though I don’t know what kind of company would want to employ you to sell anything!” replied Collette. “You are not in a position to bargain here, Collette! Do you really vant to push me here?” “Yes,” replied Collette, and for once she was actually telling the truth. She had no other options. “Vhy do you think I vould even consider your ridiculous offer?” asked Curiosity. “Well, I think that you’re eager enough to get rid of me that you’d be willing to sacrifice some of your money to end this charade.” The doctor shook his head. “Zat doesn’t fly vith me, Collette.” She laughed, and despite the chains, she seemed to stand taller than the doctor, becoming the focus of the room, and the one with the power. “Okay then, there’s also the fact that everyone knows that I have come here. If I end up getting tortured and killed, who are they going to suspect? With our history together you’d be arrested quicker than you can blink. You can’t go around killing people, Chris. The world just doesn’t work like that.” The doctor’s face was torn by indecision. He had no real intention of harming Collette more than was absolutely necessary, but her words were ringing true. That path of action seemed to have been taken away from him. If he let her go though, she could cause enough disruption in his private life to really bite into his preparation time for the Latch Key Kid. “Give me the money and I’ll be gone for good. It’s your only option. I’ve told people that I’m here, and if they don’t see me back soon then they’ll come here asking questions, or they’ll tell the police. Don’t think this is Scooby Doo, Herr Doctor,” said Collette, mocking Curiosity’s accent with her final words. Doctor Curiosity rubbed his chin and thought about whether or not he had any choice at all. He didn’t know how it had happened, but the petite woman in the chains seemed to be entirely in control. He thought briefly about just inflicting violence upon her, but it seemed so stupid. She’d called his bluff, and despite everything he ached to do to her, he had to concede. “Fine, you can have it,” he said, disconsolately. She smiled, triumphantly, and the doctor left the room. His one source of consolation was that he’d still not unlocked her… but it was only a matter of time. ------------------------------------------------------ “How goes ze digging?” asked Doctor Curiosity, having returned to the moat. Eegor was resting on his spade, and getting his breath back after an afternoon of hard work. The sun was dipping behind the horizon, giving the doctor’s brother respite from the punishment of the oppressive heat. “Not too bad. I’m going to put in the concrete tonight, and it should be set by tomorrow. We’ll have the water back in here before your next match. Speaking of which, have you got any plans? What’s this next guy like?” “He’s strange,” replied Doctor Curiosity, though his mind seemed to be elsewhere. “His opponents seem to vanish in mysterious vays.” “Another hitman?” “No, I think zey have mostly been apprehended. Zis ‘Latch Key Kid’ is some sort of do-gooder. Vhile I find his motives alvays laudable… sometimes shortcuts have to be taken. Sometimes bad tzings have to be done.” Eegor nodded, and reached down into a black sports bag that rested near his feet. He withdrew something, wrapped in a cloth, and looked up into the eyes of his brother. “You can have this.” He removed the cloth and presented Doctor Curiosity with a gun. The good doctor recoiled almost instantly, looking at Eegor with a mixture of shock and revulsion on his face. Eegor didn’t move, and slowly the doctor recovered some poise. “Vhat do you intend to use zat for? She von’t be intimidated any more, Eegor. I have decided to give in to her demands. She can have everything she wants.” Eegor laughed a little, before becoming more sombre. “She doesn’t know?” A subtle shake of the head from the doctor. “Still… it doesn’t matter,” continued Eegor. “You can’t let her do this to you. She’ll hold it over you forever. The situation now is one thing, but she wants money from you in the future too, doesn’t she?” This time the doctor’s head bobbed up and down. Yes, that was precisely what she wanted. “You can’t let her take away everything you’ve worked for. She doesn’t deserve any of it! You’re the one risking your neck in the ring against the athletes!” “Vhat can I do? Shoot her? Don’t be absurd, Eegor!” “We could say it was self-defence!” protested the assistant. “Self-defence? One of ze top wrestlers in ze vorld and his brother against an unarmed voman? Are you insane?” “But… but… I can’t let her do this!” complained Eegor. The doctor placed an arm around his brother in conciliatory fashion. Eegor dropped the gun to the floor, and buried his head in his hands. This was not how it was supposed to end. She was not supposed to win. “Let us not bother ourselves vith zese guns, Eegor. Besides, I vould never use something so dull to murder someone. Vhere are ze flashing buttons? Vhere are ze levers zat serve no real purpose?” Eegor laughed, and wiped a frustrated tear from his eye. He nodded his head in understanding, and went off to continue his tasks. Doctor Curiosity was left standing alone. He felt safe that he still knew more than Collette, and that all he could lose would be money. He’d never been in the business for financial gain, so it was something he could afford to lose. What he could not afford to lose, though, would be the match against the Latch Key Kid. He would need to prepare his dossier and get in some training before he could think of tackling his next opponent. He followed his brother out of the moat, stopping only briefly to pick up the gun. “Zese tzings shouldn’t be left lying around,” muttered the doctor. “Somevon could get hurt…”
