Light and darkness

8 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-08 Light. A universal constant without which life as we know it would not, could not, exist. At its basest it is life, without it we wouldn’t be able to survive. On other levels it brings us things that turn simple existence into living. The colours of a rainbow, a work of stunning art, the first glimpse of the soul mate for whom you have always waited. All possible thanks to light itself. From the dawn of mankind we have revered the light. We have called out to it to protect us from the evils of the World and hallowed it as something mystical. Even terms such as good and evil have been transposed into light and darkness. Whether you choose to believe it or not the Bible itself clearly uses this allegory well. The beginning of things, Genesis, states that at the start there was no form, only the void; only the darkness. God’s very first words were to command the light into existence and of course he thought that the light was good. His second action was to create, for eternity, a division between the light and the dark. That is the poet in me waxing lyrical, but even from a cold and scientific point of view light plays an important part. You see the velocity of light itself plays a central role in astronomy and in physics. According to the Einstein's Theory of Relativity, nothing in our universe can exceed the velocity of light; thus, it is a kind of cosmic speed limit against which all other velocities may be measured. It is one of those rarest of things; a proven constant. It doesn’t matter who you are, it doesn’t matter where you are, light always moves at the same speed. So, if light is so good, not to mention the fastest thing in the known Universe against which all speed is measured it beggars a single question. If the light travels so fast then why is the darkness always there before it, waiting? "This has to be simply one of the most amazing weeks ever!" Khaled stood just inside the entrance to the penthouse apartment that belonged to his friend, and wrestling partner, Ember. He raised the bottle of champagne that he held in one hand, slamming the door closed with his foot as he did so, to his lips and took a swig from it, his body shuddering slightly as the cold alcohol hit the back of his throat. "So you have said Khal," Ember stated wearily as he slumped into one of the black leather chairs that graced his sparsely furnished living room "at least four or five times now." Jumping down the three stairs that separated the entrance hall from the living area Khaled joined Ember, sitting down in a chair opposite him and proffering the bottle across the small gap that separated them. With a shake of his head Ember indicated that he didn’t want any and simply allowed the comfort of the chair to surround him. While Khaled may look back over the preceding days and proclaim them to be amazing it wasn’t his body that had been on the line; it had been Ember’s. "Well here is to you bud" Khaled intoned with a solemnity that was slightly marred by the slurred nature of his words, "two massive wins in one week, that is impressive!" "… and a miracle to make it three" Ember drawled ironically, "considering that it is a frikkin’ PCW World champion that I have to face next." He knew that Khaled meant well but no matter how hard he tried to be as enthused as his friend he simply couldn’t. Every muscle in his body ached and he was sure that he had bruises in places that he hadn’t even been aware had existed previously. Cyrus Skar had been the first of the two matches that Khaled was inferring to, and even Ember had to admit that it hadn’t been as much of a challenge as he had assumed that it would be. The point of the fact was that in the normal scheme of things Skar wouldn’t have worried Ember at all; of course no matter how you looked at things the last couple of weeks couldn’t be called ‘normal’. Don’t think about that Ember thought to himself, quickly banishing the thoughts that were fermenting to the back of his mind. Instead the cause of Khaled’s celebrations, and Ember’s physical pain, came to the forefront of his thoughts. He was the new WWA World champion. Even as tired and sore as Ember was he couldn’t help the small smile that grew on his face. He knew that the powers that be within the WWA had deliberately scheduled him in two of the biggest matches of his career in the hope that he would fail one, or more satisfactorily both, of them. He could just imagine the glee on certain people’s faces ‘back home’ if he were to burn out of the GTT. While it hadn’t been his choice to take part in the damn thing in the first place they knew him too well; once he was in it he was adamant that he would do his very best. The fact that he had also had to take part in the WWA’s biggest pay-per-view event of the year, Birthday Bash, at the same time, only two nights after defeating Cyrus Skar – five nights before facing his next opponent in the tournament – didn’t surprise him at all. The WWA saw him as a cancer after all, and wanted to make sure that by trying to split his focus for two matches they would ensure that he would lose the one that they perceived to be the most important. The World title match. Unfortunately for them he hadn’t lost that match either. Cyrus Skar was only the first man to be defeated that week, but he certainly wasn’t the last. Tracer Bullet, along with every active member of the WWA roster who took part in the gauntlet that was the Birthday bash main event, tasted defeat too. So while he had entered the GTT as a relative unknown, a rookie with no pedigree to speak of, he was now not only going to face his biggest challenge ever but also he was facing it as a different man. He was going to face it as a champion. "… I said Earth to planet Ember, come in planet Ember" Breaking his reverie with a little effort Ember looked up to find Khaled staring at him with a grin on his face. The champagne bottle was resting on its side on the table between them and Ember realised that some time must have passed for Khaled to have finished it. "Sorry, what did you say," Ember asked apologetically, "I was miles away." "I noticed, and I was saying that that Dorkstar isn’t anything to be worried about you know, I mean he has been out of wrestling so long that people even thought that he had died," Khaled quipped "but you know, my friend, maybe the docs are right after all." Khaled’s voice trailed off and he stared at Ember expectantly, waiting for the eruption that he was sure was coming. The WWA officials, in their latest endeavour to show Ember who the bosses actually were, had decided that since his ‘episode’ as they had taken to calling it medical treatment was called for. The episode in question, as far as Ember was concerned, was nothing to be worried about. However, as the WWA medical official had stated with more than a little enjoyment, waking up in a street three blocks from one’s house was definitely ’something’ to be worried about. The night that Ember had received the official invitation to take part in the GTT, arranged, he had later discovered, by the WWA themselves without his knowledge, something had happened to him. He remembered passing out, or fainting, in his apartment only to awaken hours later in the middle of the City, surrounded by a crowd of onlookers. His lawyer had quickly arranged his release from hospital, but not quick enough to stop the press from finding out about it; from there it was only a matter of time before the WWA also found out. They had tried to use it against him, citing medical liability as a factor from removing him from active competition. It was only his lawyer, again, who had forestalled his removal to the injured list but only at a compromise. Ember could stay active, could keep wrestling, but he had to see a specialist. He had to see a psychologist. Ember felt his jaw tighten and the sensation of his teeth grinding against each other as the thought of having to visit a shrink came back to him. The distaste he felt at having to do this was extreme but as his lawyer had stated it was the only way to keep doing what he wanted, and besides which he had managed to garner one other, small, concession out of the WWA. He would get to choose his own doctor. "What the Hell do you mean by that Khaled?" Ember tried to keep his tone neutral, knowing that the bottle of champagne on the table was the second one that Khaled had consumed that night. He had watched his larger friend drink gallons, seemingly, of beer and lakes of whiskey and still be able to walk a straight line better than a circus-trained acrobat. Champagne, though, seemed to go right to his head. So while Ember knew that the alcohol was voicing itself through Khaled Ember still couldn’t keep all of the ire out of his voice. "Well you know Ember," Khaled started to reply a little louder than usual, counting of a list on his fingers, "sleeping in the street in the middle of the day, having nightmares every time you sleep, seeing strange women who no-one else can see and now zoning off into your own little world … that can’t be normal can it?" Khaled didn’t notice the tension that crept over his friend’s body; he didn’t hear the cartilage in Ember’s fist cracking as his hands turned into fists. He couldn’t, thankfully, see through the mask to view the expression of fury that covered Ember’s face. He would never know how close he had come to provoking Ember into something that they would both regret. If anyone else had spoken to Ember like that then they would have most definitely lived to regret it, even though they may have wished that they hadn’t. Pain was like that, Ember knew, and sometimes even the strongest of men would cry like children and beg for death rather than face pain. Khaled, however, was different. He wasn’t everyone else. He was the only person that Ember called friend, the only person that Ember cared about. That was the only reason that Ember called back the fire that had coursed through his body before it could reach out and consume his friend. "Khaled, I told you all of that in confidence" Ember stated through clenched teeth, "and you had better not have told anyone else, especially not in the WWA, about any of that!" Ember knew that it was bad enough that his home fed knew that he had passed out, or at least assumed that it what had happened anyway, but he knew that there would be nothing that they could do if they found out the truth of the matter; if they found out everything that had happened to him, everything – and more – that Khaled had just rhymed off. "… ‘course not, Em" Khaled slurred as he patted the side of his nose and winked one eye, "thatsh a secret." Ember breathed a small sigh of relief at the words, knowing that he could trust Khaled even if he did infuriate him sometimes. A small smile played across his face, his body relaxing, as he watched Khaled fall asleep in front of his eyes. It only took a couple of minutes before Khaled was lying back in the chair, snoring heavily, as a thread of drool threatened to fall from its precarious perch on his bottom lip. Finally Ember thought quietly to himself as he watched his friend sink deeper into slumber. Getting slowly to his feet Ember draped a blanket over Khaled’s body before walking back up the steps to the entrance to the apartment. He reached into a closet beside the door and pulled out a long black coat and wide brimmed hat. Taking one last look over his shoulder at Khaled’s sleeping form he left the apartment and closed the door quietly behind him. He had known that talking Khaled into a celebration over the dual wins would be the best way to get some time alone. Ever since the ‘episode’ Khaled had turned into his shadow, never leaving him alone for more than a few minutes at a time. While it was gratifying, and filled him with an unfamiliar warmth, to know how much his friend cared it was also something that Ember was finding difficult to deal with. He was, by nature, a solitary man and having to share his personal space with someone, even someone that he truly cared about, was difficult to get used to. This, though, was something that he had to deal with alone. "Jesus Ember don’t you see that they have played you?" Khaled had asked, his voice rising in anger. It wasn’t Ember that he was angry with, but the WWA officials. By forcing him to see a psychologist they had worked him into a corner. Khaled knew very well just how adept Ember was at playing mind games off his own, the psychological element that made him such a devastating opponent, but Ember was seemingly oblivious to exactly what visiting a shrink would entail. "Its nothing to worry about Khal" Ember had tried to reassure his friend, but his words only seemed to frustrate him even more. "Of course it is," Khaled had interrupted, "you are going to be seeing a doctor whose job is to see if your mind is sound – and the first thing that he is going to see is a man who refuses to take of his mask!" Khaled had done well to stop himself from pointing out that Ember didn’t seem to even trust him enough with the knowledge of who was behind that simple piece of black material. He had tried once but had had to settle from Ember’s statement that the mask was all that mattered about him, that the mask was him. Khaled knew exactly how that would sound to any doctor, and was furious that Ember couldn’t see it too. "Like I said Khal, it is nothing to worry about" Ember had reiterated, "I have it covered, trust me." Ember had sounded so sure, so confident, that Khaled had known that it was pointless trying to argue with him. He would probably have had more luck telling the wind not to blow or the fire not to burn, he knew, than he would talking Ember around once his mind was settled. Instead he silently resolved to ensure that he was with Ember when he spoke to the doctor and do his best to somehow diffuse the situation when the topic of the mask came up. Lying there, almost unconscious in his drunken stupor, Khaled recalled nothing of that internal promise; oblivious to the fact that Ember had left the apartment and was already pulling up outside the downtown practice of the psychologist that his lawyer had found for him. Handing a few loose notes over to the taxi driver Ember gazed up at the three-storey brownstone building that he had been deposited at. With a deep indrawn breath he hunched his shoulders and walked resignedly up to the door, ringing the bell and waiting for an answer. A female voice greeted him, her tone somewhat electronic, and when he stated his name the door was quickly buzzed open. Making his way into the building Ember looked around at the décor in the dimly lit hallway and then turned to face the voice that spoke to him. "Hello there Mr Ember, my name is Carole Gordon" the clear female voice rang out with subtle confidence, "Doctor Carole Gordon; I am pleased to meet you." A small smile played around the corner of Ember’s mouth as he walked over and took the proffered hand of the beautiful blonde woman in his own. "Pleased to meet you too, Doc" he said lightly as he felt the small hand grip his own, staring in triumph at her other hand - almost wishing that Khaled could have been there to see that he had indeed had it covered – and the white cane that she held.
