A Divine Tragedy

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 "Dance with the Deville and the Deville don’t change. The Deville changes you." -A slightly paraphrased Joaquin Phoenix ~<>~<>~<>~ A Prologue… "Are you out of your fucking mind?" Steven Caldera was never much one for theatrics. As owner and operator of Global Championship Wrestling, a subsidiary of his umbrella company Caldera Enterprises, he had come to be known as a man who could rationalize anything to anyone just as long as the end result was a profitable gain for the company account. His business had flourished based solely on the irrefutable fact that he was one of the most level-headed hunters in the ever expanding jungle of American economics. "I mean… Angelo Deville, for Jesus’ sake! Are you fucking crazy?!" Just not today. "Ain’t nobody ever accused me of bein’ sane, Steven." Rich Rollins’ expression turned bitter, more a result of his quickly disappearing whiskey sour than any sort of emotional affectation. "And if you’re lookin’ to change my mind, you won’t. So don’t try." Caldera scoffed, but the steady look in Rollins’ eyes put to rest any half-hearted attempt to talk some sense into them. Steven Caldera contented himself instead with a deep swig of cold Starbucks coffee as he watched the line at the counter slowly expand toward the door, scanning the tired faces of another dozen nameless ants trudging their way through the numbing routines of the colony. "Why him?" Caldera finally blurted, breaking an uneasy silence. "Why Deville? Of all the people in the wrestling world, why did you have to call out Angelo? Just for my own peace of mind. Please." The Renegade began to chew absently at his upper lip as he mulled the question over. He knew it had been coming; it had been smeared all over Caldera’s pleading face from the moment he had walked through the door to the second his slack-jawed mouth started moving. Staring outside at the frenetic lunch hour frenzy that had become a trademark of Chicago’s downtown Wabash Avenue, Rich Rollins’ mind began to wander off into another place altogether. He knew exactly why he had chosen Angelo Deville. "Rollins?" Whether Caldera would understand, well, that was another issue. "Rollins!" He quickly snapped out of his self-indulgent daydream, blinking his way back to the present. Caldera had adopted a rather skeptical look, almost as if he weren’t sure he wanted to know what the Renegade was thinking after all. It made Rollins smile all the same. "If I’m going to get beat, then I want to get beat by the very best," he said matter-of-factly. "And if I win, I want to move on knowing I beat the very best to get there. It’s personal, Caldera. Just let it be." Caldera frowned, offering little more than a dejected shrug. "Fair enough." "We done?" He’d already said his peace, not to mention both his muscles and his nerves were just itching to unload on a heavy bag. Caldera nodded once, suppressing an obvious sigh. "Yeah, we’re done." Smacking pursed lips after tasting his bitter cup of corporate coffee, Steven Caldera could only shake his head. "But you still didn’t answer my question." The skin on Rollins’ unshaven face tightened in a dry smile as he rose, collecting the black suede jacket that hung over his chair. "That’s ‘cause I’m all out of answers. See you soon, Steven." And with that, the Renegade took his leave. ~<>~<>~<>~ I need a hobby, thought Rollins as he drove in silence through the midtown madness of the Miracle Mile. He couldn’t listen to the radio anymore. Every time he did, PTC’s overplayed half-dozen GTT5 commercial spots assaulted his sanity, reminding him over and over that one of the biggest matches of his career was mere days away. Bookies from Las Vegas had been ringing his cell phone nonstop, each one of them wanting to know the same stupid answers to the same stupid questions. "You feelin’ good about Deville, champ?" they cooed. As if his saying "yeah, sure" had any bearing on the odds. That’s why Rollins had eventually chucked his cell phone into Lake Michigan. He figured it was symbolic of something, but of what exactly he wasn’t quite sure. The silence since had been golden, nonetheless. His television? After the first Hoyt Williams promo, a solid boot through the screen had shut them both up for good. It was just too much to take. Swerving around a sluggish city bus, Rich Rollins maneuvered his precious baby blue Mustang towards the freeway ramp, ready to head back to the relative haven of the abandoned warehouse he called home. With any luck he figured he might be able to get some actual rest before the big fight. Primetime Central wanted him on location a day in advance for promotional purposes, whoring out his image so the office could turn another dime on his likeness. Rape his privacy so they could hawk and shill their vaunted Global Champion. And they were doing a damn good job of it, for at just that moment a massive billboard approached him alongside the highway. Where not a week earlier an image of the local Channel 8 broadcasters had sat for months, greeting travelers on their way into and out of the downtown loop, the smug visage of Rich "The Renegade" Rollins sneered instead, his Global title glistening as the great hype machine kept turning. "Mommy, where do migraines come from?" asked the inner child of Rich Rollins’ overactive imagination. "From the Stress Stork, kido," his imaginary mother replied. "And from not watching where you’re driving." Wait, what? It was the only thought that ran through Rollins’ head before he realized his attention had lingered on his own billboard a few moments too long. The last thing Rollins swore he heard before rolling his car over the concrete median and into head-on traffic was the sound of Angelo Deville’s voice. The laughter was unbearable. ~<>~<>~<>~ The First Echelon: Expiation Please. Not yet. Not like this. Oh… oh god… "God has nothing to do with this." I don’t want to die. "Well good, because you’re not dead. Not yet, at any rate." I’m not? "No. Now open your eyes before I pluck them from your thick-headed skull." It were as if Rich Rollins had stumbled across the proverbial rainbow as he allowed his senses to slowly drift back to him. His abruptly black world had just as suddenly exploded into full technicolor as an endless horizon of charcoal water met his burning eyes, blinking under the eerie glow of a voilaceous sky. Sitting on a boat built for two, the Renegade pulled himself upright as he gazed over his surreal surroundings. That’s when he saw the second passenger. "You!" The word came out choked, almost panicked. Clad in the finely-tailored pinstripe drapes of the damned, Angelo Deville offered a quirky smirk as he dipped his hand into and broke the water’s placid surface. His mounting shock and confusion intensified as Rollins looked into the ocean’s onyx face, for in its reflection a righteous storm was ravaging the sky above. Jerking his eyes heavenward, the Renegade wanted to cry. There wasn’t a single cloud in in the red and purple sky. "Before you jump to obvious conclusions, no, I am not Angelo Deville," the Deville-esque figure said. "I’m what you might call the personification of your superego." The Renegade’s brain refused to operate. "Huh?" was about the most intelligible response he could muster. "In other words, I’m your Guide." The Angelo look-a-like looked straight into Rollins’ pale face, emotionless as a snake. "To where?" Even though he already knew the answer, Rich Rollins couldn’t stop himself. The Deville smiled. "Through Hell, of course." It was then that a bolt of lightning lashed up through the reflection of the stormy sea, releasing a very real spray of inky water not thirty feet from their tiny boat. Rollins began to shake involuntarily. "Your own arrogance put you here, Rollins. Consider this a penance for acting like a spoiled fucktard." The Angelo-like apparition sighed. "You’re on the Ocean Osiris now, and there won’t be any turning back until you’ve seen whatever it is you need to see." Trying to work moisture into the desert his mouth had understandably become, the Renegade grabbed hold of the boat’s edge to help him balance. Inhaling slow and deep, he took a few moments to let the gravity of what was apparently happening to him sink in. Finally, he asked: "What exactly is it that I need to see?" Deville’s damning imitation offered little more than a casual shrug. "It’s your Hell, not mine. You tell me." ~<>~<>~<>~ Long before any trace of dry land floated over the horizon, the sound of familiar music filled Rollins’ ears. "What is that?" asked Rollins. His Guide remained quiet, looking out over the bleak expanse of the Ocean Osiris with unusual interest. Angelo’s eyes narrowed sharply, seemingly looking beyond the realm of reality and into a world where Rich Rollins most certainly did not want to follow. "There." His Guide pointed into the distance, but Rollins could still see nothing. "We’re almost there." "But we haven’t even moved-…" Rollins was cut off with a sharp gesture. "Ssshhh. Listen." When the tiny island finally materialized over the bridge between sea and sky, a strange soothing sensation began to calm the Renegade’s frazzled nerves. The sight of something other than black water was enough to ease his anxiety, but all things considered, he still wasn’t exactly in good humor. Once the small island came into clear view, the strange music’s influential melody finally sunk home. Rich Rollins’ heart sunk through his heels. As their boat finally beached itself in shallow water, the small isle’s entire surface became instantly visible to the Renegade. No larger than a football field, a single stage filled the center of the tiny atoll, the only standing structure amongst a mob of bobbing heads. Rollins didn’t want to believe his ears at first, but once his feet touched the smooth colorless sand, the figure on stage was indisputable. Gliding through the chorus of "Purple Haze" to a crowd of soulless spirits, Jimi Hendrix played on. Rollins’ mouth opened in wordless wonder as he watched the grotesque shapes of creatures that might have once been human thrashing around every angle of the massive stage, fueled by the blistering aural assault that poured forth from Hendrix’s solo guitar. Taking notice of Rollins’ troubled expression, the Deville duplicate placed a tender hand on his shoulder. "In his own words, music was Hendrix’s religion." The Guide motioned to the crowded masses sadly. "Unfortunately for him, the Pearly Gates don’t open for a flaming guitar." Allowing himself to be led like a horse by the bit, Rollins followed his Guide closer to the metaphysical mosh pit that surrounded the stage. "This almost seems like someone else’s Heaven," he quietly mused. The Deville grinned over one shoulder. "I’m sure it is." "Who are these people?" the Renegade asked. "Who, them?" Rollins’ Guide gave the moshers a cursory glance. "Those aren’t people. They’re part of the show. Look closer." Even as the words fell from his escort’s mouth, the tiny faces in the mud below the rampaging creature’s feet had already started to become visible. The soft moans of the damned drifted from under the crowd, falling into morbid counterpoint to the acid-rock that blared from Hendrix’s corpse-like fingers. The hairs on the back of the Renegade’s neck snapped to full attention. "Oh Jesus," he breathed. "Those are the real lost souls," his Guide replied. "This is more or less a level of purgation, set aside for most of the world’s atheists and the like. These are the people who refused to believe in anything save themselves, and in punishment, they’re stomped into the soil of Osiris." They began to circle the outskirts of the macabre crowd, heading toward an open side of the stage. The closer they came to the rollicking ravers, the more uneasy Rich Rollins felt. "Where are you taking us?" he finally implored of his oracular host. "We’ve got a backstage pass." Without breaking his stride, the Deville-but-not-the-Deville pushed his way into the demonic cluster. "Just keep up." And so he did. ~<>~<>~<>~ Rollins had always envisioned the gates to Hell as a massive husk of intimidating black iron. Ten-foot serpents were supposed to be keeping watch, hissing and spitting globs of acidic venom at every passing carcass until the flesh was scoured off each one in turn. He had imagined inverted crosses of fire and wailing sinners pleading of their eternal souls to be at every footfall, another impaled yet still screaming corpse around every corner. Thus, when a cheery-faced ticket taker asked to see his bus pass, the Renegade was a little short on words. "He’s with me," said the Deville with a wink. Locked away in a large Plexiglas booth, the woman actually blushed. Pushing through a large yellow turnstile, Rich Rollins couldn’t help but glance down at the meter: 47,538,110,438,364 Oddly enough, his own number didn’t tick as he cranked his way through the gate. A large yellow Charter bus rumbled expectantly on the other side of the gateway, and Deville was already hanging his head out of a window. "You aren’t especially thrilled about the prospect of being left here, are you?" he called. "I don’t know, I could listen to Jimi for a real long time," Rollins snapped back, already trotting his way up to the purring vehicle. Not a single creature - living or otherwise - occupied the bus. Rollins frowned as he swung by the driver’s seat, his total and utter perplexity not going by unnoticed by his host. "Just hit that button on the console," said Deville’s doppelganger, indicating the blinking green light next to the radio. A steady bead of sweat ran its way down Rollins’ nose only to take a swan dive onto the sticky black plastic that coated the floor. Pushing through his hesitation, the Renegade finally pressed the button. An intercom clicked to life. "OnStar, how can I help you?" Rollins blinked. He turned around helplessly, searching the smooth features of Deville’s imposing face for an explanation to this insanity. All he gleaned was a glimpse into Angelo’s eyes, and the message behind them was quite clear. "Sit down, Rollins." His host’s suddenly not-so-hospitable tone commanded obedience, and the Renegade wasn’t about to piss off the only lifeline he had in this entire dizzying nightmare. Perking his chin, the Deville addressed the OnStar operator. "We’re headed out of Osiris, and in a bit of a hurry." "Why the hurry?" Rollins asked. His host ignored him. Instead, he turned to Rollins with a devilish grin that seemed far too natural, even for one such as Angelo Deville. "Hold on, sinner." The bus lurched forward on its own accord, rumbling slowly over the sandy expanse of land that stretched between the stage and the black waters of the Ocean Osiris. As the bus picked up speed, Rich Rollins began to get anxious. The waves crawled ever closer. With nothing but the sterile green light of the blinking OnStar button to fill the void, the Renegade’s world plunged into dark uncertainty as the bus plunged into the back waters. Rollins caught one last glimmer of violet moonlight as the sickly waves enveloped his windows. He prayed it wouldn’t be the last time his eyes saw natural light, but it was hard to keep the optimism going as they sank quietly into the sea. ~<>~<>~<>~ The Second Echelon: Duplicity "If you aren’t Deville, then who the hell are you?" The Renegade was starting to get sick of this game. Even in the dark, Rollins could feel the heat of his host’s pretentious gaze. "You can call me Gideon," the Deville double finally replied. "It was my name in life, but it held little meaning for me in death. If that doesn’t suit you, be my guest to call me anything you like." How about asshole? Rollins thought darkly. "How about… no?" said Gideon. Silence again. Rollins swallowed. Hard. ~<>~<>~<>~ They arrived on a plateau far below the surface of the sea. Before Rollins was aware of what was happening, he realized their bus had vanished and he was standing on dry land, but where he was exactly he couldn’t even begin to guess. The Renegade’s ethereal Guide Gideon struck a match in the darkness, producing a torch from God knows where. The flames licked Gideon’s mockingly familiar face as he said: "We’re here." Leading by torchlight Gideon began to walk, and all Rollins could do was follow blindly. The ground was layered in uneven cobblestone, littered every few paces with the tattered shreds of a shirt or charred remains of a pair of business slacks. When the Renegade looked closer, he realized that every article of wayward clothing was either a business suit or tuxedo. His brow furrowed in contemplation. "So, are you gonna tell me where we are or I am gonna have to guess?" Rollins pried. Gideon spoke toward his destination without turning to look Rollins in the eye. "Patience is a virtue that you could really use more of," was the only answer he offered. Rolling his eyes, Rich Rollins continued to walk the cobblestones. A large wall constructed of what looked like ivory materialized out of the darkness, striking Rollins as an ominous gate just itching to share the secrets that lay within. Gideon led them both to an incredibly ordinary-looking wooden door set inside the smooth bone, running a free hand across its unmarred surface. Reaching out in turn, Rollins pressed his fingertips against the glossy structure… …and immediately jerked away as if scalded. The ivory wall felt clammy and damp to the touch, filling the Renegade’s nostrils with a putrescent odor that nearly knocked him off his feet. "YOU AREN’T WELCOME HERE," an impossibly deep voice bellowed from the shadows. Rollins almost did fall over, at that. "The mortal is with me," Gideon responded quietly, reaching his hand for the doorknob. "Pay it no mind, Rollins. Your presence here upsets some of the regulars, as you could probably guess." And with that, his Deville-like attendant threw the door wide open. ~<>~<>~<>~ Once again, the scene on the other side of the ivory wall caught Rollins completely by surprise. A field of computer screens sat across endless rows of linked desks and tables, wired back-to-back across miles of cable that stretched far beyond the Renegade’s visible spectrum in every direction. Each console was occupied by a different man, woman or child, each of them held mesmerized by the images that scrawled across their screen. Fidgeting nervously with the knot in the back of his bandanna, Rollins would have swallowed had his mouth not turned to sand. "The beauty of the internet," Gideon intoned sarcastically. "Easily the most tangled web of deceit ever conceived." They walked amongst the rows at random, getting a glimpse of a few terminals and their inhabitants. One woman sat rigid with her face practically licking the screen, watching in dejected humiliation as every lie she ever told was played back to her in streaming RealOne video. A nun watched apathetically as she made love to an alter boy. Another man wept as he saw himself professing his undying love for his wife, only to turn around and fuck her best friend. It wasn’t until Rollins saw a face he recognized that he finally stopped. There, sitting in silence, was John F. Kennedy. He watched in slack jawed denial as he and Marylin Monroe pleasured one another in crisp Quicktime detail. Sitting next to him was Marylin Monroe, blinking bug-eyed baby blues at her own moments of damning infidelity. "I am not a crook!" JFK screamed to nobody in particular. "Hey, that’s my line!" came Richard Nixon‘s indignant reply from two rows over. Shaking his head sadly, Rich Rollins shot Gideon a wry smirk. "This must be the politician’s ward, huh?" His host shrugged. "They’re all over the place, actually. Although this is where most of them turn up, a few find themselves in hotter water with the Big Man than an eternity spent looking at homemade porno. Sometimes quite literally." "Maybe I should stop screwing my psychiatrist’s wife," Rollins reflected thoughtfully. Strolling through the herd of adulterous sheep, Gideon beckoned for Rollins to follow. "Don’t stare too long," he admonished. "The internet is a seductive mistress." Renegade At The Wheel Chicago, IL - At approximately 12:47pm, local celebrity and professional wrestler Richard Andrew Rollins met with disaster along the Bishop Ford Expressway when he collided head-on with opposing traffic. He had been counting the stairs to pass the time, but had lost track somewhere in the quadruple digits. The slow curve of their passage prevented him from getting any inclination of where they were headed, but Rollins had started to get the sense that it really didn’t matter what he did and didn’t know. He knew he wasn’t going to like it, and that was good enough. The awkward silence between Rollins and his escort was starting to grate on the Renegade’s nerves. Trying to say something - anything, really - to break the tedium of their endless journey into the nether, he spoke up. "So, what did you do in your so-called former life? Did you have one worth remembering?" Gliding just a few steps ahead of Rollins, Gideon replied in a voice that suggested it wasn’t a subject that was open for discussion. "I was a medical practitioner." He left it at that. It wasn’t near enough to sate Rollins’ inquisitive nature. "What the hell did you do to end up down here? Did the townsfolk get wise to yo-…" "You’ll have to be quiet as we enter," Gideon interjected coldly. "If the debate hasn’t started yet, it soon will." "Debate?" Every step forward brought with it yet another unexpected turn, and at this point Rollins was surprised he could even be surprised anymore. It was then that their ceaseless spiral finally reached its end in the form of a locked iron gate. Whipping out the sleek six inches of an ebony switchblade from his breast pocket, Gideon approached the wrought iron lock like a burglar about to pillage the village stock house. Steering the black blade into the keyhole’s expectant crevice, the eternal Guide let its full length slide into the heavy hasp before halting to turn eager eyes on the Renegade. "Come closer. I want you to see how this is done." A healthy hesitation caught Rollins in the throat, but he willed his way through it without looking back. "See how what is done?" "This." Promptly withdrawing his knife from the ancient fastening, Gideon’s face was a hollow mask as he lunged at his willing pupil. Before his mind had the chance to recoil, Rich Rollins’ body felt the cold stinging bite of the black blade’s bitter edge sliding across both of his wrists, numbing sense and dulling sound in one swift slice. Stumbling backwards to fall up the uneven staircase, the Renegade’s mouth fell to the floor as he looked down at his arms, both displaying the open pink wounds of his freshly opened arteries. He watched the deep crimson flow of his own blood begin to spatter across the stones at his feet as if it were a cruel joke, mouthing wordless curses that held no breath to support their meaning. A sudden weight fell upon his brain, numbing his reactions as if he were twelve drinks deep into an all-night binge. Crouching down to meet his rebellious rook at eye level, Gideon slipped the switchblade back into his pocket as he spoke in a tone so soft that it almost eased Rollins’ sudden panic at the first word. "Quiet now," he whispered. "Those who dwell beyond this gate are guilty of a sin so vulgar that it breeches the natural order of life, for in their final hour, they took their own." Finally surrendering to the abyss that swirled in his brain, Rich Rollins faded to black. In the depths of ultimate vulnerability, his spirit moved beyond. ~<>~<>~<>~ When Rich Rollins awoke - if you could call it "an awakening" - his initial response was one that wasn’t exactly customary for a man of his testosterone-fueled lifestyle. He screamed. An entire lecture hall filled with annoyed faces turned around to look straight at the Renegade, which did little to calm his hysteria. His eyes flew across a hundred sour expressions, and some of those expressions belonged to bodies that he knew. Vincent van Gogh shook his head sadly, absently rubbing his one good ear. Virginia Woolfe cast a shrewd scowl in his direction before resuming her needlework, knitting scarves for the children she never had. Sigmund Freud pushed a pair of wire rimmed glasses up his beak-like nose, mumbling quietly to himself. Kurt Kobain shrugged his shoulders in lackadaisical disinterest, slumping back in his bucket chair. Sitting in the back row of the Auditorium of the Damned, Rich Rollins scrambled around in his uncomfortable plastic stadium seat as he looked down at his arms. The wounds were completely gone as if they never were. Crying out in confusion, Rollins’ desperate howls were silenced as a sharp poke nudged him in the ribs. Quite nearly hyperventilating, Rollins jerked around to see which one of the squalid spirits had touched him. His racing heartbeat was quickly filled with unexplainable rage. Raising a finger to his lips, Gideon shushed his frantic pupil’s protests with that damnable Deville-like smile as the assembly burst into uncontrollable applause for the two figures who emerged onto the waiting stage. Entering from opposite wings to take their place behind matching podiums built of sturdy oak, Hunter S. Thompson and Ernest Hemingway acknowledged the crowd with little fanfare. Their dress was strikingly similar; a bearded giant of a man, Hemingway wore an unremarkable pair of bermuda shorts and worn out loafers while Hunter sported a fading Hawaiian shirt spotted with palm trees and dark red sunglasses, puffing away on a newly-lit cigarette. As each man situated himself behind their chosen soap box, the gathered masses fell into a respectful hush. "I think I’m about to pass out," the Renegade moaned to himself. "If you do, I’ll rip your eyelids off," Gideon warned. Offering his idol a courteous nod, Hunter S. Thompson exhaled a cloud of thick nicotine as he waited for his turn to speak. Clearing his throat, Hemingway began: "You are all here to witness a transcendence," he began. "We are all of us sinners, and despite what you’ve been led to believe, there are no saints sitting in this room today. Not me. Not my esteemed college Mr. Thompson. Not anybody. We are all here because we struck the eye of the Creator with a hot iron the day we decided to take His plan and change it by taking the life that was given to us, entrusted into our care only to be snuffed out in a fit of selfish violence that not a one of us can go back and correct." Leaning over his dais, Hemingway grinned madly. "Until today." A low rumble started to emanate from the crowd, but Ernest silenced it with a raised hand. "One of us will be allowed to pass back through the Gate that keeps us chained away in this nefarious cesspool of self-loathing and doubt, devoid of His presence and absolved from the responsibility we chose to surrender. Unfortunately, that man will not be me." Turning grizzled old eyes on his opponent, Hemingway gave the floor to Hunter. The Good Doctor took a long drag of his cigarette, drinking in the reverence and respect that oozed off the captivated crowd like sweet nectar. Straightening his back, Hunter offered a queer sort of smile as he addressed his audience. "So, who here got sick of all the bullshit and decided it was time to take matters into their own hands?" A hundred palms spread into the air as every one of the sordid suicides admitted to their sin. All of them, that is, save one. Ernest Hemingway shook his head. "I was merely trying to clean my shotgun." Rounding on the Great Poet, Hunter snorted. "Sure. And Courtney Love killed Kurt Kobain." "Fucking bitch," Kobain mumbled from the fifth row. "Face it Ernie," said the Doctor, "as the conservative nomenclature of the day would say, you ‘homicided yourself.’" Rollins tried to restrain himself, but he couldn’t stop his sudden laughter. Ernest Hemingway shied away from Hunter’s accusing stare. Satisfied, Hunter S. Thompson rounded back on his audience. "I’m pissed as hell to be here, and that’s not meant as a pun," he stated. "There are a lot of people that deserve to be right here next to me, but I don’t see Richard Nixon or J. Edgar Hoover amongst you today. I don’t see the veritable laundry list of third-world dictators and pretentious politicians’ faces in this crowd, men who deserve more than I to be sitting in this wretched cage of denial and damning isolation. It’s Hell folks, it’s not exactly a tropical resort in the Keys." A tingle ran up Rollins’ spine as the crowd replied: "Amen." "And yet I can’t tell a lie, even though at this point the truth is just a reflection of an ideal that holds no meaning for us anymore. That being said, the truth is that I don’t deserve to pass beyond that hateful Gate, because my sins are unforgivable. I made my bed, and I’m tired of fighting powers that are larger than I am or will ever be. It’s time that I lay down." Rollins’ brow creased in total and utter confusion. "What the hell is this, then?" he implored of his Guide. "This is insane. If they want to petition for their release, why are they both refusing to be set free?" Patience settled on Gideon’s smooth and knowing features as he sighed. Pointing to the aisles that ran alongside the audience, he indicated the robed squadron of listless spirits that had begun passing out tiny cups of Kool-Aid like communion wine. His mind may have not been able to force his body through the motions, but Rich Rollins’ body moved to its own rhythm. Here, in the deepest bowls of his own personal Hell, the victorious Doctor was handing Rich Rollins a victory of his own, a victory he had sought and dreamed about for years. Temptation seized hold of Rollins’ entire being, urging him to take the tantalizing offer before it was too late, before he woke up as a paraplegic and his wrestling career was over and done with. He could see the power in Curiosity’s penetrating green eyes, surging with confidence and limitless possibilities.
