Everybody's Fool Part Two: Questionable Reality

24 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-24 Look here she comes now Bow down and stare in wonder Oh how we love you No flaws when you’re pretending But now I know she Never was and never will be You don’t know how you’ve betrayed me And somehow you’ve got everybody fooled - Taken from "Everybody’s Fool" by Evanescence 06:08am Thursday 19th January 2006 New York City I bolt upright in my bed for the second morning that week, wide awake immediately, my face soaked with sweat and panting wildly. The same nightmare. The same gory images lodged inside my head. Implanted with the same terrifying sensation of vulnerability and fear accompanying those images. I sighed, angry and exhausted. It was only when my heart rate normalised and my panting stopped that I realised something was very wrong. At first, I was unable to pin-point precisely what, but when I absently raised my right hand to wipe away the sweat from my forehead, I saw it covering my hands. I held my hands out in front of me in horror, somehow already knowing what the unfamiliar substance was. Warm. Thick. Sticky. Blood. I threw my bed covers off and leapt out of bed, unable to think clearly with the shock of it all. I glanced back, wild eyed, and saw my once white bed covers laced with the stuff, but that wasn’t the most disturbing thing of all. Upon landing on my carpeted bedroom my feet detected something that my brain just wouldn’t accept, despite the completely destroyed bed covers and my clearly stained hands. More blood. My carpet was somehow laced with the stuff and from what I could see, it left some sort of disturbing trail into the living section of my apartment. I squelched onward, determined to find the source of the blood, no matter what the consequences or how mentally scaring the sight would inevitably be. I opened my bedroom door inwards, slowly, half expecting some knife-wielding lunatic to jump from behind the cupboard, but I already knew nobody was in my apartment. I couldn’t hear any footsteps, movements or even breathing anywhere. As I opened the door, I realised that some was leaning against it on the other side and the weight of the object forced the door open a lot more quickly than I expected. I reacted quickly as the object fell inward and onto me. It all happened so quickly that I didn’t notice it was a dead body until I held it upright in my hands. A shudder of disgust shot through my body like a lightning bolt. I gently laid the body down upon the carpet and flipped it over. I had to know who it was. Upon seeing her face, I found myself oddly unsurprised for reasons I’m too scared to examine in any great detail. It was her. The woman from my nightmares. Dead. In my apartment. Blood all over my hands. I fell backward against the foot of my bed, my legs giving way through an unhealthy mixture of shock and despair. Am I responsible for this? Did I kill her last night? I searched my memory for something, anything that might shed light onto the subject, but the search came back empty. If I did kill her, I’d remember it…wouldn’t I? I didn’t know anymore. I couldn’t think straight. I didn’t know what to do. For a time there I couldn’t even remember how to breathe. Struggling for some idea of how to deal with the situation, my mind firmly rested upon the one person I could trust in this world. Aeryn. She’ll know what to do. She’ll know how to get to the bottom of this. She’ll help me. I stood up, pulled on a pair of jeans and a shirt I’d been wearing the night before and grabbed my knee-long leather jacket I found draped over the arm of my sofa. Aeryn will help me, I told myself, desperately wondering just how in the hell I was going to explain all of this to her. 10:12am. Thursday 19th January 2006 Stakiri Towers New York City Stakiri stared at the security monitor output of the lobby area of his building and raised a speculative eyebrow. "Interesting…" He picked up the receiver of his phone from his desk in front of him and hit the button for a direct line to lobby security. The security officer answering the private line picked up his receiver a lot faster than Stakiri had expected, which was a pleasant surprise to say the least. "Yes, sir," the brash voice replied. "A woman has just entered the building," Stakiri said. "What does she look like, sir?" The security officer replied, immediately scanning the area. "She’s got black hair. Pretty. You may remember her face," Stakiri told him. "She and I have had business, of sorts, in the past, though I haven’t seen her for a while. Goes by the name of Aeryn." "Ah, I see her. Want us to escort her out, sir?" The security officer ventured. "No," Stakiri told him. "Check her for weapons then escort her to my office." Stakiri watched on via the monitor as the security guards approached her cautiously. These critical few seconds would tell Stakiri everything he needed to know. If she resisted his security guards, then she was here to cause damage - probably to him - and the security guards sure as hell wouldn’t be much of a match for her. If she did nothing, then she would cause no problems. At least he hoped she wouldn’t; she always had been unpredictable. The security officer nearest to her gently grabbed her right arm and whispered something into her ear. Once finished, she glared at the man for a second then nodded in acceptance. Stakiri let out a brief sigh of relief and smiled, a little more pleased with than he ought to have been. It was all part of his plan after all. She turned up a little sooner than he expected, but he expected it nonetheless. Soon Jonathon Winters would come back to the set up too, then things will certainly get a lot more interesting. 06:28am Thursday 19th January 2006. Rooftops. New York City. I waited at the designated meeting place, absently staring at the sun rising over the distant building tops; the early morning traffic being to form in small pockets here and there. She was over five minutes late now and the entrenched feeling of anxiety buried within my chest had descended toward my stomach, providing me with a distinctly queasy sensation that wouldn‘t go away. I couldn’t blame her for being late. I woke her up when I called her from the public telephone, a couple of blocks from my apartment. She sounded tired and incoherent over the phone, making me repeat almost everything I told her. Obviously, I didn’t go into great detail about the nature of our meeting and that made the task of getting her to come all the more difficult. However, she recognised the high levels of anxiety laced within my voice and realised that something was out of the ordinary. I’d had five minutes now to think about just how I will explain this all to Aeryn, but I still found my mind blank, vocabulary limited and my mind filled with the image of the lifeless body still occupying my bedroom floor in my apartment. In the end, I tried to calm myself by going over my chosen way of tackling the Bradley/O’Shea/Rollins match at the upcoming GTT5. Thinking and being involved in the wrestling industry had always been a great comfort to me, but on this occasion even it wasn’t enough to take my off things. I didn’t hear her approach until she landed upon the rooftop, coming in from a south-easterly direction. Sloppy, I know, but I was distracted. "This better be important," Aeryn declared, sleepily rubbing her eyes. "It is," I assured her. "Remember the nightmare I told you about yesterday?" Aeryn sighed as though she were already bored of the subject. "Of course." "This morning I awoke to discover that it’d actually happened," I explained. Aeryn developed a confused expression. "What?" "On the way back to my apartment from our meeting last night, I heard a woman screaming for help in a nearby alleyway…" I trailed off. "What does this have to do with anything?" Aeryn interrupted. "…something clicked at the back of my head and I felt an overriding compulsion to check it out," I continued. "The woman was about to get mugged and when I got a close look at her, I discovered that it was the woman from my nightmare." "The Sarah Michelle Gellar look-a-like you told me about last night?" Aeryn questioned, sceptically. "Yes," I replied. "It was her. She was in the alleyway. She is - was - real." Aeryn raised an eyebrow quizzically. "Was?" I sighed. This was the difficult section of the talk. I’d hoped that words would suddenly leap into my in order for me to better explain everything to her, but they didn’t and so I decided just to take the blunt route. "I had the nightmare last night like usual," I informed her. "And this morning I found the woman actually dead in my apartment, with blood everywhere." I held out my blood soaked hands to illustrate my point. "Just like the nightmare…" Aeryn trailed off. "Exactly," I replied, pleased - yet oddly surprised - that she believed my story so readily. "I don’t know how in the hell she got in my place, I don’t know how she died and I don’t know why her blood is all over me. I went asleep last night fine and woke this morning covered in blood. What the hell is going on?" Aeryn developed a sympathetic expression, then hugged me. "It’s going to be okay," she told me. "We can deal with this." "I didn’t kill her," I said. "You believe me, don’t you?" Aeryn pulled away from the hug, in order to look me directly in the eye, then smiled. "Of course I believe you." She said as though she was a little hurt that I even had to ask her and that, in turn, made me feel a little better. I sighed deeply and lengthily, a lot more relieved than I thought I’d be. Her acceptance and confidence in me had taken the majority of the weight off my shoulders, even though I was fully aware of the fact that she has no basis for believing that I didn’t do it. How could she know whether I’d done it? I wasn’t entirely certain of the fact myself. Was there more to this than she was letting on? Upon allowing the thought more consideration than I reasonably should’ve, I dismissed the idea without further deliberation. Aeryn would never betray me. Not now. Not like this. Aeryn put her arm around me. "Let’s go deal with this…okay?" I nodded absently, unable to shake the after-effects of my previous overly paranoid notion, a little disgusted with myself. "Jonathon," She began, "it’ll all be okay…we’ll figure this out. Don‘t worry about it." 10:16am. Thursday 19th January 2006 Stakiri Towers New York City "Aeryn! Good to see you!" Stakiri greeted, standing as Aeryn entered his office. He added a smile for good effect, which, as he expected, Aeryn didn’t reciprocate. "I thought you’d dropped off the face of the Earth! Take a seat," he gestured toward the chair opposite him and added, "So, what can I do for you today?" Aeryn folded her arms and rolled her eyes around the back of her head pointedly. She didn’t take a seat. Of course, she knew all of this was a gigantic heap of bullshit, but she played the game nonetheless. They were both players and not to play the game would just be disrespectful. "Cut the bullshit, Stakiri!" Aeryn demanded, her raised voice alerting the security guard somewhat. He glared into the office through the transparent panel on Stakiri’s office door agitated. Stakiri just waved it away as nothing. "I know what you’re doing…I know what’s going on!" Stakiri developed a confused expression. That was unexpected. He expected Aeryn to come here and ask Stakiri to help Winters with his recent mental problems, but she knew something about his plan. Perhaps her transition into being useful to him needn’t been the long, drawn out process he expected it to be. Interesting. Of course, before he could explore that avenue he needed to know how much she really knew. "What are you talking about?" Stakiri responded, shifting his facial expression into neutral. Aeryn is a clever one and he didn’t want to give her more to work with than he really had to. "Your master plan to make Jonathon believe he’s lost his mind," Aeryn added. Stakiri raised his eyebrows. Interesting. She’d figured out the basis of the plan. The question is, does she realise just how deep this goes? It was doubtful. He’d been careless about something along the way in order for her suspicions to get this far, he knew, but he hadn’t been so careless as to provide her with all of the gory details. She’d obviously neglected to inform Winters himself about her suspicions; otherwise Stakiri would be dead - or close to it - right about now. That little snippet of information told him enough. Enough for him to manipulate the situation to his liking and dictate where this conversation headed. "Master plan?" Stakiri asked, acting confused. "What the hell are you talking about?" "Don’t give me that, Stakiri," Aeryn said. "I know for a fact that you’re the one responsible for Jonathon’s current mental state!" "Winters has mental health problems? And you think that I’m involved in some way? That’s an awfully heavy accusation you’re throwing around there," Stakiri told her, leaning back in his comfortable leather chair, "sure you can back that claim up?" "The woman you had involved in your latest stunt," Aeryn responded. "He recognised her from his past, though he couldn’t remember quite from where, but when he described her to me, I realised who she was. I know she’s one your regular ‘associates’. Just what game are you playing here, Stakiri?" So that was it. She made the connection between Jessica, his ‘associate’, and himself. There was nothing he could do about that now. An unforeseen variable in his veritable tapestry of lies and deceit. Only a minor blockade. Nothing he couldn’t manoeuvre around. "It had to be done Aeryn," Stakiri told her, "and you have to understand that." Aeryn developed a confused expression, with not-so mild undertones of anger, then placed both hands upon Stakiri’s desk and leant in closer toward him. "Had to be done?" She repeated, her voice raised slightly. "Why did this have to be done?" "The treatment is wearing off," Stakiri told her. "Our research department predicts total degradation of the memory blockades we put in place within the year. He‘s going to remember everything." Aeryn rolled the information around in her head. Stakiri knew she understood that process was not permanent, but she probably hadn’t prepared herself for the reality of the situation. Whether she would approve of his methods to deal with it, was another matter entirely. 06:43am Thursday 19th January 2006. Jonathon Winters Apartment. New York City. I stood on the fire escape balcony, outside the window, desperately trying to find the strength to enter my abode. Aeryn stopped within centimetres to my rear and placed a comforting hand upon my shoulder. "I can’t go in there," I told her. "Not with the…" "It‘s okay," Aeryn interrupted, "I’ll go first." She gently brushed past me and climbed through my open window. Reluctantly, I followed. Upon entering, I found Aeryn sweeping the room blankly and I noticed that there wasn’t any blood near my bedroom door at all, which was clearly visible from our position. There was blood all over the door and the surrounding carpet when I left here earlier…wasn’t there? I scrunched my face up, desperately sifting through my memory for a definitive answer. "Where’s the body?" Aeryn asked me. I didn’t answer her right away, instead I made my way over to my bedroom door and opened it without hesitation. I stepped backward away from my bedroom, slightly shocked and completely confused. "What is it?" Aeryn asked, rushing over to my side. She followed my gaze and stared blankly at the empty bedroom. Nothing. No blood. No dead body. Nothing. "I thought you said…" I nodded in response, interrupting her, then added, "Yeah." "But she’s not…" Aeryn trailed off. I tried to maintain my composure, fighting off the frustration and fear that threatened to engulf me. "I know. I‘m sorry, but it was here, I swear it." Aeryn nodded and rested a hand upon my shoulder. "Yeah, Of course it was." I couldn’t tell whether her words were laced with sarcasm or whether she was simply trying to be sympathetic. I hoped it was the latter, but with Aeryn I never quite knew where I stood. Then I remember about the blood on my hands, I held them out in front of me and inspected them. My hands were clean, the blood no longer visible. "My hands," I began, thrusting them into Aeryn’s vicinity. "They’re clear. There was blood on them, you saw it!" "Well, Jonathon…it was dark out there, I just took your word for it," she told me. "I didn’t actually see any of the blood." "What?" I asked her, bewildered. "How could you not see…" She rubbed my shoulder, sympathetically. "Those nightmares have you stressed, Jonathon." I shook my head. "No. It was real. It…" "The nightmares combined with your WWA obligations and the GTT5 tournament have you more stressed-out than someone really should be," Aeryn explained. "Stress?" I asked her, rolling the thought around in my mind. "It could cause…this?" "Absolutely, you‘ve been pushing yourself so hard lately, something had to give eventually…" Aeryn assured me. "Then I’m not…crazy?" I asked. Aeryn giggled slightly, find that last statement of mine amusing for some bizarre reason. "Of course not, and I never once thought that you were." I smiled, relieved. No dead body. No blood. No chance that I’d killed her. But my senses were still telling me that it was all real and generally speaking, my instincts were more than reliable. 10:17am. Thursday 19th January 2006 Stakiri Towers New York City "I remember being briefed on the drawbacks of the treatment, Stakiri," Aeryn told him. "But what do his returning memories have to do with making Jonathon lose his mind?" Stakiri sighed. "It’s a contingency plan. Not a very good one, but it is the only we have." "A contingency plan?" Aeryn questioned. "I developed it when we first tried to deal with this whole situation," Stakiri informed. "But…" Aeryn trailed off. "…I didn’t tell you about it," Stakiri interrupted. "I knew you wouldn’t approve. Hell, I don’t approve…" Aeryn nodded, the anger in her eyes had dissipated completely now. She knew full well that Stakiri wasn’t being completely on the level with her, but she understood that this contingency plan of his might be the only way to keep Winters from remembering life before Stakiri put the blockades up to hold back his memories. And that was the most important thing in this entire situation. "Elaborate, Stakiri. I need to know more," Aeryn demanded. "The contingency plan was designed to prevent Jonathon from remembering his past at any cost," Stakiri explained. "So in order to do so I have been planting false memories, having him meet false people from his past, basically a complete head fuck. So that when his real memories do return, he won’t know which memories are real and which are false." Stakiri sighed and then shuffled uncomfortably in his seat, before continuing, "But first we had to, um, break him mentally, in order to make him more receptive to the process our researchers developed to implant certain memories." "Break him?" Aeryn questioned, an odd mixture scepticism and anger. "How the hell did you do that?" "We put him in a position where he had to kill you - or more accurate something he thought was you - or let someone else die," Stakiri answered, bluntly, not even trying to soften the blow. "Ah," Aeryn replied, "the clone of me. When that guy - The Legend was it? - came looking for trouble. Jonathon told me about that…was that absolutely necessary." "Probably not," Stakiri admitted, "but it wasn’t planned, it was just a coincidence that we capitalised upon." "Coincidence?" Aeryn questioned, confused. "I originally sent the clone out to take care of ‘The Legend’," Stakiri explained. "He’d came to town looking for Winters and began his search by destroying one of my strongholds on the outskirts of the city. Naturally, I assumed the lunatic was coming for me, so I made the appropriate preparations." Aeryn couldn’t actually tell whether Stakiri was being completely honest with her, but at this point it didn’t actually matter. "Then what?" "Breaking Winters’ mind allowed the memory inducing drugs to work without too much resistance," Stakiri explained. "Obviously, we supplemented the drug’s effect by throwing in a few of my associates to create contradictory memories and upsetting images. For example, the afore mentioned woman - Christine - whom you recognised." "So you only used drugs with that?" Aeryn questioned. "You didn’t actually have the woman killed and placed her in his apartment?" Stakiri shook his head amused. "No, Aeryn. The human mind is a powerful thing. The drugs simply made Winters see those things and make them seem real to him at the time. There was nothing more to it than that." Aeryn nodded, reluctantly bringing herself to accept Stakiri’s word. "Not the most humane method, but I admit it might be the only way to deal with this. Him remembering and eventually becoming the man he once was, is a considerably worse scenario." "I won’t lie to you, Aeryn," Stakiri began. "We both have vastly different motives for our actions, but ultimately we have the same goals." Aeryn nodded, unable to bring herself to agree with Stakiri in any verbal manner. "I take it you haven’t ruined the contingency plan by sharing your discovery with, Jonathon?" Stakiri asked. Aeryn shook her head. "I didn’t know anything when I last talked to Jonathon, I only had suspicions." Stakiri nodded and grinned. "Are you going to tell him now that you know the entirety of the situation?" "No," Aeryn replied. "But I want in on all of this." "You want in? On what?" Stakiri questioned, obviously toying with her. "Don’t play dumb with me, Stakiri!" Aeryn warned. "I want in on this whole process. I want to observe how you‘re handling him and to make sure you‘re not doing anything untoward." "Of course," Stakiri responded with his patented vicious smile, "but there is one condition." Aeryn sighed. She figured that there would be. There always was when you made a deal with the devil. "What?" Stakiri leaned forward, his vicious smile wider now. "I want him working for me again and you‘re the only one who can talk him into it." Aeryn nodded a non-verbal acceptance of the terms. If he ever found out that she was making these kind of deals about his future, knowing what she knew, Jonathon would never forgive her for her betrayal. And she knew that would be how he would see it. However, the risk was ultimately worth it in order to keep his past a mystery to him. Nobody, least of all Jonathon himself, could handle that scenario if he was ever allowed to remember. No matter how bad she felt treating Jonathon in this manner, she was ultimately doing the right thing. Protecting Jonathon from himself, or more accurately, from his former self. It didn’t much matter to her what she had to do to enforce it. 13:30pm. Thursday 19th January 2006 Stakiri Towers New York City Stakiri sat back in his leather chair, exceedingly pleased with himself. The ‘meeting’ with Aeryn had gone well and he’d been able to manipulate her into helping him with the Winters situation after all. It’d been a lot easier than he thought it would be - in his original plans, he hadn’t expected to reach this point for another four months. Ring, Ring The monotonous signalling of his office phone snapped him out of his self-congratulating reverie and brought his mind to focus upon the next obstacle in his path. His clean-up crew was right on time. Stakiri picked up his receiver. "Talk to me." "Jessica has been taken care of," The voice on the other end replied. "All evidence regarding her departure has been dealt with. A cover story regarding her sudden disappearance has been fabricated and regurgitated to her family and friends. No loose ends." "Excellent," Stakiri muttered. "You’ve excelled yourself this time boys. Payment has already been transferred into your various accounts. A pleasure doing business with you." Stakiri placed the receiver back down into its holster and sat back, continuing to be smugly pleased with himself.
