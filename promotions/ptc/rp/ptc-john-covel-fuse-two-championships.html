IG 29 - John Covel (FUSE)

8 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Spring had arrived. Weather was still blustery and it hadn’t reached March 21st as of yet, but as far as John Covel was concerned it was spring. The opening games of the Cactus and Grapefruit leagues had taken place, putting excitement into a lot of young Major League hopefuls. He may not have been young, but John Covel was hopeful he would end up in the Cubs starting rotation by spring’s end. He remained in Puerto Rico honing his skills in the Winter League playoffs. At the same time, he had been traveling back to the states to fulfill his commitments to FUSE Wrestling. Much to his surprise they crowned him their Universal Champion in early February, seeing him as the man to lead the company. He wasn’t sure why; he knew they had heard of his intent to head back to the major leagues after his contract expiry in April. He also knew if he wasn’t careful in his final few days away from the mainland he might not have the chance to play ball again. John had broken one of the main guidelines for his acceptance back into baseball: No new commitments to wrestling. Since the first night he had been there he had been wrestling. Thank God nobody had found out. November 7th, 2006 5:16 P.M. AST What was John going to do? He knew he couldn’t accept the job offer from the independent promotion. It was against the rules. The man on the other end of the line waited patiently as millions of things ran through John’s mind. He scanned the room seeing his new and former teammate, his LiveWire Championship and wrestling gear and then his baseball glove. The money sure would be nice. “I’ll be there.” John said. He sighed as Kyle exclaimed just a few feet away. At least one of them was really excited. John ended the call, not really sure of what he had done. Twenty-five hundred dollars a night to wrestle was good money. Who was going to find out? “That’s awesome man. Jose is going to make you the star of that place.” Kyle replied. Whoa. Star? That’s something that John definitely didn’t need. He was going to have eyes on him while in Puerto Rico as it was. He had done too much hard work to get where he was. He was going to be a Chicago Cub again. John handed the cell phone back to his old friend and sighed. Things were going to be interesting during the winter league. November 7th, 2006 8:57 P.M. AST John Covel ducked a charging opponent’s clothesline attempt and spun around. He caught the man with a boot to the mid-section before grabbing his arms and lifting him up into a piledriver position. John paused for just a second before dropping to his rear and nailing one of his signature moves; the packaged piledriver. He covered the man as the crowd cheered and counted along with the referee. They erupted at the third slap of the mat that brought John to a standing position in the center of the ring. The referee raised his hand into the air as the small crowd continued to cheer. “This is awesome.” John thought to himself. He had wrestled in front of thousands of people all across the United States as well as on TV and pay-per-view, but there was something about the electricity from the four hundred people in attendance that gave him the chills. He stepped from the ring and made his way towards the back, receiving high fives from fans on the way. As he stepped through the curtain, Jose Raymond, the promoter was right there offering him a forceful handshake. John’s appearance had packed the building for the first time in months. “Gracias, John. You put on one hell of a show tonight. I hope you will come back tomorrow night. I have another worker that you could have a fantastic match with.” The owner stated. Tomorrow night? He just couldn’t step away from wrestling. There was no way John couldn’t deny that he didn’t feel warm and fuzzy after the reaction from the folks that night. Twenty-five hundred dollars was nice, but John knew that five-thousand dollars was even nicer. John was never greedy, but money was always welcome in his hands. “Sure thing Mr. Raymond.” John replied. “Please call me Jose.” The owner said. He placed his arm around John and walked him into the dressing room, joking with him. John had prepared mentally for the grueling schedule of flying to and from the states for FUSE, but adding another wrestling promotion to his schedule in addition to his baseball games and practices? He’d tell Jose tomorrow night that he’d only do a few random shows and end it. John NEEDED to concentrate on baseball. It was his love. January 1st, 2007 3:11 P.M. AST The air was warm and filled with the rays of the sun. John could get used to this. He laughed as he thought about his friends and family in Illinois shoveling the feet of snow that had fallen this winter. It was nice to go out in shorts in January. Real nice. John tossed a few warm-up pitches to Kyle, loosening up for his start that day. As he released the lob of a pitch, his shoulder tweaked slightly, bringing a grimace to his face. Remember that talk he was going to have with Jose on November 8th? It didn’t work. John had been working in ring five days a week. Tuesday through Saturday was the longest days he had ever experienced. The previous night John landed wrong from a suplex, tweaking his shoulder slightly. Nothing that hadn’t happened before. John would just work through it and come out just fine. It was funny that he had become okay breaking the rules of his re-instatement. Nobody had called him on it since his first night in the uncrowned American state. His stomach dropped as he peered past his catcher and spotted a few suit-clad men making their way towards him. “Uh-oh.” He muttered under his breath as he continued tossing the ball. He squinted through the sun, finally recognizing one of the men as Bud Selig. The others weren’t familiar to him, but he was still worried. What if they were lawyers coming to announce legal actions? Or well dressed cops? John didn’t even think he could be arrested for breaking the guidelines of a re-instatement program, but anything was possible. He put a little more effort into the final pitch as they reached him, struggling to hide the pain from his shoulder. ”Hey there John.” Bud said, offering his hand. John accepted and produced a firm handshake. He then greeted the two other men as Bud introduced them as his appointed case workers. Their visit was simple. It was time for a progress report. “We’ve been here for a few nights now, observing your behavior and asking around for opinions.” Bud stated. Busted. The last time Bud observed him, John’s every move was recorded into a file. It was almost certain the three men had watched him enter the Rico Sports Convention Center and go against his commitment. “Did I do good sir?” John inquired. A long pause much like that of his father’s filled the air. Not quite as long, one of Selig’s associates spoke up. “We’re very impressed by your development, John. You still show great skill and stamina. We don’t want to interrupt your warm-up, so just keep up the good work.” The young man said. Ever get that feeling that you’re invincible? John had it. It was like he was a six year old that had snuck cookies from the cookie jar before dinner and gotten away with it. He felt like he could do anything. “Thank you very much sir. I won’t let you down.” John replied. “That was easy.” He thought to himself. The men waved and walked away. They’d be watching from the stands. What they’d see was eight no-hit innings from Covel before giving up a double with two outs. He met with the men after the game again and learned they’d be flying out at six o’clock. He’d be a nice young man and accompany them to the airport before rushing back to his hotel to retrieve his gear and head off for another main event match a block away. This was great. February 10th, 2007 10:01 P.M. CST John Covel’s bloodied face peered out to the thousands of people in the United Center as he climbed to the second rope, stalking Tigera. He laid punches to the side of her head before hooking her arm and head for a superplex. He paused for a second not only allowing himself time to prepare for the move, but to build anticipation. Finally he lifted her into the air and stalled for a moment before dropping to a standing position and driving her head into the turnbuckle. She fell to the canvas after being hit with one of the most devastating and spectacular moves in the business. John heard the fans yelling at the top of their lungs as he dropped to cover the Universal Champion. Tears escaped his eyes as the referee’s hand struck the mat for the third time and the bell rang. “Here is your winner and the NEEEEEEEEEEEWWWWWWW FUSE WRESTLING UNIVERSAL CHAMPION, JOHN COVEL!” Skye stated across the sound system. John had never experienced anything like this in his life. The happiness that coursed through his veins escalated as he was handed his new title belt and the fans erupted once more. He had worked so hard for this company and it finally paid off. John had defeated his long-time adversary for the biggest prize in FUSE in the main event of the biggest show of the year. He remained in the ring an extra few minutes, taking in the love that the FUSE faithful were giving out. As he stepped through the curtain he was greeted with a round of applause from numerous of the guys. What a job he had. He thought back to his three perfect games. The men before him reminded him of the teammates that had given him an ovation for those feats. But this was still different. The past year had been great for John and this was just icing on the cake. The show of respect from his co-workers touched him deeper than that of his baseball friends did. IT was something John just couldn’t understand. He stepped through the crowd of wrestlers shaking hands and hugging along the way, ready for a shower to wash the blood from his face. It was something John could never deny. He loved wrestling. February 26th, 2007 8:55 P.M. AST John stood on the turnbuckle as he had numerous times in the last few months, lifting his opponent into the air. Camera flashes lit up the Rico Sports Convention Center as Covel held the man upside down and dropped him on the top turnbuckle. He dropped down and covered the fallen man as the referee counted three and signaled for the bell. For the second time in three weeks, John had won the top prize in a wrestling company. He had won multiple titles since entering the wrestling world four years prior, but the feeling deep down remained the same. It felt like his hard work had paid off. It showed him that people believed in him. John thrust the title belt high above his head as the crowd cheered. He cherished that moment for as long as he could before stepping from the ring and walking to the back. “I love this. This is surreal.” He thought to himself. The wrestling world never ceased to amaze him. As he stepped through the curtain he saw Jose Raymond and his workers applauding him. John knew the wrestling business was something special. If he hadn’t been convinced before, he was now. He clutched the belt as he slapped hands and received pats on the back from the guys as he walked by. Three months after coming to the Pure Wrestling Syndicate, he was crowned their Heavyweight Champion. As he made it past the guys and walked into the dressing room he laughed. “I guess I’m doing pretty well for this being my second career.” He thought. February 27th, 2007 7:54 P.M. AST The crowd was in a frenzy as John stood on the mound. He stared at home plate, looking for his sign. He shook off the first two offerings and accepted the third; a fastball. He wound and fired, placing the pitch dead center. He took a deep breath as the batter swung and missed, bringing the count to two strikes. It wasn’t like John hadn’t been here before. Three times before he had faced the twenty-seventh batter with a perfect game on the line. Each time he set that man down and etched himself into the history books. This time, a championship was on the line. His team was one strike away from winning the Winter League championship. “One more John. Four perfect games in your career. That’ll earn you some money this season.” He nodded to the first sign Kyle showed him. This was it. He knew it. He sighed, kicked his leg and fired. Time slowed down as the ball flew towards the mass of leather waiting for it. John watched as the ball started letter-high and inside on the right-handed batter. As it came closer, it began to dive downwards and break left. The batter flinched, considering taking a rip, but his eye got the better of him. He halted the early motion and watched the ball cross the plate, low and outside. The smack of leather echoed in the air for only a split second before being replaced by the umpires call. The crowd erupted as the batter was rung up, ending the game. The dugout emptied and rushed towards the mound, engulfing John. “Two different championships in twenty-four hours. Incredible.” As he celebrated with his teammates, he looked to the crowd. For some reason, the feeling was different from the night before. Goosebumps didn’t clutter his arms. He wasn’t tingling. Tears weren’t even close to lining his cheeks. He was happy, but it just wasn’t the same. He remained on the field celebrating his unprecedented fourth perfect game and championship win, but for the first time in his life, his heart wasn’t on the baseball field. March 2nd, 2007 1:17 P.M. PST His time in Puerto Rico had ended. He promised Jose he’d be back the next week to drop the title until he was sure he could return. John’s wrestling had never been discovered by Major League Baseball. He was a much richer man because of it. Today he sat inside a fancy restaurant with Cubs General Manager Jim Hendry and a few other people. The success of his winter had given John the nod to head to his dream. He was going to play in the Major Leagues. It had been expected from day one that he’d re-sign with the Cubs, but nothing official had been discussed. Jim Hendry was an easy man to talk to, allowing negotiations to occur over this simple lunch. The men shared laugh after laugh, discussing specific details here and there. Finally, Jim reached into the briefcase that accompanied him and produced a manila envelope filled with documents. He handed it to John after their meal concluded ad waited for his reaction. “Six years?” John asked aloud. Jim nodded as John continued to file through the paperwork. If signed that day, he’d be introduced as a Cub in dramatic fashion; he’d start the spring training game the next day. The benefits of the contract got better as he read. Nothing could have prepared him for the dollar figure that waited on the last page. $146 Million John wasn’t sure what to think. The Cubs had already spent $300 million that off-season as it was. The amount of money that John was looking to make was nothing short of astonishing. He set the documents down and took a gulp of water, trying to regain his composure. “I take it that means you like it?” Jim joked. John nodded as he took a deep breath and looked over the papers again. Suddenly, thoughts began to rush through his mind. He had waited to pitch in the majors all his life, but suddenly his heart wasn’t there. For a moment he wondered where it had gone. He should be wiping tears from his cheek as he scribbled his name onto to contract. Instead he sat there, imagining himself standing in the middle of a wrestling ring, holding the FUSE Universal Championship into the air. He frantically searched inside his mind for the explanation. After allowing the table to remain silent for nearly four minutes, it finally struck John. “I love the wrestling business. It’s all I want to do for the rest of my life.” Could it be? John’s life long dream was mere hours away. All he had to do was sign the contract, but he couldn’t. As much as he had grown to love the smell of the hotdogs and the sound of his fastballs hitting leather, the feeling he got after performing in front of wrestling fans around the country overshadowed it all. Wrestling was in his blood. It was something he couldn’t turn away from. “Mr. Hendry, I’m not sure how to say this. I have just experienced an epiphany. I don’t think baseball is for me anymore. I’m a wrestler.” John stated. The reaction was more than John could have expected. He envisioned punches landing on his face, bullets flying at his body but nothing as simple as a generous nod from the man who had believed in him for six years. In appreciation, John took his wallet out and insisted on paying for the lunch. It was the least he could do. After all, he was making $200,000 beating his body up night in and night out. He loved his job.
