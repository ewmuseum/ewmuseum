Grace

24 April 2007

December 24, 1993 10:00pm Not tonight. A hundred times before - a thousand maybe. But not tonight. The sounds were familiar, traveling recklessly from the living room and crashing with abandon through the walls of his room. Sounds that, at one time, only yesterday - only all the days before - had sent him face first into his pillow, praying that God might come down and just put an end to it all. Crying. He prayed with every ounce of him. But not tonight. Tonight, he replaced his pillow with a Louisville Slugger - his prayers with determination. He clutched the bat hard - hard as he’d ever prayed on all the nights before. His other hand was on the door knob, slick with sweat, while he trembled, waiting. His jaw was a vice. And once more, for courage, he told himself: Not tonight. “You think I don’t see the way you look at that motherfucker!?” Chuck’s voice boomed loud enough to startle Jason. His courage shook briefly, and then he tightened his grip on the door. He breathed in hard, staggered breaths from his stomach. “They all told me you was just a fucking slut anyway! I don’t know what the-” “Who!?” Grace fired back, her voice cracking to a shriek. “Who told you that!? Fucking Jimmy, out with all his-” “It doesn’t fucking matter who!” Chuck exploded again. “How many times you fuck that cocksucker!? Huh!? Two? Three fucking times?” “Like it even matters to you,” Grace Snow’s voice was laced in ice, and as soon as Jason heard it, he closed his eyes. He knew this part of the routine well. His mother was prodding for the button; the one that would finally make Chuck snap, as he always snapped - as they all snapped. “You fucking slut,” his words were hissed, but still clear through the paper thin walls of the little house. In his mind, Jason saw Chuck shaking his head, his eyes filled with venom. “Maybe I just wanted to find out what it felt like to be with a real man,” Grace fired back - another hiss. Jason opened his eyes, and then he opened the door. He knew what was coming long before he heard the flesh of Chuck’s open hand slap against his mother’s cheek. Long before he heard her cry out. Before she wailed “you fucking bastard” from her knees in the living room. He could see her from the hallway now, as he walked in long, determined steps toward the scene. His mother didn’t notice him. Neither did Chuck. The bat connected with Chuck’s ribs with a hollow, aluminum twang. Jason had swung it with terrible intentions, and his victim collapsed to one knee, face to face with Grace. He screamed guttural without opening his mouth, and then slowly turned to look over his shoulder. A bead of sweat ran quickly from his brow down to the tip of his nose, and then, as if hesitating by some will of its own, slowed before dangling there for a moment, and then finally dripping down onto the floor. “You shouldn’t have done that, boy,” said Chuck through clenched teeth. His eyes flared under a bushy mat of black curls, and he painfully pulled himself to his feet. “Leave him alone!” Grace exploded hysterically, and then she threw herself onto his back. Chuck struggled for a moment, her claws digging into his cheeks and beard, and then finally he flung her off. She landed awkwardly on the coffee table and then rolled off to the floor, watching helplessly as her thirty-three year old boyfriend stalked toward her only child. She called his name in a shattered, desperate voice. “Now are you going to put the bat down or am I going to have to take it from you?” Chuck asked as he approached. Both his steps and his words were slow. Jason stayed silent and cocked the bat again, ready for another swing. With this one, he planned to take the bastard’s head clean off his fucking shoulders - to see the pink matter of his brain spray against the far wall. Anything less wouldn’t be nearly enough. He planned to swing not only at Chuck, but at every man who’d ever hurt his mother. For every time he’d found her crying in the floor, her faced bruised or bleeding, he would have his revenge. He would protect his mother, as a good son must. He swung the bat in a hard, upward arc, as hard as his intentions could hope, but Chuck stepped in catlike at the last moment and smothered the blow beneath his arm. “Got you now, you little bastard!” Chuck hissed, and he disarmed Jason of his bat. They struggled wildly after that, and a rage set deep and buried in Jason’s guts released itself, for the first time on a grown man. It didn’t matter that Chuck was a half foot taller. It didn’t matter that he was fifty pounds heavier. It didn’t matter that he was nearly twenty years older. Jason punched, kicked, and clawed like a madman - like an animal. He lost himself in the red-raw rage. He drowned in the heat in his head, and no longer did he feel pain when one of Chuck’s punches landed. He no longer heard his mother wailing from the floor either. He wanted to feel Chuck’s heart beating in the palm of his hand, and nothing else would possibly be satisfactory. In their violent, clumsy struggle, they danced near to where Grace sat crying on the floor, and then they tripped over her legs and crashed through the coffee table. Chuck winced as the impact took him on his bad side, where the bat had connected with his ribs. Jason didn’t notice. But all the same, he took the upper hand in their wrestling from there, and soon enough, his punches began landing - first sloppily, taking him in the neck and collar bone, but then more accurate, colliding with sickening crunches into his nose and mouth. Chuck’s blood soaked the front of his shirt and splattered in waves of droplets onto the floor as each shot landed harder than the one that preceded it. Jason’s right arm was soaked to the elbow, and yet still he fired away, the red heat of his temper turning to cold, absent black. Tears burned rivers down his cheeks. Chuck had stopped throwing punches back, and now the best he could do was hold both arms straight out, pushing at Jason’s face - trying to get a finger in his eye. And the blows still rained down on him. Two rights, and then a left. Another right. He felt a hand on his shoulder, yet both of Chuck’s were in his face. There came another hand on his other shoulder, but he was too busy to pay them much mind. His mother’s nails dug through his thin t-shirt and into his skin as she pulled frantically. “Jason!” She shrieked. Desperate. “Jason, stop it! Fucking stop it! Jason!” Her hands went to his hair, pulling it in handfuls, and then to his face where she clawed again. And finally her voice sliced through the black and crashed into his mind, and somehow, time finally seemed to catch up with the moment. He could hear his mother pleading. He could feel her nails tearing his flesh. He pulled his fist back for one final blow, but instead collapsed onto his side, weeping hysterically at his mother’s feet. “You’re fucking crazy!” Chuck spat as he scurried to his feet. His face was a mask of blood now. It flowed freely from his broken nose and streamed out of his beard. He looked wildly at his hands, which were also soaked, but angry as he was, he knew better than to try his luck a second time in a fight. “Fucking crazy!” “Get out!” Grace boomed, her arms protectively draped over her son as he wept. He did not know why he wept - he’d done exactly what he’d set out to do. What the fuck is wrong with you!? He screamed at himself in his mind. “I’m fucking out of here,” Chuck grumbled. He tried to wipe the blood from his face away with his forearm, but it only left a smear. And then he turned around and walked out the door. Jason never saw him again, except for one brief time at the local store a few years later, and they didn’t speak. No sooner than the door closed and the room fell silent, his mother’s fingers, on the back of his neck, went cold. Stiff. It seemed that for a brief moment, she had truly felt like a mother. But now the moment had passed. “What do you think you were doing?” she asked, half a whisper. And then, more sternly, she said: “you could have gotten yourself hurt.” “I just wanted him to go away,” Jason whimpered, sitting up enough to look his mother in the eye. He found her face as cold as her fingers. “He’s a good man, Jason,” she told him. Her face was colored in running mascara and a coming bruise on her left cheek. “He was hurting you,” Jason stammered. “He...” his mother’s voice trailed off and she pulled herself to her feet. Her eyes turned to what remained of the coffee table, and then to the red-black stains on the living room carpet. She frowned at those. “We could have worked it out.” The words crashed into Jason’s brain as hard as the sounds of their fighting had - as hard as the sounds of his mother being slapped. Beaten. “What are you saying here?” He got to his feet and backed away a step, ashamed of the fact he was crying. Men don’t cry, the boy told himself. “Worked it out like you worked it out with Bobby? With Bill? With Dad? How many times are you going to let them do this to you, Mom?” His voice was desperate. Pleading. It was her turn to be ashamed, and she turned away to compose herself. When she spoke again, her tone was defensive. “I don’t see what business any of this is of yours anyway,” she said, though he couldn’t believe she said it. “He was hurting you,” Jason said again. “You’re too young to understand,” she shook her head, and then she began picking up the pieces of the broken coffee table. “These stains are never going to come out...” she said, glancing down at the carpet. It’s never going to stop, Jason realized, backing toward his room, his eyes on his mother. He was supposed to put an end to it tonight, but he saw now, that it would never end. After Chuck, Bobby would come back. And then Pete, and then Bobby a third time, and then Jake. And never again would Jason intervene. It wasn’t what she wanted. March 23, 2007 11:35am There came a noise from inside the house, and then another - closer. Jason almost lost his nerve. Funny how that worked. Jason Snow had been in the ring against men twice his size. On the streets of his city, he’d faced down men with bats, knives, guns, and worse. Not once had he ever lost his nerve in those circumstances. And yet now, with his mother approaching the door, moments from seeing her face to face, he had an urge to sprint back down the sidewalk and away to his rented car. He hadn’t laid eyes on her in five years; Haley had still been a baby then. Raven had still been alive. He remembered his little wife crafting hand-written invitations to Haley’s first two birthdays. She invited her to every Christmas dinner from the time of their marriage. She sent birthday gifts. “I don’t know why you bother with any of this,” Jason had told her one morning in bed, a moment after she had groggily announced from the crux of his arm that they should invite her for Thanksgiving. “Because she’s your mother,” Raven had replied, “and I’d like her to be here.” She never came. Five years. He remembered the last time, six days after Haley was born. He flew Grace down from Canada for a visit, as often he’d offered in the years before and since. That had been Raven’s idea as well, as always the ideas were hers. But Grace came that time, to see her first grandchild. She even seemed pleased. She rocked Haley in her arms for most of a day, with Raven hovering nearby, still recovering from her pregnancy but unable to take her eyes away from her newborn child. Raven had been a mother from the time she was born. Grace... well, she had given birth, he supposed. He remembered watching her on that day, rocking little Haley in Raven’s chair with a warm smile and a soft voice. It angered him. Perhaps a part of him had known, even then, that she wouldn’t be back. That this love she showed for her grandchild, genuine as it may have been, was only on the surface. In the years that passed, he thought perhaps she even wanted to visit, but Grace had always had peculiar priorities, and Jason supposed that like many other things, Haley had been put on her backburner. More than five years had gone by. Not a word came south. Her phone number changed shortly after, so even Raven’s calls went unanswered. Raven’s cards and letters went without reply. Five long years, and three of them - the three since Raven died - were years that he could have used a mother. Five years. And suddenly she was there. The door seemed to open in slow motion, though he was sure that was only in his head. He was holding his breath. “Hi,” she said after a moment of hesitation, her voice painted in soft surprise. There was a cigarette in her hand. She hadn’t changed much. “Hey,” he replied, and then a nervous smile slipped out. The years had been kind to her, and if anything, she looked younger than the last time he’d seen her. The tired bags beneath her eyes had vanished, and her hair seemed freshly dyed a brighter blond than it was naturally. She was forty-six years old - not quite nineteen more than him. She looked nervous. “So you got a new place?” he tried to sound polite, not accusing. “A few years back,” she smiled. “I got married.” She held out her hand to show the ring. He didn’t know what to say. “It’s been a long time,” she sighed, and before he could wholly digest the news of her marriage, she surprised him with a hug. He hadn’t expected that. Slowly, cautiously, he brought his hands up and placed them on her back. Married... the word rung his brain time and time again. “Come in,” she said as she pulled away. She held the door as he entered the house. “This is a nice place,” he told her, taking in his surroundings, and indeed, it was a house of surprising quality. His thoughts wandered to her husband. Was he another Chuck? Another Bobby? When his eyes turned back to her, he couldn’t help but search her arms for fresh bruises. “Al’s in construction,” she informed him, smiling. “Al?” he raised his eyebrows, still smiling. Forcing the God damned smile through a cast iron jaw, tight as a vice. “Vaserio,” she said. An Italian, Jason smirked. There was a certain irony in that. And then he realized his mother was now Grace Vaserio, and he remembered the peculiar look he got when he referred to her as Grace Snow earlier that morning. “So when’d this happen? The wedding, I mean.” “A couple of years back,” she blushed, looking back to her ring again. And then he thought she might have read his face, because she added like a child who’d lost his homework: “We would have sent invitations, but it was kind of sudden. We didn’t really plan it or anything. Um... would you like something to drink?” “No thanks,” he said as she moved by and started a pot of coffee. “Coffee’s the strongest thing I drink these days,” she informed him in a way that made him think she’d been waiting to inform him for a long time. It was another surprise - a pleasant one, and it explained her new healthier look. “Been going to meetings for two and a half years now - that’s how I met Al.” She beamed over her shoulder. “That’s great,” he told her honestly. A silent moment passed, and Jason became uncomfortably aware of the flask in the front pocket of his jeans, cold on his thigh. “I see you’re back on television again,” Grace said to break the silence, and instinctively, Jason’s jaw clenched. “Things are going well,” he said modestly. Things were going better than “well.” GTT was the biggest tournament in the wrestling world, and Jason was its champion - after a three year lay off, no less. But he knew none of this would impress his mother. “You’re just like your father,” she sighed. “I thought in those years that you were off, that wife of yours talked some sense into you.” “I’m good at it, Mom,” he said in a quiet, defensive voice. “Good at being a thug,” his mother turned around long enough to roll her eyes. Suddenly he was nineteen years old again, just getting his feet wet in the business. “Glorified thug,” his mother used to sneer at him. For a time he thought she hated him, but it wasn’t him she hated. “I’m telling you, all that money went to your head,” she told him, as she’d told him before. “Mom...” The meeting had had such a pleasant start... and now, just minutes later, after five years apart, they were slipping into their old routine like a familiar pair of shoes. “But what’s to be done? You’ve always been just like your father. Always looking for someone to hurt. Is that how you want to be remembered? I warned that wife of yours before you got married...” De ja vu... “I want you to have something, Mom,” Jason said as he reached into his jacket. He produced the PRO WRESTLING ILLUSTRATED magazine, unrolled it, and lay it on the table. “You should read it... They say some pretty nice things about me in there.” She didn’t answer, but she sighed, and that said more than a million words. More than a million flattering articles. And when she did speak, he wished she had just kept her silence. “And then there are those rumors,” she said, eyeballing him hard. “You running with those Italians - greaseballs. You know what kinds of things they’re saying?” “Those are just rumors, Mom,” he lied. “It’s embarrassing to go into the grocery store! Everybody knows.” He turned his head to the side and gave it a slow shake, regretting this visit already. “I didn’t come here to fight, Mom,” he told her, though his lips barely moved when he spoke. All they ever did was fight - was he crazy to think that after five years they might at least be able to hold a polite facade of a real relationship? Then again, he had known it would go this way all along. It didn’t change anything. That wasn’t what he was here for. “Then what are you here for?” she asked - snapped, her arms folded defiantly across her chest, as if she had to defend herself from him. He stared at her through eyes that glossed over against his will. But he wouldn’t let himself cry. Not in front of her - not in front of anyone. Men don’t cry, the boy inside himself insisted. And definitely not now. February 26, 2007 4:40pm The bruises were healing slowly, but that was no excuse. Jason had been neglecting his obligation to PRIME for GTT6. It had worked out, with a triumphant victory in the tournament, but he’d planned to jump headfirst back into PRIME when it was over. But there were aches and pains and little nagging consequences that he supposed were common after such a grueling tournament. And as much as he hated to admit it, it was hard to step back into the ring, week after week, with such lowered stakes. GTT comes but once a year. With a bundle of envelopes in his hand, Jason entered his home, ever-conscious of the throbbing in his right shoulder. It was healing, at least - he knew that. And he’d been through worse injuries a hundred times. But there was a guilt that came along with neglecting his responsibilities. I’ve fought through injuries before, he sighed to himself, and then he sat down at the kitchen table, paying no mind to the heart-beat pain pulsing in his right knee, and began to shuffle through the mail. They were bills and advertisements for the most part; also, the last trickle of “congratulations letters” for GTT - most of them from sponsors of the tournament itself. None of them offered him endorsement deals anymore - not like in the old days. Now there were rumors - whisperings passed through phony smiles or straight faces about his ties to organized crime. It was a source of entertainment for them, he supposed. Finally, he came to a letter addressed to him by hand. That was a rarity in this day in age. He plucked it free from the others curiously, and sat the rest on the table. At first he thought a young fan must have stumbled across his home address (perhaps he lived close by), for the penmanship seemed likely to belong to a child. But then he noticed the postage stamp: Queen Elizabeth II smiling tight-lipped beneath her crown. In small white letters along the bottom, the word “CANADA” appeared, all in capitals. Mom, he thought, and suddenly, from deep inside himself - from twenty years past - he began to tremble, ever so slightly. After five years, had she finally decided to write? He cursed his trembling finger as he jabbed it beneath the seal and slid it along the envelope’s length. It was a moment or two before he could muster the courage to remove the letter, and a good minute before he could unfold it - two folds to make three sides. Like a triangle. The letter itself was typed, unlike the writing on the envelope. That was peculiar. He closed his eyes for a moment to prepare himself, took a deep breath, and then he began to read. Dear Jason Snow, I regret to inform you that on Friday, February 9, Harold Jeremiah Snow, your father, past away after a long battle with stomach cancer. He was fifty-six years old. If you have any questions or concerns, feel free to contact me at the number provided below. My sincerest condolences, James Harmon Case Manager, Rennouse Federal Penitentiary, New Brunswick, Canada
