Family Matters: Part One

9 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-09 "Ugh…" Standing in front of a full-length mirror in a New York City hotel room wearing only his boxers, Seymour Almasy was less than pleased. "I fought that guy two, three weeks ago," Seymour managed, grimacing at his reflection. "Why do I still look like I got my ass kicked?" Bright bruises dotted Seymour’s jaw line, the product of the powerful lefts and rights that the Back Alley Brawler was known to throw. His ribs showed off duller, yet still equally painful markings, products of more punches and the Brawler’s crude power game. Fuck, even his legs were battered. Say what one might about his lack of big game experience, but Joe Balboa had beaten the shit out of Seymour Almasy. Sure, Seymour had won, but at what price victory? "I wonder what that bastard’s up to now," he mused, shaking his head. "Probably in mourning over the Giants like the rest of the town." Seymour hadn’t seen the game. He’d been too busy preparing for any number of wrestling matches he had coming up. The first WorldWide of the new year, where he would team with Michael Stevens in GCW. A World Title match against Khristain Keller and Max Danger in ACW, his first shot at that company’s top prize. And then, in GTT5, a match against a man considered by most to be a joke. A bumbling tag team specialist fortunate to win even one match in the tournament, let alone two. Clyde Walkins. It was funny, though, the way underdogs seemed to turn the wrestling world on its head before matches with Seymour. Last year, Craig Goat had come from virtually nowhere to defeat three people en route to conquering the Final Fantasy. The dominating LeStatt Knight and the up and coming Drake Coleman had both fallen to the Forsaken member. And that was just for starters. "The PRIME Universal Champion," Almasy whispered. Much like Seymour himself had once upon a time, Walkins had grabbed notoriety by the throat and refused to let go. His efforts had been rewarded by the ten or so pounds of gold that now resided around the waist of the Clyder-Man, ripped away from the seemingly unstoppable Tchu. Seymour looked again, at his battered reflection in the mirror. He had no title belt around his waist, no seven match winning streak. The only thing clinging to his hips at this point in time were a pair of admittedly cute chocobo boxers. "I should probably put some clothes on. Looking at me with my ass kicked is just depressing." Seymour dressed hurriedly, throwing on a pair of leather pants and a shirt. Once again, he looked at himself in the mirror, now more clothed, but no less worried. "Why does the entire world decide to collapse around me ONLY when a tournament’s going on? You’d think the law of averages would give me one friggin’ event off from freaky shit happening. Aerith…God." He hadn’t spoken to his ex-wife since the discovery of her, err, partner in her house weeks ago. That meant no conversation on Christmas Eve, no exchanging of gifts, and no New Year’s Eve together. Even when apart, they’d always spent the latter day together. No Aerith to ring in the new year had been…odd to say the least. So he’d done what he always did when life decided to kick him in the ass: throw himself into his work. Ten mile runs. Repeated sets of squats until the point of exhaustion. When Seymour’s personal life went to Hell, sure, he’d eat ice cream and sob like others, but after that, he would get himself in the best shape of his life. And as badly as he felt emotionally, physically, he was more than prepared for the third round of the most important tournament ever. Well, discounting the mind numbing pain, anyway. "Owww…" Hands dug into sore muscles, screaming from both Almasy’s own personal form of Hell, and the Brawler’s unique brand of combat. "I’d better not feel this badly come round three, or I’m going to get Goated again." Goated. A verb Seymour had come up with in the aftermath of his GTT4 loss. Essentially, it meant ‘to be outdone by an inferior’. As arrogant as it might have sounded to say so out loud, Almasy knew that Goat wasn’t the better man. But for three seconds, Seymour’s shoulders hadn’t known that. And an arena expecting Seymour to dominate instead saw him defeated. "Not happening again," he said aloud, gruffly, as if trying to force the memory from his mind. "Besides, Walkins is a far better athlete than Goat…" ‘Whoa there!’ a tiny voice in Seymour’s head exclaimed. ‘Making excuses already in case you lose?’ More grumbling followed. Ever since that one damned match against Craig Frickin’ Goat, there existed doubt where there was once sureity, worry where there was once confidence. Doubt + Worry = Defeat. That, if nothing else, Seymour was sure of. Knowing that, then, why the fuck was he so worried, so negative lately? All he needed to do was purge those negatives from his soul and get ready for battle. Like so many things in his life, the answer had little to do with professional wrestling. "I can’t believe it’s this day already," he whispered, shaking his head from side to side. "January 9, 2006. God, it’s been…what, ten years. Time flies." Wordlessly, Seymour reached for a black leather jacket in his closet, pulling it from the hanger and draping it over his shoulder. As he left the hotel room, he checked to make sure that everything was okay. Pocketing his keys, he opened the door, and closed it quietly behind him. All was still inside the darkened room 306. All save for a candle, flame flickering softly in the stale air of the room, lit in memory of what happened on this day ten years ago. --------------------------------------- Death is never easy. Ever. People cry at movies. They cry at TV shows. And if you’re Jason Seymour Wilson at the age of 14, you certainly cry at videogames. "Y-y-you….m-MONSTER!" The Super Nintendo controller fell from Seymour’s hand as he watched the horrific scene play out on the television set. General Leo fell, done in at the hands of the notorious villain Kefka. Seymour would, of course, cry many more times playing video games. But this was the first time, and as such, it was devastating. "How COULD he?" Seymour asked, to no one in particular. His mother was out, at one of the many parties she enjoyed frequenting in the neighborhood, and his father was finishing up a hard day of work at the bank, so his lamentations went unanswered. It was the way things usually went. Seymour had a key to his house from an early age. Every morning, Mom kissed him on the cheek, and sent him out the door to school, while she took care of the things she needed to do, and then later, the things she wanted to. Like party. Like drink. Like cheat on her husband while partying and drinking. Years before the show ever hit capable, Mrs. Wilson was the original desperate housewife. But Jason didn’t know that at the time. Neither did Mr. Wilson. The Final Fantasy only found out years later, through friends and friends of friends. Regardless of the circumstances, Jason was alone in the nice house that the Wilson family lived in, fighting his way through the epic quest of Final Fantasy 6 (III in the states), letting out whoops as he beat bosses, and groans as he was defeated. The death of General Leo, though, struck in a way he couldn’t imagine. Death was a foreign concept to young Jason. No one in his family had died. And besides, death was curable with Phoenix Downs in RPGs. Unfortunately, life didn’t have phoenix downs. And life, as it turned out, was knocking on his doorstep at that very moment. *knock knock* Jason looked around in confusion for a few moments, almost expecting his mother or father to answer the door, before remembering that neither was home. Slowly, he walked over to the door, peeking out the window. He knew that he wasn’t supposed to open the door for strangers, but the man standing at the door was dressed in a policeman’s uniform, and a police car was parked in the Wilson driveway. Obviously, this was important. Tentatively, the young man opened the door, to find the police officer standing there, with a look of surprise on his face. Obviously, he hadn’t expected a young adult to answer the door. "Hello there," the officer said, voice low, eyes cast down to the ground. "Is..err, your mother there?" "Nope," Seymour replied, reaching up to wipe a stray tear caused by Leo’s demise from his face. "She’s at the store." "And she left you here?" "Of course!" Seymour saw nothing wrong with this, so he elaborated. "She leaves for the store in the morning, and comes back really, really late!" That statement raised the officer’s eyebrow, but there was something far more important to be told today. Slowly, the policeman realized the task in front of him. He had to tell this news to a child. A poor, unsuspecting young man who was going to have his life turned upside down. Gritting his teeth, the officer sighed. Life simply wasn’t fair. It was a fact of life that had been reinforced over his years on the force, and was being demonstrated once again. "I don’t know how to say this, but…your father was in a car accident." "Car…accident?" The policeman’s stomach did a flip-flop. Several flip-flops, at that. The poor kid didn’t understand. Young, and innocent, and about to have that innocence shattered… Life DEFINITELY wasn’t fair. "He’s dead. Your father is dead." "…dead?" Jason couldn’t have known it then, but from then on, things DID change irrevocably. He was taken down to the police station, and asked about his mother’s ‘store trips’. Pretty soon, it became evident through his statements and corroborating testimony from neighborhood sources that his mother was neglecting her son. A trial followed, as did a sentence of jail time. And with his father dead, and mother in jail, Seymour was sent to the closest blood relative that would take him in: his paternal grandfather, Cid Wilson. Thus were the seeds of Seymour Almasy planted. And thus, like with so many other future greats, was potential greatness born out of tragedy. ---------------------------------------- Really, then, was it any wonder that Seymour Almasy was standing on the sacred ground of a Catholic cemetery mere days before an extremely important match? The hour drive out of Manhattan to Long Island was Hell, as it always was, but on January 9th, it always seemed that much worse. Everything else seemed insignificant, even his career, when he simply sat down and thought about the fact that his father was dead. Without his dad, he never would have gotten into the video games he so loved. For better or worse, depending on one’s perspective, Seymour Almasy was made on the Almasy family couch Saturday afternoons playing Final Fantasy or the Legend of Zelda. "Wish he could see me today," Seymour whispered, beginning a walk that he had made for as long as he could remember, ever since he had moved in with his grandfather Cid. "I wonder what he’d think of me. Never did go into banking like he wanted…though I think he’d understand. He more than anyone else told me to follow my dreams." If nothing else in his career, Seymour Almasy had done exactly that. Cradling a bouquet of flowers in his hand, Seymour made his way through what appeared to be miles of headstones. Making a left turn here, and a right turn there, he nearly disappeared among the monuments. If not for his yearly journeys, he would be hopelessly lost. Experience, though, lent wisdom, and before long he was kneeling before one particular headstone, reading the inscription there with a tear in his eye. JAMES MICHAEL WILSON 1943-1996 Loved by friends and family. It was, on its own, a powerful statement. But, as he had requested, there was more etched on his tombstone, the final words of a mortally injured man on the way to a hospital he would not reach alive. "Life is too short for doubts. Tell my wife and son that I lived without them And for them to do so as well. Jessica, Seymour, don’t doubt that I’ll see you again." -- JMW When faced with a statement like THAT made in the very face of death, any worries that Seymour had about life seemed trivial indeed. Slowly, Seymour placed the flowers in front of the tombstone. Remaining in a kneeling position, he spoke to the headstone, raising his eyes to the heavens above. "Hi, Dad…" Almasy sighed, shaking his head slightly. Usually, he had always had good news to tell his father in these visits. But for the most part, the past 365 days had not brought much good for the Final Fantasy. "It’s been a bad year, dad. Everything with Laura finally collapsed, and my career took a downturn. But you know something? I think everything’s going to turn out fine this year." Seymour was surprised to hear the words escape his lips. He’d intended the first few sentences, but the last two were a bit of optimism that he didn’t think he was capable of in his current mood. Everything had been going so wrong. How could he possibly retain any naïve belief that it would improve? Seymour didn’t know. But the fact was, he did. "Big match this week," he whispered, eyes still raised. "Against the PRIME Universal Champion, a guy with all the momentum and motivation in the world to take me out. GTT5, third round. You remember the other tournaments, right?" He paused, as if waiting for an answer. Of course, none came, but it felt as if one did. "I’ll give it my all. Just as I always have. Just as you always told me to. I promise." Finally, Almasy’s head lowered, and he bowed in silence. As he did so, thoughts of past and present flowed through his mind. Thoughts of his childhood. Of adulthood. Of being married, and divorced. Of victory, and of defeat. Suddenly, though, he heard footsteps behind him, growing closer and closer. His eyes opened in surprise, blinking rapidly before he stood, and spun around to face the newcomer. One look was all it took for his jaw to drop. Standing in front of him was a middle aged woman. Curly hair was rather obviously dyed brown, but the woman’s earlier beauty was still evident. Her eyes, though, seemed to indicate something mildly sinister lying just below her surface, though invisible to one who didn’t know any better. Seymour, however, seemed to know better. "What the Hell are you doing here," he asked, voice low, clearly angered at the woman’s presence. "Come on, Jason," she replied, in a level yet unnerving tone of voice, "is that any way to treat your mother?" "No," he admitted, eyes meeting hers, "but then again, no one can ever accuse you of properly treating your son, either." So much for optimism. (To be continued.)
