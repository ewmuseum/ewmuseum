TTD - Invincibleness~! -v- Lunchroom Patrol

14 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The scene opens in a middle school auditorium. The seats are filled with youngsters, most of whom are ignoring they stage. Many take advantage of this break from the classroom and chat with their friends or shoot spitballs at each other. A few students are trying in vain to take naps while others sit at the back and pretend to be above it all, and let's not forget those sad few who are working on homework to hide their sorrow at missing a class. A short, portly man in an ill-fitting suit waddles onto stage. He leans into the microphone. Portly Man: Ahem. The shriek of feedback rings throughout the auditorium, causing everyone to cover their ears. The man shoots an evil look towards the sound booth before cautiously leaning in again. Portly Man: Testing...testing. Alrighty, now that I've got your attention we need to go over a few things...... You can almost hear the students roll their eyes. Great, another boring lecture from Mr. Drake. I can't fucking believe I'm doing this. After having finally come to my senses and making damn sure that everyone knows that I'm Matt Dixon and not just that uber-good guy Doughboy, I'm taking who knows how many steps backwards and stepping back into my shell. How the hell did I let Saij talk me into this? Now we find ourselves backstage at the same building. Mr. Drake is heard rambling on in a monotone as the students quickly fall asleep. Standing here behind the curtains are two men that are familiar to some while strangers to many others, despite their successes. The Lunchroom Patrol, XUW Tag Team Champions, Doughboy and the Latch Key Kid, Matt Dixon and Brad Jameson. These two men are many things to many people, so who exactly is it that we'll be seeing here today? At one time, back when they began teaming together there were no such complexities to ponder. There was no doubt that the Lunchroom Patrol was made up of two men who always did right with no hesitations whatsoever. They were the bad guys' worst enemy and never gave the fans any reason to doubt them. Over the past year though, things have changed. Both men have shown themselves to be three-dimensional, to have dark sides that parallel their good. While the fans haven't always liked the failings that they've seen, the team has never fallen apart and indeed, has only grown stronger. At the moment LKK is sitting calmly while Doughboy paces all over the area seeming to get more and more frustrated. Finally he stops in front of LKK who calmly looks up at him. LKK: Yes? Dough: I can't do it! I just can't! LKK: Do what? Dough: You know damn well what I'm talking about. LKK: First, you'd better watch your language while we're in the school here. Dough: Why the f- LKK: You know darn well that we have to set a good example for these kids. Once we get out of here you can curse a blue streak for all I care, but in here we play by the rules, got it? Dough: *mumbling* Fine. LKK: Besides, you knew what you were getting into when you agreed to come talk with these kids. Dough: It's not like Saij gave us much choice. Much choice? Sheesh, that's an understatement. He pointed out that damn public appearance line in our contracts and told us if we didn't meet our quota he'd have to strip us of our belts. Then on top of that he emphasized that these kids need to see the "farking good role model" of Doughboy and not the conflicted real me. Bastard. Fade back into the auditorium. The crowd is divided between the hopelessly restless and the comatose thanks to the ramblings of Mr. Drake who might actually be close to wrapping up. Mr. Drake: And to wrap things up we've got some special guests here with us today. Those of you who follow that wrestling stuff might recognize them as I'm told they're champions of some kind. They're here to talk with you about the importance of believing in yourself. It's the Latch Key Kid and Doughboy...the Lunchroom Patrol. The two men walk onto the stage, their tag titles over their shoulders. The kids suddenly wake up and hop to their feet, cheering for the fan favorites. Dough's frustration seems to have vanished as he and LKK smile and wave to the kids, then pick up microphones from a stand. LKK: Hey kids, it's great to see you all! Dough: Thanks for that welcome! We're so happy to be here. Shit, they might be happy to be here, but I'm sure as hell not happy to be cooped up in this shell again. This ain't gonna last long, I can guarantee you that. LKK: You see, if there’s one thing I learned long ago, it’s that you need to believe in yourself. If you don’t believe in yourself, nobody will. So when you think that things aren’t going your way, when you think there’s no way things can turn around, and when you think that you’re done for...well, that’s when you need to dig down deep inside, and find your inner strength. Dough: He’s right, of course. Without yourself, you’ve got nobody. I’ve been going through some rough times lately, what with the situation with Tim and Film and Jen, among other things. "The situation". Oh, that's rich. Having my world turned upside down and my heart ripped out is just a situation....right. Running out of milk is a situation. Getting a flat tire is a situation. Being told I have a son and then having that taken away while being betrayed by one of my best friends is a hell of a lot more than a situation, but these kids don't care about that. They just want a funny guy to look up to. Bunch of spoiled brats. Dough: But if there’s one thing that’s kept going through all of that, it’s believing in myself. You know, there’s a quote I’d like to share with you, that a wise man once told me. It goes like this. ‘If you think you’re beaten, you are.’ LKK: Hey, I told you that. No shit, Sherlock. Dough: I know. Now be quiet. Kids, and everybody else out there watching, never lose hope and faith in yourselves. You just can’t afford to. Most people are pretty good judges of what someone’s thinking. If you’re not giving off a positive feeling about yourself, there’s really no reason for anybody else to look at you positively. Never mind that sometimes that's exactly what you want. I don't want anyone looking up to me right now because I know that I damn well don't deserve it. Never mind that though, you've gotta give the fans what they want and damn the consequences. LKK: It’s all about putting out a positive vibe, kids. That’s what the Lunchroom Patrol is all about, it’s what we’ve always been about. Just remember where I came from. You all know the story about the time in fifth grade, when I bodyslammed that kid on the sidewalk after school, and- Here we go again... Dough: Please, not that story again. LKK: Not the whole story, no. Before that, though, I didn’t believe in myself. I didn’t think I could fight back against the bullies. I didn’t think I could stand up for myself. And I don’t know what got into me that day, but I do know why it got into me. I was tired of it. All of it. I was tired of being the kid who got bullied, who didn’t know how to fight back, who was afraid to fight back. And the rest of the kids picked up on that. They know what I was all about, they knew what would get to me. But after that, well, it was a different story. I felt better about things, about myself. I started to project a more positive image, and in no time at all, other kids were picking up on it. And after that, school was a lot more enjoyable. I realized that I had been missing out on a lot. And there wasn’t anything special that did it. I just realized that I needed to believe in myself, so I did and things changed. Learn from me, kids. Learn from Doughboy. Learn from the Lunchroom Patrol. Sheesh that was a lot of pretentious nonsense, and then he has the gall to tell them to learn from us? If they're really been learning from us over the past year I imagine we'd have a lot of parents who were pretty pissed off. Kids would be hitting their best friends with chairs and messing around with each other's girlfriends and doing all sorts of other crazy shit. I'd better keep playing my part though. After all, I'd hate to disappoint the little snots by acting like myself and not like the precious Doughboy. Dough: Learn to believe in yourselves. The crowd politely applauds and Mr. Drake waddles back onto the stage to stand next to the LP. Mr. Drake: That was excellent, gentlemen. I see that we've got a few more minutes before the next period, so would you like to take a few questions from the audience? Dough: Sure thing! Here comes the usual crap. Mr. Drake: Ok everyone, just raise your hand if you've got any questions for these two. *almost all the hands in the place go up* Oh dear.... LKK: Don't worry Mr. Drake, we can handle it from here. *the principal nods and waddles off* How about you, right there in the blue shirt. Boy #1: Yeah, I want to know why you've stuck together as a team for so long. Dough: I'd have to say that the most important reason we've stuck together is because we're good friends outside of the ring. Even though we've gone through some tough times we've been there for each other when the chips were down and it mattered the most. Those kinds of bonds aren't easily broken. Lord knows we've tried enough things that should've broken them. It's just too bad that our entire friendship was built on the lie of me being the inhumanly perfect Doughboy. Brad doesn't know a damn thing about Matt and that's not a good situation. If he doesn't know me, how can we have a friendship? And if we don't have a friendship, how can we have a team? Dough: How 'bout you in the purple. Girl #1: What's happened to Nicole and Katherine? We don't see them much anymore. Are you guys still going out with them? LKK and Dough exchange sheepish glances. LKK: We've all been going through some rough times lately, but I'm sure you haven't seen the last of either of them. They're too cute to let go. They both laugh and the girl seems satisfied with the answer but as she sits down she misses the look of doubt that flashes across Dough's face. Oh, Nicole. It has been a while, but that's not her fault. After all that's gone on I just don't know how to try and pick things back up. Somehow I doubt that little Susie there wants to hear that though. Just give her the storybook ending and she'll be happy. Just wait for her to hit reality, that'll wake her up. Dough: You over there in stripes. Girl #2: I heard that you're in a tag title tournament, but that doesn't make sense because you're already the champs. Can you tell me what's really going on? LKK: You're right that we're the XUW champs, but we're also going after the PTC tag titles. PTC is a place where wrestlers from a bunch of different federations can compete with each other to see who's really the best. We're going to be taking on several different tag teams that are all at the top of their federations in order to prove that no matter how good they are, we're better. Dough: That's right. We've got one round of the tournament behind us and are getting ready for the next one. Right now we're even tied for the lead in our bracket, and can pull out front this week! Yep, that's us, better than everyone else no matter what. Sheesh, could we be any more egotistical here? Sure we won last week but that's only because the other team was a couple of pussies who couldn't even bother to show up for the match, but do you hear us saying that to those kids? Of course not! Heaven forbid the Lunchroon Patrol show any signs of weakness or doubt in our ability to win. You can't call us cocky though, after all, we're just believing in ourselves, right? LKK glances at Mr. Drake who holds up two fingers. LKK: Looks like we've got time for two more questions. How about you in the XUW shirt. Boy #2: Who are you going to be facing this week in the PTC tournament thingy? LKK: We're taking on a team called Invincibleness~!. Dough: What?!? Invisibleness~!? How can we wrestle them if we can't see them? This isn't fair! The kids burst out in laughter at Dough's mock horror causing him to wince a little. Invisibleness? I'm sure they haven't heard THAT joke before. Good thing they're new to this crowd because there was no way a joke that lame should've gotten that much of a reaction. Dough: For the last question let's get you in the shirt that says Smark. Boy #3: I've heard of that team before and happen to know that they're pretty good. You two are going to have your work cut out for you. Lately I've noticed a bit of a similarity between Doughboy and Invincible Gabe. Could you comment on that? Dough: I'm not quite sure what you're referring to. Boy #3: Gabe doesn't hide the fact that when he's in the ring he's playing the character of Invincible Gabe and out of the ring he feels free to be regular 'ole Gabriel Brown. Recently you've made it clear that Doughboy is just a persona that you can slip into but not someone you are all the time. Is this a change that you've made on purpose, perhaps in an attempt to become more like Invincibleness~!? He thinks that this is just a persona I can slip into? Does he think that changing my reactions, my attitudes, my emotions, hell, everything about myself is like putting on a change of clothes? Stuffing myself down into this shell is nothing so easy as pulling on a new shirt or changing into some shorts. Maybe he should try suppressing everything that makes him who he is in order to play the part of someone else. Then he can tell ME what he thinks it feels like. Insolent little brat. LKK: That's a heck of a question there, but I think you're reaching a bit. When Matt started going through his problems we hadn't even heard of Invincibleness~! yet. Everything that you've seen him going through is one hundred percent reality. We don't- Dough: That's not entirely true, Latch. LKK: Excuse me? Dough: Not everything is real, and I can give you a prime example. LKK: Shoot. DB throws his arms up in exasperation. Dough: This whole freaking show! We've been standing up here, rattling off the usual stuff about believing in yourself and trying your best and all that crap, acting like everything is all hunky-dory with us. He takes a few steps forward and drops to sit on the edge of the stage. Dough: Kids, I'm going to level with you. I'm not perfect. I'm very far from perfect to tell the truth. I know that at your age you want to have people to look up to, people to try to be like, but let me urge you to be careful. Don't ever put all of your faith into any of us who are public figures, no matter how good we seem to be, because you'll only be setting yourselves up for disappointment. Don't believe in the Lunchroom Patrol and tell yourself that if we can win these PTC titles then you can do whatever you want. We're just one team out of many and it would be stupid to pin your hopes for the future to us. Our time here with you is almost up, so let me just leave you with this thought. You're all going to go through rough times in your life, that's just the way things are. Take it from someone who's learned the hard way, don't be afraid to show your feelings and don't be afraid to let others know you aren't perfect. If you try to hide it you're only hurting yourself. Mr. Drake comes out and leads the crowd in a round of applause as Dough stands back up. Mr. Drake: Latch Key Kid, Doughboy, we'd like to thank you for coming out here today and sharing with us. Good luck in your wrestling matches. LKK: Thank you. God bless. Latch and Dough wave to the crowd as they make their way to the back. You damn pussy. You should've let them have it, but no, you've gotta give them hope, don't you? That's just like Doughboy...taking the easy road. This ain't going to last long though. You don't have the strength to keep up this front much longer, and then it's back to reality, and just in time for this match. Good timing. They disappear through the curtains and the scene fades out.
