There But For The Grace of God Part Two

19 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-19 :|: Lies - Two Days Ago :|: It had only been a couple of hours since it happened; since Mary Connell’s son was kidnapped right before her eyes. Now, she found it difficult to even breathe - much less take the advice from Mr Stakiri to get some sleep. Instead, she just sat in the living room of her apartment, right next to the phone, just waiting for somebody - anybody - to call with some information. Mr Stakiri had told her to wait at home. He told her that he’d deal with the cops and assured her that everything would be okay and it would be better this way. Mary had nodded, absently, in agreement and allowed Stakiri’s bodyguards to escort her home for protection. What did these kidnappers want from her? What did they want with Michael? She didn’t have anything of value that they could possibly want and she wasn’t even remotely rich. From that logic alone, Mary decided that this wasn’t about her or her son at all. Someone wanted to get to Mr Stakiri and when they saw him with Mary outside of his building the other day, they got the completely the wrong idea. I mean, how moronic do you have to be? It didn’t take much digging to discover that Mr Stakiri even have a wife and the only child he did have was a grown woman. She shook her head angry with herself. Trying to find reasoning behind the actions of a group moronic thugs was about as useful as a chocolate fireguard. The phone call finally came sometime in the early morning and, despite being sat right next to the phone, she reacted slowly, hardly believing that it the thing was actually ringing. Finally, the haze of anxiety and exhaustion subsided as Mary realised that this was actually the real thing. She reached out to pick up the receiver, but then stopped herself. What if it’s bad news? What if they’ve found his body? She asked herself, eyes welling up just thinking about the worst case scenario. Will I be able to handle it? She shook the thoughts from her mind and picked up the receiver. "Hello?" "Mary," Mr Stakiri’s voice replied softly. "I’m afraid there’s been some bad news…" "What? What happened? Did they hurt Michael?" She questioned, hysterically. "I don‘t think so," Stakiri assured her, for whatever good it did, "these kidnappers have somehow gotten the wrong end of the stick. They believe that you’re my wife and Michael’s my son…" "What?" Mary asked. "I know," Mr Stakiri began. "The police have indicated that these kidnappers aren’t the sharpest knives in the draw and aren’t the most efficient crime operation…" Mary developed a confused expression. "They looked quite professional when they jumped out of their black vans…but what about Michael? What are they going to do with him? Did you explain the situation to them? Did you tell them they had the wrong boy?" "Yes," Mr Stakiri replied. "The police kidnap experts told me to tell them that in order to gain a better bargaining position." "And?" She asked, hopefully. "They didn’t react too well…" Mr Stakiri trailed off, leaving the statement hanging in the air for a moment. Mary felt unstoppable tears stream down both cheeks. "Why? What did they say? What did they do?" "We don’t know," Mr Stakiri told her. "They hung up. The police say it isn’t a good sign. The police say…" "The police say what?" She questioned, not even attempting hold back a faint sob. "The police say your son is probably dead by now…" The phone slipped out of Mary’s hand and it the wooden flooring with a clunk, it could’ve broken she really didn’t notice nor care. She felt completely helpless and did all that she could do in this situation; she cried. :|: Reflection - A Few Minutes Ago :|: I stood upon the rooftop of the building directly opposite my prospective target, casually leant against an old, worn out billboard waiting for something to happen. Sunset was now only a few minutes away and the bright red sun was now partially concealed by a beautiful white cloud in the distance, giving off a beautiful dark red, purple glow. You would not even believe it if described to you, but standing there witnessing it for yourself, you cannot help but to accept what your eyes are showing you. I wondered for the hundredth time that minute just what in the hell I was doing here. Just what in the hell I was doing helping out somebody I didn’t even know. I still needed to make a decision regarding the GTT5. Will I going to enter the competition? Will I face Teresa Tomas in the second round? The idea of fighting a woman in general, never mind one of the most prestigious professional wrestling events of the year, is nothing less than repulsive to some, but in this day and age its becoming more commonplace. I’d heard rumours about some guys in various other wrestling federations actually backing out of matches because they didn’t want to fight a woman. Upon hearing this, I couldn’t help but shake my head in disgust. It didn’t much matter to me. An opponent was just an opponent in my books, dwelling on the subject just distracted you from the task of taking our opponent apart, piece by piece. If women want in, then they’re in. I just don’t want them calling fowl play when they get their asses handed to them by the men that’s all. I shook my head, almost shared of thoughts passing through my head. If Aeryn ever heard that kind of talk from me, she’d hang my ass out to dry without giving it a second thought. Damn it, I cursed myself for thinking about her again. These days, after what happened with that ‘Legend’ character in the abandoned warehouse on the lower east side of the city, my thoughts always seemed to flow back to Aeryn. Unsurprising really, considering what went down there. I killed her. She didn’t give me much choice in the matter, but that was neither here nor there; I have no wish to trivialise the matter. She was the reason why I went through with this little deal. She was the reason why I’d decided to help Mary out. Mary reminded me of her; her strength, her pride, her determination. Aeryn would’ve wanted me to do it. Perhaps I should give the GTT5 another shot; everybody deserves a second chance, no matter how young or old they are. :|: Truth - One Day Ago :|: Mary Connell didn’t know why she decided to do it, but something niggling at the back of her mind - perhaps hysteria or even paranoia? - had told her to ring the police to confirm what Mr Stakiri had told her. Of course, she was a little surprised when they’d told her that the incident hadn’t even been reported with them, much less had their help in dealing with the kidnappers, but not much. What the hell was Stakiri thinking? He lied to her about it. He didn’t call the police and pretty much condemned her to lose Michael forever. It was her anger at Stakiri and the driving force of wanting her child back that made her do it in the end. She didn’t much care about the repercussions of her actions - though she realised that they could be great - she just wanted her son back. If not, she wanted the kidnappers dead. An ultimate act of vengeance she probably would never forgive herself for in the long run, but in the short term in seemed a more plausible avenue for her to explore. She went to work extremely early the day after hearing the news about her son. The security guards wondered why she’d come back to work so soon and gave her their empty condolences, which she emptily thanked them for and told them she needed to be at work to get her mind off the whole thing. Upon making it up to the third floor, she made her way from the elevators, placed her handbag on her desk and entered her employer’s office. The third floor was all but empty, just an old cleaner making the rounds, but regardless of this, it was common practice for her to enter her employer’s office first thing in the morning. She made sure everything he needed for the day was there and all the messages were sorted in order of relevance. But this morning she did not do any of those things, instead she made her way to Stakiri’s desk draw, whereupon she extracted his PDA. The password was not difficult to crack given the clues provided to her by various sources (it turned out be the name of his daughter, Alexa) and she quickly scowered the device for the information she needed. When devising the plan, Mary remembered her first day on the job and what she accidentally over heard over the phone system. She found the name and address of Jonathon Winters with the greatest of ease, but, as a bonus, also found information regarding the previous days activities with the kidnappers and her son. Everything had fallen into place for her with the greatest of ease, the question was: did she have the will to go through with it? That question didn’t need any sort of answer, she copied the details down from Stakiri’s PDA and then placed it back where she’d found it, unaware that her actions were being recorded and reported, by the little camera in top corner of the room. :|: Action - Now :|: Mary had discovered the information regarding the kidnappers, what they wanted and how they wanted to do it already, though she neglected to tell me precisely where from. It turned that these guys were amateurs, they had no idea about the extent of Stakiri’s resolve and connections - much less his love life. They’d just rolled into town and heard that he was a major player in the criminal underworld in this part of the world and saw that he appeared nothing more than a harmless businessman. Even Stevie Wonder would know that wasn’t the case; you don’t get the kind of reputation Stakiri has for nothing, you know. I’d wager Stakiri had already made plans to whack these guys, the problem was Stakiri didn’t much care about what they did with the boy in the mean time - I had to. Despite not getting anywhere with Stakiri, the kidnappers had decided to keep Michael alive (I witnessed this first hand when I saw them arrive with him moments ago). What’s the point of kidnapping when you kill your primary bargaining tool? Yes, they couldn’t get anything from Stakiri for the boy, but they could at least get a small sum from the mother for him. They were probably planning on contacting her within a few days or so. I waited until the guards securing the perimeter had gone past and then hurled myself across to the adjacent building, landing perfectly upon the concrete roofing, making minimal noise. I made my way to the door attached to the small building, covering the steps leading into the heart of the warehouse and tried the door. Predictably, it was lock. I cursed, under my breath, before grabbing a hold of the very sturdy-looking door handle with both hands and then forcing myself backward with venomous force. The door easily opened and, in fact, broken away from its hinges. I was nearly caught under the weight of the thing at first, not expecting it to break away so easily, but I adjusted myself quickly and managed take the brunt of the weight. I delicately placed the door on the rooftop surface, making sure that it caused no great sound. I entered the stairway and kept the shadows once emerging onto the top floor of the warehouse. However, upon further consideration it didn’t appear to be an ordinary warehouse at all. It looked as though it had once been some sort of factory, the heavily rusted, metallic production line still standing - in one form or another - and the office building on the top tier seemed in tact. This was also where the kidnappers had congregated with their human bargaining chip. I spied them from the shadows, regulating my breathing to both calm myself down and making me virtually soundless to the average ear. These people may not be the best - or most intelligent - criminal’s in the vast expanse of the underworld, but they sure as hell were well armed. The majority of them sported a variety of rather menacing-looking automatic weapons. Just Great I thought to myself. It’s never easy! Why isn’t it ever easy? A variety of possible tactics, gained through my considerable experience in these matters, shot through my mind just as fast as I shot them down. None of them would work in this scenario and I wouldn’t get anywhere near the boy without being riddled full of bullets. Then I noticed the small rusted loose rivet resting upon the floor and a thought shot into my mind like lightening cutting through a tree. I bent down, picked the small metallic object up and threw the rivet at the metal machines on the lower level. Upon hearing the sound, the leader of the kidnappers rushed out of the office building extremely animated indeed and ordered three of the guards down to the lower level to investigate the sound. Now was the time to act. I stuck close to back wall, as the leader continued to glare down the metal steps, wanting to know immediately what the hell was going on. He didn’t seem as I came within inches of him and quickly, I placed my hand around his mouth to stop him making a sound and pulled him out of the office door. He struggled for a second and attempted to elbow me in the gut furiously. I dodged his feeble attempts and with my free hand grabbed the back of his neck and forced his face into the wall, removing my hand from his mouth before impact. The blow knocked loose some of the already decaying plaster work and made a considerable mess of his face. Just then, another one of the goons popped out of the office wondering just where in the hell his boss had gone all of a sudden. I reacted quickly, knocking his handgun in a direction that wasn’t directly at me, then grabbed his throat. I whirled around when I heard a number of footsteps rattling against the metallic steps. His men hadn’t noticed me yet, but that wasn’t where the threat ended and so I decided to kill two birds with one stone. I lifted the thug off his feet and hurled him down the stairs at the others. They fell down the stairs with loud clangs and wet snapping noises of numerous broken bones - I couldn’t help but smile at the carnage. I darted into the office room and grabbed the boy. The boy appeared to be unconscious for some reason, but I had no time to check the boys pulse to see if her was still alive, I got the hell out of there before the other kidnappers pulled themselves together. I exited the office room, noticing the goons getting back to their feet and, more importantly, reaching over for their weapons. There was no need to conceal my tracks this time, so I moved with great pace back the way I came, hearing the rap of bullets hitting the wall a couple of steps behind me. I rushed up the steps, leading to the roof, the boy still under arm and didn’t miss a step as I threw myself across back over to the adjacent building. About thirty minutes later, at high pace, I managed to get back to my apartment, where I’d left Mary. Upon the sight of us both, she held her hand over her mouth in shock and began to sob. "What happened?" She asked. "I don’t know," I told her, resting the boy on my sofa. "He was like this when I found him." She checked his pulse and found it to still be vibrant enough and then scooped her boy up. "He’s alive, but I think he’s been drugged. I’d better get him to the hospital!" I nodded in response and slumped down into the comforting embrace of my sofa. Despite having the boy in her arms, she managed to reach out and place a reassuring hand upon my forearm. I glared at it, then at her, puzzled. "Thank you," She said, before heading toward the door. I remained silent, but nodded to accept her thanks. She left. :|: Realisation - A Few Minutes Later :|: Mary, with Michael in her arms, made her way quickly out of the apartment building and gratefully slid into the sleek black limousine, nodding at the driver who held the door open for her as she went. Upon sitting down, she ungracefully slumped the boy into the seat next to her, allowing him to fall to one side, against the frame of the opposite door. "Well?" Mr Stakiri asked, in the seat adjacent to her own. "Did he buy it?" "Yeah," she replied. "Didn’t even questioned me much on the subject. He did get rough at the beginning like you said, but nothing I couldn’t handle. I take he didn‘t try to check on anything your end." "No," Stakiri said with a smile. "It was impossible to keep her death from reaching the newspapers, but I know Jonathon Winters. If he agreed to help you he’ll just do it, regardless of the costs, regardless of the pieces to the puzzle that don’t seem to fit. So predictable…" "Her death was planned I take it?" The woman asked. Stakiri smiled. "I can neither confirm, nor deny that statement. Let’s just say my office has more security than anybody actually realises…" She grinned, knowingly. "You still didn’t tell me the point behind this little exercise." "That’s simple, my dear," he replied. "To test him. To test to see if he’s still good enough. To punish that gang of morons who actually thought that I’d bow down to ransom demands and, of course," he smiled viciously at the woman, "to save an innocent boys life." She chuckled at this. "What are you going to do with the boy?" "I’m not sure," He responded. "Make sure he has a good upbringing perhaps. Maybe, sell him to the black market slave trade or I might just give him to my genetics team to do with him as they wish…I haven’t really decided yet." Then, the boy stirred and sleepily opened his eyes. He glanced around the limo warily. "Where am I?" Stakiri leant closer and smiled. "Your safe now!" Michael looked at Stakiri and then at the woman who sat opposite him. "Where’s my mom?" :|: Death - One Day Ago :|: Mary Connell left Stakiri towers feeling elated, yet nervous at the same time. Yes, her son was still with the kidnappers, but at least there was hope now. At least there was a chance now; no matter how small it may be. She just had to follow through with it. She walked onto the road and never saw it coming. The car slammed into her with venomous force, she hit the bonnet, slid over the roof and fell back to the floor in a mess if broken bones. It killed her almost instantly, the piece of paper she had in her hands at the time floated away onto the street as passers by rushed to her aid. Mary Connell died in the street, whilst the car drove quickly away, as though nothing had actually happened.
