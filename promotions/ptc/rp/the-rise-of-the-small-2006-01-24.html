Date: 2006-01-24 The Rise of the Small A bloody Egg yolk. A burnt hole Spreading in a sheet. An en- Raged rose threatening to bloom. -May Swenson ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ He walks at a brisk pace, his immaculately shiny black shoes, clacking off the linoleum floor that covers this stretch of the corridor. It is depressing gray, almost to the point of nausea and he likes that because it heightens the misery of the fortress he has created, the fortress he has passed on to others.. With a sharp flick of the wrist, a silver Rolex watch is revealed. It tells him it is one pm. It also tells him they have the prisoner for another thirty six hours. And this in turn makes him smile. Each action triggers another and that is why so many had come as men only to return as boys. "Patrick O’Shea…" he ponders, extenuating the word O’Shea, allowing it to roll forth from his lips. It was familiar yet he didn’t know how. Perhaps it was this mysterious and unprecedented curiosity that washed over him upon hearing the name, that had paved the way for his re-emergence into the force. Just to vice this fickle wonder that had excited him so perhaps? He would have been bluffing if he had said there was nothing curious in that he had been retired for five years, and despite many offers, had refused to make a reunion until now. He was a man who normally worked action from action, not action from instinct. But he was being magnetized back here now and perhaps soon, he would get answers as to why the name O’Shea has suddenly become so very significant. He adjusts one of the buttons of his pinstriped suit, puffing out his chest to make himself look as menacing as possible for a sixty five year old just as he rounds a corner. Well, answers or not, this O’Shea boy hasn’t ever experienced anything like he’s about to dish out. Young men were so big on being strong, but everyone could be broken, everyone had their weakness and thirty six hours was ample time to find it. He was going to hurt him more than any of those wrestling folk ever could. Oh yes, he’s read the file, hell he’s read the file right down to the parts describing his favorite breakfast and first love interest. But it was his profession that intrigued him the most. Would the fact he was a trained fighter produce a speed bump on his way to answers? He believed not for although he had never interrogated a wrestler, he had seen some of the biggest men be crushed under his word. Yet, once again he was suddenly curious. Could their be a link between this PTC tournament he is in, with the events of early in the week? Did they lend a hand in the bombing? Once again, he doubted it, but never strike out a possibility, no matter how minuet the chances. That was rule number one in this game. He did not know a lot of wrestling yet if his information served him correctly, he was accomplished. A fan favorite in two wrestling organizations, more predominantly AWC where he serves as company face. A good win loss record and a powerful appetite for tournaments. GTT5. Unbeaten in three. This file told him how he had vanquished the Uber Judge…some kind of Infinite Gauntlet champion. It played back in his head as impressive and so it is noted. Next is Jason Warr, another O’Shea had steamrolled through. Finally, Doreen Pepperpot. A man that beats a women? The picture he was painting was already taking shape in his head. And now he will wrestle three others. The man couldn’t help but snigger as he rewrote the sentence in his head - he was going to wrestle three others. That was until he met Morris Livingston. Combing salt and pepper colored hair back with his fanned fingers, Morris remembers one other thing now of the call that was to entice him back into work. Oh, you won’t want to miss this…you’ll appreciate we brought you in. they had said. Goddamn surprises, he hated them and normally he wouldn‘t have even given into the curiosity. Yet it all led back to the name. O’Shea Morris rounded a final bend so that there were a number of doors lining each side of the increasingly narrow corridor. "Forty three…" he muttered and cause brought action as he felt his legs pulling himself towards it, being compelled by what lay ahead. Opening the door without pause, he swung it open and found himself smiling. The huddled male that begged for mercy in the corner was oddly, a blast from the past. The face…it was so familiar. O’Shea…haven’t we met before? Well not you…your Father…we tried to send him down once…he got through but hell, we’ll send him down in time. Until then… one O’Shea is as good as another… Morris Livingston peeled his belt off and extracted a sheet of paper from his back pocket. "Sign it…or else." ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ When all beside…a vigil keeps… The West asleeps… The West asleeps… ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ He sits in the corner, sucking in breathe in feverish little gasps. He knows he’s breathing, he can hear it sliding down his throat and then being puffed back out again, but that doesn’t change the feeling that he’s drowning in this little corner of this little room, with eyes from all around marking where he sits. Cold and unsympathetic they demand answers that he just doesn’t have. Beside him sits a sheet of paper, he never could read so well but he gets the quist of this. Here is a confession, key to a nice shiny badge for each of them and a twenty year prison stretch for him. All has to do is sign it and the pain stops. The pen that lies at his feet is beckoning him to do so. His damp crotch is playing up again, irritating his skin once more with it’s incessant burning sensation. Not that he cares very much, the pain is too great in his head and gut to worry about such minor detail as that. Hell, he’d gladly bathe in a bath of piss if they’d just let him close his fucking eyes. The pain re-emerges once more and comes in throbbing waves that threaten to swallow him whole. It hurts to the point where he has to howl to maintain sanity. Maybe…maybe if he just closes his eyes, just for a second. Maybe it…will…go…a- A sharp kick to the head jerks him back into consciousness. If only he could bite their fucking feet off, he wouldn’t be kicked anymore. Blood enters the peripheral of his vision as it meanders down the grain of his bone structure, just at the side of his eye. Probably reopened a split, there’s quite a few up there. There another thing in his peripheral vision and that’s a huge lump nearly the size of a golf ball, just under his left eye. And Jesus that fucking hurts. There never has been a pain like this he believes, not in his existence on this earth…then again, he’s been kicked so stupid he can’t remember what day of the week it is. Man, he might live after this if he sees it to the end, but the chances of him being able to walk again have become a serious outsider bet. He faintly remembers falling out of a tree when he was twelve that left a split so bad he had to get seven stitches at the crown of his head, and the jolt that had corseted through his arms as it held him motionless like its puppet. But that pain wasn’t as bad as this agony. You couldn’t get stitches in to heal him from what he was receiving, not when the biggest wound is when they rip away your freedom. A name flashes into his head. Morris Livingston. He has done this. Rage like an red hot fist grips him by the throat. His breath finally begins to ease as he scans the room for the perpetrator of all this. There’s three men leaning against the far wall, one sitting on the table and another one beside the door. Yet none of them are Morris Livingston, none of them wear the crop of salt and pepper hair, slicked back, without a hair out of place. He’s away to the toilet…don’t you remember him telling you? It was only a minute ago Paddy mutters something to himself about how he forgot, then he rubs his head vigorously trying to get it to work right. Something isn’t right now, some cog have been kicked loose and he knows it. Though give him a second and he’s sure to forget that. Morris Livingston Paddy glances out to the hall through the opened cell door. He can see the shadow of the beast, twisted and elongated against the concrete wall. In his inner eye, he prays to God for a noose to attach to that shadow’s neck and hoist him into the air. He can see shadow Morris eating a shadow sandwich with glee, with shadow lips licking the shadow sauce off his shadow fingers. Perhaps he’ll go and be a nice little chappy and phone the police to save this fragmenting little boy. Ridiculous…he is the law. He hears Morris talking to a voice he can’t hear. A mind game he would have told himself if his brain was actually a singular object. "How longs left? Forty eight hours you say?" Impossible…he’s been here two days…can’t to be right? No windows Paddy, how would you know when day passes into night I…I…it…can’t be. Not another two days…please, no. To be exact, Paddy had two hours left but Livingston was frustrated. Paddy had held and held and held and held. Any indication he was near the finishing line and he might grow a new set of balls…and then he escapes through the net just like his Father. No way, not on his fucking watch, that’s for sure. Paddy palms the lump below his eye, putting a little pressure on it then releasing when the pain begins to scorch him. Now the shadow is changing and Paddy’s eyes are fixed on it. Everyone else is staring at him. If Livingston can’t do it, either can they…but they have to keep up the pressure, they have to bring that son of a bitch down. The shadow bobs and swells on the wall as it draws nearer, his voice heard once more. "Don’t worry, this bastard is going to get it now." Suddenly he appears at the doorway. Staring at Paddy with expressionless eyes that remind him of shards of glass,. He flashes a dry smile. "You look a mess Paddy," he mutters, holding out both hands briefly then allowing them to clap back to his sides. "Whatever can be done." "Please…" Paddy whispered, now aware of how swollen his throat feels. "I know," Morris sighed, forcing himself to look sympathetic. He spoke as if everything he said explained everything he had done, as if his words put the situation into a rational context. He turns and stares fixedly at the dismembered chair they had beaten him with. It’s an iconic statue for what put him down. But he won’t stay down, he can’t, surely not now. Forty eight hours Morris walked slowly around the table, casual and carefree, he then bent down beside Paddy, rapping the sheet at his base, with his knuckles. "We will beat you…and it will be the most horrible thing you’ve ever felt…we’ll beat you until the time is up AND then we’ll beat you some more…we won’t let you walk away from this, you’ll be carried out. Now do you want that, or are you going to admit to the crimes you have done?" Paddy looked into the emotionless eyes that were like windows to his evil mind. I mean, what was he waiting for? More fists until he went numb, dead from the neck down? They had him in their snare and they were about to feast, why not break free when the opportunity arises. He stares at the sheet of paper. Because you’ll be running into a room exactly the same as this. "No…" Paddy whispers, his voice quivering. Morris keeps eye contact and then nods with a sigh. He gets up and walks into the makeshift circle the other men have coordinated. And now he’s shouting. "Okay…we want that signature boys! Take it whatever way you can!" Paddy slides up the wall onto his feet not ready for the next assault but not prepared to do it on his ass again. He pedals his feet backwards, moaning and shaking his head. One of the shoes begins to slide off his foot as he continues pedaling. He can feel fresh pains as he arches back once more into the wall, bruised muscles tickling bruised muscles. It feels like a rusty coat hanger cutting into his skin from the inside. And then he sees the future, uniforms and steel bars, and now he’s sucking it all up, ready to take one of them down at least. And they come, with heads lowered and arms swinging like pendulums at each side, they come with batons in hands. "Leave me alone!" Paddy screams and then he’s being battered into the wall, flailing desperately as he tries to hit something, then being pounded back from whence he came. "Aye haven’t done anything!" And without relent, they keep swinging, all the while Morris stokes the fire. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ "What did he say again, dump him outside?" "That’s what he said, just out in the carpark for all he cares". As Sergeant Smith and Sergeant Fenn hoist the unconscious body of Paddy O’Shea down the hall, despite the line between their culture that says they must hate each other, a feeling of awe washes over the pair. Because Morris Livingston came to conquer this small yelp of a man…and he had walked away beaten. Three days he had lasted their trials, hell, the dried black blood that scoured his face was homage to this. And now he was to be dumped outside - an innocent man, and left ot his own devices. And this was the fucking law. It just wasn’t right. "Poor fella…I didn’t mean to hit him so hard. I was just so sure he did it." "We were only following orders. No point on dwelling on it." But they would dwell on it. These naïve youngsters had been introduced into the world of interrogation on IRA suspects…and they left with a steely taste sickening their mouths. They would think back to the day they beat an innocent man within an inch of a life and pray to God he might see fit to forgive them. He was bloody egg yolk. A burnt hole spreading in a sheet. An enraged rose threatening to bloom. He had taken what was given to him and spit it out, asking for more. And for that, this man had their admiration. "Put him down here…if Livingston doesn’t do anything about it in the next fifteen minutes, we’ll take him to the hospital." Fenn nodded as they emerged through the exit of the buildings, before gently laying him down on the sidewalk that bordered the small carpark. With a sigh, they turned and reentered the building. It just wasn’t right. How long did he lay their unconscious? Who knows? Hell, he was so disorientated even when he didn’t know where to start. For one thing he couldn’t move. There was darkness. One, two, five minutes passed as the chill set in, Paddy not sure he was awake or asleep. Then there ten, twelve, fourteen- Then a hand grabbed him by the wrist and hoisted him to his feet. It was his- "Da," Paddy mumbled, using as much energy as he could muster to sound surprised. He was staring into the eyes of the Father he hadn’t seen for fifteen years. And those eyes were leaking. "Aye knew aye’d find ye," Danny said, trying to fight back the tears. "Aye feckin’ knew aye would." Danny put his arms around Paddy and held him in a tight embrace. Right now, history could wait, Paddy needed someone to comfort him, to help him live for God’s sake. "Aye won’ ever let ye get hurt again…comon, the car’s over here. Aye’ll take ye t’ a hospital. Paddy nodded weakly and with Danny’s help, stumbled over to the rented Cavalier. Oh how bitter the Lord is that he would take what Paddy had earned. He never heard the approaching footsteps that came from the building. The butt of a rifle pounded the back of Paddy’s head and he fell forwards on the concrete, too exhausted to use his hands to break the fall. He did hear shouts though, those of his Father’s. Paddy rolled over onto his side, gurgling almost like a child. Although he was gargling blood. And he seen something more horrible than anything he had seen in the last three days. He seen his Father being dragged inside by four soldiers, while Morris Livingston stood to the side, a proud grin on his face. "Thirty six years Danny…and we finally got you. Remember to thank your son for making such a flawed plan, foolproof." Then Paddy faded to black because despite what he was seeing, there’s only so much grief a man can take before he folds. There was something between Livingston and his father…there had to be. END
