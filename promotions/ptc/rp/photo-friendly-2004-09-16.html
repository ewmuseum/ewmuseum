Photo Friendly

16 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-16 **shutter** **click** (The intense light from the a flash bulb illuminates the small room momentarily. For a brief sliver of time, every detail of the small space is accented, from the plaster flaking off the ceiling to the dull whitish paint coating the walls. Even the musky smell that pervades the chamber seems heightened by the flash, some how. Photo negatives hang from twine strung across the room, a sure sign that what we are seeing is a dark room, used for developing pictures. The room revisits its state of darkness once more, and all that is left in this dank slice of nothing is the silhouette of a large, long haired man sitting on a stool in the middle of the room.) Desolation: A camera. What a useful little contraption. **shutter** **click** (Desolation makes the camera he’s holding flash once more. His green eyes twinkle divinely for a brief instant.) Desolation: Practical, portable, but, at the same time, the single most intrusive technological advance of the last two hundred years. If there’s anything that has been with us in our most intimate times, it’s been the camera. **shutter** **click** (The camera flashes once more, lighting up Des’s coy smile.) Desolation: It’s flash like the bright flames of the youthful Phoenix. (Des chuckles to himself.) Desolation: Pardon my pun. (Des continues to speak, almost poetically.) Desolation: So, what’s so special about a camera? Well, I’ll tell you. A camera is embodied with an unusual ability. The ability to see right from wrong. Good from bad. Desolation: You see, nobody wants to capture the bad things with a camera. Only the good. Everyone’s family photos show things like Mother hugging Father after a long day of work. They show Sister and Brother playing in the sandbox on a bright summer afternoon. They show the excitement a new puppy brings, and the joy felt on Christmas morning. But what about the other things? What about the details that go unnoticed by the camera? Where’s the photo of Father as he raises his fist to Mother? Where’s the photo of Sister biting the kneecap of Brother as he rips fistfuls of hair from her scalp? Where’s the photo of the new puppy, now grown, as he lays on the side of the road, his yellowed innards dripping from his mouth? They don’t exist. There aren’t any pictures of the things we least want to remember. Who would want to keep the thought that there are things in our life we are ashamed of? (The camera “claks” as Desolation lets it drop to the floor) Desolation: But you wouldn’t know anything about that, would you Jay Phoenix? How could someone as proficient as you in the ring be ashamed of anything? Look at everything you’ve done. Do you remember the first title you won in PWW? That was a career defining moment, wasn’t it? You probably keep a picture from that match on your mantle, ready to show it to all your friends. And what about when you won the UWF World title? I’ll bet there’s a picture of that in your bedroom so that you can sleep at night, secure in the thought that it all really happened. And, of course, there’s the infamous Coal Miner’s Glove Match with Psymon. That really was a great match, wasn’t it? I’ll bet there’s a photo of that taped to your bathroom mirror so that it’s the first thing you see in the morning. That way, the proud feeling you get knowing you were involved in such an epic match stays with you for the rest of the day. Then, of course, there was the first GTT tournament where you made the finals and proved to everybody that you were one of the best players in the game. I’ll bet you keep a 5x7 of that in you wallet, next to all of your most personal photographs. Desolation: With all that in mind, how could the great Jay Phoenix have anything to be ashamed of? What could possibly make this man self-conscious? All of your cohorts would probably say “Nothing, absolutely nothing.” (The camera focuses in on, what we can only imagine through the darkness, is Desolation’s face.) Desolation: But I, for one know better, Jay Phoenix. I know you have plenty to feel ashamed about. That UWF Title reign of yours? It was nothing less than embarrassing . How long was it, again? A month. Was it even that long? Do you even remember? Surely you’re not proud of that. I bet you don’t keep a picture of Digital Mortality holding your belt, do you? And that Coal Miner’s Glove Match? Didn’t you lose that, too. Where’s the picture of you laying under Psymon, your face oozing blood? Don’t have one, huh? I didn‘t think so. But what about when you reached the GTT finals? Surely that’s something you can hang your hat on, right? Wrong-o, J.P. Not only did you lose that too, but you lost it to a cheater no less, and that’s a thousand times worse. I would love to see a photo of Jay Phoenix losing to a cheater, if there were such a photo. Jay Phoenix has nothing to be ashamed of… [scoffs]… maybe you aren’t as great as everybody says you are. Desolation: So, I guess we’ll see later this week, won’t we? We’ll see if this GTT tournament will be a photo-worthy event for Jay Phoenix, or if you’ll just have to add another item to list of things that you’re ashamed of. Either way… don’t forget to the camera. (Desolation kicks the camera lying on the floor and it hits the tripod of the video camera in front of him.) **shutter** **click** (The camera flashes again, giving us a shot of Desolation’s backside as he exits the room.)
