Big Billy Rides Again

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 Word traveled fast among the folks on the Skoal Rodeo Circuit. And this was no exception. The one thing that everybody…cowboy, clown, and crewmember alike…had feared had happened. Nobody knew what to expect next. Hell, nobody ever expected it to come to this. But it had. And now that the ball had started rolling, it would be damn near impossible to stop it. They knew the Skoal Rodeo Circuit would never be the same. Yes, it was true. Big Billy Blackwell and Texas Red had finally come to fisticuffs. ------------------------------------- Y’all just sit right back and let Ol’ Chuck tell you the story of how it all went down. I know better than anybody, seeing as how I was there the first day each of them showed up on the tour. Oh, Texas Red, he had been there for what seemed like forever. He was the established one, the vet’ran if you will, always acting as if he were on his high horse. No pun intended, o’course. Most people, they didn’t care much for Red. See, all that talkin’ he did only bred arrogance. And if you know anything about folks on the rodeo, and cowboys in general, it’s that they ain’t much for arrogance. Not in themselves and definitely not in others. Add in the fact that Red was just an asshole to most people, and you can understand why. But like I said, I was there when he came on the circuit. He was fresh out of some hillbilly college in Texas, rode equestrian or something there. National champion, he said. I don’t know what he was the champion of, but I just imagined him as having all sorts of ribbons and goofy ass buttons and whatnot for his prizes. Yeah, that’s about how I pictured him. Anyway, I let him know early on that all the equestrian ridin’ in the world weren’t gonna get him nowheres on this circuit. No sirree, it was gonna take honest to God rodeo skills. And what did Texas Red do then? He spit some tobacco juice on the ground at my feet, tipped his hat down over his eyes a piece more, and hopped up on the biggest horse we had. That horse was takin’ a nap or somethin’, cause when Red hopped on him, that horse was like to buckin’ like no horse I ever seen buck before. Red held on, though. You could tell it was takin’ all his energy, but he was managin’. And he stayed on till that horse tired out and decided he weren’t gonna buck no more, and then Red wound up trotting him around the corral like some sort o’ tamed mare. I ain’t never seen anything like it. Till Big Billy showed up, at least. But as I was sayin’, Red made his mark on the circuit in no time flat. Sure, the older guys begrudged him the fact that he was a youngster overtakin’ them, but the truth is, he was just flat out better than them. Here you had these men who had spent their whole lives getting’ to the top of the circuit, only to have some college boy step in and put them to shame. Add in the fact that he created some goofy ass name for himself, and well, there ya go. What? No, I don’t know how he chose that name. He had red hair, yeah, but he weren’t from Texas. Oh, he claimed to be, but I have it on good authority from my cousin that he’s really from Oklahoma. He just went to college in Texas. I guess he figured Texas Red sounded mean and menacing and what have you. Anyway, I think he just lifted it from a Marty Robbins song, to tell the truth. But Texas Red, yeah, he was something, that’s for sure. ------------------------------------- Big Billy walked into the XUW Arena, and his eyes lit up immediately. This was it. He was finally here. XUW, the place he had longed to be forever. Or at least, since his rodeo career ended. Or rather, since he gave up trying to fish professionally. In other words, not for very long. But he was here, dadgummit, and he was gonna make the most of it. Make these folks rue the day they ever heard the name Big Billy Blackwell. He was gonna show them just how mean a cowboy he was, just how tough he could be, and just how good of a wrestler Big Billy Blackwell was. Only one problem. He had never wrestled before. Okay, two problems. He was fat and out of shape. Well, make that three, why don’t ya. He was gay. Not flamingly so, but enough so that he didn’t exactly strike fear in people’s hearts. Yes, Big Billy Blackwell definitely had his work cut out for him this time. But, as he told himself, he had been here before. ------------------------------------- So anyway, Texas Red went on for years and years, just rulin’ the Skoal Rodeo Circuit. I mean to say, folks would come from miles around to see him. This one time, we were on down in, uhm, hell, Del Rio I think it was. Piss hole town down in Texas, on the Rio Grande, like many of the towns we went to. There were folks came from as far away as San Antonio and Monterey. They had people crossing the dadgum border to come see him. You ever hear of that? People going to a different country just to see a cowboy? But they did, and Texas Red ate that up. Oh, you have no idea how much he loved that. For one, because he just flat out loved the attention. But two, because he knew how much it pissed off the others. And this went on for a while. And things were okay, if not a bit strained. And then Big Billy Blackwell showed up. And then things changed. Big Billy, you see, was just about the antithe…the anti…well, he was the opp’site of Texas Red. Whereas Red was mean and tough and talked down to the others, Big Billy was happy-go-lucky, wore his hat up so you could see his face plainly, and he always had a kind word for everyone. And boy, how that pissed off Texas Red. By the time Big Billy came around, Red had been around long enough to be considered one of the vet’rans. And you gotta expect that Red reacted to Billy the same way the older guys reacted to Red hisself when he first showed up. I mean, Texas Red was beside hisself cause the attention Big Billy got. Here’s this new guy, all flash and glitz and bein’ a pretty boy and all. And dang, how the girls loved him. You shoulda seen the way they flocked to him. All of a sudden, Red weren’t the hot thing on the tour anymore. The new boy was. ------------------------------------- "It’s just wrestling," Big Billy said, as he tipped back the bottle of beer and took a sip. He licked the excess residue off his handlebar moustache and smiled at the bartender. "I mean, it cain’t be the toughest thing I’ve ever done. After all, I rode Large Larry." The bartender shook his head and grimaced. "Big Billy, Large Larry is the reason your rodeo career ended." "Oh, yeah," Big Billy said, scratching his head. "Well, I…uhm…did a bunch of other stuff on the circuit, too." "Like that time the other guys took you across the border to go to Boys Town?" Big Billy shrugged. "How was I supposed to know?" ------------------------------------- Big Billy looked around at the spectacle that is Boys Town, and his smile slowly faded away. Another cowboy tapped him on the shoulder. "What’s wrong, Big Billy? Don’t this look like fun?" Big Billy shook his head. "Sure, I suppose so. But, uhm, where are all the boys?" "Boys?" the other cowboy replied. "Look at the women, Big Billy." "I am. That’s why I asked where all the boys are." "Uhm…Big Billy? Are you, well, you know…" "Proud to be a cowboy? You bet yer sweet ass I am." "No," the other cowboy protested, "I mean, are you…gay?" Big Billy smiled. "Well, hell’s bells, boy, of course I am. Ain’t you?" ------------------------------------- "Well, hell, how was I s’posed to know what that place was?" Big Billy asked the bartender. "I just assumed…well, what with the name and all…" The bartender set a fresh bottle of beer in front of Big Billy. "I swear, Big Billy, sometimes I wonder about you." ------------------------------------- And that was how come we got to find out that there was something off about Big Billy. And then we all knew why he shied away when all those girls flocked around him. He didn’t want any of that. But that didn’t stop anybody from coming to see him, nope. The crowds still showed up, ‘cept they were coming to see Big Billy and not Texas Red. And that just set Red off. Big Billy’s rodeo career took off in a flash. He was the one to ride the toughest horses, he was the one who could stay on the bull for eight seconds, he was the one who could rope a calf like it was standing still. All that stuff that Red had worked so hard to gain, Big Billy swooped on in and took it over hisself. And Big Billy became great. He was even better than Texas Red in his prime. And he became mean, too. Not to his friend, nope, but to those who were mean to him. Boy, he became hardened. I guess it was on account of how he was gay and all, and he figured that would be a disadvantage once everyone found out. So he toughened hisself up. And he became one of the toughest and meanest I ever seen. Then the hard times came. Red got to thinkin’ of how he could get Big Billy off the circuit. ------------------------------------- "PTC? What’s that?" Big Billy asked the flier posted on the back wall of the XUW Arena. A random arena worker walked by just at that point and snorted. "Some sort of group for people from all sorts of wrestling federations. They’re having a big tournament, I hear. Fatbadger and the Uber Judge were talking about it earlier." "A big wrestling thing? A tournament? Wrestlers from all over the place? Oh, my, I think I need to sign up for this." The arena worker stared at Big Billy. "But, Big Billy, you haven’t even won a match in XUW. What makes you think you can win in the GTT5?" "Who said anything about winning?" Big Billy asked. "I just care about the men from all over the place." The arena worker shook his head and walked off. ------------------------------------- "So that’s why you wanted to enter this thing, Big Billy?" the bartender asked as he wiped off the bar counter. "Well, yeah. I guess it is. That and I wanted to see how well I can do when I’m new to something else. I had no problem when I was new to the rodeo. I took over that Skoal Circuit, and pissed off Texas Red something fierce at the same time. I was a newcomer to that and I did alright for myself. And if I did that well in rodeo, why shouldn’t I be able to do that well in wrestling?" "The difference," the bartender replied, "is that you knew something about rodeo before joining the Skoal Circuit." "Well, yeah. I knew them cowboys rode horses and bulls and stuff, and the clowns…mmm, the clowns…but I didn’t know much more than that. I was fresh game, you might say." "No, I won’t say. Okay, fine. You’re in the GTT5. And you’re facing Emilio Rage. What makes you think you can beat him?" "Well," Big Billy started, then began to sing that song from Annie Get Your Gun, "Anything he can do, I can do better/I can do anything better than him." The bartender sighed. "Okay, Big Billy, that’s great. But let’s leave the singing cowboy gig to the original one, Gene Autry. Okay?" Big Billy frowned. "But Gene Autry’s dead." The bartender nodded. "Excactly." Big Billy pouted. The bartender shook his head. "Anyway, what makes you think that? He’s wrestled before. He’s faced XUW people. Heck, he won the PTC Global Title from the Latch Key Kid in a big four-way match." Big Billy suddenly stopped singing. "Did you say four-way?" "Yes." "Hmmm. And this was in PTC?" "Yes. It was after the Latch Key Kid won the Global Title after GTT4." "Oh," Big Billy smiled. "Well, there’s your answer. That’s why I entered the GTT5. To get a four-way out of it." "Nothing’s guaranteed, Big Billy." "’Cept me havin’ fun in a wrestlin’ ring just like I had fun in the rodeo ring." ------------------------------------- Oh, Texas Red was a crafty one, of course. You don’t spend that much time on the circuit without learning a thing or two about being sneaky. Trouble is, Big Billy turned out to be a lot craftier than Red gave him credit for. Anything Red did to try to take out Big Billy, Big Billy somehow dodged the bullet. Till Red decided to slip something into Large Larry’s food on that fateful day. ------------------------------------- The cop used his nightstick to nudge the drunk in the park. He poked him again, and finally the man began to stir. "Let’s go, buddy," the cop said, "you can’t sleep here. You gotta get moving." "Leamelone," the drunk replied. "I’msleepin." "Not here you’re not, buddy. Now come on, let’s move." The cop knelt down to get a better look at the man, and realized he was looking at Big Billy Blackwell. "Hey, I know you! You’re Big Billy Blackwell. My dad used to take me to watch you in the rodeo all the time when I was a kid." "Rodeo…" "Yeah, that’s what I said. Rodeo. Look, I know what you’ve been through lately, so I’m not gonna take you in. But you can’t sleep here again, okay? Just take it easy." The cop walked off, and after a moment looked back over his shoulder at Big Billy. "Fuckin’ disgrace, he muttered to himself." ------------------------------------- "So you really think you have a shot at winning this match, Big Billy?" the bartender asked again. "Sure. I mean, I figure I’ve got as much of a chance as Emilio Rage," Big Billy replied. "Except for the fact that he’s in better shape than you, he doesn’t drink like a fish, and oh yeah, he’s a wrestler." Big Billy shook his head repeatedly. "I’m a wrestler too, man. I may be newer to this thing than good ol’ Emilio, but so what? I was new to the rodeo once, too, and I did okay for myself. I had to fight for that, and I got what I fought for. I had to fight to get into XUW, and I got there. It wasn’t easy, and they told me the same thing. ‘You’re no wrestler, Big Billy. Get outta here.’ I didn’t listen to them, either. I kept at it, and kept on persistin’, and I made it. And now I’m in GTT5? Don’t think I ain’t gonna fight this time. My whole life has been about fightin’ to get to the top. Why stop now?" "Well, that’s a pretty good attitude to have, Big Billy. I gotta give you credit for it." "Thanks," Big Billy replied. "But there’s another reason, too. There was a time not too long ago, I was walkin’ down the street. A little kid and his father passed me, and this guy told his son who I was. The boy looked up at him and said, ‘Big Billy the rodeo guy?’ and his dad said, ‘Yep, that’s him.’ The little boy said, ‘Was he as good as they said he was, dad?’ and his father answered, ‘No, son, he was even better than that. He was even better.’ And that’s what I want again. The chance to be better. The chance to make my mark. The chance to make people remember me. What better way to do that than by entering GTT5?" The bartender shrugged and flipped his bar towel over his right shoulder. "Good question, Big Billy. Sounds like you’ve got your mind made up. Best of luck to ya, I’m sure you’ll do well." Big Billy sighed and pushed off his bar stool. "Thanks. I think it’s about time I head home. Can you call me a cab?" "Sure," the bartender said, reaching for the phone. "It’ll be here in a few minutes." Big Billy headed for the door, then stopped and looked back at the bartender. "Say…thanks again." The bartender waved playfully. "Don’t mention it, Big Billy." And with that, Big Billy Blackwell walked out of the bar. ------------------------------------- So do I think he can win this match? Ask me if I thought he could be a rodeo champion the first time I saw him, and I would’ve said no. He proved me wrong. I ain’t gonna give him the chance to do that again. So yeah, I think he can win this match. You just listen to Ol’ Chuck, and you’ll turn out just fine.
