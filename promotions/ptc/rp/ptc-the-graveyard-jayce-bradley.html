The Graveyard (Jayce Bradley)

27 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A lot of time had passed since that skeleton was buried in the ground. Detective Nick Pride had been on the job for quite some time and could easily recognize that. He just did not know how long. He turned to the local Sheriff. “Who found it?” “A couple of hikers stumbled upon it early this morning. I’m thinking that the heavy rains that have fallen these past few days washed it out of the ground. Hell, half the mountain slid into the river during that storm.” His stare narrowed on Nick’s face. “You must have broken quite a few laws getting over here so quick.” “You don’t know the half of it.” “You think this is somehow connected to one of your cases?” “I’m not sure.” He paused for a brief moment, taking a closer look at the remains. “Nah, the bones are too developed.” “You’re looking for a kid?” “A teenager.” He had been since he learned about the kid a few months ago. “No one mentioned anything about the age range on the skeleton.” The sheriff bridled. “Look, I don’t deal with stuff like this on a daily basis. We ain’t nothing like the big city you come from.” “You recognized possible knife wounds on the rib cage, so you have to have had some sort of—” “Look, I don’t need guys like you coming around and slamming me, Pride. I run a tight ship around here, and we don’t need no city cops sticking their nose where it don’t belong. I let you come here out of courtesy, but don’t you ever disrespect me again.” It was a mistake, Pride thought to himself despairingly. It had been a while since he had slept through the night without waking up covered in sweat, nightmares of Russell Braun’s fatal wound striking his daughter instead usually kept him from having any sort of peace. “I’m sorry. I didn’t mean to offend you in any way.” “Well it did offend me. You don’t have a clue about what goes on down here. This town gets filled with tourists throughout the year, and you have no idea how many of them get lost or hurt in these mountains. We may not have to deal with drugs or murder on a daily basis, but we protect and serve just like you do.” Pride lifted his hands in surrender. “Okay, I get the point.” His gaze wandered out across the mountainside, following the stream as it cascaded down and over the edge into the river below. The sheriff’s voice breaking his train of thought. “This is not something that happens up here though.” He scowled. “It has to be one of yours. People don’t go around killing people and tossing their bodies into the ground like that around here.” “Maybe. I just don’t see anyone taking the time to come all the way out here to get rid of a body. Although, it would be a long time before a corpse would be found out here.” With a nod, the sheriff agreed. “If it weren’t for the heavy rains, it could have been years before we came across this one.” “It may have been that long already. I’m going to get going, no sense in keeping you from your work. I’m sure you’ll have someone here to examine the remains soon, and I don’t want to be a bother.” “I’ll be honest, because I’m always willing to admit when I need help.” “If I were you, I’d call down to San Antonio and get them to send someone from their Pathology department. I’m sure they’d be willing to help.” “You think you could do it?” “I would, but I’m not actually here officially.” “Wait. What?” The Sheriff’s brow furrowed in confusion. “You failed to mention that when you flashed your badge and started asking questions.” “Yeah, sorry about—” “You’re Nick Pride.” Pride nodded. “Yeah, I told you that.” “I know, but with everything going on I didn’t put two and two together. You thought this had something to do with the guy who abducted your daughter, didn’t you?” “I guess word gets around, huh.” “Like a wild fire. Any idea how many people that son of a bitch killed?” Pride shook his head. “Not at all, but someone like him had to have been doing this for years. The fact that he was never caught just messes up any clue as to how many people he killed, and if he has done this anywhere else.” “Whatever the case, the bastard deserved to die.” “Tell that to the relatives of the countless victims he may have left behind.” The sheriff’s eyes lowered as he stared at his feet, not realizing the full extent of his words. “I—” Pride watched as the sheriff choked on his words, knowing that he knew he too had made a mistake. He did not wait for the sheriff to come up with the right words, instead choosing to make his way down the mountain. There was nothing for him here, at least nothing he was looking for. “Sheriff!” The walkie-talkie at his side buzzed with life. “PRIDE!” Nick looked back up the cliff, at a frantic sheriff out of breath and yelling down at him. “Yeah?” “They found more.” The word hit him in the chest like a ton of bricks. “They found more skeletons. One of them may be the one you’re looking for.” =¤=¤=¤=¤=¤= They had found them. He turned off the television, leaning back in his chair to think about what could come of this. As far as he knew, apart from the last few months where Braun had become sloppy, none of their kills had ever been discovered. They had always been so cautious—so meticulous—always going the extra mile to keep from getting caught. At the time, they had traveled quite a distance to keep anyone from discovering their little late night hobby. He and Braun had gone out of their way, this graveyard becoming one of their favorite spots to rid themselves of their victims. And now, because of Mother Nature wanting to be a bitch, they had been found. Was it an accident though…or an act of God? A case could be made that the Lord himself came down and pulled the cadavers from their resting spot, his own way of keeping him from going unpunished. He just smiled. He could care less what anyone thought. For all anyone knew, the murderer had already paid for his sins with his own life. Atonement for a would be hero, had he not had a hand in the crimes himself. There was no reason to worry, any mistakes would have been washed away by the mud and rain. He could hardly remember who they had buried up in the mountains anyway, nor did he care to remember. His life was finally on the right track, and nothing could take that from him. Not even Hoyt himself. This was nothing more than an interesting development. Something to entertain him as they struggled frantically to find any sort of clue as to who had done this, only to find that the man responsible could no longer be punished. At least, as far as they knew. As far as they would ever know.
