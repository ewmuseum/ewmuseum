Faith - Part III: Resurgence

11 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-11 Faith – Part III: Resurgence “Ring, come on ring!” I have to utter under my breath as I sit over the telephone. Over at the other side of the room the staff are being treated to drinks as part of our newspaper’s anniversary, I don’t want them to see me here at my desk, hunched over the telephone in the hope that somebody rings even though they probably know why I’m not over there right now anyway. I’m waiting for a phone call, from anybody, with any details about Dave’s possible whereabouts. I have written a story in the newspaper about it, unfortunately it didn’t make the front page, but I left a hotline number for people to call if they know anything. But I haven’t had a single call all day. “Please… come on…” It’s no use. Surely somebody has seen Dave somewhere… there’s something not right here. Dave is not dead, I refuse to believe that, but I have a feeling… I think Dave may be in grave danger. But there’s nothing that I can do… I’ve been out to the streets searching for him. I’ve sent out a plea for help in the newspaper with no reply, it’s no use. There’s nothing I can do. A uniform chuckle bellows from the opposite end of the room as one of the editors makes his custom gag for the evening. It would usually elicit a giggle from me, but I’m in no mood for laughs. It’s not their fault, I shouldn’t blame them for wanting to celebrate their anniversary, whether I’m in this much distress or not. I just can’t stand this any longer though. I can’t put up with a room full of happy joyous people while I sit here, a miserable wreck, and wait for news that just isn’t going to arrive. Actually there is one more thing that I can do for Dave… I quickly push in the power button of my PC, slide my chair backward, get up and walk over to the coat-stand by the door. I’m not sure if it will be of any use, I’m not a religious person, I haven’t been to church since I was a child and was forced along by my mother, but this is the only thing I can do now to help. I pull my fleece jacket over me, grasp the handle to the door and open it, looking back to the crowd of people in the hope that they don’t notice me leaving so soon. There is one last thing that I can do to help Dave… I’m going to pray for him. -----=====|=====----- “Have you seen this man…?” The priest mutters questioningly to himself as he flicks his way through the newspaper settling on this one particular story. He has finished his duties for the day and is ready to leave and head back home. He just has to lock up and wants to have a quick read of the newspaper he bought this morning but hasn’t had the time to read yet. He doesn’t want to hang around too long though really, just have a quick flicker through the morning paper to see what he’s missed and then head home, but this story he’s finding rather intriguing. It isn’t the title that is catching his attention, as much as a professional wrestler in a big wrestling tournament match this week suddenly going missing is an intriguing enough story in itself, it isn’t that which is making him read it. On the page there is a photo of Exceptional Dave Hurst in his ring attire lacing up his red and blue boots before one of the matches in the tournament. On the bench across from him, looking straight at the camera, a colourful object with a beaming grinning beak is sitting. It’s Ducky. Although the priest doesn’t know the duck’s name, he does recognise the toy straight away. It’s the toy that he rescued from an impending crushing by the feet of rushing workers heading to their daily jobs this morning. Turning his head to the first bench of the church, the priest eyes the small plastic yellow duck, before looking back at the newspaper for confirmation, and then to the duck once again for a final check. They look absolutely identical. Of course they do, because it is the same duck in the picture that sits on the bench in front of him. This gets the priest thinking. Why isn’t the duck with this wrestler? Did the wrestler drop the duck on the high street this morning? Is the wrestler in danger? An abundance of questions circles the mind of the priest. He can’t help wondering whether he has intruded in the search. He has the wrestler’s duck in his church, he can’t just ignore this and hope things will blow over, maybe the duck will be a great help for the newspaper in finding him. So he scans through the story rapidly, taking in as much information as possible, but more importantly reaching the end as quickly as possible to see the hotline number. He takes the newspaper with him as he walks back up the aisle and into his office. He places the newspaper on the desk and grabs the handle of the telephone unravelling the cord and holding it up to his ear. Eyes down on the paper, he carefully pulls the dial of the extremely old-fashioned black telephone making sure each digit is correct. He finishes the final number and waits patiently for the tone to ring and for somebody to answer. One ring… two rings… three rings… no answer. Four rings… five rings… six rings… still nothing. The phone continues to ring repetitively without any answer. This is strange. It is still normal working hours, there should be at least somebody answering the phone, especially a hotline. Perhaps there’s a problem with the telephone exchange over there at the moment. The priest was going to leave anyway, so he closes the door to his office behind him and locks it, heads over to the plastic duck sitting on the bench, picks it up and walks out of the large wooden doors of the church building. -----=====|=====----- It’s too late, David. Nobody cares about you. Nobody is here to save you. It’s time to say goodbye to your pathetic little life! No! You bastard! You said you wouldn’t do this! Oh did I? Oh no, so that would be me going against my word now wouldn’t it? God forbid The Devil himself telling a fib! I’m a fibber! A big fibber! Oh I’ll burn in hell for this one for sure! HAHAHAHAHA! The Devil’s laugh echoes through my mind like a foghorn, no matter how much I try, I just cannot ignore it. It both irritates and alarms me, the massive irritation that comes from someone having power over me and not being able to do anything about it. The alarm comes from the exclamation point of the predicament I find myself in. The Devil has complete and utter control of my body, all I can do is watch as he moves me ever closer to my impending doom, as I stand on the flat roof of the building I have had my back to for the last few days, staring down at the horrifying distance to the solid hard concrete pavement below me. The fall will kill me, that is for sure, there is no way I can fall from three stories up and avoid death, especially when the whole situation is controlled by The Devil, miscalculation is no weakness of him. I shuffle my feet closer to the edge of the roof. The Devil makes me lean slightly forward ready for my fall leaving me to contemplate everything before I go. Well… this is it. The end of my life, before I could ever achieve anything in my career as a wrestler, before I could ever get to spend enough of a life with Wendy, before I could ever get to spend some decent time with my Ducky. My eyes start to well up. Not like this, please, not like this. A tear swells up in the corner of my eye and then falls from it, separated by the weight, leaving the liquid drop descending down and down before hitting the pavement below with a whimpering unspectacular splash. But that’s the only thing that fell. I still stand, leaning forward overlooking the eventual plummet I am bound to encounter. The plummet is not coming though. I step backwards from the ledge of the roof, I’m not going to fall it seems. Could The Devil be having second thoughts? As if there would ever be any compassion in the heart of an icon of pure ruthless evil… I’ve changed my mind. You’ve changed your mind. Why would that be then…? There must be a legitimate reason why you aren’t going ahead with this. It will obviously have something to do with my match with Angelo no doubt. Perhaps killing me would spark too much controversy, putting a spotlight on Angelo and possibly exposing him and you, to what you have been doing. … … So that is your plan. You don’t want anyone to know about your links with Angelo, you don’t want to attract any attention to what you’re doing. So you pretend to kill me instead, the only way you can ever get the satisfaction of bringing me to my death is to feign it. There is something you fail to realise though, Mister Hurst. Whether you die or not, you are not going to win this match. I may not kill you, but I will keep you here to be tortured over and over again until they have announced you the loser. What makes you think I won’t tell anyone about this? And what makes you think that anyone will listen? Let’s face it you aren’t the most mentally stable person are you? You used to have split personality disorder and now you parade around as an idiot with a toy duck as a pet. How can you possibly think that anyone will believe a word you say when you tell them that Angelo Deville is possessed by The Devil? … … …Exactly. I walk over to the edge of the roof once more, but this time turn and begin to slide myself backwards from it, clutching hold of the drain pipe with both hands and letting my legs fall from the roof, swinging and then clasping the drain pipe with both feet. I slowly make my way down the drain pipe to the floor and then sit once more against the wall, where I have been for days previously. AAAAAAAAAAAAGHHHHHH!!!!! Once more the ultra-high pitched sound penetrates my head like a spear through paper, tearing away at my senses. All I can do is cringe and endure the sound and endure the intense pain that it creates, but it all becomes too much as my eyes flutter and then slowly shut, my consciousness replaced by nothingness. -----=====|=====----- It’s quite a walk from the church to the offices of the Washington Gazette, a long stroll up a very long road, but the priest doesn’t care. He feels obliged to help the Washington Gazette find their man and the duck he holds in his hand may be the key to it. It is quite a strange thing for the priest, carrying a plastic duck in his hand makes him feel a little bit uncomfortable, almost embarrassed in case anyone sees him with it. He finds it perplexing to imagine anyone else carrying this around as a mascot or pet everywhere he went. The walk is a long one and the street seems eerily quiet for the time of day. It has just turned six o’clock, people should be returning home from work now, the road should be very busy, but instead there is nothing, no-one. The street is completely silent. The only sounds created are the sounds of tapping shoe on concrete as the priest walks over it. As he surveys the surroundings, everything seems bizarrely contrived. Almost as if this street was not a street at all, but something built for something else, like a movie set. The walk seems endless for the priest, the further he walks up the street the longer it seems in the distance to the end. Then all of a sudden the priest doesn’t quite concentrate on what he’s doing and the duck clips the back of his leg causing him to drop it to the ground. Surprised, the priest stops and goes back to pick the duck back up, as his fingertips make contact with the plastic shell a sudden yelling is heard in the distance. It came from a little further up the road, on the left-hand side. Is that person in trouble? The priest isn’t going to hang around to find out. He begins to run up the road as fast as he can to aid the person in need. There, sitting with his back against a wall with both legs sticking out in front of him but both eyes closed, no signs of life, is Exceptional Dave Hurst. The priest walks ever-so-slowly towards Dave, a little apprehensive to go near him and see if he is okay, he fears the worst. With his face cringed in doubt he reaches his arm over to Hurst’s neck and places two fingers onto the side of it. “A pulse…” he mutters to himself, completely shocked that he would be outside in the cold for so long and not be dead. The priest stands there for a few moments more, just watching Dave, waiting for him to move or show some signs of life except for the pulse, but nothing. He has to do something, so he walks over to him and pulls him up to his feet by under his armpits. He places his head under one of the arms and lets Hurst drape over his shoulder in a fireman’s carry ready to carry him away. -----=====|=====----- Please Lord, I don’t ask very much of you. But please, please, bring Dave back to me. I sit in the front bench of the church, staring up at the candles and the crucifix of Jesus Christ above it. My hands are clasped together as tightly as I could squeeze them, streams of tears flooding down the side of my cheeks and then dropping to the floor. Please… just bring Dave back to me. I’m begging you, please… The wooden doors to the church burst open and smack the wall with a loud thud. I turn my head sharply around to see what it was creating the intense noise. Standing there was the priest of the church with a body over his shoulder. He walked over to the first bench and laid him down in it, I knew exactly who it was the moment I saw the priest in the doorway. I quickly get up from my seat and rush around to the aisle, running up it and to the first bench to make sure it really is him. Looking down at the bench, there with a fuzzy beard forming on his face, eyes closed and looking incredibly frail is Dave. “Dave…” I say softly in relief looking down at him, tears still running, but no longer tears of despair. “Are you from the Washington Gazette?” asks the priest, he obviously has read my story. “Yes.” I say sniffling, “I wrote the story.” “He still has a pulse. I brought him back here so I could telephone an ambulance for him.” “Good idea.” The priest starts to walk over to his office, as he does so I lean over Dave looking down at his frail and fragile face. I slowly lean forward and press my lips softly against his forehead giving the slightest of kisses. I bring my head up from his and look down once more at his frail expressionless face, his eyelids closed shut, but as I look at his eyelids, they flicker all of a sudden. Is he coming back…? -----=====|=====----- From the blackness and the unbearable pain reaching a crescendo suddenly everything just stops. The pain is gone. The stifling restrictions of body and mind are gone. I feel free from the shackles of The Devil. I slowly flicker my eyelids open and then shut them once more, there is an intense light in front of me and I’m unable to adjust quick enough. One more try… the light is pretty bright, but I can just about put up with it, my eyes manage to adjust to the light slowly and the objects in front of me begin to become clearer. In front of me is… a pair of beautiful blue eyes, complimented by golden streaks of hair and plush rosy cheeks, it would have to be Wendy. But what is Wendy doing here on this street? What happened to The Devil? “Dave!” Wendy exclaims as she realises my consciousness, wandering back over towards me is somebody else, I can’t quite make him out yet my eyes are still adjusting. “Is he conscious now?” the other person says, it sounds like a man, a British man, no less. My eyes begin to adapt to the surroundings a lot better. I’m definitely not out on the street now that is for sure. The place is illuminated by candlelight. There are tinted windows forming various pictures around me and at the end of the room is a big crucifix of Jesus Christ. I’m in a church… That explains the lack of pain; it explains The Devil being gone. This is the house of God. I sit up from the bench so that I’m sitting normally, Wendy sits next to me moving her hand to mine and clasping onto it tightly. “I’m so glad you’re alright,” Wendy says looking straight into my eyes. “Me too,” I reply solemnly looking back into hers, “At least that is over and I won’t have to go through that again.” Oh no... Maybe I will go through that again. It was so easy for The Devil to penetrate my mind and control my body in the first place, there’s nothing stopping him from doing so again. “What happened?” the priest asks, “were you mugged?” “No…” I reply bluntly in a serious tone, “…I was possessed.” “Possessed…?” the priest says, a little confused at the reply and trying to fathom the situation, “by… a demon?” “By The Devil,” I reply sharply, still trying to show the priest as much as I can that I mean every word that I say, "And I think that the moment I step out of the church that he will do the same again.” “Okay… well…” the young priest says, he is not well-versed in such matters obviously and is a little unsure about how to deal with it all. “Do you think a blessing will work?” “It’s worth a try, anything is worth a try.” “Okay, give me a second here.” The priest stops, closes his eyes and bows his head, collecting his thoughts and what he is about to say. “Lord, bless this man, for he has endured great suffering at the hands of Satan. Protect him so he shall not endure the same suffering again. Watch over him, through bad times and the good, for his hour of need will come when he will encounter Satan once more and will need your guidance and protection… Amen.” And with those last words, in the night sky a beam of glowing golden light shines through the closest of the tinted windows and surrounds me in a rainbow of shimmering colour, enveloping me in the light of The Lord. Wendy and the priest stand with their mouths agape in awe of the wonderful spectacle in front of them. The time has come. The Devil has been driven out of my head. There is no way The Devil can harm me now, for I have God watching over me. In a few days it will be me and Angelo Deville, not me and The Devil, not a handicap match that I’m sure to lose. The Devil has been negated by The Lord, and with that it leaves things down to just two men… …The good guy versus the bad guy. And everybody knows that the good guy always wins. THE END.
