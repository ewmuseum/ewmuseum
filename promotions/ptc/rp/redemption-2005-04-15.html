Date: 2005-04-15 Title: Redemption Prologue The strange orange glow of the morning sun seeped in through the windows, squeezing between the pale blue curtains and the stained glass separating his room from the outside world. Here he was alone, strangled by the rigors of reality, worn and weary of the years that had gone by. With slow but certain precision, his feet touched the cold white tiles, sending a chill up his spine. He sighed, his mind made up. He knew that this was the day. He had promised. Now he was going to keep it. Using his wrinkled hands to push him off the bed, he limped across the room, his glassy eyes stuck in a cold stare into the emptiness. His feet shuffled against the smooth texture of the floor, obeying instructions from the mind without question. Slowly, his lips formed a smile. A poignant, soft touch to the face on which tears had streaked so frequently over the past couple of years. He stared, his crystal blue eyes fixed on the reflection on the wall-sized mirror in front of him. For him, time had already stopped. Stood still in the constraints of reality, waiting for the king to make his next move. Sadly, time no longer had a bearing on him. It used to, but not anymore. In a split second, his memories flooded through his head, drowning him deeper in the fragmented world of dreams. The emotions were killing him slowly, a delicate cut deeper and deeper into his soul, unrelenting and stripped of any conscience. Deep inside, he was already dead. The cold icy fingers that touched his coarse cheek was no longer remembered, a stranger to the world that he was currently drowning in. He was alone, no longer troubled in his mind by the vicious beasts of imaginations. He knew he was close, and he wanted to cherish every moment. The minutes froze, and the seconds trampled by, stripped of direction. It was all a matter of technicalities now. Deep in his heart, he knew that what he was about to do wouldn’t justify the hurt that he had caused all those years back, devoid of any form of conscience that shoved him into committing that hideous crime. He was a murderer, but yet not punished by society. None knew, at least not until now. But somehow, relief overcame him. He no longer needed to bury himself deep in guilt, released by the most unlikely of allies, the dealer of death. He still remembered those words whispered into his right ear three days back, the same tone ringing in his head through his dreams day and night, burying itself deep in the depths of his memories. “Do not fear, for fear will bring you nothing. Don’t worry, after this absolution will be yours.” A stranger to him until then, strangely his soft tone relaxed the old tired muscles strained by restlessness throughout the years. And then he stared through his swirling ocean blue eyes as the stranger walked through the night, dealing him two cards face up with the difficult choice of one. Yes, he had already made his choice. Surprisingly, it wasn’t even that hard when there was no way out. Yes, he reassured himself, there was indeed no way out. With the smile that had not escaped his lips, he reached his wrinkled right hand into the top drawer and gripped his fingers around the handle, all the time his eyes fixed upon the reflection in the mirror. “I will indeed see you soon, my love.” He whispered, bringing the cold metal barrel up to lean against his temple. A tear slid down his eye, his complexion rid of all troubles and guilt. As it traveled slowly down his cheek, streaking it with a line across his face, his eyes closed softly. “Absolution. It is indeed beautiful.” Those were his last words as deep red blood splintered across the mirror, his frail reflection the last thing his warm blue eyes had seen. A split second later, his body slumped to the floor with a loud thud, overshadowed by the thunderous crack heard by the whole district just a couple of split seconds earlier. Red covered the wooden floorboards as the color rippled out in a slow but sure manner, expanding in diameter by the second. It was indeed beautiful. Chapter 1: Strangers The sirens blared as the hordes of strangers standing around slowly dispersed, creating space for the cars to screech through. Officers rushed out covered in familiar light blue, making haste in securing the perimeter to avoid any form of evidence being tainted by the public. “Another suicide attempt huh?” Jessica Knight muttered to a detective jotting down quick notes in a vivid fashion on his notepad, her face grim, deep in thought. She waved her hands at the curious public, pleading them to head off home, but she already knew through experience that it never happened. Deep inside, she just needed that extra bit of space for her nerves that had been frayed lately due to the weekly suicides that had been happening within the city walls. In all her years as a detective in the police force, she had never experienced so many deciding to kill themselves in such a short period of time. What’s amazing was that the fashion in which they did it; it was done in such a simple manner that there wasn’t any form of clues to search for. Doctors, lawyers, and engineers - why they took their lives, she needed to find out. She sighed and coughed, her thoughts embedding themselves deep in her health. She turned her head, and as she did so something gripped her. Standing in the corner was the same guy she noticed standing in every suicide scene that she had been in for last couple of weeks. Suited out in black with his hands in his pockets, he leaned against the nearest building with his shoulders on the bricks. Calm overshadowed his pale face, as he looked uninterested in the furor around him. She stared, squinting her sparkling green eyes as her brown hair circulate around her face as a result of the strong winds. In her mind, she was furiously taking down his description in memory. He smiled. ========== Nathan Dwight was a respected lawyer among his peers, who lived his life in a manner in which crime was his job. Never one to kill an insect, he was a perfect example associated with the word harmless. Always a cheerful character around his friends, he tried to distance himself away from society since the death of his wife about three years ago. Just a couple of months ago, he quit trial work and decided to just work on administrative tasks in the office. He had lost the burning desire for law, and he was strong enough to admit it. He saw over the years where people just kept going, only to bury themselves deeper and deeper in the hole until there was no way out. He wasn’t going to let that happen. Sandra Dwight was his loving wife, and a strong character that was always there when her husband needed her. Holding a job at the library on a part-time basis, she held it as just a form of hobby to keep her from getting bored. They didn’t need the income for the family, just because Nathan was already earning thousands a week. They tried over the years, but they were never granted any children. Sandra Dwight was believed to have committed suicide three years ago. Today, Nathan Dwight took his own life on the same date. But alas, there was but one difference. There lay on the dressing table was a note pinned to a box. “I killed my wife three years ago. Signed, Nathan.” Three years ago, Sandra Dwight was found dead in her home with a bullet through her head. Her files were finally reopened today, but her murderer was already punished by death. Why he killed her on that fateful afternoon, no one would ever find out. ========== “It’s done.” He spoke with acute precision into the mouthpiece of his mobile phone, his ocean blue eyes staring down at the concrete pathway that he was walking on. “I’ll be there soon, and this time you’d better give me a challenge.” And with that, he hung up with a wry smile on his lips. He checked his watch, and hastened the pace of his steps. Frustration showed on his face as realization came over his thoughts; he was going to be late. She was going to kill him this time. ========== Michael Rivers checked the phone, and slowly placed it on the set. With a soft smile playing on his face, he brought his feet up to the table and crossed it. He brought his two palms together and started tapping his fingers, numerous thoughts running through his head at the same time. “This boy has got some talent,” he mused out loud, breaking into a short laugh. “He does indeed,” Vincent agreed, nodding his head. The bulky bodyguard never usually spoke about his differing opinions to his boss, and this time it was no different. “You think he’s going to be up for this next task?” Michael spoke in a deliberate manner, trying to find the answer to his own question. Again, his bodyguard nodded. “We’ll see.” ========== “I want every single clerk to be searching for this guy’s description on the database!” Jessica shouted from across the room to her secretary, who simply nodded her head and busied along her way. With her fingers massaging her temple, she tried to sooth the strain from her forehead. Finally, she might have a lead in these mysterious self-murders that had been occurring of late. She strode into her private room, and heaved herself onto her comfortable black leather sofa, trying to rest her eyes for a couple of moments. She hadn’t been having enough sleep for the past week, staying awake days and nights trying to find a way into the mystery. With a smile, she thought she finally did. “Miss Knight?” Jessica shot upright and blinked her eyes, as someone spoke in front of her. After a few blurry seconds, she managed to portray the figure of her secretary in her eyes. “Yes?” she managed to blurt out. “We found something.” Her secretary replied, while handing her over a file. In her excitement, Jessica ripped the file open and two words stood in front of her eyes like a gold mine to men. Alexander Willow. ========== Born in the streets of Sydney, Australia, Alexander was the only child given to David and Christine Willow. On the night of the 18th of 79, he forced his way into his world after his mother ran into major birth complications. With a pale complexion along with crystal blue eyes and light blonde hair, Christine carried him on her arms before fainting on the hospital bed, the white sheets covered with deep red blood. She passed away on his birthday, at the young age of twenty-five. He never remembered seeing his mother, and all he got to remember her by were pictures given to him by his father. She was beautiful. David Willow meanwhile lived his entire life working as a wrestler. Living his dreams on the mat, he was passionate about wrestling and Alexander spent his early childhood days working out his technical skills on the wrestling mat. David was a champion in his generation, and his skills transferred well to Alexander, who learnt the ropes of wrestling extremely well considering his young age. But that dream was never to be, as Alexander soon lost interest in wrestling much to David’s frustration. And frustration soon turned into anger as David tried to force the wrestling occupation upon Alexander. Being the strong character that he was, Alexander flatly refused and decidedly left home to seek his trade and take his life in a different direction. That was the last time he was to see his father in good health, for the next time he was not to be as lucky. Three years passed, and Alexander passed through life working in different occupations, trying to make do with what he had in order to create a better life for himself. He soon failed. But one thing stood out in him that was apparent in all his employers – his tremendous sense of creativity. A couple of months later, he got a call from the local hospital saying that his father was in critical condition. Never one to hold a grudge, he rushed there just in time for the final minutes of his father’s life in this world. In that short period of time, he asked for forgiveness with tears flowing down his cheeks. Yes, he was forgiven. But there was a catch. His father’s last wish was for Alexander to follow in his footsteps and become a wrestler. That was David Willow’s last wish before he parted with the world. And as hard as his mind wanted to reject it, his heart reluctantly followed. ========== Chelsea Matthews closed the file of her client after she finished the profile, her eyes frowning as she looked at the clock hanging on the pink-painted wall. Just as she was gazing back to the cover of the file, a knock was heard on the wooden door followed by the entrance of a man. Dressed in black jeans and a leather jacket, he removed his shades and placed it in his pocket. With a slight smile on his face, he approached Chelsea’s desk. “Am I late?” He asked in a calm voice. “Mr. Willow, please take a seat.” Chelsea ignored his question, and motioned for him to take a seat in front of her. With a firm line on her lips, she opened his file again. “How may I address you?” Chelsea enquired, as a form of formalities. “Alexander will be fine.” Chelsea nodded her head as a form of agreement. Alexander couldn’t help but notice that his doctor was a beautiful lady who was around his age, and he smiled to himself. She picked up his file and flipped it open. “I’ve finished going through your profile, and I must say that you had quite an interesting life.” For the first time since he walked through that door, she actually smiled. “Not quite as interesting as my life now,” he replied, smiling widely back at her. Again, Chelsea ignored his calm demeanor. “I have to ask you something. Are you still wrestling? Because your profile says you are.” She went on, her eyes turning into small slits in concern. “Yes, I am actually.” He replied, his smile disappearing for a quick second. “And no, that isn’t going to change.” “You know that wrestling on a regular basis is affecting your health. Your body already has enough work trying to fight itself without having you inflicting more pain upon it.” Chelsea argued her case, raising her voice a slight notch while waving her right hand as if to stress her point. Not even ten minutes had passed, and Alexander had already gotten to his feet. He knew that this discussion wasn’t going to head anywhere. He had enough experience going to doctors all over the city, with each making the same argument to him. “I can’t stop wrestling, you know that. That was my father’s last wish. And I will fulfill that desire of his. It’s been nice meeting you, Ms. Matthews. I believe I made a mistake in coming here.” Alexander spoke curtly, and ended it with a sarcastic smile. He turned and made his way to the door. “You don’t have much time, Alexander. Your condition is getting worse. The disease is getting the better of your body.” Chelsea pleaded from behind him, trying to make him change his mind. “I feel fine.” He refused her words of advice like thorn to his ears, and beckoned her to keep silent. “You will die soon.” And with those words from Chelsea, Alexander smiled softly, before slamming the wooden door behind him with a loud thud. ========== “How’s your wrestling coming along?” Michael asked politely, with a slight smirk on his face. “Fine thanks,” Alexander followed up with a quick mutter. He already had enough talk about wrestling today, what with his frustrating encounter with his doctor earlier. “I hear that you’re currently in a cruiserweight tournament sponsored by Primetime Central. How’s that coming along?” Michael continued with a short laugh. He never understood why Alexander was wrestling, when he was making money by the thousands each day. “Let’s get back to business, shall we?” Alexander answered with another question. He offered himself his own seat in front of Michael and heaved himself down. “I like your style,” Michael smiled, pulling open his top drawer and taking out the top file in the pile. He slammed the file against the table. “There’s your next assignment.” With a dry smile, Alexander picked it up. “This better be good. At least make it a slight more challenging than the last one.” “Trust me, it will be.” Michael spoke, waving his finger. He leaned back in his chair, and waited for Alexander to open his file. Alexander looked at Michael from the corner of his eye in suspicion, before finally flipping the cover over. He turned pale within an instant. He held his breath, thinking that his next breath might just kill him. To him, the name on the paper was like looking at death in his eyes. He looked up at Michael Rivers, his crystal blue eyes glazed. He looked down again, trying to sink everything in. Sweat was slowly trickling down his face, sliding along its smooth texture. And without him realizing it, he spoke the two words out loud for all to hear. “Christine Willow.” ==========
