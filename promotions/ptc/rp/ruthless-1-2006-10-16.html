Date: 2006-10-16 3 months ago Have you ever thought of death as your only way out? The end of existence as you know it, to be the only reprieve from an existence that seems so futile? I’m at this point in my life, this is where I exist. I, myself, the superhuman is to the point of cocking the hammer of a .45 and pointing the barrel under my chin. Do you understand the deranged pain that is ever present here? Every time the tip of the needle edges closer and closer to my body, I can feel the slight reprieve of death edging ever closer. One time, maybe the next time I press this needle into my flesh I’ll die, just maybe. How pathetic is it for a superhuman like myself to be pushed to this edge? Once the drug baron, now the drug abuser, I constantly sit silent as I watch the world unfold around me. Before I would reach up and tear it open, now I just let it slowly fall into place. This is my existence, complete and total nothingness. Every breath brings me one step closer to my death. Some say I’m at a mid-life crisis stage in my life. I’m only 30 years old, am I at my mid life? Or should I of had this crisis fifteen years ago? So if I do put the gun to my head and pull the trigger, do I manage to avoid my inevitable mid-life? Or am I forced to endure my body failing for an eternity in a way I’m completely unsure how. The superhuman has body failure… Of course I figured this would happen eventually. My bones began to shrink, my muscles began to ache. I’m only 30 years old, this shouldn’t be happening to me. No fucking way should my body feel like this. Twelve years of heavy drug abuse, ten years of violent fighting, five years of fighting at the highest level, and two years of solid wrestling. My body should not be dieing already, I should be out training, waiting to get another chance to take my stab at the limelight. Yet here I am in this room with this needle, waiting silently to give my skin a slight prick and float away… Floating silently and aimlessly into the complete nothingness of my mind, pondering existence and why I’m here. This is where I like to be, or do I really like to be here? Is my high that lets me get away from this fucking world that doesn’t care about me anymore, is it worth anything? Is the reason I’m here more or less because nobody out there wants anything to do with me? Is that why I’m in this state of confusion? Lost in this state of disbelief? This world has already forgotten about me, yet here I am. I still live, and I already feel like I’m dead. This high means nothing once you break it all down, I just sit here in this chair and stare at the ceiling for hours, and finally it all soaks in and I fade away into a land I’m unsure of myself in. A land where I see the old demons that used to haunt me in reality, like Kodiak Vic Creed, Villam Ender, Azrael Ravenall, all of these people who haunt me slightly… The real nightmares come from the people I’ve killed. Sometimes when I’m high I can see them talk to them again, I can see myself and the words I say come out of my mouth. I realize how terrified they actually were, I smell the same smell of fear and get excited. That excitement, I think that’s why I let myself live. Knowing your in complete control of a mans existence besides your own, I think that’s why I’m here. I want to play God a few more times before I leave. How do you get to be a God though? I do not want my acts to be thrown to the wayside as being done by a junky or a traitor… How do you get remembered forever? -- Chairs are silent but sometimes you feel more comforting in between their arms than in the arms of a lover. They never leave you; their silence is sometimes the best answer to any question. Today this is where the monster sleeps, well if you’d consider the monster some washed up fighter stoned out of his melon wondering what country he’s in or debating the realities of the world, or if the monster is a former drug lord whose killed more men than you can count on your fingers and toes and always got off on technicalities, then once again the monster is in front of you. Token sighed as he rolled over; his high was ending as he slowly came down to reality. He slowly rose to his feet, this was never pleasant. The withdrawal from a heroin induced night always got a bit violent. As this happened in the past, his apartment slowly went from a nice decent looking place to live to a room of holes in the walls, needles strewn across the floor. The kitchen was a mass of fast food bags, molding decaying food, and random alcohol containers. This was the life Token Weed had gone to after his world came crashing down on him with the fWo hiatus imposed by the Disney Corporation. Of course he still did some odd jobs for spare cash, but those needles did need to be filled with a substance for them to give their desired effect. Token slowly got to his feet, dodging the random objects that littered the floor. He walked first to the bathroom which was a disgusting mass of clothing and other objects. He got ready for his day with a quick shower, and then went about getting dressed and other nonsense. *knock* The knock shocked Token; nobody had done that for the past few days. When a knock at the door happened, it was either his drug dealer or his land lord coming to see if he was alive. He walked to the door and opened it slowly, he hadn’t paid his dealer in a week or so, he was working on credit. As he opened the door he slid his .45 magnum out the slot first, and then poked his head through. Of course, no one was there. The silence that enveloped that moment as he noticed the piece of paper taped to his door, it sent chills up his spine. Or was that just the Heroin withdrawal already kicking in? "Legend, I’d like to inform you of a chance to get out of your drug induced haze and get off your lazy ass… GTT 6 [detail withheld] From, The Kid" Token glanced up and down the hallway; he heard a door slam and sighed. "Fucking Frog," he croaked before walking back into his apartment. -- 2 Months Ago The Alexandria Care Center in Los Angeles was an odd place. Nobody that was inside really knew they were inside. Some did not know their families placed them there, some did not even know who they were. The Alzheimer’s Facility glistened against the city backdrop; it was full of high end clients. Token Weed stood silently at the door, his mother was trapped inside of these walls, while she was also trapped inside of her own mind. He had not visited his mother in a year, the last time was to inform her that her other son Draven had died. When he told her that, she simply responded "Draven honey, you’re not dead." Of course he hadn’t been back since that day, who would want their mother to think that they were their dead, little brother. Token sighed and walked into the building. The stark white walls are the first thing that hit you when you walk into the building. The walls that look like a dream sequence where you meet your God. Those walls exist in this building. The wooden desk sat in a corner of the room, a few small plants around it to make it look inviting. "I’m here to see Maria Williams," Token’s gravely voice echoed in this room. The small woman looked up from her work and instantly put on a fake smile. Her fake warmth was meant to sooth and comfort, Token quickly shrugged it off. No amount of soothing and comforting would help him. "Servant of Azrael cometh." – The Servant of Azrael 1:1 She stared into the steel blue eyes, the unforgiving depths. Many men have done this and many men have died doing it, and yet in his eyes she felt comfort. The soothing always seems to come before the end. "I stared at her and realized her end was near, as I approached I felt the magnitude of her sins and I wept." – The Servant of Azrael 1:2 "She’s down the hall way, room 111," she said trying to over ride the awkwardness in the room. Token nodded, smiled, and began to walk. The hall way continued on as Token walked, it seemed like it all took so long and finally he stood outside of door 111. *Knock* The knock startled Maria Williams; the only people that ever came to her door were either her doctors or her nurses. She owed a few of the nurses money for them buying her cigarettes. Of course this was irrelevant, she just knew that she owed them something and was not sure exactly what that something was. Sometimes she would give them money for the milk they brought for her mother. They would hand her a pack of cigarettes. Sometimes they would come in and talk to her about her disease; all she really wanted though were to see her children again. Sean and Draven were her life before she came to the white place. All she wanted were her little boys back. "Come in" Token fidgeted with himself; for once he was not confidant with his actions. Anxiety was something he had not experienced in a long time. In turn anxiety quickly turned to fear, which then turned to anger, which then subsided into empathy. Empathy for the woman on the other side of the wall, slowly he opened the door. "Hello Mother," Token’s grungy voice stated as he walked in the door. "The black void that was her mind echoed through the halls but truthfully, only the heart of a mother can show true emotion." – The Servant Of Azrael 1:8 She stared silently, there through the door came walking her eight year old son Sean, she walked up and gave him a big hug and a huge kiss. Led him to the table and quickly went for the cupboard. "The Usual, Oreo’s and Milk?" she stated as the boy looked at her confusedly. Token watched his mother go through the motions as if he was a little boy, he gave a brief smile. "Sure," he stated as his mother quickly got a few cookies and a glass of milk out for him. "Mom, I have to tell you something before I go and do it." "What’s that honey?" That soothing voice that he remembered all too well, childhood memories always led to something bad. "I’m going to fight again." The look of shock his mother gave him was almost legendary, she had heard of little boys fighting, but never a planned rumble. "Sean, if you get in another fight at school they said they’d throw you out," she stated bluntly staring at her son. "I informed her of my plans, the look of shock and distress awed me for a brief moment, before I remembered that it was my job." - The Servant of Azrael 1:10 "I know mother; but I have to," Token stated as he stared into the same steel blue eyes he had. Her frame had grown frailer, he knew she was dying. "Sean! You can’t! We’ll have to move again. Draven just started kindergarten, do you want that?" The name shook Token to his bones. He missed his brother for the first time in six months. "I’m going to my room," Token stated as he walked up and gave his mother a kiss on the cheek. "Dinner is in a few hours," his mother stated. "I know, I love you Mom," Token said as he walked out of the room. "I love you too Sean." "The Love of a Mother is the only thing that can rival the love of our Father." – The Servant of Azrael 1:15 --- Today The Servant of Azrael 2:1 - 2:20 1Have you ever sat silent when you had a feeling of déjà vu? 2To the point you knew the very next thing that was going to happen in a conversation? 3That is not you being insane, it is a gift. 4Some days our father looks down upon us and feels the need for us to know what will happen next. 5Just to show you his existence. 6As if he needs to? 7The only reason were all still here on this earth is for maximizing our potential before he takes us, or the other one takes us. 8It’s a constant war in the heavens, and here we are at the recruiting grounds. 9I’ve fought on the wrong side of the battle for a long time, when the other thought he had his army at the point of eclipsing fathers. 10I went to war for demons and devils my entire life until my life changed, until moment’s echoed into eternity and my soul felt calmness like it never had before. 11 Understand that life changing moments only happen once in awhile. 12 Sometimes you don’t even notice their existence and other times the impact and shock waves they create in your own life force you to notice but you still don’t listen. 13 I’m fortunate, when my life changing moment came along I saw it and knew exactly what it was. 14 Our father blessed me with the ability to perceive and understand this moment. 15 The foresight he gave me led me down the path I knew I had to take up. 16 All my actions were a second behind, my mind was telling me what to do with vivid pictures. 17 Every step I took, I knew the exact sound it would make as it hit the concrete. 18 When I reached out and dismissed her from this plane of existence to go be with father, I knew the sound her neck would make. 19 I knew every slight popping sound. 20 I knew. --- Two Months Ago The Monster drove silently down the highway. His head spun with possibilities, everything seemed to be changing around him. For years his only form of comfort had been his mother, and now she was gone. She was no longer able to answer his questions and give him honest responses, she was a shell that did not even know it was not 1986 anymore. Her entrapment pained him, it angered him, and yet he could do nothing to help. He sighed. Then there was the thing Frog talked him into. GTT6, the shear fact it existed stunned Token. The fact he had an invitation to compete stunned him even more. The fact Frog talked him into going pissed him off even more. He hated that little brat. Token leaned his head against the window as he drove, occasionally paying the cars attention as they went blowing by him. He was driving the speed limit for once in his life, and of course in L.A. that was driving thirty miles per hour too slow. Then a white Cavalier blew by him, the speed shocked Token for a second. He looked ahead on the road immediately, he could see two trucks trying to pass each other, blocking the roadway off. He looked on the shoulder at a stopped van with people trying to change a tire. He looked back at the white Cavalier and sighed, he knew what was going to happen. He had seen this before. The Cavalier careened out of the far left lane into the right one, then onto the shoulder. He knew who was in the car, and knew she was in a hurry. It all seemed to happen in slow motion, the Cavalier impacted the van, sending it rocking forward. Her frail small body exploded from the wind shield. The man changing the tire was sent flying underneath the oncoming trucks. The van rolled off the embankment, as the Cavalier flipped over it’s owners body and also landed over the embankment. Token tossed his hazard lights on and quickly slowed down and pulled onto the apron. He looked over the embankment at the now burning vehicles, he looked over into traffic and saw the body being ran over repeatedly. Ruthless, this world was ruthless. He shut his eyes for a moment and slowly he watched himself reach for the door handle. Slowly his own hand did the same, he stepped outside of his car and walked to the woman, listening to the glass crunch. He did the same. As he approached the woman, he looked down at his hands, they were covered with latex gloves. He knew he had these in the car but didn’t remember putting them on. He stood over the woman, and bent down to check her pulse. It was feint, but she was still alive. He sighed as he put one hand on her jaw, and the other behind her head. He could feel fluid from her brain leaking out. He knew she’d live if he didn’t do this, but somewhere, it all seemed right. The motion was quick, And Ruthless.
