Burden of Proof

Date: 2004-10-08 .:: Her eyes sparked with fury. This was unacceptable, it was highly unacceptable, and she could hardly believe that someone was trying to pass it off on her. ::. “No! You tell him he pays the full price or he dies!” .:: Riven winced, holding the phone away from his ear. ::. “I will do what I can, but he is as adamant as he was the first night he approached me. I don’t think he’ll easily be persuaded to change his mind.” .:: Riven paused, lowering the tone of his voice slightly. ::. “He says that if you take his second contract, he will pay you the full payment for this one, and add payment for the second as well as a bonus… If he is satisfied.” “If he is satisfied?! Was offing that worthless Necromancer not enough? I didn’t get into this to play games. I didn’t ask for a lifelong contract, and I didn’t sell my soul to him because I agreed to take out one worthless Giovanni prick! What the hell is going on here, Riven?” “I don’t know, Shadow. But I don’t think refusing is an option at this point.” .:: When she responded, her voice came over the line icy cold. ::. “Oh, I’m not refusing. I’m accepting, because this time, if I don’t get my payment, he’ll pay with his head.” .:: The line went dead after that. Shadow had hung up, so Riven assumed. And she certainly was in an ill temper. He folded his phone back up and slipped it into the pocket of his coat, sighing. With a furtive look around, he slipped back into Club Tenebrae, back through the thronging crowds of people, juicebags and Kindred alike. He was hungry, wanted to consume one of these fleshy mortals, but that would have to wait. He slipped as elegantly as he could into the shady back booth that Julian Falcon was still occupying, his arms crossed over his chest like a hulking Aztec god. Riven tried not to glare at him, but that was hardly successful. ::. “She’ll do it.” .:: Falcon laughed. He was pleased. ::. “Good.” .:: Falcon paused for a moment, then went on. ::. “I don’t think you like me much behind that mask, do you?” .:: Riven snarled behind his mask. People telling him how he thought made him angry. But he kept his temper in check and answered in a cool, disinterested tone. ::. “You make the requests, I make the phone calls. It’s not my job to like you.” .:: Falcon laughed again, a big, deep throaty laugh that was thick with amusement. ::. “I see why you and the little Assassin get along so well. Neither of you have a bone of humor in your bodies. You’d break if you smiled.” .:: Riven simply glanced at him, as Falcon amused himself. He’d talk for hours, so Riven guessed from past experience. So long as Julian Falcon was pleased, he’d never shut his trap, and most of the time it was extolling his own virtues to boot. But that didn’t matter right now. Right now, he was thinking about Shadow, and how she’d been so angry. ::. At least she hasn’t forgotten everything she was taught. .:: The night waned on, Julian Falcon’s voice the only dreary accompaniment to the symphony of discord and disenchantment welling in his head. If he’d been the Assassin, he would have offed Falcon right here, instead of mincing words. But he wasn’t, and she was. Perhaps that was her good fortune, he reflected. Or perhaps she loathed her existence as much as he loathed his, every time he looked in the mirror and saw his shrunken, mottled, distorted face leering back at him. It was enough to make a man tear the skin off of his face. Well, what skin he had left, anyway. ::. .:: ::: ::. .:: She didn’t know the name of the little juicebag in her arms, but she didn’t guess it mattered too much. He was limp, almost—limp, pale, little Kine. She guessed that was the benefit of being pretty, unearthly so. All she had to do was flash a little leg here, a little smile there, and they were putty, just putty, limp putty, limp little… ::. .:: With a growl, she let him drop to the ground. Her hunger was slaked, and she supposed that was all that mattered. The little Kine fell to the ground, mewing pathetically, without even the spine or strength to push himself to his feet and look one last time upon the strange woman that he had accosted in the middle of the street. Served him right. ::. .:: Caring wasn’t something Shadow was good at. For the life of her, she didn’t understand why she was supposed to be so damned good at it. Here she was, having survived year after year after year, all piling gracelessly atop one another like stacking bodies in a mass grave. They added up to the same thing bodies would too—dust. After everything she had been through in her long, long life, she didn’t understand why in the hell she was supposed to still care. Caring had gotten her stabbed in the back more times than she could count. She was an Assassin now. Caring would get her killed. ::. .:: She hadn’t believed Amir when he had first told her that—told her that she wouldn’t have any need for the cares of her mortal life. Perhaps she’d just been denying the inevitable, or perhaps she’d just been stupid; in either case, she didn’t see how she could possibly have believed that even as long as her life was going to be, she would still keep caring about the same things that mattered to her when she had lived in Paris, a stupid and overconfident little girl in a young woman’s body. Even then, even in Paris, she’d been different. ::. .:: Childbirth had left no marks on her body, not like it did other women. And to think, back then, it had been a sight more horrifying as well. She’d scarcely been a baby herself when she gave birth to Angela, but she’d thought it would make things better between herself and Eryc. He still called himself Eryc then, after all. But when she’d given birth, she’d felt no joy—all she had felt was an oppressive weight, tying her down to the earth by her body. She was nothing now, she was not Apollonie, she was just a mother. That night she had cried. Eryc had thought it was because she was so exhausted and moved by the whole ordeal, but it had really been because she had realized that a child wouldn’t fix things, a child would only push Eryc further away, and a child would only take more of his love from her. It was already starting to wane, she could see that in his eyes every time he looked at her. And it tore her apart. ::. .:: Shadow stepped out of the alleyway, wiping the excess vitae off her lip. She stepped onto the street, black cloak swirling about her. Nobody would notice her in this part of town, and if they did notice her, they’d have about two seconds to think about it before they were dead. She was just in that kind of mood tonight, and it would have been dangerous to trifle with her—most dangerous indeed. ::. .:: It wasn’t just this Falcon thing that had her in a foul mood. Granted, it would have put any Assassin in a foul mood, having someone go back on a bargain like that. ::. I should have just killed him the instant I saw him. .:: Shadow had a way of smelling untrustworthiness on people. Sometimes that sense failed her, but only when she allowed her feelings to get in the way. She’d sensed it from Julian Falcon the moment she met him, an odor rising foul and rank from his soul. An Assassin had to have these sorts of senses purely in order to survive. She should have listened, and she knew that now, but it wasn’t quite that easy, either. ::. .:: WMW was perhaps not what she had been looking for. And she knew that. It gave her a sense of vindication to step through a set of ring-ropes and win a title, and defend that title, but… it wasn’t quite enough. She may have been a wrestler by training, training both at Alamut and later in her life when she decided on a change of vocation, but she was an Assassin by heart and by blood. WMW was not the life that she wanted to live. ::. .:: And so, when Julian Falcon had proposed an assignment to her, she had more than gladly accepted. It was in her nature to do this, and she knew it. For too long, she’d been denying herself, thinking that it was acceptable to just stay in WMW and validate herself by winning matches, but it just wasn’t the same—it wasn’t what she’d been built for. ::. .:: Since Amir had died, so long ago, she’d been telling herself that she didn’t need the Assassin part of her to be whole. It was a well-constructed lie—well constructed enough for her to start believing it as time passed, at least. But she’d known it was a lie when she made it up. She’d made it up out of some pathetic human resistance to what she was. And perhaps that was why she’d never been as happy as she wanted to be—so long as she was refusing to accept herself for who and what she was, she couldn’t possibly be happy. Yet, no one seemed to understand that. ::. .:: She’d left her own promotion with her sister and brother in charge. How they’d managed to survive all this time was beyond her as well—Phelan didn’t talk about it, and Diablo swore that he didn’t remember. In any case, she didn’t suppose that it mattered. She’d left her own promotion, and they’d said they understood, but she didn’t know if they truly did. Even though Diablo was her twin, the Embrace had separated them in an immeasurable and sickening way that was disheartening. For years after they’d been married, Eryc hadn’t wanted her to see him, or any of the rest of her family for that matter. She’d been a liberated woman of the French Republic, but Eryc had seen things a little differently—and Eryc certainly had the strength to make things work his way if she showed any signs of resistance. And then by the time she’d gone looking for her family, they’d been gone, and no one on the entire Rue de Provence knew where. By the time she’d found them again, they’d been Embraced. Confusion had torn through her at seeing them again—after all, one usually didn’t expect to find one’s relatives weathering the centuries as well. But it had been almost a warm reception—at least as warm a reception as Kindred could muster at any given time. They’d managed to survive quite well—but Shadow wasn’t sure they understood, or that they would ever understand. They might have understood when she was young Apollonie, living on Rue de Chanverrerrie with her new husband, but they did not understand her as Liana, student of her master Amir, and they did not understand her as Shadow, a shell of the person and Assassin that she once was. It was an unstoppable yawning maw between them. ::. .:: Eryc slash Corvus had been even worse. It seemed that it didn’t matter what he called himelf; inside, he hadn’t changed a bit since they’d been married. Still the same selfish, domineering jackass that he always had been. He’d wanted Shadow to conform to everything he desired, and she hadn’t been able to give him that, she’d never been able to give him that. Even when the two of them had been living in France as husband and wife, she hadn’t wanted to give him that, but she had because it was the “right” thing to do. It was her duty as his wife. This last time had been the worst—this last time, he had placed the same old expectations on her, just like she knew he would. But she’d practically begged him for it. She’d gone to WMW specifically to find him, specifically to remind him of who he was and what he was supposed to be doing. And she’d done her job well, if she did say so herself. But she’d gotten shat all over, so to speak. She didn’t know what else she was supposed to expect, all things considered. It was just as much bullshit as him leaving her in Paris without any warning had been, but she supposed she couldn’t have expected anything slimier out of him. She hated what he had become, mostly because he hadn’t changed at all. ::. .:: Worst of all, he’d expected her to keep the damned Masquerade. He’d expected her to sit and pretend that she wasn’t what she was. He’d let a Kine stay in the house, under the same roof, pretending that he was some benevolent eccentric. He wasn’t that pure. Shadow knew that he thought about draining the little brat’s blood—she’d done it often enough, but maybe that was just because Slayer irritated her, and his little girlfriend did too. ::. .:: She should have drained them when she had the chance. She knew that now. But for some reason, Corvus always seemed to induce these happy delusions in her, illusions that things were acceptable, that things were going to be just fine. Things were never just fine when he was concerned. Nor did Shadow want them to be. Her heart was not a heart that thrived on empty peacefulness. She was an Assassin—her heart thrived on the multitudinous heartbeat of her trade. Paris, Nice, London, Milan, New York, Cleveland… it didn’t matter where she was, so long as she still was still doing her job. ::. .:: And what was that job? Since Amir had died, she hadn’t been too interested in it. Maybe she’d lost the heart. One of the last important jobs she had taken after he died was offing Lucien Forlani in London, and after that, it had been nothing. She could have been one of the best Assassins the clan had, and she knew it. But she had given up on it. ::. .:: The more and more Shadow thought about it, the more it seemed that she was always giving something up for someone else, and not herself. She wouldn’t have minded so much if she’d actually been making sacrifices for herself, but she’d never done that. Never. Maybe it was some holdover of being married to a domineering asshole. She’d never really thought too much about how to explain it. In the end, it didn’t really matter what the reason was, only that she was always neglecting her own needs in favor of making someone else happy. The only problem was, no matter what she did to make things “okay,” the other person was never happy. ::. .:: Riven seemed to be the exception to that rule. He didn’t place any expectations on her, and perhaps that was one of the reasons that she liked living with him as much as she did. True, he was a bit strange, and oftentimes, he reminded her too much of Amir for the thought to be comforting, but he was much more acceptable company than Corvus. ::. .:: He didn’t expect her to be like him. Maybe that was the part that relieved her the most. Corvus had expected her to think like him, act like him, accept his lifestyle as the only way to make good on the eternity of time that she had to waste before she simply slipped into a disinterested torpor. But living with a Kine? Pretending to be an eccentric man who slept during the day? Pretending to drink “wyne” instead of vitae? It was too much, even for her, even for a woman who had to be flexible in order to be the genius Assassin that she was. ::. .:: And yet, she had to wonder if she still had the talent at all. It had been a detailed hit, “disappearing” Lucien Forlani like she had. She shook her head. That had been back in the days when an Assassin’s word meant something, when she could expect to be paid in full for every job that she undertook. Secretly, she suspected that had been because every clan was terrified of the Assassins back then. But the blood curse had changed all that. ::. .:: It struck her as odd—she had never submitted to the blood curse, so she supposed it really didn’t matter, but nevertheless… most Rafiq had, and thus it concerned her. But it struck her as odd that even though the blood curse had been revoked, there were still Kindred out there who treated the Assassins as though they were nothing more than lapdogs, to be led around at will, and dumped on the side of the road in a box when you were done with them. She sniffed. Lapdogs indeed! ::. .:: That brought her back to Julian Falcon, and whatever she was supposed to be considering right now. She wasn’t entirely sure what this next job was going to entail. It still made her blood boil that she’d been roped into another job. She growled under her breath. ::. Amir didn’t teach you to stand for this, Shadow, so why are you doing it? .:: Despite the fact that she’d been trying as hard as she could to escape her past lately, thoughts such as that were often on her mind, now that she was back in “the trade,” so to speak. ::. .:: So many things to think about. She had to think about researching this kill. She’d been prepared for Alessandro Pazzi because Alessandro Pazzi had been nothing. He’d been a paranoid sack of shit, sitting around in his camera-secured haven just waiting for someone to kill him for getting in too deep with dirty business dealings. ::. .:: But she was not a killer of businessmen. She was not a killer. She was an artist, and it was her art form. And in order for her work to be artful, she needed time to plan. ::. .:: She stalked down the street, her eyes stormy. Fury burned within them, the same fury that always burned there. It was just that sometimes, people seemed to look away from it, or write it off as some evidence of her being a spoiled, emotional brat. This was not emotion. This was inspiration. ::. .:: Julian Falcon had one more chance. If he screwed this up, he’d pay for playing games with a Rafiq, with one of the most brutal Assassins operating in North America. He was blind to her artistry, though, and she could tell that just from looking at him. He didn’t understand that there was patience involved. He didn’t understand that it wasn’t just walking into someone’s haven and slitting their throat, or blowing up the building. It was much more than that. But some people didn’t see the transcendence in it. Some people didn’t understand the power of a true artist’s presence. ::. .:: Oh, she’d listen to Julian Falcon. She’d give him one last chance. The blood was flowing somewhat insidiously through her veins now, pushing her to take this project on, She’d take it on, and she’d make Falcon see—she’d make him see why it was ridiculous for him to think he was dealing with some small-time knife addict. She was the highest echelon. She did her job the best. It was her name that was whispered in Camarilla circles, the fearful pangs rising in stomachs that were hardly more than dust. It was her name that was on the Ventrue hit-list, still, after all these years, just because of one well-placed and ill-expected hit. She still had it in her. She still had the skill to make Ceaucescu look like Mother Theresa. ::. .:: A smirk faded onto her expression almost lazily. She was confident now, confident that Julian Falcon would eat that cocky confidence and fall at her feet once she’d proved to him the magnanimity that he was dealing with. She sauntered down the street, headed back toward the haven. She’d meet Julian Falcon to discuss details tomorrow night, like she’d planned to. And he wouldn’t have the upper hand in this dealing, oh no. Julian Falcon would realize that he was dealing with a superior power, and he would show the respect that he should have shown the first time he asked Riven for her services. ::. .:: One last chance. That was all the bastard got. ::. .:: ::: ::. .:: There were times when the city sunk into stasis. Nothing moved, save the breeze, and the breeze was something transient. Cities were all alike anyway, so Shadow had concluded. ::. .:: Stench was all around her. Perhaps that was why most Kindred feared Assamites, she reflected. No fear. ::. .:: A clump of ashes threatened to fall off onto her fingers, so she flicked the cigarette absently, sending a cascade of the dying embers to the busted and cracked concrete below. Assassins were never ones to sit around and quibble with people over such things, but there was an unwritten code when dealing with the Children of Haqim. You told them what you wanted, they handled the specifics… and under no circumstances did you think of breaking your bargain with them, or showing up late. She supposed times had changed. After he told her what he wanted, she’d have specifics to deal with—the where’s, when’s, why’s, and how’s of this operation. The things that Julian Falcon was too short-sighted to consider. It was her job to fill in the blanks. Of course, she guessed he was pretty dim anyway, considering he couldn’t just take care of this business himself. Or perhaps he was a closet coward, someone who didn’t want to dirty his hands with the blood of others. No, after that, it’s “you got lucky.” Or something like that. I’m tired of living in someone else’s little box. I don’t fit there, and I don’t know why people are consistently trying to make me into something to fit THEIR needs. I take them as they are—I see what they show me, and I prepare accordingly. I suppose the problem is, most of the time, I’m just preparing to take on some foul-mouthed little child who doesn’t know any better than to regurgitate patriarchal notions of women as the standard defense to anything that I say. I don’t care about that. I’m not some crusading feminist. What I care about is being lumped into the same genre as every woman who’s tried to rise up and change her stars and then failed. I’m not the mascot of womanhood. I don’t want to be. Women are just as pathetic as men want to make them. What I am is a force on my own, something set apart from considerations of gender roles. Something set apart from considerations of what a little Kine woman is “supposed” to act like. I’m starting to wonder why no one notices that I’m any different. It’s always “oh, you have to talk down to the women so they’ll understand you.” Nobody’s talking down to me anymore. Whatever it takes, I will prove myself. I’m tired of bowing and scraping to what other people need, and I’m tired of listening to people and getting pissed off when they try to sell me short. No, I’m coming at this all wrong. There’s nothing more for me to prove. I’ve proven myself over and over again, and I’m not going to become a slave just because there are people everywhere who don’t believe in anyone but themselves, and will do everything to sell their opponents short in order to keep that little dream alive. What dream, I don’t know. Maybe it’s looking like a real man. Maybe it’s impressing someone in the back. Maybe it’s winning a title, or moving on to the next round in the tournament. I am tired of being a scapegoat. I am tired of being a dumping ground for human insecurity. If Grest has to talk respect to make himself feel better, let him. If Oni has to be flip to make himself feel better, let him. It’s not going to matter. I’ve set my mind. I am not going to lose this, I CANNOT lose this. I am a part of something stronger than they will ever conceive. .:: A pair of thudding footsteps interrupted her reverie. This man was clumsy—she could tell it was a man simply from the weight of the tread. Something about the arrhythmic sound of the gait gave her the impression of an inebriated drunk, stumbling down the alleyway, perhaps to harass her, or perhaps to sleep… maybe even to die of alcohol poisoning. ::. .:: The putrid stench of drunken Kine flowed into her nostrils. Had there been anything for her stomach to reject, it would have done so. The strong odor encompassed her. She felt as though she would pass out if the scent did not pass soon, but she simply stayed put, leaning casually up against the wall as though she had not noticed the interloper. ::. .:: A sour smell sank into her nose as the drunk approached her. ::. “You should pay more attention, lest you be caught unaware.” .:: The voice was Julian Falcon’s, but it was hushed and urgent. She looked over this interloper, clad mostly in brown, although whether the garments were brown from dye or filth was hard to discern. She checked her scent a little closer—alcohol, yes, and the sweating stench of Kine, but deeper down, there was something cloying, and sweet. A scent only possessed by those who were Undead. It was him. She narrowed her eyes, scrutinizing him, and seeing the lump underneath his big brown cap where his nearly waist-length black hair was tucked. She made a distasteful face. ::. “Are the theatrics necessary?” .:: Falcon shrugged casually, pushing on the cap with rag-clad fingers. ::. “I take precautions to make sure I am not recognized. Perhaps you should do the same.” .:: Anger welled within her. A Munafiqun telling her how to do her job! For a moment, she was tempted to sink her fangs in and make Julian Falcon a memory, but then she took a deep breath—she wanted her payment, not a body at her feet. She snarled at him. ::. “You are late, Falcon. If I am to do business with you, I expect that you will keep appointments and not waste my time.” .:: Her voice came in a forced out hiss. Falcon looked as though he was going to start something, but before he did, he held up his hands in a conciliatory gesture. ::. “My apologies to you. Business became a bit more involved than I had expected.” .:: Shadow narrowed her eyes at him. He was lying, and she could tell. Irresponsibility was something that she abhorred in her rigid, disciplined lifestyle. She kicked open a door behind her foot that was before unseen. Inside was a room no more than closet-sized. She didn’t know what it was used for, but it would suffice. The two of them stepped inside, and it was barely big enough to contain the both of them, with her trailing coat and lithe form and Julian Falcon’s hulking breadth. Inside this room, the Kine stench was worse than ever, and she almost gagged. ::. “Talk.” .:: Falcon appeared almost disarmed by her brusque manner. His hands were beginning to clam up with blood sweat. She could smell it oozing through the fabric of his rag gloves. ::. “Your work with Pazzi was highly satisfactory. The police have no tips, no leads whatsoever, and the Giovanni in this city are left baffled. You have managed to fool the higher-ups of one of the most tightly organized Giovanni operations in North America. And now they are at a loss—no one cooks the books as well as Pazzi, it would seem, and they have run into some trouble trying to find a replacement. To say that I am impressed with your work would be an understatement.” .:: She did not even nod. ::. “I am not interested in flattery. Why did you not keep up your end of the bargain?” .:: Falcon paused again. The smell of sweat was stronger, mixing in with Kine and alcohol in a thick, disgusting mixture that hung heavy in the stagnant air. ::. “Because I have something else to offer you, something more of a challenge. As I told Riven last night, we have run into an unexpected problem with a Giovanni ghoul by the name of Geffroi LaBatte.” “LaBatte? I thought they did not Embrace or Ghoul outside of the family.” .:: Suspicion was rife in her voice. ::. “Typically no. LaBatte is a talent that they needed, though. He is their mouthpiece for infiltrating the daylight corporate world. He is also somewhat of an organizational genius. It is said that even Vittorio and Cadenza Giovanni take advice from him when they need it.” “Incestuous morons taking advice from Kine… interesting.” .:: Shadow’s comment was not intended to offend—it was a well known fact among Kindred that the Giovanni did not often sire out of family lines. And while they were mortal, they often did not mate outside of family lines, either. ::. “Don’t underestimate him. LaBatte is valuable to them because he is just the kind of stock that they look for. Slimy, vice-ridden scum just like the lot of the Giovanni clan. But on top of that, he knows how to conceal his vice, and that’s something that the Giovanni will forever be in need of. In fact, that’s what will help bring down their structure here—if LaBatte is out of the picture, then several of their cover-ups will fall through, especially since there won’t be anyone acceptable to smooth things over with the police and keep the palms greased. LaBatte is tricky. You’ll need time.” .:: For the most part, Shadow glossed over nearly everything that he said. Falcon’s blathering wasn’t the important part, nor was the entire history of the Giovanni clan in this area. What WAS important was that he wasn’t pressing her to do her business this time. ::. “So, you’re giving me an opportunity to know what I’m getting into this time? Why thank you so much, Falcon, to what do I owe the honor?” .:: The ire in her voice was fairly obvious. She hated Falcon just as much as Falcon hated Riven. And he probably hated her too, but he needed her. She was still irritated, though. Irritated that Falcon was trying to use her. Before he could speak again, she continued. ::. “Payment will be expected in full this time. For ALL of my services, not just Pazzi.” .:: Falcon nodded. ::. “You may expect it upon successful completion. I did not intend to offend you, Shadow. I had to have a way to hook you in—no other Assassin would have been fit for the job.” .:: This enraged her. She grabbed Falcon by the collar, surprising him with her strength as she yanked him far closer than was comfortable. Falcon tried not to look into her disturbingly flame-ridden eyes, and she tried not to vomit at the smell of him. ::. “I don’t play games, let’s make that explicitly clear. Whatever bargaining tool you thought Pazzi was, you were wrong. I don’t know what you know about Assassins, but the first thing you need to know is that so long as there’s no payment coming in, I have no loyalty to you… OR your safety. Are we clear?” .:: She shoved Falcon up against the opposite wall, and then shoved the sticking door open back out into the alleyway. She wasn’t going to stand around and mince words with this asshole. She’d do her job, and get her payment, and never have to speak with him again. His voice called her back for a moment. ::. “For all the trouble dealing with Assassins is, it’s a good thing the service is worth it.” .:: Shadow laughed. What a pathetic fool. She slipped something out of her pocket and held it up for him to see as he stumbled out of the small room and into the loose gravel of the alleyway himself. ::. “Let’s not forget our priorities. Don’t even think of backing out on your end of the bargain this time. I have all the proof I need to make you a very miserable being, if I choose. Don’t make me choose, Falcon.” .:: Falcon stared dully at the small black micro-cassette recorder that she had in her hand. Something akin to fear thudded in his chest, but he tried to brush it off. He wasn’t going to let anyone who was working for him abuse him like that. He opened his mouth, but there was nothing left to say. ::. .:: Shadow smirked, and slipped the object into her pocket. ::. Don’t ever think I’m not ready for you, Julian Falcon. You don’t even know what you’re dealing with.
