Date: 2004-05-16 Otherworld. I "A yes or a no sets in motion a 1000 live or dies." After a mere 25 years in the world, she looked far beyond them. Frowning had formed creases, which became wrinkles, leaving contour lines of anguish and despair mapping her face. Where once bright, vivid emerald eyes shone, her lids were barely held far enough apart to get any look though the windows to her troubled soul. She didn't even like people looking into them, and made any excuse to shield her inner-thoughts with dark glasses. It helped make life a little, only a little, more comfortable. "Semore'll be houndin' you 'gain soon," came a gruff voice from down her hallway. Where off-white wallpaper once hung, damp air and years of neglect had turned it crusty and yellow, to the point that sheer will-power kept it clinging to the crumbling plaster. "Ya know what he's like come rent day." Nearly a half of her measly pay-packet to live in squalor. When they finally came to fix her faltering heating, she couldn't help but wish it had never been reported. Maybe after a few more months of incomplete burning, or bad ventilation, carbon monoxide would save life a problem, and let her go in her sleep. After all, bed was the only place she seemed to find comfort nowadays. "Shame he isn't so eager to make this place less of a dump," her apathetic reply came, words sounding tired and half-hearted... As if they were being said just for show, to fit in. She didn't pay enough attention to anything anymore, not enough to care about a coat of paint of a sweep of the floor. Mousy, un-tamed hair was still matted down by the rain, sending a steady trickle down a sloped nose, where it then teetered on the brink... Before dropping onto her dry, peeling lips. Her right foot felt damp, as it had all through the spring rains. Despite avoiding the puddles, the shredded sole of her sneaker sucked the water up and into her un-washed socks. The key that forcefully turned the sticky lock didn't open a door to a bright new world, only to one of sanctuary, a brief hideaway from the pressures of the world. "Umph," the young woman exhaled as her knee slammed into the door, who's damp, swollen frame made just opening it a chore. But there was no such problem closing it, when a defiant slam echoed throughout the decrepit building. "Hey!" screamed a shrill voice through the thin ceiling. "You keep noise down! Me tell! You always noisy!" She'd given up yelling back, and now just settled at flipping the bird in the cracked ceiling's direction. Over a growing pile of bills and junk mail she then stepped, before taking a route of carpet stepping-stones through the mess... And then, with a releasing sigh that could blow away life's problems for just long enough, she collapsed down onto her bed. Single. Creaking springs. Loose headboard. Tatty blanket. 5 pillows. Heaven. The window did little to block out the sounds of the city, as car-horns, police sirens, and revving motorcycle engines crept through the gaps... Despite the trails of blu-tack that did their best to make a protective barrier. All night she heard the voices, heard the arguments, and every now and then, a short, sharp explosion ripped her from her sleep. She told herself it was an engine back-firing, but then again, she told herself a lot of things. "Rent day tomorrow!" a familiar, sinister-sounding voice then bellowed as it's owner paced up and down the hallway. Not even the courtesy of a knock was afforded to his tenants, just the usual hounding and inflexible demand. Semore Tiduski, landlord, sleaze-back, and likely violator of just about every building code in existence. Sliding under the covers, she put the largest and freshest of the pillows over her head, helping drown out the sounds of the world. Tomorrow was pay day, her scant reward for a fortnight of mind-numbing, minimum wage toil. Half would pay for rotting bricks and dodgy utilities, the rest would be split between cheap, barely nutritious food, and enough Vodka to numb her senses long enough to make it from one day to the next. Everybody follows a path to get to where they are. Nobody wakes up in their mid-twenties and starts their life... Choices map their path, turn the corners, and set the speeds. The guy screaming at a thoughtless motorist outside got to where he was by his decisions, same as Semore, who could be heard storming up the stairs. But she... She wasn't meant to be there... Something was wrong. II "The future never happened." It wasn't meant to be like this. When Sandra Wolfenden told her husband, Lawrence, about her first pregnancy, it didn't seem like the world contained enough joy to express his emotions. "I don't understand you," her fearful words mumbled. "I thought you'd be pleased." The scene was set for anything but an argument. In their brand new, grand, suburban home, light flooded in through the gaping windows. The interior was all soft-pine and plentiful plants, while through the French doors, a spacious garden looked ready to accept a growing family. But it hadn't come cheaply, and in paying for a family home, Lawrence Wolfenden had priced himself out of having any more children. "I am pleased," Lawrence tried to explain, pacing back and forth in front of the fireplace. "I'm... It's..." Sandra's head dropped, shielding her face under sandy blonde. "There's no use searching for the right words," her defeatist voice lamented. "Just say you don't want another child." She couldn't have been any more wrong. There was nothing Lawrence wanted more, and in his own way, he was hurting just as much as his wife. He felt weak, inadequate, a failure. Even as a high-rising executive, tipped to replace just about everyone on the rung above him, he couldn't just send a memo down and make things happen. At work, suited and booted, he was as close to untouchable as you could get... People pushed and shoved to be the first to offer their support to his projects, but away from the office... ...This. He was human, and it was only just dawning on him. "How can you say that?" he hit back, while managing to keep any hint of anger out of his voice. "You and Max are the most important things in the world to me. But what if... What if things at work take a downslide? We won't even be able to pay the mortgage, let alone support another child!" They met in their teens. A few fleeting conversations about music and movies grew naturally into talk of marriage and kids. Lawrence had no fear of commitment as far as Sandra was concerned, to the point that he was numb to any pre-wedding nerves. The day Max was born, he'd never felt more proud. He was a father, he had a son to carry on the Wolfenden name... But more than that, he was able to give Sandra the child she craved. And now, through reason rather than passion, he was trying to deny her a second. "So, will you be the one to drive me to the clinic?" Sandra asked, regrettable hatred in her voice that troubled Lawrence the second it entered his ear. The thought seemed more than he was able to cope with. "Not that," he pleaded, immediately trying to tell Sandra that it wasn't what he wanted. "There are thousands of couples who can't have children, who'd give a kid a loving home..." Her head rose, and Sandra flicked the locks from her face, staring right at the father of her son. "And you'd wake up every morning, knowing someone else was calling your child their own?" Lawrence hadn't realised, but he was starting to cry. "Or I could wake up every morning," he began to reply, before feeling the tears escape, "knowing I can't provide for my family..." Before Sandra could hit back, Lawrence was heading out the door... Not wanting his wife to see him break down in front of her. To know he wanted something so much, yet feel so unworthy of having it, was a feeing that he just couldn't cope with. In the world, there's so much talk about people taking responsibility for their actions, but what if the most responsible option would ultimately be the most destructive? Lawrence didn't know it, and could never have known it, but his dilemma had already been mapped out into an infinite number of outcomes... ...Which others would have to live with. III "A change of billing." "You get the tickets?" "Yeah." "Awesome, man... Gonna be a great show!" "Damn right, we finally get to see that Ethan Knight guy." "No, we get to see that Ethan Knight guy get his ass-kicked by Seymour Almasy." "Man, your Final Fantasy addiction worries me." "I didn't see you returning that Rikku porn I printed off for ya." "...Shut up." Outside the ticket office, the queue of fans snaked around the 4 rows of railings. The Primetime Central Cruiserweight Tournament had finally come to a head, after weeks of competition, and the finalists were known. As part of the advertising outside the arena, the finals brackets were hung on a banner, alongside the card for the show itself. Seymour Almasy def. Syko Arsonist. Ethan Knight def. Dawn McFarlane. "Sorry, no ringside seats left." "What's the closest you've got?" "Section D, row 21." "Fine, I'll take 4 there." The Final Fantasy against the most skilled of Violence Jack's followers... It was probably the popular prediction for the final. "Man, they'd better not sell out before we get to the front." "Yeah, the touts fleece you here." It seemed the natural choice for the final. PTC's tournament specialist, former Global Champion Seymour Almasy taking on someone from the new, hungry, fledgling promotion of RPW. RUSH Pro Wrestling had flooded the entries with their talent, using it as a chance to showcase their cruiserweights, as well as showing the PTC community that it's female wrestlers were a match for the best men in the business. Cays showed that was true, only losing out to Syko Arsonist in a close match. "There are your tickets, first bell at 8, gates open at 6:30." "Wait, Knight made the finals?" "Yeah, he took out McFarlane." "Really?" "Err... Yeah." "Hmmm..." "What's wrong?" "Nothing. I just thought someone else made the final..." IV "A girl out of time." It was barely 9pm, but the young woman curled up under the covers and hugged a pillow, almost wishing it was someone she could feel for. She had nobody to hold her, and not even a stuffed animal to name... She had bedding. It wasn't that she was unattractive, and in her relative plainness there was something ultimately alluring. Her life just wasn't something she wanted to share with another. It was best that she go through it alone, solitary, and not let her misfortunes infect others, dragging them down. No, she wouldn't inflict herself on someone simply drawn to her by basic human attraction. She could sleep for 12 hours a night, easily. Sleep was her break from her life, as the less she was conscious, the less she had to deal with... Because she knew better than anyone, that she wasn't coping anymore. "Where’ve you been?" a screeching female voice questioned through the walls, just a split second after the young woman winced from the slamming of a door. "You reek of booze... Why do I even bother, huh? TELL ME WHY?" "Because this life is all we have," the young woman mumbled, commenting from under her quilt. "Whether we want it or not..." The walls of her building might as well not have been there. Their only purpose seemed to be for the occupant's modesty. Every word was broadcast to the next room, every argument inflicted upon neighbours, and every deep, sexual breath and yelp of escaping ecstasy made voyeurs of those above and below. She'd heard far more of their conversations with others than she'd ever experienced with them... Talking was an uncomfortable thing when you can't even say things to yourself. "Shut up," the moaning husband slurred, his footsteps sounding like a long stumble. "My money, I'll do what I want..." A dull thud echoed through the building as his hefty frame dropped onto a couch, followed by a repeating, chesty cough. "And what am I meant to buy food with?" his despairing partner yelled back, tired of their money being drank away. Under the covers, the young woman sniffed... Not out of nasal irritation, nor from a cold. She was crying herself to sleep. A double barrier of pillows was pulled over her head, it's stuffing doing the best it could to keep the arguments at bay. She'd heard the same thing a hundred times before... And she'd only had to hear the first end to know it was best for her sanity if she didn't listen to any more. The beating couldn't be mistaken for the stumbling of a drunk man, the falling to the floor wasn't onto the softness of a couch, and the screams were of anything but ecstasy. If this were a one-off, it would still trouble most people... But it wasn't a rare occasion. It was existence, and it was life. Again, she sniffed, and the ticking of a tear crept over her cheekbone, before dropping onto the top-sheet of her spongy mattress. "Shut up," she whispered, pleading and praying for the woman who's screams managed to tear through the pillows. "Just don't make it worse..." Life is good. Life is great. Life is a gift. But sometimes, life can be a burden... Especially when the life you live wasn't meant to be yours. Sure, everyone can use that excuse. With more loving parents they could have made more of their lives, or with more money they could have been a Doctor or a Lawyer. But the people they blame can say the same thing, and it all just recoils, back into the past, where one action, one choice, one anomaly started everything. Laying completely still, the young woman just relaxed her body and mind, waiting for the sleeping tablet to kick in. For 12 hour a day she was in stasis, and it was like heaven to her. "Why am I even with you?" the shrill voice continued, permeating wood and plaster. "Why am I stuck here, in this dump, with no future, Huh? WHY?" Why, indeed. V "A mind is torn, and reality's fibres fray." Sandra Wolfenden had been watching her son at play for almost an hour. Unfaltering. She smiled with every topple, and laughed with every clap he gave himself. All the others mothers in her social circle had hired a nanny, relieving themselves of maternal duties in favour of going back to work. She would never say it to their faces, but she found them selfish. A child wasn't something you have just to give yourselves an heir, or because you really should at that point in your life. A child is a responsibility, and it's the parents' job to sculpt them into proper human beings, and instil them with morals and principles. The younger you are, the more you take in, as the mind is at it's most absorbent when curiosity dominates. A nanny was never an option, so the thought of adoption was too hard to even think about. "Look, Mommy..." the playful Max blurted out, attracting his doting mother's attention. "Look!" Even the smallest achievement sent a sense of pride coursing through his mother's body. Max was too young to even realise what the perma-smile on his mother's face was for, and he was totally oblivious to the fact that his sibling's fate was being argued over. "That's my boy," she beamed, applauding along with him as he sat behind a tower of blocks. "And soon you'll have a brother or sister to play with." She didn't know how, but she was going to convince Lawrence. There was a life growing inside her, made from their own bodies, their genes, and she wasn't going to give it up for anything. It was growing inside her, it was feeding off her, and it would stay with her until she was torn from the realm of mortals... Whether it meant Lawrence being a father to it or not. That thought alone caused the smile to subside. Was she really ready to bring up two children on her own? Could she alone provide them with all they'd need? Love she could serve up a never-ending supply of, but what about college? What about a roof over their heads? "Why us?" she muttered, looking around their new home and wondering how such blissful surroundings could house such troubles. Sandra appreciated all her husband did. He wasn't working until he dropped just so he could drive a better car, or be able to order a few more employees about. Lawrence was doing it all so that they'd never have to face these kind of problems, face the dilemma, but it just happened to pop up before he'd secured everything. Even if they sold the house, in fees, depreciation, and rent, they'd hardly be any better off. This was the home she wanted Max to grow up in... She just didn't want him to grow up alone. Deep in thought, Sandra looked past Max and out the front window. The secluded cul-de-sac was the perfect place for kids to play. No passing traffic, a close-knit community, it's own neighbourhood patrol. "Lawrence," she then acknowledged as her husband appeared, walking up the driveway. Squinting, she tried to gage his feelings. He didn't look angry, and if anything, he looked apologetic... But was it apologetic in that he was wrong, or apologetic in that he couldn't overcome his fears and change his mind. The key slid in, the lock turned, and the door closed behind him. Before he'd even entered the room, Lawrence could see Max playing. His "little champion", the boy he'd take to the Baseball, the teenager he'd teach to drive, and the man he'd proudly attend the wedding of. How could he have such dreams for one son, and try to block out all hopes for the child Sandra was carrying? Deep down, he feared that another child would crumble his hopes for Max, splitting the resources they had for their child 2 ways, and making their early years a struggle rather than a joy. "I think we need to talk," he spoke softly, entering the room with his head hung low, still with only one child beside him in his dream of the future. VI "I died today, and it rained tears of pity... Or were they of joy?" Every morning, she felt like getting out of bed was a waste. She was never going to be of any value, never going to do an important job, never going to be a loving wife or a caring mother. As life went on all around her, she was stuck at a dead end... No purpose, no reason, nobody to miss her. Surely, her just being alive must have been an oversight. If life was one huge equation, she was the annoying remainder that only served to complicate things. "Yeah, don't apologise or anything," she moaned as the morning rush to work jostled her from side to side. Arms brushed, shoulders nudged, and toes clipped heels. It was an awkward mass of humanity, all swarming in different directions, but all ultimately going to the same place. There were the sharply-dressed executives, but they'd go hungry if it wasn't for the dowdy waitresses. They all had their place in life's equation, making the sides balance. "Can you spare any change?" a wheezy man pleaded, sitting under what appeared to be a spare piece of carpet at the entrance to an alley. "A cup of coffee for an old-timer?" They didn't even look at him. You could bet that at their next social occasion, those who could afford to give would be ego-stroking one another with talk of charity events, and doing good for their community. The most bizarre of well-meaning charities would have a gala devoted to them, or an overly-expensive dinner named after them... Attended by those who'd walk past the real charity cases on the street each day. "There you are," the young woman warmly announced as she dropped to one knee beside him. "Don't you go blowing it on fast cars and loose women." Her lighter side seemed easier to find around complete strangers... As only with those she'd likely never see again could she be herself. In 25 years, she'd had more one night stands than she'd had loving relationships. They were too hard, needed too much effort, too much trust. She could be herself with people she didn't know, they didn't judge. "Thank you, Miss," the man politely replied with the utmost sincerity, relief on his voice that someone in the city could actually hear him, and care. "Your welcome," she then said, departing his company for the crowd. Most people have a list in their head of things they need to do each day. Wake up the kids, feed the cat, get the children to school, do some grocery shopping, mail off a birthday card, pay the bills, book a table for 2 tomorrow night, make sure to call their mother... Such burdens were really a luxury. Waking up under a bleak cover of dark cloud, all the young woman had to do was go to work, get paid, settle her rent. Lather. Rinse. Repeat. If only she'd known what the day had in store for her, she might have thought harder about those who might miss her... And said a last goodbye. A rumble of ominous thunder rolled overhead, ripping the sky apart and opening up the heavens. Dense rain pummelled those beneath, soaking jackets and flattening hair under it's relentless downpour. Scurrying for cover as if water would kill them, people shielded their heads with newspapers or umbrellas, and quickened their pace. The young woman, however, welcomed it. Although her coat wasn't water-proof, and the soles of her shoes were worn, she walked without a care in the world through the rain. Dropping her head back, she let it's cleansing do the job of the shower she skipped that morning, freshening her up and cooling her face. The alleyway which she took to work was already flooded, it's drains blocked with leaves, litter, and a plethora of other things that it was better not to think about. A fitting metaphor for the world. Splashing through the murky puddles, the woman scraped the sodden hair from her face, and squeezed through the small gap between bins. A glittering tower of an office it was not. The rotting door, who's paint had peeled to show the previous 5 coats, was already ajar. There was no need for security in the places nobody cared about. Nowadays, most petty thieves would have been put-off by the mud and grime. Every morning, she passed Doctors on their way to save lives, Fireman who's jovial banter disguised the fact that they would likely brave death in their everyday work, Bus Drivers who kept the city's populace mobile... All these people might not have thought of themselves as special, but to have a purpose in life was very enviable to her. When your job involves ringing strangers from a dank basement, on the off-chance you can sell them unscrupulous insurance, even a gas-pumper would be above you on the social ladder. She hated her job, yet didn't strive to better herself... She was content just to exist, and sometimes ever that was an exaggeration. "Greg?" she called down the stairs, hearing none of the usual pre-work banter that she always shied away from. Anything she said would only kill the conversation. "You there? Marie?" Each step was a hazard, giving it's own unique creak that someone with an ear for music could probably play a tune on. "Don't say the phones are out again?" Navigating the tight corner of the staircase down, the woman turned to look at her workplace... Deserted. Only a pair of the old, dodgy phones were left. Dialling lists, chairs, desks, and ever the stationary had been stripped from the bare room. "The fuck?" she questioned, hushed in disbelief. "Where is everyone?" "Greg's done a bunk," came a low voice, emerging from around the corner where the sink was housed. "Looks like payday seemed a good time to cut his losses." Walking past the young woman, and up the stairs behind her, the co-worker - Marie - decided there was little point in sticking around... It was time better spent finding a new job. "Hope you weren't banking on the cash." She just dropped down, sitting with a bump on the bottom of the steps. "Yeah," she breathlessly mumbled, "you could say I was..." Her job, albeit a depressing and unrewarding one, was gone. As was the money she was owed, the only currency she had to her name. No money meant no rent meant no home, meant no chance of getting another job. In such despair, the most worrying thing was that she didn't care. VII "A change of heart never hurt anyone." "Whatever it takes." Those were the words Sandra Wolfenden defiantly spoke to her husband. She knew that he was working to the point of exhaustion, and that he couldn't even find enough time to spend with the child they already had, but Sandra would find a way to keep her baby... She had to. "But how are we going to afford it?" Lawrence asked, the mildest hint of shame in her voice as he looked right into his wife's eyes. Side by side on the sofa they sat, hands held, while Max continued to play, having no idea the importance of the conversation happening beside him. "This house was a stretch with just Max, and if interest rates go up, I'm not sure how we'll afford the mortgage." "Then I'll work for as long as I can before the baby comes," Sandra appealed, disregarding her vow not to be the sort of mother who went back to her job while her child was still young. "I can do enough hours to cover the extra, and fit it around childcare. My mother could take Max during the day, and I'm sure we could find someone suitable to watch him when she can't." "It's bad enough that I don't get to spend enough time with Max," Lawrence replied, turning to look at his oblivious offspring, "without you working all hours too." He had a good point. She found it hard enough to leave him for just a few hours when they enjoyed a rare night out... Leaving him for someone else to feed, put to bed, or play with. "I know..." she conceded. "But it's seeing Max less for 6 months, or never seeing this child inside of me after they're born." There was little that he could say to that. Lawrence was starting to realise how much crap he'd been told in his life. Study hard, get into a good school, secure a job with prospects, move into a family home in a great neighbourhood with your beautiful wife and kid, work hard to get yourself promoted... And it still couldn't make him happy. Somewhere, there was a guy who didn't finish high school, who worked a dead-end job but was able to make his family happy. Lawrence envied him. "I don't care if we have to move, I don't care if I'm eating the same cheap thing every night for a year, we can't give away our child, Lawrence," she bluntly stated. "We just can't." Sandra always worried that she'd appear as a gold-digger. Lawrence was always a man marked for a great life, while she wasn't blessed with the same such upbringing. She worked harder than any to get to where she was, but people would always see her as "less" than her husband... But if there was a choice between his money or his love, she'd set fire to the $100 bills herself. "I'll phone the bank tomorrow," Lawrence then conceded. "See what they can do with the mortgage repayments, then see the Estate Agent." "Really?" Sandra's head shot up as she spoke, eyeballs glistening with tears. "So you're..." "Yes," he reassured her. "And I'm sorry... I've been an idiot..." "You haven't," she soothed him with as they hugged, tightly, and for nearly a minute... Neither needing to say anything as the second hand completed it's orbit of the clock face. "I love you." VIII "From the past, the future hears an echo." She was stood in the middle of a swirling world. The ground moved slowly beneath her unsteady feet, while the people across the street flashed out of view in the blink of an eye. She was drunk, to the point that all her concentration was given to just staying on her feet. What little funds she had to contribute to her rent had been blown in the last hour, buying a small, farewell round of drinks for total strangers at a grimy bar. They all just looked at her as if she were a waste of life, but were happy to take the drinks her tatty notes bought... While she knew one or 2 were pondering whether she were drunk enough to force themselves onto her. "To life," she boomed, taking another swig from the brown paper bag. "Life rules! Yeah!" Her words barely formed, and parents crossed their children across the street to avoid her. Having tired of her shoes squelching with each step, the young woman had kicked them off, and discarded her socks too, choosing to walk barefoot along the wet streets. "That's it," she yelled to those shielding their kids' eyes from her sight, "keep them away from the crazy drunk gir-Whoa..." Tripping over her own feet, the intoxicated young woman lunged forward, just managing to put her hands out to secure herself against a lamp-post. Leant forward, and with the sudden jolt upsetting her stomach, she gagged... Then vomited into the gutter. Choking coughs followed, before a string of mucus-rich spit was flung from her mouth, finally dropping off her bottom lip and down the drain. "Wish I had a mother to do that..." Not having pulled her hair back, the front of her mank mane carried flecks of vomit, not that she noticed... Or would care if she did. Everyone, whether they'll admit it or not, has a vision of the way they'll die. Some choose old age, surrounded by their family. Some would want to go down fighting, protecting what is theirs... Few would choose drunk, homeless, inebriated, and without a friend in the world to miss them. There would be no lovingly engraved tombstone in a family plot for her. No tears cried over her, and no sense of loss... That was the way she wanted. "Disgraceful," one old lady muttered as she past her, revolted by the sight of vomit being washed into the drains by the pounding rain. "Yes..." she instinctively shot back, her words slurred and throaty. "I'm a disgrace to everything... I'm a waste of oxygen... You think I ASKED to be born... Huh?" It was useless though, as the old lady closed her ears to her before the words even came out. She had a point though. So much is talked of a foetus' right to live... As if everyone is grateful to have been born. It's fine for those who have loving parents and a safe home, they can say they’re happy to be alive, grateful even. But what about those for whom everyday is just another 24 hours of misery? They didn't ask to be born... And all they can do is count down the days until they die. For some, the countdown is over. "Right on time," she muttered with a hint of a smile as the silver bus washed the sidewalk with waves of water from the street. Moving to the curb, she just looked as if she was waiting to cross the street... But her leading foot hung forward, poised and eager. Not needing to slow down for at least another 100 yards, the driver kept up his speed, guiding the bulky vehicle to it's stop. "Goodbye," she mumbled, her head dropping forward and eyes scanning left to see the bus approach. "Good. Fucking. Bye." Without looking up, not wanting the driver to have to look into her eyes before mowing her down, the young woman stepped off the kerb, and waited for what little life she had left to be thumped out of her. The bus didn't stop. It didn't need to. There was no bus. IX "Did I blink?" As her light show danced behind the black curtain, the thin gap between the hanging fabric cut a blue beam down her body. Golden blonde, choppy hair fell in waves either side of her head, almost grazing against her prominent cheekbones. Burning emerald eyes were housed behind blue-tinted glasses, while a thin layer of shiny pink covered her thin lips. There was no real emotion on her face, other then expectation. All the sounds of the show flooded into her ears, and she managed to separate them, able to listened to each. The cheering of the fans, the frantic communications of the sound crew, and then the howling introduction to Auf der Maur's "Followed the Waves". The flashing of the lights grew more erratic as the music swelled, it's sound dulled as it filtered through the black fabric... But she felt the bass deep in her throat. "And Wolfenden out in 5..." the entrance co-ordinator began. "4...3..." There were no nerves, only anticipation. The whole entrance thing, the intrusive publicity and the prize at the end were all just complications to be ignored... It was all about the fun that was to be had between the bells. She'd scoped out the ring, checked it's ropes, made herself at home on the top turnbuckles, and they were now friends. The canvas had an inviting bounce, perfect for rolling landings, while the guard rail surrounding the ring was just thick enough to leap on to from the apron. It was a dangerous playground, the kind she preferred. "2...1..." Sure, Almasy wasn't going to make it a mere work-out for her. She'd hurt the next morning, and more than appreciate a long soak in the bath to sooth her bruises, but there's no fun to be had wrapped up in cotton wool. Hardly a regular outlook on life, but then again, hers was more than unique. The choices, events, and actions that led her to the curtain made her Karina Wolfenden, and she hadn't regretted a single one of them. Deep down, she was happy, and one single choice years ago could have changed that. Time is an awkward medium, and one that it's best not to play around with... All you can do is choose what feels right. "And you're on..." "About time," she muttered as her gloved hands burst the curtain apart, "feels like I've been waiting forever." The end.
