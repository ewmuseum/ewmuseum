Extreme Debutante

13 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-08-13 "What is objectionable, what is dangerous, about extremists is not that they are extreme, but that they are intolerant. The evil is not what they say about their cause, but what they say about their opponents." --Robert Francis Kennedy (1925-1968) With these words in mind, the Extreme Title Division of Primetime Central seems just that, extreme. Weeks before a match is scheduled to happen, two or more men let words fly and the verbal bashing of their opponents is nearly as extreme as what goes on in the match itself. This can be said in confidence, as it was once said that words hit harder than fists. Perhaps a man is mentally beaten down long before he enters the ring. Perhaps a man can walk out on the stage with his music blaring in the background and the crowd chanting or cursing his name, and already know that he is going to fail. Perhaps as the pyro rises and the lights flash, his mind is filled with doubt while his eyes are filled with hope as he marches towards the ring where his battle will be fought and lost. Perhaps his face that is bloodied and broken after the long and grueling match is simply a mask to hide the truly bloody and broken mess inside. This point could be argued. The Extreme Title Division of Primetime Central is a prime example of this point. Their are gladiators that battle in this division that are experts of mentally and physically breaking down their opponents, even if they seem mentally broken down themselves. Like DDT... Even if they have been a champ before, and have nothing at all to prove. Like Sean McClancy... Even if they have not competed in a Primetime Central ring for some time. Like ICE... Even if their trust for others is thin. Like Shaman... Even if their mysterious figure is more menacing than their words. Like Kyo Kushinaga... Even if their record for Primetime Central action is not impressive at all. Like Joey Vendetti... In this division, it would seem that each of these six gladiators is well suited to compete for the biggest prize. All six will battle to obtain the belt... But could one of these titans lose before they reach the ring? Joey Vendetti. For most of his life, the definition of a stereotypical mafiad. The Vendetti family was a very important and prestigious family in southern Italy and the island of Sicily, this we can be sure of. For generations the Vendettis terrorized the countryside with their own breed of organized crime. The Vendettis claimed that they were only protecting the families of the countryside from the corrupt police force that molested the women and killed the children, not to mention slaughtered every man that refused to pay the extra tax. In reality, the Vendettis were no better than that police force that they despised. The Vendettis took what they did seriously... The Vendettis were exremists. So if the Vendettis were extremists, does the earlier Robert F. Kennedy quote have merit? Did the things that the Vendettis said do more damage than the things that the Vendettis did? Is it possible that the Vendettis could have mentally demolished an entire countryside? Of course. Leading people to believe that they were being protected, then dashing that hope against the wall in spatters of blood was the ultimate mental breakdown. The Vendettis' words set the people of Sicily up for the last fall that they would ever take. For one reason or another, the Vendettis found reason to do this. Debt, revenge, anger, lust, and respect were all reasons that people met their maker at the hands of a Vendetti. After this short history of his family, we realize why Joey is the way that he is. Joseph Marciano Vendetti is a cold-blooded sadist who was meant, at least in his own mind, to compete in the Extreme Title Division of Primetime Central. For Joey Vendetti is an extremist... In more ways than one. Joey's first excursion into the Extreme Title Division was much less than successful. After three taps on the mat the bell rang and Moraless had picked up another victory in PTC. As Moraless stood victorious over his fallen opponent, the opponent himself began to journey through his own mind. Why was the pain too much for him to handle? This was the first time that Joey Vendetti had ever submitted and it was to a man that he hardly even knew. Sure, Joey had seen Moraless before in matches with XUW's own DDT, but had never seen him in person. Imagine Joey's shock when he stepped into the ring with Moraless and lost by submission only minutes later. Anyone that had ever watched XUW knew that Joseph Marciano Vendetti simply didn't tap out, it just was not a normal occurance. But on that night he did, and on that night Moraless moved further ahead and on later to win the title. Could Moraless have actually been a true extremist and broken Joey down before the match? Could Joey have tapped out long before the match merely because of a few words from Moraless? No... Then if Moraless was an extremist, and he must have been to have won the title, could it be that Robert Francis Kennedy's theory about extremists is wrong? Could it be that the words spoken to an opponent haven't an effect? Not exactly. Going into his match with Moraless, Joey Vendetti was a mere shell of his former self. Just out of a bittersweet victory over the Army of Darkness in a War Games match at XUW Reborn, Joey was injured and only at about half of his normal strength. Joey received the call early one morning that he was to compete in a PTC match against Moraless. His manager and image consultant Marti Pfuffkin had signed him for a match to get him back into condition. Joey wanted no part in in-ring work, he wanted to heal his leg, or so he wanted everyone to believe. The actual truth, Joey's leg was completely healed. His back was no longer sore and had not a single pull in any muscle. If Joey was completely healed, why did he dread in-ring competition? Could it have been the headlines?..... "Will Vendetti return to the ring?" "An Italian Target: Vendetti is Done!" "Joey Vendetti: We Barely Knew Ye..." As vicious as the headlines were, Joey knew that he could prove them wrong. The real question was, did he want to? It's almost a silly question when it's thought about for a period of a few moments. Of course Joey would want to prove them wrong. Despite hardly ever holding gold or having ever defeated one of the "big boys", Joey Vendetti was an icon of XUW. But if Joey wanted to prove his point against the press, then he would have to be successful in the ring. Joey would have to be successful against Moraless. But Joey was not successful. Why? Why couldn't Joey pull off the victory? The answer was and is quite simple. Because Joseph Marciano Vendetti was more of an extremist than Moraless was. It sounds crazy, but if one were to sit and ponder on it, it actually makes a great deal of sense. In Joey's own doubt, he began to question himself. Joey began to question whether or not he would fair well enough against a wrestler or Moraless's caliber and be able to prove himself. We take you back to Joey, sitting and thinking out loud in the den of his large home. The large and lavish Vendetti Mansion stretches over the rocky shores of New Jersey and casts a shadow that appears to be nearly a mile in length. Inside the Vendetti home, Joseph sits and thinks to himself. He has merely three hours to go before his matchup with Moraless, and of course he's dressed to kill and ready to go. The large plush chair that Joey is seated in sits at an angle that turns it towards the large window that gives us a view of Joey's front yard. Joey is wearing his normal "SoS: Not Calling For Help, but you Might Want To" t-shirt and loose blue jeans. His black sneakers scrape the bottom of the chair in a rythmic motion as he glances out the window, then back to the clock on the wall that reads six pm. Joey sighs and looks back out the window. Joey: What if I don't win this thing? Those damn news papers will keep running their crap and I'll be out of a job. I just have to win this thing... The focus on Joey's face is intense. The look in Joey's eyes is determined. Joey's mindset however... is extreme. I've failed so many times before and it can happen again. I know I'm better than this mook, and I know I can take his ass out... but I just don't know... I just... And slowly, Joey did his own damage. Without knowing it, Joey had pressed the blade of a razor-sharp knife to his skin and he was beginning to cut. Joey was defeating himself before he even stepped into the ring, while simply sitting there in his den. So now, we can assume that Robert Francis Kennedy's theory was correct. The words that Joey spoke to himself mentally broke him down, and Joey tapped out. So what can be done? If Joey broke himself down before his last PTC match and tapped out, then what can be done to help Joey succeed? This very question has raced through Joey's head ever since learning of his contention for the Extreme Title Division's belt. He knows that to win this match, to win this belt, he must return to his most extreme form. Joey Vendetti must return to the extremist that he used to be. How would he go about doing that? Well Joey has already taken several steps toward returning to his most extreme form. He shed extra baggage when he layed waste to his own partner. He began a hunt for an enemy that would satisfy his thirst for competition, and found that in XUW's Dave Kipfer. And last but not least, Joey Vendetti willingly entered an Elimination Chamber match for the Extreme Title of Primetime Central. "This won't be like last time!" Joey decided early. He had applied for the match, knowing that it may very well be the last match of his career. An Elimination Chamber match... Snap back to reality. Joey Vendetti is once again dressed to kill, only this time he's not seated in his large, elegant home. No. This time Joey is standing in front of a mirror, and the camera is angled so that Joey and the mirror can both be seen. Joey's back is turned to the camera, and his image in the mirror wears a fine armani suit and a smile. The black suit with a white undershirt and lengthy red tie is very elegant indeed, and must have cost a pretty penny to buy. Joey normally has no problem coming up with a few bucks to make himself happy, but one thing Joey does seem to have a difficult time coming up with is a success that can be called "unrivaled". Joey grins in the mirror as he straightens his tie and continues primping himself as he speaks. Joey: So this is what it's gonna come to? An Elimination Chamber match is the match that I'm gonna have to prove myself in? That's fine... I'm up for it. I always am. It's just sometimes other people don't think I'm up for things like this. That's how I get pinned before I ever get to the ring. Joey's voice, smooth as fine silk, floats over the room and the confident look on his face makes the mirror shine. Don't get me wrong. As soon as I started talking, you started thinking I was doubting myself. You started thinking I wasn't giving myself a chance in this match. On the contrary friends, I'm giving myself the best chance in this match. This match, I'm going to win. I have done my fair bit of research, I've looked around at my competition in this match, and I've thought of a million ways that I could defeat them, the only thing is... This match won't be like any other match. Joey drops his arms to his side and stares into the mirror smiling. See, as I stand in front of this mirror and contemplate ways to be successful in this match, I realize that all five other men must be thinking the same thing. Each of them has to think that they have a strategy that's going to work, even if their strategy is to go into the match without any set gameplan. Everyone has a plan that they think is going to work.... But those people are wrong. Joey turns from the mirror and walks to a steel chair in the center of the room and sits down in it, placing his hands together with his fingers extended. He stairs into the camera with his smile fading to a look of intensity. You see, an elimination chamber match is different from another match. When we enter this match, we don't know who's going to be wrestling first. We don't know who's gonna be taking on who. It could be any two people, and after the time limit, any other person could step into the ring. It's a bizarre mish-mash of confusion and violence inside a steel cage that favors no one. No one has the advantage in this match. Six men will battle inside the cage... But before we even make it to the cage, the six men will already be battling. How much mental strength does a person have, that they can zero out all the words and insults thrown at them by their opponents? Think about it for a minute. Shaman, you view yourself in your mind, and you wonder what the other men in this match think of you. You wonder if maybe Joey Vendetti or DDT or any of the others would doubt you or underestimate you before we head into this match. You wonder if we recognize that you're a threat, just like you recognize that we are threats. Of course we recognize you as a threat Shaman. But do I, Joey Vendetti, believe that you are an easy target? Joey grins into the camera and sits back in his chair. Yeah, I do. Know why Shaman? Because you're already being broken down before my eyes, and I didn't even have to say anything. They say that a true extremist can do more evil and do more damage with his words than with his fists. You must be quite the extremist Shaman. Because, like me, you have broken yourself down before you even reach the ring. You have lost the match already. You are no token. But take my advice Shaman, stop worrying about what I think of you. Because now you know. Joey looks down at his shoe and sees that it has somehow come untied. He bends over and starts to tie it as he continues speaking. Sean McClancy, the only former champion in this match. He has to be the favorite going in... he has to be the one that everyone thinks is gonna take this thing. Joey finishes and sits up, stopping himself before he insults himself further. But you know what they say, the bigger they are, the harder they fall. And without a doubt, McClancy is the big dog in this match. The Irish Bastard.... heh. Irish. What good ever came from the damn Irish? Joey stands and marches to the mirror again, checking his hair, which is spiked with a raven black color. ICE... Kyo... those two mean nothing to me. ICE hasn't competed in an age, so ring rust will be a problem for him.... Joey cocks his head to the side in the mirror. My verbal assault has already begun. I can't wait to hear what he has to say about that, because I'm different now. Their words aren't going to defeat me, and neither are their actions. Let 'em say what they need to say. Let 'em do what they need to do, they're not going to contend with me in the ring. Not in my condition now... because I'm going to take that Extreme Title, and I'm going to keep it. Joey turns away from the mirror as if to avoid his own eyes, and speaks into the camera. And lastly, DDT... Shaman made a mistake using DDT's real name... Shamn says he isn't afraid of what DDT will do when you use his real name... but I know what DDT will do... Joey turns back to the mirror and a huge smile crosses his face. Because if Shaman is the extremist that he says he is, and he can do damage with his words... then he is in for a surprise. With DDT, the words that Shaman spoke will earn him a few nights in the intensive care unit. I know DDT. I know his moves. I know his tricks. I know his thoughts. So I have to think he knows me the same way. But if a few simple words are going to drive DDT to madness, then he is no token either. I'll defeat DDT... Again. Nothing's going to stand in my way this time. This title belonds to Joey Vendetti... and after I break every bone and after every shoulder is pinned to the mat, I will be holding that belt high above my head in victory. Joey starts to head towards the door of the apparent locker room, but before he makes it all the way out the door, he stops and turns his head, looking into the camera with a sadistic grin on his face. Robert F. Kennedy said that what is objectionable, what is dangerous, about extremists is not that they are extreme, but that they are intolerant. The evil is not what they say about their cause, but what they say about their opponents. So I have to ask you fellas.... Any last words? Joey smirks as he switches the lights off, and the scene is black.....
