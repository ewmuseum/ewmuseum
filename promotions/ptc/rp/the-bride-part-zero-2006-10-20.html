The Bride : Part Zero

20 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-20 The Bride : Part 0 - 1 - You don't even need to smile. Muscle-memory has sculpted your face into a visage of beaming pride. Rising with the sun, you synchronise yourself with the world around you. You provide lead vocals for the choir of early morning birdsong as you switch the radio on during the change-over to the breakfast show, then dance a fleet-footed, shivering jig as bare feet touch down on night-cooled tiles. A sudden onslaught of amber spotlights sends emerald eyes retreating behind their heavy lids, while routine maps your path around the breakfast bar. Without even noticing, you play hop-scotch on the tiles, skipping on tip-toes over the gaps. The news bulletin does its best to depress you with tales or torment and woe, capped of by forecasts of heavy rain to follow, but every gloomy thought is lead away by the chirpy, pied-piper’s whistle that you accompany the following song with. The rich fragrance of coffee begins to fill the kitchen, just a waft of the scent enough to explode your pupils and kick-start the synapses. The mechanical symphony of toaster, cutlery drawer, coffee machine, and their supporting cast of kitchen implements starts up, welcoming you into the role of conductor. That first cup of coffee reaches down into you, lighting the furnace. All remnants of sleep are then purged as you erupt into a feral yawn, spreading the buzz out into your extremities. You then light up, and it has nothing to do with caffeine. A soft pitter-patter of feet is heard scurrying down the hallway, and then the sun truly rises on your morning. "Morning, Mommy." - I - There is no God. There is no Goddess. You are the mistress of your own destiny. No divine intervention paves the way before you. You believe in yourself, totally, absolutely. Your brain pulls a cat‘s cradle of puppet strings, tightly wound, interconnecting. Beneath you, your board slashes your signature through the snow, scribbling out the tamer paths of those who’d been before you. The crisp, sapphire sky above ushers a sense of calm down the slopes, but the raucous cavalry charge of OTEP through your earphones threatens to trigger an avalanche in its wake. You read your destiny like a roadmap. Thinking 10 direction changes ahead. Even against a wash of powder white, you have a feel for every contour, at one with the mountain… a bond of trust between woman and nature founded on mutual respect and admiration. You accelerate as a cascade, every extra meter per second of speed giving you extra gambling chips to cash in for another. To slow, to show reservation, would be to doubt the very thing that drives you. Self-doubt would crumble your world. Your confidence keeps reality glued together. Nothing will go wrong. Nothing can go wrong. - 2 - The crystals in the air trap the exhaust fumes, leaving a saccharine, heady scent hanging where the car’s wheels skid in the ice slush. The sickly scent of petroleum bleeds into the cinnamon and cloves of mulled wine as, hand in hand in hand, you emerge from the side streets into an onslaught of unadulterated festive cheer. "Silent night," the carol singers begin to sing, their voices entwining into an angelic harmony, "holy night… all is calm, all is bright…" You’ve always loved Christmas. Even with your agnostic stance wiping some of the heavenly splendour away, the infectious holiday spirit got to you at a young age. Your gloved hands are held either side, to the left by your husband, to the right your daughter. Inseparable, all wrapped up tight and warm in scarves and winter coats, faces flushed red with chill. As you slalom through the crowds, you pass the glowing amber of the toy shop window. You see her eyes light up, creating a circuit through all three of you. She was you twenty years ago. Boots treading through the mushy snow underfoot, you all look up in wonder as another flurry of snow flitters out of the bruised black sky, tickling your noses. A rejoicing tribute of sleigh bells and extra jubilant carol singing heralds its arrival. "Round your virgin mother and child, holy infant so tender and mild." The crackle of roasting chestnuts acts as the lightning before the thunder, as a few seconds later their heightened smell floods the crisp air. "Sleep in heavenly peace…" He who watches over you shakes his snow globe as a swirling wind sends the tumbling blankets of flakes on an orbit around the halo of festive cheer that has encircled the Norwegian Christmas tree. It’s a scene straight off of a Christmas card, complete with Robin twittering down to perch on the Holly littered around the domineering tree’s base. It’s the Christmas that you used to dream of as a girl, hanging up your stocking, then drifting off to sleep when you could not longer keep that weary, peeking eye open. So lost in the child’s dream, you miss the herd of Reindeer dashing past out of the corner of your eye, hooves dancing across the frosty night sky. "…sleep in heavenly peace." - II - You were wrong? You‘re never wrong. …are you? - 3 - You were a little sceptical about re-incarnation, until it happened to you… without actually dying. Somehow, the soul of a loving, house proud mother was reborn into the body of a living, cynical Generation X’er. "Wants to be a hunter again," wafts around the living room, dancing on tip-toes across the haze of polish. "I want to see the world, alone, again…" You even listen to Dido. You even enjoy listening to Dido. You even SING ALONG to Dido. And you’re not even heavily medicated. "…to take a chance on like again," you accompany the CD with, in that hushed, half singing along, half lip-synching way. "So let me go, let me leave…" That, alone, should be conclusive proof of re-incarnation. Even the mundane tasks of housework are carried out with a potent blend of pride and gusto. Every attachment of your vacuum cleaner is used, even the brushy one. You beam with achievement as the second dusting of talcum powder, left overnight, is brushed away to reveal not so much as a hint of a red wine stain. You’ve become quite the domestic chemist, with your cocktail of alcohol, distilled water, and a splash of lemon to clean the mirror. You mix up a chaser of reddish shoe polish, darkened with a little iodine, to make the scratch on your cherry coffee table an afterthought. A vanilla scented candle stood in a little rose-water then purges the chemical tang. Strolling out into the hallway, you trail the duster behind you, letting its fluffy orange lap up the last specks of dusk from the door handle. It’s then that it catches your eye. You try to ignore it, but it nags. It worms its way into your thoughts, spreading until your brains itches to intensely that you have to scratch. You shuffle bills and bank statements to the back of the deck of the day’s post, until the joker you left at the bottom rears its goading head. Addressed to Miss Karina Wolfenden, you feel younger, lesser, just reading it. As if every letter along your name regresses you back through your life. The PTC stamp tries to pick the lock of memories past. But guarding the trophy chest of your glories are disappointments and failures. Like a deep, murky moat they flood all around your successes, dirtying them. Your security alarm, your insecurity, rings shrill, and the safety doors slam down, snapping you out of your thoughts. Within it, you know an offer of a GTT spot awaits. Within it, you know that folded piece of paper is forming a slippery slope. While it means glory to some, for you, it’s a snake rather than a ladder. Even to win it would mean you’d been defeated. A loss to yourself, your life, rather than a pinfall to an opponent. Hands grow heavy, envelopes become paving slabs. Fighting the subconscious forces, you return the invitation, still in its protective casing, to the back of the deck. "Junk mail." - III - You‘re plummeting at such a speed that the abrasive air peppers your skin, trying to scuff it raw. You go limp, deliberately, wisely. Better to bend than break. You wish you could say this hasn’t happened to you before. You wish you could say you’d never pushed through your limits before letting go. You’re in control of a body out of control. You control your breathing, gravity controls you. You choose when to open your eyes, momentum chooses how roughly to yank you down the slope. You decide whether to ball yourself up, the mountain decides your fate. You could paint this by numbers. After a few sharp bumps you go numb, senses blur, and you remember sensations of the womb. Trapped in the protective fluid of your tenderised body. Right on schedule your hearing begins to buzz. But… this isn’t how you remembered it the last time. As your ears ring, you’re still hurtling down the slope, rather than sliding to a halt in the powder’s buffers. And then you no longer feel the mountain below you… for a few, relenting seconds of weightlessness, before you feel your bones crush within your torso as you compact the snow beneath you, removing its cushion. "Ugm," you add to the English language, its meaning being ‘noise a young woman makes as her ribs and lung can no longer co-exist in the same torso without trying to stab each other’. You go limp. Not deliberately, not wisely. - 4 - Like pruning a plant, sometimes you have to cut yourself off to let someone grow in the right direction. "We should have home-schooled her," you mutter, mocking humour disguising the empty feeling, like a 3-day-old hunger that’s swelling the worry within you. He grips your hand that little bit tighter in reassurance, before flashing just that hint of a smile that betrays his alike feelings. All around you, parents suffer the same reservations, hesitant to release their offspring into the tide flowing through the school gates. "Seriously, honey," he quips, adopting your humorous defence mechanism. "If we lock her away in the basement with a couple of Encyclopaedias and a chalk-board, the neighbours will talk." It takes all of zero seconds for him to read that strained jokes won’t ease your fears. "It’s all part of growing up," he changes approach with, grounding words in reality. "New worlds, new dreams, new people." "I was never much of a people person," you continue to mask your fears by stating. "Or so I was always told… by people." The day only serves to put a further downer on your demeanour. Rather than skipping playfully off into the sunshine, she’s wrapped up warm in coat and scarf as a bitter wind taunts the late summer. The youthful, energetic chitter-chatter blankets the street. You suddenly feel old, and a little sorrow that your own memory of such a day has been forced out by the everyday bumf that accumulates as a 20-something. And then the moment comes. You crouch down, folding your 6 foot frame so that you’re face to face with Molly, your daughter. As you plant a motherly kiss on her forehead, your fingers tie knots around themselves as you go to hand over her rucksack. Anything to keep the bond, no matter the material. As a sheen on teary, motherly pride falls across your eyes, you straighten yourself back up as your husband, her father, prepares to release her into the wild of her schooldays. Sharing her mother’s awe and curiosity of the world, she’s eager to overdose her young brain on knowledge. The hard stuff. Nothing cut. A force of eager gravity pulls Molly towards the school gates, but your hand still holds hers. "Mommy," she then asks, wide-eyes turning from wonder to panic. "What’s wrong?" She shrieks, turning all eyes towards you. Your fingers crumble to dust in her gentle grip, while terror etches itself deep into the face of your greatest accomplishment. A collective gasp threatens to create a vacuum all around you. Panicking, you reach out with the remnants of a ruined hand, only for the earth to rupture in front of your daughter. The world splits into two, and then the sky falls, muffling your last words.. You don’t get to say goodbye. - IV - As your eyes open, the fluorescent blocks in the ceiling above scorch your retinas. Like a rabbit transfixed in the headlights, you can't even screw your eyes back up, slamming down the shutters of their lids. Like watching the Manhattan Project from miles and miles away, a single, fierce pin-prick of light spreads exponentially, obliterating the sense. But, with your sight pick-pocketed, its 4 allies step up their game. The soapy taste of bleach that saturates the air overcomes you, turning your mouth creamy. The harsh, sterile tang burns your nostrils. The shrill beeping of the heart-monitor brutally stabs away at your ear-drum. The fabric of the sheets grinds between your clenching fists as you violently gag on the breathing tube. Emerging from comatose stasis into the dynamic waking world, you're left behind as a flurry of activity erupts all around you. Colors and shapes slowly seep back into your vision, but motion-blur smudges them across your retina's blank canvas. Every slap of a sole down on the floor tiles resonates up through the bed, chiming the gong of your skull to send a splitting pain coursing through your brain. Every voice echoes eternally within your ear, bouncing back and forth until all your head is a constant, dull ringing. You're crying. They think it's your eyes watering from coughing the breathing tube up. They're wrong. A voice surges out though the dull murmur swamping your ears. "karinaKARINAKARINA?" you hear it ask, the sound splitting as if they need to twist your ear around to properly tune you in. Your arms shoot up, clasping at thin air. Knowing what's at stake, you persevere, trying to latch your grip onto the component's of the air itself, reaching out through the confines of the universe to drag yourself back towards your moment of bliss. Your moment of perfection. The embodied apotheosis of all you've ever wanted. Senses overloaded, you go numb, only feeling the taunting trickle of the tear down your cheek. You no longer feel your fingers as they brush desperately against one another, nothing to anchor you to the world you were awakened from. You screw your eyes up, praying to the God you've shunned since childhood that this is all just a nightmare, but as they burst back open, a probing light pierces through your vision. A tuft of pink fringe then drops over your face, the missing link that finally connects you back into the mainframe of the real world. You're you again, and you hate yourself for it. All you want is to curl up and cry, retreat into solitude to mourn what only you know has been lost in the blink of a tearful eye. Your audience watches on bemused as they go about their duties, wondering what nightmare you've emerged from… and you hate them just as much as yourself. You try to instigate that falling sensation that often jolted you from your dreams, but the bed is solid beneath you, barring an escape route. Your senses feel tainted being triggered by this world. The palette of colours seems dull and grainy, the smells and tastes rank and sour, the touch dirty, the sounds grating. You've gone from glorious Technicolor to drab grey, from sweet nectar to bitter sewage. You've been kicked out of heaven, and Saint Peter has slammed the pearly gates shut behind you. "No…" you mumble, throat still raw from the tube's rapid exit. You’re grieving. Starchy white sheets replace the traditional black of mourning. Decorative flowers from family replace the wreath. For what could have been a split second, or a lost decade, you’d lived the chorus of Enjoy the Silence. All you ever wanted, all you ever needed, was there, in your arms. You want it back. YOU. WANT. IT. BACK.
