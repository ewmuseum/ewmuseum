Eegor - IG72 - Monkey Removal

5 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It was a beautiful March day on the sandy shores of Barbados, and Eegor was relaxing in a manner to which he could very well become accustomed. The lobster lunch he had just polished off had been exquisite, and the rum punch that he was steadily working his way through was both deliciously sweet and packed full of the local spirit. Looking down at the gold belt that he carried almost everywhere, this was a time of reflection for the wrestler whose parents knew him as Paul Anthony Goodman. His was an exalted position; holding a coveted title belt without even being a member of a single wrestling organisation. Yet he knew that this could only go on so long. The reflected glory of his brother Chris, better known as Doctor Curiosity, could only take him so far. If he didn’t keep up his end of the deal, if he didn’t bring enough celebrity or glamour to the title belt, they’d soon find a way to take it off him. Having an underdog win a title is great for business; having them fade into obscurity with the belt is somewhat less so. And that was the reason why he was not the only person sitting on the beach, sipping on a brightly coloured beverage. “Do ve really need zis camera crew here vith us?” complained Doctor Curiosity. “Yes we do, Chris, and you can take your ridiculous costume off now. We’re relaxing.” The former GTT winner eyed his brother suspiciously, not best pleased with the recent developments in their relationship. When he’d fixed it for Eegor to get a chance to prove himself in the Infinite Gauntlet, it hadn’t crossed his mind that his brother might actually win. “Nein! I alvays dress like zis!” “Have it your way,” sighed Eegor, rolling his eyes at the man dressed in a Harris Tweed suit and laboratory coat despite the almost oppressive heat of the Sun. “My vay vould be you getting back to your usual job of serving me! Jah!” “Well, as long as I have this title, that’s not happening. We agreed on that, yes?” “Jah.” “And do you need to keep up the German accent?” “Jah! I am German!” replied the Doctor, looking around nervously at the camera crew who were filming their every move. “If you say so.” Eegor got to his feet, with only a slight misstep due to his current level of inebriation. He turned to face the camera, and addressed it head on. “Hi there, people of PTC. I’m your Infinite Gauntlet champion, and I’m just trying to keep you in the loop about what I’ve been doing since I won the title from August Monday. Whilst it may look as if I’ve let it all go to my head and am taking it all for granted, please be assured that is not the case. I’m very much looking forwards to facing off against Baron von Blackberry and defending my title successfully. So why am I here, in Barbados? Well, I think we all need a little down time on occasion, and while I’m mulling over which federation I might join and which wrestlers I’d most like to compete against, why not go somewhere with good scenery? Let’s face it, nothing irons out the kinks in your muscles like a good massage and a relaxing few hours in the Sun.” Eegor smiled. “So, PTC, now you know what I like to do in my time off, and where I like to vacation... but there’s an awful lot more about me that you don’t know, but by the end of this week I think you’ll all be up to speed, and then if I do choose a place to wrestle on a regular basis, everyone won’t be starting from scratch.” The former doctor’s assistant gave his most charming smile to the camera, and then signalled for them to stop rolling. “How’d you think it went?” he asked of the cameraman. “Not bad. You seem pretty natural in front of the camera.” “Of course he does,” interjected Doctor Curiosity, his accent abandoned as soon as the cameras had finished rolling. “He learned from a true master!” “And you are?” asked the cameraman. “And I am? And I am?” spluttered the doctor, indignant. “If it wasn’t for the fact that it would spill my mojito, I would doubtless hit you with the Schrodinger’s Smack and leave you gurgling face down in the seaweed!” “The Shoe Flinger’s What?” “That’s it! Don’t try to hold me back, Eegor! I’m going to kill this man! I said don’t hold me back!” “Your protests would be a little more effective if you’d actually moved at all,” replied Eegor. “Ah, I can’t be bothered. This rum is goooood...” *** A week passed. Eegor trained, relaxed, and told the world about himself. Despite his brother’s best efforts, there was no great drama, which just goes to show that sometimes even professional wrestlers on the cusp of the biggest match of their career need not have a massive personal trauma just days before they’re due to wrestle. In many ways, the brothers continued to bond. Since the doctor’s NFW schedule had slowed down, and he had agreed to vouch for Eegor to PTC officials, it was almost like the roles had been reversed over their usual time spent together. Eegor was the dominant half of the partnership, and though the pressure and responsibility sat a little uneasily on his shoulders, he felt that he was beginning to grow into it, and wanted to keep things that way, at least for a while. Doctor Curiosity, on the other hand, was now cast in the role of Eegor’s assistant, and he was really surprised to find that he didn’t actually mind doing it. The years of his brother’s servitude had brought them together, and despite his regular protests, he felt that he was, in his own way, helping to make amends. So as Eegor trained, swam, relaxed and talked to camera, his brother made restaurant reservations, booked rooms and packed all their stuff away. The time soon came, far too soon in their opinion, to catch a flight out from Barbados and back to reality, to see if Paul Goodman could defend his Infinite Gauntlet title. It was a pleasant journey, with little turbulence, and it was with a spring in his step that Eegor passed through airport security and let his brother collect the bags. Waiting for him was a camera crew, and the request for one last promo to hype up the title defence. Eegor was not going to disappoint them. “Baron von Blackberry may think that he can win this title. He may think that he can walk all over me, intimidate me and use his guile and experience to become Infinite Gauntlet champion, but I’m not going to let him. I’ve earned this belt. I’ve put hours and hours of effort in the gym and years of effort to even be allowed to step into the ring as my own man. So don’t think you’re going to be facing a fresh faced rookie, Baron, because I’ll be ready for every trick in your book, and I will not let this title belt be taken away from me.” “Ummmm, Eegor...” said the doctor, appearing and trying to interrupt. “Not now, Chris! This title belt means everything to me. It’s a symbol of what I have achieved; it’s something tangible, something I can look at and know that I belong in your company.” “Eegor, I need to...” “I can look at my reflection in the polished gold and see not just my face, but those who have gone before me,” continued Eegor, paying his brother no heed. “People like Jason Snow, Jacob McKail, Rich Rollins and The Uber Judge. This title is so steeped in history but it’s also my future, and I care more about it that just about any other material thing in the world.” “Vhich is vhy...” “What? C’mon... you’re not even going to let me cut a promo on Baron von Blackberry?” “Jah, but...” “But what?” “I kind of left ze title belt in ze hotel.” “You did WHAT?” “I vas packing ze suitcases, and I placed ze belt in your case, but zen decided zat it should go on top, not at ze bottom. Vell, zen I saw your laundry, and...” “I don’t care!” bellowed Eegor. “Where the hell is the belt? Barbados?” “Ummm... jah.” “Oh great, my first title defence and I’m not even going to have a belt to defend. Maybe all I just said wasn’t true. Maybe I’m just a sideshow, someone to give a quick feelgood win before they get the title back on the real wrestlers. I mean, how stupid am I going to look walking down to the ring without the strap?” Doctor Curiosity blinked in the face of his brother’s anger. “I...” “You what?” demanded Eegor. “I can go back and get it. Jah! If I get ze return flight now, and a ticket for ze flight back here, I can make it just in time for ze match!” “Are you sure?” “I tzink so,” said the doctor. “Chris. I’ve got a lot riding on this match. Nobody might even want to sign me if I crash and burn so quickly. Can I count on you?” Curiosity looked to his right, and winced as he saw the camera crew still filming. “Yes, Paul. You can count on me.” “Thank you. Jesus, I couldn’t stand losing my belt and not even being able to hand it over to the next guy.” “Don’t worry, my brother. I’ll be back in time, and I have a feeling that you’ll not be handing over that title any time soon.” Eegor laughed. “I hope you’re right.” “Oh,” replied the doctor. “I know I am.” With that he was gone, turned around and racing to the desk to sort out the necessary flights. Paul Goodman could only laugh as he watched the madcap sprint of the man in the laboratory coat; a maelstrom of activity miraculously avoiding all those that stood between him and his goal. And yet, there he was, working for Eegor. Maybe things would be okay, and Baron von Blackberry would be vanquished. Yeah. Perhaps Curiosity was right. After all, who else in the world had as much experience dealing with a wrestling mad scientist?
