Sympathy For The Demon

22 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-22 In his career, he had seen all manner of brutal and unsettling things, both inside the ring and outside, during his “extracurricular” activities, but nothing could prepare him for the sight he found himself looking at right now. It was obvious that what had happened in this once quiet motel room had been nothing short of a massacre. Despite what his gut was telling him, that this room was full of bad omens, he stayed true to his course, climbing in through the carefully and quietly opened window. In his imagination, he pictured a scene where he stepped down, treading a piece of viscera into the carpet with an horrific squelch, but he was thankful that the crime scene investigators had removed all but the traces of what had happened. However, as he stepped through the open frame and down, closing the window quietly behind him, he knew in his heart that whilst they had removed all the physical evidence and remains, this room was still full for someone who had the misfortune of knowing where to look. With the skill of a burglar but none of the intent, he moves across the room to the door, listening in for any signs of life outside. All he can hear is the occasional squeak of cheap motel furniture mixed with the sighs and the movements of the two policemen guarding the other side of the door. They had not heard him enter, and he was grateful, he was not in the mood to deal with the law right now. Moving away, his eyes dart around the room, the glow of the street lights outside showing him enough for his needs. Despite his need to keep quiet, he can’t help but whisper to himself. “God …” Whether that was a statement unto himself or a question aimed at the man upstairs, only he knows. He kneels down, one hand resting on the floor, the other running slowly over a patch of dried blood, as if hoping to feel what had happened. In the dim light he was working, he could see that the whole room was covered in similar patches, coagulated blood on the floor, arterial spray across the walls, smears made by what were once hands and faces, he did not realise but from the moment he had entered the room, he had been crying. A truck drives past outside, the intense headlights of the big rig filling the room with a momentary blinding glow, and as he raises his arm to shield his eyes, he feels the tears, slowly wiping them away as a sad smile creeps across his face. Regaining his composure, he stands up slowly, looking around again for any sign that may have been left for him, be it obvious or not. Even in this dim light, he looks intently, searching for that one little piece that he needs, that one little clue that would, no, should have been left for him to find. The police would have overlooked it, he is sure of that, forensics wouldn’t have been interested in it, he hopes it will be here, specifically for him. Moving as quietly as he can, he moves towards the small en suite bathroom. He thinks to himself how this room is no bigger than a closet, with just a shower, toilet and washbasin, but even in the dim light, he can still see that the massacre was not confined to the main room. There are several head sized smashes in the wall mirror and even the tiled walls of the shower are smashed in places, the sheer force involved staggering. This room holds no signs, no clues for him, only the knowledge that whoever died in this room suffered as badly as a human being could suffer. Resting his back against the wall, he slides down, his knees controlling his descent, his breath leaving his body in a controlled but emotional fashion. Trying to separate himself from the violence, the sheer gut wrenching horror that this room contained was draining even his tainted aura. Another big rig goes past the window, illuminating the room, and as he kneels slumped against his cheap wooden resting place, he tries to focus his mind, tune his thoughts to the scene, to the victims, to try and find that which was left, but the more he tries to focus, the more his mind wanders, remembering insignificant details, pointless memories, things that can only lead to more suffering in this already haunted room. Suddenly, his pause for thought is interrupted by the noise of radios outside the room. He cannot ascertain what the radios are saying but he is preparing to take flight, should the door open by even a hairs breadth. He holds his breath, waiting for what is going to happen, but nothing does, his heart racing for nothing. The officers outside the door stand still and fast, not making any kid of sound or vibration that would be detected inside the room. It’s only then that he realises that not only can he not sense anything from the police outside, but he can no longer hear anything from anywhere else, not a creaking bed or a footfall, not a car in the distance or the wind in the trees. Slowly, he stands, twisting his head from side to side, trying to notice anything, any sign of movement, but he can notice none. His eyes widen as he realises, and he jumps across to the window, losing any essence of stealth, one foot bounding off the bed as he does so, moving as swiftly as he can, but as he opens the curtains, he sees that the world outside has stopped. “How in the Hell…” As shocking as it may be to look outside of the window and find that the world has stopped, from the smallest gust of wind to the mightiest Mack truck, more of a shock came from the voice from behind that answered his own internal rhetorical question. “Now that you mention it, Hell did have something to do with it.” Looking up into the window itself, he freezes as he sees the man in the reflection, standing in the bathroom doorway that he had been resting in not thirty seconds before. He wonders how this figure, this apparition, can stand there, adjusting the Windsor knot on an expensive looking tie, flattening any minor imperfection in his slicked back hair, as though appearing out of nowhere was an every day occurrence. Puzzled, he slowly turns around, trying to seamlessly shift from looking at the reflection to looking at the man himself, or itself, hoping that in that split second his eyes leave contact, he will remain. “Oh come on, you think I went to all the trouble of creating this little bubble of time if I was just going to up and leave, I figured on you being smarter than that.” Now he didn’t know what was more disconcerting, the fact that the being before him had the power to do such a thing, or that his thoughts where no longer private. Looking straight ahead, he tries to assess the man, trying to place him, who he is, where he comes from, but something about him makes that impossible, he doesn’t seem to be there, to be real, despite standing not ten feet away. He moves to take a step forward, his long legs stretching out towards the mysterious being, but this is soon halted as the suited apparition raises his hand only slightly, but generating an invisible force that sends him flying backwards into the cheaply upholstered chair in the corner. “I’m sorry, but I have to insist on a little physical distance, no offence intended.” If this was some sort of mystical attempt at comedy, it wasn’t working, for as he sat in the chair, winded and aching from the impact, now more than ever, he had the desire to punch the being square in the well groomed face. But, despite his growing feelings of anger, he knows that for whatever reason this thing has appeared, a fistfight is not it. So instead, he speaks his first words to another being since he learnt of the massacre some two days ago. “Just who are you?” Not the most poetic of words, he had to admit, but right now, he wanted answers, not riddles, but he saw in his adversaries reaction that maybe riddles was what he would have to decipher. Smoothing over his apparently freshly shaved face like a Gillette model, he, or rather it, smiles, a cockily handsome grin. “Who am I? Now that, son, is the question. I could give you quite a few answers, ranging from some that contain words such as destroyer, harbinger, annihilator, whereas another answer may well be that I am just a servant, a man doing a job, but all you need to know son, is that I am your boss, your owner, your own personal lord and master, how’s that for an answer.” Most people upon being told this by a complete stranger would be shocked, perhaps a little angry that they were being treated like property, but to him, none of those went through his head. All he could think was, as unlikely as this may sound, sheer confusion. “I think, for all your apparent gifts, that you have be confused with someone else, I’m owned by no one. The problem, you see, is that he has never been much of a speaker, much preferring to do his talking with his actions, so to him, that sounded beyond terrible. “On the contrary, son, I am in no way confused, I know just who you are, and I definitely know that I own you, body and, quite literally, soul. And I wonder, who do you have to thank for this little arrangement?” In his mind, he knows the answer, but he dares not say it or bring it up, because he knows of the pain said answer would bring. “Why would they?” Another cocky smile, but this one is more sinister. The man turns away, admiring his reflection in the smashed mirror. “If you want to know the whole story, you had best first tell me, who were these people, or rather, who did you believe they were.” He knew this was coming, he knew that he was going to have his own past, his own beliefs shattered by this answer, and it was obvious that the being in front of him took great pleasure in this. “They are, or they were, kind, gentle people who worked to help, to educate others. That is who they were, that is their legacy, don’t try and taint it. He knew he had lost, the false anger had no real power behind it, and he could see as the being smoothed down his hair again, that he was about to receive the news he did want to get. “Let me start from the very beginning, which as the song goes, is a very good place to start. Let’s say that, thirty five years ago, a happily married couple, one a historical librarian, one a teacher, were living a happy, if boring existence, just the two of them. And let’s say, for arguments sake, that one of them discovered a certain secret about the way everyone’s fate lies, how everyone’s fate is worked out. And, for more arguments sake, let’s say that the found a way they could change their destiny. These happily married people, normal, plain, approached a being such as, say, myself, and made a deal.” Shaking his head, he couldn’t listen to anymore about these people right now, he had to hear something different. “How do our fates lie, how are they all worked out?” He hoped it had worked, the momentary distraction to change the subject for at least a minute. “Oh very well, if you must interrupt. You see, everyone has this tendency to believe that they can change their fates, that they can choose whether to be good and find peace and happiness in the afterlife, or whether they can choose the path of evil and damn themselves to a life of boiling flesh and razor sharp pitchforks, but the secret is something a little more disturbing. You see, from the moment that “daddy’s seed” reaches “mummy’s egg” and makes a “little bundle of joy”, that little bundles fate is assigned. Even as an embryo, everyone who has ever been born or ever will be is marked, whether to be good and live a life in the company of the man upstairs, or whether to be a son of a bitch and burn for eternity. You can’t stop it, you can’t control it, there is no way of changing what is marked. Well, there is one way that can work, and that is the way that this happily married couple found out about.” Confusion and sadness was now being replaced by curiosity. “And they came to you, this couple, to change their fates, to escape from where they were going, Hell?” A truly mocking laughter fills the room, as the being before him throws his head back, guffawing for no one to hear, until he snaps his head straight back upright again. “Oh no, you have it all a little backwards. You see, being good and kind and going to Heaven is all well and good, but as you can imagine, a life of purity can be so damn boring. You can drink and do drugs all you want on this mortal plain but the moment you are about to die, something will happen to send you up to the clouds above, whereas I have known pillars of the community cry as they are torn apart down below because in their dying moments, one stray thought sent them that way. No, you see, this happily married, boring couple, came to me and offered a trade. They didn’t want to be boring anymore, they didn’t want to be good, so they offered me three Heaven bound souls in return for a life of hedonism and sin, and who was I to say no to such a good price.” He rubs his temples slowly, as if trying to digest all this. “How could they offer three souls when there were only two of them.” The being before him does nothing but shake his head and tut with a lack of sincerity. “Oh and I thought you were bright. You see, they knew, through what they had found, that any child they had would be heaven bound, and so, in all their wonderful self obsessed glory, they offered me their souls and that of any children they would have, in trade for a life of pure evil. And yes, before you ask, I took it. However, they tried to trick me. You see, the woman, she was as barren as could be, and they thought they could hide this from me. But when I took their souls, I gave her that which was lost, I gave her the chance to make life, and lo and behold, years later, she became with child. And you can imagine their reaction. Amongst all the drugs, amongst all the sex and the hedonism, they had the third soul to offer me. And that was when the fun REALLY started, believe you me.” Agitated, he fidgets in his chair. Trying to take all this in without exploding was proving difficult. “Once they had their child, they could have reneged on the deal, tried to cancel, but no, they were having too much fun, so what they did, was they handed that baby off to her elderly mother and they went on with their life, living to excess, running from the future. And that baby was named by that superstitious old woman …” He closes his eyes. “Kashiel.” The being claps his hands and yelps out loud. “Got it in one kid, she named you after an angel to try and protect you, and you know what, it actually worked. I couldn’t claim you. Lord knows I tried, I tried very hard to claim you, but I couldn’t. So that was why, after these thirty long years, I sent some basement level monsters to wait for that happily married couple in this room, for them to return full of cheap alcohol and even cheaper drugs, and, well, you thought the word yourself, it was a massacre. I don’t know if you have ever see a human ribcage pulled out through the skin but it is a truly invigorating experience. Verily, they did indeed…” The being doesn’t get a chance to finish as finally, anger wins out and he flies out of his chair, seizing the being by the throat and slamming him against the nearest wall. Through strangled throat, he still manages to speak. “Oh I have waited for this moment, when I truly own you Kashiel. Why do you think you have done so well in the ring, why do you think you have become such an expert in that, albeit fake, occult that you practice. That was my influence, and who do you think is responsible for that burning urge to get back into the ring, that’s me. I own you, I have owned you since you were sperm and egg, and soon, I am going to claim you, just as I claimed these miserable souls in this room. And when I claim you, when I come myself to collect you, you can see just how your parents died, over and over in your own little personal corner of Hell.” And in a blink, he is gone. No hair, no grin, no attitude. The strength leaves Kashiel’s arm and it drops down. He looks around the room one last time, realising that the clue that he was searching for was left for him all along. Not in a symbol or in a last gasp effort to make a sign while being torn to shreds, his sign was in and of himself, he was the sign, and with the information he now had, all he could do was find a way to fight it. He knew what he had to do, as he stood in a motel room that was caked in the blood and visceral stains of his own parents. He knew where he had to go and who he had to speak to. In his head and in his heart, he knew what had to be done, and whether his soul was marked for Hell or not, he knew, he just knew. And it was that thought that lingered in his mind as the door flew open, two policemen training their weapons at him as he goes flying through the glass window and rolling through into a sprint outside. In the past, the man called Kash would have hidden, would have folded under this, but not now, not now he knew what he had to go and do. Kash might have been weak, a coward, but Kashiel, he had a mission now, and wherever that took him, whatever he had to do along the way, be it in the ring or not, Kashiel would be strong, Kashiel would be brave, Kashiel would be ready. Fighting was what he had been born for, he knew that now, and whoever he faced off against now would see a different side to Kashiel. A freak no more, focused to the death. Let the hounds of Hell chase me, he thought to himself, I’ll run them into ground and then turn them against their master. And so God or Satan help me, I’ll do the same to anyone who gets in my way. He may own my soul, but my body is free to do damage, that much he is sure of as he sprints off into the night, focused for the first time in years. TBC…
