Lonely Soldier…Go Home

19 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-19 Lonely Soldier…Go Home. It isn’t fair. He shouldn’t have asked this of him. He should never have brought himself to write a letter after all this years, address it to O’Shea residence so the postman could deliver it the Paddy shack, in the back of fuck knows where. The selfish bastard thought he could pass the guilt of the fact he was going to be executed onto Paddy? Thought he would show up and cry a little for him? Hell no! Where were you five years ago for Christmas dinner? Where were you ten years ago for his birthday? What happened to the promises of comrades forever? What happened to the oaths? They had fought together, bled together, killed together. They were the personification of inseparable. That was until he walked away, just sold his house and up and left. Paddy wrote a few times in spite of his ‘friend’s’ ignorance in leaving with barely a handshake goodbye. His ‘good friend’ had been so decent enough to return to sender. And now, after all these years, he wants Paddy to take some of his valuable time from his life just to ease the man’s passing? Pfft, you can go to hell mo chara. The day you decided you were too good for Paddy O’Shea was the day you made your own vasectomy, you’re not a friend, you’re not a chara, you’re not a comrade, a pal, buddy, companion, mate, chum. Consider the links cut. You walked out of his life, and this is a one way street hoss so just keep on truckin’ the way you were heading. I mean, even if Paddy did look past the fact this man has the audacity to request a visit just because he’s in the queue, there’s other issues to consider. Firstly (and coincidentally, lastly) – Paddy is a hit. He’s the Uber beater. He’s hot, he’s fresh, he’s new. To all those PCW marks out there, he’s a blast from the past, the guy who hinted to you with his potential then teased you by fading back into the shadows. Well, the sun is high overhead, and the sky is cloudless. The shadows of yore have cleared and look whose slipped back out. He’s knocking on the door of superstardom. Knock knock knock let him the fuck in. The mighty Uber Judge, the once proud bearer of the Infinite Gauntlet Champion title, laid down his own gauntlet; one the PTC Global Champion had twice been unable to come through, and by God, Paddy O’Shea did it. The people at the start, they didn’t know him, they didn’t give him a hope in hell. Uber versus who? An Irishman? Paddy O’What? Funny how things can change. He is the Uber Slayer and nothing will hold him back! Except…Tony Mullen… Like I said, he shouldn’t have asked this of him. I can see it now: ‘Paddy O’Shea, convict sympathiser’, ‘Death Row regular Paddy O’Shea’, ‘PTC cuts links with Paddy O’Shea’. Ahem. No thank you. Keep your goddamn headlines, Paddy O’Shea won’t be adding any fuel to that fire. So thanks for thinking of me, sparing a few of your last moments alive to write to me, asking me how I was doing and you wouldn’t mind throwing away your reputation for me like I did our friendship. Yours grovellingly Tony Mullen. Thanks for that, but no thanks. You reap what you sow, I’m reaping fame and you’re reaping…well actually somebody named Grim is reaping you but that’s neither here nor there. Ta-ta love. Send me a postcard. … You’ve changed you know… What? Who said that? I’m not talking to you. You’re everything that’s wrong with Paddy O’Shea. You’re like a cancer tearing apart what was once a good man. Well it’s time we balanced things up. Time for Paddy to formulate his OWN decisions, not with you turning him into a hateful bastard. Paddy… I’m your conscience and it’s time to get moral here. Oh God…I thought we lost you after the whole Amsterdam issue… Silence. Paddy, it’s time to cut the crap. This new attitude of yours, this ego, it’s NOT YOU! You were happy before! When you were winning matches, content to be making a living! Now, some magazines- A legion of magazines. Fine, a legion of magazines. Now that these magazines are telling you could be still in this tournament come the final, you think you’re a hotshot. These magazines, my God, I think they’ve created a monster. Cocky, arrogant, flashy. What happened to the Paddy the fans loved? You love the fans don’t you? You live for them. So why are you turning your back on them? That’s beside the point! God, why does the conscience that lives next door to ME have to be a fruit? Look Paddy, who cares if you lose a few fans? Hell, it will benefit you in the long run! It’s about time people stopped saying you only win because of their support! This is your game Paddy; you have the control panel in your hand. You win because of you. Not because of them! It’s just as bad with the situation for goddamn Tony Mullen! He’s thinks he can saunter back into your life? Fuck that! Fuck things holding you back! Time to walk away form hangers on like Tony Mullen, time to forget about the fans. Time to do things by YOU! That’s bullshit! You know Tony Mullen was never a moocher! You were best friends! He saved your life, lest you forget about that! Think back Paddy. Do you remember 1989? Do you remember the IRA? You were young and innocent, you thought you could walk on water back then. You thought killing was a buzz, thought the girls would flock to a guy with a gun! And what happened when you were pinned down by soldiers? Who was it that took two bullets for you so you could escape? Who went to jail for you? Who did four years in jail for you, refusing to give your name? That was loyalty. That was love. He gave four years so you could be free. Do you remember what he wrote to you, two weeks after being sent down? Er…no. I wasn’t talking to you! Paddy, it said "Here’s to the wings of a bird". He loved freedom…he fought for freedom if that makes any sense…technically, you took it from him. He didn’t have to be the hero, but the bond you’d made with him sentenced him to do so. He was the perfect friend. And did you visit him? No! Did you write to him? No! He didn’t ask. He didn’t have to ask! And now he wants a few hours out of your fucking life, the one you wouldn’t have if it wasn’t for him, so you can save face over a wrestling career?! Fuck you Paddy! You’ve changed you selfish bastard. No wonder he walked away without a word, who would stay? FOUR YEARS! And you didn’t even thank him! You thought you were so great with your gun, like you were some kind of Billy the Kidd! He had to act mature for both of you! You didn’t even believe in the cause, you didn’t care about freedom. Now at sixteen. And now you take this high and mighty stance? Fuck you! The Uber judge was a fluke! Yeah, that’s right, and I can’t lie, I’m you goddamnit. It was a fluke. You didn’t earn it. He struggled for a year to get to that tournament, forcing a name for himself. And you think you can just walk in demanding respect. Go to hell, you self-seeking prick. You’re not worth the steam off my piss. You’re a state of mind, you can’t piss. Shut up! I’m not done because there’s a few other things I want to say about this new attitude of yours. Jason Warr- Pfft. Is better than you as well…because he’s struggled through shit too. He’s young and fast. And he deserves it more than you. And why? Because he’s a good man. And when he swore to never forget who he was the day he entered HSW, he sure as hell meant it. You…well, I don’t even recognise you anymore. If I could lease this property, I’d get the hell out right now… Oh brilliant, kill Paddy’s self esteem, good job mate. He might as well find a bridge now because in your book, he’s so far from the path he can’t- Wait…Paddy…you can redeem yourself… Fuck that! Don’t change him! He’s good now, he’s winning! He won before. Only thing is, this time he’s also a heartless prick. Paddy, you can win this as a bastard or you can win this as a good man. Being the good man helped you beat Uber. No…it wasn’t a fluke. You deserved it. You said you can’t lie! I lied. Damn…one of those mind jumbler things! They hurt my brain. Yeah, go into the corner or something, you barking in my ear isn’t doing anyone any fucking favours. Now as I was saying…you did deserve it. But I have to bring you down Paddy, I need to clear this rot, break you down before I can build you up again. Please Paddy. Do the right thing…be the man that you are. Me, I’m not going anywhere, but at the same time, I can’t make you do anything. All I can do is beg you to listen to the voice of reason. Be a good man. Be different. Only then, can you beat Jason Warr. Hey! Did you pee on the toilet seat? Wow…I have so much to look forward to. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Paddy O’Shea, hunched over in a Rent-a-car Chevrolet he’d picked up at the airport, drove onto the sobering and foreboding high-walled grounds of Central Prison, El Paso, Texas. Almost as soon as he had driven through the retractable gate, he’d felt uneasy. He felt eyes watching him. Some were to protect him, he sensed, many others had a different intent. Paddy drove up the small path leading to the actual prison housing, pulled into the small lot, and parked it. He got out and found himself gazing at the security housing unit. It’s like a prison in a prison… It was surrounded by razor sharp wire fences and deadly electronic barriers. Armed guards, equipped with batons and guns, strolled around purposefully whilst others sat in watchtowers. This was one of many Death Rows in Texas. Funnily enough, Paddy decided there and then he wouldn’t be going out of his way to admire the others. "Scary," Paddy whispered without noticing. His eyes were fixed on those windows, lining the building in a linear fashion. From his angle, they looked black, like big dead eyes. As dead as the men inside anyway. Unsettled and unhappy, Paddy shook the feeling off and made his way to the entrance of the building. Pushing a reinforced glass door open, he entered a small waiting area; a reception was seen through a glass panel to the right hand side. "Hello?" Paddy paced over to the reception and rapped on the glass. Inside he could see one small desk, completely devoid of papers, with a computer to one side. There was very little else that stood out from the room, the colour of lark both unimpressive and depressing. There was no answer, so Paddy rapped again. "Yes?" The voice came with a hint of static to his left. His head arched over in its direction to see an intercom. "Er…Aye’m Paddy O’Shea. Here t’ visit a Tony Mullen? Aye phoned ye’s last night?" "Just a second please." There was silence for what seemed like half a minute until the woman’s voice returned once more. It was glum he noticed. Who wouldn’t be? You’d have to be able to sum your personality up in twenty words or less to work in a place like this. "Yes, you’re expected. Just wait here please for the guards to come and escort you."! Guards? A thought suddenly appeared in his mind, one he hadn’t thought about before. Even when his mind was jostling between whether he should go or not, he’d completely bypassed the most relevant of questions…what exactly did Tony Mullen do to wind up here? He’s killed before, yes sir, but that was so many years ago and it was through the war in Ireland. If he was in Death Row, he must have killed someone here, in the US, out of war. He shouldn’t have asked this of him…this is too far…too much…too quick. Then two muscle bound guards emerged from a code locked door and it was suddenly too late to turn back. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Once Paddy walked through the door connecting the lobby to the cells, a blind fear gripped his heart. Blind because he couldn’t see anything, he didn’t know what there was to be afraid of; fear because the high level of security that continued with this walk urged him to be wary. Paddy was asked to wait behind to sets of steel bar doors whilst documents were processed. He was subjected to a metal detector, then had to present photographic ID. One of the guards cheerfully informed him that the ‘El Paso Rocks!’ bumper stickers were made here too. Er, thanks for that. Paddy made a mental note never to buy another bumper sticker. There were hundreds of controlled steel gates in the high security prison, Inmates couldn’t move outside their cells without handcuffs, leg irons and security guards. On the actual Death Row, each block consisted of sixteen cells, eight on the bottom, eight on the top. Yet again, everything here was painted the colour of lark. Paddy thought he would scream if he didn’t get that nauseating shade out of his mind. Paddy was led on past these cells and onto a small corridor. "Paddy…" The voice was that of Tony Mullen’s. He had seen Paddy standing in the narrow corridor outside a special hearing room. The door was lying open and Tony was peering out. Paddy looked at a man he hadn’t seen in ten years and almost didn’t recognise him. He had crows’ feet around his eyes, ones which would emerge when he laughed (trenches that would never emerge again). His face was lined with scars, his eyes were tired. His hair was laced with grey and receding at the corners. He had changed so much. Tony began to get up from the other side of the table where he sat, an ignorant frown from Paddy made him sit back down. He was labelled now, the orange-red jumpsuit, nailing in the fact he was ready for death, if nothing else did. "Aye’m glad ye came Paddy…yer the only person tha’ came…aye thank ye fer tha’." Paddy sucked in a breath but tried not to show it. He sat down on the seat opposite Tony and tried to avoid looking at him although he knew fine rightly Tony was boring a hole into his head. "How are ye Paddy. How’s the village? The people, how are they?…Paddy?" "They’re fine…jus’ fine," Paddy mumbled, agitated already at the fact Tony was trying to make friendly after all this time. A silence followed which Paddy found difficult to bear. He didn’t want to appear weak to this man, one who had been such a strength in the past. His mere presence was daunting and unwelcome. The past should have been left in the past. He shouldn’t have asked this of him… Tony sighed. "Aye want ye t’ know tha’ ye comin’ is the best thing that’s happened t’ me in a long time…best thing in the two years aye’ve been waitin’ in here fer the hangman anyway." Paddy didn’t stop to think if the man’s words were sincere. Ten years Tony. Ten years. Paddy sniffed, and looked away. "Aye…" "Why did ye ask me t’ come here. After all this time. Why?" Tony looked up so that he was now staring directly into the lightning blue eyes of Paddy O’Shea. O’Shea was intense, a lone index finger tapping the desk at a half beat rhythm. Tony seemed to fold for a second, and then regained himself before sitting up straight in his seat. His voice croaked in his throat when he spoke. "Paddy…aye…well, it’s two things really…" Tony licked his lips and rubbed his hands nervously. "Aye need t’ right me wrongs before aye go…and aye need to explain why aye disappeared without word. Paddy…when aye went t’ prison, everything aye ever held dear was ripped away from me. Mefamily, me friends. And fer a long time…aye blamed ye." "Me?" Paddy was suddenly interested again. He banged his hands forcefully down onto the table and seemed nigh on leaving until Tony held up a wavering hand to calm him. He didn’t look like the tough ass commander that had taught Paddy how to fire his first shot anymore. He just seemed exhausted. "Just listen t’ me Paddy…hear me out please…" Paddy rolled his eyes then crossed his arms, indicating he would indeed stay. "Aye hated ye because…well…aye took those bullets fer ye while ye escaped. Aye was the commander, there should have been no bitterness on my part when aye was sent away. As a member of the IRA, aye did wha’ aye was expected t’ do. Aye had t’ sacrifice. Aye should have done me time content in the knowledge aye’d served me country well…" Tony scanned the bland walls for an answer. There was a few gaping apertures in the wall signifying the memory of past struggles inside these confines. There are no answer here though Tony, so hurry up and spit it out. "Paddy…aye hated ye…so so much. Aye needed an outlet and the image of yer face laughin’ at me was too much t’ bear. Many times aye felt like handin’ yer name an’ yer address over out of spite. But aye didn’t. Aye held true an’ kept me feelings t’ meself…yes Paddy, aye took a bullet fer ye an’ yes ye did thank me when aye came out. Ye didn’t visit me an’ tha’ was wise; ye visitin’ would have made the peelers suspicious. Back then though, aye thought it was plain ignorance on yer part." "So wha’? Ye moved away because ye hated me?" "No! Aye didn’t hate ye…well aye did, but at the same time, aye still loved ye with everything aye had. Aye was so confused. When aye seen ye in the bar the day aye got out, aye wanted to hug ye but at the same time strangle ye. Aye was so fucked up, so unstable, aye had t’ move away fer yer and my safety. Aye didn’t want t’ keep in contact by letter or anythin’ because as far as aye was concerned, keepin’ ye dead t’ me was the only way o’ keepin’ ye alive…" Tony paused and seemed on the verge of breaking down. He was doing all he could to gain an ounce of understanding from Paddy. His eyes were bulging, staring at Paddy, willing him to say ‘I understand’. He needed to right wrongs; he needed to erase mistakes of the past. He had to, before he met the man upstairs. Paddy exhaled air slowly and looked away. The story seemed right but he didn’t want to believe it. Sometimes hating somebody is so much easier than loving them and forgiving them. "Tony…wha’ did ye get sent here fer?" Tony put his head in his hands. They muffled his voice as he rocked backwards and forwards. "Aye don’t know….aye got int’ some bad shit over here. Aye was on coke, crack, Jesus anythin’ aye could get me hands on. One night was particularly bad. Aye thought aye passed out, probably did after it happened." "Wha’ happened?" "All aye kno’ is, the morning after, aye’m lying in me living room with a bloody, dead girl in me arms. Five minutes later, while aye was still tryin’ to figure out wha’ the hell happened; the cops arrived an’ aye was arrested an’ taken away. They said aye stabbed her seventeen times, aye probably did, tha’ shit can send ye crazy. But they believed me t’ have done it in me own frame of mind. They overlooked the drugs, said it was only a side element an’ tha’ was had been plannin’ it fer a long time. The fact she was me neighbour didn’t help matters. Aye had a picture p’ her. Didn’t mean aye stalked her! She was fun t’ be around an’ we were friends. Aye don’t know if aye did it so whether aye deserve t’ be here isn’t clear t’ me, tha’ doesn’t matter, aye’ve accepted me death." Paddy seemed shock and it must have showed because now Tony was talking again. "Paddy aye’m not a murderer. Aye might have killed her but aye had changed me ways from the IRA. Aye had renounced violence just like you did. Aye didn’t want to kill or maim, hell, the reason aye was on crack was because the memories were driving me mad. Blastin’ a hole in the back o’ a policeman’s head, Jesus it gives me the shivers. Why were we brought up like tha’ Paddy? It wasn’t right tha’ men felt inclined t’ do tha’. Don’t get me wrong, fightin’ against an enemy tha’ is killin’ yer people isn’t wrong but aye guess, the men who took on this task take on the burden tha’ they can never get rid of the past. It caught up on me Paddy and tha’ be why aye’m sittin’ here." Tony’s lip trembled. No, he wasn’t ready for death, no matter what he said. He lowered his head onto the table so his brow was touching the hard wood. He banged it softly a few times while Paddy looked on. He was thinking, trying to decipher, trying to figure out if this man was what he said he was. Paddy looked up at a small window, metal bars lining the view outside, and there, standing between two bars, was a small white dove. It looked at Paddy curiously with a beady eye, and then fluttered down onto the table, landing gracefully with a soft chirp. Something spoke from inside Paddy and he didn’t try to stop himself. "Aye can’t help ye, no’ now, but aye’ll forgive ye. Fer the lost time, it was as much my fault as yers. An’ aye do believe ye walked away from the killin’ an’ tha’ it just caught up with ye…but like aye said, aye can’t help ye now." Tony looked up for a second, gazing at Paddy, then smiled softly and nodded in gratitude. The bird meanwhile fluttered onto his right shoulder like it was home. Paddy chuckled. "Jesus Tony, ye’ve been watchin’ too much Shawshank, how did ye train it?" "Aye’ve never seen this bird before…" Tony urged the young bird to clutch his finger, and then he stood up and walked to the window, before despatching it to the outside. He sat back down and once again he was solemn. "There’s something else Paddy. Aye want ye t’ be a good man, the man aye aspired t’ be. Whether it’s fightin’ fer the cause or jus’ makin’ waves in Ireland’s name, be the man they can talk about years form now. A man o’ Eire. Aye would love t’ say aye’m dyin’ in tha’ chair fer me country but obviously aye’m not. Murderer or drug addict, neither isn’t goin’ t’ go too well on me gravestone…" Tony struggled once again, his eyes definitely welling up this time. Paddy seemed to well up too but only because he’d never seen Tony Mullen cry before. Never. Not when he was sent down, not when his father was shot by British soldiers in front of his eyes, not when he watched his country tumble on Bloody Sunday. Never. "Paddy…before aye go…aye need t’ know if aye was a good man. Aye can see the words yes formin’ on yer lips but tha’ be because ye don’ want t’ hurt me feelings. Believe me Paddy, at this late hour, all any one would want is honesty. Aye need t’ be able t’ hold me head when aye walk through the pearly gates knowin’ wha’ aye was. Good or bad, aye need t’ know…" Paddy thought for several seconds, gazing at the small window, feeling Tony’s desperate eyes fixed on him. He thought of the good times, the way he’d been older then him but would always stick up for him, even when a mob was calling him a dirty pikey. He remembers the bad, the ten lost years, the brutal IRA training regimes. The day Paddy decided he’d had enough o’ the punishments for bad attitude and screamed in Mullen’s face. Tony had punched him hard, breaking his nose for the first of several times. Don’t’ cross me. Don’t ever ever cross me His words were still fresh in his head. Then Paddy slowly got up, Tony looking a little confused. "Tony, aye need t; think. Ye need an honest answer, well give me time t’ think." "But aye face the chair tomorrow." "Ye’ll know before ye pass. Aye swear." Paddy pushed in his seat and walked out, his head spinning from the information while Tony thought about the implications of this period of thought. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Twenty four hours before it happened, Paddy O’Shea signed an agreement to be searched before he entered the execution room. Then, at one O’Clock in the morning, eight men and three women were led into the viewing room inside the prison. Relatives of the deceased no doubt. Anger was apparent on their face and Paddy supposed he couldn’t blame them. They needed closure and whether he agreed with the death penalty or not, he still felt he was obliged to feel something to them. Paddy had thought long and hard about this question. He supposed the fact Tony gave him four years and two bullet wounds for him made that a fair trade. Don’t ever cross me. Don’t ever ever cross me. Yer me friend, and a soldier o’ Ireland. Now go out an’ make yer country proud. Aye hated ye fer so long. "Keepin’ ye dead t’ me was the only way o’ keepin’ ye alive…" And still he couldn’t make up his mind. He didn’t know how to measure a man’s virtue, there was no meter to compare. He hoped it would come to him before it was too late. Because he too had made a promise, one he felt he had to keep at all costs. At twenty minutes past one, the black drapes to the execution chamber were opened for the witnesses. Paddy suddenly felt nauseous. Suddenly all those feelings of indifference towards Tony were overwhelmed with genuine sadness. His ‘friend’ had been replaced with a friend. He was watching the slow process of his former comrade being taken from this world and the time it was taking was excruciatingly slow. Sitting in the chair confined was Tony Mullen. His cheeks were red from tears. Only underneath the brightness of the fluorescent light did Paddy full appreciate the change in Tony Mullen. His face was gaunt, his eyes darting about so desperately. On the order of the prison warden, the lethal injection executioner approached Mullen. Only now that he had regained some feeling for his comrade did he appreciate the horror that was unfolding in front of Mullen’s eyes. He was watching man take another man’s like. This wasn’t the way. This wasn’t right.The movement of the technician startled Mullen temporarily. He turned his head for the first time into the viewing room while the warden asked him if he’d like to make a statement. His eyes bypassed the victims’ relatives, the sight of their anger would surely crush him and anything he said wouldn’t be appreciated. Tony’s eyes found Paddy’s and he held the contact. It was powerful, incredibly so, as if he were about to lose Paddy as he fell into the deepest chasm. Then Tony spoke. His voice was reedy initially but soon it found its feet and soared above all others in that room. "Aye die now, a victim o’ me past an’ aye’m sorry, whether aye did it or not. Aye’m a good man…Paddy…Slán Mo Chara! Tiocfaidh Ár Lá!" Tony gazed at Paddy, while the old Irish phrase of "Goodbye my friend. Our day will come" set in… Then Paddy stood up and saluted him without any hint of shame in the presence of those around him. He didn’t hesitate, Tony’s voice lifted him to his feet. "Aye, yer a good man Tony. Slán, Chara na hEireann! Tiocfaidh Ár Lá!" A tear trickled down Paddy’s face slowly but he held his head high and proud. As did Tony. Proudly he kept his chin up, a cheeky smile now apparent on his face. Proudly, like a solider on parade. The white dove now appeared at the window on the back wall. It was peeping through the bars at the scene unfolding. In this picture, it was the only thing that truly made sense. "God bless ye Paddy. Now, let’s go Jesus, let’s see wha’ ye got! WOOHOO!" Tony chuckled. He was still chortling as the executioner stepped forward and injected a dose of Pavulon into his arm. He was smiling as his body relaxed, his breathing being slower. "Here’s to the wings of a bird," Tony whispered, still grinning. Not long after, his heart, lungs and brain stopped functioning. He was pronounced dead with a smile on his face, a soldier of Eire laughing in the face of the enemy. Paddy was still saluting as they carried him out. Then, as the drapes were pulled back over, he sighed, then turned on his heel and walked away slowly. Just before Tony Mullen died, he’d realised he was privileged to be viewing the final moments of a genuine man, a good man maybe not in the conventional sense, but because he was different. Brave and true. He supposed the days he spent with Tony had rubbed off, because he suddenly came to the simple conclusion, with no ego, that he too was a good man. And he promised there and then, he would triumph for the glory of all the good men. In GTT5, Tony Mullen’s memory would live on. Slán Tony. Slán.
