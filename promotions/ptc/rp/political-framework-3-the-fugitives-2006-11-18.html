Political Framework 3 - The Fugitives

18 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-18 It was only a matter of time before Richard Holmes and Miss de Lorme started to meet more frequently following the detective's confrontation with Cedric Riley. Nobody really knew what was involved in these private sessions, save for the participants. However, there they sat across from each other, separated only by Holmes's desk. The black backdrop to the bright stars that painted the sky outside the window made it obvious that it was after office hours; however, Holmes himself never really established consistent 'office hours' since his former boss's death. "Heather …" the detective began, "There's something about this equation that just isn't adding up. We've been through this a hundred times … and I still can't come to terms with why you decided to come to me with the information on Cedric so long after we first talked." He stopped, allowing time for Heather to give a response while he fidgeted with his pencil. The former maid had no response to his bemused claim. Richard sighed. "I know you are still grieving over what happened to James … hell, we all are … but he would want us to resolve his murder, and he would want us to do so in the most accurate and fair way possible." She bit her lip nervously as her eyes averted her interviewer. Finally, she responded, "Being of service to James was always something I had trouble dealing with. He … I don't know, Richard … he's so mysterious. He was always making private phone calls, and even on his vacation. He invited people to the house that I was not familiar with, and they never acknowledged me," she explained. "Not like it was a bad thing – I wanted to be unnoticed." "James was the man that you describe, yes … please, continue," the detective offered. "So when I saw Cedric in the house the day that James got murdered … I just passed him by as another one of his guests. I didn't dare ask James about it … that would be breaking the conduct of being a maid … especially to Mr. Sawyer," she said carelessly. Her thoughts were in motion now, and being thrust into the open. Holmes shifted in his chair. He couldn't decide if what the maid had offered was a well-constructed lie to aid her in avoiding the blame. She seemed sensitive about it, which would somewhat explain why she didn't come to the detective with her information so late in the game. "Let's put it this way, Heather – I mean, Miss de Lorme – I want to trust you, and I'd almost go on to say that I do, but the problem is … we lack any concrete evidence that Cedric was even in the house that day, let alone anywhere near the liquor cabinet," the detective elaborated. The former maid nodded, a blank expression masking her face. She bit her lip again and closed her eyes, as if fighting off tears. "I need to go, Richard – I mean, detective," she said. Heather got up from her chair and moved towards the door. Richard got up quickly and walked around his desk, saying, "Heather, wait! You don't have to do this …" She had reached the door by then, and her fingers were around the handle, ready to open it. She stopped, tears welling into her eyes, and the detective gently laid his soft hand on her shoulder. "No one is trying to put this on you, Heather," Richard said, then slowly continued, "Especially not me." She paused to wipe the tears that had leaked down her cheeks, took a deep breath, and responded exasperatingly, "I'm sorry, Richard. Ever since this happened .. life has been so …" "Difficult," he finished unexpectedly, as if he knew exactly what she was going through. "It's been hard for me too, Heather … I was so close with him … he was like a father to me." "Oh, Richard," she responded quickly and embraced him in a hug. The detective reacted uncertainly to Heather's arms being wrapped around him, but eventually gave in, and his strong arms slowly reached and cradled the woman. Resisting the temptation was a thought being pushed aside as desire took him over. Heather reared her head from the man's shoulder and they found each other's eyes. The sexual tension was thick as the detective's eyes wandered; the maid moved her hands to caress his neck, then pulled him in. Their lips locked, the barrier between professionalism and temptation broken. The mutual spit-exchange was passionate, but the detective backed off after that luscious moment as if an alarm went off in his head. He released her from his arms. Heather looked at him worriedly. "What's wrong, Richard?" "I – I'm sorry," he fumbled, trying to find his words. "I can't do this … I mean, we can't do this … it's wrong …" "But … Richard …" she weeped. "I'm sorry, Heather," he said. "You have to go …" He opened the door, sweeping his hand towards the door, indicating to her the way out. Heather crossed her arms and stepped out of the door, then turned around and surprised him with a swift kiss – only a touch of her lips on his ever-so gently, while she laid her soft hands on the front of his shoulders, her palm resting gracefully on his collar-bone. And then she turned away, strolling confidently towards the exit. Richard, with one hand still grasping the door handle, touched his lips with the fingers of his other hand. He lowered it, clenching his fist, biting his tongue .. and then … "Heather!" She turned towards him, and the detective began the long strides towards her. He would reach out to the vulnerable woman, but he could only hope of doing the same in a less literal sense. * * * The Law firm that Alex Barnett worked at was not unlike the Sawyer and Associates office, although there was much more activity. It was morning, and Cedric Riley had waited almost an hour to meet the lawyer he had acquainted himself with. He finally sat in Alex's office, preoccupying himself by tapping his foot and looking around at all of the awards the respected lawyer had been honored with. Across from him sat the man who had won all these awards, and he was having difficulty wrapping up a telephone conversation. Alex finally hung up the phone and rubbed his temples, clearly annoyed at the caller, then looked at Cedric. "Good morning," he acknowledged, shuffling through some of the papers that dominated his desk. He grabbed his cup of coffee and took a sip from it, but cringed – it had gone cold. "I wouldn't exactly call it good, but nonetheless …" Cedric began. "Of course," Alex agreed, "I would be hard-pressed to say that I've had a good morning in the last month … hell, ever since I chose to be a lawyer. But we ought to make it seem like a good morning, shouldn't we?" "Maybe," Cedric said. "I want to thank you for bailing me out of jail, first and foremost." "Don't worry about it," Alex offered. "Second, I was wondering if you were interested in being my lawyer," Cedric added, getting to his intention quickly. A faint smile grazed Alex's face. "I was under the impression I already was." "Well, I'm sorry if you felt that way," Cedric sympathized, "But I have the feeling that I'm in deep here …" Alex shrugged. "It's better than it seems, trust me …" "How so?" "Well, first of all … the only thing they have on you is that maid's word," he explained. "If Holmes is following the book, which he has been known to do, there's no way they can even take it to the courts." "If he thinks I did it, it's because of my brother's jail sentence and my history with Holmes …" Cedric said, recalling the conversation with the detective at the LAPD station. "You murdering Sawyer because he put your brother behind bars, as ridiculous as it seems, is something that he's considering because he has nothing else," Alex responded. "It's almost questionable why he's still holding off on the maid …" "What reason would she have for killing him?" Cedric asked. Alex shrugged again. "You never know in murder cases … maybe Sawyer mistreated her … or took advantage of her. I don't mean to point fingers, but I'm starting to think Holmes isn't the right man for this case. It's too personal." "Then why did he assign himself?"Cedric asked. "FBI asked him if he wanted to, and he said he would. Sawyer's company is one of the best, Cedric, and now that the boss is gone, Holmes might be the detective to hire in this city." "In any case, it appears that asshole thinks I did it," Cedric concluded bitterly. "He's a detective, he considers everything and pursues that option as if it's the answer," Alex explained. "It's your job not to be intimidated by him, and it's also your job not to say anything next time he interrogates you unless I'm around. Understood?" "Yeah, I got it … anything I can do until then? I have some ideas …" said the client. Alex raised an eyebrow. "I suppose that's under your discretion; I have no control over you when it comes to that … but breaking into the maid's hotel room was a little rash, I might add. What did you have in mind?" "I think it's time I paid my dear old brother a visit." Alex hesitated. "Well … I have no reason to stop you. Just keep your family affairs in order, or we might have another witness to deal with." "Family affairs … right. I'll see you later, Alex," Cedric bode, getting up from his chair. He shook Alex's hand before leaving. He had a date with Nathan Aspinall. * * * Unfortunately, someone had already beat him to it. Richard Holmes walked briskly up the steps that lead to the California State Prison. He was dressed in a suit, and had his game-face on – he was ready for interrogation, and Nathan Aspinall was his subject. Holmes had made the decision to pay Cedric Riley's criminal brother a visit. Given his recent, ill-advised affair with his prime suspect and witness, Miss Heather de Lorme, he had been considering every angle that Riley was involved with Sawyer. He was digging for evidence without any solid leads, although Nathan Aspinall may have been the link that he needed to find. In any case, he was going to have a little discussion with the criminal, and had a few ideas of how to get what he wanted. He feared one idea in particular, and hoped the interrogation wouldn't come to it. After a quick request from the front-desk clerk, a flash of his badge, and a short wait, he entered the visiting room, a familiar setting for the detective. Sitting down at one of the booths that faced a pane of acrylic glass, his fingers tapped the desk in front of him impatiently. Nathan Aspinall walked into the opposite half of the room, and one of the guards pointed him in the direction of Richard. He pulled up the chair and sat down across from his visitor, and they both reached for their phones. "Good afternoon, Nathan," Richard greeted. "… Who the hell are you?" Nathan replied, not quite as nicely. Richard pulled out his badge, and showed it to the criminal. "Detective Richard Holmes," "I remember you," Nathan grunted, recalling James Sawyer, the man who had put him in jail. "What is it now? Putting me in jail for five years isn't enough?" "You're lucky it isn't twenty, for all that money you stole," Richard commented, "Not to mention that mass car-stealing operation you were involved in …" Nathan shrugged. "I'm here now, so why the hell do you care so much?" "I don't know, Nathan … why do you think I care? Might it be because my boss has been murdered?" The criminal was silent, then sympathized, "I'm sorry to hear that." "Sure you are," Richard shot at him sarcastically. "Let me ask you something, Nathan .. when is the last time that you spoke with your brother?" The respondent almost seemed surprised that the detective would know such information, but eventually replied, "Earlier this year … why does it matter?" "Cedric Riley is a suspect in the murder of James Sawyer, Nathan … that is why," the detective said. Nathan's eyes widened. "Wow. That comes as unexpected." Holmes looked unimpressed. "So you know nothing about it?" "Uh … no?" Nathan responded unbelievingly. "So you don't think that Cedric would have done such antics as vengeance for Sawyer putting you in jail?"the detective pushed, hoping for an answer. "Doubtful," Nathan replied, leaning back in his chair. "He's a celeb now, no reason to risk it for his bad-ass brother." "Well, then …" Richard said, as if let-down. He paused for a moment to think about his situation, then continued. "What if I were to say, hypothetically speaking, that I could ask for some favors that might get you out of prison a little ahead of schedule?" Nathan's eyes almost lit up. He shifted in his position, then said, "I would ask you what you had in mind." "I would need your cooperation," the detective offered. His opposite shifted in his chair again. "What kind of cooperation, detective?" he sneered. "To testify," Holmes said quickly, then after a pause followed with, "Against your brother." Nathan smirked. "And why in the hell would I do that?" Richard shrugged. "Other than to get yourself out of prison, I don't know, Nathan … why else would you do it?" A tense moment followed as they locked eyes. "I'll leave you to think about it, Nathan. Good day." And the detective was gone. Nathan did indeed have something to think about. * * * It was only the next morning when Cedric Riley walked into the visiting room at the California State Prison. He had been in such places before, but it had been quite a long time, and he felt almost uncomfortable as he pulled up a chair to one of the stalls. Facing a pane of acrylic glass, he saw a man in a blue jump-suit enter the opposite half of the room. He recognized his brother immediately – Nathan Aspinall, the man that Cedric had basically sworn he would never communicate with again. Nathan and Cedric had a history, to say the least. The former CIA agent's younger brother was a criminal, and had been known to ask for the odd favor from Cedric, including a ten-thousand dollar loan only seven months prior to this visit. While Cedric was apart of the agency, he had helped Nathan out of a sentence on the charge of grand-theft-auto; needless to say, the professional wrestler was tired of looking out for him. Hence why Nathan was now in jail, and Cedric was visiting him. 'Visit', however, wasn't quite the proper description – Cedric had a very unsentimental reason for coming to see his kid brother. "Well isn't this a surprise," said Nathan after picking up the his phone, a smirk lining his face. "Can't two brothers have a civil conversation, or have I flown the coop?" Cedric said in response. His brother smiled. "I think you're long past that, boy." "At least I'm not in jail," Cedric insulted. "Oooh … I'm impressed, you're still a smart-ass," Nathan said. Cedric rolled his eyes. "Listen, Nathan, I don't want to argue with you. I'm not even going to make it a big deal that I didn't know you were in prison," he stated, trying to get to his point. "Well then, why are you here?" the younger brother asked, as if he already knew the answer. Cedric hesitated. "Y'know … wanted to know how you were doing …" "Right," Nathan said, adding a chuckle; he certainly didn't believe that, but went along anyway. "I'm in jail, Cedric." He paused, then continued, "But it could be worse. How about you? How's the wrestling gig working out?" "The wrestling thing is great. I'm in round three of an inter-fed tournament … apparently it's a pretty big deal. And I qualified for the main event at Triangles …" Cedric elaborated, trying to make his life out to be more positive than it actually was. "Well, that's great … I'm proud of you," Nathan congratulated, although he did not understand any of the wrestling vocabulary his brother was losing. "What about real life?" Cedric laughed. "Well, y'know … not so great …" "Tell me all about it …" Nathan requested, looking for the dirt. "In the last month one of my best friends betrayed me and tried to kill me, my girlfriend and I are on the ropes, I was drugged and kidnapped by Garbage Bag Johnny, and …" Cedric paused for a thought, then continued, "Oh … right … I've been suspected for involvement in a murder." "Wow," was all Nathan could respond with. "Yeah … and actually … the last reason is why I'm here," Cedric admitted. A pause followed and Nathan shifted in his chair. The younger brother's intent was to give no indication that he knew about what was going on in the case, to pretend that the detective hadn't come to see him only the day before. "I just wanted to warn you, Nathan, that the guy who wants me in jail might come pay you a visit. He might ask you some questions about some stuff that went on between me and Sawyer years ago …" Cedric explained. "Oh, really?" Nathan said, trying to sound as bewildered as possible. "They think I killed Sawyer because he put you in jail," Cedric told him. "Well, that's ridiculous," Nathan replied. "I know!" Cedric returned, frustrated. "I just … I don't know … something strange is going on here." "Well, I'm sorry to hear you've had such a terrible month … and I suppose I owe you an apology," Nathan finished. "Huh?" "For never listening to you …. look where it's got me. I've had some time to think about it … and, well … I guess you were right," finished Nathan. Cedric was almost taken aback by the sudden U-turn the conversation took. "Well, I wasn't expecting that …" Nathan chuckled. "Of course you weren't." "Well .. thanks. And I don't mean to spoil the moment, but I have something else to ask you …" Cedric said, changing the direction of the conversation. "Shoot." "Are you familiar with a guy named Juvian Cohol? He was held in this jail for a bit …" "I've heard the name, and seen him around, but he wasn't here long … I don't think I can help you out … I don't know much about him, except he was in for trafficking," Nathan said, then added bitterly, "And he escaped during his transfrer. Lucky prick." "That's fine," Cedric said. "How about seeing if you could find anything out about him?" Nathan shrugged. "I'll ask around," he agreed. "Great … I think that Sawyer put him in jail … might help my cause," Cedric hoped. "That's understandable." "I hope so," Cedric said skeptically. "But … I should be going. I have a flight in one hour … I'm wrestling in four days for that GTT tournament I told you about." Nathan raised an eyebrow. "And what does the detective think about that?" "Well, he doesn't have to know, does he?" Cedric responded with a smile. "I have a lawyer in town here. He can handle it. I'll see you around, Nathan." "Take it easy." They hung up their phones, and Cedric left the building. * * * A private hospital in the west end of Los Angeles was a temporary home to questionable patients, most that you wouldn't expect to be in a hospital with ugly injuries or diseases. Very wealthy people arrived with gunshot wounds and STDs, and paid large sums of money for one of the most hospitable services in Los Angeles. In some cases, it acted as a hide-out for those that sustained injuries from illegal activity. A man walked through the halls of the hospitable, recognizable as Juvian Cohol, who was on the run as a prison escapee. In the weeks before he had been in Spain; his reasons for returning unknown. He walked into a room on his right. A bed in front of him supported a man underneath a blankets; a heart monitor beeped near him. His eyes opened as Juvian entered the room. "Juvian," the bedded man greeted in a hoarse voice. "Hello, Victor … I came as soon as I could … how are you?" Juvian acknowledged. "I'm as good as an old man with a bullet hole in his shoulder can be, my friend," Victor said with a weak smile. Juvian pulled a chair up to his bed and sat down. "Then what happened?" Victor smiled faintly. "It was just a drug deal gone wrong … nothing to worry about …" Juvian looked unimpressed. "I think it is something to worry about if you were shot and had me summoned here from Spain. I don't know if you forget, Victor, but your cronies helped me escape from jail. I fled for a reason; the authorities are still after me. I'm a wanted man for life, for Christ sakes!" Victor frowned. "It is unfortunate that you aren't very considerate of my condition, Juvian, but the steps I have taken are indeed necessary …" "I hope so, Victor. I am no longer welcome in this damn city .." "I understand," Victor accepted. "But you must understand that you … oh, how shall I put it … are still in dept to me." Juvian's face went blank. "I thought we agreed …" "Things change, Juvian," Victor interrupted harshly. "Now … I had you brought here to do me a favor. My son … he needs to be taken to his mother, in New York City." "And why do I have to do it?" "I trust no one else with little Ricky, Juvian. You know this," Victor explained. "I don't understand …" "The men who shot me, Juvian… they did so for a reason. They are not satisfied with how the deal went, and made threats towards me and my family," Victor said solemnly, his voice weakening. "I will no longer risk anyone's life but my own …" Juvian nodded. "Fine." * * * A day later, Cedric Riley was at the Los Angeles airport, laying down on the floor of the waiting area with his head resting on his bag as about a million things circled through his head. The excitement and anticipation for wrestling Killean Sirrajin in less than four days was certainly on his mind, but he couldn't help but mull over his current situation in the Sawyer case. His flight for Athens, Greece had been delayed an hour for some reason – he could not care less, for he had become so accustomed to flying throughout his life that delays were to be expected. Earlier flights were always booked for this very reason. "First call for Flight 761 from Los Angeles to Athens, Greece," rang from the speaker as Cedric perked his attention. He rose from his lie-down position and grabbed his bag, then headed for the terminal. After a short line, he gave the woman his ticket and stepped through the gateway. She accepted it and he thanked her as he heard someone say from behind, "Hurry it up!" Already through the gate, Cedric turned round to respond. Had he have done so he probably would have regretted it; he didn't, though, because he was distracted by detective Richard Holmes and Miss Heather de Lorme parting ways in the airport lobby. The suspected woman was heading towards another terminal.
