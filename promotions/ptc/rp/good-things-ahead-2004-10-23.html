Good Things Ahead

23 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-23 Dallas Reeves vs Dave Harley. Pyrenees Bracket Semi-Final. Both competitors have managed to claw their way here through grit, determination, skill and luck. These men have never faced one another before, but Harley has been doing his homework on the young upstart from the NGW, even going so far as to send his personal assistant down to that federation’s headquarters in New York. Harley, known for his cunning both in and out of the ring, may seem to have a leg up in the preparation aspect of things. But Dallas Reeves hasn’t made it through to the 4th Round by being unprepared himself. After having beaten the likes of Kenny Mercury and the previously Unstoppable Eugene, Reeves’ manager “Alberta” Jack B. Carrington whisked him away to Montmartre, Saskatchewan, to the rural farmhouse of one “Pepsi” Joe Trudeau. Trudeau, a 63 yr old former wrestler, now hires himself as a specialized trainer known for his irregular coaching methods. Carrington knows that if Dallas Reeves is to beat “Extreme Machine” Dave Harley, he’s going to need a hand. The scene opens in the musky barn that lies on the farmhouse property. Shards of early morning light filter in between the barnboards, and cast an eerie hue over its occupants. In the forefront stands “Pepsi” Joe Trudeau, wearing a full black ensemble. His silver hair cascades around his aged head, with a glaring bald patch running up the middle of his skull. Half of his left ear is missing, and he has a black patch covering his right eye, injuries suffered years ago during his time in the ring. He paces back and forth, in front of three restless figures, before stopping to face them, arms akimbo. Half-crazed from his time in the ring and his increasing age, Trudeau commands full attention from the three figures. To the left of the man in the center is the slim Frankie “the Cameraman” Calrissian, a look of boredom on his face. He exchanged his exclusive camera duties for Reeves’ mentoring in the wrestling world. To the right of the man in the center is the hulking “Beastor” Brandt, bodyguard to Reeves. His disheveled hair and mangy, free-roaming beard, coupled with his 6’8” 335 lb frame, strikes a most intimidating presence. During his time in the ring, he and Trudeau were embroiled in a feud that led to Trudeau’s disfigurement. The only reason the loyal Beastor is here right now is to keep an eye on his old enemy, as Trudeau still harbours resentment for him. The man in the center is none other than “Hollywood North” Dallas Reeves, and he keeps a close watch on the one-eyed, one-eared senior citizen. Trudeau: Right. Dave Harley. The “Extreme Machine”. Leader of the Devil’s Own, a big faction over in the WWA. So what, Mr. Reeves? You think you can take down a 280 lb man than can wrestle with the agility of a man half his weight? And really, all this time you’ve been spending making movies, tv shows, guest spots and what have you…you think you’re honestly set to go into a match and wrestle a man that is renowned for his hardcore tendencies? Reeves opens his mouth, ready to say something in his own defense, but Trudeau stops him before he can speak. Trudeau: Granted, you have surprised me so far in this tournament, but there’s an old saying, Mr. Reeves. “Even the sun shines on a dog’s ass.” Reeves cringes his face, trying to wrap his mind around Trudeau’s not-so-Aesopian statement. Dallas: So, what? Am I a dog’s ass then? Trudeau: Ha! You wish! Dallas: Are you telling me I’m lucky? That my moment in the sun- Trudeau: It’s a rhetorical question. Dallas: “Pepsi” Joe, it wasn’t a question, it was- Trudeau: If you wanna learn hardcore, here it comes! Frankie, put this blindfold around Mr. Reeves’ face. Trudeau tosses a black blindfold to Frankie, who begins to tie it around Reeves’ head. The Beastor glares menacingly at Trudeau, and begins to emit a low grumble. Frankie: (whispering to Dallas) Sorry man, but it’s better you than me. Trudeau points a wrinkled finger at Beastor, warning him. Trudeau: You can stop that grumbling right now, Chewbacca. I know the best way to train Mr. Reeves, and it’s by totally immersing him in the hardcore lifestyle! Dallas: Hardcore lifestyle!? “Pepsi” Joe, you’re 63 yrs old! How hardcore can you be? You drink a bottle of Nyquil each night before bed! Trudeau: That’s just to take my hardcore edge off, Mr. Reeves. If I didn’t drink it, I’d be a little too much of a handful. It helps keep my hardcore urges down, helps keep me sane. Dallas: I think it’s time ya upped the dosage. Trudeau produces a kendo stick from behind his back, and lunges forward, screaming like a maniac. Frankie bolts to the side, cowering. Beastor slowly walks to the edge of the barn, keeping an eye on Trudeau and one on Reeves. Trudeau whacks Reeves in the ribs with the kendo stick, and Dallas takes a knee, sucking wind. Dallas: Wuff! Johnny Damon that hurts! How’s my wearing a blindfold and you attacking me with a freakin’ weapon gonna teach me about how hardcore Dave Harley is!? Trudeau has a weird glint in his eye, and looks totally lost in the moment. He snaps back to reality for a moment. Trudeau: Who? He again thwacks Reeves, this time in the shoulder. The Beastor grumbles from the edge of the barn, his back turned to the commotion. The sound of running water is heard, but it’s actually just Beastor relieving himself in the corner. Reeves’ ears perk up. Dallas: I hope you’re downhill, Beastor. Trudeau: Downhill this! Again Trudeau lunges forward with the kendo stick, but Reeves shoulder rolls to his right, evading the charge. Trudeau instead ends up thwacking the hapless Frankie, which manages to get a chuckle out of the still-urinating Beastor. Frankie, near tears, lets out a yelp, then scampers up the nearby hay bails to safety. Trudeau turns to Beastor. Trudeau: So…first you take my ear and eye, and now ya laugh at me, eh? Well…have at thee! Trudeau finally snaps, unable to hold his rage against Beastor in check. He heads after Beastor with the kendo stick, but Beastor turns around ready to confront him, still urinating. The two meet near the edge of the barn, and Beastor knocks the kendo stick loose of his grip. They begin trading blows, while Reeves takes the blindfold off and can only look on in bewilderment. Through the entrance of the barn walks his manager, “Alberta” Jack B. Carrington, looking very dapper in his purple smoking jacket. He has a cigar at his lips and his trademark white Stetson atop his head. He shakes his head in dismay as he sidesteps the fracas, and joins Reeves. Carrington: Ah see Beasteh an’ Joe ah back at it, eh? Well, pe-haps havin’ Joe teach ya ha’hd-co’ wasn’t tha best of ideas. ‘Sides, ya’ve won a coupla Extreme Titles in ya career, Dallas. Maybe ya matches haven’t bin against opponents as high a calibeh as Dave Hahley, but ya don’t have much else ta lose. Yer without a regulah federation ta call ya own, an’ Ah think that makin’ it ta Roun’ Fo-uh has given ya some credentials within tha PTC as it is. Dallas: JB, I’m gonna beat Harley. I have to. I’ve got nothin’ else in my life right now. Carrington: That’s not true. Ya got Frankeh up they-uh. He thinks ah ya as a god. Dallas: I’m not ready to retire from wrestling to become a manager just yet, JB. I’m not even thirty! Carrington: Okay then, ma boy. Beat Dave Hahley then. Simple as that. Ah’ll do my best ta get ya some work in anotheh federation. All ya gotta do is beat Hahley. Dallas: Vyper…Mercury…Eugene…Harley. That’s got to be impressive enough to get some interest, right? Win or lose? Carrington: Win. Ah think ya got it in ya. Do some a that over-achievin’ yer always yammerin’ on about. Dallas: Okay JB. I’ll do the wrestlin’, you work on the contract. Frankie begins to cheer on Beastor as the two old rivals really begin to throw the punches. Frankie: Get ‘im Beastor! Get ‘im! Dallas: Think we oughta break this up, JB? Carrington: Na, this is beyon’ you an’ me, ma boy. As Beastor and Trudeau continue on, from the barn into the yard, Reeves, Carrington and Frankie follow, with the interest of watching a car wreck. Dallas Reeves has wrestled his entire career for the sake of wrestling. He loves the sport. He never used his popularity to work for him, except in the case of getting himself a foothold in acting. He knows that Dave Harley is one tough cookie. However, since his second round victory over Kenny Mercury, Dallas has been waiting for the other shoe to drop. He entered the GTT4 with the intentions of winning it, and bringing some type of much-needed publicity to the NGW, his home for the past 9 months. But with its impending closure, he now sees his performance in the tournament to end all tournaments as a way to garner himself some new employment, preferably within the PTC. He knows that a third straight victory over a Primetime Central stalwart would cement himself a new beginning and a solid future somewhere. And for a man who has always lived in the moment, for the moment, he seems in pretty high spirits going into one of the most important matches of his career. He’s done hardcore before. If he has to put his body through it one more time in order to get himself a new contract, and to imbue himself to a whole new fan base, he will. For wherever Dallas Reeves chooses to wrestle, success seems to follow. Dave Harley vs Dallas Reeves. The Pyrenees Bracket Final awaits one of these rugged competitors. And this time, just being Dave Harley may not be enough. The scene fades to black, the words “Harley vs Reeves” appearing in gold letters, then fading out.
