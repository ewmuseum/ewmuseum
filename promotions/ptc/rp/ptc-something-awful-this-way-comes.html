Something Awful This Way Comes "Wait just a second. I need you to repeat that. You did what?" "I..., well..." Lawrence (Larry) Spiderman, for the first time since his childhood, wished that he had the power to travel through time and change the course of history. In light of the current news that his assistant, Stacy McNamara, had shared with him his first use of this power would have been to travel back fourteen minutes into the past. Then, with his foreknowledge of the future, he would have seized the opportunity to temporarily unplug all of the phones in the office, have the number changed, or perhaps even requested that the service be shut off immediately. "Spit it out, Stacy." "Well, it's just that he sounded so nice on the phone, and..." All of this was because of the phone call that his assistant had fielded - a phone call where she had agreed to setup a meeting with Mr. Spiderman two weeks out. Jared Sykes was coming to New York. "Please," he pleaded, his head cradled in his hands, "please just tell me that this is a very bad, very early April Fool's joke so we can move on with our lives." Stacy shuffled uneasily, fidgeting with the day planner she was carrying. "I'm sorry," she said, her eyes fixed on the floor, "it's not a joke. He really called, and he, well, he sounded really upset. Like, you know how sometimes you can just tell when someone's not right?" Larry scoffed. "Oh he's not right, that's for sure." "I know he's never been the easiest person to deal with," she paused, "Okay, so maybe he's a little eccentric, but I get the feeling that something's up. Plus, the way things have been going around here I thought that a little boost to the business wouldn't hurt." He had her there. The last few months had seen a reduction in clients, and while Larry tried to convince himself that it was only a temporary problem, the fact remained that the revenue stream had shrunk, and on occasion he genuinely believed this to be the beginning of the end for his small company. Larry leaned back in his chair, loosened his tie, and sighed. "Yeah, I guess it would." The hesitation in his voice was obvious, as though he was about to concede defeat. "Alright, fine. The appointment stands. You think maybe in the last few months he became un-crazy?" Stacy had to stifle a laugh. "Yeah," Larry continued, "Neither do I." -*-*-*-*- "There's a lady who's sure all that glitters is gold, and she's buying a stairway to hea..." Click. "Not today, Mr. Plant." It'd been almost four months since Jared Sykes had been in this situation, trying to make himself as comfortable as possible for the three-plus hour train ride from Boston to New York. He was planning on meeting with his agent, Lawrence Spiderman, for the first time since Christmas, and in thinking about what he was going to say had zoned out. He hadn't even realized he'd been listening to music until the latest track advanced to the first verse, and something deep in his subconscious shouted a resounding "No!" Evidently, this was neither the time nor place for Led Zeppelin. For a moment he played with the scroll wheel on his iPod, moving from track to track. His thoughts lingered on the small device in his right hand, wondering why, once aware of the music, it was imperative to select just the right track for the moment. After a while he began to question whether the shuffle setting had it out for him, as no fewer than fifty songs were skipped before he finally settled on something. Content with his selection, he slipped his hands back into the pockets of his hooded sweatshirt, and tilted his head to get a better view of the landscape through his window. He tried for a while to let his mind wander, doing his best to tune out the world and focus on nothing, but was ultimately unsuccessful. There was still about two hours left in his trip, and Jared was now convinced that he was going to be forced to experience every dull minute of it. Never one to deal well with boredom, he quickly reached into his pack and produced a small laptop, a "netbook", hopeful that maybe the train was broadcasting a wireless signal. Some frantic typing, a few muttered curses, and one aggravated sigh later meant Jared had come to terms with the notion that he would be without the Internet for the duration of the trip. In the hopes that it might keep him occupied he began browsing through the various folders, looking for something interesting. He found it in the form of a video clip he'd downloaded in the fall, "Italian Spiderman". The clip started a chain reaction in his head. His mind drifted away from the clip and wandered towards the theme music for the live-action Spiderman television show. This in turn merged with thoughts of his upcoming meeting, and the grief he had been known to cause Larry Spiderman over his name. Before he realized what he was doing, Jared had started singing under his breath. "Spider-Jew, Spider-Jew; Do whatever an agent do; Makin' deals to get the cash; In the bank he don't spend it fast; Watch out, he is the Spider-Jew." The realization of what he'd been muttering hit him suddenly, and the embarrassment washed over him like a wave. Slowly and sheepishly he lifted himself from his seat, quickly surveying the adjacent seats in the hopes that no one was close to enough to hear him, or that anyone there wasn't paying attention. Luck was on his side. The only nearby passenger was asleep. He quietly slinked back into his seat and slouched low, folding his arms over his chest. He'd spend the rest of the trip in self-amused silence. -*-*-*-*- It was an awkward silence that hung in the air of Larry Spiderman's office. On one side of the desk sat Mr. Spiderman, the picture of professionalism in a pale gray suit and dust blue shirt. Across from him, on the other side of the desk, Jared Sykes fidgeted with a button on his cargo shorts. Neither man looked or felt particularly comfortable. The last time they'd seen each other was in a hospital only a few days removed from Thanksgiving. Jared was a patient, having suffered some injuries during a Sin City Championship Wrestling event, and had told Larry that he was getting out, that he was done, that the next event would be his last, even if it meant throwing away the only world title shot he'd ever had in the entirety of his career. Given the ramifications that shot could have had to both of their careers, Larry did not take this news very well. "So," Larry said, the first to break the silence. "Yeah," Jared countered, accomplishing nothing. For a brief moment there was eye contact. Larry wondered whether his currently former client had some half-baked idea that he was about to share, one that would no doubt unravel the company he'd worked so hard to maintain over the last few months. Jared wondered just how much whiskey had gone missing from Larry's bottom drawer in order for him to agree to this meeting. “So who gets to go first?” “Well, you’re the one who asked for this meeting, Jared, so I assumed that you had something you wanted to talk about.” “Yeah, I’m just not quite sure how to put it.” “Don’t think too much on it. Just say whatever’s on your mind.” Jared nodded, slipped his hands into the pockets of his sweatshirt, and began softly tapping his feet. The silence between the two men continued, the only noise coming from the sound of sneakers on carpet. “Are we still working together, you and I? Like, am I still your client? I honestly have no idea.” “That’s kind of a weird question. Four months ago you told me you were done, that you were getting out. I assumed based on the conversations we had then that we wouldn’t need each other anymore. Why do you ask?” “I dunno,” came the hesitant response. Again the room was silent for a moment, as Larry Spiderman pondered his next question. “Can I ask you something? Something that might be a little personal, I mean?” Larry leaned back in his chair, unsure of what the response would be. Jared just cocked his head and shrugged. “Shoot.” Larry inhaled deep. He was about to try and answer the nagging question which had been bothering him for months, but at the same time was fearful of how Jared might take it. “Sin City,” Larry continued, “You were on top of the world there. A tag team champion, and more importantly you were next in line for a guaranteed title shot. What did you tell me before, that it was the only one you’d ever been granted? You could have been huge - we could have been huge, but,” he paused, entertaining the notion of whether or not he should finish the question, “what the hell happened?” “It was, and I left,” the response was curt. Larry Spiderman was treading on sensitive ground. The answer made that clear. Larry opened his mouth to speak, and then his better judgment took over and he quickly closed it. The stare from across the desk played no small part in his decision. “Okay, alright, fine,” Jared’s voice was forceful, direct. He rolled both of his sleeves up a little, and leaned forward on his knees, staring intently across the desk, “You want to know why, that’s pretty obvious. Here’s why: sometime between waking up and learning that someone who’s like my little brother might die I started thinking that maybe there are more important things than being really good at taking a beating. I started thinking that maybe there are more important things than being world champion, or having some pretty gold belt that serves no other real purpose in life except to make my luggage heavier.” “But you’re here, and I know that you’re not ’done’ done, because I still get calls about that Infinite something-or-other. I just got one the other day about some guy named Steve Knox.” “The chick from Fleetwood Mac?” “No, not Stevie Nicks.” He was beginning to get a bit frustrated. “Whatever, I just don’t get it.” Jared fell back in his chair, his head turned away. “It’s all I know how to do,” he said, deliberately trying not to meet the gaze of Larry Spiderman, “All I’ve ever known how to do. Even if I wanted to stop, I wouldn’t know how. I don’t want to be the guy who sells insurance, or real estate, and I’m sure not going to be that guy on local television trying to sell you a used car. The problem with me is that it’s this or nothing, and don’t think for a second that thought doesn’t fuck with my head. Every day, every fucking day I have to deal with the idea that my life starts and ends there, and if I think about it too long it makes me crazy.” “So, does that mean that you want me to help you get back there? To Sin City? Maybe we can still get you that shot, and…” He was cut off by a short, humorless laugh. Jared shook his head briefly, and then finally turned his head back towards his agent. “Dude, honestly? It wouldn’t happen. They don’t want to deal with me at this point. Nigel, Dr. Kensington, whatever – he’s a good guy, but we don’t see eye-to-eye on much.” Larry nodded. He’d expected this response, though deep down he’d hoped for a different answer. It was obvious to him from Jared’s response that he’d touched on something that Jared rarely spoke about “Sorry. I don’t mean to lay all this on you like that,” Jared continued softly, “but you, you know, asked.” “So what do we do now?” “Well, I do have something that might be useful.” Jared opened his pack and began to fish through its contents. After a moment he produced a rather crumbled stack of paper, some of which were stained and torn. “What the hell is that?” “It’s a contract, one I got last fall. I don’t know if it’s still valid though.” Larry reached into the breast pocket of his jacket, retrieving a pair of glasses. He gently slipped them into position on his face, and extended his hand to Jared. “Mind if I take a look?”
