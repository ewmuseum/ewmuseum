A Brief Discussion of the Moral Implications of Certain Television Programs

30 September 2005

Date: 2005-09-30 I. So hey listen to this story that this friend of this acquaintance of mine overheard on an overseas flight, which flight's destination, my acquaintance said, was New York City where his, my acquaintance's, friend lives and where apparently the elderly gentleman who was telling this story to the younger gentleman sitting next to him on the flight (and who were both, the elderly gentleman and the younger gentleman, sitting in front of the friend of my acquaintance, which is how he, the friend, happened to overhear this story. The story was, he would later say, much more entertaining than the Julia Roberts vehicle which was being featured on this particular flight) also lives though he, the friend, had never seen either of them at any social functions before and but even if he had he probably wouldn't remember them so maybe he did, but I digress. Anyway, my acquaintance's friend spent the first part of the flight attempting to enjoy the J. Roberts vehicle and so missed out on the first part of the story, which, if this story was structured in anyway similar to any other conventional narrative, would have been comprised primarily of things such as scene setting and character introductions; in other words, the context within which the ensuing narrative actually happens. And so but anyway, the story, he said, concerned a person called woof woof--who woof woof is my friend's acquaintance didn't know for the reason detailed in the sentence which preceded this one--who it seems one day was walking along a pier somewhere on an apparently overcast autumn day and the wind was blowing and so the collar of his pea coat was popped so as to provide a barrier against said wind. Why this woof woof was wandering around down near a pier is anybody's guess, though I assume that the elderly gentleman probably knows why and would probably include the reason were it he and not me who was at present telling you this story, but you don't know the elderly gentleman and I don't know the elderly gentleman and neither of us is probably ever going to run into him and even if we did we probably wouldn't realize it because he probably looks not markedly different from your every day run of the mill elderly gentleman, displaying no external features which would say, 'I know the reason that woof woof was wandering around down near a pier' and so it's best to probably forget it and just move on with the story. So anyway, possessing no reading material and not wishing to sit through 90 minutes of Julia Roberts's oddly large mouth (seriously, have you seen that woman smile? Jesus Christ.) my acquaintance's friend decided to lean slightly forward and listen to the narrative which was being related less than a foot in front of him and so after it was established that this woof woof, who, it soon became apparent to the friend, was the main protagonist of this particular narrative, was walking along down by the pier on a cold and blustery autumn day the elderly gentleman then told the younger gentleman that a strange ship emerged from the fog which hung low over the pier that day. At first this woof woof took no notice of the strange ship and just continued to walk along, minding his own business. As he walked, the elderly gentleman said, the ship seemed to be following him. Now whether or not this particular ship had a previously established narrative significance is unknown, so, yeah. But anyway after a while woof woof apparently noticed this strange ship which seemed to be following him and so, my acquaintance's friend said the elderly gentleman told the younger gentleman, he stopped and looked at it and apparently yelled out, 'Hey, you there, strange ship in the fog! Why are you following me?' and the ship apparently replied with a Toot Toot of its horn which was strange because the ship was a wooden ship and wooden ship's don't usually carry horns and whether or not woof woof knew this my acquaintance said his friend couldn't say, being that he was unfamiliar with woof woof's knowledge (or lack thereof) of maritime vessels, but anyway woof woof responded to this Toot Toot by staring blankly at the ship for a short period of time for reason's unknown. My friend said that his acquaintance heard the elderly gentleman in front of him say that after this short period of staring blankly woof said, 'Hey, ship, I asked you why you were following me?' And it was after this second question that a group of men dressed entirely in black jumped off of the deck of the ship and on to the pier and abducted woof woof and took him back to this ship with them. It was at this point that my acquaintance's friend acquired a valuable piece of context as once the men got woof woof back on board the ship they told him that they were a band of pirates that abducted professional wrestlers and took them to a secret jungle island where they, the abducted professional wrestlers, were sacrificed to the Great Lizard God in a ceremony that the pirates said was as old as the earth itself and so from this piece of information he, the listener, inferred that this woof woof character who was the apparent protagonist of this story was a professional wrestler since if he wasn't why on earth would this band of pirates have abducted him in the first place? And so but anyway the pirates threw woof in a cell below the deck and walked away laughing and left woof feeling in general pretty downbeaten and dejected and depressed andc. I mean wouldn't you be if you were abducted by a band of pirates who were planning on sacrificing you to some kind of Great Lizard God? I know I would. So, the elderly gentleman said, woof woof was left alone in this cell with nothing for company but his thoughts and so he sat down and started thinking about a story that a friend of his had told him the other day. II. …been on this flight to Paris when he overheard the married couple in front of him arguing about something. He didn't know what they were arguing about since he hadn't started eavesdropping until about a third of the way into the argument. From what he could pick up, the husband had slept with the wife's terminally ill sister as an act of kindness, but that wasn't what had the wife so angry, no she was angry because, apparently, the husband hadn't been satisfactorily pleasurable for the terminally ill sister. 'You know she only has a few weeks left and you couldn't even be bothered to fucking get her off more than once,' the wife said. This was a bit much for woof's friend's acquaintance who had then put his headphones on and gone back to watching the in flight movie. He fell asleep a few minutes later. Thinking back, woof realized that this was a pretty shitty story and that he kinda wished that he could know more about the argument between the husband and the wife and so since he wasn't going anywhere for a while he decided to make up a story involving them, the husband and wife. He would later ask one of the pirates for a pad of paper on which to write the story down. It went like this: The sun hung low in the sky as Gerald Walters drove home from work on the I-10. As he drove he listened to the radio. It was tuned to an oldies station, which was currently playing a song by the 50s pop group Ronnie G & The Crying Vs. The song was called ‘Glenda.’ The chorus of the song went like this: Oh Glenda [How I love you] I can’t pretenda [How I love youuuu] You make me want to sinnng! Oh Glenda! Gerald’s face had the character of a man who’s been beaten down by life. Who maybe had some dreams some years ago but now, now all he had left was a wife and a house and a car and sometimes he thought about maybe going to a gas station and buying a can of gas and just burning the whole thing down one day but whenever those thoughts entered his head he just told himself, ‘Hey, hey, don’t think about things like that, Gerry. Sociopaths think about things like that. You’re not a sociopath, you’ve got a mortgage’ and then he went into the basement and played with his toy trains for a while and after that he didn’t think about the can of gas or any of that for a while. Gerald had begun playing with toy trains two years ago. He wasn’t sure why he suddenly developed an interest in toy trains, but he did. He was sitting at a restaurant with his wife one evening eating a plate of chicken parmesan when a thought suddenly popped into his head, ‘You know what I’d like to start doing?’ he thought to himself. ‘I’d like to start playing with toy trains.’ And so that Saturday he drove to the nearest hobby shop and bought a toy train set. When he brought it home his wife said to him, ‘What’s that?’ and he said, ‘It’s a toy train set’ and she said ‘What are you planning to do with a toy train set?’ and he said ‘I’ve decided to take it up as a hobby.’ and she said ‘What made you decide to do that.’ And she said ‘Oh, okay. Have fun, honey’ with the slightest hint of condensation in her voice which he only realized had been there later that night when he was lying in bed next to her and she was asleep and he wasn’t because he hadn’t ever really been able to sleep well and often laid in bed awake for hours before finally drifting off to dreamland. At this point, one of the pirates suddenly appeared in the cell block and the writer of this piece decided to suddenly switch over to script format. Pirate: Matey! woof: Huh, what? Pirate: Ye was sleeping woof: I was not. I was writing a story. Pirate: Don’t ye lie to me, land-lover. I knows sleeping when I sees it and yeh was down here sound asleep like a little baby woof: Are you calling me a baby? Pirate: Hey, if it walk like a duck and quack like a duck, then it be a duck. woof: I’m not a duck Pirate: Aye, so you aren’t. Now, get up. woof: For what? Pirate: The captain wants to see you in his quarters. woof: Hey, why didn’t you have a pirate accent when you said that last line? Pirate: I, uhm…yarrgh matey. Shiver me timbers! I’ll send ye to Davey Jones’s Locker if yeh talk back like that agayne! Now, up with ya. The cap’n don’t like to be kept waiting. And so I follwed him out of the cell block and up a rickety flight of stairs to the captain's quarters. The captain sat in a high-backed chair behind a table that was covered in various very delicious looking food stuffs. I'd list them off if I had time, but, alas, I don't and so you're just going to have to use your imagination to imagine all the different types of food set out on this table in front of this pirate captain. After we entered the room the pirate who had come to retrieve me from the cell block bowed and then turned and exited the room leaving me alone with the captain. Things to cover: the quality of the food, woof's questions re why he's been abducted & the captain's refusal to provide answers, the captain slipping out of his pirate accent and woof catching him and the captain abruptly ending dinner leaving woof woof very perplexed and intrigued indeed] After woof woof was returned to his cell he resumed the story about Gerald Walters and his wife and his wife's sister One evening Gerald and his wife and their son and their daughter were sitting at the kitchen table eating dinner when the telephone rang. Gerald stood up to answer it but his wife told him to not bother. 'Don't bother,' she said. 'It's probably just a telemarketer. Just sit back down and eat.' And since he didn't feel like pressing the issue he did as he was told. After dinner was over Gerald offered to wash the dishes and as her way of saying thanks his wife didn't even acknowledge this. As he was washing the dishes, his wife picked up the phone and heard the beep-beep-beep before the dial tone that indicated that someone had left a voice mail message and so she dialed the voice mail number and entered their pass code. A few moments later she came into te kitchen with tears in her eyes. 'What's the matter?' Gerald asked. 'That was my sister who called during dinner,' his wife said, trying to hold back a sob. 'She went to the doctor today and, oh God.' 'What's wrong?' Gerald asked, growing concerned. 'They said she has cancer.' 'What!?' 'Yeah, it's terminal. They said she probably only has a few months left.' 'Oh my God.' And with that the two of them embraced in the middle of the kitchen and she sobbed and he patted her on the back and said, 'There, there. There, there.' woof woof didn't know what he was going to do, He was starting to get sort of worried. These pirates were going to sacrifice him to their crazy island God and there was nothing he could do to stop them. And what about that big match he was supposed to participate in at Super Series? He couldn't very well wrestle in a big match if he was dead, could he. No, he couldn't. Dead people can't wrestle, he thought. They don't have the proper motor skills! Oh God, he thought. I don't want to die! And then he started to cry and the pirate who was standing uard outside his cell turned around and said, 'Yarrrgh, is you some kind of baby, matey?' And woof replied by attempting so force a, 'Oh fuck off' through the tears but all that came out was a garbled, incoherant grunt. The pirate guard just laughed and took a swig of rum and then walked away. Sometime later on the pirate ship would run into some rocks and the hull would split open and woof woof would find himself presented with an opportunity to escape, which opportunity he would readily grab and swim back to shore and pull himself out of the water and walk home and drink a warm glass of milk and go to bed.
