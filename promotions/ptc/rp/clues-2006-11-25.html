Clues

25 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-25 Steve Watson had no idea where he was being taken. The back of the van was dark, the ride was bumpy, and the two men accompanying him on the trip where hardly fountains of useful information. Steve and Lisa had been separated by the armed men as soon as they got out of the arena and each had been ushered into the cargo area of a white, unmarked step van. Steve felt blessed that he no longer had to listen to Lisa’s nonsense, but he could hardly say he felt good about the latest turn his night had taken. The two guards had stonewalled any attempt Steve had made at gaining even a token of information, leaving Steve’s mind free to create its own suppositions about his plight. Were his captors with Bagley? Or Hainesworth? Or some third party heretofore unheard from. Steve felt fairly confident that the men were there to guard him, but he wasn’t sure whether their assigned duty was to prevent his escape or to keep him from harm. The ride seemed to take about an hour - although Steve was sure that it was actually far shorter - until the van finally slowed and, after a few distinct turns, came to a stop. The back door of the vehicle opened and two more men haul Steve out into the shadows of a large and desolate parking garage. Steve noted that the other van was nowhere in sight. From there Steve was escorted down a long hallway and left alone in what appeared to be the kind of holding cell/interrogation room that one might expect from your typical network TV police procedural. It was here that Steve remained, in silence, until Agent Chambers arrived. Steve recognized Marquis Chambers as the tall man in the blue suit that had spoken to them at the arena. For some reason, Steve felt an odd sense of relief at the lack of formality with which Chambers entered the room. "We were worried when you didn’t answer our emails," said Chambers, "So we figured we might have to extract you." "Extract me?" Steve wasn’t quite sure what this meant. "I’m sorry," apologized Chambers, "Where are my manners? I forget that I know you a whole lot better than you know me. Mr. Watson, I am Special Agent Chambers, Federal Bureau of Investigations – Anti-Trust Division." Chambers extended his hand to shake Watson’s "I am heading the task force charge in the investigation of Heckert, Bagley, and Dannon, LLC." Chambers continued, "And we believe you may have information vital to our inquiry." "Join the crowd," said Steve, "Everyone seems to think I know more about this whole mess than I do? "Or else, perhaps, you actually know more about it than you realize." Steve looked over to the door to where the new voice was emanating. He recognized the man standing there. Chambers started an introduction, "Mr. Watson, this is…" "Chad." said Steve, "Chad Ferris, from out IT department." "Agent Ferris has been our inside man on the investigation for the better part of the last two years," Chambers finished. "Steve," said Ferris, "It appears that you have stumbled across something during you expense report evaluations that could well blow this whole case wise open." "Honestly Chad," answered Steve, "I don’t mean to be vague or evasive here - I did find something; but I never had the chance to actually sit down and figure out what it was." "No?" said Chambers. "Not really," said Steve, "The raw data is all on my laptop. But I lost that back in Denver a little over a week ago." "Yes, you did," inserted Ferris, "And apparently either Bagley or Hainesworth got to it before we did. The GPS had it tracked back to the Executive Offices before it was deactivated." "Look guys," said Steve, "as much as I would truly love to sit here and chat with you about my missing computer and whatnot, I have the distinct feeling that my pregnant wife might be in trouble." "Yes, Mr. Watson," said Chambers, "She probably is. And she won’t be out of trouble until this whole sordid mess is resolved. If you want me to send a car by to pick her up and take her to a safe house, I can do that." "Yeah," said Steve, "I think I might want that." Chambers pulled out his Blackberry and quickly typed in a quick message. "Consider it done," Agent Chambers said as he hit send, "Now I need to ask you to do me a favor in return." "Why don’t I think I like the sound of that?" replied Steve. "We need you to go back in and get that laptop" Chambers continued. "Excuse me," said Steve, "No. No, I don’t think I’ll be doing that." "Steve," Ferris chimed in, "This thing seems to be moving to a whole new level. Hainesworth has been trying to nudge the Old Man out of the way for some time now – planting seeds, building relationships, securing power – but he’s been doing it slowly, subtly. But ever since you made your discovery in the expense reports, he seems to have kicked it up a gear. Now we aren’t sure if the information works for him or against him, but it certainly has proved a motivator and it’s starting to look like things are about to get ugly. And, when that happens, people could get hurt." "You make it sound like some kind of mafia turf war" said Steve. "Yeah," said Ferris, "Something like that." "Mr. Watson," asked Chambers, "do you know who your company’s most important client is?" "Well, I am not really on that end of things," said Steve, "But I do know we do business with a number of higher ups down in Washington – Senators, lobbyists, a few members of the administration. Mr. Heckert keeps his primary office down there. But his personal staff handles all of the day today books, I only get them year end." "Actually Mr. Watson," said Chambers, "Heckert’s portfolio is only a portion of what we are looking at. How much do you know about a company called Onuetige Uitvoer Internationale?" "Yeah, I know them," said Steve, "Belgian import/export company. One of Ed Dannon’s clients. Big in terms of dollar volume, but not what I would necessarily consider an ‘important’ client." "Steve," said Ferris, "OUI as an entity is responsible for 80% of the business brought in be the Bagley/Heckert/Dannon partnership, although only a small portion of that is reported by the limited liability company. And perhaps more importantly, Interpol believes that they are, in all likelihood, a corporate front for the Saudi Roya…" "Chad," Agent Chambers interrupted, "I think we are moving into ‘need to know’ territory, here." "Right," Ferris stood corrected, "My apologies." "Suffice to say, Mr. Watson," said Chambers, "Your employers are into some fairly heavy crap here. And it looks like we are going to need your help to bring them down." "Look guys," said Steve, "This sounds like some serious James Bond stuff we are talking here. I’m just an Assistant CFO -slash- masked professional wrestler." "Oh, so you’re just incredibly smart and physically blessed," said Ferris, "I don’t know what we were thinking. Mark, maybe we should see about calling in Agent Dash if the CIA will let us have him." "Chad," said Steve, "I didn’t say no. I just need you to be really clear about what you are asking of me." Chambers answered this one, "We are asking you to go back in; ingratiate yourself to Bagley; find out where the laptop is; and – if possible – deliver it to us before your match at Round Four of GTT6." "But Chad, if is already in IT," said Steve, "Wouldn’t he be the better one to track down a wayward computer?" "What do you think he’s been trying to do for the past week?" said Chambers, "Trust me; I wouldn’t be pulling a civilian in harms way if I thought I had a better option. And it’s not like you aren’t already completely wrapped up in this thing. I doubt you’d be any safer if we just opened the doors and let you walk out free and clear of this all." "That’s probably true," said Steve, "But aren’t we forgetting about one thing?" "What’s that?" inquired Ferris. "Lisa Somers," answered Steve, "I get the feeling she won’t exactly be cooperative in maintaining my cover." "Actually Mr. Watson," said Chambers, "Lisa Somers will likely prove absolutely essential in maintaining your cover. Especially after you rescue her" "Rescue her?" "That’s right," continued Chambers, "For some reason, Lisa Somers is under the impression that it is members of Haineworth’s group that has grabbed you. And, after a bit of light interrogation on their part, I am certain that your heroic arrival will be all we need to win you all the familial good graces that you’ll need." "And you know something, Steve," added Ferris, "I always got the sense that she may have had a shine towards you already." * * * The "rescue" went off without a hitch. Steve found Lisa huddled in the corner of the storage unit that Chambers had sent him to. She was obviously drained; but, physically, seemed none the worse for wear. Steve had burst in like someone out of a Bruce Willis movie, gun in hand - but knowing he wouldn’t have to use it. He helped Lisa up and together they stepped over the "overpowered" guard before "finding" a car with the keys conveniently left in the ignition and speeding off. Any anger that Lisa might have had towards Steve was now fully redirected towards Frank Hainesworth. How dare he betray her uncle like that? How dare his thugs lay their hands on her? They made him; and they would tear him down. Steve might have felt bad for having any part in the ferocity of venom being spewed if not for the fact that its target had himself set Steve up to play the role of sacrificial pansy just days before. The car arrived at the Bagley Estate without further incident and Lisa was up in her uncle’s study almost before Steve has a chance to put the car in park. There she regaled the Old Man with her tales of Hainesworth’s litany of offenses – sufficiently augmented for effect, Steve was sure. Steve wasn’t exactly sure about everything that went on at the Estate that night. All he knew was that a lot of phone calls were made as the Old Man worked fervently to solidify his power base for the coming battle. The next morning, Steve was awoken early from his guest bed, given a new Armani suit to wear, and told to meet Bagley downstairs. The Old Man was waiting in the car. "So where are we headed?" asked Steve. "To work," replied Bagley, "Hell if I am going to let some snot-nosed usurper run me out of the company I built." "Where’s Lisa?" asked Steve. "She’ll meet us there. I had a few things I needed her to finish up," Bagley replied. Bagley had not been a regular fixture in the HB&D offices in years, preferring to come and go when and if he pleased in the manner that a man of his age and stature had well earned. And he was never there before noon. So when he walked through the glass doors at 9:00 am prompt with Steve at his side, people took notice. Old Man Bagley has instructed Watson to go about his business as if nothing was amiss; but, given that Steve had been out of the office absent all contact for over a week, he wasn’t sure how exactly he was going to accomplish this. Peggy, for one, was thrilled to see Steve again and was there in his office waiting to greet him almost as soon as he sat down at his desk - wanting to know all about Denver. Evidently word had never come back from the Wassermans about his non-attendance at the conference – or else Hainesworth had intercepted it if it did. "It was pretty uneventful, all and all," Steve lied. "I wish I could have brought back some postcards, but you know how full these conference itineraries can get." "Yeah, I’ve heard about those," said Peggy. "So how’s Tim?" Steve asked. "Oh you didn’t hear?" asked Peggy "I guess not." "He’s out of the coma, but doesn’t remember anything about the night of the accident. And, after that Masked Marauder character didn’t pan out, the police have no idea where to go with it." "Of course not," said Steve, "keep me in the loop if there are any further developments." "Absolutely," responded Peggy, "So I guess you still need finish up that expense account review too, huh?" Steve thought that was an odd question for Peggy to ask and decided it best to perhaps take things back underground. "Actually, Peg, it looks like that whole thing was a false alarm," Steve told her, "You’d think with a amount of time and money I spent getting advanced degrees in this sort of thing, I’d be able to catch a simple subtraction error on my own before calling in the National Guard." "Oh my, Steve," Peg felt slightly embarrassed for him. "Yeah, try not to let it get around, okay?" said Steve, knowing full well it would. "Of course not," replied Peggy. "Thanks Peg," Steve said, "But anyway, I have a full week’s work that needs catching up." "Yes, I am sure you do," said Peg, "You know where to find be if you need anything." Steve found it easier to fall back into the flow of things than he’d thought it might be. Numbers are still numbers no matter where you approach them from. They are safe, constant, predictable – not at all like the people who seek to misuse them. Outside of Steve’s office, the tension in the air of Heckert, Bagley, and Dannon was almost palpable - if unstated. No one was used to seeing so much of the Old Man, striding about the halls. Rumors ran from layoffs to retirements to corporate sale and restructuring. If one had studied Frank Hainesworth during this time, they would have noted a man incredibly ill at ease, but doing his best to hide it. Although the CFO had yet to speak to Watson since his return at the side of the Old Man, Steve had caught more than one sidelong glance directed his way from his soon to be erstwhile superior. If there was a battle for dominion over the company taking place, most of the shots were evidently being fired behind closed doors. The feds and Steve had agreed that it was probably best not to make a play for the laptop before he had become fully ensconced in the Old Man’s good graces. Although, with Bagley’s insistence that Steve stay at the Estate for the time being, that was proving perhaps easier than anticipated. Norbert Bagley had made a lot of money during his long life and it was completely alien for him to imagine it not being the prime motivating factor in any human decision. So once he promised Steve Hainesworth’s position and salary, he was certain he had bought his undying loyalty. One of the benefits of staying at the Bagley Estate was the state of the art training facility and A/V room. Steve wasn’t quite sure why a seventy-four year old man needed with a full sized sparring ring; but, according to Lisa, he once was an amateur boxing champ back in his army days – so who was Steve to question a gift horse. If Steve was being honest with himself, he wasn’t sure he deserved to be fighting in the Fourth Round of GTT6 at all. After losing in round one and being played back in, Steve had hardly impressed in the next two rounds – achieving his victories or Bobino and Tigera in ways that he was less that proud of – and he swore to himself that he would change all of that this week when he faced off against the mystery man known only as Ember. He would be ready for anything {and he knew that Ember was capable of just that}. He would prove to the world that he deserved to be there. He was a man on a mission. In more ways than one. Over the next few days, Steve trained at least four hours a night while going about his business at work during the day – trying to maintain a routine while keeping his eyes open for his missing laptop and hoping the world didn’t come crashing down on him again in the mean time. It was some point late on Wednesday that Steve finally worked up the nerve to ask Bagley about the computer. It would really help him if he had access to all the files on his C Drive. But the Old Man didn’t seem to have any idea about what Watson was talking about. And, for whatever reason – call it intuition – Steve believed him. Friday came and Steve had still made no progress on finding the computer. He had told Chambers and Ferris that he wasn’t a secret agent when this all started, and now it looked liked he was proving just that. Steve got to work at his usual time that morning and boarded the elevator for the twelfth floor as he always did. But, just as the doors were closing, a hand reached in. The doors slid back open and in stepped Frank Hainesworth. Steve did his best to ignore the Chief Financial Officer, not meeting his eyes. And, at first, it seems that Hainesworth was trying to pay him the same awkward courtesy. But once the last of their fellow passengers exited the car on the ninth floor, it became apparent that this wasn’t a chance encounter at all as Hainesworth’s hand extended and pushed the stop elevator button just before they arrived at their destination level. "You really screwed up, Steve," Hainesworth broke the silence. "Frank, I don’t think this is the appropriate…" "Yes sir," Frank interrupted him, "You totally picked the wrong horse in this little race." "Maybe so," replied Steve, "But I didn’t have much choice considering that the other horse was in the process of tossing me under a bus for his own selfish gain." "Listen, punk," Hainesworth scolded, "I don’t know what game you and that Lisa girl are playing with the stories you’ve concocted for that old man’s ears, but it’s not going to work. Because it’s already too late. Bagley’s going down. I’ve already taken things far enough to make certain of that. All you are doing with your foolish actions is making things a lot messier than they have to be. "Why are you telling me this, Frank?" said Steve, "I hope you aren’t still trying to ‘protect’ me. Because, last time, you almost did me in with your tender care." "No, I am telling you because there is nothing you can do to stop in. I am telling you because I know you will go running to you sugar daddy benefactor and tell him everything that I just told you," Frank expounded, "And while you’re at it, why don’t you go ahead and inform him that I have taken the liberty to called a board meeting for next Monday? So let him know that he has about three days to prepare his resignation letter if he wants to exit with grace." "I get the feeling that’s probably not going to happen," said Steve. "Oh, I am counting on the fact that it won’t happen," replied Hainesworth, "What’s the fun of a full overthrow if you don’t walk out with a head or two to stick up on a pike outside the gates?" And, with that, Hainesworth hit the button to start the elevator on the completion of its journey. "And Steve," said Hainesworth, "you should be ashamed of yourself running around with the boss’s niece when you have a pregnant wife at home – or wherever." "I am not doing anything with that girl," protested Steve. "Of course you aren’t" chortled Frank, "So how is Elise, anyway?" Hainesworth laughed contemptuously as he disembarked the elevator. Why, though? Why the laugh? Did Frank know more about what was happening with Elise than Steve did? Steve had to admit that he had gotten so wrapped up in recent events that he had just assumed the feds had taken care of her like they said they would. Had they though? Steve had been getting used so much of late, he wasn’t sure who to trust and with what. Steve quickly made his way from the elevator to the IT hallway before ducking into Chad Ferris’ cube. "Chad, we need to talk," said Steve, "Where is she?" "Steve," rebuked Chad, "We really shouldn’t be having this conversation here." "I just had a run in with Hainesworth" Steve explained, "I made a comment. I know you guys are telling me she is safe, but I just need to know for certain that’s the case." "Understood," said Chad, "Would a hand written letter suffice?" "It would," replied Steve. "We’ll get it to you tonight night at GTT." "Okay," Steve found that satisfactory. "Steve," said Chad, "While I have you here; any luck with the laptop?" "Bagley doesn’t have it. So I am thinking it must be with Hainesworth’s group. And, given where I stand with him, it doesn’t look like I am going to be of great assistance here." "That’s too bad," said Chad. "Yeah, I’m sorry," Steve apologized, "I guess you should have gone with AgentDash, huh? Thanks for getting me that letter though." "No problem" responded Chad, "but at least we have a better bead on its location. Steve walked out of the IT Department feeling a bit better about the status of Elise, but still unable to shake the sense of impending dread that weighed down heavily upon his chest. But that all changed the moment he stepped into the doorway of his own office; because, there, right in the middle of his desk, sat a familiar looking Hewlett-Packard laptop computer. Steve rushed over to the desk. It was his computer all right, right down to the dent in the casing from when he dropped it out of his bag while getting out of the cab in Atlanta and they had to replace the LCD screen. Steve opened it up and hit the power button. The screen flashed and the drives whirred to life. It was then that Steve noticed the note sitting on his desk right beside the PC. He picked it up and read it: Steve, I thought that you might need this now that you are back at work. Please have the expense summaries on my desk by the end of the day. And don’t forget to return this machine to IT when you pick up you severance check on Monday. Still Your Boss, Frank W. Hainesworth Steve looked back down at the computer. It was still trying to boot, but unsuccessfully. Slowly it started to dawn on Steve; the hard drives had been wiped clean.
