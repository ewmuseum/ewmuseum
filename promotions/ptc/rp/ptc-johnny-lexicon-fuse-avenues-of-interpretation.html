IG29 - Johnny Lexicon (FUSE)

12 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Avenues of Interpretation The salty sea-breeze washes over the beach like the warm breath of a lover caressing and consoling all those that it touches. Suspended by a thin string and hanging high above the heads of children and their mothers wobbles a multicolored kite, dipping and weaving upon that lover's whisper; gently guided by a calloused and battle worn hand. The sun, dipping low on the horizon, kisses Johnny's skin with it's last tempermental rays before plunging beyond the border between day and twilight. A plain white shirt left wholly unbuttoned flutters and pulls on his broad shoulders, matched by the white slacks covering his legs. Bare toes wiggle and work themselves through the sand as he walks slowly, his footprints heavy and left deep as he follows his kite with languid nonchalance. Casual indifference to the suit clad man of concern who approaches quickly. "Mister Lexicon." His voice is a clear soprano and only slightly cracked by his forty years of service. "Detective Hoffman." Johnny's own voice is a rolling baritone, melodic and deceptive in its nature. "You're a very hard man to find." "I've been here all day." Johnny replies, yet to pull his eyes from his kite in the sky. "Flying a kite?" Hoffman stops a step behind the wrestler trying valiantly to control the chaotic motions of his ankle length duster. "A woman at the bar suggested I give it a try." "That's great Johnny, do you have a problem with answering your phone?" "My phone." It was a statement and not a question. Realizing this, Hoffman allows Johnny his cryptic pauses of melodrama. "That's kind of hard. I left it somewhere in the gulf of Mexico." "Jesus Johnny, you weren't supposed to leave the country!" Hoffman is instantly irritated. By Johnny's indifference and more-so by his disregard for the detective's very presence. "I'm back now, no harm. No foul." "Now you listen to me." Hoffman puts a stern hand on the wrestler's shoulder. Johnny snaps his head around and locks those blue eyes onto Hoffman's own as his kite plummets silently out of the clouds to the waves as they crash upon the shore. His expression is nothing short of an emotionless mask. "The only reason you're not behind bars is because we made a deal. You give us Marcus Van Mill and in return I let you keep this little facade." "My father's dead." Johnny looks away and starts rolling in his string. "We've gotten reports he back in Paris, operating as usual." This causes Johnny to stop. "Ten years now I've let you keep your new name, Johnny Lexicon, and now you're going to help me. We're going to Paris." "I've got a prior engagement." "Prior engagement my ass, you wanna keep being a wrestler?" Johnny doesn't respond and so the detective carries on. "You want to keep this life you've built for yourself then you're going to come to Paris and we're going to bring Marcus in. If you don't then Johnny Lexicon dies and Chris Van Mill goes to jail. For life." Hoffman puts as much emphasis on his words as he can muster but Johnny simply continues to wind his kite out of the oceans grip. A long moment of tense apprehension hangs between them, the detective waiting for Johnny to agree or sign his own warrant. "When did your reports come in?" "Yesterday just after noon." Hoffman is quick to respond but masks his smile as Johnny collects his kite and turns to face him. "One of our agents sighted him on a stake-out. We have reason to believe he's planning something big." "He's not in Paris." "How would you-" "My father has been doing this longer than I've been alive. If there's one thing he knows, it's how to get away from you." "Alright." Hoffman swallows his pride with an audible gulp. "Then where is he?" "Berlin." "Berlin?" "Berlin." The pair of them start heading off the beach. "If he let himself be seen in Paris, he's misleading your agency, he's gone to Berlin to gather his resources. You have a jet ready?" "With the engines running." "I have a very important match on Sunday, if we're not back in time then I take my father's position and you will never see me again." Hoffman stops and again puts a hand on Johnny's shoulder. "You don't threaten me you little-" "Is it cliche to say that it's not a threat but a promise? You need me because I'm smarter than you, don't forget that and never." He brushes the hand off his shoulder. "Touch me again." Johnny walks away as Hoffman stifles a growl and eventually kicks up his heels and follows. Less than an hour later Johnny is sleeping fitfully in an cushioned chair, headphones pumping soft melodies into his brain as an entire team of agents bustle and prepare themselves all around him. "How can he sleep?" A young man barely halfway through his twenties asks as he pauses his perusal of a booklet. "Gather in everybody." Hoffman calls, waving his hands to the half dozen people gathered. Upon having the rooms attention, minus a slumbering Johnny, Hoffman continues. "We're going after Marcus Van Mill, you've all read the profile. He's wanted in Paris, Germany, England, Israel, and the good old U-S of A. We know he's got ties to the Governments of several countries and to ensure a conviction, I want him back in DC. We're going in quietly and then we're out even quieter. We have to assume he knows we're on to him so we proceed with the utmost caution. I don't want heroes, I want agents. Are we clear." A chorus of "Yes sir"'s answer his question. "What about him?" A redhead in a crisp blue suit points at Johnny. "Christopher Van Mill." "His son?" The woman asks incredulously. "The very same. He's lived and operated the past ten years as Johnathon William Lexicon, professional wrestler. Prior to his name change he was considered Marcus' right hand man and is wanted in as many countries as his father. He's helping us in exchange for a blind eye, but do not trust him. He is as smart and manipulative as they come, he is here to help us and no matter what he says we are not his friends. You do him no favors." "Of course sir." The red-head lowers her emerald green eyes to a note pad as her pen scribbles across. "We'll be landing in a couple of hours, get some sleep, have something to eat, this is the day we've been working for for five years people. No mistakes we're coming back with Marcus Van Mill." There's a series of nods as Hoffman collects a folder and moves towards the front of a plane. "He's lying to you." The baritone startles the young male agent and he turns brown eyes on a half smiling Johnny as he watches Hoffman. "Excuse me?" "He's lying. He's been on this case for about twelve years and never so much as heard my father's voice." Those brown eyes turn now to the fifty something detective. "Johnny Lexicon." Johnny offers his hand. "Terrence Blance." They shake. "That french?" "Uh, yeah. I'm from Quebec." "Don't tell me they're pulling Canadian's in on this. I thought Hoffman was greedier than that." Terrence chuckles. "No. I moved to Cleveland when I was six." "That explains why you don't have an accent." "So you're. You're his son?" "We're not afforded the luxury of deciding who gives birth to us." "Blance." Hoffman snaps and the young agent clears his throat. "We're not supposed to talk to you." Johnny cocks a grin and leans back in his chair. "I understand." He replaces the headphones but does not feign sleep once again. His eyes curious on the redheaded green eyed agent as she tries with all her might to not return his look. "Candice Walker." Terrence offers. "Huh?" Johnny pulls his headphones aside. "Her name is Candice Walker, Hoffman pulled her out of Michigan a few months ago to work this case." "She's a profiler?" "Criminal Behavior Analyst to be specific. She's helped on seven of the last ten major serial killer busts." Johnny nods slightly while a grin tugs mightily at the corners of his mouth. "So why is she working this case? Last I understood Marcus was considered a terrorist, not a serial killer." "Is there so much of a difference?" "Purpose." Johnny doesn't miss the look Terrance gives him and continues. "Terrorists don't kill people because it gives them enjoyment, but because they see something they want changed. They have a purpose, however misguided their methods the lives lost are considered." He pauses. "Collateral damage." "Is that why you did it?" "It?" Johnny doesn't offer the young agent spite or anger, only a smile of understanding. "File says you worked with your father for years." "Marcus doesn't find his monsters in the closet or in the shadows under a bed. He makes his own. I won't say he's responsible for what I've done, or for what anybody does in his service. He offers people a means to an end, he gives them an ideal. And an ideal is the hardest kind of criminal to catch because it lives in the very heart and soul of the people it touches." "Is that why you're here?" "Don't misunderstand, I'm here because Hoffman has got me by the balls. I haven't taken a life in over ten years, I was blinded by the same ideology that grips the hundreds of people working for my father. And when I finally opened my eyes." He doesn't look Terrance in the face. "I didn't like the person I saw in the mirror. But once you've started a train moving it takes more than a change of heart to stop it. I wanted out, but I didn't want to go to jail." "That's why the name change? The switch of careers?" "No." Johnny speaks as one would when explaining to a child. "Ironically it was Marcus who gave me my way out. Johnny Lexicon, that is, the real Johnny Lexicon was doing humanitarian missions in Thaiwan. If Marcus had come for me himself the mistake never would have been made, but even I had to admit the resemblance between us was uncanny. An innocent man died so that I might have a fresh start. They say that God works in mysterious ways, I wasn't going to question or refuse his mercy. Wrestling was Lexicon's dream. He gave me his life, the best I could do for him is realize his dreams. It just turned out that I'm good at it." Terrance nods the nod of one who's putting the pieces of a complex puzzle together in his mind. "I'm surprised none of this is in my file." "Your file is confidential." Terrence stops talking as one would when they think they've revealed more than they should. Johnny puts a reassuring hand on the young agents shoulder. "I haven't been the person Hoffman thinks I am in ten years. After this." There's a morose note to Johnny's tone. "After this that person will be dead forever." "Blance." Hoffman snaps again, his brow knotted together. "'Course sir." Terrance offers Johnny a half hearted smile before retreating towards his supervisor. Candice Walker turns that emerald gaze of hers onto Johnny as Terrance passes by without a word. Her lips are tight and drawn together, her eyes narrow as though she peers past the layers of skin and flesh encasing the stranger with more than one name. Cutting through blood and bone as though she might catch a glimpse of the heart and soul of a killer. Johnny winks and she looks away almost instantly, her pen once again running rampant across her note pad and Johnny chuckles to himself, replacing the headphones over his ears. It feels as though his eyes have been closed for only moments before they've landed. Agents collect themselves and their gear, disembarking under the cover of darkness and into the chilly night. It's early spring but large soft snowflakes flutter down from above, melting as they land on anything warmer than ice and creating a sheet of ice across the ground. Perhaps it's luck, or perhaps something more clandestine, that allows them to even land in this weather. There's a trio of black Durango's waiting as the team unloads their equipment from the plane. Johnny, his hands cuffed and a black hood pulled over his head, makes his way silently behind Terrance. There's a startled cry as Terrance loses his footing and spills to his backside. Surprisingly it's Johnny to respond first, hands bound he leans low and hauls Terrance to his feet. "Thanks." He says, but before Johnny can respond his elbows are grabbed in iron grips and he's led towards the middle Durango. Without pause he's loaded inside and out of sight, a heavy set black fellow climbs behind the wheel and Candice into the passenger seat. A few moments later Johnny's silently watching Berlin pass by through half fogged windows and blurred by the wisps of billowing snow that clouds the street lights. "Hands." Candice demands and Johnny offers her his wrists long enough to unlock the cuffs. "Appreciate it." Rubbing his wrists Johnny makes no move to accept the wires being passed to him. "I'm not wearing a wire." He states. Candice passes an unsure look to the heavy black man driving as he scrutinizes Johnny in the rear-view mirror. "It's for your safety." She explains. Johnny frowns. "I was under the impression that this was a snatch and grab." "It's not my job to brief you on the assignment." Candice says coldly. "I was instructed to outfit you with a wire, and you'll either put it on or we can leave you behind." Johnny smiles and leans forward until their faces are only inches away. "You can let me out here." "No." The black man says, his voice the same texture as stones rubbing against each other. "Forget the wire." Candice retreats to her seat and Johnny leans back casually. "I get the impression you lot don't like me very much." "Don't get me wrong Mr. Lexicon." Her voice is like ice. "We've dedicated our lives to putting people like you behind bars." "People like me?" Johnny provokes. "Last I heard it wasn't a habit to employ Criminal Behavior Analysts on terrorism cases." Lips pursed tightly those emerald eyes send daggers at the man in the back seat. "On the plane, you were profiling me." It's not a question. "No I wasn't." She lies poorly. It takes only a slanted look from Johnny to tell her that she's lost. "Tell me, what kind of person am I?" Candice takes a moment to check her watch and choose her words before continuing. "You're a person who suffers from sever OCD." "Obsessive compulsive disorder? Funny I don't notice that." She twists in her seat to look him in the eyes. "Some people chew paper, or keep their houses meticulously clean. You play with people, you can't help yourself. You don't have many people you would consider friends do you? That's because to a person like you friends are only pawns. You're the type of person who rarely shows anger." "That's a bad thing?" Johnny veils his expression with a half hearted smile. "You've heard the expression: Don't get mad, get even? You're also a womanizer." "I love women." Johnny protests. "You love the idea of a woman. Weak, submissive, compliant. You're the type of person who doesn't allow another to have the last word. If it doesn't end in your favor, it just doesn't end. You don't wear jewelery, you have no tattoos, nothing to outwardly identify you or draw attention away from what your saying. If they're not paying attention, they're not being manipulated, like you manipulated Agent Blance." "You got all that from a plane ride?" Johnny composes himself. "I read your file." "So you've access to confidential information." "I have authorization to review any and all information pertinent to a case." "We're here." Johnny interrupts, looking out the window. "Go another block." He instructs. The black agent nods as Candice lifts a radio to her mouth and relays the information as Johnny passes it to her. A few minutes later the team is assembling a block down and around the corner. All but Candice Walker and Terrence Blance suit themselves in body armor and load their weapons. Agent Blance reviews blue prints of the building on his computer as Johnny walks them through what he remembers. Hoffman instructs his agents forward but remains a moment longer, his aged eyes boring into Johnny's own. "For your sake, I hope he's in there." "He's there." Johnny replies without doubt. Hoffman turns and wheels his hand in the air and the squad moves out with hardly a sound. The building is well secured but as Johnny had instructed they avoided two camera's in the back and subdued a guard before he knew there was a raid. They bypass the elevator and later investigation would confirm the pressence of a switch to manually release the brake. Everything happened nearly exactly as Johnny had laid out and no more than ten minutes later a voice crackles over the radio. "We've found him." It's Hoffman. "It looks like we're too late." "What's wrong?" Terrence queries. "Looks like mass execution. There's bodies everywhere. We're gonna need Lexicon to identify the body." Late the next day Johnny opens the door to his hotel room, throws his bag to the side and falls onto the mattress. His eyes haven't the chance to close before there's a rapid knock on his door. Groaning he drags himself to his feet and approaches the portal, without hesitation he pulls it wide. Candice Walker hands a briefcase to him with an expressionless mask. "You gonna stay?" Johnny asks. Candice smiles slowly and steps into the room. "Somebody wants to talk to you." She says as Johnny closes the door, retrieving a cell phone she dials a number. Johnny places the briefcase on his bed and clicks the locks, lifting it slowly his lips spread at the site of stacks. A half million dollars. "He's here boss." Candice passes Johnny the phone and he puts it to his ear. He seems to be listening as Agent Walker reaches around him from behind and starts unbuttoning his shirt, pressing her lips into the nape of his neck. "It's good to hear your voice Dad, how's Mexico?"
