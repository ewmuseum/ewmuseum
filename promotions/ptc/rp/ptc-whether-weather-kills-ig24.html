Whether Weather Kills (IG24)

24 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
There are a lot of things that I don’t know about. One is cricket. All this reverse swing, and leg before wicket, and bails and seams, and over, and out; it sounds more like a walkie-talkie than a sport. And calling yellow clothes ‘whites’, and wearing those jumpers. (If cricket is played in the summer months and football the winter, then why do cricketers need jumpers, and footballers not? (If someone who plays cricket is a cricketer, and someone who plays football a footballer, why is someone who plays rugby not a rugbyer? (Snookerer? Tiddlywinkser? – Tiddlywinker?))) Another is why oil and water don’t mix. I know that they don’t mix. But I don’t know why. Another is why uniform little bubbles appear in water if you leave your glass half-empty for a long time. Or half-full. Another is time signatures. Another is the Futureheads; except that I do know that they don’t seem to know about time signatures either, or at least that’s what my friend Marcus said when I told him that I don’t know about time signatures. Another is the earlobe. Drugs. (I have never abused a drug.) Children. (I have never abused a child.) Why all the cows in a field face the same way. Publishing. The digestive system of a flea. Felching. (I don’t think I want to.) (Know about it. Not wanting to do it is taken for granted.) (Right?) Shredding – Marcus explained it once but I can’t remember. (That’s shredding with reference to guitars, not paper-shredding. I know a little about paper-shredding. But maybe not enough:) Paper-shredding. Life in South America. Life with cerebral palsy. Cerebral palsy. Nappies. Linux. The Beat generation. Currency markets. I think I would like to know about more things; I think I would like to know about most things. But there are not enough hours in the day. * Why is it only ever fat people who are described as ‘bubbly’? Is it because they’re shaped like bubbles? * There is one thing that I do know a lot about. No, it’s not wrestling. I know that’s what you expected – considering Where I Am and Who You Are and What This Is. But no. I am not a wrestler. I am only entering the Infinite Gauntlet because time is running out; time is not as infinite as this competition. (Can there really be degrees of infinity? X is more infinite than Y? Finite and infinite; black and white; but is there grey?) I have but twenty-three days to make good on a nine-years-old promise. It was a bet. (But betting, that’s not the thing I know a lot about, either.) We both picked out the least likely job for each other, me and Lindsay. Hers was stripper. Mine was wrestler. (This was before it all happened. Obviously.) What I do know about is the polar jet stream; it’s a ribbon of high velocity wind spun around the earth by the Coriolis effect, basically. It affects/influences/dictates our weather, basically. And everyone’s like who cares? and so what? and fuck off before I kick you in the balls?. But it’s important, because it affects/influences/dictates our weather, basically. And our weather, it affects/influences/dictates our lives, basically. Like do you go here or there or nowhere. And does this happen or does this not happen. Everything you do affects everything I do; action and consequence; Sliding Doors. This raindrop on this day could scupper that silver wedding anniversary on that day. 999 – fast/slow. (Or 911 if you’re in America. Or 112 if you’re on a mobile. And that one’s Europe-wide. I think. I don’t know.) Ice on the road. Heatwave; air conditioning; nuclear meltdown. Just like that. Snap; it was sunny today so the world fell apart. This and that. Weather. So what I know a lot about is the polar jet stream. * I don’t know very much about the subtropical jet stream. But probably more than most. I studied meteorology. I am twenty-seven years old and live on my own in an East London bedsit. My next-door-neighbour has blood up the walls and dirty mattresses. My room is pristine. I hate this. * “So if you’re lonely, why do you say you’re not lonely?” The Fratellis, “Whistle For The Choir”. * I know why there are leap years. I know why leaves turn brown and dry and shrivel and shrink and fall, fall to the dirty, horse-chestnut-strewn, footprint-ravaged, downtrodden ground in the autumn. (Or the fall if you’re in America. Or 112 if you’re on a mobile, and that one’s Europe-wide, I think; I don’t know.) I know why a balloon will stick to your jumper if you rub it against it hard and repeatedly, and why that same balloon won’t explode with a pop but simply deflate with a whimper if you stick the needle in a particular way, and why the set of twelve that this very balloon came in costs £1.29 where ten years ago it cost 85p. But these aren’t very consequential things to know. * You have blonde hair. You tie it back with a pink hair-tie or sometimes blue. Maybe even white. Some days you might use other colours but I don’t see you every day. You’re sixteen years old, yet I adore your every movement. You’re sixteen years old, so I adore your every movement. You only work Saturdays, when I adore your every movement. Everyone thinks I'm a bit sad for always volunteering to work Saturdays. There’s meant to be a rota. But I always do it. To see you. You have blue eyes, big wide irises that gleam in the strip lighting from above. Your small black pupils expand and contract and dilate. Your eyes are sometimes bloodshot. I don’t think you get enough sleep. You have lips. Everyone has lips; well nearly everyone, not people whose faces have been shot off, like that girl in Invisible Monsters, which I didn’t think was that good, by the way. She shot it off herself, by the way, you find out near the end, by the way; I think I just spoilt it for you. Well I would have if you were even listening. By the way. You have lips. They’re not big or wide or full; they’re not ruby or mocha or rose. They’re just lips; quite normal lips. Just normal lips; who could imagine that normal lips could inspire such conflict in me? I want so much to kiss them, but they’re sacrosanct. I love to see them move; to open, to close together, to form an ‘o’ or open wide to laugh or shout. You don’t shout. I don’t know why I said shout. Why don’t you shout? Well. I'm nothing to shout about. * Why does the sun come up? I know that it does and when it does and what it does, but why? Why does the sun come up? Why do any of us even happen? * I am five feet and six-and-a-quarter inches tall. Deleting all but the two first and one last words from that sentence would make it a lie. * “She’s hearing voices call her; she’s hearing voices warn her.” Bloc Party, “She’s Hearing Voices”. * So what happened, was that Lindsay, she started getting these phone calls, and she was annoyed but not scared, and then one day they blew her chest apart with a shotgun. * I wear trousers with a 30” waist and a 34” inside leg measurement. I am thus tall and skinny. * It was a 17 Mach 2 caliber, 41” long; that’s including 24” of barrel. Its nominal weight was 8 lbs. You can buy exactly the same gun from winchesterguns.com for $1,014 (MSRP). I might. * I like v-neck t-shirts because when I wear them a little bit of fuzz pokes out and it makes me feel like I have a hairy chest. My arms are thin and nothing happens when I tense my biceps. Until I was twenty-three I thought the pectoral muscles were in the buttocks. * “Shotgun wedding, shotgun wedding.” Panic! At The Disco, “Boys Will Be Boys”. * That very day. That – very – day. The practical me moaned, “But how will I divorce a dead woman?” The hard-up me realised with a wave of shock that the place I’d rented the suit from would never take it back now. All the others mes caught all the other hers in their arms and wept over their dying bodies. One day earlier, and I’d have been a bachelor still. It was cruel to contemplate it, but her death had ruined my life. Being me, it was hard enough to find a first wife, let alone a second. I told people I was divorced, because it was easier. For them and for me, I thought. But it being easier made things a lot harder. Divorced at twenty-three leaves little room for manoeuvre. So I went back. To study. My first degree, in Accountancy, was just as worthless to me as the lifeless body of the woman I’d loved. I never visited her grave. I'm not sure I remember which it is, or even which cemetery. No, I do remember which cemetery. But not which grave. To study. Meteorology. Because the weather had killed my wife within seconds of our being bonded in holy matrimony. Because those phone calls would never have started if it hadn’t been raining. * You don’t shout, because I'm nothing to shout about. I adore your every movement, because you’re her, but not. Marcus knows about the Futureheads and designs computer games for a living. * Is zero plural or singular? * (I have never abused a drug.), but my neighbour does regularly. I’ve seen him. He leaves his door open and once I wanted to borrow some sellotape, because you know, when you need sellotape you’re not going to go all the way down to the shops to buy some, you just want some sellotape, you just want some fucking sellotape right away. So even though loud parties and bed-heads against thin walls had put me off any form of social contact with the Guy Next Door, I knocked, timidly, heart pumping, and got a “Come in, it’s open”. It’s like a scene from Trainspotting. (I walked out of the cinema. I don’t want to see needles into skin up close like that. It’s distressing.) He’s sitting there, belt round his arm and between his teeth, whatever foul substance he’s planning to inject is in the needle, and he’s just closing in. But then he sees me – spits out the belt, as if he’s forgotten that he invited me in just point eight three seconds ago – and carefully lays the needle down; “Alright mate, what do you want?” Sellotape. As if. * Why don’t square brackets form a square? [] * (I have never abused a child.), and nor has my neighbour, but my friend when I was little, I think his dad used to beat him up. He’d come round mine a lot – I mean a lot, and at the oddest times; not straight after school or straight after dinner, but half-past-ten once when even my mum had gone to bed, or six in the morning before we were even up. And bruises, not on his face, never his face, but on his ribs, and his arms. We saw in the changing rooms. He wore second-hand uniform; it was the wrong colour, but only slightly. The school didn’t say anything. He cried sometimes if a teacher raised his or her voice. Even at someone else. His name was Joseph. * “Touch the divide.” Muse, “City Of Delusion”. * It was raining that day; it’s only London, but it was raining hard; I'm talking Florida hard. She saw him run out, a package in his hand, leather jacket on, properly sprinting, being chased we think/thought. Dark wet locks flopping soppy against his neck. White face turning towards the car. She was only going thirty-three in a thirty. But it was raining hard; I'm talking Florida hard. The brakes locked. * Why do we inconvenience 100% of our people for the benefit of those who provide 2% of our national output, in employing daylight savings time to suit our farmers? * I read books about the weather. I don’t watch the weather reports on TV; they’re too simplistic. The Internet is my saviour. You don’t study meteorology to be a meteorologist. You study meteorology to understand. And after studying meteorology, I understood. A blocking anticyclone. Volatility. A pressure gradient on the increase. Scales tipped. The straw that broke the camel’s back. Cloudburst. Depression. Too much depression. My name is Horace Winstanley, and yours? You decide. After studying meteorology, I understood. * “East London is a vampire; it sucks the joy right out of me.” Bloc Party, “Song For Clay”. * You know who I am. -- if BBCode supported strikethrough some of this would look neater.
