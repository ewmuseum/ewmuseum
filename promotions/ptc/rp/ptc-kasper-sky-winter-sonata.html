Kasper Sky: Winter Sonata

19 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Title: Winter Sonata Her stare was as cold as ice, unwavering. Arrowed into tiny sharp slits, the ocean blue swirled into a whirlpool of hate, revolving her anger around the index finger pressed against the icy metal. Her fingers were numb, blood spiraling back to the other end of her body. The wintry breeze caressed her pale face, its frigid fingers sending a chill down her spine as billowing flakes of snow clouded her vision. With her lips set in firm line, she braved the cold buffeting against her smooth skin, ignoring the moisture that welled up in her eyes that were becoming clouded with a shimmering film of tears. She was but a shadow of her former innocence, drawn unwillingly into the worldly constraints of hate and fear. Deep inside, where once she was so vibrantly alive, she was already dead. Killed by the lies of mortals, and numbed by the malicious whispers of deceit. Death to her was meaningless. After all, what was death to one who was already damned? Nevertheless, every tale has a beginning. But with the offerings of a beginning, there was also the promise of an end. Already with death we are talking about endings, about finality, but every tale has to have a beginning before it gets to its conclusion. So first let me take you back through the realms of time, back to where anything began. Travel with me back to the time where the seeds were already being sown that would inevitably lead to the events of this day. And yet, after you contemplate everything from the outside, you get a glimpse of the plans of the heavens. That which we call fate. And through all the jigsaw pieces of eternal memories, I will paint you a beautiful picture out of broken dreams. Hopes that were shattered and burned, fears that were exposed and exploited, and kings shamed in the glory of their own thrones. I will show you all that, and more. I will define you the frailty of the mortal faith, in the blurred line between family and enemies. We all traverse the same rocky path; with the hope that one day it will bring us to within a hair of our imminent grandeur. For some, that day will never come. I can almost hear it in the chill of the evening breeze. An ending that will be played to the sonata of the winter winds. <><><><><> He brushed the blonde wisp of hair covering his crystal blue eyes with a deft flaunt of his right hand, the white snow glimmering in the morning sun. The gutsy wind swept through, fluttering the ice as he rolled from side to side, cackling with heartfelt laughter. “Hurry up, slow kid!” he screamed through his lungs, the shrill of his voice echoing through the trees. Footsteps followed shortly, the friction between ice and feet sounded crisp enough that he was able to hear his sister coming from a distance. He smiled, a chill running down his spine as he giggled to himself in growing numbness from the cold. She trudged through the snow heavily, heaving huge breaths of air with each passing moment. “Why did you leave me behind?” she questioned with a touch of annoyance, pulling her hat down closer to her face. He answered her question with a grin spread across his pale complexion, and pulled himself up from the bundle of white. “Come on, let’s head to the cliff. I’ll show you a view that you’ll never see again.” He spoke excitedly, his oceanic blue eyes sparkling in the light. “This had better be good,” she muttered. With pouted lips, she grudgingly reached out for her brother’s hand. “Do you miss mum?” He turned his head, surprised at the sudden question. He thought for a moment, and then another. “I do.” A soft mutter was all that he could muster at that point of time. He frowned, trying to recall the faint memories that he had of his mother. She left them when he was only at the age of five, leaving them to their father to hold care over them both. He cringed, his face darkened in grim. “What was she like?” she asked, a glimmer of hope in her voice. All he could do was smile back. She was holding on to every dream that she could imagine from the stories that he had been telling her. And sadly, that was the only thing she could have done. Piecing together a beautiful dream. “She was beautiful, as beautiful as the snow that you’re threading on right now.” He smiled again, sadness slowly seeping through his skin. “I wish I can still remember her.” “You still do,” he whispered softly, close to her ear. “In your dreams, she will always remain beautiful.” <><><><><> I am at the final chapter of my life. Standing here right now, looking back to the days and years that had gone by accompanied by despair and hope. But yet, repaid by none. Burned from within by the memories of my mother, who left me when I’ve yet learned to spell her name. Ridden of imaginations of her beautiful smile and her glistening face, she is but a shadow of hope torn mercilessly into shattered dreams. It would be better if I were dead. Life has been but a dream riddled with questions, broken into pieces by the undying hate of fate. Through the years, I had prayed for nothing more than the jigsaw pieces to fall complete. And now they have. I wish they hadn’t. Time is running out for me to atone for the sins of my past. I wish I can turn back the hands of time, and live through all this again. But yet, I can’t. The memories that I lost during those five years of unconsciousness, I have now regained. I remember everything. The deaths, the lies, and the undying hate within the bloodlines of mortals. I had committed an unforgivable sin at the price of love. I had killed at the cost of despair. Everything still feels like a dream. I wish it were. But yet when I stare across the names of my parents carved into the hardened stone, reality just seems to drown me to the depths of eternity. Child of the damned. That’s how the gates of heaven will call my name. And yet at the back of my mind, a voice seemed to be telling me something different. Maybe I was the one who saw everything in an obscure manner. Maybe I am the damned. <><><><><> “You killed father!” Krystal snarled, her lips twisted in a fit of rage as she tried hard to control her anger. Her grip tightened on the metal barrel, her fingers shaking feverishly as she directed her sight through the middle of her brother’s figure. The cold was eating deep in her flesh, sending shocks of nerves down her body. Kasper peered across the cliff, deep down into the heavenly sky darkened by the evening cloud. Slowly, he smiled. A tinge of contentment filled his expression, tears welled up within his crystal blue eyes. “I did. I won’t deny that.” He whispered softly, but yet the humble tone of his voice echoed through the mountainside, resonating across using air as its medium. “Why did you do it?” He turned around and faced his sister. It had been ten years since he last saw her, and she had grown into a beautiful woman. Almost as beautiful as his mother. “Out of love,” he replied, shortly. Krystal’s eyes widened incredulously. “Love?” she questioned. “You call killing my father being done out of love?” She waved the metal around aimlessly, almost hysterically. She was nearing breaking point, wandering in between the thin line between hate and despair. “Yes. Love.” <><><><><> I walked through the kitchen door, fresh from the rigid workload of school. Curious from the noises I heard from the outside, I stepped in cautiously. And there she was, the missing piece from the past years of my life. Struggling in between my father and a kitchen knife, I remembered her face in an instant. I was fifteen at that time, and I never remembered what exactly happened next. But somehow, I found the strength to protect the mother that I never had quite learned to love from the sharp jaws of death. But yet, I was rewarded by a twist of fate as death claimed another in the form of my father. By my own hands. There she stood, by the doorway, her eyes widening her horror. My sister, as tears started flowing down her cheeks. I was already at a loss of what to do. It was at that exact moment, that I thought of killing myself. Running down the road as fast as I could with a uniform drenched in red, I flew down across the flow of wind, taking me deep into the forest. I cried. I cried hard. I recalled the moments of when I pierced the blade deep into my father’s chest, and then everything else went blank. I didn’t even know where my mother was after that. I remembered nothing. I plunged. Deep into the lake. Trying to hold my breath as long as I can in those few minutes, as memories came flooding through my head like thorns forcing deep down my skin. I thought I was dead. For the next five years, I was as good as dead. The brain damage from the plunge forced me into a coma that would last five years, as doctors almost gave up the hope of recovery. But recover I did, but at the price of losing my memories. I lost the most valuable possession in my life, and I wasn’t even aware. I started a new life, a new beginning. In a different town, in a different way of upbringing. And then I had to find the means to support myself; I had to start making my own living. That was when I became a wrestler, under the guidance of the man who kindly volunteered to take me in from the hospital. Until now, all I did was try to piece together my past. One bit at a time, slowly unraveling the mysteries of my life. I never even knew I had a sister up until now. And then the memories flooded back. Maybe ‘now’ is a bit too late. <><><><><> A tear creased her pale cheek, as she shook fervently, her energy sapped away from her body. Krystal lowered her right hand, and collapsed to her knees. And there she cried, her tears almost seemed never-ending. “Why didn’t you tell me all this before?” She managed to blurt out in between her shudders, trying to keep eye contact with her brother. “I never got a chance.” “Where is my mother now?” Kasper looked down, his light blonde hair covering most of his face as he tried to answer the question in the best possible way. “She’s gone.” She stopped crying, dead in the realms of time. “When?” she stammered. “A couple of years ago. That was the last time I saw her.” He walked over to his sister, threading slowly on the snow, careful in his steps. Kasper slowly picked her up and brought her to her feet. “I’m sorry, Krystal.” “Please tell me where she is buried. I really need to know.” He pulled a piece of paper from deep inside his pocket, and pushed it into her smooth right palm. “At least now I know, that before I go, I have tried to seek redemption.” “Thank you, Kasper.” Krystal took a step back, and peered at the piece of paper. She managed a soft smile. “I love you.” She looked up, her blonde hair fluttering in the winter winds. She peered across the mountainside, deep into the evening heavens. He was gone. <><><><><> My final chapter is almost complete. Standing at the sunset my glory, I seek for one last glimmer of hope to rescue what had been a lifetime of broken hopes. This will be my final chance to capture the mystical prize, that I had worked half my life for. That is to be crowned the GTT champion. And it all starts here, in the open invitation round of the Infinite Gauntlet, to seek that one ticket that will put me a step closer to my dream. Then, everything will be complete. To achieve the ultimate prize, that will restore the imminence and grandeur of my life. So that I can look back before death, and smile at the one dream that I had been able to achieve. But not yet. The time will come. One final challenge for the crown. Now is the time to bring all of it back home. I will conquer my destiny. <><><><><> Krystal stood alone in the empty courtyard, the winter winds thundering against her beautiful face. She smiled, finally able to rest their darkest demons. She stood there, minutes on end, facing the carved stone that bear her mother’s name. “I wish I can see you for one last time,” she muttered under her breath, loud enough for only the breeze to hear. She hung her head, deep in disappointment. But now, she can finally rest in peace, knowing that her mother is safely in the hands of the heavens. She prayed, hoping that one day she will be able to glance into the sparkling eyes of the woman who gave her life in this world. “I will always love you. Remember that.” She dangled her feet over the side of the cliff, her eyes dancing in the shadows of the glowing morning sun. “It’s beautiful, brother.” She glanced to her side, gleaming excitedly at her older brother, gripping his hand tight. “I knew you’ll love it,” he whispered back in her ear, sending a sound of comfort deep through her heart. “Can you tell me something brother?” she muttered, turning her head. Her blonde hair glistened in the light, enveloping the both of them in a bright shield. “Will I ever be separated from you in this world, brother?” Krystal whispered to herself, her eyes fixed on the ground where her mother had been buried. A tear dropped silently, and flowed down her cheek in a soft delicate path. He laughed softly at his sister’s question, glancing back into her deep eyes. He held her hand tight, and hugged her close to his body. “Never.” Krystal turned around and faced the empty courtyard, as her blonde hair covered part of her pale complexion. She smiled, quite certain that she heard the answer that her heart wanted to hear all these past years. A word that was carried through the evening breeze, to the sonata of the winter winds. THE END
