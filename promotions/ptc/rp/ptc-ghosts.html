Ghosts

27 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Character: Kyle Lamen (PRIME) * * * * * 9:00. Red flashing LED lights on an alarm clock. Above the clock is a bruised white wall with several gouges and black scuff marks. A wiry hand snakes out, and slaps the top of the alarm clock. Hi. My name’s Kyle, and I’m a professional wrestler. 9:15. Same clock. Same wall. The wiry hand reaches out, and this time grabs the alarm clock manfully, and smashes it against the wall behind it, leaving another big gash in the wall. The lights disappear. That sounds like an AA introduction, and to be fair, that’s not too far from the truth. If you’re in the business, you know that wrestling is like an addiction. And I, according to people who know me best, have an addictive personality. Kyle Lamen, short blue hair plastered about the crown of his head, pulls himself out of a medium-sized bed, tossing the sheets aside. I’d rather think of it as obsessive, but hell. There’s not enough time in the world for semantics. Kyle goes into his bathroom, which is messy. The mirror is spotted, and the sink has water spots. He blinks the dust out of his eyes, and lifts up his toothbrush. He sprays a nearby can of Reddi-Whip on the toothbrush, and vigorously starts brushing his teeth. There’s not enough time in the world for anything we want to do, for that matter. Now, we see Kyle sitting at a rough, scarred wooden table, picking at a bowl of rice with a pair of chopsticks. He pulls a bit of cheese and chicken out of the rice, and carefully pushes it into his mouth. A corner of his mouth pulls back in enjoyment. But before I talk about that, I’d like to talk about breakfast. I love breakfast. Love it like I love a crowd reaction, or a new Alkaline Trio single. We close in on a bowl of rice, cheese, chicken, and egg. Some sort of green dust is sprinkled over the top of it. Breakfast is a great meal. I eat this bowl of food just about every morning for breakfast while I’m at home. But I love all breakfast foods. The bowl suddenly disappears. Pancakes. A dish of steaming pancakes sits on the table, covered in butter and syrup. Waffles. Waffles appear, with a side of crispy bacon and a glass of orange juice. You name it. A bowl of Fruity Pebbles cereal, floating in orange juice instead of milk. I love breakfast because you eat it at the beginning of the day, and it carries all the promise of success with it. After a great breakfast, you feel like you can do anything. You can pin any wrestler, you can write any hit single, you can climb Mount Everest. A bowl of Cheerios, except the O’s are carefully arranged in a smiley-face pattern. It was after a breakfast like this one day, that I found I was diagnosed with terminal brain cancer. The same bowl of Cheerios, except it’s now making a frowny-face. Breakfast is hope. It’s the promise of things to come in the future. And sometimes, breakfast is a downright lie. Kyle Lamen pulls on a black sweatshirt over his six-pack, and the sweatshirt reads “H20 – Faster Than The World.” As we pan down, we can see that he’s wearing blue track pants, and he quickly leans down to tighten up his Nikes. Yep. They’re tight alright. Like I said before, I’m a professional wrestler. I work for a company called PRIME. I’m what’s called in the business a “career mid-carder.” Kyle throws a beat-up blue New York Mets cap on his head, and passes by a mirror quickly, then comes back and checks himself out in it. Looking pretty good, brother. I’m one of the guys who gets paid to go out, win a few matches against guys who can’t draw a crowd and look good. Then I go and wrestle the guys who are the top stars, and I lose to them, so that THEY look good. He locks the door to his apartment, on the outside, and then bounds down a narrow flight of stairs. It’s not on purpose, but it’s always how it works out. I’ve never really been good enough than to get more than a few fluke wins over big names, then flake out. I sell a bunch of merchandise, but it’s usually to fourteen-year old guys and twenty-year old girls. Kyle takes three steps out the back door to Jack’s Music Shop, bastion of Red Bank, New Jersey cool, and then breaks into a fast jog. He’s off through the parking lot, and towards the nearby Broad Street sidewalk. And as it stands now, because of my condition, I’ve got about nine months to make the most out of my wrestling career. I’ve got that much time to stop being a mid-carder, and step up to the plate. I’m supposed to fulfill my potential. Kyle jogs manfully down the street, having worked up a nice lather of sweat on his forehead, which seeps a little down past his eyebrows. He brushes it aside with a wave of his hand, as an older man jogs up next to him. The older man claps Kyle on the back with a big, meaty hand, and Kyle almost falls forward with the force of the blow. This is Jason. He’s my trainer, and the next best thing to a father I’ve had. He’s always said that I’ve got potential. For those of you who don’t know, potential is something that you have, that causes everyone to be really pissed at you when you fail. If you succeed, you don’t have potential. You have success. But if you fail, and people don’t know WHY you fail, you have potential. Jason speeds up, as if threatening to leave Kyle in the dust. Kyle makes a face and groans a little. Potential sucks. Kyle manages to catch up to Jason, and the two of them make their way down Broad Street, jogging down the slightly busted sidewalks of Red Bank. A few people have their heads turned by the duo, as they are almost not-really famous. When I started wrestling, in PCW, several years ago…people said that I had a lot of potential. Probably because I lost a bunch. But then I beat Kid Wonder. Then people said I was pretty good. Then I lost to Thug RoC, and people asked where the potential went. As Kyle passes by the window of Tropical Smoothie Café, a pretty blonde with her hair tied back catches Kyle’s eye, and gives him a confused look. Oh. That’s Sarah. My former assistant. Kyle jogs backwards towards the window where Sarah was. She’s gone. Sarah’s not really there. Maybe I should explain. I think it has something to do with my tumor, but every so often I see people that aren’t there. They’re typically the same three people, and Sarah’s one of them. Kyle puts his hand up against the window, and peers inside. Sure enough, Ms. McIntyre is nowhere to be found. Kyle frowns a little bit, then walks off. I embarrassed myself by trying to kiss her at New Year’s, and that was kind of a breach of the employee-employer relationship. She quit and ran off with my buddy Kensuke to Seattle. But she wanted him all along, so I don’t really blame her. Jason and Kyle pull up to the front of a garage door, which Jason lifts with one of his massive arms. The two men step through, while Kyle flips on one of the lights. One by one, the fluorescent lights click on one after another, revealing Jason’s gym. Her job was to take care of the incidentals, while I wrestled. In my life, I’ve only ever been good at two things. I’ve been good at wrestling, and I’ve been good at music. Everything else was basically an incidental. In the center of the building is one of the old CCW wrestling rings, kept in perfect condition. Jason rolls into the ring under the ropes, while Kyle just leaps up to the apron in one bound, then steps through the ropes. Incidentally, I ruined that by running Sarah off. I know I loved talking to her, but I dunno if I really loved her her. But it was the closest I’ve come to feeling connected to a girl in a long while. Not good at the relationship thing either. Jason quickly locks up with Kyle in a collar and elbow tie up. Jason grins close up in Kyle’s face, almost as a threat. Kyle grins back. So now all I’ve got is wrestling and music. And I’m better at wrestling. Jason, the much bigger of the two, flexes his arms, shoves, and tosses Kyle to the mat. …well, I’m not GREAT at wrestling. Kyle pulls his legs back, on the ground, and kips up to his feet, dusting himself off. And I don’t hesitate to admit, that everything I’ve learned about wrestling, I’ve learned from Jason. The massive man Jason Core brushes his shoulder-length black hair aside, and advances towards Kyle, slightly limping and favoring his right knee. Jason’s a former PCW Extreme Champion. Which is cool, since this Infinite Gauntlet match could be a gateway to a PTC Extreme Title match. And against the person who ended Jason’s career, Hoyt Williams. Kyle takes a quick step forward, but Jason just throws a quick right cross, which catches Kyle in the forehead. Then a second. Then a third. He’s a tough teacher. Molded me into the guy I am today. I’ve been training with him for almost ten years, since I was sixteen. With my mom having her hands full with work, my sister and stuff. The fourth punch drops Kyle. His face rolls over, and his cheek rests on the canvas. Ouch. When Jason works me, stretches me, runs me through some punches or some new moves, it hurts. But it could’ve been worse. He could’ve ignored me like my dad. It could’ve been awful like high school. From Kyle’s point of view, we see a busty young woman, sitting cross-legged on the floor, her legs crossed, barely covering anything naughty underneath her short plaid skirt. Her Red Bank Catholic jumper was unzipped halfway. Jess. My first serious girlfriend in high school. Crazy and beautiful. She hated me, and she couldn’t keep her hands off me. We argued seventy-five percent of the time, and made out the other twenty-five. Kyle’s eyes close, and when they open again, the girl is gone. Training for the Infinite Gauntlet. Which basically consists of Jason beating me up for a few hours, until I get in a few good licks. I just have enough time to go home and shower, before it’s time for dinner. You’d never think getting beat up would feel so good. Kyle, having changed into a black polyester jacket and black jeans, with a white Ramones logo t-shirt underneath, walks up to the front of a restaurant. This is my restaurant. Modern Osaka. We’ve even gotten some good press recently. It kinda surprised me. The idea was only that I get to eat the things I loved back in Japan. Okonomiyaki, some croquettes, a bento-box type thing Kyle walks in, takes a few steps towards the bar, and offers a handshake to the bartender, who smiles at him. Yeah, there’s sushi, but instead of hibachi, we do yakiniku. It’s slices of meat and veggies, and you cook them yourself over a small brazier at the table. We haven’t been sued yet, which is a total bonus. Over by the bar, a lovely young Japanese woman in a Holly Golightly little black dress rests against the bar. Her body is perfect, 36-24-36, and her smile sets the room alight. And her face is smooth, clear, and flawless. Miho Kurosaki. On second glance, there’s no one at the bar. No one at all. We fell in mad love back in Osaka while I was living there. It was crazy…I was trying to get my band off the ground, and she was this crazy popular idol singer. We met randomly in a bar, and we were nuts about each other. Kyle reaches back behind the bar, and pulls forth a bottle of Johnnie Walker Red Label. He takes a long pull from the bottle, makes a face because no one’s really THAT hardcore, and slams the bottle down on the bar. One day, just like these weird visions I have, she was gone. Not a word. Three months of ridiculous dating bliss, but after we had sex, nothing. She’s gone like a ghost. Never got to see her again. Still don’t know why she left. Kyle leaves the restaurant out the main door, and then goes to the next door over. He steps through the big double front door. Next door is LiveHouse. No, not Lifehouse. Fuck those guys. Kyle walks down a small, cramped staircase, into the basement of the building. Clubs for American rock’n’roll in Japan are called “live houses.” As he reaches the bottom, the place is alight with neon lights, video screens, and an epic stage with six wireless microphones sitting on it. Around are a couple of tables and chairs. It’s the absolute best karaoke place in the state. Giant screens, a library of over twenty-thousand songs and growing. Instantly, the place fills up. Gen X’ers and younger abound, with half a dozen people milling about the stage. Babes in their club clothes, scene kids with their emo pants, club guys in silk shirts. You haven’t seen anything until you’ve seen hundreds of people from my age bracket singing ‘To Be With You’ by Mr. Big. Sure enough, a few minutes later, Kyle appears onstage, without his big jacket. His voice is clear, clarion. He commands the stage, the same way he tends to command the ring during his matches. Tonight, I’m doing one of my other favorite songs. ‘I Remember You’ by Skid Row. I’ve got a pretty good Sebastian Bach voice, so it typically goes over like gangbusters with the regulars. He moves about the stage. He’s confident of his body. He owns it. I love the music. Kyle looks over the microphone and smiles. It’s a good day. Despite my condition, despite my continual failures, I have a lot of good days. Leaning casually against the back wall, are the three women we’ve seen earlier. Even though I keep failing. Failing at these relationships, failing to win the big matches…I’ve still got good days. Jess Breyer’s dye-red hair is cropped short, and she wore a form-fitting, albeit self-mutilated Rock Kills Kid t-shirt. The plaid pants were tight, and the accessories were the very picture of “scene.” She threw a wink in the direction of the stage. And I’d better win this Infinite Gauntlet. I can’t afford to keep failing. I’ve got no time left to fail. Sarah McIntyre looks classy. Hair’s pulled back in a ponytail, her dark red glasses frame her light green eyes. Her white blouse is unbuttoned one button too low, a concession to the late hour. And her black dress skirt, well, skirted the line between mini and business. She shook her head, but released a wry grin. I’ve screwed up so often, I’ve got to keep the hope to do better. Miho Kurosaki’s onyx hair was piled carefully high, her face clean and innocent. Her smile was open, and honest, and she smiled with her whole face. Her skirt was short, and flared, and she knew to accent her best assets with high white fishnet stockings and a low-cut red halter top. Wrestling is hope. The music cut out, and left Kyle in the adulation of the bar regulars. The screens around him cut out, going from the end of the video to darkness. Music is hope. Kyle takes a deep bow, and wipes the sweat from his eyes. His gaze races back to where it was before, where the phantoms of his past stood against the wall. Breakfast is hope. Jess was gone. It’s the promise of things to come in the future. Sarah was gone. And sometimes... And Miho…the Japanese idol princess unbelievably still leaned against that wall, and still smiled. …sometimes breakfast isn’t the best part of my day. Kyle wiped his brow, and smiled back at her. Sometimes you get a great day.
