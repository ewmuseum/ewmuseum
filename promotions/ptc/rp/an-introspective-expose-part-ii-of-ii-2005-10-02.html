An Introspective Exposé (Part II of II)

2 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-10-02 Shouldn’t I be seeing my life flash by like some psychedelic strobe light right about now? I’ve been waiting for my own personal slide show for years. I’d always imagined it would be so perfect, like my own little movie. Can’t you just picture the marquee? “Prudence Jane Walks Memory Lane,” directed, produced and starring yours truly. Me. My own masterpiece, for one showing and one showing only. Cue the birth. Cut to puberty. Jump to that first kiss. Wipe to the wedding that never happened. The children who never existed. Edit out the inevitable divorce. Cut to right here, right now. Fade to black. I’m afraid to blink. I wouldn’t want to miss it. This pen is getting heavy. I wonder if this is how Rollins felt the night I injected my toxic little cocktail into his nervous system. All I wanted was to speak to him one more time. I needed to hear Rollins’ voice so bad that it hurt. Call me old fashioned, call me sentimental, but I just needed some sense of closure to the whole thing. Maybe I shouldn’t have reacted the way I did, but then again there isn’t a rulebook you can consult in cases like this. Maybe somebody should write one. Maybe they already did.. I think they called it the Bible. My book would be a little different. More along the lines of, “Becoming a Parent‘s Worst Nightmare,” a how-to handbook by Prudence Jane. A New York Times bestseller if I ever heard one. ~<>~<>~<>~ WKPT Tower, Harvard Campus Radio… Francis Harold Waldorf looked just like you’d expect an Ivy League on-air radio personality to look like; with equal parts nerd, closet case shut-in and law school haughtiness rolled up into a two hundred-plus pound frame, sprinkle on the 4.0 GPA holier-than-thou posture and an Adam’s apple that bobbed every time he swallowed and you had a recipe for the perfect mismatch between voice and body. He had the lulling intonation of a young Frank Sinatra. He looked more like an old Elvis Presley. The bloated version. Rich Rollins couldn’t help but wonder if he could carry the same tune. Somehow he doubted it. Thumbing through a stack of neatly organized manila folders, Francis plucked out a file marked “Primetime Central” and set it on the small expanse of mahogany desk stretched between himself and the Renegade. Trying not to sound too cynical, Francis offered Rollins what he hoped was a polite enough nod as he opened the tiny dossier. “Thanks for doing this on such short notice,” Francis began. “We needed to do some sort of promotion on the Super Series event tomorrow, aside from the mountain of fliers you people sent us. This intervie-…” “Funny, I didn’t see any fliers on my way in,” Rollins interrupted dryly. “Guess I must be going blind in my old age.” Francis chuckled nervously. “Ah. You should have tried the cafeterias, we’ve got all sorts of posters inside the larger communal buildings. We have a strict no-solicitation policy on campus, which is why we didn’t post them on any facility doors. PTC may be a large market, but Harvard is still Harvard and low brow entertainment is still low brow entertainment. You understand.” Rollins did. The condescension was thick enough to carve his name in it. “Whatever you say, doc,” he replied with a nonchalant shrug. Skimming through a random sheet in his folder, Francis began absently stroking the thin layer of hair he tried to pass off as a goatee and cleared his throat. “So, are there any topics in particular you want us to cover?” he asked. The Renegade shook his head. “Keep me guessin’, I like to work on the fly.” Francis nodded, but it was obvious he wasn’t paying any attention. “So you’re from Global Championship Wrestling?” he asked based on the information in his file. Rollins wondered how they selected him for this interview in the first place, honestly. “That’s correct,” he replied. “Any major accomplishments?” Francis inquired, even though it all should have been right there under his nose. The Renegade’s head began to throb. “Well, let’s see.” Rollins tried his best to keep his tone civil, but it was a struggle. “I wrestled in the main event on GCW’s first broadcast almost five years ago, and somewhere along the road since then I got inducted into the Hall of Fame. That’s the reader’s digest version for you,” he paused, then added, “but I’m sure you already knew that. You Harvard folks are so smart.” Francis Harold Waldorf brought his eyes up from the dossier to shoot Rollins a guarded look. The gears were turning behind those Ivy League eyes, but Rollins wasn’t sure whether the kid knew he was just being a smartass. He didn’t want to get on the guy’s bad side before they went on the air. Then again, this spoiled little silver-spoon-fed doctor/lawyer/upper class ass kisser of the future didn’t want to see his bad side either. Especially not on live radio. “This little cock sucker is going to try and humiliate you out there, you know.” The voice was all too familiar to Rollins by now, but it still made him jump whenever Dave Olson decided to poke his imaginary nose into his personal affairs. “He’s going to make you look like the biggest piece of purebred white trash that America’s ever seen since Being Bobby Brown got put into syndication.” “Bobby Brown is black,” Rollins shot back before he could stop himself. Francis gave a start at the comment, almost depositing the PTC folder across the floor. “Pardon?” Rollins gnashed his teeth. The pounding in his head started to feel more like a steel-toed marching band was bouncing around his brain. “Nothing, just thinking out loud.” He knew it was lame, but Francis seemed to forget the outburst almost immediately. A single bead of sticky sweat escaped from under Rollins’ black bandanna and journeyed south across his cheek. His eyes darted around the small but well furnished office, trying to find his imaginary tormentor. A few scattered bookshelves and the sloppy spattered canvases of nameless modern art pieces were all that greeted him. His invisible antagonist was nowhere to be found. “It says here that this match you’re competing in is a… scavenger hunt? What exactly does that mean?” Francis once again peeled his eyes away from the folder, searching Rollins’ face with scrutinizing interest. “I wish I could tell you,” Rollins replied. “From what I’ve gathered, we’re all going into a huge clusterfuck of a medallion hunt and it won’t end until somebody finds the big prize.” Francis smirked. “A medallion?” “No.” Rollins sighed. Now it was his turn to be condescending. “Two big shiny gold belts. We call them ‘championships’ in my line of work.” The subtly wasn’t lost on the Harvard undergraduate. With one finger tracing the information across his dossier, he said: “The PTC Universal and Extreme titles, correct?” Holding this kid’s hand was starting to get annoying, and the levy holding Rollins’ patience finally broke. “Look man, just read your little cheat sheet and leave the details to me. It’ll go a lot smoother, I promise.” “You might have to wipe this kid’s ass for him, too.” Dave Olson again, rearing his ugly head from somewhere deep in Rollin’s subconscious. “He looks like his mother breast fed him until he was at least sixteen.” Sucking at his teeth to keep from screaming like a lunatic at someone who didn’t exist, the Renegade merely leaned back in his Italian-tailored leather chair with a slow, inward breath. Completely oblivious, Francis simply nodded as he folded the PTC profile closed. “This should be fun,” said Francis, smiling the most disingenuous smile Rollins had ever seen. There was a quick knock on the door before a new face materialized in the open entryway. He had the mussy look of a sound tech, someone who obviously wasn’t the embodiment of a Harvard man. “We go live in five,” he said, shooting Francis a five-fingered open palm before disappearing as abruptly as he had arrived. Collecting his reference material, Francis Harold Waldorf motioned for Rollins to follow as he stood. “Let’s do this thing,” he said. Rich Rollins couldn’t help but grin. Francis was absolutely right. “Time to make radio history,” cooed Dave Olson, nestled somewhere in the recesses of Rollins’ imagination. This was going to be fun, alright. ~<>~<>~<>~ My mother always said to live life in the moment, for the moment. She said that too many people wasted their tomorrows and turned them into forgotten yesterdays. She said her moments with me were her favorite yesterdays to remember. She said a lot of stupid shit like that. Cue my feigned sympathy. Cut to my false regret. Fade into the fact that I just don’t give a shit anymore. I’ve got a lot of yesterdays that I haven’t forgotten. Only problem is, I wish I could forget. I wish I could erase those very real memories of joy when I was with him, those moments when I thought I had finally moved beyond the sense that my life was going nowhere because he told me we were going somewhere. I was a nobody who finally thought she was a somebody. Silly me. I should have realized I was just a nobody who thought she was a somebody. We used to mock-argue back and forth, Rich and I. We called it ’Tangent-land,’ our own private universe where we could make one another shout and snicker at the same time, talking about anything and everything without really talking about anything at all. A lot of people know Tangent-land, they just don’t have a name for it. I’ll meet him there again, be it in this life or the next. The twenty-some Nembutals in my stomach are telling me it’ll probably be the next. I can’t say I disagree with them. My words are starting to blur together, but I can’t stop writing. Not yet. Not yet. ~<>~<>~<>~ The following is a verbatim transcript of the Rich Rollins interview broadcast on Harvard Campus Radio, October 1st 2005. It would mark the last time the Board of Directors would allow a non-student interviewee to be featured without a thirty second tape delay. [The tail end of the Harvard University alma matter plays itself out before Francis Harold Waldorf fades in.] FHW: Welcome back, listeners! Today we have a very special show in store for you all as the incomparable Rich “The Renegade” Rollins is in the studio and ready to field the big questions before heading into the main event tomorrow night, as Primetime Central’s Super Series kicks off right here in our every own backyard! Glad to have you, Rich. Renegade: Glad to be here, Francis. FHW: So if I’m not mistaken, this will be your first shot at a PTC championship, is it not? Renegade: No, that’s not entirely true. I beat the Uber Judge for the Primetime Central Infinite Gauntlet championship back in July and managed to keep it for about two weeks. It was that event which allowed me to participate in the Super Series Scavenger Hunt in the first place. FHW: I see. Isn’t the Uber Judge himself participating in this scavenger hunt as well? Renegade: That he is. That verbose little bastard is walking in with the Extreme title tomorrow night. [An awkward silence fills the room, followed by the crinkling static of somebody touching a live mic.] FHW: (muted) Watch the language, Rich. I don’t want to get fined or pulled off the air, or both. [Another second passes by in silence, but the interview quickly resumes.] FHW: Indeed. Do you intend on dethroning him for a second time? Renegade: If the opportunity presents itself, possibly. I’ve made my point with UJ. I’ve got a lot of respect for a man who walks big and wears a wig, even if he needs that ridiculously huge gavel to get the job done. [Cue canned laughter.] FHW: Everyone seems to have something to gain from a match as… unique… as this one. However, only you have something to lose, and that’s the privilege of hand selecting your first round opponent in the upcoming GTT5 tournament, correct? Renegade: Yup. And it’s a fringe benefit I don’t intend on letting go of, if you catch my meaning. I worked too damn hard for this opportunity, so if anybody thinks that they’re walkin’ out with my pick-an-opponent clause, they ain’t thinkin’ too clearly. I’ll damn sure help straighten out their thought process though, I promise you that. [Francis Harold Waldorf coughs audibly.] FHW: Moving on. I thought we’d do a little word association, Rich. What do you say? Renegade: I can hardly wa-… Dave Olson: Don’t fuck up now, bucko. The world is listening. [Silence.] FHW: Rollins? Renegade: (clearing his throat) Sorry, I was just spacing out. FHW: (chuckling) It happens. Dave Olson: To some more than others, you arrogant pencil-neck prick. Renegade: You have no idea. Shoot. FHW: Okay, word association. First one… Seymour Almasy. Dave Olson: More at home playing Final Fantasy XI than in a wrestling ring. Renegade: My white whale. FHW: Interesting. What makes you say that? Renegade: He’s the one guy that practically all others are compared against. He’s a two-time Universal champion. If you can’t beat him, you can’t cut the mustard in the main event, period. FHW: All right, next one… Karina Wolfenden. Dave Olson: The Deville’s whore. [There is a long pause as the Renegade shifts uncomfortably in his seat.] Renegade: Karina Wolfenden… the one woman in wrestling whose skill in the ring far overshadows her cute ass. I can’t wait to get a piece of her, if you know what I’m saying. Dave Olson: I doubt he does. Look at the man and tell me he’s not a virgin. [Cue canned laughter.] FHW: Okay, Michael “Spitfire” Stevens. Renegade: Spitty? FHW: If you say so. Renegade: He’s keepin’ my Infinite Gauntlet crown warm, but Super Series ain’t even the same game as the Gauntlet is. If he thinks he can block my shot at PTC gold, he’s sorely mist-… Dave Olson: Generic, generic, generic. Stop pussy-footing the issue and tell them the truth. [Rich Rollins clears his throat.] Dave Olson: He’s nothing more than a glorified mid-carder with a meal ticket to the main event. FHW: Do you need a glass of water? Dave Olson: Say it. Renegade: No, I’m fine. Michael Stevens is nothing more than a glorified mid-carder with a meal ticket to the main event, ‘nuff said. Dave Olson: Now we’re having fun. FHW: Okay, how about the Uber Judge? Renegade: For every little kiosk that fucker puts up, I’ve got a bottle of kerosene and a zippo ready to take it back down. (he laughs) But seriously, UJ has my respect. He managed to beat me not once, but twice. I don’t intend on giving him the opportunity to hit his grand finish by making it a trilogy. Dave Olson: Christ, you’re such a tool. FHW: Good stuff. Moving on-… Renegade: Would you just shut the fuck up? [Silence.] Dave Olson: You can’t silence what nobody can hear, Rollins. Renegade: (yelling) I’ll fucking end you, you arrogant fuck! Leave me alone! FHW: (trying not to panic) Rollins please, there’s no need for… Dave Olson: Tell the fat man to shut the fuck up. He’s getting on my nerves. Renegade: You’re getting on my nerves, pal. I swear to Christ Almighty, if you don’t stop talking I’ll-… Dave Olson: You’ll what? Blow my imaginary brains through my imaginary skull? FHW: That’s all the time we have for today, folks. (muffled) Cut the feed, now. Renegade: I’ll fucking kill you! Get out of my head! Dave Olson: Smile, Rollins. You’re on Candid Radio. […static…static…static…] […static…static…] ((Author’s note: Highlight the text in the previous interview. It’ll make a lot more sense.)) ~<>~<>~<>~ Now we’re past the point of no return. No amount of stomach pumping could make me regurgitate this decision. Whoever finds this letter, I just want you to know that it was the best decision I ever made. I didn’t have a choice whether I wanted to be part of this world or not, but I damn sure have the choice to remove myself from it. Whoever finds this, know that I wanted to go out this way. Cue the violins. They’re playing just for me. Cue the big black curtain. My sad little stage play is about to hit its grand finale. Cue my one real regret. I wish I could have seen the look on his face right now. I wish I could have seen that I finally made him as fucking crazy as I am. It’s time I took a one-way trip to Tangent-land. I hope he won’t keep me waiting. This is where I fade to black. ~Fin~
