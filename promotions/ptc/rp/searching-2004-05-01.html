Date: 2004-05-01 Three down, three to go. It was the only way that Seymour Almasy could look at his current situation. In all of the RPGs he had ever played, there always came the epic final battle, the gauntlet of bosses that concluded the adventure. In Final Fantasy VII, the Northern Crater held Jenova SYNTHESIS, and two forms of Sephiroth. Final Fantasy VIII brought the practice to an art form in Ultimecia’s castle, with Sphinxaur, Tri-Point, Krysta, Trauma, Red Giant, Gargantua, Catobeplas, Tiamat, and FOUR forms of the final boss standing in the player’s way. Seymour’s task was somewhere in the middle. Five final fights, provided he continued to win. He had beaten two bosses in the first one, and a demonic priest in the second. Compared to those two tasks, his third was somewhat more mundane. But he still hurt like hell, and a competitor such as Horazon could easily pick apart a sore Seymour. And so it was that the Final Fantasy embarked on one of his great tournament traditions: the vacation. Almasy had visited many fine exotic locations over his tournament stays. But none quite so fine as this. Seymour and Aerith had journeyed to the Land of the Rising Sun. Home of, well, pretty much everything Seymour loves. ------- The streets of Tokyo, Japan hustled and bustled with tens of thousands of people. While many looked uniform in suits and the like, every so often a person or two stood out. Two of these people were a man with white hair clad in armor and a woman in a pink dress. These two were, of course, Seymour Almasy and Aerith Heartilly. Of course, Almasy has no concept of Japan. Looking around at the classical archetecture mixed in amongst the ultra-modern buildings, he could only conclude that the land was Wutai, the Oriental-styled country in Final Fantasy VII. Seymour Almasy: My, Aerith, Wutai has developed spectacularly since Holy saved the planet! Aerith could do nothing but nod. Aerith Heartilly: It has…it’s such an interesting land, isn’t it? Seymour Almasy: It is, Aerith. Such a wondrous combination of the old meeting the new. In many ways, that was exactly what Japan was. Ultra-modern buildings and institutions combined with a unique culture that could not be found anywhere else in the world. It was a culture that found most of Seymour’s hobbies and interests far more acceptable and mainstream than did any other. Seymour Almasy: This land is amazing. So many people wearing items glorifying the heroes of my favorite quests… Aerith clung closer to her husband. She reveled in contact, giggling slightly as she thought of her intentions for the day. The Japanese culture had an institution unmatched by anywhere else in the world, seemingly designed for people like herself who had insatiable desires pop up at odd moments. An institution known as the love hotel. But for now, thoughts of passionate love in the middle of the day would have to wait. Seymour Almasy: Thanks for agreeing to come with me here. I’m quite tired from the early battling of this tournament, and Pietro Geist was no picnic. Heartilly looked over at her husband. Already, the familiar signs of battle were there. The scratches on his face, the cute little limp that he walked with, both were signs of what Seymour had been through. Aerith Heartilly: Sometimes I wonder why you want to retire, considering how much you love the Arena… Seymour Almasy: But? Aerith Heartilly: But then I look at you after a series of battles. Seymour smiled wryly. He was just as aware as Aerith of the various defects he suffered in the Battle Arena. Seymour Almasy: I know what you mean. I see peers of mine doing absolutely insane things…using weapons on a regular basis, things that should cripple them quickly, but somehow they survive. My style is somewhat high-risk, I know that going in…but I’m always shocked at how fast everything deteriorates. Aerith Heartilly: It’s okay, Seymour. Seymour Almasy: It is…but it isn’t. I love this so much…having to give it up is a blow to me. It means I have to start thinking about a future…a future without the career that I love. It’s difficult. Aerith clung still tighter to Seymour. This was turning out to be a much sadder occasion than she had thought. Seymour Almasy: But in the end, there’s really no reason to be sad. I have you. Even though I will no longer be able to pursue world titles, I will have you to be with me, forever. Aerith Heartilly: Thank you, Seymour…that means a lot. They continued to saunter down the streets of Tokyo, minds crammed full of things to worry about. Usually, Almasy had but his opponent to worry about. Today, however, on his mind were both Horazon and THE horizon, namely his career after this tournament. Seymour Almasy: It isn’t like I haven’t accomplished anything. I’ve held the highest title in Primetime Central and the Universal Wrestling Federation. I’ve obtained honors everywhere I’ve gone. But sometimes…I just feel like it isn’t enough. I know my name is cemented with the great warriors before me…but, oh God this is going to sound silly. Aerith Heartilly: What, Seymour? You can tell me. Seymour Almasy: As wonderful as it feels not to have aches and pains…it just doesn’t feel right. The pain that I feel after a battle is a part of me. It lets me know that all is okay. Almasy flinched after he spoke, looking his wife in the eyes. To his surprise and relief, however, she looked back warmly, and nodded. Aerith Heartilly: I know it’ll sound odd…but I understand, sort of. You’re so used to it…it’s just natural. Seymour Almasy: As natural as anything. More so then a lack of pain…it’s crazy, isn’t it? Seymour could do nothing but chuckle. It was an odd life. He knew it from the minute he put on the armor for the first time. He knew it as he entered the ring and as he left it. He knew it as he was awake and asleep. Seymour Almasy: No matter how many times I try to justify it to myself, there is no way that a completely sane person would be willing to step into the arena day after day. Aerith couldn’t deny it. Seymour WASN’T completely sane. Not even close. But then again, when she thought about it, few people really were. She wasn’t, she had decided to marry him. Logic would have dictated that never happen, but she followed her heart, not her head. Her thoughts were interrupted as Seymour continued. Seymour Almasy: I’m not sane. I know that…but I do not care. With three battles left if all goes well, I can be as insane as I choose. I can and will risk what I have left. As my prior opponents discovered that, so too shall Horazon. He will discover the willpower I have to achieve my Final Fantasy. To stand atop Gagazet, just one more time… The Final Fantasy pulled Aerith still closer, and looked around. It was such a wonderful city. For the first time, despite appearing entirely different from a vast majority of the other people in it, Seymour truly felt like he belonged. Seymour Almasy: This really is a lovely place… Aerith Heartilly: Good, Seymour, I’m glad you like it. She had been on the verge of telling him, just about to reveal his identity, reveal the falsehood he had been living. But just like the thousands of other times she had considered it, she couldn’t quite do it. She couldn’t bring herself to break his heart. Instead, she leant her head down, snuggling against his shoulder, prompting a happy sigh from Seymour. Seymour Almasy: Aerith, you are too good to me… Aerith smiled, seeing her destination in the distance. She clasped her husband by the hand, and began to pull him towards the love hotel. Tommorow, they could worry about battle. For today, she would proceed to be very good to Seymour, indeed.
