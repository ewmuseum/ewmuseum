"Intentions"

9 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
* * * * * 14 Days remaining… Confusion washed over Clinton Sage much like the waves of the ocean crashing against a sandy beach. Rational thoughts were pulled into the crushing depths by an unseen current, one that he couldn’t quite escape from. For the last 17 years, he had searched for a meaning to the tattoos on his back. Since his tenth birthday, when he first asked his mother about the markings, he had remained interested in their origins. Clinton viewed them as a brand, forever marked with ink rather than the white-hot metal prods used to mark cattle. Like the branded cattle, he wondered to whom he was property. Clinton watched the scenery pass by in a darkened blur, most of the hour-long journey from Harrisburg to Philadelphia spent in a mind-numbing trance. If he ever decided to believe in a God, now would be the time to pray to Him for answers. Haddleston occasionally glanced back at him, who once again was in the backseat of the police cruiser like any common felon. Clinton occasionally noticed that Officer Haddleston would curse at the night, probably at some unknowing driver who cut off the black-and-white cruiser. The night itself was quiet, with a cool moon illuminating itself with stolen light from the sun. No clouds clawed themselves across the sky, only millions of stars speckled the dark expanse. Haddleston finally directed a comment towards Clinton, “You alright? I’ve seen addicts who would envy the distancing from reality you seem to be detached.” Clinton found the officers southern drawl to be annoying, and his smokers-voice equally as grating. He ignored the question. “Oh I see how it is, you let him get to you.” Haddleston continued, realizing Clinton had heard his question, “But if you do that, you let him win. Don’t give Harlan Raines such gratification in the last two weeks of his life.” It was approaching nearly 3am, Harlan Raines had slightly less than two weeks to live, Clinton thought to himself. Something danced around in his mind. Haddleston had unknowingly tossed Clinton Sage a life preserver, and he was floating back towards the surface of rational-thought, rather than being pulled under it. He finally breached the surface and took a deep breath in the form of a question. Clinton’s throat was scratchy when he spoke, “How is it that Raines could make these demands? He’s been in prison for more than half a decade, not to mention that this is the first time I’ve heard of a Death Row inmate ordering a meeting with someone outside of the prison.” Haddleston smiled, “Well there you are! I was afraid I left you back in Harrisburg.” Clinton got the impression that Haddleston was stalling, or avoiding the question, “Answer the question.” Haddleston sighed, “All I know is that he promised to deliver the bodies if he was given what he wanted. I’m just a regular officer, and they don’t find it necessary for me to know much more than that.” For a moment, Clinton’s mind wandered back to the murders that Harlan Raines had committed – and admitted to. He remembered the disgusting poses he had put the bodies in, and the photos he had sent out to every newspaper that would print them. Disgust tickled his neck like the legs of a spider, sending a chill across his flesh. “What about your partner – was it Officer Toomes?” Clinton asked, “Does he know anything else?” “He’s clueless. I had to tell him about your connection to Raines – those eye tattoos on your back – otherwise he would still be sitting on his thumbs wondering what was going on.” “Where is he now?” Clinton asked, his eyes shifting to the empty passenger seat. “He didn’t want, as he put it, to waste his time ‘chauffeuring an asshole around’ and decided to drive back himself.” Clinton Sage let out a humorless laugh. They passed by an exit on the Pennsylvania Turnpike for West Chester. Clinton recognized the sign and realized that he was only 20 minutes from home. Mentally exhausted from his encounter with Harlan Raines, Clinton was also physically exhausted from his match earlier in the night. There was a good chance dried blood was still under his fingernails. Clinton made a mental note to wash his hands as soon as he arrived home. He yearned to lie in his bed and sleep the night away, hoping that he would wake up and realize this was all a crazy nightmare. That would have to remain a wish, however, because he needed to be up almost immediately – training for his next match, which was an open invitational for a chance to fight in the Infinite Gauntlet. Clinton found it coincidental – perhaps ironic – that a huge weight was cast onto his shoulders right before an equally huge match, the prize of which would be a match against the wrestler known as the Uber Judge. “So those tattoos…,” Haddleston started, pausing as he shifted the cruiser into the passing lane, “How long have you had them? And don’t give me that shitty excuse you give the other wrestlers and fans…” Clinton studied Officer Haddleston a moment before answering, finding the officer to be curious and intuitive, showing no signs of his intelligence fading as his age climbed. He answered the question honestly, “Since birth, or so I’m told.” “Wait…you were born with them?” “Hardly…they were etched onto my back sometime during my first year. That’s why they look slightly disfigured, stretched, and faded.” “Oh, interesting. So why are they there?” “I’ve been trying to find that out for a long time, Officer Haddleston.” “So why does Harlan Raines have the same markings on his back?” asked the older policeman, his curiosity speeding along just as quickly as the police cruiser itself. “Your guess is as good as mine, but his were faded and stretched as well. I could barely see them buried within the surrounding tattoo.” The police cruiser snaked down the turnpike, continuing to weave between slower cars. Clinton wondered why Officer Haddleston was keeping such a frantic pace, even though there was no deadline to meet. He figured it was just the way Haddleston drove, hoping to connect with a youthful past that has long since passed him by. “You think he’s had his since birth?” Haddleston asked, his eyes once again watching Clinton in the rear-view mirror. “I hope not. That would only make me feel even more uneasy. I’m 27, and he’s going on 50. I would much rather have a mystery surrounding my own life, not pre-dating it by two decades.” Clinton paused for a moment, and decided to turn the tables on Officer Haddleston, “You ask a lot of questions, Officer. Why are you a measly officer when you obviously to yearn for that Detective badge?” Clinton Sage studied the rear-view mirror, watching as Officer Haddleston’s eyes squinted slightly in a hurtful dismay. Clinton smiled after the snide remark, but Haddleston didn’t notice because he kept his eyes glued to the road, and ignored the question. * * * * * The cell that Harlan Raines called home was hardly a cell at all. It was a small room, barely large enough to fit the bed – barely larger than a folding cot – the toilet, and what appeared to be a small bookshelf that was bolted to the wall. The cell was painted in a brilliant matte white, with a few dirty smudges here and there from where Harlan Raines did his daily push-ups and strength training. Immediately after being sentenced to death, Harlan knew he would be on Death Row for quite some time. He decided long ago to stay in top-physical condition for when he ascended past this world and into the next. He had completed the tasks they had asked of him long ago, and was just awaiting his time. The murders had been the first step. His execution would be the second step. When he looked into the eyes of God, Harlan wanted to be in peak physical form. Since he was in solitary confinement, forbidden from using the prison’s weight-room, he made due by using his own body-weight as resistance with simple exercises within his cell. Harlan’s hair might be graying, but his body was lacking any signs of aging. Quietly he lay on his bed, glancing up at the bookshelf. “The Count of Monte Cristo”, by Alexandre Dumas, book-ended the edge of the shelf closest to him. His thoughts quickly jumped to the fate of the main character in Dumas’ story, Edmond Dantes. Both Edmond and himself had been imprisoned for many years, and just like Edmond, it would only be a matter of time – two weeks to be exact – before he finally escaped. Harlan Raines smiled, and decided it was time to write the note. Sitting up, momentarily perched on the edge of his cot, he then proceeded to knock on the door that separated his own cell from the others in the Death Row wing of the prison. A moment passed before a pair of eyes peered in through the small glass window on the door, and a gruff voice followed, “What is it this time, Mr. Raines?” “I’d like to pass along a note, if you don’t mind. Grab a piece of blank paper, and a pencil.” The guard watched for a moment before answering, “Is this another one of your games? You were supposed to give us the location of the bodies. Last time I checked, nothing came of your little meeting with that wrestler.” Harlan watched through the small sliver of a window and grinned, “Patience is a virtue, dear boy. Now go and fetch that paper and pencil, and the first body will show up in less than 24 hours, I promise.” The grin turned into a full-blown smile as the narrow window became vacant, and footsteps could be heard echoing down the hallway just beyond the door. Everything was panning out beautifully. * * * * * As the police cruiser quietly passed through the suburbs of Philadelphia, the curiosity in Officer Haddleston had once again taken over. He was firing questions at Clinton Sage like a boxer would unload jabs at a staggering, stunned opponent. Clinton felt numb answering them all, but he figured it was the only way to pass the time. And maybe, just maybe, Haddleston would run out of inquiries. “Turn left, I’ll take you on a shortcut.” Clinton demanded, and a moment later, the officer complied and the car was taking Clinton’s shortcut through a winding road. During the day, it would be a beautiful road to photograph – one of Clinton’s hobbies – but during nightfall it might as well have been a road to hell. Twisting left and right, with trees lining the road only feet from the doors of the police cruiser. In the soft moonlight, they looked like large creatures looming just out of reach, their gnarled extending overhead. “So, let me ask you something. Did it occur to you that Harlan’s speech seemed a little…forced?” Haddleston asked, “I mean, it seemed like one of your little wrestling promos. Like he only ranted because he knew you were listening.” The thought had occurred to Clinton before, but only fleetingly. It did seem staged, but Clinton had shrugged it off because of the fact that Harlan Raines has had nothing to do for several years, except practice a speech to give to some future visitor. George Clooney and Matt Damon had made a humorous scene out of such a moment in Ocean’s Eleven. Clinton didn’t expect to be that visitor, but answered Haddleston’s question with a quick shrug of the shoulders. He also preferred the original Ocean’s Eleven, with Frank Sinatra and Sammy Davis Jr. The newer incarnation was filled with pretty boys and bad acting. “If you ask me, he was giving you some sort of clue.” “Why would he want to do that, Officer?” “You got me. Why would he want to drag you out there in the middle of the night? Why do you two share the same tattoos on your back? There are a million different reasons you could come up with, Clinton, but I just get the funny feeling like he was planning this for a very long time.” “Well, its not like he’s had much to do lately, right?” Clinton said with a smirk, brushing the officers observation to the side, “Turn right, my driveway should be right up the road.” The officer did as commanded, and within a minute, they were pulling up to the gated-driveway of the Sage residence. Clinton watched as Officer Haddleston quickly admired his home. The cruiser rolled to a stop at the gate. “Jesus, how much did this place cost?” asked the aging would-be detective, staring in awe at the home. Clinton’s home wasn’t massive, but it definitely showed his wealth. Columns on each side of the front doorway supported a massive pointed archway. To the left, a huge bay window was backlit by a chandelier, even though nobody was home. To the right, a three-car garage was separate from the home. A soft glow came from behind the garage, which Clinton knew was the lighting of his in-ground pool. The entire property held a gentle ambience that barely registered over the moons lightfall, but on a moonless night, would shine like a star itself. In the center of the front lawn stood a flagpole, the American flag being tossed by the night’s breeze. It was softly illuminated by two lights hidden behind a small granite wall, which enclosed the base of the flagpole. ”Well I’ll be damned, aren’t you patriotic.” Haddleston exclaimed with a smile. ”Just pull up to the keypad, and type in the following password: 1980. And there is absolutely no harm in loving your own country…” Clinton said matter-of-factly. Haddleston’s eyebrows shot upwards in surprise, “Oh, how trusting of you?” “You’re a police officer. The last thing I’m worried about is you breaking into my home. Just do it…I’m exhausted.” A moment later, the large black gate parted and the car was slowly making its way towards the Sage residence. Haddleston continued to admire the surroundings. “You know, you’re not so bad. A few smart-ass remarks here or there, but I see no reason to hate you.” Haddleston remarked. Clinton smirked to himself, and then pointed towards the bay-window on the front of his home, “Look past the window, you’ll see that I’m putting an addition onto the house. It’s a spare bedroom, mainly used for the college-girls I end up bringing back here.” Haddleston’s shoulders sank a little. His eyes fell away from the residence. “It’s pretty easy to go into one of the college towns, wear a tight muscle shirt, and get a girl drunk enough to come back here, where I’d proceed to fuck her brains out. I figured…why not just give them their own room? It adds to my aura in their eyes. I’ll just have the maid change the sheets once they leave in the morning, long after I’ve left. I usually have a taxi waiting for them.” Haddleston pulled the car up to the front door, putting it in park. He stepped out and then opened the back door, letting Clinton get out as well. As Clinton stood up, he noticed that Haddleston refused to make eye contact with him, with the officer’s face once again painted in disgust. “Oh, did I disappoint you, Officer Haddleston? Clinton said with a wry smile, “Well, it’s late and I must be getting to sleep. I trust you know how to find your way back home?” Officer Haddleston nodded, and then extended his hand, “Here is my card, call me if you think of anything.” Clinton nodded, and watched as Officer Haddleston got back into the police cruiser. He slowly drove off with the same courtesy he had exhibited while entering the driveway, then sped off into the early-morning darkness as he reached the edge of Clinton’s property. Clinton was left standing in his driveway, wondering: Did the Officer have a point? The murderer’s speech seemed a little too forced and practiced…perhaps there was some underlying meaning hidden in it? Clinton decided that a good night’s sleep would better suit his deductive reasoning. He turned and walked up the steps to his front door, unlocked it, and walked inside. No more than 10 minutes later, he was lying in bed trying to let exhaustion be the lullaby that charmed him into a deep sleep. He didn’t forget to wash his hands, and even clipped his fingernails. * * * * * The guard returned within a minute, and was placing the sheet of paper and pencil into the security drawer that would allow Harlan to access them, but was stopped before he passed them through. ”You write the note, I don’t want to cause any trouble by possibly placing a secret message into the text. You capitalize it however you like, and write it on the paper however you like, so you and your bosses are sure of its innocence. I don’t want to cause any trouble” Harlan bowed his head slightly, and smiled. The guard stood there for a moment pondering the request. Harlan could see that the guard realized this was the smarter route, giving him less hassle and possible paperwork to fill out later in the evening. “Go ahead.” The guard demanded, pressing the paper against the door. “Very well.” Harlan said, once again stepping to the edge of his bed and taking a seat, “Dear Mr. Dancy,” “Wait a sec, who is Mr. Dancy?” the guard asked, confused. His head peaked back into the narrow slit, waiting for an answer. “William Dancy, he is a reporter for the Philadelphia Inquirer. He is the person you will be faxing this letter to.” “Faxing?” “That’s right, faxing. You can destroy it once you’ve faxed it. It’s of no importance.” The guard sighed, “Alright, whatever. Go on.” “As I was saying…Dear Mr. Dancy, if you would like the break in your career that you’ve long-sought, please follow me with that infallible instinct, and beautiful camera of yours.” Harlan waited, allowing the guard to catch up with his writing. He could hear the small scratching noise the pencil made as it scribbled on the paper, which was pressed against the steel door. “I kindly request that you please keep yourself at a distance where you do not disturb me, and I promise that within 14 days you will make the front page of every newspaper in the country.” Harlan once again waited a moment, then continued, “You will know how to find me, as I am somewhat of a local celebrity. Just keep your eyes open, and your distance far…and soon the fame and fortune you seek will fall into your lap soon thereafter.” The guard looked through the small window, waiting, “Is that all?” Harlan rose from the bed, taking a step towards the door. He noted the guards shoulders tense up at this sudden movement, “No, not quite. My only other request is being able to sign the note, as it’s quite personal. I’m sure with my name attached, it will get his attention rather quickly.” The guard nodded, and pulled the security drawer open. He placed the paper and pencil into it and slammed it shut. As he did so, the drawer opened within Harlan’s cell and he quickly snatched the items up, signed the note, and placed the them back into the drawer as promptly as possible. ”There, you see? That was harmless. You’ll find Mr. Dancy’s fax-number on the Philadelphia Inquirer website. My only request is that you hurry, because I’d like him to be able to react to it before he leaves for the office this morning.” Harlan asked, his voice calm. ”And this note will give us the first body, as you’ve promised?” the guard asked, obviously hoping to further along his promotion in the prison by listening to Harlan Raines request. ”I promise, especially if you hurry. The quicker Mr. Dancy receives this note, the quicker the body is in your possession.” Raines said, once again lying on his bed. He closed his eyes, “Now if you don’t mind, please dim the lights so I can catch some sleep.” Harlan’s assumption was correct, as he heard the guard whisper to himself “Oh, the boss will love this” as he scurried down the hallway. A moment later, the lights in his cell were dimmed, and he started to drift off into sleep. He only hoped that the guards eagerness to ascend the ladder of promotion clouded his vision before he faxed the note, blinding him to the fact that the name signed at the bottom wasn’t Harlan Raines. * * * * * Clinton’s wandering mind kept him tossing and turning in bed, unable to sleep. He tried to recall some of the speech that Harlan Raines had given him, searching the killer’s words for clues much like an astronomer would search the star-laden sky for extraterrestrial life. “Through the eyes of hell, I saw the world differently. I saw there was a way to ascend past this world, and into the next.” “Much like the Ben Franklin Bridge spans the Delaware River, I will span from one life into the next.” “Through the eyes of hell, I saw a world that the general populous couldn’t understand.” Right before Clinton Sage finally slipped into unconsciousness, he came to the conclusion that he would make the ten-minute drive into Philadelphia, and visit the Benjamin Franklin Bridge before heading out to wrestle in the Infinite Gauntlet qualifier match. Perhaps he would find a clue to Harlan Raines’ intentions for this evening’s charade. Clinton decided that he had absolutely nothing to lose by making the trip to the bridge, only everything to gain. Little did he know that was the intention of Harlan Raines all along. * * * * * As the darkness crept westward with the slow ascent of the morning sunrise, the fax machine at William Dancy’s home stirred to life with a quiet hum. After a few seconds, a fresh fax was resting in the tray, ready to be read as soon as William prepared to leave for the Philadelphia Inquirer offices. The signature beneath the note read: “With eyes wide open, Clinton Sage.” * * * * *
