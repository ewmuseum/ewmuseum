Where the Road Ends

24 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-24 Brooklyn, New York November 3, 2006 "May I come in," Sammy Brown asked as he walked through the door, slowly making his way closer to where Bradley Stevenson was seated with his head in his hands. "Excuse me, Mr. Stevenson." Stevenson looked up and came face to face with the man who had just recently made his life a living hell. In his mind, Sammy was responsible for all the troubles he was going through. It started a few weeks ago, when he offered Sammy five hundred thousand dollars to throw a match that had been stacked in his favor for some odd reason, a simple task that Sammy could not follow through with. The substantial bet that was placed on Sammy's opponent would have netted him and Michael DeAngelis millions of dollars with the right bet. It was strange, because no one would have ever thought to bet on a wrestling match. Especially, when you think about the rumors that wrestling is fake and rigged in the first place. The only difference was, this tournament was different than any other. This tournament prided itself on the talent and ability of the wrestlers that signed up to compete. The list of elite names in the business that would come and try their hand against the best names they would otherwise never face was long, and tradition favored the unknown. Vegas had taken note of the competition, and how someone could come seemingly out of nowhere and claim victory as the pinnacle of the industry. Wages began to be made in underground gambling casinos, bookies started making odds to favor those big names that everyone thought would win, and suddenly wrestling wasn't just some pre-scripted action soap opera to entertain children. It was a way to make money fast. Bradley found this out when Sammy asked for time off to partake in the event, half surprised he was even accepted in the first place. Now that Sammy had explained the mistake that the bookers of Primetime Central had made in accepting the wrong Sammy Brown, Bradley knew why the odds were so stacked in his favor. The thing was, the odds of him winning the entire tournament were twenty to one, and the bet had already been made through DeAngelis. It was a fool's bet, but considering he had already made it that far… Well, who's to say that he wouldn't make it any further. That is, until he found out that Sammy had dropped out of the competition. "Look, Sammy," he began, though he never lifted his head to face him. "Unless you're here to tell me that you're leaving for Greece. I don't want to hear it." Sammy shook his head, he knew what Stevenson had invested in his progress in the tournament. He also knew that it wasn't where he belonged. He was chosen as a mistake, and his victories called nothing more than dumb luck. What was the point in being somewhere you never should have been in the first place? "I already explained my reasons to you yesterday, and I'm not going to repeat myself. If you want to be angry with me, that's fine. Just don't hold this against me here at work. You're my boss, sure, but it doesn't give you the right to hold something over my head that has nothing to do with what is going on in this hotel." "Is that what you think," Bradley chuckled as he lifted his head. "That I'm going to use my position to get you to do what I want?" Sammy stepped back as Stevenson stood to his feet, his hands planted firmly atop the desk to prop himself up. "Sammy, my life is over if you don't make it back in time to compete. The guy I told you about that fronted the money, he doesn't play games. If I lose his million dollars he is going to kill me, my wife, my kids, and probably you too." Sammy stiffened when he was brought up. "Me? What did I do?" "If you were to just lose, Sammy, it would be another thing. But for you to just walk away, that would be the ultimate slap in the face to a man like DeAngelis." Bradley stood up straight, then turned to face the window that was behind him, overlooking the crowded street with people marching to and fro to destinations only they were aware of. "If you don't want to win anymore, I don't care. It was a risk he was willing to take, considering he could make twenty million dollars if you end up winning the whole thing." He turned back to face Brown, his hands clenched behind his back. "That five hundred thousand we promised you is pocket change compared to what is at stake. That's why I told you that you could keep the hundred grand I already gave you." "I wasn't wanted there," Sammy managed to say. "Sure I won, and I could probably win again, but what's the point. I only did this because I thought my acceptance was a sign, that I was finally being given a chance to prove myself against some of the best. But I was wrong." He slowly shook his head from side to side, his lips curled down into a frown. "I walked away from something I've wanted all of my life, and as much as I want to go back and prove to those bastards that accepting me was not a mistake. I just don't have it in me anymore." "So you're just going to let them win?" "Don't try to turn this around. I understand what this means to you, and what can happen to you, but that was a mistake you made. Not me. I've already lost everything that ever meant anything to me, so dying doesn't scare me anymore. Not when I don't have anything worth living for." "You selfish bastard," Bradley screamed as he slammed his fist onto the desk. "I'm selfish," Sammy questioned as he placed his palm against his chest. "You're making bets that can make you rich, offering me about an eighth of what you can win to help you, and you're calling me selfish. Fuck you!" "Do you really think this is about the money anymore?" Both men have reached a point where talking is a distant memory, they are practically screaming at this point. "Yes, I do." "They're going to kill my wife, you prick. I know I made a mistake, but all you have to do is go to Greece and face some schmuck that probably thinks the World revolves around his silly ass. I don't care if you win anymore, Sammy. I just want you to go get in the damn ring and make it look like you're trying. That's it." Sammy's face is as red as a ripe pomegranate as he bores holes through Stevenson. "I can't do that." "You think they'll turn you away, that they won't give you your shot?" "No, I know they'll let me compete. Jenkins practically begged me to stay when I was leaving, but I'm through with that life. I wanted to be there and they didn't want me, and now I'm supposed to jump through hoops like a damn dog because they changed their minds. I don't think so." "Wait, they asked you to stay?" Sammy nodded, his nostrils flaring with every breath. "Sammy, you told me this was something important to you. You turned down half a million dollars because of your stupid pride, and now you're just going to walk away with nothing?" "I finally realized that I am too old to be chasing a dream. I've wanted this so bad my entire life, but when it was right there in front of me the door was slammed in my face… It's over. I realize that now." "They asked you to stay, Sammy. They wanted you to compete, but you're too blind to see it. Swallow that damn pride of yours and go back." "You just don't understand." "No, you're the one who-" "I quit." "I know you did, but-" "You didn't hear me." Sammy laughed softly, his eyes glossed over from the tears that began to well up within them. "I quit." Stevenson watched as Sammy turned around, and he realized that Sammy wasn't just talking about wrestling - he was talking about his job as well. "Sammy, wait." He begged. "Please, don't do this." He pleaded. "I'M BEGGING YOU!" But it didn't matter. Sammy was gone. ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ Athens, Greece November 3, 2006 "Yes sir, I understand completely." Reginald Jenkins had only been working at Primetime Central for a few months, he was a young guy with a hell of a lot of ambition. He proved during his first interview with the CEO that he could be very valuable to the company, but it was times like this that he dreaded the most – when things were going bad. When Sammy Brown walked away, it left Jenkins with a very interesting dilemma – how was he going to figure out how to plug someone else in to the vacant spot. While he and the committee that ran GTT had decided that Brown could ultimately tarnish the reputation of the tournament, the fans that actually shelled out the cash and showed up to the shows had made up their own minds about the man. They loved him. He brought something new to a tournament of this magnitude. People could relate to Sammy because he was just like them, a man trying to get somewhere through hard work and determination. He wasn't one of the main event draws everyone expected to win because they had been there before, and that was something Jenkins failed to notice when he attempted to rid his tournament of the old geezer that had slipped through the cracks. "What did he say?" Jenkins shrugged his shoulders, passed a quick glance across the desk, then answered flatly. "That I better fix this shit." Sitting there comfortably, his arms folded across his chest as he took notice of the frustration that began to fester within his associate, Tom Bellmont could do nothing more than nod. "I just don't know what to do, Tom." Jenkins let a sigh escape his lungs, the air warm against the moisture on his lips. "Sammy Brown doesn't return my calls and the next round is only a few days away." "I had this same problem last year when Rich Rollins didn't want to wrestle against Pete the PG Tips chimp." "What did you do?" Bellmont had surely peaked Jenkins' interest. "I threatened him." "You threatened Rich 'The Renegade' Rollins?" Bellmont nodded his head as a curt smile creased his features. "If you know what means the most to someone, they'll hardly ever tell you no. Take Rollins for example. The guy was the Global Champ, and the last thing he wanted to do was lose the title. A little mention of the Uber Judge replacing him as champ and shit was pulled together." "Yeah, well, Sammy's not even close to being anyone of importance." Jenkins began to tap his fingers against the top of his desk, while his mind raced with ideas about how to convince Sammy to return for the next round. Time was running out, and he was nowhere closer to figuring out an answer for his dilemma than when Sammy had decided to just up and walk away. "I think I know what we can do." Jenkins turned his attention toward Bellmont. "Well, what is it?" "How about I call Brown and tell him I'm a PTC attorney," Tom noticed Reginald's eyes as they lit up. "I'll tell him that I was told he had walked out. Then I'll use big words to mention that we could sue him if he didn't wrestle." Jenkins' eyes were as wide as silver dollars, while the rats in his head had begun to spin the wheel. "There's no way a man with no money would risk something like that." "Let's hope you're right." Jenkins reached for the receiver, pulled it from it's perch and pressed a few digits before he placed it against his ear.
