Embrace Do you believe in destiny? Do you believe that everything that we do, every act that we take, decision we make, is predestined? Is the fate of every individual predetermined beforehand … like a giant ball of twine being unwound by a nebulous hand in the sky to form a skein of interlinking possibilities, with no chance of deviation from the unseen pattern … before they are even aware of it? Is every road that we travel already mapped, is every left-turn off the interstate pre-prepared, the final destination known before the first step of the journey itself is actually taken? … or, do we as individuals, get to choose for ourselves what we do, how we do it, who we are and what we make of our lives? It would be too easy to use destiny as an excuse anytime that an endeavour failed, or an achievement fell short. It wasn’t my fault, I wasn’t destined to do it. It could become the crutch by which a failure lives and hope dies, and the safety net for never pushing beyond one’s own limits. It is not my destiny to be great, so I won’t even try. It could become the mantra of the lazy and the fearful, of those who look from the sidelines talking the talk but never actually walking the walk. If I am destined to do it, then it will happen. Why try at all? Why try? Why wake up each and every morning and hope that there is something new to discover and achieve? Why know that your personal best is to life two hundred pounds but keep on aiming for two hundred and ten? Why aim for the stars instead of staying grounded in the dirt? Why? … because the challenge is everything. The desire to be more than you already are has to burn bright and hot within the soul like a never ending flame. An eternal flame of a mythical creature that endures time after time no matter what; no matter what failures have come before. Failing is always going to be an option … a possibility if you prefer … but not even trying is a slow descent into death before one even has the opportunity to live. … and the most that anyone can hope for is to live their life to the fullest, to be the best that they can be, and to meet their destiny head on and scream at the sky that they will not settle for anything less than everything! You see that is the difference between one view of destiny and my own. I don’t believe that destiny rules me, I don’t believe the my life is set out before me and I just follow it along like a dog on a leash, pausing at every tree to sniff at the stench of those yapping idiots that went blindly before me because that is what I am ‘meant’ to do. For me destiny is something much different. Destiny is not something that I HAVE to accept. Destiny is something I choose to go after. Destiny is what I make of it! Destiny is mine. I don't stand in the darkness, with eyes wide shut, the light of life hidden from me by a shield of fears so great that they are palpable each and every time I draw breath into my body. Instead I reach out and touch the sky, reach out and touch the flames of destiny itself and ensure that it does not become something to fear, or to simply accept docilely like a lap dog getting fed scraps from his master’s hand. Instead of waiting for destiny to find me I look directly at its unfathomable mystery, I pull it to me. I fold it in my arms. I hold it tight. I can embrace it. So, do I believe in destiny? Do I believe that everything that I do, every act that I take, decision we make, is predestined? Is my fate predetermined beforehand … before I am even aware of it? Is every road that I travel already mapped, the final destination known before the first step of the journey is actually taken? If you still need me to actually answer that then you are are not actually deserving of an answer at all! You see I know that as long as I take each day as it comes and try my hardest to be better tomorrow than I was today … as long as I never give up looking for another challenge to face, and another mountain to climb … as long as destiny doesn’t get in my way, and I don’t get in it’s way … then it doesn’t really matter WHAT I believe. There is an ancient Chinese proverb which states that every journey begins with but a single step; that is the way that I choose to live my life. I don’t care what destination destiny thinks that it has in store for me as long as the journey itself is mine to take; as long as it is me that chooses the the trial that I leave along the way. Destiny holds no sway over me, it holds no power over me for I believe … no I KNOW … that we can only be what we give ourselves the power to be! So, as long as I look it in the eye. As long as I face it head on. As long as I embrace it. Destiny is what I choose to make it. ----- "Are you sure that this is what you actually want to do?" Full of concern the voice reached out to him where the speaker - no matter how much he wanted to - was unable to physically do so. It was obvious by the tension in his body, the quaver in his voice, that he ached to reach out and enfold him in his embrace - probably hoping to never let go - but it was also obvious that he wouldn't do that. Not this time. Only a matter of feet seperated the two figures but the darkness of the room was as much a physical barrier as the unspoken words still left between them. Sometimes it was easy to talk but really say nothing; and sometimes it was even easier to just say nothing at all. "You know that you don't have to ..." he started, once more, but his voice trailed off into silence as he knew without knowing that he would get no answer this time either. Talking to someone's back was bad enough but knowing that his words weren't just falling unheard but being deliberately ignored was so much worse. It had been like this for the last few days, ever since the decision had been reached. For over a year now they had been apart from everyone and everything that had tormented them previously. For over a year now they had been alone in each other's company; needing no-one else and wanting nothing more than to discover the truth behind their emotions. For over a year now they had lived for each other, they had loved each other and - simply - two souls had become one. Now - so quickly - that was all over. A clarion had called and a Siren sung; and now he answered. They had argued before, of course, in fact their first words had been heated but that had been nearly two decades before when they had both been nothing more than boys. Growing up into adulthood they had disagreed about many things and, at times, gone their seperate ways in life. A passion for journalism had called one while a passion for wrestling the other. It wasn't until their two Worlds combined, it wasn't until one found the other alive after being thought long dead, that their passions turned towards something else; something different; something new. Each other. They knew that there were words - hurtful and numerous - for what they felt for one another; for the physical acts they discovered that they enjoyed together. They also knew that the thoughts and opinions of no-one else mattered in the slightest as only one word truly summed up what the felt for one other - what they had found in the other's embrace. Love. Even that love, burning as strong as the hottest of fires, couldn't stop the old hungers coming back. Every so often, over the last year of travelling the World on a honeymoon by any other name, he had often seen the look of nostaliga brimming behind the surface of those dazzling jade eyes. A poster for an upcoming show, the frequent fan that would recognise him, the glimpse of a show on a hotel's television. Each time, of course, he had denied it. Of course I don't want to go back, he would say, that is my past, those days are gone ... you are my future now. Still, though, the faraway looks remained; and grew more frequent. Three weeks ago they had returned to the States. The jade eyes were heavy with jet-lag when they opened up the house in Flagstaff that had been empty for so long. Each and every room brought back a reminder of the days before; trophies here; titles there. There wasn't a single corner of a single room that didn't evoke some memory of the past. Apart from the bedroom, of course. Plain white walls and a new bed large enough for a car to park on was all that took up the space; it was meant to be their room, their space. On that first night, however, it was their first night apart since they had found each other. Waking up in darkness, he he had looked to the empty pillow beside him, his mind recalling that he had been told that he would be joined soon. Leaving the coldness of a bed that should have been so warm it didn't take him long to find out what had caused him to sleep, unembraced, alone. The jade eyes were staring at a title belt, hung in pride of place above the fire, and they hardly seemed to even notice his approach. That night was their first argument as lovers and while the harsh words had been forgiven, while the recriminations had been swollowed, neither had been forgotten. Now, in the house that shuld have been their home, in the room that should have been their sanctuary, he stood and watched his lover's back as he once more adorned himself in the garb of his chosen profession. A body suit of plain white covered his body from neck to ankle. Boots and gloves of the same colour covered his feet and hands. A tight braid of hair could just be made out throught the gloom, tied at the nape of his neck. Without turning around the figure reached up and drew something, slowly, onto the top of his face and paused. " ... I don't want you to do this." Finally the words had been spoken. Looking at his friend, his partner, his lover, all that he saw - at that moment - was a stranger. It wasn't the clothing, though admittedly he had never seen him wear this before, but then agin no-one actually had, but the demenour. He had thought that this life was over, he had been told that this life was over but now, here he was, staring at the back of the man that he loved and knowing that all he could do was tell the truth and set his heart on the line. He knew that once he heard the words ... once he understood just hoe much that this would hurt them both .. then he would change his mind. "I know." Pulling the plain white mask over his head the figure finally turned to stare at his lover. The mask was seamless, completely opaque, and only the shape of features could be made out. He knew that it was designed to give him an edge over his opponents, to make sure that they had no idea about what he was thinking, but as he looked at it all he could feel was repugnance. "I am not sure that I want to do this, I know that I don't have to do this," he said, his voice muffled by the material of the mask, "and I I know that you don't want me to do this." Reaching out, his body shimmering in the faint light of the dimly illuminated room like some ghostly spectre, he tried to embrace the man in front of him. His arms feel to his sides, his shoulders slumping, as his lover flinched and backed away. "... but I know that I have to." His voice was soft, barely perceptible through the mask, but even if it had been louder - even if it had been shouted - it would not have been heard for as he looked up he saw that he was alone in the room. "I have to." ----- Someone who believes in destiny believes that everything is foreordained, everything is pre-planned, and everything ... no matter what they do ... is laid out in front of them. Each step of the journey, no matter how much they would love to deviate and perhaps pause to see the sights, is marked down on the road map of their life, the destination set in immutable stone from before they were even born. Written in the stars so to speak. It is like learning to dance by following those delightful little black footsteps that you place around the room, simply placing one foot after the other in a pre-established routine and then lo and behold you find that you are waltzing along to the tune. … what happens, though, when the band play something different, what happens when a foxtrot is called for an all you see in front of you are those little steps laid down by someone else that you blindly follow no questions asked? I wouldn’t know I have never felt the need to dance to someone else’s tune, or follow another architect’s blueprint for my own life. I want something built I will use my own two hands, I want to dance I will feel the rhythm and do it my own way. It isn’t to the stars that I look for inspiration; it isn’t to the past I look to learn how to deal with the future. It isn’t destiny that I hope will rule my life because I am too weak, too confused, too stupid to do it myself. Someone who believes in destiny, someone who looks to the stars and sees in them more than the long dead legacy of a collection of gases twinkling their ghostly illumination. Someone who believes in and follows destiny is someone who has willingly chosen to neuter themselves … to castrate themselves with a rusty knife … to smile politely, bend over and say ‘yes please’ when the man known only as Bubba drops the soap. Someone who believes in destiny is someone who has willingly chosen to relinquish all power that they ever had in their own life, in their own pitiful excuse for an existence. You see it is all well and good to muse on failures, to ponder the meanings of life written in the stars, but when push comes to shove what you screwed up on before, the man you were in the past is nothing more substantial than a handful of rain. Running through your fingers. You cannot do anything with it you know, after only a few seconds there is nothing left but memories. Exactly where the past should be. The future is nothing more than a handful of wind. You cannot see it, you cannot really touch it, you can only assume that it is there and wait to see if there is any proof positive of it’s existence. The future hasn’t happened yet and until it does I refuse to bet that it will. Only today matters, only the here and now, only the present. Learn from the past, that is fine; hope for the future, if you must; but embrace the present. Live for it! ... and just hope that you can live with yourself too.
