What Love Can Do Part One

5 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-05 Love is no longer a term that can be synonymous with roses, amateur poetry, and two individuals that share a heavy liking to each other. No longer can it be used to refer to personal feelings of the opposite, or same, sex. Love’s reign as our strongest emotion is dying. Not because there is any less of it, no on the contrary there is more of it, but because Love no longer refers to the magic between two people who share a heavy liking to each other. Love has now surpassed beyond trivial notes in school, daytime soap operas, and expensive jewelry. It’s kingdom now lies beyond the kingdom of human lust and wanting and expands into human emotion, joy, spirit, and desire. Love is doing what you want to do and being happy with it. You can love a person as much as you can love playing your favorite sport. For the love of the game, isn’t that such a catchy phrase these days? Love means so many things now to so many people. No longer do you have to be in love to know love. No longer do you need to know love to feel its power overwhelm you. Love makes us do crazy things, whether it’s for a person we love, or a dream we love. It forces us to become weak, to react with our emotions and hearts rather than our minds. It drives us to constantly push our limits to reach our dreams such as being in the Olympics. Everyone views love as the most tender and powerful of human emotions. But they fail to realize that it can also be one of its more hurtful emotions as well. A loss of a loved one is a great loss. It is even greater when the power is too strong that it forces you to lose your own life as well. Such is the price of Love… New York University Campus: Division I Wrestling Division Arena Locker Room June 3, 2004 16:45pm (Two days before the selection) Ross Howell walked into the confines of NYU’s gym brushing aside the rainwater that had amassed on his business suit. He was a tall, sturdy man of the ripe age of 45. He sported a goatee, shaved sideburns, and walked with a purpose. Ross Howell was Ryan Howell’s father, the Captain of NYU’s Division I wrestling team and frontrunner for the last spot on the United States Olympic Team. Ross walked down the corridors of the gym, his hair patted down to his head from the rain, carrying a briefcase. He walked upright with extreme confidence, a proving tell of the differences between him and his son Ryan. Both Howells were revered for their determination and seemingly uncanny success. Ross was a successful lawyer who had accumulated a lot of court decisions and thereby a reputation as the best in New York. Likewise, Ryan Howell earned a reputation of being one of the best amateur wrestlers in New York. A distinction he shared with Sebastian Moraco. As much alike in success as father and son were, their methods were different. Ross was a hard charger and realist. Ryan was a sky larker; a vulture feeding off the scraps of others and taking every benefit his father could give him. Ryan Howell once even failed a drug test but was quickly swept under the rug by his father and his associates. It wouldn’t be the first time Ross intervened, or the last time. Ross knocked firmly on the door to Coach Melcher’s office before letting himself in. Mr. Melcher looked up from his desk and nodded his intrusion. If it had been anyone else, Melcher would have bellowed at him or her and made them clean the entire gym. However, he was used to Ross Howell’s intrusions by now and he expected this one. “Always the busy man, Mr. Melcher.” Ross ignited the conversation taking a seat on the sofa by the magazine shelves. Melcher didn’t bother to look up from his desk. “Just doing some routine work, Ross. Looking ahead at our schedules, doing the lineup for the summer, inventory check, trying to figure out what I’m going to say the press. You know, just routine stuff.” “Like figuring out who the best wrestler on your squad is right?” Ross intruded with obvious desire to change the subject. “I bet that one’s a really mind twister. There are so many good wrestlers on your team like my son Ryan, and that boy from Spain, what’s his name? Monaco?” “Sebastian Moraco.” Melcher corrected him. “A real good person too. That kid has more heart than anyone on the team. He’s definitely someone I’d like to have for the future. I’d have him help me to build up this program real good.” “That’s fine for the future.” Ross responded in his raspy voice. You’re like me, we both think ahead. We don’t waste time with flavors of the week. We know what the real stakes are. But sometimes we got to think about the present, no? Sometimes we gotta say, “fuck the future”, and worry about right now.” Melcher stopped his routine paperwork and looked up impatiently. “What are you getting at, Mr. Howell?” “In two days, the last remaining spot on the United States Olympic team will be closed out and you are the one person who has the say of who gets in, or not.” Ross answered. “Let me make this bluntly clear to you: My son will be on a plane to Athens next week.” Melcher slammed his fist on the desk. “How dare you make such a demand? Being selected for the team isn’t something that can just be handed out to anyone. Your son is a great athlete, but until he can show that he can lead without error, he doesn’t stand a chance of going to Athens.” “Why not?!” Ross bellowed. “He’s the best you got right now and you fucking know it. Who else are you going to send? That spic Moraco? My son’s your only choice.” “He’s arrogant, a drug-user, lazy, doesn’t want to obey our criteria, has a me-first-team-never attitude, and quite frankly if it weren’t for your interfering he wouldn’t be on the team. Your son is a great athlete with too many flaws for such a privilege.” Meanwhile… Sebastian walked down the corridors of the gym in his workout uniform, on his way to his daily routine. Today, however, would be a little different as he thought of stopping to see Coach Melcher first about the results of his test. Sebastian carried a light bag filled with towels and a change of clothes over his right shoulder and was about to knock on the door, before listening in on the argument going on inside. Sebastian wanted to leave and come back later, but the conversation inside startled him and he was hooked into eavesdropping. “This isn’t a time to be moral, Melcher.” Ross snarled. “For Heaven’s sake, the reputation and pride of our nation is going to be on this. Ryan is your best chance, no matter what. He can shape up, I promise you that.” Melcher sighed and shook his head. “I’m sorry, Mr. Howell. You’re son still has two more days to prove his worth. Until then, Moraco is my pick.” Ross sighed and then stood up from his seat. “You know, the problem here is that we aren’t communication. But that’s alright, Melcher, believe you me.” Ross tossed his suitcase onto Melcher’s desk and began to unlock it. “I learned a new language when I traveled to South America. Ironically, I learned the same language when I went to Europe. You want to know what my biggest problem was?” “The exchange rate?” Ross laughed at the mild joke. “No, Melcher. It was communicating. I couldn’t speak Spanish too well, nor Portuguese, nor French, nor German. It was terrible. But the one language I could speak that they all understood was money.” On cue, he opened up the latch and spun the briefcase around showing off to Melcher one hundred grand in small-unmarked bills. “What the hell is this?!” Melcher demanded, knowing full well it was a bribe. “Just a few hundred thousand friends telling you they love you.” Ross answered. “A few thousand as in there is more to this, if you keep your end. I know how much your family is struggling these days. You can’t hide it.” “This…” “…is merely a token of my advanced appreciation.” Ross answered for him. “Just make sure Ryan is on a plane to Athens.” “It is unfortunate.” Melcher answered bringing the suitcase closer to his person. “It is unfortunate that Sebastian would have to hear such bad news. But this will be good for him in the end, I believe. The boy has to learn that disappointments are a way of life.” Sebastian stepped back from the door. His heart sank and he wanted to bust into the room and overwhelm Melcher and Ross in a fury of conventional punches. Fortunately his brief moment of wanton destruction and violence was merely only a passing reprieve. Sebastian tossed his bag halfway across the gym, ignoring those who shouted for his name. He walked, nearly ran, out onto the campus and sat by the parking lot benches, gripping his State Championship Medal. He looked down on it, biting his lips from crying, knowing full well he had lost his dream. He had lost what he loved. Sebastian looked down on his medal once before hoping in his mind he would be holding Olympic Medal. That dream was no longer. Two days later, Ryan Howell was selected for the Olympics Team. Two weeks later, Howell failed his drug test, the spot was blotched and closed, and Sebastian’s dream was over. NYU Campus: Present Sebastian sat on the same bench he had sat three months ago gripping his now faded gold medal in his hand. He spun the ribbon around his hand nonchalantly and enjoyed the moment of serenity he had. Three months ago he sat here wondering what would become of him. Now he sits there wondering how far he’ll get in the Prime Time Central’s GT Tournament. Will Jeff’s promise of exposure and a second chance at Olympics be true? Sebastian hopes it will be. “That’s the gold medal you won in the State’s Championship, right?” Sebastian looked up and saw his assistant coach [name withheld] standing in front of him, noticeably tanned from his trip to Hawaii. “How was Hawaii?” Sebastian asked him. “It was pretty fun.” Jeff answered. “Except for that part, you know where the person you’re trying to help leaves the island and doesn’t tell you.” Sebastian laughed. “Get over it, kid.” Jeff answered not in the mood for jokes. “I’m trying to help you get back into the Olympics. I know you had a tough match, but try to work with me here. What happened? Did you lose again?” Sebastian scoffed. “Relax, old man. I won. I don’t understand why you are so worried about this. This is supposed to be my dream we’re trying to save right? So relax. Besides, P. Vetra was so easily defeated, it was like he didn’t show up.” “For your sake, I hope so.” Jeff replied. “It doesn’t get any easier from this point on. Yet, all you have to do is to continue winning and being the good sport you are. People are starting to notice you now, Sebastian. I got a call from the New York Post yesterday asking me if I would like to speak on your behalf.” Sebastian looked up in mixed awe and discontent. “Is that so?” Jeff nodded. “It seems nice.” Sebastian said. “But like I said, this is my dream to fix. And if you’ll excuse me, I want to be alone.” “Aren’t you even going to train for this match?!” Jeff bellowed. “I hear your upcoming match will be most difficult.” Sebastian stood up and dismissed Jeff. “I told you before, leave me the fuck alone. I’ll handle this my own way.” Jeff shook his head and left Sebastian alone. Sebastian walked away from their conversation and slowly made his way through the parking lot. He walked until a black 1994 BMW revved up next to him. It was Aaron inside the car. “What the hell are you still doing here, Sebastian?” he joked. “It’s almost nightfall, and you’re still at school.” Sebastian stopped, along with the car. “Funny.” Sebastian retorted. “I should be asking you the same thing, what the hell are you doing here?” “I’m on my way to pick up Stacey.” Aaron answered. “I don’t understand why she just doesn’t come to NYU, but oh well. Hey, your car’s still in the shop, right?” Sebastian nodded. “Get in.” Aaron said. Sebastian got inside and Aaron started to drive out of the parking lot. “I have to ask you something.” Aaron said. “Stacy told me that you got a call from Jeff while we were in Hawaii. What is that about?” “Oh…” Sebastian cleared his throat. “That’s my fault. I guess I should have told you this earlier, but it doesn’t matter. Jeff called me to tell me about getting my chance at the Olympics in Beijing. He wanted to make amends, and had this dastardly idea of me fighting in an unconventional wrestling tournament. He said the exposure would expose me to coaches and trainers everywhere. From that point, I’d be an easy selection for the World Championships and I’ll get my shot at joining the team in Beijing.” “Wow…” Aaron said bewildered. “I’ve never even heard of any kind of wrestling tournaments. So how’s this working out?” “I lost in the first round.” Aaron couldn’t help himself as he cocked his head back and laughed. “Wait to go, man. You really showed them huh?” he quipped jokingly. Sebastian couldn’t help but let out a chuckle either. He knew it was pretty ironic, and somewhat funny, to be eliminated in the first round when you’re trying to get a second chance at your dreams. “I had a tough match. The tournament committee gave me a second chance though.” Sebastian quickly added in a monotonous tone. “This time I won’t make that mistake. I’ve already beaten my second round opponent.” “So, like how are these people?” Aaron asked, still driving his way out of NYU campus. “I don’t think they’re anything like what you’re used to.” “No.” Sebastian agreed. “The matches are much more violent, fast, desperate, and more real. P. Vetra was easy though. It was almost as if he didn’t show up.” “Hopefully, all your matches will be like that.” “Me too.” Sebastian nodded. “But I still want to be tested. It will be no fun if none of my opponents show up. This next one, Digital Mortality, is supposed to be someone very good.” “How good?” “As in no one expects me to win kind of good.” Sebastian added in. “People are already looking over to round four with him and guessing who he’ll face. No one is giving me a chance, and I kind of like it.” “I dunno man, I think I would hate that.” Aaron chimed in with his own two cents. “To know that no one gives you any hope, that’s got to suck. I’d be mad as hell.” “I know what you mean. But this is the first time that I haven’t been a favorite going in. It’s always been Sebastian can do this, he better win this, he better beat this, and so on. It feels good to go into a match without any expectations. I know I can beat Digital Mortality and that’s all that matters.” “Yeah well….SHIT!” Aaron cried out as he swerved the car to a break by the exiting curb to NYU campus. Just a few feet away from the car was a man on the cement pavement. Just moments earlier he had stepped onto the street in what seem to be a voluntary impulse to get in the way of the car. Both Aaron and Sebastian checked themselves for any injuries before Aaron turned off the engine. They walked towards the man who nearly killed himself with a mixture of anger and fear. The man looked like he was in his mid thirties. His face was young, yet his body and face were marred with dirt, and his clothes were wrinkled and dusty. It almost looked like he was homeless, or at least didn’t care for hygiene. In any case he was the worse for the wear as he stumbled back onto the sideboard as if nothing had happened. “Hey man, you all right?” Aaron asked squinting his eyes to see what was the matter with him. It was at that point that both he and Sebastian realized that the man had been crying, probably only just recently. “No, leave me alone.” The man shouted at them trying to leave. Sebastian stopped him and tugged at his arm. “Hey come on now, you’ll be alright.” The man pulled his hand away forcefully and shook his head in complete denial. “No, I won’t be all right! I just lost my fiancée and my unborn baby. She was hit by a SUV and died on the way to the hospital!” The man began to tear up again and Aaron tried to console him. “Yo, it’s going to be all right.” He said. “I’m sorry for what happened, I know that doesn’t mean much to you though.” The man shook his head. “You’re right it doesn’t. I don’t care what happens to me. Without her, I’m nothing. We were supposed to get married. I was supposed to be a father. But I neglected her in her final weeks and now she’s gone!” Sebastian sighed. He didn’t know what to tell him. He had never known love of this magnitude before. He had several girlfriends but none of them ever mounted into anything serious. The only love he ever knew was the love of following his dream to be selected for the United States Olympic team. He had lost that love, but still did not know what kind of pain this stranger felt. Love was such a powerful emotion, and in its strongest form left every man weak. Just as Sebastian was about to attempt to console him, the man jumped back onto the street in the way of an oncoming car. “Jesus Christ!” TO BE CONTINUED
