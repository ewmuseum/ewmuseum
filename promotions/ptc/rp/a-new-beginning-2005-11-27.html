A New Beginning

27 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-27 Bright neon lights bathe the seedy back streets of downtown Tokyo, dancing across the wet pavement in a forgotten section of the modern metropolis. These are the streets owned by the Yakuza, the most powerful crime organization in all of Japan. Prostitution, gambling, all matters of vice can be attained by any passer-by from any of the unscrupulous establishments lining these streets, all courtesy of an intricate organization of Japan’s most powerful gangsters. Pedestrians pass each other quickly and with a minimum of eye contact, all respecting each other’s desire to maintain a low profile. A light rain continues to fall, affording patrons the opportunity to hide beneath their umbrellas as they scurry out of view as quickly as possible. The streets were blind, willing to forget their faces so long as they leave their yen behind. And so it was that a five hundred pound man, owner of one of the most infamous faces in all of Japan and painfully conspicuous, found this to be the only place in the world he could maintain his anonymity. It was that very anonymity that had first drawn him to the mean streets; it was that same anonymity that had pulled him back in. A pair of lifeless charcoal eyes peer out from beneath the brim of a black fedora, pressing onward through the mist. Pulling his trenchcoat tighter around his frame, he finally arrives at his destination; bright blue Japanese characters flicker over a modest, unassuming door. It isn’t often that a five hundred pound man, master of the martial arts and possessor of unrivaled strength and agility, can be found hesitating. But this door gave him pause. It had been ages since he had stood in this spot; but fate had brought him back. With a deep breathhe pushed through the door, his colossal frame gliding through without a hint of its passing. Inside, a deep techno beat pulses through the air, subtle ambient lighting illuminating just enough of the room to light the center but keep the corners private. A large musclebound bouncer stands a few feet away, immediately blocking the way. "Can I help you, sir?" he asked firmly but politely. Pushing the brim of his fedora back on his brow, he finally raised his head and revealed his face to the bouncer. Immediately the young man’s eyes grew wide with fear. "Kim- …Kimbusa? I was told you were…" "Dead?" Kimbusa chuckled in his perfectly annunciated Japanese. "It would appear you have been misinformed. Where is he?" Not daring to stand against him, the bouncer dropped his head in a show of respect, pointing to the other side of the club. Kimbusa passed by the humbled servant and began making his way through the club, which was particularly quiet on this night. Most of the clientele had receded to the shady corners of the room, where they were negotiating a few hours of service. But Kimbusa had no desire to indulge in the pleasures of the flesh again. Kimbusa came to an inconspicuous door in the back, flanked by a pair of young Japanese boys. Another glance at Kimbusa’s face, and the opposition melted away. With a deep breath, Kimbusa stepped inside. The room was lit only by a small array of candles, circling a low desk in the center of the room. Behind it sat an old man, clad in a fine business suit, hands folded upon the desk. "Come in, Kimbusa-san," came the weathered but intense voice. Kimbusa cautiously stepped into the room, pulling the door closed behind him. Keeping his eyes forward, Kimbusa attempted to survey the room. He knew they were not alone, but he could not find the guards in the darkness. "I did not expect to see you again," the old man said, sitting motionless behind his desk, looking almost artificial in the candlelight. "What can I do for you?" "I will not waste your time trying to explain myself," Kimbusa began, speaking quietly and with as much respect as he could muster in his deep, gravelly voice. "I need your help, Shindo-san." A brief moment of quiet was broken by a shrill laugh from Shindo. "My help? And what could you possibly offer me in return?" Kimbusa smiled. "You are fully aware of my… talents. What I need to do I cannot do without your aide. I ask you for you assistance… Shindo-san." Shindo grew quiet, considering this. Then, slowly, he slipped his hand into his breast pocket. "My son, things have taken a turn for the worse since you left. Things aren’t the same. But there are those of us who still operate in ways others consider… old fashioned." Without warning, a pair of iron grips latch onto Kimbusa’s shoulders, forcing him to his knees before the desk. Kimbusa’s hand shot into his jacket, reaching for his sidearm. But the metallic click of a gun cocking behind his head gave him pause. Reluctantly, Kimbusa allowed his hand to be pulled from his jacket and slapped onto the desk. Shindo, eyes cold as ice, produces a glistening silver blade from his pocket. "If you want our help, Kimbusa, you must again prove your loyalty… The true Yakuza way." Shindo’s voice was unwavering as he slapped the blade down before Kimbusa with his left hand; Kimbusa clearly saw that half of Shindo’s left little finger had been removed, the ancient Yakuza sign of eternal loyalty. It became clear to Kimbusa what he must do. Kimbusa picked up the blade with his right hand, his dark, piercing eyes unwavering. The gun presses hard against his head, warning him of the consequences of refusal. Placing the knife against his finger, Kimbusa closed his eyes. This would set the tournament in motion. With one quick, firm slice of the blade, the first phase of his plan had begun.
