the Quest: part Two

2 October 2005

Date: 2005-10-02 The Final Fantasy stood, stock-still, almost unable to believe what he saw. He had expected something terrible. Perhaps a big burly man with a knife, or a scarily dressed individual with a gun. What he saw now, however, was a little girl. By Seymour’s estimation, she couldn’t have been more than seven. She wore a little dress, and appeared to be absolutely terrified. In spite of this, she was first to speak. “Mister,” she managed, “I lost my mommy…” Seymour nearly fainted at the sound of the innocent, high pitched voice. He had expected to be mugged, expected to have to fight for his life against a demented killer who had randomly selected him for execution. Expected to feel a cold knife sink into the tender flesh of his neck and draw blood. But now? Now it was incredibly silly to be afraid, considering it was a seven year old girl he was dealing with. Besides, the heroic component of Almasy’s psyche kicked in. He would find this girl’s mother, and see to it that mother and daughter were reunited. Such was Seymour’s way. “Don’t worry,” Almasy responded, with a nod of his head. “I’ll help you find her. Where was the last place you saw her?” The young girl thought for a few moments, absent-mindedly wrapping a lock of hair about her finger as she struggled to remember. Finally, her face brightened, however slightly. “I saw her outside the toy store,” she explained, slowly, “but then I saw this really neat thing go down the street. I chased it in here, but I couldn’t find it. I’m scared…” Her vague explanation actually helped Seymour. He knew this area pretty well, and as such, knew that the only toy store in the vicinity was on the other side of the alleyway, only about a block or two away from the swordsmith. Excellent. “Come with me,” he said, extending a hand down to the girl. She accepted it trustingly, her little legs doing their best to keep up with Seymour’s admittedly slowed pace. “What’s your name, mister,” came the question from the inquisitive youngster, even as they emerged from the alleyway into the bright glow of the streetlights. Seymour guided her into the left turn onto the sidewalk, and they were off, walking down the streets of New York. “Seymour,” he told her, remembering to answer her question. “Seymour Almasy.” “That’s a nice name,” she said, nodding, before suddenly breaking out of his grip at a dead run. “Mommy! Mommy!” Seymour looked up, and saw the sign for the toy store. Ah, here they were already. Presuming by the fact that the little girl was now hugging a very teary eyed woman, he supposed that mother and daughter were together again. He watched the happy reunion from a distance, unable to hear the words being spoken, but that didn’t matter. The gestures and body language conveyed happiness words never good. After finally separating, the little girl jerked a thumb back in Seymour’s direction, and said something. Obviously, she was explaining his role in the reunion. Without a moment’s hesitation, the mother stepped forward, daughter in tow. She wore a warm smile on her face, and extended a hand that Seymour shook. “Thank you, Mr. Almasy,” she said, obviously having been briefed on his name by her daughter. “I owe you.” He dismissed the comment with a wave of his hand. “You don’t owe me anything, ma’am. I found her in an alleyway, what was I supposed to do, just leave her there?” Seymour smiled at both mother and daughter, and they reciprocated the gesture. The young girl stepped forward, wrapping both of her arms around one of Seymour’s legs in a hug. Almasy gave a half-chuckle, half-nervous giggle as he felt the grip tightened. “You appear to have a way with children,” the mother said, grinning. “Do you have any of your own?” Seymour shook his head. “Can’t say that I do, no.” “Are you married?” “No.” It was a truthful answer, but not the entire truth. Still, it was the answer he gave people who didn’t know him. There was no point in bringing back up the pain and agony that he felt every day of his life, anyway. “That’s a shame,” came the woman’s response. “You’d make a fine father. Well, we have to go. Thank you very much for helping my daughter!” They walked off to continue their day, having no idea of the blow that they had just unwittingly dealt the former Global Champion. Seymour leant against the brick wall of one of the nearby buildings, using it to prop himself up. His vision became blurred, as the beginnings of tears began to crystallize in his ducts, obscuring everything but the emotions he felt. He should already BE a father. Seymour and Laura had talked about it a half-dozen times. He would wrestle for a year or two more, and then they would adopt a child. There would be no need for him to continue destroying his body, no need to live in a perpetually jet-lagged state. The Final Fantasy, vaunted ‘legend’ of PTC, would spend the rest of his days playing with his kid. But, no. He’d fucked that up. Seymour had robbed himself of that life, all because he couldn’t keep his dick in his pants on a night he couldn’t even remember. Because he finally gave in to one of the fangirls who seemingly followed him to every UWF show. THAT particular bombshell had set off the arguing. She was overprotective. He was a cheating asshole. She made an annoying sound when she chewed. He always left the toilet seat up. Every tiny issue was turned into a relationship-ending rift. And so he was here, out under the stars, on a quest for a sword. Remembering that fact gave him the motivation he needed to tear himself from the wall, and go on, knowing that his destination was a block or so away. Seymour did his best to smile as he trudged forward. It might only have been a thirty minute walk, but it was taking a hell of a lot out of the Final Fantasy, more than he could have imagined when he set out on his Quest. “What a night,” he muttered under his breath, able to see the sign for the swordsman’s shop coming closer and closer. “I should have just taken a cab here.” A cab would have been the simple route, Seymour knew. Too simple, an insistent voice in the back of his mind set. Nothing was really worth anything unless it came at great cost. And not monetary cost, either. There was a reason that no RPG had ultimate equipment that was purchasable in a shop. No, the Ultima Weapons and Masamunes of the world were hidden, guarded by fearsome beasts, or buried in the middle of deserts. Almasy’s new ultimate weapon was in the middle of a jungle. An urban jungle. Already he had confronted so much. The damned faux-pracher who used religion for his own gain. He’d seen a new authority figure come to power, with designs on using his authority to shape society. And last, but certainly not least, he’d learned that the darkness was not all it was cracked up to be; that what appeared to be frightening wasn’t necessarily so. As he sank to his knees, though, he knew that there was one obstacle he’d forgotten about. Himself. He felt inexplicably exhausted. The only thing he could think of to do was crawl along the sidewalk. Slow but steady progress was made, although it felt like something was attached to him, trying to hold him back. So close. So GODDAMNED close! Seymour shook, trembling with the exertion. It seemed that lead weights held him down as he surged to his feet, bones shrieking with pain. Damn the past holding him back. Damn ANYTHING holding him back, tying him down. “I….AM SEYMOUR ALMASY!” he screamed, largely to indifference from other passersby. It might have seemed a silly thing to say, but he needed to remind himself of that fact. He was a man who loved what he did. A man who gave it his all, every time out. A man who would rather die than disgrace himself. And slowly, each step became easier. The pain was blocked out, bit by bit. Just like he did in the ring, Seymour shut his eyes, and simply willed himself on. Step. “I will not fail.” By step. “I cannot fail.” By step. “I MUST NOT FAIL!” And then, the doorknob he had sought, on a half hour journey that had in all likelihood taken at least double that. Seymour threw the door open, taking deep, heaving breaths. The swordsmith saw Seymour immediately, saw the exhausted looking man in his doorway staring at him with basest desire in his eyes. It was an expression the man knew well, for he had created blades for Almasy before. “Seymour,” the man said, eyes glimmering. “It is ready.” Almasy froze. It was all worth it in the end. All the pain. All of the self-doubt. Everything that went into his Quests that made him wonder why he even bothered embarking on them at all would be embodied in his glittering prize. It was brought to him on a velvet pillow, sword and scabbard lying at one another’s side. Seymour didn’t notice the sword’s fine hilt, though, even as beset with gems as it was. Nor did he notice the scabbard, covered in velvet itself. The perfect sheath for the perfect weapon. He didn’t even notice the long, curved blade, tipped with diamond dust to make it among the sharpest weapons known to man. Grinning, the swordsmith followed Almasy’s eyes to what he DID notice. Nodding appreciatively at his own handiwork, he addressed the blade’s new owner. “I engraved exactly what you told me to, Seymour. I hope it is to your liking?” Seymour returned the nod, giving a single bounce of his head. For there, engraved on the sword were the owner and the name of the most powerful blade in existence. “SEYMOUR ALMASY” was the owner. “INVINCIBLE” was the name. ------------------------------------------------------- About thirty minutes later, a newly energized Seymour Almasy had already gotten into a cab, and returned to his apartment. He made his way up the stairs with a bounce in his step. Everything had gone according to plan. In spite of the dangers, and in spite of the trials he had encountered, his Quest was over, and he had been successful. Seymour dug into his pocket for the apartment’s key, and made his way to the door with quick, powerful strides. He couldn’t wait to see how he looked with his prize, to see the might that was now strapped to his waist. He couldn’t turn the key in the lock fast enough. Finally, his nervous hands succeeded in opening the door to his domicile. But if he expected anything to have changed, he would have been woefully disappointed. The television was still exactly where it was. The recliner remained in the same position that he had left it. RIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIINNNGG!! And, perhaps most distressingly, his cell phone was STILL FUCKING RINGING. Or was it? It was entirely possible one of his friends was calling. Or maybe Grandpa Cid. Closing the door behind him, Seymour decided to find out. He picked his phone up, and flipped it open, to see who was calling. JONATHAN CALVER [detail withheld] RIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIINNNGG!! Oh, for the love of Yevon. Suddenly though, Seymour brightened. Before his quest, the ringing cell phone was a problem he couldn’t deal with. Now, though, his prize would let him solve the problem quite nicely. He unsheathed the Invincible cleanly, marveling at the weight of the weapon in his hand. Despite all of the differences, it still felt just like his other blades, the Divine Blade Imperials Mach I and II. He’d held two such blades in his hands before. But there was something undeniably awesome about holding a third. With a Cheshire cat grin, Almasy picked up the opened cell phone, which was still ringing, of course. His left hand tossed the telephone in the air, as Seymour’s practiced eyes tracked its arc. Left hand joined right on the hilt of the Invincible as he swung upwards, a perfect arcing blow that cleaved the screen from the keypad. Both parts of the phone hit the carpet at the same time, and Almasy paused for a moment to admire his handiwork. Yes, this third was VERY good, indeed. RIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIINNNGG!! Somehow, the phone still rang. Seymour supposed that it was because the ringing mechanism was contained in one part of the phone, or the other. Raising the blade high with both hands, he plunged it down into the keypad, sending electronic innards flying everywhere. Finally, Seymour had slain the Evil Cellular Telephone Menace. Now, he could suit up for battle. Triumphantly he walked to his bedroom, down the hallway connecting it to the rest of the apartment. He caringly, lovingly placed his new sword back in its scabbard, undoing the tie that held it around his waist as he entered the bedroom. The sword was placed on the bed as he stripped wordlessly, standing only in Cactuar boxers. Into his closet he went, for attire he had not worn in a year and a half. The greaves he saved for last, as the rest of the lower body armor had to go on first. He clapped the leg pieces into place, almost immediately feeling the familiar sweat start to rise on his skin from contact with the heavy attire. Oh, how he’d missed that… He finished fiddling with the leg armor, able now to slide the greaves into place. Still, though, he’d worn this semi-recently. A year ago. He remembered wearing JUST the leg pieces for the image of him that was projected to the world thanks to the Prime Time Central website. But for a battle of the magnitude he had coming, he needed all the protection he could get. He needed the chest plate. And there it was, shimmering in the light of the bedroom. The chest plate, with the black X on it that symbolized one of Seymour’s favorite Final Fantasies. Briefly, he struggled with the unfamiliar weight, before managing to strap it, too, onto his body. Now, he was complete. He hadn’t been this complete in what seemed like forever. The bedroom door, when closed, had a mirror on it. Gathering his sword, and strapping it back to his side, Seymour decided to take a look at himself. “Perfect…” He could come to no other conclusion. He looked like SEYMOUR ALMASY again. There were many other outfits he’d worn since he won CW1, outfits that had detracted from who he was. The Final Fantasy was not meant to wear a fancy suit, and attend business dinners for GCW. Nor was he meant to storm into Tournament Director Wool-Knit’s office and make demands like a spoiled prim Donna, as he had during CW2. Seymour Almasy was built for the Battle Arena. He always had been. He always would be. And now, thanks to a Quest, he had realized that fact again. “On my honor,” he swore to his reflection in the mirror, “I have made many mistakes. All this time I have thought that denying who I am would help, would lead me to success. But now I see that I must be myself. I am once again the man who won the Global Title twice; once again the Warrior who holds it above all other treasures!” Seymour smiled, genuinely, for perhaps the first time since CW1. Wrestling wasn’t a business. It wasn’t a way to get movie deals, or any nonsense like that. It was about proving who the better man was. “On the campus of Harvard,” he vowed, “I will get back what was once mine. After all, this is a Scavenger Hunt! And when it comes to searching towns for useful information that will help in the completion of a Quest….WHO IS BETTER THAN SEYMOUR ALMASY?!” The Invincible was unsheathed again, and Seymour gazed into his reflection. The sword waved back at him, almost in greeting. Staring back at Seymour Almasy was not a businessman. Seymour stared back at Seymour, the image and the man once again one and the same. With the Invincible in his hands and raised high, Seymour knew that he had another Quest on his horizons. The Quest for the PTC Extreme Title. The Quest for the PTC Global Title. The Quest…for wrestling immortality.
