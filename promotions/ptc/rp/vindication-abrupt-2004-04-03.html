Vindication: Abrupt

3 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-04-03 Journalists live by the Rules of the Write. Whether working on a school news-rag, or serving as a reporter for a major media machine like BBC or The New York Times, there are six things to keep in mind; six immutable commandments that make up the Bible of Storytelling. A quick review lays down the information for posterity. Who: Jimmy Diablo What: Won his match in PCW's Face-to-Face Challenge Where: PCW Turmoil When: March 27th, 2004 Why: It would be his first full step on the way to his return to wrestling... his first full step on the way to vindication. How: A surprise rollup to gain a pinfall victory over the much larger Propaganda. Those were the facts as they stood. However, that was only half of the story. As telling as those six categories may be, they didn't cover the seventh and most important category to storytelling. Without it, all would be meaningless words lost upon closed eyes and deaf ears. The seventh category: So? No one really knew why anyone should care about Jimmy Diablo. Not even Sandy Wong, who had been dispatched to conduct an interview with him, was sure if Diablo required any of her reserves of give-a-damn. Diablo was a visiting free agent who somehow got himself into the Open Challenge, which was normally reserved for PCW members. He was booked against Propaganda because he left it up to management to fill in the name of whomever he challenged. Propaganda needed something to do, and so Jimmy was booked against God's Ultimatum. Even though Jimmy Diablo won, it was hard to call it as anything more than a fluke. The guy had his teeth kicked in over and over again that night. Even when he somehow drudged up the adrenaline to mount a comeback, he only hit one or two punches to no effect before Propaganda slammed him into the mat once more. A surprise rollup isn't much to be impressed about. The fact is, the news week was slow for PCW, and so Wong was sent out to prove that she should be getting a paycheck. Jon Kano himself was reported to have said that he was keeping her around for her ass, rather than her journalistic talent. That’s not much of an endorsement. That type of glass ceiling was something that she probably couldn't work out from underneath. If the paycheck wasn't so good, she'd go elsewhere. Sadly, an exotic young beauty with a penchant for miniskirt suits and a decently ample cup size finds it hard to be taken seriously anywhere. She sat across from Diablo inside Timpano's, a cigar bar and Mediterranean-themed restaurant in Las Olas, a section of Fort Lauderdale, Florida. After Turmoil, Jimbo K. felt that Jimmy Diablo needed to relax a bit. He was still in pain, but he'd need to at least be in a better state of mind. Good food, good atmosphere, and wonderful women; what better remedy to a little back pain? At least that's what Jimbo believed. Up until Sandy showed, it was a slow day in the women department, it rained cats and dogs, and for some reason, the servers (both male and female) were all on a case of PMS that made them hostile to any and every living thing in the vicinity. The first question that fell out of Wong's mouth, quite off-topic, "Where's that cute guy who was with you Saturday?" Jimbo was not with them. He had some sort of engagement at the nearby Big City Tavern. “He might be around later,” Jimmy said, ripping apart some of the steamed mussels that the cooks did so well at the establishment. Wong, a recently-converted vegetarian, ate her pre-meal bread with a mixture of red wine and olive oil that made one’s mouth water just by the tiniest drop upon the tongue. “How did you decide on this place?” She was no stranger to this taste, having been to Timpano’s in the past when she dated Skylar G. At the time, South Florida was one of his usual haunts, and Las Olas was one of his more favorite places to relax. A local musician did an odd acid-jazz cover of Metallica’s “Enter Sandman.” Quite an ambitious, and perhaps avant-garde performance, but the patrons didn’t seem to mind. It probably helped that they didn’t know what he was covering in the first place. JD shrugged. “Jimbo suggested it since it would be close to where his cousin lives. Wanted to catch up with the guy.” A bit of the olive oil dropped on Wong’s blouse. The color was red anyway, so the stain wasn’t so noticeable. She wiped at it with her left hand while setting a digital sound recorder on the table and pressing the ‘Record’ button. “I’m sure you know how this works,” Wong said. “I’ll just ask a few questions and hope that you don’t attack me.” Recently in PCW, people were getting attacked left and right by wrestlers. Liability insurance rates were rising, and the non-wrestling crew and staff were complaining that the words ‘PCW Security’ were an oxymoron at best. Wong herself commented, when asked about worker safety, I think it would be a good idea. The upper half of Jimmy Diablo’s face contorted in such a way that a blind person could see his shock and hurt. “I assure you, young lady, that I am the consummate gentleman when it comes to media relations. Even so, my personal code of ethics keeps me from striking women, children, handicapped persons… I’m restricted to a wrestling ring to actually do any damage… and I don’t even do it well there.” “On that topic,” Sandy began, the start to the interview itself for PCW’s news-site, “I wanted to ask you first of all about yourself. A formal introduction to PCW fans about who you are, why you are in the tournament, how well you think you’ll do… that sort of thing.” Melanie, the server, took the sautee pan of empty mussel shells from the table that sat between the two at their six-seated table. The establishment was relatively empty, most of the bodies and activities around the front of the two-sided, 7x15 wine and spirits closet the side of which closest to the door also served as the bar with a counter in the front. The other 12 tables and 10 wall unit booths sat undisturbed, spread out through the surprisingly large cigar café. Everything was so neat and tidy that it seemed a harsh imposition upon the clean look to seat more people in the dining section. Jimmy Diablo scratched his head. “Well, you know I’m Jimmy Diablo. Not sure what else to say about who I am.” “Tell us about your wrestling experience,” suggested the more respected of the PCW female journalists. Neither of them would win a Pulitzer anytime soon, though, and it wasn’t as though Tiffany Jennings was great competition. Diablo shook his head. “Unimportant. I’ve been out for eight years with a serious neck injury. All my past experience is pointless now. All that needs to be said is that I’m a free agent.” The Greek symbols expertly drawn upon the walls in Mediterranean Blue add to the atmosphere. Timpano’s has a style and flair all its own. Pegging it as one distinctive thing is hard to do. Servers were mostly sitting in the lounge section near the bar, or outside smoking cigarettes. The bartender just passed out drinks at the counter, getting the most work for the slow day. Sandy Wong attempted to get things moving along. “The only free agent to win a Face-to-Face match. And against Propaganda, that’s something.” “I won by a quick count,” so he shot down her praise. “I got killed in that match, but didn’t prove anything. That match meant nothing. It didn’t serve my purpose.” Now things were getting somewhere. “And what is your purpose?” she asked. Diablo sipped the half-full (JD being a habitual optimist) glass of Pellegrino. “I’m old, worn out. I should have stayed retired, but I didn’t. This is my last chance to win.” “But you already won your match with Propaganda.” Jimmy Diablo sighed. “… Look up the Diablo Curse on the internet and see what comes up. Suffice it to say, I have to win this tournament, or the PTC Cruiserweight Tournament, before I retire for good. If not, I failed myself and my family as well.” Jimbo rushed in from the rain. He hadn’t changed since Saturday. Given that it was Monday, that wasn’t a good indication of his hygiene. Sandy didn’t think that he was as cute now, seeing his disheveled appearance. Haste was evident in his body language, as well as the panting from a jog to get to Timpano’s. “JD, we gotta go, man,” he said, leaving it at that to turn around and begin to leave. Sandy Wong found this rather rude. “What’s going on?” “I don’t have time to explain. Jimmy, let’s get moving.” Jimbo was never so hasty to get in and out of someplace. At least, not unless something was wrong. Diablo didn’t waste anymore time. Bowing his head politely to Sandy Wong, he took his leave. Only over his shoulder did he apologize, “I’m terribly sorry for the abrupt end. I’ll give you another interview at a later time.” And with more speed than he had shown in his match with Propaganda (which probably would have done him more good), he was gone.
