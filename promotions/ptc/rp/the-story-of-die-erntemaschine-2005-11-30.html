The Story of Die Erntemaschine

30 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-30 Ladies and gentlemen, tonight we’re going to explore the story of one of the most infamous serial killers of the new millennium. Deep within the city of Chicago, Illinois… a supposed psychotic individual was putting the entire city in a state of fear. An assassin, whose pinpoint accuracy would make a special ops agent blush, and a identity that lies behind a mysterious aura to this day. What name did this heartless killer go by? Die Erntemaschine… also known as, The Reaper. At the turn of the 21st century, Chicago was at a time of business success. With financial institutions booming in the Windy City, the growth of the already heavily populated town began to soar. Leaving many who already inhabited the area to lose their homes to those coming with more money and new higher paying jobs. With this problem going relatively unnoticed, gang violence and children turning to the streets reached all time highs. Territories were drawn and crime became almost incapable of being handled by Chicago’s Police force, leaving the law into the hands of those on the streets who wanted justice. It wasn’t long before the deaths of two African-American males rippled through the underground scene in Chicago. James Weston, 25 and Jerome Merris, 23 both from the Southside Chicago area were found dead on April 22, 2000 in front of an alleged drug house. Both men were shot once in the head, by a military grade sniper rifle. The death’s were so silent and quick, the tale was that they could’ve only been done by death himself. Two weeks later, a young Caucasian man by the name of Thomas Marcy of Chicago… was found dead on the balcony of his downtown apartment. Once again, a single shot to the head was the cause of death. Three shots, three lives… every single shot seemed to come from the sky. The streets rumored that these killings may be the hand of God, seeking vengeance on the wicked. It didn’t take long for Police to realize this murdered needed to be stopped and began to investigate. Seemingly the two murders had nothing in common. Thomas Marcy had never met Weston or Merris before in his short life. The clues were not there… the Police were left stumped at the hands of the assassin. Months went by and the word on the street had spread about the deadly assassin, and crime lords and petty thieves alike were after the services of such a professional. Near the end of May… the assassin struck again, killing two more. This time a son and father were the targets. One shot in the head, both of them died immediately after the bullet. There was no link between any of the murders… and this worried Police even more. Was there some type of connection between all these people beneath the surface, or does the killer get some sort of sick satisfaction out of killing random victims. The Police would never find out the answer to this question, but instead put out a reward for the capture of the Windy City Slayer. $50,000 for the capture of the killer, dead or alive. The next victim was on June 12, 2000… it was a very young woman of Russian background. Mika Miasnikov, the ex-girlfriend of Zenith Corporation Vice President Christian Ulrich Zenith. Mika was suspected of stealing nearly one million dollars from Vice President Christian Zenith, and had a warrant out for her arrest for nearly a year. With the ties to the corporation, this death was looked at to be the most significant thus far by Police. It was the first death so far that appeared to have a motive. There was at least one person in Chicago who wanted this woman killed. Christian Ulrich Zenith remained under a microscope throughout the following years, in which nearly 26 people ended up dead with the same cause of death. A single shot through the skull. Police had long suspected Christian Zenith to be at least affiliated with the killer, as well as being very public about it in conversations with local gangs and underground leaders. Thus Zenith became extraordinarily popular on the streets. You could find his name spray painted somewhere on almost every city block around Northern Chicago. As more bodies popped up throughout the years, absolutely nothing could be pinned on Zenith, and no witnesses were ever found for the murders. Yet the assassin became known to everyone as Die Erntemaschine, German for The Reaper, due to Christian Zenith’s German heritage. In October in 2002, another significant killing showed up. This was the most high profile murder to the gang community. Darius Germaine Mack and James Kennedy Carlisle of Chicago turned up dead on a public sidewalk in front of an apartment complex. James Carlisle was said to be the leader of one of the most powerful gangs in Chicago. Not to mention another connection to Christian Zenith came about not too long after. Carlise’s fiancé, Vivica Julian Valentine of Highland Park, soon became very close with Christian Zenith. Just months after the death they had been seen in public together, nearly inseparably. In the next year and a half 9 more murders came about in the same fashion. Bringing the total death count to 32 at the hands of Die Erntemaschine. In January of 2004, Christian Zenith and his fiancé Vivica Valentine had left Chicago and the everyday running of his business to a trusted advisor. After Christian’s departure from Chicago, not a single murder had occurred in this fashion. Seeing no other option, the Chicago Police Department put a warrant out for Christian’s arrested. He was found in Las Cruces, New Mexico two weeks later, where he was brought back to Chicago to be put on trial for 32 counts of murder. Christian Zenith was found innocent of all charges and was later found out to also be a victim of Die Erntemaschine. No murders have occurred since that trial… until now.
