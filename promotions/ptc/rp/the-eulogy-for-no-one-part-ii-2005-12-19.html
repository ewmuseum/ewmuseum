Date: 2005-12-19 File #047 – The Eulogy for No One (Part II) by [name withheld] In the United States alone, the Riley estate encompassed several hundred marvelous architectural wonders from libraries teeming with knowledge to museums shining with freshly polished marble and from hundred-story high hotels to modest, yet exquisite summer homes on both coasts. When Calvin, heir to the family’s impossible fortune, was exiled from the country where he had spent the majority of his complicated life, all of these structures were torn from his access and doomed to lie eternally vacant – though he still owned them all – and so, once he was allowed back into the States for the Global Title Tournament, he was allowed to enjoy the fruits of his dead kin’s labor and it made him proud all over again of how far the Rileys had come. Therefore, though thoroughly focused on his secret plot, he took the time to longingly and lovingly stroke every ancient wall and railing of everything in reach that bore his family’s crest. It so happened, that Calvin’s favorite inherited home was Quaker Manor in the suburbs of Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. It was the first mansion that his family had built upon their original emigration from Suffolk to the New World and had done everything in it’s time from being one of the original stops of the Underground Railroad to weather the company of several famed American figures – including President Abraham Lincoln as a stop on the way to a certain Gettysburg Address. And so, Calvin, who had always been fond of history, felt inclined to house the entire "Drake Camp" here – even though there were a great many closer (but far less historical) Riley manors nearer to Washington D.C. where the Global Title Tournament was being held. What was ironic about it all was that while Kasidy Drake trained and his mentor, Calvin Riley, enjoyed the atmosphere of freed slaves and dead presidents within Quaker Manor, the one man who stood in the way of their master plan was several miles away comfortably eating lunch in a café near Center City because his girlfriend, though quite attractive, could not cook. "So," said Julia conversationally and trying to sound as undisturbed by her boyfriend’s ravenous appetite. "Three cheeseburgers, huh? The marines really must not feed too well." She swore that she saw Karl swallow a quarter of his latest burger whole before he took a swift drink from his mug and exhaled in temporary satisfaction. "Oh, they feed okay. But you’ve got to remember, I’ve been floating – alone - in the middle of the Atlantic for several weeks now babe eating… well, I don’t quite know how I survived. There certainly weren’t any rations on board. Well maybe there were. I don’t remember." And with that, he went back to his gluttonous feast while Julia tried to politely ignore the stares they were both receiving from the other patrons – mostly truck drivers and old men. The café owner, however, looked quite pleased, and kept patiently tapping his fingers on the front counter near the cash register. Julia turned back to Karl, whose face was now glistening with exhaustion from his banquet even though it was twenty degrees outside and the café’s heaters didn’t work quite right. She was worried. First, the marines report her fiancé MIA, and then dead, and then they had claimed that nobody by the name of Karl Stanley West Jr. had ever enlisted in any armed force. Then, two weeks later, he showed up at her doorstep in brand new clothes, with a fresh hair cut, and an appetite to shame the homeless. With the novelty of her honey’s safety, she became increasingly aware of the fact that none of it made any sense. "Karl… baby," she moaned lightly while furrowing her brow and placing her hand atop Karl’s free one. "You never explained to me how… why the marines say that you never enlisted." Karl swallowed what was in his mouth and looked up at Julia’s face silently. "I mean, we all just figured that the paperwork had gotten screwy in all of the chaos – there’s a war going on over there after all – but … I mean, if it hasn’t- "Then it’ll mean that they can’t send me back," Karl sneered as his hatred for the war rose in his throat again. "And I don’t have to shoot anyone else over some stupid oil. That’s what the whole thing was about you know. Oil. Greedy Texan bastard…" Several of the truck drivers gave the both of them dirty looks which Julia chose to ignore, "Karl. I don’t know, but I get the feeling that this goes a lot deeper than some kind of theory on the war. None of your credit cards have worked since you’ve gotten here, your mother’s not answering the phone – and when we drove past the cemetery on the way here, the tombstone we put up for you when we thought you were dead… well, it wasn’t there." "Mom’s been dodging bill collectors all her life. And like you said… some paperwork’s probably gotten messed up somewhere-" But Julia cut across him, "Look baby, you’re not listening to me. Stop thinking about that stupid war for a moment and think about what I’m telling you. It’s not right. This isn’t right. Your mother’s been answering the phone every day since the funeral, and, the last I heard from her, she couldn’t get a copy of your birth certificate from downtown for a scrapbook she was making for her – for you. It’s almost like you’ve become some kind of ghost…" In a great clatter, Karl dropped his fork suddenly, and sat up quickly starting Julia, who was already quickly becoming distraught, and the others in Geno’s Café. His heart had skipped a beat and suddenly, a thousand repressed memories begun to unwrap themselves in his head. But it had all just been a dream hadn’t it? "… you’re going to become an electronic ghost by the time you get home… "Karl?" Suddenly, he had begun to remember some pretty place far away; a peaceful meadow above a chaotic green sea. A strange flag waved above it – plain white except for a small and thin black cross in the top-right corner… " …your credit cards, your birth certificate… all will be gone by the time you get to the shore…" There had been beautiful people there – so beautiful that it was impossible, and though Karl had been the perfect heterosexual all of his life, even the men, naked beneath their membrane-like cloaks, stirred a deep arousal in the pit of his belly… "Karl? Answer me…" "…if you’re able to find anyone whom you can convince isn’t going crazy…" And there had been other men there – more fully clothed, and quite less perfect. One of them was anorexically emaciated, but he seemed stronger than all of them somehow. His long hair had been wispy and silver – not gray, but silver – and there was something of a lost soul in his blue eyes… "Karl!" Julia screamed frantically as her recently resurrected love’s eyes glazed over and a steady trail of drool seeped from the corner of his mouth. "Karl! You’re scaring me!" "…they’ll think you’re crazy…" His name had been odd for a man and it was probably spelled contrary to how it should have been. He was going to do something… something horrible. Something that even he, detached as he was, did not seem to want to do. But there was a man… beyond the bushes… wearing white… the man in the white suit didn’t seem to have any problem with what was about to happen. In fact, it had been his idea in the first place, and the man with the silvery hair was going to help him do it this time… because last year he had tried and had failed. Had tried at … some kind of tournament. That was it! There had been a tournament… there was a tournament… "Sir? Sir, are you okay?" asked the ratty woman who owned the café.. "You’re debit card’s been declined. Sir?" "You’re dead." "KARL!" The dimly-lit café suddenly popped back into view and along with it Karl had remembered something horrible. He gripped Julia’s hand terribly, "Julia… the tournament. The Global Title Tournament!" "What? Wrestling?" Julia’s babbled now more frightened than concerned. "What are you going on about-" "They left the island to come here!" he cried as if it all made perfect sense. "To put their man – Cassidy… no, Kasidy with a ‘K’ – to enter him in the tournament. So that they could get into the country. But they don’t care about winning, they just need time to set something up. Something horrible!" "What are you talking about!" demanded Julia, but suddenly more grand realizations popped into Karl’s brain, his eyes widened and he suddenly grabbed Julia around the wrist, pulled her from their booth, and sprinted out of the café without their coats or Karl’s useless Citizen’s Bank card. "Hey!" yelled the old woman. "Come back here and pay you kike!" But they were already outside in the middle of JFK Boulevard frightening the other pedestrians as Karl ran for his life and half-pulled a confused and frightened Julia alongside him. "Where are we going!?" she cried. "Did you just dine and dash!? You never-" But Karl’s newfound insanity had fully taken hold of him now, and he ignored Julia while he pumped his legs with the kind of devilish speed he had reserved for battle in the Middle East. He dodged in and out of the wide and busy intersections, pushed old women out of the way, and nearly nudged an idling police officer into oncoming traffic until he finally reached a gritty payphone behind McDonald’s, ripped the receiver from its resting place and started pumping change into the coin slot. Julia simply stood there, her erect nipples plainly visible through the tight white shirt that she had unwisely worn. It was freezing. She simply glared at him not knowing if she should run away from the maniac that had seemingly possessed the body of her "dead" fiancé or slap him for not grabbing their coats in his hurry. A passing hobo whistled obscenely at her unseasonable wardrobe and a bit of shame leaked into her panic-stricken mind. "You nearly got that cop killed!" she yelled at Karl once the hobo had gone. Tears were filling her eyes – and for good reason. Nothing made sense anymore. "He’s probably after us. Going to arrest us. Put us in jail. And then they’ll find out that you went AWOL…" "Fuck the cop," Karl mumbled as he finished dialing and waited. "You know good and well the entire force is too lazy to stop a crazed maniac from running down a crowded street." He sighed after a few moments and hung the phone up again not bothering to redeem his money, "Mom’s line’s been disconnected. Besides. How can they arrest someone who’s already dead. Or worse… never existed." Now she was sure that he had lost his mind, "’Never existed?’ What the fuck are you talking about Karl?" "Let’s go. I’ll explain later-" "No!" Julia commanded and Karl’s drive dimmed slightly as he looked into his lover’s tearing eyes. "I want to know what the fuck is going on now. Why is all of this shit happening? And where’s Michael?" "Michael’s dead," Karl said automatically specifically attempting not to think about what he said as he said it. "I was able to escape but they killed him." Julia, at last, wailed uncontrollably, and embraced Karl suddenly. It was now Karl’s turn to be caught off-guard, and after an unsure moment, the emotional value of everything punched through the wall of logic he’d just discovered and he began to bawl too and returned the tightest hug he’d ever received in his life. Though Julia cried just as hard as he did, Karl secretly wished that they could switch places. The full effect of Kasidy’s premonition hadn’t reached her yet. The only people in the world that either of them had at that moment were each other: two lost lovers wrapped up in each other in the middle of a dingy McDonald’s parking lot on December 19, 2005 ridiculously dressed like it was July. Only now had Karl realized how cold the world was. "Give me your ATM card," he choked after a while somberly. He already knew what he was about to discover. "I need to see something baby." Julia handed it over, still sobbing uncontrollably from the knowledge that Michael, who had been a best friend to both of them since high school, was dead. She did not dare ask who the "they" that had killed him was. She didn’t want to know yet. Karl swiped Julia’s debit card through the ATM outside of the McDonald’s. And nothing happened. They had gotten her too. "…an electronic ghost…" "We’ve been erased," he told her silently once he returned to the other side of the parking lot. "Both of us. And our families. Everyone whose ever known us is probably dead by now Julia… if I hadn’t escaped that island, and if I hadn’t gotten you when I did, we’d be dead too." "Why?" Julia managed to ask between sniffs. "Why would someone do this?" "Because I ran away," Karl uttered bitterly as tears came to his eyes again over his own stupidity and selfishness. "Because I ran away and saw something that I wasn’t supposed to see. And I know." "And exactly what is it that you know Karl…?" "I know what they’ve got planned. They’re going to kill all of the participants in the Global Title Tournament. Probably once Kasidy Drake wins the thing. And no, I don’t know why," he commented before Julia could ask the question. "But we’ve got to tell someone." He only got one big solemn sniff in response. He took Julia’s hand and led her out of the parking lot and down the street. They walked all the way to South Philadelphia once more and to Wolf Street so that they could regroup and consider their options. Once they got there, however, they found a clean but vacant lot where Nine-Thirty-Seven had been and no one on the block seemed to notice or find it strange that an entire house had vanished into thin air. Who in the hell were they dealing with, was all that a desperate Karl West could think to himself as he hugged his fellow ghost tightly to his chest so that neither of them would fall. What was ironic about it all was that while Julia Davis, stood in front of where her house should have been and cried in the arms of her boyfriend, Karl West, who had just recently realized how many lives he’d indirectly ended just by running away from a war, Kasidy Drake trained and his mentor, Calvin Riley, enjoyed the atmosphere of freed slaves and dead presidents within Quaker Manor - specifically the grand gymnasium that he’d gotten installed in place of the antique pool room. Kasidy Drake was on his thousandth effortless push-up "Tell me again," Drake growled and he continued to push himself up and down. "From the beginning." "With pleasure," Calvin chortled adjusting his scarlet sunglasses and pulling a piece of folded paper out of his pocket. He unfolded it carefully and began to read with as much polite sarcasm as he could muster. "A-hem. ‘Christian Ulrich Zenith was born in Rammstein, Germany. The son of a German business man and a homemaker, Christian only has one sibling who is supposedly dead now - as is the rest of his family. After moving to the United States, Christian was living the American Dream until this was spoiled by the riches of his father. Not only did he learn from best tutors money could buy, his vast knowledge of Vale Tudo was taught to him by the greastest Vale Tudo fighters in the world. It was amazing what the almighty dollar can do to the life of a German Boy.’ Oh, doesn’t this sound like a happy camper…" "Reads like a soap opera script," Kasidy scoffed as he finished another push-up. "The typical story of another fat ungrateful little boy whose perfect little family got popped and now … let me guess – he ends up on the streets?" "Indeed he did," Calvin laughed with a knowing smile as he lounged slightly in his ringside chair. "But before his family died. ‘His money and power combined with his hard-nosed business skills turned Christian Zenith into a legend on the streets of Chicago.’" "A legend is he?" Kasidy laughed as he grew bored with push-ups, stood up and began to do some simple calisthenics. "Oh, but ‘on the streets’ of course. Because he’s supposed to be tough, right? So did he turn into some kind of vigilante in a bat-suit or what?" "Nope," Calvin reported with glee. "Apparently, now ‘there is nothing Christian Zenith enjoys more than the battling of the minds. A psychological battle to show his superiority to all men’ and ‘although he is fairly skilled in the ring, his mental attributes is his strong point.’" "…which is a nice way of saying that he’s crap between the ropes," Drake sighed in great disappointment and stopped exercising altogether. "Why am I even wasting my time? This guy might as well not show up at all tomorrow." He leaned against the apron of the regulation wrestling ring that Calvin had had erected for training purposes although he was not worn out at all and had barely broken a sweat. Calvin looked at him through his reflective and ominous red glasses and smiled knowingly. "And Llevar keeps trying to tell me that you’ve gone soft," he said with a soft laugh as he took his cane and got to his feet. "And I have to admit that, for a while, I was starting to believe him - after you wouldn’t kill that idiot marine before we left Eden and that lackluster match with Mookie. I realize now that I was wrong to have ever doubted you Kasidy." "But of course Opmokek," Kasidy said with a slight inclination of the head. "You know that you can always trust in my services. After all, I’m your Edenic Prince." "Yes, yes," Calvin laughed approaching Kasidy and clapping him on the shoulder with his free hand. "And you killed the other one… Karl West, was it?" "Yes Opmokek. I destroyed that filthy eahjixok sinner right at Midnight when the Sabbath was over. Riqq tip wek hato Sovereignty of course.." "Riqq tip wek hato Sovereignty indeed. This… Zenith person – you beat him before, I think. During an interpromotional match for Core. And that was long before you realized your true power… he’ll be no match for you," Calvin said – more to himself than to Kasidy and then, for a moment, his ever-present grin vanished and even Kasidy’s heart began to beat a little faster. "The fool thinks he knows what money and power is just because he has a few dollars in his pocket and some silly insurance company in his pocket… after tomorrow, he’ll realize what true power is. Just like those two idiot marines. Good night Kasidy." And with that, Calvin left the room, the long white cape draped over his similarly ashen suit trailing behind him until Kasidy was alone again. If Karl West knew what was good for him, he would have drowned himself on the way home while he had the chance – but why he had helped him get away in the first place, he still did not know. Nothing was clear anymore – and even though he had every physical and mental mean to crush the likes of Christian Zenith, just has he had Mookie, he wasn’t sure. She had been right – he really didn’t want to win deep down inside. What else was there, though? What were his choices? Death, or a less guilty death? Cassidy, Karl West, Michael Edwards… they were all weighing on his conscious. But not nearly as much as the man who’d e-mailed him this morning.
