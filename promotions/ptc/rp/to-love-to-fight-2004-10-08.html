To Love & To Fight

8 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-08 “AND I DON’T EVER WANNA FEEEEEEEEEEEEL / LIKE I DID THAT DAY / SO TAKE ME TO DA PLACE I LOOOOOOOOOOOOOOVE / TAKE ME AAAAAAAAAAALL THE WAAAY!”-“Under The Bridge” Red Hot Chili Peppers This was becoming an annoying habit. Screw that, this was more annoying than a rampant tumor. Jamar Gordo honed his body, the only weapon he ever relied on, for battle against the “great” Violence Jack… And he was nowhere to be found when it was all said and done. Since that match, Jamar had no reason to smile. He thought in Jack, he would find his chance to prove his dominance in a true fight. In Jack, competition would hurtle its way head on. In Jack, Jamar could silence his critics and prove his advancement in the tournament was no mistake, but merely divine fate with equal skill. Thanks for nothing Jack. So we’re back to square one as usual for Jamar since entering the Global Title Tournament, intense training with no rest in sight until the actual fight. However, with all the no-shows as of late, it would be a lie to say that the Master of All Styles didn’t think Jimmy Bong wouldn’t show. He pounded away on the faded and corroded heavy bag in the dank basement of Vincent Gordo’s restaurant, his first and fondest training ground since the age of 13. To imagine this basement without the musk of sweat, the engulfment of sheer darkness, and the tapestry of bloodstains on the floor was an impossibility for Gordo. This was home. His sculptured, ebony body dripped with sweat as he continued to pound the bag, moving about it in a circular motion like disciplined boxer. The Red Hot Chili Pepper’s “Under The Bridge” continued to blare in the background from decrepit boom box in the corner, Jamar lucky he could find the cassette for the song with the radio nor CD player a working option on the relic. He needed that song at the moment. He most certainly could relate to it. Because he DID NOT want to feel the way he did the last two rounds of (non)combat. If competition was the drug of Jamar, then the last two rounds tried to make him quit, cold turkey. His breathe heaved, his exhaling and inhaling growing ever deeper as his exercise continued. Even more heavy at the moment was his mind, which kept focus on his next opponent, Jimmy Bong. He was clear on many issues. He had no clue who the hell Jimmy Bong was, he probably could guess his favorite pastime by his last name, and it made no difference what his style of combat was. But like the teacher of a group of honor students, Jamar expected more then average from Bong. The concern now wasn’t even so much the actual fight anymore…..it was Jimmy actually showing to fight in the first place. He could expect to earn the Master of All Styles respect win or lose, if he just showed up. But make no mistake, if he showed, there was pain due from the past two fights. The recollection of Jamar’s disappointment flowed through his mind, not even “Under The Bridge” able to soothe it as he slammed his fists intensely in the bag. POP POP, POP POP, BAM~! To lose to Bong would bring shame to Gordo. All his critics would have the ammunition they needed to bury him. “Gordo only made it so far because there was no real competition,” they’d say, and with great zeal after a loss to his GTT opponent, no matter how hard the Master of All Styles tried. His detractors were merely waiting on the correct opportunity to discredit his advancement. POP POP, POP POP, BAM~! Unfortunately for Jimmy, it would NEVER get that far. If Bong wanted the win, he’d have to be able to live with murder on his conscience. Murder is what it would take to break Jamar. Yes, you heard that correctly. If he wanted that win, he would have to take the very life force from the veins of Jamar, because losing now would be death regardless. POP POP, POP POP, BAM~! This could no longer be considered a fight for pleasure anymore. If Jamar wanted to go to the next round, he would have to punish Bong the way he punished the heavy bag at that very moment. POP POP, POP POP, BAM WHAM~!~! And he certainly had no problem with that. At that moment, the feelings of intense want were broken in an instant. The bloodlust for Bong’s defeat were erased in what seemed like an instant, a proverbial light turned on in the darkness that was Jamar’s mind…maybe even his heart for some scarce moments. Surprisingly, it was not a rude sound or a boisterous action that halted his intense training. It was a voice as soft as satin coming from the top of the stairs, the kind of voice that could bring the innocence of Hitler to the surface with the right words. “Jamar, you’re still down here?” And there she stood in the middle of the stairwell, the proud owner of the voice. Sule was an enigma from the moment Jamar met her the day he returned to Atlanta at his master’s request. Beauty, intelligence, and a confidence that almost terrified him in its similarity to his; it was as safe as a life vest to say that the millionaire playboy was intrigued by her. It was only after taking in the glorious sight of the Brazilian beauty in her low-rise jeans and “Juicy” belly shirt that he finally responded, “Yes. Been down here since two this morning.” She shook her head with a soft smile as she leaned against the stair rail, creaking mightily in an announcement of the rail’s old age. “Look, even God rested. Come upstairs, I was hoping to talk to you before the restaurant opened,” she politely requested of the proud fighter. It was amazing how just mere minutes ago, the weight of his anger could have crushed the world. What was it about her that managed to tap into this piece of him? The armor of “assholism” and zeal he proudly wore was stripped with her around, leaving him naked to her; a feeling he thought he would never know again after his ill-fated relationship with Erica. He hated the vulnerability, but the delighted tingle within made it worth it. “Sure, I’ll be up. Let me wash up and I’ll join ya.” A nod let him know she understood as she turned to ascend up the stairs to the restaurant above. Looking about his surroundings then back to the heavy bag, Jamar built up all his energy, calling from whatever power he had left to expend as his eyes locked upon the battered heavy bag. “AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAIII~!” That bag was Jimmy Bong. That bag was his frustration. That bag was the no-shows. That bad was his critics. That bag was the doubt he had. That bag was EVERYTHING he harbored hate for at that moment in time….and his foot was the means to destroy it all. The force in his foot was immense, the ball of his uncovered heel BURSTING into the bag with all of his leg up to his upper thigh crashing through, sticking out at the other end. There Jamar laid sprawled out, his leg caught in the heavy bag as the basement became an upside down world for him. He smiled, almost wanting to let a laugh escape his lungs as he let himself dangle at the mercy of the ruptured bag. It was the release he sought diligently since 2 o’clock that morning and it felt so good to find it. With the Cheshire grin reclaimed on his face, Jamar knew what had to be done when he squared off one-on-one with Jimmy Bong….he just had to do it. “Now, how do I explain this one to Vincent,” he asked in child-worthy wonder, unconcerned at the moment about the true answer. At that moment as the blood rushed to his head, his world turned upside down in reversed splendor… He was free, even if just for a little while, he was free not to stress about that battle ahead. There would certainly be due time for that. Minutes Later…Upstairs in Gordo’s Seafood & Steak Restaurant Emerging from the depressive bellows of the basement with clean clothes and body, Gordo was overwhelmed by the aroma of green and red peppers with the hearty addition of seasoned sausage. Jamar knew good cuisine when he smelled it, following it to the kitchen of Vincent pride and joy restaurant to find Sule herself at the helm of the preparation. “Have a seat, I thought we’d talk over breakfast,” she insisted as the burner knob was flipped to the “off” position, Sule removing the pan from over it. With Vincent being an avid fan of eating after preparation, Jamar was by no means surprised to find a modest table and two chairs within the kitchen by his hand. Taking a seat, he was joined by Sule as she spooned the peppers and sausage into a bowl already on the table. Forks and knives shortly entered the fray along with separate plates as the two took a portion of their breakfast. “Why do you fight so hard Jamar,” abruptly asked by the lovely Sule as she took a bite of the spiced sausage, her sincerity obvious when it came to her question. That question halted him from taking his first bite of the appetizing meal, Jamar never recalling the question being asked to him before. His eyes turned to the window in the kitchen, taking notice of the sun rising regally on the horizon, a sight the Master of All Styles could never resist gazing at. “I fight because it’s all I’ve been doing my life. I don’t know what it is to be handed anything Sule, so it’s made me a hard person in general. The only person I can really give props to for being a genuinely good person to me, no strings attached, would be Vincent. He took me in, taught me how to fight, and I use what I know to amass victory, simple as that. Life has made me fight, so that’s what I give it in return.” His answer was punctuated with a bite of a pepper, almost forgetting what he was talking about with the euphoric flavor overtaking his mouth. A good cook too? This woman grew ever impressive by the minute. Sule smiled, looking to him as she stirred up her food in the midst of her response, “I figured as much, Jamar. You certainly didn’t seem like the most approachable person I had ever met, leading me to believe what they say about you in the papers is true. That’s why I made you breakfast.” “I don’t get it. What’s breakfast gotta do with me being a hard-ass,” he asked with a smirk, wondering if the stupidity of her response would live up to his expectations. “I figured MAYBE, if someone gave you something besides Master Gordo, you might make it a point to be more polite. That and you couldn’t use it ammo in your “I fight the world” speech,” her answer accented with devilishly misleading eyes. He certainly didn’t see that coming from her. A simple gesture, like the breakfast before him, serving as an olive branch for Jamar’s callous feelings. It made an excellent breakfast godly in his view, although he would never admit it freely to her. She seemed too much like that type that would never let him hear the end of it. For once Jamar had no comment, smart-ass or sincere, for a situation and it killed him; a man known for having something to say about anything. “By the way, I know Vincent tried to talk you into trying to marry me,” she informed Jamar as if some huge secret was uncovered. Indifferent to whether she knew or not, but certainly not denying it, Jamar replied, “He certainly did. A used car salesman he is not however. How did you know anyway?” “I figured he told you because he told me the same thing about you before you even arrived. I almost think if he could make an arranged marriage like in my country, he would.” “Why aren’t you married yet Sulie? Saving yourself for Ben Affleck? Vin Diesel perhaps?” She playfully scolded Jamar with a slap to the knee, finishing her current mouthful of food before firing back, “Very funny, wiseguy. As for your question, I’m not marrying against my will. That looked to be where my life in Brazil was headed until I was blessed with the choice to move here. When I marry, I want it to be for true love…that will make me truly happy, like fighting makes you.” “You can’t compare my happiness fighting with your happiness with marriage, Sule. You could compare my feelings about fighting and being around you better then what you just mentioned.” “Is that so,” retorted the beauteous Brazilian, her intellect shining that morning with Gordo over breakfast. “Very much so. Your happiness doesn’t come directly from the act of marriage, more so that you got to make the actual choice on who you spend your life with. I have no choice, I am drawn to fight like a moth to a flame…like I am to you.” Sule grew silent, not knowing what to think as her fork lowered. Jamar looked to her, his inhibitions lowered strangely as he continued his comparison, “Fights are enigmas. You don’t know what could happen or how it can surprise you. You Sule, are quite the same. I’m intrigued by the prospect of both and I’m sure that there will be no satisfaction until I have claimed victory.” “Well I’m sorry Jamar, I am not a victory to be claimed like your fights. It’s refreshing to hear a guy be sincere, but you’ve just expressed why we could never be together. I fear you’re more passionate about winning then anything else and unlike your fights; my heart isn’t just come conquest you can gloat over. You intrigue me too Jamar, but for different reason then the way that I intrigue you. I hope you enjoyed breakfast, the restaurant opens in a few minutes.” She rose from her seat, exiting the kitchen without another word as Jamar sat at the table in contemplation of what just happened. Sule was not the average ditz he would meet in his playboy escapades. She was as deep as she was beautiful and that kept her on his mind without question, but the problem was made clear for him to see. Did he want Sule because she would be an obvious challenge to attain….or because he indeed actually wanted her, for everything she was. It was funny how confident Jamar could be until feelings like love came into play. With fighting, it was so much simpler. Losing was not an option, so the will to succeed became all you could feel. Then love rears its head and you feel confusion, assurance, anxiety, lust, and a bevy of other complicated feelings at the same time. WHAT THE HELL WAS IT HE SHOULD FEEL?! Unfortunately, those thoughts would have to wait. CRASH~! Jamar’s thought pattern was broken without question by a sharp shriek behind the kitchen door, the tinkle of glass following as well. Bursting through the door with adrenaline pumping through his veins, he was surprised to find smoke followed by spreading fire engulfing a set of Vincent’s wooden tables in the restaurant. The small fire was fed by the scorched would to create a larger blaze, Sule catching sight of with a frightened look on her face. “GET OVER HERE QUICK,” yelled the Master of All Styles, Sule about to race over when another window was suddenly broken by a flying bottle containing a strange liquid within. “SULE, GET DOWN!” As the bottle spiraled closer to the ground, Jamar raced in Sule’s direction and tackled her down as best as he could without hurting her, using his body as a shield for her. The bottle smashed into the floor of the restaurant, exploding into a dance of flames as more of the establishment caught on fire. The essence of burning wood filled the air, making the two cough as the orange flames were fueled enough to dance to the ceiling. If it weren’t for needing to protect Sule at this vital moment, Jamar could have gone numb at that moment at the spreading destruction of the sanctuary that changed the course of his entire life. The smoldering heat pressed against his skin, Sulie panicking beneath him as he held her firmly. His mind raced with a million thoughts at once, time being lost with every wasted second as the flames grew ever stronger and their chances of survival dwindling with that strength. Would there even be a match for Gordo at GTT? Not if he couldn’t win this fight with the unremorseful flames surrounding Sulie and him, a battle he could not win with fists or feet. And to lose, would mean paying with not only his life, but hers as well. Losing is not an option. Forever to be continued….
