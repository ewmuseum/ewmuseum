Crooked Timber, Part 1: American Rust

22 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-22 Golden city lights burned brightly through twilight's gloomy veil as a city that was supposed to be sleeping drove itself towards the small hours. Swarms of vehicles buzzed around the neon glow of downtown Dallas' lit-up skyscrapers and office blocks and a commuter plane cruised overhead. Hordes of white collars filled with a “hard day at the office” tiredness crossed paths with buoyant crews of revellers, their calls and cries swirling with whirring engines and honking horns in a perfect urban symphony. He gazed down from his twenty-first story perch, nursing a crystal glass of sweet alcoholic nectar in his paw. Three ours after the nine-to-five mob were supposed to have finished work and still the city was buzzing, but it was hardly a surprise if everyone else in this district was as diligent as those he shared a building with. “You'll get vertigo if you keep gawking out that window, you know.” A brash Texan voice from behind, accompanied by a chuckle. The source was a smirking Eric Avila, leaning forward on a large leather office chair, tipping his glass as his eye's met Foyle's. Using a cocktail stick to swirl ice cubes around the thin, brown liquid in his glass, Foyle reached for a cord at the end of his office's floor-to-ceiling window and pulled the lengthy set of slat blinds closed. “What can I say?” Foyle said, shrugging. “Gotta some value outta these ridiculous bits of glass. Besides, city just looks so Goddamn pretty when the sun's gone down.” Foyle stepped away from the veiled outside world and slipped into the chair behind his varnished mahogany desk, the surface of which was akin to Ike Eisenhower's war board. Rising his glass to his old, cracked lips, Foyle let the smooth single malt slip down his throat on it's way to nestling in his stomach's pit at his wordless associate watched on. “Good feeling, ain't it?” Foyle said, letting his chair recline itself as he pulled his size twelve Pierre Carin's up onto his desk. “Just kicking back after ten hours of solid grind. Thirty years gone and this one small pleasure still ain't lost it's edge.” “For a guy that's actually accomplished something today, maybe,” Avila retorted, his twentysomething voice rife with dissatisfaction. He sat back and took a swig of his own drink, taking a moment to brush back a rogue strand of perfectly straight black hair as he did so. “I'm getting nothing but shit just now; we're talking bottom of the barrel here, not even C or D-listers.” “Slow day, huh?” “'Slow'? It's been fucking glacial, this whole month has. I spent half the day trying to put feelers out for this sick kids benefit show some guy that played for the Bears in the 70's is putting out, and nobody's wanting any of it. Not even the Sun Times, man,” Avila paused and shookhis head. “Ain't like I'm the only one, either; Gillian, Weinstein, Thomas... all those guys got nothing at the moment. We're here at six every morning and whadda we have to show for it? Barely a buck.” Foyle raised a mitt to his jawline and scratched an itch beneath his thin, grey beard, sending a tiny shower of unwanted beardruff sprinkling down towards his well-fitting blue shirt. “It's like any trade; business comes and go. Sometimes we get good patches, sometimes we get barren ones. Just gotta wait, ride it out, and hope things come full circle again.” “Oh, I know the will, it's just waiting for it to happen that kills me. I was at my desk for four fuckin' hours this morning and I couldn't sell a story to one source! It's not exactly doing wonders for my morale--” “-- and I bet your bank balance misses the commission, huh?” Foyle interrupted, smiling. “Heh. That too.” Eric sighed as his boss finished his whiskey and set about pouring himself another. He took a moment to cast his eyes around a room he'd been in many times before, yet never tired of admiring. Glossy wooden panels ran a metre up the wall where they were met with a wine red coloured wallpaper speckled with little golden insignias, lined with framed newspaper snippets and magazine covers documenting the success of the state's top PR company. Brilliant artificial light spewed from the gold fixtures on the roof and walls, illuminating the shelves upon shelves of books and accolades in all their splendour. It reminded him of something out of an old Sherlock Holmes movie. It was truly an office befitting of it's occupier. At fifty-nine, Foyle Bradbury had ground his way to the top of the company pyramid and taken his position as it's leader. Still in his teens, he'd worked his way from mail room bitch and office runner to become the firm's sole director, and he'd done it on his own back without an ounce of help from anyone else. A giant kick in the teeth to anyone who'd try to tell you that the American Dream doesn't exist. He was ageing, but you wouldn't know it from looking at him. He'd taken good care of himself over the years and his skin was as smooth and clear as it was twenty years ago, though there were a few more wrinkles here and there. Foyle's hair was greying but not thinning, and if you didn't know any better, you might confuse him for a man a decade younger. “That's just how it goes sometime,” Foyle continued, pulling his legs down from the desk. “Something big happens in the world and all of a sudden nobody cares about how many monkeys Matt Damon has saved in Brazil, or how many African slums Tom Brady's been visiting. You really think the media gives a damn about some old football player putting on some kinda charity concert these days? Shit, they're still trying to milk stories from Michael Jackson, and that was four months ago.” Foyle stopped, looking the man he'd taken under the wing in his cold, blue eyes. Eric adjusted his posture. “Well, it's all I've got. What am I supposed to do?” “Broaden you horizons.” Avila was caught off-guard. “Huh?” “Think about what you're trying to sell here, Eric. Benefit shows, charity stuff... nobody cares anymore. It's too nice, too safe. Think about it, if you see David Beckham wearing a Unicef t-shirt on the front of a magazine, it's not exactly gonna make you wanna buy the fuckin' thing, is it?” Foyle raised his brow, but didn't allow time for a response. “Those stories are nice and at the right time they can make you a pretty penny, yeah; but this just ain't the right time, friend. Just remember there's more to this job than making people look like saints.” “What you talkin' here? Dirt?” a puzzled Eric asked. “I guess that's one way of putting it.” Slowly Avila nodded as Foyle pulled himself from the chair. “I know it ain't as honourable, and doing too much of it'll turn you into a first class piece of shit, no doubt; but sometimes kickin' up a little shit can be very rewarding.” Slowly Foyle walked over towards the bookcase. “Sleaze sells, Eric. You wanna make a couple bucks? Find a skeleton in someone's closet and the vultures out there'll lap it up. This country loves to build someone up and tear them back down again.” “I don't know,” Eric meanders, pondering. “Not sure I like the idea of doing something like that.” “Morally it's not the easiest thing in the world, but you've been here for how long – a year now? You're doing fine, but if you really wanna peak, you gotta get your hands dirty every now and then. Ain't many people in the building who like doing it, Eric, but that's how the business is. People wanna read about A-list rappers snorting cocaine in nightclubs, not no-name child stars from the eighties handing food to refugees.” Foyle made his way back towards the window as Eric sat there, thinking. He was still relatively new to the business and had yet to learn the full extent of it's ins and outs, and the idea of publicly smearing a celebrity didn't exactly fill him with excitement. Still, his monetary situation demanded attention, and his co-workers did it all the time. Surely it couldn't be that difficult. “Tell you what, son,” Foyle said, turning. “I know you're reluctant, everyone is at the start, but if you sit on your ass drumming around bullshit stories all-day for much longer you're gonna lose your mind. I got no problem with you wanting to keep your nose clean, but if you ever wanna dive into something a little meatier, gimme a call. I'll hook you up.” Eric felt the slightest of smiles twang the corner of his mouth. “Fuck it. What've you got?” * * * * * Andy M. in... Crooked Timber I: American Rust * * * * * He woke just as the sun was beginning to peek it's rays through the curtains and it took great effort to pry his body from it's slumber. Pushing a thumb and forefinger towards his temples and pulling them down across his closed eyes, the GCW World Champion groaned and rolled over to read 09:23 flashing at him in neon green digits. For a moment he considered another half hour beneath the sheets, but he was feeling too lazy to stumble out of bed and close the curtains. Instead, Andy M. pushed himself up against the headrest and reached for last night's glass of water, feeling no more awake than when he'd gone to sleep. He swallowed the lukewarm fluid but couldn't shake the grog from his head; it had been another in a series of restless nights that had grown to be such a nuisance that he was considering professional advice. He couldn't put his finger on what was causing it, but the endless nights of tossing and turning had pushed his patience to the limit. He'd gone to bed earlier and woken at reasonable hours but still found himself only managing a couple of hours each night; even the over-the-counter medication he'd picked up at the pharmacy wasn't doing the trick. Something was making The Scottish King of Cool restless. Maybe the stress of being one of the busiest sportsmen in the continent was finally catching up with him. Maybe he wasn't eating as well as he should, or maybe it had something to do with that new air-conditioning system he'd just had installed in the house. Maybe it had more to do with the fact that he was sleeping alone for the first time since he was a teenager. The man wrestling fans knew as The Scottish King of Cool rolled himself out of bed and pulled his bathrobe from the floor. Rising to his full 6'7”, Andy slid the garment over his boxer short clad body and tied it at the waste. With a yawn so deep and bellowing Zeus would be proud and a quick ruffle of his unkempt bedhair, Andy pushed his feet into his slippers and went downstairs. If he was more awake he might have noticed the silence. All thirteen rooms of his sizeable Tallahassee home were enveloped in a quiet stillness that he'd become acclimatised too over the past couple of weeks. Marvin had surely made his way to school by now, and his brother would be out on the road for at least another fortnight. The place was empty, and Andy M. was alone save for the yapping dachshund that he shut in the utility room each night. For the first couple of days, the peace and quiet almost seemed like a blessing compared to the tumultuous GCW backstage environment, but soon he found himself yearning for a sign of life. Nothing massive; a simple “morning, pop” from Marvin or the sound of his brother belting out terrible 1980's at an ear-damaging decibel. Something to remind him that he wasn't the social exile he felt like every time he came off the road nowadays. Still, it did give him a much-needed chance to get some work done. Since his late-2007 return Andy's career had reached a level he never thought possible, and now, holding his first piece of championship gold since 2005, he was a man in demand. Finding time when he wasn't out with GCW or Just was rare, and the fact that he was forced to spend these off-days alone was doing his psyche no favours at all. Such is the nature of the beast. It wasn't as if he could just pluck Marvin from school and take out on tour; the kid needed to stay grounded despite his privileged upbringing, and there was no way he could drag his son away from his education and social development. Andy was a single, thirty-one year old professional wrestler. There was only so much he could do to maintain that father/son bond without ruining Marvin's life in the process. Doing his best to sweep his concerns away, the still groggy Andy paced through the house's oversized great room and stepped into the kitchen. Pulling open the fridge and pulling out an aluminium bag of ground coffee, Andy set about making himself a cup of joe by grabbing a discarded, unwashed teaspoon and plopping two heaped spoonfuls of ground beans into the top of a coffee machine that looked more like something NASA had built in on of their 1960's labs. Impatient, he slammed the lid down and pressed the large green button on the front. For a couple of seconds the machine spun into action, only for the sound of a dying electric motor fizzing out to bring it to a complete halt again. Andy's face contorted with confusion. He bashed the button and this time the machine failed to start again. “The fuck?” he croaked, trying again to no avail. He checked the socket. “Definitely still plugged in. Why ain't it working?” Bash, bash, bash. Three times he hit the button, then a fourth and a fifth. Nothing. “Piece of shit.” Andy shook his head. He turned towards the staircase and inhaled sharply, but before the first syllable of Kayla's name could pass his lips the stark realisation overcame him. She was gone. Over a month had passed but the thought still sobered him as it should've. She may have prematurely terminated the life of a an unborn baby he never knew existed, yes; but he'd loved her. He was going to give his life to her, and he'd retire from the business a few years after they'd married. Maybe they'd even have a couple of kids together and everything would be just hunky-dory. He was so overcome with the idea of everything being perfect that when it all came crashing down he didn't know how to cope, and still he struggled. Andy abandoned the coffee machine mission and turned around, viewing his barren house with a heaving sigh. There wasn't a single thing he could do to fill those rooms with her voice again; nothing at all. In wrestling he had everything. On-air he could reduce an audience of millions to putty in the palm of his hand, and backstage he was wasn't exactly short of friends and allies, but what was waiting for him when he came home? His son would rather spend time at a friend's house than hang out with his Dad, and his brothers were too busy carving a niche as a tag team on the US independent scene to stay grounded into Tallahassee for too long. Andy M. was a conundrum; a man who had everything and nothing at the same time, and oh, how he loathed it. * * * * * Her excitement was tangible as she slammed down the telephone, so much so that she could help but clap her dainty hands together and giggle like a schoolgirl. For a long time she'd waited for this, and now things were finally coming to fruition. Too long had he basked in the limelight while she'd struggled below the breadline, but now things would be different. A grapevine had been extended her way, and soon she would emerge from her exile to wreak glorious vengeance on the man who'd torn her life to pieces. Every time she turned on the television his face was there. All slick black hair, round blue eyes and perfect white teeth, oozing charisma and confidence as the pro-wrestling industry's Mr. Nice Guy. Every time she saw a magazine rack his visage beamed back at her from the sports section, and it was driving her round the bend. He was an idol; a genuine global megastar who'd built his name on a squeaky clean image and a flawless reputation. A man who'd completed the miracle of making a massive success of himself without so much as denting the code of values and strong moral fibre he'd had instilled in him from a young age. A man who was enjoying the lifestyle of a movie star, with enough money to retire on at thirty-one, and here was she, struggling to get by, alone in her shoebox of a studio apartment. No more. The time had come. The world would turn against him when they found out the truth, they always did. When everything was out in the open he'd topple like a house of cards, and she would be content once again. Her story would be told. Perhaps they'd even be able to find a publisher for her book, and then the sparks would really begin to fly. He'd taken everything from her, and she hadn't even seen her own son since her forced Texan relocation because he wouldn't allow it, but he could hide from it no longer. So breathless with rampant anticipation was she that she had to sit herself down and control her breaths. “Calm down,” she whispered to herself as she glanced at the clock on her scabby walls. Still three hours until the meeting. Perfect. Plenty of time for the women with nothing to finalise the downfall of the man with everything. * * * * * “So d'you wanna tell me why you thought it'd be a good idea to drag me outta the house at this ungodly hour?” Andy M. raised an eyebrow as he laid a pristine white napkin on his lap and prepared to tuck into his chicken dish. “'Ungodly hour'? It's just gone midday, man. C'mon!” Rueben Dallas chortled, although he was quite unsure if his client was fucking with him or not. “Even The Scottish King of Cool needs his beauty sleep, you know.” “Heh. I can see that,” Rueben said, glancing curiously at Andy's uncharacteristically messy appearance. His short, black hair – while clean – was still scruffy and all over the place, and a few day's worth of stubble shrouded his jawline. More telling, though, where the dark, sleepless bags that hung beneath his eyes. “I know you want a little R&R when you get back, bro, but runnin' a razor over your face every once in a while wouldn't hurt, ya know.” “Gee, thanks for the tip, Donatello,” Andy said before shovelling a forkful of chicken meat into his mouth and chewing. “You know I'm just messing with yah. Besides, who else is gonna keep that giant ego of yours in-check, huh?” Rueben smiled, but immediately felt that his humour was lost on Andy, who let the flippant comment sail over him completely. With a mouthful of Coke, the African-American super agent abandoned attempts at humouring his client and began pondering the best way to demolish the heinously expensive lunch that sat before him. It was a decent enough restaurant with a patio area to eat a la carte during those long, summer evenings, and the lunch service was never too busy. The fact that it wasn't full of gumby, meathead wrestling fans made it the perfect location for a little agent/client get together too. “Just thought it would be a good idea for you and I to touch base, face-to-face,” Rueben said as he swallowed his first mouthful of an exotic fish dish that he couldn't remember the name of. “We ain't done it in a while and we got some big things coming up, and I know you ain't exactly been havin' a great time as of late, so let's get it all sorted out so we can keep movin' on up, huh?” “Uh-huh. Sure.” Andy nodded, but Rueben could tell there wasn't any interest. He'd heard about Andy and Kayla, although not from the horse's mouth, and there was an elephant in the room that was just begging to be shot in the ass. “Sure you're alright, man?” Rueben quizzed, “or was the only reason you came down here to get a free lunch outta me? 'Cause I'm not exactly getting good vibes from you at the moment.” Andy sighed. He had no desire to trudge through the past few months again, but the fact was that the business was just about the only thing keeping him sane, and if he couldn't talk professionally with his agent then what could he do? He looked up from his meal, using his thumb to dab some sauce from the corner of his mouth. “I'm just tired of all this bullshit, man. Just wanna get my head down, put all this behind me, and concentrate on being The Scottish King of Cool, seeing as wrestling's about the only thing I can do these days without shooting myself in the foot.” “It's been a trying time for you, though. I heard what happened.” “Yeah, well. It's done now, and I've let it marinate in my head far too long. Gotta get passed it and keeping rolling, because if I let it stew any longer I might not even have a career left,” Andy spoke slowly and with authority; his were the words of a man who'd thought long and hard, and who knew exactly what he wanted to say. “It's hard, and I'm still fighting it, but I'll get there man. Time's a good healer.” “Now that's what I like to hear,” Rueben said enthusiastically, pointing his fork at Andy and smiling. “When the world knocks you down the only thing to do's pick yourself up, dust yourself off, and go harder than you've ever gone before. And shit, man; it's not like you're in a completely rotten place. You are the fuckin' champ, after all...” If Andy could find the words to tell him that a strap of gold and leather was little consolation for a broken relationship, he would have used them. But he couldn't, so instead he chomped down on some salad and nodded. “That's true.” “Sure is, but shit, if you want another little thing to help take your mind off things, there's a certain tournament coming round soon...” “GTT? I know.” “You want in.” “Already taken care of that.” “Shit, you have?” flabbergasted, Rueben was so taken aback that he dropped his fork on the glass-topped table. “Why the hell didn't I know about that?” “'Cause I only signed up yesterday,” Andy said. “Come on, man! You're supposed to be the brains behind this operation, and you hadn't even heard? What the hell am I paying you for?!” For the first time that afternoon, Dallas saw a smile creep across Andy M.'s face. “Yeah, well. Maybe if my client fuckin' told me once in a while...” Punctuating his joke with a wink after failed attempts earlier, Rueben laughed to himself. “Shit, look at you. Sorting shit out for yourself for a change. Proud of ya.” “This is about the only thing left in my life that I've got left to sort out, man,” Andy said. “Bullshit comes and goes, that's life. Just gotta keep rebuilding when the walls crumble.” “Well I'd hate to put a dampener on things here, but if it's rebuilding you're doing, you might wanna be more careful about who you're seen in public with for a little while.” “Huh? Whaddaya mean?” “Valentine.” Andy flashed his agent a confused glance. “What about her?” “Stop letting the paps snap you together,” Rueben, now in full-on agent mode, said. “You guys are good friends, I know that, but it doesn't take much for rumours to starting circling, and that wouldn't be a good thing for either of you at the moment. I mean, it's not exactly negative press, but it's press you don't want.” “Ahhhh,” Andy said, leaning back in his chair and leaving his cutlery on the table. “Well I guess the press are gonna have a fuckin' field day when they find out we've secretly been an item since August.” Rueben blinked. “... what?” Andy nodded, slowly. He kept the admission hanging between them just long enough to see Rueben's jaw drop, but he couldn't contain himself for too long. “Jesus Christ, man. I'm just messing. Holy crap, the look on your face...” “Fuck, man. I was just about to say...” They share a moment's laughter like old friends often do. Eventually Rueben looks up and his highest-earning client. “I'll tell you one thing that's not a joke, though – GTT.” “Oh, don't worry about it.” Rueben's brows raise. “Oh?” “Yeah,” Andy said. “I plan on winning the fucker.” * * * * * “No, no, no! You listen to me, cunt muscle!” Eric Avila, red-faced, growled into his office phone. “Every fuckin' day I phone you, and every day you give me the same bullshit fuckin' excuses again and again. I'll have your job, you speck of shit. In fact, put me through to your supervisor before--” Click. “Hello?” Nothing. “Hello?!” The dialling tone returns. “You fucking fuck!” Violently, Eric slammed the phone against his desk and bashed his balled fist into the surface. Barbs of pain shot through his hand as one of his knuckles crashed into the wood, but he didn't care; all he needed was stress relief. The day had gone much like those that had came before it; fruitless. He was wasting his time, getting nowhere. Nobody wanted anything to do with him and he didn't know how much more he could take. Suddenly a knock on the door. “Well someone sounds cheery today.” Eric didn't appreciate the sarcastic remark, and his first instinct was to lambaste the sprightly woman that stood in his doorway, but his opinion suddenly changed when he saw her. Clad from head-to-toe in an elegant black outfit, and with pouty red lips and deep, hazel eyes, the brunette before him was a woman who could have even the sternest of men wrapped around her little finger with just one provocative glance. She ensnared him straight away. Shuffling awkwardly in his chair, Eric struggled to greet her. “Ummm, yes? How can I help you?” “I spoke to your boss,” she said matter-of-factly as she strode into the room and sat herself down without invite. “Foyle, is it?” “Foyle Bradbury, yes.” “Right. Well, forgive me for intruding, but he told me you were the guy I needed to come and see. It's my husband ex-husband you see. I want him ruined, and I hear you're the best man for the job.” Recalling his meeting with Foyle the day prior, Eric suddenly clicked into focus. He sat up. “Uh-huh. And your husb-” “Ex-husband.” “Right, ex-husband. He's quite prominent, is he? Somebody people would actually stop and take notice of if they saw his face sticking out on the front of one of the redtops?” “Oh, he's very prominent,” she smiled coldly. “I'm sure you've heard of him.” “And your name is?” She extended a hand. “Jane. Jane M.”
