MInd Games Part Two

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-10 24th October 2005. WWA Meltdown. Salt Lake City, Utah. I didn’t realise that something was wrong until a couple of hours later. Freddie Arson came to my locker room right before my match; it was only then that I discovered what the deal with everyone was. He burst into my locker room ranting and raving about his disapproval of my actions during the week. Earlier in the week I appeared on a rival wrestling organisations flagship television programme, helping a friend settle into her new environment. At the time I actually had to think just what in the hell he could’ve been upset about. It turned out that because of this he, and upon extension the rest of the WWA roster, now believed that I was some kind of traitor to the WWA. I know how bizarre that sounds; I was equally shocked when I first heard the words muttered out of Arson’s myself. Of course, there was more to than that for many people. I believe the roster were just jealous and most likely afraid of my brilliance, especially Arson. On the WWA Meltdown show the previous week, I managed to decisively beat him for the Television Title. He’d gone unbeaten in the WWA and had held the TV title for so long, he hadn’t yet adjusted to the reality of his situation. Being my stoic self, I probably made the situation a whole lot worse than it really needed to be, but in the end it was their problem not mine. In my eyes I didn’t do anything wrong. Later on in the evening, I’d be visited by another WWA Superstar. Now. A Bank. Manhattan, New York. "WHAT THE HELL DO YOU MEAN ‘HE FELL’?" The robber screamed, his spittle spraying my face liberally. "What do you want me to say?" I asked him, rhetorically. "He slipped on the floor and knocked himself unconscious." The robber drew closer, jamming his face just a few millimetres away from my own. "You really expect me to believe that bullshit?" "Hell, I don’t know what to tell ya, I…" I trailed off. "Hhhnnnn…huh…He did it!" The asshole muttered, still keeled over on the floor from the blow to the gut. I sighed and the bank robber made his way over to the asshole, never taking his eyes off me once. A wise stratagem; even I know I can’t be trusted. "What did you say?" The robber questioned. "Hnnn…He did it," The asshole spluttered pointing in my general direction. "He hit him." "Asshole…" I muttered for everyone to hear. The bank robber angrily strode back toward me, his shotgun cocked and pointed at my head. Just then, one of the man’s colleagues appeared from the vault, carrying a large, black bag presumably full of money. He tapped the robber with the gun pointed at my head on the shoulder. "What the hell are you doing, man?" He asked. "We gotta get outta here!" The robber with the gun nodded at his unconscious colleague on the floor and then said, "Looks like we got a vigilante in the crowd." I smiled at the irony of it all. If he only knew. "I say we show him what we do to vigilante’s…" The robber with the black bag sighed. "No killing, dumbass. We gotta get a quick get away!" "And just how are we gonna do that?" The robber with the gun questioned, still not taking his eyes off me for second. "Batman here knocked our buddy unconscious. What are we gonna do…carry him?" The robber with the black bag looked at me for the first time and I could see, even under his mask, a wave of recognition pass through his eyes. "Winters…?" I remained silent for a second, briefly shocked at the possibility that this man recognised my face. But then it struck me. I shouldn’t be so surprised, after all I do appear more or less every week on the WWA Meltdown television show and more recently at the GTT5. "Yeah," I replied. "What of it?" The robber with the black glared at me for a few seconds and then looked at the one with the shotgun. "We get this guy to carry him and then we’ll deal with him later." The robber with the gun smiled. "Good call. Now pick him up and haul your ass outta here." "Fuck you," I responded. The man with the shotgun grew extremely animated indeed, surging forward and roughly pressing the barrel of the gun against my forehead. "You’ll do it, or I’ll kill you now." "Go ahead," I told him. "I’m wasn’t planning on doing anything this afternoon anyway." The robber with black bag took a couple of steps forward and pushed the shotgun downward, away from my general direction. "Pick up the body…please." I developed a confused expression. At first, I thought he was just some kind of huge wrestling fan, but then I realise I had no fans, but the tone of his voice…just the way that he said made me sense that there was something more to this, something that I couldn’t put my finger on. The fact that the bank robber had asked me politely was another dead giveaway. Reluctant I stepped forward and made my way to the unconscious bank robber, stopping along the way to viciously boot the asshole who’d ratted me out to the bank robbers, in the gut. "Asshole." "How the hell is this guy supposed to pick him up?" The robber with the shotgun asked his partner. "Frank has to outweigh him by at least thirty pounds." The robber with the black bag sighed. "Don’t use names, dumbass! Besides, he’s stronger than he looks." As I hauled the unconscious robber up and onto my shoulders, I memorised that last sentence of his. It didn’t sit right with me and I knew from that point on there was something more to this than meets the eye. 24th October 2005. WWA Meltdown. Salt Lake City, Utah. My extreme match in the WWA Extreme Title tournament went well - at least for me. I managed to overcome my opponent, Vex, with my Indifference Maker superkick and advanced in the tournament. The encounter was quite an ordeal and it took more out of me than I’d care to admit. It was just after that match, when I’d managed to return to my locker room, that my second visitor of the night showed up. It was Armaan. In previous weeks he’d pretty much been arguing and fighting with Freddie Arson, so it was more than a little surprising to find Armaan taking sides with his enemy. Armaan was a lot less aggressive than Arson was, but anger was not in his nature - at least not before what happened with Murphy later on in the night. Again, I made things a lot worse than they really needed to be. I even managed to upset Armaan in that instance, quite a task to accomplish - perhaps the only thing Steven Murphy and I have in common. Like Arson before him, he couldn’t quite handle the fact that I was the WWA Television champion. He took to man-handling my title, somehow needing the feel of the cold, yet comforting, feel of the metal to ground himself into the reality of the situation. But when I told him that I didn’t actually care what other people thought of me that set him off, though I can’t even pretend to know why - and he wasn’t the only one who got offended by this. Armaan promptly left my locker room, unbeknown to what awaited him on the streets of Salt Lake City. Now. Get Away Car. Manhattan, New York. "What the fuck is he doing here with us?" The driver of the get away car yelled, obviously meaning me. The guy who’d had the shotgun pointed at my forehead sighed. "Terry made a friend." I made a mental note that the robber who seemed to recognise me from somewhere was called Terry. "No names, dumbass!" The get away driver yelled. "We’re gonna kill him anyhow," the shotgun guy declared, "so what does it matter?" "We are?" The driver questioned. "I don’t want anything to do with that! Why is Frank unconscious?" "He fell," I responded. "We aren’t going to kill him," Terry decided. His colleagues glared at him puzzled and at the same time exclaimed, "What?" Terry took his balaclava mask off and ran his right hand through his wavy, straw-coloured hair. "I know him - I owe him." I was a little taken aback by his comments, but when I saw his face, something at the back of my mind clicked and for some reason I recognised his face, though I was reasonably certain that I hadn’t met him since waking up in a cornfield outside of Kansas. That would mean…Oh shit. "You know me from before the amnesia?" I asked him. The shotgun guy developed a confused expression. "What amnesia? What the fuck is going on here?" "I know him from my time working for Stakiri…" Terry trailed off, glaring at his feet, ashamed. "Stakiri, man," shotgun guy shook his head disgusted. "That fucking guy…" "You worked for Stakiri?" I needless blurted, suddenly feeling hot under the collar. "Yeah," He replied. "I just came out of prison and he gave me a job as muscle, a hired hand, if you will. One of those jobs was hauling you out of his car and into that cornfield." "What?" I questioned, eager to know more. "The car was moving fast at the time and man, you hit the ground so hard I thought you were dead…" He explained. "Why did he just dump me out there, only to try to get me to work for him later on?" I questioned. "You’re working for him now?" Terry asked, suddenly angry. "I was, but not anymore…" I responded, still trying to take everything in. "Stop the car!" Terry yelled. "Wait, why?" I questioned. The driver put his foot down, hard, and the car screeched to a halt. "The guy is scum even to us," the shotgun guy explained. "Stakiri dropped me from his service right after we dumped you," Terry told me, "then tried to whack me for my troubles. Whatever he did to you or what you meant to him - it was important." "Wait," I pleaded. "I need to know more." "I don’t know anymore," he informed me. "Besides, I can’t have anyone close to him finding me out." The shotgun guy pushed me out of the car, onto the road. I was too dumfounded to stop him, as I watched the car speed away into the distance. 24th October 2005. WWA Meltdown. Salt Lake City, Utah. I decided to take a walk around the streets of Salt Lake City, eager to find out whether it was a dull as everyone said it was. It turns out everyone was right, not that I was in much of a condition to go out and party. I was setting out when it happened. I sensed Armaan a few hundred yards behind me as I exited the arena, but I was first alerted to something being wrong, by the heavy screech of tires upon the icy road. I turned around to see Armaan crash over the bonnet of the car, with a sickening thud. The EMT’s arrived soon after, but before they did the driver’s door opened and out he stepped into the street. It was the very first time I ever set eyes on Steven Murphy and he didn’t even try to hide his actions. He didn’t even try to conceal that he was the perpetrator. He just came out to gloat. How could I not be impressed at that? Someone with the same out of viciousness that I am capable of. Our third round match-up in the GTT5 will be extremely interesting to say the least. Later. Alley Way Somewhere. Location Undisclosed. "Well?" Mr Stakiri’s voice rasped. The man who’d been going by the alias of ‘Terry’ the entire day sat forward and smiled. "He took out John without any of us seeing a thing!" Stakiri smiled. "Figures." "Tell me something," ‘Terry’ began. "Why go through with this charade? What does it gain you?" Stakiri’s smiled widened. "Mind games, what else?"
