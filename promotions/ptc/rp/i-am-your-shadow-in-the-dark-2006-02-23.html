I am your shadow in the dark

23 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-02-23 Shanty Jones sat in his hotel room, a towel resting over his head, recuperating as he did after every round of GTT5. The battle against Masquerade had been hard and long, but it was not aching muscles or the enormity of the semi-final match ahead that occupied his thoughts. It had been a long road to get where he was, but his journey was far from over. There were many problems that still plagued Shanty. It had been a long road. A long, long road. It had undeniably started when he was seventeen. Just a rebellious teenager who saw a man running down the street; someone who was surely a thief, being chased by an irate shopkeeper. Shanty, a promising rugby player when he wasn’t spraying graffiti on to the sides of honest businesses, decided that the thief deserved to be caught, and so he tackled him to the ground. It wasn’t out of any feeling of social responsibility, but instead out of a sense of superiority. Shanty had judged the man as a thief and wanted to see him punished. When the pursuer drew a gun and fired it into the man’s head, Shanty realised that he had made a mistake that he could never take back. He’d never find out exactly what had been happening, but he would know for the rest of his life that he had made a snap judgement and that this had cost a man his life. The experience would shape Shanty, changing him from a drop-out to a devout Christian as he searched to try and make sense out of what he had seen. All he could do was think that he had been punished for judging someone. He would never judge another human being again, until he saw the factories. A light electronic sound and the unmistakeable clicking of tumblers indicated that someone else had entered the room. "Jah! I knew you vould be here," said Doctor Curiosity, as he moved towards the bed where Shanty was resting. "Hello," replied Shan in his soft Welsh accent. "I was wondering when you would come back." "Vell, I guess I can’t stay away… plus Eegor and I have seen some really weird shit. You’ve really got to come and take a look at zis." Eegor was Paul Goodman, Doctor Curiosity’s brother, and general person about whom not enough was known. He’d been Shanty’s friend in prison, but he’d always harboured secrets. Shanty had ostensibly been his friend, but Paul was definitely the senior partner in there. He was respected throughout the prison and had contacts on every side. As one officer had said, he was the ‘closest thing to a God that most people had in there’. Yet he had escaped and broken free. Nobody knew how he had made it out with not a single door unlocked or a single trail left, but escaped he surely had. His precise were a mystery, but avoiding the people who sought to recapture him was only half of what he intended to do. Shanty had been told that Paul was involved, was complicit in atrocities that matched those that he had seen in Iraq. Whether this was the truth or not bothered the wrestler, who had considered Paul as being the closest thing he had to a friend. "What is it?" asked Shanty. "You’re going to have to see it. Besides, you spend too much time in zis room, and it smells of gunsmoke." "It always will," replied Shanty, shaking his head. That’s what happens when you have to condemn someone. When Shan was in a position where he had to pass judgements, the vicious irony of the situation did not escape him. A life spent avoiding the situation had ultimately left him in a position where he was doing what he had sworn never to do. In the Army, in Iraq, he had been pushed a little too far, and under extreme stress and pressure he had set a bomb, packed it with nails, and killed his fellow soldiers. Never let it be said that God does not work in mysterious ways, for Shanty survived, or perhaps was allowed to return, scarred terribly around his jaw-line, and with a task that would prove both confusing and onerous. One by one the dead returned to life, inhabiting the bodies of people they knew closely, being allowed to confront Shanty Jones and bring him to task for his greatest sin, for taking their lives. The deal was not without catches, though, for every aspect of Shan’s life was revealed to them, one detail at a time, until they knew both his nature and their own. At that time they had a choice; to try to kill Shanty or to accept their fate. It still pained Shanty that not all of them chose the path of redemption. He had been God’s instrument, an Angel-in-Waiting, the one who would carry out His will. So, the ones who chose peace found their way to Heaven, and those who chose violence and revenge, who chose hate over forgiveness, they went the other way. Each and every soul left a bitter taste of judgement in Shanty’s mouth. The car ride was quiet, with neither man in the mood to say a great deal. Doctor Curiosity had started off as a spectator, someone who was just along for the ride, but somewhere he’d gotten himself involved in some heavy stuff, and he wasn’t sure he wanted to be associating with the likes of Shanty Jones, or even his own brother, Paul. There were things at stake here higher than a mere wrestling tournament, though he himself would know that these things could spiral out of control better than perhaps anyone alive. "Where are you taking me?" asked Shanty. He too was unsure of the happenings of the previous week. He had thought that his job had been done, had been completed with the passing of the soul of Steven Atkins. The last of the dead were gone, and he had been expecting to be carried aloft by angels, or at least allowed to go to Heaven. None of it had come to pass, and instead he found himself wrapped in conspiracy after conspiracy, each layer defying belief. "Ve’re going to meet vith Eegor." "You told me that already." "There’s someone else you have to meet. Someone different. You’ll never have seen this before." "I have seen things you can’t even dream of…" muttered Shan. The factories were what had sent Shanty Jones over the edge. Entering them only when their base came under attack, Shanty and Steven had found that under their very own compound lurked a den of horrific secrets, where experiments were carried out daily on both the living and the dead. Genetic material was injected from one person to the next, and radiation was applied in focused bursts, testing and probing for mutations. All of this and more, and so much more was being run by Jayenne Industries, on the largest stockpile of human beings that they could replenish without anyone noticing. The middle of a warzone is a heck of a place to carry out experiments on those believed to be dead. Steven counselled caution and silence, to forget that they had ever seen such things, but the images would not leave Shanty Jones alone. A woman with her tongue burnt out of her head, a man… no, just a boy with his eyes so blank that it was as if his entire life had been sucked out of him, and the one image that Shan could never, ever shake. The huge needle that came through the top of the man’s head, spiking directly into his brain, injecting in God knows what. Steven readjusted and ignored what he had seen. Bad things happen in times of war, and in the city of Basra it was indeed a time of war. Shanty tried to ignore it too, but it wasn’t easy, and the stress began to show. Everything they did took on a new light. They were not bringing liberation from tyranny, but rather insidious torture and death. One day he could not take it any more, and the bomb that killed Steven Atkins and nine others was supposed to be the final resting place of Shanty Jones. "Explain to me where we are going," said Shan, after a long period of silence. Curiosity had been driving in silence, worrying in his own mind about how he could extract himself from the whole sorry situation. Things were fine with just Shanty, but bringing in his brother, and then The Ripper… it was getting scary. And that was before they met the new guy. "Ve vill be zere shortly. Maybe you should tzink of living closer to here… it vould make ze driving easier." "I like the hotel just fine." "Even ze smell?" asked Curiosity. "It keeps me grounded. It reminds me why I’m here." "But you could move to zis neighbourhood," protested the doctor. He looked out of the car windows and saw that they were driving through what could be described lovingly as ‘the slums’. Broken bottles and trash littered the street, and houses were boarded up as often as inhabited. "Zere are… ve have some vonderful vooden vindow features in zis district!" Shanty closed his eyes and tried to count to ten, but it was hard to ignore the doctor once he got going. "Varied vildlife," he said as stray dogs ran past. "Even very good access for a nearby crack house… if you like zat kind of thing." Shanty was not in the car because of the doctor. He was in the car because of Paul Goodman, the doctor’s brother, more commonly known to the wrestling world as Eegor. He was in the car because of potential links between the mild mannered murderer he had met in prison, and the shadowy, supremely confident figure of The Ripper that Shanty had met prior to defeating Masquerade. Shanty needed to know about Paul Goodman. He needed to know if the allegations were true. He needed to know if he had links to Jayenne Industries, the company seemingly owned and operated by The Ripper. Jack Nobbledy… JN… Jayenne. The dark man had said to him that Paul had been running a factory of his own inside the prison; that he was using a different style of technology, a less invasive one, but that everyone there was a subject, was under the microscope. It had seemed fantastical, but after all that he had been through there were no things that he would have discounted out of hand. If Pope John Paul II had come by to ask Shanty to a party with Elvis and JFK, he would have genuflected, just in case. Was the betrayal possible? Could Jack have been telling the truth, and if so, why tell him now? The opportunity was there for Paul; the prison had not been the most sane of places, and he had been unusually well connected, even for a so-called important inmate. The only way to find out was to ask him, to confront him and see what would happen. Shanty balled his fists up and then relaxed them. Tension would not be his ally here. The car pulled up and the doctor turned the key in the ignition, cutting off the remaining sounds and leaving them to hear the high pitched metallic notes of the engine cooling down. The sky looked bleak outside, the weather typical of the end of Winter. Spring and the promise of life was not yet upon them, but the icy cold hands of nature’s harshest season were relaxing their grip on all things that lived. "Ve are here," announced the doctor. "I had noticed," replied Shanty. The Welsh wrestler followed Curiosity around to the back of the car, and then across the remains of an estate’s small parking lot. From there a couple of alleyways, all the same, smelling of piss and beer, and they came upon a door. "They are in here," said the doctor. "You couldn’t have picked a nicer place," said Shanty, his voice never rising above his habitual murmur. Curiosity unlocked the door with a seemingly ancient key and pressed against the imitation frosted glass, which bowed at his touch and pushed the portal open. Inside was a corridor that had clearly not been redecorated since the 1970s. Brown spotted wallpaper hung down the sides, still not dark enough to cover the myriad stains that had at one time or another blotted out the original colour. Some looked like blood or other effluvia… some didn’t even bear thinking about. Another door at the end of the corridor, one unlocked and on the latch. Shanty nodded towards it, and turned to see the reaction of the doctor. "Yes, that’s the one." Shanty turned back and left Curiosity trailing in his wake. Legs that used to power through rugby defences brought Shan up to speed and at the door within seconds. He pushes it open hard, enjoying the satisfying crunch it made against the wall on the other side. Inside the room that the door led to was Paul Goodman, stood in the centre underneath a low wattage bulb. There was nothing else within the room other than a small wooden chair in the corner. On it sat a man, whose eyes were as blank as if his entire life had been sucked out of him. Shanty was stopped in his tracks. "Who is that?" he asked. "I don’t even know," replied Paul. We found him when we left you last week. We think he was put there for us to take care of." "What?" "The Ripper. He’s leading us to do something." "Why?" "It’s what he does. You may as well ask why the Sun shines, or why birds sing." "No. Why did you follow his lead." "I can’t help myself," said Paul. His eyes looked different, and his demeanour had changed. Gone was the man from the prison, the man who ran everything. Gone was his surety and confidence, the very thins that had drawn Shanty towards him. In their place were resignation and despair. He was like a puppet, being manipulated from above, forever at the behest of the master. "What has he done to you?" asked Shanty. "Nothing, that’s just it. The anticipation is the worst thing of all. He has control of me, but he doesn’t exert it. Just the knowledge that it is there is enough to make me follow every lead he sets. I can’t even think about what he could do if…" "Did you run one of the factories?" Shanty’s interruption silenced his friend. Paul looked down at his ankles, as if they could come to his rescue. "Paul, look at me. Did you run a factory for Jayenne?" Goodman sighed and looked up. "Yes, Shanty. Of course I did." Blood raced through Shan’s body, coursing through veins and capillaries, bustling down arteries and feeling as if it was more viscous than ever. His face became reddened and he would have sworn that he could hear the red and white cells crashing through his heart, his brain, his soul. "What did you do to people?" "I’m sorry Shan. I didn’t mean for you to…" "For… Paul! What did you do to me?" asked Shanty, his calm demeanour starting to rise towards panic. "I shouldn’t have done it. I feel I betrayed you, but I had no choice! I didn’t know what I was doing until too late, and…" "And?" "…and by then you were gone! By then I had nothing left but what they were giving me! I knew I had to get out, and try to come here and reach you. I knew you would still be here, for the tournament. It’s so important that you…" Paul’s voice tailed off again, and Shanty started to grow impatient. "Paul, what did you do to me?" he growled, his voice rising in tension and anger. Shanty took two steps towards Paul, but a single arm raised up prevented him from moving, scant feet away from contact. Shan thought that he had reflexively stopped, but when he tried to move again, he found himself immobile. "What is this, Paul?" said Shanty, a note of terror in his voice. Of all the things he had ever prided himself on, control was foremost among them. "We’re joined, Shan. The two of us… and others too. We’re joined at such a level… it’s beautiful if you can see the bigger picture. It’s…" Shanty tried to move, as Paul’s voice started to choke up. Sobs were wracking the body of the doctor’s brother, but nobody in the room seemed able to move. "We’re… we’re bound together, you and I," continued Paul. "We’re amalgamated, sharing not just on a genetic level, but on a molecular one. Nothing can change that. That is why you listen to me, and why your body will not obey you. That is what we’ve been working on, been moving towards. Oh, the plans…" Shanty Jones was not listening any more. His entire self was focused internally. Every ounce of mental or emotional power that he had ever known existed tried to overcome the forces of the muscles, of the forces that were keeping him still, and inch by inch, painful though it was… he was succeeding. It was as if the very atoms of his body were rebelling against him; the pain was at such a level that it permeated everything he could see, feel, taste, hear or smell. The colour of his world was pain… but he was moving. Paul looked on at him in shock and awe. This was meant to be impossible. The tests that had been done so far had been conclusive. Something must have gone wrong. "Stop, Shanty. You’re killing yourself!" Through gritted teeth that sang with agony, Shanty tried to speak, but it only came out a primeval grunt. Still he advanced, one small step after the other, his entire body resonating with the molecules that wanted to move in the opposite direction. Inch by inch, he drove towards Paul, who found himself rooted to the spot. "How… how are you doing this?" Shanty finally reached him, and with a gargantuan effort reached one hand up and grasped Paul by the throat. "Cure…" rasped Shanty, words coming hard to him through the soul-searing pain. "No." "Cure me!" insisted Shanty, blood starting to drip from his tear ducts as he squeezed tighter and tighter around Paul’s throat. "Stop! I can’t do that. It’s within your body… the particles, the cells, everything. You can never take it out." "Let… me… go." Shanty’s hand tightened even more, and Paul was having trouble gaining breath. "I can… I can do better than that," he whispered, terrified. "Do it!" said Shanty, his endurance being tested to the absolute limit. "Promise me…" "No promises." "Promise you won’t kill me," whispered Paul, before dropping his arm and releasing the invisible pressure on Shanty Jones. The wrestler fell to the floor, and drew himself up on one arm that felt as if it had nearly been torn clean off. "Now… now cure me," he said, fighting for breath with lungs that would finally cooperate. "I can’t do that, Shan, it’s in too deep. I can go one step further though." Paul reached into his pocket and withdrew a capped needle and a vial of some clear liquid. "What’s that?" asked Shanty. "It’s me. I can change things." "Change?" "I can give you the control. You will have dominion over me." Shanty looked at the man, who seemed more like a terrified boy. He could see right through to his core, and he knew that Paul Goodman was no longer in a position to lie or manipulate anyone. "Do it," said Shanty. Paul fumbled with the casing for a few moments, but it wasn’t long before all was in place. He was ready, no, eager to hand over his responsibilities and his power. His hands trembled only slightly as he readied the injection. And Shanty Jones could only close his eyes as the needle slid, not for the first time, directly into his brain.
