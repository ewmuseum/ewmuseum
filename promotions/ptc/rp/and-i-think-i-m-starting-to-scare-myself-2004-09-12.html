"And I think I'm Starting to Scare Myself"

12 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-12 The room was dank, dark and had a distinctly rising smell of damp. In all essences, it was a dive of the lowest quality and no self-respecting person would ever consider inhabiting it, even if they couldn’t afford anywhere else. “I’ll take it” Justin Vivazi Locke couldn’t believe his dirty green eyes. It was perfect, and he had found it at the first time of asking. His voice, however, portrayed none of this emotion and flowed from his lips like the dying testament of a begrudging man. The landlord, a fat, skin headed guy dressed in a string vest and caked in tattoos, grunted in acknowledgement and dutifully left the room with a loud slam of the thin wooden door. Sliding his backpack from his thin shoulders, Justin slowly moved over to the dust coated bed and sat down wearily. He’d had a long day so far, having caught plane, bus and taxi to arrive at his current destination. He’d even had to put up with listening to the gravel like voice of the Landlord while being drenched in his spit as he laid down the rules and regulations of the room. He’d have taken a shower if not for a couple of circumstances- firstly, the water was a dirty shade of brown and resembled more of a pond during a flood. Secondly, and just as importantly, he had written a nice little ditty on the back of his hand during the bus ride down and he didn’t want to lose the ink before he had committed it fully to memory. The cream embossed wallpaper was peeling slightly in the corners and, in places, appeared almost to be supported by the masses of cobwebs. The only furniture was the bed, awkward and uncomfortable, a scratched and chipped table and a damaged wardrobe that had clearly seen better days. The decaying window frame was hidden by a pair of thin lime-green curtains that had been well used in the last few decades and seemed out of place in the otherwise plain room. Noting all this in his memory, Justin slid across the room and pried at the handle of another thin white door, forcing it open with a charge of the shoulder. This led to the bathroom, which he had already been shown by the landlord, and he wanted to take a closer look at the facilities. They met neatly with his needs, as he planned to spend as little time there as possible. In fact, he’d more than likely find himself showering in the rain that he would in an appliance held together by duct-tape. Cupping a skinny hand softly to his ear, he noted the audible dripping of water from a pipe above the ceiling with a sigh. “Annoying.” He muttered whilst ghosting back into the sleeping area and slamming shut the door on the monstrosity behind him. “Depressing, dull, dank, dirty, decaying… delightful.” He said aloud as if playfully singing to himself. No, he hated his new home, but it was what he wanted anyway so he couldn’t complain. Laying himself flat out on the bed, he drew a small leather-bound book from his backpack and unwound the soft entwined leather cord carefully. He always did this part carefully; it had become a daily ritual in his life recently, and acted as an agent to keep him sane. He smiled briefly whilst mentally touching upon this subject. Human sanity was a favourite of his and he could give a perfectly structured thesis on it if he ever had the motivation. From a pocket of his brown corduroy trousers, he removed a pen encased in an intricate green mock-stone plastic and slowly placed the lid on the upright end whilst sliding a hand into the leather bound book and opening it to a certain page which, already, was half full of poetic jottings and other such smudges as his hand caressed the page in writing. “Buses – the transport for the people who don’t like transport and, henceforth, don’t use transport. Smile politely at the passengers, bear the pearly whites and such but sit and ignore any thoughts of human contact; this is convenience not fun. Oh, Justy, you never change. You won’t let yourself.” He spoke aloud, almost delightedly, as he copied down the words. It wasn’t far from the truth; he never had been one to let himself change willingly. He put it down to not needing to change, despite openly admitting (to his self and his self only) that change was the way forwards. A point he often chose to ignore was that he decided to move purely for the change and in the hope that this change may alter his fortunes in some way. His answer was to refuse acceptance, as always. His teachers at school always said he was too stubborn to succeed. Placing the pen down on the off-colour sheets, he used his left hand to sweep the heavily greased, dirty blond hair from his eyes and back behind his ears before focusing in more detail on the journal before him. Slowly lifting the pen in his left hand, he began to write. “-Phase one complete- Clipping and Isolation.-” This he wrote boldly, the letters angular, sharp and detailed with neat precision. This was his usual title format, and the rest of the entry was in more of a scrawl, untidy with practise as the blue ink began to freely flow. “I’ve succeeded in hiding myself away, but I’m no longer sure that it was my original intention. Change of scenery, admittedly, was interesting for all of a couple of seconds during the time it took me to re-accustom my eyes to being open. But I am here, whatever reason I am too scared (or stupid) to admit to. I probably just don’t want to. Refusal to admit feelings is supposed to be a weakness. Society conditions us to agree. Funny, last time I said that to somebody I was called pretentious and told it was a stuck up way of saying I’m a stubborn fool. But it sounds so right in here. ‘I’m an angel with broken wings…’ Its cold here, the window is open and I have no urge to close it. Something tells me that it should stay open, that or maybe I just don’t want to close it. I don’t know. I should probably get used to my new base. I don’t know exactly where I am, nor do I really want to. If I go outside I’ll be ignored, if I stay inside I’ll be ignored in peace. I can feel a rapidly deepening self-pity trip coming on here. ‘I hate myself and want you to die’ But you were the reason I came here in the first place (I think). Why should I fear you when I’ve already done the running? I don’t know. And all this time I’ve been led to think that I am the problem, when its clearly you. You are the persecutor and I am the helpless. It’s the way it’s always been. ‘Painting pictures in red and blue, A portrait bruised just like you.’ You are the broken one and I am the successor. Yeah, I like the sound of that. You are bruised, broken, etc. I am here and flourishing. I think. No, here comes that ambiguity again. I’m not sure, and I’m now over half way through the third book and I’m still unsure. If this is a good way to sort out your mentality then I’m the kind of guy who’s friendly to a screaming child I meet in the street. Whatever. Total waste of three days psychiatry. ‘And I think I’m starting to scare myself’ ” He closed the book softly, caressing the well-worn leather with his thin fingers in a ritualistic pleasure. Every day he would add to the book on the advice of a psychiatrist he visited three times as a child. His mother had forced him and he had never forgiven her. To Justin, it demonstrated a lack of confidence that she could tan him with a mental problem just because he wasn’t in to sport or gawping and wolf whistling at passing women. He hated people like that, people who saw others as objects. They were the same people that ignored him, and ignored him so brutally. He hated them with a volatile anger and he felt the adrenaline course through his veins when even bringing up images of them mentally. “Won’t you just forget!?” he shouted, throwing his prized pen angrily at the bed. It bounced softly and the lack of violent recoil wound him up further. He wanted nothing more than to forget and be someone new. The problem was that he wasn’t going to let himself change, not without a fight. A rhythmic rapping at the thin wooden door awoke him from his mini-episode, and he eyed it with a confused glare. He wasn’t expecting visitors. In fact, he hadn’t been visited at his last home for so long he began to forget that he had detached the doorbell and soundproofed the door so he couldn’t hear any visitors anyway. That would generally explain the lack of visitors, he thought, as suspicion arose through Justin. Who would want to visit him? Who ever noticed him? There was no more time to think as the door opened and a small man dressed in black entered the room and glanced around enthusiastically. His black hair was heavily greased and combed back, stretching his face and making his rat-like features stand out gravely. His beady eyes seemed like they would be better suited to a weasel than a person. “Hi, I’m Jack and I live across the hall. Just wanted to come across and meet my new neighbour. My, that’s a nice little book you got there. Oh, and the backpack, very nice. Say, have I introduced myself?” the stranger spoke with such speed that it seemed like each word was racing to find its way from his mouth. The pause that ensued seemed very out of place. “Yes” Justin spoke slowly and with as little enthusiasm as possible. “Yes you have.” “Say, the names Jack, old buddy old pal.” He continued anyway, much to the annoyance of Justin. “That must be nice for you.” Justin replied with a river of sarcasm. “Now if you don’t mind I was just going to look out of the window in contemplation.” There was another pause. “Alone.” Jack The Weasel began to pace small circles around the room, his leather coat much too large for him and not far from dragging along the ground. His steps raked up dust from the carpet in a choking cloud, which danced in the late afternoon sunlight from the window as it fell back to the ground. “Well you wont mind me hanging around then? I haven’t got any appointments today, you see, and good old Jack likes to look around other rooms. Say, that’s a nice book you have there…” Justin snatched up the book and pocketed it before Jack could place a finger on it and motioned with his head towards the door. Noticing that Jack hadn’t taken the hint, Justin sighed and closed his eyes peacefully, his hand still caressing the book in his pocket. “It’s such a shame that you had to leave so early, John.” Justin proclaimed as he climbed to his feet and placed a hand on Jack’s shoulder in mock friendliness, moving him softly towards the door, which he opened with his free hand. Jack opened his mouth to protest, but Justin had already pushed him through the door and was beginning to close it on his hand as he gripped onto the frame. “I’ve got an appointment free next February if you have, how about the 31st?” He closed the door with a thud, just as Jack removed his fingers, and slid down with his back blocking re-entry to the room. “Jack wants a friend, poor guy, but I’m not a good friend. I’ve got to wrestle and I’ll win the tournament if its kills me. Exposure will get me a job if nothing else, exposure will make people like me.” Justin began to address his age-old problem. He was a wrestler and some would say he was damn good at it, but he drew the heat of an average Mexican Wave. Charisma was his failure, but he liked it that way. He had coined it that his role in life was to pave the way for the hordes of charisma lacking people into the world of Celebrity. GTT4 would be his first footstep on the ladder. Muppet Man was the first rung.
