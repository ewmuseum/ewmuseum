12 June 2004 I Never Thought I Would - Part I Infinite whiteness. Like a blank canvas, full of endless possibility. A picture yet to be painted. A story yet to be told. The whiteness slowly dims, revealing the interior of a bar named the Peanut Barrel. It could best be described as somewhat rustic -- the floor is made entirely from rough, dusty wood panels, and the tables are a lackluster pine. The dimly-lit plaster walls are painted a shade of off-white, covered with mirrors, framed vintage posters, and the odd painting here and there. It is very late at night (or early in the morning, depending on how you look at it). The chairs have already been turned up, and there is only one customer remaining at the bar, with a half-empty bottle of Captain Morgan and several empty shot glasses sitting in front of him. The bartender is standing down at the opposite end of the bar, eyeing his patron warily while wiping down a few empty glasses with a white rag. The customer, whose back is turned, is wearing a pair of faded blue jeans, a black t-shirt, and a pair of white athletic shoes. He sits hunched over the bar, resting his elbows on the table, almost in a brooding sort of way. After taking a moment to run his hand through his short, spiky brown hair, he returns to his reverie. The mystery customer, sitting at the bar with his back turned, reaches into his pocket. After drunkily fumbling around a bit, he procures a folded, crinkled envelope, which has already been opened. He unfolds it and removes an equally crinkled letter from within. Leaning forward, the man rests the side of his head in his left hand, while holding the letter in his right. He idly musses up his hair while reading it for a few silent moments. The bartender shrugs, and turns away... BAM. The sudden sound of a clenched fist pounding the top of the bar makes the bartender jump slightly, as he turns back around. The customer's left hand is now tightly balled up and rests on the bar, the obvious culprit. He lets out a long, beleaguered sigh, and hunches forward to rest his clammy forehead between the bottle and shot glasses. His right arm remains rigid, still holding the worn letter. Without moving, or even giving it another look, he quickly crumples up the letter into a paper ball and tosses it aside. Mumbling something inaudibly under his breath, he lifts his head and slowly gets up from his seat. "Sir, are you alright?" asks the bartender. Wobbling a bit in his stupor, the nameless drunkard grabs onto the side of the bar to support himself as he tries to stay on his feet. He slowly turns to the bartender and grins stupidly. "I'm morrre than ffffarkin' alright there, bucko!! I'm gonna be the neeeeeeeew Global Shampyun thingy!! Huzz... Hurrz... Harra... er, HUZZAH!!" The bartender cocks an eyebrow, as the inebriated fellow raises his right arm high into the air, and then quickly swipes at the bottle of Captain Morgan and snatches it in one grand motion. "An' I'm takin' thisssh with meeee! Hic!" The customer points at the bartender with his left hand, then spins around in a circle for a moment. He stops, barely managing to keep his balance. Now pointing at the exit, he stumbles his way towards the door. After a few steps, however, he bumps into one of the tables and knocks a few chairs to the floor. "Awwwww sheeeeze!" His face aghast with a comical paranoia, he turns back to the bartender and lifts a finger up to his mouth. "Ssssssh! Don't tell the bartender I did that!" he whispers cautiously. "Uh... right. Wouldn't dream of it," responds the bartender. The man smiles and nods approvingly, then turns and immediately runs into another table, knocking more chairs to the floor. He gets a panicked look on his face, and decides to make a run for the exit. He haphazardly flings himself across the room, running into several more tables and sending a plethora of chairs crashing to the floor, much to the dismay of the hapless bartender. He reaches the front door, swings it open, and exits the Peanut Barrel. The door shuts behind him. Slowly, everything turns back to white once again... Yeah, that was me... man, what a mess I was, eh? I bet you're probably wondering why the hell I ended up getting so smashed. Where to begin? At the beginning, I suppose. Let me start by asking you a question. If there was something in this world that you wanted -- something that you wanted so badly, so desperately, so passionately, that you simply could not fathom living without it -- what price would you be willing to pay to get it? What would you sacrifice? What sort of chance would you take? Would you forsake your own morals? Place yourself above the rules? Disregard your own health and safety, and put your life at risk? Would you? I never thought I would. Never thought I'd be faced with such a hard decision. That's the one thing in this world that I know for certain I'm not good at... making hard decisions. Kind of ironic, considering I'm the commissioner of a world-class professional wrestling federation. Oh yeah, speaking of that... guess I haven't mentioned, but my name is Scott. Scott Andrew Isaac Jeffries, to be exact. It's a mouthful, I know. In the world of professional wrestling, I keep my name shortened. You can just call me Saij. Everyone else does. Well, sometimes my parents will greet me with "hey there, stupid!" But that's about it. As I said, I'm a commissioner... the commissioner of a federation called Xtreme Ultimo Wrestling. I'm primarily a wrestler, but a near-fatal injury sidelined me a few months ago. But now, at last, I'm finally returning to active competition in the ring... and that's what my story is about. Hard decisions. I could explain it to you... but then, my version of events is all you'd be getting. Perhaps it's best if you judge for yourself. Perhaps it's best to simply show you the events that led up to where we are now. Just remember to ask yourself... what would you have done? It was just another normal day in the locker room... The whiteness dims once again, this time revealing the locker room of one of XUW's long-running stables, the Boomer Sooners. Of course, "locker room" is a very loose expression in this case. The place looks more like a studio apartment inhabited by a group of frat boys. One might find it odd that the Sooners choose to practically live in their arena locker room, considering they travel to at least two different arenas every week. In fact, one might say it's logically not possible. But then again, the Sooners aren't the sort who care much for logic. After all, one of their members is a life-size cardboard cut-out of former XUW superstar and Sooner, Chase Wilson. Known mostly for his work as a play-by-play announcer for some other wrestling federation, JR has also been with the Sooners for nearly two years now. Everyone slowly turns their heads to look at the door leading out into the hallway (yes, somehow Cardboard Chase does too). The noises sound as if they're coming from the other side. Saij is seated in the back of the room on a cushioned iron bench, his back against the slate-papered wall. "FARK-O-MANIA IS DEAD: NO HOPE FOR GLOBAL TITLE VICTORY By Shane 'The Shane' Blake Lo, how the mighty hath fallen. 2003 went down in the history of Xtreme Ultimo Wrestling as the "Year of Fark-O-Mania," a mystique that practically dripped from the pores of the man responsible for creating it, wherever he went. But this is 2004, and I must break the news to those who don't know: Fark-O-Mania has been dead for months. When the first whispers in the wind came to my ear, bringing word from afar about huge news concerning the Primetime Central Global Title, it never once occured to me that such a monumental announcement would include such a monumental has-been. It was recently made public that XUW's Saij, who suffered near-fatal injuries in a horrible accident last January ("That was no accident, Ric Austin shoved me off the Ultitron!" Saij mutters under his breath), has been granted a shot at the belt in a June 19th match-up against the champion Hessian, of Rush Pro Wrestling, and co-challenger Digital Mortality, of the Universal Wrestling Federation. At first I thought this had to be some sort of cruel joke; how in the world could a washed-up, no-talent cripple like Saij get a chance to win the Global Title, I wondered? After all, wasn't he still recovering from his injuries? Sure, he had gotten well enough to be given a token role as XUW's commissioner, but he still hadn't been given a doctor's blessing to wrestle again. No doubt he abused the power of his office to finagle himself a triple threat match with Hessian and Digital Mortality, which rumor has it was originally slated to be a one-on-one contest before Saij stuck his glory-hogging nose into the picture ("That's not even close to being true!"). What boggled me more than anything is how the Global Title committee could even think Saij has a realistic chance of winning this match. Perhaps if he was still in top shape, as he was when he triumphed in the Chris Williams Memorial Cup tournament, then he might have some slight hope of overcoming the monster Hessian. However, considering: we don't know what the full extent of his injuries may be; the fact that he hasn't wrestled in months; and the fact that he is diving straight into a match against not one, but two of the finest wrestlers in the world, Saij's chances of walking out ofth the Global Title, much less under his own power, are somewhere between 'zero' and 'none.' Admittedly, it is a sad thing to see a young wrestler, not even twenty-two years old, already past his prime with a career cut tragically short by injury. But, it is probably for the best. Professional wrestling needs its clowns, and Saij can still be a clown as much as he likes -- but he'll do so out of the ring, where he should stay permanently. As Hessian described, a guy like Saij is not champion material ("Oh, right, I guess all those months I held the XUW Intercontinental and World Titles were just one tremendous fluke then."). Post-injury, Saij has been reduced to being just a mindless dolt who runs around cracking insults and spouting out nonsensical jibberish. He has no business being a professional wrestler, and I for one will not be surprised when Hessian forces Saij to leave the arena on a stretcher come June 19th. That is, if Saij's injuries don't prevent him from showing up. If he has any brains at all, which I doubt, he'll stay as far away from the match as possible. The PTC Global Title is a level far beyond Saij's ability, and he has no hope of ever getting there." Saij just stares at the page, too angered to say anything more, or even to think. I don't know what it was. Maybe I was just sick of hearing people saying I had no chance of winning. It's not like I hadn't heard it before, and it's not like I'd ever really cared when people said it before. I always ended up proving them wrong. I always came out on top, even against the toughest odds. It didn't matter what anyone else said, the only thing that mattered was that I knew I could win. I'm the only person who ever seems to know this, no matter how many times I prove myself, day in and day out. I'd heard it all before. But this time was different. Now people were starting to say I shouldn't even be in a ring because of my injuries... almost making it sound as if it was my own fault for nearly being killed by another wrestler. That's what made me more angry than anything else. I was just trying to do a job that I love to do, more than anything in this world... and that was taken away from me. Nobody who loved something so much, but was just beyond it's reach, would ever chalk it up to "life isn't fair." Not as long as they could still do something about it. And I was sure I could.
