Recrudescence

25 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-25 A warm wind rife with abstruse warning whistled along the winding valley, snaking between a foreboding coniferous wood and the charred ruins of what was once a wondrous estate. Abandoned long ago, the immense property lay stagnant and unkempt, overrun by weeds and the dull senescence of neglect. Where once there prospered a beautiful palace and imaginative landscape full of life and vigour, now there festered an unmistakable lack of flare and vibrance. Sterility, emptiness, and dreariness reigned. With sombre eyes full of lamentation, Angelo Deville regarded the pitiful monstrosity that had been his home, before he had ordered it burned and razed with his own martyred body nestled inside. He was fully aware it would degenerate to this in the aftermath of his death, but picturing it in his mind and witnessing the actual effect was like a flung snowball next to an avalanche. The former was cause for concern, a fleeting pain; the latter was murder. Seeing it, he felt like dying all over again. The urge to restore it to its former glory, to rebuild it as it once was, flared up within him like a match struck at a gas station. His mind's eye traced out the possibilities, feeding the flames of his ambition and discontent. If he gave it just a little bit of effort, a small hint of care and finesse, his kingdom would flourish and once again rival any heavenly creation. If he just for-- Blowing out the match before it could cause an explosion, he stepped on the urge and mercilessly ground it to dust. Even if he wanted to recreate what once was, deep down he knew he never could. Even deeper down came a whisper that he never should. The chance that the cycle would come full circle was a gamble he was not prepared to make. He couldn't bear to go through the pain of watching the grandeur and eminence go up in smoke again. Even though it was of his own design, the experience was something he could only stand once an eternity. When he had first made his return known, by annihilating the cause for the plan in the first place, the whole world took his death as a hoax, perpetrated in order to make Salvatore drop his guard. While that was the intent, there was nothing false or put-on about his demise. Angelo Deville died that day, just like he had planned, and he had an ceaseless reminder of that cold fact buzzing incessantly in the back of his mind. What are we doing here? Most of the time he was able to slam the voice behind a wall, but The Great Lord of the Dark, Satan, was nothing if not resourceful. He could wriggle through the holes in a quark. When the Devil and The Deville had made their fiendish agreement, the former told Deville that in returning him to the land of the living a small part of himself would have to go along for the ride, but Angelo never imagined that small part would be a nagging voice he could never escape. What are we doing here? If answering him would shut him up, Angelo would have done so in a second. But it wouldn't. And even if it would, Deville wasn't sure how he could answer. What was he doing there? He had already accomplished the goal of both his life and death, and he could live comfortably and happily on some remote island without ever catching even the scent of danger. He could retire as a king. A legend. So why was he back there, flaunting himself and taunting death? Why did he return to where it all began, making himself a target for the countless hordes who wanted revenge, and the immeasurable numbers who sought to make their name by killing him? Why choose the life of the hunted? It would be so easy to just leave it all behind. What are we doing here? The Deville was about to answer that question, along with the others, when a bright light and a sharp pain erupted in the back of his head. Then everything went a cliched shade of black. ~><>...<><~ The fingers tapping tunelessly on the steering wheel were part excitement and part anxiety, as was the continuous swivelling of the driver's head. The road he travelled was bare and desolate, with an unharvested field stretching as far as the eye could see on one side and an unremarkable graveyard on the other, but it wasn't outside obstacles that he was looking for. In fact, he wasn't really looking for anything. He was simply too nervous to sit still, to keep his eyes on the road. Today was going to be the biggest day of his life. He could feel it in his bones. He could taste it in the fetid air roaring in through the open window. Today he was finally going to make a move that would get him the recognition and acclaim he was meant to achieve. Am I really doing this? The butterflies making a dance floor of his stomach confirmed that it was so. Finally, after so long scraping the bottom of the barrel, he was taking fate into his own hands, preparing to catapult himself straight to the top. For too many years he had allowed himself to be swept aside and overlooked in his Family, tackling insignificant task after insignificant task with no avenue to prove himself a power-broker. His wife, who had gotten him inside, wanted him to lay low and concentrate on the love and happiness he could find with her, but he had grander plans in mind. He had dreams that extended beyond a casual beer on the couch and a loving word whispered in his ear. He was going to be the biggest threat the mob had ever seen. He was going to rule it. Ever since he had been a boy, not even affiliated with the organization, he knew he was destined for great things. He didn't even need the constant reassurances of his mother to remind him of the fact; he could feel it in his heart and in his soul. Yet he still couldn't believe he was doing this. Was he really ready to take the big step? To accept the glory that was his due? With a smirk he pushed all the doubt form his mind and regarded himself smugly in the rearview mirror. Of course he was ready. If not now, then when? Sure, he had failed countless times in the past, but that was in the past. He had grown significantly since then. His entire life only one person had had faith in him, had given him the trust to do well, and now she was going to get the sweet reward for her faith. She would see him succeed, and together they could revel in the shock of all those who had the audacity to doubt him. He started to tremble with anticipation as he saw the driveway he sought and the rounded brown pyramid beyond it. His wife's black convertible was not parked beside the huge square entrance to the sandhouse, but that did not surprise him. He told her to park inside, so as not to arouse any suspicious interruptions. Someone nosing around in there on the biggest day of his life would not be a good thing. Pulling into the driveway, he rejoiced at the throbbing of his heartbeat in his head, like a triumphant drum announcing his arrival to the big time. He couldn't wait to see the look on his wife's face when he popped the trunk and revealed his surprise, the thing that would make him rich, famous, and powerful all in one fell swoop. No one would expect it. No one would believe it when it happened. But there, in the sandtrap, his destiny awaited. He was finally going to make his mark. He was finally going to get the acknowledgement he deserved. No one would be able to doubt that he had finally arrived. He needed this, and it was his. He was too strong and smart to wallow at the bottom. Too handsome, too. He knew this as well as he had ever known anything in his life. Because his mother had told him it was so. ~><>...<><~ Waking up was slow and arduous. The darkness that had marked The Deville's fall was not gone, despite the fact that his eyes were now wide open. He was in a small dark place, and every part of his body tingled with a distant numbness that made him want to crawl out of his skin. If not for the smell of steel and gasoline, plus the unmistakable feel of a tire iron digging into his back, he would have thought he was dead. You should be so lucky, the Devil mocked, an unpleasant reminder that none of his private thoughts were private anymore. Angelo tried to quarantine the annoying voice, but Satan deftly dodged his clumsy effort and saturated his mind with a grating cackle. I told you not to go back there. I told you it would bode poorly for us. Cramped, numb, and unable to block away the Devil's presence in his conscious, The Deville forgot himself and took the bait. Stop saying "us", damn you. Satan's laughter once again filled his head. Oooooh. What's the matter, Angie? Angry that your stupidity got us locked in this trunk? There isn't supposed to be any us. The deal was that I got to come back, not you. You leeching onto me and distracting me from what I need to do was never supposed to happen. The Deville seethed in the darkness, as helpless to escape the Devil's clutches as he was to pop the trunk open. The sudden turning of the car rolled him over onto his face, into a puddle of grease, and there was nothing he could do about that either. Except vow revenge. Which he did. I warned you quite explicitly, Satan responded nonchalantly, that I was coming along for the ride. You never said it would be like this. You never said you'd be a second voice in my head. You never asked. The Deville cursed silently, then began trembling with rage as Satan lauded his choice of words. When I get back to Hell, it's so fucking on it's not even funny. Lucifer smirked. Pretty big words for a guy trapped in a trunk. You're gonna have to get yourself out of here, first. What happened to "us", asshole? A vision of the Devil shrugging popped up in The Deville's mind. It only fits when it's going to piss you off. The car lurched to a stop, much to Angelo's delight, and the engine abruptly died. Hopefully now he would be able to get out of this trunk, and he'd be able to set things straight. He ached to set things straight. Concentrating as hard as he could to hear what was going on outside over Satan's whistling -- a complex and sympathetic tune that he managed to make sound like a fiddle -- Deville waited with baited breath to hear who it was that had captured him, and why he wasn't dead yet. That it was an amateur who held him in his clutches was beyond question. If it was anyone with any sense, anyone who had been around the block, Deville wouldn't have had the chance to wonder. He'd be in Hell right now. A woman's agitated voice penetrated the din, followed by a deeper voice full of smug, ignorant reassurance. Her protests continued without pause as their footfalls brought them around the car, right to the trunk, and then there was the satisfying jingle of keys being put to the lock. The latch clicked open and the trunk popped back, revealing a very ugly yet extremely pleased looking man beside a stunned woman. Her surprise quickly mutated into horror as her eyes locked on The Deville's. "You . . . fucking . . . idiot." The words were barely audible as she backed away from the trunk, and obviously meant for the man beside her despite the fact that she couldn't break eye contact with Angelo. Her reaction was somewhat satisfying, and Deville could stare at that contorted mask of horror all day, but if his body would have accommodated him, he still would have leapt out of the trunk and killed them both. "What?" the man asked the woman, his voice bordering on supreme agitation. "What's wrong, honey? This isn't a time for worry. It's time for celebration." The woman finally tore her gaze from Angelo, levelling a glare on her husband that should have melted him where he stood. Shifting himself to get the blood circulating again, The Deville waited patiently, trying to ignore the cold rush of the pins and needles coursing through his body. It was nearly impossible, yet easier than ignoring the devil in his mind. "Do you have any idea who that is?" the woman demanded incredulously, whispering as if Angelo might not hear her. "Are you out of your mind?" She was shaking more than Deville was, and for better reason. "What?" the man squawked again. "Do you know how much his head is worth?" He rushed forward and seized his wife by the arms. "This is our big break. I'm finally going to earn your father's respect, and the cash will set us up for life. What? What's wrong? Smile, honey. This is a great day. We finally made it." The woman shrieked when she saw Deville climbing out of the drunk on shaky limbs, muttering to himself about the kind of shit he had to put up with, and apparently arguing with himself in two subtly different voices. "You didn't even tie him up. YOU DIDN'T EVEN TIE HIM UP?!" The man looked insulted by the question. "Why would I? I have a gun." Finally back on his feet and more than tired of the exchange, Deville went through the motions of dusting off his oil-stained suit. "You'd better use it, pal, and fast. Do you have any idea what I do to people who lock me in their trunk and ruin my suit?" The woman sagged to her knees, crying silently, as her husband drew his pistol and aimed it at Deville with a shaky hand. In the heat of the moment, he didn't catch the rhetorical cast of the question and answered on impulse. "What?" Deville paused, tilting his head to the side, then blinked. Then grinned. "I don't know, to be honest. This is the first time anyone has ever made this mistake." The man seemed to have recovered himself, and he made his voice rigid. "I can assure you it'll be the last, unless you stay right where you are." He flashed a set of crooked teeth. "Nothing personal, Deville. You're just my ticket to the easy life." Angelo chuckled and shook his head. In his head, Angelo could see Satan tapping his foot impatiently, checking his nonexistent wrist for an equally nonexistent watch. Could we hurry up and kill these fools, please? Satan asked tiredly, then suddenly perked up. Unless we're going to torture them, of course. We haven't tortured anyone in so long. I think I might have forgotten how. Deville tried to shut the voice out again, but again the attempt was futile. No, no, wait, the Devil mused, ah . . . There we go. It's all coming back to me. Let's do this . . . Horrifying images filled Deville's head. Horrifying enough to make even him feel nauseous. Focussing his attention on the man pointing the gun at him, Deville took a menacing step forward. "Please don't kill us," the wife wailed. The husband shot her a withering look. "I have the gun, not him!" "Shut up," Angelo instructed in an overly casual tone. "Good. Now listen. Here's how it's going to go, idiot. You've already sealed your own fate, but it's not too late for your poor wife. If you do as I ask, I promise I will only kill you, and I'll do it as quickly and painlessly as possible. Your wife will continue on without you, and in my humble opinion, she'll have a much better life." Nice, Satan commended. What are we going to do? There was an anxious silence. When Beelzebub spoke again, his voice oozed suspicion. . . . How are you blocking it from me? The man blinked hard and barked a coarse laugh. "I heard you were crazy, but this is too much. Don't you see this fucking gun? I'm the one in control here, buddy. You're the sacrificial lamb. If you think you're going to get in the way of my destiny, I'll shoot you in the head right here and now." The man's eyes twinkled. "You're worth almost as much dead as you are alive." "Funny you should mention it, because that's my condition." Deville smiled at the cloud of confusion hanging over both their faces, and doubly at the bewilderment echoed in his mind. "Shoot me in the head right here and now, and your wife will live. You'll still die, of course, but there's no way out of that no matter what you do." "What?" What!? Deville sighed, taking another step forward. A bare two feet separated him from his abductor now. "Shoot me now, or you both die." Silence dominated for a long moment, except in Deville's head. It was never quiet in there. Oh, you dumb sonofabitch. Don't you dare put us through this again. I fucking swear on the soul of that slut Mary . . . Obviously shaken by the strange twist of events, the man looked at his wife, clearly not wanting to put a bullet in his sack of cash. With his eyes averted, he was caught completely off-guard when Deville's arm bolted out and grabbed the gun, his hand crushing the man's against the steel. The man yelped and tried to wrestle it away, but Deville held his arm and the gun in place effortlessly. . . . you fucking do it. I'll make you eat your balls over and over again for eternity. I'll . . . The Deville's eyes swept down to the woman, full of sympathy. "I'm sorry," he told her, then spoiled the moment with a wink. "I tried to give you a chance." . . . pitchforks up your ass like a motherfucker, you inbred son of a . . . Grinning, The Deville pushed in on the man's protesting finger, depressing the trigger and blowing himself away.
