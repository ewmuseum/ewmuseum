Date: 2004-10-23 You actually thought I was going to kill him, didn't you? Hearing the hurt, almost offended timbre in Satan's viscous voice, Deville wanted to spit. Up until Satan hijacked that young girl's life, that was exactly what Deville thought his intentions were, and Satan had certainly said and done nothing to dissuade him of that opinion. Even if he had, there was an underlying paranoia about taking anything The Devil said or did at face value. Angelo had already done that once, and he was paying the price of his folly with every passing second. Of course I did, Deville snapped, it's not like you've given me any reason to put such a thing beyond you. Satan actually gasped, as if stricken. Angelo could picture him pressing a dainty hand to his heart in mock affront. I've been nothing short of cordial, considering the circumstances. I know you think you're getting the short end of the stick right now, but in every successful relationship there must be give and take. I gave you the life you so desperately needed, and now I'm merely taking a taste for myself. I'd say that's fair, wouldn't you? Angelo fumed in silence. Before this body-sharing business, you and I always got along so well, Angelo. I'd even go so far as to say we were friends. Sincerity pulsed with every word, and Satan actually cleared his throat before continuing in a sheepish voice. I know you're not feeling it right now, but I hope we still are. Right, Deville growled. Of course. The two bosom buddies drove on for a while in silence. Or at least without conversation. Satan's thoughts swirled a mile and minute, all images and declarations of his friendship for Angelo. Listening in disgustedly, knowing the charade was for his benefit, Deville couldn't place his finger on what Satan was up to. For the last half hour, he hadn't taken his eyes from the road once, making it next to impossible for Angelo to discern where they were, or where they were headed. Where are we going? The suspicious demand interrupted Satan's harmonious thoughts, slicing them neatly in half, but it had absolutely no effect on The Devil's chipper mood. It's a surprise, he responded giddily. Deville didn't like the sound of that. He'd never heard of Satan unleashing a pleasant surprise. What kind of surprise? Satan tsked. I can't spoil it. It'd ruin the fun. That wouldn't be fair to either of us. Just be content knowing it's a surprise for you, and it's something I've been wanting to do for a while now. Deville was about to prod him for more information, hoping to wriggle free some piece of information he might be able to use or plan for, but Satan's attention was suddenly diverted. His eyes were no longer on the road. They were taking in a large sign off to one side, a monstrous poster spread across two second-story windows of a large farmhouse tucked back a good distance from the road. The massive front lawn was made up like a graveyard, with cardboard tombs jutting up from the grass and bedsheet ghosts flapping in the wind as they hung from orange-leaved trees. An assortment of jack-o- lanterns grinned at them from the porch. The sign, which had Satan riveted and at least a little bit angry, was what made Angelo's figurative stomach drop. "Nightmare on Rail St." it announced in stylish black script, and right underneath that, in red lettering wreathed by flame, "Scarier than Hell!" If that had been the end of it, Deville wouldn't have cared, and he definitely wouldn't have been worried. If that had been the end of it, he imagined Satan would not have slammed on the brakes and peeled into the driveway. Unfortunately, that wasn't the end of it. Beside the two proclamations, taking up one full window, was a large caricature of The Devil himself. Not a depiction of the awe-inspiring fire-breathing Devil that graced most Hallowe'en advertisements, but a hastily drawn picture of a wrinkled up goat, with skin more pink than red, wearing an idiot's leer and a large brown diaper with a whole team of green stink lines pushing away in all directions. Motherfucker, was all Satan said directly to Deville, but his thoughts were a whirlwind of death and destruction and pain. Normally he liked his holiday as much as any other beastie it celebrated, but when people starting taking liberties with his reputation, tactlessly insulting him for their own menial gain, things were getting out of hand. Apparently he had left the world of the living alone for too long, and people were now getting above themselves. He'd have to remind them what terror truly was. ~><>...<><~ Walking up to the haunted house arm in arm, the gravel drive grinding softly beneath their shoes, Antoine and his girlfriend read the sign aloud in unison, her voice full of child-like wonder and anticipation, his of tired patience. She squeezed his arm and giggled as they ascended the steps to the front door, then gave a little squeal of delight as he grabbed ahold of the latch. "Honey," he said exhaustively, as if about to launch an argument he knew he wouldn't win for the hundredth time, "this stuff is for kids. It ain't scary." Pushing down the latch, he paused to look at her before opening the door. "For me, at least. This ghost and devil shit is nothing compared to the streets. Belie' dat." She stuck out her lower lip and slouched. "I just want to check it out. I haven't been in one of these things since I was ten." Antoine sucked his teeth audibly and sighed. Eyeing his girlfriend dubiously, he wondered what they were doing together. They had very little in common, and the only reason he started banging her in the first place was to exact revenge on a friend of his who had fooled around with his old girlfriend. Somehow, that had blossomed into a romance, albeit a distant one, and now the product of their lust was growing in her belly. He wasn't sure he was ready to be a father; could he really put the extravagant life of slinging crack and knocking off two-bit junkies behind? He doubted it. The Life really was quite sweet. But who knew what would happen? He now had a child on the way, a little piece of magic that would be looking to him for strength and guidance. He had an abundance of that, at least. Though he had never liked children -- they were so arrogant and condescending -- he thought he might enjoy being a father. Perhaps even a husband. Maybe if his girlfriend popped out a half dozen more of his kids, he'd break down and marry her. If she was lucky. There were plenty of other women out there in need of his attention, and begging for it daily. Pulling up his pants, which had slid halfway down his buttcrack, he grinned and pushed the door inward, waiting for her to lead the way into the unknown darkness. Casanova had nothing on him. The entryway opened up into a large room that must have taken up the entire first floor. What had once been an open concept kitchen-dinette was now stripped bare of all modern furnishings, with no evidence whatsoever of fridges or stoves or couches. In their stead, fold-away tables adorned with labelled jars containing things like "bat brains" and "frog intestines" dominated the middle of the room, and fake cobwebs obscured every corner. A pair of stuffed witches hung from the ceiling by ill- concealed string, mouths open wide in a perpetual cackle as they straddled their brooms. A mummy's sarcophagus, glittering in the dimness with cheap jewellery, leaned against the bannister of the only stairway. "The Funhouse", the maniacal theme song from the horror movie of the same name, blasted from speakers hidden around the room. Off to the left, built into the wall out of brick and imitation marble, was a square fireplace large enough for several people to hang out in comfortably. A large cauldron was on its side just in front of it, having apparently been roughly knocked out of the alcove not long before. The contents of the cauldron, chunks of meat and potatoes dyed red from the thick broth they had simmered in, littered the floor in a sea of what looked like blood. A thick bed of embers still burned fiercely, gradually roasting a large piece of meat that was turning on a makeshift spit. The large piece of meat was actually a man, stripped down to his boxers and hogtied. His tortured screams rose and fell in waves, adding authenticity to the soundtrack and drawing the attention of the visitors right away. His face was pale yet blackened. All of his hair had been singed off, and the skin underneath was now blistering and peeling. His belly and chest were already cooked a light shade of brown, and every time the spit slowly turned him facedown, one or two droplets of grease would splash down on the embers with an angry hiss that somehow managed to make itself heard over the other noise. On the other side of the room, nailed to the wall as if crucified, was a naked woman drenched in blood. There were cuts visible all over her ravaged body, but the one that stood out the strongest was the dark jagged line that stretched all the way across her lower abdomen, zigzagging from one hip to the other as if the incision had been made with deliberate slowness. In a pile on the floor beneath her rested her innards, her large intestine stretching up to where it was still attached inside of her, like a lifeline to her lost organs. Her screams had long been exhausted. Clenching her hand around her nose, trying to block out the awful smell of cooking flesh and excrement, Antoine's girlfriend shot him a look that said something wasn't quite right. But Antoine wasn't paying attention. He was mesmerized by the man on the spit, regarding him with wide-eyed wonder. He moved forward slowly, like in a dream, raising a shaky hand to point uncertainly at what he was seeing. "Th-that . . ." Suddenly remembering to maintain his macho pretence, Antoine cleared his throat and made himself stand up straight. He looked back with a weak smile that did nothing to mask the fear bubbling within. "That almost looks real." When his girlfriend nodded her worried agreement, he added another hasty "Almost." "W-what's it made out of?" she asked in a high-pitched voice, scurrying forward to latch onto Antoine's arm for protection. "Dunno." Antoine took as hesitant step forward, pretending he wanted to investigate, but luckily she held him back, whimpering pleadingly when he looked at her. He barked a nervous laugh. "Don't be scared, honey. I won't let anything happen to you." Her smile said she hoped that was true. "Kill me!" came a delirious shout from the man on the spit. Suddenly he was shaking and writhing, straining against his restraints, trying to get free. Antoine and his woman both jumped at the outburst, then watched with barely contained panic until the man quieted down to hopeless sobbing. "Please kill me," he begged the air, "please." He never let the last word end, dragging it out like a siren that grew gradually louder and louder, morphing into a scream. "Fuckin' crazy white people," Antoine mused, looking all around the room again, completely oblivious to the glare that earned him from his caucasian girlfriend. "This shit is messed up. Seen enough yet?" he asked derisively, eyeing his girlfriend as if she was a lunatic. She nodded hurriedly. "Let's get out of here. Now." Neither of them saw the dark man appear on the stairwell as they turned to leave, or the dark smile that played across his angular face when he caught sight of them. If they had, they likely would have run screaming, all their careful illusions of bravery abandoned in the face of pure evil. "Sorry," the figure called without any hint of apology in his voice. "Didn't hear you come in." They both jumped again, then turned back around to goggle at the curator of this mad house. His face was veiled by a welcoming smile when they saw it, a sharp contrast from half a moment before. He floated down the stairs like a ghost, his black silk suit fluttering in a wind that wasn't there, and approached them with a steadily widening grin. "Welcome to my humble abode." Antoine gave his girlfriend a warning look, then positioned himself protectively in front of her. "Uh, yeah," he muttered, unconsciously tugging at his dreadlocks. "Nice place you got here." "You like it?" The man in the suit asked, suddenly beaming. "Why thank you. I was just in the middle of redecorating." To their horror, he gestured to the fireplace and the woman hanging on the wall. "Who are you?" the girl squeaked. "I, my dear," he offered haughtily, dipping into a slight bow, "am The Deville. Pleased to make your acquaintance." Antoine moved his hands behind himself and began walking backwards, pushing his girlfriend along ahead of him. Over his shoulder, out of the corner of his mouth, he whispered, "this dude's a nut. Let's get the fuck outta here." "Leaving so soon?" The Deville queried innocently. Both of their heads bobbed back and forth hurriedly. "Pity. I was just about to serve supper." Again gesturing towards the man on the spit, who was still emitting his siren of suffering, he slowly licked his lips. "And there's always room for more," he added ambiguously. "Nah man," Antoine snapped, then forced a laugh. "We, uh, we got other shit to do." The Deville took a step forward. "Surely one bite wouldn't hurt." His smile had degenerated to a baring of his teeth. "Stay the fuck away from us." Antoine yelled, then cast a panicked look over his shoulder to see how far from the door they were. Not far. About five steps. "Scared?" "Fuck no," Antoine sneered. "I seen . . ." Swallowing hard, Antoine forced the words to come out, despite no longer believing them. "I've seen way worse than this." There was no hesitation or falseness in The Deville's voice. "Me too." Arriving at the door, Antoine turned and reached around his girlfriend to free them from this nightmare. He seized the handle and yanked hard, but the door only budged a crack. Desperately, he tried again, with the same terrifying result. "Let us out!" he screamed without looking back, rattling the door like a madman. His girlfriend, dancing from foot to foot beside him, was shaking like the only dildo at a lesbian slumber party. "No." "Let us the fuck out! Now!" Spinning around, pulling a nine from his loose waistband, Antoine pointed it at The Deville's face. "Don't make me fuckin' kill you, cracka! Let us the fuck out!" The Deville only laughed. "Cracka?" he mimicked. "Cracka? Am I supposed to be offended by that?" Antoine cocked the gun, to show he meant business, but Angelo went on as if he hadn't noticed, or simply didn't care. "Oooooh, I see. You think it means I'm soft and brittle, like a soup cracker." His lilting laughter was entirely out of place, not only for the surroundings but for the situation as well. "How wrong you are," he intoned menacingly, stepping closer to Antoine and the gun. "We're not called crackers because we crumble in your hands. Quite the contrary, my black friend. My ancestors earned that nickname from your people because we're the ones calling the shots. I'm the one in charge here. When I want you to do something -- kwa-tsch! -- I crack my whip, and you bloody well do what I say. So yeah, call me cracka all you want. It just reaffirms my authority." Angelo was but a pace away from Antoine and the door. He took the final step forward and pushed his forehead against the barrel of the gun. "Now either you drop that gun, or you prove yourself more than I think you are and use it." "Fuck you, man! Leave us alone!" The Deville smirked. "Scared?" he asked again. The hysterical response came in the form of a gun shot, aimed at the center of The Deville's head. The girlfriend screamed, taking up Antoine's place yanking at the door, as Antoine waved the thin cloud of smoke out of his face. When it cleared, he screamed as well. Angelo was still standing there, grinning, his forehead clean and unscathed. "You missed." Antoine started yelling another obscenity as he brought the gun back up, but when Deville snatched it from his hand, the word was stolen as well. Angelo chucked the weapon across the room, then shoved Anthony hard, knocking him so he sandwiched his girlfriend between himself and the door. "How about now?" The Deville whispered, the question barely audible over the demented carnival sounds. "You scared yet?" Antoine, defiant until the end, screamed that he wasn't. "Funny," Angelo said pensively, a serrated hunting knife materializing in his hand. "Usually people are scared out of their skin at this point." He flashed the blade menacingly, deftly twirling it between his fingers. "Here. Let me help you out of yours." ~><>...<><~ Back on the road, Satan didn't want to talk about the horrific things he had just done to all those clueless people in the haunted house. Angelo didn't want to talk about it either, but given a choice between that and the subject Satan was presently on, Deville would choose to relive that horror every single time. I'm sorry to say I don't think you'll like the surprise, my boy, Satan divulged, half-apologetically, half-taunting. But you've certainly got it coming to you. He had already told Deville that they were on their way, and it wouldn't be long. Now he was abusing him by hinting at the details. At least he wasn't looking only at the road anymore. Trying to ignore him, Deville focused on the blurry landscape whizzing by out the window, trees and houses and corner stores he thought he might have recognized. Tell me, Satan kept on, his voice still happy and unconcerned. Do you like being able to read my thoughts? Angelo hated it more than anything else in the world, and he told Satan so. That's too bad. Angelo cringed and held his breath as they whipped past his junior high school, worried that Satan might pull in to re-visit his old mentor. Thankfully, they sped right on by; at least that wasn't Satan's surprise. Me? I loved it when our situations were reversed, tuning into your darkest, innermost thoughts. Gave me a thrill. Deville didn't know where this was leading, but an icicle of worry replaced his normally titanium backbone. He let the landscape float by unnoticed for a moment, waiting with baited breath for The Devil to make his point. I especially liked it when your thoughts turned to me, my friend, and all the delectably devilish things you were planning to do to me once you vanquished me from your mind. The good-naturedness had fled from his voice. Malevolence burned there now. You're really quite creative when properly motivated. The Deville waited, petrified, trying to call up all of the things he had promised himself he would do to The Devil. There were too many, and some were so hideous he wasn't sure he wanted to remember, on the off-chance that Satan didn't as well; there was no sense giving the Lord of Darkness any bright ideas. Cowering in the back of his mind, he concentrated on emptiness, trying not to think. I wish things could continue as they are, The Devil went on ruefully, but because of those thoughts, because I know you still harbour them as hopes, they simply cannot. With a rising sense of dread, Angelo realized where he was. He recognized every house that went by, every tree, and even some of the potholes in the road were familiar. He had seen this same street countless times before, on trips back from the zoo, or rides back from the grocery store, or school, or football and wrestling practice. He recognized it from the golden memories of his youth, could describe every nuance of the neighbourhood in his sleep. After the death of Angelo's father, he and his mother had moved to a cosy upper-class neighbourhood so they could be closer to other people and his mother could make "normal" friends. Angelo hated it at first, but gradually he made friends, and came to love everything about the neighbourhood, from the smell of barbecue in the summer to the way only one in five street lamps ever worked at the same time. Normally, this was Angelo's sanctuary, a place to go for safety and warmth, a utopia where he was always safe. It was the absolute last thing he wanted to see with Satan looking through his eyes. Images of it pillaged and burning roared up in his mind, but they paled beside the true worry, the real reason Deville once again found himself wishing he could cry. Please, Angelo begged, clawing at Satan's mind with imaginary hands, not my mother. Please no. Anything else. Please. The Devil sighed. I'm sorry, Angelo. I wish I could say it's been fun, that I want to continue sharing this life with you, but it's time for us both to move on. His cackle sounded just as Deville's old house came into view down the road. It's time for me to drive you out of my head.
