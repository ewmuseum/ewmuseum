Part 2: Nowhere To

8 November 2009

Date: 2009-11-08 The world is a dangerous place. We put up defenses to protect ourselves from these dangers. These defenses are called "walls." Some people have put up walls in every direction, as well as one overhead. This is called a prison. # # # Over the course of his career, Wyatt Connors had achieved a level of success enjoyed by very few. He had won championships, crushed enemies, amassed a small fortune, and had his name cursed by millions of people who paid for the privilege. There were not a lot of people who could say the same. Those that could generally lived in large houses, surrounded by loved ones and symbols of their wealth and success. They did not live in small rooms inside office buildings, shielded from the outside world by distance and paranoia. Generally. "Note Forty-Five." Wyatt sat on the edge of the single-size bed, next to his carefully-laid suit jacket. He leaned forward, his elbows resting on his knees, and spoke sharply into the small tape recorder in his hand. "I am becoming increasingly concerned with Trevor's behavior. He took some personal leave before the San Antonio event, and he's been...a bit off since. I have pried, but he refuses to discuss the matter. I will let it lie for now, but I must deal with it sooner rather than later. His attention span is laughable on a good day; if he loses any more focus, he will be useless to me." He stood up, taking a moment to smooth out his white shirt and blue tie, before he continued. "Deacon's performance is unchanged. As long as I am fairly specific with my request, he can be expected to do the job. He never seems to take initiative, which is unfortunate. Also, he is developing an absurd interest in my personal matters. Were it anyone else, I would suspect an ulterior motive; with Deacon, I assume he is merely attempting to fulfill his role as a faithful toady." "I am hoping to insert them into the SCCW title chase on some level soon, they have performed well in most situations, especially in my recent match with Amelia Campbell. They deserve to be rewarded, not that I'll tell them so directly. The last thing I need is for Trevor to have a larger attitude problem." Wyatt shook out his left hand--he was not shivering--and paused the recorder. Laying the device down on the bed, he then took his suit jacket down from the hanger and quickly slipped it on. Connors grabbed the device once more, and resumed recording. "The political climate in Sin City has changed a great deal in recent weeks. Amelia Campbell is trying to change her stripes, which I find quite amusing. It will probably work with the fans; they forget the past quite easily. Her fellow wrestlers, however, have long memories. It will be interesting to see how seriously Desade takes this new approach, as well." "Dusk has returned, now with followers. Time will tell if they can be considered a true threat, their recent attack notwithstanding. One of them is Mayweather, who I recently defeated in this GTT that everyone is making such a fuss over. Perhaps she's come for revenge...I wonder if my next opponent will do the same, should I defeat him. I have little information on Draeden Darksky, but from what I've learned, he is--" Connors' speech was interrupted by a slamming door, and muffled voices. "My henchmen have arrived. Will continue note later." With that, he pressed the stop button, and set the recorder back on the bed. He checked his tie in the mirror--one must look professional, even in front of one's underlings--and opened the door into his office. As he did, he caught the tail end of a conversation. "--telling you, I don't give a shit," the smaller one said. He wore a brown leather jacket and a black hoodie, which was probably to separate the leather from his shirt stains. His long hair was tied back in a sloppy ponytail. "He don't care about us, or he wouldn't have let us get punked out by Dusk and them other turds. So if he wants to live in a box, scared of his own shadow all the time, then fuck it--let him." He might have gone on, were it not for the bigger man giving him a smack on the shoulder and a thumb in the general direction of their employer. "Gentlemen," Wyatt began. "It's about time you got here." Deacon Dale showed his watch to Connors. "But we're ten minutes early." "Hmm? Oh. Well...good. Finally showing some initiative. Good work. Well, we might as well get started." Wyatt took a seat behind his desk, while the other two sat in plastic chairs. T.J. Ratigan wiped his hand on his pants, leaving a smear of chocolate. Deacon stuffed his hands into the pockets of his navy blue Denver Broncos coat. "Why do you have your coats on?" Connors asked. Ratigan snapped, "Because someone's too much of a cheap douche to turn up the heat." Wyatt accepted the answer, ignored the insult, and started the briefing. "As you know, there are a lot of important matters to discuss. There are several new arrivals in SCCW, plus there are many of the same issues as before--Pierce, Campbell, Marshall, et cetera. I think it best if we were to--" He was interrupted as Deacon Dale raised one of his large arms in the air. "What." It was less a question, and more an accusation. "You're not still living in that tiny room back there, are you?" Deacon asked. The scowl on Connors' face told him it was the wrong question to ask...and just in case, a healthy dose of abuse would get the point across. "That is none of your fucking business, Deacon." Yet, the big man of the Conspiracy refused to let the matter go. "I think it is, kinda. I mean, I don't want to see you flake out and completely bail on us, like you did in the WWA." "I appreciate that you're trying to think, Deacon. But maybe you should practice at home a little more before you show the teacher, hmm?" Dale may have had a point, but of course Wyatt Connors would never admit that. Dale raised his voice a little--not much; he still remembered who was in charge--but he was clearly getting agitated. "Besides, I thought you said you weren't going to do this secret crazy shit anymore. We jump through your hoops all the goddamn time, I think you owe us something." "Oh, you do?" Wyatt stood up and focused his annoyed glare not on Deacon, but on T.J. "Trevor? Would you care to tell us what you have been up to for the last few weeks?" The question was scarcely out of Wyatt's mouth before Ratigan barked a response of, "Nope." The Scorpion turned back toward Deacon, but now with one hand pointed at Trevor, as if offering proof. "There. If he doesn't have to tell me, and I'm the boss...then I don't have to tell you shit. Clear?" Deacon grumbled in acknowledgment. He didn't like it, but he could tell the situation wasn't going to go any further. "Now then. If we can get back on track? Thank you. The most pressing item is the second-round GTT match, against Draeden Darksky. Information has been scarce, but it has been said he has impressive martial arts skill. Now, who among us can tell me how to deal with an opponent with extensive martial arts training?" "Kick him in the nose, throat, and kidneys," Trevor offered. "Ah, the Karate Kid method," Wyatt answered. "Well-known, but effective." "And the balls, too." "Thank you, Trevor." Deacon raised his hand again. Wyatt frowned, but nodded. "Yes, Deacon?" "Is that a new tie?" Deacon asked. Connors' face scrunched up, as if he had just taken a bite out of a lemon. "Would it kill you to stay on topic? Even Trevor is doing a better job than you." "Well...you only ever wore the red one before...and I don't see anything sticky on it, so I know T.J. ain't touched it yet." "Hey!" "Just...wondering if it was new, that's all." The big man felt a little sheepish...it was a strange question, after all, and the boss was already in a bad mood. But it just looked weird, and Deacon figured it was better to get it out of the way now, rather than let it bug him all night. Connors looked down and shook his head in exasperation...but oddly enough, the necktie did catch his eye. "No...I've had it for some time..." And he remembered. # # # January 4, 2002. He had been sitting on the leather sofa for half an hour, watching the double doors. Every once in a while, he'd adjust his light blue necktie, to make sure it still looked professional. He was not fidgeting. Wyatt Connors did not fidget. Connors leaned back slightly on the couch, and his eyes darted around the room as he impatiently waited for his summons. The lobby felt as if expense and tackiness had a child--expensive furniture, flowery plants, and the same three letters emblazoned on a dozen different surfaces. He hoped the office itself was better, but he wasn't about to hold his breath. A secretary sat behind a large desk, clicking away at a keyboard. At least the man knows how to hire a secretary, he thought. At least eighty words per minute. Clean desk. Reasonably attractive; not so much that she can get away with incompetence. The last month had gone somewhat poorly for Connors. He had been stripped of the PUW Heavyweight Title, due to his duties as NWC World Champion. Just a few days later, he lost the World Title in only his second defense, and to a far less capable opponent than those he had beaten. Still, he had managed to parlay these events to his advantage. His World Title reign had gotten him noticed. The other belt had gotten him this meeting. A light beep came from something on the secretary's desk, and she pushed a button to silence it. "Mr. Van Iten will see you now," she said. He nodded courteously at her, and walked toward the double doors with three large gold letters on them. B. V. I. As Wyatt opened the doors, the first thing he saw was a shiny gold belt with his own name on it. The leather strap strained around a large waist, completely failing to cover an exposed stomach. A glance upward found a massive frame stuffed into a far-too-small shirt depicting the Snorks, and the kind of smile that can only be made by someone who is cataclysmically stupid. The retarded behemoth bodyguard. Of course he'd be here. The big man looked down and said, "You're a poop." As Wyatt cocked an eyebrow, the man's face brightened. He thrust his hands into the air and shouted, "HAHAHA I CUT A PROMO ON WYATT CONNORS! FADE TO BLACK!" "Shut up, Chester." Chester hunched over a little, deflated by the rebuke that came from inside. He slowly backed away from the doors, allowing Wyatt to enter the office. It was better than he expected, but not much. There was a wet bar along the wall on the left, with a liquor selection to rival most drinking establishments. A large walnut desk filled the center of the room. Rested upon it were a cigar box, a telephone, and a coffee mug that read, "#1 Boss." The wall on the right was covered with assorted framed pictures, all with the same subject. One man, gaining victories over the NWC's best and brightest. In the center, a series of wall-mounted display cases, each one containing a replica of the NWC World Heavyweight Title. "One for each initial," according to his t-shirts. There, next to his personal shrine, was the man himself. The face of the Uppermost Echelon, Mr. Razz & Pizzazz, the man who "=SCCW," Ben Van I. Van Iten faced away from Connors, and bent over slightly. He was staring at something on the floor, and did not bother to look up as he addressed his visitor. "I see you got my message." "I did," Wyatt answered. "The singing telegram was a nice touch." "It was, wasn't it." He made a quick motion that was obscured by his body. A small white ball emerged from around Van Iten's foot, rolled across the green carpet, and into an overturned glass on the other side of the room. "Ha!" Ben Van I. lifted up a solid gold putter and rested it over his shoulder, before turning to face Connors. He offered a hand, which Connors shook weakly. "Sorry to keep you waiting so long. Needed to see if this new carpet really helps my putting. Cigar?" "No, thank you," Connors replied. "Suit yourself." Van Iten sat behind his desk and opened the humidor. He plucked a cigar out of the box--Cuban Montecristo? Nice.--and clipped off the end. "By the way, thank you for the gift. As you can see, Chester's taken quite a liking to it." Wyatt took a brief look at the Tower of Mental Power, whose idiot grin spread out across his face. "I see. Well, I'm just glad to see it in the hands of someone who deserves it." Ben chuckled as he lit his cigar. "Hey, Chaz? Why don't you go outside and play for a while? Got some business to take care of." "'K," Chester said, and lumbered out into the lobby. Wyatt shut the doors behind him, and then sat down in a chair across the desk from his host. "All kidding aside," Van Iten began, "we've had our eye on you for a while. You've been raising some hell down in that craphole company you work for." "I try. Too bad there's no challenge to it. Most of the people in that company aren't fit to run the concession stand, let alone wrestle." "Too bad, really. I was hoping they could put up some kind of fight against us at the next big show." Connors laughed without humor as he recalled the announcement--the main event for Friday Night Fury 5 would see three-man teams from PUW and the UME stable face off, with the winners representing their faction at the Civil Wargames event. "Maybe if they sent the whole roster after you. Three of theirs against three of yours? That's not a wrestling match; that's a joke. But then, so is PUW." Van Iten smiled. He seemed terribly amused, if unsurprised, at Connors' opinions. "I figured it was bad when they announced their team. Was that the best they could come up with?" "Just about. The only reason I'm not involved with that debacle is because I was the World Champion when they chose sides. Fine by me; I've got no interest in fighting battles I can't win." Ben nodded as he flipped open a steel Zippo and used it to light his cigar. Jesus. He's even got his initials on that. "I think that's what makes this last stunt that much better. Your boss picks a fight with us, and you send me the heavyweight title as a Christmas gift. Genius." Connors grinned with self-satisfaction. "Like I said...I try." "But c'mon. You can't tell me you just did this to wind up your boss, right? You want something." Van Iten blew a few smoke rings as he waited for the answer. There it was. After the ritual backpatting, it was time to get down to business. The man is a slave to his ego, Connors thought. Feed it. "In short, Mr. Van Iten..." "Don't be so formal. Call me...Mr. Pizzazz." Wyatt afforded himself a brief snicker before finishing his thought. "In short, Mr. Van Iten...I want in." "I'm not surprised. The Uppermost Echelon...hell, we ARE the NWC. But we're not really accepting applications right now, you know? Besides, you don't really strike me as the t-shirt-wearing type." Connors shook his head. "If that was what I wanted, I'd have talked to Devin; I've worked with him in the past. No...I've heard a rumor here and there that you've got something in the works...something people are going to be talking about for years. That's what I want in on." "I'm afraid I don't know what you're talking about." Ben was obviously lying, and judging by his tone, didn't care whether Wyatt knew it or not. "Don't be modest, Ben. You've already done everything there is to do in the Council. You've won top regional titles several times. You've headlined regional and Council pay-per-views. You won the Epic tournament. Three NWC World Championships--no one can match that. You're part of the most powerful stable in Council history, and there's no way you're going to play second fiddle to Jason Oswald much longer. There's nothing left that's worth your time...but you're still here. Why is that, unless you've got something big in the works?" Ben Van I. said nothing for a while, contentedly puffing on his cigar and blowing more smoke rings. When he finally spoke, he did so with a cocky smirk. "Gotta hand it to you, Wise Guy Wyatt Connors. You're just as smart as Devin says you are." Connors smiled pleasantly, even though he suspected that was an insult. Devin Fox was notorious for not complimenting the abilities of anyone not named Devin Fox. "I'll put it like this. Describe the state of PUW in three words." Connors didn't even have to think about it. "Jesus fucking Christ." "Exactly. Thing is, most of the other regions aren't that much better. Tri-State's a mess; they couldn't get people to come to their shows if they handed out free hookers with every ticket. Sin City is failing, thanks to all the big names they've signed. Plus, anyone who shows up has to deal with us, and you can imagine how well that goes." "About as well as it will for the vaunted Team PUW?" "Not quite that bad, but you get the point. Hawaii's doing all right, but that's not going to last with Saotome's old fuck-toy in charge. The only place that's running strong is Liberty City, and...well, let's just say that we've got some influence there." He winked at the Wise Guy, so as to convey a Secret Meaning. A takeover? Wyatt thought, but of course he didn't say it aloud. You don't step on someone's lines when they're revealing their big plan; it just isn't done. "So...what, then. You're going to kill off the Council?" "Kill it?" Van Iten asked incredulously, and stood up from his chair. "The status quo--that's what's killing the Council. These regions, with their general managers that are all ego and no intelligence...they're the ones who are going to drive this place out of business. Unless they start losing money quickly, and all at once, so that it's either sell out or go bankrupt..." Van Iten was obviously leading him, so Connors chose that moment to take the bait. "Leaving you to buy them up for a song." "Exactly!" Ben exclaimed. "I knew there was reason they called you Wise Guy Wyatt Connors, Wise Guy Wyatt Connors." "You're going to corner the market on the NWC. That's...brilliant." "It is. It's going to be like that Eddie Murphy movie, except with less Jamie Lee Curtis. Or more, who knows. I bet she needs the money. So, what do you say?" "Let's see. It's going to be a challenge. It's going to be lucrative. It allows me the chance to work with Ben Van I. How can I say no?" "Glad to hear it, pal. Otherwise I'd have Chester bury you in the garden at home, and I don't want him finding his old puppy, y'know? Now, let's seal it with a drink. What's your poison?" "Have scotch?" "'Have scotch?'" Ben repeated. "Buddy, I've got more scotch than Three-Fucking-M." He walked--bounded, almost--to the bar, where he quickly selected a bottle and two glasses. He poured a generous amount into each, and handed one to the Wise Guy. "A toast! Here's to..." "Here's to BVI Equals NWC," Wyatt suggested, and watched the smile grow on Ben Van I.'s face. "Perfect," he said, and they clinked glasses. Connors sipped his, savoring the taste--Macallan, twenty-five years old--while Ben drank half the glass in one shot. Van Iten threw an arm around Connors' shoulder, and the Wise Guy had to resist the urge to shrug it away. He had grown accustomed to fighting, but most other forms of physical contact made him slightly uncomfortable. "It's going to be beautiful, Wise Guy Wyatt Connors. The whole Council, united by one vision. My vision." "And then what?" Ben thought for a moment, and then wagged his finger at Connors. "Oh, no. You don't find that out yet. Only I get to know everything; I'm the one in charge." # # # An hour later, after hashing out a few more details and drinks, Wyatt Connors left the office of Mr. Van Iten on good terms and in high spirits. The secretary had called for a cab, which was waiting for him by the time he left the building. "Damn, she is good," Connors noted as he approached the orange and white cab. He tossed a half-hearted wave at Chester, who was running in circles on the lawn, waving the PUW Heavyweight Title in the air, and yelling, "WHEEEEEEEEEEEEE!" Seven-foot man-child bodyguards had become quite the rage in the National Wrestling Council as of late...but even Wyatt had to admit, nothing beat the original. Connors slid into the back seat of the cab and said, "McCarran," before the driver had time to ask for a destination. "You got it, buddy," came the small voice from the front seat, and the cab pulled out into traffic. That caught Wyatt's attention, and he looked into the rearview mirror for a glimpse of her face. The driver was a young girl. The age was hard to pin down; it could be anywhere from fourteen to eighteen. "How old are you?" he asked. "C'mon, mister. You should know you never ask a lady that. What's the problem, you think I ain't old enough to drive a cab?" "You don't look old enough to have a driver's license," he quipped. "I try to keep out of the sun," she fired back. "But don't you worry, I know this town like the back of my hand. My dad was a cabbie all his life, an' I practically grew up in the front seat of one of these." Connors started to zone out as the driver continued her life story. He knew he shouldn't have had the first drink, let alone the third, but he needed Van Iten at ease. Wyatt hoped to dictate his own role within Van Iten's schemes, and to do so he would need to stay in the man's good graces. For the next few months, his primary role would be simple and easy: keep PUW from becoming a viable operation. The job was half-done already; since PUW's inception, Connors had a working agreement with his main rival, Dirk Hogan--their bitter feud was just an excuse to monopolize the main events. Since neither man had any sort of redeeming quality, the fans had no one to root for, which kept the interest down. As long as no big names signed with the company--and why would they?--Phoenix Underground had one foot in the grave already. "So anyway, we moved here from Frisco about ten years ago, and Dad's been showing me the ins and outs of this town since," the woman continued. Connors nodded absent-mindedly, giving no sign of the little red flag that went up in his mind. He casually looked out the window, checking for means of escape rather than scenery. If he were to open the door and dive out, he would probably survive...but he wouldn't play the violin for a while, that was for sure. The cabbie prattled on for a few more minutes, and eventually pulled the taxi up to the street. "Here we are, mister." "This does not look even remotely like an airport," he said dryly. Before the driver could offer an explanation, the street-side door opened, and a gorgeous pair of legs got into the cab, followed closely by an equally gorgeous body in a short, tight black dress. Wyatt greeted her with a curt, "Miss Shaw." Kathryn pouted a little. "Aww. I was hoping to surprise you." As the car pulled back out into traffic, Shaw set her purse on the floor, so that it would not obstruct Connors' view in any way. "The driver one of yours?" Wyatt asked. "She's not bad. The accent was good, but she needs work on her local dialect. Nobody from Frisco actually calls it that." Kat gave a cross look to the driver. "How many times do I have to tell you, Lauren? The City." "Sorry," came the sheepish reply. "We'll talk later," Shaw answered, and then turned her attention back to Wyatt. "Now then, Wyatt darling. I trust the meeting went well?" She could probably see the slight flush to his cheek, caused by the alcohol, but he chose to downplay his success anyway. "Reasonably well." "Ooh. Tell me all about it." "Not much to tell. We talked about how awful Phoenix is, and he gave me my official 'I Beat Hunter Sabuani Club' membership card." Katsidy's lips curled into a frown, although Wyatt couldn't quite tell if she was being playful, or if she was actually annoyed. "I do hope you're not holding out on me, Wyatt." She sidled closer to him, and crossed her right leg over her left, so that her foot rubbed against Wyatt's shin. "I can play the bad cop, but being the good cop is soooooo much more fun." Wyatt answered--and, in a heroic display of willpower, managed not to salivate while doing so--"Only having a bit of fun. Yes, Ben put out a line, and yes, I took the bait." "Good boy," she cooed. "And what did we learn?" Connors thought about denying her the information, and about possible escape routes...but ones again, difficulties with stringed instruments came to mind. "It's actually quite clever," he answered. "He wants to devalue the individual regions so that he can buy them up." "Oh, Ben. He always was the clever one. I'm sure he has enough money to do it, too. Very good work, Wy-Guy." She put her hand on his chest, and ran her fingers up and down his necktie. With her serpent-hiss whisper, she said, "I think you deserve a...special reward." Maintaining concentration became increasingly difficult, so it came as a bit of a relief when Katsidy backed off and reached into her purse. Focus, you stupid bastard, he chided himself. Don't let her get in your head. Better men have ruined themselves for a piece of ass. Her posterior wiggled slightly when she straightened back up, almost as if she knew what he was thinking. When she turned back around, she was holding something close to her chest. It was a long, slender manila envelope. "For you," she said, and offered it to him. Thankful for something else to look at, he opened one end. Inside the envelope was a rather substantial stack of hundred dollar bills. Ten grand, or close to it, he thought, and couldn't help but crack a smile. "Just a little present. We'd like you to play along with Ben for a while. Keep up on what he does, who he sees. And keep us informed, of course. There will be plenty more envelopes like that one...and if all goes well, later you can make some real money." Wyatt's ears perked up a little, as avarice succeeded where lust (narrowly) failed. "I think I'm going to enjoy doing business with you, Miss Shaw," Connors said. "It has definitely been an enjoyable experience so far." "Same here, handsome. I think you're going to do just fine." He nodded politely, and then leaned forward, toward the driver. "If you could pull over here, please?" "Thought you wanted the airport?" Lauren asked, as she steered the car back toward the curb and slowed to a halt. "I'll find my own way, thanks." As he left the vehicle, he gave a slight bow of the head to the Sex Kitten. "Miss Shaw? Always a pleasure." "Until next time," she purred, and he closed the door behind him. Wyatt Connors tucked the envelope inside his jacket and walked two blocks in the opposite direction. From there, he flagged down another cab, who took him to a bus station. Two transfers later, he exited near Caesar's Palace, where he got a ticket for the monorail. He left the rail halfway down the line and, satisfied that nobody was following him, caught another cab that took him to the airport. He had to eat the cost of his original ticket to Phoenix and get another one, but that was a small price to pay for peace of mind. Ten years later, he still didn't have that peace of mind...but he wasn't dead, either, and that was good enough. # # # "So we're doing a 'you don't fuck with us, we don't fuck with you' thing, right?" Trevor asked. "Hmm?" Wyatt mumbled, before reality caught up with him. Somehow, he'd managed to go through an entire planning session on autopilot. "Umm...yes. That's right, Trevor." "Gotcha." Ratigan gave a thumbs-up. "So, that's it?" Connors, still not quite sure of what had been discussed, nodded with false certainty. "Yes. Just be sure to remember what we talked about; I will be asking questions later." "Fuckin' hell. High school was bad enough once." "Yeah, learning's pretty tough when you can't read," Deacon scoffed. Ratigan's answer was to give him the finger. "Read that, shithead." Connors took a look at the desk clock, and then out the window. Darkness had already fallen on Chicago, and there was still much to be done. "Gentlemen, I believe it's time to call it a night. I'll leave you to it." He leafed through a few papers that laid on the desk, while his charges shuffled toward the door. They paused before leaving, however, and had a hushed conversation. Wyatt couldn't hear it, but caught a few words. "Needs to leave" came up, as did "do some good." He definitely heard Trevor call him a "snarky dickhole." A moment later, Deacon Dale was standing in front of Wyatt's desk again. "Hey, boss?" he asked. "We were going to head downtown for a few beers, and we wanted to know--" "You wanted to know," Trevor corrected, and got a finger for his efforts. "We wanted to know if you wanna come along. Y'know. Get you out of here for once." Wyatt looked up from his work and stared at Deacon as if he were a dog that had just shit in the sugar bowl. "Seriously? Drinking piss beer, breathing stale, smoke-filled air, and associating with the dregs of society? What part of that do you think would appeal to me?" Dale shrugged. "Just an idea, man. Don't have to bite my head off." "Can we go?" T.J. whined. "There's beer that's not being drunk by me as we speak." "Yeah," Deacon answered, and rejoined his partner in crime. Wyatt kept an eye on them as they went to leave. He felt like he should abuse them further for the suggestion, but couldn't work up the energy. He just felt tired...so very, very tired. "Hell with it. Why not," he said, and stood up. "Let me get my coat." As the three of them walked down the stairs and into the cold night, their heavy footsteps covered the sound of another of Wyatt's cardinal rules being broken. "Always keep your guard up."
