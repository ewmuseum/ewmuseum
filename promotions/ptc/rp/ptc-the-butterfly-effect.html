The butterfly effect

19 September 2005

He tried to scream but the fear that enveloped his body with its icy grasp, the fear that had turned his knees to jelly and the blood in his veins to ice-water, had robbed his voice of all volume and all that escaped his mouth, wide open, a gaping maw, rictus like with lips white and drawn back to expose his nicotine stained teeth, was a snuffling whimper; an old man of ninety years who had just caught his sagging ball-sack in the rusty zipper of a too tight pair of old jeans would have made more noise than Darren Blake managed as his eyes clouded with a shimmering film of tears … not quite obscuring the sight in front of him, the vision that had reduced him to this quivering mess. Taking a gulp of air in through both his open mouth and his mucus filled nostrils Blake tried to draw the breath deep into his lungs so that he could again try to scream for help, knowing that only feet away there was a bar packed with men who would surely come to investigate the noise in the rest-room. All thoughts of rescue, all thoughts of screaming, were forgotten as a hand wrapped itself around his throat and squeezed, the thumb and forefinger placed so that they pinched the windpipe in just the right position and with just enough pressure that they cut off the flow of oxygen. A second hand, also covered by a soft leather glove, reached up and patted Blake on the cheek, almost as tenderly as a lover’s caress, the sound of leather rubbing against the two day stubble that covered his face sounding like sandpaper. “Don’t even think about it,” a voice whispered from less than a foot away from his face, the acrid smell of coffee pungent in his nose as the speaker continued, “it would be a mistake. Another mistake I should say.” The grip around his throat, vice-like, didn’t lessen as the other hand moved tenderly down his cheek and caressed his neck and then chest before dropping away from him. A whimper escaped Blake’s voice, but it was almost soundless as the lack of air started to make everything dance in front of his eyes. Little white stars were juxtaposed with flashes of darkness, as black as a moonless night but twice as frightening as he realised that he was starting to pass out. He tried to struggle, tried to take a step backwards, but his head connected with the solid wall behind him as his heel crunched on the broken light bulb underfoot. “Now, now, don’t make this any worse than it has to be my friend” the hand around his throat tightened it’s grip ever so slightly as its owner spoke, again almost tenderly. The pseudo-emotion was offset by the speaker’s actions as his free hand once more came into view and out of the corner of Blake’s eye he saw a small glint of reflected metal just as he felt the razor like edge of a blade slide against his exposed throat. The sensation of a hot stream of urine flowing down his left leg was the last thing that he felt as his bladder opened without his conscious control; the smell of his own hot piss escaping from his body as if he was a frightened two-year old wetting the bed was the last thing that his senses registered; as his eyes rolled back in his head, as darkness overwhelmed him, he could only think one thing … “How the fuck did I get here?” . . . . . . . . . . “Daddy, please can I have an ice-cream?” The early afternoon sunlight beat down on Darren Blake’s head with a constant, incessant fury. Already feeling delicate after a night filled with one too many beers and not enough sleep to dispel the alcohol from his blood stream, the pleading tone of his six-year old daughter’s voice sounded more like the grating of nails on a blackboard than it did a typical childish plea. Five years of marriage had ended in a divorce that not even the most optimistic of people could have called anything other than acrimonious. Katie, the bitch-queen herself, told the courts that she was sick and tired of every cent that she earned going to Darren’s bookies. She was sick and tired of waking up to find her bed empty and another piece of her jewellery pawned to pay of yet another loss at the tracks, or at the poker table. She was sick and tired of crying herself to sleep every night not knowing if her poor excuse for a husband was coming home or if she would have to open a body bag to identify him the next day. Most of all, though, she was sick and tired of him. The courts, of course, didn’t give a damn about his point of view, they just listened to her lawyer and she got everything. All he got was the visitation rights with his daughter once every two weeks, whether he liked it or not … and most of the time he didn’t. “Daddy, I said can I ha …” Julia said, tugging on his shirt and dislodging the hand that held his head up. With another groan Darren stared bleary-eyed at the girl that he had thought of as an angle the first time he had seen her. That had been nearly two days after she had been born, but of course a man had to wet his daughter’s head to celebrate didn’t he … it wasn’t his fault that the celebrations had got a little bit out of hand and he had woken up in that woman’s bed. Besides which it wasn’t like Julia would remember his absence. “I heard you baby, I heard you.” Darren said through clenched teeth as he felt a wave of nausea wash over him. Running his hand through his thinning and greasy hair he groaned as he got to his feet and glanced around the park at the happy and smiling families. The laughter was too loud and grated on his nerves, the sunlight too bright and stung his eyes. Even the smell of the flowers irritated his nose but he knew that if he sneezed he would probably pass out or throw up. Possibly both. Crouching down he looked at his daughter’s face and wondered when his little angel became a demon, and then internally chided himself for such a thought, blaming it on the night before. A weekend in Vegas all expenses paid, and a visit to his favourite hooker would put a large dent in it but it would be well worth it. He hadn’t remembered, of course, that he still owed his bookie nearly ten grand from the last game of poker he had played. Then again at that moment he could barely remember his own name. Three hours of cards and Jack Daniel chasers to the bottles of Miller that seemed to materialise out of thin air every time he finished one had turned his World into a sea of grinning faces and smoke filled dreams. Dermot O’Shannon was not just running the game but he was also Blake’s bookie and loan shark. A killer combination if ever there was one, but Blake didn’t seem to notice the conflict of interests in the setup. He was one hand away from taking the pot of twenty grand. One hand away from the win of his life. … and a full house, tens with jacks high, was THE hand of his life. It didn’t matter to him that he didn’t have the cash with him to stay in the game; Dermot was a friend. In fact after that much alcohol Dermot was his best friend in the whole wide World. Dermot, still stone cold sober, was more than happy to lend him the money to put in the pot, and Blake was more than happy with the terms of repayment. Why wouldn’t he be, he was about to win twenty thousand dollars. “C’mon then baby” he said to Julia as he picked her up into his arms. “You smell funny daddy,” she protested, wrinkling her nose as the aroma of stale, alcohol-induced sweat assailed her. He hadn’t changed his clothes from the night before, there were still stains of half-eaten food on his shirt … at least he hoped it was half-eaten stuff that he had dropped and not stuff he had regurgitated without knowing. It wouldn’t be the first time that he had puked in his sleep though he admitted to himself. It wasn’t like he had had anytime to get changed or shower this morning … he hadn’t even had time to brush his teeth. Waking up outside a nightclub while passers-by look down on you like a piece of crap that they nearly stood in was not the best way to greet a new day, especially not when you couldn’t remember how you got there in the first place. In fact not when you couldn’t remember a damn thing about the night before. Placing Julia back on her feet Blake bit of the sharp retort he had been about to hurl at his daughter, but then another wave of nausea hit him and he decided that he could teach her all about manners when they got home. The last thing he needed right now was a screaming brat and interfering busybodies who would no doubt over-react and call the slap that she so richly deserved all out assault. His own father had beaten him black and blue, and it he turned out all right, didn’t he Blake thought to himself. With a sigh though he left the past buried deep in his head and grabbed his daughter by the hand, trailing her along beside him as her oh so small legs struggled to keep up as he made his way towards the ice-cream stand. It was the hastily scrawled note pinned to his shirt that filled in the blanks from the night before. He almost didn’t spot it, but after he had stood up he had thrown up into a nearby flowerpot and, as he was wiping the residue of vomit from his two-day-old stubble, his arm knocked it to the ground. Bending down to pick it up had almost killed him, at least he had felt like he was going to die as his brain tried its best to escape his skull, but when he picked it up and the words finally filtered into his booze soaked consciousness he suddenly found himself feeling very sober. It only took a few words to bring his World down around his knees and make his gorge rise again … but when those words told you that you owed someone like Dermot ‘O’Shannon twenty four thousand dollars and that he wanted his money that day it didn’t take War and Peace to get the point across. “Two cones” Blake almost growled at the kid dishing out the ice-cream as he fumbled in his pockets for a handful of screwed up dollar bills. Last night he had been only minutes … seconds … away from having thousands of dollars in his pocket and now he was reduced to blowing his last three dollars on treats for a six year old. After reading the note from O’Shannon it had all come back to him and he felt tears well up in his eyes at the recalled frustration of the night before. Triumphantly he had thrown his cards to the table, already reaching out for the money before he even saw the other hand. He knew that he had won it, why wait? Then the moment that his heart nearly stopped in his chest, and his tongue felt too large for his mouth. Dermot had thumped his large, calloused hand down on top of his own and seemingly without effort he had held him in place. Fixing him in a steely gaze O’Shannon had dropped his cards to the table and then grinned as he watched the colour drain from Blake’s face. Four Queens. He didn’t remember anything else after that, but the plastic faces of the four queens came back to him and he felt as if he had been punched in the gut as his bowels tightened. “Four fuckin’ bitches … story of my fuckin’ life” he muttered to himself as he took the ice-creams from the kid behind the counter, throwing the crumpled notes down as he did so. Handing one down to his daughter he grimaced as his stomach protested once more. Great, just great, he thought to himself, after everything else and now I am going to get the shits. “C’mon kid, let’s get out of here” he growled through clenched teeth as he dropped his untouched ice cream in a nearby bin. Grabbing his daughter by her free hand he half-dragged her towards the carpark. Vainly trying to keep up with her father, Julia tottered along on her tip-toes, concentrating on not letting her own ice cream fall to the ground. She smiled in triumph as they reached the car and went to take her first taste of the ice cream, but her father picked her up and placed her into the passenger seat, slamming the door closed. “Put your belt on kid” he stated as he got into the driver’s seat, not checking to see if she listened. Starting the car, which was twice as old as his daughter, on the third attempt he reversed it out of the parking space with a squeal of brakes. His head was still pounding, his tongue tasted like a rat had taken a crap on it, and his stomach was about to let loose at any moment. Julia’s face lit up once more as she managed to hold onto the ice cream while the car … and her oblivious father … did their best to upset her balance. Staring out of the window she gave a gasp of delight as she saw one of the most beautifully coloured and vibrant butterflies that she had ever seen. One of her only memories … at least only happy ones … that she had of her father was him painting her bedroom with butterflies for her fourth or fifth birthday. He had spent all day doing it and even let her help. By the end of it they were both covered in nearly as much paint as he room, but that didn’t matter, it had been her day. A special day. “Look daddy a butterfly!” she squealed in delight, spinning around in her seat to point out the wonderful creature to her father. The ice cream in her hand was forgotten in her desire to share another special moment with the man who she knew was still the same, but somehow hadn’t felt the same for a long time. As she span around it fell from her hand and landed in his lap, cone up. “For fuck’s sake what the Hell are you doing?” he shouted as his hands swerved on the wheel, sending the car into a near spin. Concentrating he managed to gain control of the vehicle and then slapped his hand down hard on Julia’s exposed leg. There was a crack as skin met skin and for a moment there was complete silence. The surprise of the blow meant that Julia felt no pain for a few seconds, and then as she saw five red welts … in the shape of her father’s fingers … start to rise on her leg the shock wore of and the pain hit. Hard. The scream was out of her mouth before she even realised it, and she scared herself with the intensity of the sound, hardly registering at the start that it was her own mouth that made it. Since recovering from testicular cancer two years previously Tony Smyth had done everything in his power to make sure that his body was the best that it possibly could be. Never a heavy drinker to begin with he had completely cut alcohol out of his system. He had quit smoking the day that he had heard the word cancer in his doctor’s surgery, and even went so far as to cut red meat out of his diet. Jogging became his hobby, his life, his obsession. Every morning before getting to the office he got dressed and went out for at least a thirty-minute run … and every evening after work he did the same. “An hour a day keeps the cancer away” he thought to himself as he pumped his legs faster, relishing the feeling of the impact of his Nike clad feet against the tarmac. His doctor had told him that a healthy lifestyle would improve his chances, and Tony had taken this onboard and then improved upon it two hundred percent. His whole life was like that. Never fall under a target, never even meet a target. If you couldn’t improve on a target, if you couldn’t excel at something, then there was no point in doing it. So when his doctor said get fit, Tony started training as if he was entering a marathon. Twice a day, every day, he ran. Never mind the post office, wind, rain, sleet nor hail would stop Tony. He had even tried to run through a class three gale just four months ago and had cursed himself as a loser when he had to turn back after only ten minutes. His class mates at high school had known exactly the sort of person that Tony was, and would become. Most likely to overachieve was one comment in his yearbook … most likely to burn out and die at forty was another not so nice one. It didn’t matter to him though; he lived his life by his rules and let nothing or no one get in his way. That was until the speeding car nearly blindsided him moments before his vision went white. He had heard of people having strokes while running before, something to do with the pressure build up in the brain, but he had never heard of anyone suffering a white out while running … and why on Earth was his face so cold all of a sudden he thought to himself as he reached up and started to wipe away the ice cream that had hit him in the face. It only took a couple of minutes, but eventually he had told Ember everything. “Well then my friend, this could be your lucky night after all” Ember said with a grin. He reached down and took Liam’s wallet from out of his pocket and pulled a handful of one hundred dollar bills out of it, before dropping it back onto his unconscious form. He handed the notes to Blake and then put an arm around his shoulders, companionably. “Take this and find yourself a bookie … I would suggest a new one my friend, as I don’t think that daddy O’Shannon is going to be all that fond of you, “ Ember quipped, “ And make another bet.” “You ever hear of the PTC … a wrestling organisation? “he asked and smiled when Blake nodded, “… good, good. Then what you do is bet everything that you have on the next round of the Infinity Gauntlet. Bet on Ember to win.” Blake stared down at the money and shook his head in confusion as the words filtered into his mind. “That’s you isn’t it?” he asked softly. The hold across his shoulders suddenly became less friendly as Ember tightened his arm around Blake’s body and pulled him close so that he could whisper into his ear. “Oh yes that is me and I know what you are thinking … you are thinking that you can tell Liam’s daddy who broke his little boy and clear your debts that way. You are thinking that you can tell the police who did this and get a nice reward.” Ember almost purred into Blake’s ear, chuckling as a look of guilt crept over the man’s face. “See I knew that was what you were thinking, but I can promise you two reasons why you don’t want to do that my friend. The first is that if you do place that bet you are going to be very wealthy anyway and can pay of your debts that way because I promise that I am going to win that match” Ember stated matter-of-factly. “… and the other promise?” Blake asked as his curiosity won out over his fear. “That one is easy, I can promise you that if you mention one word about me to anyone, just one little word, I will hunt you down and make you pay for it” Ember smiled as he whispered this, seeing the look of fear in Blake’s eyes. He tightened his grip around the man’s shoulders and then turned him so that they were face to face. “Before you get all brave Darren, I should point out that it won’t be you that pays the bill … it will be your lovely little daughter Julia, it will be your bitch of an ex-wife Katie. Then, and only then, will I turn my attention to you … and what Liam just did to you, the fear that you felt … what I just did to Liam, the pain that he felt …” Ember licked his lips and leant in closer, his nose touching Blake’s own, “ … will be nothing compared to what I do to you.” Patting Blake on the cheek Ember stepped over the prone body of Liam and walked out of the darkened men’s room, his last words floating back to Blake like butterflies wings on a light breeze. “I promise.” . . . . . . . . . .
