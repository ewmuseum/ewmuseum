Worthless

26 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-26 Most people view the world as it is, a cesspit filled with swarms of cockroaches fighting to be the top of the pile. Each one worth exactly the same. Absolutely nothing. The inhabitants of the planet like to think they’re educated and know nearly everything there is to know about the environment in which they reside, ignorant to the fact they have hardly any knowledge of anything. Take for example the human mind, something which we know very little about; yet we pretend to be experts on the subject matter. Psychologists seem to think they can judge what a person is thinking based on behaviour and how the react, however a lot of people would disagree with this thesis and say that we cannot know what other people are thinking. We could take this to its extreme conclusion and realize that in reality, we cannot know what anybody else is thinking, or for that matter, that anybody else is thinking. Though diverting away from what we can know, we have to live our lives with principles of what we think we know. When we look at others we have to believe that other people are thinking and that we can have an idea of what we’re thinking so we can communicate and socialize. However human nature dictates that we will always seek more, curiosity being one of our worst vices. Our failure to understand something only makes us try harder. Concepts we cannot grasp seem to make us salivate, a hunger we try so hard to feed. The concept of multiple personality disorder is one that many Psychologists have tried to solve for a long time. The trouble is that all they can do is observe, they will never have first hand experience of the condition. Nobody will ever be able to experience what an individual experiences, this is what Philosophers call ‘Qualia’; it is what separates us from the rest of the animals on the planet. It is what makes us all have unique, individual experiences and sensations and it is why we all see the world differently. It is this that makes us think we are special, when in reality; we are no different from cockroaches. Yet, our belief system leads us onto the concept of individuality and looking at the world through our own eyes, it is almost certain that Cyrus Skar fits into the character of an ‘individual’, for there are very few with a similar condition to him and very few with a similar background. Cyrus Skar is one man, maybe two, who has found himself in extreme circumstances. But at the end of the day, he is worth the same as your ‘Average Joe’ walking down the road, for we are all equal. And we are all worth nothing. ----- “I’d like you to start with something small Cyrus, you don’t mind if I call you that; do you?” the Psychologist spoke from behind the abnormally sized man lay on his brown, leather couch. He’d never had a patient quite like Cyrus Skar before and this was evident as Cyrus’s legs dangled off the edge of the bed. A nod of the head was all that the Psychologist received in reply to his question; which already led him to believe he would have a tough time with Mr. Skar. Cyrus was a complex individual indeed, but this was the first time he’d ever gone to a proper certified Psychologist to ask for help. Although Cyrus had not actually asked for help, he didn’t want to; he hated the thought of somebody else solving his problems due to his arrogance. Though he needed some assistance, he would admit that much. “Cyrus, I’m going to say one word to you. Memories.” This word immediately caught Cyrus’s attention, his ears pricked up and he suddenly was sitting bolt upright on the couch. The Psychologist placed his hand gently on Cyrus’s shoulder and tried to calm him. Memories were something Cyrus wanted to leave behind, as far away as possible, so they could hurt him no more… ----- It is said that we never forget things, that everything we experienced is stored somewhere in memory. So in theory if the right techniques were used; anything could be retrieved from memory. Many experts all around the world have mastered such techniques and have managed to exploit the potential of their memory. Supposedly the best way of maximizing your powers of recollection is to imagine your mind like a house, within each room a different memory. The people that can develop this technique can find anything that they desire, they are valuable men and women, yet hardly any know they can do it because they have never really tried. Cyrus however is going to have to learn how to delve into the depths of his mind, into places that he’d locked away never to be reopened. For he seeks guidance and the only way he believes he is going to find it; is by looking in the past. Most of his childhood is just a void in his conscious mind, a white space where happy memories should be; removed because of all the pain they caused. The Psychologist was about to bring this to the forefront of the encounter, it was going to hurt Cyrus, but it had to be done. It would help him in the long term, only if it didn’t destroy him first. “I’d like to ask you about some of the things you wrote on this questionnaire I gave you before Cyrus, is that ok?” though the Psychologist received no answer he knew that Cyrus was ok with the question. Cyrus had calmed himself down and was trying to be focused and not let his past trouble him, but he was having trouble containing it. “First of all I’d like to ask you about your father, what can you tell me about him Cyrus?” a calm, clear tone was adopted by the man sat next to Cyrus to try and ease him into a state or relaxation. He would speak more openly if he was comfortable with his surroundings and himself. “What’s you’re first name?” this reply was quite unexpected and took the Psychologist aback. He took a few seconds to think about why Cyrus might have asked this and then answered it with another question. “Why do you wish to know that Cyrus?” “Because, I don’t believe you’ve properly introduced yourself to me.” The Psychologists mind began to whir, the cogs began to click into gear and then he realized who he was now talking to. It was not Cyrus Skar, the man lay down before him; it was his other personality speaking through Cyrus’s mouth. He’d already introduced himself to Cyrus, but he hadn’t even mentioned his condition. “I’m very sorry, my name is David Hill.” This short explanation would not suffice though; the voice inside of Cyrus let out a note of disproval and then continued with the interrogation. “Are you married?” “Yes.” “Any children?” “Two, a boy and a girl.” “What are their names?” “Michael and Stephanie.” “How old are they?” “14 and 17 respectively.” Then the voice seemed to stop whilst it thought things over, the roles had seemingly been reversed with the patient interviewing the Psychologist. Mr. Hill did not even realize he was being assessed by Cyrus’s other personality, he just wished to please it. He’d heard about how violent it could be if it took over Cyrus’s body as well as his mouth. “So, I see that you’re a family man. Does that make you happy? Does it fill the void that life seems to leave?” a strange sense of jealously had crept into the tone of the voice that emerged from Cyrus’s mouth, as though it envied Mr. Hill’s situation. “I’d like to say that I’m happy yes, but that’s only because of my wife and my children. Without them I don’t know what I’d do.” Mr. Hill was becoming slightly agitated now as the voice’s questions seemed to be penetrating further and further into his mind. He had to stop this now before it gained complete control over him. He had to try and communicate with Cyrus. “Cyrus, if you can hear this, I want you to try and enter your memory. Look in the past Cyrus and you’ll find some answers I’m sure.” As soon as the last syllable left Mr. Hill’s tongue Cyrus’s fist came crashing through the underneath of his jaw, causing the Oxford graduate to fall to the cold, tiled floor, staining it with a shade of red. Speckles of blood dropped down from his broken jaw as he lay on the floor; a broken man. “I thought you were a smart man Doctor. Obviously not, because if you were, you would have realized that I would have done that.” With that the voice walked away from the shaking, quivering mass on the floor before him and towards the door. It took one glance back to the fallen man and then turned its back on him and slammed the door shut. As soon as the door clicked into the lock the voice felt a surge of pain course through Cyrus’s brain. It fell to its knees clutching at its head as Cyrus tried to re-emerge from the depths of his mind where he had been banished too. Fighting over control the two souls staggered down the corridor, knocking into tables and chairs, sending lamps crashing into the floor as patients and staff alike looked on. The monsters fighting each other in a battle only they could witness. Somehow the mixture of souls and body managed to struggle to the door leading out into the open, together they crashed through it, sending a shower of shining glass flying in all directions, glistening as the light refracted off it; causing a few grunts from the onlookers. The body tripped and fell down the steps up to the building, the wounds to its mammoth shell causing it to lie still for a few minutes. Cyrus was the first to take control and did so with authority. He’d been caught off-guard in a state of relaxation when he was about to speak to Mr. Hill. He’d put himself at risk and his other personality had taken advantage. But Cyrus had come away with something, he’d come away with the very small possibility of finding some answers to the questions he needed answering. He wanted to put the past away, but before he did that, he needed to see it again. He needed to remember what exactly it was that he was storing away, otherwise it would trouble him for the rest of his life. He made his way home and didn’t hear from the voice, it’d lost its attempt at taking over this time, but it would try again sooner or later. Cyrus walked up to his front door feeling tired; but now he had some sort of direction. He entered through the front door after carefully unlocking it and went and lay down upon his soft bed. His head ached from the fight with his alter-ego, the throbbing intensified each time it stung. Now it was time to try and enter the past. He let his eyelids close down over his eyes, blocking the light of the world from his mind. He let himself slip inside his own brain and visualized as best he could. Shades of brown and white seemed to merge together before his eyes, distorting changing until finally settling on the shape of a mansion covered by snow. His feet felt heavy as he trudged through the masses of white and entered the unlocked front door of the house. His gaze shifted to the sheer expansiveness of the reception area. Hundreds of doors lined the sides of the walls. In awe Cyrus walked over to one of the doors in the middle, he went to open it but it was locked. He tried again, a little more force exerted this time, still the door would not budge, so he took a step back before volleying it hard with his right boot. The door swung open and darkness engulfed the room, Cyrus felt his feet losing grip and started to slide towards the open door. The room began to spiral downwards towards a black hole in the middle. Cyrus desperately flailed trying to grab onto something to stop him from falling, but there was nothing to hold. Down he went further and further into the darkness until eventually he just stopped. He opened his eyes to see the familiar bars that surrounded him. Back when his father used to lock him up, back when he used to spend days in there. Having nowhere to defecate or urinate, he did his best to hold it in, but it was no use. His father would hit him for soiling his pants and he felt disappointed with himself. He felt so ashamed of what he was. He hated it. Cyrus awoke with a start, perspiration now covering his brow. Shaking slightly from the shock of how vivid his memory was, he got up and walked over to the bathroom. His hand fumbled at the tap and eventually managed to switch it on. He splashed cold water on his face, refreshing his body and mind and then looked at his pathetic worthless face in the mirror. He was worth nothing.
