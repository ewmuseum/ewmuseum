A true artist

29 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-29 As a child I was never a big fan of traditional art. I never wanted to visit a museum and look at paintings and sculptures done by those who have been dead for hundreds of years. I also never liked those more modern abstract paintings which they consider dumping paint on a canvas art. I mean I did the same thing in Kindergarten, but instead of my work being put in a museum I got sent to the Principles office for making a mess on the floor. But with saying that I do not hate all art, there are many artists I have a great amount of respect for. They are artists whose work has a meaning, has a purpose, like architects. Their art give people a visually pleasing and easily livable housing or workplace. They design 100,000 seat NASCAR tracks or 200 seat little league fields. Graphic Designers have their hands in almost everything from package design, magazine layouts, posters advertising everything under the sun, websites, TV graphics, logos and the list goes on and on. Now tell me, what purpose does painting 15 different Madonna with Child serve?……..That’s what I thought, none. I call myself The Hardcore Artist because I paint a masterpiece every time I step into the ring. My brush is whatever weapon I can get my hands on. My media is the ring canvas and the blood of my opponents. I take pride in every trail of blood that flows down my opponents forehead and with every drop of blood that stains the floor of the ring. Now I can see your little arm in the air, asking how my art differs from that of Picasso, Rembrandt or Michelangelo. And I have a very simple answer for you. My artwork provides entertainment to millions of people weekly. Did you ever hear about Picasso selling out 20,000 seat arena to see him paint The Old Guitarist or even to see Da Vinci paint The Mona Lisa? Of course not. But if Da Vinci had battled Mona Lisa in a fight, that would of drawn in the people. And if that happened maybe the Mona Lisa would of looks a little bit different then it does today. To be honest I think she looks good with gold. Almost makes her look a little sexy, but now I’m starting to sounds like Frankie. God only knows what he has done to that poor painting, probably has something to do with his lifetime ban from The Louvre in France. So Alex Lancer, I look at you as a block of clay. A block of clay which I will shape, mold and carve into my next great masterpiece, and you, just like the rest of art will become history. Never to be seen or cared about again.
