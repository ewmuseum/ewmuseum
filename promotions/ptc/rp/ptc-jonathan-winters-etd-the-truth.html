[Jonathan Winters ETD rp]: The Truth. Prologue. Chaos. That’s all Jonathan Winters knew now. What fragile stability had previously knitted his life together had ultimately dissolved, seemingly overnight. It all began when his illustrious employer, Cornelius Stakiri, was first targeted by a rival organisation. When one depicts such events in terms such as this, it provides certain connotation of wealthy business transactions and corporate concerns. Well, strictly speaking, it is the truth. However, there are two levels to Stakiri’s formidable empire. The first has already been explained. The second, partially explains such chaotic times. Cornelius Stakiri has long since transcended the strict transient borders of legitimate business and over the years through power and money, Stakiri has carved himself a sizeable territory right across the east coast. But, as they say, all good things come to an end and for Stakiri, that end is in sight. Three months ago, one of Stakiri’s primary operatives, Aeryn, returned from a scouting mission of a rival organisation with a very similar set up to Stakiri Corp. Or so was first thought. It later became apparent that this rival organisation was vastly superior, with resources far outsourcing that of Stakiri’s. The war had begun and Stakiri was losing. Sent into a state of paranoia, Stakiri suspected someone within his inner circle was responsible for the rival organisations superior intelligence gathering. The finger was pointed squarely at Winters and Stakiri had him tailed to no avails. Turned out, Stakiri was wrong. Winters was promptly targeted by a task force sent to take him into custody, ready for interrogation. He initially suspected Stakiri gave the order, but yesterday, Winters discover his suspicions to be false. It wasn’t Stakiri at all. It was Aeryn. Aeryn had betrayed him and now Jonathan Winters wants to know why. *** Memories. JFK Airport. Jamaica, New York. Walking through the elegance glass sliding doors and admiring the peculiar exterior architecture, the painful memories Winters had associated with the place, came flooding back to him and he couldn’t hope to block them out. It was the airport in which Aeryn came through, upon returning from her long term assignment in Salt Lake City. Winters met her with a smile that day and she returned it without flinching, despite their heated goodbyes. Was that even Aeryn? Did she transform into this monster after returning from her assignment? His mind raced. Or did the change come before? He decided on the later. He avoided eye contact with the security officers and any civilians that he didn’t much like the looks of and attempted to remain anonymous as he sifted through the crowd of people. In the background, a shadowed man using the public telephones, glared at him intently as he passed and once he was clear, the man replaced the receiver and began to follow. She must’ve been made, he decided, they must’ve uncovered her true identity. He swallowed, hard. Then they must’ve done something to her. Changed her in some way. Yes, that’s it. That’s what happened. There was something else. Something deep down that his subconscious wanted him to consider, but his mind wilfully denied. However, such thoughts often rise to the surface, regardless of whether one wants them to, or not. What if they didn’t do anything to her, he reluctantly digested. What if she chose to betray me? *** Pleasantries. One thousand miles above the ground. Somewhere over the US. Over the brash noise of crying children and annoying elderly citizens and crammed into cramped, inadequate seats, Winters wished he could’ve gotten himself a place in first class. But you don’t want to draw attention to yourself, do you? He reminded himself for the hundredth time that minute. But surely, it would be worth the compromise? “You like William Gibson?” A deep, jolly voice questioned. Broken out of his reverie, Winters had to check that the book in his hand was actually Neuromancer, before shooting African-American men next to him a fake smile and a nod. “Who doesn’t?” The man glanced around the plane pointedly. “Probably a lot of people,” he told him. “Science fiction is a joke to most people.” Winters sighed. “Don’t I know it.” “And when they find something that’s sci-fi that they like,” the man began, breaking out into a chuckle mid-sentence. “They label it a ‘compelling drama’ and simply ignore all links to science fiction.” Winters nodded a response and joined in the laughter, albeit half-heartedly. “Like the new series of Battlestar Galactica...” “Precisely,” the stranger began, seemingly unable to control his laughter. “It’s sad really...” “It really is,” Winters agreed, eying up the gentleman carefully. “It really is.” The man smiled and extended his hand. “I’m Jemal.” Winters took the hand and shook. “I’m--” “You don’t have to tell me who you are,” he told him. Winters’ mind immediately screamed danger at him, but then Jemal added, “I’m a big fan of PRIME.” “Yeah,” Winters agreed, promptly dismissing the warning. “Who isn’t?” *** Infiltration. OmniTech Corporation. Salt Lake City, Utah. The journey from Salt Lake International Airport was as eventful as any other. The desperate dash to the onsite care rental agency, hoping upon hope one doesn’t acquire a vehicle that’ll case functioning five minutes after the key is jammed in the ignition. Jonathan Winters parked up right outside the building for everyone on the security detail to see. Without doubt, if these people were as good as he expected, his face would be on their watch list and their security technology would immediately put a name and reputation to his face. He opened the car door, closed it behind him and leant upon it’s cold metal surface. Come on, fuckers, He silently cursed, idly glancing at his watch. How long does it take you to assemble an assault team and scramble them to the damned gates? Winters glanced around at the supposed scenery. He rarely listened to speculations and usually didn’t believe anything unless he saw it with his own, but now that he’d witnessed first hand the Utah sights, he had to agree that the place was fundamentally boring. But then, perhaps he wasn’t looking in the right places. A smile crept across Winters face, as the sound of crushed leaves and broken twigs assaulted his ears. They were here. “Put your hands in the air,” the voice demanded, from the forest to his rear. “Now!” Winters complied, turning to face the perpetrators in doing so. “What took you so long?” The assault team, dressed in a fearful mixture of green beige camouflage and black, edged closer, their assault rifles firmly trained onto Winters position. Well, there’s no going back now... “Are you alone?” The leader of the team, questioned, edging closer to Winters than the others. “In a manner of speaking...” Winters trailed off. A glint of anger shot across his face. “I repeat, are you alone?” Winters grinned. Definite military training. Efficient and effective in equal measures. “Check the car.” The leader of the team made a number of military hand gestures, Winters didn’t even attempt to decode and moved within touching distance of Winters, as the rest of the team flanked the area. One of them yanked open the car door and glared inside at its contents. “What the fuck...?” The security detail leader forced Winters’ hands downward and quickly strapped them tight with a resistant plastic restraint. When his prisoner was secure, he glared at his colleague with engaged interest. “What have we got?” “You’d better see for yourself, sir.” *** Token. Inside OmniTech. Salt Lake City, Utah. The corridors of the building were just as Winters expected them to be; plain, white and sterile. Winters had wrestled in the state a number of times over the years and to the best of his recollection, the majority of the corridors in those frequented arena’s were pretty much identical. Must be something in the water, he silently noted, as the security guard behind him shoved him onward. Winters shot the little prick a cautionary glance and he received a cocky grin in response. “What you gonna do, boy?” Cave your head in, you little prick, he silently threatened. The guard’s grin widened, he could tell what Winters was thinking and they both knew it. Winters turned away. It’s not fucking worth it. Not yet. “Pussy.” “Cut the chatter,” the leader demanded, as the reached a final security door. He swiped his card through the security outlet and then punched in a security code. The oval door swiped open, before them lay a pristine chromed room, worryingly laden with a variety of medical equipment. Winters was shoved forward again, through the oval door and now, with a better view of the room, he could see a woman door at the centre of this metallic contraption, clothed in an expensive-looking business suit. Her brunette hair was tied back and her spectacles and red lipstick made her look hotter than she probably actually was. “Jonathan Winters,” she greeted, with a pleasant smile. Winters developed a suspicious expression and suddenly got on edge. “It’s good to see you.” “No it isn’t,” he told her. “Don’t lie.” She chuckled under breath and the security detail leader cut free his plastic restraints. Winters shot him a confused expression, to which he echoed. “Don’t ask me.” “I take it you’re here in regards to the Aeryn incident?” She questioned. He nodded. “Am I that easy to read?” “Kinda,” the woman answered, extending hand. “My name his Miss. Weiss and I’ve been authorised to make contact with you.” Winters ignored the hand and chose to rub his marked wrists intently. Then one of the other security guards stormed forward, hauling the body with him. The woman shot the security detail leader a questioning glance. The security leader shrugged. “He said it was a gift.” The woman frowned and then removed the black hood that concealed the man’s face. The African-American man stared up blankly and murmured something that was rendered undecipherable by gag covering his mouth. She glared at Winters and he smiled in response. “He told me his name was Jemal,” Winters explained. “Probably an alias. Caught him tailing me at JFK airport. He strategically positioned himself next to me on the plane ride over here and I apprehended him on the way here from the airport.” Miss. Weiss’s confused expression visibly intensified. “So?” “He works for Stakiri,” Winters explained. Miss. Weiss glared at Jemal for a second and then back at Winters. “I see.” “View it as a gift,” Winters told her. Miss. Weiss chuckled and shook her head. “Why should he be considered a gift? We have full details of Stakiri’s operation and this individual’s face isn’t in any of the files.” “I know,” Winters said. “And that makes him more valuable to you, than you realise. He’s unlisted. One of Stakiri’s inner circle. You think Stakiri would waste anyone else tailing me?” “Perhaps you value yourself too...highly, Mr. Winters.” Winters smiles. “Perhaps you don’t value me highly enough.” Miss. Weiss nodded with acceptance. “So, what do you want in exchange for this...gift?” “You already know what I want,” Winters told her. Miss. Weiss shook her head. “I can’t give you Aeryn. She’s too important to our operation.” “I don’t want Aeryn,” he informed. Miss. Weiss produced a sultry smile. “Well, Mr. Winters, you are full of surprises. What do you want?” “I want to know what you did to her,” He explained. “Nothing more.” Miss. Weiss nodded. “Okay. We discovered that Stakiri had sent an operative into our organisation via restricted resources and we captured her.” “And?” Winters prodded, impatiently. “We modified her.” His brow furrowed. “Modified her?” “We used technology to alter her memories and thoughts,” Miss. Weiss told him. “Precisely what, I cannot divulge.” His heart stopped, but in the face of his oppressors, he tried to hide it the best he could. It wasn’t her! She didn’t betray me! What they transformed her into, did! Thank god... “Now, if that’s all...” Miss. Weiss trailed off, gesturing toward the exit. “You’re just going to let me go?” He questioned, almost unwilling to believe his ears. “Of course,” Miss. Weiss told him. “Our intelligence reports indicated that you’re no longer associated with Stakiri. You’re a civilian and you’re not a threat to us any longer. That is accurate, isn‘t it?” “Absolutely,” Winters agreed, immediately thrusting himself backward and jamming his elbow into the bridge of the cocky guards nose. The other soldiers instantly directed their weapons at him and Miss. Weiss smiled. Winters shrugged. “He was asking for it.” “Get him out of here!” The security guard leader ordered. *** Epilogue. OmniTech. Salt Lake City, Utah. Outside of the OmniTech building and back in his rental car, Winters breathed a sigh of relief. It wasn’t Aeryn. That was all he needed to know, or so he thought. He hadn’t figured into the equation precisely what he do after learning what, deep down, he already knew to be true. Except one thing. KKKKK BOOOOOOOOOOOM!!!!! The explosion ripped apart the main part of the building and slowly rippled into the variety corridors which connected everything together. It was quite a task strapping the unconscious with enough explosives to level the white house, but ultimately it was worth it. Now he could move on with his life. Now he concentrated on the PTC unification match. But he should thoughts were false. Aeryn was still out there somewhere running around false memories and personality traits OmniTech had crammed into her head. He had to find her. He had to stop this madness. One way, or another.
