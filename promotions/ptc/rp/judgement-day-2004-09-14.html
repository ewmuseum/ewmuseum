Judgement Day

14 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-14 The barrel of the gun pressed firmly against his head as sweat runs down his forehead, tracing the lines of this weary face until running down into Salaok’s ungroomed goatee and being absorbed. The time was now for the shaking had become uncontrollable and the memories haunted him more than ever If he was ever going to change the way in which his life had been run, this one moment in time would be that. Alone he sits, facing out the window as he looks out over the Nevermore Mansion grounds and at one spot which has haunted him for a year now. The small grassy hill in which his half brother and the son of his best friend stood and fired a single bullet into the mansion. One single bullet. A bullet that would drive straight into Salaok’s beloved wife, Lisha. Tomorrow will be one year since her passing and six months since the passing of his son, Darrell. With hatred running rampant through his body, he pulls the trigger. Click. Nothing. This game of Russian Roulette with himself had been going on for days now. Each day he would load a single bullet into the gun and spin the barrel and thus far, he had lived through the game for ten days. Surely tomorrow would bring the relief in which he wanted so badly. Like everything in his life, he couldn’t just kill himself normally, he had to make it into a mind fucking game. A game that would eat away at your will to live each day that you don’t succeed in finding that one bullet. The one bullet that would burst through his skull and send his brain and fragments of bone flying into the air while his body would drop helplessly to the floor. That’s all he wanted. Was it so much to ask for? He had become sick of this world, as there was no point to it anymore. Once upon a time, there were those who were willing to commit the crimes that needed to be done and there were those who were happy to just sit back and let the others take care of business. Times have changed and now those who were willing to let others do what had to be done have wilted away to number too small to even recognize there existence. No longer was The Lair in control through their organized crime, or at least so it seemed to Salaok. For if they were still in control of the underground movements, then why is it that Salaok’s life could be turned upside down and nothing as far as he could see was being done about it. It was almost like his being had been ignored and the Lair had turned it’s back on him. Turned it’s back on a Harvester? Surely not. Frustrated at yet again failing his attempt to end the pain, he soon finds himself throwing the gun across the room, sending it through the glass doors of the lounge room and into the hall. The gun goes off. The echoing of the blast seems to last forever in the otherwise dead silent house. One bullet away. That’s all the end of his life was, one bullet away. “I’m disappointed in you Salaok.” The voice ringing out with the low mono tone which was able to allow the sound to carry for some distance without raising his voice. Straight away Salaok knew who it was that spoke to him and turned to try and see where he was standing. Again the voice rings out. “Look at what you have become Harvester! A man of once great power and self-control, now stands looking at the place where Mani stood that fateful night, trying to kill yourself.” Still no idea where his old friend’s voice is coming from, Salaok answers back. “You’ve been gone a year Quartz, things have change.” Salaok is about to go on but finds himself being cut off by the powerful voice of the man known as Quartz. “I know Salaok. I know that Darrell is dead but there is nothing you can do about it now. I watched you from a distance at his funeral but I still heard the words in which you spoke. You dubbed your son a Harvester, which we both know is something that Darrell had wanted to be since he was able to say the word. You giving him a Harvester title Salaok told me one thing that you still believe in and that is the pride of the Harvesters.” Quartz pauses and then continues as his heavy footsteps can now be heard throughout the house. “And the rules in which follow our ways. Rules which you are clearly trying to ignore in your own death. You took as oath when you were dubbed a Harvester for the first time Salaok and although your Harvester title may have changed over time, you are still bound by that oath. Everything you do must be a benefit for the Lair. Your death would not be a benefit at all Salaok.” Salaok can’t take it anymore and finally fires back at his old friend. “Was The Harvester of Blood and the Harvester of Death benefiting the Lair when they killed Lisha? Were they oh great wise friend of mine?” “Don’t start using tone with me young Salaok. I still out rank you despite what you may have been lead to believe in your own mind.” Quartz fires back, instantly putting Salaok into his place. “We don’t have time for this Salaok, it’s time to go.” Just as Quartz says this, he opens the door of the lounge room and walks towards Salaok. His massive body towering over Salaok which for Salaok is strange, as he is no small man himself. “Where?” “It’s time we all went home Salaok. It’s time to go back the The Lair.” Quartz replies which only gets a look of confusion from Salaok. “Before we go anywhere Quartz, I have just one question. Where have you been for the last year?” “As I told you when I left… Taking care of a contract that will end this mess.” What turns and begins to walk out of the room knowing that Salaok will follow him. But the mood in the air isn’t right, something is wrong. Very wrong. What need is there for them to return home to The Lair’s castle? What contract had Quartz been taking care of all of this time? As Salaok walks out into the hallway, he looks at his gun sitting on the ground and before he can even make a move towards it, Quartz voice booms out knowingly. “You will not be needing that this time Salaok.” Salaok looks at Quartz, well, that back of his head at least as they walk down the hallway towards the front of the house. Passing a small red wooden table in the hall, the result of Salaok’s stray bullet is clearly visible as a hole in the family picture of Lisha, Darrell and himself has formed, strangely enough right where Salaok’s head is in the photo. The trip to The Lair’s castle is a long one, but one that went by with little to no real talk between the two old friends. Was it because they didn’t need to fill the void with meaningless chit chat, or was there something else in the air? After everything the two of them had been through in the last year, surely they would have something to say to each other, and yet there was mostly silence. They were still the two men who had fought by each other’s side for a decade but something had changed inside of them. Had they grown within themselves during the last year to the point that the other didn’t rank in their thoughts anymore? But once arriving at The Lair’s Castle, things begin to change as the darkness of the place consumes them and reminds Salaok of the way things used to be. He was still a Harvester and this place would always be his home which is why the feeling of discomfort seemed to out of place, and yet was very present inside of him. Walking past the statues of The Harvesters, his memory clicks in and thoughts of their pasts begin to riffle into his mind. But it is only when he comes to the statues of The Harvester of Emotion and the Harvester of Innocence that he stops. The Harvester of Emotions, was his wife and the Harvester of Innocence was his son, Darrell. Kneeling before his he crosses his chest and quietly says his words as he touches each corner of his cross. “Ateh, mulkuth, ve-Geburah, ve-Gedulah, le-olahm AMEN.” Pausing for a second, he suddenly finds Quartz voice booming out yet again, another order. “GET UP!” His voice sharp and direct this time. Salaok’s eyes open and look up at the two statues before suddenly turning and staring at Quartz who simply continues to walk with purpose through the building. “What’s changed Quartz?” Salaok yells out to the big man as he begins to walk after him? However there is no response only the sound of his footsteps as they move over the cold stone floor. Finally after what seems forever in Salaok’s mind, Quartz speaks out again as he stops at the door to the throne room and waits for Salaok to catch up. "You have changed Salaok, you have changed and The Harvesters are becoming tired of your being.” Salaoks tops dead, just a few feet away from Quartz with his eyes wide open, not knowing what is going on. However, without saying another word, Quartz pushes the doors to the throne room open and indicates for Salaok to go on inside from were the red glow billows out from. Salaok knew this room very well, as he had spent many days in his after the changing of the guard in The Lair when the power of control was passed down to all of the Harvester at the time. And as Salaok walks into the room and looks at the big long table in the middle of the room, he sees some of the Harvesters which were handed the power. Kullypso and Grendel, the Harvesters of Pain and Suffering. His older brother, Johnathon Davis, the Harvester of Fear. Quartz walks in and takes his seat next to Johnathon, as he is the Harvester of Violence. Salaok takes his usual seat at the opposite end of the table from Johnathon, as Salaok is the harvester of Minds. But it is the seats which are left unfilled which is the reason for such a problem and by the looks of things, one of the major reasons for Salaok being called to the castle. Johnathon, the clear head of the group remains silent and allows Quartz to do the talking which surprises Salaok to have his best friend judging him in such ways. “Things have changed Salaok and as I told you outside just a second ago, nothing has changed more than you. Over time we have been willing to turn a blind eye to some of your mistakes but now it is time for us to step forth and bring you back in line.” Still stuck by shock, Salaok sits there with nothing coming out of his mouth despite the fact that his mouth is quite clearly moving. But then it happens, Johnathon begins to talk and the cold heart voice of his older brother strikes at his heart as Quartz gets up from his seat and walks over to the window. “Brother, it was your job to bring new Harvesters into the fray and look at us. Although The Lair as a whole is a strong foundation, those who vow to protect it, The Harvesters have become weaker in numbers. So far you have brought in, Draven, Silent Wind, and Sage Sabbot. But where are they now Salaok? Where are your new generation of Harvesters?” Before Salaok has a chance at defending his choices, Quartz begins to speak again as he leans out the window, his voice different to normal as he now has a job to do. “Your choices were weak Salaok and those choices have not only hurt you, but hurt the rest of us!” At this stage, Salaok can’t hold himself back anymore. “One of those choices was partly yours Quartz. You were the one who thought that making Silent Wind a Harvester would be the best way for us to keep him under our control, so don’t go blaming me for all of this. And one of those choices Johnathon, is your…. Our half brother that you kept hidden away from me. And why was it able to be born in the first place? Because Quartz couldn’t do his damn job and kill our father before it happened. How dare you judge me poorly for what has become of The Harvesters, as we are all to blame as much as each other.” Salaok fires at them, looking each man in the eye apart from Quartz who has his back turned to the whole situation. “That may very well be true Salaok, but regardless, you were put in charge of rebuilding The Harvesters and what have you done? Nothing! Five of us sit around the table now when once our numbers merited that of an army.” Johnathon’s cold voice points out the blatantly obvious when suddenly the door to the throne room swings open again. Its weight from the heavy metal means the door swings slowly and gives everyone the chance to turn their heads towards the door. Standing at the door in a long black robe and hood stands Sage Sabbot, the Harvester of Tears. “You’re late!” Quartz says loudly without taking his eyes away from the window. “I’m hear by invite only, Quartz. You turned your back on me, not the other way around remember.” Answers Sage straight back as he walks to the chair with a tear drop embedded into the head rest marking his place. “We are not hear for that Sage, as Salaok is our major topic of concern at this point in time.” Johnathon says calmly, getting things back on track and then turning his attention back to Salaok. “So one of your students still stands with us, but what of the other two? What of Draven and Wind?” There is silence as Salaok sits in his chair, his head facing down blocking any eye contact with his older brother. “Well, Salaok? What of them?” “I don’t know Johnathon. But I don’t understand why you are so cynical of my efforts? Are we not still feared?” Salaok reply clearly wasn’t that of what Johnathon was wanting which causes the booming voice from the window to sound out. “Answer his question Salaok! What of Draven and my son? You say you don’t know which clearly isn’t good enough. Do you want me to answer for you Salaok? Do you need to be told of those you recruited into our holy ranks? My son is still alive despite the contract you put on both his and Mani’s heads last year. They still walk the world completely free of their crimes against the Harvesters and more so your family. As for Draven, he now works for himself as a free agent in the underworld crime world. A free agent that used to be one of us and one that could be extremely useful but you couldn’t keep him under control could you Salaok? You had too many other things to worry about!” Again there is a silence but this time it is far longer and only interrupted by a long sigh before Salaok finally speaks. “I lose my wife and my son within a year and you pull me in here to answer as to why Silent Wind and Draven aren’t standing with us? I’ve lived the past year without the help of The Lair, I’m sure I can go for longer.” “Have you Salaok? Have you really gone without our help? Or have you just been oblivious to it? To the running of your companies thanks to Grendel and Kullypso? To having your contracts completed, except for two of them? Having guards outside of your house every night knowing full well that Mani and Silent Wind are still hunting you?” Johnathon points out and then continues more so to another point. “We are here now to rebuild the organization and so be it with only six Harvester to control the rest of the mob, we are going to do it.” Quartz’s voice once again interrupts in it’s usual booming manner. “Can we trust you Salaok not to resort back and hide away again? Can you be trusted with hits, and the contracting there of? We are old friends Salaok, but The Lair comes before any friendship. We are all friends here, except for maybe one, and we all know that should the benefit of the Lair be more so without another one of us here, then killing them would be no pausing moment. These things just need to be done Salaok and I know you understand that. But I doubt if you are in the mental state any longer to carry out the tasks that we need of you.” Instantly Salaok’s hand slams down onto the table and small fragments of wood fly off into the air as the frustration of being doubted by those he had served with for so long becomes too much. One long deep breath is then all that sounds forth from his lips as silence fills the room in wait for his answer which takes it’s time in coming out. “You doubt my mental state? You don’t think that after seeing my wife murdered before my very eyes that I would be sane? You doubt that finding my son with his neck slit from ear to ear only six months after what happened to Lisha that I wouldn’t be right up stairs? Fuck you Quartz, I’m completely in control of my situation and have been since day one. I chose to leave the world behind and stay in my house. I wasn’t forced to do it, I chose it and I have my reasons for it.” “Which were?” asks Johnathon as he sits back in his chair folding his arms over his chest and tilting his head slightly to the side. “I didn’t feel ready to take control of things yet, but I am now.” Although not a spoken word, a rumbling comes from Quartz’s direction at Salaok’s response to the question which sparks Salaok to fire back at him instantly. “If you have something to say my dear friend, then say it to my face.” Pushing himself off the window ledge, Quartz turns himself around and looks him straight in the eye as he lets a sigh slip out of his lungs. “You say you are ready to be in-control of your life again? Then why is it I found you today ready to blow your brains all over the lounge room’s floor? How can we trust you with our backs when we can’t trust you to keep your own life safe?” “You don’t need to trust me anymore Quartz! All you need to know is my track record in the past and know that is what I will forever be no matter what. If a job needs doing then I will not hesitate to do it and if I need to make a call, you can believe that it will be the same Salaok which had been making the calls ever since I was brought here to receive my Harvester title. You say that I’ve failed The Lair with my recruitment of Harvesters but just look at what has happened? One sits here now, defending our name in an area we used to run, but chose to leave and leave it to him. Another one was not only able to be apart of the murder of my wife, but was able to trap you and put you into a mental ward. He is the same man that you have been staring at out that window for the past five minutes, if I’m not mistaken?” Salaok grins as he finishes what he is saying, knowing full well that Silent Wind, the son of Quartz and fellow Harvester stands down on the grounds looking up at his father defiantly. Pushing himself back away from the table and standing up, Salaok leans on his hands and looks around the room at each of his peers. “If judging me is all this meeting was for, then you have all sorely wasted your time. You can doubt me all you like, but I am still the man I used to be and I will prove that to you all. By the end of this week you will remember exactly why it was that I was made the Harvester of Minds.” Salaok takes one final look around the room and then moves towards the window and sees exactly what he knew he would see. The masked assassin in which he had such a large roll in training stands with his arms folded and the wind blowing his black trench coat around behind him. His eyes hiding behind his black and red mask as always, and yet a single nod gives the recognition of Salaok’s appearance. Returning the nod, Salaok then turns and makes his way towards the door, brushing past Quartz in the process but neither man budging or retaliating. In complete silence, Salaok walks out of the throne room leaving his fellow Harvesters behind knowing full well that he must now do something big enough to gain their approval once again.
