The Way of the Samurai

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 "It is said that what is called ‘the spirit of an age’ is something to which one cannot return. That this spirit gradually dissipates is due to the world's coming to an end. In the same way, a single year does not have just spring or summer. A single day, too, is the same. For this reason, although one would like to change today's world back to the spirit of one hundred years or more ago, it cannot be done. Thus it is important to make the best out of every generation." As his eyes hovered over that paragraph something sprung up and hit him, as if he had been bitten. There was something more to these words, a secret meaning that was hidden from him but inside he knew it applied as if the words had been written in direct relation to him. As endless thoughts flashed through his mind, it struck him. His preparation had been wrong, all of it. He scanned through the paragraph again he smiled; it was a suggestive smile, hinting that an understanding had been reached. He had gone about it all the wrong way, from the minute he had finished his application form for the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament his preparation had been the same routine he had used to psyche himself before a big match so many times before. Therein lay the problem. His eyes wandered from his book finding his window. He set the book upon his bed as he rose. Standing now at the window he inhaled the deeply the cold air of the bitter Irish winter as he opened it, looking out over his beloved countryside. It occurred to him that he was no longer the same man that had wrestled in High Octane Wrestling and the Dominant Wrestling Organisation, looking back now he knew that was the height of his ability and that he had been in a steady decline ever since. On the other hand though, he was also no longer the shell of his former self that had competed in the World Wrestling Alliance. Upon joining their ranks he should have excelled, but events were in motion that could not be un-done. He had forgotten the love that once burned so brightly for his profession, drugs and alcohol had blurred his outlook, changed him irreversibly. But hindsight is a powerful gift; he had been given another chance at life. As he thought about it he broke into a cold sweat, haunting memories swept through his mind. This chance was certainly not going to be wasted. He conceded once again that perhaps it had taken him to reach this lowest ebb of his existence to grow enough as a man to correct his past mistakes, there was no perhaps about it. He felt good now, a feeling long overdue. The blood running through his veins was clean at last; finally the mist that had clouded his mind had lifted. His confidence returned more and more each day, he glanced over to his bedside table to his bible and smiled. This smile was pure, Chris B. the man known as SurReal had added a new weapon to his arsenal… faith, and it was a strong weapon indeed. And it was this newfound faith that made him feel at peace with himself at last. No, he was no longer the man who won many accolades as a wrestler several years previous. Nor, was he the lost individual who gave in to his demons in the more sinister recent past. He was a new man, a stronger man, pain had made him thus. He was a better man, confronting that pain had made that possible. Often in the past he had been arrogant but now he felt only pride. Pride was still fairly new to him but every day he found more and more to be proud of. I’m sure I don’t even need to mention the pride he found in his heritage; they say all Irishmen are proud to be Irish, he was no exception, his love of his homeland is unmatched. He found pride within his family as well, those he had revered and rebuffed in his youth were now the foundation upon which he began the long process of rebuilding his life. His bitter relationship with drugs was now firmly behind him. In his time he had struggled with various substances; cocaine, ecstasy, marijuana, alcohol and painkillers. After the World Wrestling Alliance released him from his contract just over twelve months ago his demons had once again gotten the better of him. This time, he suffered worse than before; he felt that nothing in his life justified his existence. He had pushed everyone he cared about, over and over again until, one by one, they could take no more and eventually they all turned their backs on him. He could have no complaints as he reflected over those thoughts; he had brought such misery upon himself. Entering the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament was the confirmation that he had returned, a better person without a shadow of doubt, all that was in question was whether or not he could still wrestle so well? How have the last year’s events affected his body? How have they affected his once innovative, fluid technical wrestling? At this point there was no answer he could give, only time will tell. "According to what one of the elders said, taking an enemy on the battlefield is like a hawk taking a bird. Even though it enters into the midst of a thousand of them, it gives no attention to any other bird other than the one that it has first marked." The draw had been made, through his weary Irish eyes he saw at last the bird which had been marked first. His name was Jack Murphy, another Irishman who at 6’5" would tower over SurReal, he held a slight weight advantage too, and as one of the tournament favourites this Murphy would be much more of a challenge than he had anticipated at this early stage of the tournament. Perhaps it was for the best that he had drawn a competitor such as Murphy, in his heart he was all too aware that Murphy would be too much for him. After the troubles of the last twelve months he questioned his sanity in getting in the ring with Jack Murphy. But he strengthened his resolve. For the first time in his storied career, SurReal went in to this match, not worried by the prospect of defeat. All he wished for was a good showing, to prove to himself that he could still wrestle, to prove to himself that the chance was their for him to improve his level once more, to try and reclaim former glory and reach the dizzy heights of his peak. Also though, he wanted to prove to the World Wrestling Alliance that they had made a mistake, this had played on his mind over the last few weeks. In a few short days from now he would get his chance. His broad Irish accent cut through the air as he thought out loud. "Either way, at least there will be one Irishman in Round 2" Quotes taken from Yamamoto Tsunetomo’s "Hagakure: The Way of the Samurai"
