Memories, Gold and Questions

22 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-09-22 The London Streetlamp sent its gentle gaze down across the dull, ordinary grey sea of pavement, languishing in mediocrity before it. A slow gust of wind made its way down the otherwise empty street, picking up unintended passengers of crisp packets, and fallen leaves, on its way down towards some unidentified location. The soft sound of pattering drips of water, falling from the arching signs of old shops, and new covers, made sure that the utter silence did not prevail. Was this place dead? It seemed to be a scene from some depiction of horror…evacuated city streets perhaps. Life suddenly burst into the empty road, as a small, well used Purgeot 106 came slowly down, sending its headlights forward, filling the dreary silence with its rusting engine…and caressing the relative darkness with this new haven of light. Music was heard from inside the car, the perpetual beating of some bass drum, often complemented by the sound of a hard bass line and snare drum. The windows of the car were closed though, and thus it would be impossible to realise what the tune was. The light from the car revealed an old woman sitting in a doorway. She held a blanket over her, and a bottle of Imperium Vodka sat idly beside her, nearly empty. She wore a woolly hat, and a brown overcoat over her frail body. She muttered to herself. Delusions of grandeur perhaps. She was once something. She told her secrets to the silence…her whispers of greatness to the stars. They were avid listeners, and they would never interrupt her. Around her wrist, a gold watch. Old, the face scratched by the inevitable bustling of time. It didn’t work any more…but it was worth more to her than any other possession in the world. She seemed to regard it with an apathy, deserving of companionship…and together, she and her guardian, her bastion of rememberance, lay confused and lost in this world of fast pace and high power. They didn’t belong here…she knew that. But she still whispered her dreams and memories to the wind… Suddenly, a sparkle caught the eye of this lovely scene. This quiet, romantic scene of the perpetual dance of relative silence, darkness and stillness. The tranquil urban garden was introduced to a bright light. One of the windows above this street glared on. Even the old woman, lost in her monologues, was interested enough to peer up through the darkness to this new haven of light. A figure moved, a shadow behind the blind covering the window. It stood, looking down at something. It then looked as if it took a seat, and began to study methodically something that had pressed its mind enough, to wake from its inevitable slumber. This was London. The once great bastion of civilisation in the western world. Named by the Romans, and created through adversity by people of high character. Even now, beyond its days of great grandeur, it stood as a symbol of rich character. Multi-cultural, multi-facetted and until recently, the greatest financial center of the world. It was still glared upon with envy by world leaders, and looked upon with hope by those looking to scramble from their shores of war and poverty. This street, this was Argyle Street…lying just off of the busy commercial haven of Oxford Street. It had known some great times, and now, in this silence…it was almost as if the very bricks were reminiscing. It was not only these inanimate objects though, that gave their silent stories to the silence. Nor was it the old woman, with her tales of things that may not have even happened… ************************ Over the road from where this old woman sat, covered in her blanket, shielding her from the inhospitable poisons of cold and silence…across from her, a gentle light peered through a window of a third storey window. It was nothing like the light that had briefly come on over the road, where the figure had come and gone, as quickly as it had needed. This light had been on the entire time. But it was the quiet, soft light of a lamp. Inside the room, a man sat at his desk, reading over something. His thoughts dwelt on the past, and perhaps he wasn’t even reading what lay in front of him. Various old magazines lay sprawled over the table, the light bringing them barely into view. This figure was Jonathan Alexander Spencer Kae. The current Evolution Champion of Headstrong Wrestling, and British Wrestler extraordinaire. But, as he sat in his chair, it wasn’t his title his thoughts dwelt on. It wasn’t the Headstrong Wrestling organisation he now held employ with. It was his memories. They haunted him now. Even in his palaces of businesses and mansions…his apartments scattered across the western world…his homes over the east…even here, he wasn’t free from his memories. Why did he think so hard on them? He thought he had escaped their glare when he hung up his wrestling trunks to focus on more important matters. He had taken Kae Enterprises to new levels of financial achievement…things his father would have been very proud of. Why, then, had he been forced back into this game of physical battling? Into wrestling? What had it been that he couldn’t say no to… Of course he knew…it was the gold. He had always craved recognition. The economic journals, and financial analysts could give him praise about his endeavours in the world of oil, commercial enterprise and the ever moving world of stocks and shares…but that simply hadn’t been enough. It had always been about him, and always would be…and he needed this business, this wrestling enterprise to satisfy his own lust for glory. That was why he had returned to the ring. Returned to a small company named the VWF. Become World Champion. But then, it had always been more…world recognition. The HSW gave him that gateway into the world of high TV deals, and national recognition. And now, he did hold the gold…but once again…that wasn’t what his thoughts dwelled upon. What was he reading? The view came into the light of the lamp, as it was revealed. A page in a magazine…quite old…maybe five or six years in the past. It was him. A younger, more vibrant Jonny Kae, standing atop the turnbuckle, with the ICWF World Heavyweight Title around his waist. Good times. Good times. ”Good Times” The words had escaped his lips before he’d had a chance to think of them. That was the trance he was in. Such a past…a past he had left behind so effortlessly…thinking it had been over. But of course, it wasn’t. “Never Over. How could it be? Who was I kidding?” This time his words didn’t surprise him. There was something comforting in this silence, of hearing his own words speak his thoughts. He had won that title nine times…been a legend. Then, three years later…it was all forgotten. Afterall, who remembers the heavyweight Boxer who retires, three years after he has gone? But that was what this was all about, wasn’t it? Legacies and remembrance. He wanted to be a real legend… Suddenly, Jonny Kae’s stupor was broken abruptly by the ringing of the telephone. It sent its annoying tone out into the room, destroying forever, this silence that had wrapped him in these thoughts. He was startled, and almost jumped in his chair, but then, regaining his composure… “Who in the hell is calling at this time?” He took a look at his watch. Four thirty in the morning? What on earth did someone want in these early hours? He lifted the receiver from its place on the desk, and put it to his ear. “What? It was Michael. His brother. He had been a part of this past too…until Jonny had so ruthlessly betrayed him. But, then, he should have seen that coming. Michael got that title shot before Jonny, and Jonny simply couldn’t see his younger brother taking his spotlight now, could he? “Well, why the hell are you calling at this hour? Michael explained to his brother that it was only half past ten over in Chicago…at the Kae Enterprises headquarters. But still…what was this important? “Ok. Well, what on earth do you want?” Michael wanted to check up on Jonny. No one had seen him training, or at any house shows in ages. Not since the HSW PPV, Global Domination, had Jonny Kae stepped into a wrestling ring. The brief stint with an organisation named UCW had come to an abrupt end, when Kae had no showed for his matchup against Supernova. Michael was worried about his brother’s preparations. Coming up, he had the HSW World Champion, Deszaras in one on one action…and this wasn’t a match to be taken lightly. “Oh Mike…you worry too much little brother. That reprobate won’t stand much of a chance when he steps into the ring with the Most Perfectly Attuned Body in HSW. It’s all in hand Mike.” His brother wasn’t convinced. Afterall, he was a Kae. They could smell suspicion a mile off. Michael Kae pleaded with his brother to come over to Chicago. They had Trent over there already, waiting anxiously to get into the ring with Jonny, and prepare him for what could be the biggest match since his return to the ring. Michael was right, of course. Jonny wasn’t ready. He had been wallowing in his self pity…his remembrance of days gone by. He had been so preoccupied with the past. How could he ever emulate the things he’d done before? How could he become that great warrior again? That fire had been taken out of him, he thought. How could he get that back? Not even when he had won the Evolution Title had that fire come back. It had been a hard road, to hold onto the title while in this frame of mind…and now he was questioning the very reasons for him being a professional wrestler. He didn’t need the money. He already owned enough companies under the umbrella Kae Enterprises banner to occupy himself with work. Why did he need this any more? The quest for gold? He wasn’t sure even about that any more. Afterall, the winning of this Evolution Title hadn’t been nearly as addictive as his nine title victories in ICWF. Winning the world title in the VWF hadn’t satiated his hunger. So…why was he still in this business? Michael’s voice interrupted his rhetoric in his mind. He was worried about Jonny, in more than just in ring ability. He needed his family. Their mother was coming over to Chicago to live with the family again. Old wounds had been healed. It was a great time for the family, and now, now Jonny should come over to make the reunion complete. They had stood tall at Global Domination, until the World Champion, Deszaras had intervened, and destroyed the wonderful picture of family bliss. Michael reminded Jonny it had been Deszaras who had been around every corner, and that it had been this world champ who now stood in their way again…this time on a much grander stage than a single wrestling organisation. This was Super Series… “I know brother. I told you, everything is fine. I…I’ll be ready. I always am, aren’t I?” A forced smile crossed Jonny Kae’s lips. He was trying to convince Michael of his own intentions, but maybe he was trying to convince himself too. “I’ll be ready for it. Trust me.” Michael was made of much sterner stuff than to accept a half assed commitment like that. It had already been arranged. Tickets had been bought for Jonny Kae to come over to Chicago, and a limo was coming over in the morning to pick him up. Within eighteen hours, he would be at the Kae Enterprises Headquarters, with his brother, and maybe even…his mother? It had been years since they had talked. It was a long time since they had even pondered communication. She had been the major reason behind his rush to success in this business. She had been the instrument behind his victorious domination of the ICWF. His manager…his mentor…she had taken he and Michael Kae to the tag team gold on many occasion. And now, she was there? Waiting for him? Something reassuring about all that… He smiled warmly at the prospect. How could he say no? “Well…I…I don’t know Mike.” There was no room for discussion. It was done…a sealed deal. The limo driver had been told, and was on his way already, from the company. Jonny finally decided to surrender to all of this. Besides…what was the worst that could happen? “Ok little brother. Ok. You win. I suppose I’ll see you tomorrow then?” Yes, was the answer. And then, before he went, Michael told Jonny those words that meant so much. He loved him. He was proud of Michael. Even through all the things that Jonny Kae had done to him…both in wrestling and out…he had come back to him. He had come back into his arms…and they were together again. Perhaps there was hope afterall? He wouldn’t know until he was there… Kae put the receiver down, and couldn’t help a smile crossing his face. Time to pack. Time to prepare. To go to Chicago…his home…his headquarters. The family refuge for the time being…time to rebuild his life… ****************** The black limousine drove swiftly through the empty London streets. It was only six in the morning, and the gentle murmer of the business world was only starting to arise. There was no traffic at this time…and that meant for a smooth ride. Jonny Kae sat in the back, caressed by the black leather seats of this oh so familiar ride. He didn’t know the driver…it didn’t matter. He didn’t care who the driver was. He cared about seeing Michael again. The caress of the sunlight, coming over the landscape was somewhat reassuring to Jonny Kae. The dawning of a new day, kind of reminded him of the dawning of a new saga in the Kae legacy perhaps? He was enthused with the excitement of reuniting the family. It had been so long! Of course, he still had reservations about how he would go about his wrestling career…but the family took front seat in this journey up through memory lane to the present. He remembered first winning the tag team gold. His first title. With Michael Kae at his side. They had been so happy…so full of life, exhuberance. The fans had cheered them on…every week coming out, winning match after match. He had even exelled in singles competition…winning the intercontinental title. But that was then… He then remembered the pain that came years after his betrayal of Michael Kae. Jonny Kae was already a five time world champion…and he couldn’t stand to see Michael take the title. He had still been at the top of his game, and Michael Kae had been given the shot before him. It was outrageous. He had put his brother in hospital…in hospital for nine months. And his brother had vowed never to come back into the wrestling ring, and had disowned Jonny. They weren’t brothers after that. Not for so long…until now. He had never been humble enough to apologise, and he thought he never would…but he was sure Michael forgave him. He simply couldn’t let Michael take the spotlight…that’s what it all boiled down to. The years of training…the years of moving up the ranks. He had become an icon in the company. And no one would take that away from him. Then, his mother. She had helped him screw over Michael. Together, they had gone on to dominate the company. She had bought it, and he had reigned as the world champion almost untouchable. Then, abruptly, she had sold the company, and left. And he left too, soon after that. She had never said anything after that to him. He never knew why she sold the company, or why she had become so distant. Perhaps this was a time to find out. Or, if they could forget about it all, and start fresh…that would be fantastic. The driver stopped suddenly. They were there…Heathrow Airport. Jonny Kae wondered why they weren’t using the private jet. Maybe because the pilot wasn’t available at the time. Well, he would just have to put up with flying first class on one of these dingy airlines. American Airlines was his flight. The driver opened the door for him, and handed Jonny his tickets, and opened the boot. He carried Jonny’s luggage into the airport, and to the checkout lady, pushing past the others in the queue. Of course, he deserved to be first. He always did. They checked his bags in, and Jonny bid his driver farewell, with the knowledge, that his regular driver, and Jolene, would be waiting for him in Chicago. That was reassuring again. In all of this, he had forgotten about his pretty flower…his little vivacious secretary…Jolene. He had missed her. Though of course he wouldn’t tell her. He still maintained that if she slacked in any of her responsibilities, he would have no reservations in firing her. But it would still be comforting to know that she was there…that he would see her. Something about her…it made him happy. Happiness that eluded him most of the time. But, that’s not important at the moment he thought. The family…that’s where this journey leads. Kae stepped through to the airport lounge, as he looked at the times of the flight. It was boarding in ten minutes. That was good timing by his driver. Best go through to departures now. As he went, Jonny looked forward to sleeping on the plane. He might even dream about the old days…or Jolene. He would dream about something pleasant he decided. There was too much unpleasantness to give into that… The security guard searched Jonny Kae as he stepped through the metal detector. Something had gone off. They agreed it was his belt buckle, and let him through. On to departure lounge. Jonny sat, waiting for the call to get onto the plane. It eventually came, and he joined the bustling crowd looking to get onto this plane. How common, he thought. These people pushing and shoving to get to their seats as quickly as possible. What a state of affairs. They had no class…no real upbringing. Not quite as bad as the Americans…but bad enough. On the plane, Kae was shown through to first class, and given a window seat. It was comfortable at least, and there was time to sleep, he knew. He immediately ordered a bottle of single malt delivered to his table, and a small glass. He pushed the blinds on the window down, and rested the seat back a bit. Have a couple of drinks, and drift off…that was the plan. It was going to be a long flight…and tonight, they would be together…and could prepare for the match.
