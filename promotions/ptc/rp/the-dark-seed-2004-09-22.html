The Dark Seed

22 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-22 How well do you adjust to change? What do you do when you wrestle your first match as a singles wrestler following the dismantling of your dominant tag team? What do you do when you don’t have your partner in the corner, ready to tag in and take over the match? What do you do when faced with adversity? If you’re the Latch Key Kid, apparently you don’t adjust too well. His first match following the temporary demise of the Lunchroom Patrol was his first round match in the GTT4. Latch lost a close match, but he still lost. Doughboy wasn’t there in the corner to make the tag. Nobody was. And Latch fell. Was it because of Doughboy’s absence? Was it because he was wrestling in his first real singles match in nearly a year? The world may never know. Heck, Latch himself may never know. But one question remains. What do you do when faced with adversity? Do you sit back and feel sorry for yourself? Do you wallow in self-pity? Do you wonder, what-if, just…what-if? If you’re the Latch Key Kid, no, you don’t. You have no time to. Not when you’ve been offered another chance at life in the tournament. You take that chance; you make up your mind to have a better showing than last time. And you get off your ass and you face Hirokazu. Except, like many things in life, it’s unfortunately not that easy. No matter how grateful you are for a second chance, no matter how much you appreciate being picked to move on, you can’t get past that little bit of doubt in the back of your head. It feeds on your emotions, and it plays with them. And if you let it go for too long, it can consume you. Doubt is not a nice thing to have to think about. Self-doubt is even worse. No successful wrestler should ever doubt themselves. But should doesn’t always happen. “I don’t know, Frank. I’m glad I get another shot at it, don’t get me wrong, but part of me can’t help but think that - ” “Well, that’s where you get into trouble, Brad,” Frank replies. “You can’t let those things get to you. Sure, you ran into some bad luck in the first round, but you took the loss like a man and that’s a good thing. Now you’ve been given another go at it. Make the most of it.” “And I plan to, Frank. But I can’t shake this feeling that I don’t really belong here. Maybe this whole singles thing isn’t for me. There’s got to be somebody else in XUW that I can team up with. At least until Doughboy gets back.” We find ourselves back in Frank’s Coffeehouse, of course, site of so many of the Latch Key Kid’s emotional struggles over the past two years. Though busy enough with running the shop and making the usual rounds, Frank always seems to have time for the Latch Key Kid and Doughboy. As Doughboy is nowhere to be found, that just leaves more time for the Latch Key Kid. Latch sips from his mug of latte while Frank sighs and taps his right index finger against the side of his head. “That’s doubt, Brad. It’s planted its seed in your head, and your brain is feeding it unwillingly. Gotta do something about that, or it’s going to take over completely. And this isn’t just any sort of doubt, this is self-doubt. It’s the worst kind there is. Once your thoughts water that seed even just a little bit, it’ll start to grow. It can consume your mind quickly, and once it really sets in, that’s it. You’ll be defeated before you even wrestle that match.” “But I don’t have to stay in singles action. I can always put out a feeler for a tag team partner in XUW, and see what happens.” Would Latch really turn his back on Doughboy, even though Dough is temporarily out of action? With no word from Dough, it’s impossible to know how long he’ll be gone. It’s never been in Latch’s nature to turn away from a friend, even when that friend wasn’t around. The very foundation of the Lunchroom Patrol was built on friendship and a mutual understanding of what it means to be a team. Dough and Latch had been through trials and tribulations, good times and bad. And at one point or another, each needed the other to be there for them. Surely there would be another wrestler Latch could turn to, to team up with in Doughboy’s absence. Who wouldn’t jump at the chance to team with a multiple-time tag team champion? Who wouldn’t want to try to build a new team with one of the most successful tag team wrestlers in XUW history? No, Latch would be able to find somebody else. That is, if he had the desire. “Replace Doughboy? Are you nuts?” “You’re right, Frank.” Latch pauses to sip his latte. “I don’t think I would seriously consider that. It’s not Doughboy’s fault he left like that. He’s tending to some personal business, and I know the break will do him well. It wouldn’t be right to find another partner, even if it is just temporary.” The fans would see it as Latch turning his back on Doughboy. Latch himself might even see it that way. And Doughboy, well, even if he did see it that way, he would never admit it. Dough always was a pretty humble guy, quick to forgive any of Latch’s earlier lapses of judgment, and always taking things in stride. Dough would most likely never admit how much that would hurt him, but Latch knew. And that would haunt his mind. “It wouldn’t be right, Frank. I think that’s what it boils down to. I just couldn’t do that to Dough, not while he’s away.” “And there is some sense left in your head, I see. Now the question turns to, what are you going to do? Are you going to mope about your tag team partner being away? About losing your first round match?” “No,” Latch shakes his head, “moping won’t really do any good, anyway. I guess I just need – ” Latch is interrupted by the ringing of Frank’s cordless phone, which is sitting on the table. Frank picks it up and answers it. “Frank’s…Oh, no…not at all? Well, do what you have to do…no, no, no, it’s okay…well, sort of, actually. Yeah, Nancy called in…Bill, also. No, it’s okay. I’ll make do. Just work on getting yourself better…okay. Thanks for calling.” Frank hangs up the phone and sets it back on the table with a sigh. “Bad news, Frank?” “That was Betsy. She’s sick, can’t come in today.” “Well, at least you still have Nancy.” Frank shakes his head. “That’s what I was telling Betsy. Nancy called in, too. Bill couldn’t make it in, either. So that’s why I’m by myself right now.” “Well, Frank, you should have said something earlier. No sense in you sitting here talking to me when you’ve got a shop to run.” “Look around, Brad.” Frank pans his arm out in an arc. “It’s not exactly hopping right now. Although, now that you mention it, perhaps you could - ” “Yes?” “Help me out a little? You know, simple stuff. Behind the counter, make coffee, stuff like that.” “I don’t know, Frank. I’ve never really worked in - ” “Nonsense. You’ll do fine. C’mon, let me show you.” Frank and Latch walk over to the counter, and Frank motions Latch in ahead of him. Frank shows Latch how to use the coffee pot first. “Now, it’s usually pretty easy. But every now and then it will cut out on you for some reason. It’s kind of old; I’ve been meaning to replace it. Actually, Nancy was supposed to pick one up on her way in today. I guess I’ll just have to take care of that when I head to the bank.” “Tomorrow morning, huh?” “Who said anything about tomorrow morning?” “Well, you just said you have to run by the bank and then pick up a new coffee pot, and since nobody else is here, I just - ” “Hey, you’ll do fine,” Frank says, as he grabs a money pouch and his car keys. “I’ve got faith in you. If I didn’t, I wouldn’t leave you here by yourself while I go to run my errands.” “I’m sorry. Did you just say you’re going to leave me here by myself?” “Well, I see your ears are working. Keep it up, Brad.” “But, Frank,” Latch protests, “I’ve never worked - ” “A register. Right. Better show you how to do that real quick.” Frank sets the money pouch and his keys back on the counter, and gives Latch a quick course in using the cash register. “See, it’s simple, really. Punch in the cost of the order, then how much they give you, and then hand them their change. Just make sure you never, and I mean never, hit that red button on the left.” “What does it do?” “Best if you never find out. Okay, I’m out of here!” Frank grabs the money pouch and keys and heads for the door. “See you in a while. Good luck.” “But, wait, Frank! I don’t - ” But it’s too late, as Frank is already out the door. Latch sighs and pulls a barstool up to the register. He sits down and starts to tap his fingers on the counter. After a few moments, a scuzzy-looking fellow approaches the bar. “Hey, man, don’t I, like, know you from somewhere? Not that it matters. Can I just get a refill on this coffee?” “Sure,” Latch says, taking the kid’s mug from him. He turns around and walks to the coffee pot. “Don’t jam, don’t jam, don’t jam,” he mutters to himself, while filling the mug with coffee. He sighs with relief when he finishes without the coffee pot jamming. When finished, he walks back to the counter and hands it to the kid. “Here you go. Nice and fresh.” “Thanks, man. I appreciate it.” Latch smiles and nods as the kid heads back to his table, his friends and a round of poetry. “Well, that wasn’t so tough. I think I can handle that until Frank gets back soon.” Again, he starts to tap his fingers. After a few moments, he gets bored and decides to start cleaning up. Even though the counter doesn’t look too dirty, Latch grabs a rag and starts to clean the counter. Satisfied, he sets the rag down and grabs a towel, tosses it over his shoulder and heads out from behind the counter. He spots a few empty mugs on vacated tables, and heads to collect them. A group of customers stops him next to their table. “You’re that Latch Key Kid guy, aren’t you?” a twenty-something girl asks him. “I am, yes.” “What are you doing here?” the guy next to her asks. “Well, I was in here visiting with Frank, and then he had to go run some errands. He left me in charge for some reason, even though I’ve never worked here.” “Well, he must have known you’d do a good job. Anyway, welcome to the team.” “The team?” Latch replies. “Yeah, man. The team. That’s what Frank calls his employees. He says it makes things feel less informal and more tight-knit. I kinda dig it, myself. Wish my boss was cool like that.” “He really calls his staff a team? That’s interesting.” “Yeah,” the girl jumps in, “they really like it, too. He’s a really laid-back guy, you know. He’ll go out on a limb for his emp…err, his team members. Someone needs some time off or a pay advance, he’s usually cool with it. He must be a great guy to work for.” “I wouldn’t know, really.” “Sure you do. You’re part of the team now. You’re in, man. Just like that. Welcome aboard.” “But I already told you, I don’t work here. I’m just filling in.” “Nobody just fills in around here, dude. Frank doesn’t allow fillers. He’s got faith in you, that’s why he walked out and left you for the next few hours.” “Did you say hours?” “Yeah. That’s usually about how long it takes him,” another girl says. “But he just said he was going to the bank and to pick up a coffee pot,” Latch says worriedly. “Guess he didn’t mention the hardware store. Or the bakery. Or…well, I guess that’s probably about it for his run today.” “Oh, no. I don’t know if I can handle this for a few hours.” “It’s easy, man. People walk in, you give them coffee, they pay for it. Other than that, just chill out, dude. I see you in here plenty, and you always look so calm. It’s no different now, except for you being in charge and all.” “Thanks, I think. Anyway, I should probably get back to work.” Latch walks off, shaking his head. He stops to pick up the empty mugs from a few tables, and wipes each table off in turn. He puts the mugs in the sink behind the counter, and sits back down on the barstool. The next two hours pass in a bit of a blur for Latch. As we see the hands of the clock tick around in circles, Latch pours coffee, serves snacks, successfully uses the cash register, cleans up, talks with customers, and manages to keep the coffee shop running. As Latch is busy serving a customer and talking wrestling with him, the door opens and Frank walks in. He walks up to the counter, unseen by Latch, and leans against it. When the customer walks off, Frank speaks up and startles Latch. “A fresh latte, please. And take one for yourself.” Latch spins around and looks surprised to see Frank there. He looks at the clock on the wall and shakes his head as if to clear cobwebs from it. “Wow. It’s already been over two hours?” “Yep. And from the looks of things, I think I could’ve left you here all day and you would’ve done fine. Looks like you did a good job.” “It was easier than I thought, Frank. Not that running a place like this is easy, I’m sure, but after a little while, I got the hang of it. And I was really getting into the swing of things.” “And the customers? They treated you well, I hope?” “Oh, yeah. In fact, a few of them even welcomed me to the team. You never told me that’s what you call your employees. All this time I’ve been coming in here, and I didn’t even realize you made everybody feel so close.” “There’s probably a lot about this place that you didn’t realize before, Brad. And now you know some of it. And you know what else? You didn’t burn the building down; heck, you didn’t even scare off any customers. And that just proves that you really can do something different that you’re not used to. Thanks for filling in today, Brad. I knew you would do a great job, and you just proved it to yourself.” “You know, I never even looked at it like that, Frank." "And that little seed of self-doubt? Where is it now?" "That seed? I think it disappeared about two hours ago. It needed to. If it hadn't gone away, I never would've been able to handle this place without you. So, thank you." “And thank you,” Frank replies. “Now how about getting those two lattes I asked for?” Latch laughs and pours two mugs of coffee, setting one on the counter in front of Frank. Latch wipes the counter down and tosses the towel over his shoulder. “Say, Frank,” Latch asks hesitantly, “where’s that new coffee pot you were going to pick up?” “Oh, yeah,” Frank says, “I guess I’ll have to get that tomorrow, when I run to the bank. It must’ve slipped my mind.” “Frank…” Latch starts, then stops. Then he smiles and nods his head, and shakes Frank’s hand. “Thanks.”
