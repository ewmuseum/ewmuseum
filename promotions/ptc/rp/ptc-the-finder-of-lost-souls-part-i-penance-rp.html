The Finder of Lost Souls: Part I (Penance RP)

4 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
As her hand brushes against my crotch, my first thought is that she’s young, way too young to be cupping my balls so knowledgeably, with nary a hello. The sign on the door says “21 to enter”, but I’m not buying that one for a second…though for some reason it’s hard to peg an exact age on her. Likely the lighting has something to do with it; dim and red, it’s hard to see much anything in the bar that isn’t right in front of your nose, and the evanescent fog of cigarette smoke doesn’t make it any easier. What I do see, sad to say, isn’t exactly unappealing. Giving her the once over, it’s her eyes that immediately grab me, eyes like a lighthouse beacon shining through the smoke and the dark, inviting, promising shelter from the storm. They’re outlined in heavy black, the eyes, to match the lips, the pageboy hair, and, well, just about everything else. Mosquito bite tits inefficiently fill the bust line of this dress she’s got on, but seeing that it cuts off about eight inches above the knee, it’s unlikely that she’s expecting many guys to be fixated on her chest tonight. I’m not aiming to be one of them, though, whatever it is she may be expecting. My life, it’s already full of trouble, all the way full, with no vacancies, and shit, I read the papers. Society isn’t exactly forgiving of sex offenders. Better a goddamn murderer than a sex offender. So when I grab her hand gently by the wrist and politely, as far as such an action can be considered polite, remove it from the immediate vicinity of my scrotum, I feel only the smallest twinge of regret when she looks up at me, her expression quizzical, slightly hurt. But like I said, no vacancies. So sorry, my little gothic Lolita, so sorry: there is no room at the inn. The silence between us is tense as she unyieldingly holds my gaze, and for a second I fumble for a means to extract myself from the situation. It suddenly occurs to me, in a burst of inspiration, to flash her my ring. “Sorry honey,” I tell her, smiling inwardly, trying to sound apologetic as I needlessly readjust the gold wedding band on my finger, “but I’m a married man.” I try to step past her, wanting to go sit at the bar, but walking backwards, she manages to stay in front of me. She bites her bottom lip, smiling slightly in practiced flirtation. “That’s never stopped any of my other customers,” she says slyly, reaching toward me once more. I flinch backward. Something’s not right about this one, and it’s not just her age. The sound of her voice, there’s something about it that seems forced, unnatural. Panicked, I look at her again, this time really looking at her, past the ostentatious show of seduction, past the garish make-up, past both the role she plays and the mask she hides behind. Cosmetics can’t disguise everything, after all, and when my eyes fall upon her Adam’s apple, unpronounced as it is, it’s all I can do to keep myself from retching. “Is something wrong?” she asks, but I’ve already staggered past her, my mind reeling and with it the entire world surrounding me. I’m suddenly aware of the dryness of my throat, feeling almost foreign, as if it belongs to somebody else. I feel my stomach clench itself like a fist, feel my esophagus shivering with revolt, and I’m gagging by the time I burst into the men’s room, legs giving out on me, stumbling chest first into the sink. Grabbing the basin, I pull myself up, bracing myself against it, trying to get my wits about me again, trying to slow down my thoughts, which have abandoned themselves to chaos. A few deep breaths are all it takes to free me from the grip of nausea and I can already feel my strength returning, yet for some reason I stay there, leaned up against the sink, unmoving, staring. Staring at my reflection in the mirror. “It’s dirty,” I mutter, running my hand across the surface. It’s surprising how dirty the mirror really is, so caked with accumulated grime that I can barely see my reflection. Seeing the outline of my face, it strikes me that it’s almost as if it were my face that was so dirty, not the mirror at all. The thought, for some reason, is a particularly chilling one, and automatically, reflexively, I find myself frantically scrubbing away at the mirror with the palm of my hand. My fingers are hardly capable of smudging the dirt, let alone removing it, so I run water from the tap, flinging droplets in all directions as I move my hand from the sink to the mirror and back again. After a good minute of work, I finally manage to reveal a swatch of unsullied reflection. I’ve opened a portal to another world, a universe that runs parallel with our own, and I see bloodshot eyes stare out at me from across the void. I meet the gaze, eye to eye, believing that if I look deep enough, probe to the very bottom of the gaping maw, of this other that is not an other, I can pierce through the veil of eternity. “Fucking crazy,” says a voice to my right, yanking me unceremoniously from my reverie. That girl, boy, whatever she was, must have really gotten under my skin, I figure, because I hadn’t even noticed this guy walk up to the sink next to mine. That had to be it, what else could it be? At first, I think he’s talking about me, pointing out my obvious insanity for the world to see, but thankfully, mercifully, the expression on his face is not one of accusation. Which is such a relief, because what the hell had gotten into me, anyway? “The horror,” it sounds like he says. “The horror.” “What?” I ask him, uncomprehending. Or, perhaps more precisely, afraid of comprehending. “The whore,” he repeats, his New York accent clearer now. “The fucking tranny whore. She’s been hanging around this place for the past month, all over every guy that walks through the door. I know we’re supposed to be a more open-minded generation and all, but I don’t mind telling you, that kind of thing freaks the hell out of me.” I let out a weak laugh, fairly certain, for the moment, that I’m not losing my mind after all. And the stranger, he seems to be a pretty alright guy, in spite of his appearance from out of nowhere. He’s got this middle-aged businessman feel to him, one of those guys that seems to have been destined for the life: tall, authoritative, and conservatively dressed, I watch him run his fingers through his salt and pepper hair as he looks into the mirror. All in all, this guy seems to be a bastion of normality, which, at this point in the evening, is definitely something worth clinging to. “Yeah, fucking crazy,” I agree, prompting another laugh from him. I have to laugh as well. Fucking crazy is right. Fucking craziest night of my life, no doubt. “Say, you want to talk over a beer?” he asks, turning toward me. I must have looked hesitant, because the next thing he says is, “Don’t worry, I’m not coming onto you. Not everyone in this country has lost their goddamned minds. Just looking for some friendly conversation. Christ knows, it’s scarce enough in this shit-hole.” I voice my acceptance, and we leave the bathroom, before making our way to the nearest vacant table. There’s a waitress there to take our order at almost the instant we sit down, and we both ask for domestic drafts. In the air, on all sides, the smoke hangs thick and noxious, streaming from the ends of anonymous cigarettes. Looking around, I’m surprised by how many people I see sitting by themselves, staring vacantly ahead as they sip from their mugs without enthusiasm. I’d been to funerals that were more lively than this place, so it’s no wonder this guy was looking for some friendly conversation. “So I’ve never seen you around these parts before,” he says to me, as the waitress sets down our drinks on the table. “To what does this fine establishment owe the pleasure of your business?” “Long story short, I got into a fight with my wife,” I tell him matter-of-factly, though even as I say it, I’m trying not to think about it. The last thing I want to do is reopen the still-fresh wound. “It wasn’t pretty, I’ll tell you that much” “It never is,” he says, nodding sympathetically. “I needed something to take my mind off of it, so I decided to come out for a drink,” I begin to say, though I stop suddenly to raise a hand to my forehead, confused. “Though to tell you the truth, I can’t really remember coming all the way out here.” “Sounds like you knocked back a couple before you got here,” he says with a laugh. “I figured you must have just wandered in, though. You don’t seem like the type that usually frequents this place.” I’m about to ask him just exactly what type of person he’s referring to, but before I get the chance, I let out an involuntary shudder as I see the waif who approached me earlier, sitting all cozied up next to this corpulent mound of flesh, this fat-ass motherfucker at the far end of the bar. She whispers something into his ear, and he lets out a chortling laugh, his jowls quivering with perverse glee. “That’s disgusting,” I mutter, turning my gaze away. I’m somewhat dismayed to see my new acquaintance smiling back at me. It’s a good-natured smile, but there is something lurking behind it, something dangerous. Threatening. “You don’t even know the half of it, my friend” he says, leaning forward, as if eager to dispense a particularly choice tidbit of information. “That fat motherfucker she’s talking to is her father.” “No.” Just no. That’s all. There are some things the mind’s just not equipped to process, not without adequate preparation. So you don’t believe it. He can’t be serious. It’s got to be some kind of morbid joke. “You’re joking, right?” “No joke. A whore child of a whore mother. He was her pimp, now he’s pimping out both of them. Heard him bragging about it a couple nights ago, how dressing the boy up like that brings in more money. Lets him play both sides of the fence.” He talks as if giving a sermon, speaking in a strange moralizing tone. “Yeah, supporting children is easy when you got no morals. Just paint ‘em up and they can support themselves. With a little kickback tossed your way, of course.” “Wouldn’t know it just looking at ‘em, though,” he continues, making a flippant gesture with his hand. “That’s the thing about appearances. They can be deceiving. There are things in life one’s better off not knowing.” “I don’t understand how you can just talk about this so casually,” I say frantically, making a sincere attempt to apprehend it all, all this madness. “Why doesn’t anyone alert the authorities?” “That’s not how things work around here. Half the people in this room are involved in the sex trade. Or the drug trade. Take your pick.” “What about you?!” I burst out, unable to sit still and listen to this any longer. I have to fight the urge to reach across the table and grab this man by his collar. I don’t know why, but it feels as if I’m at the very limits of human psychological endurance. “You seem like a pretty respectable guy. How do you know all this stuff, about all these people?! What do you do anyway?!” “I suppose I am what you could call a finder of lost souls,” he tells me, with a real air of pride. Reaching a hand into his suit coat, he removes a business card and slides it across the table, keeping eye contact with me as he does so. I glance down at it, but my eyes have only just scanned the name at the top of the card, MARCUS ISCARIOT, before his next words grab a hold of my attention like a hand around my throat. “You seem to be troubled, friend. Is there anything you would like to get off of your chest?” “Wha- what exactly are you driving at?” I respond, startled and surprised at the defensive tone I hear in my voice. “I meant no offense. I merely have a sixth sense about these matters,” he apologizes, raising his hands in contrition. “If you don’t want to talk about it, I certainly won’t force you to.” “There’s nothing to talk about,” I spit back, eyeing him warily. “Alright, alright,” he says with a modest smile, before glancing casually down at his watch. He stands up reaching his hand across the table, which I accept automatically in a firm handshake, without really thinking about it. “I’m sorry to cut this short, but I have an important appointment I need to keep,” he explains, as our hands unclasp. “If you ever change your mind about…unburdening yourself…my number is on the card.” I want to tell him that will be unnecessary, but instead find myself nodding my head dumbly. And with that, he’s gone. For minutes after he leaves, I just sit staring into my drink, not knowing what to believe, if anything, and all I know for sure is that I’m scared, real scared, like a five year old with a monster in the closet, waiting to be devoured by this force beyond comprehension. It’s not just what he said, but the way he said it, so calm, nonchalant almost, and how could anyone say the things he said so calmly, even if they were lies? This inhumanity of humanity, this cancer, this blight on the human race. What really lurks beneath the surface of mankind? He was right. I don’t know, and I don’t want to know. Please don’t let me know... Please don’t let me know... Please don’t let me know... I start to think about going home, before I remember the fight earlier. I can’t go home, not yet, what would I say? Sitting up from the table with a jerk, I look around for something, anything, to support myself with, anything at all, anything at all. I can feel the cracks beginning, small at first, irradiating outward in concentric circles, threatening the infrastructure. I scan the bar, looking for a friendly soul, anybody, but all I see are the murderers, the rapists, the dealers in human flesh. No longer people, but dark reservoirs of evil, just waiting to be tapped. Just when I am ready to give up hope, my eyes fall upon an elderly gentlemen sitting at the end of counter. I have to do a double take, because I can hardly believe my eyes when I see the priest’s collar around his neck. This is certainly the last place I expected to see any evidence of the church, but who’s to say that miracles aren’t possible. Unable to shake the thought that this man is heaven-sent, that my salvation is waiting in the wings, I walk over to the bar and take a seat next to him. “So tell me, Father,” I ask him somewhat nervously, causing him to turn his head in my direction. “What brings you to this Gomorrah?” “Tell me, my son, are you of the faith?” he inquires, a hint of a smile forming at the corner of his mouth. I don’t know how to respond at first, but then I begin, hesitantly. “If you asked me that question two hours ago, I could have answered yes fairly confidently. I’ve heard things tonight, though, such horrible things….” I look up at him, my lower jaw trembling. “This place, I can’t seem to shake the feeling…is it hell?” “Hell?” he repeats, his eyebrow raising inquisitively, his mouth tensing into a brief frown. “No, this isn’t hell. From my experience, the only hell on earth is the one you create for yourself.” “I’m really glad to hear you say that,” I say, and I can hear the quavering note of discord in my voice. “This is by far the worst night of my life, Father. I feel that I’ve gone as far as I can go, I’m at the brink, and I’m waiting, just waiting for something to push me over the edge.” “I see. It’s a common misconception of unbelievers that religion is a crutch for the weak. They fail to realize that the pursuit of a working spirituality is a hard, hard thing.” He smiles warmly back at me, a smile full of hope, and I feel my spirits raise. I begin to think that it was a very good idea coming to talk to this man. He continues speaking, arms folded out in an open gesture. “To be able to dedicate one's self so completely and unshakably to a faith in the face of all the evils of the world takes such strength.” “So is that why you come here? To test yourself?” I ask him hesitantly, not quite sure if I understand. “To test your faith?” “I don’t know if test is right word for it. Perhaps fortify is a better fit. To fortify myself against the powers of evil, in their myriad forms.” Turning, he looks me straight in the eye, passionate and triumphant. “For I know if I am capable of staring evil in the face without flinching, I truly have the grace of God on my side.” For some reason when he says this, I’m taken back, taken back to the bathroom and the mirror and staring, staring straight into the gaping maw. In a flash it all comes flooding back to me. I’m holding the revolver in my hand, pressed up against the butt of her jaw, and she’s screaming, oh god, the screams, they rend me asunder, but I do what I have to, she’s pleading with me, as if I were still in control. Shut the fuck up, I scream, shut the fuck up, how could you do this to me after fifteen years, and she’s crying and I’m crying and the floor drops out from underneath me, world spinning, and my mind is just not working, it’s not working, not in any logical sense. And she’s trying to explain herself, telling me she still loves me, that he’s just a friend, but I’ve already closed my eyes, tears streaming down my face, finger on the trigger, and it’s just a little pressure, that’s all, and then…the deafening discharge…and then…nothing, just the silence, the maddening silence. Eyes still closed, tears still streaming, I can feel the warm gush flow over my hand, feel the presence of this other disappear, as this liquid stream of life departs from her body. Oh my god, the blood. There’s so much blood. How much blood can the human body hold?! It’s all over me, my hands, my face, and I’m scrubbing them in the sink, but it won’t come off. It’s dirty, oh god, it’s so dirty. Oh god. Oh god. Oh god. “Oh god. Oh god. Oh god.” Coming back to reality, I realize I’ve been muttering these two words to myself, over and over. Turning to the Father, I see that he’s stepping back from me, his face twisted into a mask of primal terror. I turn to him, attempting to explain myself, reaching out my hands in supplication, but he’s already jerked away from me, running toward the door. I run after him, out the door, around the corner, as fast as I can, for as far as I can, in a delirium. It seems like I’ve been chasing him for hours before my feet finally give out from under me and I stumble in the street. Collapsing to my knees, I look up at his departing figure, tears streaming down my face. "Forgive me, father," I choke out, "...for I...I have sinned." But he is gone, and I feel for the first time in my life truly lost, not knowing what to do with myself. I've burned all my bridges. I have been cast out, exiled, the Mark of Cain is upon me. May God have mercy on my soul. I reach into my pocket, fumbling around to see if I can afford bus fare to anywhere, when my hand grabs a hold of something. Pulling it out, I see it's the card the man handed me earlier in the night. Looking down at it, I read the rest of it. It says: MARCUS ISCARIOT In the Esteemed Service of the High Priest Jacob Solomon. It is never to late to start anew. Dedicate yourself to a higher cause. Salvation awaits the most steadfast of sinners. An illogical calm descends upon me, as I grip the card reverently. And as I read the phone number at the bottom of the card, the thought crosses my mind that perhaps, just perhaps, there is some hope of penance awaiting me yet...
