Winners Never Quit

4 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-04 Day: 1 For most of the world fast approaches a season of holiday cheer, gift giving and meal sharing, yuletide fun, egg nog and mistletoe. Spending time with those you love and cherish. But for others, one hundred and twenty eight to be exact, fast approaches not Christmas, but GTT season. There will be not gifts to be had, only defeat, only victory. Loved ones are left at home, christmas trees abandoned undecorated, children spending family time over the phone. Sullen in the back seat of a taxi, feeling and, to be honest, looking rather Grinch'sh, is Johnny Lexicon. Just one of those one hundred and twenty eight people. Eyes of vivid blue peer out a half frosted window at the houses that pass by. Calgary is a nice city. The streetnames, shop signs, and familiar land marks bring many memories flooding back to the scarred warrior. A phone rings. "Hello?" Lexicon answers on the first ring. "Lex, it's Midas." "Oh." Audibly disheartened. "What's up?" His voice takes a dry disinterest. "When you comin' back to LA man, I've that guy from McDonalds chewing my ass, he loved the work we did for GTT5." "I'll be back in a couple days, stop in before the first round." "This can't wait that long, I need an answer by tomorrow at the latest. You have a fax?" "At my place, be there in a few minutes." Lexicon's eyes wander the interior of the cab. "I'll send you a copy of the proposal, I need this by tomorrow man. By the way…" "What?" Half irritated at having to inquire. "Congratulations on beating Toxic." Lexicon's eyes wander a bit, the corner of his lip turning up in a bit of a grin. "He's a bastard, he won't let me stand tall, it's not how the game works." "What's that supposed to mean?" "Hello?" "Nothing…" "Johnny? You okay bro?" The cab's door opens. "That's not your place is it?" The cab driver asks, but Johnny is already climbing from the seat. "Hey you owe me twenty bucks!" His long black coat nearly brushes the ground as his boots crunch the freshly falling snow. Cars and fire trucks clog the residential avenue, lights and worried faces add that needed touch of drama as Lexicon wanders through the maze of steel and strangers. "Buddy you owe me twenty bucks!" "Lex man, what's going on? Talk to me!" "I'll have to call you back." Breath pours from his mouth like smoke. Fishing in his pocket Lexicon drops a few bills on the ground for the pursuing cab driver and takes up a jog towards the epicenter of the disturbance. A dirty hand reaches shakily and lifts the money from the snow, the cab driver looks at Lexicon's back as he meanders around a group of spectators and then back to his fare. "There's like - Four hundred bucks here." "Excuse me." Lexicon pushes past a fireman. "Hey!" The man protests, an older black fellow with a grey mustache. Lexicon pushes the hand away and walks to what was once his house. "Hey! Ya need t'get back!" The fireman shouts. "This, this was my house." He says in a stunned voice. "This was your house?" "This was…" "This was your house?" "Yes!" "Well son I have some bad news, it seems that while you were out a fire caught in your house and it burned clear to the ground. It'll be about an hour before we're done here.." Blinking Lexicon looks at the man sardonically. "You had insurance, right?" "I'm never home…" Lex looks away. "I was here for an hour." His voice is lost as he stumbles towards the place his front door would be. A phone rings. "Hello?" Lexicon answers it on the first ring. "What's going on?" It's Midas again. "My house burned down." "You own a house in Calgary?" "Two bedroom town house with a pool. And now I've got one hell of a sun roof." Lexicon peers up through the snow flakes. "How am I supposed to fax you this proposal? "I'll think of something, I'll call you back." Day: 2 The early morning sun peeks through heavy curtains and stains the bed. Half naked and staring at the ceiling Lexicon suddenly blinks and looks towards the intrusion. His left hand falls to the standard bedside alarm clock and turns it towards him. 6:15 am. For the world the day is just starting, the sun spoke of new chances, promised a better yesterday, perhaps. But for Lexicon, it stretched into another hour, another minute, another day. A phone rings. "Hello?" Lexicon answers it on the first ring. "Hello mister Lexicon, this is your wake up call." Clearing his throat. "Thanks." "Have a great day, sir." His hand falls away, the flip phone nestled into his palm. After a long moment he stumbles from the bed, wincing as he moves the muscles in his bruised back, and groaning as he adds weight to a sore knee. He stumbles to the bathroom a mess and emerges a man. Collecting his long black coat he pulls a red scarf around his neck and leaves. "Johnny." She says evenly, those half asian eyes peering up at the wrestler through wire frame glasses, hand offered and taken by the warriors thick fingered weapon. "I'm sorry Johnny." She continues. "Your father was a very loved man and will be missed by many people." His head nodding Lex clears his throat. "Thanks." She turns to lead the way down a long darkly decorated hallway, her brown pony tail bouncing with each step, every sway of her hips. One of two large wooden doors stands open, permitting entrance into the room beyond. Outside the door is a sign settled on a tri-pod, and the aged face of Edward Lexicon smiles at his son. Johnny takes pause to look at the picture. "Johnny." Her voice calls him. "We're about to start." His eyes trail lazily through the door, up the aisle and to the open casket on the dias. "I'll… I'll be there in a minute." Nodding the woman steps to his side, a clear foot shorter she reaches up to rest a dainty hand on his shoulder. "I'm glad you came." She says quietly. "We all are, your mom hasn't stopped crying and Joey hasn't spoken since…" "I'll be there in a minute, Koryn." He says with a bit more assertion. She leaves without another word. His hand coming up to cover his mouth Johnny looks through that door, he looks at the backs of heads of the people he loves. His family he hasn't seen or spoken to in almost a year. Sacrifices were made in the pursuite of redemption. Koryn's hand touches Gail Lexicon's shoulder and her lips whisper something into her ear. The black veil turns around to look towards the empty entrance. Koryn's lips twist as Gail looks up at her in confusion. Day: 3 Sweat drips down Lexicon's shaved head, streamlining his frustration, dancing about the edges of his scar. A nasty line on the left side of his forehead, jagged and pink, it seperates his eye brow into three distinct portions. 'Character.' He calls it. 'Everybody has scars.' He reasons. 'Everybody had their weak moments.' He whispers to himself. 'Everybody but me!' He screams inside. Powerful shoulders roll methodically, he rises and falls with relentless reason. A machine working it's gears. Built for combat, designed for endurance, programmed for victory. A heaven sent instrument. The standard bedside alarm clock reads 3:45 am. 3:46. 3:47. He slams his hands into the floor. He rises and falls, pushing himself, he slams his palms into the carpet again. He pushes himself to his feet with a growl. It's snowing once again when Johnny steps over what was once the front door of his home. A home he rarely saw, a home he hadn't even fully unpacked. The flakes are thick and heavy, coating everything in a layer of mystique and angelic purity. The incadescant beam of Lex's flashlight plays across the rubble, the charred remains of a place that should hold memories, regrets even, but nothing. There were in fact very few things Johnny wanted in that home of his, and perhaps none of them were ever to be had again. It doesn't take much picking for Johnny to locate a burnt but otherwise intact tin box, A phone rings. "Hello?" Johnny answers on the first ring. "Johnny it's Koryn." "Hi." He replaces the lid on his box with dissapointment. "Johnny your mom's in the hospital, she tried killing herself." "What?" "I'll come pick you up and explain later." "No don't." "What? Why not?" "I don't want to see her like that." "She's your mother!" Johnny has no reply to offer so instead he barters silence. "Fine, be an asshole." Click. With a sigh he gathers his tin box and flashlight and heads for home. Day: 4 "Most of you won't make it in this business, most of you haven't got what it takes. Wrestling is fine, being a pro wrestler, so what? The point of everything is to make it, you work the mic and the ring, to win the belts and make the money. That's making it. And nine of every ten of you won't. The business is rough, it will eat you up if you let it and sometimes even if you don't, blue eyed pretty boys don't make it on a smile. This is something you have to work like a war, this isn't a game, this is your career, your livelihood, this will feed your children and support your retirement. You have to want it more than hundreds of other people, and you'll have to prove that over and over and over until you don't know if you can prove it anymore. And then once you think you might have made it, you're gonna have to prove that you deserved it. There are times you'll stop being people. There are times you'll have to be warriors. I see the looks of determination, the sparkle in your eyes, but when you break your first bone, I'll be there to see you cry." Johnny didn't cry when he broke his first bone as the veteran had said when he signed up for wrestling school. He looked that old bastard in the eye and hit him so hard he knocked three teeth loose. A phone rings. "Hello?" What shreds of sleep Johnny had been grasping at fell away. "Yo man, I thought you was gonna call me back!" "Shit. What time is it?" 4:30pm. "Time to get your ass up at least. Man I knew something was wrong with you, deal with your shit and get it together, you can't afford to blow this." "Don't patronize me when I'm sleepy, it's not nice. There's something here I have to find, it's part of my story. This is my time to step up and be what god gifted me to be, when the time comes I'll be ready." Johnny couldn't make it from the cab to the front door of the modern made to fit town house. The walkway was shoveled perfectly, just as every other house on the street. Halfway up a form darted through the door. "It's your fault!" The person screamed. "Joey…" Lexicon falls short of words. "Where the hell where you? He was sick for months and you never came to see him!" The rage in the younger brothers voice stung Johnny's pride, for with that rage is mixed the guilt of truth. "You could have at least come to his funeral!" Joey is only a few inches shorter than Lexicon, seven years younger he is about a hundred pounds lighter than his older brother. "Mom tried to kill herself because of you, because you left, because you could have come and you left!" Tears were coming from his eyes now. "Why couldn't you just be here? Why couldn't you just say you're, you're sorry he's gone or somethin!? Huh? I mean, is it so hard for you to show a little emotion?" "Say something damnit!" Joey screams at Johnny. "Joey come back inside!" A feminine voice interrupts with failure. "You got nothing to say!?" Joey advances. Koryn comes down from the front porch just as the younger brother takes a swing. Johnny catches it but Joey swings another, and another. Johnny catches all of them before grabbing his brother, he struggles and they stumble into the snow bank. Johnny holds him, his powerful arms keeping Joey restrained despite his rage filled efforts to get free, to fight, to tantrum and blame Johnny for their father's death, for their mother's attempted suicide. Koryn stops and watches a few feet away as the struggling comes to a stop and all that remains are Joey's pain filled sobs of emotional agony. Johnny's face holds a frown, but in his eyes is not a single tear. "All I ever wanted to do was make him proud of me. And when I heard he was dying, when I knew I'd never get that in this life time. I couldn't face him. I became The Marauder, I started my personal path of redemption, so that I'd get to tell him all the great things I've done when I get to heaven. Don't mourn for your loss, celebrate the beginning of his time with God." Joey suddenly erupts, pushing Lex so hard he falls completely into the snow. "Shut up!" He yells at his brother. "You weren't here, you didn't see him sick! You think you can come back now with a new attitude and everything's gonna be okay? Just get out of here!" Joey yells and runs past Koryn for the house. Koryn looks at Lex with sadness in her eyes. "Come inside, he'll talk when he's calmed down." "No, I have a plane to catch. Koryn." She meets him with dissapointment. "Thanks." She gives a half hearted smile. "You've given up alot to be a wrestler, to claim your glory. Your friends, your family… Me. I hope it's worth it in the end." Johnny's expression holds true for the long moment of silence. "Me too." He agrees. Day: 5 A phone rings. "Hello?" "Konichiwa!" Johnny's face lights with a grin. "Hey baby girl, how's the weather in Tokyo?" "It's okay, mom said I have to call and wish you luck in the tournament." Koryn's voice crackles in the background. "Your mom's plane got in safely?" "She got in this morning, said you were a real dick this week." "Hey watch your language Claire." "Whatever dad! Good luck in your tournament." "Thanks." "Mom wants to know if you got some sleep." "Like thirteen hours." "Good, I gotta go meet some friends, love ya bye." Click. Johnny closes the flip phone with an expressionless face. Everybody makes decisions, some of them are big, some small, the true measure of man's worth is not in the choices he makes, but how he deals with the consequences. And how he faces the future knowing that it is not ment to get easier, that life is a struggle, one battle after another. Everybody wants the things that are worth fighting for and the more you want it the harder you can expect it to be. Johnny doesn't want anything more than this. Quitting is always an option. Putting his untied boot on the bench the stoic warrior starts prepping his battle gear.
