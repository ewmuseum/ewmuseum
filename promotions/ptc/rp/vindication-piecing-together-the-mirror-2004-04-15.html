Vindication: Piecing Together the Mirror

15 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-04-15 The simple light blue color scheme of the room seemed to match the color of the ice outside. The early morning sky greeted the students with the familiar pitch darkness that they saw every day and night without fail. The stars in the sky, stars which hadn't been seen clearly in many cities for years on end thanks to the light pollution of the twentieth century, brightly shone for all to see. "Another day in the grind," Seth rolled his eyes with a yawn. An average student, he never really wanted to learn anything. Most of the students felt the same way. Lined up five by five in ten rows of desks, they all too their seats and prepared for yet another three hours of drudgery known as History Class. The instructor, Mr. McGinniss, entered through the slide-hatch door with an enthusiastic grin widening his face. In his body language, and the way that he chirped, "Good morning, class!" excitement registered to all. If only the children felt the same way... The instructor's pastel yellow jumpsuit marked a stark contrast to the silver jumpsuits of the students. They always dreaded uniform policy; their worse nightmares came true three years prior when all Europans were required to wear a designated uniform based on occupation and rank during their effective operating hours. The adults didn't seem to mind; the youth, what with their want for individuality, hated the White House's decision. "Screen down," the instructor plainly commanded. The voice-recognition system only worked with calm voices, anything else did not register to the computers. The holographic screen slipped from the ceiling through a retractable panel near the edge of the north wall. It was translucent and transparent; no images showed up until the instructors said the words “Lesson Begin”. Beginning with his lecture, the words appeared on the screen as he spoke, along with the appropriate accompanying pictures. This allowed for the students who learned through visual methods to absorb the material better. “It was in the early twenty second century,” Instructor McGinniss started, “that Jonathan Roedecker finally perfected his vacuum cold fusion method for creating a virtually inexhaustible energy source. Free-roaming hydrogen atoms (the simplest and most abundant atom in the universe) were trapped by a device which was attached to a machine which could convert the energy from the atoms into usable energy. The energy cost of running the machine was covered by the vacuum fusion process itself, and there was more than enough to spare after that to make the energy for consumer use. “While this was a great ease upon the aging and strained power sources that the Earth had been relying on, there was still a far more serious problem: that of world overpopulation. Thanks to disease treatment and prevention, internationally-implemented health and fitness programs, and the access to free healthcare to all peoples of the world, the population had reached record highs. Families were having children routinely at older ages, and living much longer lives in general. Human numbers rather unexpectedly and even miraculously swelled to 18 billion people, and even previously largely uninhabited places such as the Andean mountains, the Sahara Desert, and the western lands in China began to fill up. “For all of humanity’s great achievements, the one thing that it did not count on, and did not expect, was for the writings of Malthus to come true. The world had grown too large to support its population. In a decade, the consumption of raw materials and processed goods was 83% greater than the world’s production output. There were too many people, and even with new technologies, smart vegetables that could grow anywhere, and the large-scale movement to synthetic meats and vegetarian diets, they couldn’t all be fed. “It would have been unthinkable to kill off the population to stabilize it, but even though family planning was heavily stressed, and in fact made a requirement by the World Administrative Council for Continued Peace and Prosperity (the former United Nations). Still, it wasn’t enough. The current numbers of the world population was much too much to be supported by the planet Earth. “Making matters worse, disposal of waste was not getting any easier, and city dumps grew so large that it became one of the biggest industries. “The earth couldn’t handle it. The humans were making their own planet unlivable. Within 60 years, the world would be unable to support human life.” Seth rolled his eyes. He knew all of this information. His father had tutored him privately within their quarters on the icy moon. While most Europan children were playing, he was stuck inside studying. Playtime was restricted to indoors; the technology to safely traverse the moon’s surface hadn’t been developed. “Immediately, possible solutions to the problem were drawn up. The most favorable turned out to be the sending of humans to colonize new areas in the solar system upon which to live. Roedecker’s vacuum cold fusion could power old new engines that had been developed (but kept in storage for little practical use in the late 21st century, as space travel took a backseat to geocentric affairs) that could reach potential sustained speeds of 50%-55% light speed, the fastest that could ever be reached by humans. It was thus a possible notion to travel to other planets and set up shop there. “Knowing what they did about each planet, the scientists of the world drew up a list of the places that would be most suitable to human life. The rest of the solar system was generally an inhospitable place, but with some modifications to the terrain, some places could be made livable. With a map and a plan of action, people from all around the world signed up. Each of the major nations of the world (USA, Brazil, China, Russia, England, France, Germany, Japan, China, Sweden, Canada, India, and Australia) claimed an area in which to colonize, and sent half of its population into space. Everyone needed to be between 14 and 40, at their physical peak and within child-rearing years. They would have to basically reestablish the human race in a different area of the solar system. “These nations shared the same space-faring technology; the engines and ship designs, the same hypersleep technology to keep their people in suspended animation until they reached their destinations. Each country (in the order listed above) built its living ships independently, and then blasted off into space.” Natalie, a blonde young girl with a spunky attitude and the rare birth-defect of bright red pupils, raised her hand. Her jumpsuit crinkled from the motion. “Excuse me, Instructor?” Mr. McGinniss recognized her with a nod. “Um…” she tried to phrase her question the right way. “What does this have to do with Jimmy Diablo or the PTC Cruiserweight Tournament?” ***|||~~~|||*** *Ring* *Ring* *Beep* "Jimmy, where are you, man?" It seemed that Jimbo K. couldn't leave him alone for even a moment. As soon as Jimmy Diablo was left to himself, he'd go off. For being a 42 year old man, traveling with him was like babysitting a child: if one wanted to keep track of him, one couldn't let him out of sight. Diablo answered, "Near the edge of town by the Cracker Barrel." Jimmy Diablo held the cellular phone to his ear. Ambient noise forced him to cover the other ear. He dreaded having taken the bus; getting back to the hotel would be a hassle now, especially with the lack of covered bus stops in this section of the town on the outskirts of Orlando where no one actually visits (save to reserve a hotel room away from the theme parks). “What are you doing over that way?” the trainer wondered. “Just because the workout’s finished for today doesn’t necessarily make it free time.” Jimbo seemed to be in a car. The radio was playing “When It All Falls Down” by Kanye West at low volume on a bad-quality radio. There was a lot of interference. He’d rented a Saturn Echo. It’s a terrible car if the driver or passengers are accustomed to power ANYTHING. “I’ve just got something that I have to take care of,” Jimmy said plainly. “I’ll call you when I’m done.” Jimbo replied, “Don’t ass around all day, we’ve still got a bit of stuff to take care of. And we’re out of town in the morning.” Jimmy Diablo was well aware of their timetable. That’s why he was doing this now. He wouldn’t get another chance in the foreseeable future to handle it. At least not without it being too late. He hoped that she was home. “I know. I’ll call you.” Diablo ended the call with a press of the button. Standing in front of the apartment building, Jimmy let the rain fall down upon him. Second thoughts are killer; he had been telling himself not to go through with this for weeks. Here he was, standing in front of the building in which Jocelyn Rodriguez currently resided. The dark midday skies seemed to be telling him to turn back, but something compelled him to press onward. There was still a good chance that he’d catch something standing out in the cold rain, even with the dark green rain coat covering his body. He looked like Solid Snake at the beginning of Metal Gear Solid 2: Sons of Liberty. He wasn’t concerned about his health at the moment, though; he had other things on his mind. It had been eight years since he had seen Jocelyn; and they hadn’t exactly split from contact on good terms. After the neck injury, they had an argument; the worse they’d ever had. Jimmy cursed at her; something that he’d never done before. She blamed him for what happened. He didn’t deny his fault in it, but told her that she was too ignorant to possibly understand what was going on. Both were shouting in the head of the moment. Jocelyn regretted what she said, and to some extent (but not completely), Jimmy felt the same way. He had already disappeared, though. They would never make up, and never speak to each other again. Until today. ***|||~~~|||*** Eight years ago… The black Toyota Camry traveled through the “safer” (relative term compared to the United States) streets of Mexico City. Even in the capital of Mexico, things were far from good. Breathing the air was equal to smoking two and a half packs of cigarettes daily. On the weekends, in the bad parts of towns, it was not uncommon to have muggings, kidnappings, and murders numbering near the hundreds for those two days when the tourists (both Mexican and foreign) were more apt to arrive. The late hour did nothing for congestion in the streets. It was 11 at night and cars still filled both sides of the highway. There was a league soccer match that had finished. The people were headed home, proud of the local team dominating the game with a 4-0 finish. The driver was a kid from Baja California: Alejandro Dominguez. Tijuana, probably; he seemed to have a bit more American influence than the usual kids his age, probably from all the tourists and college kids who came through the town. In the front passenger seat was Jose Maria Casas: the bodyguard. He was only 5’8”, 175 pounds. His bulk wasn’t what made him the bodyguard of choice for the VIP passenger; it was his ruthlessness. Jose spent a good deal of his adult life as a professional boxer. He could and would hurt anyone who began to look at his client the wrong way. Proficiency with a handgun also helped his job-skills. The man to whom the car, and the two men in the front seats, belonged was Hijo Del Diablo. Even now, pushing 90 years old, he commanded respect. Respect in the sense that he came from a good legacy started by his father. Respect in the sense that even though he was stricken with the Diablo Curse, he never quit trying to prove himself. He never quit trying to break the curse. And given that he had his father's earnings to live off of in trust, as well as his own money from working an average of 200 nights a year, he commanded respect in that he was one of Mexico City's more wealthy citizens. And then, there was the third passenger within the car... "Very well done," Hijo del Diablo (HdD) nodded. His voice was rather hoarse; whenever he spoke, it sounded like a dry exhale than speech. He knew only Spanish, and the added articles of the speech, along with the slight discomfort that came with speaking, made him save talking for the most important issues. "The timing, and the follow-up; perfect. No one will suspect a thing." This was definitely an important issue. The stop-and-go traffic was annoying Alejandro, who mirrored Jose in size (but not fighting ability). He preferred open roads; long stretches of flat land on which to speed through the night air with the windows down and the radio playing at full blast. He liked being away from civilization as much as possible. Having been in a "tourist town", he'd seen enough of people in his lifetime to just want to get away from it all. HdD continued; "The painkillers worked as expected. Nothing could harm you. All you had to do was make it look convincing. You even had me wondering if the drugs weren't working at first." The multi-story buildings on either side of the road weren't illuminated much, the overcast skies blotted out the moon. All headlights were on low-beams, and the street lamps weren't set wide enough to do anything away from the street in the way of providing visibility. Every now and then, the inside of the Camry would light up from the street lamps enough to contrast the black suits of HdD, Jose and Alejandro from the gray upholstery and console/dashboard. The third passenger, sitting behind Alejandro, wore his own suit: white. "And the use of that mist: brilliant," HdD coughed in mid-sentence. His health hadn't been failing him, he just caught a cold recently. Still a very fit 80+ year old man, he had no reason to believe that a death of natural causes would come before he hit his own centennial. "No one would suspect that the entire thing had been planned for Jimmy Diablo to lose." A solemn nod, given in response to Hijo del Diablo's statements. The third passenger: Sarushima Rikyu. The man who would go on to be called one half of the most notoriously evil tag team in the history of Mexican wrestling along with Red Slaughter. And the man who would also come to be known as the man primarily responsible for the death of Jimmy Diablo…. {To Be Continued...}
