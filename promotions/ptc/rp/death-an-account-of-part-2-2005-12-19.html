Death, An Account Of (Part 2)

19 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-19 This was the third time he was trying and, still, no answer. He heard the voicemail message begin to play and quickly cut the phone off. Waiting a few seconds, he dialled again. Come on, pick it up. Having already rung three times before, he knew voicemail would begin again after seven rings. One. Two. Three. Four. Fi- Click. "Hullo?" came a sleep-ridden voice. "Hello. Alec?" "Yeah," came the reply, followed by a long, deep yawn. "Who’s asking?" "Alec, it’s Armaan." "I don’t know what time it is in India, mate," Alec muttered before another, albeit shorter, yawn, "but everyone’s sleeping down here." "I am back in America," Armaan replied, smiling wryly. "Oh." Armaan could hear the struggle Alec was going through trying to sit up. "Look Armaan, could I call you back in fifteen minutes?" "Sorry to wake you up." "It’s okay, I’m up now so, well, I’m up. Just give me fifteen." "Okay." Armaan cut the call and threw the phone back on the dashboard, where it always lay when he was driving. He wasn’t sure how far off the freeway he had come, but he liked where he had come to a stop. In front of him lay forestry as far as his eyes would let him see and, had he not shut himself inside the confines of his car, the sweet sounds of chirping birds would have greeted him. For winter, it was a pleasant day. The sun had been beating down since rising, but the chill was reason enough for Armaan to keep the windows rolled up. He looked down at his watch; barely two minutes had passed. The sooner he talked to Alec, the better. Having had no sleep in days, he promised himself at least as much as a nap now. Just needed to get this phone-call out of the way. Reaching down, he pushed his seat back and made himself slightly more comfortable. It had been a tough week. Armaan let out a deep sigh and folded his arms. He had ten minutes to kill. "Bhaiyya! (Brother) Bhaiyya!" Leave me alone. "Bhaiyya, wake up!" Armaan opened his eyes, expecting to be greeted to something new, but nothing much had changed. The family that had come into the waiting room crying were still there, now a lot calmer. He couldn’t remember if the old man in the corner ever left. Just as Armaan had spent the last four days in the hospital, so had this man. Always here, always in that same corner, on that same seat. He turned towards Armaan and they shared a small smile, each man expressing sympathy for the other’s misfortune. "What’s the time?" Armaan finally asked, rubbing his eyes to wake himself up. Sujata scanned the waiting room walls in search of a clock. "I don’t know, there isn’t a clock anywhere. Wait." she said, pointing at the teenage girl sitting with the family of five. She had been the worst when the group arrived and, even now, was physically shaking with grief. "That girl over there has a watch. I’ll ask her." "No," Armaan refused, lightly putting his hand on her shoulder to stop her getting up, "I’ll check on my mobile." Armaan reached into his pocket and pulled out his phone. Ignoring the numerous signs against them, he switched it on and waited for it to go to the main screen. "The doctor!" Sujata uttered loudly, shoving her elder brother’s arm to get his attention. Armaan looked up away from the mobile and, quite rightly, the doctor had stopped outside the waiting room door. Getting up, the Indian jogged to the door and tapped the doctor’s shoulder, who had begun to walk away. "Excuse me, doctor," Armaan spoke softly, "is it possible to see my mother now?" The doctor stood without saying a word, first looking at Armaan and then shifting his glance to the seated Sujata. Sighing, he turned back to Armaan. "Visiting times are over, son." "I know, bu-" "If I let you go in, then I have to answer to all other family and friends who are wanting the same thing. We cannot bend the rules for one person. If you come in early tomorrow, you can spend the whole day here." "Doctor," Armaan pleaded, "you know it’s not that easy." A tear began to trickle down his face. It took a lot of thought from the doctor, but he finally gave in, placing his hand on Armaan’s shoulder and nodding slowly. "Half an hour. That is all I will give you. I’m only going to allow one of you at a time, though." Armaan nodded in both agreement and gratitude, before heading back into the waiting room. "I’m going to see Mama," he muttered to Sujata as he reached halfway into the room, "do you want to meet her?" She nodded her head vigorously, her eyes swelling up with tears. "OK. Let me go in for fifteen minutes, then you. Is that okay?" Sujata nodded again, her initial excitement now lessened. Armaan reached her and gave her a soft kiss on the forehead before turning and exiting the waiting room again. "Hi Mama," Armaan said, forcing a quick chuckle afterwards, "it’s me again." The bed rested in the centre of the room, surrounded by medical machinery. A window looked out to a small garden outside, but there was nothing particularly interesting to see. For Armaan, anyway, his attention was focused solely on sitting at bedside. Mrs. Singhania lay motionless on the bed, being helped to breathe by an oxygen tank that stood beside the bed. Except for the occasional beep of a machine and the heavy drone of forced breathing, the room was eerily silent at most times. Even over the last three days, when someone had always been by her side, sound was rare. Now, though, Armaan felt like talking. "They let me stay for another half-hour. Isn’t that great?" Her oxygen mask covered her mouth and nose, leaving only her eyes exposed to see, and they too were shut. "Sujata’s in the waiting room too. She’s waiting to see you. That’s good, isn’t it?" He watched her eyes, hoping for some movement. "Huh?" Nothing happened. Armaan restrained himself, as his eyes began to fill with tears. He sat silently for a few seconds, rubbing his eyes before the tears were given a chance to fall. "I’ve been praying for you every day. You used to say that prayer always worked, no matter what the problem was. No cure like the one given by the Lord himself. You said that once, remember?" Armaan leant forward and brushed aside a hair from in front of her eyes. In truth, the strand of hair was nowhere near her eyes, but it gave him an excuse to touch her face. "I was supposed to be wrestling this week. Against a man called Chandler Tsonda. They phoned me up yesterday too. But I told the man that I couldn’t make it." He stopped, as if choosing his words carefully. "You never asked me how my wrestling career is going in America. It’s going really well. I have made many friends, and even the people who watch like me. I work for a company called the WWA. It is very popular in America, and I enjoy wrestling for them. It is hard, but worth it." Not getting a response of any sort, Armaan stood up and walked over to the window. "I haven’t got a girlfriend yet, if you’re wondering. I never have the time. I don’t think girls like me anyway. When they see me do strange things on television, they probably think I’m crazy." Armaan looked back at the bed, a dry smile on his face, hoping his attempt at a joke would be returned. The oxygen tank continued to drone. "But you know, I get shy around girls anyway. I never could talk to them properly, especially the pretty ones. Remember at school, when I tried asking Reena to the ball? I had to get you to stand behind me when I did it, in case something happened. Nothing did, because I got scared and ran away." Armaan waited again, hoping for a reply. Not getting one, he sat back down. First he leaned back against the chair, his head in his hands. Suddenly, he grabbed her left hand, his eyes red. "Come on, Mama," he managed to choked in between sobs, "wake up. We have to go home. Sujata hasn’t slept in days, and Papa’s waiting to see you too." He squeezed her hand gently as tears rolled freely down his face. "I want to see your smile again, Mama, I haven’t seen it in years. But you have to wake up for that. Everyone’s waiting for you to get better, but you’re taking too long. I’ll leave everything for you, I’ll stay at home with you all the time. Just, wake up. Please." Monitors suddenly began to beep loudly, and the room filled with a distorted disturbance. As the sounds grew louder, Armaan’s head began to pound. Three nurses rushed into the room and quickly attended to Mrs Singhania. "We need you out of the room, sir!" Armaan gets pushed off the chair and pointed towards the door by one of the nurses, before she turns back to the patient. Armaan goes only as far as he is pushed, standing still in bewilderment. A doctor rushes into the room, and begins to converse with the nurses. A barrage of noises begin to echo through Armaan’s mind, the Indian struggling to find some sense. "What’s the problem?" "Heart rate has increased." "Blood pressure above normal." "She need-" "What if w-" "Please, Mr Sin-" "Monitor her hear-" "Leave now, sir!" He wasn’t sure of anything, but someone had dragged him out of the waiting room. He could hear Sujata beside him, muttering to him, but her voice was distant. Suddenly, a loud vibration ensued in his pocket and the phone began to ring. "Please, Mr Singhania, turn the p-" "What’s wrong, Bha-" "Phones are not allowed anyw-" "Is Mama o-" "Turn the phon-" He picked the phone out of his pocket and stared at it. Then down at Sujata. The door of the ward was still open, and he looked blankly as his mother was surrounded by a multitude of nurses. He could hear the phone’s constant ringing. He could hear Sujata trying to talk to him in a blend of questions and throttled cries. He could hear medical equipment ringing in trouble. "Hello?" "Yeah, it’s Alec." "Hello. Sorry again for waking you up." "It’s OK. Good to hear from you. And good to know you’re back in the States." "Yes." Armaan took a quick glance at the clock in the dashboard. He had nodded off a little longer than expected. The call had come later too. "I decided to come back." "So, what made you decide to call me so early in the morning?" His voice was more refreshed now. "I have changed my mind," Armaan said slowly, wiping away a small tear from his eye. "I feel like wrestling." "Oh." "Is it still OK to wrestle Chandler Tsonda, or is it too late?" "No no," came a reply, rather abruptly, "you’re not late. Are you sure you’re ready for it? I mean, you won’t have a lot of time to prepare." "It will be enough. Just let everyone know that Armaan is still taking part in the tournament." There was a short pause, neither man saying a word. Armaan cold hear Alec’s hoarse breathing and, probably, likewise. "That’s great, man, I’ll let the guys know. Good to have you back. Any chance you can pop by now to go over some things?" "Sorry, Alec, not right now. I can come in about three or four hours. I have some business to attend to right now, but I will come in a while." "OK, I’ll see ya in a bit." "Bye." Armaan threw the phone back onto the dashboard and rubbed his eyes. The nap was still too short, but enough for the time being. Turning around, Armaan leant over to the passengers seat and picked up a bunch of flowers. He got out of the car and, flowers in hand, began to walk through the thickets. It didn’t matter that thorns were trying to pierce through the cotton of his black suit; he was too busy trying to use his hand to push his way through the forestry. Finally, he stopped in front of a small building. It seemed derelict, but its walls were still intact. With a small smile in the corner of his lips, Armaan ambled slowly up to the wall facing him. Searching through his blazer pocket, he pulled out a black marker pen. Carefully, he began to write on the plastered wall. Finished, Armaan kissed the bunch of flowers and placed them on the ground against the wall, and walked off. As he made his journey through the forestry back towards the car, his written words resounded in his head. Your body may lie in a place many miles from here, but your soul is here with me.
