The Last Stop

4 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-04 The driver stood on the sidewalk, idly shuffling his feet in an attempt to stay warm. The rain was coming down even harder now, though he could see nothing of the clouds through the overwhelming lights of the city. He wasn’t sure what Kimbusa was doing inside, but it couldn’t have been good. Either way, he had his instructions, and he was going to follow them. He hadn’t been there more than a few minutes when Kimbusa reappeared from the building. He had his collar drawn up over his face and was moving quickly; the driver didn’t need to be told that they were in a hurry. He knew very little about his passenger’s odious past, but what he did know suggested that he should probably be leaving before the authorities arrived. Kimbusa blew by the driver and headed towards the limo, which had been illegally parked across the street. The driver scurried after him, flashing one final glance back at the high-rise. The street around them was curiously quiet, the streets empty save the sound of the driving rain. The two resumed their places in the limo, and the driver pulled back out onto the street. After a moment, the intercom crackled to life. "Entertainment District," demanded the harsh voice from the cabin. Shit. The driver wasn’t particularly familiar with Tokyo; he’d only flown in earlier that day. But he had been briefed on the Entertainment District. Caldera had anticipated Kimbusa going there, and wanted the driver to be on his toes. It was Kimbusa’s old refuge, where he’d spent those dark years working as the most feared enforcer in the history of the Yakuza. It hadn’t taken the driver long to notice the tip of Kimbusa’s pinky missing on his left hand. It was the place where Japanese businessmen and well-to-do visitors went to indulge themselves. Whatever the vice, it could be found in the Entertainment District, all regulated and controlled by the Japanese mafia. The driver shuddered to think what Kimbusa might want there, but he had a feeling it wasn’t drugs or blackjack. The limo rippled through the watery streets, winding through increasingly dense traffic towards their destination. After some time they turned a corner, and abruptly the lights went out. The brilliant reflecting luminescence descended into an ominous murk, and the driver knew they’d arrived. A few provocative neon signs danced over nondescript sidewalks, all but the faintest shadows obscured by the blanketing gloom. The sidewalks were busy, but there was no conversation taking place on the street. Outside of their refuges of pleasure, the patrons moved soft and silent as ghosts, their eyes never turning to those they passed by. Anyone could be perfectly anonymous here, people of any shape… and any size. The driver didn’t need to be told where he was heading. He’d found the route earlier in the day, during which the streets had looked remarkably different. The daylight revealed the Entertainment District to be not unlike any other block in Tokyo; but when the sun went down and the darkness descended, the streets changed, as if they were transported to a different realm. Still, having seen them revealed by the day was some small comfort to him, as he felt confident he could navigate his way back out if the sudden need were to arise. In the back, Kimbusa was sitting deep in thought, his eyes paying little mind to the all-too-familiar streets rushing past him. His name was still legend on those streets, whispered only in hushed tones while hurrying through the dark streets. Every now and then, some powerful businessman walking with whatever female company he’d purchased for the night would stop before crossing a dark alley, where he would peer down into the shadows, wondering if some nightmare were lurking there. It was a power that had thrilled Kimbusa. He was most satisfied with the fear he struck in the hearts of those who regarded him under the bright lights of a professional wrestling arena. But there was still a part of him that enjoyed the fearsome grip his legacy still held on the mean streets. He was going to see Shindo. Shindo, the Yakuza boss who had found him on the streets, taken him into his home and given him a new life, a new name. Kimbusa, he’d named him, the mighty samurai warrior reborn to battle once more. Kimbusa had had three births in his life. One to his mother, whom he had never seen since. One to his father, the only father that he’d ever known, this Yakuza crime boss who’d twisted him so. And one to himself, just one week ago, the final rebirth. The only step left would be the grave. Shindo was the only one left from this former life to resolve. He didn’t know what to expect; Shindo had been old when he left, and that was many years ago now. But from the smell of the place, he knew Shindo was still there, still managing his empire from his pyrite throne. Still marveling at how little his visit to Epiphany had affected him, Kimbusa wondered what to do with his final stop. If he could settle his business with the Yakuza, to leave that stage of his life behind once and for all, he would clear the final barrier between himself and complete, unfettered power. His match with Shadow was rapidly approaching, and yet he knew he would be unable to commit himself fully to this tournament until he had settled all old business. Epiphany was no longer a mystery. Once Shindo was gone, Shadow would be the lone target of his rage. And, Kimbusa knew, once his rage was singularly focused, there would be none who could resist him. The limousine pulled to a stop out front of an eerily lit club. A bright green sign with flickering Japanese characters stood atop the building’s contemporary façade, and impeccably dressed Japanese men and women were filing in and out of the large wooden doors quietly. A doorman stepped forward and opened Kimbusa’s door, taking a step back in surprise as the 500 pound man rose from within. Kimbusa walked to the door, ignoring the two armed (he knew) bouncers flanking the entrance. No words needed to be exchanged. They knew who he was, and why he had come, and they were afraid. Kimbusa entered the glistening club and was hit by a steaming wave of sweat. Topless girls danced on poles in the back of the club to the beat of throbbing techno music, while pulsing neon lights penetrated the thick cloud of cigar smoke that hung in the air. Maniacal laughter seeped through the pounding bass, as men high on power and cocaine relished in their deviant splendor. Kimbusa hated this place. He walked to his right, pressing past a pair of scantily clad American waitresses, heading for a narrow staircase. He was met there by another pair of guards; one a young Japanese kid in a white suit, and the other a massive Mandarin, clad all in black, who wore a clearly visible automatic weapon over his chest. Neither of them hindered Kimbusa as he began scaling the steps. He rose to the second floor, where the VIP lounge was located. Here men shelled out unheard of sums of money simply for the right to relax in a more "exclusive" environment. But the vices and the faces were all the same, though the avarice was a bit thicker up here. Kimbusa walked to the back of the room, where an unsuspecting door hid in a dark corner. There was no guard here; word had already risen from below that an old friend had returned. The path was open to him. All that stood between Kimbusa and the final nemesis of his mind was that door. Without hesitation, Kimbusa swung the door open and entered. He found himself in a familiar compartment. The smell of incense and opium was overpowering, and the walls danced with the light of dozens of small candles. There, behind a desk of modest proportions and upon a throne of wood and silk sat Shindo Katayama. The most powerful man in the Tokyo Yakuza, the proprietor of every major vice in the city, hunched over in his chair, wheezing from the struggle of drawing breath, his platinum beard grazing the floor. His dark eyes glanced upward, but no voice followed, only a hard hacking cough that nearly caused the old man to collapse from his seat. At once Kimbusa felt resolved. No words were necessary, no great act of rebellion needed to free his soul. Again Kimbusa saw that no power was great enough to counter the ages of time. Time had crippled Shindo, and soon time would kill him. And so time would set him free. With only a scornful scoff for the fearsome tyrant, Kimbusa turned, left, and soon was back on the street. His limo was waiting. The doorman let him back inside and slammed the door behind him. The limo pulled back out into the street, and presently Kimbusa found himself speeding away from the Entertainment District for the last time, leaving behind only an old relic and his fading empire to rot. "You may leave me at the airport," Kimbusa said into the speaker. He had returned to Japan expecting an epic end to his former self, and instead found that the person he once was had simply faded away. The transformation was complete. He was focused now, driven to move forward in an unrelenting path of destruction. There would be no stopping him now. A few feet ahead of him, the driver sat shaking his head. "I’ll take you to the airport," he said to himself. "But that’s not where I’ll leave you…" A few days earlier… Grumbling, Steven Caldera picked up the phone and hit seven on his speed-dial. He’d always wondered why he kept the number saved. Now he knew. The phone rang twice before it was answered. "Hello?" Caldera closed his eyes and grunted. "It’s me." "Oh, hello, Satan! How’s hell?" "Well, it’s not the heat, it’s the humidity that gets you. Listen, I need a favor…"
