What is in a name?

24 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-24 Prologue One: Present Day… John Winters bolted upright in his bed, beads of sweat pouring from his face. It was the nightmare again. The one with the ditch. When Winters woke up for ostensibly the first time six months ago, he experienced a number of bad experiences, the worst of which - excluding the pretty disturbing sensation of amnesia - involved him, a ditch and a sheriff. Winters threw his bed covers off, got out of his bed and made his way over to the chest of draws on the opposite side of his apartment. He hesitantly opened the top draw and pulled out the wallet. The wallet was incredibly significant to him for a number of reasons. It was a link to a bad time, to a lesson learned and to how he chose a name for himself. This is that story… Prologue Two: Winters, a Ditch and a Sheriff…* The sheriff aggressively opened the car door and dragged Winters out of the car. “What do you intend to do?” Winters asked, managing to remain calm. “I intend to give you the ‘special treatment’.” The sheriff simply replied, throwing Winters into the mud-lined ditch by the side of the road. “I don’t feel good about this, sir!” The deputy said, nervously taking a couple of steps back. The sheriff then rushed over to his deputy and grabbed him by the collar of his shirt. “Turn away now,” He warned. “You will not witness a thing!” “I don’t know, sir!” The deputy bleated. “Look,” The sheriff said, now in calm tone, unhanding his young understudy. “If we don’t teach these punks a lesson then we’ll get this type of behaviour all the time! Everything will be vandalised. Do you want that, boy?” The deputy hesitantly shook his head. “No, sir! But this isn’t a young punk. He looks about thirty…he looks older than me!” Winters observed the sheriff seem to think about this prospect for a second, obviously trying to come up with some kind of argument. Winters figured that Johnson, the deputy, wasn’t the sharpest knife in the draw…and he didn’t see the wad of notes the farmer handed to the sheriff before they departed his farm. “The same thing applies! If we let this outsider get away with who knows what we’ll find vandalised tomorrow…” “Technically you don’t know whether or not I’m an outsider…” Winters butted in. The sheriff turned around to face him, wearing a nasty scowl and landing a nice kick in Winters‘ gut, winding him severely. “Shut up you! I‘ve never seen you before and that‘s good enough for me…” “Damn yokels…” Winters mumbled, successfully getting to his knees with his hands still tied behind his back. “What did you call me? What the hell did you call me?” The sheriff shouted edging closer to Winters. Winter developed a defiant smirk. “You heard me.” “You see Johnson?” The sheriff said not taking his eyes away from Winters. “This is what these people are like. We have to stem the problem before it becomes a catastrophe.” “Okay…but I’m not watching!” The deputy finally conceded. “That’s fine,” The sheriff replied. “This ain’t for the weak of heart anyhow…” With that the sheriff proceeded to attack Winters, kicking and punching him, while still wearing the handcuffs. After a couple of minutes the sheriff was out of breath and so stopped the vicious attack. Winters lay in the ditch, beaten to a bloody pulp. “Ugh…does…that make you feel better? Beating a man while he’s cuffed…” Winters managed. The sheriff drew closer, developing a snug smile. “Hell yeah…and thanks for reminding me…I’ll take those cuffs back now.” The sheriff stood on Winters head, reached over and unlocked the cuffs. “I’ll be taking those back now…and remember if I ever see you around here again…I’ll do a lot worse.” “No,” Winters spluttered. “You will not…the next time you see me - if you see me coming at all - I will not be cuffed.” “I’m terrified I’m sure…” The sheriff said, walking confidently back to his car. Chapter One "People seldom refuse help, if one offers it in the right way." -A. C. Benson Michael and Jeanne were driving back from the local diner when they came across him. It was extremely dark out, but when the lights of Michael’s truck first grazed over the ditch, Jeanne saw the body out of the corner of her eye and shouted for her boyfriend to stop the car. Michael obliged Jeanne, despite having reservations about the idea. It was getting late and he had an early rise in the morning. He parked his truck on the opposite side of the road to the ditch, keeping his powerful lights firmly fixed upon the body. Reluctantly, Michael climbed out of the truck and slammed the door behind him, Jeanne followed to suit. “Jeanne, get back in the car!” He demanded, instantly regretting raising his voice to her. “Why?” She asked, innocently. “Because we don’t know what the hell it is yet,” he answered. “It could be dangerous.” “Okay.” She said looking slightly deflated. Michael smiled. “Thank you.” Michael cautiously proceeded towards the body and as he got closer, it looked more and more like a man, than a creature. He stopped and turned back to Jeanne. “It’s not an animal…it’s a man!” He shouted to her. “Oh my god!” She screamed. “Is he still alive?” Michael shrugged. “Not sure.” Jeanne ran over to her boyfriend’s side, to get a better look at the body herself. She studied the body for a second. “I can see his chest moving up and down…he’s still alive!” She said. “Perhaps we should call the sheriff and get him down here.” “We can’t do that!” “Why not?” “Because the sheriff may get here too late and he may die!” “But he’s in a better position to handle this than us!” “The hospital is miles away! We’re better off taking him home with us and try to heal him there!” “We’re not trained physicians! We have no idea what’s wrong with him!” “We’d better take a look then hadn’t we?” Michael saw that look in her eye again. She had when he wanted to paint the kitchen yellow. She was determined to get her own way and there was nothing that he could do or say to change her mind. The ending would be the same, so he decided to just cut his losses and just agree to go along with her plan now, despite his reservations. “Okay!” He finally conceded. “Let’s go see how he is.” Michael tried to ignore the smug, victorious look that now was absently spread across her face and led the way towards the injured man. Upon reaching the body, he knelt down and felt the man’s pulse. It was strong. He wasn’t in any critical condition. “He’s not in any serious danger…” Michael trailed off noticing the disgusted look upon Jeanne’s face. “Oh my god!” She screamed. “What is it?” Michael asked. “Look at his face!” She replied. The man lay on his side, his face facing away from Michael, so he didn’t notice the cuts and the bruising all over him. He’d been beaten and beaten badly. “What do think happened?” Jeanne inquired, drawing closer to the body. “I don’t know,” Michael began. “Mugged perhaps?” “I know you don’t like the idea, Mike…” Jeanne trailed off. “But we need to get him back to our place.” Michael finished. “Yes. It‘s the right thing to do you know…” “That’s debatable…” Michael muttered, dragging the unconscious body towards his truck. Chapter 2 "Reject your sense of injury and the injury itself disappears." -Marcus Aurelius, Meditations Unconsciousness. A void. No thought, no senses, only darkness…until a few seconds before regaining consciousness, when your body reboots it’s systems to bring you back from the void. A second before he woke up, Winters’ senses came online and registered for the first time since losing consciousness the pain his body was constantly experiencing in the meantime. Winters awoke, his senses snatched by the sensations of pain in multiple areas, his ribs hurting the most. After a couple of seconds of being absolutely still most of the pain subsided and the rest was manageable enough to suppress. Winters considered where he awoke. The last thing he remembered was lying in a ditch in the middle of nowhere at night time, then he figured he must have lost consciousness. Now he was in a comfortable bed, white coloured walls, under the cover of white sheets that had various depictions of flowers all over them. Strange. Who would want to help him? By the sheriff’s own admission Winters was considered an outsider in this town and therefore instantly disliked. It was a worn out cliché, he knew, but still that’s the way it was. Moreover, when you consider in the same equation the state they would’ve had to have found him in, then the idea of being rescued by someone from the town was pretty much unthinkable. The only other situation that Winters could possibly devise was that an ambulance was called or saw me as they passed by and helped him. However, this room did not look like a generic, sterile, hospital room to him. Unless they all looked like this around here. One thing was certain: if he just lay around here waiting to be told what was happening, then he may be in for a wait. He needed to know now. Winters mentally prepared himself and then tried to push himself up, to be at least in a sitting position. The pain seemed to be subsiding a little more now. He was a fast healer apparently. Then, he heard footsteps outside the room, then the door knob turned and then finally, in what appeared like minutes to Winters, - but could’ve only been a matter of seconds in reality - the door opened. Winters didn’t know who or what he expected to see walk in the room, but it certainly wasn’t what he actually got. It was a woman and a man. Perhaps a couple? They looked, well…nice. “Um…hey.” Winters awkwardly greeted. “Hello.” The woman greeted, drawing closer. “How are you feeling?” The man questioned. Winters detected a hint of suspicion and disapproval in his voice. It was quite clear that the man didn’t want him here. “Okay. I’m getting there I suppose.” Winters answered. “Are you sure? Do you need Aspirin or anything?” The woman asked. Winters shook his head. “No, I’m good…thanks.” “So,” The man said. “What happened?” Winters hesitated with his answer. He didn’t want to offend anyone here because they pretty much helped him. He didn’t want to name names because there was a distinct possibility that these people know the sheriff and the farmer. However, he needed to tell them some truth in order for his to establish trust. He needed their support at least until he felt well enough to walk around. “As you have probably worked out by now, I was beaten…quite badly.” Winters replied, trying to read the expressions on the faces of the two. “Why?” The man promptly replied. “Pretty much because I’m not from around here…apparently.” “Where are you from, Mr…” The woman trailed off, obviously wanting to know his name. “I don’t have a name, as far as I know, and I don’t know where I come from.” Winters replied. “Yeah, right.” The man replied. “You know,” Winters began. “That’s pretty much one of the things they said before they beat the hell outta me!” “You mean you didn’t know your name before the attack last night?” the woman asked, obviously bemused by the whole situation. Winters nodded. “Indeed. Yesterday afternoon I woke up in a field in the middle of nowhere.” “Seriously?” the woman replied. “Ain’t that a kick in the pants.” Winters exclaimed. “Why the hell should we believe you?” The man asked, reaffirming his position in this whole matter,. “I never asked you to. I never asked you to help me either, but you did nonetheless. You made a decision to bring me into your home and now you have to live with it. I don’t know what my name is…who I was before, but I know one thing. You helped me and for that I’m grateful. If you’re not willing to trust me that’s fine. I’ll go right now…” Winters finished off the sentence by attempt to throw the bed covers off him, but helplessly fell back to the comforting embrace of the bed. Winters noticed the concerned look upon the woman’s face and was tempted to take the look as a sign of pity, but ultimately resisted the urge. Then she said. “You’re not going anywhere.” Winters checked the face of the woman’s boyfriend. He stilled seemed understandably sceptical, but he would condone his stay for the time being…he may be wearing the pants of the relationship, but she was the one who chose which pants he put on. “I’ll be better in a couple of hours.” Winters assured them. The man looked bemused by this. “I think it may take longer than that…” “Have you not noticed, Michael?” The woman said. “Noticed what, Jeanne?” Michael questioned. “The bruising around his face…it’s not nearly as bad as it was!” Michael came a little closer and had a proper look at Winters face. “Yeah…that’s clearer up kinda fast!” “As I said I heal fast.” Winters added. With that both Michael and Jeanne made their way back to the door, ready to get the hell out of there. “We’ll be back in a while…try to get some rest.” Jeanne told him, before both of them promptly left. Chapter 3 "When you make the finding yourself - even if you're the last person on Earth to see the light - you'll never forget it." -Carl Sagan Winters awoke for the second time that day in a slightly more pleasant mood. He felt as though his injuries had cleared up a little more since earlier in the morning and felt as though he could now walk about a little…perhaps even get the hell out of this place. He grit his teeth and prepared for pain, as he pushed - with surprising ease - himself into a sitting position. However, no pain came. Winters waited a few seconds more just to make sure that there was no delayed reaction or his senses were not deceiving him, but nothing ever came. Satisfied by this, he then went a step further and threw the bed covers off and confidently swung his legs around to the side of the bed, resting his feet on the decidedly comfortable, thick carpet. At this point, Winters realised that he had nothing on, which he also found surprising. He figured they must have undressed him when he was unconscious and he was so tired and disorientated when he woke up a couple of hours ago and he just didn’t notice. He spotted his clothes folded neatly on the chair next to the bed. He hadn’t noticed them before, perhaps the girl called Jeanne had put them their while he was sleeping. He picked his black T-shirt up and held it out in front of him. All of the mud was a gone and there was a clear lemon zest smell emanating from it. She’d obviously washed them. Winters stood in order to put the rest of his clothes on, when his ribs gave him a little twinge. Winters figured that it was just a subtle reminder that although he apparently healed fast, he was still less than one hundred percent. During getting dressed the injury did resurface again and so Winters decided that he was up to getting out of here right about now. Then, on the chair where the pile of clothes had one been, Winters noticed a black leather wallet just lying there. Strange, Winters thought. When he checked his pockets before - when he’d woken up in a field with no memory - there was nothing in his pockets, but he didn’t ever recall checking his back pockets once. On the hand, there was a distinct possibility that it belonged to Michael and in that case he probably shouldn’t touch it. He weighed up his options and decided that it was an acceptable risk to take. He flipped open the leather storage device and looked at the picture held back a clear plastic laminate cover, then he saw the sheriffs badge. His eyes widened at the sheer shock of it…this wasn’t his wallet, it was the wallet of the sheriff, the one responsible for his current condition. Winters felt the anger swell up inside him as he recalled the incident that happened the night before…cringing each time he remember a particularly vicious blow with the sheriff’s night stick. Up until this point, revenge had been the furthest thought from his mind, but now he fully remembered what they did to him back at the ditch, the assuring embrace of vengeance had apparently totally enveloped him…he would get his revenge. And shouldn’t he? What the sheriff did to him was horrific and he should be punished for such a crime. Then it struck him. What the hell was the sheriff’s wallet doing in Jeanne and Michael’s house? Did his rescuers know this mindless thug? He was sure as hell going to find out. Chapter 4 "Adversity is the first path to truth." -George Gordon Byron, Don Juan Winters folded the wallet back up and made his way out of his assigned room. He found Jeanne in the room at the bottom of the stairs. It was the kitchen, and she was preparing some kind of stew for dinner. Michael was sat on a chair around the dinning room table, reading a paper. It took a while for them both to acknowledge his presence. “Hey…you shouldn’t be up yet!” Jeanne said, with a hint of anger attached to her voice. Winters did not verbally respond to the woman’s comments, he simply threw the wallet on the table in front of Michael. Puzzled by this action, he put down his paper and picked up the wallet. “What’s this?” Michael asked. “A wallet.” Winters replied. “Whose is it?” Michael questioned. “Open it up.” Winters said. Michael did as he was told, and when he saw the picture of the sheriff a wave of realisation swept over his face. He threw over to Jeanne. “Looks like, our friend here found your dad’s wallet!” Michael added. Winters glared at Jeanne. “Your father?” “Yes…the sheriff.” She hesitantly answered. “That thug is your father?” Winters asked. “Thug?” She replied. Winters drew closer to her, causing Michael to stand up and make his way to her side, gently resting his hands on her shoulders. “Yes…thug!” Winters added. “Her dad isn’t a thug…he’s a good man! He helps us out all the time!” Michael declared, taken aback by Winters claims. “Really? Maybe it’s because you’re from around here!” Winters shouted. Winters could tell by the shock etched into Jeanne’s face that she had realised where he was going with this, she placed her hand over her gaping mouth. “Oh no…” She said. “What?” Michael asked, looking at them both blankly. “Did I miss something?” “Her father was the one who did this to me!” Winters said, his anger noticeable to them both. “He beat me to within an inch of my life because I was an outsider… simply because I am not from this town.” Michael shock his head in disbelief. “I don’t believe it.” “Oh my god,” Jeanne said, realisation spreading across her face. “Remember what he was talking to us about yesterday afternoon just before we went to the diner?” Michael’s face quickly adapted the very same look as Jeanne’s. “Yeah,” He said, uncomfortably. “He was talking about giving some out of town vandal a lesson.” “At the time we didn’t know what he was talking about…” Jeanne trailed off. “I guess we do now…” Michael concluded. Winters sighed. He was venting his anger out on them, intimidating them, scaring them, and truth be told, it wasn’t their fault. It is now crime to have a father that is a simple thug parading around pretending to be an honourable lawman. They did save him after all… “It’s okay…it’s not your fault. You can’t help being his daughter.” Winters said. “So,” Jeanne said, still on edge. “What are you going to do?” “I don’t know yet,” Winters said, grabbing the wallet out of Michael’s hands. “But I’ll probably start by giving him his wallet back.” With that Winters headed towards the door and left before Michael or Jeanne could stop him. He had a lot of thinking to do… Chapter 5 "It is a revenge the devil sometimes takes upon the virtuous, that he entraps them by the force of the very passion they have suppressed and think themselves superior to." -George Santayana, The Letters of George Santayana It was later afternoon when Winters finally made it to the sheriff’s department. As it turned out, it was easier to find than Winters ever suspected. It was located in the middle of the town area, which was a little more dense than area that the farmer lived in, with around seven or eight different buildings huddled together for various different needs. Jeanne’s and Michael’s place about an half hour walk away, after taking the directions from a few of the locals. Most of whom were pleasant for the most part…although there were a few who simply ignored him. Winters stood across the road from the Sheriff’s place, leant on the side of the pharmacy building, trying to mentally weigh up his options. On this occasion his emotions had got the better of him and coming here proved that he hadn’t thought things through properly. Lying in the ditch he made a promise to himself to only look out for himself, to make sure he was okay and nothing more, to properly think through any proposed action and he found himself here, about to follow through with his emotion filled decision. It wasn’t too late to stop and think things through. He did actually have to do anything to the sheriff…he could still walk away. However, he realised that walking away would not be the correct course of action in this case. The more he thought about it, the more the emotion-filled choice seemed logical to him. Back in the ditch where the sheriff had left him for dead, Winters promised the man that he would get revenge for his actions that night and as he remembered the smug little grin the sheriff had on his face at the time, the thought of his future actions were more and more reaffirmed in his head. Also, the way the sheriff made him feel helpless, so totally helpless, was also another good reason for revenge. His mind was made up. He only had to answer to and lookout for himself now and his logical train of thought concluded that he needed to do this in order to be happy, in order to leave this town without having anything niggling at the back of his mind. Winters had already come to realise that this would be one of those things. Without wasting another thought, he stealthily began across the road, aimed at the sheriffs office. *** The sheriff sat on a chair, his feet up on his desk, reading the local newspaper. He’d just finished all the paper work he needed to do for the day and had just finished his dinner, so he was pretty much content with himself. Then the bell on top of his door sounded indicating that someone had entered his office. He figured that it was his deputy, Johnson. “What the hell do you want, Johnson?” He said, not bothering to look up from his newspaper. The sheriff waited for a couple of seconds for a reply. A reply never came, angrily he folded his paper and slammed it down on his desk, only then looking up to realise that it wasn’t his sheriff…it was Winters. Winters stood there, arms folded, with a massive grin firmly placed upon his face. His smile widened when he saw the look on the sheriff’s face. “I told you I would be back,” he held up his arms and waggled his fingers. “And this time I don’t have any handcuffs on…restricting me!” (see prologue 2). The sheriff slowly stood and took a couple of steps back. “I know what I did was wrong…but you’ve got to understand, I…” Winters took a couple of steps towards the sheriff, his demeanour as intimidating as ever. “I don’t have to understand anything…besides I’m an outsider, why would I understand anything that you do?” With that the sheriff mounted a surprisingly quick attack, thrusting his nightstick forward at Winters. He figured that the method of attack was a useful one to develop, but Winters thought that the attack was fundamentally without aim, and that made all the difference. Winters had time to grin before side stepping left, effectively dodging the blow. He then hit the lawman in the gut with a sharp knee strike, followed up by a blow to the base of the man’s neck with his elbow. The sheriff fell helplessly to the floor. Winters figured that he would probably be paralysed for at least five to ten minutes. Ample time to think over his policy toward outsiders. Winters was momentarily disrupted from gloating over the sheriff’s prone frame, by the bell on top of the sheriff’s door. He quickly whirled around to see Deputy Johnson stood there, froze on the spot, obviously not knowing what to do. “You!” He finally managed, pointing in Winters direction. Winters didn’t even answer, he just attacked. The deputy had no chance to react and Winters punch connected sweetly on the deputies chin, knocking him sideways into a metal filing cabinet. Remarkably, he had remained conscious. “Why…?” He trailed off. “You were there! You could have stopped him! You could have said something! You’re just as bad as he is…!” Winters said. “Remember my face whenever your thug of superior tries to mug an innocent victim.” Winters left the deputy with a swift kick in the gut, before storming out of the office, not waiting to see if anyone was following him. He was too angry to think at the moment. Epilogue He was sat on the back of a wild chicken truck, staring blankly at the sheriff’s wallet. A lot had happened since waking up in the cornfield, but now that he found himself alone and out of danger…he realised a very important thing: He didn’t even have a name. Yes, there was the distinct possibility that he could retrieve his memory in the future, but who knows how long that could take? Could be years. He couldn’t go for years without a name. He had made his decision. He glanced at the name on the sheriff’s badge…it read John Winters. It was a good name to have, strong, demanding and it had a rather special stigma attached to it. Taking the name would forever remind him of the events that took place here…and that there was only one person he could ever reply upon: himself. John Winters rode out of Kansas and towards his destined future.
