Date: 2009-12-02 September 4, 2009 Justin tipped the stainless steel pot filled with instant grits over his bowl, putting it over to the side of the stove before turning over the sausage patties he was frying up on the other burner. His eyes felt dry, a mild sleep deprivation headache dulling his senses as he tried to keep his attention on what he was cooking. He’d never been that great of a cook, but breakfast was always easier to make because all it meant was frying something up or following directions on the side of a cardboard box. Ever since he’d gotten the job with Unity Movers, he’d stopped making his own food, mostly because he was too exhausted from the long hours and the heavy lifting. The meth made it even tougher. He rubbed his eyes with the heels of his hands, grease bubbling up and spitting as the pork crackled. Grabbing a spatula, he flipped a few of the patties over before moving to the window just above the kitchen sink. A school bus was rumbling down the dirt road. There’d been plenty of mornings he’d used the hiss and groan of the bus as a watch, telling him it was time to get off the couch and get into bed. Ana become pretty lonely over the last few months. He watched the neighborhood kids move on their way to school, the young ones scurrying in front while the older ones sluggishly sauntered behind. School had just started up again. From where he stood, seasons stopped having meaning once he was out of school; everyday was just another shift hauling people’s furniture and power tools from one home to another, sweat stinging his eyes and his lower back wound up in knots. Ana Marie was the same way, just that she was pushing keys at a gas station register, fetching pairs of cigarettes for those who asked. Days just bled into one another. He’d never had a real family; mom ran out west someplace and he didn’t even know who the hell his dad was. Crank and backbreaking labor just seemed like it was his course if he wanted to stay afloat. He was on his way to better things though, saving up small chunks of his paycheck to get Ana an engagement ring. Then, they could start a family. She was his everything; the only reason he got up in the morning. The only problem was the hopelessness he felt in not being able to provide better because he’d never graduated from high school. Even if he wanted to start a family, that entailed a whole new slew of problems. He wasn’t ready to face life. Drugs cushioned his depression. But he wanted to put an end to that, to get to something meaningful; it only took losing his source of income because he got caught using meth while on the job for him to realize that. Smoke wafted around her long black mane, her doe green eyes blinking while she continued to groan herself awake. “You’re looking good.” She took a long drag off her cigarette, putting it between her fingers before holstering it as she flashed a meek smile. “Thanks.” “You want any?” “Sure,” she said as she looked down at the bowl of grits, moving to the kitchen table and sitting down while Justin got another bowl out of the cabinet just above the stove. “Why didn’t you tell me that you lost your job until this morning?” He froze for a moment, closing his eyes as he put the bowl on the stove top. “Ain’t put no butter or nothing in them yet.” “We have to talk about this.” He sighed, pushing away from the stove and turning to face her. “I know.” “You took a big step though.” She wasn’t happy about the situation; Justin was making just enough to make sure they had their rent taken care of. He knew that. But he also seemed to know what had made them become so estranged, what had caused him to get himself into the mess in the first place. Flushing his stash of methamphetamine and promising to quit was all she could hope for now. “It’s hard to kick the habit.” “You should know.” “It’s not easy, Justin.
