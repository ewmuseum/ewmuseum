Light.

19 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-19 Some things in life are bad, When darkness consumes you, the only thing that matters is finding that little bit of light. The one thing to hold onto, as long as there is light, darkness will never be able to grab you totally. The light can be any number of things, it can be a physical light, which pours in through the darkness like a beacon, guiding you, pull you towards it against the grasp of the darkness, or it could be something else, like a dream, self belief. No matter how dark it gets, there is always hope, there is always a light you just don’t see it until you enter the darkest places. They can really make you mad, Every so often though, the light fades so that it’s almost invisible. It flickers like a candle in the wind, ready to disappear at any second, once the light has gone, it can never come back and the darkness will have won, once and for all. For as long as there has been dark, there has been light, it is just how the world works, but people are different to the world. We rape and we destroy, in one fell swoop our humanity, our light can be torn from our very beings, leaving simply a shell, the dark. Other things just make you swear and curse, An abandoned warehouse, as is often the case, somewhere out of the way, somewhere, where no-one would ever think to look for you, until they were searching for a body, but everyone knew that Ethan Frost was alive and well, he was supposed to be facing Angelo Deville in round two of the GTT, a match that would thrust Ethan Frost onto the main stage, against an opponent as acclaimed as Deville, everyone knew that Frost was ok, until the news had begun to filter through. Ethan Frost had quit. When you're chewing life's gristle, Sam Gordon, a bitter and twisted woman, with a personal vendetta had seen to that, forfeiting on his behalf, for his own good she had said, when really she wanted to just ruin him, any small sleight he may have made, she would magnify it, turn it into a big deal, a personal attack, anything she could use as ammunition against him. Now the rumors were beginning to circulate. It hit the internet first, the so called fans who sit there over analyzing every move instead of just enjoying it for what it is, coming up with their own conspiracy theories about events, they ranged from drug abuse to being abducted by aliens, however there was one common consensus, which seemed to over power everything else. Ethan Frost was scared of Angelo Deville. All you had to do was look at the odds going into the bout, essentially Frost was a million to one shot, much greater men than him had tried to take down Deville, who had somehow managed to worm his way back into the tournament, despite being eliminated by Rollins, that had all been forgotten now, as far as the fans were concerned it was just a slight bump in the road, exactly what Ethan Frost would be to him. An abandoned warehouse, as is often the case, somewhere out of the way, somewhere, where no one would ever think to look for you. The wind blew freely around the open plan building, rotting window frames, no longer containing glass allowing the air to fill the room. Night was falling. Tied to a chair in the middle of the room was Ethan Frost, his eyes covered by a simple piece of black cloth, his head hung limply, a trail of now dried blood ran from the back of his head down his neck and seeped into his t-shirt. He didn’t realize what was happening in the real world though; he was too busy in his dreams. In the darkness. Don't grumble, It had been an almost perfect plan from Sam Gordon; she just had to keep him out of the way for this one bout. The draw hadn’t been rigged as far as she knew, as it had been previously pointed out, she didn’t have that much sway in PTC affairs, luck must have been on her side. With this she could take anyway out, there were always loopholes but she was pretty sure she could make this stick. The coward story was one that proved to be most fruitful, the utter shame that someone from HER company would turn tail and run away from a challenge, a quick cancel of the contract, pay him off and he would be gone. All she needed to do was to make sure that the REAL story didn’t get out. Otherwise there would be hell to pay. Give a whistle In his chair Ethan Frost began to stir, that part of dreams where the real world influences you, for the time it feels that you are invincible, that no one can hurt you. Yet you know you aren’t fully in control, being led somewhere by a force that’s unseen, part of you knows that in just a few short moments, you will open your eyes and everything will be back to normal. Slowly Frost lifted up his head, and then opened his eyes. And this'll help things turn out for the best. To complete and utter darkness. And… An abandoned warehouse, as is often the case, somewhere out of the way, somewhere, where no one would ever think to look for you, was suddenly filled with the screams of a desperate man. A man who had been plunged against his will into darkness, the initial scream was the shock, that momentary lapse in memory where your mind tells you that you are actually blind, relying only on its immediate senses, as opposed to what has occurred before. The screams were then replaced by grunts, struggling against his binds, he was tied fast and he still had no idea where he was. He could remember he had been hit from behind, hard that was probably why his head was throbbing, but after that he had no clue about anything. He could hear the wind blowing strongly through a large building, it had turned colder, probably indicating that it was night or approaching it at least. He tried his bonds once more, the rope simply rubbed into his wrists further, tearing away the skin slowly, leaving a burning sensation. Reluctantly, he stopped struggling, there was no point, the rope was too tight for any sort of maneuverability and there was no point wasting energy. Instead he regulated his breathing, reclaimed some form of control, despite the immense feeling of helplessness that was beginning to engulf him. It was all down to her, she’d arranged this, it wasn’t a random attack. Thinking back now Frost realized every little piece of the puzzle had been worked out in the slightest detail. Her movements positioning herself at the entrance to the side road, distractions it was all there, but retrospect is a bitch for things like that. Frost leaned back in the chair slowly, the wood creaking under his weight, despite him not being the heaviest person in the world; the wood was clearly old, weak, ravaged through time. Slowly Frost rose to his feet, the chair had been tied to him, his chest, stomach and arms restrained but his feet and legs remained untouched, hunched over Frost began to explore his surroundings, each step tentatively, his foot out probing for a wall or something, some indication of his surroundings or at least the boundaries of the room. High above in the rafters of the warehouse, two figures stood on a balcony looking down, cloaked in shadow, yet the feminine silhouette of one of them was a blatant enough clue to anyone that it was Frost’s captor, Sam Gordon, watching with a level of curiosity at this man simply walking round like a deformed turtle. "I thought he’d have worked it out by now, why doesn’t he simply leave?" A gruff male voice whispered in the wind, for a brief second below Frost paused, not sure if he’d heard something, as he remained still, nothing moved but the wind, he continued his search. "Because he’s been plunged into darkness. The only thing scarier than what you can’t see. I what you can see, he’ll work it out soon enough though. The fire is burning in him, you can tell, I’ve taken something away from him." Sam Gordon spoke, notably quieter than the male voice, as she stepped forwards and leaned on the railing of the aging balcony, the wood creaked gently, once again causing Frost to pause… He was sure that he could hear something above him, it could just be his imagination but everything seemed wrong, everything that led him here had seemed too surreal. He relented and sat down on the chair and breathed deeply, once again the wood creaked dangerously as he leant back, the wood being put to its limits, unknown to Frost. Everything about this was surreal. He was supposed to be preparing to face Angelo Deville, a man who quite literally needed no introduction. If you asked any fan to name their favorite wrestler, nine out of ten would name him. The aura around him, the way that he held himself, conducted his business in and out of the ring, his confrontations, he through himself one hundred percent into everything he did. Frost knew that it would have been a million to one shot, stranger things had happened in his life, his rise from your average Joe, who trudged from day to day in a dull life, part of the system, just another number. Then his numbers had come up, everything had changed. He was an individual, with money who could do what he wanted so he pursued a dream that had been left behind, being a wrestler, it had all seemed so simple then, but wrestling changed people, suddenly lives were turned upside down and events so surreal they just had to be true occurred. Such as being kidnapped and tied up. But this was supposed to be his moment. In his heart he knew he couldn’t win. He simply wasn’t good enough, but he would have liked the chance, people would be let down and for the rest of his life, he would always be cursed with two simple words. What If? It’s like that girl at high school you watched from afar, the girl that you wanted to ask to prom but you didn’t have the guts to go through with it, instead going on your own and having a shit time of it. What if she was the one? It’s the sort of thing that eats away at you until it could drive you insane. He leaned back once more, mostly through frustration and disappointment and suddenly felt the world disappear below him. A resounding crack reverberated through his ears as the aging chair’s leg snapped off and send Frost tumbling backwards to the ground. As he landed the wood underneath his back shattered and splintered, freeing the rope from around his chest, leaving him free to raise to his feet, his arms still bound tightly behind his back, but this was a glimmer or hope, this was an opportunity to free himself. He dropped to his knees and scrabbled around the dirt with his hands and eventually found a large piece of splintered wood and began to rub his bonds against it briskly. It took some time, but he had a mission now, he was nearly free, he just needed to loosen the ropes and he was be able to see again, he’d have his light back. From the balcony above, Sam Gordon watched with interest and for the briefest of moments allowed herself to smile wryly, she’d wondered if the ropes had been tied too tight, but it appeared that they weren’t, everything was coming together. His hands now free Frost removed the blindfold and slowly opened up his eyes, taking in the surroundings, what there was of them. It seemed to be little more than an empty warehouse or at large barn. His intake of his surroundings was cut short though, by an extremely slow, sarcastic clapping above him. "Where are you? I know you’re here Gordon, show yourself!" Frost screamed as he rubbed his hands, the rope had cut into his wrists enough to draw blood. He turned frantically, stepping over the broken pile of wood which once resembled a chair, his eyes scanning for Sam Gordon, there was no where to hide, so why couldn’t he see her? It was just a large floor, presumably once filled with life stock or machinery. "I wondered how long it would take you to work that one out, I have to say I’m not too impressed." The feminine words of Sam Gordon floated down from above, causing Frost’s head to shoot up sharply, almost giving him a minor case of whiplash and there she was, casually leaning on the rail on the balcony above him. Frost wondered how long she’d been there, if she had taken a perverse pleasure in watching his torment and suffering, even watching him whilst he was unconscious. Under his breath he snarled at her, he knew the general rules of chivalry, but if they were on a level surface, they would have been thrown out the window, quickly followed by her carcass. "Shouldn’t you be running along, I’m pretty sure that you’re supposed to be wrestling in about an hour, not long left to get there." Frost was torn, he wanted to end this now, he wanted to confront her here and now, but she was right. A quick look behind him revealed that the light was slowly disappearing on the horizon; the darkness of the night was coming out to play. He’d been given a second chance though, all he had to do was get to the tournament and then beat Deville. He froze. It had all been so simple planning it in his head, what he’d do how he would do it etc, but the dream was quickly becoming reality. He would have to face a foe he had no possible hope of beating, all he could do was hold off the Deville’s horns, but sooner or later he would get tired and would subside, then he would be struck down and the dream, would be over. "Tick Tock, Frost, what are you going to do?" Sam Gordon was goading him, he knew what he should do and he knew what he wanted to do, sadly they conflicted each other. Quickly turning on his heel Frost darted out of the doors of the warehouse leaving Sam Gordon laughing audibly until he was out of ear-shot, at which point she let out a loud breathy sigh, allowing herself to slump on the balcony, resting her head deep in her arms. "I didn’t think he’d buy it." She whispered quietly to herself, her tone had changed, no longer was her voice filled with hate, or the vindictiveness that had often been thrown Frost’s way. Quiet relief and concern had replaced it. The man in shadow behind her had not moved, his head had watched Frost dash out of the warehouse and had remained silent until now. "Do you think he can do it?" There was another silent pause, only the wind added to the conversation, doubt lingered in the air. "I hope so." Said Sam Gordon. "He didn’t have a hope in hell before, but now he’s angry, maybe it will be enough. It’s a million to one shot." ~~~ His heart felt like it was going to explode out of his chest His lungs were pumping like bellows on an ancient steam ship. His legs were burning as they ran across the ground. But he couldn’t stop. ~~~ "What about his injuries?" Gordon sighed, if she’d had another choice she wouldn’t have done it, but there was simply no other way. "His wrists are just a bit burnt from the rope, nothing major there. I’m slightly worried about his head though. He lost more blood than I intended. I’d tried to patch him up as best as possible, but I only know basic first aid, I couldn’t stitch the wound." ~~~ Frost had now reached the venue, he hurt more than any pain a wrestling match could induce. He was aware of the strange looks he was getting as he walked through the lobby, the hushed whispers in the corner as he slowly but surely made his way towards the main desk. Normally he would enter through the stage door, but he’d been blocked. As far as they were concerned, he was out of the tournament. He allowed his hands to slump onto the desk, much to the shock of the receptionist who had literally stopped mid phone call, her mouth agape. "I’m here to fight Angelo Deville." "But sir, I thought you forfe…" "I’m here to fight Angelo Deville." "But sir… "I AM HERE TO FIGHT ANGELO DEVILLE GOD DAMMIT!" ~~~ "Did you sort everything out with PTC to allow him back in?" "He was never out, he just needed to think that he was." ~~~ Now came the silence, the silence that always occurred just before he faced someone. Frost sat eyes closed on a bench in his dressing room. Earplugs rooted in his ears. This was his time of calm, a time to forget what had happened before, that didn’t matter, that would be taken care of when he was done here. He needed to focus on his one target now, the person ahead of him. The ethos and mysticism that surrounded him. Angelo Deville was just a man like any other. And then his eyes flashed open; a burning intensity in his eyes, all the rage and anger had been harnessed into this one moment. Every muscle and tendon in his body ached with unspeakable pain, but this was his chance to shine. Rising to his feet Frost left his dressing room and without turning back, went to face The Deville. It was a million to one shot, but as everyone knows. They’re the best chances you’re going to get. Song Lyrics:Always Look On The Brightside Of Life, by the mighty Monty Python
