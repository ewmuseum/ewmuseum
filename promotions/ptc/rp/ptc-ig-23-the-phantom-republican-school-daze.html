[IG 23: The Phantom Republican] School Daze (aka... the seeds are planted)

15 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Prologue While The Phantom Republican, Gordon Oliver Powell, is far from a new character, he's new to the world of PTC. Outside of those in this community who are or were in NEW and ACW, he is an unfamiliar character. So as with any unfamiliar character, GOP needs to be properly introduced. This series of tales sets out to do just that, establish who this mysterious conservative wrestling booster is and what makes him tick. This series of flashbacks will not necessarily be in chronological order, but they don't have to be. Each piece fits together regardless of when it happened. -- As a young lad, Gordon Oliver Powell... well, he was Gordon Jonathan Powell for starters. His given middle name didn't fit the gimmick he was given, so some promoter changed it. But that's not the point. The point is, as a young lad, Gordon Jonathan Powell was an energetic child. Running around the house, banging on pots and pans, breaking pottery, the little man was a handful for his poor mother. Alice Powell would hide a fifth of bourbon in her pantry every time something would crash to the floor. CRASH! There went a flower pot. Another swig from the flask. CRASH! Lava lamp. Man, that was a bitch to clean up. It warranted three long gulps. CRASH!!! Grecian urn that contained the remains of her father. When she found herself draining a three-quarter full flask of bourbon in one sip over that one, Alice decided she had enough. Drastic measures had to be taken. So everything sugary was taken out of the house. Gordon's regular Pepsi was replaced by Caffeine Free Diet Pepsi, with NutraSweet. Ah yes, the 80s, when the word "carcinogen" probably sounded like an alien race from Star Wars. As a last salvo in her plan to stave off child-induced cirrhosis, Alice planted her son in front of the television as much as she humanly could. Thankfully for his mother, Gordon liked a lot of programming. He-Man, Thundercats, Transformers... but his favorite show came on Saturday morning. WWF Superstars. Oh yes, the cavalcade of personalities who came on the TV enthralled young Gordon. Junkyard Dog, Andre the Giant, Hacksaw Jim Duggan... these were the guys that Gordon clapped and cheered for in front of his TV. However, there was one man who captured Gordon's attention the most. "You know what, Mean Gene..." There he was, his blonde Fu-manchu moustache moving with every word. He talked as much with his largest pythons in the world as he did with his gravelly voice. The only reason why Gordon said his prayers and ate his vitamins was because the man in the yellow tear-away shirt and red bandana told him to. Yes, Gordon Jonathan Powell was a Hulkamaniac. Even though he never wrestled on Superstars, Hogan still was the must-see wrestler for Gordon. Even though Saturday Night's Main Event was on past his normal bedtime, Gordon could always turn up the brattiness to get a reprieve on his curfew. His mother would even order the pay-per-views for him; every time Gordon asked with a slight whine in his voice, Alice just looked over at the liquor cabinent at the bottle of bourbon and decided she didn't want to enter rehab; ordering WrestleMania was far less expensive. Yes, Gordon was quite the little wrestling fan. The fires burned deep within him, and Hogan was the one fanning the flames. As a preschooler, all he could talk about with his playmates was the time Hulk Hogan bodyslammed Andre the Giant. Kids wouldn't sit next to Jimmy Claridge because he shit his pants at exactly 10:32 in the morning every morning. Kids wouldn't go near Wanda Simmons because she bit people who came to close to her dollies. Gordon Powell was another such outcast, not because he smelled or was violent. Every time you got in ear shot. "HEY! Do you wanna play wrestling buddies with me! I got Hogan, you can have The Million Dollar Man!" "Man wrestling was awesome last night. The Ultimate Warrior kicked butt! Hogan's still my favorite though." "I got a new Hogan tee-shirt yesterday! Wanna look at it!?" He was annoying. Even the other wrestling fans in his class shied away because he was obsessive. He never wanted to play house or CandyLand or even tag. Kindergarten saw little Gordon get a little better. It wasn't all wrestling all the time, partially because Hogan wasn't the Champion anymore, and he felt a little disappointed in his hero. Mainly though, it was because he didn't like having his recess pretzel and juice by himself. Sitting in the lonely corner of the room got pretty old pretty fast. Even though his mind couldn't wrap around the concept of multiplication yet, he knew that it wasn't good to be alone all the time, and that he had to change himself, even if it was just a little bit. So one day, a group of his classmates were eating their pretzels and drinking their juice at recess, engaged in one of their seemingly mandatory debates. Today, it was comic book characters. "Well, I think that Superman's the best because he's the first superhero and everyone else copycats off him so bad." "Nuh-uh, all anyone gotsta do is to have that green rock in their pocket and then Superman's toast. The best guy is Batman." "Batman? Naw, he's lame. He don't gots no powers. He's just a guy. Now Spider-man, he's a cool dude." Gordon approached them a little sheepishly at first. He got to the outskirts of their group, clearing his throat to try and garner some attention for himself. When that didn't work, Gordon figured that subtlety wasn't his forte. Forcefully nudging himself into the circle though, that he could do. "Hey, who let you in this conversation?" Gordon tried to find something to say. Umm... umm... he was interrupted. "Yeah, we weren't talking about wrestling, you nerd. We're talking about real cool stuff, like comic books. Go away." "Hey, I... I like comic books." The children's interest was peaked. "Oh yeah?" said the one lad. "Who's your favorite?" "Hulk..." "No, not wrestler, you goober." "Hey, you didn't let me finish poopface. Hulk. The Incredible Hulk. He turns all big and green and it's cool when he says 'Hulk smash!' He'd probably smush that stupid Spider-man like... a spider." Success. He had gotten them to listen to him and take him seriously. He wouldn't have to sit alone during recess anymore. People wouldn't stay away from him during playtime. Every day from then until the end of the school year, Gordon and his newfound friends would talk about all different things. Sometimes they'd be about wrestling, but most of the time, it wouldn't. That was okay though. Gordon didn't mind. It didn't stop him from his Saturday morning ritual of watching WWF TV. It didn't quelch his love of wrestling. The times that they did talk about wrestling further strengthened his love of the sport. He felt smart, like the center of attention. Most importantly, he got to share something with his friends, and mommy always told him that sharing was good. Kindergarten was only a half-day session for Gordon. Life was good that year. In the mornings, he'd go to school and play with his friends, and in the afternoons, he'd come home for lunch. Alice always made sure that he'd have a peanut butter and jelly sandwich waiting for him when he got home at lunchtime. On top of that, he got to watch as much TV as he wanted while mommy went upstairs in her room to read. Sometimes, she went next door for an hour, and when she'd come back, her hair was all messed up and she'd smell a little funny. Gordon never knew what his mom did, but his curiosity to find out could never conquer his love of Thundercats and Transformers. Regardless of any anomalies, Gordon loved being in kindergarten. He never could want anymore from life. Summer vacation came and went. Towards the end of August, Alice let her son know he was going back to school. Gordon got excited. "Cool! I really liked kindergarten!" "No sweetie, you're going to first grade." "Aww, what's that? Is there going to be playtime?" "No, sweetie." "Doh... what about coloring books?" "Maybe, but I don't think so." "Aww... will you still pick me up to be home before lunch?" "No sweetie. You stay at school for lunch." "No! Then I won't be home in time to watch Voltron!" "Of course you won't, sweetie. You're going to be at school until 3." "What? That's not fair! I can't watch Thundercats or He-Man or Transformers!" "I'm sorry sweetie. But you have to go." "No... is there at least recess?" "Of course sweetie." Gordon was a little relieved. As long as he had recess, he had his friends, the people who listened to him and who made him feel like he was part of something special. Their recess gang... nothing could hurt them, nothing could bother them, the young lad thought. Gordon couldn't sleep the night before his first day of school. He was too excited. It wasn't totally a "I can't wait to get to school and LEARN! YEAH!" excitement, but a "what the hell is this going to be like?" excitement. Anticipation for something different, something completely new. Kindergarten was awesome. What would first grade be like? The bus got to the Powell household at 7:18 AM on the dot. Godron was dressed sharply, his hair combed over and bookbag nestled snugly on his back. He turned to go out the door, but a gentle hand on his shoulder stopped him. "Sweetie, you forgot your lunch." Gordon turned around to behold a wondrous sight. His mother held in her hands a lunchbox, but not just any lunchbox. It was a bright yellow box with a picture of Hulk Hogan on it. "I packed your favorite. A peanut butter jelly (TIME!) sandwich." Gordon's face was beaming, a grin from ear to ear. His mommy was the best. He quickly snagged the lunchbox from his mother and hurried out the door to the bus. With the bright smile showing no signs of dimming, he hopped on the bus and headed right to the middle, where his Recess Gang buddy, Joey Mitchell, was sitting. He plopped right down next to Joey with the lunchbox featured prominently in his lap. "Whoa! Cool lunchbox man!" "I know! My mommy got it for me!" "I wish my mom would get me a Hulk Hogan lunchbox. All's I gots is this brown paper bag." Yes, his friends were impressed. Mostly everyone who saw his lunchbox was impressed. Except for one kid who sat in the back of the bus... There two first grade classes at James Madison Middle School. Luckily, everyone in the Recess Gang was in Miss Shockley's class except for Danny Mays, who got stuck in Mrs. Gormley's class. It was okay. They'd see him at lunch, because Mikey Johnson's older brother said that everyone eats lunch together. They were all excited. Another school year, another year of goofing off, talking about different stuff. Joey and Mikey couldn't wait to talk about their summers. Arnie Laycock went to camp. Jeff Lewis was quiet and didn't really want to talk at all. He spent his summer at St. James' Roman Catholic Church Bible Camp, so everyone else assumed he thought it boring. Gordon's tales centered around the summer in wrestling. Mainly, SummerSlam, Hulk Hogan and Randy Savage tagging together to beat the Million Dollar Man and Andre the Giant. Gordon was the only kid whose parents actually bought the pay-per-views for him. He was their personal Dave Meltzer, recapping the event for them to imagine. Suddenly though, someone interrupted their circle. "Wrestling's fake, you nerds." Everyone turned around, Gordon especially. They saw one of the new kids, someone who wasn't in their kindergarten class. He stood a little taller than them, was a lot bigger and unlike the Recess Gang, he never looked happy. Kids, meet Aaron Herbert, the first grade class bully, second year running. Gordon stood up. "Nuh-uh! Wrestling's real! It's the best!" "You're such a baby to believe that. I bet you think that Hulk Hogan really is the toughest guy in the world, don'tcha?" "Y... yeah, I do! What's it to you?" "I hate nerds, and you're the biggest one, wrestling geek. I'm gonna take that Hulk Hogan lunchbox and shove it up your butt come lunchtime." Gordon gulped hard. His buddies looked at him and gulped a little too. By the time lunch rolled around, the Recess Gang had put the incident at the period of their namesake out of their heads and were once again talking about the stuff that kids talk about. Again, they were interrupted. "Hey, wrestle dweeb!" Aaron was there, this time with some cohorts, his brother, Johnny, and the only other person who got left back in the first grade, Richard J. Ellening. "Are you ready to pay the price?" Grodon stood up. "What are you gonna do?" Aaron looked on the table at Gordon's peanut butter jelly (TIME!) sandwich, still in its baggie. "Nothing if you give me your sandwich." "B... b... but that's mmmmy lunch!" "Oh yeah? Well, would you rather eat and get your butt kicked, or would you rather give me your sandwich and be safe?" Gordon's head slumped forward. He stepped aside and Aaron took his sandwich right from the table. "Haha, and Mom said I wasn't going to be able to have lunch today." The bully and his posse walked off. Gordon sat back down and sipped his Juicy-Juice, the only part of his lunch that still remained. His buddies looked at him out of pity. This humiliation continued every day for the next two weeks. Aaron extorted Gordon for his lunch every day, and every day, Gordon took it quietly. His friends didn't, but more importantly, couldn't do anything about it. Aaron was twice as big as any one of them. Of course, when you should be in third grade after you've repeated first grade and kindergarten (think about it... this kid must have some malfunction if he had to repeat kindergarten!), you're going to be pretty big in comparison to your classmates. Gordon tried to find ways to get out of his humilation. He tried lying to Aaron about not having a lunch, but just hiding his lunchbox under the table wasn't enough to fool the dimwitted bully. He tried faking being sick, but his mother saw right through it. One day, he even asked his mother to pack him a different lunch. "Mommy, can you pack me liverwurst and onions today?" "Liverwurst and onions? Geez, your father eats that crap. And he wonders why he doesn't get laid at night..." "What was that, mommy?" "Oh, nothing... sweetie, you don't want liverwurst and onions. It's gross. What's wrong with peanut butter and jelly?" "Oh... nothin'... I just wanna... have somethin'..." Gordon got choked up. A tear welled in his eye. "...diff'rent." Alice Powell saw right through that. "Sweetie... what's wrong?" "Some... someone keep stealing my lunch. He takes my sandwich every day and if I don't give it to him, he threatens to beat me up." "Well, that's not right. I'm going to call school right away. What's this bully's name?" Gordon was silent. He didn't want to be a rat. He saw what happened to tattle tales. They got the crap kicked out of them, even in kindergarten and first grade. He just shook his head. "C'mon babe. Don't you want this to stop?" He nodded. "Okay then, who was it?" He sheepishly said, "Aaron Herbert." That day, after recess and before lunch, Gordon and Aaron were both called to the principal's office. When they got there, a skanky looking blond woman with bags under her eyes was there with Principal Jefferson. "Children sit down." They sat in the desk in front of the principal as the woman stood impatiently next to them. "Now, I understand you two children have been having a misunderstanding. Aaron, Gordon's mother here has called and said that you keep stealing his lunch." "That is a lie!" the blond woman blurted out. "Mrs. Herbert, please, let Aaron tell his side." "Umm... no... Mr. Herbert, I would never steal someone's lunch. My mommy packs me a peanut butter and jelly sandwich every day." "Damn straight I do. That bitch..." "Mrs. Herbert! No language like that in front of the kids!" "But yeah... my mommy... she loves me so much. She'd never send me to school with nothing to eat for lunch." "That Mrs. Powell... she's a BAD mother! Sending her child to school without a lunch every day and then trying to get my child in trouble. That bi... err... her child should be punished!" Gordon tried to get a word out, but he was cut off. "Listen, I really don't know who to believe here. I doubt a woman would call up the school trying to get someone in trouble unless her child was really being affected. But your story seems to corroborate. I always see Aaron with a sandwich at lunchtime, and I never see Gordon with one." That's because Aaron keeps stealing my lunch and you never get to the lunchroom til after it's too late, the lad thought. "So I have no choice but to let you kids go. However, if there are any more incidents, you're both in trouble. Do you hear me?" Gordon sheepishly nodded, while Aaron replied, "Yes, Mr. Jefferson." On the way to the lunchroom, Gordon walked with his head down, his Hogan lunchbox held loosely by his side. Suddenly, he was grabbed by his shoulder into the broom closet. *THUD* He was slammed against the wall and a fist found its way into his stomach. He dropped his lunchbox to his side. "YOU RAT!" A second fist went into his gut. "Don't you EVER do that again! You never tell on anyone, NEVER! Especially not ME!" A THIRD punch. "If you tell anyone what happened now, or if you tell your mommy again and she tells on me, then I'll steal your lunch every day AND kick your butt so bad that your mom won't even know it's you. Do you understand?" Gordon nodded. Aaron bent over and grabbed the sandwich out of his lunchbox. He took it out of the baggie and took a bite. "Liverwurst and onions? How'd you know this was my favorite, dweeb?" When Gordon got home, his mother was right there to greet him. "Hi sweetie! How was school?" Gordon had a vacant look on his face and sheepishly said, "Okay." "That's good. Did you get that issue straightened out?" Gordon nodded very weakly. "Aww, see? I told you everything was going to be okay. I'm going to go next door and help Mr. Burrows with some plumbing. You sit tight, okay?" Alice pecked her son on the cheek as she walked out the door, oblivious to the fact that her son's body language belied his affirmative answers. The next morning, Gordon was watching WWF Superstars. He wasn't watching it with his normal fervor. His mind was somewhere else, thinking about ways he could stop this bully from stealing his food every day. Hulk Hogan came on the screen with Mean Gene, but Gordon was still in his daze, staring into the screen. Then, suddenly, something registered with him. "Hulkster, you jeopardized your relationship with the Macho Man Randy Savage when you took Miss Elizabeth to the back. Why did you do that?" "Well you know what Mean Gene? I had a choice to make. I coulda left her out there in harm's way. But I didn't. Y'see Mean Gene, you gotta fight for what's right sometimes, even if that means that you gotta strain a friendship. You gotta do what's right. You gotta fight for what's right." "You gotta fight for what's right." "You gotta fight for what's right." Gordon realized what he had to do. That next Monday, at lunch, Aaron came around again. "Hey nerd, what's on the menu today? PB and J, liverwurst or something different?" Gordon heard him and ignored him. "Hey, you, I asked you a question. What's for lunch today?" Gordon ignored him again. "Hey! Do you want to get your butt kicked again?" Gordon was silent. "Hey! Answer me!" Gordon stood up and turned around in defiance. "No. I'm not giving it to you today." Aaron looked at him shocked. "What, do you want to get your butt kicked?" Gordon shook his head. "Then give me the sandwich!" Gordon shook his head again. Steam almost shot out of Aaron's ears and his face turned bright red. He cocked his fist back. "I'm going to give you to the count of three to give me that sandwich, or else I'm going to knock you out." Gordon stared at him belligerently. "One. "Two." Then, it happened. CRASH! Aaron went down in a heap. Gordon had leveled him with a clothesline. Despite being twice Gordon's size, Aaron fell like a ton of bricks, partially because Gordon put as much as he could into the clothesline, partially because Aaron was taken so much off guard. Gordon took two steps back and Aaron got up looking absolutely, God-awfully pissed off, like Mrs. Burrows would look if she had ever walked in on her husband and a certain next door neighbor of theirs fixing the plumbing. "Big mistake, goober." Aaron charged in like he was going to pummel the living Christ out of Gordon, but... THUMP! Gordon got his foot up. Aaron ran into his Payless store brand sneaker and crumpled to the ground again. The Big Boot. As Gordon saw what he did, he could only think of one other thing he could do. He ran over to Aaron, still prone on the lunchroom floor... ...and he dropped the leg. The lunchroom exploded. Gordon got up and all his friends surrounded him, cheering him on. Friends patting him on the shoulder. One pat seemed a little hard, and he wouldn't let go. It wasn't one of Gordon's friends though. It was Principal Jefferson. The principal's office was a little more crowded this time. Alice Powell made the quarters a little more cramped than it was with just the other four in by themselves. Of course, it didn't help that Mrs. Herbert was going ballistic. "What kind of school do you run, you stupid son of a bitch! My little angel was ASSAULTED for doing absolutely NOTHING! This little bully has thrashed my son! I can't believe it! I'm going to sue you and this school district for every penny that it's worth! Do you hear me? Do..." "Mrs. Herbert... settle yourself. I will take care of this matter, just calm down, and I'll make everything alright. Gordon Powell will be suspended." "No! I want him EXPELLED!" "I can't do that. He wasn't the only one implicated in this. Your son had a part in it." "Are you implying my special little angel would do something wrong? Slander! Slander!" "Mrs. Herbert! Settle down. He had a role, but it's not worth doing anything. Just to warn you, if he acts up, I will have to discipline him. But for now, I just want to send him home for the day. You can escort yourselves out of my office now, and you'll be hearing from me later. "Oh yeah? Well you'll be hearing from my lawyers. Come Aaron." The Herberts stormed out of the office. "Mrs. Powell, first, let me apologize..." "No, let me apologize, Dr. Jefferson..." "It's Mr. I only got my Masters." "I'm terribly sorry about that and about the way my son acted." "Thank you, but I'm sorry too. I should have believed him when he told me that he was the one who's lunch was being stolen." "What? I... Gordon, you told me that it was resolved!" "No, apparently it wasn't, Mrs. Powell. I can't do anything though because there's no real evidence, and while that doesn't matter here, the evidence, that is, I don't want to give this woman any more fodder in her case against us." Mrs. Powell huffed. "Alright... well, I'm still terribly sorry." "It's alright. Your son is suspended for two days. Standard procedure for anyone who starts a fight, whatever grade they're in." Mrs. Powell nodded as her and Gordon left the office. The car ride home wasn't as pleasant as it was back when Gordon was in kindergarten. "I can't believe it! First you lie to me and tell me that everything's okay, and then you pull this stunt? Mister, you are in big trouble. You know suspensions stay on your record? What if you don't get into the magnet high school? What if you dont' get into college? What if you... *gasp* have to work at McDonald's for the rest of your life, huh?" Gordon didn't feel much like talking, thank you. He sat sullen in the passenger's seat. "Ooh, that's... wait 'til your father finds out... you're grounded. Two weeks. No television, no action figures, no nothing! Do you understand?" Nothing. "I said, do you understand?" Nothing. "Answer me! I'll make it three weeks if you don't. Do you understand?" Gordon nodded. Everything just swirled around in Gordon's little head like the clothes in the washer. What did he do wrong? He didn't do anything wrong. Aaron was stealing his food, lying to his mother and the principal about it and threatening him with bodily harm if he didn't comply. All Gordon did was fight back. He fought for what was right. And he was getting punished for it. It didn't make sense. He just felt like crying. The torment of his own mind, still immature but being forced to grow up a little bit in this time of trying, almost consumed him during the two days he was suspended. Thursday morning came. Gordon went downstairs, looking kept and neat, to wait for the bus. His mother greeted him there. "Please honey... please, no more. Please behave yourself." Gordon just looked at her sullenly. She handed him his Hulk Hogan lunchbox with an empty look in her eyes. The horn sounded, and Gordon went outside. As he walked to the bus, his head was still filled with the doubts about his actions, about how he was punished for fighting for what was right. The bus doors opened, he walked up the steps. As he turned his body around to face his peers, he looked away, but out of the corner of his eye and in his ears, he perceived a very surprising reaction. The entire bus, every single student, save for one big kid in the back, stood up. And they cheered him. They clapped, hooted and hollared for Gordon Jonathan Powell, the kid who stood up to a bully twice his size and who should have been two grades ahead by then. Everyone, his Recess Gang friends, the nerds, the cool kids, the girls, the boys... even the district's only retarded kid who rode the big bus because there was no way to justify getting a short bus just for him... they applauded him. Gordon turned his face around, and his head cleared immediately. Right then and there, he knew he had done the right thing. *- The Hulk Hogan "quote" is "inexact" (IE, I kinda made it up) and the timeline for when that event REALLY happened is a little off.
