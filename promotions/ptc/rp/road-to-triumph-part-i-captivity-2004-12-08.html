Road to Triumph – Part I: Captivity

8 December 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-12-08 Road to Triumph – Part I: Captivity It has been a long and eventful trip down the road to the GTT title, from the mountains of success to the pits of despair and back again. But I have managed to keep going, through the rough terrain and the smooth, and still travel down this road with no apparent reason at all, no particular reason to continue this quest, or at least that’s what the outside world thinks. After all, there’s no way I can win this match they say, I’m all out of flukes they say, there’s no way I can pull another rabbit out of the hat. But I keep going, not because I don’t agree with the doubters, I’m fully aware that I’m the outsider in this contest and even though I’ve defeated a number of people in this tournament in this match I’m probably an even bigger outsider than in previous matches. But I keep going, for one reason and one reason only… Just in case… I pull one more rabbit out of that hat. It can’t be done they say. There is no way that I can do the unthinkable once more. I might have beaten one of 4WW’s top draws Kyo Kushinaga. I might have defeated the wrestler who the knowledgeable fancied, Sapphire. I may have defeated GTT1 finalist, Jay Phoenix. I may have beaten established WWA star Odium. I may have defeated Angelo Deville, the favourite for the tournament and previous GTT winner. I may have defeated tournament specialist Kasper Sky, a man fancied by a fair few as their ‘dark horse’. But still, after proving myself against all of these credible players, the critics still doubt me. I can’t cause another fluke upset. I can’t pull another miracle out of my arse. Well I have three people who believe that I can pull another win out of my arse, and they happen to be the only three people who matter. My girl, my faithful duck and me… -----=====|=====----- The 53 bus is packed with people, its major stop the local hospital in D.C which I’ve already visited a couple of times previously the past couple of months. People sit on the worn out faded red seats with a whole host of injuries. There are slings and plasters left and right of me. The bus is made up predominantly of people going to the hospital, as am I. Why am I going to the hospital? Well I just received a rather strange message! I just received a text message from Wendy. She is apparently unwell, a pretty nasty throat infection to be precise, and cannot speak, hence the message. So I’m heading off to the hospital to see her before the check-up. It’s a tad strange for her to go to the hospital for something that a GP could quite easily look at, but she’s obviously pretty unsettled by this and wants to make sure it isn’t anything more serious. It’s bloody miserable in here I tell you! These people may have illness and injury, but a smile wouldn’t go amiss. It’s like sitting in a minibus on the way to a Radiohead concert, these people would rather hang themselves than go and see whether they’re okay. To take my mind of the misery, I take a look out of the window at the busy streets with swarms of people moving about the city in a hurried scuttle. With many crossroads and traffic lights, a queue of cars trying to make their way though, the whole thing just reminds me of my own journey these past few months. It’s a strange thing to grasp, bettering 189 of the best in the world, in a hurried frenzy of match-up after match-up, and although I was set amongst some of the greatest of obstacles, some of the tournament favourites, no less. I have managed to overcome it and continue down that road. This road has been long, this road has had its low points and its high, but I am here for the end of the journey. Whether I finish this the victor or the loser, I’ll always be able to look back and see just how far I have travelled. But I’m not here for the trip. This is a wrestling contest, and one I thoroughly intend to win. The bus nears its stop at the local hospital. All the miserable people pull themselves slowly to their feet and hobble up the aisle to the door. I hope Wendy’s okay. There’s all likelihood that it is just a throat infection, but if it’s bothering her so much to go to the hospital then she must be under a lot of pain, it might be something much more serious. I guess I’m about to find out… -----=====|=====----- “Alo…?” I say, leaning over the counter of the hospital’s reception. This seems to distract the young lady sitting behind the counter who was tapping something into the computer. She turns her head and bears a wry smile. “Hi,” she says flashing her crystal blue eyes in my direction. The look distracts me for a second, her eyes instantly reminding me of Wendy’s and alerting me even more to the possibility that she may have throat cancer, or something equally serious. “Erm…” I say, stuttering as my mind wanders to Wendy, “Wendy Sanchez…? Where is Wendy Sanchez?” “Heh…” the girl replies with a silent chuckle, slightly puzzled by my distant look and jumbled speech, “well… I’ll have a look for you.” The girl turns back to her computer, giving a few rapid taps on the keyboard and then looks back to me with her face crumpled up in bewilderment. “Did you say Wendy Sanchez?” she asks as if she must have made some kind of mistake. “Yeah, that’s right.” I say assuring her. “Hmm… she doesn’t seem to be on our database, sorry, are you sure she hasn’t gone to a different hospital?” “It’s possible, I suppose. Thanks anyway.” That’s strange… the text message did state this hospital. It’s the same hospital I’d visited a couple of times over the last few months… strange that she isn’t listed there. I walk over to the waiting area and plonk myself down on one of the seats to re-check the text message. Pulling out my mobile phone I notice in the corner of my eye Santa Claus, with a little boy on his knee asking what he wants for Christmas no doubt. There’s nothing that I want for Christmas, everything I ever wanted I have with Wendy and Ducky. With them two around, everything else is insignificant in comparison. The phone flickers on. I press my way through the menus to the text message and begin to read it once more. “Ah! Mister Hurst!” I’m interrupted by a strange voice with a thick foreign accent. Tilting my head upwards to see the source it appears to be a doctor, the white coat really gives it away, you know? “I am sure you are expecting to see your friend, Vendy Sanchez, correct?” How does this guy know about Wendy? “Yeah… do you know her?” “Personally…? No… she is here at ze hospital, I am her doctor.” That’s strange. Maybe it was some kind of computer error or something. They always have these kinds of things no doubt. “How is she?” I ask, concerned. “Vendy is fine… come zis way I vill take you to her.” Apprehensively, as if something is bothering him, the doctor moves up the corridor signalling for me to follow him by waving his arm wildly. It may just be the man’s eccentricity, but there is definitely something very strange about this doctor. He doesn’t look like a conventional doctor by any means, his coat ragged, his hair messy and an aura that is more madness than anything professional. -----=====|=====----- The doctor pushes the door open, and as it slowly swings around and I peer in, I expect to see a wide open, well lit hospital room with a bed and the like, imagine my surprise to see a scarcely lit opening to a set of wooden stairs. This isn’t a hospital room, I’m quite aware of that. This is nothing but an abandoned basement. And I thought the NHS was bad! Somehow I don’t think this is very normal, but before I can question the promised location of Wendy, the doctor circles around behind me. He violently shoves me forwards through the doorway causing me to hurtle forward, fall down the wooden stairs, and crash chest-first into the solid concrete floor of the basement with a thud, the impact almost making me feel like my ribs have crumpled inwards and my breath totally taken away. As I recover from the winding, and slowly push myself upward bearing the pain of the fall, my eyes slowly look upwards and I see something that was slightly expected, but not something that I had hoped. There was the long blonde hair, the glistening blue eyes and the plush rosy cheeks. Before me was my Wendy. But she didn’t have any kind of throat cancer, I doubt she had any illness at all, the only thing she did have was a wooden chair and a long rope wrapped around her. She was kidnapped. Before I could react at all a devastating weight pressed down on my back crushing me into the concrete surface and making my ribs burn up with pain. A large hand pushes my head into the ground and then grabs my hands and brings them up behind my back before wrapping a rope circling around my wrist. “Vell done, Nurse,” the doctor said commending whatever large oaf had a knee into my back. “Sit him up on zat chair over zere.” I get hoisted into the air by this behemoth onto my feet and lead across the room. A mixture of feelings run through me… the pain of the fall, the relief that Wendy has no terminal illness, the fear of what these people may do to Wendy and I, even intense anger that this man has done something evil for no apparent reason. As much as the anger flows and every aching moment makes me want to run at him and break this man apart, I can’t. I’m tied up. The problem still remains, we’ve been captured by this mad man and this ‘nurse’ and there doesn’t seem to be any way out of this… TO BE CONTINUED…
