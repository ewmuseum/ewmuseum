Internal Passion: The Process Part II

28 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-28 June 12th, 2003 5:15 PM CST John couldn’t make his hands stop trembling. The harder he tried the more they seemed to quiver. He still hadn’t accepted his actions of the hour previous. The series of events ran through his head over and over again. Why did he pick up the baseball bat? Why did he hit that man? He wasn’t sure if he’d ever be able to answer that question. The office area he sat in was quiet except for the television that blared from across the room. One of the officers sat there watching the local news. John tried his best to focus on the boob tube, but the images kept invading his mind. He placed his face into his hands and did something he hadn’t done since his dog Marvin had been hit by a car when he was ten. He wept. He wept as silently as he could, trying not to bring attention upon himself. Everything had finally overcome him. He thought for sure his life was over. There’s no way he could escape repercussions for his actions. The sobs tapered off after a few moments and he was able to collect himself. He thought of how the simple crying he had done had drawn his mind from the haunting thoughts that ran a course through it. It was certainly a little refreshing at that point. His mind was suddenly snapped back to the situation at hand as the police officer cleared his throat. "Hey kid, you’re on TV." He blurted out. Covel looked to the screen and spotted himself being led to a cop car. The shame enveloped him again and he shook his head to break the stare he held at the TV. The cop began speaking under his breath about the story he watched. John could tell they were ignorant comments shot in his direction. "John, would you please come with me?" Says a voice. John looks to his right to see one of the Sergeants standing in the doorway. John nodded and followed the man out the door. They traveled a short distance and entered a small interrogation room. The door shut, making John fear what could be next. The sergeant joined Covel at the table and stared at his trembling hands. "Why don’t you take a deep breathe, John." He offered. He tried but he couldn’t. His hands continued to tremble. He was so worried that this meeting was going to end with handcuffs being clasped onto his wrists. He could envision himself being led out of the office and into a concrete box. "I can’t settle down sir. I’m just too damn nervous." John replied. The sergeant let out a half chuckle as he took a deep breathe of his own and leaned back into his chair. John shot a disgusted look in the man’s direction. What a heartless bastard. How could he be laughing at a time like this? John dropped his head and stared at the floor, mouthing disrespectful phrases. "Son, I brought you in here to give you some good news." The officer said. John lifted his head. His face had lit up. Any good news was music to John’s ears. He scooted his chair in and finally took in a full breathe. He felt cleansed in a small way. He shook out of his false sense of security, realizing he didn’t know the news yet. For all he knew, it could have just been that someone had posted the one thousand dollars for bail. "What is it, sir?" John asked. His voice was almost inaudible. The seconds began to seem like minutes as he waited for the next set of vibrations to be emitted from the man’s vocal chords. He cleared his throat and placed a hand on John’s shoulder. "Son, the charges have been dropped. You’re free to go." He stated. John stared at the man blankly. Did he hear correctly? How could he have been so lucky to have the charges dropped? He attacked a man with a fucking baseball bat. What kind of man would forgive that act? John owed a great deal of debt to that man. He’d pay it too. He just didn’t know it yet. John stammered through a few words before being interrupted. "Yes. He dropped the charges. You can gather your things and head home." He said. The sergeant smiled and nodded as he stood. The blank expression still hadn’t left John’s face as he followed suit. The officer opened the door and motioned for John to exit. As they retreated down the hallway, the blank expression finally left John’s face. It had finally hit him. A smile emerged onto his face and the butterflies seemed to fly from his stomach. He was on cloud nine. "I can’t believe this." John said. They rounded a corner and headed for the front desk. Suddenly the smile disappeared and for the second time in less than a half hour he cried. This time he openly wept for everyone to see. What had he done to deserve this? Why had he been given a mulligan on life? The sergeant placed a hand on John’s shoulder and stopped him a few feet shy of the lobby. "Son, you should believe it. These kinds of things don’t happen too often, but when they come around they sure feel good. I don’t condone what you did, but he should have never said what he did." He said. John collected himself and looked up. He wiped the few remaining tears from his face and sighed. He nodded because he knew both comments were true. He could have re-acted a thousand different ways to the words spoken by the umpire, but he didn’t. "Yes, sir." Is all he could muster. He nodded and cleared his throat. He turned and began walking again, really looking forward to getting home. The sergeant ducked behind the desk and spoke to the secretary briefly before turning and looking at John. "You’ll be all right, kid. Don’t worry about it." He said. John smiled as the man turned and headed into an office. The middle aged woman at the counter set some documents in front of John. Her voice sped at a hundred miles an hour as she explained what he needed to do. Normally he would have shook his asked and asked to hear it again, but there was so much adrenaline pumping through his body he was able to catch every word of it. "So, why’d you do it?" She asked. John stopped in the middle of signing his name and looked to the woman. Her expression didn’t change at all as he glared. He sighed and looked back down to finish his autograph. He knew he had better get used to it. He was going to get it a lot over the next decade. "Heat of the moment." He said. She grinned and nodded as he flipped the page and signed one final time. He hadn’t really thought about anything in the last fifteen minutes and knew he’d have plenty of time to think about things on his drive home. He scribbled the last line in his name and set the pen down. "Hang onto that signature. It’s going to be worth something." He said with a wink. She scoffed as he turned and pushed through the glass door leading into the parking lot. He was really in a happy go lucky mood right then. If he only knew what awaited him four and a half hours later. June 12th, 2003 10:37 PM CST John was mentally exhausted. He made the final turn into his driveway, eager to get back home. The drive had given him sufficient time to contemplate his future. That’s one thing he had never considered until his cell phone rang around the Quad Cities. He hadn’t thought about the professional repercussions he faced. The call certainly had awoken him from the dream he was living. Now he arrived home awaiting a return call from office of the commissioner. "Have I had any calls?" He yelled. He burst through the front door of his home and headed for the answering machine. Nothing. He stood by the phone, expecting it to ring any moment. He dropped his bags and took a short lived seat before his mother approached him. "No. I was so worried about you. Why didn’t you answer your phone?" She asked. He felt horrible. He could hear the concern coming from his mother’s voice. He knew she had called him nearly a dozen times during his drive, but hadn’t answered them because he was too busy convincing himself that everything was going to be fine. "I was pre-occupied." He said. He stood and tried to push past her, but she stopped him and placed her arms around his neck. He accepted the embrace and sighed as he watched his father enter the room as well. He never liked when he was in the room with his both his parents. He always expected he was in trouble and about to receive a talk. "John, come on into the living room. Let’s have a talk." His dad said. No fucking way. How could they still do this to him? Anger filled his body as he followed them into the next room and sat. He was a grown man and they were still giving him lectures? He thought for a second and decided to give them a shot. "Quite a day?" His mother asked. He nodded and sighed. This was it. The first awkward silence of the evening. Why couldn’t they just let him listen to music and wait for his phone call? The music would clear his mind. It always did. Anytime he had a problem he could put in a CD and sit there, getting lost in the beat. Before long he’d have let the problem drift from his conscience. "What happened, John?" His father inquired. At first he just shrugged, but he knew that wouldn’t be good enough for his father. He let silence fill the air again as he looked anywhere but at his parents. If they locked eyes, it was over; he was in it for the long haul. "I don’t know. My emotions finally just overcame me. I was so pissed off that he said those things to me. It hurt me." He said. He knew what they were thinking. It wasn’t like him to let emotions overcome his better judgment. But sometimes even the best of people make mistakes and rash decisions. The emotions they’ve suppressed for so long finally pop the figurative condom that had been placed over them. That’s how John had put himself at ease. That was his explanation. "That’s not you, John." His mother said. He began feeling sick to his stomach. The interrogation and anticipation jostled his insides intensely. He thought about just getting up and leaving the room, but couldn’t bring himself to it. After all, they were trying to be there for him. "Sometimes things don’t go the way they’re supposed to. I’d let emotion build inside of me for years and it finally came to the point where I couldn’t control it. Shit happens." He said. Immediately after he said it he cringed. "Shit happens? You assaulted a man with a baseball bat!" His father said. John stood and threw up his arms. "Do you think I’m proud of what I did, Dad? I regret every fucking thing that happened on that field. But I can’t take it back now." He stated. He ran his hand through his hair and began pacing, still awaiting the ring of the phone. He so desperately wanted it to ring. Not just to set his soul at ease, but to end this "meeting." "No you can’t change them. But that kind of shit doesn’t happen on a daily basis. By all rights you should be sleeping on a metal plate in Des Moines tonight. For reasons I’ll never know, let alone understand, he dropped the charges." His mother ranted. Suddenly the tension was broken by the ring of the phone. John looked to his parents with fear as it rang a second time. He bolted into the kitchen and picked up the cordless phone. He stared at it as it rang for a third time. He gulped and pressed a button, putting it to his ear. "He-hello?" He stammered. He figured his parents would have joined him by now, but they didn’t. They remained in the living room. "This is." He confirmed. His hands began trembling for the first time since he left the police station, making it hard to hold the phone. He listened intently as an indistinct male voice spoke into his ear. He nodded along with his "yes" answers. "I understand. I agree." He spoke. As the conversation continued, his voice became shaky and slightly broken up. For almost three minutes, John remained silent. All he did was listen. He took a deep breathe and closed his eyes before hearing the click on the other end. He slowly turned the phone off and set it on the counter before slowly making his way back into the living room. "Well?" His mom asked. He leaned against the empty wall across the room from his parents and slowly slid down to a sitting position, burying his hands into his knees. His breathing began to become heavy, bringing concern to his mother. She rose and rushed to him, placing a hand on his shoulder. Moments later he took a deep breathe and looked up with a tear rolling down his cheek. "I’ve been banned from professional baseball in the United States." October 19th, 2006 3:37 PM EST John’s stomach sank. He saw his final attempt at re-uniting with his true love become what he believed to be futile. He was so sure he was re-instated that he hadn’t even thought of what he would do if he had failed. He sat in his chair and looked up to Selig for a definite answer. "I, the Major League Baseball Commissioner Bud Selig vote…" This was it. Never had time seemed to slow this much. It seemed like John was the only person or thing in real time. In the split second he had, EVERYTHING flashed before his eyes. His games in t-ball, little league and high school. The day he got drafted was there too. So did his three perfect games. But something was missing. The dreaded day wasn’t there. "…yes." Suddenly it was like two years of John’s life had been erased. It felt like he was back on the bullpen mound warming up for his game when he received the call from Jim Hendry. The crowd murmured loudly as John’s head fell against the table in front of him. He felt numerous pats on the back, congratulating him as he tried to take everything in. Just like that June day, his emotions overcame him again. This time he didn’t lash out with a weapon. This time he stood from his chair and began accepting handshakes from people around him with tears streaming down his cheek. He had a right to cry. He had taken the mulligan he was given and turned it into a hole in one. "This hearing has officially reinstated one Jonathan Michael Covel into professional baseball with a final vote of sixteen to fifteen." It was official. John had re-united with the love of his life. He could already smell the freshly cut grass and the hotdogs. The crack of the bat he never wanted to hear rang in his ears, much to his delight. "However we will have to go over a few things Mr. Covel. I expect to see you in my office at nine AM on the first." Bud instructed. John nodded. He was back on cloud nine. He knew he’d be there at seven AM. The hearing was dismissed and more people approached to congratulate John. Nothing could have brought him down. Or so he thought… "John?" Came a voice. John would have never forgotten that voice. It was the voice that had set him off in the heat of the summer. He slowly turned and spotted the man he attacked standing before him. The joy rushed from his body, replaced by apprehension. "Hi." John said. The man half smirked at John’s reaction. "John, I know what happened caused you a great deal of mental anguish." What? It caused John mental anguish? What about the physical suffering this man went through? John wanted to snap him out of it and realize what had happened. "I haven’t fully forgiven you for what you did and probably never will. But I just wanted to tell you that I’ve moved on and wish you luck on your new lease on baseball." He extended his hand to John, whom simply stared at it. After a few seconds he came to and accepted it, smiling back to the man. He still couldn’t believe the man. Three years ago he laid in a hospital bed awaiting his second back surgery. Today he stood there, making a civilized conversation with the man who put him there. "Thank you. You have no idea how much weight that lifts from my shoulders. Thank you very much." John replied. They nodded to each other and went their own ways. Slowly the room had emptied. John followed suit and exited, only to be stopped by another familiar voice. "Hey kid. You want to grab lunch?" Inquire the voice of Jim Hendry. Covel smiled and extended his hand. "Sure thing." He replied. Now who could deny his popularity and skill? Five minutes after becoming a free agent, he was already negotiating. The fun was no where near over. It was just beginning.
