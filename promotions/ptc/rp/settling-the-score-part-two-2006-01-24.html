Settling the Score: Part Two

24 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-24 A few years ago, Craig Goat would have been terrified. Soundbytes flowed through his mind as the Latin chanting that constituted perhaps the best known song in RPG history, "One Winged Angel" left the gathering at the wrestling event at something of a loss. "He ruined my career,"Craig heard Seymour Almasy say, on one of a dozen wrestling talk shows. A loud crack assaulted his ears, but it wasn’t part of the song. It was from a terrible memory, the memory of Seymour driving a steel chair into the cranium of the woman he loved. Getting revenge on him was one thing. Attacking Nurse was another, entirely. "Esme, get over here!" he called, and she immediately obeyed. The tone of Goat’s voice was perhaps more confident than he really felt, but at the same time, there was little fear in his heart. After all, what could Almasy really want? A wrestling match? Sure, it would be awkward, but he could take on Seymour here and now. And win, too. Nurse clutched Craig’s arm, eyes scanning the park for any sign of the former PTC Global Champion. None was present, save for the blaring boss music which had so rattled everyone. "Where is he," she asked, frustrated, half-expecting him to pop out of nowhere as if he was a character in one of his games, or perhaps descend from the sky, sword drawn, to impale her. But no, Seymour Almasy was but a man. A man driving a Corvette that finally came into view. A man who, as it turned out, parked that Corvette right next to Goat’s own modest Honda Accord. A man who was getting out of that Corvette. Craig Goat should have been terrified, but he wasn’t. To scores of athletes in federations all over Primetime Central, Seymour Almasy was a god. To scores of fans, he was untouchable. But to Craig Goat, Seymour Almasy was neither divine nor invulnerable. He was simply a competitor. A good one, sure, but certainly not unbeatable. After all, he himself was the proof. ------------------------------- With the exception of during his match with Clyde Walkins, Seymour couldn’t remember a recent instance where his adrenaline had been flowing quite this much. Walkins had been, by all accounts, this year’s Craig Goat. The man who would rise from near anonymity to make a huge run in the tournament. Like Goat, he’d pulled off several astonishing upsets. Like Goat, he’d drawn Seymour Almasy. But for Clyde Walkins, the clock had struck midnight. The man who had rode his momentum to an improbable PRIME Universal Championship had fought hard, but Seymour had managed to get the victory over an underdog that had eluded him last time out. So why, then, was he here? He’d avenged that defeat in the best way he could. Goat wasn’t Walkins, but the two men were pretty close. That didn’t seem to matter as Seymour walked around to the trunk, opening it to remove his finely crafted blade. He grinned, looking at the scabbard, and clapped his weapon to his side. Everyone else, it seemed, killed to get where they were. Who knew how many people Angelo Deville had killed en route to winning GTT3? The man was a noted member of the Mafia, and had blood colder than ice. Even the bumbling Doctor Curiosity was rumored to have done horrible deeds during the course of his run to the GTT4 title. Who would care, Seymour thought wildly, if he struck down Craig Goat? It seemed that tournament was a perfectly legitimate excuse for murder. With the steady gaze of an assassin and perfect posture, Seymour began marching towards the wrestling ring in the park. His hands, though, did not go for his sword. They remained at his sides as he continued to walk. Unlike seemingly everyone else in this tournament, murder was not on the mind of the Final Fantasy. What, then, was? ------------------------------- Only Seymour knew. That meant Craig Goat didn’t. Which meant that HIS adrenaline was also going a mile a minute. Despite the potential for danger, a delicious thrill ran through his every nerve. Life out of the public sphere had been fine, but deep down, a small part of Craig Goat loved this, loved getting to play the hero for his wife. By no means was Nurse incapable of taking care of herself, but the mere fact that it was Craig protecting her and not vice-versa spoke volumes about what GTT4 had done to Mr. Goat. Closer and closer Seymour Almasy came, until the wrestlers in the ring and the fans around it could see just who it was that had shown up. "Whoa, no way!" "That’s--" "Yes," Craig replied, as the relatively short man’s face came into focus. "It’s Seymour Almasy." Goat’s eyes met those of the Final Fantasy, holding there tight. He remembered their last staredown, mere moments before locking up in the fourth round of GTT4. Craig knew that he had looked away from Seymour back then, out of pure trepidation. ‘Then’, however, was a distant memory. Craig tried to look into Almasy’s soul, to determine if he was going to have to fight, but Seymour’s eyes were too confused to reveal much. Esme’s face, however, wasn’t blank at all. It was angered, enraged. How DARE he come here? How could he possibly have the audacity, after what he’d done to her, to just saunter up to them? How could she let him get away with it? She couldn’t. And so, before Craig or Seymour could stop her, she let go of her hold on Goat’s arm, raised her right hand in the air, and promptly smacked the Final Fantasy across the face. HARD. "Ooooooooh!" The fans surrounding the ring gaped, no doubt thinking that this was just part of the show, something that had been pre-arranged. But for Goat, Nurse, and Almasy, this situation was all too real. Seymour’s head lay to the side, only to shoot back up to look at Esme, fire burning in his eyes. Silently, Craig stepped in front of her, interposing his slight frame between Almasy and Nurse, seemingly cool as ice. "Well," he asked, voice level, "do you have something to say?" "As a matter of fact," Seymour replied, grinning, "I do." Thoughtfully, Seymour reached up and rubbed his cheek, which was bright red from the force of Nurse’s slap. He leaned closer, looking into Craig’s steady gaze. Boy, had things ever changed. But one thing that didn’t change was Seymour’s resolve. Now, he would do the one thing that he had been waiting to do ever since that contest so long ago. He extended a hand. "Congratulations, Craig." Goat blinked. Nurse blinked. So did the fans. "This isn’t a trick, is it," a suspicious Craig asked, before accepting Seymour’s handshake. Firm though it was, Seymour didn’t use his grip to pull Craig into some sort of wrestling move. Instead, he reached his other hand up to clap the other man on the back. "No trick," Seymour replied. "I’ve owed you an apology for a while now. Talking about how you ruined my career, how you destroyed everything…I’ve put the blame on you for so long I almost forgot whose fault it really was. Mine." That realization had hurt, just like his ex-wife proving to be a whore. Just like his mother tearing into him. But like those two realizations, he couldn’t run from them forever. He would eventually have to confront them. This, though, was where he had to start. With the event that had sent him into his tailspin to begin with. "And I owe you an apology too," he admitted, sheepishly, turning to face the Nurse. "I never should have hit you with that chair." Sighing, he patted his still-red cheek. "I deserved this." Even with the confession out, there was still tension hanging heavy in the air. Tension that remained until snapped by a young fan, who inquired something of the former Global Champion. "Um, Seymour, you’re not going to hurt Craig, are you?" Seymour shook his head. "Of course not." "Good. Then can I have your autograph?" Seymour looked at the boy, then back to Craig. Craig looked at Nurse. Nurse looked back to Seymour, and then back to the boy. Everyone chuckled, the tension broken. ------------------------------------------ As it turned out, Seymour ended up signing lots of autographs. A call on his cell phone had also procured lunch for all in attendance, evidenced by the stack of pizza boxes in the middle of the wrestling ring. Sitting on the mat, surrounded by fans and fellow wrestlers, were Almasy and Goat, laughing and chatting cordially. It was a sight no wrestling fan could have considered a rational possibility mere hours earlier. "So, tell me again why you stole my move," Craig asked, playfully rapping the Final Fantasy on the shoulder. Almasy glared at Goat for a moment, before replying sheepishly. "At first, I was mad. REALLY mad. I wanted to make sure that I didn’t forget that night…and what better way to do it than to incorporate the move that beat me into my arsenal?" The more he thought about it, the more flawed the logic seemed. But really, there was little logical about raw emotion, the fuel that Seymour Almasy usually operated on. "The more I thought about it, though, the more silly I felt. Somehow, I took that match as my birthright, and you winning it was completely against the ways of the world, as if you’d committed an act against God." Silently, Craig nodded. In some ways, he couldn’t blame Seymour. Undefeated in two straight PTC tournaments, Seymour believing himself to be invincible wasn’t such a far fetched idea. "So," Seymour continued, looking at Craig questioningly, "why didn’t you continue on? You could have had your pick of federations after that performance. You could have been one of the biggest stars in the sport. Why’d you disappear?" Craig smiled. The answer was one Almasy likely wouldn’t understand. But Seymour Almasy and Craig Goat were two entirely different people, and the reason made perfect sense to one Mr. Goat. "Because," he said, wry grin still in place, "I never asked for fame. Nurse pushed me to enter GTT4 because she wanted to become famous if I did well. I was a pawn at first. But the tournament taught me something. It taught me to stand on my own two feet. To be my own man. And it also taught me that I didn’t want to do this for the rest of my life. Every once in a while it’s fun, but you know what I really wanted to do?" After a few moments of dramatic pause, he continued. "To open up a truck stop." Seymour laughed. He couldn’t help it. Most people would have converted their GTT4 success into a multi-million dollar wrestling career. Craig turned it into a truck stop. "And so, I married the woman I loved, and opened up that truck stop. And you know what? I’m deliriously happy." Esme nodded in agreement, and leant in to kiss her husband on the cheek. "So am I….oh, you know? I probably owe you an apology too, for preventing your wife from having a match with me back in XUDF…" "Actually," he replied, "given current circumstances, apologizing for past misdeeds against Laura isn’t necessary." Craig gave Seymour a sympathetic look, while the Final Fantasy pondered what Craig had just said to him. Craig Goat was, by his own admission, deliriously happy. And Seymour Almasy was anything but. Shaking his head, he looked at Mr. and Mrs. Goat, as if he had just come to an important decision. "So much shit’s happened to me since that night," Seymour told them, "and I’ve tried to ignore it. Tried to sweep it under the rug. I’ve got skeletons the size of dinosaurs in my closet. I have to exorcise them." Seymour wanted to be happy again. Happiness meant confronting that which made him sad, or angry. It meant not hiding from adversity. "What brought this about," Craig asked, genuinely curious. Seymour had seemed so emotionally distant the first time they met in the ring, a far cry from the man who now sat before him on the verge of tears. "You did, actually," came the reply. "You’re happy now. In spite of all the shit that happened to you. Your mother died. If rumors are true, you nearly killed Nurse. You’ve been through as much, if not more adversity than I have. And yet you’ve managed to emerge with a good head on your shoulders, a loving wife, and a career you enjoy. If you can do that, I see no reason why I can’t." He stood then, heaving a 180 pound frame that suddenly seemed extraordinarily heavy off the mat. Looking down at the seated couple, he nodded to them once. "On that note, it’s time for me to go. Thanks, Craig. You’ve taught me more than you’ll ever know you have." Stepping free of the ring, Seymour made his way out through the throng, and headed back to his Corvette. As he disappeared into the distance, wife looked at husband, and tilted her head to the side. "You think he’ll manage to sort out what’s got him so upset," Esme asked, voice tinged with a hint of what sounded an awful lot like concern. Her husband thought about it for a few moments, before giving a firm nod of his head. "I think he will," Craig said. "I don’t know why, but I think he will." ----------------------------------------------- With his peace made, the open road beckoned once again. Final Fantasy music blared from the speakers of the Corvette, as it hurtled off down one of America’s vast highways. His destination was obvious: the fourth round of GTT5, where he would face three men. Kimbusa, William Gwynn, and Ember were about as different as three men could be, in the ring and out. In most respects, his task in this fourth round was even more difficult than his task last year. He had to survive two men to qualify for the bracket final, not one. But even so, somehow, Seymour Almasy felt confident, as if a great weight had been lifted from his shoulders. He’d decided to change himself a while ago, sure, but he hadn’t really understood what that meant. It meant not blaming others for his problems. It meant that, somehow, everything that was bothering him was at least in part his fault. Sure, Laura might have become a slut. Sure, his mother might have been a disgusting human being. But neither of those facts meant that Seymour was unaccountable for his actions. Realizing that to be truth gave the Final Fantasy a new perspective on things. Now that he finally really understood, he could go about starting true change in his life. It would start this week, as Kimbusa, William Gwynn, and Ember would face a newly motivated Seymour. A more honest Seymour. The man that had existed from the end of GTT4 for now had been burned on the pyre of knowledge and realization. And like the mythical phoenix, a new man would rise from the ashes. Fin. (Again, much thanks to Maria L. for use of Nurse Wretched/Lovely and Craig Goat. :-))
