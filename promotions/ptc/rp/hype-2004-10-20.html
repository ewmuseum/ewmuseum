Hype

20 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-20 Aaaaaaaanthoooonyyyyyyyyyyyyyyyyy... Aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaanthoooonyyyyyyyyy... Hello Anthony. Surprised to find me in your head? You shouldn't be. Not if you take a moment to consider who you are, and who I am. You do know who I am, don't you, Train? ...You don't mind that I call you Train, do you? Of course not. We know one another so well, after all. You can still call me The Deville; the spelling is slightly different than you might remember, but the pronunciation is exactly the same. You might think you recognize me from the quaint stack of profiles you've gathered on your competition for the fourth edition of the GTT. You probably believe you know a little bit about me from all the newspaper headlines and internet dirt-sheets dedicated to my successes. I'll bet you think you know what I'm about from the whispered warnings of others in the business proclaiming me unbeatable, those who say the tournament is merely a formality to restore the celebrity of my coronation. You take all of that in and figure that you know me. You barely skim the surface and arrogantly believe that preparation enough to wage war against me. You think you have my number. You think I'm nothing but hype. I guess you don't recognize me after all. I could go on and on about the myriad schemes I've finessed during my long years in "The Life". I could dazzle you with epic stories of victory and defeat, of surprise and conquest, of love and honour and betrayal. I could literally hypnotize you by recounting just a single chapter from the interminable tome that is my life, rendering you a directionless wreck whose only purpose is to get me to continue speaking. I could burn your mind to ash as quickly and easily as thought, as quickly and easily as you hypocritically condemn cliches. Or I could just snap your fool neck and let the legend that never was die without being known. But I don't want to do that, Train. After countless millennia torturing the hopelessly defenceless, the routine of putting your kind in their place bores the hell out of me -- or the me out of hell, if you prefer. I want something more, and to be perfectly blunt about it, you quite simply can't bring it to the table. It is because of that -- and only because of that -- that I'm offering you an out. You see, while you may not recognize me, I know all about you. From the doubt dwelling in the darkest depths of your soul to the raging delusions of grandeur plaguing your mind. You've spent your entire life an eyelash shy of hitting the big time, always striving to achieve it while subconsciously sabotaging your own efforts. You'll go on little winning streaks and capture meaningless titles, running roughshod over other nobodies that no one has ever heard of or will ever care about. Then it comes. The chance. The opportunity to leave your history of not-quites and damn-shames behind. The break you've always been denied for various reasons you can never make sense out of. You speed toward it with everything you have, pulling out all the stops and tapping the very ends of your resourcefulness. You do whatever it takes to keep your grand charade of supremacy intact. You take out your opponent "by any means necessary". Though you never realize it at the time, and the result always baffles you after the fact, it is that attitude which prevents you from achieving your goal. Knowing you're in over your head, you do something so idiotic and bizarre that your big chance gets called off. You get suspended, or fired, or written off by those who matter, pushing yourself out of the spotlight to struggle through the darkness, where you're the most comfortable. You lash out at those people, claiming they're holding you down out of some sort of sublime fear that you and only you can strike into their hearts. Fear and jealousy. You tell yourself and everyone who will listen that the only reason you're not at the top of the heap is because of spiteful conspiracies to make you less than you truly are. Most people can't see through the subterfuge, writing you off as insane instead of seeing the real you. Most of the time not even you can differentiate between what is put on and what is real. It's the only reason you're still here. Me, my eyes are wide open. I see you and know you better than you know yourself. The periodic sabotaging of your career is also the only thing sustaining it. It's an ironic form of self- preservation, but one that's served you well these many years. Every time you find yourself on the verge of becoming a name, consciously you want nothing more. Subconsciously, though, you know you're neither ready nor capable of being one. That fear, so ingrained you deny its very existence, makes you do something you know will sabotage your hopes of superstardom, while at the same time sustain your career. You get yourself kicked out of the big show so that you can continue talking about what should have been. How you'd be renown the Universe 'round as the baddest wrestler in existence if only people could get over their silly sensitivities and let you work. Not a totally bad plan. It's brought you this far. But eventually you had to know the camouflage would falter, that someone would see through the careful disguise. Eventually you had to know that someone would renounce your diabolic image and expose you for what you really are. Either that time is now, and your whole world comes crumbling down, or you embrace my gracious offer and continue on as before. I know it's hard to accept, especially with how well you're doing in the GTT. Really. I mean, not only did you eke out the unstoppable B. Yeager and conquer tournament favourite Arch Angel, but you absolutely demolished the most fearsome man the wrestling world has ever seen, the incomparable Eddie Benoit. Without a doubt, my friend, you are on one hell of a roll. My money's on those three matches going down as the three best of the tournament. Be that as it may, however, this is your stop. Of all those glorified champions you could never bring yourself to fight, the best is as a candle next to the sun when standing beside The Deville. No matter what your brain tells itself, your heart and soul are in agreement that you cannot win this match. Deep down, you know you're doomed. One way or another, you're going down. How it happens is up to you. You can try and perpetrate some villainous assault on me in an attempt to get disqualified, thereby preserving your reputation and career as you have for so many years. Resort to whatever means you can muster to take me out before the match. If you do that, though, I must warn you that my means are much more varied and deadly than yours, and I set my limitations for punishment beyond your wildest nightmares. You can try to come down to ringside and eliminate me legally in the squared circle, proving to yourself and the Universe that you really are what you hype yourself up to be. If you do that, though, I must warn you that I'll do my damnedest to make sure you don't walk out of the arena. And my damnedest is pretty damn damned. Or you can accept my proposal. You can drop out of the tournament. Think about it. Think about your options. No one has to know the real reason why you're doing it, that you're a guileful coward without the skills to go toe-to-toe with the best. You can continue hiding that deep dark secret, perhaps even from yourself, and go on with your life. Tell everyone you need to bow out in order to care for your newfound daughter, or that your home fed's schedule keeps you too busy to worry about something as insignificant as this tournament. Hell, claim schoolwork got in the way for all I care. Lie about it however you please, but make no bones about lying. If you don't lie here, the truth is going to come out, and its going to bury this laughable legacy of yours. Your reputation, your income, your career -- everything you have will be gone. How do you think your daughter will view you when she finds out that your whole life of fearsome brutality was just a trick of mirrors to mask your cowardice? How will your girlfriend -- oh wait, pardon me: your baby's momma -- feel when she discovers that The Tony is really a phony? You needn't ever know. Just keep the damning secret to yourself. Let your child instead think that you gave up your biggest opportunity for success out of love for her. Let your baby's momma think that you're the best type of man there is, one that will walk away from a fight in order to protect his family. Let them believe you're a hero. Resign. Remove yourself from the tournament. Save me the hassle of showing up. In exchange, I'll let you keep your rep. I won't bat an eyelash when you address the media about how you would've schooled this entire tournament if more important commitments hadn't come up. I won't even crack a smile when you tell them how you were planning to show me what evil was all about, that I'm not in your league. Continue your life of subversive cowardice and your secret is safe with me. Make me lift a finger fighting off your pathetic assault and life as you know it is over. Angelo Deville would run circles around you in the ring, exposing you for the supreme lack of talent you truly are. But then, you already know that; you've taken the initiative of reading his file, after all. You already know that in a wrestling match he'd make you seem like a quadriplegic blind man. Me? I'm not quite so merciful. I'll make you wish you were one.
