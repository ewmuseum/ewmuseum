Heaven & Hell ETD Match Roleplaying

15 May 2006

"The Storm" -- Archibald MacGregor * * * * * Lying next to Heather, with Aaron in a crib at the foot of their bed, Archibald couldn’t sleep. He had tossed and turned all evening, but just couldn’t manage to let his mind slow down enough to fall into a slumber. He wasn’t worried about anything, oddly, considering the circumstances of his situation. He wasn’t hyped up on caffeine from a soft drink or chocolate, nor was he yearning for an alcoholic drink, of which he barely touched since Aaron’s birth two months prior. Archibald just couldn’t sleep, is all. The night blazed past with all the speed of a snail, and he could feel it. The sun would be rising in a few hours, and he knew that it was his day to watch Aaron as Heather tried to get back into shape by shopping at the local mall, and then doing Pilates workouts with her girl friends. He looked forward to these days, just father and son. The days that he loved so much as a child, even though his own father tried to pressure him into being great at anything, were the days when it was just him and Dad. Even though Aaron was barely past two months in age, he figured he might as well get these days in while he can. With Hell feverishly awaiting his presence in the after-life, Archibald knew his days with Aaron were numbered. The night was still, with only the occasional breeze whistling through the open window in the bedroom. The moon was taking a night off, letting the stars do their job by themselves, barely casting a silver-gray color into the bedroom. The bedroom itself smelled of cinnamon, as Heather had a huge affection for scented candles, three of which burned in various places around the bedroom, giving off just enough light to navigate towards Aaron if he should be roused from sleep by hunger or a dirty diaper. As Heather snored softly beside him, Archibald was mildly started when he heard the soft strumming of a guitar, barely a few notes of a song which he recognized – yet couldn’t put a title to. They sounded far away and distant, perhaps a neighbor playing during the early morning hours, but were so brief in tenure that they couldn’t possibly be. They came in with the draft, and lasted just as long. Archibald sat up in bed, and walked over towards the window. Had his life been normal – something he often wondered about during these supernatural events – he would have simply passed the guitar playing off as a delusion. But he had heard them at least 10 times over the past two months, the first time being as he stood up at the newly erected statue of his father in the center of town. They were always brief, and never the same notes as far as Archibald could tell. Maybe 5 or 6 notes, played on what sounded like an acoustic guitar. Archibald turned from the window and walked towards Aaron’s crib. Dressed in Scooby Doo infant pajamas, the baby slept silently on his back, his head cocked slightly to the side. Even in the mild candlelight, Archibald could see his son’s chest rising and falling slowly, perfectly in tempo with the slight whistling of air going in and out through Aaron’s nostrils. Once again, Archibald heard a few notes of the guitar being played. He quickly circled the room and then once again peeked out through the window, but the notes had barely started before they finished, and by the time he completed his quick search there was nothing to find. It was starting to frustrate him, more because the song seemed very familiar – at least from what he could tell in these momentary melodies – rather than the fact that it was obviously coming from thin air. Somewhere off in the distance, far from the MacGregor home and out in the surrounding farmlands, a rooster’s call signaled the beginning of a new day. * * * * * Even though he barely got any sleep during the evening, Archibald felt energized during the day. High on life and fatherhood, he decided that since it was such a beautiful, sunny day in southeastern Pennsylvania, that he would take Aaron for his first walk around town. It was time to meet Grandpa, he thought to himself as he clipped Aaron into the baby-carrier that Heather had purchased. Archibald would have preferred one of those “baby backpacks” that hooked to the front of your upper body, but even though he is small for a wrestler, he is still above average in breadth. So instead, she purchased a small carrier that made Archibald feel like he was a famous Hollywood diva, toting around her Chihuahua rather than his firstborn child. Aaron didn’t seem to mind, though, as his eyes were wide and occasionally a toothless smile would cross his face. Archibald noticed that he seemed to like the wind blowing through his hair and across his face, often swatting at a strong breeze the way a curious and naïve cat would bat at it’s own tail. Rather than visiting a place of death and mourning, the local cemetery where his father was buried, Archibald felt it would be more fitting for Aaron to visit the two bronze statues at the grassy park at the heart of Oxford, which was only a short walk away. Even though Oxford is a small farming town numbering around 3,000 citizens, when a baby is born the whole town seems to know. Always friendly and wanting to see a newborn child, Archibald figured he stopped about 15 times along the short walk from his house to the main intersection in the center of town. Holding Aaron tightly, the traffic light signaled for Archibald to make his way across the intersection. An eerie feeling passed through him as he walked across the very point where the accident occurred with the minivan and the speeding roadster as he drove Heather to the hospital two months prior. He gripped the handle of the baby carrier tight, and traversed the intersection with no problems. As he stepped up onto the curbing, once again accompanied by a short gust of wind, he heard a few notes of a guitar being strummed. Around him, the town went on without notice. Looking down at Aaron, the baby was playing with his own fingers and seemingly trying to fit them together like matching pieces of a puzzle. His son apparently wasn’t aware of the guitar anymore than any other casual citizen of Oxford was. A strange feeling washed over Archibald as he glanced towards the sky, and saw three hawks flying high above. Silent and stalking, they circled each other with barely a flap of the wings. They seemed to be caught in a higher breeze, floating overhead. One cried out when Archibald noticed them, and the other two answered in unison. “Babe, what are you looking at?” Shaking his head momentarily to break from the stare, he glanced around looking for the source of the voice. He knew who it was, because it was soft and barely carried over the breeze, but he would never forget the voice of Aaron’s mother, Heather. In a second, he found her, sitting at the red-light at the intersection. Apparently she had watched them cross the street, and watched Archibald stop suddenly and glance towards the sky. “Nothing, you done your shopping workout?” Archibald said, feigning a smile to try and cover his tracks. Inside, his stomach was starting to knot up for some unknown reason. Heather laughed at Archibald’s declaration of what she had been doing all morning, “Yeah, gotta get this body back into shape so you’ll want to make more babies.” That honestly got a chuckle out of him, and Archibald knew it. A fake smile feels so much different from a real smile, “Yeah, yeah, yeah. I think for right now, one is plenty. I was just taking Aaron for a walk, we were going to go see Dad’s statue.” Inside the van, Heather smiled. Archibald could tell she was still beaming with pride because of the gift she had not only given her soon-to-be husband, but the town as well. The memorial for the only two sons of Oxford to ever go overseas and defend the country was quickly becoming a mild stop along the walks for many residents of the small town. “Anyhow, I’ve got my Pilates class in 20 minutes. I’m just going to stop home and change, and then I’ll be gone for two hours. Want me to stop anywhere and pick up dinner on the way home? Pizza? Burgers?” Archibald shook his head side to side, “No thanks, I need to watch my figure. Can’t end up looking like you.” He winked at her as the light turned green. There were no cars behind the minivan, so Heather sat there for a moment and grinned at her fiancé before responding, “Asshole.” ”Love you.” “Love you too, enjoy your walk.” Overhead, all three hawks shrieked. Off in the distance, Archibald heard the all too familiar sound of a car engine racing along at a high rate of speed, and suddenly he realized what was about to happen. It was as if two months earlier, on the day Aaron was born, was simply foreshadowing this moment. No sooner did Heather hit the accelerator and slowly make her way out into the empty intersection, did a car come speeding through the red-light and smash into the side of the minivan in a thunderous crash of metal grinding against metal. The glass exploding from the windows of the minivan rained down on Archibald and Aaron, as they were so close to the intersection he could feel the gust of wind the runaway car created as it sped by and slammed into the minivan. Once again, the van was send barreling through the air and came to a rest on it’s roof, mangled and bent halfway around the trunk of a tree. In the center of the intersection, the car that struck Heather’s van was bent in the exact same place as the previous wreck, and was the exact same model. This time, however, someone Archibald loved was inside. He feared for her, because the last the family inside the van was killed instantly. * * * * * There isn’t much I can say about the morning of May 3rd, 2006, except for the fact that I watched the woman I love, the mother to my son, get T-boned in the center of town by some drunken driver. It’s moments like these when your brain shuts down, and for a second, you stand there without a clue as what to do. The surrealistic feeling of a nightmare washing over you, filling your body with fear…and you just freeze. But then the second passes, and instinct kicks in. I can remember that morning down to every little detail, probably down to the location of every little shard of glass that lay on the street. Not a day goes by where I think back to that morning, and suddenly remember a vague little detail that I had overlooked dozens of times before. It was one of the worst days of my life, one of which I could simply grab a bottle of Vodka and wash away in a drunken stupor. I remember grabbing my cell phone and dialing 911, then tossing the phone into the carrier with my son. I remember screaming for help as I held Aaron in my left hand and ran over past the steaming wreck of the sports car, towards Heather in the minivan. For each step I took, I wanted to take three in the opposite direction. You don’t ever want to find the dead and mangled body of the woman you love, let alone anyone you love, and I sure as hell didn’t want to find the mother of my son bloody and torn to pieces. I didn’t want Aaron, only two months old, to be possibly traumatized for life by that sight either. I remember (and I might catch some shit for this) putting his baby carrier down under a small shrub, and even pulled a blanket up and over the handle I used to carry him. A drunken asshole might have killed my fiancé, but I wasn’t about to let three hawks pry the beautiful light green eyes out of my baby boy. And then I ran towards the mangled and twisted remains of my minivan, and prayed to God or anyone listening to spare the life of my lovely Heather. Whether or not God heard me, I’ll never know. I don’t plan on seeing him, especially after He let such a horror occur without any intervention. * * * * * The two remaining wheels on the minivan were still spinning when Archibald reached the wreck and quickly dropped to his knees by the drivers door, or what was left of it. He didn’t see Heather behind the wheel, or in the driver’s seat, which had tilted backwards from the impact of the wreck. The van lay on it’s roof against a thick tree trunk, and had collapsed down on itself from the weight of being on its’ roof. The imprint of the sports car was evident, bending the chassis into the shape of the letter “C”. Archibald frantically looked into the smashed windows for any sight of Heather, and finally saw her laying on her back in the rear of the van. She was being crushed by the dislodged seat. ”Sweetie! Heather!” Archibald yelled into the wreck, but noticed she wasn’t conscious. He also smelled gasoline and noticed that it was leaking down from above into the cabin of the minivan. The engine was still running, and it didn’t take a professor of physics to figure out what was going to happen any minute, and Archibald knew it. He reached in through the smashed window, but could only manage to snag a clump of bloody hair from Heather’s head. Archibald pressed into the wreck even further, and managed to feel her forehead with the tips of his fingers, but he was unable to get a grip. He could also feel the shards of broken glass digging into his shoulder as he tried to reach his bride-to-be. Under the nearby bush, Aaron began to cry. The pungent scent of gasoline was getting stronger, and Archibald knew his time was running out. He quickly hopped to his feet and grabbed the chassis with both hands and lifted. The van moved slightly, maybe an inch or two, but was propped up against the tree trunk and wouldn’t budge any farther. Frustrated, Archibald darted around the minivan and tried from the other side. The windows on this side of the van were still intact – albeit splintered and spider webbed – so he had no real point of leverage on which to grasp. He pressed his entire body against the van and pushed with all his might to try and topple the van onto it’s side so he could reach Heather, but the ground simply gave way beneath him before the van moved. His sneakers ripped the grass from the ground and tore at the Earth, but the van never moved. Above, the three hawks continued to fly in a circle. They fell silent, and seemed to watch. Inside the van, a faint voice called out, “Archie?” He dropped to his knees once again and pressed his fingers against the spider-webbed glass, “Heather! Sweetheart! Baby you’ve got to try and move for me!” He noticed that she was dazed, but more importantly, alive. From this side of the van, he could see that the seat was crushed and bent as well from the impact, and since Heather had seemed to be thrown into the rear of the van, she was pinned under it. And then, Archibald’s fear came to fruition. The engine ignited the gasoline, and flames started to fill the interior of the car. Almost instantly, the daze in which Heather was drudging around in was quickly ripped away by the intense heat of the fire. Heather screamed out in agony, and Archibald started to punch the window to try and free the mother of his child. Fist after fist smashed into the glass, but the gummy protective coating prevented it from shattering. “SOMEONE HELP!” Archibald screamed at the top of his lungs, towards any soul who would listen. Off in the distance, he could hear the sirens of the fire department start to whine, and he knew they would be at the scene in no less than a minute. But in a minute, Heather could be dead. Quickly, the flames grew more larger and more intense. Inside the van, Heather screamed out in the most unearthly and agonizing scream Archibald had ever heard. Once again, he rushed around to the destroyed side of the van and grabbed the frame of the broken window and tried to lift. He could feel the heat lapping at his face, but he never let go. He lifted with all his might, screaming at the top of his lungs out of desperation, frustration, and physical exhaustion. The van once again rocked an inch, but nothing more. Burnt hair fell from his head as he refused to move away from the chassis, even as it’s rear was engulfed in fire. He continued to try and lift the van with all his might, because the screaming coming from within was only getting worse. And then it faded, and stopped. For a moment, all Archibald could hear was his own feet digging into the dirt and pushing nothing but soil and stones, and the crackling and popping of the fire as it consumed the van. His hands ached, and Aaron was still crying. And then, right as the Oxford Fire Department arrived on the scene of the accident – as Archibald still used every ounce of strength in his body to try and move the van so he could pull out his beloved Heather – he heard those notes softly being strummed on a guitar. This time, it wasn’t just a few random notes momentarily being played. Instead, they continued on for a minute before a soft voice broke through, rather than emanating from the gusts of wind like they had for two months prior, the notes instead came from the stereo inside the burning van: “So, so you think you can tell, Heaven from Hell? Blue skies from pain?” It was right then, as Archibald recognized the Pink Floyd song, he was pried away from the burning vehicle by the Fire Department, and pulled to safety as they doused the wreckage with water. Archibald’s eyes were wide and filled with tears, but he couldn’t get those lyrics out of his head as he watched the firemen extinguish the flames and pull Heather’s body out of the wreckage, her upper torso blackened and charred. “So you think you can tell…heaven from hell?” High above, the hawks circled once more and then separated and flew away. Aaron continued to cry as a fireman pulled him from under the bush and put him in the Ambulance with Archibald. His mother was put into another Ambulance, and then both sped off towards the same hospital in which Aaron had been born. * * * * * Why me? That is the question I keep asking myself. Ultimately, I know that it wasn’t really my fault, but the fault of four generations of MacGregor’s who came before me. Their sins fueled the desire and strengthened the appeal of my arrival in Hell, and it only made my life harder than it should have been. But, rhetorically once again, why me? While the morning of the accident is filled with memories I can’t seem to erase, the day following is a huge blur. I can barely remember being admitted to the hospital for 2nd degree burns on my shoulder and chest, nor do I remember receiving the 40 or so stitches to sew the wounds on my palms closed. I had tried to lift the van so hard, the shards of glass from the window had dug deeply into my palms. I lost my right eyebrow, and most of the hair on the right side of my head as a result of the flames. I would have given my skin to save Heathers, if the opportunity had arose. Heather didn’t die in the fire. The van was in such bad shape, mangled and on it’s roof, that most of the gasoline had been either thrown from the vehicle upon impact, or as the police said, we might have simply been low on gas. I don’t really recall, to be honest, because with gas prices so high these days we usually always just left it almost empty out before refilling it. But there was enough gas in the tank to burn Heather, and burn her good. My son will never get to see what his mother actually looked like, unless he views photographs from before that morning. Most of the skin from her naval to the top of her head was burnt, with nearly 80% of it needing to be removed and re-grafted from other parts of her body, or donors. The extent of her injuries, while life threatening, didn’t kill her. But the Heather Davis I knew and loved and was going to marry has forever been taken away from me. Simply because my bloodline is something that will come into play in the after-life, a battle between Heaven and Hell. I ask you: What God allows such things to happen? In the days following, as my hands healed and my hair grew back, I talked to Heather as she slept in her medically induced coma. I told her that I would still marry her in due time, and that I loved her and would give anything to make sure that she lived to see our son live his life. Even if it meant sacrificing my own life, sending me to my eagerly anticipated position beside The Devil, himself. * * * * * The first-person excerpt from "100 Proof Life" by Archibald MacGregor was printed with permission from his estate.
