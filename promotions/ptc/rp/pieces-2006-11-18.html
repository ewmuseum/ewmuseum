Date: 2006-11-18 "So." The four of them sat in the cozy living room of the Jeffries residence: Brenda Clark in a blue upholstered Queen Anne wing chair; Stanley and Martha together on a beautiful white Davenport; and John Graham in a plush olive recliner, making a halfhearted attempt to stay awake. It was a really comfortable chair. The sweet smells of freshly-brewed coffee and Martha’s batch of gooey chocolate chip cookies wafted throughout the house, mingling with more subtle aromas of potpourri and lilacs found in the living room. A plate of the cookies lay on a fine glass coffee table in front of the couch and Stanley was helping himself to them quite liberally. Martha smiled at him as she sipped on her coffee, and asked the others if they needed anything else. "No thank you, the coffee is quite good," said Brenda, taking a sip of her own. Graham simply shook his head and smiled. He yearned to join the conversation; but Brenda had warned him to keep quiet, and he was keen to please her in any way possible. To Brenda’s surprise, the living room was a place of pristine order and cleanliness. "You keep a good home," she said, looking about. "Very tidy." The polar opposite of their son’s place, she added to herself. "Why thank you," Martha beamed. "I’ve always prided myself on making sure everything around the house is in top condition. It’s something that runs in the family… well, on my side anyway." She jabbed Stanley in the ribs with her elbow, causing him to spew bits of cookie as he yelped in pain. "Gah! What the fark was that for?" he whined. "Don’t talk with your mouth full, dear; you’re getting crumbs all over the place!" "Well now perhaps, but…" "Anyway," Brenda continued. "As I said before, I’m trying to find your son. He’s nowhere to be found in the area, and I have no idea when or if he’s coming back. Are you sure you have no way of contacting him?" Martha sighed. "Yes," she said. "We haven’t heard from him since little Leanne was born. He disconnected his cell phone and he never answered any of our e-mail. All we ever saw of him was on television, up until he was… oh, what was it, was he fired Stanley?" "Contract was bought out," Stanley replied after swallowing a big chunk of cookie. "Just the same though. Anyway, that was this past summer. He hasn’t been on TV since, so we really have no idea where the fark our boy is or how he’s doing. Is he okay, Ms. Clark?" "As… far as I know, yes," Brenda replied slowly. She was greatly tempted to tell them that he was not in fact okay, that he was a terrible father who had a very unhealthy obsession with his job and was utterly neglectful when it came to raising his daughter in a suitable environment, and then further proceed to question how in the hell they’d managed to raise their son to be such a moronic twit with no sense of responsibility—but that likely would not have gone over too well. "But we’re concerned about the way he’s raising his daughter," she added. "He takes her everywhere with him, you see…" "Oh dear Heavens," Martha gasped, "that’s an awful lot of traveling around for a little baby!" "I agree," said Brenda, choosing her words very carefully now. "That’s partly why I want to find him. I just… want to make sure he’s not doing anything detrimental to the child’s upbringing… and work with him, to… ensure that everyone’s best interests are being met." "Well that’s good to hear," Stanley grinned the trademark goofy grin that he’d passed on to his son. "And here I was getting the feeling that you were planning all along to just take Leanne away from our Scotty and make sure that he never saw her ever again, thus crushing his spirit and essentially ruining his life forever! But after hearing your reassurances—which by the way didn’t sound all that reassuring, but nonetheless—I’m convinced that my previous notions were all purely nonsense. Nonsense, I say! Then again, I was also convinced the world was flat until a couple years ago. But hey, you win some, you lose some, right?" Brenda’s eyes shifted around the room. "Um… yes," she lied. "Nonsense." What a bunch of fucking weirdos, she thought. "But as you can see, Ms. Clark," continued Martha, "we have no way to get in touch with Scotty, and he hasn’t talked to us in a long time either. I don’t think we can be of any help in finding him." "Perhaps you can still help though," Brenda insisted. "Were you there the day Leanne was born? In the hospital, I mean." Martha and Stanley slowly exchanged wary looks. "Yeah," Stanley answered a moment later. "We were there." "Would you mind telling me about it? I hope you don’t mind if I tape this conversation." Brenda procured a handheld tape recorder from her briefcase, and set it down on the coffee table. "No, we don’t mind," Stanley said, giving the tape recorder a skeptical glance. "But why do you want to know about that day? I don’t see how that’ll help you find Scotty." "Chances are it won’t help at all," said Brenda. "But it should at least give me some insight. You did say that he hadn’t been the same since that day, as far as you knew." "Yeah, that’s right," sighed Stanley. "Well, whaddaya wanna know?" "Start anywhere you like," said Brenda, as she pressed the record button. -~-~- May 15, 2005 "Son of a… damnit!" Saij burst into the waiting room of the Sparrow Hospital maternity ward in a panic. He nearly tripped over himself before stopping in the center of the room, looking around wildly. Several people—expectant fathers and other relatives mostly—looked at the crazed young man with puzzled curiosity. His parents were there as well. They didn’t think anything was odd about his behavior. "Hey!" he announced to the room. "I’m looking for a priest! Has anyone around here seen a priest?" "Why the fark are ya lookin’ for a priest, son?" Stanley asked nonchalantly, flipping through a few pages of National Geographic. "I don’t think they’re trained to deliver babies. Except from evil, of course—what with performing baptisms and voting Republican and everything." "I’m not looking for a priest to deliver a baby, Dad," Saij rolled his eyes. "I need a priest to marry me and Leanne before the baby is born!" Saij’s parents snapped to attention at this news. They stared at him, open-mouthed and unbelieving. "Oh my God!" Martha exclaimed. A big smile slowly broke out over her face. "That’s wonderful! Stanley, did you hear that? Our baby’s getting married!" "Huzzah!" Stanley cheered. "That’s great news, m’boy! First a baby, then a wife! What a year, eh? I couldn’t be prouder if I tried! Then again, I’m lazy." "Yeah," Saij said, looking quite antsy where he stood. "Leanne just wants us to be a married couple before the baby is born; old-fashioned stuff, or something. I was gonna propose anyway, so I figured what the fark? We can get married now. Only thing is I can’t find an available priest anywhere." "Well, did ya try the chapel?" Stanley asked. "Yeah. Nobody was there." "What about the intensive care unit?" Martha suggested. "It’s hopping with dead and dying people," Saij shrugged. "They seem to have a monopoly on any sort of clergyman in the hospital today. They’re all too busy to do a quickie wedding." "Well that’s rather rude!" tutted Martha, shaking her head. "I don’t see why people who are about to kick the bucket should get to hog all the priests. It’s not like they’re going to be around tomorrow to enjoy them." "Yeah, spare a few for the healthy people, that’s what I say!" Stanley nodded in agreement. "Well, what about the cafeteria? Any priests there?" "Nope." "What about the bar down the street? I hear priests often hang out in bars with rabbis and ducks. Or Nazis or something." "Where do you think I checked first, Dad?" Saij shook his head incredulously. "Yeah, shoulda known. Hm… check the men’s room?" "Yeah, nobody." "What about the women’s room, dear?" Martha chimed in. "Uh… I can’t really check there, Mom," Saij said, cocking an eyebrow. "It’d be useless anyway," chuckled Stanley. "Whoever heard of a woman priest? Honestly!" The waiting room got deathly silent. Stanley slowly peeked over the top of his magazine, to see every eye in the room staring at him in disbelief… including Saij and Martha. "What?" he asked. "What’d I say?" "Dad, there’s woman priests everywhere these days," Saij replied quietly. "You’re kidding!" Stanley exclaimed unbelievingly. "Wow, how about that? But still, that’s crazy!" He folded over his magazine and pointed to a NASA photograph of an astronaut on a spacewalk high above the earth. "I suppose next you’re gonna tell me there’s woman astronauts too!" "Yeah," said Saij. "There’s been for like, thirty years or something." Stanley scratched his head as he looked back at his magazine. "Huh," he said thoughtfully. "How come I never… OH MY GOD!" He jumped out of his seat and pointed wildly back the NASA photo. "THE WORLD IS ROUND!" He nearly fainted. "That’s nice dear," said Martha, gently patting her husband on the hand. "Now sit down before you strain yourself. You’ve had enough discoveries for one day." "Yes… yes, I think so," Stanley gasped, slumping back into his chair. "God, this is the sort of day that just changes your whole perspective on life, isn’t it?" "Sure Dad," Saij said, rubbing the back of his neck. "But I’d like to think it’s more because, well, I’m having a baby and I’m getting married today. Now where the fark am I gonna find a priest?" He looked around the room. Someone tapped his shoulder from behind. "Excuse me sir," said a greasy-faced young man, who looked about the same age as Saij. "But did you say you were looking for a priest?" "Yeah," Saij said, exasperated. "You know where I can find one?" "Why my good sir," the young man smiled, "I am one!" "You are? Sweet! I need you to perform a quick wedding for me and my girlfriend, before she has our kid." Saij grabbed the bewildered young man by the arm and started dragging him down the hall. "Oh… uh, sir?" the young man interjected. "I think we have a misunderstanding… I’m not that kind of priest." Saij stopped, and looked curiously at the young man. "You’re not?" he asked. "Then what the fark kinda priest are you?" "I’m a Level 15 Neutral Good Cleric of Pelor," the young man said with a sniff. Saij groaned. "Are you for real? You’re a farkin’ Dungeons & Dragons player? I need a real priest!" The screams of a woman in labor pain could be heard coming from down the hall. "Bah, screw it!" Saij decided. "There’s no time! You’ll have to do. Do you know how to perform a quick wedding ceremony in the name of Pelor? Oh, and we’ll need these too." He dug into his pocket and pulled out a pair of shiny new gold wedding bands, and shoved them into the young man’s hand. "Uh… I think so, maybe," he replied. "Great! Let’s go!" They headed for the delivery room. -~-~- Brenda shot a dark glare at Stanley and Martha. An evil glare. As if she was trying to make their heads explode using her sheer willpower. What she wanted to say: "WHAT THE FUCK KIND OF STUPID FUCKING STORY IS THAT!? YOU DIDN’T TELL ME ONE FUCKING THING THAT CAN HELP ME, YOU FUCKING IDIOTS! WHY DON’T YOU DO THE WHOLE WORLD A FAVOR AND FUCKING KILL YOURSELVES!?" What she actually said: "Um… that’s… interesting. I don’t think that gives me the insight I’m looking for, though." "Well just wait," said Stanley. "There’s still more!" Brenda looked like she wanted to cry. -~-~- The waiting room was quiet a few hours later. The mood had changed; a cloud hung over the room, and nobody seemed willing to break the somber silence. Martha and Stanley sat in their chairs, looking at each other sadly and holding hands. Martha dabbed at her eyes with a ragged Kleenex, while Stanley gently stroked her face. This was not the scene Saij was expected when he walked in, returning from a quick errand. He was carrying a vase full of flowers. "Huzzah! I’m back!" he said cheerfully. "I ran down to the flower shop and got some lilies for Leanne! She’s gonna love these—they’re her favorite. Hey, maybe ‘Lily’ would be a good name for the baby! I guess Leanne and I never really sat down to discuss what names we’d like, but now that we know it’s a girl… guess we oughta pick on soon, huh?" Saij looked down at his left hand. A gold wedding band was now snugly placed on his ring finger. "Man, we got that wedding in just in time," he chuckled. "A few more moments and the baby would’ve been born out of wedlock. But hey, all’s well that ends well, right?" He looked at his parents. They weren’t looking back at him. Martha seemed to be thinking about it, but the thought only made her cry even more. "Uh, hey…" Saij said, as he slowly began to realize something was wrong. "Why uh… why are you guys looking so glum?" For a moment they said nothing; finally, Stanley let out a long, beleaguered sigh. "Have a seat, son," he said. Now Saij really knew something was wrong. "Uh, no thanks," he said, his voice beginning to shake. "I’d… I’d rather stand." Maybe if he didn’t sit down, the news wouldn’t be so bad, he thought. People only sat down when there was bad news, he figured. Yes. If he remained standing, it wouldn’t be bad news. But Stanley rose to his feet, and slowly placed his hands on his son’s shoulders. Saij and his father were hardly known to be serious people; but this was a serious moment. There would be no laughing this off, no ridiculously explaining things away with unreasonable reasoning. This was incontrovertible. "Son," he finally began, "while you were gone, the doctors came and told us… they told us that Leanne had some hemorrhaging that they couldn’t stop." Saij could barely manage to breathe or swallow. This couldn’t be bad news… this couldn’t be bad news… No, this wasn’t happening… "They couldn’t stop the bleeding," Stanley continued, tears beginning to well in his eyes now. "Oh God, Scotty, I’m sorry… I’m so sorry… she’s gone. Leanne’s gone." The vase dropped to the ground. Glass and water shattered, and the lilies scattered on the floor at Saij’s feet. "No…" Saij whispered. "No… you’re lying…" He stumbled back a step, accidentally crushing one of the lilies beneath his heel. He jerked his head down as he felt it. "Oh." He absent-mindedly stepped off of it. "Scott, I’m not lying," Stanley said. "Shut up." "And I know it’s a horrible thing to hear, and I’m so sorry to have to tell you…" "Shut up." "But there was nothing they could…" "SHUT UP!" Saij pushed his father away from him, and ran down the hall. "Leanne? LEANNE!" he shouted, praying to hear his wife’s voice just for a moment. But there was no answer. -~-~- "He found her lying in her hospital bed—she hadn’t been gone too long. He threw himself on her and just cried and cried. The doctors tried to get him away, told him they had to move the body…" Stanley shook his head. "He wouldn’t leave her. It was hours before he was finally too tired to fight back." Brenda sat back in her chair, drawing a long breath as she processed everything she’d just heard. Now this was more interesting. "What happened after that?" she asked. "A day or two later, he took his daughter home with him," Martha sighed. "Leanne was cremated and buried at Oak Lawn cemetery—there wasn’t a funeral. She didn’t have any other family left, and Scotty didn’t want to speak to anyone. He hasn’t spoken to us since. The last thing he ever told us was that he was going to… going to make sure that Leanne would never ‘truly’ be gone. So he named the baby after her." "He couldn’t even bear to look at us at the hospital," Stanley added. "Almost as if he blamed us for Leanne’s death, since we were the ones who gave him the news. Maybe he was racked with guilt because he wasn’t at her side when it happened. In any case, he certainly changed after that day." Yet he’s still just as dim-witted as ever, Brenda thought. "It’s so unlike Scotty," Martha said, holding back tears again. "He was always so loyal to his family and his friends—always called every week, always came for our birthdays, always visited over the holidays… always…" she pulled a Kleenex from her pocket and blew her nose. "Yeah, every year without fail," Stanley nodded. "Rain or shine. Our boy was always very thoughtful like that. But he hasn’t done that in over a year… not for us anyway." Something inside Brenda’s mind clicked. She hastily reached into her briefcase, pulled out a manila folder, and quickly leafed through its contents. She stopped at a particular page. Then, she pulled out her date book and consulted the calendar. She looked over at Graham, who had unceremoniously managed to fall asleep in the recliner. His voice came ringing back to her: "Had to stand in line for half an hour to get them, too—some jerk was keeping the owner on the phone, complaining about lilies or something." "Graham, wake up!" she shouted, startling Martha and Stanley in the process. "Wake up, you idiot! We have to go!" She got up, walked over to him, and slammed her briefcase shut right in his ear. "GAH!" he shouted, sitting up quickly. "I’m awake! I’m awake! Did I miss anything?" "Only everything," Brenda growled, grabbing him by the hand. "Come on, we have to get going! I think I know when and where Mr. Jeffries is going to show his face again." She nearly yanked his arm off as she pulled him from the chair. "Mr. and Mrs. Jeffries, I thank you for your time.," she nodded courteously. "You’ve been most helpful." "Wait a farkin’ second!" Stanley protested, rising. "Whaddaya mean we’ve been helpful? Where are you going? Where’s our son gonna be?" "I’ll make sure to let you know how he and Leanne are doing," Brenda lied as she and Graham walked out the door. -~-~- Nighttime settled quickly over the dead fields of Oak Lawn cemetery. The sky was pitch black, and a crisp autumn chill stung the air. A few oak trees dotted the landscape, long stripped of their leaves by the change of seasons. Rows and rows of headstones stretched across the small plot of land, an out-of-the-way final resting place for the dearly departed. Two shadowed figures—one tall, one very short—stood before a particular stone. The silence surrounding them was absolute; surely they were the only visitors on this night. "Go ahead and place the flowers, sweetheart," the tall one said. "’kay Daddy." The smaller figure slowly walked forward, and gently put a vase of lilies down at the foot of the tombstone. It read: LEANNE JEFFRIES "You will live on forever." November 18, 1982 – May 15, 2005 "These fo’ you, Mommy," said Leanne. "Hope you wike them." She stood for a moment, then added, "I wanna be a Queen wike you someday!" With a smile, she turned and walked back to her father. "That was very nice," Saij said quietly, patting his daughter on the head. "Now wait here for a moment, okay? Daddy just wants to say a few things to Mommy." "Okay Daddy." Leanne sat down in the grass, as Saij stepped forward and knelt in front of the tombstone. He ran his fingers over the letters, caressing them gently. A tear formed in the corner of his eye, and ran down his cheek. "Hey baby," he whispered. "Happy birthday." "Daddy?" Leanne’s voice was hushed. "Just a second, sweetheart." "Daddy…" "Leanne, please, just give Daddy a moment!" "Daddy, the icky lady is here!" Click. Saij froze. A chill ran down his spine, and it certainly wasn’t from the weather. He slowly turned around… "You." "Good evening, Mr. Jeffries." Brenda Clark stood before him, holding Leanne in her arms. Next to her was Officer John Graham, pointing his gun directly at Saij’s chest. "You know," she said, "you’re a difficult man to track down. It was purely by luck that I was able to figure out you’d be here tonight, and even then I wasn’t so sure. But here you are. I have to say I’m liking our second encounter far better than our first." "H-how did you find me?" Saij was nearly choking with disbelief. "We were only gonna be here for a few hours…" "The information I needed was all there," Brenda said with a smug expression. "I had your ex-wife’s date of birth in my files… November 18. That’s today. I also got plenty of information from your parents… who, by they way, are wondering how you’re doing. You haven’t spoken to them in over a year! Not only are you a lousy father, you’re a lousy son, too!" "Shut up…" Saij clenched his teeth with rage. Graham took a step forward to make sure things wouldn’t get out of hand. "And when I found out Leanne’s favorite flower was the lily, I recalled something my partner here told me earlier in the day," Brenda continued. "He overhead a heated phone conversation between the manager of a local flower shop, and a certain someone on the other end of the line who was demanding lilies. Exactly why anyone would be demanding any sort of flower in the middle of November, I’m not sure. But then I thought of an interesting question: Would Scott Jeffries be the sort of man to risk losing his daughter by coming home to lay his dearly departed wife’s favorite flower at her graveside on the day of her birth? When your parents told me how you faithfully visited your other friends and family on every sort of special occasion, no matter what the conditions… and knowing how terribly affected you were by your wife’s untimely death… I was compelled to answer ‘yes.’ It was still a long shot, because who was to say that you’d really come back? But you did, and here we are." "Well farkin’ good for you," Saij muttered. His mouth had gone dry, and his hands were shaking. "So what happens now?" "What happens now is that I’m taking Leanne with me, and putting her into the foster care system," Brenda said with a note of triumph. "And normally the idea is to re-unite families as soon as possible. But you know… I don’t think that will be happening." "What!?" Saij exclaimed. "Why!?" "You’re just not fit to be a father, Mr. Jeffries," Brenda said with finality. She turned around and began to walk to Graham’s car. "That’s for the courts to decide, not you!" Saij shouted. "Graham, keep him in line until I’ve secured the child in the back seat," Brenda said nonchalantly. Graham nodded, and kept his gun trained on Saij. Saij couldn’t believe what was happening. He tried to think of a way he could fight for his daughter’s freedom, but there was nothing he could do. He was helpless. Absolutely helpess. He was losing his daughter, and there was nothing he could do. And all of a sudden, he knew it. He went numb. He couldn’t move. The shock was too great. He began to break down. "Leanne…" he whispered. His daughter said nothing. She was too confused to what was happening to say anything. "Leanne…" Saij fell to his knees, too weary to fight. "No… no, I’ve lost you again." Leanne and Brenda disappeared into the darkness, leaving Saij and Graham alone. "I’ve lost you again… I've lost you for good… then… then what do I have left to live for?" He took a step toward Graham, who tensed up. "Hey, stay where you are!" he said, more in a pleading tone than a bossy one. "I don’t wanna have to hurt you…" Saij took another step forward. "Hey! Aren’t you listening?" "Nothing… to live…" Another step. Another. Another. "Get back, I’m warning you!" But Saij wouldn’t get back. He kept walking forward. And forward. And forward. Until the barrel of Graham’s gun was pressing right against his face. "Pull the trigger," Saij whispered. "What!?" "I said pull the trigger. Kill me." "G-get away! I’m not doing that!" "I SAID PULL THE DAMN TRIGGER!" Saij grabbed at the gun, and tried to press on Graham’s trigger finger. "NO! STOP!" "DO IT!" "NO!" "I SAID FUCKING DO IT!" "NOOOO!" The barrel remained pressed against Saij’s skull. He struggled against Graham, pressing and pressing, until… BANG.
