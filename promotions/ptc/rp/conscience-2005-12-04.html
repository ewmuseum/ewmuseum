Conscience

4 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-04 He shouldn’t be here. He wasn’t welcome. It was his dreams that financed its present and future grandeur, but the open arms and welcoming smiles that used to greet him had long since fled. The ears that used to pique with intrigue for his every sanguine syllable heard only the echo of his damning obsession, the thunder of his Machiavellian mission. Eyes once alive with kinship and awe were now dulled by distrust and resentment. He shouldn’t be here. His penultimate visit was supposed to be his final, his begrudged goodbye. With the tropical air buffeting the silk of his suit against him, whipping around his head in a vain attempt to misplace even a single hair, trying to push him away, he knew it should have been. It would have been, but questions had arisen that required attention, a challenge that needed to be answered. Sucking a hiss through clenched teeth, he raised a fist to rap on the door, hovering before it uncertainly. Nothing made him nervous – his nerves had suffered enough crippling shock throughout his youth that they now danced their warning with Jesus – but faced with this door, with the man waiting beyond it, a tinge of anxious excitement made his stomach rise. He shouldn’t be here, but he knew that the paramount suspicions that would greet him awaited him everywhere. The myriad jilted victims of his past loomed in every shadow. Fearful contempt permanently filled his window to the world. The difference was that this place offered something more; beyond that door was the only man in the world Angelo Deville respected enough to elicit advice. So he knocked, an authoritative booming that filled the Barbados sky. Having made acquaintance with more celebrities than most people saw on television, Deville was constantly cocooned by perceived greatness. He’d dined with the Presidents of four countries as a guest in their homes, he’d attended banquets thrown by the men who truly ran those nations, and he’d crossed immeasurable stretches of canvas to lock up with and defeat the champions of the most prestigious federations. In every instance he knew, as they did, that he was confronted by a subordinate, inferiors obligated to showboat their equality in public but who would bend neck from their knees in private. They had the admiration of the civilized world, but to The Deville they were merely pawns awaiting their placement. The man whom opened the door was cut from a more regal cloth. Gawking at Deville from within a tangled cowl of auburn curls that dangled to his shoulder blades, he was the only man to ever inspire veneration to flow the other way, the only man The Deville ever idolized. "Christian," Deville intoned gravely, giving the slightest inclination of his head. Christian "Xavier" Kannon instantly overcame his shock and shushed for quiet, stealing a worried look over his shoulder as he stepped hastily onto the porch, closing the door before his wife could inquire after their visitor. Deville smiled in spite of himself, staring over Kannon’s shoulder, thinking of the outrage that would consume Eleanor’s normally porcelain face if she discovered his presence. Christian felt no such amusement. "You shouldn’t be here," he stated in whispered warning, again glancing over his shoulder. "I was just thinking the same thing," Deville responded with no effort whatsoever to smothering his voice, his smile growing. "Funny that we’re still on the same page after all this time." The two paragons of wrestling lore had first met three years prior, in the immediate aftermath of GTT3, the largest and most exalted tournament the world has ever witnessed. As the winner of the tournament, Angelo Deville was presented with the Universal Championship, a symbol and a testament to his lordship over every World Champion, every federation, every wrestler in the cosmos. The belt and its defender transcended all. Knowing that, recognizing Deville was the man to make it happen, Kannon, at the time the unquestioned overlord of Primetime Championship Wrestling, bade Angelo to appear at the next PCW payperview to defend his title in an impromptu match. Kannon knew as Deville did that Inphino Blitz was not the proper launching pad for the title’s opulence. In order for it to be respected as the best, it had to be fought over by the best; the champion would have to overcome the best. That torch was Xavier Kannon’s to pass, and that was exactly what he proposed during their fateful first conversation. Kannon’s face darkened. "Hilarious. What do you want?" That angled victory had never sat well with Deville. He was a competitor in the truest sense of the word, and he sought to be a champion in the same vein. The impetus that drove him to face Kannon was never titles or imaginary prestige; Deville wanted the experience of throwing down against his equal, of entering a ring with genuine wonder over whether or not it would be his hand raised after the final bell. Kannon, with his gift, robbed him of that. Up until the night of the match, Angelo thought it was going to be a real contest, not a parlour trick to amuse the masses. For years Deville doggedly pursued him, slighted, striving to regain the dignity that façade had stripped away. Kannon perpetually refused to acquiesce with his wishes. Even after Deville had fallen for the first time against Alan Kriegman, even after Kannon in turn defeated Kriegman and claimed the Universal Title he’d helped establish, he wouldn’t agree to the match. Instead he retired, and in what Angelo perceived as an abominable insult to him personally, retired the Universal Title along with him. Despite all that, The Deville’s awe never waned. Xavier Kannon was still the benchmark he measured himself against, the champion he’d gone through the Primetime Wrestling Academy idolizing, the archetype of wrestling supremacy he dreamed of dethroning. "You know what I want," Angelo answered, his smile fading to a hard line. "Don’t even pretend you haven’t been paying attention." It wasn’t until this past year that The Deville finally concocted an infallible scheme to bring the legend back to the ring. Heading to PRIME, the new Mecca of the professional wrestling world, he coerced Eleanor Kannon-Hall to manage him, to help him re-establish his dominance. Christian had warned her against it, knowing the depths of The Deville’s mind as she didn’t, but her need for the spotlight, to secure their future, made her ignore every wise word of caution. Before long Deville had leveraged his advantage, framing Eleanor for crimes only he had the proof of. The blackmail was academic; Xavier Kannon could either finally agree to meet Angelo Deville in the squared circle, or he could watch with impotent rage as his beloved wasted away behind bars. Christian heaved a deep sigh and shook his head, squinting at Angelo as if measuring him, as if trying to see beyond the polished exterior to the motivation beneath. It was an unnecessary stall. He was as astute as Angelo insinuated. "This is about Rollins, isn’t it?" Eyes twinkling, The Deville nodded. The extortion had never materialized. Finally developing an appreciation for the torture his adversaries experienced, finally feeling love and the vulnerability that accompanied it through Karina Wolfenden, The Deville, for the first time in his life, couldn’t carry through his devilish scheme. For the first time in his life, Angelo Deville was a victim to conscience. Kannon smiled mirthlessly. "Feels odd being on the other side of an obsessive challenge, doesn’t it?" Instead of putting Eleanor’s freedom on the line, attacking Kannon’s chivalry, Deville anted up a million dollars, assaulting his pride and the need to provide for his family. Christian had squandered the money he’d made during his reign, and without the spare cash to even play the lottery, this was the one chance he had to instantly secure not only his future, but that of his children as well. The Million Dollar Match was set, and it aired the next week on PRIME television before a record audience. The universe delighted as the first and final Universal Champions finally took to the ring to settle their eternal challenge. "It feels . . ." Faltering, Deville looked down to his feet, shaking his head. Though it was The Deville’s hand raised in triumph with the final bell, both men emerged victorious. Angelo had finally realized his dream, Christian had realized his future. While Eleanor would never forgive Angelo for the betrayal and exploitation, for jeopardizing her injured husband’s back, Christian understood him, where he was coming from, the passion that made him defy sense and decency. He knew what lay underneath the overwhelming titanium periphery; he knew that inside the champion’s chest beat the heart of a child. Angelo licked his lips. "I feel guilty." Inching his head up ever so slightly, Deville studied Kannon from the tops of his eyes for any reaction. "If Rollins finally dedicates himself, he’s the future of the industry." Kannon, one of two people in the world Angelo would open up to, was stone. Deville shrugged uncomfortably. "I . . . don’t know what I should do," he forced the words out, fighting not to swallow. "Should I thr—" Cutting himself off, he cleared his throat and met Kannon stare for stare. "When we met, last time, were you . . . did you let me win?" With a sigh, Kannon reached out and clasped Deville’s shoulder, giving a comforting squeeze. "I gave you what you paid for, Angelo. I did what was right. You deserved it." Angelo blinked at the ambiguity, wondering as he always did at the meaning behind indirect words. Anger began diffusing into his calm, defusing it. "I paid for your honest competition, and now I want your honest answer. What do you mean by ‘what was right’? Don’t twist words with me, Christian, just tell me: did you let me win?" Opening his mouth to respond, Kannon never got the chance. The door banged, then cracked open like a whip. Eleanor Kannon-Hall stormed onto the porch in a fury, shoving her husband out of the way and barrelling into Deville with a finger in his face. "How dare you come here!" It wasn’t a question, but an indignant statement of disbelief, quietly spat with enough venom to incapacitate an army of shouts. Deville backed down the walkway with his hands raised in forbearance, shaking his head and moving his mouth soundlessly. "After all you’ve put us through you have the nerve to show up here? Over this? Over your pathetic insecurity?" Deville stared over her head to Christian, but he had his arms crossed and his head glumly down, offering no help whatsoever. Eleanor’s tiny hands against his chest snapped his attention back to her. Her shove lacked the force to move him, but he continued backing away, being chased from the property and the answer that he needed. "You wanna know if you should let Rollins win?" Eleanor kept on, getting louder with each word, laughing cruelly in his face. "How can you still be that blind to the business?" Feeling hollow inside, Deville turned on his heel and fled, knowing as strongly as ever that he shouldn’t have come here. "Of course you should let him win! That’s what veterans are for! Christian did it for you! You know he did, and you know why! Now it’s your turn, Angie-dear! Your time is done!" Another cackle devoid of humour hit him in the back. "Give back for once in your miserable life! Follow the example of a real man!" ~><>…<><~ Sacrifice had never been part of The Deville’s lifestyle, and in truth, it only existed in his lexicon as a derisive description of others’ actions. Every memory he held detailed a reclusive struggle for personal success and achievement, with benefits extending beyond himself only in the case of coincidence or reciprocation. The philosophy was exceedingly lucrative, as evidenced by the incredible wealth and notoriety The Deville had garnered in his meagre twenty-seven years. He’d never bothered to shop around for a price, but he imagined he could purchase the NFL if he were so inclined, or wipe out the debts of several smaller countries. With more and better automobiles than the most depraved rappers, as many tailored suits in his closet as the average person owned socks through their entire life, a dozen Fortune 500 companies, and enough employees to fill Central Park – both on the books and off – his unofficial net worth could sneer at Ted Turner’s bankroll and nickname it "slim". To put it in perspective, Angelo Deville could erect a Statue of Liberty made up entirely of crack in Mike Tyson’s backyard, and maintain it. His withdrawal from active involvement with the underworld had lessened his income somewhat, but consequently freed up his schedule considerably. With all the time and money in the world at his disposal, there were no whims he couldn’t or wouldn’t pursue, no restrictions to his agenda. This compelled him to chase his every impulse, no matter how bizarre. Disembarking from one of his private planes, donning a wide-brimmed white fedora to shade him form the blazing African sun, he didn’t feel in the least bizarre. His altercation with the Kannons, not to mention the driving force behind it, still weighed heavily on his mind. Over the past six months he’d been steadily distancing himself from the iniquitous influence. He was a model humanitarian by no stretch of the imagination, but he’d been trying. What was the worst thing that could happen if he allowed Rollins to defeat him? He could certainly afford to swallow the loss, and Rollins stood to gain far more from the victory. If Angelo won, people would simply frown and nod, offering witty insights like "Well, he is The Deville." On the other hand, if The Renegade won, it would mark his first major victory in a tournament of this scale, arguably the biggest of his career, washing away the bitter taste of his defeats in GTT3 and GTT4. Angelo had always resented Kannon for throwing their first match, yet he couldn’t help but admire the selflessness of the act. Was it the same spirit persevering in their final meeting, graciously silent to spare the pain to Deville’s obsessive ego? Eleanor said it was, but she’d insist the sky was made of gelatin if she thought it might hurt his feelings. Still, while he could easily dismiss her scorn, some of what she’d said to him rang irrefutably true. At what point did he stop hunting his own dreams and help others realize theirs? Maybe it was only in his own twisted mind that victory unearned was not true victory at all. If he made that gift to Rollins, would it be thievery in disguise, or a blessed blow for all that is righteous? Was such altruism really beneficial to the recipient? Christian seemed to think it was, even after it blew up in his face. Eleanor most certainly believed. Angelo thought he might be starting to come around. That was what brought him to Africa. Television had never been a hobby he enjoyed, but with his rampant recent downtime he’d caught quite a bit of it. Most of it was unimaginative fluff devoid of entertainment, or scandalous scams that he trusted would bilk only the least intelligent of the species, but some of it reached inside and brushed his heartstrings, some of it stole into his mind and made him think. Early that morning he’d tuned in for a commercial sponsored by the Christian Children’s Fund, an anguished plea for help. It featured a wizened old Samaritan with a shock of white hair surrounded by starved children with distended bellies and sunken eyes rife with hopelessness. Calling their living arrangements primitive would have been a gross overstatement, and if the old man could be believed, things were much more dire than they appeared. Doubt that the old man could be believed, that this was something more than another ploy by the search to infiltrate the public’s pocket book, forced Angelo to witness the unfortunates firsthand. The presented cost of delivering these children from poverty and disease was astounding – a mere eighty cents per day. If that were true, Deville could single-handedly save every one of those pitiable children. On the outskirts of what could barely be called a village, the repugnant stench of human waste assaulting his refined nostrils, Deville spotted a pair of young girls drawing water from an old-fashioned pump, the most advanced piece of technology in eyeshot. They had to jump to muster the strength to force the handle down, and clad in skins, Deville couldn’t help but notice their thighs were no thicker than his wrists. He called to them as he walked over, and they regarded him hesitantly, with awe, touching dried lips with tongues that left them no wetter. Angelo smiled and removed his hat, dropping to a knee before them, forcing himself to stare into their cadaverous eyes instead of the skeletons poking from their skin. The old man’s words echoed in his brain like a command. "Only eighty cents a day. For less than the cost of a cup of coffee, you can give these children hope, food, an education. Pick up the phone. Do what you’d do if you were face to face, and tell them that eighty cents a day isn’t too much to ask." Resting his hat on the ground before him, Angelo took one of their hands into each of his, giving a heartening flex to show them there was still strength in the world. His eyes darted back and forth between theirs, glistening. They were trembling, scared, uncertain of what to make of him. Licking his lips as he nodded, he pulled them close enough to smell their wretched stink, and told them, "Eighty cents a day is too much to ask." ~><>…<><~ I don’t follow in anyone else’s footsteps. I blaze my own trail, streaking across the sky, and I expect others to do the same. My unrivalled success makes you want to believe that I owe everyone, that I’m somehow indebted to those I’ve already bested and others uprising whom may someday play their hand. Like I’m supposed bow aside because I’ve had my time in the sun and others deserve a turn. If you subscribe to that notion, even deep down inside, I have some bad news for you. This isn’t a democracy. This isn’t a popularity contest. This isn’t a game of politics. As much as it pains some of the perverted incompetents, this isn’t about entitlement. This is a sport bent on heralding the most cunning and capable, not an old ninny’s bridge game where everyone gets a chance to deal. I’m not here to plead for your approval or adulation, to bullshit you that I’m the moral choice, to tell you that if you scratch my back I’ll scratch yours. I never asked for any of this. I never attempted to "pay my dues" or to simply put in time awaiting my turn. Everything I have, I took. Everything I am, I made. Every awed look I receive, every whispered exaltation of my lordship, I earned. Some of the people that challenge me acknowledge and respect that, and they approach the encounter expecting to fight for their life. Others mistakenly believe I have what I have solely to share it with those that don’t, that my existence has been reduced to that of a stepping stone, that my role is to put over up-and-comers. Frankly, I couldn’t care less why you challenge me. People throw the gauntlet at my feet all the time, and it’s my eternal policy to pick it up and slap them with it. Regardless of the expected execution, I understand the underlying motivation. You think that if you beat me you’ve accomplished a heroic feat, that by pressing my shoulders to the canvas for a whole three seconds your name will be enshrined in the annals of wrestling history. That’s not true, but I know you think it, and believe me, I’m flattered. What you need to understand is that just because it’s the biggest match of your career doesn’t mean it’s the biggest of mine. You’re an amazing talent, Mr. Rollins – I’ve been touting that for years – but I’ve conquered bigger, badder, and more skilled than you, with much more on the line. This isn’t some epic battle of morality or supremacy to launch your legacy. All it is is another opening round squash that won’t even inspire its own chapter in mine. You might be the Global Champion in these days where pretenders like Dave Hurst get made a gift of tournament finals, but to me you’re nothing more than an eighty cent challenge. I could easily afford to lose it – it wouldn’t make a dent in my fortune – but in so doing I’d rob us both. I’m not a thief, Mr. Rollins, and to the collective chagrin of the undeserving would-be entitled, neither am I a charity organization.
