"Dolls & Bouquets"

27 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-05-27 * * * * * It had taken some time to contact the housekeeper of his grandmothers long-abandoned home, but he managed to visit her and pick up the key. Archibald Allen MacGregor slowly pulled into the driveway, his Nissan 300Z’s exhaust thundering almost as loudly as the oncoming clouds in the far off night sky. Above him, the constellation Orion kept watch, hunting any interstellar predators without fail. Soon, he would be hidden behind veils of thunderous clouds filled with lightning and rain. The house itself was three stories, Victorian in design, and had pointed arches above the windows leading up towards the equally pointed roof. While the house held many fond memories over the years for Archibald, he couldn’t shake the feeling that any wicked witch would be instantly at home in his grandmothers home. Archibald grabbed his I-pod from the front passenger seat of the car, and opened the door. He intended to listen to music while he went back over the years through his bloodline. He walked up to the front door, inserted the key, and made his way inside. Instantly, the smell of age filled his nostrils: a faint trace of mildew, and the stuffy, thick air heavy with dust. Clearly the housekeeper hadn’t been doing her job, at least, not recently. Archibald made his way up the stairs, which creaked slightly under his weight. He didn’t know where his grandmother kept the family photo albums and any other possible information, so he was going to need to look around. The flight of stairs came to a plateau for the second floor, and then turned on a ninety-degree angle towards the right, heading up to the third floor. He would make it there eventually, but he needed to search the second floor before he ultimately made it up the steps. When he flipped the switch, Archibald MacGregor was startled to find a miniature doll sitting at the far end of the hallway, clad in the easy-to-identify army fatigues. Downstairs, he could have sworn he heard a floorboard groan under someone’s weight. * * * * * Heather Davis sat behind the wheel of her Honda Accord, still parked in her driveway, and debated on whether or not she should go see Archibald. It had been a little over a week since she broke up with him, and was still fully enraged at the fact that he seemed to care no less about their breakup than he did when his drink was empty and needed to be refilled. Even in the dark, the stars on the horizon were slowly being blotted out by an oncoming storm. There wasn’t any lightning yet, or any thunder to accompany it, but it would be here soon. Her stomach rolled end-over-end in a nervous tumble. She bit her upper lip, and started the car. They might not be in a relationship anymore, but she still cared about Archibald enough to tell him something. Something important. * * * * * The noise downstairs was probably just the old house, settling on its wooden frame as it aged. He gave it no more attention, and turned on his I-pod. Walking down the hallway, towards the doll sitting at the other end, Archibald casually glanced into the doorways of the rooms adjacent to the corridor. They were all dark, and most of them had sheets covering the furniture and various other boxes that had been stored in them. When he reached the doll, he recognized it immediately: it was his father, or a representation of his father. Confused for merely as long as it took him to reach the doll, Archibald soon remembered that his grandmother had spent her many years alone knitting a collection of dolls, usually modeled after those she cared about. This doll had been knit long before his father committed suicide, and it showed by the smile on its small white face. He glanced in the room at the end of the hallway, and saw the rest of the collection. Holding the doll in his hands, he entered the room and placed the doll back with the rest of the pile. As the operatic singing of Maynard James Keenan, lead singer of the band Tool, filled his headphones, he looked around the room to see if any other notable objects were present – namely anything dealing with his family’s history. He saw a few boxes, and immediately began searching through them. After a few minutes, he came to an album that held what he was looking for: Newspaper clippings dealing with anything in relation to his family or its members. His grandmother, bless her soul, had always saved anything and everything that dealt with the MacGregor bloodline. Long ago, their family had been widespread and proud, today, two people only survived it and one of those was an alcoholic. As the song blaring in his headphones came to an end, one of the doors in the hallway slammed shut. Then another. Archibald’s hand began to quake, and it wasn’t the lack of alcohol causing his muscles to spasm. * * * * * The drive from her house to Archibald’s lasted only 5 minutes, as Oxford wasn’t exactly a vast cityscape to get lost in. Hell, she even caught both traffic lights when they were red, and still managed to arrive sooner than most Dave Matthews Band songs took to complete themselves. She smiled at the thought of her favorite band. Sitting in the driveway, her Accord still running, she was at war with herself. Does she just walk up to his house, knock on the door, and flat out tell him? Or does she hope he isn’t home, and leave a note for him to find later? The house was dark. Almost as dark as the sky, which was quickly covered with the dark clouds. The faint smell of rain was already in the air, and she knew the storm would be unleashing its torrents of rain any second. She shut off the Honda, and got out. She built up the confidence she needed to walk towards the front door, and started her trek. As she made her way up the steps, she could have sworn she saw a shadowy figure dart away from the nearest window. Perhaps Archibald was home and just had the lights off? As she drew closer, she realized the house wasn’t dark. There was a faint reddish glow coming from the living room, where the shadowy figure she thought she saw had been standing. The sky threw axes of lightning towards the ground, and they ripped into a far off tree with a thundering roar. The seal had finally broken, and the heavens let loose their flood. She knocked on the door, only to find it already drifting inwards. * * * * * Soon, it was Chevelle filling the headphones. His I-pod was stacked with more than 300 MP3’s, hardly a collection to brag about, but it kept him satisfied. The band was in love with thunderous guitar riffs and drums that rattled any normal set of speakers. At the present moment, his heart thumped against his sternum with equal vigor. He was drumming himself to death from the inside. Archibald put the family album under his arm, and stood up. He passed the collection of dolls, and stepped out into the hallway. At the far end, on each side of the hallway, the far door nearest to the stairway was shut. Both had been open when he initially passed on his way to inspect the doll. He clenched his jaw, and made his way to the doors. As he passed by the two other open rooms, he saw the first flash of lightning flood the house in a pale, yellowish light. Almost immediately, thunder shook the house hard enough to rattle some of the glass windows within their frames, and although the I-pod’s volume was turned up, he could hear the crash over the song. The storm was directly atop Oxford, crushing it under its heavy rainfall and jabbing at the ground with spears of lightning. Archibald ignored the storm, and made his way towards the doors. His palms were quickly covered in a thin glaze of nervous perspiration. He tried the door to his left first, and swung it open as if he were a police officer raiding the home of a drug kingpin. Inside, he found nothing but dust and covered furniture. The window remained shut, so a sudden gust of wind hadn’t sent it crashing closed. He turned, and tried the right door. This time, he took his time at the threshold, and peeked his head inside through the small opening. The room was darker than the one directly across from it, and he flipped the switch. There, sitting in the center of the room, was the miniature doll of his father. It stared up at him with two eyes made of buttons, and a smile of jagged yarn. * * * * * “Archie…Archie are you home?” Heather asked as she peeked into the house, starting to feel a little uneasy about the circumstances of her arrival. The door drifted inward with a slow creaking noise that reminded Heather of the Haunted Mansion at Disney World. She quickly flipped the nearest switch, and the bottom floor of Archibald’s house filled with light. She stepped into the home, and shut the door. Off to the right, where the reddish glow and shadowy figure had come a moment earlier, she noticed an object on the coffee table that filled her heart with delight. It was a bouquet of roses, freshly bloomed. The reddish glow was coming from the vase they were bundled within, which had cheesy-but-still-cute red lights around the top. Nestled between the beautiful petals of the roses was a white card, and she recognized her name scribbled across it. Oh Archibald, you shouldn’t have. * * * * * Archibald’s heart crashed against his chest hard enough to turn coal into flawless diamonds. What was happening was impossible, and improbable, because he had just put this doll back with the others only a few moments prior. Hell, he had been in the room with the doll itself the entire time. He knelt down and picked it up once again. Perhaps his grandmother had made two dolls of his father? After all, he was her son, and perhaps this doll was made after his suicide. He turned towards the door, stepped back out into the hall, and made his way down to the collection of dolls once more. When he looked to where he had placed the doll earlier, it was now missing. His heart sank to depths he hadn’t known. Something was wrong. Very, very wrong. With another flash of lightning, everything became eerily silent. He heard no thunder. He heard no rain. More importantly, what bugged him the most was the fact that his I-pod was in mid-song when it went silent with the flash of lightning. He checked the face, and it was still on. He checked the battery meter, and it still held an adequate charge. Once again, the doors in the hallway slammed shut. Not just the far two, like previously, but all four. Simultaneously. * * * * * Heather’s face couldn’t contain the smile she tried to cover it with. Her lips were pressed tightly together, and it felt like tears were starting to well up in her eyes. Maybe he had come around? This gift was so unlike Archibald, she wasn’t sure of what to make of it. But either way, she was happy. Insanely happy. She had come here to tell him something important. Something that would change his life for the better, as she had hoped, and she didn’t expect such a gift in return. But these roses, and the card, had given her promise. Maybe, just maybe, things were going to turn around. Love is one of the strongest forces in the universe, and she was undoubtedly in love with Archibald MacGregor. She had doubted his love for her, but she knew it existed in at least a small fashion. She reached for the card, but noticed the name on the card wasn’t hers. Her heart sank, and the smile was instantly wiped away. She opened the letter regardless. * * * * * Archibald froze in horror. His back was facing the hallway, and all of the doors that had just slammed themselves closed in an impossible fashion, but he was terrified to turn around. He had never feared death, and probably never will, but something was holding its grip around the throat of his confidence, and squeezing. Was this Hell’s way of trying to persuade him? If it was, it wasn’t working. He turned slowly, and saw another doll sitting at the far end of the hallway. This time, it was himself. In the same clothes he was wearing at this very moment. Next to the doll were miniature bottles of beer, carefully knit with a surgeon’s precision. He couldn’t move. His muscles just didn’t seem to react to his thoughts, and his brain was doing laps around logic, trying to come up with a rational explanation for the events. Suddenly, his I-pod roared to life. But instantly quieted itself. It blared only a single word: YOU. Once more, it roared to life. One word followed, and quickly it fell silent. The pause between words was much shorter the next time, as it came to life once again, and instantly fell silent. CANT. RESIST. He turned the I-pod in his hand, and looked at the LCD faceplate. The menu was quickly being cycled through by something, selecting certain positions in certain songs. Fear had finally fully paralyzed him. All he could do was listen to the message coming from the I-pod – translated to Archibald in small snippets of his songs. DON’T. RESIST. EVERYTHING COMES. FULL. CIRCLE. Outside, the rain, thunder, and lightning had once again roared to life with a house-shaking blow. Archibald remained frozen in place, listening. YOU. HAVE. BEEN GIVEN. POWER. SEIZE. THE. POWER. What sort of message was this? Who was this from? If Heaven had sent Archibald Matthews down to convince him to stay away from Hell, and enter the Cruiserweight 2 Tournament…this must be Hell’s indirect method of persuasion. It was making him afraid, but not convincing him. POWER. POWER. POWER. SHE. IS AT. YOUR HOUSE. SHE. IS AT. YOUR. HOME. SHE. HATES YOU. HATES YOU. His eyes widened with terror. Did they mean Heather? HATES YOU. HATES YOU. HATES YOU. He darted from the hallway, keeping the family album tucked under his arm. He hopped over the miniature version of himself, knitted perfectly to mimic him, and made his way down the stairs in three-step volleys. The message continued as he stormed out through the front door, into the pouring rain, and hopped into the 300Z. WE. HAVE TAKEN. YOUR. ONLY FAMILY. The engine roared to life as Archibald tried to make sense of the message. He slammed the accelerator to the floor and whipped the car out onto the street. The rain prevented the tires from fully gripping the pavement, and the car fishtailed as he tried to speed home. It continued to taunt him with song fragments. WE WILL. TAKE. HER. UNLESS YOU. ACCEPT. YOUR. FATE. And then it went silent once and for all. His house was barely a mile away from his grandmothers, and he should be there in less than 2 minutes at the insane rate of speed he’s driving, regardless of the speed. He prayed silently that Heather wasn’t at his house. Please God, not Heather. * * * * * Christina Cave, the late-shift nurse at the Retirement Hospice where Archibald MacGregor’s grandmother was staying, looked down at Olivia MacGregor with teary eyes. She had known Olivia for the entire tenure of her stay, and knew her to be a peaceful woman. It brought Christina to tears to know that Olivia suffered such a violent death. With a gentle swipe of the hand, she closed Olivia MacGregor’s eyes. When Archibald came to identify the body, he had no reason to see the blood that filled his grandmothers once-loving gaze. Her heart attack had been so violent, and so sudden, that it caused the blood vessels in her eyes to burst under the stress it was putting her elderly body through. She passed into Heaven kicking and screaming, but not because she didn’t want to perish. But because the pain was so intense, and so unbearable. * * * * * Heather Davis stumbled out of Archibald’s home and into the pouring rain. Over the thunder, she could barely hear the sports car thundering down the road and skidding to a halt out in front of the house. The rain hid her tears, although by the time Archibald was at her side, he knew she was crying her eyes out. “What’s the matter? Are you ok? Are you hurt?” he asked frantically, his voice quivering. “Stay away from me!” she yelled at the top of her lungs, and pushed him away with both hands. They had been out in the rain only a moment, and both were already soaked. “What? What are you doing here?” he asked, searching over her for any possible wounds. She stormed to her car, and was fumbling with the keys when he put his hands onto her shoulders. She spun and slapped him across the face with such force and such a sharp sound; the thunder crashing above them might have felt jealous. “I came here to tell you that I’m pregnant. I don’t know how. I don’t know why, because we always used protection. I came here to tell you that hoping it would lure you away from the bottle, and to become a better man. But obviously it won’t, because I found your bouquet of roses in your living room, and the card to whom they were addressed.” Archibald stood there, confused. A father? She continued, in a mocking voice, “Thank you for last night. Hope to see you soon. Yours, Alison.” She slammed the door shut, and started the engine. Earlier in the night, Archibald MacGregor had been frozen by the mysterious happenings at his grandmother’s house, mostly out of fear. He was currently frozen because he just received the biggest news of his life. A father? Heather’s Honda sped off into the storm, and Archibald slowly stumbled towards his front door. Soaked with rain, and confused about the evening’s happenings, he paid no attention to the mocking tone of the letter that Heather recited. It wasn’t his letter. He wrote her a small poem, and tucked it between the petals of the roses. He planned on visiting her in the morning, and giving her the bouquet. But when he entered his living room, he found the bouquet torn apart and tossed onto his living room floor. The card was laying among the tattered rose petals, and when he opened it, he read aloud its contents: “Thank you for last night. Hope to see you soon. Yours, Alison.” He fell to his knees. Something was amiss. Tonight was just wrong in every imaginable way. He sobbed quietly, until the vibrations of his cell phone roused him. He flipped it open, and listened for a moment. If he hadn’t already been on his knees, he would have fallen to them. The nurse from Oxford’s Retirement Hospice informed him of his grandmothers passing, yet the blow wasn’t as hard as it should have been. After all, he had been warned. He stayed on his knees, sobbing, for a few minutes before he could collect himself enough to stand. The storm was still thrashing about, but he paid no attention to it. For the last week, he had kept himself strong and away from alcohol. But when he stood, all he could see was his collection of fine Wines, Scotches, and Brandy’s on the nearby cabinet. The pain was unbearable. It had been unbearable throughout the early parts of the week, but he managed to find the strength to walk away from his liquid demons. Sobbing, he stood up and made his way towards the cabinet. He reached for a glass, and then paused. Through teary eyes, he stared at the glass and the bottles around it. Strength to turn his life around had kept him away in days prior. The strength had been ripped out of him on this rainy evening, and he poured himself a glass to ease the pain. * * * * *
