Heroes and Villains, Part 2

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-10 Doreen poured Jack Daniels into a plastic beaker and handed it to Coach. Unused to alcohol, the measure she gave was more than generous. "Is that okay? I can run inside and get ice or something if you like?" Coach shook his head no and sank further into the pile of junk he was slouched on top of. His venomous denouncement of Paddy O'Shea had taken much out of him and left him with a wash of raw emotion coursing through his body. His periods of lucidity were generally brief, but this one was cruel enough to linger… leaving him with the memory of the murder imprinted on the forefront of his mind. "You're not my son, are you? You can't be… I killed him, didn't I Doreen?" Mute, Doreen nodded. It was the first time in what seemed like forever that he had called her by her name. With a jolt, she realised that she had gotten so used to him thinking she was the embodiment of his dead son, that she'd come to accept it. Wasn't that why she entered GTT5 in the first place? Not because Coach had faith in her, but because it had been his dream for his estranged budding wrestler of a son. All her previous pride at her success so far leaked away like air from a slow punctured balloon. She wasn't a winner. Not Doreen as Doreen, it was Doreen when she pretended to be someone else that had won through to this stage of the tournament. She hadn't gained true self confidence, just imagined what self confidence would be like if she were someone else! Was Coach the only insane one here? "Doreen. What have I done? I killed my boy… and now I'm killing you!" "No!" Doreen exclaimed with passion. "You're not killing me, you're trying to free me!" Coach simply shook his head. He was sending her into the ring against a vicious man with nothing to lose and everything to gain. He'd make mince meat of her. But he couldn't stop himself, he'd destroy Doreen in the same way he couldn't have stopped himself from destroying his son. His only son. John Vincent. John had been a sweet child who had loved his father until his mother took him away and poisoned his mind, told him that his father didn't love him back; a lie. He grew into a difficult teen; desperately lost and desperate for acceptance. He had taken up the sport he knew his absent father had excelled at, eager to prove something. Strong, smart and good looking as he was, it didn't take long for him to make some small name for himself on the circuit. He tracked down his father, who now worked for Doreen's family, and together they tentatively started to rebuild the relationship that had been interrupted so many years before. But it wasn't long before the arguments started. Coach had started to hear voices again, whispering in the dark recesses of his mind, and began to take advice from them. He pushed his son into matches he could never win and into situations where the only possible outcome was pain. John protested and begged to be allowed to live his life and conduct his career in the way he wanted. John left, and Coach was broken. But he regained enough insight to know what was going on and so recommenced taking the colourful little tablets that made the voices fade away. Eager to reconcile with his son, he drove across state to watch him wrestle and win. High from victory, John agreed to a truce, to go grab a burger and a beer with his father. It's a strange thing, psychiatric medication. The side effects are many and can differ from person to person. Coach knew they made him drowsy at times, but it wasn't really a problem. The box did say not to drive when taking the things, but he figured it to be over cautiousness on the part of the drug company. He never even saw the other car, his eyes dragged shut by chemicals coursing through his blood stream. "I'm a murderer Doreen. And for all that this illness wears away at my soul and is probably leading me to destroy you too, I think I'd rather suffer it than the clarity that makes me remember what I am." He drained the beaker and slouched further. His eyes glazed over as he sank back into his all-consuming madness. Early the next morning, Doreen awoke with a jolt. Someone was in the house! She heard the unfamiliar sound of high heels as they clacked across the kitchen, came up the stairs and stopped outside her room. Doreen pulled a sweater on over her immense banana yellow pyjamas and yanked the door open, baseball bat in hand. "Well really dear. Is that anyway to welcome home your mother? Kiss kiss" Gillian Gervais mimed kissing at the air precisely ten inches from both sides of her daughter's face. She looked her over appraisingly and shook her head in disappointment as her bony fingers took hold of her daughter's meaty wrist. "Come downstairs. There's someone here to talk to you and Coach is there as well. Sharifa can sleep on though. She's the youngest so can't be held responsible." Fear welled up in Doreen's throat, threatening to paralyze her. Her mother was supposed to be in Europe! That she had returned early could only spell trouble. "Don't just stand there gawping like a beached manatee gasping for air! Do as you're told." Gillian tsked and clacked her way downstairs, pulling Doreen behind her. Coach was already seated in the kitchen. He fiddled absently with a bottle of table sauce while a young woman in a wrinkled skirt suit stared across at him. "Doreen, this is Mandy Williams. She works with the team that looked after Coach when he was… experiencing his little difficulty." Mandy smiled her widest at Doreen, eager to appear friendly and non-threatening to the huge girl with eyes like a doe caught in the headlights of an 18 wheeler. "Hi Doreen! Pleasure to meet you. I understand you've been looking after Mr. Vincent. That's very nice of you. But we feel that maybe he needs more than just the wonderful care I'm sure he's been given." Wary of the psychiatric nurse, Doreen mumbled, "Doesn't need looking after… he's fine." Mandy continued to smile, "But Doreen, it's very obvious to me that Mr. Vincent is in fact very very ill! He hasn't attended any of his clinic appointments since the tragic accident where his son died, and I know he hasn't renewed his prescription." She laughed, "and even if that weren't the case, I have two eyes in my head." She pointed over at Coach who was staring into the middle distance, muttering in response to a conversation that only he could hear. "He's not well Doreen, and it's been very naughty of you to hide it from us. Mrs. Gervais…" "Please, call me Gillian." "Thank you. Gillian, we have tried to visit the house on numerous occasions in the past month. Sometimes, the house is obviously empty, but at other times, it appeared that they simply wouldn't answer the door. This is most worrying. Especially as there are children involved." Gillian pursed her lips tightly. "Yes Doreen, what do you think you were doing, endangering not only Coach, but involving your darling baby sister in this farce! I expected so much more from you. Mandy, she's always been such an obedient girl. I don't know what's gotten into her." The women exchanged a look that seemed to say like, 'Children these days; you can't expect anything good from them.' Unable to just sit there and take the implied insults, Doreen shouted, "But you'll kill him! He hates the medication, he hates the hospital! It'll break him. I know it will. You don't know what's right for him. I won't let you hurt him!" Mandy rounded her saccharine smile on Doreen once again, "Oh but my dear, you're the one who is truly hurting him! The longer he goes unmedicated, the more the illness will wear him down. He'll lose his true personality. All those mad homeless people you seen wandering the streets? They once were normal you know, but if you leave the illness unchallenged or refuse the medication, it erodes the very core of the person, leaving a sad shell behind. God only knows what damage has already been done to Mr. Vincent's mind. It just gets so much harder to treat at this stage." Doreen struggled to take on board all this new information. She was the one at fault? She hadn't helped her friend at all? Was this true? The conversation continued between the two women, but Doreen barely heard another word, so caught up was she in this damning concept; she had hurt Coach. "Of course, hospital may not be necessary for the time being. We can wait and see how he responds to the medication first. Despite being a fighter…" "A wrestler." "Yes, thank you Gillian. Despite being a wrestler, there is no history of Mr. Vincent ever becoming violent when unwell. However, if at any stage you feel threatened, do call the police and they'll bring him straight to the institute. I'll leave you with a prescription." A little later with the nurse departed and Coach put back to bed, Doreen was left alone with her mother. "Dear, you could at least try to be pretty. What on Earth possessed you to wear such a terrible outfit?" "I hate you! Can't you ever be nice? What made you come back?" Doreen thudded up the stairs without waiting for an answer. She felt so frustrated and angry. Frustrated that her mother could still reduce her to a blubbering mass with a mere handful of words and frustrated that she cared. She thought she had long ago accepted this, but once again was proved wrong. Still, that wasn't the main focus of her anger. As is often the case with those lacking in self esteem, Doreen turned her emotions in on herself. She had hurt Coach. That's what the nurse had said. She had hindered his chances of ever recovering. But how was she to know these things. The words, "I'm just a kid" screamed through her mind. She thought back over the past few weeks. Had there been signs? Should she have known? But the memories that sprang easiest to the fore were not of Coach and his predicament, but of Doreen and her new found happiness. There was no denying that wrestling had brought her as much joy as it did pain and now no doubt that she had been the only one to benefit from the tournament. It had not brought the peace and pride she had wished for for Coach. It had turned her sister somehow away from her and worst of all… it had brought her mother home. "I can't ever truly win can I?" Her heart heavy, Doreen had come to a decision. This time, she would do the right thing and make the ultimate self sacrifice. She would give up her newfound hopes and dreams of becoming a world class wrestler, and drop out of GTT5. There was little choice really, she'd never get away with it with her mother in the picture. She had just picked up the phone to inform the tournament bosses when her bedroom door opened. "Who are you calling? It's not like you have any friends." "Not now Sharifa. I'm not in the mood for fighting with you." Ignoring Doreen's attempts to shoo her out of the room, Sharifa perched lightly on the end of the king size bed. "I heard you talking with Mom earlier. You've really pissed her off." Exasperated, Doreen replied through gritted teeth, "Seriously, can you just leave me alone? I have important phone calls to make. Wrestling stuff. You wouldn't understand." Sharifa smirked, "Oh I think I know more than you think I might know… or something. Whatever. Don't you want to know what naughty things I've been getting up to? They concern you after all." "Oh for fuck's sake! Will you just spit it out so I can be rid of you?" "Don't get angry with me sister dear. You're mine now. Mine to control. I know what you're thinking; that you'll have to drop wrestling now that Mom is home and your idiotic plan…" Sharifa twists her face and voice until it mimics the very picture of patheticness, To make Coach proud of me… boo hoo hoo!" Laughing, she collapses back on the bed. "Sis, you're going to wrestle at GTT5 because I say so. You're going to win the whole damn thing!" Incredulous, Doreen can only stare down at Sharifa. "What the hell are you talking about? You can't make me do something I don't want to do! Pound for pound, I outweigh you ten to one! And why the hell would you want me to stay in the competition? You've been miserable for the past week because of it." "Yes I've been miserable, but not for the reasons you think. I'm not jealous of you or missing school or any of that shit. I was in love and you ruined it!" Doreen cast her mind back, desperately trying to remember any boy she might have seen Sharifa with and how she possibly could be blamed for destroying it, but came up with a blank. "I was in love with Brian Arson… my God but he's gorgeous! He should have won last week, not you. He should be the one facing O' Shea, not you! I was devastated when I thought that I'd never see him again, never get so close to ringside that I'd be able to smell him again. But I realised something important this week watching you; if a fat ugly whale can get her hands on him in the ring, then someday maybe a beauty like me could too. You're going to show me everything you know about wrestling Doreen. You're going to travel the circuit and earn that reputation I know you want so badly. And when you do, you're going to make it easy for me to follow… no, overtake you in the game. Every contact you make, I'm going to be the one to make the most out of them." The hate in Sharifa's words hit Doreen like a blow to the stomach. It all made sense now. The cheering and cajoling ringside had not been for Doreen, but her opponent. Picturing Brian Arson, she failed to see what had so enamored her sister and assumed it must have been his reputation. Sharifa had always had a soft spot for the villains in any story. "You're twisted Sharifa. As if I didn't have enough on my plate dealing with Coach's madness and Paddy O' Shea's delusions, you have to go and act barmy too. Your plan has a flaw. You're never going to be able to make me do what you want." Sharifa slowly lifted up her shirt to reveal a huge purple bruise stretching all the way across her abdomen. Everything gets put on hold as Doreen's protective instinct kicks in, "What happened! Who did that to you? I'll kill them!" "Well," Sharifa smirched, "if you don't do as I say, I reckon I'll tell those nice people at social services that your beloved Coach did this to me. I hear the place they put the violent nutters is far worse than Coach's usual suite. Oh stop looking so shocked! When are you ever going to learn that in real life, people aren't like you. I'm quite happy to lie to get what I want." "But…" "And before you ask, Mom isn't going to be a problem." Sharifa reached into the pocket of her jeans and pulled out a key and a familiar looking piece of paper. "Mom's safely tucked away in the cellar and once I get this prescription filled, will be blissfully unaware of what's going on. You weren't the only one she abandoned you know." With one graceful movement, she was at the door and then outside. Doreen heard a key turn and realised that she was trapped. Trapped in her room, in a lie and in a tournament she no longer wanted to be part of. But most of all, Sharifa's deception had trapped her into having to face the one man in wrestling who scared her the most. Coach had taught her that there was nothing more dangerous than a defensive rat caught in a corner… and who could be more defensive than an idealistic terrorist desperate to justify himself in a world where there was no longer any acceptable excuse for the slaughter of women and children. If a man could kill for an idea, for an unrealistic dream; could he also kill for a title, for money?
