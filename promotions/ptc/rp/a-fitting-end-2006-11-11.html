A Fitting End?

11 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-11 Lisbon, Portugal October 31, 2006 With his hand on his jaw, he moved it back and forth carefully. The pain beating on him like a drum as he did so, leaving it's idle state in the back of his neck to pound against his jaw like a jackhammer. The kick that almost ended all of his glory had definitely left it's mark on him; the pain a deadly reminder that there was nothing to look forward to after this. In case you have yet to figure it out, we're backstage at the Pavilhão Atlântico Multiusos. It's been more than a few minutes since the cameras powered down and the pay per view audience at home were left with memories of countless upsets. Doctor Curiosity out early in a GTT? Speechless was the word that many were left with, including most of the wrestlers that had decided to continue watching the show from backstage. The matches had everyone glued to the monitors. Everyone except for him… everyone except Sammy Brown. He had managed to shake off the cobwebs, after being downed by an unexpected shot to the face from Easton Hall, and managed to pull out a victory from seemingly nowhere. He collected himself, wiping away the crimson syrup trickling into his eyes, and walked out of the ring under his own power. But, as he did, something different happened to him as he made his way up the aisle. The people suddenly started to cheer for him. He stopped short, pulling his arm away from his chest, and touched his fingertips to his forehead. He was unsure of the amount of blood that seemed to flow freely from the open wound atop his head, as he walked up the aisle, but he knew that stitches would be necessary. He squinted his eyes a couple of times to ward away the burning sensation of the blood mixed with sweat that was pooling within them. Just as he did, his senses were assaulted by the flashbulb-popping, sign-waving, applauding mass of people that were all around him in the arena. Then something else different happened to him. He smiled. For the first time in a long time, he had something he could be proud of himself for. He smiled up at the fans, as they cheered and showed their respect for what he had done inside the squared circle just minutes ago. He smiled as they screamed his name out in unison and frantically waved signs with his name written on them. He smiled because these people were coming to accept him as he was, not who he was. Of course, it was more than just their acceptance that he cherished… it was their respect. His innards felt like they had been through a meat grinder, and his bones ached as if they were brittle and worn. All he wanted to do was get backstage, to lie still and let his wounds begin to heal. Instead, he raised his hand in the air one more time, then pointed to the Heavens above before he pounded his fist against his chest twice then pointed into the crowd. The crowd broke into a deafening chorus of approval right before he turned and disappeared behind the curtain. It was the perfect picture of a man developing into the person that he had always dreamed he could be. It was a moment that would be etched permanently into the minds and hearts of those fans present to watch as Sammy Brown not only won his match… but won a lifelong battle with his own mediocrity. The grin was present even now, as his sore jaw begged him for a bag of ice to be pressed against it. He wiped his brow with the back of his hand and stared down at the concrete floor beneath his feet. He had gone out to the ring and gone head to head with people half his age; people that no one ever thought he would be able to beat. He'd gone out to the ring and fought his hardest against all the odds. He'd gone out to that ring and come back victorious… And now he had to walk away. ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ Brooklyn, New York October 29, 2006 When he walked into the bedroom, the first thing he noticed was the open suitcases being filled with clothes. His heart skipped a beat, and the lump in his throat made it difficult to breathe. She couldn't leave. "Isabel," he called out to her, but he knew full well she was the one inside the closet; where clothes seemed to be thrown out onto the bed. "Please stop." With a bundle of clothes wrapped up in her arms, Isabel Stevenson walked out of the walk-in closet. Her face was as red as a tomato, and the moisture from her tears glistened in the room's soft glow. "I already told you, Brad, I can't do this anymore." She tossed the clothes onto the open suitcase, then turned back toward the closet for another run. "You have to," his voice was calm, but the way he said it sent a shiver down her spine as she stopped short and turned to face him. "Robbie phoned the police and told them we were missing." "Good." "No, it's not." He walked slowly toward her, his eyes welling with tears as well as it hurt him to tell her that she had no choice. "The police are going to be curious as to where we were, and we're going to have to be on the same page when we tell them that we decided to take a little break from everything and go hide in Atlantic City." "You want me to lie for you," she laughed. "For them?" Again he shook his head. "No, I don't want you to," he paused slightly, "but you have to." "I don't have to do anything, Bradley." Her voice was raised an octave as she nearly bit his head off. "This is your mess… you clean it up." "As of yesterday," he grabbed her by the shoulders, then looked deep into her eyes so she could see the seriousness of the situation. "We're in this together, or we both die." The words cut through her, and her eyes grew wide. "How could you do this, Brad? This was all supposed to have ended a long time ago. You told me I didn't have to worry, that you would quit your gambling. You almost died at that casino, and we were both almost killed this time. This wasn't supposed to be an issue anymore, was it?" His body tensed up. "I'm sorry." "It's too late for sorry." The words seemed to stumble over one another. "You ruined my life the last time this happened, and you apologized and told me that I had nothing to worry about. You said you had learned your lesson, that you loved me too much to ever let it happen – Don't touch me." She stepped back. "This isn't the life I wanted to live, Brad. We were supposed to grow old together, to watch our hair turn white. Do you even realize how I feel right now?" "I know how I feel." "Are you proud of what you have done? You lied to-" He pulled her close to him once more, then pressed her head against his shoulder with one arm as the other wrapped around her waist. "Stop crying. It's over." "No, Brad, it's not. We still have to live this lie, to act as if nothing that happened last night ever did. You're never going to change." She started to tremble in his arms. "I'm not strong enough to do this." "I can't do this alone, Isabel. I need you, and right now I realized just how much you mean to me." "This never should have happened, Brad. We were happy." "It's out of our hands now, honey. Whether Sammy Brown realizes it or not, he has our lives in his hands. He needs to win this tournament." ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ Lisbon, Portugal October 31, 2006 The roar of the fans still resonated in his ears as he pushed the door that lead out of the arena with his hand, then stepped through it. With his head lowered as he made his way down the practically empty sidewalk, he pulled the duffel bag tightly on his shoulder. He used his free hand to align one of the few hairs atop his head back into place, for that classic comb over look, which still happened to be moist from the shower he took after winning his match. Every breath he took was ragged and long, as it was forced through gritted teeth. "Hey! Mr. Brown! Wait up!" Sammy recognized the voice, after their previous conversation and the harsh words that the person the voice belonged to had said, it was a voice he doubted he would ever forget. So instead of turning around and acknowledging the man, he continued on his way. He never even hinted that he would stop. The man was not easily rid of though, and he continued to run in Sammy's direction with his arms waving above his head as if Sammy could see him. "Mr. Brown! Please, stop!" He heard the man's staggered breaths as he approached, panting like a man who was out of shape and had just finished running a marathon. Then he felt a hand land on his shoulder. "Please… stop." Sammy sighed, then turned his attention to Reginald Jenkins. He looked a complete mess, with his shirt untucked and disheveled, his tie hung over his back to where it dangled on his shoulder. He was bent slightly at the waist, one hand on his knee as he tried to catch his breath. "I'm doing what you asked and leaving, Mr. Jenkins." Jenkins lifted his right hand and wagged his finger, and though his head was lowered and he faced the ground; he shook his head as well. "Wait." "There's no reason to wait," Sammy replied. "All I ever wanted was to be here, to get to do something like this and prove that I could. You ruined that for me." "I'm… sorry." He continued to have trouble breathing, but still managed to talk in broken sentences. "We just didn't expect-" "You didn't expect what? That I could be any good… that I stood a chance against your talented former champions because I never was one?" "We know… now… that we were… wrong." "Well, now it's too late." Sammy shook his head, and his grip tightened around the strap of his duffel bag. "They love you, Mr. Brown." He pointed back toward the arena. "Much to our surprise, you belonged in there. You deserved to have that chance, and we were wrong for trying to take it from you." "Like I said-" "No, please…" He could tell that Jenkins was getting nervous. "I made a mistake and I apologize for it. I know I painted you into a corner, and I made you feel unwanted. I was wrong, and those fans that cheered for you… that chanted your name. They deserve to see you continue. You deserve to continue." Sammy shook his head, and he swallowed back his pride long enough to give the man a reply. "No, I don't." Jenkins' eyes grew in disbelief. "You were right all along, Mr. Jenkins. I don't deserve to be in there. I'm too old, and my body doesn't heal as fast as the younger kids in this thing." He could feel his throat as it tightened, it was difficult for him to admit this. "I was kidding myself to think that I belonged here, but the truth is… I was lucky." Jenkins' heart sunk in his chest, not only because of what Sammy told him, but because there was no way to replace him. Not that they couldn't find someone eager to step in this far into the tournament, but because the fans were really starting to warm up to him. He was one of them, just an average guy that struggled through life like the majority of the men in the World. He was the blue collar minority worker that no one wanted in the country because he was taking their jobs, jobs that no one else was willing to do. That was Sammy Brown, and he couldn't lose him. "Whatever you want," he pleaded. "You just name it… and it's yours." Sammy smiled, one of those genuine smiles that you only see once in a lifetime. "I just want you to let me go on my way. Don't make this harder on me than it already is." He could feel the tears producing in their ducts. "Just let me walk away." And with that, Sammy Brown turned on his heel and left Jenkins standing there alone. A solitary tear trickled loose, then marched down his face and settled on his chin before being wiped away by the back of his hand. This is where the song 'Boulevard of Broken Dreams' by Greenday would start to play, as it is a fitting song to the situation.
