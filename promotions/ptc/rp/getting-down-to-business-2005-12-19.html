Getting Down to Business

19 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-19 The press conference is a standard in every successful wrestler’s life. Before big matches, or sometimes after them, bloodied and battered, they have to withstand a mental assault from various journalists and broadcasters trying to hype up their television shows. I’ve never even been to one, and as far as I knew neither had Shanty Jones. As an unknown entrant not even signed to a wrestling federation, very few people had been at all interested in him before his first round match against Belial Kahn. It was true that few more were interested when Shanty progressed to the second round without having to wrestle at all, but the rules were the rules, and it was his turn to be quizzed by the media. Doubtless there would be little incident, for this was not Rich Rollins they were interviewing, or the returning Angelo Deville, licking his wounds after experiencing what can only be called a rare defeat. The flies of the press would not be congregating around this one, hoping to get the scoop of the year, and Shanty was fine with this. He would come, answer three generic questions and then ask if he could be given time to prepare adequately for his match. He’d try to be calm with them all, and speak softly as was the way he preferred. He’d never liked talking to others too much, enjoying his own counsel and some peace; the calm before the oncoming storm. He’d travelled a long way from the valleys and pleasant scenery of Wales, had Shanty. He’d gone through the hustle and bustle of London to the deadly streets of Basra, and back via New York, Philadelphia and ultimately San Diego, switching coasts in an attempt to start a fresh and new life. None of it had worked, of course, and the violence that followed his was down to his tendencies and something inside that was trying to force its way out. He didn’t know what it was, and neither do I, but it was as if he was being taken somewhere, shaped by fate and dropped into prison at a precise moment, for a precise purpose. Whatever had happened inside had changed Shanty, though. Gone were the regular bouts of mindless violence against anyone who he deemed to be stupid, ugly, or just in the way. Just talking to Paul Goodman had soothed him, had almost tamed him in a way, and now he was a lion ready to pounce, to maul someone… but he was a little out of practice. He’d been thinking of going around and causing some trouble at a bar, or a club, but at the end of the day he was at the tournament to do a job. Paul had put him forwards for this as a way out of his previous life, though Shanty still had that lingering seed of doubt in the back of his mind that he was being manipulated. Not that anyone else would broach such a subject. To look at him was to see the kind of man who would stride ahead, and fate would follow in his steady footsteps, trailing in his wake as he did whatever he wanted, whenever it pleased him. It would be that swagger, that demeanour that would walk in to the ring for the second round match of GTT5, and that very same swagger that would leave it, and Damien Ryan be damned. *** I’d like to think that I was very different to Shanty when I was alive, though I’m not sure why I would take any particular pride in it. He seemed like a decent enough man, at least before he got caught in the bomb blast and his jaw and cheek became a swathe of scar tissue. After that, well, we’ve gone over his time in prison often enough to know what happened next. Before the blast though, he was a quiet man, but I always liked him. He seemed to be fairly spiritual, and I think I saw him with a cross and a rosary at one time, but it might have just been a necklace or some other memento. I don’t know what turned him to religion, but I remember seeing him when he was sixteen, and on his way to becoming a drop-out, an underachiever who hung around with the wrong crowd. Then I saw him when he was eighteen and he was almost born again. I guess that’s why they call them Born Again Christians, because it was as if he realised he’d been given a second chance, and he took it with everything he had. Nothing could stop him, and he went back to the top of the class, regained the captaincy of the rugby team and went on to be a success at University and beyond, in the City of London. He’d become a man with a decent amount of money, a known reinsurance broker, speaking to underwriters and other clients in his measured tone, but with such assurance that they knew he wasn’t messing them around, or if he was then he was one hell of an actor. I’m not sure what made him join the Territorial Army. I think he might have just wanted to keep fit and active in a way that didn’t involve hitting the gym endlessly, running on a road with no end, a solitude of creaking knee joints and rivulets of sweat collecting in crevices. I think he probably missed playing rugby. His job didn’t give him sufficient time to get away and play the sport he had enjoyed throughout his life, and it was much easier to work around a commitment as large as that of being in the TA. I doubt he thought he’d ever actually go to war. I doubt he thought he’d ever fire a gun in anger, and see exactly what bullets can do to the inside of a human being. I doubt he ever thought that he’d be damn near killed in an explosion in a foreign country very, very far from home. I know I didn’t. *** Shanty Jones arrived at the press conference at exactly the time stated on the letter that he had been sent. This prompted a quiet laugh from the security guard and press officer. It also prompted a two hour wait until it was his turn to actually face the journalists, the powers that be having worked out long ago that wrestlers were not usually the most punctual people. Jones noted that one down to experience, but in truth didn’t mind having a couple of hours to kill. He could relax, and clear his mind of all that floated around it, disturbing him and bringing up memories better left undisturbed. Eventually it was time. He stood up and somewhat reluctantly walked around past a curtain, coming out in to a small room with a table up on a stage. Behind it rested a couple of microphones and a single, very large seat. They’d originally gone for a regular one, but with the size of some of the people in the tournament they decided that the one with the most reinforcement would perhaps be the best idea. Elsewhere in the room was an array of normal, human sized plastic chairs. There were maybe fifty of them, arranged neatly in rows, but only a few were occupied. This pleased Shanty, as it would be easier, and shorter. "Next up is Shanty Jones. He’s wrestling as a free agent, and got to round two via a walkover against Belial Kahn." The woman introducing him stepped away, and Shanty was alone. There was no round of applause as he had secretly thought there might be, just a stony silence. One of the six or seven people in the audience raised his hand. "Yes, Samuel Heap from PTC Radio. I was wondering if you were looking at this tournament as a career move, and perhaps hoping to attract attention from one of the member feds with a view to signing with them?" "Yeah," said Shanty, nodding his head. "I guess that’s the plan." "Well, are there any in particular that you’ve taken an interest in? PRIME or GCW perhaps?" "Ummm, I’ve got to be honest here. I don’t know anything about these federations. This is a new start for me and I’ll be open to talks with each and every one of them." "If the price is right, yeah?" continued the reporter. "That seems to be the smart move." The first reporter sat down and a second stood up. She smiled at Shanty and consulted a piece of paper that she had been carrying. "Your next opponent is a man called Damien Ryan, of the federation HATE. Do you know anything about him?" "I’d love to say that I’ve scouted all of his moves, but I haven’t." "So you’ll be going in on instinct alone? Don’t you think that’s a risky tactic?" "Last time I went in somewhere that had a plan, it didn’t turn out so well," said Shanty, recalling the events in Iraq. The female reporter sat down, and a third one took to his feet. The reporter was wearing a large coat, and a hat drawn down over his eyes. "I vas vondering… I mean I was wondering if you have been taking this match for granted, Shanty?" "Not at all. I respect my opponent, but this is just the way that I do things. It’ll be the same whether I’m facing the tournament favourite or a rank outsider. To me they’re just a man or woman that I have to go up against. It’s all the same." Shanty closed his eyes. He was starting to sound like he fitted in with this way of life, and he didn’t want to. This wasn’t what he was meant to be doing, sitting around and talking about things… "All ze same? I mean, all the same? He is "Mister I Don’t Give a Fuck!" after all." The female reporter raised her eyebrows, but Shanty let it go. "What do you mean?" "Vell… I’m not sure, but zat is a pretty intimidating name, do you not tzink? I mean… he doesn’t give a fuck! Vhat more do you need to know?" "Well, maybe he won’t give enough of a fuck to turn up, like the last guy," said Shanty, smiling despite himself. "NEIN!" yelled the reporter, throwing his hat down to the floor, and revealing himself as Doctor Curiosity. "You need me to formulate a plan! He doesn’t give a fuck! He could do anything to you… he could pull a gun and shoot you!" "He wouldn’t do that." "He doesn’t give a fuck! Don’t you understand? He might bring a rabid pit bull terrier with him and have it savage your ankles." "Didn’t I tell you to stop following me around anyway? What the hell is wrong with you?" "I’m acting in your best interests!" protested Curiosity. "My best interests lie with you very, very far away." "I can protect you!" "I can protect myself," said Shanty. "Now get him the hell out of this room." Security converged on Doctor Curiosity quicker than Shanty would have expected. As they arrived to grab the doctor, a card was produced. "I’m a genuine member of the press! Ze Daily Wrestling Orphan!" "That’s just a business card, sir." "So vhat?" "It’s written on the back of a napkin… in lipstick." "Your point being?" asked Curiosity. "We’re going to have to escort you out of the building." "I’m ze reigning champion!" "That doesn’t matter. There are two ways we can do this, sir. Which is it going to be?" "What are ze options?" That was a step too far, and the doctor was immediately grabbed by four sets of burly hands, and whisked from the room. Shanty Jones looked around the room again and smiled. Perhaps he was getting used to this kind of thing. Perhaps it was growing on him *** I see things from the past, from the lives of Shanty Jones and Steven Atkins. I don’t always understand them, but I can see into motives for the most part. I’m trying to make sense as to why this has been revealed to me upon my death, but it’s hard sometimes. I can see Shanty Jones again, though it can’t be from far in the past. He’s having a confrontation with someone, and it’s in a locker room. I don’t… I don’t understand where he is because he’s dressed as if he’s ready to wrestle, but he never had a confrontation before his first match, or lack of match, as it ended up being. This isn’t his first match. Looking at the length of his hair… this isn’t even his second. It’s grown out from his crew cut and that means. It can’t be the fu… and I look at the other man in the room, who’s threatening Shanty, and I recognise him, though the face is blurred out. All blurred out because I should recognise him. I should place him, and just then, in that moment, I glimpse his eyes, and they’re mine. They’re mine and they’re not mine, and I don’t know what this means but I know it hasn’t happened yet. I just saw the future. What does it mean? *** Shanty Jones leaned back in his chair, feeling more and more at ease. The questions hadn’t continued for long, and he was just about to wind things up. "One last question, thank you." The first reporter stood up again. "So, you’ve said you’ll treat all of the people you face in the same way, but Damien Ryan does have a reputation as a dangerous man. What do you think about his burgeoning reputation, which was enhanced by his win over Logan Caine?" "Well, Sam, really…" said Shanty, with a hint of the devil in his eyes. "I don’t give a fuck."
