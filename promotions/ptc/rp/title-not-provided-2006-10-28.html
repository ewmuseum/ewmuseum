Date: 2006-10-28 The thing about movies is that no matter how overblown, or how off the wall something seems, the only reason anything has any effect on our emotions and how we feel is because everything is based on at least a slim shred of reality. Even things we don't want to believe can happen in the real world, must have a slim chance of plausibility or else it's cast aside as fantasy. Most producers use this to tug at our heart strings, or to make us roll with laughter. Although, the most effective emotion to induce seems to be fear. The locale seemed to be the origin of every movie asylum, ever. The dim lights caused an awkward glare across nearly every surface as nurses and caretakers that didn't really seem to care paced up and down halls and halls of identical doors. Each door indistinguishable from the next, sans the number on the front, marking the patient number. Inside of room number 512, the lights seemed to be quite a bit lower than anywhere else. The darkness did the worn down, grungy padding no favors. The protective walls were obviously old, and in some spots seemed to be worn completely through. Random odd stains were spotted around the room, and with the lack of light, were quite difficult to identify. One stain near a completely uncovered section of walls seemed different from the rest. The minute amount of light the penetrated into the room seemed to gleam off of this stain. A small trail of fresh liquid seemed to emanate from the bare cement wall, and down to what was most likely the source. The long, white hair was now tarnished with a pale, red streak. The stained hair protruded through a dark gray set of leather head gear, that seemed almost like overkill. He seemed completely oblivious to the gash in his head, as he rested himself against the unforgiving wall. He stared off into nothingness as his red goggles covered his eyes, tinting his entire world a crimson shade. He gently swayed back and forth, thinking to himself, as the steel door swung open, and two rather large men stood at the entrance. He didn't respond at all, no fear, no anger, no joy. Nothing. The two men walked into the room, and dragged the bloodied patient over to a treatment area, where they would bandage his wound. ~*~ Bobino sat in the trainer's room after a grueling match on KillZone. His surgically repaired knee had been giving him a lot of trouble throughout the match, and Bobino sat on the table, an ice pack making it's home on his lower limb. The trainer entered the room, and sighed seeing Bobino in yet again. "What are you doing here, you know what I'm going to say. Ice it down, and wear a hard brace for a week or so. If it gets any worse go to the hospital." The trainer recited the line, obviously pointing out the number of times he had given the same diagnosis to Bobino. Bobino smirk, as he slowly lowered himself off the table. Bobino smirked a bit, as he began to head towards the exit. "One of these days, I'm gonna get hurt, and you're not going to let me leave. Just wait." ~*~ "Kristopher, do you know how many times we've had this discussion?" The voice seemed more concerned than it did annoyed, but the small tinge of aggravation showed through in his words. The response was what he expected, too. Silence. He was beginning to get tired of this game. "Kristopher!" Quickly, Kris's eyes locked with the doctor's, making a point to stare directly at him. "Dammit! How many times do I need to tell you? It's not Kristopher any more! -MY- name is Krisko." The doctor simply rolled his eyes, obviously having heard this before. "Listen Kriskto-…. Krisko. Just do what I say, they're considering your release, just don't blow it. I'm trying to help you out." ~*~ The crowd was on it's feet. This was the type of match that they loved. Both men had brought each other to the limit, and there was a clean winner. Bobino stood on the second opes, his arms out stretched basking in cheers that he knew came out of respect for the show he just put on. Bobino didn't get to celebrate with the crowd often, and he was enjoying every second of it, secretly, at least. The crowd watching on TV was as impressed as the live audience, many tuning in just o his match. On TV sat a few feet away from it's viewer, whom had been watching Bobino's matches for too long now. He sat watching, as his freshly bandaged head ached slightly. Krisko watched in admiration of "The New Shit" and recounted the words the doctor spoke in their meeting early that day. The doctor offered to help Krisko get out of the clinic, but Krisko saw no reason to agree. The doctor pleaded with Krisko, for his own good. "There's got to be something out there in the world you really want. I'll help you, we can get whatever it is you've been wanting since you were brought here as a kid." Krisko sat, nodding his head as FUSE KillZone faded from his television. He continued nodding to himself long after the picture faded. The guards brought Krisko back to his room, and he sat there, nearly motionless, trying to behave. The doctor told him that he simply had to control himself, and allow himself to be diagnosed by the doctors, and there's a good chance he could help him out. Krisko used every bit of will power to behave that entire week. He kept his mind on the prize, and when Wednesday rolled around, he had his weekly meeting with his doctor. "You've done excellent, Kristopher. They're just waiting on a few pending CAT scans, but it looks like we're going to succeed. Aren't you happy? You get to go home!" Krisko had spent the last nine years of his life in this place. Calling anywhere else hom now seemed illogical. He slightly remembers the shelter that he lived in before. He slightly remembers his parents. His memories are very weak and distant, except for one. Krisko will never forgot that day. You can't forget the day that your flesh and blood, your friend, your brother, your hero… stabs you in the back. The doctor mumbled a few more things, but Krisko didn't pay very good attention. He was thinking about the hour, realizing that KillZone came on soon. He couldn't believe, of all professions to choose, he chose that one. Krisko had been watching since the beginning of his career, viewing the XoW shows on special request from his doctors. The clinic even specially got the Play Boy channel, simply so Krisko could watch FUSE KillZone. At first, the doctors thought the violence was inappropriate for him, but they couldn't ignore the calming effect the show had on him. It was the only way to keep the boy in line. Krisko enjoyed another episode of FUSE's weekly show, and went back to his padded room, realizing that shortly, he'd be free to see these shows live, and to meet back up with his one and only memory. He cracked a sadistic grin through his headgear, as he softly whispered to himself. "Soon enough… Soon enough…"
