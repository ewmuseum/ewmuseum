The Saga Of The Day : A New Uber Judge Dawns

7 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It was the dawn of a new day. The Uber Judge woke from slumber. He rose from his bed, casting the sheets to the side and looked out the window into his back yard. Today the sun was shining, but the birds were certainly not singing in the trees. They were clustered around the birdbath; the morning (and last night, and probably all day actually until evening) held a traditional Cleveland dry heat, high temperature, little wind, enough humidity to make you believe that it could rain at any moment (but it wouldn’t for a few days at least). However, the Uber Judge was quite pleased. The yard looked immaculate as usual. Today would be a splendid day to weed the flower patches, and tend to the vegetable garden. The Uber Judge looked to the top of his prized elm tree, and frowned. Then he scowled. He turned from the window in a state nigh unto sulking as he headed for the bathroom to engage the morning rituals of cleanliness. He didn’t see the crow this morning. That special bird who would punctuate his thoughts and thinkings with his pessimistic “Caw!” had initially irritated the Uber Judge. He felt that the crow mocked him, mocked his struggle, and mocked his efforts to uphold his responsibilities. But as time had passed on the Uber Judge’s Amazing Adventure in the Ranks of the PTC as a Wrestler (sometimes referred to as the Saga of the Uber Judge, and then a colon, and then something like a chapter title-subject heading-thematic representation, since those things are all the rage) the Uber Judge had come to feel that particular crow had become a companion on the journey. While it was true that the crow was a companion that didn’t have many constructive things to say, apart from “Caw!”, it at least represented a familiar voice, a familiar point of view. And that, thought the Uber Judge, was something that was important. Finishing his bathroom ritual (up to an including leaving the bathroom in as good condition as he arrived in it, yes including scrubbing down the tub after the shower) the Uber Judge headed down the steps into his living room where he greeted the Under Minor like this. “Morning,” The Under Minor, who was hastily writing on a dry erase board that had appeared in front of the Uber Judge’s entertainment center, said, “Morning, go ahead and get your juice, I'm not quite finished here.” “Okay,” replied the Uber Judge and proceeded to his kitchen, and the ritual of morning beverage of his prized Orange Juice, clean glass, and coaster. It was at the point where the Uber Judge had the bottle of OJ, coaster, and glass all on the kitchen table that he stopped. Returning to the living room, the Uber Judge said, “You have a dry erase board in front of my entertainment center.” The Under Minor, who was finishing some notes in the bottom right corner, said, “Yes.” “It’s just in front of my entertainment center, right?” asked the Uber Judge, “Because the dry erase board that I mentioned is actually big enough that I can’t see my entertainment center. It’s true that I don’t watch a great deal of television, but I can’t help but feel very nonplussed by the idea of that dry erase board replacing my entertainment center and all its contents.” The Under Minor, who had backed away a step from the board and was reviewing it a final time, said, “Oh, it’s right behind it. And better, when we’re done, the dry erase board actually can go right behind the entertainment center.” “Good,” said the Uber Judge, “I'm going to get my juice now.” He did. Returning to the living room after replacing the bottle of OJ, carrying the glass and coaster, the Uber Judge sat down on the couch and waited for the Under Minor to begin. “Okay,” said the Under Minor, “For round six we’re looking at-” “Before you begin,” said the Uber Judge, who out of pure deviousness and a tiny bit of malice had deliberately timed his interruption, “I’d like to let you know straight up that I'm going to lose this round.” “Good,” said the Under Minor, who continued with, “For round six we’re look…ing…at.” The Under Minor turned slowly to the Uber Judge. He blinked, pointing to the Uber Judge, and then slowly to his notes on the dry erase board and then back to the Uber Judge. The Under Minor coughed, and said, “Would you mind repeating that? I thought I heard you say that you’re going to lose this round.” “Your hearing,” said the Uber Judge, “is as accurate as ever. That’s precisely what I said.” The Under Minor regarded the Uber Judge while stroking the stubble on his chin. He hadn’t shaved this morning. He’d woken up early from a dream, and had been inspired to make The Uber Judge Victory Game Plan. He had rushed out the door into Office Depot, bought a dry erase board and markers, and had set everything up in advance to be ready the moment the Uber Judge came down the stairs. It would be a very fair and accurate statement to say that the Under Minor’s feelings were a little hurt. He now had a decision to make. While at the dry erase board, he was in control, but with the Uber Judge’s comment, the Under Minor would have to relinquish control to inquire further into the Uber Judge’s … misery inducing prediction. “Why do … what makes you say that?” The Uber Judge sipped his Orange Juice, and said, “Oh, I'm just certain of it. But go on with your presentation. You’ve worked very hard on it, and I’d like to hear what you have to say.” “Right,” said the Under Minor, who took his handy rag, and wiped clean his exhaustive work for his presentation. All the little hand drawn graphs, the stupid acronyms, the diagrams, the flowchart, the footnotes at the bottom vanished. “Our main dilemma going into round six,” said the Under Minor, as he wrote “UBER JUDGE” across the dry erase board in large letters, “is…” The Under Minor wrote an equals sign (=), and then below it “ASSHOLE”, and said, “that you’re an asshole.” “Mmmm,” said the Uber Judge in his best attempt to seem interested. “Now why are you an asshole?” asked the Under Minor, “Well, let’s look at that.” The Under Minor wiped clean the board with the rag, “Well, the first reason is that he doesn’t appreciate his number one supporter,” and the Under Minor wrote, “NO APPRECIATION” “Why doesn’t he appreciate?” asked the Under Minor, “Well, let’s look at his characteristics that lend themselves to unappreciative behavior.” The Under Minor wrote, “SELF ABSORBED”, “SELF CENTERED”, “SELFISHNESS”, and “SELF SATISFACTION” one below the other under NO APPRECIATION. “All these qualities have one thing in common,” continued the Under Minor. He circled the vertically lined up “SELF” in the beginning of each word, and said, “They are all wrapped up in the Uber Judge and his self. His decisions are about him and him alone, and largely ignore other people.” “As these thoughts continually cycle around in your mind,” and at this the Under Minor drew a little spiral on the dry erase board, “they bypass many aspects of rational thinking, and rational emotive thinking. You’re not looking outside yourself, so you’re not making comparisons, you’re not testing the validity of the data that you’re processing, and you’re not accessing your point of view outside of yourself, since you’re focusing it entirely internally.” Drawing a box around the spiral drawing, the Under Minor continued, “So you’re cut off from the outside world, and you can’t appreciate the efforts of people around you.” “Like you,” remarked the Uber Judge helpfully. “Right,” said the Under Minor, “like me going to great effort to help prepare you for a match that you’re not going to try to win.” The Uber Judge sipped his orange juice, “I never said that. I intend to do everything in my power to win. But it won’t work.” “Why not?” asked the Under Minor. “I’ll show you,” said the Uber Judge, who finished his orange juice, grabbed his glass and coaster and headed into the kitchen. The Under Minor followed, watching the Uber Judge put the glass into the dishwasher, and the coaster into the cute little wooded coaster dispenser rake thingy that was tastefully carved with frogs on it. “Is it the dishwasher or the frog coaster holder upper?” asked the Under Minor. The Uber Judge leaned against the kitchen counter and said, “Play the message on my answering machine.” Warily, the Under Minor complied. == The machine spoke. “Good afternoon, Mr. Uber Judge! It is I, the Vice President of PTC Sponsored Tournaments. I’d say I'm sorry that I missed you, but that’d be a lie. I’d hoped that I’d get your machine as this will be much easier to do while leaving you a message. You see, I have to admit that I was wrong about you. You’ve become something of a phenomenon in the PTC as of late, what with your great success in the Infinite Gauntlet, and your joining of UXW or XUW or whatever that place is called. The Infinite Gauntlet has been very successful, and we here at the PTC can’t help but feel that you’re largely to thank for that. You’ve been a dynamic force in the competition so far, and you’re gaining a lot of interest. Initially, we weren’t sure who’d be interested in the Gauntlet, but it’s exceeded our expectations. The fans have taken a big interest, and further, so have some of the more famous names from PTC tournaments. This is very good. Very good indeed.” The voice paused. “If it were someone besides you.” The voice took on a more emotive feel, the loathing dripping off the answering machine into small puddles on the floor, “I hate you Uber Judge. I loathe you. You have entirely too much power, and we at the PTC absolutely hate it when power is taken away from us. You were shit judging those tournaments. You want to know why? Well, I’ll tell you why. Because the tournaments became more about the Uber Judge than about the bloody fucking tournament! No one cared about who won, they wanted to hear what the Uber Judge had to say about it. The tournaments became about the Uber Judge, and the PTC was marginalized. You ruined it. They were going to shitcan me, Uber Judge. They were going to declare that tournaments weren’t effective uses of the PTC’s resources. Where would I be then, huh? Where would I be, the VICE PRESIDENT of PTC SPONSORED TOURNAMENTS without any fucking tournaments to PRESIDE over? So I fired you as an administrator, and dropping you down that trap door was one of the most satisfying moments of my life.” “He has a trap door in his office?” asked the Under Minor incredulously. “Implausible,” said the Uber Judge. “And I don’t care how plausible a trap door is!” shouted the voice on the machine, “I had it built just for you. The red button on my desk has initials on it. Know what those initials are? U and J, and you can guess what they stand for. It felt GOOD.” The voice caught his breath, and then continued, “But look what happened. The Uber Judge started competing, and I okayed it because I delighted in the idea of you having your sorry little ass handed to you by the PTC wrestlers who held a grudge. The idea of you being beaten half to death made me swoon. Much better than the trap door joy. But you haven’t been beaten half to death.” The Under Minor remarked, “Rollins made a pretty big mess of you that one time though.” “Well, there was that one time with Rollins,” said the Vice President of PTC Sponsored Tournaments, “but you managed to win that match somehow, so that doesn’t count. Anyway… where was I?” The Uber Judge was silent (as he heard this before), but the Under Minor said, “You were saying-” “Shut up!” shouted the VPOPTCST, “this is an answering machine message, you imbecile! Ah, yes, anyway, you were out of my hair, and then you’re back in it. I just can’t seem to dispose of you, can I, Uber Judge? Well, now you’re doomed. You’re facing three other competitors. Two of which came THIS close to beating you before, and one special extra feature. Clinton Sage!” “You know,” said the Under Minor, “this is a lot like the presentation I had prepared earlier.” The VPOPTCST continued, “I have heard from unreliable sources that Clinton Sage has occult super powers that allow him to channel hatred from fans for some nefarious infernal purpose that probably has to do with allowing serial killer from Pennsylvania facing execution to bypass the afterlife or to rule hell or something implausible like that.” “Implausible,” said the Under Minor. “Very,” said the Uber Judge. “And he’s going to become the new Infinite Gauntlet Champion. I’ve bribed the refs. I’ve bribed the judges. I’ve sold my soul to a demon in exchange for seeing you fail, and I’ll have you know, it worked for Doctor Curiosity in the GTT4 finals!” “That was very implausible,” said the Uber Judge. “But he still won,” replied the Under Minor. “I’ve also put a bounty on your head in XUW, so that any wrestler who beats the ever loving snot out of you before the fatal four way match, gets a free entry into GTT5.” The Uber Judge snorted, “If they remember that I work there.” “Yes, Uber Judge,” said the VPOPTCST, “there’s a grand conspiracy at work, and it’s entire purpose for its existence is to make absolutely certain that you lose your next Infinite Gauntlet Match! You’re doomed! Doomed doomed DOOMED DOOMED DOO-” The VPOPTCST coughed, “Ahem. Doomed.” He coughed again. Then he stifled some giggling laughter. Then he must’ve put his hand over the phone, as the Uber Judge and the Under Minor could just barely hear him say, “Guys! Shut up already! I'm almost done!” causing the UJ and the UM to glance at each other with eyebrows raised. “Anyway,” continued the voice, “I have one more thing to say.” The VPOPTCST took a deep breath and shouted, “I hate you soooooooo much!” Click. Beep. == “Well,” said the Uber Judge, “there you have it.” The Under Minor was still stunned as he watched the Uber Judge leave the kitchen. Frowning, the Under Minor shook his head from side to side, trying to shake the unreal quality of the phone message from his thoughts. He followed the Uber Judge and sat down next to him on the couch. Both men were quiet for a few moments. With a deep breath, the Under Minor said, “You’re taking this remarkably well. This had to have come as a harsh blow. You were doing so well, and then to find out that a large conspiracy is out to prevent you from success… that’s got to be disheartening to say the least.” “Eh,” said the Uber Judge, “it’s not so bad.” The Under Minor blinked and looked at the Uber Judge as if he’d grown a second head. “Is my hearing going again?” “No,” said the Uber Judge. “You, the guy who worries about everything to death, aren’t freaked out by this?” “No, I'm not. Which is a little surprising even to me,” said the Uber Judge. As the Under Minor stared at him, he noticed that the Uber Judge was smiling. “Dare I ask why you’re not freaked out? Have you started taking meds for your OCD?” “Sure to the first, no to the second. It’s just…” said the Uber Judge. “What?” said the Under Minor, who had glimpsed what was going on, but needed to know for sure. “Well, I'm having the time of my life with this. The Infinite Gauntlet has been so much fun. I'm competing, I'm entertaining, I'm doing pretty good, and it just feels… good. And you know, the fact that the Vice President of PTC Sponsored Tournaments isn’t having any fun with this, makes me appreciate all the fun that I'm having all the more. If Clinton Sage really has occult super powers or not, well, it just doesn’t matter to me. I'm going to go out there into that match, I'm going to do everything within my normal human ability to succeed, and whatever happens… well, it happens. If I win, that’s great. If I lose, well that’s too bad. I’ve had a fantastic run, I did help put the Infinite Gauntlet on the map. Fans are watching it, people are interested. And even if I lose, I’ll probably have a chance to compete again later.” The Under Minor smiled and shrugged, “People lose matches all the time.” “Sure they do. Even the greats lose here and there. Thinking about that message, thinking about what I’ve done so far since I decided to compete, I can’t feel bad. I’ve done a little bit of good by competing, and I'm very proud of that.” “Well,” said the Under Minor, “I'm proud of you. I'm sorry I called you an asshole.” “It’s okay,” said the Uber Judge. “So,” said the Under Minor, “do you have a plan for the match?” “Yes,” said the Uber Judge, “I'm going to try to cripple Clinton Sage, and hope Rich Rollins wins if I can’t win myself.” “Oh,” said the Under Minor, “are you going to tell Rollins this?” “Of course not,” said the Uber Judge, “don’t be silly. That’d almost be like cheating. Nah, the plan works better as a surprise anyway.” And with a conclusive sigh, the Under Minor said, “Good enough.”
