GOP, the College Years! (part 2)

4 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-04 As Gordon Powell walked back to his dorm room from his meeting with Brent Cowart, head of the Campus Democrats, like the weight of the world had been lifted off his shoulders. Although he really didn't have any sort of vendetta against Brent or the Democrats, he felt a knot inside of him for the longest time, a yearning to say something really loud in someone's face, to stand up and assert himself. He forgot about being booted out of the lab, forgot about his roommate fucking without consideration, forgot about everything bothering him. He was walking on air, as if each concrete square on the sidewalk was a cloud. He osmosed through the door into his building and floated up the stairs, swooping into his room. As he fluttered into his room, he turned around to find his roommate, Jerry O'Quinn, sitting on the edge of his bed. He looked up at Gordon and then stood right up to confront him. Gordon's airy mood seemed to dampen and his eyes became timid as Jerry got in his face. "You fucker, Shaniqua didn't come last night. She said she was freaked out by you being there. Asshole, let's lay down some ground rules…" Right then and there, the meekness left Gordon's eyes and the fire that he displayed minutes earlier returned. "No, let me lay down some ground rules, asswipe. Firstly, no sex in this room on any night where I have to get up the next morning early. Just because you're a fucking retard and didn't schedule any classes to start until 1 doesn't mean the rest of the world is as slacktastical as you, alright? You wanna get laid, go back to the whore's room. I fucking flunked out of my lab because of that shit." Jerry was about to say something, but Gordon ended the pause with authority. Or as Cartman would say, "AUTHORITAAAAHHH!" "Secondly, this is not Jerry-house. We don't do whatever you want to do. Thursday night is not Organic Tofu Night. No night will be Organic Tofu Night. That shit is nasty. If you want to eat it, go right ahead, but don't bitch at me if I want to go to Taco Bell and get a fucking gordita. I happen to enjoy gorditas, mostly because they taste really good, but also because I know it's made out of animals! Hell, I fantasize about the steer that goes into the meat for that gordita being slaughtered having to watch the cow he just banged the night before being milked to produce the cheese for that gordita right before he gets clocked with the tackhammer." "Dude, that's just wrong…" "SHUT UP! I'm not fucking done yet! Finally, you will not talk to me about politics. In fact, you will not talk to me about anything except for school, the National Football League and the musical genius that is Eddie Money. Anything else, and just keep it to yourself because it'll probably piss me off and I didn't come to college to get pissed off. Do you hear me?" Jerry just stood there, jaw dropped wide open. "Now I'm finished. Do you hear me?" Jerry just shook his head with his eyes all bugged out. "Dude… that took balls man. Big brass balls. I respect that man. I fuckin' respect that. You're good people, man. Good people." Jerry patted him on the shoulder as he walked out the door. "I'm gonna go get a coffee. You want one?" Gordon shook his head. Jerry left the room, wondering what the fuck just happened. Gordon, however, his airiness changed to a feeling of almost orgasmic bliss, how he felt when he rubbed one out to Baywatch or the lucky occasion he was able to catch a softcore movie on Skinemax. He had to sit down on his bed. The crystalline calm melded with sensual warmth rushed to his head and manifested itself in a sigh of satisfaction. Then though, it hit him. He still had to make friends. Shit. While Jerry seemed cool with him after the tirade, Gordon knew he could never hang out with him and his friends again. He could never go back. What would he say? What would they think? He knew he had to make new friends. He just didn't know how, but for now, that was irrelevant. He had a free morning to catch up on some sleep he missed out on. The next day, Gordon woke up, refreshed and revitalized after the emotionally taxing day behind him. He glanced over to Jerry's bed, but noticed Jerry was missing. Probably out banging another campus slut. Gordon walked over to the mini-fridge to get himself an orange juice. As he walked over to his dresser, he noticed something out the window. He looked down into the quad and saw someone who looked a lot like Jerry playing with a Frisbee. Gordon stared out the window for a minute, looking at him and the group of people out on the quad tossing it around, reminding him that he needed to find friends of his own. For someone who would have aced an advanced chemistry lab his freshman year if he wasn't booted out for other reasons, for someone who could get up in front of an entire high school class and deliver a speech without batting an eyelash, making new friends was daunting, almost horrifying. This was the first time in almost 13 years where he had to make a friend. As he walked to class, the thought consumed him, almost ate him whole. For the first time, he went to Organic Chemistry class and he didn't immerse himself in the lecture. He fiddled with his pen, doodled nervously, looked at the clock, did everything but pay attention. Gordon was so scatterbrained that he didn't hear his professor dismiss class. As everyone else got up, Gordon remained seated. "Class is over, son." The voice startled Gordon. It was his professor, Dr. Harley. "Something bothering you?" "Oh, no… just had a rough night last night. Personal." "I see. Well, get some rest tonight." Gordon got up and walked out of the lecture hall. As he stepped out, he didn't notice that someone had been waiting for him. A short little girl with dirty blonde hair and hazel eyes, she cleared her throat as Gordon exited. The frazzled young lad didn't pay her any mind though. Gordon walked away somewhat quickly, trying to get back to his dorm to work up the courage to go to the cafeteria and sit at a random table, or see if he could start pledging for a fraternity even though it was a week late. The short little girl wouldn't give up though. She almost broke into a sprint to catch up to Gordon. Finally, she was right behind him. Instinctually, she tapped him on the shoulder, since it seemed sound wasn't going to get his attention. As he felt the unexpected but soft hand on his shoulder, Gordon jumped up and then turned around. "'Scuse me, Gordon?" Gordon stammered and stuttered for a bit. "I'm sorry… did I catch you at a bad time, sugar?" Her Texan accent soothed him just enough so he could form a coherent sentence. "Oh, oh no, you… you just startled me, that's all." "I'm terribly sorry. May I introduce myself?" Gordon nodded. "My name's Maggie, Maggie Meltzer. I'm in your Organic Chemistry class." Gordon thought it to be miraculous that at the point where he was stressing himself out over friends, and one just happens to tap him on his shoulder. "Maggie, oh… well, it's nice to meet you." "Me too… I've heard so much about you." What? This couldn't be good. What did she hear? Damn, she's not a friend, he thought. Dammit, dammit, dammit. "Oh… what have you heard…" "About how you told off that Brent Cowart." She heard about… that? "You mean… you're in the Democrats too?" Her face scrunched up like she had just taken a huge bite out of a fresh lemon wedge. "Oh hell no, sugar. I wouldn't be caught dead hangin' 'round with those pantywaists." "Oh…" This was promising. "Then how'd you hear?" "It's all around campus, at least in the political club circles. You actually told him off and flabbergasted him. No one in my group ever was able to do that. But you… you done did what no one else could." "Your circle?" "Yeah, I'm the Freshman Leader in the Campus Republicans." Oh no. Another recruiting mission. Gordon swore off politics after his ordeal in the Democrats. "Oh, well, I'm sorry… I'm not interested in joining." He was about to turn away and walk when Maggie gently grabbed him by the arm and turned him back around. "Oh sugar, no… no. If y'all don't wanna be in our group, that's fine. I kinda wanted to get you in… but it's okay if you don't. I just wanted to get to know you… the man who told that sonuvagun Brent Cowart off." A small smile cracked on Gordon's face. He thanked God in his heart for sending him an angel. "Well, if you like, you can get to know me over some gorditas at Taco Bell." "Taco Bell?" Oh no, she doesn't like it… "That sounds great. Although I'm warnin' y'all. I'm more of a Meximelt gal." Whew. She passed the first test. Gordon and Maggie immediately hit it off. They didn't have everything in common, but they shared enough; love of Taco Bell and science (Maggie was a polymer science major), hatred of Brent Cowart, and while she didn't like wrestling all that much, she didn't mind that Gordon was a nerd when it came to the squared circle. It even made friend-making easier for Gordon. Maggie introduced him to folks she had encountered in the Republicans. One of them was Jameson Bossy, a senior who was the chair of the club. Jamey was a lot different than Brent in many ways. He didn't exude charisma, but he asserted his own personality. It was just more subtle. He had quiet charm. One day, Gordon met up with Maggie at Taco Bell after her meeting. Jamey and his girlfriend tagged along. After some light conversation, Jamey thought he'd at least put it out there for Gordon. "Hey Gord, have you ever thought about joining us?" Gordon furrowed his brow. "No, not really." "How come, if ya don't mind me asking?" Gordon had to think. From what he knew about the Republican Party, he thought he'd actually fit in, especially with some of the things that he was learning about his own political beliefs. He had planned to register Republican for the next year's election, but he'd always resisted the temptation to join the group. "Well… I… I just don't think that active politics is for me, y'know? I mean… I do appreciate the right to vote we have, and I exercise that right. I know about the issues, and I know some people, but I'm a man of science, Jamey. I don't need to be caught up in that. It's just… not me." Jamey nodded. "well Gord, I respect that. I think you'd make a hell of a junior council member on here, get some things done, but if you don't feel it, then I respect that." "Yeah," Maggie said. "You can leave all the politics to me." She held his arm tight and cuddled up against him. Gordon leaned back and smiled. At this point and time, life was perfect. If you didn't notice by now, Gordon and Maggie were an item. They spent plenty of time with each other, but they also knew the concept of space. Monday night was Gordon's to watch wrestling, and God help you if you interrupted him while the Outsiders were making life difficult for the WCW establishment. Just ask Jerry, who asked Gordon for some tinfoil for his bong during a Mankind promo. Gordon got so pissed that Jerry wouldn't wait til a commercial that he tossed the bong out the window. Maggie had her Republican meetings. But between classes, they couldn't be separated. One day, as they were walking back from their Organic Chem class… "Well, if it isn't Gerard. You jumped sides pretty fast." Gordon and Maggie both turned around to see Brent right there taunting him in the middle of the quad. "Oh, it's you. What did I tell you about the next time you got my name wrong? I do have that textbook in my backpack." "C'mon now, in front of all these people? You don't want to be charged with assault now." Gordon was about to prove him wrong, grabbing for the book in his backpack. Maggie stopped him though. "No, it's not worth it." She turned around to Brent. "And for your information, he's not part of the Republicans." "Typical. He's palling around with you, and you are the Freshman rep." "Hey, just because we're dating doesn't mean he's in my group. It's not a crime…" "Oh, I know, I know. I just wish he could have been a little more forthright in his reasoning for quitting our group." "Hey, he…" "Sweetie, let me fight my own battle." Gordon stepped forward. "Listen, I don't want any beef with you other than the fact that you keep getting my name wrong. I already blew up at you, you know how I feel. You should also know that your group swore me off to public politics. Go ahead and ask Jamey. I'm not in the group and I don't go to meetings. Swear on my Bible." Brent backed off a bit. "Y'know, that's alright. I believe you. But that still doesn't mean I can't be angry for the way you quit my group." Brent gave him a scowl and walked away. "Babe, forget him." "Trust me, I already have." Gordon looked straight ahead as Maggie looked at him with sad eyes. Then, she got a bright idea. "Hey, I know what would cheer you up." Gordon perked up, and Maggie whispered into his ear. Gordon's eyes became wide, but then he cracked a slight smile. "Really?" Maggie nodded. "Okay then… we have to stop at the drugstore then." With a CVS bag in one hand and Maggie in his other, Gordon flung open his dorm room door. Jerry was eating a plate of tofu at his desk, listening to Sonic Youth. The door startled him. "Dude, you're a bull…" Interrupting his roommate, Gordon asserted, "Out. Get out." "Dude, why?" "Sexile." "C'mon man, you said it yourself. No kicking out on a school night. Go back to her place." "Ummm, it's Friday night man. And I know you don't have a Saturday class." "Oh… umm… well, I've got plans… here tonight." "No you don't. Dude, Mikey told me you guys were smoking pot tonight in the alleyway down by Jester and then you were going to another one of your Democrat rallies downtown." Jerry looked flabbergasted. "Alright man. You win." Jerry took his plate and threw it out. He went towards the door, and on his way out, he turned to Maggie. "Have fun." The door shut behind them. Gordon and Maggie were alone in his room. Nervous, Gordon froze like a statue. Maggie looked at him and then put her arm on his shoulder. "It's alright, I won't tell anyone that I made the first move." Maggie leaned over to him and kissed him. Gordon pulled away after a few seconds. "What's wrong?" she asked. "Oh… well, this is the first time I've ever… y'know… done it." Maggie shook her head. "It's my first time too, sugar. Don't worry about it." "Oh… okay…" They began to make out and moved over to the bed. The next morning, Gordon woke up with Maggie asleep in his arms. He smiled at her and tussled her hair. As he pulled his finger out, she woke up. "Mornin', sunshine." "Good morning babe. How was it?" "Well… I don't know. I ain't got nothin' to base it on. But I'm glad my first time was with you." Gordon smiled. "Me too." Maggie was about to lay her head down on Gordon's chest, but she shot it back up again. "Crap, what time is it?" Gordon looked over at his clock. "Eight AM." "Whew… I thought it was later. I have to be at the quad at noon." "Oh? What for?" "Campus Republican activity." "What kind of activity?" Maggie was a little surprised. Gordon never took interest in the Republican stuff before. "Oh it's an Affirmative Action Bake Sale." "Really? Cool! I like bake sales!" Maggie looked at him like he had three heads. "What?" "Sugar, do you know what Affirmative Action is?" Gordon shook his head. "Not really, although my last stepfather would say that term whenever he'd get pissed off about black people." "Oh, well there's a reason for that. Affirmative Action is this government initiative where companies have to have a certain quota of people from each race on their workforce, regardless of their skill level. So in protest of that, we're havin' a bake sale where our prices differ for whatever race you are. Like for example, white folk pay a dollar for a cupcake, whereas Asians pay 75 cents and blacks 50 cents." "Oh, so it's a protest?" Maggie nodded. "Pretty clever." "Yeah, it was Jamey's idea. He's gonna man the table. I'm just supposed to be stopping by at noon to see what's going on." "Cool… hey… d'ya mind if I come with?" Maggie was startled again. "Uhh… yeah… sure." "Cool. Can't wait." Maggie smiled at Gordon. A million thoughts ran through her mind, and one of those thoughts just slipped out of her mouth. "I love you." Gordon looked at her again. "I love you too… that's why I'm coming." As Gordon and Maggie came up to the table, they saw a huge crowd surrounding them. It wasn't a throng of Republicans or even neutral people buying. Gordon first spotted his roommate Jerry, and Maggie saw Brent with a megaphone. "Campus Republicans, we're protesting this racist and crass attempt at publicity," the leader of the Campus Democrats shouted through his megaphone. "We're going to be nice and give you another 15 minutes before I call campus police and have this event broken up." "Jerk," Maggie said to herself. "C'mon sugar." Maggie and Gordon rushed over to the table. Jamey was shouting something over the people who were between him and Brent. "C'mon now. We're not hurting anyone. We're just protesting." "You hurtin' me," said a black bystander. "My people suffered through many years of oppression. Too many to have to watch this." "Look, we're just pointing out the idiocy of this government program…" Jamey looked over and saw Gordon and Maggie just arriving to the table. "Maggie, thank God you're here… and Gordon… wow, this is a pleasant surprise. Thanks for coming… but we're in trouble." "I told you we might have run into this problem, especially if the College NAACP Chapter showed up." Gordon couldn't believe what he was seeing. People protesting a protest. Every time he saw a protest on television, no one was there trying to give them a hard time. Well, every time he saw a peaceful protest. Even the one he went to with the Democrats. No one protested them. No one threatened to call the cops on them. "Why are they doing this to you guys?" "We're doing this because this is racially insensitive and a clear cry for attention," Brent said, coming over to the table himself. "What, and you think Affirmative Action isn't racially insensitive?" Jamey answered. "No, I don't. It's necessary and it's right. There's no question about it. How do you expect black people to make their way if all the opportunities are being swallowed up by The Man and his cronies?" "Your allegations of racism are unfounded at best. I'm not going to deny that racism doesn't exist, and I'm not going to deny that people of all races need opportunities, but doing it like this? Quotas? C'mon Brent, even you know that's a quick fix." "No it's not. It's the right thing to do. These people have suffered enough indignity. They shouldn't have to have a program that benefits them questioned like this in the open." Gordon's rage was boiling over an extra degree with each exchange they had. Finally, he'd had enough. "What, and it's okay for you to question a process that gives closure to murder victims' families in the open?" "You butt out of this, Gerry." Gordon clenched his fist and was about to swing it right at Brent's face, but Maggie grabbed it before he could even make the motion. "No, no violence." Gordon turned to Maggie. "Well, I have to do something." Gordon broke his arm free of his girlfriend's grasp and swiped it at Brent. He didn't connect with a punch, but he wasn't trying to. He nabbed the megaphone out of his hand and leapt onto the table. "Everybody, everybody listen up. Over here!" The arguing crowd simmered down a little and turned their attention to the kook with the megaphone on top of the bake sale table. "Alright, I'm going to make this simple. You, you right in front of me." Gordon pointed to a young, black male right in front of the table. He pointed to himself and Gordon nodded to him. "What's your name?" "Tyrone." "Alright Tyrone, you're a student here, I assume. Right?" He nodded. "Okay, now, what if I were to tell you that you only got in here because the University of Texas needed to fill a quota for black people. What if I told you that?" "I'd kick your fuckin' ass, cracka. I worked damn hard to get in here." The crowd got a little restless again. "Hey, hey, settle down. I'm not saying you didn't. I'm trying to make a point here. Now, if you found out that it was the case, that you got into Texas because of your skin color and a quota and not because you worked hard, how would you feel?" "I… I'd be pretty ticked off, man. I'd feel disrespected." "Disrespected. He'd feel disrespected, and you know what? He'd have the absolute right to feel disrespected. I'd want to get into college or get a job because I earned it, not because there had to be a certain amount of my race in there. Don't tell that to the folks who passed this Affirmative Action stuff. No, they'd rather disrespect every black person, every Hispanic person, every Asian by letting them in not because of their skill level but becuase they need to fill a quota. They say that it's to combat years of racism, but really, it only reinforces the fact in these minorities' minds the perception that they're inferior and that they're only getting ahead because they have to be there. Meanwhile, very skilled and very talented white people sit at home unemployed. Why? Because of a quick fix. We don't want to solve the problem at its root, which would be making sure more black people are skilled from get-go one." A slight cheer came from the Republicans behind him. Gordon turned around and told them to cut it til he was finished, and Brent took the opportunity to try and grab the megaphone back. Gordon swatted him away. "I'm not done yet!" he said with a scowl. "Now, you have guys like this, the guy who just tried to take my voice away from me before I was done talking. He's trying to take our voice away. He's led so many damn protests on this campus and very rarely has he had the cops called on him, but here he is, threatening to get campus security to shove us away, and why? Because we're wrong, apparently. Wrong. Yet, if Tyrone, someone who would be affected by this policy, if Tyrone thinks we're right, then wouldn't we have the right to be here? We're not bothering anyone. We're not hurting anyone. We're just trying to make a point, a poignant point. If you want to narc on us, go ahead, but you'd be branding yourself a hypocrite. If any of you had any decency, then you wouldn't let this blowhard call campus security on us. Am I right?" A cheer came up from the crowd again, this time a little louder, but still concentrated in the Republican quarters. Brent stomped on the ground and then looked up at Gordon. "You son of a bitch, you're wrong! You're still being racially insensitive, and you're leading these poor people into believing that your system that would deny them is right. Give it up now, or I'm calling secur…" Brent was grabbed from behind on the shoulder. It was Jerry. "Dude, you know I'm with you 100 percent in ideals man. You know I'm behind Affirmative Action and you know I think these guys are tools." Jerry looked up at Gordon. "But Gordon's right in one regard. We can't narc him. They're allowed to protest about stuff too, man. It's their right as much as it's ours." "But… they're offending people! Look at all those black people getting pissed off." "Yeah, and if people didn't get pissed off, the world would be boring and nothing would get done. If they want to risk getting their asses kicked by the Black Panthers, let 'em. But don't fuckin' narc on them. You'd be no better than the man." Jerry walked right past Brent and over to Gordon. "Dude, you suck." Gordon shook his head. "But I still respect you man. Even if you do suck." Gordon smiled a little. "I'll catch you back at the room." With that, Jerry walked off. Brent then went over to the table and looked Jamey straight in the eye. "Are you sure he's not in your group?" "I'm 100 percent positive, Brent. But y'know what? I think him joining us would be a damn good idea." Jamey looked over at Gordon, still atop the table with the megaphone. Gordon looked down at him and started to agree that maybe it would be a good idea if he joined the Campus Republicans.
