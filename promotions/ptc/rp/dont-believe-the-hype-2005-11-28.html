Don’t believe the hype.

28 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-28 Don’t believe the hype. That is the saying that is on everyone’s lips at the moment. It almost seems to have been elevated to almost mystical status in an unbelievably short amount of time, sitting right up there in copywriter’s heaven alongside things like ‘just do it’, ‘impossible is nothing’, or even ‘it’s the real thing’. Every time I turn the television on I see yet another advert fading out with the gruff voiced voice-over, that strange Australian accent, with the very familiar words. ‘Don’t believe the hype’. Every other billboard that I walk by seems to have been bought and paid for by the PTC as they show scenes from the GTTs that have graced us in the past. Larger than life images of wrestlers from all over the World that have taken part, and made their mark, in the most prestigious of tournaments known in wrestling. Seymour Almasy wielding a sword high above his head as he shouts his challenge to the skies; Doctor Curiosity twirling his white lab coat around his shoulders, a mad gleam in his eyes; Angelo Deville, half shrouded in darkness, standing impassively with a Mona Lisa-esque enigmatic smile on his face. Below them all, four words that overshadow everything and everyone. Don’t believe the hype. Even mainstream news and sports seem to have picked up on the imminent start of the fifth annual tournament. Perhaps it is simply because of the death of Eddie Gurerro being still fresh in people’s minds but wrestling is on the mouth of sport’s anchors across the channels, spoken in the same breath as the more accepted industries of baseball and football, or perhaps it is because even the ‘real’ sports recognise that the GTT is not your usual wrestling spectacular. To see CNN closing their sports broadcast with a story on Primetime Central and the gathering of one hundred and twenty eight of the businesses top stars is something hardly believable though; four little words that you could blink and miss. Don’t believe the hype. For the first time in the history of the sport the outside World seems to have been caught up in the hysteria and mounting excitement that the smarks, the marks, and even the wrestlers themselves find themselves in. Ask any man on the street if they would recognise Karina Wolfenden and most of them would undoubtedly say no. Ask any man on the street if they could tell you one sentence that sums up the PTC and its annual tournament and there is a better than average bet that they would come out with the same thing. Don’t believe the hype. So much is being made of this tournament, so much is being made of the industry itself, and yet the powers that be in the PTC came up with THAT slogan to promote their flagship offering to the masses. Self-effacing and almost patronising inwardly as it is, it is also a flash of momentarily brilliance at work. It is TELLING everyone exactly what he or she needs to do. It is ORDERING people to look deeper, to open the cover of the book before judging it, to not assume that they know the truth of things before the facts are discovered. Each and every year the GTT rolls out and each and every year the World decides, in advance, who is going to win. Who is going to come out as King of the mountain. Before the first blow is felt or the first match started the onlookers, those people who can only sit at home and dream of walking the path of the modern day gladiator, ruminate with glee about who will stand and who will fall. They not only believe the hype, they buy into it body and soul, they live and breath it. It isn’t their fault, I suppose, that they cannot read four simple words that are spelling out everything that they need to know. Don’t believe the hype. Me, though, I am the cautious type. I like to wait and see before I make my opinions known. On top of that I am able to read, and I can follow instructions rather well. I won’t sit back and join the blood frenzy as people predict what will happen in the not-so distant future. I won’t proclaim that Almasy will win or that the newest unknown up-and-comer will surprise us all. I will do one thing at the moment, and one thing only. Don’t believe the hype. I won’t.
