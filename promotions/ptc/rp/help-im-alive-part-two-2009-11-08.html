Help; I’m Alive (Part Two)

8 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-11-08 I - Now There’s a expanse into which brother and sister drift upon an unconquerable tide. It exists in the gulf between the brashness of youth and the regret of old age. A youth spent absorbed in petty bickering as the tight-knit family triggers claustrophobic lashings out. An old age of rare reunions, family funerals as the backdrop for fond reminiscing or heartfelt apologising. One shore treacherous and rocky, the other sedate and weathered, the waters between are treacherous, currents dragging those treading them apart and under. As the crushing pressure of the depths began to suffocate her, a woman reached a desperate hand out toward the blurry silhouettes keeping their heads just above the choppy water. “How do I get back from here?” Karina Wolfenden pleaded, each brittle word crumbling as it left her dry, cracked lips. Hiding behind the swirling veil of steam from her sobering coffee, Karina didn’t look up for an answer. The burden of shame weighed heavy around Karina’s neck, hunching her shoulders as eyes traced the grain of the table back and forth. Every second of silence fed the vacuum in the pit of her stomach which threatened to consume her from within. Outward appearance reflecting the mood within, Karina’s unrestrained hair was dyed a mournful black and her lithe frame engulfed in a charcoal hooded sweatshirt. Staring emptily at the top of his monochrome sister’s bowed head, Max Wolfenden had no words; be they of false comfort or shattering damnation. With Max sporting a crisp white shirt, a Windsor-knotted pastel striped tie and thick brush of sandy hair highlighted with distinguished, silvery-grey flecks, to any outsider the siblings bore no relation; just two strangers having sunk into the last two empty chairs. Awkward silence waited in the wings, leaving the stage to the ensemble cast of the coffee shop. “…-anana-pecan muffi… -at’s what I told hi-… -eave a tip you tigh-… nk I know he-…“. Detuning the chatter, a still peace settled around their table. “That’s that then…” Karina concluded from the silence. Blowing onto the surface of her coffee, Karina’s head lifted. Emerald eyes, the one feature they shared, connected. Brow weighed down by unease, Max studied the face of the woman who just two days ago had awoken on his kitchen floor looking as if she’d crawled out of an air disaster as the lone, miraculous survivor. “You’ve barely got a scratch on you,” he remarked, a little bit of fear tainting his bewilderment as he deftly side-stepped the question. “Your…” “I heal kinda quick,” the self-conscious Karina mumbled. Even through a list of their similarities would struggle to reach beyond parentage and surname, Max’s love for his sister meant he’d never dream of wanting to clip her wings, to tame and domesticate her. Her sense of adventure was bred from his caution as she ventured out to explore a world he was too timid to guide her through. That wasn’t enough to prevent the image of the bloodied and bruised Karina dominating his thoughts, front-and-centre. It curdled the latte in his stomach. How many times had she shot him a wink when just a few hours earlier the eye had been swollen shut under a mottled, festering bruise. How many times had she revitalised him during the dourest of family functions with a cheeky grin from lips that had had left bloody lipstick marks on her pillow the night before. Even the radiant terracotta couches littered throughout the shop couldn’t defrost his spine. But, that was the exactly sight she’d imprinted in the memory of his daughter, the morning of her birthday. “I don’t think there is a way back,” he spoke to nothing. Unable to look his sister in the eye while he wrote her out of his family’s life, Max spoke to what wasn’t there, like Bruce Willis’ widow in the Sixth Sense. “Alice, sh-” “It’s okay,” Karina’s condemned words tried to sooth as her brother’s shaking hand drum-rolled spoon on plate. “I’ve fucked enough things up in my life to know how this goes.” Part of him wished his little sister’s bratty side would take over, that the L’Enfant Terrible would lash spiteful words off a forked tongue to vindicate her banishment. Instead, she sniffed back a rare tear, disguising its substance behind a blowing of her nose. “When Alice told me she was pregnant with Erin, I was terrified,” Max confessed, guilt forcing him to pour out something to balance the scales. “I didn’t… I couldn’t go home for three days. She, Alice, she even filed a missing persons, thinking I was laying dead in an alley somewhere. We’d been trying for two years and I still couldn’t handle it!” Every selfish urge of Karina’s goaded her storm out mid-confession, but she knew that this was something Max needed to say. If listening to him unload his soul of such a burden would be the last thing Karina did before going into exile, she could live the with that. “I didn’t want to be our father, Kari,” he continued to spill, eyes staring down into the frothy whirlpool he was creating in his coffee. “I didn’t want Erin to be trophy trotted out to paint me as a family man, to fulfil someone else’s expectation. I didn’t want to be a him, I couldn’t… I shouldn’t be a father if I was like him. So tried… I tried so fucking hard be nothing like him. Every choice I made, I had to think what he’d do. I had to make sure that I was making it, that it wasn’t what I’d learned from him.” Max then realised that not dignifying his sister with the courtesy of eye-contact was exactly what his father would do. Focus tore itself out of the depths of his swirling latte to look Karina straight in the eye. “I am a good father,” Max proudly declared. “You’re a great father,” Karina proudly declared. Their words didn’t collide between them and trample over one another, instead they embraced, amplifying one another and drawing a final, fond smile from their respective speakers. “That’s why you have to do what Alice says,” Karina decreed, a stoic mask hardening across her face to stem any outpouring of emotion. “And so do I.” Karina’s trembling fingers juggled a twenty, nearly knocking over the vase under which she tucked it. A sharp, clean cut then severed Karina from her brother as she stood faster than her aching body would have wanted. “I love you,” she whispered as her mask began to crack under the strain; physical and emotional. “I‘m sorry... for everything.” II - Then A primal, larynx-shredding roar erupted from deep within Frank Kilcullen. Overhead, the suffocating blanket of cloud fizzled with static, a spark igniting a cascade of thunder which threatened to rip the heavens open so that Frank could howl to God himself. The eroded foundations of a once proud man crumbled, and he collapsed to his knees. Teeth gnashed within the thick brush of a peppered beard as knuckles clobbered the concrete. Over and over, his fists drummed a guilt-ridden beat, stippling the cold pale grey with rich, raging crimson. “Gnah… gnah… gnah… gnah…” he grunted as a raw, fleshy mush consumed the tattoos that adorned his knuckles. “Billy,” he spluttered as the first tears he’d cried since his mother’s death began the perilous descent down his pitted cheeks. “Mah boy, mah boy…” A mighty oak of a man felled by a festering rot of hubris, Frank Kilcullen collapsed onto the blood-splattered pavement. His head slumped down against the concrete, then again, and again, until blood began to mat his dark, bristly eyebrows. Three hours ago, it was the blood of a wheezy, cowering wrestling promoter which had stained his forehead courtesy of a vicious headbutt. A contract for the green Billy Kilcullen was rewarded by fear, signed in blood. Three hours later, he was told his youngest son may never walk again. Once again, Frank vented his rage at the heavens, but could only muster a fragile wail. Streaming cobalt eyes stared at his mutilated fists, specks of ivory knuckle visible through the pulped flesh. The scars would brand a reminder of the blood that was on his hands, the same blood that ran through his own veins, the same blood that oozed down his forearms onto the concrete. The maddening cheers of the crowd still rang in Frank’s ears, even another fierce eruption of thunder above failing to exorcise them. Retreating into the memory, Frank could feel his shoulders buffeted. He tasted the stale, sweaty air. He felt the pride at seeing Billy bound down the aisle, green and white clover-leaf tights proudly sporting the Kilcullen name. He relived the frustration at every missed beat and sloppy execution. He saw his youngest son’s neck folded up underneath his limp body. He heard the hall’s collective gasp, and chewed at the vacuum which was left in its wake. He read the referee’s panicked expression. He felt that icy dagger plunge into his spine. He saw a nonchalant Karina Wolfenden sitting beside his lifeless son, and he relived every vile, vengeful urge which flooded his thoughts. III - Now The overbearing gravity of shame scuffed Max Wolfenden’s soles across the dusty sidewalk, his feet no more able to lift than his head. A piercing chill wafted down the sleepy street, an icy stab to his sunken heart. Listening out in vain for his sister’s voice on the wind, Max hunched inside his winter coat, plunging hands deep into pockets as his leaden feet dragged along the concrete. A pariah, the streets had cleared for his walk of shame, even the clouds condemning him to solitary, leaving behind an anaemic powder blue sky that was devoid of any undeserving warmth. “HELP!” A shrill shriek shredded the frigid, crystalline air. Almost bundling Max over, a young woman staggered barefoot from the alleyway, trembling hands clamping onto his arms. “You have to help me,” she stuttered, breathless and frantic. A queasy portrait of distress warped the woman’s youthful features. “She’s taken my baby… she’s taken my baby girl…” Her fingernails dug in through Max’s sleeves like grappling hooks, dragging him into the alley with the draw of a pleading Siren’s song. “Please,” accompanied a fiercer digging of nails. “She can’t of gotten far. Just one second. I only looked away for one second…” Max had read about those people who crossed the street, pretended their earphones drowned out the pleas, or wound their windows up and just kept driving along. He didn’t want to be one of those people, looked down upon and spat at by those who’d never had the choice thrust into their lap. As the woman’s nails detached from his arms, Max freely followed her slapping bare footsteps down the alley as the high rises either side cast a veil of shadow. Throwing her head around, the young woman began to back away from Max, retreating behind scarlet bangs. “I’m sorry.” A crowbar whistled through the freezing air, ploughing into the back of Max’s knee. Letting loose an agonised wail, Max collapsed, giving the damp, gritty leaf litter a tooth-chipping kiss. Grinding the enamel off his polished teeth, Max emitted a growling wheeze as clawing hands tried to hold his searing knee together. Covering her face, the young woman turned away as a pack descended on the fallen Wolfenden sibling, steel toe-caps unleashing a barrage of bone-splintering punt kicks to his torso. “Enough,” a rumbling Irish brogue commanded from the shadows, instantly sapping kinetic energy from any kicks still to impact off their target. As the brutal onslaught ceased, the pack retreated to leave a convulsing Max spluttering blood with every rib-torturing heave. Eyes screwed up, Max reached out trembling fingers, before sucking in a sharp wince as the broken arm which sported them raked jagged bone through flesh. An imposing, barrel-chested figure crouched down beside the shivering Max Wolfenden. As weathered fingers stroked a dense beard, the man twisted his wrist to catch a ray of scant light, illuminating a knuckle duster or rough scar tissue. “Oim gonna be leavin’ this with yeh,” Frank Kilcullen‘s husky words instructed. A tattered envelope containing words he’d written a long, long time ago was tucked into the cowering Max’s coat with eerier tenderness. “Wouldn’t be wantin’ it gettin’ lost in the post, now. Oi‘ve been waitin‘ a long while to send this.”
