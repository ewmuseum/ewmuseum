It Really Wasn’t That Pretty…

2 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-12-02 Out of the night that covers me, Black as the Pit from pole to pole… Hazelnut eyes looked up and down at the worn and tired figure that stood, sullen on her doorstep. It was midnight in California, but being California that meant that a warm humidity still hung within the chill. Even at the edge of November. It was the biggest reason why Monet Samuel would leave Alias standing on the doorstep... it wasn’t as if he’d be left out in the cold. “I thought you were dead…” She said, a sarcastic tone to her voice. She hadn’t seen this man in more then a year. Their daughter hadn't seen her dad in more then a year. The disquieted look in his eyes made her hang her head and sigh. When someone said something as glib as that, it was usually meant in jest, but when one said it to Alias… it seemed to almost have a Nostradamic quality. “So the story goes.” The Pulp Hero rubbed at the bridge of his nose, rubbing his rough hand through his hair sheepishly, he had been up for long enough to forget the number of hours which he had been up. Monet opened the door, ever wider, and let him in. I thank whatever gods may be For my unconquerable soul… “Coffee?” Monet asked, as she pushed the dark purple house coat back over her light brown shoulder. The months spent in California had brought out the French-Indonesian heritage within her. For a moment, she wondered if Alias was drunk as he stumbled against the living room couch. It would certainly be a worse idea letting him in, if he was. “Love to, but I can’t.” he growled. “I can wake Izzy.” Monet said, trying to find out why Alias was visiting. What would make him happy. Was it to see their daughter? “You shouldn’t. I didn’t come here to visit.” He himself was already feeling more then guilty for being here, in these circumstances. There is no way he wouldn’t have come to the home of the daughter he became responsible for and her mother, who he loved. Monet could only shake her head though, because really she felt like she should have seen this coming. “You never do…” In the fell clutch of circumstance I have not winced nor cried aloud. “I can’t.” Alias was quick to respond. She had been so kind in taking him in without much hassle. “That’s the thing. I wish life where that easy.” “You…” Under the bludgeonings of chance My head is bloody, but unbowed. “He’s back.” Alias was referring to Christopher Sheffield. The real Christopher Sheffield, whose life Alias can taken over as his own. Who had taken Alias and tortured him, and put in place a chessgame of sorts, with the Original Pulp Hero. “Who?” Monet said for a moment, it was midnight after all. “You know who. You know who exactly who I am, so you know who.” Alias repeated, his words heavy with worry, but strong as the face of the old salt was who had said them. Monet’s hands reactively drew to her face, shocked. The secrets that her and Alias shared were many. At the center of them all was Monet’s little girl Issabella. She had to stay safe. Beyond this place of wrath and tears Looms but the horror of the shade… “You’re Issabella’s dad. That monster might be...” Her voice trembled, and as it did Alias stood up from the couch and held his arm out to grab Monet by the shoulder and draw her into his chest. “Yes, he is her father. He’ll come for her, and he’ll come for you too,” was Alias’s none to reassuring reply. Though they both knew that what Alias said was the truth. Monet’s voice trembled once again, but this time there was a demand behind it. “So you better protect her, god damn it.” Alias held Monet tighter; his mind thinking of the safest place his two girls could stay. “Stay with your mother, stay with the Jacobs, just don’t stay here.” He said, nodding at each idea. It was times like these that Alias was happy that he and Vince Jacobs had buried the hatchet by almost burying each other. In the process, Monet had grown closer with Vince’s wife Victoria. There daughters where close in the age, and what they didn’t have in common was a bridge gapped by being army wives of a sort. A creak on the staircase behind Alias and Monet, caused Monet to push away from the Pulp Heroes grasp as she put on a false smile. Little Izzy stood at the mid-way of the staircase, peaking over the wooden banister. “Mommy… the monters under the bed where tickling my feet aga—“ Izzy sleepily rubbed at her eyes, before noting her father in the room with an excited gasp, “Bigby..? Bigby!” And with a certain youthful delight, that belied her midnight sleepiness, Izzy ran down the stairs and latched her arms around the waist of the Original Pulp Hero before he knew what hit him. “Hey baby girl.” Alias sighed, but still grinned happily. The twinge of guilt that had knoted inside him since he had arrived at the doorstep, untwisted with contentment. A crack from just outside the front door shattered his quiet loving solitude, however. Just as quickly as it had built up within Izzy’s arms. Someone had followed Alias to the house. And here he thought he had been so damn careful. And yet the menace of the years Finds, and shall find me, unafraid. The man in the dark outside of the Samuel household in southern California had spent Monday and Tuesday buying clothes and luggage, things that he could more easily burn when finished, and deciding what to do about his gun. Would he bring it with him, or would be bring a bludgeoning object... much like the one that had been used to kill his uncle? In traveling as far as he did, he simply couldn’t bring the gun he was used to using. Hell, in flying from New York to California he wasn't even able to bring nailclippers. So while in Los Angeles, he bought a tire-thumper instead. Aside from the funny name it seemed like a very useful tool. It was a hard, lacquered piece of wood with a lead pipe imbedded in the center that semi-truck drivers used to check the air of their large solid rubber tires. He wondered if he was going to enjoy beating a helpless woman and a little girl, no older then eight, to death. The man in the dark decided that he would. It was a shame that, by the time it took to make it to the doorway, accidently tripping on the fringe of the welcome mat in front of the door… that he broke his cover. Or would have, he thought, if he had anything to worry about in terms of the people inside this house. He decided to quickly enter the house, as quietly as possible. Just to make sure. The large angry figure that rushed towards him and slammed him over the fern hedge in the read rocks and into the front lawn, quickly changed the plans of his night from revenge to ones of survival. Monet and Izzy ran out after Alias, shocked looks on there faces. The both of them were shocked for different reasons. Izzy had never seen her dad fight another man, outside of a wrestling ring. Monet was quickly being made aware why Alias had been so still and quiet within the house… it seemed that he had been biding his energy in the off chance that he had to do something like this. Still, Alias’s speed surprised her, as he rolled to his feet and barked towards the two people that he loved so dearly. “Go. Now.” As Monet listened without a beat of hesitation, dragging Izzy away from this scene that would otherwise scar itself into her memory onto adult hood, Alias continued kicking away at the man on the ground. Alias watched as Monet slipped out of her driveway, and the knowledge of not knowing exactly where she was going comforted the Original Pulp Hero. The time which he had given himself to make sure his daughter and her mother had escaped, however, had given the man on the grass time to take a hold of the tire-thumper once again and crack Alias in the shin with it. With an angered groan, the Pulp Hero tumbled to the green grass in the now chilled California air. The man grabbed the back of Alias’s head as he fell to the ground in pain, and started to drive it into the brittle ground. The cracked dry dirt underneath the grass tore at the flesh on the face of the Original Pulp Hero. As his nose broke, the Pulp Hero tasted the copper of his own blood at the back of his throat and something feral within him arose. His family had just been threatened. His life was now being threatened. All by this man, who he didn’t know. Enough. Alias tore around with a hard elbow, clipping the man in the jaw and causing him to fall to the grass beside the Pulp Hero. But that wasn't enough. Alias immediately jumped on top of him. Then proceeded to bite the man’s left ear off. It was the action of doing so that snapped Alias out of his feral, angered state and he instinctively spit the man’s left ear out, to the side. Panting, he wondered what had caused him to do such a thing as bite another man’s ear off, and he looked down at the screaming, pain weary man that was writhing below him. He then spit on this man, because he still pissed him the fuck off. It was the mixture of the pain, the lack of sleep, the mental and physical stress of the last month. Alias looked at the man, and then looked at the man’s left ear, white and red within the green grass. It matters not how strait the gate, How charged with punishments the scroll… “Whooo sent you here?” “Fuuuck yo—“ “WHO SENT YOU HERE?!” The man whimpered for a moment, the blood dripped down his face in gurgling spurts. As the stars stirred above them, and neighborhood lights started flicking on as if they where those multitude of stars, Alias gripped his mitts ever harder against the man's throat. “Paulie Morello… Fat Paulie. I’m his people, cocksucker. You killed one of ours, we kill one of yours… and then we take that Black title from around your waist and get whatever it’s worth… Chris Sheffield.” The words where venomous coming out of the man’s mouth, though the reasoning behind what he was saying only made half sense to the Original Pulp Hero. Of course they were after the Black title… but who the hell was Paulie? It was the way that the man said Alias’s name, or at least what used to be Alias’s name, that made the Pulp Hero angry. With ever-bloodier knuckles, Alias rocketed a fist into the man’s face. He growled, once again releasing a fire within him that sounded like exploding tar and concrete. “You wanna know what the Black Title is worth?!” Crack. “Death.” Crack. “The Devil, and all his men.” Crack. Alias broke the nose of the man who was here for another man, named Fat Paulie. The blood trickled down the side of the man’s face and trickled into the space where he once had an ear. Alias grabbed the man by the collar and forcefully brought him up off the ground. “Nothin’ but the black… at the end of it all…” He breathed heavily, they both did. Then, with a blink, Alias shook his head. He released his hold on the man and let him drop back down to the grass. Maybe the neighbors had heard, maybe they hadn’t. They most likely did, let's just say. There was a chance that police would be coming, and when they did… Alias didn’t want to be where he was. Not at this moment. He would leave this man behind, and this man would probably collect his ear and do as Alias was about to do. Run. Alias growled down at the man. “Nothin’ but the end. The end… and the name ain't Chris... it's Jack. Whisky Jack.” I am the master of my fate; I am the captain of my soul. The Pulp Hero clutched his cellphone to his ear, as he made his way into the cool California night. Finally, the other end picked up. “Russel… it’s Alias. You better be sittin’ down with a pen, and listenin’ because I won’t be repeating myself.” “Okay…” “My name is Jackson Chase. Not Christopher Sheffield. I was born in Memphis, not Bartlett, Tennessee. January 1st, 1971.” “…” “Dig hard enough and you’ll find all the cracks in the story, all the directions you shouldn't be looking, get who you have to get involved in this, get everyone… do what you want with all of the information… then watch your head. Keep safe.” “Whatever this is about…” “Burn down my world if you must. Hell, you’ve got my blessing.” Invictus is a short poem written by William Ernest Henley.
