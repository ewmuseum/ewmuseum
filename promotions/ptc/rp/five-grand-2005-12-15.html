Five Grand

15 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-15 Saturday afternoon served to be chilly. The bite of winter nipped in the November breeze. This time next month it will mostly likely be snowing. After a hearty breakfast of biscuits, sausage gravy, fried eggs, and milk, Teresa kissed her aging mother good-bye, grabbed the grocery list and took off out the door taking a set of car keys with her. Parked in the driveway was four vehicles; Eric’s black Jeep Wrangler, Mama Tomas’s red Pontiac Grand Prix, and Teresa’s infamous midnight purple Chevy standing tall beside her vintage 1978 gray Monte Carlo. She had already decided the Monte Carlo would be today’s auto of choice. The beast had a 350 four-barrel engine and duel Flow Master Exhaust with a muffler that silenced the motor to the point that it sounded like a low growl. It was music to the ears of the classic automobile fan. Teresa pulled the handle and got in. The interior was a deep shade of red with wood trim on the dash and gear shift behind the steering wheel. She had spent most of the summer with Darrel fixing the old goat up. Now he runs almost as smooth as the day he rolled out of the factory. She loved her truck, but the Monte was her baby. With a couple pumps of the gas and the turn of the key, the engine turned and was purring like a tiger. Teresa smiled and headed out. She had a couple of hours before her meeting with Darrel; a couple of hours to come up with the five grand he owed this Miguel. Ordinarily, she would tell Darrel to fix his own problems but she knows he’d just get himself killed for being stupid. Teresa couldn’t live with that. Family was family, bad blood or not. An idea came to mind and at the next stop, she made a left toward downtown. Downtown served for the most part a tourist attraction. One would receive the feeling of nostalgia if he/she would drive though the narrow street. On both sides was what was left of the ‘mom and pop’ stores. What was once family owned and operated was bought out by the county for the sake of historic preservation. In other words ‘Mom and Pop’ no longer own these businesses. The half century remolded buildings was the only remnants of the town’s true history. Among these businesses was a pawn shop. The building itself was made with brown and sand colored field stone. A white sign hung over the brown door. In a rusty red colored letters read "Bob’s Pawn Shop". Teresa pulled into the gravel parking area in the back and parked the Monte Carlo. The traffic was growing due to the upcoming lunch hour. Not many tourists this time of year, so the traffic was bearable despite the two lanes and one traffic light. A bell on the door jingled as Teresa stepped inside. A tall lanky red headed fellow stood behind the glass display counter wiping it with a paper towel. Inside the case was a variety of second hand and pawned jewelry-mostly small diamond rings. Handguns of different makes and models were also inside this glass case. Hanging from the wooden walls were dozens of shot guns. In the widow a red Fender Strat guitar sat with a five hundred dollar price tag tight loosely on the neck. The room smelled of gun powder and Windex. "Teresa! Long time no see. What you been into girl?" The lanky red haired man dropped the paper towel into a small trash can giving Teresa his full attention. He appeared to be close to her age, maybe older. Teresa scanned the room and picked out a 12 gauge that hung on the wall. She aimed it at nothing in particular as she examined the gun. "Where have you been all these years Bob? In a hole? Turn on your television sometime. Listen, I’m not here for chit chat." Teresa replaced the 12 gauge back where it hung and walked to the door. She turned the Open sign around so that it would read "Closed", locked the door, and then approached the counter where Bob stood. He watched her with suspicion. "Whoa Tomas, I’m not into that stuff anymore. I got out about 2 yrs ago. Honest to God, I’m clean. So don’t even bother asking." A large semi passed through causing the windows to rattle inside. Teresa leaned over the counter and gave Bob a smile. Her baby blues eyes stared a hole through his own lying green eyes. "Bullshit Bob. I know you a bit too much. It was no secret the shit you were getting into in high school. Your lucky ass was just too smart to get caught. I’m not an idiot. It’s not like you to just up and leave easy money. This pawn shop is just a cover up." Bob’s suspicion rose. The expression on his face tightened. She and Bob went way back. They were good friends. In high school, he got caught in with some dealings she didn’t want any part of. He eventually went his own way digging deeper into the underground while Teresa dug her own hole by entering the wrestling business. Bob opened his mouth to speak, but Teresa interrupted. "I need your help. It’s a pretty huge favor but I can repay you in time." This was a long shot, Teresa knew. Who in their right mind would ask for five grand and actually expect to get it? Bob sat down on his stool and watched her as he thought as she explained the situation. "I hope Darrel realizes how much he owes you. I can loan you the money, but considering that’s a hell of a lot for a small town like this, I need collateral." Teresa raised her eyebrows feeling almost stupid. This WAS a pawn shop but what does she have that she can hock? Bob crossed his skinny arms over his chest and tilted his head toward the door. "I watched you drive by earlier in that Monte Carlo. Give me the keys and I’ll give you the money." "What?! My Monte? I can’t just hand you the keys. What am I supposed to drive? I have an appointment at noon and I can’t be late. C’mon Bob. Help out an old friend." Bob sighed and slid off his barstool. He paced the small space behind the glass counter for a moment while Teresa watched impatiently. "Listen, if you won’t help me, fine. I’ll just leave. But if those damn Mexicans kill my cousin, his blood is on your hands and when I get done with ya, you’ll wish you were dead." Teresa turned to leave. Her hand reached the door knob. "Teresa, wait. I’ll give you the money, but you have to do this one thing for me."
