The Focus

Date: 2004-09-26 It was a tomb. Train's home...and it wasn't normally like this. It hadn't been more than 7 months ago when Train had bought the place. An old, run down warehouse space on the outskirts of Dallas, the real estate guy didn't even know how long it had been empty. The same went for the for the vacant lots and vaguely similar buildings around it. It hadn't been much of a surprise though. It's not like people were chomping at the bit to get hold of it. Grass and wild flowers crept up the cracks in the concrete surrounding the building and snaked it's way through the cinder blocks, pipes, trash cans, tires and other remenants of a time gone by when it had been a place of industry. Industry didn't want them because the buildings weren't set up to sustain modern business. Individuals didnt' want them because the neighborhood creeped them out. On the other hand, Train loved them. They reminded him of home. From the time Train put the money down on it the place had been alive with some kind of activity. Hardly ever silent. For the first few weeks it had been the contractors that made most of the noise. They broke down walls and tore out wiring. Tore out rusted metal and layed down pipe not covered in decades of decay. Everything in the upstairs was gutted making way for the bed room and master bath. Most of the downstairs had been hauled out so that the space behind the sliding garage door could finally be used to park a vehicle again as opposed to old oil drums and dusty cardboard boxes. The metal cabinetry and lockers that had held God knows what was not an empty space, save for some of the best weight machines that money could buy. The various other piles of metal and garbage had given way to a modern kitchen that, besides the counter tops that glimmered with fuax marble, glimmered with the glow of brush stainless steel hung for the cabinet doors and the lined the outside of the refridgerator. But that was months ago. Now, aside from the time he was a sleep it was echoing with music from the stereo and he put on something agressive in tone to push himself in his workouts. Or it rumbled with bass as the explosions dislaying themselves visually in the plasma screen in the bedroom manifested themselves with wall shaking power in the subwoof of the surround sound system. On rare occassion the system pumped as people danced and partied after being invited back...as the party always followed a champion. But more likely than not, if there were guests making noise, it was likely the party's music was being drowned out by the moans and screams of some chick who would discover being hit by a Train isn't all that bad when it's in the right spot(s). That's the way it normally was. The entire space was silent. For a long while only the sound of hollowness hung in the air of the high ceilinged building. Finally the air conditioning kicked, breaking the monotony only slightly. It was still too quiet though. It was more than unusual. It was ominous.... dripping with tension and something else. Something that hung in the air... freezing like ice and burning like fire at the same time... Nothing was moving. Yet the place was bubbling with rage. Train sat on the edge of the work work out bench, his eyes locked onto the floor. He was silent and unwaivering. There was no sign of stress or danger, but his body was tensed and locked into place. The only thing that moved were his eyes. As his body leaned forward over the edge of the leather bench they glided over the ground in front of him. More accurately, they blazed a trail over th eline of papers that lined the floor in front of him. Each paper was a person. It wasn't exacly life histories, but professional ones. The remaining men in the GTT4 tournament all held a seat on the floor. Their pictures...arrogant smiles to cold scowls all stared up at him. His eyes slid over them, His response to them uniform. An ice cold stare that could break the laws of physics and manage to set them ablaze. Right at this moment they were the source of the tension in the room. Right at this moment the rage boiled over from Train and poured out onto them. It wasn't that they deserved it really. It's not as if they were the source of it. It simply they were the only ones he could focus on. Now there was no one else he could focus on. And that...that was the real source of it. That's where the rage came from. It turned his stomach. Everytime he thought about all he'd put in, it sickened him now. The blood and sweat... aces and pains... rips and tears... scratching and clawing to hold on to that Platinum Title with everything he had...so it would mean something... And for what? So they could just drop him? Terminate his contract... With hardly a word.... Not an ounce of respect.... Motherfuckers. The anger dripped from every poor as Train continued to study the men that stood in his way. There was only one goal now. There was no more representing for anyone else. No trying to win so others could notice. Now it was time to win so others would regret. And when he became the biggest thing in the business by winning this tournament that's all they'd have... Regret. The cold faces that stared back at Train were all just obstacles now. But that was ok. He'd smashed through too many of them in this life time to let that intimidate him. It was no secret in when he was in GW. The fans didnt' always like him, but damned if they didnt' get what they paid for. They paid so see somebody get their ass whupped. Train was always up to give the people what they wanted. The fact some other poor bastard had to be the performance piece? Tough fuckin' luck. Now...now that there was nothing else to do, but show the world what kind of mistake the bastards had made by letting him go? It was time for someone else to suffer for Train's art. Now the only audience Train gave a damn about was himself. And the one who had to suffer? The tournament organizers had made that an easy decision. Train filtered through the papers on the floor and found the one. Found his victim. Train leaned down and used his hand to brush away the excess paper and grabbed the one he needed to focus on. Slowly Train sat up straight and tall. His eyes locked onto the man's photo. Eddie "The Pitbull" Benoit. Nothing special about it really... Big, bald, goatee...a few tats to make him look tough. Yeah... scary. Train let his eyes glance over the printout, trying to take in all the info about his opponent. As if it was important....as if he gave a shit. Really there wasn't anything about him that distinguished him. Just another young kid playing a role, trying to make a name for himself. Too bad for him he hadn't managed the shit yet. Fact is there was nothing about this kid or anything he'd done that would make him jump out as a threat in the kid's bio. a few rinky dink feds here and there... decent win, loss... few nickel and dime titles... Not bad for a starter. Nothing outstanding on his record. Oh, but he did indeed stand out. Yeah... He had managed to accomplish a little something. He'd made it so Train knew his name. He'd made it so Train gave two shits about him. All by using what was obviously his biggest asset... A big, fucking mouth. The kid might not have done much in the ring, but he'd run his mouth about every place he could find a mic. He'd made the rounds, telling the world why Train should be afraid of him. How he'd derail him. Yeah...that had made quite the impression on Train. Hell, how could it not? First of all, it was so fucking original. The kid had been just so right when he assumed that was such a creative thing to say because surely no one else ever had done it before. But, in the grand scheme of things that was really nothing. It was more his nerve. The son of a bitch had a hell of a lot of it. Train sat and thought about the fact that every computer nerd/wrestling fan living in their grandma's basement was still out there laughing at him. This Benoit, he was a funny guy. He'd accomplished virtually nothing, but still managed to make Train the butt of the joke. His were out there, making the rounds to all the wrestlign websites. Hanging motionless in cyberspace to come along snatch them out of the air...to here how Train was going to get a reality check. Yeah... he'd run Train's name down pretty good. For a virtual unknown he'd succeeded and showing a hell of a lot of disrespect. Apparently the kid didn't do his research the way Train did his. Apparently he hadn't heard why Train lost his job. Or maybe he just didnt' understand the implications of it. McNasty opened his mouth and talked trash about Train. He wound up narrowly avoiding being crushed to death in the back of a garbage truck because of it. McNasty wanted to play a childish prank and disrespect Train by spraying shit allover his $70,000 Lincol Navigator. That was the begining of the end of it there...that's why Train was where he was now. Left with no real alternative, but to focus on the tournament for redemtion. Everyone knew he had to even the score. He'd been disrespected. It was just part of the game. What happened after that...it wasn't really his fault when you think about it. The son of a bitch had asked for it. He knew something was coming to him. It's not Train's fault he wasn't ready for it. It wasnt' his fault the staff wasn't ready for it. It wasn't his fault the lawyers had to run around like chickens with their heads cut off, trying to protect the company from any liablity after it was done. Train thought about it for a moment... "You snatch up one dude's bitch by the throat, make him get in the trunk of a car and launch that muthafucka off the top of a parking garage and suddenly you're a bad guy." Train said aloud, allowing the only smile he'd attempted in days to work it's way across his face. It didn't long for the smile to creep back into the darkness from whence it came. Train was back to staring...his glare cutting into picture as if he might be able to spot his soul in the flat, poorly copied photograph. He thought about Mr. Benoit for a moment. He'd told the world this match was going to be a reality check for Train. Disrespectful prick... Train clenched his fist tightly. Eddie crumbled into a ball within it. The Pitbull had it right. Half of it anyway.
