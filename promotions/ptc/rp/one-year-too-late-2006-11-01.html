One Year Too Late

1 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-01 Generally, for a professional wrestler, waking up in a hospital bed is a bad thing. After all, it usually means you lost. Sure, on occasion you’re in a knock-down drag out brawl where both guys need medical attention, but that’s not a regular thing. So when Seymour Almasy awoke in a Singapore hospital, he was pretty sure that he was minus the ten pounds of gold that had heralded his resurgence. Gone. Just like that, in one match, as quickly as it had come. "Caldera got me," Seymour managed, weakly, trying to sit up and groaning at the pain in his ribcage. Having a 500 pound man sit on you tended to leave bruises. Thankfully, though, the rib pain seemed to be the extent of his injuries. There were no casts on his person, no medical staff hovering over him waiting for him to wake up. He was alone, and now, it was all over. "So that’s that," he whispered to himself. Out in the first round for only the second time in his tournament career. To his surprise, though, there wasn’t a whole lot of anger floating through his mind. Instead, what he felt was relief. "The weight’s all on Kimbusa now," the Final Fantasy mused. Kurokame now could bear the mantle of PTC Global Champion. Now Kimbusa would have to deal with the pressure of being the favorite, the constant media presence. Seymour would be left alone. In spite of the crushing defeat he had suffered, Seymour Almasy smiled. His quest was now, for all intents and purposes, over. His story had ended. Undoubtedly, he had done enough to cement himself as one of the best. Perhaps not THE best, but Seymour could deal with that. He heard noise at the door, and in came a doctor. Seymour had dealt with doctors for years now, and a quick reading of the medic’s face told Almasy that good news was coming. "You’ve got bruised ribs, Mr. Almasy," was the doctor’s diagnosis. "We’re going to keep you overnight for observation, just in case, but you’re free to go in the morning." That, really, was all the doctor had to say, but he lingered, eyes focused on Seymour. "Go ahead," Seymour said, good-naturedly. "Ask me whatever you want." "How does it feel," came the question. "You’ve been doing this for what, four-five years now? How does it feel for it all to be over?" "Well, it’s not ENTIRELY over," Almasy replied. "I still have a few dates left to finish up. But to answer your question…it feels a lot better than I thought it would. A year ago, I’d be enraged right now…but now, I’m fresh out of regrets. I lost to the better man tonight. Kimbusa beat me in the middle of the ring. Mostly, though, I feel free. I can do whatever I want now. When I leave here tomorrow morning, I can do the things I’ve always wanted to do." The physician nodded. Seymour thought he detected a hint of jealousy in the man’s eyes. If it was jealousy, he could understand. In a twisted way, Steven Caldera’s quest for revenge had freed Seymour from the Hell that had become his career. Angelo Deville and Rich Rollins were still holding on. Jason Snow had recently returned. The more Seymour thought about it, the more he wondered why they bothered. Deville and Rollins had fought through incredible personal tragedy. Their bodies had to be at least as battered as Seymour’s, if not more so. And Jason Snow! He’d been gone for three years, and yet he still decided to return. Seymour might not have been able to compare to the greatness of those names in the minds of many fans, but he had one thing on all three: he knew when to walk away. Sure, he hadn’t walked away at the height of his fame, like the best truly did, but he could see himself starting to go downhill. He could see the path that awaited him if he continued. So many wrestlers ended up taking painkillers like Smarties to keep going…and few of them wrestled the sort of breakneck schedule that Almasy had. For years, doctors had been telling him his pace would lead to confinement to a wheelchair, and he’d never believed it until a few months ago, when every move he did began to hurt like Hell. Years of wrestling all out on two or three TV shows a week, two or three PPVs a month had pushed the Final Fantasy to the breaking point. But now, he could heal. Now, he could take the time off to have an ordinary life. Just Triangles and the UWF farewell show to go, and he could bid PTC farewell. Seymour slowly leaned up to wave goodbye to the doctor, but the physician had left long ago. Lost in thought, he hadn’t heard the medical professional’s final words of "goodbye, and good luck". Letting his head hit the inadequate pillow once more, Seymour pulled the threadbare covers around his battered form, prepared for what he hoped would be his last night in a hospital bed. Tonight had marked the near-end of Seymour Almasy. Tomorrow would see the rebirth of Jason Seymour Wilson. The next day Suffice it to say that the first thing on Jason’s ‘to-do’ list after checking out of the hospital was hopping a flight back to the United States. After all, he’d recently bagged an advance, complete English copy of Final Fantasy XII, and a new Final Fantasy game meant one thing for the fanboy: locking himself in a hotel room and not leaving until it was finished. Excessive detail here would be unfair. Seymour wouldn’t wish a detailed account of his nerdery on anyone, not even Rich Rollins. Suffice it to day, however, that much room service was eaten, much Mountain Dew was drank, and many, many evil creatures found themselves slain. Roughly 100 hours later, with the game laying at his feet, defeated, Seymour realized that he now had to figure out what ELSE he wanted to do. Oddly, it was easier than he thought it would be. The first step was merely to put the controller down. Six days later… He looked out the window one last time, taking in the awe-inspiring sight of New York City after dark. Despite living in NYC much of his life, the sight never ceased to amaze him. "I’ve done it all," he said, wonder in his voice. "Everything I wanted to do. I’ve seen sights the world over. Accomplished everything on my list. All save one, of course, but that’s tomorrow’s project." He felt silly for never really trying to live before. Sure, his Final Fantasy games were fun, but the real world was an experience unto itself. Seymour had gone to Las Vegas again. Usually when he went, he was saddened. After all, he and Laura had been married there. This time, though, the memories made him smile, and he played poker with a nice man named Daniel who was far, far better than Seymour was, but that was okay. "Still beat him that one hand," Seymour said, with a wicked grin. Had AA to his KK. I’ll remember that for the rest of my life." Really, his trip was full of those moments. Snorkeling in the Pacific Ocean was far more awe-inspiring than he could have imagined. Raw beauty wasn’t something he had much time for ordinarily, but the clear blue water made it so easy to see all manner of aquatic creatures. As nice as computer-generated graphics were, they couldn’t touch what mother nature could produce. Seymour had returned to Japan once again, as well. This time, though, he kept away from the anime-themed brothels and simply enjoyed the traditions of the country. He took part in a traditional tea ceremony. He went to Mt. Fuji, and was suitably impressed. Did Mount Gagazet compare? He couldn’t quite tell, save that both were important, revered mountains. In truth, he felt sort of silly for never really looking around at the world before. His world had always been the games and his immediate surroundings. Never had he really tried to explore. Ironic, really, that a man who claimed to be perpetually questing never went on a real quest. A week or so didn’t make up for a lifetime of neglect, but at least now he knew what he’d been missing. The pajama-clad Final Fantasy ambled over to his king sized bed, plopping himself unceremoniously amidst the bedding. After all, so much adventuring tended to make one sleepy. It didn’t take long for him to tangle himself properly, letting his head hit the pillow. Inwardly, he smiled. This was a far better pillow than the one he’d had in the hospital in Singapore, yet so much else was the same. He felt even freer now, liberated from responsibility. Just one more thing to do tomorrow, and he could move on. That night, he dreamt again. He dreamt of the glories that he had achieved. He dreamt of the PTC Global Title. He dreamt of the great battles waged over the course of his career. And most of all, he dreamt of Laura Winters, the woman he loved. As always, she was taken from him far too soon. Tonight’s dream, though, was different. Tonight, when she replied "soon" to his query of when they could be together, the subconscious Almasy nodded. --------------------------------------------- Considering all he had done, camping might have seemed to be a silly way to end a list of incredible things to do, but the smile on Seymour Almasy’s face certainly seemed to lend credence to the activity ending his quest for fun. Upstate New York was filled with many forests and lakes, and Seymour had found his way to one of them. A rented car sat a few feet away from the Final Fantasy’s tent. Night had fallen a few hours ago, and Almasy sat by a roaring fire, roasting a marshmallow on the end of a well-chosen stick. The car’s sound system, as per usual, was playing one of Seymour’s mixes of RPG tunes. He smiled wryly as a new song came on, "Otherworld" by Nobuo Uematsu. It was the song that he’d made famous in PTC, his theme music for most of his career. Usually, it marked his arrival, but tonight, it marked a departure of sorts. "It’s really over," Seymour whispered, nodding his head. His career was all but done now. Pulling his stick back before the marshmallow got too charred, Seymour popped the gooey treat into his mouth, savoring it as if it was the last marshmallow he would ever have. Seymour hummed along with the song, simply watching the fire burn. He wondered just how long it could burn if left to its own devices. Into his pocket he went, as Otherworld reached its chorus, a portion of the song he’d heard a thousand times. From his wallet he fished out his cell phone. He flipped it open, and pushed the series of buttons to check his voicemail. Jamming the phone to his ear, he listened to the sole message that he had: one from Primetime Central, offering him a playin spot against Killean Sirrajin in Round Two of GTT6. Almasy all but laughed as he slammed the phone shut. "Looks like Killean’s got himself a bye to the third round," he said, dropping the cell phone to the leafy forest floor. Perhaps someone would find it, someday. Into his other pocket he went, pulling out his wallet. There were no credit cards inside it. No money. No identification. A mere two objects resided inside, and one happened to be a plane ticket to Los Angeles, California. He turned the piece of paper over in his hands several times, before tossing it forward, into the flames. "Sorry, Pierre." Seymour Almasy was not going to AWC’s Triangles. Pulling the other object free from its place of honor, Seymour gazed at the picture of his wife. The wallet-sized photograph of her on their wedding day was slightly frayed, but the image remained as lovely as it had always been. Solemnly, Seymour placed the photo back in his wallet, and lay it on the ground, next to his phone. "Otherworld"s final few bars expired, and the song was replaced by the mix CD’s final tune, a haunting melody known to all who have experienced Final Fantasy VII. To Seymour, "Aerith’s Theme" meant but one thing. "It’s time…" Almost ritualistically, Seymour began a long, slow walk to the water’s edge. With a deep breath, he entered the body of water, wading through the abnormally clear water until he was almost up to his neck. He turned to face the middle of the lake, eyes cast downward to the sandy bottom. There, covered with a thin layer of sediment, lay Seymour Almasy’s greatest sin. At Treelawn Cemetery on Long Island, there indeed was a grave for Laura Marie Winters. Were anyone to exhume that gravesite, though, all they would find would be an empty coffin. For, like the woman whose name she took for much of her career at Seymour’s side, Laura lay here, at the bottom of this secluded lake, placed here by the grieving man she loved. "Hello, my love," he whispered, reverently. Even through the sediment, he could see patches of the pure white of the funeral shroud he laid Laura to rest in. He hoped no one ever found out. If Laura’s parents ever realized that their daughter’s body wasn’t where they went every week to pay respects, they would be crushed. At the same time, if they ever did find out, he hoped that they would understand. Laura was too important to him to leave in the ground for all eternity. Instead, he buried her here, beneath twenty feet of water in a lake so secluded most didn’t know it existed. That was why he had chosen it for her. That was why he had chosen it for himself. "I told you I’d see you soon," Seymour said, mouth curved in a strange, knowing smile. "I’ve done everything I’ve wanted to. And now, I can do what I want to do more than anything else in the world." The haunting, sad melody still reached him, even halfway into the lake. Reaching into his pocket for the final time, Almasy pulled out the final contents. Somehow, anything he could say now seemed hollow. There was nothing profound, no words that could really sum it all up. And yet, in his head, he heard the dying words of the Sorceress Ultimecia. "Reflect on your… Childhood…" He had been a sad, frail boy, teased by all of his classmates, and left alone by his parents. For him to become what he had was nearly unthinkable. Somehow, he had risen to become a champion. A warrior. A hero. No, not somehow. He knew exactly how. He knew exactly why. "Your sensation… Your words… Your emotions…" It was her love for him that had inspired him to greatness. Her kind words. Afternoons spent chatting with her throughout high school. Time together that was entirely platonic until the UWF had made them share a hotel room to share costs, and they had given into the desires that they had secretly harbored for over a decade. "Time… It will not wait…" Time together that, ultimately, proved far too short. "No matter… …how hard you hold on. It escapes you…" He could still remember coming here for the first time, with his lifeless wife lying in the back seat of his car. It was the worst day of his life, a day that nothing could have adequately prepared him for. Letting her slip from his hands into the cold lake was the most difficult thing he had ever done. Now, though, it would be okay. Because Seymour knew that he was mere moments away from joining her. "And…" Even after taking a quick dip in the lake, the barrel of the gun in Seymour’s hand was still caked with dried blood. His wife’s dried blood. Without a moment more of hesitation, Seymour opened his mouth, and shoved the barrel inside. The coppery taste of the blood assaulted his senses, made worse by the lake water that seemed to slowly be loosening it. His face contorted in disgust, as he breathed in through his nose, taking in oxygen for the final time. Closing his eyes, he smiled around the barrel. Now, he knew what his final words would be. "I love you, Laura." With his life thus lived, he pulled the trigger.
