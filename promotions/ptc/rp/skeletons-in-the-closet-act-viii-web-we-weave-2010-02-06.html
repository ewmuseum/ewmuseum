Date: 2010-02-06 Lost Like her mother, Quinn Gregory was a very particular person. One Saturday afternoon, the nearly sixteen-year-old girl carefully cut off the crust of her turkey and cheddar sandwich, speaking over her shoulder. "You really don't have to do anything." "What do you mean I don't 'have' to do anything?" It was nearly two in the afternoon, which meant Amy Campbell was just starting her second bowl of Froot Loops. The tiny redhead moved like clockwork – albeit one that ran slow by several hours. "A girl only turns sixteen once, Q. Come on... you've got to want something." The teenager spun on her heel. "I don't know," she whined. "Mom already got me the best—I guess I'd like to do something with Brandon. You know, maybe hang out. Someplace cool." "Cool, huh?" Campbell's grin was wide and curling further. "I can do cool." If the teen had seen that smirk, she would have been (rightly) worried. That was two weeks ago. Three days later, they'd surprised the girl's mother with her own office. Tonight wasn't about Alexandra Pierce, though – tonight was Quinn Gregory's Sweet Sixteen party, and the girl had never been so nervous, sitting on the edge of her bed. It wasn't that Amy wouldn't tell them what she had planned. It wasn't that her mother wasn't there, stuck at the office sorting out the legal paperwork necessary to get her private investigator's license. It wasn't that she was as gussied up as a Pierce got. No, what was weighing on Quinn's mind was that afternoon in the airport, the girl she'd pretended to be, the barista trying to get her dad to buy her a car. Gregory glanced into the mirror, summoning a smile, bright and cheery. "Thank you for coming to Starbucks," she murmured, pausing to shake her head. "Should've done an accent." The doorbell rang, a soft bong through the apartment. The sound shattered Quinn's façade, the normal, pensive girl reemerging from behind the cheery exterior. "Quiiiinn!" Amy singsonged from the room down the hall. "It's your daaate." The girl couldn't help but smile as she popped to her feet, pacing quickly to the front door to the condo. She glanced quickly to the reflective brass cover to the hall clock, making sure her tar-black hair was in place as she pulled the door open. "Hi," she smiled, a small little, nervous thing as she adjusted her glasses. Not nearly the smile of Erin the Barista, but she didn't care. Brandon Jacobs was a lanky kid, his brown hair not so much styled as it was combed back, trying desperately not to ogle his de facto girlfriend – at least until he was sure her mother (either of them, really) wasn't around to bloody his lip for it. "Hey," Brandon said. "You look... nice." "Thanks." Quinn blushed, smoothing down the front of her dragon t-shirt, but, in truth, it was the skirt (an honest to goodness skirt, dark blue and speckled with a thousand-thousand tiny, multicolored dots) that made her nervous. "Uh. Come in, I guess. Amy's just finishing getting ready, and I think we're going to stop by the office and get mom." Jacobs stepped into room, awkwardly reaching for... something. It was a handshake, then Quinn raised both hands and it became a hug, then both of them stopped and it became sort of a handshake and a pat on the back at the same time. Neither of them heard the master bedroom open, and neither saw the tiny redhead slip out. "Yeeeeah, no touching." Campbell smirked. In a white v-neck t-shirt and skinny jeans, her tattoos were on full display – but they still couldn't outshine that smile. "It might be your birthday, but it's not his. And are you two ready?" We just going to pick up Mom on the way, then?" "Wait, you said that was what we—" Brandon began, but a quick squeeze of his hand quieted his question. Amy snatched her BlackBerry from the kitchen table, touching the screen to bring it to life. Nothing. "You haven't heard from her, have you?" "No," Quinn said. "I know the state is giving her a hassle about becoming licensed. Maybe she's just caught up?" "I dunno... she would've called." Campbell stared at the screen for almost a solid minute. "Tonight is the closest brush she's had with your – our – world, and I doubt she will take it well." "I know she won't, Malcolm," Alex whispered sadly. She tried to muster a smile when the headlights hit her. "I'll be there for her, no matter what. Always." "You had best be." As Pierce was loaded into the backseat of Amy's Cadillac, Girard receded into the shadows. Quinn and Amy (mostly the teenager) wouldn't shut up on the ride home, but Alexandra Pierce didn't mind – the words chased away the darkness, if only for a little while. Epilogue: If Only... Later that evening (or, really, the next morning), Amy Campbell and Alexandra Pierce were intertwined in bed, as they had been many, many times before. There had been no torrid lovemaking on this evening, no screams of passion raised to the heavens (the types of which made Alex soundproof her bedroom). On this night, the two strong-willed redheads were lost, not in each other, but in themselves. It'd been this way since they came home. Once Malcolm left (off to "find someplace to have a chat once our man is back on his feet," he'd said), Elijah took over, assuring them both that he would give them the solace they needed. Their presence faded into the background, as unobtrusive as they could manage. Only Donnie's brief visit – to return Alex's shoulderbag, found at the scene – disturbed them, and only long enough for him to be shooed away. It was nearly four in the morning before Quinn finally fell asleep, passed out with her head on her mother's shoulder, and nearly five before there was nothing left on the television except the morning news and they retired to bed. They lied there in silence twenty more minutes, the morning sun leaking through the blinds, Pierce propped up by a stack of pillows, Campbell on her stomach, head on the other woman's shoulder. They should be peaceful – they should have been happy, but neither was. "Amy, I—" Alex began, but she was quieted by a small movement from the smaller woman, Campbell's fingers skidding along Alexandra's hip. "I got shot at, Alex. That's enough crazy for one night. I want to sleep, not talk." "Okay." Alex turned, pressing her lips to the crown of Campbell's head. "I understand. Get some rest." Five more agonizing minutes passed, and both were still awake, two sharp minds rocketing through images they both wished would go away. "Are you asleep?" Amy asked finally, miserably. "No. You?" Campbell's smile stretched, lips barely brushing Pierce's arm as they spread. "Because I'm generally coherent when I talk in my sleep?" "Ah... silly question, hrm?" "Little bit." Amy opened one eye – the right one, a brilliant blue sapphire. "What... what was that? What did they do to you?" Alex shifted, turning her face away slightly. "I'm... not ready to talk about it yet." The smaller redhead nodded gently, rolling onto her side. "Okay. Then we sleep?" "Then we sleep," Pierce agreed, curling her arm over Amy's shoulders. "We always have tomorrow." "Tomorrow." It was ten minutes before Amy Campbell's exhaustion claimed her for a fitful slumber, and another five passed before Alexandra Pierce joined her. Neither knew it, but both drifted off with the same thought: "If only." "If only Amy could understand what they did to me – what I really am." "If only Alex could live a normal life – even just for one day." If only they actually had tomorrow.
