Date: 2004-10-09 Faith... Faith is a funny word. You can have faith in many things. Faith: 1. Confident belief in the truth, value, or trustworthiness of a person, idea, or thing. 2. Belief that does not rest on logical proof or material evidence. See Synonyms at belief. See Synonyms at trust. 3. Loyalty to a person or thing; allegiance: keeping faith with one's supporters. 4. often Faith Christianity. The theological virtue defined as secure belief in God and a trusting acceptance of God's will. 5. The body of dogma of a religion. 6. A set of principles or beliefs. -- Dictionary.com definition Some people are slaves to their faith. Some rely upon it for everything. Some are those that attmept to spread it. Some are those that feed upon it. Which is “The Prophet” John Adams? Who is he without that “Prophet” moniker? What is he withing being a man who is a soldier for 'god'? Who is he really? Is he a prophet or just someone who's faith has made him delusional? And why the hell would a prophet come out to a perfectly good Metallica remake of an old DiamondHead Song about taking revenge for Christians burning a boys mother alive for being a witch? Imagine if he knew just what it was that was under the skin of his opponent this coming round in the GTT4. Blaise William Todd McClancy on the outside appears just as human as you and me. In fact for the first 24 years of his life, that's what he knew and believed in. Even with his father constantly going on about the 'wee folk', 'Tautha de Dannan' or the 'Seelie and Unseelie courts of Tir Na Nog'... he'd always mark it off as fairy tales. And then there was his grandfather to deal with. The old man that always went off about 'spirits' and 'skinwalkers', the 'manitou' and other beings. How could you accept something that you couldn't see? But then when Blaise had his crisis of faith just a little over a year ago... he suddenly awoke a part of himself that allowed him to see what the others couldn't. He had pierced the veil and had seen his father for what he really was. Let alone the others in his family who hide behind a facade for the rest of the world to see. He couldn't deny it anymore. He wouldn't either. Like taking the blinders off of a horse, and letting it see everything, the shadows that had always been creeping in the very edge of his vision became fully formed. He saw the pooka, bodaks, piskies, and others abound. A normal person would have cracked, but something withhin Blaise tugged at him and told him this was where he belonged. Inside the Veil, walking among the humans but able to see everything in the world for what it truly was. Some would call him insane. But until you've actually seen a real troll up close and personal, you've not truly seen anything. But we come to the now... Blaise has accepted his mantle. The term used by the Courts to denote that someone of their kind has found his true self in the constant cycle of rebirth that their kind uses to stay young and vibrant in the world. He's accepted his place in their scheme of things. He knows where his heart, mind and loyalties lay. Only one thing was missing, his spirit… if he had one. ~~~~~ Valley of Fire National Monument Moapa Paiute Indian Reservation 30 miles north of Las Vegas, Nevada They’d been driving for about 20 minutes to the back side of the reservation, over near where it ran into the northern part of Lake Mead. Near the site of the old jet feul processing plant that blew up a couple of decades ago and dropped tons of JP-5 and 10 into the drinking water for about 75% of the population of the entire South Western United States. The Moapa were still trying to clean the stuff up when it came onto their beaches, while the people in the actual cities of Las Vegas, North Las Vegas, Hendrson and Boulder City itself injested the crap on a regular basis, supposedly after it’d been filtered through the treatment plant. Whatever… Blaise had basically tried to ignore his grandfather for the most part. His inane conversation usually bored him or infuriated him. But the man had always been there while he was growing up. He’d been the one who accompanied him to Japan when he went to the South Wind Academy at the age of 16 to learn the martial arts and how to wrestle. He’d been there during the days when his father would fly off into a rage and head back to Ireland to fight for his rights to do what he wanted to do. And he’d been there when Blaise had come home after being gone for so long. But why he was going along for this, Blaise didn’t know. But he’d soon find out. “So, that little woman of yours, Ukyo, she ever trained as a geisha? You know when I was going back and forth with Japan, I had this one Geisha woman that…” “Geishas would never go with a gaijin, Billy. You know that. Don’t bullshit me. And no, not that I know of.” “But is she good in the sack?” Blaise looked at the older man at that. He watched as the old coot spit a load of tobacco juice into an old coke can he’d found in the trash bag behind the seats. “I could just kick you out of my truck, you do know that?” Grandpa Billy just smiled with his yellow and black stained teeth and then pointed over towards an area right off the road. “Right here, boy. We’ll have about a hundred or so yards to walk. Did you bring your little trinket?” “It’s in my laptop’s bag. I left that in the back.” Blaise pulled the truck to a stop. The engine went quiet quickly and both doors opened in unison. Blaise hopped out of his seat and slammed his door while his grandfather made his way down to his feet. As the older man started to walk towards a patch some sort of flowers and trees that were actually growing out in the wates of the desert, Blaise headed to the back of the vehicle and opened the back gate. He quickly retrieved a wrapped bundle from his one bag and then let the door slam shut. He ran to catch up with the spry old man. “You ready for this, boy?” “For a few months now.” They continued walking towards what could only be called an orchard or large garden. It looked like it had beeen a community project at one time. An irrigation system looked to be bringing water straight up from the lake to keep everything green. “You do know why your father was asking those questions of Ms. Kushinaga, right?” “Yeah, he seems to think I’d be just like my cousin.” “Exactly. And you aren’t?” Blasie stopped. His grandfather heard his foot steps stop and then turned to face him. Blaise could barely hold his composure on that statement. “No, I’m not. As I had to explain to Chris West last week before I kicked his ass in the ring in a WRESTLING match. I’m not my cousin. I’m not into this gimmick shit. That’s why I leave everything that happens outside of the ring on the friggin outside. When I’m in there, I’m the same person every time. I’m just the man who will beat you and get it taken care of. Sean is just a spot machine anymore. His whole ‘Hardcore’ schtick has become a crutch.” “Not what I was talking about.” His grandfather had used a stern tone on him. “You know what you and your father’s kind do. I know what you can do. I like your father, but in the back of my mind, even with all he gave up, there will always be the throught that he basically forced your mother into loving him. Even though she says that she willingly infected herself with the lust of the Dryad, just as Ukyo did… it’s still there. Your cousin…” “My cousin is now going to get married to a woman who chased him across two continents before she got infected, and has apparently infected him right back. But yes, I know that before that he’s left tens… hundreds… maybe even thousands of women pining for the firehaired Irishman who one night rocked their world and made them feel things they never thought they would. He’s ruined marriages and such. But I’m not him in that aspect either. I had been open to Ukyo about what I was. I’m a dryad, a nature spirit. But one that when he has dealings with a mortal, they want no other after that.” His grandfather smiled. “Come on. Let’s get this done so you can get back to your parents house and knock the bottom out of that little geisha of yours.” “Yeah, thanks a lot. You know if she ever heard you say that, she’d probably forget you were my grandfather. She already broke the nose of Becca’s current meal ticket when they came to Japan to try to get me to sign over full custody of Breanna and Kaylie.” William Slyfox chuckled. “Good for her. I hate that man. And that woman.” “Hate’s a strong word, Grandfather.” “But it’s a true word for what I feel when I think about the betrayal that my family faces with that woman and the prick who she’s with. They’re my great granddaughters that she’s trying to keep from me. Now, tell me as we go to the Orchard, what do you know about Dryads? You’ve hopefullly studied up on that half of your heritage?” Blasie moved to catch up. He almost dropped the bundle and staggerd a bit as he made sure he had it within his grip before he started. “Well, Dryad is a greek word actually. But it’s the most fittng. Basically a creature that’s connected to the forest of its native land. In the case of us of the Tautha de Dannan, the land of Ireland, or Tir Na Nog. Usually connected to an oak tree, they’re also creatures of lust and to the Tautha the only ones that are considered of royal blood because of their connection to the forest and the great trees there.” “Yes, you’ve done well. Continue on, boy.” His grandfather openly spit out a large wad of tobacco juice onto the rocky ground. “Well, basically the females are called Hamadryads. The males are just called Dryads, although a running joke in the courts from what I hear is that the Treants or Ents were actually male Dryads protecting the forests from the early settlers in the Tir.” “Bah, they were just protecting all the really good poon…” “Ahem…” “Sorry, well you’ve done your work. You’re a dryad. An immortal? Maybe so. I don’t fully know yet how it fully works. I know youre father is basically a self exiled King to them. Your cousin is not only Dryad but half Fomorian, a race of giants. You, Your father and your female cousin, Fey, are the only ones who are considered fully to be of the Tautha de Dannan. But you are more then that, boy.” “How so?” Blaise was getting inquisitive now. “You’re an anagram… a remix in a new age. Kinda like a few of your generations bands remaking the songs of my age. Your father is Irish, a potential king of an underground kingdome that the rest of the world doesn’t know about. But he ran away, renounced his position and married a woman from the new world. And he’s the first McClancy in 12 generations to not do what even you have done. You didn’t produce a set of twins. You’re a carrier of something that you need to know about… you’re the carrier of a destiny to their kind.” “Like what?” “I don’t know. I’m not exactly welcome in the dealings with the Courts. But I know that for about 4 months when it was announced that you were back with the Order and you had gone to that school in Texas, the 4 Winds Academy to finish your training, we had visitors every few days. Most of them were pointy eared, or long nosed, and spoke with heavy accents from all over the old world. You’ve got them talking. Between the political moves Sean… Bres… now begins to make, brining the Leanan Princess and the Greek goddess into the playing field, and the move Maeb made by having the children of the leader of the Order, they know that a power play is about to be made. And they know that you’re going to play some role. I mean, Ian’s even trained you as an assassin now, right?” No one was supposed to know. “How’d?” “Don’t worry about it. I know more about your escapades then you probably do. I’m not a practicioner of the arts for nothing.” “Since when did you ever become some wise man, or shaman?” “I’m not. I’d never be considered holy enough for that. But come now… tell me before we finally get there, how does it feel?” “Feel for what?” “To be truly not worried about your soul?” “I don’t get it?” “You don’t yet. I’ll let you answer that later on. I hear you’ve got issues with that DeAngelo guy still?” “Yeah, I’ve got them all around. Between DeAngelo claiming to be the Modern Messiah to whatever religion, being sent off to face some DeAngelo wannabe who claims to be a modern prophet in the GTT, I just don’t get some things. Bad enough that after I take care of this Adams guy from LOW, I could very well be facing Tarrant, a former ‘god of destruction’ the round after that.” “Ah, yes. The world is just gone all wacko in the last few decades. Even more and more of your kind crawl out from their Kaerns. The Skinwalkers rove the deserts out here. The old gods are trying to grab a small hand hold on things. Let’s not forget that the god whose supposed to be in charge of all of this appears to have just gone absent. I’m beginning to wonder of the Order’s mission won’t be soon to come to fruitation.” Blaise had no answer for that. He knows what the Order was about. While it was run by an excommunicated clergy student now, his old teacher Ian Childress, it’s whole purpose was to actually try to help the apocalypse come along. It was there to ensure that soon, judgement day would come. His grandfather was right. Wrestling itself was getting weirder all the time. John Adams, his next opponent in the toruney thought of himself as a Prophet. In the good old days, he would have been put to death for such. Derek “Dark Angel” DeAngelo thought of himself as a modern Messiah. Even though he was one of the most vile beings around. In fact there were so many others. Things weren’t right. You had to be ready for almost anything. Maybe Ukyo’s brother, Kyo, was right, pretty soon tentacles and shit would just pop out all over the place as the evils of the Anime world suddenly started to try to take over also. Who knows. That’s the big issue here, being ready for the big unknown. Blaise knew that this week’s big unknown was a man who was from a fed he’d had a dark match in, once upon a time ago. John Adams was an enigma as far as Blaise was concerned. His actions were one thing, but his style was another. Typical wrestling hoss. He’d get by him and then face a man that a few months ago, when he was playing ‘the game’ between a wannabe god and an angel who had fallen to the ground, he hadn’t been allowed. Then again, Tarrant was that wannabe god. Adams was an enigma, but one that didn’t raise the hairs on Blaise’s neck. “Here you are!” Blaise came out of his trance, to see his grandfather standing in front of a fully grown Joshua Tree. But it wasn’t like the others around it. The other Yuccas, as they were properly called, looked like they were only half grown. This one looked to be fully gornw, and right int eh middle of it. It stood a good 15 feet high, and a foot around. A good sized one. Although it was still young. Most of the Valley hadn’t seen a Yucca go larger then this in a long time. “Okay, it’s a good tree. Looks like their keeping up with it.” “It was planted at the same time as all of the others.” “Okay?” “You don’t get it. I said something I meant there. Here YOU are.” That’s when Blaise finally felt it. He could see the tree for what it was. It glowed, an ever so slight glow in his vision. “What the hell?” “Your father knew this day would come. He knew that one day you’d accept your life. But you were different. When you were born, he did what he needed to. He tried planting your tree three times. He tried Oak, an dit didn’t work. You just didn’t connect. He tried Maple, and it wouldn’t do it. It doesn’t matter what type of tree, as you’d share your life force with it. But finally, he found one that worked with no issues. You’re a dryad, a member of the Tuatha de Dannan, Blaise, but you’re also a native of this land. You’re the new deal, and this proves it. While the traditions of the old world failed, a tradition of a new world succeeded. This damn tree is only native to this area. It was me who suggested it to him. When one small drop of your blood hit its seed, it sprouted almost instantly. We had to bring it up here to plant it so that it wouldn’t really stand out.” “I don’t friggin’ believe it.” The words just rolled off Blaise’s mouth without any sense of realization. He didn’t see his grandfather flip his pocket knife out and then suddenly slash at one of the branches on the tree. A small bit of sap began to come out immediately, thick and viscous. Blaise grabbed his arm and held it, as the pain suddenly hit him. He pulled his hand away and a small bit of blood flowed from a new cut that had not been there a few moments before. “What the hell?” He just stared at the cut. His own blood flowing just like the tree’s. “Now, you know why you must know your tree. Why you must protect this at all cost. This is your only true weakness. As long as you have everything else in check, this is the only thing that can bring you down. That’s why I asked for your fetish that you created. Normally your father would do this, but I’ve studied the ways of his people… and my own. Like I said before, you’re a remix. An old thing done new again. We’re going to use your little trophy from your battle with the Angel Zadikiel to protect this tree. You’ve already used it once to heal Kyo Kushinaga.” “How’d you know?” “Like I said, I know. I have taken care of this tree for a long time. It knows what you do. It told me. Now, the fetish that you’ve collected, your trophy, give it to me.” Blaise pulled up the bundle that he carried and handed it to his grandfather. He quickly unrapped it from it’s muslin material that kept it hidden from the rest of the world to reveal something that was basically grotesque. It was a stip of a broken bone, the shards stil sharp where it had been torn from the limb it had once held the structure of. Attached to the bloody bone were the remnants of some flesh and long white pin feathers with a small black design on each one. Some of it was unnaturally gray to a black by the means of preservation that it had gone through. It had been smoked and blessed in a traditional Paiute method after Blaise had collected it. It was his wand, made in the manner that Native Amerindians had made their feather and wing wands for millenia. But this one was not made from any bird found on this earth. It was a trophy from one of Blaise’s hardest fights when he was known as the masked wrestler, Shoudou. For during that time, he had started off as a veritable slave, an assassin for a man known as Zadikiel. A man who ended up being one of the abominations that don’t belong on earth. One of the fallen angels that the Order occasionally does business with to get them to give a part of themselves to it. This one gave the tip of its right wing too. And it had become something that Blaise had used as a means of focusing his meditations that he’d been following for a few months now. Grandpa Billy pulled it out fully, and dropped the cloth to the ground. He then handed it back to Blaise. “I think you know what to do with this?” Blaise took the wing wand fetish from his grandfather and then moved towards that which was also him. That which he could tell no one else about. ~~~~~ A few hours later. ~~~~~ The Chevy Avalance pulled back up the the McClancy homestead. Blaise climbs out of the truck and whips the keys to it quickly into his jean pocket. He looked fine, no marks or anyting… even the cut was gone from his arm. He looks up at his family and the person that he really wanted to return to at this time, Ukyo. Blaise breaks off at a trot as he makes his way up the steps to the porch. As he gets up to the top he walks over and picks Ukyo up and kisses her hard onto the lips. After a few moments they break apart. “Well, you must feel like a million bucks.” He just smiled at her. “You could say that. You could say that. I hope my father hasn’t bored you to death with all his old wrestling tales?” “No, not at all. He’s been quite the gentleman and host. And your mother is a doll. Now, how about we get a bite to eat? I’m starving.” “Wherever you want to go. You and I have a few things we need to talk about anyway.” As the two stood there, his father butted in. “I be thinkin’ tha’ we’ll be makin’ t’nigh’ a family afair. I think I know jus’ the righ’ place t’ go. Bu’ it’ll be a dressy outin’.” “Crap.” Ukyo knew what that meant. Blaise just smiled at her… “Time to break out that dress.” End.
