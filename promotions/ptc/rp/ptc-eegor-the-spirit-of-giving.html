Eegor – The Spirit of Giving At first he thought it was turbulence. The old man had been on more flights than most, and wasn't easily panicked, but soon he could detect something different about the way the plane was moving. The initial jolts had woken him up, but a sudden drop had caused his seat belt to dig in to his bad hip, and through the pain he saw the worried look on the faces of the flight crew. He was pretty sure they weren't getting agitated over a hip that had seen better days ever since he took a powerbomb through a stack of tables. “Please remain calm,” said the announcement, trying to react t the growing noise of discontent, but they was fighting a losing battle. No sooner had the words been spoken than the plane veered sharply to the left, throwing the nearest hostess headfirst into the back of a seat. As she dropped to the floor and rolled over, Paul Goodman knew that this was the end. Holding tight to his seat, he turned to look out of the window, and saw a world spinning, blue sky and white clouds mixed with the oranges and yellows of flame, as if a sunset had gone horribly wrong. And as Paul's plane tumbled and plummeted from the sky, parts of his life flashes before his eyes. Yet it wasn't the awards or the titles or the fame that he remembered in those moments. It was a day at the start of 2010 that had begun just like any other. *** “Eegor! Fetch me some toast!” demanded Doctor Curiosity. It was part of the routine that had become established in the Goodman household. The required foodstuff was not always the same, but whatever was catching not-so-good doctor's fancy that day would be ordered, and woe betide the assistant who failed to bring it to him. “Certainly Master, it will be ready in a few minutes,” replied Eegor, swinging his feet out of bed and regretting, not for the first time, that he'd installed an intercom system between the two bedrooms. The house that the brothers shared was a modest affair, a million miles removed from the faux-Gothic grandeur of the former Curious Laboratory. Dr Chris Goodman, the one known to all and sundry as Doctor Curiosity was starting to lose his appetite for the role. He still wrestled in NFW, but that was a part-time job at best, and the remainder of the time was spent flitting between his various hobbies. Paul Goodman, former college wrestler, once-cuckolder of his brother and ever since then his servant, was free to do whatever he wanted with most of his time. Breakfasts were his job, but after that he could put aside the facade and do what he wanted. And what he wanted was to wrestle again. Oh, he'd gotten involved in matches throughout his brother's career. He'd dressed up for a Disney costume match in fWo, he'd taken chair shots to the skull in XUW and had accompanied his brother to the ring in his recent exploits, but he'd never had an official match. “Your toast, sir,” he said, handing the plate over to Doctor Curiosity. “Jah!” said the receiver of the grilled foodstuff, salivating at the tray of assorted preserves. “Will there be anything else?” “Nein!” said the doctor, gleefully buttering the first slice of steaming toast. “Okay then,” said Eegor, and all of a sudden his posture changed. The inferiority complex just washed away and he was just a guy in his 30s, chatting with his brother. “Vhat's... oh.” said Curiosity, noticing halfway through the change in his brother. “What's up?” “I've got something to ask,” said Paul. “Go on then... no wait. Is this about the birds and the bees, again?” “No, no. I got that now.” “Then what?” asked Chris. “Well, I've been practising recently. Working out a lot, that kind of thing.” “Yeah, sure, you look like you've lost a couple of pounds. Well done.” “That's not what I mean.” Chris Goodman nodded as his less famous brother, and motioned that just as soon as he finished that mouthful of delicious toast he was going to respond. “Oh. So, what do you mean?” “I want to wrestle.” Chris narrowed his eyes. “What, you want to go for that Tag Title shot I have in NFW?” “No, I want to go solo.” “Why? You're a terrible wrestler.” “No, I play a terrible wrestler on television.” “And I play a madman with plans for world domination.” “So?” “So they're both accurate depictions! Jah! Jah!” “Stop that,” said Paul knocking the toast from his brother's hand. “Toast thief!” “I'm serious, Chris.” “Okay. So you want to wrestle. Join a fed. There are tons of them out there. Hell, they let von Doom in most of them so the bar can't be too high.” Paul sighed. “I'm tainted though, aren't I?” “Oh crap, is it catching?” “Chris, look. You've been a star. You've won a GTT, you've managed an unknown wrestler to the finals of another one. You've won titles in numerous federations, and all that time I've been your lackey.” “Jah!” “So nobody will take me seriously. Who's going to believe that someone who's been a stooge for years, a failure for half a decade will be able to hold their own against the best today has to offer? I'm not young any more. I know I can compete and I'm in the best shape of my life, but who'd give me a job other than as a sideshow? Some crappy fed run by a sleazeball trying to exploit his workers for a bit of cash? I don't want people to cash in on your name by becoming a freak show performer that people come to look and laugh at.” Chris nodded. “So, what's the plan? What do you need me for?” asked the doctor. “The Infinite Gauntlet.” “Can you not win by yourself? I had kinda expected you to go in as a babyface!” “Chris, I need help for them to let me in. They let all sorts compete, but the next round is different. They just need a promo from me, but I wouldn't even know where to start.” “A promo you say?” was the reply, a familiar nefarious smile coming across the face of Ze Curious One. “It's got to be loud, and probably obnoxious. All I need is for it to get me a shot, and I can take it from there.” “You want me to dress up as you?” “No, Chris. I want you to write for me. All I'm good for is taking shots and throwing rocks. I freeze when they ask me to speak. I never know what to say.” “You can learn that. Shit, when I started my German accent was really bad,” said the doctor. “Chris, your accent is appalling.” “Jah! Part of my charm!” “Then help me. Make me charming.” “Like in zat Disney match from fWo?” “No, just charming enough so that I can slip in through this next round. I need to get through, but not seem like too much of a threat. I need something over the top, almost painfully bad but intriguing, so that they can't help but put me through to the real show.” “And then?” “Then I write my own scripts.” “And what about me?” asked Curiosity. “You don't need me any more. Not in the wrestling ring.” “But what if I need a distraction?” “You'll come up with something. You know, Chris, you've actually become pretty good over the years.” “Really?” “Let's face it. You'd have won those titles without me.” “No, Paul,” said Chris Goodman, smiling and shaking his head. “I'd have achieved nothing without you.” The two brothers looked at one another for a moment, their bond unshakeable. “Now get me more toast, you miserable svine!” cried Chris, back into full-on mad scientist mode. “Yes, master,” chuckled Eegor, as he made his way out of the room. As the door clicked shut behind him, Paul stopped and smiled. In a certain light his face looked fresh, handsome even. The kind of person the crowd could really get behind. “Hi, Sam?” said the voice from behind the door, as Chris spoke on his cellphone. “I'm going to need one of your camera crews. Oh, and get me the number of someone involved in the Infinite Gauntlet. I think I'm gonna have to make a sizeable bride... I mean donation to their upkeep.” And with that acceptance, Eegor was freed. *** Paul Goodman was brought out of his reverie by a trolley that clipped his leg as it fell past him towards the front of the plane. He felt sorry for all of the other people with him, but he had no regrets of his own. The years had forged a bond between him and Chris stronger than he could have hoped, and whatever decisions he had made along the way were what had made them who they were. Chris and the Doctor Curiosity persona were long gone by then, but not before he'd lived to see his brother become the kind of pure wrestler that he never could have been. They had embraced when Paul Goodman, no longer wrestling under the name of 'Eegor', had lifted his first World Championship, and it had all started back with that one phonecall, and that one favour. “Thanks,” whispered the old man, just two years shy of making it to a round hundred. His brother had given him so much, and he couldn't wait to see him again.
