Brian Spaes versus King Blueberry

4 October 2008

(King Blueberry) Appendix A :: Mervin Appendix A :: Mervin It started, of all places, at a Denny’s. I’d been home for a few months, but hadn’t really let anyone back in yet. Some of them knew what happened, but those who did got the vibe that I wasn’t really feeling like talking about things, at least not yet. I remember sitting there at the table with Mervin Humperdink, a guy with a catastrophe of a name who also happens to be a raging nerd and my best friend. We’d been keeping the conversation pretty light so far, and while I could tell that he really wanted to know what was up he was cool enough not to say anything, not at first anyway. Merv’ was never the biggest guy in the world. I’d guess he was about five-six, maybe five-foot-seven, and he couldn’t have weighed any more than a buck-thirty. After hanging out with me for the better part of 15 years you’d think he’d have learned how to dress himself, but he always looked the part of the stereotype. I may or may not have been drawing on the placemat with a set of crayons. That part I’m a little fuzzy on. Merv’ had ordered a Grand Slam something-or-other, and he kind of laughed when I ordered my usual: boneless Buffalo wings and a stack of pancakes. I seriously must have the worst diet in the history of professional wrestling. It was actually his idea to head out that day, since it’d been a while since we’d seen each other, but it worked out for the best since I wanted to talk to him about a few things too. I think he was hoping I’d have just come right out with it, telling him my side of everything that happened in Japan, why I’d fallen completely off the map for a while, and where I’d spent the last few months. I had other things in mind. “So I’ve been getting some feelers,” I said, “from a company called FUSE.” I have this thing about companies who all-caps their name. I’m pretty sure that the only way to say it is to shout it, otherwise they wouldn’t have spelled it that way to begin with. Stylizing be damned, I’m shouting. Sorry. Kind of wandered for a minute there. So Merv’ just sort of nods, but I can see the wheels in his head spinning. The guy’s a certified genius, and was always set for life even without me carting him around the world. Come to think of it, I have no idea why someone in his position would want to spend all their time following around some jackass pro wrestler when he could have very easily set up shop in Manhattan, Silicon Valley, Raleigh, wherever, and made a shit-ton of money in computers. “Well, they used to be called FUSE. I think they’re going with Sin City now. Apparently a few of the old crowd are hanging out there, so it’d be kind of like going back home.” I think he was a little surprised at how I said it. Given everything that had happened in the last few years I doubt that getting back into the sport is something that he’d have expected me to be so cavalier about. Ha. Cavalier. A word I’ve never used, ever. Actually, now that I think about it, I was drawing robots on the placemat, but they were like those old school wind-ups, like something out of that “Danger, Will Robinson” show. Lost in Space, or something. That’s when he said it. I honestly didn’t think he had the balls, but sure enough he dropped it on me like, I don’t know, a heavy thing. “Are you sure that you should do that?” he asked me, “Are you sure it’s not too soon? I heard about what happened, Jared. Maybe you should just lay low for a while, and take it easy.” If I knew the words to explain what it’s like to think you’ve lost your mind I’d have told him, but unfortunately for me I’ve never been the most eloquent (another word I’ve probably never used) speaker in the world. I remember smiling. I think it made him a little uneasy, because he got all fidgety as soon as he saw it. “But this is what I do,” I said, hoping to reason with him, “this is more or less who I am. I’m not saying that I can’t do anything else, but it’s that whole ‘falling off the horse’ nonsense.” I hate clichés. I seriously do. “You’re probably right,” I went on, “I probably shouldn’t, but at the same time I almost have to.” I really hate clichés, even more when I know that I sound like one. I’m such an ass. “I’ve even got an ‘out’ in case things get to be a little more than I can handle. I figure that I might be able to pull this off in such a way that folks won’t realize it’s me, at least not right away, and then I can kind of go at my own pace. If I decide it’s too much I get out. If not I keep going.” That’s when he made the mistake of asking me what it was, which only made me smile wider. Watching his reaction was hysterical, even more so after I told him what I was going to do. I’m pretty sure there’s no good way to tell someone that you’re going to dress up like a giant blueberry and throw a crown on your head, and if there is than I’m pretty sure it can’t be done with a straight face. Didn’t stop me from trying, though.
