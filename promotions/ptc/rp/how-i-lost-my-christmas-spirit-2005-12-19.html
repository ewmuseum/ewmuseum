How I lost my Christmas Spirit

19 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-19 January 3rd, 2001 05:07 PM Los Fiesta was the busiest Mexican Restaurant in Albany, New York. With the Winter Weather freezing everyone outside on the sleet covered sidewalks, it often became a popular eatery after the Christmas holidays. Tonight was no exception. Had you walked in from the street that night, the Hostess would have informed you about the lengthy wait for a table. The sitting room was absolutely packed, not one chair available. If you took a left into the bar, you'd find that every stool was occupied. In others words, it was a claustrophobes nightmare. Packed to the Brim. In the dining area, families and couples enjoyed warm spanish cuisine to the tune of some new age music relaying over the speakers. The serene atmosphere was broken in an instant. The man in the black armani suit crashed through the dinner table. Besides the fact that he was thoroughly humiliated, sitting amidst wood wreckage, covered in Salsa and Nacho cheese, he was also in an immense amount of pain. Without missing a beat, the cause of the throw, a blue haired individual in a tattered leather jacket had leaped on top of the man, and he started to wail on him, wildly. No precision, no thought, just blows. As fast as the incident began it ended, with the Blue haired man leaping to his feet and high tailing it out of the restaurant, and into the cold winter night. -- 05:05 PM Ross Warrington had brought his family to dinner for a reason. It was a matter of importance, and after viewing the upbeat nature of this Mexican Restaurant, he regretted the choice of venue. He was nervous, as anyone in his position would be. He played mindlessly with the collar of his black armani suit, scratching his hands, sweating. Across from him sat his lovely wife, Linda. On each side, his beautiful daughters, Madison and Rebecca. Sitting between his wife and daughters was his son, Jason. He was rather shy around his family, sort of sat there, and didn't speak. His blue locks fell down near his neck, and he looked a far cry from the others with faded jeans, converse all stars, and a leather jacket. Ross cleared his throat. "There's something I have to.. tell you.." he said, adressing the table. The family became attentative, all but Jason. Waiting for the announcement. "I.. This is very difficult to say.. but It needs to be done.." he continued. Everyone leaned in, Jason seemed uninterested, poking his Enchilada with a fork slowly. "I've been having an affair". He didn't have time to dodge leather clad bullet headed straight for him. -- Present Time Jason Warr shook himself from his recollection. He stood, in a winter wonderland of sorts. Snow capped the streets, carolers sang around the corner. He was in Albany, home. Because he had something to accomplish. The shock of setting foot into the town he'd once lived for the first time in nearly five years had sent him into a daydream, of the reason he'd left. It'd all been unresolved, nothing had been found out. He'd rushed out of that restaurant and never looked back, heading for the city. If his father hadn't changed over the years, a long black limousine would be pulling down this very street any second. Ross Warrington was on time, all the time. Never late. If he was ONE second tardy this merely meant you owned a faulty watch. As the pounding music from his concealed iPod blared in his ears, he saw the car pulling around the corner. Wasting little time he sprung to his feet, into the street. He ripped the passenger door opened, to a scream. -Fin.-
