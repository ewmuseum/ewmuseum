Piece by Piece: Part Five

16 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-16 Onward. The fourth rendition of the GTT is, for Seymour Almasy at least, all about moving on. Moving on from his marriage to his soon to be ex-wife Laura. Moving on as the PTC Global Champion. So far, Ash, Darek DeAngleo, and Joey Vendetti have all purported themselves as men who could block Almasy’s path. All three were pushed aside. The newest challenger, however, is a man of slight stature. Yet it is who he is, and who he knows, that will force Seymour to take a look back. After all, we cannot move onwards without at least understanding what brought us to this point… ------- The Jade Dragon was known for being an exquisite, if little known, Chinese restaurant. For those who knew its secret, however, it was culinary paradise. Enjoying a meal in this paradise was PTC’s Global Champion, his grandfather, and his attorney. Various platters of Chinese delicacies rested on the table, varying portions of each already consumed. It was a meal that had been a week overdue. A meal that was as much about business as it was pleasure. Grandpa Cid: You know, this is much better than Pagliachi’s would have been. Good food, good ambiance…AND NO WORRYING ABOUT MOBSTERS BLOWING OUR HEADS OFF! Seymour Almasy: Oh, come on Grandpa. It’s not my fault Vendetti decided to no-show. At any rate, it’s a good thing Tournament Director Argyle called before we went inside, or we MIGHT have had some problems. Grandpa Cid: MIGHT?! They would have chopped us up and served us to the patrons as meatballs! Mr. Fields laughed, spooning some more noodles from a platter of lo mein onto his plate. Mr. Fields: But the fact is, they didn’t. So let’s just enjoy the meal, shall we? Grandpa Cid: Eh, you’re right. No use dwelling on the past, right Seymour? The words were jarring. Mostly because, in between bites of stir-fried crispy beef, Seymour was indeed dwelling on the past. Seymour Almasy: Actually, Grandpa, I can’t help but dwell a little bit right now. Cid’s follow-up question was only a single word. Grandpa Cid: Tournament? Seymour Almasy: Yeah. Grandpa Cid: Your opponent’s someone you’ve met before in the ring, eh? Wolfenden again? Seymour Almasy: Not quite. My opponent is…Craig Goat. The elderly man looked at Seymour as if a frog had just hopped on the Final Fantasy’s head. Grandpa Cid: Who the hell’s Craig Goat? Almasy began to answer, but Mr. Fields encyclopedic knowledge of wrestling was brought to light before the UWF North American Champion could even speak. Mr. Fields: He’s only wrestled three matches, the three in GTT4 that he’s won. He’s better known as being the attendant and manager of XUW’s Nurse Wretched. Grandpa Cid: Then how the hell do you know him, Seymour? Seymour Almasy: Well, it’s interesting, really. Back in the XUW’s development federation, I was managing my soon to be ex-wife. She was doing really well there…but then she lost three straight times to Rhinox. At any rate, it’s not like I know Goat well or anything, but I do know him. And thinking of XUDF automatically makes me think of Laura. Cid immediately shut up. As much as Seymour put on a happy face, and as much the Final Fantasy would never admit it, grandfatherly intuition told Cid that Seymour wasn’t taking the divorce well…and that there was much more to it than met the eye. Mr. Fields: On that note, why don’t you tell me how the divorce came about? I don’t think this case is going to be especially problematic, not once I tell you her demands, but still… Seymour took a deep breath. It was a story that he hadn’t told anyone before. As such, he really didn’t want to go into any more detail than he had to. His feeling for Laura were still quite jumbled up in the tangled mess of thoughts that formed Seymour’s mind. Seymour Almasy: It wasn’t really anything at first. Everything just kept snowballing. I, well, Mr. Fields, you are aware of my history, right? Mr. Fields: That you believed you were an RPG character, more or less? Seymour Almasy: Yes. Anyway, because of that, Laura was very protective of me. Very loving…but also very doting. In a lot of ways, it never gave me a chance to grow up. Even after I found out who I was…she wouldn’t let me live. She did everything for me, knew everything I’d do before I did it. I had to find a way out. Cid sat dumbfounded. He had no idea that Seymour had felt this way, that mixed in with the obvious love he felt for the woman that had, in all probability, saved his life, was a growing amount of resentment. All he could do was stuff some noodles in his mouth, as the attorney asked the questions. Mr. Fields: And then what happened? Seymour Almasy: Well, it all started while the Cruiserweight Tournament was going on, when I was still in Global Wrestling. They had me paired with a manager named Ashley…who basically dressed up as Rikku from Final Fantasy X-2. When I quit GW to go back to the UWF, she got released. I felt badly, so I took her out for dinner, to make sure everything would be okay. Turns out she was fine, thankfully, but we started talking. One thing led to another, and before I knew it… Mr. Fields: You were back at her apartment. Seymour Almasy: Exactly. Mr. Fields: How many times did you sleep together? Seymour Almasy: Only twice. Of course, I’d already slept with Alyssa Sanders while I was in LOW…although on THAT occasion I was drunk. Still three strikes, and I was still out. The Final Fantasy’s grandfather looks on, a mix of anger and pity evident on his face. He didn’t raise his grandson to cheat on women…but at the same time, he is sure that Seymour isn’t using the circumstances surrounding his misdeeds as an excuse. Grandpa Cid: Was it a bad argument? Seymour Almasy: Yes, to say that it was a bad argument would be an understatement. Lots of yelling and screaming…and it ended when she slapped me, and walked out. Mr. Fields: Have you two spoken since? Seymour Almasy: No…but I’m going to have to speak with her soon. Mr. Fields: Well, obviously, considering the divorce proceedings and all Almasy firmly shook his head no. He had something else in mind. Seymour Almasy: Not just for that. I need to speak to her in regards to my match. To see if she knows anything about Goat that she managed to pick up watching Nurse Wretched in XUDF. Both Fields and Cid fell silent. Neither could believe Seymour. At a time of personal turbulence, going through one of the most difficult procedures a person could go through, he was still focused first and foremost on his match. Mr. Fields: But you two aren’t even on speaking terms, why would you go to her now? Seymour Almasy: Because, her help would be useful. I’m not ashamed to admit it. Mr. Fields: You care so much about this match that you’d go to one of the people who hates you the most in this world for aid? Seymour Almasy: Yes, I would. Cid Wilson could only take so much. To him, the entire thing was ludicrous. Wrestling was a career. This divorce, however, was life. Wilson had always put his personal life ahead of his career, and was shocked at the ease with which Seymour placed his career first. Grandpa Cid: Seymour…how do you do it? How, no matter how grave a situation in your life is, do you manage to focus on wrestling? It’s RIDICULOUS! Life is far more important! Seymour’s mouth opened to speak, but no words came out. In his entire life, it was the first time he could remember his grandfather sounding truly disappointed in him. It clawed as the pit of his stomach, as he tried to figure out how to explain the way he was to someone who could never understand. Seymour Almasy: Grandpa…you don’t get it. My entire life, I’ve been dependant on someone. Whether it was my parents, you, or Laura…I’ve never been on my own. Never had to fend for myself. But in the ring…it’s different. Wrestling is more important than my life, because it is the life that I never had. It is Cid’s turn to stare, mouth agape, as Almasy continues. Seymour Almasy: My success or failure in the ring is based on my decisions. Who I choose to ally with. How hard I have to train. Everything has consequences. That was something I didn’t know for TWENTY-THREE YEARS! I thought life had a reset button. And everyone around me acted as enablers. No one told me differently. And for years, I thought it was because everyone loved me. But then, I realized that there was another reason. No one had the guts to tell me. Grandpa Cid: Seymour, I-- Seymour Almasy: Save it. I’ve already heard it from Laura nine-thousand times. “Oh, Seymour, we couldn’t bear to hurt you”-- it’s bullshit. All of it. If you really loved me, you would have told me, if only to save me the shame and embarrassment of being an adult who thought Chocobos were real! The proud visage of Cid Seymour Wilson began to crack, as his face heaved, and tears began to flow from his eyes. His grandson, however, is still not done. Seymour Almasy: Damnit, Grandpa, you’re no better than Laura! In fact, you’re WORSE! You could have nipped this in the bud when I was young and allowed me to lead a normal life. But no, you had to use my affliction to teach me! And in the end, you taught me NOTHING I CAN REALLY USE! What good is being fluent in three languages if I never learned how to do my own laundry, or cook myself dinner? An increasingly frustrated Seymour grabs at his hair. Finally, everything was coming into focus, or at least, the Final Fantasy thought so. Seymour Almasy: All I’ve ever TRULY wanted is independence! The ability, the opportunity to take care of myself. But it’s been denied to me ever since I was a child. To deny it to be when I didn’t know better is one thing. To deny it when I did is absolutely inexcusable! Now…now I have that control! A mighty yank sent strands of Almasy’s famous mane flying through the air of the Jade Dragon. By now, various restaurant patrons were staring, but Seymour could have cared less, as he finished with a flourish. Seymour Almasy: THAT’S why I cheated on my wife. THAT’S why I can put my career above everything else! Because in my career, I’m in control. I call the shots. I can LIVE, with no false beliefs, or people trying to control me. I do WHAT I WANT, WHEN I WANT. And right now, I want to get the hell out of this restaurant, because I’m quite tired of looking at you, Grandpa. He angrily pushed his chair in, reaching into his pocket and throwing down a handful of bills. The glare that he shot his grandfather could have killed. Cid looked as if he would wilt, while Seymour turned to regard an obviously uncomfortable Mr. Fields. Seymour Almasy: For dinner. Mr. Fields, is my wife contactable? Fields winced noticeably. Still, like it or not, he had a job to do. Mr. Fields: Yes. Her attorney told me she’d been out of the country for a little bit, but she just came back. You should be able to reach her on her cell. Seymour Almasy: Good. I’ll keep in touch. Mr. Wilson…I’ll see you around. The grandfather could take no more. His face squinched in agony, as the tears continued to fall, staining the white tablecloth. Mr. Fields moved around the table to console his childhood friend, while Almasy walked out of the restaurant without so much as looking back. Seymour Almasy: Bastard. It was all so clear now. What Seymour had thought for years was unconditional love was in actuality cowardice. Rational thought had left Seymour’s mind, as he continued to rage. Had he sat down and thought about it, he would have realized that they were afraid to tell because they DID love him. Of course, had he sat down and thought about it, he would not have left the restaurant. And in turn, he would not have met the odd woman that stood before him. She wore threadbare clothing, and a dark red shawl. Piercing eyes tore into the Final Fantasy, before the woman spoke. Old Woman: You have a wife, do you not? The UWF North American and PTC Global Champion stood, shocked by both the brazeness and the correctness of the intruder. All he could do was stand stunned, as probably the last words that Seymour Almasy ever wanted to hear came from her lips. Old Woman: She’s pregnant, with your child. Seymour Almasy: That’s impossible! His rage practically spoke before he did, as his fists had clenched with anger and his body shook before the words had even come out of his mouth. Rather than back away, the old woman stepped closer to Seymour, seemingly unafraid of any backlash. Old Woman: She is pregnant with your child. This is certain. My prophecies have never been known to be wrong. Seymour Almasy: But we haven’t slept together in over a month and a half! Besides…we always used condoms! To his great consternation, the old woman had already begun shuffling across the street. Suddenly feeling his legs grow weak, Seymour sat down upon the cold sidewalk, and buried his face in his arms. Seymour Almasy: Why does all of the bizarre shit always happen to me? I swear, if it wasn’t for knowing that life ISN’T an RPG, I’d swear I was in Final Fantasy XIII: Make The Global Champion Squirm. Almasy sighed, his usual reaction to the trials and tribulations of existence. Seymour Almasy: No one can ever leave well enough alone. It’s always something else…and all of the forces in my life seem to team up to kick my ass when its tournament time. Slowly, the overwhelming bitterness that Almasy felt began to fade a little. While still mad at his grandfather and ex-wife, he knew that his grandfather didn’t deserve to be yelled at. He would have to apologize later. Apologies were NOT something that Seymour was good at. He was able to put those issues aside, however, like the professional that he had told Cid that he was, and focused on the matter at hand. Seymour Almasy: Craig Goat. An absolute non-issue a month or two ago. Someone who had never before competed in a wrestling match. And now, three wins later, he probably believes somewhere in his scrambled, tortured mind that he can contend with the Global Champion of Primetime Central. I know that Nurse Wretched’s done things to the poor boy that are beyond my wildest nightmares, but I don’t even think any of that evil woman’s concoctions could make Goat believe that he can defeat me. Yevon, I don’t even think she has anything that could make him believe he could hit me with a clothesline, or a dropkick. He scoffed at the thought of Goat even being able to mount offense. By all logic, this would be his easiest match. Craig weighed 140 pounds, soaking wet. He had tried to work out, but still, he was shrimpy, even when compared to the slight Seymour. Almasy would be able to throw him about the squared circle like a rag doll. And yet…the entire thing worried him. It unnerved Seymour that Goat had been lucky three times. Seymour Almasy: Certainly, he’s been fortunate. But there’s no sign of his luck running out. It only takes three seconds for me to go from the top of the heap to laughingstock. That is why I can underestimate no one in any match. I have so much to lose, and most men facing me have the world to gain. Such is life as the best. The Final Fantasy, ever since discovering what life truly was, had become less and less humble. Achievements were for hyping, to be worn as a badge of honor. As such, he felt the need to remind himself of who he was. Seymour Almasy: I am Seymour Almasy. A two-time UWF World Champion. The reigning PTC Global and UWF North American Champion. Winner of two PTC sponsored tournaments. I have more credentials than anyone in this tournament, end of story. And yet…on occasion, there are still those who doubt me. Still those who go on their message boards and swear that I will be upset by whatever Rabite I am facing. It is certainly a good thing that Las Vegas does not have a wrestling book, or there would be many cynical fans losing their shirts by betting against me. Slowly, steadily, Seymour rose to his feet, and continued to walk. As he did so, he became aware of just how miserly he was becoming. The way he had spoken to his grandfather, the way he thought of Craig Goat, and the way he thought of the fans all combined in his head to make him feel slightly guilty. Only slightly, though. Seymour Almasy: Sometimes, I wish I could stay out of the spotlight. Stay away from people. Then, maybe things could be like they were. Life was so much simpler back when he didn’t understand. Every battle was a simple matter of glory and experience points in his mind. He didn’t have to worry about internet fans, backstage drama, and his “reputation”. At its heart though, wrestling still held the same attraction for Seymour that it once did. It was a contest between athletes to prove superiority. Wrestling also had the tendency to paint things in the easy to distinguish shades of black and white. Seymour Almasy: I should have told Grandpa the other reason I put wrestling ahead of real life. He smiled wryly to himself, as he thought of all he had endured in his career. Brutal beatings. Facefuls of Evil Blue Mist. Being dropped on his head hundreds of times in a year. Even taking all of that into account, when he pondered what he had been through outside the ring in two years, he knew that the next words out of his mouth were utterly and irrefutably true. Seymour Almasy: Wrestling’s a whole hell of a lot easier. Increasing his speed, Almasy walked on, until the Jade Dragon was out of sight. Then, and only then, did he do the one thing that he never thought he would do again. Call Laura Winters. (To Be Continued.)
