12 January 2005 Goals. Everybody sets them, but few actually achieve them. Same thing with New Years Resolutions. If you think about it, those resolutions are simply goals that one sets with a desire to meet and achieve during the coming year. Most people do a fairly good job of keeping up with their resolutions for a short time, and then they slowly let them go: going to the gym more often, quit smoking, drink less, volunteer more. For whatever reason, most people do not keep their resolutions for the entire year. Do people care about goals? Certainly. They just care about other things to a greater extent. Why give up smoking and drinking when they seem like so much fun? Why keep going to the gym when it’s so difficult to drag your ass out of the house? Why volunteer in the community when it takes up your entire weekend? Some might call it selfishness, but that’s probably taking it a bit too far; it’s simply a matter of perspective. The Latch Key Kid set a few goals for the past year, and he did a good job of achieving them. He had vowed to win back the XUW tag team titles with Doughboy, and the Lunchroom Patrol managed to do just that, defeating the Dark Alliance in January, and holding on to the belts until deciding to vacate them in August. Eight months as tag team champions was far more than either Latch or Doughboy had hoped for, so that was one goal met. A second goal of his was to become more active in PTC, so he entered the GTT4. All he wanted was a chance to compete, a chance to show he belonged, to show what XUW could do on the big stage once again. And he made it all the way to the semi-finals, falling to Dr. Curiosity and his bag of tricks. A second goal met. A third goal was to win the XUW World Title, and he had a chance to do just that at the last XUW event of the year, December 2 Dismember 4, against Dante and the current champ, DarkSeid. Defying the odds, overcoming the obstacle of facing two insane opponents, Latch rose up and drove out his demons, and pinned DarkSeid to become the new XUW World Champion. A third goal met. Not bad, he reasoned. Three major goals set, three major goals met. In addition, he had just been named XUW’s wrester of the year at their annual End of the Year Awards show, a result that surprised everybody, including Latch. And now he had his first goal of 2005, young as the year was. Defeat Seymour Almasy and become the new PTC GTD Champion. ------------------------- “Don’t you think you’ve had enough?” Katherine asked, a fork dangling between her right thumb and index finger. “First the GTT wore you down. Then came that brutal match against Dante and DarkSeid. And now this?” “Yes, now this. Me against Seymour Almasy, with the Global Title on the line. Global title, Katherine,” Latch replied, “just what it sounds like. The best in the world; not just in XUW, but in all of PTC. That belt represents a lot more than a simple championship. It represents prestige, honor, glory, and a bunch of other clichéd words, and along with it comes respect.” Katherine set her fork down on her plate, and looked down at her breakfast while trying to compose her thoughts. She had dreaded this day ever since Latch announced he was going to enter the GTT4. It tore her apart inside; she knew the odds of him getting to this point were stacked against him, yet she knew he was capable of this; at the same time, she had hoped for his sake that he would get here, yet had hoped for her own sake that he wouldn’t. “But you have respect,” she eventually said, slowly looking up at him. “You’ve gained the respect of XUW, Brad, and you’ve rightly earned it. Wrestler of the year. Think about that. You were the best last year. Not Saij, not Blake Wolfson, not DarkSeid. But the Latch Key Kid. That award was voted on by your peers, Brad. Don’t forget about Tag Team of the year, either. You have respect. Why now? Why this?” “Because,” Latch replied, his face showing a hint of optimism, “of that exact reason. I have the respect of XUW. They know what I’m capable of. My peers know I’m a worthy champion, or they wouldn’t have voted for me for that award. But PTC doesn’t. Sure, I guess you could say I made a splash in the GTT4, but out of nearly two hundred entrants, someone was bound to make a splash. I got lucky a few times; I’m not foolish enough to think that my string of no-shows is going to impress anybody. But this match against Almasy…well, if I can manage to pull off the upset, or at least hang close with him, I think that would go a long way toward accomplishing what I set out to do in the GTT4.” “So that’s it, huh? You’re doing it so that others will like you more?” Katherine asked with a scowl. Latch shook his head, and smiled. “No. I’m doing it so that others will respect me more. I don’t give a damn if they like me or not. There’s a difference, Katherine.” At least, there was in his mind; there always had been. It was possible to respect somebody without necessarily liking them. He didn’t like DarkSeid; in fact, he downright hated the man, but he sure did respect him. He didn’t like Blake Wolfson, either; he didn’t necessarily hate him, and there was some sort of mutual understanding between them, but there was also a mutual respect there. And Latch knew that there were those who disliked him and did not respect him, yet at the same time, there were those who disliked him but did respect him. He didn’t really care one way or another, because personal feelings of like and dislike had no business in the wrestling ring. The only thing that mattered between the ropes was respect. There was no time to think about how much you liked somebody as you were wrestling them, especially when it came to facing the other good guys, like Saij, the New Royal Legion, and even his own partner, Doughboy. Stop to think about personal friendships, and that’s when you’d get burned. Stop to think about hatred when facing DarkSeid or Blake Wolfson, same result. No, the only thing that you had to go on was respect. Respect your opponent, show them the respect that they deserve, and let them know you expect the same in return. After that, may the best man win. “So you’re really going through with this then?” Katherine asked, her face still showing a deep look of concern. She hated it when he did things like this. Why couldn’t she have a normal boyfriend, she often wondered. Why did she have to fall for a professional wrestler? She worried about him whenever he wrestled, no matter who the opponent was; she worried just as much when he was wrestling against Doughboy, his own partner, as she did when he wrestled against DarkSeid or Blake Wolfson. After all, like they say, anything can happen in the ring. If only he didn’t see this as a way to fight back against the bullies, she thought. If only he weren’t so pigheaded so as to refuse to back out of any match. If only…her mind froze for a moment, and then she tried to push the thought out, but it was no use…if only something were to happen that would make him rethink this match against Almasy. As Latch took another bite of his omelet, a waiter in the coffeehouse rang a bell to signify another tip. ------------------------- The bell rang, and the Latch Key Kid found himself standing across the ring from Dr. Curiosity. He was ill-prepared for this match, having not had a real match since the opening round of the GTT4. He had gone too long without facing a real opponent, and he knew that would work against him in this match. He also knew he was ill-prepared for Curiosity and whatever tricks he may have up his sleeve. He knew little about the doctor, other than what he had heard through the grapevine. But he knew he was at a disadvantage. But he screwed up. He let that disadvantage eat away at him, let it pick away at his brain, let it overwhelm his mind, let it cause his thoughts to stray. And then, just like that, the bell rang again, and Dr. Curiosity stood over him, his arm raised in victory by the referee. And the Latch Key Kid had lost another GTT4 match. No glory, no prestige, no honor…everything he had worked for, gone in the length of time it took the referee’s hand to hit the mat three times. No reward, either; save for the match he had received against the GTD champion, by virtue of being the furthest-advancing PTC-member fed wrestler in the GTT4. The time for the reward was now. But suddenly, he wasn’t sure if he could handle it. ------------------------- Latch dropped his fork, and a clanging sound rang out as it hit the plate. His eyes glazed over, blank, his mind elsewhere. Katherine had seen this happen before, and didn’t know what to think now. She tenderly reached out and lightly grabbed his wrists, shaking them ever so slightly in an attempt to bring him back to reality. He blinked, then smiled at her and looked down at his wrists. “What gives?” he asked with a laugh. “I’m not going anywhere.” “I’m not so sure about that, Brad,” Katherine replied, shaking her head. “I’m just trying to bring you back to reality. Is everything okay?” How do you respond to a question like that? Of course everything wasn’t okay. As if he didn’t have enough to worry about as the new XUW World Champion, he now had a shot at Seymour Almasy’s GTD belt. He was excited about the match; there was no doubt about that. But the outcome of his match against Dr. Curiosity had begun to eat away at him. “I’m not sure anymore, Katherine. I want this match, and I’m glad it’s finally here. But for some reason, my thoughts keep flicking back to the match against Dr. Curiosity. To be honest, it was my first real test since my first round match. I’m just not-” She interrupted him with a dismissive wave of her right hand. “You’re not starting to doubt yourself, are you, Brad? Not right before this big match?” “I don’t know,” Latch replied. “Maybe I am. After all, my record in actual matches in the GTT4 isn’t so great. I ran into a challenge against Dr. Curiosity, and I lost. What does that say about me?” Katherine smiled, and shook her head. “I think you’re forgetting something, Brad. Seymour was in the GTT4 also. Do you remember what happened to him? He lost, too. You got further than he did. He ran into a tough challenge against Craig Goat, and he lost.” “I guess you’re right,” Latch said. “We both went up against opponents we were unfamiliar with, and we both lost. I suppose there’s nothing to be too disappointed in there.” Katherine nodded. “And besides, you just won the XUW World Title. You haven’t forgotten about that as well, have you? That alone should give you some confidence.” “You’re right,” Latch replied. “I guess I don’t have much reason to doubt myself in this match. If I treat it like any other match, I should be okay. Never mind that it’s for the GTD belt; that’s just extra incentive to win this match.” Latch paused, and picked up the coffee cup in front of him. He took a sip, and then grimaced as he set the cup back down on the table. “This coffee’s crap, by the way.” “I was wondering how long it would take you to mention the coffee,” Katherine said, as she took a sip from her own cup. “It definitely isn’t up to standard of the coffee at Frank’s.” She noticed him flinch at the mention of Frank’s Coffeehouse. “Have you heard from Frank at all?” Latch shook his head. “No. In fact, the last time I was anywhere near there was when you and I left the lemonade stand in the hands of that kid, before my match against Dr. Curiosity.” That brought back memories. It seemed so long ago, even though it wasn’t. He had opened the lemonade stand after being forced to resign from his volunteer position at Frank’s. He still wasn’t sure exactly why he opened the lemonade stand on the corner outside Frank’s; maybe it was part protest, part rebellion, part business venture, part pigheadedness, or maybe something else entirely. Sure, it may have been a bit childish, but running the stand gave him something to do to take his mind off of wrestling. Soon after opening the stand, he was confronted by three goons who threatened him and warned him against keeping his business in that location. After becoming suspicious, Latch confronted Frank, who revealed that the goons were extorting local businesses for money. Eventually becoming too worried about Frank to be able to drop the subject, Latch barged into the manager’s office, only to find Frank and the three goons sitting around a table covered with money. With a twinge of disappointment in his voice, Frank confessed to Latch that he was actually in charge of the goons, and was using the coffeehouse as a money-laundering front. Latch walked out of the office, then the coffeehouse, and sold the lemonade stand to a little kid for a dime. “I wonder what’s become of Frank since then,” Katherine said. Latch shrugged and groaned. “Don’t know, don’t care. Whatever’s happened there, Frank did it to himself. This whole thing is his fault, and his alone.” He paused, and took another sip of coffee. “He always did make a mean cup of coffee, though.” Latch took another sip, then set the cup down and stared off into space over Katherine’s right shoulder. She sat still across the table from him, staring gently into his eyes, wondering what he was thinking. ------------------------- New Years Resolutions. Goals. Reconciliations. New beginnings. Everybody makes them. But how many people actually keep them? ---- 12 January 2005 The Latch Key Kid had always hated to leave business unfinished, no matter what sort of business it was. Perhaps that’s why he reached a certain point in his childhood when he decided he would no longer allow himself to be bullied, but instead would fight back against the bullies. Not bully them back, per se, just stand up to them and fight back. Several years of emotional and physical at the hands of kids a few grades ahead of him in school built up a rage inside him; eventually that rage caused him to snap, and he fought back. He refused to let the bullies get the last laugh; so he resolved to tie up those loose ends. In the end, he discovered a new person inside of him, completely different than the old Brad Jameson, and it worried him when he realized he liked the new Brad. He had always believed that he wasn’t supposed to feel this way about fighting back. He realized that he had believed wrong. That’s why it pained him so much to sell that lemonade stand to little Tommy for a dime, prior to his match against Dr. Curiosity in the GTT4. He had become so fed up with Frank’s antics, was so disappointed with what he discovered when he burst into Frank’s office, that he allowed his mind to be overcome with rage. And rather than take a stand, rather than fight back against Frank, rather than stick around and try to make things right, he left. He left the manager’s office, left the coffeehouse, grabbed Katherine and left his lemonade stand in the less-than-capable hands of a random ten-year old kid. And now he worried not only about the lemonade stand and the fate that he was sure had befallen it, but he also worried about the owners of the surrounding local businesses, who were being harassed by the three goons who he discovered were working for Frank. Frank. The name itself fueled rage within the Latch Key Kid; he had no idea what would happen if he ever came face to face with him again. In fact, he had tried so hard to not think about Frank, that he was shocked to hear Katherine bring him up over breakfast. Still, no matter what, he had to hand it to Frank; that guy could brew a cup of coffee that would rival anything from any gourmet coffee shop like Moonbeams. Latch had grown out of the habit of making New Years Resolutions, mainly because he found himself unable to keep all of them. Rather, he chose to set goals. Last year, he had reached all his goals. He had set only one goal for this coming year, but it was a broad goal and left him plenty of room to work. Put simply, he vowed to right whatever wrongs he could; wrongs against him, wrongs against his family, wrongs against his friends, and wrongs that just deserved to be righted based on principle. The year was less than two weeks old, but just now over breakfast, he had decided on wrong number one. “You know what?” Latch said, setting down his coffee cup with a look of disgust on his face. “Let’s go get a real cup of coffee.” ------------------------- “Do you really think this is a good idea?” Katherine asked, as they got into Latch’s car and drove out of the parking lot. Brad was always chasing one stupid idea or another, and Katherine couldn’t get the thought out of her head that this time, perhaps he was going a bit too far. There was a difference between fighting the neighborhood bully and squaring off against what amounted to the mob. That foolish girl. She does this all the time. I swear, sometimes I think she thinks I’m a bit on the stupid side just because I’m a professional wrestler. He turned and looked at her and smiled. “Of course it’s a good idea, babe. Why wouldn’t it be? It’ll be just like old times. The two of us sitting around with Frank, enjoying a nice latte, shooting the breeze. Besides, it always served Doughboy and me well before our big matches. It’s a nice way to relax.” She sighed. His pigheadedness was showing itself once again; this is why she worried about dating a wrestler. There was just something about them that made them think they were a little tougher than they were. Not that she had dated any other wrestlers, but she knew from talking to Nicole that Doughboy had some of the same qualities; those days seemed like so long ago now. She stared back at him. “Relax? Now? Don’t patronize me, Brad. I know how you feel about Frank now, and I know how you get when you feel that way. You’re going to barge in there and create a scene, and honestly, I don’t think confrontation is the best way to handle this right now.” “Then what would you suggest, Katherine? I can’t just let this go. It wouldn’t be right to-” “And why not?” she interjected. “You’ve let it go this long. Why not just drop it altogether? What are you going to accomplish by facing Frank over this?” She still didn’t get it. “That’s exactly why, Katherine. Because I’ve let it go to this long; too long, far too long. What will this accomplish? It’ll accomplish more than me not doing anything would. This isn’t about me, Katherine. It’s about Tommy and that lemonade stand, it’s about the other business owners who are being harassed by Frank, and it’s about righting a wrong.” “Righting a wrong? You’re not Batman, Brad. You’re not even fighting the local schoolyard bully. This is serious business; Frank’s a rough guy, and he and his friends are more than capable of making you regret walking in there. You should just back off, and focus on your match with Seymour Almasy. That should be your top priority right now.” Ah. Now they were getting somewhere. Oddly enough, they actually agreed on this. “That is my top priority, Katherine. I would like nothing more than to walk into that ring, pin Almasy, and add the GTD belt to my collection. But it just doesn’t work like that.” Pigheaded, indeed. “But why not?” Katherine protested. “Why doesn’t it work like that? Why can’t you drop this and focus on your match with Almasy?” He slammed his hand against the steering wheel, and then took a deep breath. He hadn’t meant to react like that. “I’m sorry,” he said, his voice conveying an apologetic tone. “It’s simple, Katherine. I can’t drop this and focus on my match, because I can’t focus on my match until I get this taken care of. This match is important to me; win or lose, I’m intent on putting on a good show, but don’t get me wrong, I do want to win that title. It’s so important to me, in fact, that I need my full attention to be able to perform at my best. And at the moment, some of that attention is being diverted. When I take care of that diversion, I’ll free up my attention.” “That’s strange logic, Brad,” she complained, “but oddly, I can actually, sort of, just slightly, see your point.” She laughed. She wasn’t exactly sure where he was going with this, she wasn’t sure if he even understood what he was saying. She didn’t entirely understand it herself, but no matter. He seemed to have a good idea of what he needed to do, and if it helped him focus on this match, then she was behind him one hundred percent. She just hoped that it really would help him focus more on the match, after this was all over. As they approached the strip center that was home to Frank’s Coffeehouse, Latch was surprised to see the lemonade stand on the corner. Surely it would’ve gone under by now, would’ve folded faster than a cheap house of cards, with a ten-year old in charge. But apparently, Tommy had managed to keep it in business. They pulled into the parking lot, and Latch pulled the car between two yellow lines and cut the engine. Latch and Katherine got out of the car and walked across the grass towards the lemonade stand. Tommy was standing on his stool behind the stand, with his arms folded in front of his chest. As he saw them approaching the stand, he smiled, and quickly got to work pouring two large cups of lemonade. He held the cups out to them when they reached the stand, and Latch reached out with his left hand and tousled Tommy’s hair. “Hey there, Tommy. It’s good to see you. It’s look like you’re doing well for yourself.” Tommy beamed, and spread his arms out in an arc in front of him. “Yes, sir, Mr. Latch Key Kid. Business has picked up real good ever since you sold me the stand. And I guess you’ve been doing well lately, too.” Latch looked at Tommy quizzically. “Do you watch wrestling, Tommy?” Tommy shook his head. “No, but my older brother does. He’s thirteen. He says you’re the best. Is that true?” Latch shrugged, but Katherine hugged him with her free arm and nodded. “It’s true, Tommy. He is the best. By the way, I love what you’ve done with the place.” Latch hadn’t noticed at first, but now that Katherine mentioned it, he stepped back and looked carefully at the stand. Gone were the cheap pieces of plywood that had been hammered together haphazardly; in their place were professional-looking steel poles, supporting a custom-designed and painted sign that still carried the same name: “Latch’s Lemonade Stand.” Latch smiled. “You kept the name?” he asked. “That’s very thoughtful of you, Tommy. Thank you for that.” Tommy shook his head. “No reason to thank me. In fact, I should be the one to thank you. I started making so much money because people recognized your name on this place that I didn’t dare take it off. Heck, you should see the business the other one does. It must rake in-” Latch shook his head and stared dumbfounded, before interrupting. “The…other…one, Tommy? Surely you don’t mean that you have another lemonade stand?” Tommy grinned and nodded. “Sure do. It’s just down the road a bit. In fact, the third one is due to start construction in just a few days. I tell you, Mr. Latch, it’s amazing what a celebrity endorsement can do for a product like this.” Latch turned to Katherine with a puzzled look on his face. He began to whisper to her. “I can’t believe this. This kid claims to have made enough money off this one stand to start a franchise of these. A ten-year old kid…how much can he possibly know about running a business?” This time it was Tommy who interrupted. “I’m right here, you know. You’re not exactly whispering as quietly as you think you are. What do I know about running a business? Nothing. Well, I did know nothing about it before I started. But it’s amazing how quickly you can learn with hands-on experience. I’m surprised you gave it up so quickly. It could’ve been quite profitable for you.” “Yes, well,” Latch replied, “I didn’t exactly run this place for a long time. In fact, it was just a few days after I opened it when I decided to sell it to you. But I congratulate you on your effort.” “Well thanks,” Tommy was still beaming. “There’s a lesson in it for everybody, though, just like there is in most things in life.” “What kind of lesson could there be for me in this?” Latch asked, eager to see what nonsense this kid would spew out next. “Well, my brother says you’re facing Seymour Almasy soon for the GTD title,” Tommy said. Latch nodded, and Tommy continued. “He also says you don’t know anything about Almasy, and that you-” “That’s not true,” Latch said, shaking his head. He looked at Tommy, who was staring back with a disbelieving look on his face, as if challenging Latch on the subject. “I know that…uhm…well, I know his wife, Laura, left him. I know that he likes to gamble. And I know that someone wants to kill him.” Tommy was still staring at him with a smug look. “So you see, I know quite a lot about Seymour Almasy.” Tommy shook his head. Stupid wrestler. He may be tough, and he may hate bullies, but Tommy failed to understand how someone so smart could be so stupid on a subject like this. “So you know a lot about him outside the ring, is what you’re saying? You ever seen him wrestle?” Tommy watched Latch scratch his chin. “That’s what I thought. You have no experience with him or his wrestling style. You know a lot about him, but you don’t know anything about him. You’re at a disadvantage here.” Latch nodded. “Fair enough. But how much does Almasy know about me?” Tommy chuckled. “Do you really think that matters? It might, if we were talking about Almasy, but we’re not; we’re talking about you. The Latch Key Kid. Focus here, man.” Tommy saw a flash of recognition in Latch’s eyes, and satisfied, crossed his arms and began speaking again. “Good. Now, as I was saying, you don’t much about Almasy’s wrestling style, so you could say you’re at a bit of a disadvantage. But that doesn’t matter once you step in that ring and get down to action. Once you get in there, you’ll learn quickly. Just like me and your, I mean, my lemonade stand.” This was getting out of hand, but he had to keep this kid going, if only to see what would happen next. “How is a lemonade stand like wrestling Almasy, Tommy?” “A lemonade stand isn’t like wrestling Almasy, Mr. Latch. I’ll be the first to admit I didn’t know the first thing about running a business. But look at it now. Two of these babies, with a third on the way. I didn’t have any experience ahead of time, but I learned quickly, as I was going through the motions. You’ll adjust against Almasy, Latch. You have enough wrestling experience to give anybody a tough match. Just because you don’t know about his wrestling style, doesn’t mean you won’t be able to give him a good match. Heck, I wouldn’t even be surprised if you walk out of this as the next GTD champion.” “Hmmm.” Now this was interesting. Little Tommy just might be on to something. Latch looked at Katherine, who smiled slightly and shrugged her shoulders. He turned back to Tommy. “I’ll keep that in mind. Thanks, Tommy.” He drank down the rest of the lemonade in his cup, and dropped it in the trashcan next to the lemonade stand. “Say, that’s pretty good lemonade. Much better than the stuff I made. How’d you get it tasting so good?” Tommy laughed, and wagged his finger. “Hey, I didn’t have any experience making lemonade either, but like I said…” Latch laughed, and reached out and tousled Tommy’s hair again. He turned to Katherine, and took her left hand in his right. “Come on, babe. Let’s go have a talk with Frank.” Tommy made a forced coughing sound as they walked off, and Latch turned around. “What’s wrong, Tommy?” Tommy just stared at him. “I’m not sure I’d go in there if I were you. He hasn’t been in the greatest mood lately.” Latch grinned, then turned and continued walking with Katherine toward the front door. He was still smiling as they reached the door, and caught Katherine smiling at him. “Go figure,” Latch said. “Kids say the darndest things.” Then he opened the door. ------------------------- Latch was about to face down his first goal of the new year. Now get moving, Billy.” Billy sighed and walked off to the back of the coffeehouse. He returned a few minutes later and shrugged his shoulders with a smug look on his face. “Frank, he said he doesn’t want to see you. Said to tell you to take a hike, he’s got nothing to say to you.” He walked back behind the counter. Katherine took a few sips of coffee, and set the mug on the table. “You’re right, Brad. He does make a mean cup of coffee. It’s too bad you two fell out of sorts with each other.” She kept talking, but he didn’t hear her. Not that he was intentionally ignoring her, but he had allowed his mind to wander yet again. Losing sight of the business at hand, he went back to thinking about Seymour Almasy. He wondered about Seymour’s physical condition. Sure, all indications were that he was okay after the attempt on his life, thanks to the GTD belt, but he wondered about the mental toll it had taken on Almasy. It’s not every day someone tries to gun you down in a casino. Physically okay or not, it had to have made Seymour feel at least slightly vulnerable. They say your life flashes before your eyes in your last moments alive. Did Seymour’s life flash before his? Did he think it was his last living moment? Did he – “I said, are you even listening to me?” Uh oh. Someone didn’t sound happy. Latch shook his head to clear a few cobwebs, and found himself staring at Katherine across the table. Her face was red like it got when she was upset sometimes, and the fingers of her right hand were clenched tightly around the ceramic coffee mug. She set it down on the table to avoid cracking it, and stared back at Latch. “I said, are you even listening to me? I’m here to help you, Brad, and I can’t do that if you won’t listen to me. So, were you?” He shook his head. He couldn’t lie to her; well, he could, but she made a fairly good human lie detector, and he know she wouldn’t fall for any of his crap. Besides, she was obviously well aware that his mind was elsewhere. No use in trying to cover it up. “No,” he replied, and then braced himself. She rolled her eyes and leaned back in the chair. “Well, at least you were honest. Now, mind telling me what you were thinking about?” He nodded. “Seymour Almasy, babe. I know I told you earlier that I couldn’t focus on him because of this business with Frank, but now it seems like I can’t focus on this business with Frank because of Seymour. This match is important to me, and as it’s fast approaching, I know I need to clear my mind.” Ironic, he thought, especially since he was just wondering whether Seymour would be mentally fit for the match. “I just…I don’t really know how to explain it, but it’s almost as if I can’t decide which of the two is more important.” “That’s because they’re both important, Brad.” She wasn’t a psychologist, but she considered herself a pretty good judge of people and feelings; at least, good enough when it came to Latch. “You can tell yourself that you’re only going to focus on one of them, but that’s when the other starts to take over. So, since you brought him up, why not talk to me about how you feel about this match.” “Okay. Well, like me, he’s a multiple-time champion. He’s held the UWF tag team titles on three occasions, and he’s a two-time UWF World Heavyweight Champion. But, he’s also achieved the UWF Grand Slam, winning every belt they have in their federation. So he’s done alright for himself, and he’s only twenty-three.” Katherine laughed. “All that, and he’s five years younger than you. What gives, Brad? You’re lagging.” She reached over the table and playfully hit his right shoulder. “In all seriousness, though, he does sound like he’s accomplished a lot as well. How do you compare in style?” Latch nodded. “Well, he’s mainly a high-flyer, and uses a few submission moves as well. I guess you could say I’m actually a more powerful wrestler than him, strange as that may sound for me. But I’m bigger than him; I’m about half a foot taller and fifty pounds heavier. So if I can manage to keep him off the ropes and turnbuckles, and keep the match more on the mat, I should have a decent shot at wearing him down and overpowering him.” “So that’s it, huh? Is he another one of those good guys?” Katherine asked. “I don’t think so,” Latch replied. “I get the feeling he’s more on the other side. He seems a bit arrogant if you ask me, though it’s hard to fault the guy for that after all he’s accomplished. I suppose winning so much does allow you to be a bit cocky. After all, I should know.” He watched her roll her eyes again, playfully this time. “Oh, come on. You know I’m just kidding. Look, what it will basically come down to is who can overpower the other man. If Seymour is able to use his submission moves to weaken my arms and legs, he’ll gain the upper hand. But if I can use my strength to keep him from flying around the ring and being able to apply his pressure holds, I should have the upper hand.” “It all sounds so simple. Too bad it won’t be that way during the match.” It never was, she told herself. Brad always wound up doing things the difficult way. Though the easy way was there, he seemed to shun it, even avoid it. She couldn’t understand why. She knew it would be the same way against Almasy. He’d have an opening, but rather than capitalize on it immediately, he’d go the roundabout way to gain the edge. She hadn’t quite cracked that section of his brain yet. “But I’m sure you’ll do just fine, Brad. I have total faith in you.” “Good,” Latch replied. “I needed to hear that from you. Now,” he crossed his arms and sat back in the chair, “what should we do next?” “Well, since Frank won’t come out and see you, perhaps we should just leave,” she replied. Latch shook his head. “Come on, Katherine. I can’t do that. Not after all this. Besides, you know my mind will run wild between now and the match, and I probably wouldn’t be able to compete at my best if I’m still worrying about this. Nope,” he shook his head again, “if Frank won’t come out here, I’ll just have to go back there. Wait here,” he said as he stood up from the table. She put her hand on his arm and tried to push him back down into the chair, but he resisted. “Katherine. Let me do this. Please.” Without waiting for her to respond, he walked away from the table, toward the manager’s office. Billy stepped in front of him, but he pushed the kid out of the way. He reached the door to the office, and knocked three quick times on the door. A muffled reply came from inside the office, and Latch knocked again. A little louder this time, “I said, go away!” Latch knocked a third time, and then he heard the sound of a chair being pushed along the floor. As the door flew open, the voice inside yelled, “What?” When it opened far enough, Latch glanced inside and saw Frank standing there. He pushed the door open, sending Frank falling back onto the round table in the middle of the room. It was vacant, save for the two of them. Frank began to quiver at the sight of Latch. “I thought…I told…you were gonna stay away…what are you…” “Calm down, Frank,” Latch said, holding his hands up in front of him. “I just want to talk. Man to man, okay? I’m not going to hit you; I’m not going to beat you up. I just want to sit down and have a talk. Can we do that?” Frank began glancing around nervously, and his eyes darted to the watch on his left wrist. “I don’t…I don’t think this is such a good time for that, uhm, Brad. Perhaps you could come back, I don’t know, later?” Frank fumbled in his shirt pocket and pulled out a crumbled cigarette, and picked a matchbook up off the table. He tried to light a match, but failed several times. “I didn’t know you smoked, Frank,” Latch said. “That’s such a filthy habit.” Frank tossed the cigarette on the table. “I don’t. Well, I didn’t. And I don’t…too much. But…Brad…really, you should get out of…” “Why, Frank?” Latch interrupted. “Aren’t you glad to see me? Don’t you want to-” Frank nodded. “Yes, Brad. I am glad to see you, and I do want to talk to you. But please, believe me when I tell you that this isn’t a good time. There are things I can’t tell you right now, things that I misled you on last time you were-” Suddenly, the back door that led to the alley behind the coffeehouse flew open, and in stepped the three goons who had harassed Latch last time he was here. They stared at him, and then looked in unison at Frank. The biggest of the three spoke up. “Say, Frank, what gives? I thought we got rid of this guy. What’s he doing back here?” Frank glanced between the three goons and Latch. “He’s…uhm…I don’t know, fellas. It’s like I told you last time, I can’t control him. I tell him one thing, he does the opposite. It’s like he thinks I want to see him, or something.” The goons stepped toward Latch, the biggest one speaking again. “Don’t worry, Frank. We’ll take him back in the alley and make sure he don’t bother you again.” Latch looked at each of the three men, then shifted his eyes to Frank. At that, Frank got up off the table and brushed himself off. “I tell ya what, guys,” he said in a snide tone, “how about not.” They turned and looked at Frank, and the leader shook his head slightly. “No, you know what? Let’s not do that to him. You know what? Forget all of this. I’m done. I’m not going to do this anymore. You want it that badly, then you can have it.” Latch interrupted. “What are you talking about, Frank? They can have what? What’s going on here?” Frank turned toward Latch, and sighed. “Brad, I’m sorry,” he began. “This whole thing…it was all a story I fed you. It’s not real.” “You mean these three guys who look like they want to kill me, and you, aren’t real?” Latch asked with a hint of relief. Frank shook his head. “Oh, no, they’re real, alright. Believe me. And quite frankly, I’m a bit scared of them now. But, Brad…” Frank glanced at the three goons, shaking their heads in unison. “I wasn’t truthful with you last time. I’m not working with these guys. Honestly, could you really see me running a mob like that?” Latch thought for a moment, scratched his chin, and then shook his head. “Good,” Frank continued. “Last time you were in here, that stuff I told you? They told me what to say to you, before you came barging in here. They told me what to say to get rid of you.” “I don’t understand, Frank. Get rid of me? How?” Latch asked. “Not get rid of you get rid of you, just make you go away. You know, make you forget about this place and me. It was a compromise, I guess you could say.” Latch stared with confusion. “A compromise? Why? Me going away in exchange for what?” Frank took a deep breath. “In exchange for them leaving you alone, Brad, and not taking a crowbar to your knees for nearly spoiling their racket. They figured you’d catch onto them, Brad, and they want you out of here before that happened.” The leader of the goons coughed forcefully, and both Frank and Latch turned to look at him. Frank went on, “I guess that doesn’t really matter now, though, since I just told you in front of them. Now I imagine they’ll want to take care of both of us in the alley.” The goons nodded. Latch stepped towards them. “I don’t think so, Frank.” He saw the leader of the three looking at him with intimidation, but carried on anyway. “Katherine’s sitting out there right now, drinking a fresh cup of coffee. She’s got my cell phone with her. If I’m not back out there in five minutes, she’s going to call the cops. And when they get here, they’ll see what’s going on. Now, I don’t know about you, Frank, but I’ve got no reason to be scared of the cops.” Frank nodded, and both men stared at the three goons. “Our friends, here…what were your names, fellas? Percy, Leonard and Warren, were they? Eh, no matter, the cops can figure that out for themselves.” The leader of the goons spoke up. “What do you want?” Latch stepped towards him. “I want you out of here. Now. All three of you. And I want you to leave Frank alone. If I ever hear of you setting foot in this, or any other business in the area, whether you’re trying to extort money or not, I will come after you. This is my friend you’re messing with, and that’s where I draw the line.” The three men stared at him, then turned toward Frank, who shrugged. Then slowly, they began to back toward the door to the alley. Latch grunted and lunged towards them, and they scampered out and Latch shut the door and locked it. Frank sat down, and Latch sat down across the table from him. “Well,” Latch said, “that was interesting.” Frank nodded. “Brad,” he began, “I’m sor…” “It’s okay, Frank,” Latch said, holding up his right hand. “I understand. And no, I don’t really think you’d be able to run a mob like that. All these years I’ve been coming in here, I just don’t see that side of you. I can’t see that side of you.” Frank nodded. “Well, anyway, thank you. I’ve got one question, though. Is Katherine really out there? With your cell phone?” “Oh, she’s out there, alright,” Latch nodded. “But with my cell phone?” He shook his head. “Frank, I don’t even have a cell phone, you know that.” “Hey,” Frank shrugged, “times change, people change.” “Not all people, Frank,” Latch said, “not all people.” A few moments of silence passed between them before Frank bolted up in his chair. “Aside from the obvious, why did you do it, Brad?” he asked. “Why did you challenge them like that? There were three of them, and one of you.” “It’s like this, Frank,” Latch replied. “Life is too short to let wrongs go unpunished. I knew if I could stand up to those guys, whether or not they would wind up backing down, I’d still be proving something to myself. I’d be proving to myself that I really could stick to my goals. See, I’ve always believed in standing up for myself, Frank, and for those I care about. Even though the odds were stacked against us, well, me, here, I knew I had to face them down. And what do you know, it worked. They left, just like that. They thought they were stronger than me, Frank. They thought they could take me out easily enough. But they didn’t know me, Frank. They didn’t know what I’m capable of when I set my mind to something like that. But hey, it’s like I’ve always believed; fight back against those who consider themselves stronger than you, and in the end, you’ll be able to take out the person on top.” “Person on top…on top of…holy crap, Brad! You have a match against Seymour Almasy for his GTD championship. I totally forgot about that,” Frank said, hopping out of his chair and rubbing his hands together. Latch smiled. “I didn’t. Like I said, Frank, it’s all about taking out the person on top. Now, let’s get out there before Katherine does something foolish like actually call the cops.” Latch laughed, and Frank joined in. Frank opened the office door, and they walked back into the coffeehouse. Frank grabbed Latch by the shoulder and spun him around. “Hey, Brad? Thanks again.” Latch patted Frank on the arm. “Anytime, old pal, anytime. It’s good to be back here, Frank. I really was getting tired of that crap from Moonbeams.” Katherine stood up as she saw them approaching, a surprised look on her face. When they reached her table, Frank gave her a friendly hug, and the three sat down just as Billy the barrister brought three mugs of coffee to the table. ------------------------- Goals. New Years Resolutions. Call them what you will, they’re all the same in the end. The true mark of a person is determined by their integrity; and their integrity is determined by how well they follow through with their goals. The Latch Key Kid had just met his first goal of the year. His second goal might not come as easy, but he looked forward to doing his best to reach it against Seymour Almasy.
