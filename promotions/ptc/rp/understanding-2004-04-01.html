Understanding

1 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-04-01 It wasn’t every day that he could take a rest. Usually, he’d be up at five…having his glass of orange juice…then a shower…then the low-fat bre…Basically, it was a routine. He worked on routine. He liked things that way, he couldn’t handle anything being out of place. He had enough excitement in the arenas, there was no need bringing it home. “Come on, it’ll only be an hour!” Or…at least he thought he worked on a routine. It was fine up to the breakfast part – that was the only part he ever mentioned – then it all went haywire. There was no hour-long stint in the gym. No jogging for god knows how many miles he had decided. There was always something pulling him away. And today, it was Chrissy. “Look, Armaan, I’m gonna be at the café in half-an-hour. You don’t have to come if you don’t want to, but just remember…I don’t like getting stood up.” The power of persuasion had always been too strong for the Indian. It had been his biggest weakness all the way from childhood. Most people would grow out of it, but not Armaan. He still had his child inside him, and the thought of it haunted him every time he skipped weight-training. ”Message Deleted.” It was only until those two words rang out from the answering machine, that it finally dawned on Armaan…it was a message?…he had been persuaded over an answering machine. How weak was he? Brushing his thought aside, the Indian finished the glass of juice that he had placed conveniently by the phone, and headed for the bedroom. The room seemed too out of place. While the flat was relatively small, with the lounge itself taking up half of the flat space, the bedroom seemed to be quite large. He still only had a single bed tucked in the far corner, but a TV/VCR stand rested directly to the right as he entered. A large wardrobe and a mirror stand made up the rest of the furniture, and he had thought the previous week about bringing in a large vase to decorate. He liked Chrissy. A lot. Oh no, not like that, he would never think of her as anything more than a friend. Probably the closest friend he had. He saw her as more of a sister. A very beautiful sister. He shrugged off his ‘derogatory’ remark, as he called it, and searched through a messy pile of clothes on the bed. Not finding what he needed calmly, the Indian began to chuck his clothes over his shoulder, before finally picking up a green-and-white checked shirt. There was a gleam in his eye. Chrissy likes green. It was a warm coincidence that Chrissy had called, Armaan had wanted to talk to her anyway. Nobody else was really supporting him. He couldn’t phone home to India, they still weren’t on talking terms, and everybody else he knew was associated with the SCWF. It was the same old argument…too much time on your hands, just wasting unnecessary energy. But Armaan liked tournaments, and he liked winning them. Everybody seemed not to understand all that. For Armaan, tournaments meant challenges, and he loved challenges. He wouldn’t have the SCWF championship if he didn’t. Where is the belt, anyway? He didn’t panic – the belt was in the room somewhere – but it was out of sight. He halted the search for his jeans, and instead began to look for the title. It wasn’t hard to find, in exactly the same place he left it every night – sitting on the bedside table in a neat fold. A pair of yellow pyjamas that he had flung across the room partially hid it, and he threw them back onto the bed. He wasn’t going to pick the belt up or anything, he just wanted to look at it. He resumed his search, finally picking up a pair of blue jeans. There weren’t a lot of cruiserweights in SCWF…there were a few, but they were all busy with their own stuff. Perhaps that’s what was spurring him on…lack of lightweight competition. This would be his chance to wrestle superstars his own weight. And besides, it didn’t matter what everyone else thought, Armaan knew that Chrissy would understand. She’ll understand. **************** “I just don’t understand.” Armaan’s head dropped onto the table. “What’s the point?” Armaan tapped his head on the table three times – for effect. It was a good way of proving just how upset he was at Chrissy’s response, and it seemed to be working. “Come on, Armaan, you’ve got to look at it from someone else’s perspective. You probably think you’ve got enough time, and you probably do, but why add more workload?” The Indian struggled to find words…this was his last hope of support, and it was disappearing. The waiter brought over two cups of coffee as Armaan sat back upright, staring into Chrissy’s eyes. Maybe he could find something there. “You’ve gotta pull out.” Nothing. “You’ve still got time. When is it, in a week, didja say? Jus’ phone them up and tell ‘em you can’t do it.: Nothing at all. He took a sip of his coffee, as Chrissy began to search through her purse for her mobile. She was serious. “I am not going to quit, Chrissy,” he finally said, and she let go of the phone in her grasp, it falling back into the abyss of make-up and pointless jewellery. “I want to take part in this tournament.” She sighed deeply, and Armaan was suddenly reminded why he thought of her as a sister. The sighs. The sighs of a frustrated older sister annoyed him sometimes, but it was nice to have the feeling. “Look, I don’t know a thing about this wrestling or whatever you do. It’s not for me. But what I do know is that you will just be wasting your time…but…if you wanna do it, I’ll be by your side.” Time froze for approximately three seconds, during which time a gorgeous smile adorned Armaan’s lips. He leaned back into his chair, happy that things were finally turning to his favour after the momentary scare. “Thank you so much, Chrissy, I owe you one.” “Yeah, yeah, OK, enough with the formalities…so, what’s the guy’s name?” Uh oh. “Well…there are two of them…the first one is called Fury. He is World champion in his own federation, so very good competition.” “Cool,” she replied, shrugging her shoulders. Armaan was hoping she’d react the same second time round. “What about the other guy?” He didn’t know how to say it. She’d probably pick up the cup of coffee and throw it right in his face. No more brother and sister then. “Well?” The moment of truth…or maybe not. He picked up the coffee and placed it next to his lips. Mumbling something she could not understand, he took a sip of his coffee, proud of his deception. “I didn’t hear you.” He groaned, and placed the cup back down into the saucer. Bracing himself for the worst, he revealed the name…loud and clear. “Carys.” Chrissy paused for a second, pondered over the name and whether she had heard it before. “But isn’t Carys a woman’s name?” “I know…I’m fighting a woman.” Time stopped again, but this time it was torture. The two just stared at each other, both with a blank expression. Armaan couldn’t handle the tension anymore, and clenched his eyes closed. Any second now, the burning sensation of hot coffee would greet his skin, or the stinging blow of her hand. But there was nothing…maybe she had stormed off in disgust. Here goes. Slowly, he opened one eye and then the other… She was still there, and the cup of coffee remained intact on the table. “Nice shirt,” she finally said with a deep sigh, a frown on her face.
