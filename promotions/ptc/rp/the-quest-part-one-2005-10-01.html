the Quest: part One

1 October 2005

Date: 2005-10-01 Silently, Seymour Almasy turned off the television. The phone couldn’t REALLY be ringing again, could it? No, it had to be something on TV producing the sound that, by now, the Final Fantasy despised above all others. RIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIING! For over a month now, ever since Seymour had joined Global Championship Wrestling, the promotion’s Vice President of Talent Relations had called him at least five times a day. To make sure that Almasy had made his flights. To make sure that his hotel room was okay. To make sure that room service had sent up the proper breakfast. It was driving Almasy batty. RIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIING! And there it was again. That damnable ringing sound. With the television off, there was absolutely no doubting the reality of the situation. Jonathan Calver was calling. AGAIN. RIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIING! Just waiting for him to hang up was not an option. Calver had stayed on the phone for thirty six minutes and twelve seconds waiting once. Seymour knew. He’d timed it. No, there was really no choice but pick the phone up. Seymour did so, making sure his eyes were properly rolling. RIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIII-*click!* “SEYMOUR! Oh thank God, you’re there! Is everything okay?” Jonathan’s parents hadn’t paid him any attention when he was a child, Seymour decided. That was the only way to explain his utter overcompensation when dealing with talent. “Yes, Jonathan,” Seymour said, forcing the anger from his voice and doing the level best he could to be civil. “Everything’s fine, just like it was an hour ago when you called. Say, I thought I asked you to lighten up on the calls…” The Final Fantasy heard laughter on the other end. “Oh Seymour,” the constantly pleasant voice said, “you’re such a joker! You know that with FallOut and your big match with Rich Rollins coming up, I can’t let anything happen to you!” “Like life?” the cynical half of Seymour’s mind spat, angrily. Ah well, that half of his mind would get its revenge when Jonathan was informed of Seymour’s current frame of mind. “There’s a reason I asked you to leave me alone, Mr. Calver,” a surprisingly calm Seymour said. “I’ve got the main event of PTC Super Series coming up this week.” A smile formed on the Final Fantasy’s face as he heard the rustling of paperwork on the other end. Undoubtedly, the meticulously organized VP was looking for where that request had been put in. The fact of the matter was that Almasy had gone over Calver’s head, to Steven Caldera, to make the request. There were several reasons for this. Number one, Caldera was a meat and potatoes sort of guy. He liked people who got to the point, and that meant that Seymour’s request was made and granted in the time it took to tie shoes. The other reason was to hear the sound of the annoying executive on the other end of the line squirming. “Uh, Seymour,” he said, a bit of panic in his voice, “I don’t have anything here saying you’re competing at Super Series.” “Oh,” Almasy asked, with false surprise. “I put the request in to President Caldera last week. I’m shocked he hasn’t gotten it to you…” Of course, President Caldera was a busy man. So it would be completely lost on Mr. Vice President that Seymour had kindly asked Caldera NOT relay that bit of information to his VP of Talent Relations. Seymour had placed the worm on the hook. Now, he would watch it wriggle. “But Seymour,” the concerned exec stated, “you’ve got Vince Jones on WorldWide this Thursday…” “Don’t worry, Jonathan. The dates don’t conflict. Everything will be fine. I just need time to prepare for Super Series. You know, log some ring time in training, that sort of thing.” “This is WorldWide, Seymour!” the other man screamed, growing increasingly worried. “The premiere television show in sports entertainment!” “It’s not like you have me booked in a match you’re selling the show on, Jonathan. The main event’s Rollins versus VanLorne. I’ve got Vince Jones. Not to disparage the guy or anything, but he's not Karina Wolfenden. I told you already, I’ll be at WorldWide, you have nothing to worry about.” “But what if you get hurt in training?” Okay, this was just getting ridiculous. “It’s for the PTC Global and Extreme Titles,” Almasy said, solemnly. “For those prizes, I will risk bodily harm.” The sound on the other end of the phone seemed largely like a “p’shaw”. “But if you beat Vince Jones, you’ll move one step closer to the GCW World Championship, the most important title in our industry!” Seymour rolled his eyes at that. It was the same shit he’d heard in every promotion he’d been in from fedheads, trying to tell him that their title was the bestest ever. As far as the Final Fantasy was concerned, anyone who thought the PTC Global Title WASN’T the most important belt in the land was a fucking moron. “The PTC Global Title has the best competitors in all of Primetime Central after it. Michael Stevens and Rich Rollins are both in this match. There’s GCW’s best right there, including me. But I also get to go against the best from all of PTC’s other feds. Karina Wolfenden, Brandon Youngblood, and Hoyt Williams from PRIME. The Mime from HSW. AWC’s John Edwards. The Uber Judge of XUW. And Shadow of Global Wrestling. Ten of the best superstars in wrestling, fighting for its most valuable prizes. I’ve got to be at the top of my game for it. I’m sorry, Jonathan, but as much as I love GCW, the PTC Global Title means more to me than GCW’s World Title ever could. “But--” The Final Fantasy was done with Jonathan Calver for the evening. He turned the phone off with a click, and threw it across the room onto the couch. Seymour Almasy had more important things to do tonight. Tommorow, he would begin his proper regimen for a match of this importance But tonight, he had to set off on an expedition across the city. In a small shop about a thirty minute walk from his house, a modern day Goronyudo Masamune had finished Seymour Almasy’s new weapon. It was a blade that would strike fear into the hearts of his allies. And, perhaps even more importantly, it would cut through his bond to Karina Wolfenden if those pesky PTC officials let him use it during the match. RIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIING! Calver again, Seymour knew. But his mood had brightened considerably. The Final Fantasy stood from his comfortable recliner, and walked to the closet, pulling out a light coat. After all, it was a brisk night out. RIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIING! Almasy smiled as he reached for the doorknob, pulling open the door to his apartment and looking out at the hallway. Soon, he would feel the slightly cool air and see the day at dusk. Not even Jonathan Calver and the incessant ringing bothered Seymour as he stepped outside and closed the door. The phone would ring uselessly in the apartment for the remainder of the night. Once again, Seymour Almasy felt alive. RIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIING! For, tonight, he had a Quest. ----------------------------------- It felt good to be in New York City again. He had been born on Long Island, but really, Seymour felt like he belonged in the largest city in the world. New York was a city of diversity. Of acceptance. The fact that he was a long-haired nut job who liked to carry a sword around with him didn’t deter most people. After all, some of the people who walk the city streets of NYC made the Final Fantasy look positively normal. Tonight, he was dressed down, letting his hair blow in the breeze behind him. A simple pair of blue jeans and a shirt, over which was his coat. For the most part, he looked like the average citygoer. Most people would have taken a car to their destination, but Seymour was not most people. Oddly, the New York Department of Motor Vehicles frowns on giving nut jobs drivers licenses. Even more oddly, they weren’t willing to give Seymour a chocobo license either, so Seymour had no choice but to walk around the city. He didn’t mind too much, though. One saw all sorts of interesting things in the city. “REPENT YOUR SINS, BROTHERS AND SISTERS! FOR THE END IS NIGH!” “And hears them, too,” Seymour observed, before fixing his eyes on the originator of the statement. Standing at the intersection, with a makeshift pulpit erected in front of him, was a man who had to be considered a religious fanatic. Written across the pine was the simple phrase “THE END IS NEAR. BELIEVE IN JESUS!” The man himself wore simple clothing, but stood out like a sore thumb due to his huge, bushy beard. Yellow pamphlets sat stacked on his pulpit, as he handed them to all who would take them. Some kept them and read with interest. Others simply threw them on the sidewalk as soon as they thought the zealot couldn’t see. Other, more brave souls, simply refused the pamphlet all together. One of those poor men had just done so, and was now receiving a tongue lashing. “WHEN THE DAY OF JUDGMENT COMES, SIR, YOU WILL NOT BE SAVED! I TRIED TO WARN YOU!” Seymour’s eyes narrowed. Religious zealots were not among his favorite people in the world, and this one was clearly accosting people who were simply trying to get a loaf of bread at the store, or go out to dinner with their families. Something had to be done, and Seymour seemed to be the only man who could. The Final Fantasy quickened his pace, something stirring within him at the thought of the impending confrontation. Before long, he was a mere stride from the man, and a hairy hand shot out a flash of yellow in front of his face. “MY BROTHER, LISTEN TO ME! THE END IS--” “You can save it,” Seymour said, “for someone who cares or believes what you have to say. You ought to be ashamed of yourself.” The zealot stared back at Almasy with a blank look. A miracle occurred, as for a moment, the man shut up. Only for a moment, though. “ASHAMED?! I AM SPREADING THE WORD OF GOD! YOU UNGRATEF--” “Are you a priest,” Seymour shot back. “Are you a bishop? No. You’re just a guy off the street trying to spread panic! You’re making people fear your God and your messiah! And because of what, your beliefs? No, you’re just trying to line your pockets, you hypocrite!” Seymour pointed to the damning evidence, a collection plate sitting next to the stack of pamphlets. “I NEED FUNDS TO CARRY OUT THE LORD’S WORK! WHO ARE YOU TO TELL ME WHAT TO DO?!” Almasy sighed at the man. It was unlikely that he’d understand, but perhaps an ancedote from Seymour’s personal history would help explain things. “Once upon a time,” he said, “I believed in a god called Yevon. I spread his word to the masses, whether they liked it or not, and assaulted all those who disagreed. Would you attack your fellow man if he disagreed with your beliefs?” “OF COURSE, MY BROTHER! AS A MATTER OF FACT, IF YOU DON’T STOP--” Seymour waved his hand, and shook his head. “I’m not here for a fight. The fact is, I realized that I was wrong. God…gods, depending what you believe…religion’s not about panic. It’s not about fear. Religion is about respect. Faith. Love. And by telling these believers that their world is about to end, you are inspiring terror. Please, think of what you’re doing to these poor men and women. If you really were their brother, you would not mistreat and worry them so with your false rantings. Don’t take their money anymore.” It was an earnest Seymour Almasy that looked back at his fellow man. He even smiled at the zealot, hoping that his words had taken effect. “BURN IN THE FIRES OF HELL, YOU SINNER!” … Well, crap. Really, that was the other reason Seymour hated zealots. So bloody set in their ways. If the man could not be reasoned with, other action would have to be taken. Almasy’s leg shot out, delivering a mighty kick to the pulpit. It was shoddily constructed, and so Almasy’s kick sent it tumbling to the concrete like a house of cards. Coins and pamphlets flew everywhere, leaving nothing between the Seymour and the faux-preacher, who looked ready to kill. For nearly a minute, nothing happened, until finally the zealot continued lambasting the Final Fantasy. “PILATE! YOU HAVE BETRAYED YOUR GOD! I WILL EXACT HIS VENGEANCE ON HIS BEHALF, YOU VILE, LOATHESOME BEING!” Seymour looked at the man, ascertaining the situation. The other man had whipped himself into a veritable frenzy. He was red-faced. He was frothing at the mouth. But most of all, he was entirely and utterly impotent. “If you really wanted to,” Seymour said, already turning to continue on his way, “you already would have.” The zealot raised his meaty fists in anger, and screamed at the Final Fantasy a bit more, but as Seymour predicted, he did nothing. One intersection later, and Seymour could no longer hear the rantings. “Figures this happens to me tonight,” he grumbled, looking up at the still-setting sun. “All I wanted was to get my sword.” The mighty blade sat in his mind’s eye. It was the practical end of his quest. Sure, he’d have to walk back afterwards, but he’d have his new item. He could look at it. Turn it over in his hands. It would be tangible, and it would be his. All his. He passed stores that were familiar to him. The Electronics Boutique where he bought most of his video games. The McDonald’s where he had eaten every Monday and Thursday before his return required him to get back on a proper eating regimen. It was a familiar journey, Seymour knew. He went to the swordsmith often. Usually he was there to make a smaller purchase, such as a dagger, or perhaps a replica blade. This time, though, he was after the real deal. Soon, Seymour walked in front of the local electronics store. It was a mom and pop operation, but it always had the latest in stock, and so it was Seymour’s favorite. Men in suits on one of the televisions in the window, though, caught his attention, and so he paused in time to see a CNN recap of the swearing in of Chief Justice John Roberts. "I, John Roberts, do solemnly swear that I will support and defend the Constitution of the United States," said the Chief Justice, to applause from those in attendance. Behind him, Seymour heard a voice which was clearly none too approving of the new leader of the Supreme Court. “That man’s going to destroy our freedoms,” the female voice said, to approval from her companion. Seymour pondered the point as he continued walking. That, it seemed, was one of the problems of being a judge. People required you to solve big problems. They gave you the power to do so, and yet society was never entirely happy. There was always someone who complained, and worse, they usually had a pretty good reason to do so. It was thankless work. Depressing, too. In the pit of his soul, Seymour wondered why anyone would do it. Deep down, though, he understood why. They were genuinely good people, judges, men and women willing to take such burdens on their shoulders for the greater good. At the same time, though, there was something he disliked about judges. They were…not emotional enough. They could proclaim a man’s death with no emotion, no remorse. All that was needed was the banging of a gavel. A gavel, Seymour decided, was a good thing to have. Not as good as a sword, of course, but still good nonetheless. Soon, he was startled from his thoughts by the streetlights snapping on. The sun was now barely visible, only a tiny crescent illuminating a small part of the sky, but that was okay. Manmade light was taking over for Mother Nature. Another intersection later, and Seymour had a decision to make. He could go the long way if he had to, but it would add time to his trip. Besides, it was getting late, and he wanted to get his sword and return home. That left the shortcut, then. He’d taken it many times, but always during the day. This would be a new experience for him. For a moment or two, he considered his anxieties, and laughed out loud. He was on a Quest. What was a little bit of danger, especially to a man who had survived some of the most dangerous contests in professional wrestling, and some of its most violent competitors. A simple alleyway was nothing. Less than nothing. Seymour never was the cautious sort anyway. Looking both ways, Seymour bravely made his way across the New York City street. All things considered, it was probably the most dangerous thing he’d do all night. But he made it across the street with all limbs intact, and took a deep breath. The alleyway was dark and foreboding. The only light that he could see in it came from a small lantern hanging on something, there for God only knew what reason. Taking a deep breath, Seymour stepped forward into the abyss, and he felt really silly for feeling as nervous as he was. He wasn’t the sort to be scared of the dark. Though Seymour Almasy was terrified of at least a dozen things, the dark was not one of them. Still, though, he couldn’t help the odd sense of foreboding that tore through his being at that very moment. Thoughts of his blade calmed him, if only a bit. Soon he’d have it with him, and then it would be silly to be afraid. He thought of its sharp blade and glittering sword. It would be the best sword in the entire world, and he would strap it into its scabbard and all would be right. In the soft glow of the lantern, however, Seymour noticed that something was NOT right. Against the wall of the alley he saw an image looming dangerously against the light. It appeared to be several times larger than he was, and it appeared to be moving closer. The Final Fantasy’s heart beat rapidly in his chest. His sword wasn’t with him. He was defenseless against this….whatever THIS happened to be. Terror quaked his knees, but he wasn’t going to go anywhere. Seymour Almasy did not run away from his problems. He met them head on, and accepted the consequences. Bracing himself, Seymour prepared to turn around. He tore his eyes from the damned shade in front of him, and prepared to face his fate. “Ready or not,” he whispered, under his breath, “here I come.” His hands balled into fists as he whirled, turning around and gaping slack-jawed at what he saw. “You…” was all that he could manage, for now.
