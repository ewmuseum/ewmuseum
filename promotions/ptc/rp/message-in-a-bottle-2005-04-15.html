"Message in a Bottle"

15 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-04-15 * * * * * Oxford, Pennsylvania is a town with one main street bisecting the town into two halves, appropriately named Main Street. The town is small enough that Main Street is bookended by two traffic lights, the only two within a 5-mile radius. The town itself is home to nearly 3,000 people, and the surrounding boroughs double that tally. In 1967, Oxford sent two of its sons to Vietnam. The best of friends since adolescence, they honored their town and country by serving together in the armed forces. In 1969, one came home encased in an oak coffin, buried a hero to all who knew him. To this day, a larger-than-life sized bronze statue stoically stands near the center of town, in a narrow strip of grass once coveted as the local park. Rural sprawl along with the constantly expanding spider-web of state highways have robbed a once lush plot of land of its size and beauty, emaciating it down to a few hundred feet across. Forever enshrined in bronze, or perhaps as long as the highways and expanding residential developments will allow, Archibald Matthews remains a prominent figure in the small farming community. In 1973, Oxford’s sole-surviving son returned home a decorated war hero. Brash and arrogant before entering the military, he was now humble and emotionally ravaged. Promptly taking this newfound outlook on life, Christopher MacGregor started a new tour of duty all his own: finding a wife, and starting a family. Nearly two years to the day since his return home, Christopher’s wishes became a reality. His first, and only, son was born into the world on May 4th, 1975: Archibald Allen MacGregor. Named in honor of his fallen friend. Oxford’s lone casualty of war. “Honor the fallen!” Christopher MacGregor would constantly instill upon young Archibald as he grew over the years. He did so, day-in and day-out, right up until the very morning Christopher dressed in his military-issued uniform, saluted the bronze statue of his best friend, and shot himself through the temple. * * * * * ”Oh Archie,” Heather Davis started, biting her lip, “I…I don’t know how to put this.” She paused a moment, her gaze floating like a feather around the living room of Archie MacGregor – Archie was her pet name for Archibald, even though he preferred to be called by his middle name, Allen. He stood, leaning against the fireplace, a glass of Scotch in his right hand. He was slouched over, his brown hair somewhat disheveled and the faintest shadow of day-old stubble darkening his square jaw. Archibald’s sand-colored eyes never matched her gaze, because he knew what was happening. It had happened before. “I can’t go on dealing with this, Archie. I’ve tried for nearly a year. But you’re just bringing me down with you, and I won’t let that happen. I can’t let that happen. You know I care for you, I really do…but something has to change. Hopefully this will slap you across the face, and let you address your own problems. But, only time will tell.” From behind a few loose strands of her blonde hair, Heather’s hazel eyes began to well up with tears. “Archie, please look at me.” She demanded, her voice faintly quivering. Even the bravest and most selfless actions sometimes cause pain to others, and Heather was feeling it. She loved him, or she thought she did, but much like a scale with an excessive amount of weight to one side, she felt it was impossible for a perfect balance in their relationship. Archibald took a sip from his glass, the small cubes of ice rattling against each other. His gaze remained away from Heather’s, staring out at the cobalt colored sky. He just didn’t understand, not completely. This was the third breakup since his mother passed, not more than four years ago. As Heather sobbed on the couch, Archibald refused to look at her. He couldn’t look at her. Because if he finally matched her gaze, the only thing he saw would be memories of her that he cherished. The way she clasped her hands together over her mouth as she laughed, undoubtedly ashamed of a smile that brightens a room, even though she shouldn’t be. He would remember the nights they spent laying out on his roof, watching the constellations slowly trace their way across the endless vault of the nighttime sky. The Friday dates to the local ice cream parlor, watching her try to eat the chocolate cone before the summertime heat melted it across her lithe fingers – with Heather always failing in the process. The hopeless-romantic inspired poems Heather would write as she lay in bed beside him, of which she would read aloud even though she thought he was asleep. Like Atlas carrying the globe on his shoulders, the weight was becoming too heavy. Looking at her would invigorate and revitalize those memories, a weight of which he wasn’t prepared to carry At least, not right now. Archibald also realized, from this day forward, that no new memories would be created. It was a sinking feeling. He threw back the remaining Scotch, quickly pouring himself another glass. The ice once again danced and cracked under the warmth of the freshly poured whiskey. Life posed a lot of questions, but for right now, the only answers Archibald sought were supplied in the form of a smooth, tanned liquid. By the time the ordeal was through, Archibald was quickly approaching his fifth glass of Scotch. Heather Davis left his life through the front door of his small house, walking out into the glaring midday sun, only turning to catch him finally staring at her. She was adamant at citing the fact that he needed to stay away from alcohol, that he needed to focus his life on something that he loved. He poured another glass, and buried his face into his palms, sobbing ever so slightly. The alcohol dulled the edge of the blade that was sticking into his heart, but so has previous experience in breakups. No matter how much Scotch he could drink, it would still hurt somewhere down the line. But he had to try anyhow, to momentarily dull the pain. Not more than ten minutes later, the doorbell rang and Archibald MacGregor’s face shot out of his palms, looking to see if Heather was standing in the doorway. Instead, there was an old man in a business suit wearing a bowler hat. Archibald had no idea his life was about to change forever. * * * * * I know I have my problems. Nobody needs to lecture me on what I do to myself, or what I do to others. I might not appear to care, but just like some of the world’s greatest treasures, it’s there, buried deep. Eventually, that treasure of caring will be found, and I’m sure someone will enter my life to unearth that little seed of compassion, blossoming it in into a magnificent tree. But right now, that’s not the case. I’m still hidden in the shadows of confusion and delusion. Have you ever been forced into doing something that you don’t really like, only to appease someone you care about? That was the story of my childhood, up until Dad decided to end his life. He was the heavy foot that was constantly pressed against me, mashing me to the floorboard in hopes of finding my one great talent. As I accelerated under his pressure, it was evident we were heading for a wall. Disappointment was imminent, I always told myself. I knew what I was good at, and what I wasn’t good at. Each time he forced me into a new sport, I already knew I wasn’t going to excel. “Come on, Archibald! Don’t you want to honor the fallen? Live up to your namesake!” he would say. He shot himself in the head before I finally found that talent. It wasn’t baseball, it wasn’t football, it wasn’t painting, nor was it writing. It was wrestling. Laugh if you want to, but after Dad decided to get a head start on his way to Hell, I found what I call my true passion in life. Being a professional wrestler. I just got a later start than most in the sport, but that’s ok. In fact, I’m sure these memoirs will be found years down the line and be cast off as the drunken ramblings of Archibald MacGregor. I just hope they don’t overshadow whatever career I might carve out as a wrestler. I would much rather have the people wonder why a 29 year old started writing memoirs. I would rather have them wonder what events could give a man – a young man like myself – reason to put his life into literature. In due time, you’ll find out. I promise you that. But what I can’t promise you is that all of this will make sense. I haven’t exactly been known for my sobriety, which is something I’ve come to terms with ever since my girlfriend left me a month ago. * * * * * ”I have something that will astound you, Mr. MacGregor. A product unlike anything you’ve ever experienced.” The man said, his smile earnest and sincere. To Archibald, the man looked vaguely familiar. Perhaps he had passed him on Main Street in the past few years, or had seen him at the local pub. But he couldn’t recall a name. His confusion was also helped along by the numerous glasses of Scotch that currently churned in his stomach. His Blood Alcohol Level was currently: drunk and confused. Slurring his speech slightly, Archibald replied back, “Unless you can make a woman forgive and forget, you don’t have anything I want. Or perhaps if you’re selling the key to unlocking the mysteries of the female species…” The salesman was wearing a small bowler hat and a perfectly pressed, and equally black, suit. His eyes were probably once blue a few decades earlier, but the rough hand of time has since robbed them of their natural luster, now appearing slightly gray and foggy. His smile was his best asset for a sale, because it made Archibald feel comfortable, even in his own slightly inebriated stupor. But what alerted Archibald the most was the salesman’s voice: it didn’t quite match his appearance, seemingly coming from a man nearly half his age. It was young, brash, and a heavy baritone. “I’m afraid I can’t offer you that, but what I can offer you won’t cost you a thing. In fact, I’m here to deliver a sample. If you like what I give you, you call the phone number provided, and I’ll return promptly. Then we’ll talk business.” The warm and sincere smile appeared once more. The voice still lingered in Archibald’s mind. Perhaps if he hadn’t thrown back so much Scotch, he would be able to discern from where he knew this gentleman. More than likely, it was the local loony trying to make a few bucks off of a scam – but damned or drunk, Archibald took the man up on his offer. “What are you offering?” MacGregor replied, trying to match the salesman’s smile. His was crooked with Scotch and weighed down by the loss of another girlfriend. From a small leather pouch that reminded Archibald of the bags Doctor’s used in movies, the salesman produced a dark green bottle, “Only the finest Brandy to ever hit these shores. When you’ve seen the world, such as I have, you pick up some trade secrets. I’m willing to share some of those secrets with you.” Archibald’s eyes lit up, and the salesman noticed. His hands were old, appeared brittle, and very pale. Up until this moment, Archibald hadn’t noticed just how light the salesman’s skin appeared, especially contrasted by his dark suit and hat. While not ghostly or even as white as Archibald had seen someone before, the lack of color in the salesman’s skin was certainly lighter than it should be. Archibald just chalked it up to his age, reminding himself that not everyone could sit outside and soak up the sunshine. Hell, even Archibald was a little less colored than he should be this time of the year. He chalked that up to being lazy, though. “So…this is free of charge?” Archibald asked, more than willing to accept any sort of alcohol as long as it was a gift. Some gifts were given at the appropriate time, and Archibald couldn’t think of a better gift for his current situation. “That’s correct. Now I understand that it’s not a large bottle, probably will only give you two full glasses. But I can’t be giving away my stockpile, now can I?” the salesman said with a chuckle. “And if I want more, just call the number?” Archibald asked, eyeing the label for a telephone number. When he finally located it, he glanced over it once more, just to make sure he actually found it. “That’s correct.” The man said, taking off his bowler hat and slightly bowing, “Now if you’ll excuse me, I’ll be on my way. There are other customers to attend to. Enjoy that Brandy, dear boy, I think it’ll change your life.” Archibald thought that was a bold statement, but refused to diminish the salesman’s pitch by saying that statement audibly. He simply nodded at the salesman, who winked one of his pale gray eyes, spinning on his heels, and briskly walking down the path towards the street. As the salesman slowly strolled down the sidewalk, Archibald thought he heard him whistling “Jimmy Crack Corn”. His eyes sank to the bottle of Brandy, and his thoughts once again returned to Heather, or the lack thereof. He needed to forget that she wouldn’t be in his life anymore, and the cure to that problem was currently encased in a green bottle with only a telephone number on the label. Archibald Allen MacGregor turned towards the kitchen, twisting the seal off of the bottle as he walked. He didn’t quite understand what Heather meant when she left, but he figured that he would figure it out over a glass of Brandy. * * * * * There are classes at colleges around the country that study the artwork of Picasso, Da Vinci, and all the other foreign guys who decided to throw some paint on canvas. People study the depth of the paintings, about what they really mean. They see the beauty in the color choices, the objects in the foreground and the background, etcetera. There are sports fans who remember that one homerun that won the World Series, or that one touchdown pass that turned the season around. They admire these feats for what they are – a pure example of their sport in it’s greatest form. When I see a guillotine choke applied, I get that same feeling. When I see a perfectly executed snap-suplex, I get that feeling. I’m almost 30 years of age, and for the last 5 years I have gone over the history of professional wrestling, finding new heroes that stand up to the likes of Mickey Mantle, Ted Williams, and Joe Namath. Or whomever you should ever root for in your favorite sport. The greats of the sport are what drive you to get better at it, because no matter how selfless a person might be, in the end they want to be great at something. Right now, I’m great at something I don’t like to admit. I’m great at something I can’t control. But what I would like to be great at, and what a lot of people in the business say I could be great at…is wrestling. So upon finding out that I would be facing off against one of wrestling’s living legends, I took pride in the chance to dethrone someone that is held to such high standards. At the same time, I fear that my lack of experience will be detrimental to my success…but I remind myself that everyone has their chance to stumble out of the blocks, it’s just the constant determination to get to the finish line that separates the podium finishers from the rest of the field. Olympic gold from watching the event at home, wondering. A reporter asked me the other day, undoubtedly researching for some Internet gossiping site, how I felt to be going up against the former champion of the tournament. “How are you going to climb the mountain known as Seymour Almasy?” he asked. I had watched Discovery Channel the night before, and simply told him that there are certain objects on this planet that can move, or possibly destroy, mountains. That can carve away at something of which we view as unmovable, leaving it scarred forever. I told him that I would be a glacier to Almasy’s mountain. Mountains just sit there, waiting for people to conquer them, and eventually…some people do. But glaciers are brooding and slow moving, and impossible to stop. Get in their path, and they’ll eat away at you layer by layer, until you’re just a fragment of your former self. They engulf whatever is in their path, grinding it down slowly and surely, until hardly anything is left. Rubble. Stones. Whatever. They cut mountains, leaving canyons. And I’m no different. That might sound arrogant, especially going against someone with his track record. But lately, a few have climbed the mountain known as Seymour Almasy and come out at the summit, victorious. Who’s to say that I’m different? Lately, he’s been assuming a lot about his opponents. Assumptions, especially in wrestling, are what get you beat. Granted, I might be a lush. So I might not feel the blows as I normally would. But if there’s one thing that I love as much as a smooth glass of Scotch, it’s making someone scream out in submission. Making them give up all hope at coming out victorious, simply because escape is impossible. Which is something, sadly, that I can completely relate with. * * * * * Apparently 5 glasses of Scotch, and a small bottle of Brandy was sufficiently enough alcohol to erase the entire afternoon and the ensuing evening. Archibald MacGregor woke up to the warmth of a brilliant golden sunrise peaking through the windows. While the cobalt sky he remembered was now painted an amber-peach color, he was still in the same clothes he wore the previous day, and the stubble that adorned his face was now thicker and rougher. He sat up, yawning as he stretched his arms out. In the nearby trees, the birds sang their songs to each other, perhaps happily courting each other as the sun wiped away the coolness of night. Rubbing both of his light brown eyes vigorously, Archibald was trying to remember exactly the events of the previous afternoon. When he couldn’t, he was confident that his mission was, sadly, accomplished. He could barely remember Heather leaving him, and he could barely remember the aged salesman who bestowed him with the gift that allowed him to accomplish his task: the bottle of Brandy. A small part of Archibald was ashamed about drinking himself into such a stupor that his grief was only merely a side effect to his throbbing hangover, but if balanced on a scale against his delight for doing so, it would be heavily outweighed. The green bottle was on the floor, having rolled slightly under the coffee table. He picked it up and managed to feign a smile, “You…did a wonderful job. I might need to stock up on you.” How pathetic, talking to an empty bottle of Brandy. With a chuckle to himself, he tossed the bottle onto the couch next to him, fully intending to call the salesman later in the day to place an order. Except when the bottle gently landed on the cushion, it sounded like something was rattling around on the inside of the jade colored glass. Archibald held the bottle up to the sun, momentarily turning the peach morning sun into a brilliant sapphire gem, and noticed that there seemed to be an object inside of the bottle. Was this a new type of Brandy, he wondered? Like Tequila, except instead of a worm, there appeared to be a little piece of paper wrapped up and resting against the wall of the bottle. Archibald turned the bottle upside-down and tapped the base. The wrapped piece of paper shot out through the neck of the bottle immediately, falling onto his lap. It was tied with a small red ribbon, as if gift-wrapped. He chuckled to himself, picturing the elderly salesman placing a small piece of paper in each bottle of Brandy, and then resealing each one individually. Just like a fortune cookie, except with a much stronger proof. The salesman’s own gimmick for his own Brandy, how cute. Archibald gently untied the red ribbon, and unraveled the small piece of paper, which was remarkably dry for being submerged in a bottle of Brandy for God knows how long. After a quick scan over the piece of paper, which contained only a single sentence in equally crimson inked letters, Archibald MacGregor’s stomach suddenly knotted up harder than any liquor had ever done to it. The note posed a question, “Honor the fallen?” Quickly scanning the rest of the note, his hangover merely an afterthought at this point in time, Archibald noticed that a small name that appeared in the bottom rightmost corner. A name that made everything suddenly make sense, and at the same time…not make sense. He sat there, dumbfounded at this revelation, piecing together a puzzle in which some pieces were blurry and distorted with Scotch and Brandy. The puzzle fit together, yet, it seemed like it shouldn’t. The man’s face. Archibald remembered that it seemed so familiar, as if he knew him or met him before. And when Archibald read the name at the bottom of the note, it finally fell into place. He had seen the salesman almost everyday growing up, standing in the center of town, unmoving. Sure, add a few years to the face, but the resemblance was definitely there. After all, how drunk must he have been yesterday afternoon to forget the face of the man his Father sternly instilled into his everyday life? The note was signed: ”Your friend, Archibald Matthews” * * * * *
