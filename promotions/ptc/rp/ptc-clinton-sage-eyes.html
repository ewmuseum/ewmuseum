"Eyes"

28 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Wrestler: Clinton Sage Fed: Free Agent * * * * * As Clinton Sage stepped through the curtains, the roar of the crowd didn’t die down immediately. In fact, it seemed to only amplify. They were booing him for his actions inside the squared-circle not more than 5 minutes prior. He stood just past the curtains, and listened. The sound was deafening, and as he made his way towards the backstage area, he could almost feel the thunderous disapproval rattling his ribcage. He had lost the match on purpose, tapping out to the champions submission move almost as quickly as he was put into it. But as soon as the bell sounded, he snapped up to his feet and began the beating, sending three heavy fists crashing into their beloved champions face. Then he locked on his finisher, a Triangle Choke, until his opponents face was nearly the same shade of blue you would find in the daytime sky. Where Clinton Sage saw beauty, the crowd saw horror and disgust. Blood still trickled down from his knuckles and between his fingers. It was still pooling on the canvas in the center of the ring from the gash in his opponents face. The crowd continued to rattle the arena’s foundation, their jeers reverberating without end. He stood mulling over the fact that beyond the thin veil of curtains, more than 10,000 people screamed with displeasure at his actions. They hated him because of what he had done to their champion, a man that stood for everything that was right and moral. Clinton shuddered as he wiped the blood from his knuckles, gently rolling his fingers and smearing it between his fingertips. It was clumped underneath his fingernails, and he stared at the darkened blood, wondering: What does the blood of my enemy taste like? As Clinton Sage raised his coated fingertips towards his mouth, two uniformed police officers strolled up the hallway and stopped only a stride away from him. “Clinton Arthur Sage?” one officer asked, his palm resting atop his pistol, probably out of habit. He had to raise his voice to be heard over the chorus of booing still going on in the arena. Clinton smiled, and wondered if it was because he was ready to lick the still-wet blood from his fingertips. “That’s me,” Clinton replied, deciding against his arterial dinner. He would have to save it for another time, “now what do you want?” “I’m Officer Toomes, and this is Officer Haddleston, we will be your escorts tonight.” Clinton quickly studied Toomes and Haddleston. Toomes was broad, but baby faced. A slight flush to his cheeks gave him the boyish quality, even though he was the size of a professional football player. Haddleston looked gaunt and older, with age pulling the skin under his eyes into puffy bags. He tipped his head at his introduction, perhaps a throwback to old Southern Hospitality. He looked to be in great shape for his age, but deteriorating. Clearly the police had sent their two strong-arms, incase Clinton had resisted. “Your presence has been requested in Harrisburg. We have been instructed to let you shower and get dressed, and then escort you to the state penitentiary.” Toomes replied, motioning down the hallway towards Sage’s dressing room. “For what?” “You’re not under arrest, don’t worry. Just don’t wear anything metal, or bring an object that could be used as a weapon, such as a pen…” Officer Haddleston finally chimed in, his voice coarse from years of smoking, and heavy with a Southern drawl. “Would you mind explai—“ Clinton started, only stopping because both officers stepped forward and proceeded to direct him down the hallway towards his dressing room. Haddleston shoved slightly, “Harlan Raines has requested your presence, Mr. Sage.” Clinton stopped in his tracks, searching his memory for any reason as to why that name sounded familiar. When it hit him, it landed a blow harder than any opponent ever could have. His pace was restarted again as the two officers shoved him down the hallway, “We don’t have all day, Mr. Sage. Get showered and dressed, we have a long drive ahead of us. Officer Toomes and I will be waiting.” Toomes smirked, “Yeah, wouldn’t want to keep someone on Death Row waiting, would we?” Clinton Sage was suddenly relieved he hadn’t sampled the blood from his fingers – it would have given him something in common with the serial killer who had requested his presence. * * * * * The ride to Harrisburg would take slightly over an hour, Clinton figured. He lived outside of Philadelphia, and knew his own state pretty decently. Normally it would take longer, except for the fact that the officers had turned on the flashing beacons atop the police cruiser, which was hurtling down the highway at a high speed. They even notified the Pennsylvania State Police about their speedy journey. The police cruiser sliced its way through the traffic like a mythical serpent through the sea, its fearful cry in the form of a wailing siren. The other cars scurried to the slower lanes without much hesitation. The first half hour of the journey was made in silence, except for the occasional scream of the siren when a car refused to immediately surrender their path. Clinton sat and wondered exactly what was going on, and why would a convicted murderer – a serial killer who had killed at least 12 people – demand his presence? Clinton Sage found it humorous that this was the first time he had ever been inside of a police car. For all of the horrible things he had done in his life, most of them were allowed by the nature of his sport. Had the beating he gave only an hour earlier been in any other sporting outlet, he would be riding in the back of this police cruiser in handcuffs, a criminal. Instead, he was being escorted. He almost felt like royalty, except for the nagging curiosity of the situation that continued to tug at him. Toomes was driving, with Haddleston riding shotgun. Haddleston had lit a cigarette and was being courteous enough to blow the smoke out of a crack in the passenger’s window. Between drags, he turned and looked at Clinton, “So, wrestling. How does it pay?” Clinton noted the genuine interest in his voice, “I’m being escorted to the state’s capital, aren’t I?” Toomes snorted in mild disgust, “Is that how you look at this? An escort? Aren’t you at least curious as to why you’re being, “ Toomes paused, over-emphasizing the word, “escorted to a face-to-face meeting with Harlan Raines?” Clinton ignored Toomes, and focused on Haddleston. He seemed to be genuinely interested. He gave off the natural curiosity of a pure wrestling fan, so Clinton felt this was an opportunity to toy with his police escorts, “To answer your question, it pays decent, but the real money is made in merchandising.” ”For someone so hated by the fans, I would think that your merchandise doesn’t sell as well.” Haddleston replied before taking another drag off of his cigarette. He had just proved Clinton’s assumption of being a wrestling fan. Haddleston was obviously aware of his actions in and out of the ring. “To an extent, Officer Haddleston, to an extent. But at the same time, there are countless fans who refuse to conform, and would rather seem edgy by branding themselves in my merchandise. They think of themselves as being cool and, perhaps, outlawed. Rebels, but in fact…they’re just money in my pockets. Gullible and easily deceived.” ”That’s a hell of a way to talk about your fan base,” Toomes said with a slight laugh as he weaved the car through traffic. Clinton Sage watched through the metal barricade as Haddleston ignored his partners comment. He studied the aging cop, who seemed to have an honest interest in the wrestling business. He found him to be gullible and unintelligent, just like the wrestling fans that bought his merchandise. “Then why don’t you change your image? Be the ‘good guy’ for once, and you’ll be even richer.” “Your so-called ‘good guys’ are fake and fallible. They rely on morals to make their judgment, and often overlook the harsh realities of life with a blinding ignorance. Like my opponent tonight, for example. He thought he had me beat, and assumed that since the match was over, so was the beating. But now he’s probably getting stitches, and I’m sitting here talking to you…unharmed, and happy in the knowledge that my actions put thousands of people into a volatile state of disapproval.” “Perhaps some day,” Toomes interjected, “someone will come along and give you the moral foundation you seemingly lack. And you’ll look at the world differently, becoming the thing you apparently hate the most: a admirable person.” “I really doubt that, Officer Toomes.” Clinton said matter-of-factly. “So do I.” Toomes replied, his eyes darting up towards the rear-view mirror for a moment, studying Clinton’s face. Toomes watched a grin cross Clinton Sage’s face, as the wrestler tilted his head back and closed his eyes. He was probably trying to imagine how he would live such a horrible moralized life. * * * * * Clinton Sage sat in the interrogation room, waiting. The room itself wasn’t much bigger than your average bedroom, except that one wall was completely covered by a two-way mirror, and security cameras hung in opposing corners. It was painted a battleship gray, and the steel table that was bolted down to the floor in the center of the room could have also come from a seagoing vessel. There were two doors in opposite corners of the room, undoubtedly one for visitors – as Clinton had entered through – and the other for the prisoners and security personnel. Clinton glanced over at the two-way mirror, wondering who was standing behind it. Watching. A moment later, the opposite door opened and two armed guards escorted Harlan Raines towards the table. As they restrained the serial killer by looping his arm and leg irons through iron rings welded onto the table and floor, Clinton studied the man. Harlan Raines appeared to be nearing 50 years old. His black hair was starting to gray, and was combed backwards away from his brow, which was the most prominent feature on his face. Raines had a strong brow that protruded enough to give him a permanent scowl, and perhaps in dim lighting, hide his eyes in shadow. Physically he had an average frame, although his orange prison jumpsuit could possibly be hiding a more toned physique. His jumpsuit was unzipped down to his black belt, and Raines wore no shirt underneath. For a moment, they stared at each other. The two guards left through the door they had entered. They were alone. ”I suppose you want an autograph?” Clinton finally asked. “Do you know who I am, Mr. Sage?” Raines said in a cool and calm voice, ignoring Clinton’s sarcasm. “It’s hard to live in Pennsylvania and not know of Harlan Raines.” Clinton noticed that Raines never removed his stare. His eyes were the color of sand. “I suppose you’d like to know why I’ve brought you here, correct?” Clinton nodded, trying to suppress his wandering mind. Mentally, he could see the pictures from the newspapers that Raines had sent out, with the corpses all sitting around a table, bloody. He tried to forget the fact that he was ready to taste the blood of someone else only hours prior. “We’ll get to that. But I want to ask you something else,” Raines paused, his voice never showing an ounce of emotion, “Do you enjoy what you do?” Clinton shook his head at the statement out of confusion, then looked at the two-way mirror in disgust. Raines never removed his stare to match Clinton’s, “Is this some sort of joke?” “They won’t be answering any questions, only asking them.” Clinton noticed that Harlan Raines never seemed to smile, which most people do unconsciously in natural conversation. He glanced back towards the killer“Let me get this straight. You’re on Death Row, and this is your last little fleeting chance at digging under someone’s skin, isn’t it? You drag a total stranger out here in the middle of the night, just so you can ask them if they enjoy their job?” Clinton demanded, his voice failing to remain as calm as the convicted killer. “Two things, Mr. Sage. We’re hardly strangers, and when I asked you if you enjoyed what you do, it was you who assumed I was talking about your wrestling matches…your job.” Never one to contain his anger, Clinton shot to his feet and stared at Harlan Raines. He walked over to the two-way mirror, and talked directly into it. “I want to leave. You’ve wasted my time.” “They’re not going to allow you to leave until I want you gone, Clinton.” Raines said, still watching. His head was turned towards Clinton Sage, but his body remained perfectly still. In the reflection of the mirror, it looked odd to Clinton. The killer repeated his question, “Do you enjoy what you do?” “If you don’t mean the wrestling, what do you mean?” Harlan Raines sat there, weighing the question for a moment. Clinton searched his eyes for any sort of clue as to the killer’s intention, but only found vacancy. An odd vacancy, which made Clinton feel uneasy. Raines replied, “I mean, making people hate you. Turning the public against you because they just don’t seem to understand your motives. I’m aware of what you do, Mr. Sage, and I’m also aware of the publics reluctance to accept it. I guess you could say we’re alike, in that respect. But do you enjoy it, the vehement disapproval?” “First, we’re not alike. Create whatever connections between you and I in your mind, but we are two different types of people. I can tell the difference – and have the intelligence – to understand that what you did was wrong. You probably still can’t grasp that concept. Secondly…Yes, I enjoy the fact that they hate me. It gives me some solace knowing that what I do pisses them off.” Finally showing the first sign of emotion, Harlan smiled. “We’re more alike than you’d like to admit.” “Is that all you’ve brought me here for? To belittle how I choose to live my life? Or do you have something else to waste my time with?” Clinton demanded, walking half the length of the table towards Raines. “I suppose you’re right, Mr. Sage. I can see how this could be insulting, and that wasn’t my intent. I apologize. My intent was to…enlighten.” “Enlighten!?!” Clinton laughed at the comment. “Through the eyes of hell, I saw the world differently. I saw there was a way to ascend past this world, and into the next. So I took the lives of those who could help me make that journey.” Raines started, then paused as he looked up at Clinton Sage. Clinton tried not to remember the details of Raines’ crimes. He tried not to remember the coverage the killings had created in the area. The fact that the bodies had never been recovered, and Raines was tried and convicted based solely on the photos he had sent to the press sent a shiver cascading down Clinton’s spine. The stare of Harlan Raines was the color of a warm beach, but it exuded the chill of winter. Raines continued, the pitch of his voice never changing, “Through the eyes of hell, I saw a world that the general populous couldn’t understand. I saw a means to an end, and nobody else understood. Now I sit before you, only two weeks removed from my death. They will strap me to the table, and inject me with chemicals that will not only stop my lungs from working, but stop my heart as well. Much like the Ben Franklin Bridge spans the Delaware River, I will span from one life into the next. I will reach out from these shores, to the shores of immortality.” Raines fell silent. Clinton Sage didn’t know what to make of it. “Beautiful speech, asshole. Can I leave now?” “In a moment, but first, I need you to do something.” Clinton sighed out of annoyance, “And what’s that, asshole?” “You’ve probably noticed that my prison jumpsuit is unzipped down to the waist. I’d like you to remove it, and look at my back. I’m sure you’ve heard of my tattoo. Those watching on the other side of the mirror knew I was going to make this request. As soon as you look, you’re free to go.” Clinton Sage stood there, numb. Such a glorious night had been turned to confusion and insanity by the ploys of Harlan Raines. If there was any way to end it as quickly as possible, Clinton was going to pounce on it. It finally presented itself in the form of a strange request. Clinton walked behind Harlan Raines and stood for a moment. If he wanted to, he could grab the old mans neck, and snap it like a twig. He could do the world justice, and instead of waiting two weeks for the lethal injection to rid the world of Harlan Raines, he could send one of Pennsylvania’s most notorious killers to ‘the shores of immortality’ a lot quicker. “Killing me would only make you into a hero, Clinton. And we know how much you hate the approval of the masses.” Raines said, as if reading Clinton’s mind. He knew Raines was smiling, he could feel it. Clinton grabbed the neck of the jumpsuit, and pulled it down. It hung slightly on Harlan Raines’ chained arms, but was baggy enough where he could expose the tattooed back of the killer. Clinton had heard about the tattoo, but it had never been photographed. Or at least never released to the general public. It was a scene of crucifixion. A Holy Cross spanned across his shoulders and down his spine. Nailed to it was the image of Jesus, except that the face of Christ was replaced with Harlan’s own. At the base of the cross, from which it seemed to be breaking through, was the upper hemisphere of Earth. “I bet this cost a fortune.” Clinton said, completely mystified by Raines’ intent. “What binds me to the wood?” Raines asked, waiting. Clinton Sage stood confused for a moment, staring down at the killers back. He quickly searched the tattoo for these bindings, and then his eyes ultimately fell towards the hands of this crucified killer. Just like Jesus, Harlan Raines was bound to the cross by large iron spikes. A matching spike was driven through his feet, and into the wood behind it. Harlan noticed no blood seeping from the wounds. “Look closer,” Raines said, a grin appearing across his face. Then Clinton Sage saw them, retreating a step as he gasped for air. The head of each spike had a symbol on each of them, the same symbol. He pressed himself against the wall directly behind Harlan Raines, completely numb and unknowing what to do. The killer spoke, “You have 14 days, as of midnight, before they kill me. In a fortnight, I’ll be dead…the clock is ticking, Clinton.” Instinct took over, and Clinton Sage darted for the doorway from which he entered, and yanked it open. He hurled himself out into the hallway, and crumpled to the ground, horrified. Suddenly he realized what his opponent must have felt like earlier tonight – being blindsided by something you never could have expected, but knew was an underlying possibility. The only thing he could hear was the emotionless voice of Harlan Raines, repeating over and over in his mind, “We’re hardly strangers…” * * * * * On the other side of the two-way mirror, Officers Toomes and Haddleston stood and watched as Clinton Sage darted from the room in disbelief, in horror. “What the hell was that all about?” Toomes wondered aloud. “Hey, grab me the photo of Raines’ tattoo that they keep in his file.” Haddleston requested of his partner. A moment later, a large glossy photo was in Haddleston’s hands, and he was inspecting it with a magnifying glass. He immediately went to the palms and ankle of the crucified Harlan Raines, looking at the head of each nail. What he saw was a simple object: An ellipse, with a circle in the center. “Looks like an eye,” said Toomes, also looking through the magnifying glass, “But why would that scare the kid? He seemed like he had seen a ghost?” “You’re not a wrestling fan, are you?” Haddleston asked in his coarse, Southern voice. “Not particularly, no…why do you ask?” ”Because ever since Clinton Sage’s debut in the sport, everyone has always asked him what the three tattoos on his back meant. The same exact eye-symbols, in the exact same places. They are identical to Raines’ tattoos, except that Raines has obviously dressed the rest of his back up with the scene of crucifixion.” ”Wait, so you’re telling me that Sage has those eyes tattooed on his back?” “Yup, he’s always said that they were symbolic for ‘never letting your gaze fall from your enemy’, but if you ask me…I say they have a different meaning. They look old, and stretched – as if they’ve been there far longer than Sage is acknowledging.” Haddleston wondered for a minute, tearing his gaze away from the glossy photo and looking through the mirror once more. He saw Harlan Raines, chained and sitting with perfect posture in the chair. Raines turned his head towards the mirror, and spoke aloud: “Tell Mr. Sage that when he finds what he is looking for, I have arranged one more visit for him at any time he chooses, before my death of course. But he may visit only once, I pray he uses his own discretion.” Toomes looked at Haddleston, who matched his gaze, and asked one question, “What the fuck is going on here?” Harlan Raines, chained to the table, sat smiling. * * * * *
