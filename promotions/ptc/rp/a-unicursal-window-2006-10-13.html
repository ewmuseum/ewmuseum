A Unicursal Window

13 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-13 Baltimore, Maryland. The bus seat rumbled under Easton's bottom and palms, tough washed-out blue leather over a sparse layer of foam to give some paltry sense of comfort. In his dress, at least, the Canadian was minimalist: slightly faded blue jeans from six months ago, black-and-white Vans imitation sneakers, and a black t-shirt with a faded red E-REX logo on them from an era when his jeans were new. The pews of motor commerce were filled with dreary men and women who just wanted to get where they were going, grudgingly partaking in monetary oblation. Easton sighed, taking a bit of his bushy beard between lower lip and upper row of teeth. This was where it had all begun for him all those months ago. He hadn't come to Baltimore often since then, not in the least because of what it signified, but he had to come back now. He had put this off for too long, let his thoughts dance around this confrontation he must have. A bounce, a tremble. Easton closed his eyes and sucked in air through his nose. So many shadows loomed over him now, not least of which was the GTT that he had somewhat rashly signed himself up for; the call he had received from that scarcely-known associate was unexpected to say the least. Nothing could stop him from this meeting, though. He wouldn't allow anything to. So long had he brooded over this, so long had he waited for the opportunity to face his mentor, that not even an eclipse of the sun would darken his path enough to turn him back. Something bubbled behind Easton's eyes. Easton blinked. The complete and total white light that was his reality slowly faded into several disks, spotlights shining down on him. Sweat-soaked, Easton moved his limbs, arms first, testing them. His neck and shoulders burned. Memory seeped back into his brain and he immediately hated Jack Logan; the back suplex had hurt far more than Easton wanted to admit. Easton was almost jerked up into a sitting position by a loud gong sounding. A huge gasp from the crowd overwhelmed him. Easton brushed his long, wet brown hair out of his face and leaned over to look at the gaping black mouth of an entranceway. He knew who it was. Swathed in black robes, the tall, aged Native American who was Easton's mentor swept down the ramp, red fire in his black eyes. The bus rolled to a stop and Easton rose up again, rubbing his left eye and trying to see clearly. A man in a plaid blazer jostled past him and Easton glared at the man's back, but he didn't do anything for the moment. When there was a space, the merest hint of an opportunity, Easton pushed his way into the aisle and became part of the throng that hurried out of the bus and onto the sidewalk. As his soles hit the beige concrete, Easton noticed the slight chill in the air and lamented the fact that he hadn't brought a jacket along. People strolled by on their own business, mostly looking straight ahead. Those who chanced to glance at him invariably gave him a longer look than he probably warranted, wondering what exactly a short Canadian man with a lumberjack's beard was doing standing beside a bus and wearing a t-shirt that was several hundred fashion changes behind. At least his forearms were warm; he'd covered them in gauze to hide what hid beneath. They itched terribly, though, and he scratched at them. As the bus heaved away, Easton turned his head from side to side, looking the area over. He suddenly realized that he wasn't familiar with this part of the city. A groan clawed its way up from its gut and tumbled past his lips. He had no idea where the man had stayed; when he had first met his mentor, he'd woken up at a police station and the man mysteriously knew where to find him, a fact that Easton had attributed to his attunement to whatever cosmic forces the Native American mystic had ascribed. Questions, then. Easton would have to ask questions. Soon, Easton found himself standing in front of a newspaper stand. The man behind it was a wide-set sort in a white t-shirt that showed a stain on the top of his belly. Black hair was knotted into a series of intricate braids and beady eyes stared out over a bulbous nose. "What's good, buddy?" the newspaper jockey crowed, leaning forward and pressing fat hands onto the counter. He arched an eyebrow. "I like the shirt. That some counter-culture stuff? About Enron, I guess?" Easton blinked and scratched his jaw forest. "Yeah," Easton said. "Enron. Sure." He didn't expect him to know the history and many nicknames of the illustrious Easton Hall, after all. "Listen, I need some directions." "Where to?" "There's a… fortune teller." That had to be a harmless description. "Tall guy, Indian." "East Indian or Squanto?" Easton frowned a bit. "Native American." "I don't know where there's a fortune teller like that," the man told him. A grin stretched his lips comically. "Now, if you asked me where there was a Native American casino owner…" "Thanks for the help," Easton grunted, turning on his heel and walking away. The man called after him, saying something that Easton didn't register in the slightest; his attention was drawn by the itching on his forearms. He couldn't expect everybody to know where one black-robed mystic in the entire city was. In fact, Easton was at a loss as to why he assumed that the man was still in the city. Something in his gut told him that the man was still here, though, and Easton decided against his better judgment to follow his instinct. Easton missed a step as something liquid pushed his eyeballs outwards. Revenge burned in Easton's gut and infected his brain; in a rage, his black-booted foot lashed out into the midsection of "Iron" Jack Logan. Logan jolted, doubling over and squeezing his eyes shut tightly. Easton knew this was his chance, his time. He could take this. He ducked his head under Logan's arm and pulled the man up into the air. A rushing of wind sucked all the oxygen away from the ring and into the shocked mouths of the spectators. Delicately, Easton balanced the Irishman on his shoulders. He twisted then, popping Logan's hips up into the air. He dropped onto the canvas and slammed Logan face-first into the mat. Out of fire… nothing. And yet, when Easton lifted his eyes, he met those of the man standing outside the ring. The wrinkled face of Easton's teacher studied him intently. Easton could do this on his own. He didn't need the man to cheer him on, he didn't need the man's presence to boost him. A lapse of judgment took over and Easton rose, moving to the ropes. The old man could leave. That's all that Easton was going to say. Easton had this under control. Easton gripped the top cable and leaned over it, opening his mouth. In a blur of black fabric, the robed mystic had gone airborne. Easton's eyes immediately took in all of the scene, growing wide enough to swallow constellations. The robed man's palm crushed Easton's nose inwards, a hammer to a hapless block of ice. Waves of orangish pain radiated from Easton's nose and the obnoxious bog-standard cell phone ringtone chirped loudly from his pocket. Easton jammed his hand into the pocket and pulled out the phone, flipping it open and pressing it against his ear. "Hello?" "Hello, sir. This is Mary from Allstate. Is Mr. Paul—" Easton flipped the phone shut and slid it back into place, rubbing his nose gingerly. It had been a very long while since he'd received a call from Kelly, he realized. In a way, he was glad for it—the girl had stalked him to the ends of the earth and had made trying to explain anything to his wife a certain hell—but he couldn't help being worried. She'd been a fan of his, but more than that, she was a person. There was no way she just "got the hint." He hoped that she was alright. He managed, with some effort, to push Kelly out of his mind and focus on what he had come out here for: finding his once-mentor. After walking a few blocks, Easton pushed open the door to a place advertising itself as Madam Sofia's; it was so natural it could almost be attributed to force of habit except for the fact that Easton had never been in the habit of walking into more-or-less random fortune tellers' places of business. The bell over the door jingled merrily, a stark contrast to the bleak and dark room before him. Easton rubbed his eyes again, letting them get used to the lack of light. "Ah… Madam… Sofia?" Easton called hesitantly, taking a step forward. A light fizzled on, a tiny flame atop a candle. Behind it, the image of a slender, pointed jawline and a slim neck was illuminated in red. He scratched his forearms and instantly wished he hadn't. This whatever-she-was might take it as a sign of nervousness. "Young one," answered an ironically young-sounding voice. "Take a seat, my friend. I assume you have questions for me?" Easton blinked, but he didn't raise an issue; he gripped a wicker backed chair and quietly slid into it. He frowned a bit at its stiffness. "Yeah," Easton said, letting his frown fizzle away silently. "Mysteries to solve?" Easton nodded. "Yeah," he said after a bit, realizing that she might not be able to see him in this darkness; the term seer didn't necessarily mean one had great physical vision. "Quandaries written in the stars, perhaps, that you wish me to unravel by reading the cards and divining the will of spirits? All this and more, Madam Sofia can do." Easton paused for a bit. "Actually, I was hoping you knew another soothsayer in the city." Easton didn't deny to himself that he got a kick out of stealing the woman's thunder. Still, he added: "If divining the will of the spirits can get me his address, though, I suppose you can do that." "Who are you looking for?" Madam Sofia leaned forward and dispelled Easton's notions of a lone good-looking fortune teller; she was younger than most, but a wide set nose, drooping lips, and a series of rings pierced into her eyebrows did not endear herself physically to the Canadian grappler. "Perhaps I know him. I guarantee nothing." "He called himself Black Cloud," Easton said. "Tall, black clothing. Wrinkled eyebrow." "Native American." A scowl tripped across her features just long enough for Easton to notice. Easton raised an eyebrow. "Do you have something against him?" "Nothing you would understand," Madam Sofia told him breathily. Easton got the distinct feeling she had wanted to add something along the lines of "mere mortal" to the end of that; the way her lips hung slightly open after speaking suggested it to him. "Do you know where he stays?" "I do," Madam Sofia said. A scribbling sound came to Easton's ears and, before he could think further, the star reader slid a small piece of paper towards him. Easton got the impression that Madam Sofia was staring at him curiously. "Something the matter?" Pause. "I just wanted to know if… you… had something against him." Easton's lips pursed. "Nothing you would understand." Her lips quirked curiously. "I see something in your future," she told him quizzically. "An anomaly in your fate…" Her mouth wrinkled in another way. "… or fates?" Easton leaned forward. "Care to let me in on it?" "The price, dear child, is twenty dollars a reading." Easton grunted, standing up and taking the piece of paper. He looked on it to verify there was an address before stuffing it into a pocket. "I'll pass." He turned and walked around his chair, heading towards the door. His hand grasped the brass doorknob, twisting it and pushing the door open. "Take care, stranger." Easton stopped for a second before stepping out. Her voice followed him, pulsing in his ears. He closed the door and then suddenly his knees went weak. He fell back against it. Sunlight flooded out from his green pupils. Easton threw open the curtain and surged into the backstage area. The man was gone when Easton regained his coherence and the only place that mystic could have fled was back here; the crowd would have ripped him to pieces. They liked Easton enough to at least afford him that favor. Now, however, revenge was all in his hands. No one stood in the way of the tearer of muscles as he stormed through the hallways. He didn't call out any name, but the way his eyes flashed, full of rage, told everyone who took a second's look just who he was looking for. The man in the black cloak. Each and every one of them had seen where he had gone, but they were afraid—either of the mystic or of Easton himself—and said nothing. It was all the same to Easton, though. He had caught sight of the robed man. The mystic turned the corner and Easton caught up to him, laying a hand on his shoulder. Surprised, Easton's mentor turned around to face him. The Canadian dipped his head respectfully and then, with all the force he could muster, shot his bullet of a fist into the man's face. Pain dripped across his knuckles and trickled down nervous tributaries, but he disregarded it. He had a score to settle. The man stepped back and Easton capitalized, slamming his fists and feet and shins over and over into the Native American, screaming out at the man without words. Reflexively, Easton seized the man's robes and lifted him off his feet, hurling the taller man onto the tiled ground. Easton wanted to continue the punishment, but the mystic was on his feet as if lifted by a god. Blazing almost perceptibly with malevolence, the seer casually batted aside a kick and gripped the following elbow. Without warning, his lungs expelled all air like a virus. Four iron-hard chisels dug into his ribs. Easton found himself on his knees, a puddle of blood circled by a sea of saliva. He looked up at a thick-bodied man in an overcoat. He wore fingerless gloves and his beard was several years unshaven. Greedy hazel eyes glared down at him. "Stop holding out," the man growled, grabbing Easton by the shoulders and heaving him back upwards. He slammed the shorter grappler back against the door. "Just gimme the mon—hurk!" The man spiraled on the ball of his foot before falling face-first on the sidewalk. Easton rubbed the side of his fist; the scavenger hadn't expected the hammerblow to the temple. Easton took the time to rub his eyes down and realize just how tired he was. He'd spent most of his life being tired, walking in a daze broken up by sleep, meditation, and those rare moments of true lucidity. He was tired when he set out and tired now that he was so close to this goal, so close to staring down the man who had done him wrong. Something inside him drove him, though, pulled him by the hand. He knew he had to do this. Taking a last look at the forlorn vagrant and scratching once again at his taped forearms, Easton stepped across the street and started down the sidewalk. He fished the address out of his pocket and glanced over it. Looking up at the sign, he found himself in luck; turning the corner would put him on the right street at least. Baltimore was a big city, though, and he didn't know how far a particular street could run. There was nothing left to do for him, though, but start walking. Easton glanced up at the numbers on the buildings to make sure he was going the right way. Satisfied, he turned his eyes forward and thought to himself a bit. He had spent so many months of near-retirement—those few weeks spent down in Phoenix for AXIS hadn't consumed his energy too much—telling everybody who would listen that he had to go back and find this man, that he had to have this one last showdown. He'd told his mother once over the phone and been so convinced that this was what he had to do that his mother had given him her full blessings, even considering what she had seen happen to him whenever he had a score to settle. Now that he had finally come out to do this, now that he had this tournament riding on his shoulders like the burden of Atlas, he realized that he didn't have any plan whatsoever. Was he going to stare him down and then leave or was he going to thrash him with his fists, bruise him beyond recognition? Easton didn't know if he would risk losing himself that much just to gain some sort of closure. A part of Easton hoped that he was just barely alive, that he could look down at the man and sneer at him, turn and leave without saying a word, giving him barely a look. He wanted the world, the karmic ebb and flow, to punish the seer in a way that Easton knew he couldn't. With every fiber of his body, Easton wished a cruel fate upon the black-robed man, and yet Easton knew he could never go so far himself. The man deserved death, but Easton's hand was too unsteady to be his executioner. Easton's green eyes scanned the numbers intently now as addresses on this godforsaken row came closer to meeting the one inscribed on the sheet. Easton felt himself jolted after unknowingly walking into something more than once—man, woman, or tree, he mumbled a distracted apology as he slipped along his way. Other objects seemed to blur together, everything but the address numbers. He felt like the protagonist in a math movie. As this thought floated through his brainstream, he let his eyes wander forward. All of a sudden, the hand of Athena jerked his head back and turned it towards the building he was about to pass. There, in front of him, was the holy grail of addresses. He glanced down at the piece of paper to verify it and then stepped towards the row of buildings. Finally, he had come to this place of pilgrimage. Easton's steps picked up as he walked towards his destination. The place his mentor had called home was below the street level; the door—covered in a cramped, but still slightly mysterious, alcove—was only accessible by a short series of stairs that led down from the sidewalk. Easton hurried down them, his feet hitting the ground far earlier than he'd expected and leaving him with a slight twinge in his ankle. He ignored it, though, stepping into the place where the upper levels hung over the doorway. This was it. He didn't know what he was going to do, he couldn't fathom it, but he was going to go ahead. Standing out here agonizing over it would only make it all worse. He gripped the doorknob and twisted it. Taking a step forward, he pushed the door open. His eyes blazed with incandescence as if a switch had been flipped deep in his brain. Iron-hard fingers clawed across Easton's scalp and, just as they dug away, yanked him up by his hair. Easton felt his feet land softly on the ground, but his mentor knew better than to let him enjoy the feeling of standing upright for too long; the torturer of champions batted away a hand aimed for his throat and slammed folded knuckles into the collarbone of the mystic. He felt the mystic's hold on him loosen, but just as quickly the blade of the older man's hand crushed into his neck. Gasping for breath, the Canadian stumbled backwards. The man was as strong as an ox. He felt blunt blows driving into his chest and shoulders faster than bullets. Time slowed down for Easton. His nerves were about to overload with the pain that ran along their narrow channels. His blood flowed irregularly, always fleeing the spot where the robed warrior's hands hit last. Easton's world began to fade to an obscure grey. He saw the smile spread like a vulture's hungry wings across his mentor's face and watched as the man drew his arms back in a deliberate manner, chambering his hands at his hips. Then, like some immortal game of Rubik's cube, everything slammed together. His mentor's hands shot forward. Easton's bloodflow corrected itself. Colors made sense. Calm as a god, Easton slipped to the side of the deathblow. His own hand hammered upwards into the mystic's solar plexus. Easton felt his breath come back to him as the seer was lifted upwards before falling onto a knee. He shot forward, boosting himself up off of his mentor's knee. He planted his other foot against the wall and pushed himself even higher into the air. As gravity pulled him back down, he swung himself into a spin. Once. Twice. And then he swung around the third time, swinging his dragon-tattooed arm out and slamming it into his master's temple, a comet cratering the earth. Easton landed gracefully on his feet and glared down at the fallen mystic. He opened his mouth to speak and breath rushed out of him. Easton groaned as he toppled to a knee, clutching his stomach. The table corner had been unforgiving to him. Still, he was okay. He'd survive it. He grabbed the offending table and pulled himself up to his feet, taking a deep breath to try and get oxygen back into his lungs. Still panting, Easton swiveled his head around, trying to accustom himself to his surroundings. It was the same room, there was no doubt of that. The same deep gray walls, the doors in the same place, the same carpet. The same couch. He even remembered the green plant sprouting out of the wide, bluish vase over on the other side of the room; he couldn't name the plant, but he knew it was the same. Everything was so neatly in place that Easton knew no one had been here for some time. His former mentor had been a meticulous man, but he was never this clean. Knowing the mystic, there would have been some text on the couch, perhaps a pot of tea on the low, black-lacquered table that the old man had allowed to go cold. A door would be ever-so-slightly ajar. With a heavy heart and arms that, with the eagerness to find the man that he had spent so long searching for gone, now hung from his shoulders like lead, Easton dragged himself towards the black leather couch and took a seat. He combed his fingers through his hair and closed his eyes. So where had he gone? He had skipped town, but it could just be for a month or so. Could Easton stand to wait another month, to put it off, and then return when he felt he might find the man? After all, the mystic could have truly left this place; he didn't seem a man attached to many material things, so all these possessions he'd left behind didn't indicate anything out of the ordinary. Focus was what Easton needed. Even in realizing this, however, he knew that his nerves were too frayed to meditate properly. After giving his forearms a good itch, he slipped two fingers inside his pocket and pulled out a small bag filled with crushed green leaf. The diviner's sage was his preferred meditational tool. He slipped his hand back into his pocket and produced a short metal tube. He smiled at it; painted to look like a cigarette, it was a novelty with some street use. He carefully packed some of the salvia extract into the one-hitter and made sure it sat well. He lifted the tube to his lips and brought his other hand, now holding a lighter, up in an almost ritualistic circle until he held the lighter in front of the pipe. He flicked the flame on and took a deep drag, shutting his eyes. It took a while to ignite, but soon smoke rushed into his lungs as the extract burned. Easton pulled the tube away and held his lips shut for a second before pulling a little more air in and gulping down the smoke. He sat backwards and, as was his whimsical custom, tilted his head up and blew the smoke out of his nose. A slow smile crept across his lips as he watched it lazily. It was a powerful extract he'd just indulged himself in. Now he stood on railroad tracks. And then, just a hair slower than Superman, the locomotive hit him. Easton's jaw dropped open, a process that took centuries to complete. His head seemed to float on the surface of some odd, viscous gel; he imagined it as a translucent blue, but had no idea why that would be the case. He rolled his shoulders and took a deep breath and then watched the air disperse into the winds of the four directions. He wanted to close his eyes, but something pinched his eyelids and tugged upwards. Bewildered, Easton tilted his head backwards and looked at the ceiling. His eyes narrowed for a bit before widening again. Independent of each other, his eyes traced an odd tangle of triangles—or diamonds?—seared into white ceiling. From behind his ears, Athena whispered the answer. "The Unicursal Hexagram," came a breeze of springtime from behind his right ear, throwing warm blues and reds and greens before his eyes. "Thelema," echoed a winter gale from behind the left, spewing harsh dark greys and malevolent deep-tinged oranges. Easton heard the words, registered them, but his eyes still danced across the figure. It had hypnotized him, almost, drawn him in. He couldn't take his eyes off; not that he wanted to, but somehow he knew that if he had tried, he would have failed. A maddening scream erupted from nowhere, welling up and bursting towards the heavens. The ceiling, along with the rest of the building, was ripped away in fantastic fashion. In place of the sky was another room, a scene almost like a church. The roof of the church had likewise been ripped off and behind it stood a skyline that Easton knew well: New York City. Dozens of people sat in the pews in plain clothes as if worshipping on a Sunday. The altar, if there was one, was shrouded by a crimson cloth barrier that rippled unnaturally as if some breeze was blowing in only for it. A single man stood on the steps leading up to the veil; tall and robed in all black, he shows his back to the parishioners and in his hands he holds a long, silvery lance. Slowly, almost ominously, he turned around and gave Easton his gaze. His face did not exist, and if it had, it had long ago been consumed in the fiery inferno that now occupied the hood. Yet somehow, Easton knew who it was. The mystic who had evaded him here, who had done him wrong so long ago, was now standing somewhere hundreds of miles away. Easton blinked suddenly as the spear seemed to grow without cause. Fear blossomed in his chest. Was the spear meant for him? Could the seer be so powerful that he could stab Easton through this window of perception? Then, without an indication that the man had even seen the great breaker of necks, the robed mystic turned around and ascended the last step. He pierced a hidden fold in the veil with his lance and casually parted it, revealing a woman laying atop the stone altar. She was well-formed and completely naked except for one oddity: a plastic mask over her face with the mouth disfigured like a harlequin child. Easton knew this face, too. "Kell… ly?" Easton mumbled; fear still had his nerves in a deathgrip. The woman sat forward and leaned forward to press the lips of the mask to the tip of the lance in a kiss. Fear opened its gauntleted hand slowly. As the roof returned and the window of seeing closed, Easton saw something that horrified him. The lance began to protrude, covered with running red blood, out of the back of the woman's head. Easton panted as he ripped his eyes away from the curious hexagram on the ceiling. He looked at his surroundings in a panic. As he realized he was still in the same place, he slowly calmed down. Everything was okay. The hit he had taken must have hit him harder than he thought. Far harder. He frowned a bit and scratched at his forearm and then gave a start. He felt his nails grating against the skin. He looked down and saw his arms bare, green-and-gold dragon tattoos exposed on his forearms. He didn't quite remember taking them off. His surprise was even greater when he finally located the gauze on the floor; rather than being unwound, they were cut vertically down the middle in the neatest line that Easton had ever witnessed. Easton buried his face in his hands. So the mystic wasn't here, yet he could torment him still. He had to accept that the man was gone, yet he couldn't. The urge to find him still slammed against his ribs from the inside. The thought of his betrayal still maddened him; his revenge had been so slight! Yet, Easton couldn't help but think that he might never find the seer again. Disheartened, Easton stood and slipped the one-hitter and the remaining salvia in his pockets before starting for the door. Perhaps some things were not meant to be ended.
