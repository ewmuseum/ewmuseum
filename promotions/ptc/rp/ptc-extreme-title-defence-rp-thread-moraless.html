10 May 2004 To give it your all, to not hang your head when you have been defeated but to raise your head high, look your opponent in the eye and acknowledge their win, that is what I had always been taught. When you lose it is not about you, no matter how much you hurt in side it is never about you. It was the victor’s time. You could go home and cry yourself to sleep, lick your wounds and return to fight another day, but the winner just has that one chance to celebrate. To rise onto those turnbuckles, thrust their title into the air and when the crowd reaches its crescendo you will know one thing. That you are the best. So why was it that at this very moment in time, all I can think about is how I should react when I fail. I know what the daunting task ahead of me is. A fresh champion who will be more than willing to spill blood in order to keep it, whether it will be mine or his still remains to be seen. I know I have a chance, possibly the best chance that I am every going to get yet still in the back of my mind is that fear. You don’t deserve the shot. You are going to lose. This is your ONLY opportunity. You will lose John… Moraless’ hand stopped moving over the paper, as he dropped the black biro within it on the table. Letting out a brief sigh he rose to his feet and looked at the journal in front of him, his eyes focusing on the last sentence that he had written. The words in front of him were a sure example of his fate, he had determined it himself. Moraless turned away from the book and walked towards the window, staring out at the city below him, the hustle and bustle of life thriving. He pulled the shutters apart giving himself a better view over the city’s landscape. Streetlights illuminating the asphalt below whilst a particularly eerie moon lights the remainder of the night sky. His mind was racing with thoughts as he stared through the glass, a cool breeze blowing his face, feeling almost omnipotent from the viewpoint, anything but that in his mind. Moraless was going to do battle with Sean McClancy, he was going to take his one shot that he had and he was going to lose in a blaze of glory. Moraless paced up and down his study, his eyes transfixed on the journal, no worry within the eyes, simply regret for the past few months his life had been leading up to this moment. In the Universal Wrestling Federation at the moment it seemed as though he could do no wrong, the resurrection was thriving as stable wars approached, a tag team title belt hung high above his dresser, he had the momentum, yet there was something missing. The self-confidence. It had always been a problem, from day one Moraless had always doubted that he could ever amount to anything, even when things were going well. He would pass them off as fluke or blame it on the other competitor’s inability as opposed to his own true ability. Yet he had always tried to hide it. When he was wrestling he oozed a certain charisma that drew the crowd to him, whether it was a love or hate relationship it didn’t matter. He could always wind a crowd round his little finger and pull the right strings. That was all for the crowd though, he was almost as good an actor as a wrestler. Almost. Moraless turned and sat down at the desk once more, taking the pen in his hand, rubbing his hand across his face as the pen slowly began to move across the paper. It happened to me the other day, I was walking down the street. It was nothing special, literally just a normal day. I had gone food shopping and was on the way to the gym when it happened. It was in an alley, I only really caught a glimpse of it out of the corner of my eye, a struggle maybe I thought to myself. I remember stopping in the street turning to watch the incident unfold in the secluded corner of suburbia, three men, surrounding another man, punching him, kicking him. I wanted to move, just leap forwards to join the fray but I couldn’t, my body simply wouldn’t let me. The thoughts were dashing around my head about what would happen to him if I simply turned and walked away, if I had left the man to be beaten, possibly to death. And I still wanted to leave. I know at the end of the day I am a coward. Even though I knew what could happen to the man, I wanted to leave him fearing for myself and what might happen to me if I entered the fray. Does that make me a bad person? Moraless’ pen stopped moving again, his eyes scanning the words he had written, looking for an answer to his questions deep within his mind, focusing intensely, trying to find some truth, whether he wanted to hear it or not. I don’t know. What does it matter anyway, it’s not like I did abandon him in the end, it was just that. I wanted to and it unnerved me you know, the fact that I could be so heartless. He could have been a fan, someone who looked up to guys like me, strived to be like me. How could I have lived with myself if I had simply turned away? I didn’t want to know, I didn’t want to be that person. Which was why it was so hard to walk away. Its true I left him. I took one step forwards and then another, slowly but surely taking steps to make sure that I would not see what was happening behind me, so that I could go to sleep that night and close my eyes, pretend it was all a dream. It was then I heard the scream. I knew straight away someone had seen what I had saw, the cries for help were becoming louder as I began to up my tempo, my walk became brisker as I went to turn the corner, trying desperately to put some sort of distance, between me and the incident. To most people I would probably have looked like a guilty party myself. It was then I caught a brief glimpse of myself, just a small reflection in a pane of glass covering a clothes store, but it was enough to make me stop and just look at myself. I saw my face, the scar that ran down my right cheek to my chin and I realised that I was no longer the man I used to be. Moraless lightly brushed his right hands fingers down his cheek, running along the line of the scar, his eyes closed tight as the memories of the past flooded his mind. It was then I realised why I didn’t want to help the man, the scar on my face. The one true time anyone but myself had inflicted any major pain on myself, it occurred during a mugging. It’s funny how you can spend years, repressing memories only for them to resurface at the times you don’t want them to. As I stood transfixed at the scar I realised for the first time why I hid away from the world, why I wanted solitude. I was scared. I was so very scared of being hurt, whether it was emotionally or physically, the one flick of the blade that had permanently scarred my body had apparently scarred my soul, my very being. I then turned back and headed towards the scene of the crime. I don’t know why, possibly the realisation of why I had turned away in the first place, but adrenaline was coursing through me, it was as though I had come to terms with my past, with who I was. I entered the alleyway, barging my way through a crowd of people who were simply watching as I had done, maybe I wasn’t the strange one after all. No one else would do anything either. I strode into that alley, and I hurt them. I hurt them as they had hurt the defenceless innocent who was bleeding at their very feet. The numbers didn’t seem to matter, they were flustered, shocked at the idea that someone would actually come to the mans aid. Their moves were noticeable from miles away and could be counted so easily I barely had to make any form of offence. Eventually they ran away, the crowd that had gathered was cheering but as I looked down at the man who had been attacked I could not help but loathe myself. Yet this was different, it was no longer me hating myself for no reason, for an incident that occurred years ago that was beyond my control. I loathed myself this time because I had been a coward. Moraless stopped writing and moved his hand across the desk over to a pile of paper sifting through them until he found one letter in particular, his eyes scanning over the text, placing the paper back down on the desk, just next to the journal, Moraless allowed himself a small smile before nodding and continuing to write. His parents sent me a letter, thanking me for my actions. Although when he had left he looked like a bloody mess apparently the wounds were mostly superficial, a lot of cuts and bruises and a few cracked ribs. Nothing that wouldn’t mend with time. The letter said that if I had not decided to risk my own life then their son may not have had his and that I would always have a special place in their hearts. Right at the bottom of the letter though there were four words, which really reached me though, four words that made all the pain go away, the pain of the past, which had haunted me for so long. You saved his life. When I read those words, I broke down and cried. They did not realise it and they probably never would do but their son had actually saved mine. I don’t believe in fate as such but I know that certain things happen for certain reasons. Seventeen years I had lived in a shell within myself fearing that it would always be that way. Until the life of another was put at risk. I know I’m no hero I’m just a guy who was able to do the right thing, I found myself. This is why I’m writing this now, a new beginning. So that when things get rough I can look back on this and realise who I actually am. I could have turned my back on him but I didn’t I came through and that alone will make me stronger than any amount of training will ever do. I am John Edwards a man who no longer needs to hide under the shackles of a fake name. From this moment I begin anew. Moraless turned back the journal a few pages and scanned over the words that he had written. You don’t deserve the shot. You are going to lose. This is your ONLY opportunity. You will lose John… His pen scribbled frantically over the paper before he finished and rose to his feet. Stretching he looked out of the window, the sun was beginning to rise over the landscape, the night had disappeared such as the moon had. It was a new day and a new beginning. A new John Edwards. He then turned and left the room a smile on his face, a natural smile one that was no longer forced, closing the door behind him. The journal remained open however it had been changed the pessimistic words that had been altered or completely erased, unlike actions words can always be changed. You worked hard for this title shot you’ve earned it. Who cares if you lose, as long as you give it your all that’s all you can possibly ask for? This is NOT your ONLY opportunity, you are still considered with high esteem in the division. John…the match will only be decided, by what you do. ---- 14 May 2004 It’s amazing what a small revelation will do, how clear the mind can become, the focus, the grit and the determination. This had happened to John Edwards, yes John Edwards, no longer feeling the need to hide under the moniker, which had kept his identity for four years. Much like the Universal Wrestling Federation John Edwards was going through a resurrection. He had discarded the past, the shackles which it had over him. As far as John was concerned, Moraless was dead. Sure, he would still continue to use the name in the ring, but it wasn’t going to be him. In the past, he avoided using his name, he even considered legally changing it to Moraless, but that didn’t really work out too well. When it came down to it, in the past, John didn’t exist. It was simply a birth name, the dark and tragic past of John had become too much, he wanted to black it out so hid from his existence. The thing is he couldn’t hide now, with the destruction of Moraless, came the rebirth of John Edwards and everything that came with him. It was all very well saying that he was ok, but there was still something that he needed to do, a final step towards normality, if there is such a thing. The cobbles underneath his feet cracked as he walked down the path. His chest felt tight and the air intake was restricted, it didn’t matter he knew he had to do this. It was the one thing remaining. The one thing, which caused him, pain, grief about the past. It was the one thing that he had denied all of these years, blocked out. The bees were buzzing in and around the flowers, which decorated the garden either side of the path. What with it being spring, the assortment of smells and colours were beginning to flourish, beginning their life anew for just a few seasons before hiding from the cold harsh winter. John paused his walk and looked around what could only be described as a small corner of paradise. There was a small pond, fish swimming about in it with a small fountain trickling into it. Directly in front of John though was what he had been looking for and yet hiding from for a long time. Stepping forwards he approached a large yellow rose bush. The smell attacked John’s sinuses and made him smile, memories came flooding back to him, memories of home and his childhood. He knelt silently in front of the rose bush, and bowed his head, his eyes finding something just in front of his knees. Underneath the flowers was a small wooden plaque, no bigger than a piece of A4 paper, a dark mahogany, which whilst showing the effects of time, maintained its beauty. His heart was now beating so rapidly it felt as though it would burst his chest. He felt lost, confused, not knowing how to react. He had blocked it away for so long, not acknowledging the truth. A single solitary tear fell and splashed off the mahogany as John traced his fingers over the engraved words. ‘In loving memory of Sarah and Robert Edwards, taken away in their prime. You will never be forgotten’ John’s head dropped as the tears began to flow like wine. He had forgotten them, forgotten his parents as a result of this person he had become, this Moraless. A character, who he had always hidden behind. He never expected him to do this though; he didn’t want Moraless to cause this. Sniffing deeply, John wiped the tears on the sleeve of his jacket, his eyes bloodshot trying to fight back the tears. Somewhere in the garden a bird began to sing it’s spring song, the wind increased to a light breeze, gently massaging John’s short black hair. It was as though nature had a connection with this time and place, it was as though someone didn’t want John to be sad, not here. This was a place of remembrance that is why John had come here in the first place, to restore some memory, to give a better indication of the past. The tears were a good sign in that respect, the emotion showed that feeling was coming back, thoughts and memories all cascading through John’s brain. “Hi” the words were forced from John’s throat squeaking slightly as he did so, his throat was dry and crying always led to high-pitched levels. “I don’t really know what to say, I… I just know that I have to do something, say something. I… I was so lost. I forgot who I was. I forgot you, but that’s changing I promise I know who I am now. This place is so beautiful, I…I just hope I’m remembered this well when, well when it happens. Who keeps this place so tidy?” “That would be me, John” John was cut off by the sound of a voice he knew well, he didn’t turn around and simply remained sat looking at the plaque below him. James Edwards kneeled down next to John and turned to look at him, his blond hair blowing in the wind as he also lowered to look at the plaque. “It’s hard the first time isn’t it, as an adult I mean. We were bought here as children, but we didn’t really understand it then, not the true significance, it’s true beauty. I’m glad you came.” James spoke, his words were far away from the goofy demeanour that he played in RPW, mostly because this was something that he was deadly serious about. “Yeah, it’s hard it really is, I mean what do I do, what do I say?” spoke John turning to his brother, a man who under the guise of Moraless he had taught himself to hate, his own flesh and blood who had always been there no matter how much of a dick he was to him. “You don’t really have to say anything John, this is a garden of remembrance, its what mum and dad wanted. If you have something to say, you can say it here. A lot of the time I don’t say a thing, it’s just nice to sit and watch the flowers, the fish and just think back. Make sure they aren’t forgotten.” James looked at John sympathetically, he could tell that he was hurting, he placed a reassuring hand on John’s shoulder and squeezed it gently. John smiled and let out a small breath, James had always known how to reassure John, whenever hr was in trouble as a child, it always seemed to be James who would sort it out for him, he had to, there was nobody else around. “I know it must have been hard for you to come here today John, but can I ask, why did you?” I know that you have bigger fish to fry at the moment. A certain Mr McClancy you have to deal with, as well as defending your tag team championship. I mean I’m hardly in a position to criticise your working methods, but shouldn’t you be training or something?” James asked his younger brother, releasing his hand from his shoulder as he did so. John, the artist formally known as Moraless, rose to his feet and turned away from the plaque. He walked towards the small pond, the trickling water soothing his ears and simply stood there gazing into the crystal clear fluid, watching the fish swim effortlessly. “Sean, yeah. That was always going to be a tricky one. I never thought I would be able to get this far, to be able to pose a legitimate challenge to a champion, not the calibre of McClancy anyway. Yet I don’t know, I think I just might be able to do this one. There are two things, which may just swing things in my favour. James had now also raised to his feet, taking care not to disturb any of the flowers, the centrepiece in the garden of remembrance. He moved over to where his younger brother was and began to tend to some lily’s to the side of the pond, James listened as John continued to talk. “Its just that it doesn’t seem like McClancy has paid any attention to exactly what is going on, he doesn’t seem to have followed me, scouted out what I do. He believes that match is a construction yard brawl, it’s much more than that, it’s ACWF. Asphalt, chains, weapons and forklifts. ACWF isn’t just about construction, it carries a legacy behind it. This will be ACWF IV, the previous three held at my old playing grounds of GCW. At the end of the day, if you are able to walk out of ACWF then you have already more than won. Don’t get me wrong, I know that McClancy is tough, tougher than me in fact, but I don’t think he is ready for this. He appears to be taking it far too light heartedly. I mean, Jason O’ Neil barely walked out of one of these matches alive, do I really have a chance. James had turned and stopped pruning at the mention of Jason O’ Neil. It was only now that he realised the severity of the match that John had placed himself in. In RPW, Jason O’Neil was simply put a hard as nails bastard and if he had barely survived…the thoughts started dashing through his head, fearful for his little brother and the damage that he was about to endure. “O’Neil? O’Neil barely got out of one of these, Christ John what were you thinking? Why not just go for a ladder match or hell, that submissions match with Vendetti worked out well for you, why do something this crazy if you don’t think you’re up for it. It’s just a way of shortening your career. John’s eyes remained focused on the fish in the pond, a gust of air sent ripples flying through the water as he slowly raised his head, looked at his brother and smiled. “It’s who I am James, I know that I’m not Moraless anymore, I know where to draw the line. The fact of the matter is though, that I was still him for a long, long time. He has influenced me. This maybe my one shot that I actually get at this title. Should I lose, there is no way its going to be in a bog standard ladder match, a match which has been killed repeatedly. I wanted something special, hard hitting, something that people would be able to look back on in the future and go ‘ACWF IV…damn that was special’. John continued to smile at James who had begun to smile back, James knew that John was always going to be the showman, it had always been that way, time doesn’t really change all that much when you think about it. “And besides” continued John “he doesn’t even know who I am, not really. That’s the other thing that I think will give me the edge. All he really knows about me is that I work for the UWF, other than that I may as well be a blank page to him. I mean get this, McClancy actually thought that people fear me, he actually went and said ‘I’m beginin’ t’ think tha’ Moraless thinks tha’ I’m afraid o’ ‘im.” There was silence, just for a second. John was totally deadpan, as was James. Then it happened. You could see James’ face begin to twitch upwards, as did John’s. Then as though on cue the two brothers laughed, laughed until their lungs were sore. It could have been the horrendous Irish accent that John had just attempted, but the truth was that they were laughing at the comment. “Really? He actually said that, out loud…to other people?” enquired James, who was looking extremely flustered, trying to regain his breath. John simply nodded, the sheer lunacy of the idea, that John would ever think anyone would be scared of him, had become far too much to believe. Moraless was the man who walked tall held his head high. John Edwards just a guy with low self-esteem, he’s scared of wasps for Christ sake. John stopped smiling and looked around the garden once more, it truly was a miraculous thing, this garden had bought together the two brothers, a common bond shared between them, even though it was not the happiest in the world, it was something which connected them. “How do I do it James? How do I tap into Moraless, but at the same time not allow myself to be taken over again. I know that as I am now, I can’t beat McClancy, not confidently anyway. I still want Mory, to be a part of me, but not to complete me.” Spoke John, his brow now furrowed as his mind searches for answers. James smiled once more at his brother, before turning and beginning to walk away from him. “You already have John, if you didn’t have control you wouldn’t be here now, you told me yourself earlier. Moraless will always be a part of you, but you also said you know where to draw the line. When you go out into that match it will be you. Moraless maybe the name headlining the bill, but John Edwards will be the one fighting. You do what you have to do man. I’ll see you soon, I’m not wishing you good luck, because it’s all in your hands. “Wait you’re leaving?” asked John, clearly shocked by the not so grand revelation. “Yeah, have to head back to North Carolina, meeting up with one of your old friends in fact, Lord Skywolf.” James grinned as he spoke, a large beaming smile covered John’s face as his eyes appeared to just light up. “Jobberwolf? Jobberwolf is still around, say hi for me ok,” John said, with a certain amount of bounce in his voice, possibly a little too enthusiastic. “Sure, I’ll say hi…or something like that,” replied James, who waved to his brother and walked up the cobbled path leaving John and the garden alone. John glanced around the garden one more time before returning to the rosebush. He knelt down and read the plaque once more. There he remained, silent, not needing to say a thing. Life would always continue long after John was gone, life would always flourish new generations thrown into this life head first not knowing where to turn, but in essence that’s what life is. If we knew what to do with it, if we knew where we were headed in our existence there would be no fun, life would be dull and monotonous. Destiny is everywhere, you cannot help but face it head on, because that’s destiny, it’s unavoidable and you don’t want to spend your life running away.
