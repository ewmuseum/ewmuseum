Failure? For me or not?

13 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Second chances don’t come easy for some. Most of the time they are either by accident or by the sheer sake of luck. Even the rich get a second chance so to speak. For example, O.J. Simpson. Mr. Running back. Mr. Great leader in the world. All around great guy in his community. And then what happens? BANG! It all goes away with a sudden shift in the world that thinks he killed his wife. The world turns on a guy that they loved so many years. Blasted from the world that he thought he ruled. Then a miracle happened. He was found innocent for everything and he then became the man that we all loved over the years. The same for Martha Stewart and Billy The Kid. Even for a girl like me that didn’t live up to the name that she was born with. I just couldn’t understand it. All I had to do was win. It was simple. Easy. And I just couldn’t…do it. Looking back at the years that I have lived, all I can see is failure. The time I was five years old and I was in the little league race team. I was fast from what my mother tells me. I had a heart for trying but that wasn’t enough. My weight was just to much for my legs to carry. Then there was the time I was twelve. In the spelling bee at school. I was smart. Straight A’s all the time. Was even given an award for getting the most straight A’s in my school years leading up to my eight grade year. Though under pressure, I just never made the cut. All I had to do was spell a little word called Etherealize. And even in my eleventh grade year. My looks were always the better part of me. Most always said I was a beauty queen that needed to be let free. Pretty blue eyes that sparkled in the light. Marlene Monroe look alike just without the blonde hair. But boys were never the bright side of my life. I can remember one time, a hot sunny day, maybe in June. There was a dance right around the corner. The Texas Swing dance was what it was called. He looked so good looking in his tight wrangler jeans and bright black goat roper shirt. Though as I inched closer to him, all I felt was a big blast of wet dirty mud in my face. “Ha…” Yeah. The laughs came my way and still, failure had occurred. Even when the bad men who took my Uncle Johnson away from me, forced me into trying to win, all I could end up doing was losing. But in all my years, I’ve never gotten a second chance like this. “Elia…” A voice splattered out to me as I sat there on the porch. Turning my head to the doorway there stood my cousin Kevin. Strong and brave in everyway that I wasn’t. Never failed in anything in his life. Graduated High School, College, and married the girl of his dreams all in a matter of twenty-five years. “Yes?” Answering him back in a sad but happy way. “Mother said you got a letter in the mail. Want me to bring it to you?” Replying with a smile on his face. “Sure if you want to. Other then that, I’d really like to be left alone.” Lowering my head into the open space that I had left open between my legs. It was rather hot in the early afternoon day here in Texas. June weather always brought the hottest heat. My hair, sweat infested from the sun that was shining down on it. I just kept sitting there on my butt, knowing that once again I was going to fail in someway. Uncle Johnson wasn’t talking to me, my career in GW was nothing short then a disaster, and I was yet alone again. “Here you go.” Kevin said, handing me the white envelope. “Thanks…”, I said with a sigh. Looking down at the letter, I saw the name on the addressee that gave me a sudden lose of breath. “PTC Administration.” “What are they sending me this for?” Asking myself with the confusion that I had no idea on earth what I was getting. I began ripping open the letter from the back, tearing at it with the hope that it might be something that would make me happy. The whole time, not knowing what the hell it could be. Pulling the white piece of paper out I could see the first words that were on top of the letter, “Congratulations…”. I began leaping for joy, all the way up from the bottom of the porch that my butt was touching. I began reading more of the letter, skipping the first few lines of it on accident. “We invite you to be part of the third gauntlet match in PTC were you will be facing the current champion, Uber Judge and fellow GW member, Christian Zenith.” WOW! Those words rang in my ears like I had just won the Texas Lotto. I began twisting and jumping for the ceiling, not noticing my mother and Kevin standing there in the doorway with smiles on their faces. “Good news?” My gorgeous mother said to me. “Yes!” “Well…what is it?” Kevin replied with a few claps of his hands. “Well I get a second chance at becoming great. PTC wants me to fight one more time.” Grabbing the shoulders of Kevin like he was my father. My father. Dead at the age of Fifty-four to cancer. No one knew really except my mother and really, she didn’t care. I guess she did because sometimes, just like me, I would always cry for him in my dreams and in my bed at night. I remember he was there for every soccer game I had in High School. My first goal I made, I ran to him with wide open arms, reaching upwards and wrapping him around the neck like he was Big Bird and I was a five year old little girl. “Have you told Uncle Johnson yet?” Mother responded as she tossed her grayish blonde hair out of his face. I slowly began to turn away from the answer, trying to get away from the fact that he hadn’t said one single word to me yet. I hope he would be happy but in fact I knew he would be sad. “No.” Walking away from them two and towards the green grass that flung around the country house we lived in. I didn’t get far. Like usual, Kevin was always there to either stick his big nose into my business or to be the helpful cousin. I felt his hand on my shoulder and for a split second it actually felt like it was helping. But in reality, it was only wishful thinking. “Don’t worry Elia. He will come around. You know Uncle Johnson.” Kevin said. “Yeah…”, trying not to act to sad but it was no use. “But I feel so alone right now. I just don’t want to let him down a second time by telling him and getting all his hopes up and then him seeing what a failure I am.” “Failure?” Kevin replied, twisting me around like I was a rag doll. “Do you think you are a failure?” I simply shook my head up and down toward him, trying not to say to much. “Do you remember the time I lost that one hundred dollars of grandpa’s?” Kevin responded as he let go of my arm. “Yeah…” “And do you remember how I got it back.” Kevin said as he took a few steps away from me. To be honest, no I didn’t. “No…” “You got it back for me.” Kevin blast’s out like he was on trial for a murder victim. “You got it back when no one else could or would. I trusted you with my life and you came through.” “But I was fifteen years old then. And all I did was mow some grass over a span of two weeks. How is that not a failure?” Wiping away a single tear that began to roll down my face and cheek. “Because you believed in me. You believed in yourself. More then anyone else did and came through. For that, you will never be a failure to me.” Kevin, with his strong words, took his hand and slowly laid it on my shoulder. My heart began to skip a few beats in my chest, making me lose my breath for a moment. Maybe I wasn’t a failure after all. Maybe for once in my life it was me that was causing all this pain to be brought down on my world. I swung my arms around the chest of Kevin, pulling him closer and closer towards me as my eyes caught my mother’s face crying. I never saw her crying before in my life. I gently pushed him away from me, looking at my mother who began to walk towards me. “I’m so proud of you.” My mother said as tears began to come down her face even more. Everything seem to stop around me, like I was in a place all by myself. I reached around my mother’s chest and held her there close to me like she was my oxygen. My body just felt at easy for the first time with my mother since my father died. I couldn’t remember the last time we held ourselves close like this with just love and nothing else. My heart almost came to a sudden stop as I pressed away just a little bit to look into the eyes of her. “I love you mom…” My eyes crying with tears of joy for the first time. “I love you to sweetie…” she responded. Maybe I wasn’t a failure. Maybe I was more…
