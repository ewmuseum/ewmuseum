Unleashing Darkness I : With Anger Comes Shame Part One

26 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-26 "Whatever is begun in anger, ends in shame." -- Benjamin Franklin (1706-1790). ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Recognition. Police Precinct. Orchard Beach. One day before GTT6 round one… The first thing that hit McKail, as he was dragged into the police precinct by the chafing metal handcuffs clamped around his wrists, was the smell. It was a sickening mixture of sweat, liquor and piss and as soon as the odour touched base with his nostrils, everything inside him wanted to convulse. It took all he had just to prevent himself from hurling all over the police officer who’d brought him in. "What the hell is that?" McKail’s captor questioned, to nobody in particular. His face contorted in disgust and the officer quickly placed his hand over his mouth and nose to siphon out the bitter aroma. McKail envied such a luxury. The precinct was vibrant with life; police officer’s escorted a pack of colourful hookers to their holding cells, a few more hobo’s with nowhere else to go were being released from custody and a good number of other criminals were just sat around, still cuffed, as if provided the time to contemplate what went wrong in their lives that lead to this. The majority of the police officers were rushing around the place, others were filling out wads of paperwork and others were just stood around chatting about some mundane shit McKail couldn’t be bothered to eavesdrop. Above the hustle and bustle, McKail’s attention was drawn toward a large officer stood behind the main desk. His gut hung down dangerously over his belt and signalled toward McKail’s captor. The fat man pointed toward something lay upon a wooden bench to the right. McKail couldn’t tell what the hell it was, just by sight alone, it appeared to be some old worn rags and clothing moulded together to make some kind of crude humanoid cast. However, just from the smell alone, you could tell it was human; emphasis on was. "Dead body?" McKail asked, unable to wrench his eyes away from the thing. The arresting officer turned to him, with suspicious deep brown eyes firmly fixed upon him. His baby-face features didn’t shown even a hint of blemishing or scarring and his short black hair looked as though it’d been cut with the precision of a ruler. Fucking pretty boy… McKail observed. "How is it you know what a dead body smells like, huh?" McKail couldn’t tell whether his arresting officer was attempting humour or whether he was genuinely probing the extent of criminal past. Either way, McKail ignored him. As he was dragged further toward the fat man behind the front desk, he brushed shoulders with criminal’s and officers alike. The fat man chuckled and his gut jiggled, banging on the desk in rhythm. "Naw, he ain’t dead. Just homeless. He was released from the cells this morning, but he refused to leave. Haven‘t had time to clear him out yet." The fat man paused and nodded toward McKail. "So what do we have here?" "Don’t you recognise him?" It was another officer at the end of the desk. His greying crew cut and facial features reminded McKail of the drill instructor from the movie Platoon, but from his mannerisms McKail could tell he wasn’t nearly the ball buster that guy was and McKail felt somewhat comforted by that. The fat man behind the desk shook his head. "Should I?" Crew cut chuckled. "You’re a wrestling fan, ain’t ya?" "Yeah," the fat man conceded, gawking at McKail scrupulously now, "but I don’t recognise him." "It’s Jacob McKail," crew cut announced. McKail fought back a sigh; he hated the very notion of fan interaction, especially in a police precinct, but if it could get him off the hook in time for GTT6, then he was willing to let it ride. After all, he considered, those losers in the Piglet bracket weren’t going to beat the crap out of themselves, were they? "From GCW." Upon hearing his name the fat man‘s eyebrows raised, trying desperately not to look impressed. Also, an officer stood right beside crew cut, glanced up from his paperwork and glared at McKail silently. He was a slightly overweight guy, with hair that was beginning to get out of control and a very plain face. However, the more McKail looked at him, the more apparent the fire in his eyes became. If looks could kill… "I’ve heard the name," the fat man declared, ripping McKail’s attention away from the plain officer, "but I’m more of a PRIME man myself. Tchu to win the GTT6, I say." "What about you, Roy?" Crew cut asked, turning to the plain guy. "Who are you rooting for?" The plain guy remained silent for a time, his eyes locking on to McKail fiercely. He had no idea what the plain guy’s problem was, hell, McKail hadn’t even seen the guy before today. Had he? "I‘m an Andy M. fan myself," the plain guy responded, as he scooped up his paperwork from the grubby wooden counter and walked away in silence. "What the hell crawled up his ass and died?" McKail asked them. The others shook their heads in response, obviously just as bewildered as he. Then they remembered themselves and got right back down to business. "So," the fat man began, jamming his chewed pen in McKail’s direction, "what did Mr. Wrestling here do?" ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Money. The Wildman Estate. New York. Ten Years Ago… McKail stood outside the mansion, his mouth agape at both it’s size and breathtaking architecture. He wasn’t normally interested in structural design, but on this occasion he found it difficult not to be. This, combined with the fact that it’d taken him fifteen minutes to drive from the front gate to the mansion itself, McKail quickly became completely overwhelmed by it all. Truth of it was, he’d begun to have second thoughts about going through with it. Meeting a girlfriend’s parents was daunting task on it’s own, but when they turned out to be richer than Elton John and Michael Jackson combined, it was brown trousers time. He met his girlfriend, Jean, one year previously, at a wrestling show in Brooklyn. She was an over-eager fan at one of the shows and he was a man trying to forget. That Summer, his foster brother had committed suicide and, understandably, he didn‘t much want to think about it. Jean seemed to be as good a distraction as any. After a time, however, Jean became a lot more than a distraction and their relationship got real serious, real fast. Six months ago, McKail moved in with her and so far life had been good. "I don’t think I can go through with this," McKail told her, glaring at the huge, delicately carved, oak doors. Jean rammed her elbow into his gut, playfully. It hurt, though McKail would never admit it. "Behave yourself," she told him. "We’re here and there‘s no turning back now…" Before McKail could curse an appropriate response under his breath, the massive wooden door tugged open and a butler stood before them with all the grandeur of newly-crown king. "A butler? Seriously?" She smiled and nodded at the butler pleasantly. "Afternoon, Mike." "Ms. Wildman," the butler greeted, before casting his eyes over McKail. "And who is your friend?" McKail noted the disgust etched into the butler’s voice and the niggling feeling to just turn around and fuck off home, pulsed stronger. He kind of expected as much from her father, but not from the fucking butler. He looked at McKail as though he was some bum, begging for money on the street. However, as he glanced down over his torn jeans, muddied white sneakers and simple green t-shirt, he could see Jeeves had something of a point. It was just lucky he didn’t have a mirror at hand to further illustrate the point. The very sight of his wild long hair and his unshaven face would make him all the more self-conscious. Jean rolled her eyes and pointedly slipped her hand into McKail’s. "This is my boyfriend, Jacob." The butler allowed a sigh, as if McKail’s judgement had already been made and gestured for them both to enter. McKail allowed Jean to enter first and noted the butler never take his distrustful eyes off him for a second. The butler led them to a room on their left as they entered and, rather predictably, it was just as opulent as the outside of the mansion. Perhaps even more so. The unassuming beige walls, the varied, but equally luxurious sofa’s and the various trinkets lining the selves, combined with the delicate artwork hanging upon each wall dominantly, it painted enough of a picture to make McKail feel really uncomfortable now. "Please wait here," the butler ordered, scuttling out of the room. As soon as the butler left the room, the panic set in. "I really don’t want to be here…" Jean glared at him. "You don’t want to meet my father?" McKail sighed. "You know that’s not what I mean," he responded. "Look at this place, hell, even the butler looks down on me…" "Don’t worry about Mike," Jean told him. "He’s always had a bad attitude. Besides, it’s not his approval you‘re here for." "Yeah, but you father isn’t going to like me," McKail told her. "I don’t even like me." Jean rolled her eyes, just as an elderly man rounded the corner and met them both with a wide smile. Jean rushed towards him, immediately, open armed. "Daddy!" Her father scooped her up in his careful arms and hugged the life out of her, as though they‘d been apart for years. He was a tall, slender man, suitable dressed in an expensive-looking black pin-stripe suit that was probably more expensive than McKail’s car. The father/daughter embrace finally let up and his eyes firmly locked upon McKail. Jean noticed his gaze and gestured toward McKail proudly. "Daddy, this is Jacob." Her father glared at her for a second, before his once placid face morphed into a smile. He glided forward and extended his hand, which McKail took thankfully and shook. He damn well wished he hadn’t. It felt like somebody had crammed his hand in a vice. "Hi," he greeted, so pleasantly it almost put McKail at ease. Almost. "I’m Tom Wildman, I’ve heard only good things about you." McKail desperately tried to hide the pain with a smile, though he had no idea how successful the facade was. "Exaggerations, I assure you." Tom chuckled and slapped McKail on the back so hard he thought his moustache would fall off. "I like him!" He turned towards his daughter and gave her a thumbs up. Jean smiled and hugged McKail. "I told you he’d like you." "Yeah," McKail replied, in a combination of relief and scepticism. "You did." Her father gestured for Jean to move along into the next room. "Dinner’s almost ready," he announced. "Chef has really excelled himself this time." Jean frowned. "Aren’t you coming?" "In a sec, hon," Tom told her. He turned back to McKail and developed a stern expression that made him more uncomfortable than ever before. "Me and Jacob here need to have a little talk." Really? McKail thought. We do? "What about?" She asked, a little surprised. Good fucking question… "Men’s stuff, hon," he answered. "Nothing to be worried about." Men’s stuff…? "Well, okay," she said, "but be gentle with him, daddy. I know how you can be." "Sure thing," he told her, as she left the room. He turned to McKail developing an almost menacingly mannerism. McKail maintained the pleasantries, despite the change of mood. "What do you want to talk about, Mr. Wildman?" "Nothing of great importance, Jacob," he began, "I just want to know much would it cost me to get you out of my daughter‘s life?" The words simply washed over him at first and it took him some time to fully grasp their meaning. Still he responded, "I beg your pardon." "You heard me," Tom told him, reaching for his check book in the inside of his jacket pocket. Motherfucker… ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Accusation. Police Precinct. Orchard Beach. One day before GTT6 round one… Despite it’s daunting name, the interrogation room was surprisingly docile-looking. Clean white walls, simple grey carpeting and smooth chrome table and chairs. Hell, this was better than his place. McKail sighed and leant back in his chair, its unyielding metal backbone dig uncomfortably into his kidney’s. But that didn’t concern him. He was more concerned about being dragged all the way here, by that damned pretty boy police officer. With the GTT6 just around the corner, he really didn’t need any such distractions. Okay, he had an argument with an hallucination in the street, but that certainly wasn’t a criminal offence. Of course it wasn’t, McKail reminded himself, that’s why you haven’t been arrested, you moron. It was true enough. They’d just brought him to get ‘help’ because they thought he was a lunatic. Hell, McKail considered, they’re probably right. "Pleased ta see me, kid?" The rasping voice suddenly questioned. McKail didn’t have to look around to tell who it was. Oh god no, He silently pleaded. Not this again, not now. Fuck… It was his hallucination, his guide to memories lost and McKail wanted it to just fuck off. But, he already knew that wasn’t to happen; the thing was a persistent little fucker. McKail finally glance around at it and noted that it took its usual form. Mickey from those damned Rocky movies. McKail had no idea why it chose that form, he just figured it wanted to be even more annoying than it really needed to be. And it damn well was. "Fuck off," McKail told it, determined not to give it the time of day. It ignored him. "What tha hell are yer doin’ here anyway, kid?" It asked. "Ain’t yer got a match in tha GTT tomorrow?" McKail pointed at the thing, angrily. "You fucking know I have!" The hallucination developed a smirk. "I see that darkness o’ yours has loosened up some…" McKail was just about to tell the thing to fuck off for a second time, but was prevented from doing so, as the interrogation door clunked open. The plain guy from the precinct reception -- Roy, was it? -- entered the room, not taking his eyes off McKail for a instant. "What the fuck is going on here?" McKail questioned, as the plain guy drew closer. He ignored the question with great proficiency and threw a thick file on the table before him "What the fuck…?" McKail muttered, refusing to take his eyes off the plain guy. "This is ya life, kid!" The hallucination exclaimed. McKail frowned and reluctantly eyed the file. It had his name on it. His police records? This guy had obviously dug them out, but why? He wasn’t even being arrested… was he? McKail didn’t like this one bit. "Go on," the plain guy suddenly said, looming over his left shoulder. "Take a look inside." He really didn’t want to. His police records were something that he’d buried along with everything else he’d tried to suppress. He finally realised precisely why his hallucination had turned up. It’d claimed, only few hours ago, during the unfortunate incident which ironically led to this interrogation room, that it was McKail’s shepherd along the road he had to walk. It was going to help him remember. It was going to help him deal with memories he’d long since buried. It was going to help him get his darker edge back. His police record obviously contained some of his painful memories. It’d didn’t take a genius to put the two together and McKail chastised himself for not picking up on it sooner. He glared up at his hallucination, looking for answers. It shrugged. "Yer wanted this, kid," it told him "yer wanted ta remember all o’ this…" McKail opened the file. The first thing that it contained was his charge sheet from ten years back. He rocked back in his chair again, the memories that hadn’t set foot out of his subconscious for years overwhelming him. He suddenly found it difficult to breath as the pain of it clamped down around his heart and his lungs. He couldn’t look at it anymore… "Good. You ought to be ashamed," the plain police officer told him, "and I’m damn sure going to make you sorry!" He figured out why the hallucination was here, but what in the hell was the police officer’s vested interest in all of this? Despite the memories returning, he had nothing to do with it. But that didn’t matter. If he wanted some kind of revenge over it, McKail wouldn’t do anything to stop him. Whatever came next, McKail sure as hell deserved it. To Be Continued…
