"Attention"

22 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-22 * * * * * Most people only know the sound of a silenced gunshot from the latest Hollywood blockbuster playing in the local Cineplex, but Aidan O’Brien had heard it firsthand nearly a year ago while in Canada. The familiar SNAP! immediately took control of Aidan’s mind, which then managed to send the former UTT Champion tumbling to the ground beside the small diner as the bullet split apart the air as it sailed past Aidan’s head. Quickly, Aidan scurried between two parked cars in a hopeful attempt of taking cover. He managed to figure out that the sharpshooter was somewhere hidden at the far end of the diner’s parking lot, because the bullet had come at him from behind. Or, that’s what direction instinct told him the bullet came from. When in doubt, trust your instincts. Much like taking a test back in high school, or last semester in college…Aidan was always told to trust his first instinct, even on the most primal level, because it was more than likely correct. But, instead of keeping his grade point average at a respectable level, Aidan O’Brien’s instinct was preventing him from being shot to death. Aidan couldn’t hear the second shot’s muffled report, but he heard the bullet zoom over the cars he was nestled between. A split-second later, a low, dull thud came from the back of the parking lot as the bullet ripped into the trunk of a far off tree, one of many that separated the parking lot from the adjacent properties. Given the moderate temperature on this August morning, Aidan’s forehead began to tickle with an oncoming sweat. A bead managed to collect itself and find it’s way down his brow. Sliding down past his jade eyes atop his nose, finally coming to a momentary halt at its tip before breaking free and falling down onto his quickly dampening shirt. He sat there, his heart beating like an ancient war drum, wondering why he was being shot at again. Stuck between a late-model Chevrolet and a beat up Honda, Aidan kept asking himself why he was being targeted once again, and which God did he piss off in order to deserve this punishment? SNAP! Aidan was startled at how close the third shot’s proximity was in relation to his position, only soon realizing that it wasn’t another shot from his unseen assassin, but return fire from the man who was originally sent to kill him: The mock UPS driver, Jonathan (Sail). Aidan glanced under the Honda, towards the front of the diner, noticing a pair of black shoes and brown slacks slowly backing their way towards him. After a moment, the man he had just shared a very uncomfortable meal with was by his side, taking cover between the two cars as well. “What the fuck is going on, Jonathan?” Aidan started, his voice full of anger and disbelief, “I thought you said this was an exclusive contract!” Peering up from between the cars for a moment, the man dressed as a UPS deliveryman replied in a dry tone, “It was. Until about 30 seconds ago.” They shared a glance. Aidan saw no fear in the chestnut colored eyes of his savior, yet fear pulsed through his own veins without any end in sight. He watched for a moment as the man dressed to deliver packages for a living delivered another silenced round towards an unseen assailant. ”Run, kid. Around the back of the building…towards those trees. I know where he’s hiding, and I think I might’ve hit him. Let’s just hope his brother is keeping true to their code.” Ordered Jonathan, not a drop of sweat forming on his seasoned brow. As he scrambled to his feet and ran around the towards the rear of the diner, heading for the row of trees that had absorbed a stray bullet only a minute prior, Aidan kept mulling over a series of thoughts as he sprinted to what he hoped would be safety, a mock-deliveryman quickly in tow: Jonathan kept referring to the shooter as ‘him’, as if he knew who was pulling the trigger. How many battles had this guy seen in order to inspire such confidence? And what was this code that these brothers held fast to? * * * * * The bullet ripped through his black cargo pants, barely biting at his flesh no more than a thorn from a passing briar would, before slamming into the plaster wall of the building behind him, “Aww fuck” he yelped, stumbling backwards and falling from a crouch into a sitting position. Brad Ourlach sat between two giant conifers, the pine scent filling nostrils only slightly more than the smell of fired gun casings, gripping at the new wound in his upper thigh. A small rivulet of blood started to form at the center of the gash – which was only a few centimeters long – finally making its way down his thigh and passing over his knee. He could feel the warmth of the crimson fluid as it started to pool mildly around the top edge of his sock. The wound wasn’t fatal by any means, but Brad hated being shot. And being shot at. As quickly as the bullet grazed across the top of his skin, he produced a small handkerchief and tied it around the oozing wound. He thought to himself that a stitch or two would close it completely and eliminate any chance of a scar forming, but right now he didn’t have the time to break out a needle and thread. He needed to get in touch with his brother, Brandon. He produced a small cell phone and held down the quick-dial key, and in a moments time, was talking with his twin, “That motherfucker shot me, mate.” He scathed, lips peeling themselves back from his teeth. “I’m fine, but he and the kid are on the run.” ”Alright,” Brandon started, nearly identical in tone and resonance as his brother, “Come pick me up. We’ll catch up with that bugger sooner or later…” “I could—“ Brad began, only to be interrupted by his older brother of 2 minutes. “No! It’s not worth the risk. You’ve already taken one round, mate, no need to take anymore. They’ll slip up, trust me. Now come get me, and we’ll be on our merry way.” Brad Ourlach grimaced as the dull, steady drone of a dial tone filled his ear. Brandon had hung up, so he ended the call and then hit the needle-covered ground with a clenched fist. So hard, in fact, a few amber colored pine needles flew into the air and came to a rest atop his legs. He was seething with anger, his entire body feeling as if it could erupt into hate-driven flames at any moment, of which would consume anyone who came close or got in his way. He was going to kill that fucking boy, and make sure Legion was watching when he did it. He clenched his teeth, gritting them as he stood up while favoring the injured leg only slightly, and made his way towards the black Ford Expedition he and his twin brother have been using. He started the engine, completely uncaring to his surroundings – never actually looking to see if any witnesses saw the quick shootout, or what vehicle he had gotten into – and quickly peeled out of the diner’s parking lot towards the location where his brother was keeping their carefully planned code intact. Abide by the code and it won’t matter if anyone saw him, or his vehicle. * * * * * The adrenaline was tearing its way through Aidan’s body faster than any bullet ever could. He ran through three private properties, scaling the two fences in the process, before he was side-stepping his way down a small hill towards the local Mall’s parking lot. Without turning around, he could hear Jonathan’s footfalls behind him. He kept on running, until he heard Jonathan bark an order, “Look for a car with a sunroof, possibly a V8 if you can. Nothing too sporty, either.” Aidan’s pace finally slowed as they reached the edge of the parking lot, slowing down to a brisk walk as they started making their way through the densely crowded section of the blacktop, amid a sea of cars. “Why?” Aidan asked, his heart still racing at a pace he never thought possible. “Just do it, kid. We’ve got to get a new getaway vehicle. I don’t think the UPS truck will cover it…” Ordered Jonathan, who was casually glancing back at the hill from which they just came. He had hidden his handgun in the pocket of his brown slacks. “You expect to steal a car?” Aidan asked aloud, already scanning the lot for a car that matched Jonathan’s description. He wasn’t aware of it at first, but soon felt ashamed that he was already participating and listening to a man that was sent to kill him. But at the same time, he realized that Jonathan had also just saved his life, “Do you know how to hotwire a car? Because I sure as hell don’t.” “Not to worry, kid,” Jonathan started, as he pulled out a small object that wasn’t more than 5 inches in length. It looked like a pocket knife, except more advanced. Jonathan flashed a smile as he twisted the object between his fingers, “Never leave home without it.” “What is it?” ”A tool that police officers use to enter and start nearly any vehicle.” Jonathan stated, the grin still across his face. Aidan felt a little disgusted at the fact that the man could smile in the wake of what they had just gone through. Then again, this mysterious man had probably seen worse. Much worse. The knot in Aidan’s stomach was tightening harder than any wrestler’s grip ever could. He was finding it hard to breath. “Bingo!” Jonathan said aloud as he stopped next to what appeared to be a newer Lincoln LS, “This is perfect.” Aidan’s eyes surveyed the parking lot for anyone who might see that they were stealing a car, but the lunchtime crowd was already inside the mall, rather than occupying the parking lot where they were lifting a car. Aidan’s rationale was as shot as his nerves were, neglecting to even think about the actions he and Jonathan were suddenly taking. ”You drive.” Jonathan ordered as he popped open the drivers door and pressed the automatic lock, disengaging the passenger’s door lock mechanism. In a matter of moments, he was making his way around the maroon vehicle, towards the unlocked passenger’s door. “Why me?” Aidan asked. “I don’t suppose you want to shoot while I drive, do you?” Jonathan replied, peeling away one of his hefty sideburns as he entered the vehicle. Aidan sat in the driver’s seat of the Lincoln – completely numb from the events of only a few minutes ago – continuing to lose all feeling as he watched his would-be assassin peel away what apparently was a clever disguise. “Where to?” Aidan inquired, as Jonathan peeled away the other sideburn. He then started to rip off the UPS outfit. “Get on I-95 South, you can pick it up near Wilmington, Delaware. It’s only 15 minutes south of here.” Jonathan replied, his voice showing no signs of exertion or distress. He appeared to be perfectly calm. The calmness only gave Aidan a increasing sense of fear, rather than confidence. Although the confidence in Jonathan was starting to form, albeit very slowly. “Where are we going on 95?” “Florida.” Jonathan replied, finally peeling away the chestnut colored contact lenses, revealing his ashen gaze for the first time. As the car sped out of the mall’s parking lot, south towards Wilmington, Delaware, Aidan’s mind started to wander. He traveled back over the events of the morning, reaching farther back to last year – when he had survived the attack on his life the first time. If Aidan O’Brien somehow had the ability of foresight, he never would have gotten into that Lincoln and started the journey towards Florida. It would have been in his best interest to keep on running through that mall parking lot and locate the nearest police officer for assistance. Life, however, is full of twists and turns. Life also fails to carry the incredible sense of foresight, which would guide us on the safest route to our elder days. * * * * * I was training in Danny’s old building, now under new management by a much meaner wrestling instructor, when someone I hadn’t seen in at least 8 years walked through the door. Sparring with some wet-behind-the-ears rookie, I was too busy delivering a leg-drop to hear the man start clapping at my successful delivery of the move – let alone realize that he was directing his applause towards me. It was only when the new owner of the wrestling facility, Mr. Almasey, started to notice this particular individual glancing and applauding my every maneuver, that my attention started to deviate from the rookie to the action outside of the ring. “Can I help you?” Almasey said to the applauding elderly man, his arms crossed in a defensive fashion. Almasey, who was short and crabby looking with a balding head of hair, seemed perpetually grumpy. He always acted like he was the favorite, or best person in the room. Or at least should be thought of that way, even when he knew that there were better people surrounding him. A crooked smile hid under a very ugly and flat nose, with two beady eyes that seemed to constantly shift around the room in an uneasy fashion. The man Almasey was watching, who appeared to be heavily interested in my actions inside the ring, held the disposition of a well-loved grandfather. A generous belly, probably enlarged by many pitchers of beer or slices of pizza, hung gently over his belt. His arms, crossed and resting atop his mild gut, seemed to be giving a hug to himself much like he would hug a grandson he hadn’t seen in years. His face was wrinkled, his eyes hidden behind a pair of wire-frame bifocals. A beard coated his chin, white like a fresh coat of snow, and continued up the sides of his face, attaching itself to his full head of silvery-white hair. ”I’m here to watch Sail, I’ve been a long-time fan, you might say…” the man said, his gentle voice filled with admiration. It was then, at that comment, that I stopped sparring with the kid and motioned for him to leave the ring. The youngster extended a hand, obviously admiring my talent and respecting my abilities. I returned the handshake. It was firm, and somehow, I could see this kid succeeding inside the squared-circle with a few more years of training under his belt. As I slid through the ropes and stood on the floor next to this silver-haired man and Mr. Almasey, that thought gave me hope for the future of wrestling. Maybe Danny wasn’t right. Maybe there would be others, outside of my attempt, that could succeed without the shades of gray he praised and ridiculed so many years ago. I recognized this man, but I couldn’t place it. It had to have been years ago since I last encountered him. “Can I help you?” I said in his direction, dabbing the sweat from my face with a dry towel. ”You don’t remember me, do you?” he said, his bearded cheeks glimmering in the lights that hung above our heads. “I think I do,” I started to say, “But I just can’t place it. You are?” He extended his hand, which I immediately accepted, “I’m Jeff Waters, a wrestling journalist. I believe we spoke mildly about 7 or 8 years ago, right after one of your victories early on in your career. Except what I see now – the fire to succeed and overcome adversity – I saw back then as well. But something is different, Jonathan – I hope you don’t mind me calling you Jonathan – because what happened then and what’s happening now are two different things. You’re actually winning now, something you should have done years ago.” He was bold, candid, and tenacious. It was safe to say that I liked Jeff Waters already. As much as I tried to bury the memories of my early wrestling days, soon the vivid image of a slightly younger and thinner Jeff Waters re-entered my mind, and I recall that he was interested in interviewing me back then as well. Except I wasn’t too keen on succeeding in the business, so I made it a job to avoid contact with him. “I’d like to ask you a few questions, Jonathan, if you don’t mind.” Waters said with a smile, as Mr. Almasey quickly found himself disinterested in the ongoing conversation between Waters and myself, finally leaving the immediate area. There was an inherent honesty buried within Jeff Waters’ eyes, which shimmered under the two white eyebrows that floated above his eyes like a pair of clouds caught in a summer sky. Reluctant to interview with a wrestling journalist many years ago, much like my reluctance to win back-to-back matches, I decided it was time to shed that skin as well. ”Sure, that’d be marvelous.” I said to Jeff, directing him towards a bench against the far wall of the facility, as far away from the scandalous and jealous Mr. Almasey as possible. I knew he was going to ask me about the wrestling tournament, and why my career was suddenly taking off after sputtering along for the better part of a decade. I knew I couldn’t give him the whole truth on the subject of my past, but what I could tell him about my intentions in this business would certainly sell magazines, or garner whatever wrestling website that hosted his interview some added interest. Those same answers will also make the other wrestlers keep a much closer eye on the man – the wrestler – named Jonathan Sail. * * * * *
