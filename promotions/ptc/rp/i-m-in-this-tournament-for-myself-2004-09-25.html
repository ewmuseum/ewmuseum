I'm In This Tournament For Myself!

25 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-25 Let’s face it, The Technical Spectacle is putting off the inevitable. He has done absolutely nothing to hide his current emotions with regards to professional wrestling. In fact, he has even stated how much he hates it. And, getting fired from the 4 Winds Wrestling at Maelstrom 5: Winds of War didn’t help the matter either. Isn’t that what he wanted anyway? To leave the 4 Winds Wrestling and focus on his movie deal. You could say it was for the best. But not to Jerms… Jerms wanted to really stick it to the 4WW. Go out with a bang. He had the plan all set up in his mind. He was going to work a deal with current 4WW superstar Mike Biddle and basically make the North Wind Title Contenders match a laughing stock at the Pay-Per-View. He succeeded. But then was kicked to the curb by his former friend and now 4WW executive Todd Dream. How dare them! Technically, the Technical Spectacle is still representing the 4WW in the GTT Tournament, well at least on paper. So wouldn’t it be fitting for Jerms to win it all and show the world what fools the executives from 4WW really were? Jerms would shove it down their throats alright, and then would be done with the squared circle for good. The movie studio was buzzing on this early autumn day. Stage hands and crewmen were busy rushing around making sure the set was perfect. The film, A Mission at Guadalcanal, was supposed to be released in late November but a minor setback was delaying the release. That setback was Jerms. No, Jerms was not easy to work with. He was loud, obnoxious, egotistical, and even narcissistic at times. Just ask the 4 Winds Wrestling who gave him his walking papers just a few days prior. So because of The Technical Spectacle, they had to tweak the film. And of course they had to do it around his schedule. Critics and fans alike just couldn’t adjust to the fact that Jerms was playing a hero in the film. It just wasn’t believable. For years Jerms has stood in the ring mocking the fans of professional wrestling. For years he has made it clear to everyone how much he thought he was better than they were. And now they were suppose to support him in a film in which he was to be the protagonist? Not likely… What the fans wanted was to see Jerms suffer. And since they couldn’t actually inflict pain on him without serving jail time, what better way to see it for just ten bucks on the big screen? This didn’t sit well with the Technical Spectacle. This was supposed to be his entrance into the spotlight of the movie with him saving the day in the jungles of Guadalcanal. Jerms had arrived on set two hours late, as usual. While everyone was ready to start filming the retakes, they had to wait for the star to arrive. Jerms walked on stage with his usual aura of cockiness, and seemingly the head producer had had enough. Producer: Jerms where have you been? The Producer had approached him from behind and had startled Jerms. Nobody talked to him this way, whether he worked for them or not. The Producer had a “STAFF” shirt on and a headset attached to his head. Jerms was wearing a sleek tan Armani suit with a jacket thrown across his right shoulder. His expensive dark sunglasses were still over his eyes; even though he was inside he still wore them for the look. Jerms: I was busy Ed. You’re lucky I came back here. I don’t do retakes for anybody. And who said you could change the scripts? I’m the hero here! Producer: Oh yea pal? Don’t try to act that way to me. The reason you’re here is because if you didn’t come back to do these retakes there would be no movie, and the same thing would happen to you here that happened to you at that crap hole of a wrestling organization. And I will do what I want when I want when it comes to the scripts in order to make money! This didn’t seem to sit well with Jerms. The Producer was making a mockery out of everything Jerms had down to this point. He was still seething at the thought of what 4WW did to him at the PPV. And how dare he bring that up! Jerms removed his sunglasses and approached the Producer frowning. Jerms: Do you know who I am? Producer: Yea, I know who you are. You were a talent less wrestler, and you’re an even worse actor. Jerms: You know what Ed, I don’t need this right now. I can go somewhere else and do something that’s actually worth my time. Jerms put back on his patented sunglasses and began to head for the door at an attempt to persuade Ed into cutting him some slack. Jerms didn’t want to leave, he needed this job. This was all he had left, and he couldn’t walk away from it now. But he wasn’t about to let some pushy producer run all over him either. Producer (Yelling to Jerms): Is that so? Where are you going to go Jerms? Are you going to go train for that little tournament you’re in? The one in which you spent all that time complaining that you didn’t even want to wrestle in and here you are leaving a movie set to prepare for it. Jerms stopped in his tracks and gathered his thoughts. This producer was seemingly playing the same little game that he was playing. But this had bought him some time to stick around and win the argument. He re-approached the Producer with the same air of confidence he had walked in with. Jerms: You think your smart don’t you? You think you know me? You don’t know shit Ed, what I do outside of this set is none of your concern. Producer: Hey man, despite me hating your guts and all you are still the star of this movie. I need you here one hundred percent without any distractions. What happens if you get hurt doing this GTT Tournament? Jerms smiled. The Producer had let his guard down and Jerms was planning on taking full advantage of him. Jerms: In the eight years I’ve been in wrestling I’ve never been injured. And I’m not about to let some mid-carder from a federation that is aligned with the 4WW take me out of this thing. Nick Persia was Jerms’ opponent for Round 2. Jerms didn’t know anything about this man except that he was from the UWF, a sister fed to the 4WW. Thus, an enemy! Jerms hated anything that was associated with the 4 Winds Wrestling organization and would use this match as an example for Childress, Dream and the rest of the executives that had let him go. Jerms: Don’t ever question my motives again. The GTT Tournament is second string to this. I’m here one hundred percent. Besides this thing will be over with in a few weeks and then I’m out of the wrestling business. You got it? Producer: What ever Jerms! Find your way to wardrobe and get ready we got a lot of shooting to do today. Alright? Jerms rolled his eyes and begin to walk to his dressing room to lie down for an hour or two. The Producer started to walk away when he turned around and yelled at Jerms from across the set. Producer (Yelling): And try to get here on time from now on damnit! -The End-
