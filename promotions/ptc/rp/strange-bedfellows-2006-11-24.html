Strange Bedfellows

24 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-24 This is Doctor Daniel Rourke, chief psychologist at the Weirsburg Institution For the Criminally Insane, filing my weekly audio report. This will be put in the file of one inmate number 296715, Bruce Douglas Shanahan. It seems this is becoming a trend for this particular patient. The current date is Wednesday, November the twenty-second of two-thousand-and-six. The time is 4:55 p.m. Mister Shanahan continues to cooperate with staff of this facility, though I must admit he does so at his own pace. Leaking bits of information during our daily sessions and eluding to strange and cryptic remarks, though never so much as hinting at violence, escape, or any other such endeavor worth concern. Whether he's dropping clues about his crimes via anagrams and puzzles I'm not sure; there's a possibility he may merely be playing a game with all he comes in contact with during his stay in this sanatorium, but I've discovered that downplaying any situation with this man is a haphazard way to deal with him. He's too cunning to take anything he does at face value. When he was brought to us, the state police of Vermont had captured him in the midst of an early evening raid during one of his cult's sacred rites. I know nothing of what this ceremony was comprised of and for that I'm unsure as to why he was even arrested and sent to us here at Weirsburg. However, it came to my attention early that officers present at the scene refuse to describe more than what is absolutely necessary for their reports and even long-time veterans were so shaken by whatever they witnessed that they have opted to seek out psychiatric help. Shanahan refuses to so much as acknowledge the event with any seriousness and my objective of dredging out the specifics may prove to be futile. In time, however, he may shed light on what unsettled hardened men of that sort. Until then, I won't be too hasty. It should be noted that his games grow more prevalent. I'm accustomed to being interrupted by patients in the middle of interviews and counseling sessions, but the frequency of Shanahan's queries is becoming much more common. Long ago I grew used to the more disturbed of the asylum population attempting to mimic a Hannibal Lecter and Clarice Sterling exchange as we spoke, but most asked shallow questions that were easily reversible or dismissable. Just hollow attempts to imitate a film reflecting their own situation. Bruce Shanahan is different. His views on religion, life and the workings of the universe are far more advanced thsan the typical inmate of this or any other asylum and he feeds eagerly on any reply I may offer. I admit that I find it fascinating in light of its absurdity. Most of it may make perfect sense, but surely such ideals are nothing but a product of madness. His version of reality is too bleak to exist in a sane and lawful universe. On that note, we had to reassign Rodney Sanders to a different block today. We did so for his own sanity, I'm afraid, and it was brought about by Shanahan's cruel harassment. I still fail to see how the man grew privvy to the facts he used to pry into the mind of his caretaker; there was no way this inmate, or any other for that matter, could be aware of any staff member's background. But somehow Bruce Shanahan found a way and used Sanders' troubled past as a catalyst to nearly break the man. We have prescribed medication as a way of helping Rodney cope with the traumas that his aggressor dredged up, but he's already requested a period of medical leave. I shall be granting the request tomorrow out of fear for the mental health of this institution's personnel. In light of this incident, I've issued a reminder to all staff to be cautious in revealing personal information to the inmate population. Visitation from this "Marcus Stryker" and the other agents of his "Blackhelm Coalition" have slowed dramatically in recent days. It seems Mister Stryker departed for Vermont himself earlier today. And I say good riddance, even if the leave is only temporary. For these men to support Bruce Shanahan's delusions as reality serves only to hamper his rehabilitation and my own research. Unfortunately, my hands are tied. The superintendant has ordered me to cooperate in full with Stryker and his corp of investigators. Though, I admit, I have never heard anything of this organization they represent. Not even the internet provides anything more than rumors. Their name appears on sites pertaining to the supernatural, offering little beyond maintaining their status as an urban myth, but obviously that doesn't hold true. Sitting in on some of these interviews…interrogations would be a better description…I believe that they are little more sane than the inmate himself. At the next board meeting, I plan on speaking on this. In a few minutes I shall be sitting in on a conference between the inmate and a Mister Maury Dawson, also of this Blackhelm Coalition. Though he may still retain the vengeful aggression shared by Stryker, Dawson appears much more reserved and realistic in his observations. I'll offer my own comments of this meeting in my next report. For now, this is Doctor Daniel Rourke, signing off. * * * * Wyatt Manor, Vermont November 22, 2006 8:17 P.M. EST A heinous god, betrayer of all existing intelligence. Bearer of unearthly gifts and unholy madness, always one and the same. He is the bane of humanity and the right hand of the Old Ones, ancient and timeless alike. Messenger of the Apocalypse in his most trusted form come to reign with lies and treachery, a herald for the end, a final prelude to extinction. In him would we trust and through him would we eternally fall from grace. Marcus Stryker could never get used to that monstrous image painted on the western wall, one of the many humanoid avatars of Nyarlathotep. He despised it, hated it. All it stood for was the incarnation of evil and depravity. As did the loathsome building in which he now found himself, a blackened monument built on a foundation of bloodshed and the darkest witchcraft. Even the locals claimed that God Himself has long forsaken what lay in these hills. But now he was forced to look at the vile mural every time he averted his eyes from the twisted duo across the table, flooded with the shame of having to parlay their chances on these unhallowed grounds. A truce with the greatest enemy of his organization was the only way to cleanse the world of its influence. The bitter irony made Marcus Stryker's stomach turn and chills resonate down his spine. Shanahan was incarcerated in Weirsburg, but Stryker knew that even those walls couldn't contain his diabolical plotting. His orders trickled forth back to here, his base camp in Vermont's sprawling Wyatt Manor. And into the capable hands of his most lieutenants, two of which he now met with. Horace Tully and Marion 'Hemlocke' Sterling were little more the virtuous boy (and girl) scouts than their high priest himself and Marcus had no greater desire at that moment than to draw his sidearm and relieve this world of their wicked existence. But he could afford no such thing right now. It was possible that all of Earthly order depended on his tolerance and discretion tonight and that was a painful fact to swallow. "Strange bedfellows, Marcus? That's the expression is it not?" Captain Stryker nodded uneasily in the direction of Tully. The other man rarely grinned, and thus he was saved the vile smirk that haunted his dreams courtesy of Shanahan. Brother Tully was all business as he regarded the Blackhelm agent with a cool gaze. The passion he shared with Shanahan for the Sect of Black Wisdom's expansion was equalled by his desire to see its enemies swept from the face of the earth, and the Coalition had long been in his crosshairs. "I do not question the orders of Father Shanahan. Be that as it may, I'm not the least bit sorrowful in stating that I loathe the idea of an alliance with you Blackhelm dogs, however brief it may be. There have been casualties on both sides for the sake of your crusade. To be frank, Marcus Stryker…" Tully stretched out his arms before folding his hands atop his head. Then came that wretched grin, the one in the vein of his master. "…were there a way around it, I'd have no qualms about ridding us of you once and for all. Your blood would do well to enrich the gardens, far more than the poisons of your beliefs have ever done to benefit this world." Stryker chuckled, hastily gesturing to one of his tensed guards to fall to ease. Their presence was more for show than as true muscle, however. "Then we're in the same predicament, Mister Tully. I have no greater wish than to level this God-forsaken mansion to the ground. With you and your foul kin inside, I might add. Unfortunately, it appears we both take our directives from a higher source. So any manner of duel you have in mind will have to wait until we're free to pursue our own goals again." He fondled the holster at his side, wishing his hand could grasp the butt of his gun as opposed to the empty air that was the only thing it now housed. "But enough bullshit. I'm here for a reason other than to foretell your doom, unfortunate as that may be." "Of course." Tully's mannerisms reeked of his master, but then that was to expected of a man most realized was being groomed as the next mastermind of the dark faction. Fingers pressed together in a steeple, Tully leaned back as Sterling's seductive smile caught Stryker. "We know only that this relates to the meteors once housed by the Eyes of Fear. What else do you want of us, Marcus? We know nothing of those rocks that your own reconnaissance surely hasn't unearthed. Even Father Shanahan admits ignorance on this. They were a closely guarded secret of the organization and the bloodline has been long severed." "Not the meteors and not the Eyes. Raven. He said you'd know it." Tully appeared momentarily perplexed, shooting an awkward glance in the direction of his green-clad counterpart. Ethan Knight, conspicuous by his absence, had been replaced by in this meeting by Marion Sterling. A sight much easier on the eyes for Stryker and his cohorts but no less deadlier. That is if one believed the tales of the Sect's captivating black widow. "We know of the name," she replied through lips brushed over by rose-red lipstick. They stuck out like a sore thumb amongst a lithe form garbed completely in clothes of emerald and jade green. "It's a pseudonym for Denise Janssen." It was Stryker's turn to appear baffled. "I don't know the name. An ally of yours?" "Hardly," she sniffed haughtily. "The Wraith Princess, as some refer to her. We know her only as a vengeful harlot cast from this Sect shortly after its insurrection." "Beautiful and intrepid, much like the Lady here, but far more bloodthirsty in her beliefs." Tully nodded and supplied his own addendum as she finished her venomous introduction of the woman. Stryker guessed that they had fought like cats before this Janssen's becoming a pariah, possibly over the men that Sterling so loved to cuddle with and then kill. Supposedly. "Janssen believed heavily in sacrifice and offering of suffering flesh. Apparently we were too…..'kind' for her tastes." He snickered though Marcus saw no humor in torture. But he'd come to expect that of the madmen leading this cult. "You didn't kill enough innocent people for this woman then?" inquired Stryker with a not-so-subtle snarl. "Not enough blood, death not slow and painful enough? You maniacs must have sharpened your edge after she quit on you then, huh?" There was an audible shuffle on the upper balcony ringing the room. Marcus didn't need to lift his eyes to know that the Sect's surrounding zealots felt offended by the remark and wanted their presence felt. Regardless, he felt no fear. They'd do nothing without an order from Sterling or Tully, the brainwashed cretins they'd become. Brother Tully shrugged. "We don't believe in mass murder as she did. The Sect of Black Wisdom operates on a basis of what is found in our holy texts." Stryker smiled at the explanation but withheld a laugh; he could never get over the idea of the Necronomicon or its blasphemous counterparts referred to as holy instructions. "Unlike what Janssen believes, and currently sermonizes to those who follow such madness, we find no foundation of text that calls for the ravaging, rape, and slaughter of human beings. Her cruelty is offset by a bloodlust that any of us consider an abhorrence." If the likes of Sterling and Knight were disturbed by the woman's thirst for violence, Marcus was shocked that her group had avoided Blackhelm's eye for so long. They obviously believed in laying low more better than the Sect through their brash activities. However, if this were all true and not simply a product contrived to free their fiendish mastermind, it would clear Shanahan and his followers of the massacre in Providence. The very thought brought Sttryker to clench his fist in fury, but he knew that this fact wasn't one to be withheld for sake of his own vengeance. Otherwise he'd be no better than Shanahan or these scum that bought into his black gospel. "But why 'Raven'?" "What of it, Captain Stryker?" purred Sterling. "Surely she's not the only low-life bitch in the world to adopt that moniker." Such language being so uncommon for the refined Piney woman, Horace couldn't stifle a laugh. Stryker was in no mood to share jokes. "Bruce told me to ask Jason Snow. That the two were linked, as if it was the Raven he once knew. Does that mean anything to you? Horace?" Tully pursed his lips before sipping a red liquid from a goblet that Marcus prayed contained wine. "I met him in the back once during PRIME Revolution. Two weeks after his debut. Didn't mesh well, he and I. We spoke briefly, little more." It was Sterling's turn to chuckle. "If by 'little more' you mean had to be separated by security after you attacked him, then I suppose that fits nicely." "The worm invoked the sacred name of Azathoth as a jest. Do you suggest I let that slide?" He ran a hand across his brow before turning his attention back to Stryker. "I suggest you ask Snow yourself then, Marcus. Of course I advise you to bring forth some sort of evidence of her identity before accusing the ex-love of his life of groundless genocide." "Better yet, I'd need to provide some proof that a dead woman is running a cult that makes what you lunatics oversee almost sound like a petting zoo. A dead woman that it appears the man adored beyond life itself. That does well to piss a man off you know." "Death comes in many forms, Captain Stryker." Sterling took a drink from her own chalice, a crystalline grail molded in the form of some elephantine/serpentine deity. The Blackhelm man could never keep those things straight but was more attentive to the woman's words than the geometry of her drinking glass at the moment. "As does life. As does everything, for that matter." All of this mumbo-jumbo talk made Stryker feel patronized, no matter how much he heard it. "Shanahan already fed me this garbage! Stop being his fucking lap dog and tell me what the hell you're trying to say here!" Time did nothing to alleviate that particular peeve. Sterling seemed taken aback, her face red to match her all-green attire. She went silent, drinking of her wine with eyes levelled angrily on their guest. Marcus couldn't help but recall the saying of fearing the wrath of a woman scorned, especially with a ring of crazed cultists surrounding him above, but Tully broke the tension. "It's simple, you brainless cur. Denise never used the Raven moniker except when she was at her most bloodthirsty. We long suspected possession to an extent, but…" "Nothing suggests that Snow's particular Raven was a closet butcher," interrupted Stryker. "So, that said…why would she possess anyone and why lust for blood? It makes no sense." Tully brushed his fingers lightly over the brim of his goblet, his eyes hardened with contemplation. "The universe often makes no sense, Marcus. Listen to what we have to say and you may find your answers. Retain an open mind and cast out the dogma you've been conditioned to believe, if just for the next few minutes." Stryker wanted to scream. He hated being backed into a corner like this, forced into giving credence to the lunacy that Tully and Sterling embraced. Only the bittersweet knowledge that they were as reluctant to help him as he was to help them tightened his grip on patience. There were puppet masters on both sides and they all danced about like marionettes for the sake of their politics. An internal image of seeing those strings cut relaxed him as the theories began. It took all of his will to embrace the madness they offered, and the knowledge that it was perhaps the only way to keep the world safe from itself. * * * * Maury Dawson blinked, cringing as shards of glass bit into the flesh of his face. With a pained groan he grasped the first handhold he could find, dragging himself partially through the shattered passenger side window of his overturned BMW to rest his head in the snow-sprinkled grass alongside the road. His body ached, blood trickled from a gash above his hairline and down the bridge of his nose in a thick stream. As he lay in a heap partially within the confines of the twisted metal that had been his prized material possession only moments before, he didn't pray for help or even remotely consider spitting out a cry for rescue. With his life seemingly falling apart at the seams, Maury Dawson wished he had just died. And with the way his body felt at that moment, he hoped the potential for it to still happen existed. He couldn't keep up his charade any longer. "My wife…..my career…..it's all falling away. Stop toying with me. Please." He uttered those words, tears ready to stream from his eyes more so for the agony embroiled in his mind than any physical pain he had endured in the crash. Memories drowned out the blood now stinging his eyes and the throbbing pressure that had enveloped his left leg, thoughts of all the promises he had made to Sage. Promises that had been broken without a hope of redemption. It was then that he began to mouth a silent prayer for salvation, a request for the deliverance that he felt only the end could truly offer. "Stranger, take my hand. Let me be your savior." Trembling at the voice, Dawson slowly opened his eyes. His vision was hazy and he could make out little beyond the form of a husky man extending a hand to him, his face blurred and indiscernible through the tears and lightheadedness. Maury could feel his body wavering as he struggled toward his rescuer. The voice was kind, compassionate. Full of a spirit that the banker once knew well before it fled his own soul. "I know of you, Maury Dawson. And I know of the hardships you've suffered in these trying times. I can help aid in your ambitions, and in turn you can help me. Together we can return to the glory we both once knew." Rarely had Dawson ever heard a man exude such benignancy, especially not in his line of work. Maury was a proud man, but even he realized his limitations, and the faceless man's promises spoke to his heart. ------------------------------- Weirsburg Institution For the Criminally Insane November 22, 2006 5:11 P.M. EST "All of the lies, the games, the shady deals. For nothing. Men died as we ran the course. All of it, just a big game of manipulation." Maury Dawson glowered at the one called Bruce Shanahan. The one called Violence Jack to wrestling fans worldwide. "A goddamn masquerade." And in the midst of GTT5 in 2005 and early 2006, by the moniker of Masquerade. Dr. Rourke leaned against the room's lone windowsill. His eyes never deviated from the pair, the fiery Blackhelm agent and the diabolic madman called Bruce Shanahan, inmate #296715. A year ago a masked enigma many called savior, now under investigation for a plethora of murders and disappearances. Incarcerated in an asylum for the criminally insane. How the mighty had seemingly fallen. He said nothing as Dawson fired away fervently with his accusations and allegations. Charges of murder, corruption of officials, kidnapping, every other prison time-carrying felony that a man could conceivably commit in a span of eight or so years. Dawson had recounted his first meetings with Shanahan under his angelic guise and Rourke found it remarkable that a man could alter his opinion of another so radically in so short a time. He wanted to see the mastermind fry for his crimes. Crimes that could never be proven now. "We poured our trust into you," he asserted, demeanor considerably calmed, "and allowed you to guide us to redemption. But there was none. We were your pawns, the soldiers battling for your selfish ascension. Dammit!" Dawson's palm smashed onto the table, eyes burrowing into the cold recesses of the charged madman. Shanahan never so much as blinked as Maury leaned forward, his words venomous. "You son of a bitch. I'll die before I let you get away with all of this." Bruce cocked his head to one side, amusement contorting his mouth into a wry smile. Were he not confined to a straitjacket, surely he'd offer more reason to enrage his old supporter. "It's eating you alive, isn't it, Maury?" "What's eating me alive?" "Guilt. This is your penance isn't it? Reuniting with those Bible-toting rats on their crusade against what they fear. The guilt over last year, and the remorse over your wife's death, is devouring you. You didn't rejoin Blackhelm on a whim and you surely didn't do it for a lucrative salary. You're after something. Is it the redemption I couldn't grant you, old friend? The redemption you threw away because your vision was lacking?" "You're not my friend," spat Dawson. "All of that for a fucking wrestling tournament. You're truly mad. You haven't changed." Violence Jack's face was stoic, his thin smile somber. Perhaps he really was appalled by his ex-accomplice's lack of vision. "It extended far beyond wrestling. And nothing has changed. Even now, my lieutenants carry out my plans on the outside. Horace Tully decimates the tournament opposition in my stead. And the resurrection is at hand." The man continued to claim victory even as he rotted in an asylum, sedated and silenced to the world. A will worth respecting in most men, but there was too much enmity for Maury to offer even that much deference now. "There will be no resurrection. And I wouldn't give a damn about how your disciple fares the rest of the way through your GTT. You're here until the day you die, you sick bastard. Nothing can stop that now. You've dug your own grave." That goddamn smile again. Shanahan would never admit defeat. "On the contrary. I'll be walking out of here unimpeded. My freedom is a foregone conclusion. When the time is ripe, I'll breathe the outside air again. You can't stop it, Maury, and neither can your impudent allies. The servants of the Old Ones will always be one step ahead of the Vatican's attack dogs." His arrogance was driving Dawson wild and every ounce of his own volition was being channeled to prevent himself from breaking the lunatic's jaw. It was a testament that only words came forth as opposed to violence. He pressed his forefinger into the forehead of his straitjacketed antagonist, rage spilling over in his words. Few could push buttons like the one he once knew as Chaney. "Nothing can save you! Not Cthulhu or fucking Azathoth, no funny little wyrmholes are going to open in your cell to get you out of here! I'll see you die for everyone you've hurt. I'll see you burned at the fucking stake like the animal you are! I'll personally burn down that mansion and crucify your mindless followers!" "Dawson, step outside with me for a moment." Maury paused and swiveled to face Rourke. Shanahan's overseer was noticeably aggravated as he opened the door and waited for Maury to exit. Firing a last baleful glance at the grinning psychopath in the chair, he stalked outside with the doctor on his heels. Outside, Rourke frowned and spoke in a hushed voice. "I won't have you pissing away all my work, our work over some foolish vendetta. It's bad enough that I'm forced to stomach Stryker's delusions but I'll be damned if I allow you to do the same." Dawson could read more than rage in the doctor's eyes. In that moment, he could identify hs true intentions, the covert goal he strove to uphold. Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan, that monster that had ruined the lives of nearly every soul to cross his path, was his pet project. His research guinea pig and the very thing that would make his career in his field. And that sort of immoral exploitation sickened Dawson. "You admire the man. Jesus…you respect him?!" Rourke wasn't the least bit disconcerted by Dawson's protest. His tone was steady in reply. "I admire brilliance, just as anyone could and should. But having you and your band of delusional thugs waltz in here on a daily basis, undermining every bit of progress we make with your wild hallucinations and fraudueltn charges, is something I will stand for no longer. If you want to continue this interview today then I suggest you drink a glass of water, relax for a bit, and ponder over how you're going to reword what you say to my patient under my watch." Dawson grinded his teeth in exasperation. Rourke had sidestepped most of the question, but he'd let it slide. "Fair enough. But I'll be damned if you protect him from anything he hasn't brought on himself. Besides…" He paused as one hand found an old handerkerchief in his pocket. One that had once belonged to Sage Zimmerman Dawson, and now gave him the inspiration he needed to see justice served. "…vengeance is petty. I'm only here to see the scales tip the right way." A smile from the other. "Then I'm sure we'll get along just fine the rest of your time here." Maury returned the grin, but his thoughts were far more malign than his expression would have suggested. Don't disappoint me, doctor, he silently thought. Don't let him poison you and he has so many others. Don't be entrapped by the sins that I was… * * * * Location Unknown November 22, 2006 4:35 P.M. EST Rounding the garden, he applied the parking brake and stopped. Climbing from the blue Nissan Altima he abruptly perused the sprawling house that lay before him, looming as an inanimate overseer against the countless acres of surrounding field. Much of the house was destroyed, the walls of several rooms toppled by what he assumed to be some large projectile. No matter, there was not time to dawdle on things that weren't his concern. Shifting his eyes from the ravaged manor and flag-crowned welcome garden, Ethan Knight trudged up the few steps leading to the front door. He knocked, then went still as a statue as footsteps echoed in the hall beyond. The door opened and the meeting, long fated by the stars, commenced. The blond visitor removed his sunglasses, folding them before placing them gently into his pocket. His eyes were dark, matching a cold expression forged of stone. His host smiled almost fondly and offered a meager bow. Merely a token of politeness and the grace he'd been so long used to. There was resentment on both sides, for varying reasons. "Ethan Knight," breathed the latter. They'd never met, but he recognized his guest most easily. The former smiled almost mockingly. "Clinton Sage. Pleased to finally meet you."
