Bombs Away

26 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-26 Press releases. Interviews. Endorsements. Hell, even second-rate AM radio talk shows. It seemed to Emilio Rage that his peers would do anything to get a leg-up on the competition. Although in the end this self-posturing and chest beating had little do with the fact that what it really came down to was what these men could do in the ring. And of course, some of them could, in fact, hold their own. Emilio counted on this. He thrived, and still thrives, on competition. But if GTT5's atmosphere wasn't anything but competitive, it was pretentious. The aura of cockinness that surrounded the smiling face of each of the competitors was enough to turn his stomach. But not his heart. Emilio was all game. For whether or not he opted to take part in the media cockfight, when the competitors squared off one-by-one in opposite corners of the ring, no verbal assault, no carefully prepares press package, no media sweet-talk would be enough to hold the former Global champ back as he came out fists swinging, aiming for the stars. You see, Emilio had no reason to run around like peacock strutting his stuff for the pro-wrestling journalists -highly esteemed company indeed, pfft- when there were more pressing matters at hand. Such as getting healthy. Such as getting in shape. Sure, he had come back to PRIME and had won a fed-wide rumble, had defeated Danny Ferguson, one of PRIME's top new talents, and had fought Angelo Deville to a stand-still all in the course of three short weeks. But nonetheless, the ring-rust was still there. For after being laid up for half a year -thanks in no small part to the fifteen-foot powerbomb of Vampir Nosferatu off from the top of the PRIME-A-TRON at Ulta-Violence- it's no wonder that he was not yet at one hundred percent. …Who would be after that? And so instead of wasting his time frolicking with the over-analyzing, quick-to-endores, quick-to-turn-against, odd-placing "experts" in the field of pro-wrestling, Emilio opted to take the real athlet'es path to success: training. Of course, PTC bended PRIME's wrist, who in turn convinced Emilio to let a lone camera follow him around on a day to day basis. Because, although he wasn't a favored competitor to win the tourney, he was still a former Global Champion. And to the PTC-greater community, even some of his "lesser" known accomplishments had become worthy of public interest: his defeat of Vampir Nosferatu, giving the demon his first singles loss of his PRIME career. His refusual to tap out of Deville's Saprano. His record setting time in the Dual Halo match, in which he came in second place. And of course his heroic defense of the PTC Global Title against Karina Wolfenden, in which, although he ultimately lost, he gained the respect of the PTC community, competing in merely six weeks after he broke his back. Emilio had a pocket full of memories to take with him into GTT5. There was no reason to "hype" his own presence. For his presence had a presence within itself.
