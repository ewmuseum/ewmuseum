Faith... you have it or you don't.

2 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-04-02 Religion has begun to be found everywhere. From politics, to movies, to music, and now wrestling. Admittedly, it’s all fair and equal. While the president and some of the movie makers are now admittedly bashing that bible, you have some around that are admittedly burning it. Take Pietro Geist for example; Or to a much extended reach, Angel, not to mention a few others. He thinks that he’ll be the superior athelete for this because why? Because he’s possessed by some unholy spirit? What is a spirit? Are they real? Are they a figment of many imaginations? Are they mushroom induced fantasies, or some immaterial link to the astral that many claim them to be? For Blaise, this has been a question he’s asked himself many times before. While confident in his abilities, he’s been raised by very superstitious people. But unlike some who would sit there and hear their father talk about once meeting a troll, Blaise hardned himself against that type of thing. Even once isolating himself from it completely when he spurned his family. He still spurns them for the most part. He’s a man who’s fathers side is said to be decended from the Seelie Court of Ireland itself. A partial breed of human, Fomorian and Wood Nymph, or Dryad. His full Irish family shows the signs of it in full. Even Fey’s chiseled features scream of paitings in the Book of Kells of skantily clad fairies that tempted travelers to pleasures in the wood. His father used to talk of the wee folk, and the Tautha de Dannan all the time. He still does. According to him, Blaise is supposed to be someone special. But that’s only in his eyes. His mother, a woman who came from the Valley of Fire Paiute Reservation in the northern part of Clarke County, Nevada. She was not unlike the desert indian that is Jay Phoenix. She was very much in tune with the spirual beliefs of the local tribes and such. Between the two of them, Blaise grew up forcing himself not to believe in ghosts and leprachauns. For one really good reason: as far as he was concerned, it was all talk. But then that fateful day came when he met his cousins. Sean and Fey were just as much into this old tradition crap, that it made him pain just to hang out with them some times. But in the end, they convinced the then only practitioner of Ish Shin Ryu to turn around and go to a school. To learn to be a wrestler like they were. Like his father was. Like his father before him. And he did that. He traveled until he met a man whom Sean called brother. A man named Geoffrey Ian Childress III. He was running his first school in Okinawa. The place where he had grown up. But that’s also where he learned about the Order of the Hammer. The organization that traces it’s line back to immortals, fallen angels and such. The great conspiracy theory that the modern day christian faith is corrupted with the ones who fell from heaven, and that they’re out to purify everything that it has touched. Oh, the crap rolled in thick when he was with that school. But Blaise stuck it out, and excelled, graduating from the South Wind Academy with honors, and was the one hand picked to take on Childress’ own Japanese fighting name: Tetsuo Oni. But he never really used it. It wasn’t his way. He was not the Iron Demon. He was Blaise William McClancy. He came to the states a few years ago, and got signed by some minor league fed that he then helped pull up out of the mire, shine it up and put it on a pedastal for all to see. He pulled himself out of that bog, and rose to Superiority. He rose to show the world that he was everything that he said he would be. He won titles, he won tournements. He won the hearts of millions. He made enough money to buy himself, and his fiance a ranch house in the Valley of Fire. Rebuild his fathers Pub in North Las Vegas and expand it out into a veritable small locals casino for him. And to buy his parents a new house. He didn’t do it on faith in some invisible power. He didn’t do it by making celestial or infernal baragins. He didn’t invoke the legacy of Faust. What he did was climb into a ring, and beat the living hell out of those who stood across from him. To pin their shoulders to the mat. To make them tap their little hands on the mat and cry for him to let them go. To show the fans that there was still some of the greatness left in this sport after so many years of wrestling golfers, trash men, priests, and dead men. Let’s not forget the fallen angels and all that other stuff. And in it all… the only Faith that Blaise needed was that in himself. One of his old Themes was Geoff Tate’s “Grain of Faith”. “With a grain of faith, you can move a mountain.” All he needed. All he’ll ever need. Blaise’s mind was running wild as he was finishing his final packing from his dorm room at the Order’s barracks that resided on the grounds of the “Compound”. The headmaster, Mr. Lox, had given him his leave and the doctors had told them that his knee was fine. He was going to head to Las Vegas, help Becca with Kailey and Breanna, and then they were going to head to wherever the PTC and PWW needed them to be to get this job done. And he wasn’t going to be undertaking this without the determination to see this trough to the ending. He was going to go as far as he could. But he wouldn’t be satisfied with losing anywhere near the first round. He wanted to go all the way. He would go all the way. He finished shoving the final articles into his gym bag and then slung it over his shoulder. Sliding on his sunglasses he turns and heads out of the small room that has been his ‘home’ for the last month. It was good that Becca had been able to bring the twins to see him a few times. But it had still been a month of preperations for PWW’s return… the tourney was just icing on the cake. He slipped out of the room, not bothering to lock it. Someone else would be in there by nightfall anyway. It was time to leave this place. Adjusting the bag, his strides started to take him to the parking lot where his Grey Chevy Avalanche awaited him. It would be a long two day drive, but it was better then flying. Blaise entered the central hall, and started for the final door to the outside, walking solemnly to his truck. Not seeing the cloaked and hooded figure that is walking his way. He opens the rear door, throwing the bag into the backseat, the Avalanche currently in SUV mode. He turns, and opens the drivers entry door, and then he hears a voice. That of the hooded acolyte of the Order that had walked up to him. Acolyte: Mr. McClancy. You leave us now to go to this contest? Blaise knew him. A young father at one time, and not a father like Blaise. He’d been ordained and then fell of his own demons. Alcohol and such. Not to mention the other vices of a nun that ended up being excommunicated when she had been impregnated. Then again, he probably was personally also, and as a group, being a member of the Order had him condemned for eternity by their books and orders. What a crock. Father: Blaise. You are heading for the contest? Correct? Blaise: Yeah, I’m off to go kick me some possessed backside in a wrestling contest. What of it? Father: You know you face one of the possessed? A creature of Beliel. He will have many strengths that can be used against those without faith. Blaise: Faith in what? Father: The good that surrounds us. The word of the Lord. He is a creature of deciet and evil. Blaise: Stuff it, man. Please. I’m going to get into a ring. Canvas mat, 4 ring postes, 3 ropes made of rubber and nagahyde coated steel cables, and foam and nagahyde coverings on the turnbuckles. It’s that simple. All that’s going to happen. Father: But I have seen this man’s stuff. I have seen his way with words and such. He will have strengths that you will not. You must be prepared. You have never been taught in the ways of the Warrios of the Order. You must be ready for this. Blaise gave the man a disgusted look. He’d never ever be this bad. Ever. This man was lost. He was a lost cause held captive by Ian’s ways. Although he found Ian and Fey to be the most open minded of their whole bunch. There were some that really thought that they were in some eternal war. It was truly sad. Blaise: Whatever. He’s a man. That’s all he is. That’s all I believe him to be. Father: But… Blaise had had enough of this crap. He’d listened to his mother while growing up about the skin walkers in the desert. He’d listened to his grandfather, whom he got his middle name from, about the Manitou and the stories of coyote and his many sexual conquests. He had listened to his father and the stories of the war in which the tautha de dannan had done not unlike the Europeans and the Amerindians, and raided their lands and claimed them for themselves… He hated it. But there was one definitive answer to all of this. Blaise: Look, dude. You just don’t fucking get it do you? The man was taken aback by this. People knew it took a lot for Blaise McClancy to curse. Blaise: You see, I’ve heard it all before. From those of your Mistress’ side, to even from the man whom trained me. Your colonel and Master. It’s all hokey crap. Just get off of it. Wake up, take the damn blinders off and see the world for it’s physical reality, okay? Father: How can you have no faith in anything? Blaise: Faith? Faith? Look man, I’ve got Faith in just what I need. My feet, arms, elbows, knees, my head. I know what I’m doing. I know what I need to do. You see I have the biggest weapon available over your so called Faith. Father: What’s that? Blaise: Objectivity. You see, take you for example. If you were faced with some ‘member of the possessed, one of those who serve this Beliel’, you know what you’d do? Father: Only those faithful in Christ can withstand their influence. The followes of the true way can conduct the exorcism. To drive out their demons. Blaise: Really? You’d piss your pants. You’d be crawling on the ground in fear. You don’t know what to do in the face of the enemy. Father: I would pray… Blaise: And while you’re wasting your time, you’re enemy would strike you down. He doesn’t need to pray. Why? Because you belive in your heart, mind and if it exists, your soul that he would harm you. That he would own you. If it’s one lesson with this shite, that I learned from my father, it’s a really simple one. Father: What is that? Blaise: You know why the ‘folk of the faery’ are no longer around like they once were? Don’t tell me you don’t believe in them. Your mistress claims to be one. You know this. You’ve seen them. Father: Yes. But no, tell me Mr. McClancy. Why is your spirit so impervious to the persuasions of the spirit world? Blaise: It’s realy simple. How about you? If a voudoun preist walked up to you, with a voodoo doll, and then started stabbing it with needles, saying you were going to die, would you suddenly fall to the ground, lifeless? The father thought about it. He had to remember his training from the old and the new. Blaise: Well? The man looked confused. But, finally he answered. Father: No. The voudoun has no power over those who do not belive that his ritual could cause them harm. The man still looked confused. But, Blaise had him. Blaise: Exactly. Exactly. You see, for Poltergeist to be able to do any of his mumbo jumbo to me, I have to believe it. And if there’s one thing I know… it’s all bogus, horse shit. Poltergeist will have only one thing to think about. He can’t think about having my soul, or tainting me for damnation for all of eternity. Why? Because I don’t belive in it. He’s got to worry about what the hell he’s gotten into. What PTC has tossed his talentless hack ass into a match with. I’ve noticed half of the wrestlers out there have nothing in the lines of ability. Those are the ones with the ‘day job’ gimmicks. I’m a wrestler. That’s what I do. That’s what I’ll do. Mr. Poltergeist needs to worry about the simple fact that he’s facing a big risk in this. He’s got a lot to do and a lot to think about. Maybe they should offer him Hazardous duty pay for this match? Huh? At that, Blaise climbs into his truck and slams the door shut. He puts on his seatbelt and turns the keys as the man just looks on. Blaise slams the truck into gear and spins his tires on the partial gravel as it begins to move. ~fini~
