Interlude: Preparations

19 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-19 The scene was grim in the old laboratory. The experiments were just memories now, if anyone involved was even still alive. With every day, the mold and the creeping vines pushed onward in their conquest of the former Burks mansion. Now there were even pieces of the house itself beginning to fall off; the shingles and stones were finally being cast off, unable to reject time’s advances. Once, Timothy Burks and his family had kept the estate in pristine condition; now it was just a hangout for junkies, hobos, and wayward souls. Plus, John Stamos once went in on a double dog dare. But just once and only because Dave Coulier said that the Uncle Jesse character was the biggest pussy on Full House. John Stamos don’t play that shit, B. Oh, right. There was a rattling somewhere outside the laboratory. Perhaps the souls of Mr. Burks’ mistresses were a-haunting tonight or perhaps the victims of Burks’ secret experiments were returning to the sight. A man staggered into the main room, panting heavily. He was wearing a pristine lab coat and began fiddling with the machines. At this point, they were dozens of years old and inoperable, but he tapped away at their keyboards nonetheless. He heard a noise and turned his head. He pulled something out of his jacket, pointing it towards the door. For a moment, there was silence and then the doorknob rattled. In stepped another man; he looked the man in the lab coat in the eye and broke the silence. "Do I really have to say it?" There was exasperation in his voice as he looked to the man in the lab coat, who didn’t respond. He sighed, took a deep breath, and spoke again. "The forests of Timber sure have changed." He waited for a response and the man in the lab coat now spoke. "But the owls are still around." The man in the lab coat giggled and then he flicked on the lights in the laboratory. Conveniently for the plot, there were still functional after years of without use. The man on the left was Chandler Tsonda, PRIME superstar and GTT5 combatant. The man in the lab coat was some Indian guy, neither PRIME superstar nor GTT5 combatant, but very squirrelly. Tsonda looked upset with him. "Okay, now that we’re done with this film noire bullshit, can we please move on?" Tsonda was exasperated with this fellow, who looked very young in the light. He pushes his glasses up onto his nose before dignifying the Model Citizen with a response. "Ahem, it’s not film noire. It’s from Final Fantasy VIII, only the fourth-best console RPG in the past…oh….EVER." The little Indian guy extended a hand to Tsonda. "Pleased to make your acquaintance, Mr. Tsonda. My name is Brian Suri." Tsonda extended the universal hand for "stop," arresting the conversation. "I thought you said no names." The little Indian kid, surely no more then nineteen, giggled, then caught himself and put on a serious face. "I…uhhh…changed my mind. Now, if you don’t wanna play ball, you can see yourself out." Tsonda didn’t want to play ball; he didn’t want to do anything except advance in this tournament against WWA superstar Armaan Singhania. But there was a reason Tsonda was respected in the ring: he prepared intensely against any and all opponents. And by "prepared intensely," we mean to say that he looked up the dirtiest dirt in any hopes of finding a weakness. "Kid, I’m here, aren’t I? Now why did you tell me you could help me win this match?" There was nothing spectacularly funny about the words Tsonda said, but Brian Suri chuckled to himself. Tsonda broke the fourth wall, mouthed "cuh-reepy", and jumped back into the scene. Suri looked at the equipment around him, spreading his hands wide over his head. "The first step requires the information I advised you to procure." Tsonda handed a manilla folder to Brian, who perused them, while thinking out loud. "Ah yes…Armaan Singhania. Ah yes…five foot eleven. Ah yes…generic black wrestling." Tsonda finally looked up. "What the hell?" "Oh, generic black wrestling boots. My bad. Well, this appears to have all the information, but I have some questions about the extent to which you are willing to go to defeat Armaan. He is no spring tiger". Tsonda snorted at this butchery of the idiom; he had tried his best so far, but it was very hard to take Brian Suri seriously. "First question: have you seen that wrestler’s quote? It’s freaking laughable. ‘Life is nothing without a desire burning inside?’" I mean, c’mon, this is exactly why we Indians get a bad rap. We’re not all a bunch of sissified fags." Tsonda was trying to ignore Suri as much as possible, but it seemed necessary to somehow respond to this. "Umm…yeah, he’s a pretty big douchebag." Typical Tsonda response regarding an opponent. However, Brian Suri continued on undeterred, going down Armaan’s bio. He gave several "mmhmm’s," until he was compelled to speak again. "He was born in 1986; you’ve got quite an advantage in experience, eh old bean?" Suri was quickly becoming that guy in the office who thinks he’s your best friend but who you secretly dream about killing every day with a throwing knife to the frontal lobe. Chandler Tsonda was not in the mood to talk about age so quickly after his thirtieth birthday. "He’s not a very exciting son of Vishnu, man. There’s not a lot to him other then being Indian. Oh wait, here’s something!" Tsonda’s ears perked up and he stared straight at Brian Suri, whose eyes were wide at whatever he was reading. "He uses Oasis as his entrance music. I mean, how outdated is that? Oh man, I couldn’t write stuff better then this." Now Chandler had had enough. He busted out the old school Tsonda attitude at this young Indian rascal. "Listen up, fuckbag. I’ve read everything on that sheet four times over. I know it all; I came here because you found me and told me that you had something that might give me a little bit of an edge against Armaan. Something that no one else could give me." Brian Suri began to look nervous, like maybe this was all just a scheme to meet his favorite wrestler. However, he just tried to pass it off as his being secretive, having a final card to be played. "Oh yes, I have an excellent option for you. I can create a virtual model of your opponent. The model will show you exactly the way he will interact depending on what you do." Tsonda looked very doubtful, but gave Suri the hand signal to go on. "Yes, yes, I will revive these computers with a few simple keystrokes but you…umm…have to close your eyes." This set off the red alerts for Tsonda, but he decided that maybe there was something to this ignis fatuus, for the layperson, a pipe dream. He decided to play along, putting his hands over his eyes, cracked just enough to see what Suri’s doing. It all looked innocent enough, with the Indian tapping away at a completely blank screen. Then, Brian walked over to his bag and pulled out a large piece of rolled up paper. He noticed Tsonda’s peeking and began to freak out. "What the hell?! Did I not just say to close your eyes?! No way, man, I’m done here." He threw the paper back into his bag. Tsonda was not going to call his bluff. In fact, he wasn’t going to play his game at all. He went up to Brian and grabbed the piece of paper he had just stuffed into the bag. He unrolled it and saw…Gandhi. "Listen, I was actually sent by a secret society representing Indian interests. I was going to post that picture of Gandhi as my calling card, kill you, and then leave." Well, things became quite awkward after that. The two men just averted their eyes. Tsonda looked up, still not sure exactly happened. "Wait, Armaan sent you to come kill me?" Brian shakes his head vehemently. "No, see, we’re sort of a fringe group. Since people think we’re so peaceful and gentle, they’ll never suspect us of using extreme violence to carry out our goals." "And your goals are?" Brian paused for a moment, trying to come up with a good answer to Tsonda’s question. He was stalling. "Well…so far we’ve only gotten as far as trying to kill you so Armaan can advance." Tsonda nodded, knowingly. He suspected as much; he continued prying. "And if Armaan gets Angelo Deville next round? Will you just kill him off at the risk of starting a full-out Indian-Italian underground war? I mean, pasta and curry flying everywhere! I suggest you rethink your official stance on this." Brian looked at Tsonda, knowing that the older man was probably right. But there was still some hope for him. "Could you maybe peacefully withdraw from the tournament? Cite family reasons or something; I mean, I read that last RP of yours. Everyone loves a gripping, family story!" Tsonda put his hand to his forehead, once again exasperated with the youngster. "No." But Brian wouldn’t give up. He was going through more possibilities in his head. "Okay, okay, I got it. Heads, you withdraw; tails, you don’t not withdraw." Oh, he was a clever one, but Tsonda was nowhere near that stupid. Brian put his face into his hands, dejected with his awful attempt at furthering the cause of those really polite Indian guys. Tsonda patted him on the head and then walked through the door outside. Once outside, Tsonda wiped his forehead. "Yikes."
