IG68 - August Monday

12 January 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Time heals all wounds. That's what they say. Whoever the fuck they are, August Monday would never know, but he hoped that for once, just this one time... they were right. Peering down the cobbled path to the small Califronia bungalow which housed his salvation, August felt his heart beating hard in the top of his throat, gagging his breath as he faced a demon. His hand pressed to his chest, as if checking that his heart weren't in fact lingering beneath his tonsils. Rest assured, he could feel it's beat beneath his meaty palm. A cold sweat threatened to trickle down his face from his brow that his forearm made short work of, before running over his top lip sopping up that which lingered in his grizzly beard. 'You can do this, Augie. C'mon, man. Don't be such a bitch!' he urged himself with an axe-handle to his upper thigh. 'They's jus' a couple'a old folks. Ain't nothin' to get ya fanny all knotted over.' Drawing a long breath, expelling it heavily, as if the burden he'd carried for nigh on twenty years would come tumbling with it, August took the first step toward his redemption. Redemption with himself. Redemption with the two people he'd dreaded facing ever since that fateful night so long ago. That night his Ellen Jameson were taken from him. The night his beloved were stolen from this earth. The letterbox read “Jameson's” in an old cursive script, hand painted in years gone by and in desperate need of a re-do. Augie's eyes lingered on the ancient letters. So neat and unassuming. The yard was kept neat, each plant regularly maintained and seen to with the nurturing of a patient person. Grass freshly mowed. It's scent lingered in the air. Edges done and weeds tugged from the earth. He turned to the house and stared, the door and windows almost forming a face, as if mocking his cowardice. Each step he took resonated in his soul. In his heart. That gagging in his throat, which he thought might have been his heart, was the impending flood of tears threatening to spill the façade of the monster he paraded as inside of a wrestling ring. Teeth sinking into his lower lip, August fought that feeling to flood his face. 'Get it together, man. What are ya? Some kind'f pussy or somethin'?' He stopped mid-step, fists clenched as he willed himself to move onward. Onward toward those inside that held his salvation. Half-way down the cobbled path, a tug-of-war within his stride as he to-and-fro'ed between facing his demons and running from them for another twenty years. Another twenty years sounded good to the Raging Fear. Swallowing his pride, he thought the better of hiding. Clenching those fists a little tighter, his steps followed in tow, a b-line for the front door and an almighty knock echoed throughout the home. * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * “Who on earth could that be? You expecting anyone, darl?” The grey-haired man's eyes arose from his newspaper, peering over it in the direction of the front door. The booming knock interrupted his Trailblazer updates. “You gonna get that?” There was no response from the kitchen. Just the clatter of pots and pans and oven doors being closed quickly to trap the heat. Stirring from his armchair, the grey-haired man rose to his feet and stepped across the carpet, muttering under his breath about having to get it himself. Eyes glancing at the clock. Eleven forty-three in the a.m. He unlatched the deadbolt and pulled the door open, speaking to his guest before he'd laid eyes upon them. “If you're sellin' me somethin', I'm afraid you're barking up the wrong tree. We're just a couple of old agers trying to get by on our social secur...” His practised speech petered off as he recognised the man standing before him. The colour washed from his face. “Au-... August? That you, boy?” The grizzly bearded man standing before the old man grinned sheepishly, outstretching his arms from his sides as if to say here I am. Like a prodigal son returning home. They slapped against his thighs. Shoulders slumped. Eyes soft. Heart open. “Hey, Jonah.” Disbelief twisted across Jonah Jameson's face. He stood there standing at the last person to ever share a live moment with his beloved daughter. His beloved Ellen. Standing on his doorstep after twenty plus years. “What're... wh-wh-what are you doin' here?” August scratched at his head. “I dunno. Just felt like I owed it to ya.” “Owed it to me? Whaddaya talkin' about, August? You're speakin' in riddles.” That scratch intensified. “An apology. Explanation. I dunno.” He ran his hand down the length of his face, tugging at his beard on arrival. “I... I dunno. I just dunno.” The old man before August Monday let his tears free. They rolled one after the other down his cheek and off his chin, like lemmings leaping off a cliff-face. His shoulders heaved with each breath. Face contorted with a pain he'd been bottling within himself for such a long time now. “I'm...” August chewed at the words. He plucked at his courage. Watching this old man cry wretched at his heart and he was swallowing at that lump in his throat like it were a condemned man's last meal. “I'm sorry.” A nod slowly took over Jonah Jameson, amidst the wash of tears. A slow nod which started small and turned over-exaggerated. His arms outstretched and he beckoned August into his arms with the curling of his fingers. Monday rushed into the embrace, nestling his face into the old man's neck as he howled like a little baby. Breaking down into the acceptance from the old man. Consoling pats on the Raging Fear's back did nothing more than charge the tears with more intensity and before long, the nape of the old man's neck and his right shoulder were soaked in tears. He didn't mind. He'd been waiting for this day for nigh on twenty years. “I know, son. I know.” He patted August's back some more. “You don't have nothing to be sorry about, August. Ain't nothin' in the world. Y'hear me?” The Raging Fear nodded, never recoiling his head from the security of the old man's shoulder. “We all lost her, Augie. Ain't no fingers getting' pointed at nobody, son. Ain't nobody's fault.” August pushed off from the old man's shoulder, face red and blotchy, twisted and contorted with the hate that was coming to boil inside. A hate directed at nobody but himself. His accusatory thumb poked violently into his own chest. “I was drivin'. I killed her. I killed Ellen. I did it. It's my fault. All my fault. ALL! MY! FAULT!” Slowly and deliberately, the old man shook his head. “All these years... twenty odd years... you been carrying this demon inside of you August Monday.” He shook his head again. This time in sympathy for the boy, trapped inside that ferocious man, carrying the burden alone for so long.” Face contorted with disgust, for himself, August shook his head, trying not to believe the words that were coming out of the old man's mouth. “Don't shoulder the load all on your own, boy. That's a burden nobody oughta carry. Least of all alone.” August looked away, he couldn't bare looking into Jonah Jameson's eyes. The regret and sorrow scorching scars upon his soul. Twenty years of carrying the blame. Blaming himself for the death of his beautiful Ellen. All that time. Unable to stomach the man in the mirror. “Augie.” The Raging Fear wouldn't dare look look. “Look at me, Augie.” He did. “Son, nobody here blames you. It aint your fault.” The Raging Fear held Jonah's gaze, peering into the man's soul, at the hurt. The hurt they shared. The agony they both carried. A burden shared. “Ain't no need to cage yourself up, August. Free yourself, son. Free your mind.” He reached out and clapped a hand on August's shoulder, holding August's gaze. “Free yourself.”
