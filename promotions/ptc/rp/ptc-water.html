Water

15 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
~<>~<>~<>~ Moments before the match that defined a career… He sat with his head in the embrace of both sweaty palms, trying to calm the lightning storm that was currently rampaging across his nerves. The fans out in the arena roared like a massive hungry beast, their unbridled enthusiasm for the much-anticipated Round 6 Infinite Gauntlet match-up spilling through the simple concrete walls that housed Rich “The Renegade” Rollins. Or what was left of him, anyway. His ribs were taped up, the soles of his feet were horribly blistered and scared, and his forearm bore the unmistakable red markings of the bite of a leather whip. He felt so lethargic, so pensive and decidedly elsewhere, that he had seriously considered walking out that door and just leaving. Forget the Infinite Gauntlet, he simply didn’t have the energy to deal with the three very capable athletes that awaited his arrival in the ring. Fuck that, he finally scolded himself, I’ve been through too much shit to just drop the ball and walk away now. “So, is this how you prepare for every match?” Dave Olson quipped from the corner of the barren locker room, a slow grin marching across his broad features. “I mean, I usually pull out my iPod and listen to some Kenny Chesney, but fuck… whatever floats your boat.” You would, Rollins thought, but he kept the comment to himself. His usual diatribe about his general hatred for country music usually put him in a better mood, but now was neither the time nor the place. Instead, he looked up and offered Olson a wry smile. “Yeah, well at least I’m still competing at all. From the looks of things, you won’t see any action outside of a strip club for a long time to come,” he said, indicating Olson’s right arm which was cradled in a sling close to his chest. Dave Olson merely shrugged, his casual attitude dripping with some sort of hidden amusement. Rollins caught the subtle mannerism, and instead of letting his NCAA rival enjoy his night off, he decided it was time. It was time to share their stories. Good, bad or indifferent, now was the time to release the bottled lightning in his stomach and get the awful burden of silence off his chest once and for all. “Do you want to tell me about that arm, Olson?” he asked, an uncharacteristic softness invading his tone. And just like that, Dave Olson’s good humor was instantly evaporated. His brow furrowed in consideration, an entire spectrum of quick-witted retorts sitting just on the end of his tongue, but it slowly melted away as his eyes fell downcast to his arm inside its cloth perch. The subject was a sore one, of that Rollins was most certainly aware. But they needed this. And Dave Olson knew it. Finally, the NCAA amateur looked back up and met The Renegade’s sympathetic stare with one fortified in iron, his resolve finally taking reign of his emotions. “Okay,” he decided. And in their minds, they went back into the nightmare that was the House of Judgment. ~<>~<>~<>~ The Trial of David Olson and Steven Hendrickson The room was dark and musty with the acrid stench of long-overgrown mold hanging in the air. Like a damp blanket of decomposing mildew, the odor threatened to smother the life out of Dave Olson and his trembling brother-in-arms, who sat on the opposite side in much the same manner as himself. Tethered to two poles on either end of the twenty-five foot square chamber, Olson and Hendrickson watched the lone Judge between them with unflinching interest. “The bindings behind you each have but one key,” the Judge droned with chilling indifference, “and one key only.” Holding up a ring filled with numerous keys of different shapes and sizes, the Judge gave them a derisive jangle as his blank face regarded both men in turn. The sound was dull in the confined space, but it was a sound that rang sweetly in Dave Olson’s ears just the same. Setting the ring of keys on a small pedestal that stood no more than three feet tall in the very center, the Judge remained infuriatingly silent as he began a slow circle around their only means of salvation. After his second full revolution, he simply paused… then walked out the single steel door leading from the room, which slammed shut and locked behind him. “What the hell?” Hendrickson cried after the door clanked shut. Trying to blow his long brown bangs away from his eyes, he stared across the pedestal to examine the equally-confused Olson. “I guess we try to unlock ourselves,” Olson offered. Looking behind him, he began testing the strength of his steel bindings. Cuffed by large double-wrist fastenings, the men’s chains ran down the poles and into a tiny opening in the stone floor, disappearing into god knows where. “This is bullshit,” Hendrickson finally decided, looking from his chain to the dais that contained the keys to his freedom. “We don’t have enough slack to reach the damn things.” When the sound of clanging pipes began resonating softly in the small room, a sick feeling immediately settled inside both of their chests. “What was that?” Olson solicited, his pupils shrinking to pinpoints. Without warning, a great rush of freezing spring water began pouring into the room through hidden holes around the base of the high ceiling. A wordless cry settled itself on both men’s faces, but it didn’t last long. Springing into action, both men lunged for the pedestal. As both men reached the halfway point, their chains snapped as their slack ran out. But something was different; instead of jerking backwards with the sudden stop, Dave Olson was being pulled backwards. A moment of stunned hesitation flew across his face as he looked across the chamber at Hendrickson, who was slowly gaining on the podium. And then it hit him. They were tethered to the same chain. It was the sickest game of tug-of-war he had ever heard of, and by the time the reality of the situation had sunk in, the little rugby player had nearly forced him back into the wall. As the water began sloshing around his shins, Dave Olson released the most bitter sound either of them had ever heard, seething with something that bordered on both frustration and fury. Flexing his shoulders, Olson planted his feet and began throwing his weight forward, forcing his frantic “brother” to a complete halt. Their eyes locked, the aura of desperate men fighting for their very survival bleeding through every pore of their bodies. One more step forward for Olson, and Hendrickson backed up. The water was rising quickly, making it harder and harder to grip the floor and providing more resistance to both men’s forward momentum. The frigid spring water splashed in mad waterfalls from a dozen locations around the ceiling, quickly soaking both men to the bone as they struggled against the strength of the other. Hendrickson roared with strain, refusing to submit to Olson’s sheer power. It wasn’t until the sudden snap of Dave Olson’s right arm giving way met their ears that Hendrickson finally started gaining a lead once more. Crying out in tortured anguish, Olson slipped against the rushing waves as they rose to his chest, his arm burning like the bowels of hell had decided to set up permanent residency in his nervous system. His eyes wild out of a primal sense of self-preservation, Olson simply picked himself back up. Slowly, he pulled forward. Practically frothing with an indomitable possession, Olson wrenched his entire body against his obviously broken arm, zoning out of reality entirely as he marched onward, pulling Steve Hendrickson back step by excruciating step. A look of helpless terror froze Hendrickson’s face as he began his steady decline, the podium - which was by now underwater - falling further and further away. With one last defiant scream Hendrickson lurched forward… … and slipped, falling face-first into the bitter cold of the rising waves. More swimming now than running, Olson finally reached the center of the room and immediately dove down under the surface. Unable to see through the black oasis that swirled around his head, he simply pushed his face forward and hoped for the best. When he collided cheek-first with the top of the podium he nearly lost his breath, but managed to retain enough composure to root around the flat surface with his face. The feat was nearly impossible with his hands bound; it wasn’t quite like trying to find a needle in a haystack, but more like a sack of nickels at the bottom of a pool in the middle of the night. With his lungs screaming for oxygen, Olson began to panic as he realized the keys we no longer on the podium. Drawing deeply from reserves he never knew he possessed, Dave Olson slipped down to the floor and immediately began feeling with his legs, desperate for a miracle. The chains began tugging at him again, no doubt the result of his fellow captive trying to regain his ground, but Olson was having none of it. Just as red splotches of light began flashing in his eyes as his lungs burned in their final protest, he heard the metallic clinking of the keys against the pedestal. Hitting the floor with his face, he immediately began biting at the water, swallowing mouthfuls of the stuff in an attempt to find the elusive ring. Fighting against Hendrickson’s vehement objection, Dave Olson finally snagged a single key in his teeth, shooting to the surface with the remaining keys slamming in his face. The water was over their heads now, making it impossible to do anything but swim for survival. Finally succumbing to his fellow captive’s desperate tugs, Olson allowed the chain to reel him backwards as he maneuvered his left arm around his body, carefully dropping the key chain through the water and into his waiting palm. As he slammed back against the pole, Dave began his frantic blind search for the one key that would allow him out of this hellhole. “P..pplease!” Hendrickson sputtered, his head finally visible as he fought for dominance above the surface. “Dd…don’t let m… don’t let me die down here!” “Just hold on, man.” Olson pleaded, his fingers fumbling around his lock as he tried his third key. “If I can get myself out, I can free you too.” The chains jerked again as Hendrickson tried to come closer, a battle that was impossible to win. The sudden movement almost made Olson drop the keys, causing him to curse irritably. “Damnit, stop moving around!” he yelled. “You’re making this more difficult than it needs to be, just calm down!” But Hendrickson was a lost cause. As the water continued to rise, the lack of free slack on Olson’s side began pulling him under the surface, forcing him to choke a good mouthful of water into his lungs. “G…ggod… damnit!” Olson spat, his final words before the biting darkness overtook him. His fingers worked furiously, swapping out keys with a frenzied vigor that was only enhanced by his throbbing lungs. Another key went in, and another near-fit. A sixth key, a sixth strike. Key seven, no luck. His cheeks started contracting out of hysterical desperation. An eighth mistake, and his entire body felt like it was on fire. Finally the ninth key slid in the lock, clicked into place, and freed David Olson from his watery grave. There was little more than two feet of space between the surface of the water and the ceiling, but that first breath tasted like sweet freedom as Dave Olson breached its icy grip and gasped. The water had stopped pouring in, but with no light source to acclimate himself to his eyes began to throb along with the rapid rhythm of his heart. “Hendrickson?” he cried hoarsely. There was no answer. For nearly ten minutes David Olson dove back and forth, feeling blindly through the murky depths of their tomb for his fallen comrade, but it was a fruitless venture. Had Hendrickson still been alive he would have found a way to the surface on his own, but the only response Olson ever got was the dull echo of his own strained voice. A grate on the floor near the podium eventually opened from underneath, draining the water and carrying Olson in lazy circles around the room as the whirlpool deposited the water elsewhere. When he finally slumped back to the floor, soaking and exhausted, he had already known what he would find. Steven Hendrickson was slumped in front of his pole, the listless gaze of the dead already settled in eyes that would see no more. When the Judges finally came to collect Dave Olson, they found him alone in the furthest corner of the room, slowly rocking himself back and forth. ~<>~<>~<>~ Rich Rollins was silent for a long time before he managed to meet Olson’s distant stare. He almost expected the man to break down right then and there, but something about him told The Renegade that he wasn’t the kind of guy who wore his emotions on his sleeve. Professional careers and musical taste aside, Rich Rollins realized that they weren’t so different after all. “So,” Olson finally said, gesturing to Rollins’ bandaged side, “what about you? I’m sure that has a story that’s just waiting to be told.” The Renegade stood, pulling his traditional white tank top over his shoulders and tightened the knot in his trademark bandanna before he offered Olson the slightest of shrugs. “Sure it does,” he said. “But it can wait a little longer.” With that, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins left his fellow survivor in the locker room as he headed to do battle one last time for a shot at the Infinite Gauntlet. And for the first time, his resolve was indeed just that. Infinite. ~<>~<>~<>~ Minutes after the Era of Uberness was ended… After a month of suffering the constant sting of bitter defeat, it was he who could not be defeated. After a month of living in continuous denial, it was he who could not be denied. After a month of chasing after the shadows of his rivals, it was he who finally had his day in the sun. It was Rich “The Renegade” Rollins, Infinite Gauntlet Champion, who had had the last laugh. His locker room was empty, save for the large black duffel bag he kept by an open locker against the far wall. It was better off that way; the grueling competition between himself, The Uber Judge, Sebastian Moraco and Clinton Sage had completely squeezed his physical body out of every ounce of energy it had, leaving his adrenaline-soaked psyche to deal with the aftermath. His bruised ribs burned with an inner fire that threatened to completely choke off his already jagged breathing, but he was more than willing to suffer the minute nuisances of his corporeal limitations with a smile on his face. They were proof positive that he was still alive, after all. Memories began to surface. Faces of men he had known for only the blink of an eye in the overall pattern of the world began floating in his conscience, faces of men who were now unable to share in his optimistic vivacity. “…I have kids, folks. I was taken from my home while my wife slept not four inches away…” The voice of a dead man who’s name Rollins had never known. A man who’s wife now shared her bed with the cold and lonely truth behind mortality, who’s children would grow and have children of their own who would never know their grandfather. “…they’re trying to breed out the weakest links in order to uncover the deadliest kind of athlete…” Jason Hedley, the referee who swore he was as spotless as a nun’s habit, a man who ended up being one of those weak links. A man who proved he didn’t quite have the drive to become one of those deadly athletes. And now he was just dead. Even in his greatest moment of triumph Rich Rollins was swimming in the bowels of his deepest regret, and try as he might he was unable to save himself from drowning in it. …OCK-KNOCK-KNOCK-KNOCK-KN… Startled, The Renegade jerked out of his somber musings and tossed a cautious eye towards the door. Slowly rising to his feet, he answered. “Come in.” The face of his unexpected visitor quickly poked itself into the room, a face that Rollins had most certainly not anticipated. Tossing Rollins a guarded look, the man spoke. “Mind if we have a little talk?” said The Uber Judge. Peeling a folded chair from its perch against the wall, The Renegade placed it in front of the small bench he had been sitting on and gave the Uber Judge an affirmative nod. Planting himself in the chair, Rollins watched his nemesis like a hawk as he entered the modest confines of his locker room. Carrying a bottle of water in one hand and the folded weight of his nefarious black judge’s robes in the other, the UJ gently closed the door behind him as he entered Rollins’ domain. There was no air of self-important arrogance, no smug grin that suggested the night had been a fluke, no verbose witticism of uber judgery; no, the man standing before Rich Rollins presented himself as simply that. A man. It was fortunate for the Uber Judge, as the very sight of judges in general had in recent days put Rollins in a very foul mood. That unhindered aggression had been the final amount of motivation that Rollins had needed in the ring, and its ruthless delivery had no doubt left bruises on UJ’s body that would keep Rollins in his thoughts for a long time to come. Casually tossing his robes onto the bench beside him, the Uber Judge took his seat. They sat for a long while, neither man quite sure of how to begin. After over a month of looking at one another as an enemy, it was more of a shock than Rollins had realized to see the level of respect that was plainly evident in UJ’s eyes. It was a respect that was mirrored in his own. “I’m glad it was you,” the Uber Judge finally said, melting the tension with genuine honesty. Rollins let out a reserved sigh, his rocky features suddenly looking more haggard than they ever had. “Thank you, Uber Judge,” he replied, then paused. “Do you have an actual name, or is it too uber for us mere mortals to utter?” The Uber Judge laughed. “This is just my mask, Rollins. I’m the Uber Judge to most, but you can call me ____ _____.” The Renegade smirked in amusement, a feeling that very rarely bubbled to the surface of his emotions these days. “I see. Well then Mr. _____, what can the ‘ol Renegade do for you today?” His tone suddenly very serious, the Uber Judge indicated the bandages around Rollins’ midsection. “I know what the announcing crew fed the fans, but somehow I doubt that’s a work-related injury.” He hesitated, meeting Rollins’ suddenly startled look with sympathy. “You were… dangerous out there tonight, Rich. There was a frightening purpose behind your aggression in that ring, and it wasn’t there the first two times we had a go at one another.” His mouth frozen open in wordless shock, The Renegade absently caressed his bandaged ribs as he was forced to avert his eyes to the floor. “Something awful happened, didn’t it?” the Uber Judge asked bluntly. Rollins slowly nodded his head. “I was Judged,” he replied softly. Obviously misinterpreting Rollins’ meaning, the Uber Judge frowned. “You don’t strike me as the kind of fellow who puts much stock in other people’s opinions,” he offered. The Renegade looked up at that, a firm scowl etched deep in the lines of his forehead. “I’m good at putting up walls, ____. It’s what I do. I’m not exactly perfect.” Chuckling, the Uber Judge tossed Rollins a sly wink. “I’m pretty good at pointing out people’s imperfections, Rich. It’s what I do.” The scowl fell away as Rollins turned his attention elsewhere. His face spoke volumes, and the loudest of them all was quite clear to the Uber Judge. Whatever it was, Rich Rollins wasn’t prepared to talk about it. Some walls were hard to crack, no matter what tool you used to do the chipping. But the Uber Judge decided to try anyway, holding out his bottle of Aquafina and giving it a good shake in the soft florescent light. “People are like water,” the Uber Judge said, trying to break through The Renegade’s self-inflicted metaphor, “We can take the shape of anything we want, hiding our fears and ambitions inside the vase of life’s many lessons, but deep down we’re more transparent that we want to believe.” You have no idea, The Renegade thought, suppressing an involuntary shudder. His expression remained the same, withdrawn as ever. “Bah, listen to me,” the Uber Judge said with a snort, “jumping from judge to philosopher, you’d think I would know better than to wax philosophical with a self-proclaimed anarchist.” The comment was received with not even so much as a smile, and the distant look in Rollins’ face told the Uber Judge that it was time to go. Scooping up his robes, he began walking towards the door, but stopped as his hand tickled the handle. “Good luck with Knight and Kaze,” he said over his shoulder, then added, “…champ.” And with that, the Uber Judge took his leave. Before The Renegade had time to collect his thoughts upon UJ’s departure, his cell phone began humming from a pocket in his wrestling duffel. Quickly producing the outdated T-Moble, Rollins frowned as he read the screen: 1 New Message It was a simple text message from Steven Caldera - a feature in which Rollins detested because of its redundancy, as he was sure his boss sent it just to piss him off - and he quickly scrolled through the menu and brought it up. Rollins, You kill my World champ, and I kill your career. Unless Knight and Kaze don’t do it first. -Caldera Lovely, he mused. Stuffing the phone back in his bag, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins took one last look around the sterile bleakness of his locker room, wondering if it would be as kind to him next week as it was today. But he knew it would. His journey had only just begun. ~Fin~
