Date: 2006-02-08 February 13, 2004 Home of Seymour and Aerith Almasy "Seymour," she said, looking into his eyes, "I can’t do this anymore." "What do you mean, Aerith," By his line of thinking, everything was going perfectly fine. He’d cut back on his career considerably, wrestling fewer and fewer dates. That meant more time to spend with the woman he loved. Consolingly, he wrapped his arms around her, first pulling her head down to rest against his shoulder. It was his favorite way to hug her, the way he always used when he felt she needed reassurance and closeness. Sadly for him, today, even his trademark hug wasn’t going to work. She stiffened in his arms, reluctantly pulling herself free from her husband’s embrace. "I went to the store today to get some groceries," Aerith said, shaking with a combination of sadness and anger, "and some reporter stopped me while I was in the checkout line. That’s the third time this month." Under his breath, Seymour grumbled. It was bad enough they went after him. But his wife too? That was inexcusable. "I’m sorry," he said, shaking his head. "I’ll call them up, make them stop." She shook her head, took a deep breath. She’d believed him when he said that in the past. And for good reason. He had indeed picked up the phone and spoken politely, and then not so politely, to executives at newspapers around the world. Each time they’d said they’d stop. And inevitably, they didn’t. "They didn’t stop in Jamaica, on our second honeymoon," she replied, sadly. "Nor on Valentine’s Day last year, when all we wanted was a romantic night out. Face it, Seymour, they aren’t going to stop. Ever." He nodded sadly. She was, after all, right. Seymour could protect her from any bully in the wrestling ring, and keep her secure in their home, but against the damned paparazzi, he was helpless. She looked back at him equally sadly. Aerith had never wanted it to come to this, but now, there was no choice. No easy way to say this. Because no matter how nicely she put it, no matter how she tried to spin it, she was going to do the one thing she had promised never to do to her best friend since the very first day they met in kindergarten. "Seymour…I want a divorce." Break Jason Seymour Wilson’s heart. Present Seymour Almasy had been in the ring with the best wrestlers the world had to offer. Over the course of an illustrious career, he had been hit with baseball bats, barbed wire, and steel chairs. And yet, he was hard-pressed to think of a time that he felt more like shit than he did right now. That was what happened when your travel itinerary over the course of two days looked like a leg of The Amazing Race. Flight from Tokyo from Guam. Flight from Guam to San Francisco. Flight from San Francisco to New York City. No wonder he felt like his legs were spaghetti as he emerged from the gate, nearly tumbling over to the floor as he did so. "Goddamnit," he spat, irritable from the flights and the death of a beloved family member. He hadn’t even seen Cid Wilson recently, due to the hustle and bustle of GTT5. Now he’d never speak to his grandfather again. Such was what Seymour was rapidly beginning to see life as. A series of injustices, spaced out by years, designed to cripple his very soul. The Final Fantasy sank into an uncomfortable airport chair, facing the long hallway that led to all of the terminals, waiting for his ride out of this particular circle of Hell to arrive. Even that, though, wasn’t a pleasant proposition. After all, he remembered the last time Laura had picked him up from a Godforsaken hellhole. It had ended with him finding a strange man in her bed. That, of course, had ended in his ceasing diplomatic relations with her, a state that would have continued if not for the tragedy that was a heart attack. Last time, she had come with milkshakes and a shoulder for him to lean on. After the venom he’d spewed at her the last time they met, he doubted she’d be quite so forgiving this time. "Seymour!" And, as usual when he dealt with his perceptions of others, he was dead wrong. He cast weary eyes up at his ex-wife, to find her standing there, radiant as always. She wore a black mourning gown, clearly evidence of her own sadness at the passing of her grandfather-in-law, who in truth was the man who actually paved the way to her marriage to her childhood friend. "Hi, Laura." He felt somewhat guilty for the disinterested tone of voice he used, but really, he couldn’t be bothered to care too much about anything right now. It all seemed so very hopeless. She stooped by him, kneeling down to brush sweaty bangs out of his eyes. "Poor thing. This has to have been terribly difficult for you…" He squirmed, trying to get away from her. Seymour didn’t want her touching him. Not that her hand wasn’t as soothing as it always was, but because he felt guilty. He’d screamed at her. He didn’t deserve this. "Yeah," he grunted in response. It still didn’t seem real, and it likely wouldn’t until Seymour saw his grandfather, a vivacious and energetic man even in his early 90s, lying lifeless in his coffin. He thought that his standoffishness would get her to go away. Again, he was mistaken. Gently, her thumb brushed the skin under his eye, finding it damp. "Awww, you’ve been crying, haven’t you?" Without hesitation, she wrapped her arms around his neck, paying no heed to the growing number of gawkers. Seymour felt utterly trapped, trying to pull away without success. "Shhh," she told him. "It’s okay, I’m here." He felt panicked. This wasn’t supposed to happen! She wasn’t supposed to be all consoling and caring. She was here to give him a ride, no more, no less. Everything tore at his mind now. The death of his grandfather, his breakup with Laura, all the arguments they’d had since then. GTT5’s fifth round, Kimbusa, and GCW becoming a living Hell for him. Crying was the only release he had left. "I…can’t…" Laura shushed him gently, brushing the back of his neck with feather light strokes. "Go ahead, Seymour," she whispered. "Let it go…" *click* It felt good to cry. So very good. He didn’t have to be Seymour Almasy right now, the brave warrior who stepped into the ring against impossible odds and triumphed. He didn’t have to try and scrape himself off the mat after the 500 pound Kimbusa gave him a Samoan Drop. *click* No, he could simply cry. Feel. Be, without worrying about who was watching. Sure, there were people here, but it was a bunch of people waiting for their planes who had no idea who he was. He was safe here, to wallow in both his misery and the comfort of someone who, for a reason he didn’t quite understand, still cared. *click* *click* *click* "Seymour, what’s that?" Still holding onto her husband, Aerith heard the clicking noises coming from behind her. Slowly, dutifully, Seymour raised his head from its comfortable perch on her shoulder, looking behind his ex-wife through tear-blurred eyes. No. A fucking reporter was standing there. With a camera. Snap-snapping away as if he didn’t have a care in the world. Seymour couldn’t believe it. "Wh-what’s wrong," Aerith felt frightened suddenly, and so she clung tighter to Seymour. Slowly, Almasy stood, forcing her to stand up to continue clinging to him. "It’s a reporter," he said, saying the word with such disdain that a non-English speaker would ascertain it to be a despicable curse. "Let go of me for a minute, Laura. I have something I need to take care of." Reluctantly, she let go of him, turning to look at the man with the camera. Her eyes contained equal enmity as Seymour’s, as she thought back to all that this man’s ilk had taken from her. The photographer tried to retreat, but Seymour was too quick, stepping forward to look at the man, dead in the eyes. "The…Hell do you think…you’re doing," he managed to blurt out, voice weak and strained as tears continued to fall down his face. "Taking pictures," the man stammered, nervous now that he’d been confronted. Seymour continued to glare at him, with wild, blurry eyes, and the Final Fantasy knew in that instant that there now would be no turning back. He knew what would happen if he just walked away. Those pictures would appear on the front cover of the Wrestling Inquirer, with some damning caption like "FINAL FANTASY AND EX-WIFE BACK TOGETHER!" Tessa Windsor would see that. And then, another relationship would be ruined by these fucking parasites. It was time to do something he’d only dreamed of for the past two years. With his left hand, he swatted the heavy camera down. It fell from the shocked photographer’s hands, smashing into pieces on the airport’s floor. With his right hand, he swung for the fences. *THUD!* The unmistakable sound of fist on skull echoed throughout the terminal, as the photographer dropped like a shot. Gasps followed, as Seymour spun on his heel, grabbing his former wife by the wrist. "Come on, Laura. We’re getting the fuck outta here." ----------------------------------------- Seymour winced at the blast of cold air that greeted him as he stepped out of the carpark. Behind him, an equally cold looking Laura looked at him, still shocked at what he’d done in the terminal. "Seymour! You’re going to get sued!" "I know I am, Laura," he responded, still wiping remnants of tears from wet eyes. "But I don’t care anymore. If I have to pay a fine, I can pay it, even a sizeable one. If they want me to go to jail, I’ll deal with it." She looked at him then, and saw in his face unmistakable conviction. It was the same look that he had on his face when he stepped into the ring against impossible odds. A look of determination that was nearly shatterproof. "Besides," he said, finally allowing a small grin, "you enjoyed that almost as much as I did." Slowly, she worked up the courage to nod. "Almost wish you’d done it along time ago." "Yeah," was his reply, a word he was getting a good deal of use out of today. "So do I." His own hand rose to his forehead, reaching to massage his temples. The events of the past few days were nothing short of headache inducing. Worriedly, Laura looked at him, moving closer to him to see if everything was alright. That fact still astounded him. Enough so that he felt compelled to ask about it. "Laura, why are you…err…" It was sort of difficult to ask the question, really. Framing it was difficult. "Not smacking you across the face right now?" She always WAS a rather perceptive woman, Seymour thought. "Yeah, pretty much." Laura seemed to think about that question for a while, as if she herself didn’t really know. Not that Seymour could blame her if she didn’t. This entire situation tended to bring out raw emotion rather than calculated logic. He was flying by the seat of his pants as it stood. "You didn’t deserve to see that, Seymour. I went up to the room after you left and woke him up. He’ll never know how close he was to you decapitating him…" "And?" Seymour asked, pushing the issue a bit. "And, looking at the state of that room, I can understand why you were so upset." She blushed crimson at that, and it was an unfamiliar expression to Seymour. Rarely had he seen his ex-wife blush. Usually, she was the one who made HIM blush, with rather creative techniques and speech. "I don’t even know why I went out that night," she continued, letting the entire story bubble out of her. "I just wanted a drink, and one thing led to another, that led to another…" "Until you’ve found new and exciting uses for your lingerie collection." "If you have to put it that way, yes." She sighed audibly at that, rolling her eyes. Seymour couldn’t hold it against her; he, too, had made lots of mistakes when drunk. "You ever go out and drink and…you know," she offered, weakly, and without much hope. Clearly, she thought Seymour to be an innocent. Which, to be fair, he was. At times. "Actually," he said, a small grin teasing the corners of his mouth again, "after we broke up, during GTT4, after my first round match, I went out to this club and met a nice girl. Was too drunk the next morning to remember what happened though." She was just about to nod to him in understanding, when she remembered something. "Wait a second. The club you went to…it wasn’t called the Gaia, was it?" Okay, this was getting stranger and stranger by the moment. "Yes, yes, it was. But why do you ask?" "Because," she replied, looking at him dead in the eyes, "I was at the Gaia that night. And I woke up in our apartment. Next to you." His jaw dropped. The possibilities of that happening were mind-boggling. Beyond mind-boggling, even. Absolutely ridiculous. Yet, everything made sense. The glimpses of the woman that he could remember very much fit Laura. "Seymour," she began, suddenly serious, "don’t you see? All of this is a sign. Our meeting then, everything that’s happened….you know, I talked to Cid before he died. Two days before." Suddenly, Seymour was rather interested in the twists and turns this conversation had taken. "What did he say," he asked, nervously, trying to read the growing unreadable Laura. "He told me that he wanted us to be together again. He wanted you to be happy, to get away from everything that was bothering you…" Seymour couldn’t respond. Everything was simply moving too fast for him, even before she dropped her bombshell. "Seymour…please…" "Please what?" "Retire. And marry me again." For a few seconds, he almost laughed. But then, he noticed the look on her face. The look that told Seymour that this wasn’t a joke. The look that told him that his life, one way or another, was about to change forever. (TBC)
