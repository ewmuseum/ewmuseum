Loose Ends (Part One)

8 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-02-08 EVERYBODY said so. Far be it from me to assert that what everybody says must be true. Everybody is, often, as likely to be wrong as right. In the general experience, everybody has been wrong so often, and it has taken, in most instances, such a weary while to find out how wrong, that the authority is proved to be fallible. Everybody may sometimes be right; "but THAT'S no rule," as the ghost of Giles Scroggins says in the ballad. The dread word, GHOST, recalls me. Everybody said he looked like a haunted man. The extent of my present claim for everybody is, that they were so far right. He did. -Charles Dickens, The Haunted Man Mind exhausted and body spent, a weary Steven Caldera slumps into the back seat of his limousine. He had never been so thankful for the absolute precision of his driver, who had collected him right from the tarmac at Port Columbus International Airport. It had been a long flight home from Tokyo, crammed into the suddenly claustrophobic confines of his Gulfstream G550, but he still hadn’t managed to totally grip what he’d just heard. Never in his wildest dreams could he have guessed what he would find; and Steven Caldera has a vivid imagination. The door slams closed and the limo pulls away, heading downtown for Caldera’s office. Caldera hadn’t felt like going home; in fact, he began to realize, he hadn’t been home in two weeks. Smiling in spite of himself, he began to wonder once again just why he even bothered keeping a house at all. But somehow his own problems seemed trite and unimportant, and quickly his mind wanders back to his trip. His secretary had been most surprised when he called her from the plane. Caldera rarely goes further than New York City these days, preferring to make frequent use of his videoconference equipment in lieu of traveling. So she was understandably confused when Caldera had told her to cancel his appointments for the next week; he was going to Tokyo. He didn’t tell her what he was doing there, though he didn’t have a clear idea himself. He had been relieved to board his comfortable jet in Tokyo the morning before, but now, looking upon the modest dimensions of Columbus, Ohio, Caldera finds himself longing for the dark alleys and towering skyscrapers of that urban metropolis. As the limo speeds along, Caldera reaches into the liquor cabinet and produces a half-empty bottle of Maker’s Mark. Without bothering to dirty a glass, Caldera unscrews the cap and thrusts the bottle into his mouth, swallowing a deep drag of whiskey. The drink offered him little reprieve from the chill that had taken up residence in his gut back in Tokyo, but his senses began to sharpen. Caldera cradles the bottle in his lap and lets his head drop against the window, when suddenly a stinging shock seizes him. His eyes grow wide as he peers out the window at the highway speeding by. Peering back into the distance behind him, Caldera shook his head. There was nothing there. But somehow, for just a moment, he had though he’d seen… Ridiculous. Why would he be standing on the side of the road? Glancing through the tinted windows, then back to the interior of his limo, Caldera suddenly finds himself scoffing at his surroundings. He is clad in an uncharacteristically low-key ensemble of jeans and a t-shirt, but still he had been collected in a high-class car, offering only the best of creature comforts, for a ten-minute ride back to his office. Caldera then suddenly finds himself realizing he hadn’t driven a car in over a year. Didn’t I buy an Aston Martin six months ago? Caldera’s head begins to swim. What on earth had Kimbusa sent him there for? Had he really meant for him to go all the way to Tokyo to learn the rest of the story? Whatever Kimbusa’s intentions, the trip had changed Caldera… damaged might be the more accurate term. Caldera had always prided himself in being exceptionally well-traveled, with travel being the joy that had led him to wrestling in the first place. But Caldera had abandoned the rental cars for private jets, traded in the grungy world of professional wrestling for the sunny resort towns of the world. But this trip had been decidedly different; it had been a very long time since Caldera had seen the gritty underbelly of such a city, and perhaps the first time he had ever dared to explore the dark places of the world. The limo pulls onto the offramp and circles down into the city. Downtown Columbus suddenly seems painfully ordinary, an insignificant speck on a map full of intrigue. Caldera feels himself beginning to fade away, his eyes glaze over as he retreats into his mind. The city is busy today, an unseasonably warm afternoon in the dead of winter. Then, suddenly, Caldera’s bemused stare catches sight of the same massive figure. There, standing in the shadows, barely visible behind the hurried crowd; an immense specter, rimmed in black but unmistakably human, his glowing red eyes fixed on the limo. Caldera rubs his eyes and rolls down his window for a better look, but a large bus passes before him. Caldera cranes his next out the window as the bus passes along, but the figure is gone. Whether it had slipped away or been dispersed by the passing breeze, Caldera couldn’t be certain. But he suddenly finds himself feeling decidedly uncomfortable with notion that he might be out there. The limo finally arrives at Caldera Enterprises, Incorporated, a large contemporary building in the center of Columbus. Caldera lets himself out of the limo out front and thanks his driver, then looks up at the building. As usual, it appears to be business as usual inside; they hadn’t missed him. Hell, with the exception of his secretary, they probably didn’t even know he was gone. Caldera walks up the marble steps towards the entrance, eyes scanning the uncharacteristically dense crowd. Suddenly he catches sight of it again; a dark figure, concealed behind a dark trenchcoat and fedora, standing there in the throng of people. Caldera snaps his head around and squints into the distance, cursing his nearsightedness. But again, as the crowd began to disperse, the figure was gone. First thing tomorrow morning, I’m getting lasik, Caldera mumbles to himself. Caldera makes it to the front door unrecognized and pushes through the revolving door. He pushes through the sea of anonymous underlings and slips past the elevators. The main elevators only went as high as the twenty-first floor. But the lone elevator at the back of the hall went all the way to twenty-two. Caldera pushes the call button and slips into the elevator. He digs through his pocket and fishes out his keys, swiping a small metallic wand over the sensor pad. He then pressed 22 and the doors began to close; but not before catching another fleeting glimpse of a five-hundred pound figure making its way through the lobby. The elevator rapidly rises to the twenty-second floor, which is occupied entirely by Caldera’s office and his private quarters. The gold doors open into a modest looking white reception area; the desk was unattended, as he’d given Janice the weekend off. Walking past the desk to the large doors behind it, Caldera inserts a heavy metal key into the lock and turns it. But the lock doesn’t click; confused, Caldera reaches out for the knob and turns it, and the door swings open without argument. Wearily he pushes through the doorway, peering into his dimly lit office. The room was massive, with high ceilings and tall windows. But the curtains were drawn, and only a few thin beams of light had managed their way into the room. Frowning, Caldera flips the light switch, but the room remains dark. He flips the switch up and down a few times in frustration, but the power is decidedly out. Shaking his head, Caldera steps into the office and quickly makes for the back room. There, behind a door concealed in an out-of-the-way wall, was Caldera’s bedroom and washroom. He’d had this private refuge installed for late nights when he’d stay at the office rather than returning home. But it was getting to the point that the office itself could very easily be called home. Caldera opens the door and looks inside his bedroom; just as he’d left it. But upon trying the switch inside, Caldera finds the lights in there to be inoperative as well. Then, suddenly, a massive slam erupts from behind him, causing the floor to tremble beneath him. Caldera spins about to find the heavy door to the office closed tight, and he finds himself trapped in his own dim sanctuary. Peering hard through the darkness, Caldera detects no foreign presence in the room, until a slight rustle rises from the left. The immense room had too many private corners, designed for guests to have plenty of places to isolate themselves; Caldera suddenly begins to sense he is not alone. Glancing up at his desk, sitting elevated above the rest of the room, Caldera considers going for the phone. But something keeps his fixed in the doorway, his heart pounding so hard in his chest that it begins to echo in the room. Another rustle, this time sounding disconcertingly like shuffling footsteps; closer this time, and closing in. Caldera maintained his wrestling physique, and feared no man; but a deep and definite sense of dread filled him, as if the other presence of the room was more shadow than man. Finally his peering eyes pierce the darkness enough to catch glimpse of the same elusive shadow, quickly crossing the room as silent as death. His mind unable to grasp how it was possible, Caldera nonetheless forces himself to admit the truth. He had come back. "Where are you?" he calls out into the darkness, summoning all his strength to keep his voice from breaking. There is still no way of knowing exactly what his intentions were, and Caldera felt himself decidedly petrified by the possibilities. No answer. Caldera slowly inches out into the office, searching now not with his eyes but his ears. But the sound and shadow had both gone, and Caldera once again finds himself alone. Smirking a bit at his paranoia, Caldera forces a laugh. His imagination was toying with him again, and he was in desperate need of a good long sleep. Turning back to the door, Caldera looks upon his bed with longing eyes. Then, eyes narrowing, Caldera suddenly spins around again. Before him is a massive form, dark and obscure but undeniably real. Looking down upon him with piercing red eyes was the terrifying form of Kimbusa, looking distinctly malevolent in the darkness. Caldera cries out in shock and backs into the wall, startled and alarmed by the intrusion. The two stand at odds for a few moments, as Caldera studies his unexpected guest with trepidation. But Kimbusa steps forward into the light, and the sinister shadow gives way to a tired, empty face. "Did you find what you were looking for?" Kimbusa asks, his voice gravelly and grim but non-threatening. Caldera timidly steps up towards his desk and throws back the curtains. Light pours into the office, and at once Caldera’s fears are assuaged. Kimbusa had seated himself upon a leather couch not far away, and Caldera, suddenly eager to speak again with his former friend, steps down and sits across from him. "That depends on what I was supposed to find, I guess," he answers, still disoriented but composed. "I’ve come back to…" Kimbusa began, his English sounding unusually broken. "I know where you were. I know who you spoke to at the tavern in Tokyo. Tell me what you found." Caldera sits back in his chair. "Okay… I guess I’ll start from there." --------------------------- The Entertainment District was home to many high-class gentlemen’s clubs, catering to the rich and powerful of the city with beautiful young women for purchase. Steven Caldera felt, on some level, ashamed to be there, as he had never even considered entering one of these establishments. Yet on this evening it was his mission to find one in particular, where he had been told he would receive the answers he came to find. Kimbusa had mysteriously appeared at his office one day, where he began to speak of his time spent in the Tokyo underground. But Kimbusa had stopped just as the story was getting interesting, and Caldera had felt compelled to come to Tokyo in search of the story’s completion. He had been told to find "the best place in town," which Caldera could only assume was code for something else altogether. So he found himself walking into the heart of the Entertainment District, unsure of what he might find. A light rain had begun to fall, thickening the perpetual mist that shrouded the streets. But it gave the air an ethereal electricity, and the streets were aglow with brilliant color. Dancing neon signs lit extravagant facades as far as the eye could see, but the light somehow seemed to deepen the darkness. Despite the countless fluorescent signs lining the street, they did nothing to pierce the gloom that had descended upon the main street at nightfall. It was an ominous darkness, perhaps the lone reminder of the true nature of this place. Between the flashy buildings were dark, brooding alleys, fearful places that Caldera hurried past at every encounter. There were voices on the air, low but drunk with power (and liquor); but the people speaking them were shrouded in the murky cold. Caldera couldn’t help but feel a little like Marlow heading down the river… Most of the signs were in Japanese, though some were in English characters. Caldera wasn’t sure where to begin, but before long he spotted a building that stood out amongst the others. There were no tacky neon billboards or shameless nude female images on the windows; the building was a bit taller than the rest, dimly lit, with a few platinum characters on its face: 町の最もよい場所 Fascinated, Caldera reached into his jacket for the pocket Japanese book he’d bought earlier that day. It took him nearly ten minutes to fully work out the translation, as he was completely unfamiliar with the language. But when he did, he laughed at the simplicity. "The Best Place in Town" Could it really be that easy? Caldera shook his head with disbelief as he carefully crossed the wet street. A thick fog was formed at the base of the building, perhaps artificially created by the proprietors to veil the entrance. The windows were dark and tinted, and a massive Japanese man stood at the door. He observed Caldera with doubt as he approached, looking slightly out of place. "Aikotoba?" the large man asked sternly. Caldera raised an eyebrow, his hand moving back into his jacket. The moment he produced the translator and began thumbing through it, the guard let out an audible sigh and rolled his eyes. "Password, sir." Caldera smiled a dumb tourist smile and spoke clearly: "Kurokame." The guard seemed a bit surprised, and made no motion to clear the way for Caldera. Caldera adjusted his tie and looked up at the man with confusion, unsure of how to proceed. The Japanese man lowered his mouth to his lapel, where Caldera spotted a tiny microphone hidden amongst the fabric. The guard whispered something incomprehensible and paused, before finally looking down at Caldera again, this time looking totally bewildered. "Mr. Caldera, welcome. I am to bid you to a special meeting. Please follow me." With that, the guard turned and pushed through the door, holding it open for Caldera to follow. Caldera took a few steps towards the door and hesitated, feeling as if he were about to step into another world. The guard let Caldera pass, but Caldera found his way barred by a second heavy door. The guard closed the first door quietly and turned the dead bolt, which clicked shut with an unnerving sense of finality. The guard then opened the second door, and Caldera was accosted with a wave of heat and thumping techno music. Caldera was led into the main room, which was sprawling and frenetic with intensity. A massive stage, lit with a gloomy pink light, housed several gyrating young women. White girls, Japanese girls, black girls, there seemed to be something for everyone. A swarm of Japanese men, all clad in fine suits and waving cash at the stage, congregated at the stage and laughed raucous laughs to each other. Beyond the mob in a shady corner of the room were a number of booths, all full of Japanese businessmen, alternately enjoying a fine dinner or an equally fine lap dance. But the well-tailored exterior of these men belied the primal ferocity with which they assaulted the women on stage; forcing cash into their g-strings, reaching and groping as if they’d purchased a new toy. Worse yet, them women on stage seemed content with this, resigned to their fate, aware of their place and purpose and providing their service without question. Somewhere deep inside, Caldera felt himself aroused by the absolute domination; but he was quickly overtaken by a sweeping wave of shame. Caldera was led up a golden set of stairs to the next floor, where they pushed through the doors and stepped into another world altogether. The light here was dim, the air cool and calm. But upon the air Caldera detected the unmistakable scent of smoke, and it certainly was not tobacco. Caldera felt his throat tighten as he kept his eyes forward; but to his sides he could see men smoking and sniffing all sorts of drugs; and, of course, there were more half-naked women. Caldera was led to the back of this room and another flight of stairs, this one smaller and less obtrusive. The guard stopped here and pointed upward, sending Caldera on alone. For some reason, Caldera was hesitant to part company. Caldera tentatively climbed the stairs and reached a small landing. Another guard was waiting for him here, greeting him with a menacing scowl. "Turn around, hands on the wall," he stated grimly. Caldera complied, and the guard proceeded to frisk him with great care and detail. Satisfied, the guard turned and opened a door inward, ushering Caldera in. Rising to his full height of six feet four inches, Caldera nonetheless felt very small as he stepped into the room. The door snapped shut behind him, and Caldera found himself in another dim room. On either side of the windowless compartment stood a pair of tuxedoed guards, neither of whom turned to look at Caldera as he stood in the doorway. The room was illuminated by a pair of lonely candles, flickering upon a small desk. A shadowy figure sat behind the desk, face hidden in the darkness, smoking a long wooden pipe. A thick plume of aromatic smoke rose from behind the desk, and a grizzled voice followed it. "Come in, Mr. Caldera," it spoke, followed by a hard hacking cough. Caldera stepped forward and spotted a small chair in the center of the room. "Sit down," the voice compelled him, brash but courteous. Caldera seated himself, adjusting his jacket, doing his best to look like he belonged there. "Your reputation precedes you, Mr. Caldera," the voice started in again. After another long draw from the pipe, a puff of smoke was followed by another wheezing whisper. "I must admit, however, that I am rather surprised to see you here. If you have come for the entertainment, I am quite certain we can arrange a private room for you." Caldera let out an uneasy chuff, shaking his head emphatically. "Thank you, sir, but that’s not exactly what I’m here for. I’ve come seeking information." The shadowy man took another slow drag from his pipe, quietly observing Caldera from the darkness. "You disapprove of what we do here? Mr. Caldera, I believe you will find information to be a much more dangerous commodity than anything you will encounter downstairs." "Might I ask who you are, sir, and why you’ve asked to speak with me?" Caldera inquired, fully aware that he was pushing the boundaries of disrespect. "I am not seeking an autograph, that much I can assure you," the voice replied. Finally a face pushed through the smoke and shadow and into the candlelight; it was old and worn, with thin grey hair and a thin grey beard. Despite the age of the face, the eyes possessed an ancient wisdom, cold, dark and penetrating in the darkness. "I am called many names on these streets, for my real name is a closely guarded secret. But almost certainly know me as Shindo." Caldera gulped. Shindo. This wasn’t just some hot-shot casino owner of club proprietor. This was the king of kings, the head of the Yakuza and the owner of the entire Entertainment District. Kimbusa’s former employer and master. Suddenly, Caldera got the feeling that this meeting was no coincidence. Shindo coughed again, finally suffocating his spasms with another long draw from his wooden pipe. "The information you seek, it regards Kimbusa, does it not?" Shindo asked with a knowing smile. "Well, it would appear as though you and I have something in common then, Mr. Caldera. You see, I myself am quite curious about our sumo friend. Quite curious indeed." Shindo leveled his gaze directly at Caldera, examining him closely. His eyes seemed to penetrate his defenses and bore directly into Caldera’s deepest thoughts, and suddenly Caldera was overwhelmed by the distinct feeling that Shindo was reading his mind. But as he began to speak again, he suddenly seemed to shift direction. "Mr. Caldera, have you ever heard the full prophecy of the Samurai warrior Kimbusa?" Caldera shook his head, which he found to be growing light from the aroma of Shindo’s smoke. "I… don’t much believe in such things." Shindo nodded. "Kimbusa was a Samurai, a guardian of the ancient emperor. The greatest of all samurai warriors, it is said. The greatest loremasters recall that the emperor was rarely without the mighty Kimbusa by his side. His power was unchallenged thanks in no small part to the terrible fear his subjects had of Kimbusa. The legends tell of great battles of conquest, where the mighty Kimbusa would unsheathe his sword and dismember an entire opposing army, bathing in their blood with a lust unmatched by any mortal man. Soon it came to pass that no army would stand in opposition to his greatness, and would throw down their swords and beg for mercy at the mere sight of his form. "But the emperor betrayed Kimbusa. His soldiers came in the night and slaughtered Kimbusa’s family. When Kimbusa came to the palace for his bloody revenge, the emperor sprang his trap, unleashing the full fury of his warriors upon Kimbusa. Normally, they would have posed little resistance to his fury, but the grief, Mr. Caldera… the grief Kimbusa felt from the loss of his loved ones, the few in the world he truly cared for, was so great that he fell beneath his own master’s sword. It is said that Kimbusa was truly invincible, Mr. Caldera, unless you struck his heart." Caldera sat back, soaking this in. Shindo pressed on. "But, Mr. Caldera, as he lay at his master’s feet, his dying breath spoke of his promise to return, and to set things right again. Of course, this is but a fool’s legend. But what if the legend were true? What if this Kimbusa has returned to wage his bloody war once more, and to get revenge upon the one who betrayed him?" Shindo’s eyes suddenly flared up with a threatening, accusing glimmer, as if he were looking upon Caldera with fear and pity simultaneously. Caldera’s eyes dropped to the floor in deep contemplation… Or maybe it was just the smoke. Either way, Caldera felt his head swimming, frantically trying to grasp the grandeur of Kimbusa’s plans. Then a sudden realization dawned on him. "If that’s what Kimbusa is doing… Who is he out for revenge on? You… or me?" Shindo choked on his laughter, coughing fitfully even as he took another draw on his pipe. "That, Mr. Caldera, is where my curiosity lies. For it seems both of us are men of great power, with great empires, each having struck at Kimbusa’s heart. This man, if he is a man, has treacherous plans for both of us, in my estimation, Mr. Caldera. However, if we combine our considerable resources, I believe we can find Kimbusa, and strike him down once more before his fate becomes our own." The wizened, unblinking eyes across the room now seemed decidedly cruel, dark and empty with the lifelessness of an uncompromisingly ruthless entity. Shindo displayed no remorse for his predicament, nor any hesitation in revealing his own plans. A cold shiver ran down Caldera’s spine at the prospect of conspiring with such a man, but his survival instinct took hold of him and refused him an exit. "He hasn’t won the tournament yet," is all he could manage from under the spell of Shindo’s stare. "Ah yes, this American wrestling tournament," Shindo wheezed. "That, Mr. Caldera, is where I hope you might be able to share some wisdom on our current plight. What means does this tournament serve Kimbusa, and to what end? Your trite and meaningless profession should be below a man of such great power, and yet he returns once more. Why is that, Mr. Caldera?" Caldera suddenly felt a flare of anger rise over him, his blood boiling at the insult. "And what of it, Shindo-san? What does it matter to you? Are you afraid your end will come at the hands of not a samurai warrior but a professional wrestler? Well, hear this; what Kimbusa is doing now he is doing for all the world to see, in the greatest wrestling tournament ever held. If he wins that tournament, he’ll have the entire world’s attention focused squarely upon him, which is far more attention than he could garner hidden in the darkness like you." Shindo’s face grew twisted with rage as he struggled to stand from his seat. But his coughing overtook him once more, and he collapsed back down into his chair. Caldera shook his head and scoffed, standing and heading for the door. "Caldera!" Shindo cried after him, halting him as he reached for the knob. "You can leave, but you will never know the truth! The truth about what he is! I have the answers you seek, and there is no other from whom you can learn them!" With a wry grin, Caldera glanced back over his shoulder. "I’ll learn them from the man himself." And with that, he was gone. Moments later, Caldera was back on the street. He immediately ripped his jacket off and tore his tie from his neck, breathing heavily as he stormed down the street. Even in the darkness, idle passersby dove from his path, watching with alarm and amazement as this furious American stomped along the sidewalk. Caldera hadn’t felt so infuriated before in his life; somehow, Shindo’s assault on his livelihood and even Kimbusa himself had given rise to a overwhelming anger within him. He called his car and was whisked back to the airport, while he tried to reconcile precisely what had just happened. But he knew instantly that he was prepared to aid Kimbusa in his plan, to accept his judgment. Regardless of what Kimbusa might have planned for him, he found himself hoping that Kimbusa would win the damn tournament. --------------------------- Steven Caldera sits back in his chair, eyeing a silent Kimbusa from afar. Kimbusa’s eyes had closed and his breathing slowed to a near stop, but Caldera knew Kimbusa was listening intently. Finally, after a few moments of silence, Kimbusa opens his eyes, eyeing his former employer curiously. "Kimbusa, I have to know. I’ve listened to your stories, I’ve gone to great lengths to learn all the details. Put them together for me. What are you doing?" Kimbusa stands suddenly, swiftly moving towards the door. Caldera begins to shout in protest, but Kimbusa abruptly changes course and heads for the desk. Drawing the curtains closed again, Kimbusa plunges the room back to darkness. He then crosses back to the couch and seats himself again, his eyes lowering to the floor. Finally he speaks; quietly, softly. "I am glad you saw what you saw; that you saw the places I was shaped in, the places that tried to corrupt me so completely. But Shindo… he has me figured wrong. He only knows half the story, Mr. Caldera, but you know the whole thing. He doesn’t know what you know, he doesn’t know what has happened to me in this country. He doesn’t know what wrestling has done for me." Kimbusa trails off, a small smile crossing his face. Caldera is surprised by this. "Wrestling?" Kimbusa looks up, chuckling to himself. "It’s quite simple really. What, you didn’t believe all that samurai garbage? Mr. Caldera, in short, I’m in this wrestling tournament… to wrestle." Caldera’s face washes over with astonishment as Kimbusa continues. "Shindo is blind. He thinks everything revolves around him. And don’t think for a moment that I’m letting you get off easy, either, Mr. Caldera. You’ve been equally blind. Both of you, everyone, you think I’m here for some grandiose plot for revenge? Well, I guess on some level that’s true." Kimbusa’s dark face seems to soften a bit, almost transforming into something approaching human. "But I’m not here for any reason other than escape. When I retired, I found a nice little village to settle down in. It was nestled between rolling green hills in a shady valley, quiet and peaceful and serene. But it did not take long for my past to find me there. Those simple people never had the first clue that they had allowed a monster to enter their village; but I knew. I knew that I could never rest in peace until I came out and faced my demons once and for all. "There have been three stages; I needed to face Shindo and those streets once more. I needed to find you and help you understand what I was doing. And I had to win this tournament. You see, Mr. Caldera, two years ago when you helped me through the third tournament, I was winning by strengthening my anger. Now, I win by releasing it. I have progressed through this tournament quietly, but each match I’ve come a little closer to deliverance. In that third tournament, my anger carried me only so far; I finally met a man with a greater capacity for anger than me, and he defeated me. But now, Mr. Caldera…" Kimbusa draws in a deep breath, relishing the free air with particular fondness. "Now, each victory draws me closer to my release. This tournament, this return to the ring, it has given me the chance to tell my story, to confess my sins and to release my hate. I am a haunted man, Mr. Caldera. Haunted by my failures, haunted by my own treachery, haunted by the evil I have spread. By stepping away from that terrible place, that terrible time in my life, by engaging in the pure competition of this tournament, I believe I will find peace." "But you confronted Shindo… surely you don’t intend to just let him off the hook without more than that?" Caldera wonders aloud. Kimbusa shakes his head. "You heard it from his own mouth. He despises wrestling, though not for the sport itself. He despises it because it has provided me a livelihood, an outlet far less dangerous than his own. Those streets, Mr. Caldera, they very nearly consumed me. But by winning this tournament, by achieving the fame and the glory that accompanies such a feat, I will have risen to the top of a new empire, and I will have forged a new legacy. I will always be remembered at the winner of GTT Five, not as the fearsome murderer who lurked the streets of Tokyo. More importantly, Mr. Caldera, I will remember myself as such. Then, perhaps, I may have my peace." Together, Kimbusa and Caldera sit in the sunlight for a few quiet moments, each milling about their own thoughts. Caldera finally exhales the deep breath he’d been holding in since he first saw Kimbusa, finally convinced that Kimbusa means him no harm. The normally brooding, opaque Kimbusa had grown clear and contemplative, passively meditating in the quiet of the office. For his part, Caldera studies the 500-pound man seated on his sofa, his mind still struggling to grasp the depth of Kimbusa’s story. Finally, after a long silence, Caldera speaks again. "Why me?" he asks timidly. "Why did you choose me to tell this story to? Why are you confessing to me, after all I did to you?" The words appear to have eluded Kimbusa, but he finally looks up. "If you cannot be saved, Mr. Caldera, what hope is there, then, for me? Your sins are small compared to mine. Perhaps together we can purify ourselves and leave this terrible past buried forever." Caldera nods softly, humbled by Kimbusa’s sentimental philanthropy. But Kimbusa continued, "Soon I will leave you in peace, Mr. Caldera. But if you will hear me, I have but one sin left to confess. The greatest of them all; the very moment I abandoned all hope and plunged into the darkness. One that I have never spoken of before, not even to myself." Caldera nods, leaning in. He wouldn’t miss this for the world.
