Taking a Stand

4 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-04 What do you do when life gives you lemons? According to the old adage, you make lemonade. It’s all about taking the negatives and turning them into positives. Different people react in different ways. For some people, turning the bad situations around is no problem at all. They can adapt easily, are quick on their feet, and are eternally optimistic. For others, it’s not quite as easy. It’s a constant struggle to stay above the pessimism of life, and if you succumb to it for too long, the way out may well not be worth it. Taking lemons and turning them into lemonade. It sounds so simple, and when you think about it, it is. What do you do when life gives you lemons? The Latch Key Kid takes those lemons and turns them into lemonade. What do you do when you make lemonade? The Latch Key Kid, of course, opens… --------------------- “A lemonade stand?” Katherine stood in front of him, yelling. “Are you out of your mind?” “Hardly,” the Latch Key Kid replied, motioning for Katherine to keep her voice down. It was still early in the day, as evidenced by the dim light in the sky. “It’s the perfect opportunity. Don’t you get it?” “No, I don’t get it. I’m still not sure what we’re doing here, or why you asked me to meet you here.” Latch fumbled around for a few wooden boards, passed them to Katherine, and then picked up a hammer and a few nails. Katherine held the boards together, and Latch secured them by pounding the nails into them. “See? It’s that easy. There’s really nothing to it. A few more of these, and we’ll be there in no time.” “Okay. So you’re building a lemonade stand. But why are you doing it before the sun is even all the way up?” “Because,” Latch pouted, “I want to get it up before Frank gets in.” “Frank? What’s Frank got to do with any of this?” “Uhm, Katherine? Take a look around. I know it’s still a little dark out, but this should look familiar.” “Oh, Brad,” Katherine’s face gave a shocked expression as she began to look around at the area, “tell me you didn’t. Please tell me you’re not…you are, aren’t you?” “Yep,” Latch smiled, “I sure as heck am. I’m setting up my lemonade stand on the corner, right near Frank’s.” Sure enough, they were standing on the corner outside Frank’s Coffeehouse. The Latch Key Kid and Katherine began to assemble a makeshift lemonade stand, as the sun slowly rose, replacing the cold, gray light of dawn with a soft orange hue. --------------------- “Well, you did it. You actually did it. I hope you’re happy,” Katherine chided him. “Of course I’m happy. Look at it. It’s beautiful. It’s everything I always wanted in a lemonade stand,” Latch beamed. “Brad, you’re not a kid anymore. You can’t go around setting up lemonade stands to make a nickel here, a dime there, however much you’re planning to sell your lemonade for. It’s just…it’s asinine. And to do it in front of Frank’s, well, don’t you think he’ll be a little miffed when he shows up?” “It’s Frank, Katherine. He doesn’t get miffed. Besides, I know I’m not a kid anymore. That’s why I built this. I finally had the time, energy, supplies and ability to build a real lemonade stand. Thank you for helping me. You’ve done a great thing.” “I still don’t understand why you decided to build it here, right in front of Frank’s. Isn’t that sort of, I don’t know, competing against him, even if it is just lemonade?” “Well, you know what they say, Katherine. If you can’t join ‘em, beat ‘em.” “That’s not what they say, you’ve got it backwards,” Katherine protested. “Maybe so,” Latch countered, “but the fact is, I couldn’t join Frank’s staff, so now I aim to beat them. Or at least, compete with them. And what better way to do that than with lemonade?” “I don’t know. Coffee, perhaps? If you really want to compete with Frank’s, that’s what you’d do.” Latch scowled. “Oh, come on. There’s no way I’d ever be able to compete with Frank in coffee. That stuff he brews is the best I’ve ever had. No, he’s got the market on coffee cornered on this, uhm, corner.” “So you’re just going to stand out here on the corner and sell lemonade? Is that your plan?” “As it stands now, yep, that’s the plan. There’s really nothing to worry about. I’ve got it all under control.” “I see,” Katherine said, putting her hands on her hips. “And just how are people to know that you’re selling lemonade here?” “From the sign, of course.” “And what sign would that be?” “The sign hanging on the stand, silly,” Latch grinned. “Yeah, about that sign,” Katherine said sarcastically, “it’s not there. See?” “Oh…crap. I forgot the sign. What am I gonna do?” “I don’t know,” Katherine shrugged. “Take the stand down?” “No, no, no. I can’t do that. That will be like giving up. There’s no way I’m going to give up on this thing now, not before I’ve even opened it. Let me…just…I’ll be right back.” Latch ran to his car in the parking lot, opened the trunk and rummaged around inside for a minute. He came running back with a white poster board and a black magic marker. In big, block letters, he began to write “lemonade” on the sign. He ran out of room at the end, however, and was forced to write a small “e” that looked silly next to the larger letters. He shrugged, picked up the hammer and a nail, and hammered the sign to the crossbeam at the top of the stand. He stood back, smiled, and crossed his arms over his chest. “There,” he said proudly. “Congratulations,” Katherine clapped mockingly, “you’ve really outdone yourself this time. Now, tell me, how are people going to know how much you’re selling the lemonade for?” “I…well, shoot. Hold on.” Latch uncapped the marker and wrote “10 Cents” under “lemonade” on the sign. He stood back and smiled again. “There.” “A dime? Are you crazy? You’ll never make any money selling a cup of lemonade for a dime.” “Hmm. Maybe you’re right.” Latch uncapped the marker again, put an X over the “10” and wrote “25” next to it. “There.” “A quarter, huh? Don’t you think that’s a bit much? After all, you just raised your price by 150 percent.” “Well, shoot. What do you want me to do? Give it away?” “No,” replied Katherine, “I want you to think reasonably about this. It’s not so easy running your own business, is it? Now you know what Frank goes through every day. It may look like he’s doing fine, but I bet if you asked him, or at least saw what goes on in the manager’s office, you might have a different opinion.” “Well, sure, but that’s a coffeeshop. This is just a lemonade stand,” Latch held firm. “A lemonade stand is still a business. And a business without a business plan is doomed to fail. I’m not trying to destroy your hopes and dreams, Brad, because I can see you’ve staked them on this, but it’s going to be more work than you think.” “There’s no need to be snide, Katherine. You obviously don’t think I can pull this off, so now I’m more determined than I was before to do just that. It’s like wrestling in the GTT4. I wasn’t expected to get as far as I have, especially after I lost in the first round. But look at me now. I’ve persevered against the odds. I took the lemons and turned them into lemonade. And now I’m in the finals of my bracket, against Karina Wolfenden. And once again, I’m the underdog. Most people doubt I can do it, including Mr. Webster. But I intend to prove them wrong. And if I’m unsuccessful in that, at least I’ll know I gave it my all.” “So that’s what this is about, huh? You’re not trying to compete with Frank; you’re just trying to compete, period. What you don’t get, Brad, is that you can compete. As long as you stick to what you know, which is wrestling, and don’t try something you don’t know, like running a business. You’re good enough to beat Karina, Brad. You just have to believe inside that you are.” “I do believe that, Katherine, or else I would’ve dropped out of the tournament. There’s no reason to wrestle if you don’t think you can win. But that’s exactly why I decided to build this lemonade stand. I believe I can be successful with it. Sure, it’s probably going to be a struggle, and it won’t run perfectly, but at least I’ll have the satisfaction of knowing that I ran a business. And besides, struggles only make us stronger. This is just another way of preparing myself for my next match.” “So running a lemonade stand is supposed to prepare you for wrestling Karina? How? You’ve never run a lemonade stand before.” “Well,” Latch smiled, “I’ve also never wrestled Karina Wolfenden before.” Katherine paused. “You…do you have a point there, I’ll grant you that. But a lemonade stand? Couldn’t you have come up with something better?” Latch shrugged. “Life gave me lemons, Katherine. What was I supposed to do?” “Well, when you put it that way, I guess there’s nothing I can say. Your mind is obviously made up. All I can do is stand here and support you.” They were so involved in their conversation that they didn’t even notice Frank walking up to them. He stood there for a few moments, staring at the lemonade stand, and then finally cleared his throat. Latch spun around, and took a step back when he saw Frank. “Hey, good morning, Frank!” Latch said with a smile. “Is it, though, Brad? Is it really a good morning?” “Well, Katherine and I were just discussing that, and our conclusion is that yes, it is a very good morning.” “Well good, Brad. I’m glad you’re enjoying your morning. Now would you mind telling me what the hell this is?” “It’s a lemonade stand, Frank.” “Of course it is. And who put it there?” “I did,” Latch said, still smiling. “Well, Katherine helped, so I suppose I should give her some credit, too.” Frank looked at Katherine, who smiled sheepishly and shrugged her shoulders. “I see. And just why did you decide to build a lemonade stand here, Brad…and Katherine?” “To sell lemonade, of course. Why else would someone build a lemonade stand?” Latch asked quizzically. Frank frowned. “Okay, look. I’ve just about had all I can take of your games, Brad. This is no place for a lemonade stand. I’m going to walk inside, get to work, and pretend this never happened. And next time I walk outside, I expect to see no sign that it never did happen. Catch my drift?” “So, what you’re saying is…you’re upset?” “Yes, that’s what I’m saying, Brad,” Frank replied, slowly and calmly, “that’s exactly what I’m saying. But more than that, this really is no place for a lemonade stand. You shouldn’t be out here like this. This isn’t a game, Brad, and you shouldn’t treat it like one. That’s all I’m going to say.” And with that, Frank turned and walked up to the entrance of the coffeeshop. He pulled a key ring out of his pocket, selected a key, and unlocked the front door. He walked inside, and flipped on the lights as the door slowly closed behind him. Latch turned to look at Katherine. “Gee, talk about needing your morning coffee. I’ve never seen him like that.” “You’ve never seen him before his morning coffee?” Katherine asked. “No. I’m usually not up this early, let alone at the coffeeshop this early. Poor guy. His day’s off to a bad start already.” Before Katherine could respond, a Volkswagen Jetta pulled up to the curb, and three burly men got out, and the car sped away. They walked slowly toward the Latch Key Kid and Katherine, patting the pockets of their overcoats. They walked up to the stand, looked it over, and then began whispering to each other, stifling laughter. One of them turned to Latch and spoke. “A lemonade stand, huh? Very creative. Very creative, indeed. I’ll have to compliment Frank on this one. Yet one more way for him to-” Latch interrupted. “Actually, Frank had nothing to do with this. It was all me. My idea, my business, my profit.” The three men whispered to each other again, and the leader spoke again. “You mean to tell me Frank don’t know nothing about this?” Latch shook his head. “Well, I’ll be damned. You know, kid, this really is no place for a lemonade stand.” “I know,” Latch replied, nearly insolently. “Frank told me that exact same thing five minutes ago.” The three men whispered to each other again, and again the leader spoke. “Frank’s here? You mean he beat us?” “Yeah, he just walked inside a few minutes ago. I’d give him a few more minutes before you go in, though. He’s apparently not the cheeriest person this early in the morning, and it’ll be a few minutes before he gets to brewing that first pot of coffee. Best to wait out here until he’s got some coffee brewed for you. In the meantime, though, I’ve got some lemonade if you’re interested.” “Oh, that’s okay, kid. We’re not here to drink coffee. Or lemonade.” “Oh? Then what are you here for?” “We’re here to see Frank,” the big man said. “I know. You already said that. But I’d give him a few-” “I know,” the big man interrupted, “you already said that. We don’t care about that, though.” He gestured to his friends. “Let’s go, boys.” And the three men headed for the door to Frank’s. The big man stopped, and turned around as the other two kept on walking. He walked slowly back to Latch and Katherine, and then stopped. “I’m telling you, kid,” he whispered, “this is no place for a lemonade stand.” Then he turned and walked back to join his friends. The three men opened the door to the coffeeshop, and walked inside. Latch turned to Katherine with a worried expression on his face. “What do you think that was all about?” “I don’t know,” Katherine replied, “but I gather this is no place for a lemonade stand. If you won’t listen to me or Frank, will you at least listen to those three?” “No way,” Latch shook his head. “Wait here. I’m gonna go see what’s going on.” Latch walked slowly up to the front door, and cupped his hands around his eyes as he leaned into the glass. He stared intently through the glass for a few moments, then turned around, and slowly walked back to Katherine and the lemonade stand. Katherine was busy setting up cups and a small cash box on the counter of the stand. She looked up to see Latch standing there, with a nervous look on his face. “Brad. What is it? What’s wrong?” “I think Frank’s day is about to get even worse, Katherine. Those three men who stopped by here and went in?” Katherine nodded. “Remember what I was saying earlier about being a fly on the wall in the manager’s office?” Again, Katherine nodded. “Well, I wish I were in there now,” Latch said, his voice shaking, “because those three men just led Frank into the office.”
