Date: 2009-11-08 “You... are Christopher Sheffield. Son of a bitch, coulda sworn you died.” Alias said, feeling as if he where about to throw-up. It showed on his face. He felt it deep down in his bandaged gut, and that feeling was coming up fast. “That is the difference between you and I. ‘Could have’ and ‘certain of’. You are professional wrestling and I am the real world.” A knowing smile had edged across the face of this brown-haired man, who looked so familiar, specifically because he was Christopher Sheffield. Son of Brian Sheffield. His in-ring alias had been ‘The Tin Angel’ Chris Phoenix in 1993. That was then. Now, calling him a cold-blooded killer would be forgetting the joy he took out of it all. He gripped the handle of the baseball bat tightly in his right hand, as he dropped its sweet spot into his left with a light tap. “See, I am certain that you are dead, Jackson Chase.” The Pulp Hero shook his head, and coughed, a decade of anxiety rushing to his haggard and permanently beaten mug. He hadn’t been called Jackson Chase since Brian Sheffield patted him on the back and told him to say good-bye to that life, because a second chance awaited him. He barely remembered his old Tennessee home. His mother and father, all those that he once loved in his old life, all now long dead. If one could be out of practice, when it came to hearing the name that God gave ‘em, then Alias was. “Sure I just ain’t hungover and this is just the mother of all fever dreams?” The shock of the moment had pushed Alias into a sardonic state of mind. That, and he did indeed need a drink. “You would be so lucky.” “Says the man waiting to brain me with a baseball bat.” Alias replied, as he eyed Christopher. The years hadn’t been easy on this man either, though externally, they seemed to be easier on him then they had been on the Pulp Hero. “I mean, if I am dead as you say…” Alias added, a blunt gruff tone to his voice. “Yes.” Christopher nodded, his eyes wondering. “Why aren’t I dead?” The tension of the moment was palpable. Alias waited impatiently for the answer, like only the Pulp Hero could. He was treating these moments like a bare-knuckle boxing match. Punch. Counterpunch. If a man wants to throw a punch, but knows he’ll get hit in return, he won’t throw that punch to begin with. See, most people don’t like getting hit. Sane people. “You mean more to me and mine then just witty banter. You know that, Jack.” Chris said, once again tapping the sweet spot of the Louisville slugger against his palm. “Okay then, I’ll bite.” Alias punched. A curious look subconsciously crept across his face. “Careful not to bite off more then you can chew.” Christopher counterpunched. As Chris had expected, Alias was almost delirious in shock, and in pain. He wouldn’t be co-operative, as long as he bantered. With a deft and solid swing, Sheffield cracked the Pulp Hero across the head with the baseball bat. Before Alias slipped into unconsciousness he threw-up, the mixture of bile and blood taking a chipped off piece of his upper right molar with it to the floor. As far as counterpunches went, it got the job done. RKO presents A MAN WHO THINKS WITH HIS FISTS AND FIGHTS WITH HIS MIND! THE ALIAS in… SEEDS OF SEVEN GOLDEN SUNS! Ahead, the air still crackled and glowed gold where the suns had sailed out one after another, into the shimmering tides beyond space and time. A: Yeah, yeah. What I was saying though, is that doing good in a bad world, and as much as some might want to think differently, we live in a bad world… it’s a hell of a dangerous thing. It shouldn’t be a dangerous choice, to wear that white hat, ya know? For Rhine, at his age, to have a head like that on his shoulders, it gives me hope for tomorrow. RD: Tomorrow? What about today? A: Well today is mine. RD: You share some similarities with Rhine. A: Is this your subtle way of bringin’ up the one eye? (pause) He’s who I wish I could have been ten years ago. RD: Strong words. A: Sure, but he’s still ten years off, to me. He’s history. RD: Stronger words. A: Yeah, I realize that. It's purely trash talk, though, as I realize what the man has been through. I obviously know what losing half your sight does to a man’s career. What that does to him physically and mentally. (Alias leans forward) The kind of desperate individual who would sink to that level of taking your senses away from you, to do a thing like that to a good person… Junior stared at the blasted faces underfoot, seared into the compressed and burned remains of their town, and balled his fists. He had only recently saved Sin City from invasion, in his role as the number one man in world, as it where. And now this. RD: That actually brings up a good point, which is my closing question. With all of the danger that goes with it… is the “good guy” an endangered species? A: Never. There will always be villains, hell look at the winners list of GTT and tell me if you see a pattern. There will always be anti-heroes, they’re a dime a dozen, the men and women who want to fight the good fight but believe that the rules don’t apply to them… and maybe today I’m that kind of person. I strive to be a good honest man, though. I owe it myself. There will always be good guys, there will always be heroes. Decent people, in an ugly fuckin’ world. Rarin’ and ready to save that world. From… More bodies, always more bodies. A: From each other. The Alias growled what they were all thinking, quietly to himself. “I keep goin’ to a lot of places, and ending up somewhere I’ve already been.”
