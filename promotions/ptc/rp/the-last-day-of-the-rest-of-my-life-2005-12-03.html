The Last Day Of The Rest Of My Life

3 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-03 I "I am a relic. I am just a petrified cry." - Manic Street Preachers. I say, Things were different in my day. In reply, I get a distant Yeah, delivered with such apathy that it's reduced to Ynh by the nasal, scoffing exhalation. I should be a little insulted, since it turns out I was the guy who recommended we sign him, but these days I don't really care enough to invoke such reactions, so almost choke on a throaty chuckle. I say, This used to be a craft, an art-form. This doesn't even warrant a word in return, and instead receives a dismissive Mhhm. Blahblahblahblahblahblah is probably all he's hearing. I tend to have this effect on people. Lock me in a room by myself for a few hours, and you'll find me hanging by my laces, the conversation would be that bad. But, hey, at least I'd get to give the whole asphyxiation wanking thing a try, just to make the report on my death a little more eventful than the life that proceeded it. I say, Earth control to Major Tom? He must be feeling like some weirdo who won't shut up has sat down next to him on the train, or at the bus station. The kind that won't shut up, but looks one wrong word away from spitting hepatitis-tainted saliva into your mouth, or slashing an artery in your leg, so you drop onto the back-foot with faux-polite, nervous noises of attention. While I'd like to think I lack the psychopathic, junkie hobo look, it doesn't stop the little fuck treating me as such. I say, I fucked your sister. His head turns back to me. A response. I'm honoured. Eyebrows gravitate towards the bridge of his nose, and eyes narrow in confusion. Huh? That's right, I can't even get him to listen when I'm questioning the honour of his sibling. Maybe I should have tried Your Dad's a fag! or Listen up, Cuntrag, your the result of the Milkman coming early… pun intended. I'm that far out of sync with the world around me, that I need to resort to the offensively drastic just to evoke an ordinary reaction. I say, Nothing. A half-second lapses before I add, You fucking fag cunt. Oh he remarks, unremarkably, as if I'd just left it at Nothing. I actually tend not to swear a lot, outside my head that is. But if a tree falls deep in the deserted forest, does it make a sound? If nobody listens to me, can I question who put what where in their Grandmother? There's a new stumper for you all. He says, Bye. Only he doesn't, but I at least like to think he'd gift me that much before just standing up and walking away. As far as wrestling goes, this here is the frontier. And I mean that in everything but the progressive sense. This is as far as it gets from the 'civilised' hub of wrestling, where promoters make their own rules, and have 100 excuses for when they break every one of them. If you want your bank, you'd better follow up on the threats you make when the fist recoils, and if you travel more than 50 miles without getting anything up front… your naive stupidity should deny you any right to complain. Every now and again, a young one makes the break for greener pastures, then lands hard on his ass when they toss him back to our tatty canvas and wonky ring-posts. As for me, well, I'm not really in the position to question anybody else. I say, I'm happy here. Funnily enough, I say it around the same time as I tell a bare-faced, shameless lie of convenience. I know exactly what I am. I'm the old guy that some sadistic, acne-blighted manager at a fast-food joint decides to give a job to… half out of policy, half for the sick, degrading amusement of all. Maybe they're all thinking my geriatric body will give out on me in the ring, that I'll drop my bowels in my trunks, or piss will start to trickle down my leg. I'm only 43, but in wrestler-years, I'm a certified centurion. And if you read that in any way Roman, you haven't listened to a word I've said. Lucky prick. None of them think for a second that I'll be the one to leave here, and scramble up to the next level, trek through the wilderness of this business to reach the promised land. I say, Bye, everyone. I don't even get silence, instead, just no change in ambient volume whatsoever. Not one paused conversation, not one halted phone-call. I say, I'm not coming back, so go fuck each other and die. If anything, they engross themselves further in whatever they're doing, that way they won't even have to acknowledge me. Not even the booker twigs that my match is meant to be next. II "You woke up this morning, all the love has gone." - Alabama 3. The tea-stain is uneven where the slick surface of the printer paper tried to reject it. Edges are curled and crisp, singed over the stove, and with brittle crystals of ash forming its frame. It's the sight that greets me every morning as I stagger around in a zombie-like haze, rubbing sleep from eyes, wincing at the sour, lingering liquor taste. I'm far from the most educated guy on the planet. I could spout every tired cliché about graduating from the University of Life until I’m even bluer in the face, but that imaginary diploma doesn't help you when your kid shoves his homework under your nose. Don't ask me what alliteration is, don't ask me what a verb is, and don't ask me how to conjugate one. I can't differentiate, and I can't tell you what equals the square of the hypo… whatever. But there was one piece I could help him with. And I've never felt prouder as a father. I haven’t seen him in over 2 years, and judging by the last words he said to me, he doesn't think that's long enough. My face tries to turn inside out as the bitter coffee hit floods over my tongue, a few un-dissolved granules almost sending my mouth into a disgusted spasm. Before the chemicals within get anywhere near my blood stream, the placebo effect kicks in, and the world around be becomes a little less cloudy… but the lingering dust illuminated by the fading kitchen light barely surrounds me with clarity. I wedge the mug in-between the mountain of plates and dishes that rise out of the sink, and rummage around in the drawers for my cigarettes. One carton I find has been bent by the over-filled contents, but I manage to rescue one, but then draw a blank when patting my pockets for a lighter. My agitated sigh steams like a Bull's where the heating is half-way to giving up all together. I kinda know how it feels. Turning on the stove's gas, I slap my weary palm down on the ignition button, and burst forth the blue halo of heat. Stooping down with Lucky Strike pursed between dry, cracked lips, I light it from the same hob that my boy used to age his poster. I seethe, Fuck. Eloquent, aren't I. Brushing my eyebrow with probing fingers, I feel a few hairs crumble, sending a fine shower of ash down past my vision. The smoke engulfs my lungs, letting me at least feel something inside, before sending a fine plume of ash grey across the kitchen, where it breaks formation with a swirl, washing across the fridge door. As it dissipates, I'm left looking at the faux-aged poster, styled after the famous boxing promotion posters of the mid-20th. "The Killer" vs. Jack Archer I probably don't want to think about who he'd pick to win that one 10 years down the line. It was his submission for a project entitled: What my Dad does. Above my name is a sketch of how I wished I looked a decade ago. Super-hero muscle tone, valiant pose, and a smile across my lips. I was once his hero, rather than an embarrassment, a painful memory, an unwanted DNA donor. Still, I haven't moved his distant show of affection, despite giving up hope that I could ever reclaim that privilege. It wasn't always like this. I wasn't always like this. Laughter used to fill the house, warming it through, brick by brick. There was love here, and not just for obligation's sake. I walked without a defeatist's apathetic slouch back then, and saw tomorrow as something more than a chance not to have such a shitty day. Which, until today, was the one certainty in my current life. III "One of these mornings. Won't be very long. You will look for me… and I'll be gone." - Moby. It's the kind of morning that crispens the lungs just as easily as the frost does sidewalks or lawns. A magical, jewelled kingdom, tainted only by the bleak monotony which resides within it. Despite the anaemic blue of the sky, the sun seems reluctant to drag itself out of bed and over the horizon, where the stubborn crescent moon lingers like the tipped-over grin of a Cheshire cat. I'm running slowly on two hours of interrupted sleep, and my eyes feel raw, exposed to the stinging elements. A dry blast of wind-chill leaves my lips feeling papery, and out of instinct, I whip my tongue around to moisten them… only to regret it 5 seconds later when another gust super-cools them. My breath steams before it's even expelled, lingering weightlessly in the seconds between rushes of easterly wind. The frantic towel-drying of my hair, in much need of a trim, left it damp, and running a gloved hand through it, I displace ice. This would all depress me if I didn’t already know this wasn't to be my life for much longer. I had my entry form to salvation stamped, and my plane ticket away from all this had arrived yesterday. The GTT would deliver me from these 6am mornings, from the feeling that life was seeping out through my pores, and my soul was being disguised in every crystallised breath I exhale. I say something too crude to repeat. Something that would degrade the name of mother nature to levels not even I'm comfortable with. The only bright lights in this wrestler's career are the overbearing amber torches above, and the only gift fame affords me is the paperboy deliberately aiming for somewhere solid. I'm the wrestling equivalent of the naive country-boy cliché heading into the corrupting bright lights of the big city… only I have nobody to care what I'm warped into upon arrival. I repeat saying something too crude to repeat. Just as the crisp air begins to cleanse the taste of stale cigarettes and bad coffee from my palate, the garage door begins its laboured ascent. A groggy yawn sends more steam bellowing out of me, before I again run my hand through my hair, then rub my stiff neck as it follows through. The chemical stench I keep meaning to expel lingers harsher in the icy air, and I duck my mouth and nose down into my tatty scarf. The sight of worn, almost bare types hardly leaves me enthralled with the prospect of the upcoming drive, while I try to remember exactly how I made the heaters work the last time. Behind me, a few bedroom lights start to flicker on as the early-rising sadists wake with eager smiles on their faces, loving the fact another day is soon to dawn. Somehow they see this routine as life affirming and inspiring. And it's the depressed ones they say should be medicated. The rickety suspension squeaks as I yank at the sticky driver's side door, then collapse down into the seat, moulded to my ever-increasing ass over the years. Even as I harshly pull the door to, my breath takes substance before my very eyes, while my body's heat starts to mist the windshield. Looking out through the increasingly opaque glass before me, I see a world I feel no inclination, let alone desire, to gift myself to for another day. Oh, how easy it would be to close the garage door, crank the seat back, and leave the engine to grumble on contently. With the wafts of carbon monoxide singing me back to sleep, I'd be hurled from the revolving door's of bland existence, excusing myself before it's time to leave. A rather rude exit. If that door closed on me, and the fumes of gas I have left in the tank are expended, I'd rot. After a few days of calling, ready to fire me anyway, work would just hire the next guy to walk through the door. It's 21st century western civilisation, so it's hardly likely that any neighbours will notice if I'm alive, dead, or streaking across their lawn at sunset. Maybe I'd get a call just before Christmas from my ex-wife, saying the kids were out but if I had nothing planned, I could maybe drive over and stay for a half hour. I usually reply, Go to hell. Only it sounds more like, I have plans already. There'd be no memorial shows for me. No 'net columnists ranking my best ever matches. No touching tributes from the most respected names in the business of which I'd lurked in the periphery for the past 25 years. I'd evoke no emotions, cause nobody to reflect, usher in no change. At least not at this second in time, I wouldn't. Just to remind me why my lungs aren't heaving in fumes, I drop open the glove box, where my GTT acceptance form floats out and onto the passenger seat. The red acceptance stamp the closest my life has come to being validated, making a somebody of a nobody. I now have credibility by association. In the opening round, I stand on the same first step as Seymour Almasy, Angelo Deville, and Rich Rollins. Until the first bell rings, or the first word is spoken in anger, none of us are superstars any more than we are jobbers. A diplomatic stasis freezes us in parity until the games begin. After 25 years, all I want is to be known. All I want is for my name to be remembered. Yes, I have such little left that this little matters. I gave my everything for the wrestling business. My family, my long-term health, numerous jobs, and an ever decreasing string of houses. I've invested my life in wrestling, and I refuse to sit back and get nothing in return. I have nothing to lose. So when I do lose, whichever round it may be, I won't go back to anonymity, to mundanity, to this. No. Because when I leave GTT, I leave wrestling… and wrestling is entwined in a barbed embrace with my life itself. I will not see the morning after my exit, the headlines, the tributes from opponents, memories from colleagues, a touching statement from the GTT organisers. I won't see anything, ever. Wrestling will remember me. The reason doesn't matter to me, just as I don't matter to wrestling. In life I'm lost at the bottom of the shuffle, but in death I'll join the immortals. I came out of the blue, and the GTT will ensure my name lives on as I go into the black.
