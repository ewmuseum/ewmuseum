A Moment's Peace

21 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-21 Round Four of Primetime Central’s Golden Turnbuckle Tournament. In the Pyrenees Bracket Semi-Final, the NGW’s lone remaining representative readies himself to square off against yet another opponent of high esteem and regard. Dallas Reeves (NGW) vs Dave Harley (WWA) The little-known Reeves, along with a host of previously unheralded competitors, has injected a new threat to the upper echelons of the PTC. No longer can the “household” names only concern themselves with fellow “household” names. The superstars are now forced to look over their shoulders, to catch a glimpse of the new batch of shooting stars. The jury may still be out on whether or not Dallas Reeves is one of these, though he may beg to differ. What everyone may agree on is that Reeves has already upset Kenny Mercury and Unstoppable Eugene, and his opponents aren’t getting any easier. At the insistence of his manager “Alberta” Jack Carrington, Reeves has been undergoing specialized training at the ranch of former pro-wrestler “Pepsi” Joe Trudeau. As curmudgeony as he is physically deformed, Trudeau is a hard-ass, no two ways about it. But Reeves, still flying high on his GTT4 success, throws caution to the wind, as his purple ’69 Dodge Charger races down the flat stretch of Highway 1 in rural Saskatchewan. After sneaking off to the casino in Regina with his protégé Frankie Calrissian and bodyguard Beastor Brandt, the trio are headed back to Trudeau’s farmhouse in Montmartre. The camera opens as the Charger speeds down the highway, backdropped against the endless skies of the Canadian Prairies. The stars are out in full force, mapping the heavens with heroes and myths of yore. Behind the wheel is the young Frankie Calrissian, the cameraman-turned-wrestler mentored by Reeves. He has a look of horror on his face, as well as a thin mustache. Crammed into the back seat is the hulking frame of the savage looking Beastor Brandt, his 6’8” 335 lb figure bent awkwardly. Riding shotgun is Dallas Reeves, his blond hair whipping around his face thanks to the wind from the open window. Reeves turns to look at Beastor, a disgusted look on his face. Dallas: I told you before, Beastor…not in the car! Frankie, what the hell was he eating? The Beastor shrugs, unconcerned. Frankie: I don’t know…he was at the buffet for the entire three hours we were at the casino. Dallas: Prairie oysters… Frankie: Hunh? Dallas: Nevermind, just drive. Frankie: Man…Mr. Carrington is gonna have my ass in a sling for letting you out like this, you know that right? You’re supposed to be staying away from anything that ruin your focus on the Harley match-up, and yet there you were, racking up winnings in baccarat. I’d better not get grounded for this! Dallas: Relax, Franco. We don’t have to be in Washington for another couple of days. If Trudeau wants to subject me to his insanity, he’s gonna have to give me some leeway. Frankie: Washington? Dallas: Yes, Washington DC. Home to the GTT4 tournament. Frankie: DC? Woh, back in time. Reeves pays no attention to this unfunny and rather stupid remark, but Frankie giggles to himself. A cell phone begins to ring, and Reeves’ hand dips into the breast pocket of his vintage tan leather jacket. He puts the phone to his ear. Dallas: Hello? Oh, hi Richard. Yeah, I got the fax, but I already told your people that I’m busy with this wrestling tournament for the next few days, at least. Yes, I have your number. But isn’t it supposed to be “don’t call us, we’ll call you”? Ha, that’s great, yes. You’ll have to talk to my agent, say, Monday morning. Cool? Okies, talk lates. Reeves puts the phone away, and says nothing. Frankie stares at him, waiting to hear what the call was about. Sensing his presence, Reeves relents. Dallas: That was Richard Strong over at Pyramid Productions. He wants me to do a few guest spots on a show they have in development. Frankie: But Mr. Carrington said no more acting gigs- Dallas: Until the end of the tournament, I know. That’s why I said Monday morning. Frankie: What!? You’re going to beat Harley! Monday won’t do! You’re gonna win the whole entire tournament- Dallas: While I find your optimism most flattering, Frankie, I’m just being realistic. Sure, I’m into the fourth round, but sooner or later a man’s luck runs out. Beating Scott Vyper, then Kenny Mercury was pretty tricky. Stopping the Unstoppable Eugene was no easy task. If Harley brings his A game, I have quite the daunting task ahead of me. Frankie: Dallas, you can’t talk like that! The NGW is changing hands, you have no federation to call your own…there’s no excuse to put forth your best effort in the GTT! Reeves looks out the window, pondering his young charge’s words. Indeed, the NGW is closing soon, only to be re-opened under new ownership. But after spending the last 8 months in that organization, Reeves has thought about seeking employment elsewhere. The trouble is, only one other fed in the PTC seems interested in his services. However, those thoughts soon retire to the recesses of his mind, as the eerily illuminated yard of “Pepsi” Joe Trudeau’s farmhouse now lies before him. Knowing full well that Trudeau, the aged one-eyed one-eared terror, will be waiting in anger for him, and that his own manager “Alberta” Jack Carrington won’t be impressed with this unauthorized excursion, Reeves turns his focus to the task at hand. Sneaking back in. Easing the Charger into the laneway, the trio of Frankie, Beastor and Reeves slide out of the car, and stealthily make their way towards the front steps of the homestead. Beastor pauses a moment, sniffing at the air. Something seems amiss, and Reeves crouches down along the side of the musty barn, ninja-like. He whispers to Beastor. Dallas: What is it, Beastor? Waddya smell? Is it Trudeau? (worried) Is he armed? The Beastor hasn’t spoken much since rejoining civilization, and doesn’t speak now. If anyone can pick up Trudeau’s scent, it would be the Beastor. Back in the mid 1980s, he and Trudeau had a violent feud, when both were still enjoying their wrestling careers. Beastor is the one that cost Trudeau his eye and ear. Frankie makes a series of gestures with his hands, mimicking something he must have seen in a movie somewhere. Dallas looks at him as though he has a horn growing out of his head. Frankie repeats the gestures. Dallas: Um…red team go? Frankie, I don’t speak “recon”. Frankie bolts around the corner of the barn, and a soft thud is heard soon after. Reeves peers around where Frankie disappeared, then looks at Beastor, still sniffing at the air, but now with feathers sticking out of his mouth and stuck in his beard. Dallas: Stop eating Trudeau’s chickens! C’mon, we gotta save Frankie. You won’t believe this. Beastor follows Dallas around the side of the barn, where Frankie is lying flat on his back, the cloven hoof of a black and white (Holstein) cow on his stomach. Dallas: Frankie…what the hell? Through troubled breaths, Frankie manages to utter a few words. Frankie: Guard…cow. Dallas: Beastor, if you would be so kind. Without blinking, Beastor sends a right cross towards the, um, guard cow, knocking its’ bovine ass out and to the ground. Dallas helps Frankie up, shaking his head. Dallas: I’m not even going to ask how a thousand pound farm animal got the drop on you. Now stop effin’ around. We’ve got to sneak into that farmhouse. Beastor produces two pieces of bread from the pockets of his blue coveralls, that don’t quite cover all. He stands over the fallen cow, licking his chops. Reeves puts his hand on Beastor’s chest, to stop him from whatever he had planned. Dallas: Don’t. Just…just don’t. Reeves has his “lackeys” regroup on him, and he turns his attention to the farmhouse, which appears to be dimly lit from within. They huddle up, and after a moment of hushed whispers, they clap very quietly and break like a football team would. Sneaking towards the aft of the farmhouse, Beastor leans his palms against the side of it. Frankie shimmies himself into position, standing atop Beastor’s brawny shoulders. Reeves scoots himself up Beastor, then up past Frankie’s lithe 5’11” 178 lb frame and onto his shoulders. Frankie’s knees begin to shake, as Reeves’ 246 lbs are a great stress on his shoulders. Reeves reaches upwards, the human ladder allowing him to slide the second floor window open. He gets a solid grasp on the overhanging eaves, and pushes off of Frankie’s head with his foot. Frankie can’t hold on, and teeters before toppling to the ground, landing atop the Beastor with a thunk. Reeves is left dangling, half in the room and half out the window, but manages to pull himself in. He avoids making too much noise, for fear of waking the ill-tempered Trudeau. Dallas: Mental note…work on the ol’ co-ordination. Reeves tiptoes his way across the wooden floor, the old farmhouse creaking slightly as he goes. He makes his way out of the bedroom he entered, out to the hallway, and to the top of the stairs. Not a single light is on upstairs, and surprisingly enough, Reeves doesn’t bump into anything. He takes the first step, which again creaks slightly. Tentatively, he takes another step down, only to be met with a louder and longer creak. A third step reveals a more amplified sound, and so, he decides he has better luck at sliding down the rickety banister. He places his left leg over it, then begins to scooch his way down, when he is startled by the loudest of whispers, coming from the bottom of the stairs. Carrington: Dallas! ”Alberta” Jack B. Carrington, long-time family friend and full-time manager of Reeves, stands, arms akimbo, an exasperated look on his face. As a result of this surprise, Reeves loses his grip on the banister, and careens down it, knocking the newel post clean off the end. He lands hard on the wooden floor, the newel post flying right into the waiting hands of Carrington. The two speak in hushed tones. Carrington: What in tarnation?! Where the hell have ya been!? Dallas picks himself up, dusting off his pride in the process. Dallas: C’mon, J.B., I just needed a little R & R. Is “Pepsi” Joe around? (worried) Is he armed? Carrington: Wha? No! He made ‘imself a Nyquil cocktail, and accidentally ate some a those weinehs he was tryin’ ta tranquilize ya with. He’s out like a light. An overwhelming sense of relief crosses Reeves’ face, as Carrington leads him from the dimly lit hallway into the den, a fire burning full force in the fireplace. Seated next to it in an elegant armchair is the slumbering sixty three year old Trudeau. The patch over his right eye is a little off-kiltered, and his shaggy gray hair covers the missing portion of his left ear. At his feet is an empty bottle of Nyquil and a half finished bag of hot dog wieners. The old firebrand seems much more docile right now, especially seeing as how he isn’t barking inane commands in Reeves’ direction. Dallas nears the man, as if seeing him in a different light for the first time. Dallas: Well I’ll be…look at him J.B. He almost looks human! Hell, he kinda looks like a teddy bear in this state. A crazy, psychotic, out-of-touch with reality teddy bear, mind you- Carrington: Oh, hush up, ma boy. Tamarra we got ow-selves a big day. Dallas: I know, I know…but now’s my chance. Reeves picks a throw pillow up off the nearby vintage style sofa, venturing closer to Trudeau’s sleeping form. As he slowly moves the pillow over the old man’s face, Carrington snatches it away before it can do any harm. Reeves smiles at his faithful manager, and the two share a laugh. It’s no secret that Reeves dislikes his sessions with Trudeau, and the only reason he goes along with it is his faith in Carrington’s management skills. Realizing now that it is only he and Carrington awake in the room, Reeves gets straight to GTT4 business. Dallas: Dave Harley. He’s built like a heavyweight, but can wrestle like a cruiserweight. Carrington: Yer built like a cruiseh-weight, but can rassle like a heaveh-weight. They exchange “point taken” nods. Dallas: He’s allied with a motley crew of miscreants. Scurrying into the room comes Beastor, followed by Frankie. Beastor has a few chicken feathers stuck in his beard. Frankie looks at Dallas, then shakes his head as he hangs it in shame. Frankie: He got another one, Dallas, before I could stop him. Carrington looks to Dallas, smiling. Carrington: You were sayin’? Dallas: Right. Frankie: Woh, “Pepsi” Joe is out cold! Thank freakin’ god! Beastor inches forward, his anger for Trudeau brewing within. Reeves stops him from advancing any closer. Dallas: Now Beastor, that wouldn’t be very sporting of you, would it? The Beastor relents. Finally coming to the actualization that they have walked into a private meeting, Beastor and Frankie politely excuse themselves from the moth-ball smelling den. Carrington sits down on the sofa, and Reeves, searching for a place to sit, ends up just rolling Trudeau’s body to the floor in front of the roaring fireplace. Carrington: He’s gonna hate ya in tha mornin’, if he fig-yas out what happened. Dallas: “Pepsi” Joe hates everything. Though, he may be a fan of Dave Harley’s iron-fisted way of getting things done. Harley’s all about consolidating his power, looking out for himself, and effin’ over the pals that made him what he is. Carrington: An’ you, ma boy? Dallas: I use my popularity to land me acting gigs! Reeves smiles a shit-eating grin, which quickly fades. Dallas: Okay, I * try* to land myself acting gigs. But at least I wrestle for all the right reasons! Harley is only interested in inflating his ego! Me, I’m in it for- A loud, thundering crash from the kitchen interrupts the conversation. Reeves shoots to his feet, waiting to see what Beastor and Frankie are getting into now. Dallas: Holy Johnny Damon! What the hell!? He bolts into the kitchen, only to come face to face with an angry looking Holstein. Dallas: Guard…cow? Trudeau amazingly hops to his feet, brought right out of his near comatose state. Trudeau: Susie! Is it Friday already!? Dallas: Oh dear. Trudeau: You! You’re in a load of shit, pal! As Trudeau lunges for Reeves, the camera cuts to an exterior shot of the cottage, with plenty of crashes and things breaking being heard. The camera fades to black.
