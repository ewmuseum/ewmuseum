Different Directions

16 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-16 The scene opens atop a grassy bluff, over-looking the sparkling blue waters of Lake Joseph in Ontario’s Muskoka region; cottage country. The sun is setting against the western shores, and a lone man stands taking it all in. The wind picks up, and he brushes his long curly blond hair out of his face. The mid-September evening is colder than usual, so he buttons up his brown corduroy jacket. This man is “Hollywood North” Dallas Reeves, and he has finally found a little time to himself. The last several months have seen him running ragged, competing at the top of the card in his home federation of the NGW, picking up several acting roles, and doing tons of press work for each of them. He also represented his fed at the PTC Survival card, and most recently, lost a barn-burner of a match against his best friend, NGW World Champion John Hitchin. Dallas Reeves may still be the NGW American Champ, but right now, he seems a little hesitant about his life, both professionally and privately. Through the miracles of post-production, the lone wrestler on the bluff is joined by the musical stylings of 54-40’s “Crossing a Canyon”. Sitting on the edge atop a canyon I'm a piece of time too small to name There's a sign that's hanging over me Let me disappear and try again I'm no prophet but I've seen things When right and wrong become too close In that moment of my greatness I become afraid and watch it go Dallas Reeves has never been a man that was known for his great planning. Sure, when he was growing up, he wanted to become a wrestler, but since he achieved that goal, he’s flown by the seat of his pants. He’s taken a lot of risks over the years, both professionally and privately, personally. That’s part of the irony of where Dallas finds himself now. See, Dallas grew up in a wrestling family, on the outskirts of Ottawa, Ontario. He grew up, and he trained hard, but the main reason he got into wrestling was for the thrill of it. He’s one of the lucky ones; he does what he loves for a living, and gets paid pretty well for it. But it was never about the money. For him, it was the love of the fans. When opportunity presented itself, he made a move to Hollywood, in the summer film “Braveheart 2: Brave Harder”, a comical spoof. Things just fell into place for him. While away filming, he realized that he was already beginning to miss wrestling; he enjoyed going on tour and travelling around. Between guest shots and cameo appearances, between pay-per-views and charity shows, Dallas was saving no time for his personal life. Although he loves his work, he was doing too much of it, and over-extending himself. With the gruelling travel schedule of a professional wrestler, he was apprehensive of really finding someone to settle with, or even get serious with. It wouldn’t be fair to her to be away all the time, and it wouldn’t be fair to him to back away from the spotlight that he had fought so hard and long to attain. And the day is long And the bridge is gone And the river has washed away The road I'm on Only road I know Everybody's got a problem Something to do with self control You gotta tell somebody if you got one You can't pretend you didn't know And Dallas Reeves was never known for his great planning. Then one day, he met a leggy brunette, a woman he likened to Daisy Duke. She was a traveller as well, with a hectic travelling schedule as a model/bit actress. They shared things in common, and soon shared other things as well. Alas, relationships cannot move forward without the time to build a foundation, and the two soon found themselves parted. However, over the past several months, they’ve strived to make it work. Dallas Reeves, always hesitant to give attention to his personal life for fear of it crashing and burning, let go of his own policy of not getting too involved. So far so good, although the on-again off-again relationship with his Jessika is a constant reminder of thinking twice about thrusting himself into too many work-related events. Such as inter-fed tournaments. But we’ll get there soon. Staring out across the canyon My eyes won't tell me what to see If I let go I could be floating From the river to the sea Dallas Reeves closes his eyes as he looks out over the lake. He’s been away far too much lately, and it took his manager “Alberta” Jack B. Carrington lots of convincing to get him to agree to enter the GTT4. The man who was cautious about his personal life was now a little cautious about his professional life. And the day is long And the bridge is gone And the river has washed away The road I'm on Only road I know If I let go I could be floating From the river to the sea Now, atop the bluff, Dallas Reeves is joined by three other men. The first one is an older gentleman, with silver hair and a bushy black mustache. He has a white cowboy hat atop his aged head, and approaches with his hands in the pockets on his pricey looking jacket. This man is his manager, “Alberta” Jack B. Carrington. Following him are a very large man, with bushy black hair and a rampant black beard, and a slight man in his early 20s. These are Beastor Brandt, Reeves’ bodyguard, and Frankie Calrissian, Reeves’ personal cameraman-turned-wrestling protégé. Carrington stops several feet behind Dallas, while Frankie throws grass at Beastor playfully. That’s all the time Dallas has for himself right now, fittingly so, as the sun’s last light begins to duck below the horizon. Reeves: J.B. I figger’d you’d be here by now. Carrington: Dallas, it’s time ta head back. Ah think we best be getting’ readeh fo’ this big PTC shindig. (Frankie pulls himself away from his horseplay, with his usual glee shining through.) Frankie: Yeah Dallas! Let’s go, let’s go! We gotta go, man! Dallas: Frankie, why don’t you go play fetch with Beastor. Frankie: No way! He always throws me too hard! Dallas: Beastor, if you please. (The beefy right hand of the Beastor lands upon Frankie’s slender shoulder, then jerks him backwards. Frankie lets out a yelp, then is thrown over Beastor’s shoulder, and carried away. Reeves turns around with a smile on his million dollar face, and looks to Carrington, who has a rather stern look on his.) Dallas: Don’t look at me like that, J.B. It’s not my fault Beastor is so damn loyal. Besides, Frankie- Carrington: Frankeh is yo’ cha’ge, Dallas. Against ma wishes, ya took up managin’ that odd boy. Din’t ya think ya had enough on yo’ plate as it was? Dallas: Oh come on, J.B. Managing Frankie is a harmless endeavour. Besides, he’s done pretty well for himself. Carrington: An’ you? Dallas: Me? Well, I’ve spent quite a bit of time as the American Champ. That’s nothing to shake a stick at. Carrington: Coulda been the Champion of the World, Dallas. Shoulda been. (Reeves face takes a turn towards the sour. He turns away from Carrington and focuses on Frankie and Beastor many yards away.) Dallas: Don’t start, J.B. You know I didn’t want to fight Hitch. He’s my best friend. World Title or not. Carrington: Ah think ya thought too much o’ that match. Instead a focusin’ on tha title, ya focused on fightin’ a friend. Ah’ve got news for ya, Dallas. In GTT4, there’s a chance ya could fight him again. If not Misteh Hitchin, then mebbe one of yo’ fellow NGDubbya’s anyways. Ya gotta stop worryin’ ‘bout tha fyu-cha. Dallas: I never really have, J.B. But…man, I just got back from Greece. Do we have to talk about this stuff right now? I haven’t seen Jessika, I just lost a huge match, and now I’m supposed to pick up and head off for this PTC thing? (Carrington doffs his hat, and runs his 53 yr old fingers through his silver hair. He looks at Dallas with an “I told you so” look.) Carrington: Ah wa’ned ya ‘bout signin’ up for ever’than’ undeh the sun, didn’t Ah? First ya take on Beasteh as a boddehgua’d, draggin’ me all tha way up ta north Kuh-beck to get ‘im. Then ya did that film. Then there was yo’ decision ta manage Frankeh. On toppa all that, ya rassle in tha NGDubbaya, and a few PTC events. Always thinkin’ ‘bout tha moment, and not entirely anticipatin’ tha fyu-cha. Dallas: Yeah well…my immediate “fyu-cha” has me facing Scott Vyper. So be the good manager you are and tell me what you know about him. Carrington: From what Ah gatheh, he’s been ‘round a few PTC thin’s in tha PTC. Dallas: And… Carrington: That’s about all Ah know. Ah don’t folla yo’ exploits in tha PTC that much, you know that. What you do they-uh is yo’ own wo’ld. Ah think it takes away from yo’ duties in tha NGDubbya. But you’ll be fine, Dallas. Ya always seem ta land on yo’ feet. Dallas: Thanks for that great vote of confidence, J.B. So I’m going into a match blind, eh? That makes for a bit of an uneasy anticipation of the future. Carrington: C’mon, let’s head back to tha cottage. (Carrington pats Reeves on the side of the shoulder, and heads towards Beastor and Frankie. Reeves turns once more to the stillness of the lake, then follows slowly after his manager and long-time family friend. Carrington enters the driver side of the purple Jeep TJ Unlimited, and motions the three to get in. Beastor is tossing Frankie around like a rag doll, cackling maniacally as he does so. Frankie, on the other hand, shrieks like a little girl. As Reeves passes by, he grabs Beastor by the ear and directs him towards the waiting ride. Frankie picks himself up off the ground, catching his breath.) Frankie: Ohhh, you are SO lucky, Beastor! I was just about to launch my counter-attack! Dallas: Frankie…just, get in. (Dallas sits shotgun as Frankie and Beastor pile into the elongated back of the new Jeep. Carrington starts the engine, and the vehicle heads off down the dirt road to the cottage of Dallas Reeves. The scene cuts away from on the bluff to the interior of the cottage. It’s wood panelled interior and 50s era furniture give it the stereotypical look of an Ontario cottage, where all old furniture seems to end up. Carrington stands behind the black vinyl bar in the corner, a cigar in his mouth, and pouring himself a glass of Scotch. Frankie is seated in front of the television, playing a wrestling video game. Beastor isn’t to be seen, and Reeves is on the rotary style phone, leaving a message.) Dallas: Hey Jess, it’s me. I’m heading off tomorrow, but call my cell. I’m sorry things have been so hectic lately, but I’d really like to see ya. Talk lates, brown eyes. (Reeves hangs up the phone, and heads past Frankie to the couch. He plops himself down, and is handed a frosty Moosehead beer by Carrington.) Frankie: Oooooh, Dally’s callin’ his girrrrrlfriend- (Dallas reaches over and thwacks Frankie upside the head playfully.) Dallas: Don’t call me “Dally”, Frankie. Frankie: Ow, fine. Think she’ll call back? Dallas: Of course. Our future isn’t done with yet. (Frankie mashes the controller buttons, and the video game emits a “1-2-3” count, then the dinging of a bell.) Frankie: Boom! Frankie the Cameraman pins Dallas Reeves with his own finisher! HAHA Just like one day in real life! (Dallas and Carrington exchange looks, then break out laughing. Frankie spins around.) Frankie: What’s so funny!? It could happen! One day… (Beastor comes roaring out of another section of the cottage, and tackles Frankie to the ground, presumably for such blasphemy.) Dallas: What have I told you two? No horseplay in the cottage! (Beastor looks up, startled, then slowly points towards the front door. Reeves nods, and Beastor flashes a big toothy grin. He then drags Frankie by the leg outside, as Frankie grabs at the hardwood floor, furniture, anything to save himself, but to no avail. He knocks an end table, and the lamp upon it teeters before righting itself.) Dallas: Frankie, you break it you bought it. Frankie: Beastor, noooo! (And just like that, Beastor has dragged Frankie outside. Reeves smiles, as he loves the camaraderie of his motley crew. He looks at Carrington, then takes a more serious tone.) Dallas: So in all honesty, J.B., what are my chances at GTT4? I mean, I may be a part of the organization, but I’m not well known, and I don’t know the others well either. I know you, you’re too crafty and you care too much to let me go in there without scouting it out a bit first. So lay it on me, what’s the deal. Carrington: Sounds ta me like yer havin’ some rezzavations, ma boy. Whateva happened ta not worryin’ ‘bout tha fyu-cha? Dallas: Wha? Oh, I’m not worryin’, per se, I’m just…curious. My bracket is lookin’ pretty tough, and I’ve never faced ANY of the myriad competitors before. Well, okay, apart from Hitchin, Cyrus Skar, and maybe one or two others…but for the most part, I’ve got no effin’ clue what I’ve got myself into, and I think it’s just now startin’ to sink in. Carrington: How ‘bout ya just worry ‘bout yer fi’st rounda, Scott Vypeh. Dallas: Okay…so, what about Scott Vyper should I know? Carrington: He thinks yer a pretty boy, an’ he’s just in tha tourney fo’ tha money. Dallas: What?! That’s ludicrous! How can he be in something like the GTT4 just for the money!? I didn’t even know there were cash prizes! And, yeah, I am kind of a pretty boy, but I’m also dy-no-mite in the ring, which he’ll soon find out. Carrington: Tha PTC wouldn’t let jus’ anyone inna tha GTTFo-uh- Dallas: Sha…they let in Scott W., didn’t they? Carrington: Fair enough. (Carrington sips the last of his Scotch, and sets the empty tumbler on the coffee table. He stands, and heads towards the hallway.) Carrington: Ah’m off fo’ tha night, Dallas. Don’t have too many of those Moosehead, we gotta earleh start tomorrah. I want ya ta spen’ some time thinkin’ ‘bout Scott Vypeh though. Mebbe worryin’ ‘bout yo’ fyu-cha will do ya some good. G’night. Dallas: Yes, right. ‘Night, J.B. Carrington: An’ do me a favouh. Get those idjits inside so’s they don’t keep me up. Dallas: Will do. (Carrington exits the scene, and Reeves puts an arm behind his head, slowly sipping on his beer. With thoughts of Jessika, the NGW, his acting, and now the GTT4 tournament scampering about in his mind, maybe he has bitten off more than he can chew. But he has no other direction to turn his thoughts other than to the future, and to what can and may be. He has a long, hard road ahead of him, if he can muster a victory against Scott Vyper. But then, that is merely one more victory in life he has to look at. With pressure from his recent loss in the NGW, his role as Frankie’s manager, and his own standing with Jessika working against him, Dallas Reeves looks towards the future with uncertainty, something that is new to him. He’s finally beginning to understand the situation he is in, and he knows no other way to accomplish what he wants other than to simply make the most of his time, and to seize the opportunities that will soon present themselves.) Dallas: Wow. So many meanings. (The camera fades out on Dallas Reeves, seated on the couch. This promo has been released through Dudesy Productions. MMIV.)
