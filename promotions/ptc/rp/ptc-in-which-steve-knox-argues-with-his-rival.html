In Which Steve Knox Argues With His Rival December 30th, 2009; Dallas, Texas The Squared Circle was the hotbed of wrestling in the mid-South region. It was once a promotion that had its own national television deal and toured across the eastern United States, and gained enough of a following for some to label it as the best wrestling promotion of 2004. Owned and operated by Harvard Avalon, the promotion was popular, but it was highly dependent on Harvard's unique mind and his ability to sell his product to the bigwigs. Harvard died from cancer at the end of 2004, and his wife took over the operation. The promotion never saw the same national success again, because without the word of Harvard, the television contract renewal was gone. Taking what little talent she had left, Rosemary Avalon took the Squared Circle's name and resumed operations at the end of February of 2005. Steve Knox was the man she turned to in her hour of need. In many ways, Steve was the man who rebuilt the promotion that had been Harvard Avalon's dream. He had matches with many of the best wrestlers the world had to offer, including many household names like Seymour Almasy, Coral Avalon, the Codemaster, "the Original" Jeff Garvin, and others. He also won eight championships while with the company, including the top title, simply called "the Championship", three times. However, after Steve signed a contract with New Frontier Wrestling, he saw his stock in the small company fall even though he remained within it. He lost the Championship to Coral Avalon in May, and hadn't won a championship since. On top of that, the current Champion of the organization was someone who had some real antipathy for him. After all, she had just broken up with him via text message. It was December 30th, and Steve Knox was placed into a daunting situation in the ring. He was to team with his fellow "possemates", Chris Chambers and Cosmo Kid, as well as the Codemaster. His opponents for the night were the so-called "Dynasty", which consisted of its leader, Coral Avalon, as well as Inoue Doi and the Princes of New England. Steve had been feuding with Avalon ever since he lost the Championship to him, but had found himself unable to beat him decisively since the loss. Coral's Dynasty, at one point, held every Championship in the Squared Circle, but one-by-one, they began to fall. Doi lost her Evolutionary championship to the Codemaster in July, and then, in October, Coral Avalon had lost the Championship to Alexandria, and the Princes lost their tag titles to their bitter rivals, Team VIAGRA. Tonight's match was another attempt to settle the score between Steve and Coral. In reality, Coral Avalon was one of the nicest and most pleasant men to have ever graced professional wrestling, as well as cultivating a reputation as one of the best wrestlers in North America. His trainer, Joey Malone, had often called Coral his greatest student. However, a series of events had led to Avalon becoming disenfranchised with the national level, and Coral continued to wrestle in his own style. He was strangely happy with his role in the Squared Circle, a promotion he'd been with since it opened. Perhaps it was because Coral's cousin, Harvard, was the one who created it. Inoue Doi was like Coral, a stalwart of the promotion since its beginning. She used to wrestle under the monicker of "the School Girl", dressing very much like a Japanese school girl in matches, yet possessing a violent and child-like temper, as well as some of the most lethal strikes ever seen by a woman in the wrestling world. Although she'd stopped using the name and started wrestling under her real name, her wrestling gear remained the same. Either way, the sheer ferocity displayed by this small young woman had made her a significant risk to hire nationally, and in fact, only the Avalons and Japan had been willing to hire her. Ironically, Inoue was a friend of Coral's only through his wife, Annabelle, who was Inoue's best friend. As for the Princes, who were last seen in PRIME like Coral Avalon, they consisted of Steve's arrogant brother, Simon, and another one of Joey Malone's students in Connor O'Reily. They had come in to help revitalize the tag division of the Squared Circle, which led to their work in PRIME. However, after six months, the Princes returned to the Squared Circle full-time. The stage was set for a crazy contest. * . * 6:35pm CST; Dallas, Texas Steve Knox was early that day, as he usually was. The Squared Circle show, known as "Zenith", was the first of three straight shows that the promotion planned to do that week. Steve knew it was going to be a tough week, but he was determined to make the most of his days. He had intended to take the next month off between the last show and the next "supercard". On this day, Steve decided to leave the locker room and go outside of the building for a short little while to get some fresh air. Slipping on his jacket over his wrestling gear, he opened the door and walked out into the cool Texas air. "Hey." greeted a familiar voice. "What the hell are you doing out here, Coral?" was Steve's response. Coral was also in his wrestling gear, and he also wore a jacket over it. His long hair was, much to Steve's annoyance, perfectly groomed. Actually, a lot of things about Coral Avalon annoyed Steve Knox. Coral could balance a love for wrestling with a beautiful wife and family, he could wrestle any kind of style he wanted, and he seemed to be satisfied with his relative "underground" reputation. Steve regarded Coral with two parts jealousy and one part an inability to understand the guy. Coral leaned against the wall, his arms crossed, looking over at Steve Knox. "Just feeling the air." Coral replied. "Most people breathe it, you know." Steve quipped. Coral chuckled in response. Another thing that really annoyed Steve about Coral Avalon was how easily the guy could brush off anything anyone ever said if it was meant to antagonize him. He didn't always have such a thick skin in his rookie days in Action! Wrestling, but time and experience had tempered his skin into damn near unpenetrable. Or maybe he'd turned into a giant hippie, Steve wasn't too sure. "Yeah, they do that, too." was Coral's eventual response. There was an awkward silence between the two men, before Coral continued to talk, "Big night, tonight. You sure you're ready for it?" "I was born ready," Steve said, with a quiet confidence, "The only question is, can you keep up with me?" Coral chuckled again, raising Steve's inner agitation levels into the mid-orange levels, "Geez, Steve, we're not in the wrestling ring, cutting promos on each other. Chill out." Steve stared at Coral for a second, unsure of how to respond to Coral's comment. After a few more awkward moments, Steve responded with the words that summed up how he felt about his relationship with Coral, "You know, I don't get you at all." "Where'd that come from?" Coral asked, a tone of surprise in his voice. He took himself out of his lean against the wall and stepped slightly closer to Steve Knox, mostly to get a better look at Steve's increasingly annoyed expression, "Something the matter?" "I don't want to talk about it with *you*." Steve said. Now he wanted to just go back inside the stuffy, tiny arena that tSC was holding its show in and just forget about getting a breath of fresh air. Coral responded by scratching his head, and sighing. "You don't have to be hostile, Steve," Avalon said with a sigh, "Man, you take this too seriously, you know? Lighten up. There's maybe 500 people in the arena today, it's not like you're selling out arenas around the country right now." Coral's words, though said with the tone of good humor, were like a knife getting violently inserted into his side. Right in the kidney, man. Right in the frickin' kidney. Steve sighed, "What I don't get about you is why you're content to stay around here. Don't you want to tour the world, again? Wrestle in front of tens of thousands of fans? Sell out the Garden, maybe? Why stay here, of all places?" "Maybe," Coral said, with a wry smile, "It's because I like being here. I like the sound of the small, vocal crowds. I like being able to do whatever kind of match I want to do without TV time to worry about. I like knowing the people I work with. I don't miss the egos or the politics of the national scene. I don't miss any of that. Plus, I won't have to travel thousands of miles away from my wife and she'll come with me if I happen to tour Japan." "But... you're not competing for anything important. You're just stuck here, in front of a bunch of savages who might show you respect if you do some weird shooting star press, for little reward." Steve said. He paused, before he added, "When NFW opens its doors again, Coral, I'm gonna leave this place for good. I'm gonna stop doing this before I get myself seriously hurt doing some stupid match in this place. It isn't worth it to me." Coral thought about Steve's words for a second, putting his hand under his chin as he thought, before he offered a response, "I guess that's how you are. Well, I won't trip over myself asking you to stay. If you don't want to be here, then there's nothing I can say to change your mind." Steve hesitated for a moment. He wordlessly started walk past Coral to go back inside the building, but stopped short as he reached Avalon. He then turned to see him, his eyes meeting his rival's, "And there's nothing I can say that'll make you leave, either?" "Most likely not." Coral said, flatly. "You're kind of stupid, you know that?" Steve said, equalling Coral in his flatness. "Yeah, I'm stupid for doing what makes me happy," Coral said, with a resigned sigh, as he looked up at the night sky, "Then... are you happy with what you're doing right now?" Steve was quick to lie, "Of course I am. Never better." He was, of course, lying because he wasn't quite where he wanted to be, professionally speaking. Not to mention the mess that was his social life, and the mess that was fast becoming his mental state. He was just a month removed from breaking up with Alexandria Malone by text message, he wasn't really working nationally at the moment, he held no championships anywhere, and he didn't even want to talk about what happens when he goes to bed at night. Mostly because people would think he's completely and utterly crazy. "Huh." was Coral's response. After a brief pause, he added, "Alright, then. Let's get back inside and then figure out who's better, tonight." "I got bad news for you, Coral Avalon," Steve said, with his usual, trademark wide smile that he showed people when he was in a confident mood, "I'm too awesome to fall behind the likes of you." "If you're still conscious after Inoue kicks your head in a couple of hundred times, anyway. Seriously, I'm gonna get Annabelle to tell her to do that." Coral said with a chuckle. Steve stared at Coral for a few seconds, not totally sure if he was completely serious or completely kidding or a mix of both. Steve finally decided not to talk to Avalon any more than he already had to. Later that night, Steve Knox and his group faced Coral Avalon and his group in an elimination match. And yet, when it came time for Coral Avalon and Steve Knox to do battle, they couldn't truly decide who the better man was. They brawled out of the ring and straight into the People's Republic of Countout. Although it left Simon knox to finish the match by pinning the Codemaster, the battle to settle who truly was the better man, in the minds of the fans, would have to wait for another day. For Steve Knox, he desperately wanted to prove himself the better man between himself and Coral Avalon. Not just because he was a peer, but because he wanted to prove to Coral Avalon that he was wrong about the way he conducted himself in professional wrestling. It was a savage business, one not for the faint of heart. In Steve's mind, Coral was too soft to succeed, and indeed, he felt he had already given up because he lacked the heart. If Steve couldn't prove that to him, then how could it prove it to himself that he was the best there is in professional wrestling, today?
