Insanity Prelude: The End The call had come, just as Roppongi said it would. It came at about 4:21 PM, and was extremely brief. It gave Seymour Almasy an address to be at by five o’clock, and no more. The click had startled Seymour, but not badly. After all, he was now concerned with getting to the address. Looking at the New York City roadmap in his head, Seymour figured it was only a five minute walk. Fine by him, really. And that explained why, at 4:45, Seymour stood at a rather obscure intersection somewhere in Manhattan. Almasy carried his robes and mask in one arm, the mask hidden in the folds of the black cloth, so as not to arouse suspicion. Unfortunately, being Seymour Almasy meant it was difficult to go unnoticed. “Hi,” a small voice chirped, from below Almasy’s line of sight. Glancing down, he knew that it was the young wrestling fan, that most common of creatures. Behind him, his proud parents stood, watching their son meet one of the men he saw every week on TV. The child held a piece of paper and a pen, and Seymour almost automatically took them. “Who should I make it out to,” he asked, and the overeager reply of “Brian!” came almost immediately. To most who knew Seymour, it was an utterly incongruous image. He was supposed to be a villain, one of the right hand men of Shadow in Dies Irae. Seymour wasn’t supposed to sign autographs. He was supposed to spew rhetoric. But change was never that easy. Patting the young man on the head, he sent Brian back to his parents. That allowed Seymour to continue to brood. “Damn it all,” he muttered, beginning to nervously pace back and forth, “I can’t believe I bought into it all so easily.” The purpose of Dies Irae, as Shadow stated, was to clear away what she felt to be the corrupt old guard of GCW. Men like LeStatt Knight. Men like Rich Rollins, and yes, men like Seymour Almasy. At first, he’d thought that charge to be a justification for their actions, but no, Shadow truly believed it. Truly believed Knight and Rollins to be past their primes, their places atop GCW ripe for conquering. And so, he joined them, fearful for his position of prominence. Worried that they could take it from him. Back then, he’d thought it was smart. Now, he realized how cowardly he’d been. “A year ago,” he whispered to himself, “I’d have fought them to the last. Made them rip my pride from my cold, dead hands. But I just handed it to them. I sold them my competitive soul. I quit.” The thought both rankled him and gave him inspiration. He knew that he had to win this match now, for his own pride. He had abandoned Dies Irae, forsaken Lia whom he had considered to be a close friend in their better moments. In doing so, he had asserted independence, made known his belief that he could walk on his own. Sure, he’d made it first and foremost out of sheer terror, but all that other stuff was sort of true also. The plane ride had given him time to think. So had three boring days spent looking forward to going to Club Plaisance. He’d barely left his hotel room, instead laying in bed watching television and feeling sorry for himself. “No turning back no though,” he managed, sighing. Wasn’t like he could call Lia back, apologize, and everything would be fine. No, this was a situation that would see its end in the ring on KillZone, for the PTC Global Championship. Before he could dwell on that too much, though, a rather nondescript looking car pulled up to the curb. As pre-arranged in the call, Seymour walked up to it, opened the door, and sat in the front passenger’s seat. “Welcome,” Ichiro Roppongi said, glancing over at Almasy as he fastened his seat belt. “I am glad to see you made it on time.” “Of course,” Seymour replied, evenly. “I’ve been looking forward to this.” “As well you should,” Roppongi stated, a small smirk on his face as they pulled away from the curb. “As well you should…” --------------------------------- Several minutes of a car ride and a brief walk later, and Seymour Almasy was standing outside what looked a whole Hell of a lot like any of six dozen nondescript warehouses that Seymour passed on an average drive through Manhattan. “This is your pleasure club,” he asked, incredulously. “Looks more like a fish packing plant.” “From the outside, perhaps,” Ichiro said, grinning, “but like so many other things in this world, it is what is INSIDE that counts. I must, however, ask that you close your eyes after you put on the uniform. I do enjoy seeing the look on a first timer’s face when they see the Great Entrance Room.” At this point, the anticipation within Seymour’s gut was so great he’d agree to just about anything. “Heh, almost forgot about the cloak,” he said, slipping the robes on overhead, and affixing his mask. “All ready.” Nodding, Roppongi watched to make sure his eyes closed. When they did, the Asian man led Seymour into the warehouse. Almasy noted several twists and turns in the path as they went, bumping into the walls of a narrow corridor before finally emerging in what had to be a larger room. “You may open your eyes,” Roppongi said, undeniable pride in his voice. Seymour did so, looking out through the eyeholes of his mask at pure opulence. The building was gigantic, done in seemingly endless amounts of marble and gold. If ever anything was a monument to excess, Club Plaisance was. Almasy’s eyes gazed at over two dozen passageways leading out from this immense room. “Eager to get started, are we,” Ichiro asked, grinning. He’d seen that look in many a man’s eyes before. Essentially, he operated the world’s most expensive candy store. Club Plaisance was Willy Wonka’s chocolate factory, and those with sufficient fame and resources had a golden ticket to their wildest dreams. “I am,” Seymour said, gaze raking over every inch of the room, which had to be at least twenty feet tall. Smack dab in the center of the room was a statue of a beautiful woman, a plump strawberry held between flawless, slender fingers. “Aphrodite,” Roppongi stated. “The strawberry symbolizes decadence, the forbidden fruit that we all prevent ourselves from indulging in. The gods on Olympus settled for nothing less than the finest; why should we, veritable gods in human form, deny ourselves anything?” Perfectly good logic. Especially if you had money and power. “Now come, Seymour. There is much to show you…” ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ There were, it had to be said, advantages to money and power. Being fed bite-sized pieces of Dover sole by a beautiful woman seated in your lap was one of them. Glancing around the opulent dining room, Seymour saw the scene repeated all over. Gorgeous women feeding the rich and famous. Though they were masked, some were identifiable by their builds, or their hair, or any of a dozen identifying characteristics. They were actors. Debutantes. Athletes, just like him. Club Plaisance would be a paparazzi’s wet dream if they ever discovered it, and so all precautions were taken. They were men, like himself, who were tired of the prying cameras. “Enjoying yourself,” Roppongi queried, even as he too enjoyed the woman in his lap feeding him a spoonful of Yukon Gold mashed potatoes. Seymour merely nodded at first. After all, it wasn’t polite to talk with your mouth full. “This is just for starters,” the older man explained. “Soon, we will venture deeper, and I will show you all that we have to offer here.” ‘All we had to offer’ seemed like quite a lot indeed, and he’d only been in this one room so far. The dinner was incredible, every bite of food carrying the culinary mark of a master. “And you say some in the club never go beyond this?” “Indeed,” Roppongi responded. “They enjoy good food, fine wins, and occasional female entertainment. Of a different sort than other places in the club, of course.” “So no touching,” Seymour asked, slightly amused. “Oh no,” Ichiro said. “There’s touching, just no…” “Got it,” Seymour said, sparing Roppongi from having to use a cutesy euphemism for sex. The lovely woman fed Seymour his final bite of fish, and hopped off of his lap. He frowned momentarily, but Ichiro had already risen as well, gesturing to his guest. “Come,” he said, grinning. “I’ll show you some of our, err, other pleasures.” As he and Seymour left the room, women posted at the doors placed a strawberry in each man’s mouth. Seymour sighed, taking a bite of the fruit, letting the juice dribble down his chin. “This would be our Green Jello room,” the owner of the club said, a small window in the door allowing Seymour to see inside. There, a rather pleased looking man had two women with him in a tub of green Jello, and all three were frolicking. “Wow.” Seymour simply shook his head. Not that he was one to judge, but some people got off on really strange things… “…And to your right, our cosplay ro--” “OMIGOD IT’S A QUISTIS!” Yes, standing half out of the door was a Quistis cosplayer, gesturing to the masked Final Fantasy. Seymour shot a quick look to Ichiro, who simply shook his head. “You’re not a full member of the club yet, Seymour,” Roppongi said, ignoring Almasy’s rather pathetic whimpers. “Join, and I’ll make sure she’s on shift when you’re here.” Seymour’s brain pretty much went on shutdown at that point. Lost to him were the glories of the Foreign Women Swimming Topless In An Aquarium Room, the Naughty Nurses room, and perhaps most importantly, the Cosplay Room Featuring Only Aerith Cosplayers. Poor Seymour. At any rate, though, they finally arrived at the end of the hallway. Before them were a pair of double doors. Almasy gazed at the ornately carved doors quizzically, before Roppongi placed a hand on Seymour’s shoulder. “This,” he said, “is something not seen by most prospective members. This is where the ultimate pleasure is given to those willing to pay the ultimate price. I know you, Seymour…and so I will let you in. What you see may shock you, but it will not be foreign to your eyes.” With that, he opened the door, and Seymour stepped in hesitantly, eyes focused on the large dais in the middle of the room. There, a robed, masked man waited, all of the other spectators in the room focused on the ceiling. “One moment, Mr. Almasy,” Ichiro said, suddenly, “but I must tend to club matters.” Roppongi jogged briskly to the center of the dais, through the crowd of assembled members. “Esteemed members of Club Plaisance,” he screamed, loud voice rising above the din, “once again, one of your own has proven willing to pay the ultimate price for the ultimate pleasure, to sacrifice himself to our goddess! Behold now, as she descends from on high to grace you with her presence! Behold…the Vampiress!” What. The. Hell. Seymour was, to put it mildly, not especially pleased. Especially not as he saw the creature descend from the heights of the room. Even from a distance, he could tell she was beautiful. Deadly, too, or so it seemed by how the man on the dais trembled and shook. Almost as if he was having second thoughts, but a glance up at the descending figure and he steeled himself, ready for the end. Seymour could only watch, horrorstruck, as Roppongi kissed the vampire on the mouth, a kiss that seemed surprisingly loving for a man like Ichiro Roppongi. With that done, he stood down from the dais, joining the crowd in watching as the vampiress embraced her victim, and sunk fangs into his neck. The resultant scream of pleasure echoed through the room, as all watched transfixed. The bitten man writhed this way and that, held fast by the vampire’s deadly kiss. Seymour turned away, unable to look. He knew what that felt like, knew how wonderously pleasurable it could be…but Lia hadn’t killed him then. Hadn’t taken it as far as this situation was sure to go. Seymour cringed as Roppongi returned, the kiss still enduring on the dais. “Well,” Ichiro said, a small smile on his face, “did I not tell you that you knew of what would occur? The Final Fantasy’s eyes closed, then opened. Rage was in them. Forcefully, Seymour dragged the businessman by his coat lapel away from the sickening sight, into a small nook in the wall. “You LET PEOPLE DIE HERE?” Seymour hissed, eyes narrowed into angry slits. Roppongi stammered and sputtered for a few moments, clearly unused to being manhandled. “It is their CHOICE!” he insisted, letting his stare meet Seymour’s. “It is my job as the owner of this club to give the patrons what they want!” Suddenly, something clicked in his head. Something that hadn’t sat right with him just a few moments earlier. “What THEY want?” Almasy asked, incredulously. “I SAW you kissing her! What sort of garbage IS this?” “Why shouldn’t I kiss her?” Ichiro Roppongi fired back, tauntingly. “She’s beautiful!” Most people might have let it go at that. Seymour, though, knew that kiss. Knew the way he held her. Knew the look in his eyes. “You…love her, don’t you,” he asked, sympathy starting to cloud his rage. The businessman swallowed hard. Somehow, no one had ever called him on that… “You know,” he said, his eyes glancing around as the dying man in the background gave his final scream of pleasure, “you’re the first person to notice. Everyone else is too concerned with their own pleasure, their own edification. They all see a vampire as this symbol of sexuality, of strength. But I know different…” Seymour’s brow quirked at the rapidly weakening Asian man. The light sweat breaking out on the man’s forehead indicated many things: worry, pain, sadness. His words were tinged with sorrow as he spoke what he had learned to be the sad truth. “She’s so frail, you know.” “Frail,” Seymour asked, disbelievingly. “Yes,” Roppongi replied. “You and I can go to the store and buy food to sustain ourselves. She cannot. She needs blood…and as you can see, she is in no condition to hunt prey.” Seymour took another glance at her, as she let the now-dead man fall to the ground, licking the last traces of blood from her lips. Now, he saw the cast that peeked out from under her elaborate robes. Now, he understood why her walk was so strange, so erratic. “Won’t that heal though,” Almasy asked the other man. “Broken bones heal.” “It will,” Roppongi said, “but the matter here is how she got it. She’s not as young as she used to be. Not as agile. Not as nimble. She fell attempting to chase down sustenance. So many days she has gone without blood, so many days she has been so very weak…and I can only help so much…” Sweeping aside part of his jacket and dress shirt, Ichiro revealed the tell-tale fang marks on his neck. Marks Almasy knew quite well. “You let her feed from you,” he said, swallowing hard. “I do,” Ichiro responded. “It’s never enough, but it helps get her to the next day.” “I did that for Lia once,” Almasy blurted out, in response. “I found her in the middle of the desert, and she was weak…I tried to get her water but that didn’t help much.” At that, Roppongi chuckled. “No, I tried that in the beginning, back before I knew, of course. As I’m sure you’ve figured out by now…Club Plaisance is essentially in existence to keep her from final death, an occurrence from which she can’t come back. She gave me the idea, but I never thought it would work…I never could have imagined how many people would give themselves to her, willingly. These are politicians. Actors. People with so much to live for…” “And people,” Seymour replied, “that kill themselves anyway. Drug overdoses. Alcohol poisoning. They don’t care. Sometimes they’re depressed.” “If you notice,” came the voice from behind them, as the room emptied, “it’s only one or two a night, Ichiro. Never more. Most of them know better.” The female vampire hobbled towards them both, wincing slightly until she finally leant up against her lover, supporting her weight against him. “Are you okay,” Ichiro asked, and she nodded. “I’ll be fine for a while.” Seymour looked at the couple for a few moments, before turning away. He had a lot of thinking to do now, about a lot of things that he hadn’t even pondered. “If you don’t mind, Mr. Roppongi, I think I’m going to let myself out. I’m sorry, but I don’t think I’ll be joining Club Plaisance. Though…I don‘t know where the Hell the exit is, since you led me down that twisting path here.” Both man and vampire chuckled, and gestured towards the exit, marked in giant, neon letters. Seymour almost smacked himself on the forehead. “Is it there so that a potential victim might change his mind,” he asked, looking at the couple with a slight bit of newfound respect. “No,” the vampire said, shaking her head amusedly. “It’s there because usually people end up here drunk at the end of the night and couldn’t find their way out if we DIDN’T have a tacky neon sign.” -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- Maybe one day, I’ll know what that feels like outside of my dreams. To love someone, and have them return those affections. For now, though, all I can do is walk out of this building in the absolute middle of the city and try to figure out where the Hell I am. Doesn’t help that I’m sort of disgusted with myself. From the beginning, I’ve always been wary of her because of what she is. Because of who she is. And never once did I try to look past the fangs and the stories and the killings. That’s why I ran away three days ago. Because I thought, rather than talk to me like the civilized being she usually is, that she was going to strike me down like an uncivilized savage. No wonder no one’s warmed up to me in Dies Irae; I’ve looked at them like animals. I’m going to step into the ring with a woman I once considered my friend this week in Los Angeles with a chance to become Global Champion once more. Perhaps one day, when this is over, we’ll be on speaking terms again. Maybe I can work up the courage to apologize. Until then, though, there will be no quarter asked. None given. Too much is on the line for each of us. For her, this is her chance to prove to the world that she belongs with the best in PTC. For me, it is my chance to build up a wave of momentum into GTT6 that will guide me to victory. 0-6 is an ugly record. 0-7 would be even worse. But 1-6? Considering what it would mean to me, I’d proclaim it to the heavens.
