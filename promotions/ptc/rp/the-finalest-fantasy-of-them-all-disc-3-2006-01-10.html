The Finalest Fantasy Of Them All: Disc 3

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-10 Making its way into the aptly named ‘Wonder Square’, a deformed stuffed animal paused in its steps upon catching a dull circular glow from its peripheral vision. The tiny neon light seemed to be emanating from inside the Arm Wrestling attraction, which, the stuffed animal knew, wasn’t quite right. Twisting its hefty body to the left, the feet of the creature took but a second to follow in suit, lending a mechanical appearance to its actions. Within a moment of eerie jostling from the beast, it was standing directly in front of the aforementioned game; paw-like appendages on its hips, and a crooked grin on its face. Strange Voice 1: Dammit, get your hands off of my crotch! Strange Voice 2: It’s not my hand! Strange Voice 1: …Oh, dear God, no. Please. Please, just try to get off of me. Strange Voice 2: I have been for the past two hours! This cage is hardly big enough to be used for orphans. Strange Voice 1: Orphans? To hell with it, I don’t need an explanation. Let’s just concentrate on finding a way out of here. Strange Voice 2: Find a way out?! I can’t even find my Mr. Big in here! Strange Voice 1: Your Mr. Big? Don’t tell me you’re already resorting to the prison standard of love. Because, if that’s your idea of passing the time, you can count me out right- Stuffed Animal: Excuse me, sirs. I couldn’t help but notice the two of you were crammed inside one of the games here. Do you want me to try and find a technician or something…? Ass and codpiece alike rammed up against the bars of the game booth, the familiar head of Clyde twisted around from the darkness, eyes glinting having caught the radiant glow from the light outside of their ‘jail cell’. Standing still as a cardboard cut-out, the creature itched the tiny cat on its head before proceeding with the questions. Stuffed Animal: Or would you prefer me to give you a fortune? Strange Voice 1: No, neither! Break the damned thing open and let us out! Stuffed Animal: Oh, no…I couldn’t do that. I work here, so I’m not really allowed to mangle the attractions. Strange Voice 1: To hell with the attractions! If you don’t mangle it, I’ll mangle you! Taking this threat into serious consideration, the creepy monster just shrugged and meandered around to the back of the machine. Pulling out a large megaphone, the beast began to bash the rear of the booth with it, breaking off large chunks of what appeared to be cardboard slabs. Along with every impact of its weapon, James’ skull was rocked explosively, the vomit he recently tasted on his breath beginning to make a reappearance. After a final strike, the back of the machine gave way, spilling the two heroes of the story onto the poorly-tiled floor. Stuffed Animal: Welcome to the Gold Saucer. My name is Cait Sith. Let me read your fortune. Farwell: Er, no. I’d rather not hear of the horrible things that are going to happen to me during the course of this adventure. Ignoring his protests, Cait Sith began rocking from side to side, gaining momentum with each successful step. Clyde’s eyes widened in horror from behind the machine, as he slowly chewed on the end of a half-eaten Mr. Big, whilst keeping his vision locked on the terrible creature before him. Seemingly in its own world now, Cait Sith continued to dance, despite the curious stares from the amusement park’s patrons around him. This was Sith’s time to shine. And shine he would. No more bogus fortunes for him…oh, no. He was moving up in the world. Going places. Places far beyond the reach of mere mortals. Places beyond- Howling wind snuffs the candle’s flame. Genital warts can be cured with salt. Dropping out of his ass like a bank receipt from an ATM, the fortune laid dead on the ground, along with Sith’s aspirations. Defeated, Cait lowered his head, and accepted the laughter that came with each fortune he had ever predicted. He was only doing his job…doing what he loved. Why did he have to be ridiculed for simply following his dreams? He would ponder these questions in the rare instances that he felt sorrowful, but not now. No way. Not when he had just made two new friends. Sith: So, what are your guys’ names anyhow? You must have forgotten how to talk, because you’ve barely said a word since I rescued you. Ha, ha. So, guys? What do you do for a living? What do you do for fun? Are you two partners? Do you sleep tog- Farwell: Shut up and let me speak, you overgrown stuffed rabbit! Sith: Moogle. Farwell: What did you just call me? Sith: Not you, I’m a Moogle. Farwell: I…okay. My name is James, and this here is Clyde. Allowing for a disinterested hand to wave vaguely in Clyde’s direction, James stumbled to his feet, pulling the downed Clyder-Man up by his unoccupied hand, just before realizing they had both been stripped down to their underwear. Their novelty underwear at that. James moved a quick hand to cover his tuxedo underpants from the public, the red, silver and black suit pattern on his underwear bringing him even more embarrassment than the foul stench that he had only recently endured. And then there was Clyde, standing proudly as mothers covered their childrens’ eyes, and nuns wept while praying to the Holy Father, his glowing codpiece shone with an unequivocal brilliance, putting even the neon signs around him to shame. Walkins: I’m Clyde Walkins, bitch! And my codpiece shines with the intensity of a thousand suns! Behold and revel! Farwell: Shut up, you fool. We’re each some kind of fugitive now. Don’t go around begging for attention like usual. Walkins: Clyde don’t beg for attention. He just happens to have it bestowed upon him by his adoring fans. Farwell: Stop talking in the third person, you imbecile, and get moving. We can escape the way we came in. Walkins: Speaking of which…where did we enter, anyways? Farwell: How should I know? I was…I wasn’t paying attention when it happened. Walkins: As I recall, you weren’t even around when it happened. All I can remember is a lot of pain, and a lot of stink. Farwell: Anyways, let’s just get out of here right now. Sith: Uhm. Excuse me, you guys. I know the way out. I can show you the way to go, but you’ll have to have both of your fortunes read afterwards. And you’ll have to let me come along with you so I can read more appreciative peoples’ fortunes. Farwell: No, no. I am sure we can manage on our own. Get going, Clyde. I can hold him off for as long as you need to find the exit. Walkins: I’m sorry, James. But, he’s Cait Sith. He’s the only one who can retrieve the Black Materia from deep within the Temple Of The Ancients. Dying for the prolonging of humanity, of course. Without the tear-inducing video and musical score that went along with Aeris’ demise, however. James simply turned away from Clyde halfway through his rambling, assuming that the idiot had lost the rest of his marbles after having been locked up in a smelly cage for several hours. After all, Clyde didn’t usually predict the future with any semblance of coherency in his sentences. Wait, how was that different from now? Either way, James just shook the spooky chill that kept creeping up his spine with every prophetical word that Clyde uttered, and wandered down the stairs that led to a variety of tunnels. Only to be pursued by the retard, and his new pet or whatever. Farwell: Ditch that damned thing, and help me find the way out of here. All I can see are a bunch of tunnels, and some poorly textured signs hanging above them. Hell, you probably have to buy some bizarre 3-D glasses or something to read them properly. Walkins & Sith in unison: Take the second one on the left. Farwell: No. I don’t trust you. It’s you who got us captured in the first place. And the fat thing. I don’t trust it, either. It looks shady to me. You know what? I’m going to do the exact opposite, and take the second one on the right. That’ll show you. Leaping into the cavernous mouth of the chosen tunnel, James’ stomach was nearly pulled inside out as he was shot directly vertical from a new opening, and onto the floor of the Battle Square. James began to scramble to his feet, but was impeded by Clyde and Cait Sith landing directly atop his spine. Farwell: Get the hell off of me! I heard something snap, and I’m only hoping it was one of your necks. Walkins: Nope, I feel perfectly fine. You make a swell cushion, James. Sith: I have to agree. Not everyone would be able to support my four hundred plus pound body like you do. Wriggling out from under either retard, James climbed to a standing position and stretched his back, aiming to pop one of his vertebrae back into it’s proper place. Failing at his task, James just walked forward with a slight hunch, both of his accomplices in tow. Farwell: Irreparably damaged back or not, we have to get out of here. We’re right back to where we got caught, and I’m not in the mood to be hunted down by a gang of beanbag gun-toting rowdies like before. Walkins: No, James. I can win the battles again. And then I can buy myself the Ultimate Weapon. And we won’t need to run anymore. Because I’ll slaughter everyone who looks at me cock-eyed. Farwell: The moment you said ‘Ultimate Weapon’ I sort of zoned out. No need to repeat the end of that phrase, I think I’ll be able to continue living without the mental image you were attempting to provide me with. Sith: Hey, guys. I have a great idea. Let’s gather ‘round, and tell fortunes. Oh! Me first! Spinning on his heels, James turned and walked directly back into the Battle Square, with Clyde easing away from Cait Sith, who had already begun the terrible ‘Dance of the Fortunes’ as Clyde so observantly named it. Now standing in front of the counter, James menacingly leered over the top of it, right at the secretary that didn’t even seem to remember them. Farwell: Give us our clothes back. We’ve been walking around half-naked with a giant stuffed bear or some shit following us. We would, at the very least, appreciate having the clothes you stole from us back. Walkins: And I want to enter the tournament~! Farwell: Oh, God… Secretary: Well, we only take GP here. So, unless you have some GP, we won’t be able to allow you to participate in the battles. Farwell: You took our clothes. Walkins: Yeah! That’ll be our collateral this time! You keep our clothes if I lose, and we get them back if we win…as well as all the BP we can handle. James could only try to persuade himself to disbelieve what he was hearing, but to no avail. Clyde really was stupid enough to barter his way into a fight using the clothes off of their backs. Was there no limit to how moronic he could get? And apparently there wasn’t, as Clyde’s next vocalization nearly knocked James off of his feet. Walkins: And just to prove how awesome I am, I want to fight the monsters without my super-suit, and without any weapons. Then James won’t be able to blame all of my success on some zany gimmick when all is said and done. Farwell: Clyde, you barely have a chance at winning when wearing the suit. Don’t do this to me. I’ll respect you or whatever the hell you want. Wear the suit, win, and I will be your slave for a month. Just don’t try to do something foolish all for the sake of my admiration. Walkins: Don’t say another word, James, I have this one in the bag. A seven match winning streak? The PRIME Universal Title? What could possibly go wrong? Fast forward to Clyde being mauled by some form of Chimaera or another, while a Cobra watched on, readying its Attack-O-Meter or whatever it is that made them function. Walkins: I…won’t let you do me like this. I’m…I’m Clyde Walkins, bitch! Clyde randomly threw an awkward punch into the air, and the Chimaera released a high pitched whine, before curling up on the ground, favoring its testicles. Clyde had done it. He had punched a mythological creature square in the baby-maker. Perhaps he wasn’t just blowing smoke when he told James he had this one in the bag. Capitalizing on the downed beast, Clyde wrangled the Cobra by the neck, and screamed out "I summon the almighty Cobra-God to do away with the injustice of this monster’s tyranny~!" In a less than climactic fashion, Clyde hurled the snake at the Chimaera, the serpent doing no more than wriggling futilely in the air before landing on the Chimaera’s head. And then it happened. The snake bit the monster directly in the eye, playing right into Clyde’s hand. With both of his attackers occupied with one another, Clyde would finish them off via the grandest scheme to ever be plotted! He stomped on the snake’s head. Back in the lobby, and with his BP-O-Meter now off the charts, Clyde felt the urgent need to urinate on something. And quickly. With the ever-looming threat of having his BP revoked for creating a lewd scene in the middle of the Battle Square, Clyde vowed to return and disappeared into one of the holes that had a sign dangling over it, the only discernable letters being " t ooms". James raised an eyebrow, fearing that Clyde may have just taken off to drain the lizard on a severely misspelled grave, but shrugged. They probably deserve it anyhow, if they’re buried in a freakish place like this. Turning to Cait Sith, James piqued an eyebrow once again, constantly bombarded by some kind of twisted sight wherever he looked. And this time would be no different. There was Cait Sith, his face only inches from Farwell’s, the expression plastered across its features being one of mischievous malevolence. Farwell: What did you do, and why are looking as if you want to French me? Sith: Nothing. And why would I want to surrender to you? I don’t understand. Farwell: Never mind. Just get away from me. Actually, you know what? We now have a rule. Cat Sid doesn’t come within four feet of James Farwell at any given time. That includes right now. Sith: It’s Cait Sith. And your feet or mine? Because, as you can see, mine are far more magnificently endowed than yours. James just turned back away, hoping to ignore the burdensome fat-ass that got its only kicks in life via a thin piece of paper sliding free of its butt and wafting onto the floor. Sith: So, how old are you? I’m guessing somewhere around sixty-nine. Am I close? Farwell: Not as close as my fist is going to be to your head if you don’t stop talking. With that, Cait Sith silenced himself, and began to glance around the room, his eyes not picking anything particular to rest upon. Just as James was feeling restless, the monotony was shattered as Clyde dove out of the hole, and rolled back into the Battle Square, looking refreshed and overjoyed that James didn’t just make a break for it during his absence. Farwell: Take long enough? You left me to rot with this thing constantly jabbering on about feet, and 69, and surrendering…I don’t know. Clyde stared at his partner for a second before reacting, not often one to catch onto a sexual reference. Walkins: So, that means…you two are gay for each other? Farwell: What the hell?! How did you gather that from what I said? Forget it. Just get our clothes, buy your ‘ultimate weapon’ and let’s get out of here. I’ve had about all I can take of loud carnival music, deformed robots, and fights with monsters. Taking a quick peek at his BP-O-Meter to see just how much BP he had acquired from his insane ‘nudie-fight’, his moment of overwhelming happiness came to an abrupt end. Walkins: No! This can’t be! You can’t do this to me! I will skull-fuck you! Speaking to no one in particular, Clyde ripped the BP-O-Meter from his codpiece’s support strap, and whipped it against the wall, as the LCD slid to a halt at James’ feet. Looking down at it, James could make out the words ‘BP Expired’ in an irregular digital clock font, and stared at Clyde. Farwell: How in the hell did that happen? Did you pee on it? Walkins: No. Farwell: Then, what happened?! You’re not telling me something! Walkins: Nothing happened, okay? It just does that sometimes. Farwell: Cough it up, you idiot. There’s no way in hell it just conks out and eats your…BP. Walkins: Okay, you know what? I left the Battle Square. Then my BP expired. Are you happy now? I’m obviously not as 1337 as I make myself out to be if I forget something like that! You win, James! You win… Farwell sighed and draped an arm around Clyde’s shoulder. Farwell: It’s fine. The thought of you having a weapon worthy of the prefix ‘Ultimate’ worried me to begin with. At least we can still pick up our clothes, right? Walkins: I suppose. We had better head over there and get that done, then. Both men now standing erect made their way over to the counter, and leaned diligently on it. About to open his mouth to request his clothes back, James was grabbed by a large man, whose belt had the name ‘Dio’ engraved on the front of it. Dio: You the ones that killed my janitor and made a ruckus up here a few days ago? Farwell: Days?! I though we were only out for a few hours. Dio: Naw, pops. I made sure they sedated you with about fifty tranquilizers. Get you nice and relaxed, ya know? Farwell: Tranquilizers? That’s what I’ve been craving all day? Dio: Yep, probably. They have a tendency to get you hooked on them after a few uses. But, that’s just their plan. They get you nice and addicted, and then they hike up the price on ol’ Dio. Dio needs his fix like everyone else! Walkins: If I may interject, fellows. I think I can hook you up, big guy. What say you let us go, and we’ll forget this ever happened? Then I can contact my main man, and we’ll light you up in no time at all. Dio: Naw, kid. One: You don’t have any contacts. Two: You are both going into the pit. Good luck down in the Corel Prison. With that, Dio tossed both men into the Battle Arena, then quickly released the floor, dropping them both into the Corel Prison, where only the roughest, toughest…mischief makers?…spent the rest of their days in squalid conditions. Landing in the most brutal manner possible, both Clyde and James got to their feet with the greatest of trouble, only to be quashed by the entirely-too-heavy Cait Sith. Dull moans penetrating the air around Sith’s anus, he slowly backed off of the two, and shook the cobwebs out of his head. Sith: So, you guys, know where we are now? Farwell: I don’t care. I just don’t care anymore. I hope we all die. Walkins: We’re in Corel Prison! Just like in my fantasies! An unnerving glance or two, and Clyde rephrased his statement. Walkins: We’re in Corel Prison! Just like in Final Fantasy. Clyde looked around eagerly, almost as if he expected applause, but was greeted with no more than an awkward silence. Walkins: We’re in Corel Prison! Just as Cait Sith probably said. Accepting the latest declaration, the trio advanced forward, wary of the insane criminals that could be lurking anywhere around them. Sith: Watch out. Everyone down here has performed some kind of crime or another against the Gold Saucer. Be it vandalism, or just good natured ribbing that was taken the wrong way. Each of them an evil cesspool of bad intentions. Stay on your guard. James merely shrugged off the warning and approached a trio not unlike his, although not quite similar. Farwell: Erm, do you three know how to get out of here? First Man: You can’t get out of here. There’s no way out. Second Man: You don’t go West. Anywhere but West and you’ll be free. Third Man: Go East and you won’t be attacked by a wild horde of Cactuers. Farwell: Er…okay. I suppose I’ll just go East, then. Walkins: No, James. That’s just what they want. This particular group only tells lies. You have to do the opposite of what they say. The opposite. Farwell, having quit listening to Clyde long ago, simply went East, and out a dilapidated gate until he was walking around a barren desert. Grumbling, and dehydrated, James and Company marched along until they were greeted with the unwelcome swirling, and dizzying colors that were once only products of acid trips. Farwell: What the hell is happening to me? Am I dying? Walkins: Even better. It’s another battle! Cait Sith, Clyde, and James were all lined up appropriately for the fight, whilst three bobbing cacti were in formation across from them. Farwell: What in the name of Hell is happening here? Do we really have to fight three Saguaro Cactuses? What kind of maniac thought of this?! Before James could wrap his mind around the whole prospect of fighting Cacti, the words 1,000 Needles popped up in front of him like some freakish children’s book. Just as he was about to question his misfortune, a barrage of needles assaulted his legs and abdomen, shredding the number 1000 from inside his body. Feeling faint, and presumably with a yellow health bar, James doubled over, and clutched his stomach as through it were threatening to fall out. He would remain in this position for the rest of the battle, whether he liked it or not. Now was Clyde’s time to shine. Running up to the Cactus on the far left side of the battle field, he clubbed it in the head-like appendage with a loose rock he happened to pick up while James was taking the ass-kicking of a lifetime. No numbers exited from the Cactuer, but it simply crumpled to the ground in a heap, as dead as the bird Clyde decided to feed to James the day prior to receiving the beating of his life once upon a time long ago. Clyde readied the cactus juice-laden rock for another onslaught directed toward the remaining two Cacteur, but was pre-empted by their hasty retreat, as Cait Sith did absolutely nothing the whole battle. But he still celebrated in grand style, as if he were the determining factor between life and death. Good for him. The trio’s vision faded to black, as they were once again returned to a boundless desert, faced with the only option of a long trek back to the Prison. But a long trek it would not be, for as soon as they turned around, the Prison lay expansively ahead of them, not but a few feet ahead. Whatever caused the anomaly, it was warmly received by James, as he dashed back into the Prison, and drank from a nearly dry well…with a skeleton in it. Farwell: As disgusting as the thought of drinking skeletal water is, it’s preferable to actually being the skeleton in the water. Wouldn’t you agree, Clyde? Clyde? James took a quick survey of the area around him, only to be met with the sight of absolutely no one around him. Getting up to his feet once more, James walked deeper into Corel Prison, being careful not to step on the various dead animals along the way. Although, however determined his intent, he just couldn’t avoid treading atop a few of them. Thank God for the hot sun, or else he might have a nasty little surprise sticking to his foot for the rest of the journey. Coming upon a half-ruined trailer, James hauled himself through the doorway, and onto a shoddily crafted rug…right along with his two partners. Farwell: Clyde! Where the hell did you go? Walkins: I took off after the battle. Cait Sith here told me where to go. He’s awfully smart if you give him a chance. Farwell: No, he’s not. And I found my way here without ever having been to this place. So, I’m clearly much smarter than that thing is. Motioning a hand at Cait Sith, James withdrew it as a man stepped into the trailer. Man: Hello. My name is Mr. Coates. I presume you are all here on account of wanting to be free of this hellhole? Er…place. Farwell: Damn right. How do we get out of here? Sith: If you gave me the chance to speak, I would have told you what we have to do already. We have to race Chocobos. Farwell: Okay. I gave you a chance to explain what was going on here, and you make mention of chickens or some stupid shit. There, you went and blew it for yourself. Satisfied now? Mr. Coates: Please. If you’ll both calm down, I’ll brief you on what you must do to earn your freedom. Now, you’ve all probably heard of the Chocobo races, correct? Well, what I need is for one of you to go up into the Gold Saucer, and ride one of the Chocobos to victory. You do that, and I’ll allow for you all to go free. Do we have a deal? I’ll ride him until his rump swells with pain, and his haunches can take no more of my pounding down on top of him throughout the event. And I don’t want to ride some hell beast, or have intercourse with it, or whatever your ambiguous suggestions refer to. Mr. Coates: Riight. Mr. Coates: Change of plans. The little homosexual kid is going to ride the Chocobo. We can’t have Old Man River slipping a disc, or breaking his hip, now can we? All right then, Clyde. I’ll send you up the elevator. Ester’ll fill you in on the details. Now standing in front of the tall elevator, Cait Sith was in the process of seeing Clyde off, a tearful goodbye, and a hardy handshake. That lasted fifteen minutes. Sith: I’ll miss you, Clyde. You were the best friend I’ve ever had! Walkins: I’ll be back. I guarantee it! With the Clydemus seal of approval! So, James, are you gonna wish me luck? Farwell: Screw the race, just fight your way out. And then come and get us. But, make sure to pick up our suits. Punch Ester or whatever in the head and make your escape. I’m counting on you. Walkins: Ha, ha. You’re hysterical, James. But, don’t worry. I promise I’ll win the Chocobo Death Race. I chose a chain as my weapon! Farwell: Death Race? They said it was just an ordinary race. Forget it, I’ll consider it a benefit to have you killed in the middle of an ostrich riding competition. Luck to you. Smiling in his old foolish way, Clyde turned around, and marched into the elevator, soon to disappear behind the monstrous chute’s giant doors. Walkins: SEE YOU SOON! Unbeknownst to him, however, the words never left the confines of the elevator he was in. He simply injured the ears of the woman standing next to him, ready to brief him on what he must do in order to win the race, and along with it a brand new travel buggy. Cherry red, of course. Clyde would accept no less. Now situated on top of a brightly colored Chocobo, Clyde leaned forward, whispering a few words in its ear. Walkins: Now, Green Thunder, what we have to do is…we have to get close enough to the other Chocobos to whip them with my chain. I have it all plotted out, all you need to do is approach them from the side. I’m depending on you. The Chocobo just tipped its head, as if pondering the grim scenario he was about to witness. He had a madman on his back. With a chain. If he didn’t do what the man said, he’d probably substitute for the other racers, and end up a bloodied mess in the middle of the track. He’d do what he had to do. And do it he did! Closing in on the flanks of the nearest Chocobo, Clyde lashed out a crumpled chain, the metallic ring of steel on bird skull sounded loudly in the closed circuit. Down went the first opponent, a contorted heap in the middle of the track. Several racers would attempt to guide their Chocobos around the mess, but fate was against them. A pile-up of four Chocobos was the end result of Clyde’s first attack, as he steadily approached the next competitor. Chocobo Racer: Please, no! I’ll stop, I’ll do anything! I have a family! A shrill cry of agony, and a tug on the reins was enough to bring the Chocobo down, racer and all. Clyde was ensuring victory for himself. The final opponent. A racer named Joe. Look at him, riding high atop his majestic Black Chocobo. Clyde was instantly jealous, and infuriated that he was forced to ride a lowly Green Chocobo. He would not stand for it. Although he would stand…just not for it. Climbing to his feet on top of the fast moving Chocobo, Clyde lunged at Joe, the chain coiling over itself like some disfigured metal snake. You dare use your telekinetic powers in an attempt to dissuade me of my goal? Ha, you’ll have to do better than that. Joe: What the hell are you talking about? You hit yourself with that chain. I didn’t do anything. I’m only trying to earn a living! Not hearing the man’s excuse, or simply not caring, Clyde brought the chain down once more, crumpling the hat adorning Joe’s cranium. Blood escaped from under the hat, whilst Joe hung limply from the side of the Chocobo, seemingly dead. Or catatonic. Clyde would not settle for the latter. Heaving his body from Green Chocobo to Black Chocobo, Clyde made himself home in the saddle, a stray leg kicking Joe free of his mount and into the threshing feet below. And bid’ness had now been fulfilled. Ester: How could you kill the whole roster?! We’re going to have to buy more Chocobos, and find more racers to ride them! How could you do this to me? We’re probably going to have to close this attraction now, and it’s all because of you! Walkins: Now, now, adoring fan, no need to thank me. I’m just doing my job. But, if you’ll excuse me, I believe I have a buggy waiting for me. Ester: No! No, you don’t. I made sure Dio knew about this, and you’re going back into the Prison. I hope you die down there! Walkins: Oh, no. James was right. I have to punch you. Ester: Excuse me? What was- Cut short by the impact of Clyde’s fist in her face, Ester laid motionless on the ground, obviously not used to taking such a formidable punch from a champ. Clyde merely brushed his hands together and made off for the Battle Square, where he quickly re-obtained his and James’ suit, and then it was off on the gondola for him. The exit that James could never find. - Red-eyed and giggling like madmen, James and Cait Sith sat on a crappy old couch, an apparent pipe resting on the table before them. Farwell: So, Cat, what’s with the whole fortune thing, anyways? Sith: What fortune? Farwell: Ya know…that whole thing you do with crapping out a slip of paper. Sith: Oh…OH! You mean the fortunes! Yeah, it’s my job. Farwell: No, but I mean…how? Sith: Well, I work it out using my dance, and then I read it to my audience. Farwell: Oh. I think it’s gross. Going back for another hit of the pipe, James is interrupted by the roar of what sounds like a boat’s motor, followed by the unmistakable sound of tires grinding someone’s head into the ground. Farwell: What the hell is that? Sith: I think…that we should investigate. What about you, Scooby? Seemingly a product of Cait’s imagination, James only narrowed his eyes at the air in front of Sith, before heading out the door and into the open. But, what his eyes beheld was a sight eerier than watching an old man amble back and forth down a dark alleyway. About seven times eerier. A massive six-wheeled car-like thing was staring him down, Clyde’s enormous face decorating the grill. A single wheel perched upon Mr. Coates’ head, while Clyde opened his door and stepped out. Walkins: Well, hello there, chums. Did you miss me? And why are your eyes so red? Have you been crying? Farwell: No, we didn’t miss you. And my eyes are red because we found some green herbs behind the house here and decided to-er, well, yeah. We were crying. Only crying. Dropping the pipe in the dirt beside him, James hurried to the safety of the ‘Land Rover’ that Clyde so expertly ripped off. Pulling himself into the passenger seat, James hurriedly threw on his outfit that was conveniently laid out for him, and buckled his seatbelt, unsure as to what might happen with Clyde behind the wheel. And that’s when he looked back. Sitting directly behind him was the horrendous stuffed animal that he had hoped he ditched. Dancing, and singing in a slow, melodic tune, the beast rocked back and forth, occasionally touching James’ shoulder with its paw. Farwell: Clyde! Get in here so we can leave! And does this thing really have to come with us? Walkins: Hold on, I’m just polishing myself real quickly. James only quieted himself, hoping Clyde wasn’t referring to what he thought he might be. Walkins: Alright, there we go. All done. Still a little wet, but it’ll dry off during the ride. James opted to keep his mouth shut, and not question the creepy little tidbits of information that continuously showed up when Clyde was around. Walkins: Yep, I shined her up nice and good. Girls are going to swoon when they see this massive, rock-solid- Farwell: ENOUGH! Walkins: Portrait of myself on the front of the Rover? Clyde didn’t quite understand the reasoning behind James’ outburst, but it was likely some form of jealousy over the fact that Clyde managed to get a giant bust of himself made up in the gift shop, and affixed to the front of his new Buggy. Either way, he decided it was best to just get on with their adventure, and put this whole episode behind them. Clyde made a 180°, and drove out of Corel Prison, back onto the headache-inducing fields of ‘the in-between’ as Clyde called it. Although they weren’t melding into one, as before, James couldn’t help but shake the feeling that he was being drawn towards Clyde, just very minutely. Fearing a replay of last time, James huddled against his own door, trying to keep as much distance between him and Clyde as possible. He didn’t feel the need to stink like a dead seal for a week. And then there was Cait Sith. Singing merrily and grinning like the Cheshire Cat behind the both of them, tweaking every last nerve within James’ body. Apparently he was here to stay. Perfect. Another annoying idiot for James to contend with.
