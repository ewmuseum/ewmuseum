GOP: The College Years! (part 1 of many, I hopes)

20 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-20 The sun was shining bright on William Howard Taft High School's football stadium that Saturday morning. The sky was, clear, blue and limitless, much like the futures of all the seniors in their caps and gowns. Graduation day, all two-hundred fifty-six seniors who'd worked their asses off for four years to get to this day, and finally it was here. So much excitement in the air, but it was nervous for one teenager-turning-adult. Gordon Powell was the class' valedictorian. He adjusted his cap and stepped up to the podium. "Hello, just to let you know, I'm horrible at speeches." That was a lie, by the way. Gordon was a gifted public speaker, surprisingly gifted since he excelled not in English and liberal arts, but in science. In fact, Gordon was going to the University of Texas on scholarship for chemistry. Growing up, his love for wrestling was the only thing that trumped his love for science. Sure, he was a huge fan of President Reagan, but that was because he was the man who stood up to Communism. Not because he was a terribly political person. Chemistry and wrestling. Second grade year, he got a chemistry set and a Hulk Hogan wrestling buddy for Christmas. That was the best Christmas he could remember. But I digress. "Honey, that was a wonderful speech," Gordon's mother told him afterwards. "Yeah, good speech, kid." "Dude, you were nodding off for half of it." "That man is not a dude, Gordon. He's your father." "No he's not, Mom. He's my third step-father in five years. Dad's living in Boca with his Aunt Stella." "I'm not hearing this." Man, Gordon thought, I can't wait to get to college. In August of that year, he finally did. The University of Texas, settled in the heart of Austin, would be Gordon's home for the next few years. He couldn't wait, living on his own, taking classes in a subject he was passionate about. Now, if only his dorm room got USA and TNT, he'd be set. When he found out that it did, he decided; college was heaven. However, living on campus meant that he had to share his heaven with someone else. If television and the movies had taught him anything, he'd end up clashing with his roommate. Gordon had prepared in his mind to battle with the then-faceless person with whom he'd be rooming, although he was hoping to bunk with someone agreeable. Nine months is a long time. Just ask Mrs. Powell; Gordon weighed 11 pounds and 8 ounces upon birth, a veritable boulder she had to carry around in her uterus for that amount of time. Gordon was among the first to arrive at the dorms the morning of move-in. He shuffled all his boxes and luggage up into the elevators and speedily lugged them into his room. Just as quickly, he unpacked all his stuff and was settled in his room. In two hours, he was done completely. He chose the right side of the room to set up at. For what reason, he didn't know. He just gravitated towards there. He got everything set up how he wanted it. Periodic table and Hulk Hogan poster on the wall, Hogan wrestling buddy on his bed, desk adorned with miniature statuettes of the Ultimate Warrior, Joe Montana and a scaled down thinking man. After admiring his work, he put on a Ted Nugent CD in his stereo system, grabbed one of his paperbacks, Jurassic Park to be exact, plopped on his bed and got lost in his book. The time melted away like butter in a microwave. In what seemed like an instant, ten hours passed. The only reason Gordon knew that much time had passed was the startling he got from the door crashing open . He looked at the clock and saw it read 8 PM; he was finished packing at 10 in the morning. It looked like Gordon's roommate had finally arrived. He walked into the room, hair down to his shoulders, jeans ripped at the knees, "Kurt Cobain: 1967-1994" tee-shirt on his upper body and ragged Converse All-Stars soling his feet. "Jesus man…" Gordon was hyperventilating a little bit from the startling. "Dude, you okay?" "Yeah, I'm fine… I was just reading, kinda lost track of time." "You been here the whole time?" "Yeah…" "Damn, well, y'know what, I'm sorry, I didn't introduce myself. I'm Jerry O'Quinn, I'm your roommate." Jerry extended his hand. "Gordon Powell." Gordon extended his. The two roommates shook hands. "Man though, totally blows my mind you've been up here the whole time reading instead of going out and meeting people." Gordon fidgeted a bit before he answered. He'd never really had all that many friends in high school, but he had enough to get by. He and his clique for the most part had been together since kindergarten with a few additions and subtractions. He'd heard college was different though, that there weren't so many people looking to make fun of you for what you liked and that people were more accepting. He wanted to get a jump on things, but his book took him off that, at least for the first day. "Well, I was going to, but I… I just lost track of time." "Oh man, well, that's alright. I'm goin' to hang out with some folks I met when I got here. Fact, they were the reason I didn't get up here til now, man. Some really cool dudes and some hot chicks too. You should come with me to hang out with them tonight." "Sure… yeah." "Alright man, let's go." Jerry dropped his bags. "Aren't you gonna unpack?" "Naw… I'll do that tomorrow morning." Jerry left and Gordon followed soon after. From their third floor room in their dormitory, they went to the common area right in front of Jester Hall, the largest freshman dorm at U of T. There they met up with the kids Jerry was talking to before he went upstairs. There was Garth and Mikey, two kids from Dallas who liked punk rock and hockey. Jennie Mae was a cheerleader in high school, but she broke her leg last semester of senior year in high school and lost the passion for it. She was into arty movies though. Then there was Jack, who was on scholarship to play football, and his girlfriend Leanne. Jack wasn't your typical asshole jock though; he was the punter. "Hey Jerry," Mikey called out as he saw Jerry and Gordon. "Who y'all got with ya there?" "It's my roommate Gordon." "Cool man, I'm Mikey…" One by one, everyone introduced themselves. Gordon was a bit unsure at first, but after a little goading, he was a little looser. "So yeah," Jerry said, "I got a nick bag of some killer bud that I got from home." "Cool man," said Garth. Everyone agreed except Gordon, who was a bit apprehensive at the mention of partaking in the consumption of a controlled substance. "I… I.. don't know… can't that stuff get us… kicked out?" "Don't be so dang paranoid," said Jennie Mae. "We ain't gonna get caught. Plus, it'll loosen ya up a bit. You seem a bit tense." Begrudgingly, Gordon nodded. They all went to a secluded alleyway, rolled their joints and sparked up. Gordon apprehensively took his first puff, not knowing what to expect. Then, an extreme state of calm came over him, a calmness that was reinforced with every next puff. He felt something come over him, a feeling that he'd never felt before. It wasn't highness; first time smokers rarely ever experience a high unless they smoke a lot. It was different, a feeling of acceptance. For the first time since kindergarten, he made some new friends, the people who didn't care about his fandom of wrestling or anything else they didn't approve of in him. That feeling alone got him to loosen up. Loosening up, they got him to talk with them about everything. Sports, punk rock, movies and eventually, politics. "Yeah man, what these Republicans are doing to Clinton, nothing but a witch hunt," said Jerry. "It pisses me off, man," said Jack, "All the folks back home are sayin' he should go down, an' why? Cuz he copped some head offa an intern? He ain't done nothin' wrong." "Yeah man, yeah… hey Gordon, what do you think about The President?" asked Mikey. "Well, uh… I think he's done a good job. I mean, who cares about anything else. He's helped the economy 'n stuff." "Yeah, that's what I'm talkin' about. Man, you really get it," said Jerry. Gordon nodded and smiled. Jerry wasn't finished talking though. "Y'know, I'm going to go sign up for the Campus Democrats tomorrow. You should come along too. You'd fit right in." Gordon felt a little knot in his stomach, but after a moment's hesistation, he agreed. "Awesome man. Everyone should be as forward thinking as you." Gordon nodded uneasily for a moment, but then he noticed Mikey rolling another joint. He eagerly waited for it to come to him to calm his nerves. The next week, classes started, and boy did they start for Gordon. He took a whopping six courses when most freshmen only elected to take five. He loaded up on chemistry courses, not only taking Chem II (having tested out of Chem I due to his 4 on the AP Chemistry exam), but Organic Chemistry I and the advanced Chemistry Lab that only sophomores and juniors took. The other three classes were Calculus I, English I and Ancient World History. The advanced Lab was a class he had to beg the dean to get into. The dean let him in under one condition; that he gave it the due time that it deserved. Gordon promised he wouldn't slack, and the dean made sure he wouldn't. He put a rider in his okaying of Gordon taking the class in that if he ever got a D in a lab, he'd have to drop the course. After his first day of classes, Gordon was feeling invigorated. Organic chemistry always excited him, something about polymeric chains… it was almost kinky. He flittered home with his books in his arms, not being able to wait to dive into them when suddenly, he heard a voice from behind. "Hey, where are you going?" Gordon turned around and saw Jerry running behind him. "Wait up man, did you forget?" "Forget what?" "Campus Democrats! Remember? We signed up last week." "Oh… oh yeah, I did forget. I…" "Well, you're lucky that I reminded you. C'mon, let's go." Before Gordon could make any protestations, Jerry grabbed his forearm and pulled him off. The Democrats met in the main lecture hall, between the last day class getting out and the last night class coming into session. The Dems were among the most highly populated groups on campus, but you knew that. Even in Texas, the colleges were havens for the leftists. The hall was buzzing, all the new faces and returnees milling about, getting to know each other, unaware that a stately man with shaggy dirty blonde hair and a scruffy beard was heading up to the front table. They didn't pay attention until he rapped his gavel on the table. "Alright, alright, welcome to the first meeting of the University of Texas Campus Democrats. Fer all y'all new faces, I'm Brent Cowart, the chairman of this here club." Brent was a charismatic native Texan, a guy who's father was one of his town's most notable Republicans. He started out in the Democrats as a form of rebellion, but he had since bought into the party line. "Man, ain't he the best?" Jerry asked Gordon as Brent spoke. "Yeah… yeah." Gordon was just humoring Jerry at this point. "He… um… reminds me of a young Clinton." "Exactly!" The meeting wasn't as fun for Gordon as he thought it would when he was smoking weed with his newfound friends that night. It dragged and dragged. He wished he was back at the dorm, reading his chemistry books. "…And finally before we adjourn…" Yes!, Gordon thought. "Most clubs have mixers or dances to get to know each other. We here at the Campus Democrats do something different. We feel that social activism is the best way to bring people together, so that's why Wednesday night, we're going to organize a protest in front of Representative Tex Schroder's office. As you know, Mr. Schroder is an ardent anti-choice politician, and we're going to protest his stodgy views against a woman's right to choose. Everyone's welcome to come, and afterwards, we'll all go to the diner, treat of the club." There was a healthy buzz as the meeting let out. Jerry was in especially high spirits as he walked with Gordon back to their dorm. "Man, I'm so stoked. This is going to be awesome. I can't wait to go, man. What about you?" "Oh… I can't go." Jerry was flabbergasted. "What? Why not man?" "You know I got that lab early on Thursdays. I have a lot riding on that, man." "But college is about meeting people and shaking the tree, man. Shaking the tree." "It's all about learning too. I mean, I like being in the Democrats and all, but I also love chemistry too. I can mix them both." Jerry rolled his eyes out of Gordon's sight and said, "Alright man, but you can't cop all the time." "…besides, why call him 'anti-choice' anyway? Why not call him pro-life?" Jerry's eyes widened. "Man, I'm glad I'm your roommate so I can educate you, man. Y'see, when they call themselves 'pro-life,' it's like saying that we're anti-life, man. That ain't cool. That's pigeonholing us as evil people." "But… then we're pigeonholing them by saying they're against choice." "But they are." "Then aren't we against life, techincally?" "No man! No! We aren't against life. Babies aren't alive until after they're born anyway. Scientific fact." Now it was Gordon's turn to roll his eyes out of Jerry's view. "Well, what I'm trying to say is… why can't we just refer to each other as 'pro-abortion' or 'anti-abortion?'" Jerry shook his head. "That's just how it is man. That's just how it is." Gordon shook his head too. The roommates walked back to their dorm room, quickly changing the subject. The next morning was the big morning, Gordon's first day in Advanced Chemistry Lab. He was so excited that he was the first one in the lab. Other students came in and looked at Gordon funny. To them, a student who was eager to be in a chemistry lab at 8 AM was a weirdo. The last person to enter was a stodgy-looking, bald-headed man clad in a lab coat and glasses. "Good morning class," he said in a voice as un-Texasy as you could get. He sounded more like a Connecticut blueblood. "I am Dr. Ledgely, and I'll be conducting this lab. I have Bachelor's from RPI, my Masters from Harvard and my PhD from MIT. I've been all around the world, and I've seen some of the best chemists at work. Therefore, I shall expect the best from you. That means you show up, bright-eyed and bushy-tailed for this lab. If you come into my class hungover, you will get a failing grade for that day's lab. If you come in sleepy, you'll fail that day's lab. If you miss a lab, it's a failure, you cannot make it up. And to make sure you're all here, I will take roll. Atkins…" Dr. Ledgely went through all the names alphabetically. As he got to the Ps, the butterflies in Gordon's stomach fluttered with enough intensity to rend Bangladesh asunder with typhoon after typhoon. "Powell…" Gordon eagerly responded, "Here!" "Hrm… Powell… oh that's right, you're the freshman. The Dean told me to keep an eye on you. You had better not screw up, freshman." "I promise sir, I won't." And screw up he didn't. Gordon aced the first day's lab, finishing it before everyone else and to the most thorough completion as well. While it didn't help his standing as class weirdo with his labmates, Dr. Ledgely was mildly impressed. "Good work, freshman. Maybe the Dean didn't err after all," he said as Gordon was about to leave the lab. The words just rolled right off Gordon's back though; he was in love with chemistry. That next Monday, Gordon made it a point to stay in the library long after his classes were over. He didn't want to attend the Campus Democrats meeting, and he surely didn't want Jerry seeing him walk back to the dorm again. The Democrats rubbed him the wrong way. He couldn't put his finger on it, but it didn't seem as cool as it did back when he was just hanging out with the guys and girls. He also didn't want to face his roommate again. Something about him was hypnotizing. Gordon found himself doing everything Jerry wanted to do when they were around each other. If Jerry wanted pizza, they got pizza. If Jerry wanted to rent Dead Man Walking, they rented Dead Man Walking. If he could just get away from Jerry, he wouldn't have to feel bad about making him feel bad. Gordon left the library at around 6:30 and went back to his dorm. He took his time, taking in the late summer mid-evening as he strolled through the quad. As he walked through the door, he inhaled deep, plopped on his bed and then exhaled a sigh. He made it. Or did he? CRASH~! The door slammed open then slammed shut again. "There you are!" Jerry was back. "Why didn't you go to the meeting?" "Oh… umm, I just lost track of time. My class got out early and I was in the library." "Bullshit man…" "Excuse me?" Jerry was taken aback. "I'm sorry man… I didn't mean to sound so rude, but you're not showing the proper committment to the Dems, man." "Well, I told you, my first committment was to school." "Dude, school is so much more than just learning. It's about making a difference. It's about making friends, man. You have to be well-rounded. You know Jennie Mae was asking for you today." "She… she was?" "Yeah man. You're letting all us down. You gotta show some committment. You can't keep blowing these meetings off." "Hey… I only missed one meeting." "And a protest. Protesting is essential, man. That's two events. You can't keep doing that. You have to go to the next protest with us to make it up to me. To us." Gordon furrowed his brow. "When exactly is this protest?" "Wednesday night." "Hey, c'mon now, you know I have my lab, and my professor's a dick. I need to show up ready to go or else…" "Or else what, he'll yell at you, man? C'mon, are you gonna choose some stodgy old suit over your friends? Don't be lame." Gordon lowered his head. "If you know what's good for you, then you'll show up Wednesday night." Gordon raised his head back up. "Where's it at?" "Texas State House." Wednesday night came, and the Campus Democrats were out en masse in front of the State House. Gordon and Jerry showed up together, although it seemed like they were worlds apart. They hadn't spoken a word to each other on the way, and only talked to each other about daily matters around the dorm. Brent greeted them as soon as they showed up. "Hey, Jerry, glad you and your roomie could come. We missed you at the last protest, Gorman." "It's Gordon." "Oh, sorry man. So, you guys got your placards?" "I do," answered Jerry. "I don't," said Gordon. "What are we protesting anyway?" "What? How can you go to a protest and not know?" "Jerry never told me. He just told me to come, and I came." "Well, at least you have faith in the cause, but yeah, Governor Bush signed John Boy Clovis' death warrant this afternoon. He's gonna be executed at midnight. We're gonna protest this barbaric practice until Bush signs a stay." Gordon's face turned to shock. "Wait a second, John Boy Clovis? The guy who hacked that family in Amarillo up, put 'em in a steamer chest and mailed it to their grandmother?" "Yeah him." Gordon was incredulous. "Wait a second, he's going to get killed by lethal injection and that's barbaric compared to what he did?" "Hey, hey, we don't condone what he did. But we feel that the government shouldn't put people to death." "But…" At that instant, Jerry pulled Gordon aside. "Listen man, don't question Brent or the cause. You fall in line and don't say another word. Don't fuckin' embarrass me after all I did for you." Gordon was going to say something, but he didn't. Instead, he turned around and was ready to leave. Once again, Jerry grabbed him by the arm. "I said, don't embarrass me. If you leave, you'll embarrass me." "But…" "Shut up and protest, now, or I will go around and tell everyone what you're doing and you'll have NO friends around campus. I'll make sure of that." All the color left Gordon's face at once. He shook his head and joined the picket line. The protest didn't let out until after 1 AM, and it was in vain. Governor Bush didn't even think to sign a stay, and at 12:01 AM, John Boy Clovis was executed via lethal injection. All through the warm summer night, Gordon felt cold, mainly due to the iciness between he and his roommate. When the protest let out, Gordon was the first to leave. Jerry was pulling up the rear. Gordon stormed back to the dorm, up the stairs and into his bed, throwing the covers over himself to go to sleep. A half-hour later, the door opened. Jerry walked in with someone else, a female. She was someone whom Gordon had never seen before, but with whom Jerry was obviously intimate. Jerry whispered something into the girl's ear, then turned around to Gordon. "Hey, dude, you're on sexile." Gordon pretended he was asleep. "Dude, I'm not falling for it. Get up, get out. I'm bringing a chick back." Gordon turned around. "Yeah, and I have an important lab tomorrow." "Fuck your lab man. Get out of here so I can be alone with Shaniqua. C'mon, it's a Golden Rule of Dorm Life." "No, I'm not leaving. If you can't fuck her while I'm sleeping, then you'll just have to go back to her dorm room." "Fine, it's your fault though." With that, Gordon turned back around and then Jerry started making out with Shaniqua. Making out turned into heavy petting. Heavy petting into mouths… well, let's keep this somewhat PG and just say that there was a lot of moaning. Moaning that kept Gordon up until 3 AM. When the alarm went off at 7 AM, Gordon rose out of his bed like a zombie from the grave. He got dressed, stumbling around the room like a klutz on a rocking ship. By some miracle of God, he got completely dressed, and as he was on his way out of the room, he turned around to face his roommates bed. He cleared his throat and spat right on the bed, inadvertantly hitting Shaniqua right in the shoulder. To his surprise, the girl didn't get up, but muttered in her sleep, "Ooh, Jerry, how did you know I liked that," and rolled over. Gordon was lucky to get to class right before Dr. Ledgely got there. He got to his seat, but he wasn't bright eyed and bushy tailed like last week. In fact, he had to hold his head up with his arm. Dr. Ledgely got into class, put his books down and immediately went into roll. "Atkins…" In what seemed like an instant, he got to "Powell… Powell…" Gordon was there, but he wasn't responding. That's because his head had fallen off his arm and he was sleeping on his lab desk. Ledgely caught wind of that and went over to him. "POWELL!" Nothing. Finally, he grabbed a ruler out of his lab coat and poked the freshman. Gordon awoke with a shock. "Aawwfghpphsh…" "Freshman! What did I tell you just last week about falling asleep in my lab?" "But… but my roommate…" "No excuses! You fail lab this week. Get out of here!" Gordon sleepwalked back to his dorm, aiming to go back to sleep. He opened his door and found the room was to himself. Jerry and his ladyfriend had left. He stumbled over to his bed and was just about to plop when… RIINNG! RIIIINNGG! Dammit. The phone. Gordon picked it up. "Hello?" "Hello Gordon Powell? This is Dean Pritchard. I just got word from Dr. Ledgely that you fell asleep in his lab. Is this true?" "Yes… yes, but I can expl…" "I'm very disappointed in you, Gordon. I let you in that class against my better judgement. You let me down and made me look like a fool." "But… but…" "No buts! Gordon, I'm dropping you from that lab. You'll have to wait 'til next year to take it. Maybe then, you'll have grown up and realized that college isn't just one big party." As Gordon heard the click on the other end of the line, his sleepiness turned to rage. He stormed back out of his dorm room and out across the quad, making a bee-line for the Campus Democrats' office. He charged right into the Student Union and burst through the doors of the Dems' office. "Hi," a cute brunette reading TIME magazine on the leather sofa in the common area said, "Can I help you?" "Yes," he replied, calmed down a bit, "Is Brent Cowart in?" "Sure he is. He's in the second door on the left." Gordon made a left and went in that second door. There Brent was at his desk, surfing the Web on the club's desktop. Noticing Gordon, he looked up. "Hey Gavin, how are you doing?" "It's Gordon." "Oh, I'm sorry. What do you want to talk to me about?" "I'm… I'm quitting the Democrats." Brent had a look of disappointment on his face. "Well, I'm sorry that you don't have the committment to drive on with us." "No, it's not that I don't have the commmittment. I just don't agree with your stances on life and death stuff. I can't in good conscience be a part of something I'm morally opposed to." The funny part is, this is the first time Gordon had shown anything regarding morals. "Life and death? What do you mean?" "Don't play dumb with me. Your stances on abortion and the death penalty. How can you be for killing innocent lives, yet against punishing gruesome acts against humanity?" "What? How can you agree with punishment for anything by taking a life? We as human beings don't have the moral authority to take a life." "Then explain abortion, numbnuts." "Hey, no name call…" "Answer the question!" "Okay, well, it's a scientific fact that life doesn't begin until the third tri…" This was the second time that argument was used against him. The second time was the charm, apparently. "Don't feed me that pseudoscience bullshit. I may be a chem major, but I know more about bio after one high school course and several hours of watching PBS and Discovery than you will in an entire lifetime of reading your journals from your leftist bastard street-corner biologists who get their information from Lifetime Network. And I can firmly say that any being that can reproduce on its own, even if it's just splitting cells inside a parasitic environment, that's proof enough that it's life for me. The fact that you support squelching out a life whose only crime is that his or her harlot mother and douchebag father didn't bother using protection before it even begins and upholds the lives of those who'd brutally murder your mother if given the chance turns my stomach. I don't want any part of any party that believes in something like that. And another thing, before I go, if you call me by any name other than Gordon, I will shove my Organic Chem textbook so far up your ass that you'll be burping up Appendix D, and lemme tell you, it's one big book. Good day!" Before Brent could retort, Gordon stormed out. As he walked out of the office altogether, he composed himself. He then looked around and smiled. He'd never felt so good in his entire life.
