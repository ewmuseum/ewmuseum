"And what I chase won't set me free."

15 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-15 Stranger than your sympathy, And this is my apology I'm killing myself from the inside out, And all my fears have pushed you out. The Airport I hate airports. Always have. Well, not quite always... When I was a kid, once I got used to going to a new airport every few weeks, thinking that the string of hotels my parents dragged me to were "home," I actually started to enjoy it. Of course, I was only five years old then, so it was all a matter of being in a cool, exciting place. All that stopped the day the trips stopped. The day my parents effectively abandoned me. The last time I saw them, for fifteen years was in an airport... This airport, in fact. Sure, they'd left me with my aunt and uncle, and by no means did I have a bad upbringing here in Chicago. My aunt and uncle cared for me just like Mamma and Vati... Hell, probably better. At least with them, I learned what a steady home was. But still, a place with such a terrible childhood memory tends to stick with you. And thus, I hate airports. Funny that I'd choose a life that would put me in and out of airports all the time. Sometimes I think I chose it for that exact reason. As a constant reminder of my parents, and the life we led those first few years I was on this chunk of rock called the Earth. And maybe, just maybe, to try and recapture the happiness I think I remember. Too bad only the first ever really happened. Every time I'd set foot into one of these godforsaken places, I'd stop, look around, and somehow, I'd see that five year old crying out in vain for his parents to come back. And every time, I'd find myself wanting to cry out with him. Not today, though... Not since they managed to hear my cry one day, and showed up when I least expected it. And I wished for things that I don't need. (All I wanted) And what I chase won't set me free. (It's all I wanted) And I get scared but I'm not crawling on my knees. The Reunion May 22, 2004. NAFW OBLIVION, Night Two. Key Arena, Seattle, Washington. I stood there, in Hector Gonzales' office, ready for a fight. I'd won the damned NAFW Tag Titles earlier, and the bastard had stripped them, continuing on his power trip as the Foundation's Owner. I wanted those belts back, and I did get something back... But it damn sure wasn't ten pounds of gold. Hector's goon Ryan McJohnson had me locked up quite nicely, and had done a hell of a job neutralizing my little friend the baseball bat. I'll give him that much, at least. He may have the worst name ever, but damn if he's not good at his job. Hector was going on one of his usual raves, abusing his authority - thankfully that was his last night in power, but that's another story, for someone else to tell. Telling me that I never won the match and whatnot. Whatever. Then he told me that he gave his word to a couple of friends... "Yer gonna git what's comin' to ya," is what he said. I prepared myself for the attack that was likely to come, from Gord knows who, and I told him to do his worst. That's when things got weird. He told his goon to let me go, and called over to the attached room, for his friends. What I saw, to put it mildly, blew my fucking mind. As ready as I was for an ass-kicking, nothing could have prepared me for this. There, in this bastard's office, stood two people I hadn't seen for fifteen years. Two people I thought about every time I set foot in an airport in all that time, and on countless occasions between. "Hallo, Jazon," was all she said. I melted. Her voice was exactly as I remembered it. The same voice that told me she loved me, said I was the perfect son, and told me they'd come back for me "when I was ready." I'd spent years wondering what that meant, and when that would be. Standing there in Hector's office, apparently, was the "when." I didn't for the life of me know how now, suddenly, I was "ready," but I didn't even think about that. It didn't matter. All that mattered was, they'd come. "Mamma? ... Vati?" was all I'd managed to say. Looking back on what had aired, that's where the cameras cut, but it was just the beginning of my reunion. Oh yeah, everything's all wrong, yeah. Everything's all wrong, yeah. Where the hell did I think I was? The Talk Before the initial shock started to wear off, they ushered me back into the room where they'd apparently been waiting most of the night. Thus began the first face to face conversation I'd had with them in fifteen years. Sure, they'd kept in touch off and on over the years. Letters and gifts on my birthday and holidays and such. But in all that time - three quarters of my life, for Gord's sake - I'd never seen them until this moment. "You must have a million questions, my son," said my father, his voice just as strong as I'd remembered it. Strong, but still comforting, particularly with his standard "my son." To be quite honest, I'd probably have been comforted by him reading the phone book at that point, I was so awestruck and dumbfounded. All I could manage was an idiotic nod, which Mamma responded to with a half smile. "We're sorry it's taken so long for us to come for you, Jazon," said Mamma. Nodding, Vati continued, "We had to be sure you were ready. That served to snap me out of the state of uselessness I'd been in since they stepped out of that doorway a few minutes earlier. I stared at them with an anger I'm sure they'd never seen, but had to expect. "You're damn right I have questions!" I said, jumping back to the first thing Vati had said. "Like 'why the hell did you leave me?' for starters, and how about 'how am I suddenly "ready" now, after fifteen fucking years?!' Once the words left my mouth, a part of me regretted the harshness, but damn it, I couldn't just sit there like a moron gaping at these two so-called "parents" now that all the emotions and questions had flooded back to me. Thus, another part of me actually enjoyed the pain in Mamma's eyes before she looked away. "I know you're angry, my son," began Vati. But that's as far as he got. The words that were comforting a moment ago burned now, and I wasn't going to let him use them. "I haven't been 'your son,' since you left me in that airport fifteen years ago! You can't just come in here trying to act like I'm still five years old, and was never abandoned by my so-called 'parents.'" He looked to Mamma, and said six words that made me even angrier than before: "Maybe you aren't ready after all." "Ready for what, God damn it??" This is where things got interesting. He looked at me, obviously regretting the words he just spoke. My anger was peaking, so I pushed. "Well? Just what is it that I'm not ready for, Vati? What is it that was so important that you had to wait fifteen years to apparently not even tell me?" He looked to Mamma again, and she looked up, only to nod silently. Apparently this was her giving permission for him to finally fill me in, and fill me in he did. "We want you to join us, my son. To take over from us what is yours to lead." I looked at him, still angry, but now a touch on the confused side. "Join you where? Take over what?" "Our organization. The reason we've spent our lives traveling. The thing we wanted to spare you from in your youth..." "Spit it out, Vati!" He nodded to Mamma, who took over. The words she spoke sent me right back into a state of awe even worse than the first: "In German, it is called 'Der Auftrag.' In English, we are known as 'The Order.'" Stranger than your sympathy. I take these things so I don't feel. I'm killing myself from the inside out, And now my head's been filled with doubt. The Order It wasn't the first time I'd heard the name "The Order." The first was when Krystin Thomas - who happens to be my unrequited love - had been abducted. At that time, my tag team partner, Ryan Cardinal had thrown the biggest shock of my life - up until the ones a few moments ago, of course - at me, revealing that he was an undercover agent, investigating Hector's tie with the no longer mysterious organization known as The Order, and that he thought they might be responsible for Krystin's situation. "You..." I stumbled out, "Krys... That was you? You took her? Put her through all of that?" I should have known. It only occurred to me now that Hector had addressed them as "old friends." Thanks to Ryan, I'd known that Hector was associated with The Order somehow, but how could I have ever expected that my parents were at it's core? "Our operatives were responsible, yes. We regret..." "You regret abducting and torturing the woman I love? Sorry if I don't have any sympathy for your 'regrets,' you... You monster!" With Vati sufficiently chided for the moment, I turned my attention to Mamma. "And you? You knew of this? You let it happen?" She nodded. "I'm sorry, Jazon." "Save it! I don't want your shallow apologies! I just want to know... Why? Why did you do this to her?" She turned away, and Vati put his arm around her. My lividity continued, near unstoppable by this point. I reached over, grabbing his arm and wrenching it from her shoulder. "Don't you comfort her! She deserves to suffer over what you two have done!" I'd rarely seen Vati angry as a child. Of course, I didn't have much opportunity. He'd spent much of his time out of the "house" (i.e. hotel), and besides that, I only had a few short years in their custody anyway. But the look in his eyes at that moment made me realize where my fiery temper had come from. He stood up, shoving me back into my seat, and yelled down at me. "Do you think we enjoyed it? Do you think we wanted to abandon our son? To hurt the people he loved? Of course we didn't! We've suffered every single day for fifteen years, boy! Even more in the last year, doing these things... These terrible things, to make you ready for the glory that is yours to claim!" I stood up at this, getting right in his face. I wanted to make two things clear: I wasn't the five year old kid he knew, and moreover, I wasn't afraid of him. "Well, I don't want your glory. I don't want your apologies, and I don't want you. I've done just fine without you for the last fifteen years, and I'm sure I'll be able to do just fine without you for the rest of my life. As far as I'm concerned, as of this moment, I'm an orphan." I turned and tried to storm off, until I felt a hand on my arm. "Unless you want that arm broken, I'd suggest you let go," I warned. A big part of me wished that he hadn't, so I could have snapped his arm in two, but this monster who I could no longer think of as "Vati" heeded my words, and released me. I continued to the far door, the one that didn't lead back to Hector's office. As I did, the woman - I couldn't think of her as "Mamma" anymore either - cried out. "Jazon, wait! Come back!" I stopped, and heard her sigh of relief. Relief that I was about to destroy. I just stood there, but didn't turn around. "When I asked you to come back, you didn't. Now you know about an ounce of the pain I felt that day, and have felt every day since. I hope you feel it until the day you burn in hell." As I slammed the door shut, I heard the beginnings of a sob, and like before, when I saw the pain in her eyes, a part of me - maybe even all of me - enjoyed it. And it's hard to lead the life you choose, (All I wanted) When all your lucks run out on you. (All I wanted) And you can't see when all your dreams are coming true. Oh yeah, it's easy to forget, yeah. And you choke on the regrets, yeah. Who the hell did I think I was? And stranger than your sympathy, And all these thoughts you stole from me. And I'm not sure where I belong. Nowhere's home and I'm all wrong The Friend Left Behind I walked down the hallway, absolutely furious. My mind was racing, trying to make some more sense of everything that had just happened. How could those two monsters be my parents? Was I doomed to become just like them? What could I do to avoid that potential destiny? How could I ever look at Krystin again, knowing that the ordeal she went through was some sick plot... For my benefit? All this and more was going through my head as I ran full-force into my tag team partner, Ryan Cardinal. Things between us hadn't been the greatest for a while now, ever since Krystin came back in the arms of another man... But looking back on it, he'd tried to be a friend to me, despite how I'd treated him. And so he did in this instance, not even mentioning that I'd almost knocked him over. "Jaze! I saw what happened... Were those really your parents??" I didn't want to talk about it. "Not now, Ryan." "You look upset..." Ryan was always an observant fellow. As I thought of that, I remembered again how I'd found out about The Order in the first place, and another question popped into my head: Do I tell him that the objects of his pursuit were just down the hall? It was just too much to think about right now. I needed some time to sort things out. "It wasn't quite what I expected..." "Oh... I'm sorry." "Save your sympathy, Ryan. I just need to be alone right now." Give Ryan some credit, unlike another friend, he knew when to back off and let me go. Looking back, I regret being so coarse with him, but the sentiment was very much true. I did need to be alone... And the last thing I wanted at that time - or even now, for that matter - was sympathy. Not from him, or from anyone else. And I wasn't all the things I tried to make believe I was. And I wouldn't be the one To kneel before the dreams I wanted And all the talk, And all the lies Were all the empty things disguised as me Yeah. The Airport, Again And so here I am, in that same airport where my parents left me fifteen years ago. I look around, and just when I think I'm not going to see him, there in the distance appears that five year old boy, crying out. But this time, he's not crying out to his parents. No, today he's crying out to the man he'll become. The man I am. I turn my back on him for the last time and head toward my gate. I've got a plane to catch, a tournament to win, and no time for sympathy from a five year old who died in this airport a lifetime ago. Stranger than your sympathy. Stranger than your sympathy.
