Prelude to Omina Part 2

26 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-26 The slow strum of the guitar was all that was heard in Inphino’s room. He sat there with his head hung low, only concentrating on the form of his fingers against the strings. The music was on his mind… not the fight, not the tournament, not his training… only the music. It was rare that Inphino took any form of recreation from his training. He’d think that this tournament was recreation if Mina didn’t consider it business. It wasn’t his idea to enter such things, because they were nothing but mad dashes for self-important glory. Inphino had no use for that… he only cared about himself. At least he tried to. As he played a nameless tune in that bare room, singing began to accompany it. A serenading female voice, hitting pitches that only was imagined inside his mind. He dismissed it only as the song of muses descending upon him for inspiration. And so he played on… not noticing the figure that emerged behind him, leaning against the wall of the room. The words were in Japanese, though if it were anyone else they would have heard it in Russian, Italian, or even English. That’s what made the words so beautiful to hear, and that’s what brought Inphino to peace and to continue. It was a moment that he didn’t have to think about unnecessary things, like facing Mr. Darkness or advancing in the tournament. As long as the music flowed… and singing continued… everything was grand. Suddenly Mina’s knock cut through everything like a hot blade through flesh. “Yo, ‘Phino…” the door slid open, her eyes peering into the unfurnished room. “There’s a Cyrus Skar on the phone.” The music stopped, and the eased look on Inphino’s face turned sour. Slowly gazing up at Mina, the look he shot at her could’ve killed her on spot if it were hot lead. “… Take a message.” She sighed, sliding the door behind her muttering something about his antisocial behavior not being good for tournament relations. As soon as the door was closed he exhaled slowly, and began strumming the guitar once more. But he stopped when the singing turned to speaking. “Hello, Inphino.” The scene of Mina de Margo came up again in front of the black backdrop. The camera then panned to the right, showing Inphino dressed in a black dress shirt opened at the collar exposing his muscled upper chest and neck. His hair hung down around his face, the white locks contrasting with the black shades he wore to hide his eyes. “Good evening,” he said solemnly, bowing his head to the camera. “I am called Inphino Blitz.” The interviewer was off camera, but you could hear the amusement and excitement in his eloquent voice. Maybe it was the fact that he had a legend in his midst, and he and he alone was getting the scoop on him. But of course his reasons were his own. Some people just got excited over trivial things, even if it’s speaking with a mere man such as the one before him. “Ah, you say you are called Inphino Blitz,” he stated fascinated. “Why is that, may I ask?” With the camera remaining on Inphino he didn’t seem to change expression. Only his head seemed to lower at hearing the question. “… It is merely so.” “So you’re saying that you were born Japanese, in Japan… and you have no traditional name?” He shook his head, eyes still cast down. “No… I have a name.” “Ah, well can you please tell us that name, for the fans at least.” Another shake of the head, “That I cannot do.” “Why?” Questions… too many questions… sometimes you needed to accept fact for fact, and never for fiction. Many people believed that Inphino Blitz was just a lazy worker. That was fiction. Many believed that Inphino is all hype and no talent. That… is also fiction. The only fact of the matter is that Inphino Blitz may very well be the best there is… and merely doesn’t care to flaunt his talent. That’s why he keeps to himself, hmm? Inphino kept his eyes down, the camera cutting quickly to the side to see Mina watching him as well, waiting for an answer. The only answer they got was “… I don’t know.” The next scene showed Inphino sitting under a shady tree in the grass, his shoes off and only wearing a pair of black slacks. On his right arm, it seemed like a whole spool of tape snaked from palm to shoulder. Whatever he had under there, he wanted to make sure it was under wraps. He strummed at a guitar slowly, a melancholy tune accompanying him as background music. The narrator’s voice spoke over the playing, giving a detailed explanation of things, as it seems that he always knows what to say, and what to do. “Inphino Blitz, age undetermined. Known throughout the world as the Black Dragon Master, the man calling himself Inphino Blitz continues to play himself off as an enigma to the world. He lives in recluse on Sado Island, an island that in Japan’s history was used to isolate political outcasts. He only steps outside of his own world to participate in specific events, few promotional bookings and the occasional tournament. Currently he is involved in GTT4, where lately his actions within both the ring and outside the ring have been classified as different.” “You have not visited me in quite some time, young lady.” Inphino said softly, his words almost drowning out by the reverbs of his guitar. “So I take it that your visit only brings bad news.” The figure behind him still wasn’t clear yet, a wispy visage of silver silk and hair. She spoke, but her voice was unique. Inphino heard her response, though others probably did not. “Wisdom doesn’t have anything to do with it… it’s a matter of knowing the past. The last few times that you’ve graced me, it seems like the entire world turned upside down, and everything goes bad.” The guitar kept playing, even though it seemed like it would be disturbing to do while holding a conversation. Of course the mysterious caller didn’t seem to mind as she glided across the room. It was a she; the outline of a feminine figure visible wrapped in those silver silks. Her feet seemed to pad onto the tatami mats carefully, barely leaving an imprint. “Well now, it is nice to see you too. I’ll admit that. But you know in this day and age, I care to be alone.” He responded to the silent voice, eyes not looking up as the visage passed by his right side. “I’m tired of playing your hero. Go find someone else.” He glanced up slightly, and found the woman standing directly in front of him. He stared up at her pelvis, not surprised at her sudden positioning before him, not startled by the aura that accompanied her. Things were at peace in that room, as well as Inphino’s own world there on Sado. Things like GTT and challengers he didn’t care for. He only went with it because it made Mina happy… and even she was pushing her limits. “Inphino has been seen throughout many aspects of media,” the narrator continued as a video reel played of Inphino’s past exploits; some inside the ring, others outside in acting parts. “Despite his martial arts background that lead him to wrestling, Inphino has also starred in a few feature films here in Japan, not to mention guest starred in some soap operas and television shows. On top of that, Inphino has just started up a music career, his vocal abilities kept secret for so long before being brought to the light by the young entrepreneur Mina de Margo.” “I try to expose Inphino to as many elements of entertainment as I can,” Mina explained back in the studio. “I don’t want anything in this world that doesn’t include Inphino in it. From movies to music to wrestling, we’ll only go up from there.” Cutting to Inphino, he merely stared down at the ground, seemingly not paying any attention to the filmed interview. The reporter asked a question, this time directed to Inphino himself. “Inphino, if you could describe yourself in one word… what would it be?” His head tilted up a bit, staring through those dark lenses into the camera. Then his lips parted a bit in response. “… The iron fist in the velvet glove…” He played his guitar on, despite the woman that was now pacing around him slowly. “Everything to you is a matter of life and death. I think you over dramatize things, young lady.” Just then, Mina slid the door open slowly, peering in with a concerned look. “Uh… Inphino?” He stopped playing, resting the instrument across his lap. “Yes?” he hissed tiredly. “Are you all right?” she asked, poking her head in a bit to look around. She didn’t see anyone around, and that only made her concerns worse. “I mean, you’ve been in this room alone for a while now… maybe you’d like to come out and watch a bit of TV with me? Maybe go over some papers about your CD?” “I’m busy right now… I’ve got things to discuss, so you can handle the papers by yourself.” Staring at him, she nodded and slid the door back to its original position. Behind that door, Mina pressed her back against the wood frame lightly. Things weren’t stable with Inphino. Maybe I’m putting too much pressure on him, she thought. I mean, I am stretching him across three media formats and putting him into this tournament. Maybe he shouldn’t show at all. That’d be a wise move. Just then the phone rang. Inphino sighed, leaning over and pressing his palms into his knees. “Well we know she’ll be back soon, some other caller coming to speak with me about the tournament. I don’t have time to speak to them… I’ve got my own things to do. What’s wasting time speaking nonsense got to do with the tournament anyways? Congrats or trash… either way, useless.” The woman leaned down a bit, her silver hair dangling over Inphino’s shoulder. Hands gripped his shoulders, orange nailed hands that gently rested on his broad frame. Inphino turned to look up at her, but stopped before making a full turn. “You always want me involved… you should know I don’t like wasting time… especially dealing with people in this manner. Everyone’s out for blood, and I’m the only one not trying to shed any. Everyone wants a name, and I’m the only one trying to hide the one I have. If I do this for you… my name will only get larger… and that’s something I’d rather not happen. It’s a clear compromise of my solitude, and my peace of mind.” As predicted, Mina slid the door back open. “Jay Phoenix on the phone; wants to wish you luck. Take a message?” With a flick of the wrist Mina was waved off, and with the door closed his conversation continued. “With the GTT tournament going on right now, it’s been reported that you’ve been a cancer in the entire thing,” the reporter mentioned, prepping for a question. “Why have you suddenly turned into such a rebel and an outcast?” Inphino was looking off to the side when that question was asked. And as he answered, he looked directly into the camera with emotion dead on his face. “Because I’m a douche bag… didn’t you know that?” “I’m telling you, there’s something wrong with Inphino!” Mina whispered on her cell phone, standing in the main hall of the house. The light was bright overhead, and reflected well off the glossed hardwood floors. Doors were lined on each side of her, and she stood in the middle of it with the phone pressed into her ear. “No, he says he’s busy, and it sounds like he’s talking to someone, but when I looked in there, there was nobody!” Once again, the house phone rang, and Mina huffed as she stomped down the hall back towards the phone. “Hold on.” She told the cell caller. Scooping it up with her free hand, she lowered the cell. “What is it?” One phone stopped ringing… but the smaller one began in it’s place. Mina’s eyes snapped down to the phone quickly, as if something was terribly wrong. The door slid open again, and Blitz looked up sharply this time. He was getting tired of the constant intrusions. “[name withheld] on the phone for you.” His eyebrow raised on that call. “Why would I want to speak with Mexi?” Her brow wrinkled in return. “Who? Oh… no no no, this is the representative of Sebastian Moraco, the kid you beat first round. I think he’s got some words for you.” Inphino stared at her for a moment, and then turned his eyes ahead at the wall. “It’s not my fault the kid had bad luck and drew me. I only do what I’m told, it seems. You want me to win, and so I remove everyone from my path towards victory. What else would I do?” “I—“ “If he wants an apology, tell him forget it. If he wants to rant, tell him to send an email. At least it’ll be easier to delete than ignore him over the phone.” Her eyes rolled like wheels, sliding the door back shut. “What… prophecy do you have for me this time, young lady?” Inphino asked her casually, as if he didn’t care and was just making conversation. The reporter had a question to follow on his response. “You’re loved by many people! Yet your actions are drawing your fans away. Can you explain your actions?” Inphino bit his lip. “I do what I want. That’s my nature. If you truly like what I do, then you’ll like what I do in disregard of how I do it. If you’re on the bandwagon because it looks cool, find another fad. Shuffle through the Sorpranos, or the SciFi channel, or Final Fantasy, whatever suits your fancy. I just want to be alone.” “Well you want to be alone, yet you are continuously out in the media, making movies and music. What’s up with that?” Staring into the camera, it seemed like Inphino didn’t have an answer for that question. But it only came delayed. “… I’m an anarchist.” The guitar was on the floor across from where Inphino was sitting. He himself… he was gone. The phone rang through the house, reaching each and every crevice, every nook and cranny. At the front hallway, Inphino stood there staring at the ringing phone. Then he looked up, peering around in search. “Mina?” he called out for her, but she wasn’t in the vicinity. Sighing, he reached slowly for the phone and raised it to his ear. “Hello?” The caller put caution on Inphino’s face when they spoke. Slowly Inphino nodded, though his response could in no way be received through the phone. He hung up and stood there, staring down at the phone. Something just came to pass… and Inphino knew that he’d have no choice but to play the hand dealt to him. In this world, everyone wants to be in the light, and only they want the light to themselves. There is no sharing, it’s an honor that can only belong to one person. It is solid fact that light kills darkness… never can darkness overtake light if it exists. Inphino shines on, despite trying to keep himself shadowed.
