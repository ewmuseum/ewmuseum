The Flying Vergomovs vs. The Twillight Effect

13 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A Brief History Of Everything -RP for this match...it's the new style- A Brief History of Everything Kenichi Kazuyo stared at his shoes for a little while, the nice black shiny designer-label kind. He absently kicked at a smoking cigarette butt, and wondered if the gel in his hair was too sticky...that if one of the pretty blondes inside the nightclub would be turned off, if she chanced to run her hair through it. He thought about the fact that he didn't look too terribly different than the others in the bar...other than the fact that he was Asian, he wore the same standard silk shirt with non-pleated slacks, had the same tan and even a similar build, had his jet hair styled in much the same way as everyone else... * * * * * Danny Arianhrod smiled, but not on purpose. It was one of those "gee whiz, how do I not see that coming...?" sort of smiles, where you stifle a chuckle to yourself as you remember what a fucking idiot you are to think she wouldn't catch someone else's attention while you were on the road wrestling. He angrily musses his curly blonde hair, his eyes tighten and yield crow's feet at the corners, and his nostrils and pupils open in synchronicity. She is barely twenty, is a model with a KILLER body, adventurous sexually, and wants ABSOLUTELY NOTHING TO DO WITH YOU, Danny... * * * * * It's not like Kenichi thought his race was important here in Seattle. He thought everyone else's was. The only people of African descent in Japan that he had any contact with were the "come-hentai" guys in the sex industry in Shinjuku, or Bob Sapp. And white people...Kenichi was raised that if life is a game, then having a beautiful white girlfriend is how you win first prize. In that regard, I guess he was/is a racist. So here he was, outside a club in downtown Seattle, with the Starbucks-and-commute crowd, looking for the same thing that all the others were. A good time and a warm body, and not much thinking to go along with it. His dark brown eyes glazed over a little, before he caught a whiff of perfume, and his head turned... * * * * * "Life does not work out like it's supposed to, Danny." He tells himself this, and believes it, because when he went to the University of Washington at Seattle, he was going to be a doctor, not a wrestler. He was going to get straight A's, just like high school, maybe play some intramurals, and graduate summa cum laude with a BA in biology before med school somewhere tropical like U Miami. Then he failed biology twice, but not for lack of trying. The sad truth is that he just is not cut out for that life. He hit the gym twice as hard, drank three times as hard at parties, and found out the one thing in life that he was good at...really, really good at...involved pasting people with piledrivers some of the time. No, life doesn't work out like it's supposed to, Danny... * * * * * He got one good glance. Lips, hips, and chest, the dress is red like brick, not like blood...and gone. She's gone, and she didn't even hesitate, didn't even look twice at you. The bouncer looks at her like she's anyone else, and Kenichi knows she's not. If he doesn't go back inside, and say the absolute perfect set of words to her, she will walk out of his life and away forever. If he does no act now, without hesitation or fear, the world will end, the game will reset, and there will be no chance to play again. It is a world full of 'ifs' for Kenichi, and as the bouncer steps in his way to prevent him access (hey, this is a serious bar and he's only been on TV for a few weeks), he thinks of all the drivers and suplexes that he could deliver on this man if they were alone under the flashing, living twilight of a wrestling arena roof. "If this were a different place," Kenichi thinks "I would not be walking away...I would not be alone tonight." * * * * * Danny Arianhrod sat in the front seat of his car, and wondered what drew him to the NPWA, and wrestling. Why, of all the hobbies in the world, did this one stick like glue? Was it the thrill, the rush? Was it the ease of which you could pick up moves? Or are we going to be serious, and admit that it was the fact that the guy in charge looked at him like fathers are supposed to look like sons. He was the greatest pupil, Jason saw so much of himself in Danny that he called him "son." To Jason, a euphemism. To Danny, the moment in which the decision was made that the rest of his life would be spent earning the man's respect. If he could not be born with a father, perhaps he could earn one through sweat and hard work? Danny, oh Danny...is that why you are the first to training in the mornings, is that why you watch tape until you pass out at night? There is no substitute for what you seek, and the hole will NEVER be filled. * * * * * Ramen. As much as Kenichi Kazuyo wanted to make a clean break from Japanese culture, that one thing kept drawing him back. Steaming, salty ramen. Extra pork, please. In Japan, you can turn on a television set, and if the timing is right, and you look hard enough, you'll find a TV program basically devoted to watching celebrities go into ramen houses, chopstick some noodles into their mouths and look satisfied. That's IT. Ramen subculture television. And Kenichi, as much as he'd like not to, spent a fair part of time in America looking for, eating, and generally criticizing ramen. It's not the same here, and it's almost worth going home for... * * * * * Books will never abandon you, Danny. After seven years of normalcy, they will not go out for cigarettes before bed and never come back. Myths and legends are forever, and they will not stop returning your calls, act like you never existed, not send your mother money. When you read about Odin, or Zeus, or Ra, and how they father sons and daughters on many wives, and disappear back into the heavens, you wonder if there's any justice in the world, and if the reason he left is because his throne on Mount Olympus is getting cold with his absence. Is there any chance, any at all that your father is the sire of heroes? Is that why when you pull muscles at the gym, tug an opponent's arm out of it's socket in the ring, you know that if you prove yourself to be a myth or a legend, then quite possibly, you'll at least be confirmed in your belief that your father might be the same. * * * * * Kenichi Kazuyo, like any good son, wants to go home. He wants to find all the people that he knew in his past life, his Japanese life, and see them all crowd around him, as he's a big American wrestling superstar with endorsement contracts and a pair of great knockers as his date. And when they all crowd around him, ask for autographs, bow in respect, sigh and faint and generally go into apoplectic fits, that's when he will completely IGNORE THEM. Because that's what celebrities do, or rather, what Kenichi sees them do. And if he can't be special, it's not worth being at all. Better to die than waste away in a cubicle. Better to live fast, die young, and dive off turnbuckles to fade away in the subway bathroom, with no one around to watch you go, like your grandfather did. * * * * * Arianhrod was a Celtic goddess, but Danny's mother was a devout Catholic. When the Pope passed away recently, Danny went home to the old house and found her sobbing in a heap with dress and coffee strewn about the carpet around her. She should have been a nun, or a missionary. Except no matter how she tried, her son would not ease into her faith. Danny needed more than the promises that the book brings. But Danny understood the myths in the Christian realm, and that helped make his own private collection of beliefs. His own mother believed that he would walk through the fires of damnation on his way to hell. She will always be disappointed, she will never understand. But Danny didn't need Catholicism, his family was his own cross to bear. * * * * * They both arrive at the NPWA gym near the same time...Danny is in the ring first, always one step ahead of Kenichi. He has two huge medicine balls, and he pulls and pushes at them in an attempt to confound density and gravity. Kenichi strips off his silk shirt, and destroys it when he tosses it to the puddle on the concrete floor. He stretches his arms out, Danny catches his eye, and they share a look. This is my resolve. This is where I stand. After all else is gone, I will still remain. * * * * * Maybe no one will see the match against the Flying Vergomovs. Perhaps the world will pass them by, and never know that they were here. But they will leave something behind. They will leave themselves, in broken bits and pieces, on the paths that they continue to walk.
