Faith - Part II: Hope

8 November 2004

Date: 2004-11-08 Faith – Part II: Hope “He isn’t picking up,” I shake my head in disbelief. He must be at home, there’s nowhere else he could be. I’ve tried the gym and they say they haven’t seen him for days, I’ve phoned home with no reply. Something’s up, I can sense it. “Still can’t reach him, Wendy?” asks Julie as she walks past me, noticing me on the phone once more. I tilt my head away for a second, not saying a word, she can tell enough by the expression on my face. “Well, I’m sure something will come up. You just hang in there, girl. Dave will be fine.” If only I could believe that, but something tells me that Dave is in serious danger. I’m not going to just sit here and wait for him to be found lying in a ditch somewhere, it’s time to do something about this now. So I do the one thing I do best. I turn to my desk looking at the screen, move the keyboard in front of me, turn on the word processor and I type like the wind. There must be a thousand and one different sounds attempting to distract me this afternoon, but there is nothing that’s going to stop me from doing this. People are rushing about wildly as they try to find a big scoop to fill the next morning’s newspaper. The office is a torrent of people rushing from one side of the place to the other. This is agonising to type. The more words I place onto the screen, the more I begin to bring myself to the truth. It was easy preoccupying myself trying to contact him all this time, but now I begin to realise the extent to what has been unfolding. My precious Dave… could be dead. No… I refuse to believe that. Dave is much, much stronger than that. He can bounce back from anything, something as simple as a tough match will never get in his way, no matter how insurmountable the odds. It wasn’t hard for the perils of Exceptional Dave Hurst to get the front page of our publication a couple of weeks ago, when he was a physical and mental mess in his own apartment, days before his match against Jay Phoenix. It shouldn’t be too hard for there to be a missing PTC wrestler days before an important match. Perhaps this is all Angelo Deville’s doing? I mean, you hear about the kind of stuff that he is into, all the rumours that he is responsible for the many unexplained murders that are cropping up around the country. I heard from someone that The Devil controls him, but that’s preposterous. I’ll believe that when I see it. I really hope that Dave’s okay. He was bad enough when preparing for the match against Jay, but the magnitude of his next match must be so much worse. I just hope that he hasn’t gone and done anything stupid. -----=====|=====----- Hope is a strange thing. While for some people they spend every hour of their waking lives hoping that they will one day meet that person they have been separated with for their entire lives, and never find them. For other people they may be hoping for the most trivial of things, like a small bike for their birthday present, and get it. Hope is a very mysterious thing in that respect. But there are occasional moments in people’s lives when just that little bit of hope, that little thing that they hold onto throughout the good times and the bad, hoping amongst hope that this one miraculous thing may come true actually pulls off. You could say that it is down to luck. The luck of the draw whether that thing that you yearn for will come true, that you can have all the hope in the world, but at the end of the day, you were just unlucky. There is one thing, however, that seemingly has all the luck in the world. An object of pure inevitability everywhere it stands, and everywhere it stands miraculous things happen. Exceptional Dave Hurst still sits with his back against the wall, agonising over the many horrendous things that The Devil has put him through. The Devil had promised a personal hell, and EDH sure is getting it. You can’t even begin to imagine the torturous things that The Devil is doing to him. But, even after all the things that he promised EDH with his personal hell, The Devil has one thing completely and utterly wrong. Of course, after I’ve finished with you, you will be begging me to use this barrel to end your ridiculous plastic duck’s existence, begging me to do it over and over again. While EDH sits there, in the same position, with his arms folded across his dark black overcoat, having a whole host of harrowing things done to his body and his mind, he still echoes a small, throaty whisper, over and over again. “Not my Ducky… please… Not my Ducky… please…” Repeating it over and over again, the last remaining piece of hope that his precious plastic friend is okay. And… it seems… that the small remaining piece of hope is the only little thing that was needed. You see, Ducky is that small object with all the luck in the world. And when you have that much luck, you only need that tiniest piece of hope, and all will be okay. And from what would need to be a minor miracle, when Ducky previously hit the rim of the barrel, he fell into the barrel, but at an angle so that he rolled across the wood and down into a small hole, continuing to roll through the rubble of the barrel and then out of a small gap left in the bottom of the barrel to the outside. You see this is one very lucky Ducky. The skies opened up, and from what starts as a few trickles of raindrops from the clouds above suddenly multiplies into a gush of water beating down on the long endless road. In a matter of seconds, the road begins to flood into a long puddle. Perfect duck weather you could say, well if that phrase was ever more pertinent. As the rain beats down with more force by the second, a prevailing gust of wind accompanies it, sending Ducky down the road and away from the sight of Exceptional Dave Hurst, and subsequently the sight of a watchful Devil. -----=====|=====----- “Where is he…?” I mutter to myself under my breath as I try desperately to see out of the windscreen of my small Renault Clio past the scattered raindrops splattering off of it. The windscreen wipers swing from side to side rapidly, but it is still not enough to clear the invisibility for me to spot Dave. Not that this is very likely, driving along the same route that he would have taken home in the hope that somehow I see him sitting on the side of a street somewhere. If I did that he would most definitely be dead, there’s no doubt about that. I really, really hope that something like that hasn’t happened to him. Oh no, do you think he was stabbed? It’s a distinct possibility. I really hope that isn’t the case. This is really strange for me. I don’t even know why I’m doing this. I really don’t. I’m searching for Dave in the hope that I don’t find him. I don’t want to find him lying dead on the street, but I miss him so much. I just want to know that he’s okay. Wait… who’s that? As I drive a little further up the long street I pass someone sat against the wall with a long black overcoat. It’s hard to see, but there’s definitely someone there. He’s rocking back and forth in the cold and rain. Maybe it’s Dave? I slow the car down slowly to a standstill beside him, swiftly pushing in the button and clutching the seatbelt, throwing it over my shoulder. I grab the handle to the car and yank it, swinging the door open as fast as I can move. But then as I get outside, with the rain beating down on my back and me slowly becoming more and more drenched in the unreasonably cold shower, I slow right down. The rain is now merely an afterthought. Is it…? Is it Dave…? I walk ever so slowly over to the mysterious character. With every last bit of faith that this is the man I have been looking for, I place my hand softly on his shoulder, look over to his head buried in his coat and curiously utter the words: “Daaave…?” “Feck awf, bitch!” are the words screamed back to me with a thick southern accent as he waves an arm out to try and swat me away like a fly. This definitely is not Dave. The coat folds away slightly where the zip had been broken revealing dirty old overalls. The name ‘Angelo’ imprinted on the chest. The man looks back at me with a face of pure evil. Scraggly hair, a long unkempt beard and a snag tooth are the only things accompanying his manic gaze. “Erm… sorry…” I say, trying to ease the man’s erratic behaviour, but it only makes matters worse. “Yer will be sorry bitch, when ah beat the ever livin’ hell out of ya saggy ass!” It’s no use, there’s no point trying to reason with this man. It seems Angelo is so bitter at his misfortunes that all he can do is insult people. I was going to ask him if he’s seen Dave anywhere, but he wouldn’t tell me even if he did know. I walk back into the car and shut the door behind me. Stare at the windscreen and the stream of rain beating down upon it, and break down into a stream of my own tears. Where are you Dave? -----=====|=====----- As the violent gales sends him tumbling over and over along the empty streets, Ducky hits a curb with it’s beak, and flips into the air falling onto a little puddle with a plop and a little splash. Almost as if the sound commanded it the downpour slowly subsided, the roaring wind became nothing more than a whimper and the night gave way to sunlight breaking through, morning was here. The street immediately springs to life, what was before nothing but a ghost town is now a carnival of morning-goers. Cars zooming past driven by office workers on their way to work, a gaggle of old ladies moving to the bus stop to get to the shops early, and a boot managing to connect with the Ducky’s side sending him hurtling across the pavement. “What was that?” came the source of the kicking, but Ducky suddenly found him surrounded by legs. The hustle of people on their way to work had escalated into a frenzy of legs moving past him at a rate of knots. Ducky was jostled, bumped and shoved back and forth in a tirade of swinging shins, shoes and boots hovering overhead and then hitting the floor barely an inch from him. As a squashing or stamping seems inevitable, suddenly the saving grace of a bright pink hand appears from the skies and clasps hold of the bath toy, hoisting him to safety. “Hullo there, little feller.” The saviour says in a soft British accent, a man in his late-twenties with a pale face and a comforting smile, as he holds Ducky up on the palm of his hand. The man returns to his usual routine of walking where it is that he needs to go. Then turns sharply and skips up a set of steps, pushing through some large wooden doors into the building. Where was Ducky being taken? As much as it may seem that it was just someone walking along the road that took a shine to the toy as he passed it on a busy street, you will soon come to realise that with this toy nothing is that simple. Everything with Ducky happens for a reason. The man places Ducky down on a piece of wood as he reaches for his zip, pulling down his navy puffer jacket and revealing a black collar with a white square in the centre, a roman collar. The man it seems… is a priest. The piece of wood… a bench, one of a row of benches set for the people entering the building. The building… a church, the priest was preparing for his morning service. Ducky may not have known exactly what was going on, but must have known that Dave was not his normal self and was possessed by something unholy and evil, so he’s gone and got the one thing that kicks the ever-living hell out of unholy things. He’s gone and got God… TO BE CONTINUED…
