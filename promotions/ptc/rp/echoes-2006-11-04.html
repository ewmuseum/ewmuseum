Echoes

4 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-04 .:: ::: ::. Outside of Dallas/Ft. Worth Four Winds Academy The cool of the morning permeated through the sprawling mansion in a way that ill befit Texas. Lia imagined, though, that it would indeed be difficult to keep a house this large from feeling a bit of a creeping chill once the ground started to dampen for the winter months. The house was church-quiet, so insulated in its remoteness that there were no birds to be heard in the pre-dawn hour. Her bare feet skimmed across the hardwood floor. She padded, cat-stealthy, into the room that Ian and Fey Childress referred to as 'Fey's sitting room.' Inside it was the object of her late-night ruminations, only a few hours ago- before the sun had begun to rise, before the dark of night had become infested with leaky gray blobs of adolescent sunlight. There it loomed in the half shadows. Even with the lack of light, she could see its fine details- the close-cut grain of the naturally ebony wood, the brass claw-feet at the base of each of its three legs, the strings inside gleaming metallic from the light spilling through the sheer curtains… but most of all, the beautiful keys- real ivory- that stood out starkly in the gloom. They called to her, a seductive but insistent siren's whisper, even from rooms and rooms away. She couldn't understand the lure- it was just a piano. But for days, ever since she had arrived here, a wordless urge had compelled her to stretch her fingers wide across the keys and let them dance liltingly- let them tell the stories that wound through her synapses, begging for expression that could not come through her lips, but would instead pour like hot light from her fingertips. Drawn ceaselessly on, she took the last few steps between her and the cumbersome vehicle of her emotion. She drew in a deep breath that nearly sparked with anticipation as she sat down to the bench. The cool wood pressed through her clothes and into the backs of her legs as she stretched her fingers hesitatingly to the keys. It was not quite electric as she had expected- instead, there was a willingness there, on that cold ivory. They had only been waiting in the darkness, waiting, and telling her I'm ready… tell your story. It had been years since she had sat down to play, almost too many to count, but it was muscle memory that never quite seemed to leave a person. A thousand songs ran through her mind, almost too many to choose from, but only one seemed to really stick out. Notes ran through her mind, sequences of them piled upon one another- a catalogue of the sheet music running through her head at a breakneck pace. Slowly, one bare foot depressed the damper pedal, which would allow the notes to ring longer, more sonorously clear. Slowly and quietly at first, her fingers sank into the keys with a soft, minor chord. Note poured upon note, and quietly became a procession, a march along the endless ivory steps that were willing lovers, plied for the joy of her interpretation. Rachmaninoff's second Piano Concerto, First Movement. She couldn't recall when she had first heard the song, with its striking beauty, its haunting sadness that rippled through the room as a somber eulogy before spiralling upwards into an ecstatic desperation of expression. She had loved the way the harmony vied with the dissonance for attention, the way it was impossible to choose which was more beautiful, because without the balance of its parts, the piece would not have been so stunning. She couldn't remember when she had learned how to play it, either. It was likely that at some point, she had sat down and played through it from memory of the tune, picking out notes- she had an ear for such things, which was surprising to those who thought her nothing more than a monster who had aesthetic appreciation for nothing but misery. She paused for a moment, something like pain spiking through her at the realization that her fingers had betrayed her- a crucial misstep on the timing, and she had thrown herself off. Didn't remember the next notes. She closed her eyes… "Ah…" Haltingly again, but with gathering speed, she went on, the desperation to express clamoring inside her with far too much anguish to be silenced by a lack of technical prowess, or a slip of memory. It was slow in coming, but the notes began to melt together, smoothly sliding into the first crescendo- a loud, disharmonious chord that made her catch her breath in her throat. Yes… And from there, the almost meek harmony of the following notes, an apology for the screaming dissonance of moments before. The arpeggios flowed across the keys, her fingers gliding with spidery accuracy through each motion, form perfect with wrists raised, fingers lifted high to allow each key to ring with its proper and allotted beauty. The room filled with sound as she pressed harder with her forearms- louder. To another crescendo, peaking with sharp chords and a pained expression on her face. It was a waterfall now- not even memory, but as though she was writing this piece herself for the very first time, instead of playing by rote the composition of another human being. Its plaintive and soft harmonies slip-slid fluidly through her, and for those few moments, her soul was at peace. It was this- this that she needed. The past few weeks had been a tumult of oppressive emotion. Highs and lows so pronounced that one joy was immediately accompanied by the question of just how severe the following crash was going to be. Almasy, Youngblood, Dawn, Ian Childress, Virgil, KB, Ascension, GCW, PTC, Kimbusa… water that pounded with flood force against her temples at all times, cresting and receding only to crest again with a brutally irresistible rhythm. One against the other, they danced- some thoughts mocking, others spiteful, others joyous, and still others doubtful, painful, wildly ecstatic… all opposed to one another in a tangoing whirlpool. Beneath its depths, she found herself near-smothered, hardly able to make proper sense of one before another came along and swept her off her feet, across the dance floor at a wild angle, steps too fast for her to keep up with. Then came the next, slow stately misery, with a cold, curling delicacy, prideful in its abortive movements, its unrealized grace. Caught in such tides, there were no words. There was no exit. A woman who had believed more strongly in hell might very well have come to believe that this was it- not full of fire and brimstone, not occupied with devils slinging pitchforks and operating racks and iron maidens… it was the simplest thing of all. Confinement with her own irrational, swirling emotions. Lia Vlaszy could not conscience surrendering to it- surrender had never been her course of action, even when she had been beaten to the point where rising again would have been unceasingly foolish. She would not die on her knees. She would not succumb to her own mind- she would not, could not allow herself to self destruct. Delicate, delicate now. It was the balance of all things. The harmony of all emotions. It was what she yearned for, yet it was something that was only expressed through these keys, and regretfully not inside of her. The yearning drove her so powerfully that it was the reason she had opened her eyes for the past two, three weeks. Would today be the day that the answers came? Would today be the release of these shackles? No, not today. Not tomorrow either. Days stretched end over end into the brutal clarity of the rising sun, taunting her with the idea that she would never know her answers. That she would never know her peace. It shook her down to the core, and the music shook with her, trembling to its conclusion, shrill and delicate treble bird calls in the morning with the fading, dying growl of the wolves that populated the night. Pared down to its essence, it was nothing more than a song. But as her eyes slowly flickered open, as though from a trance, her weary, troubled spirit felt a little lifted. At the very least, she had spoken her despair to the morning air, though no one else would hear it. No one else needed to. "Shostakovich?" The voice jolted her out of the trembling afterglow, and she turned slightly on the bench to see Ian Childress leaning against the window, staring out into the Texas dawn. He seemed unaffected- but something in his tone of voice belied that. Perhaps she was imagining it. "Rachmaninoff," she answered, and her own voice sounded slightly ragged in the quiet. Ian nodded, and turned towards her, taking a couple of steps towards the woman he now called 'student.' "It ah… sounded Russian…" he hesitated for a moment, then went on, "Xian used to play all the time… that kind of stuff…" She had never heard him talk much about his ex-wife, and she tilted her head at him with a characteristic, but almost wholly empty gesture of curiosity. He didn't answer her, but examined her more closely, with the hole-boring intensity of a man who had learned that most things are not, in fact, as they seem. "What's been going on with you lately?" The question was gruff, not concerned as one might expect of a mentor. She shrugged it off, glancing away from him. "Things…" she trailed off, knowing that it would never be a good enough answer. She pressed on, haltingly, "I just… there aren't any words to explain the chaos inside… one moment up, the next down… I find a footing, and then lose it mere seconds later…" Between the two of them was a silent communication for a moment- as though Lia was reaching out insofar as she could, attempting to bare a gouging wound that she had been keeping secret for so long that it had begun to fester. "Lia," he said, resting a hand on the edge of the piano, "whatever it is… erase it. Whatever it is, deal with it now, because without striking some kind of balance in yourself, you're going to bury yourself with your own hands." She sat up straighter, her eyes protesting, her brain smarting with the effort to not let it all spill forth. "But… how?" "I can't do that for you," he returned, "You've got instincts just as sharp as mine. But you're the only one who knows where your own center is. It's up to you to find it." Her fingers clenched against her thigh. It wasn't that easy- he didn't understand- she'd been looking already… all things she wanted to say, but all things that died on her lips. Ian looked away, and cleared his throat. "I just came down to check on you… Fey will be expecting me back in bed. I'll see you later." She nodded in mute acceptance, watched him turn and leave the room, his long dark hair swinging across his back, though uncovering briefly a tattoo of a beautiful woman- a woman who looked way too realistic to be some artist's creation. She gathered her strength, and pushed up to a standing position. She was unsteady for a moment, reeling on the heavy emotions that still fought within, more insistent than ever. Inside, the echoes of that otherworldly harmony still resounded. To wring forth the melody, she had only to find the proper keys.
