Rough Times, Hombre. Rough Times.

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 Mexican prisons: They smell like ass. The guards are drunk and unruly. And the prisoners doubly-so. The meal-plan consists of a daily ration of warm water, stale bread, and rice and beans for dinner. The upside being, if you put enough salt on the slop at dinner, you fortunately won’t be honored with the taste of flies. (Dead ones.) The toilets rarely work. And when they do, the last asshole usually just happened to plug the drainage up with a bag of weed he tried to smuggle up his ass- but failed at- and soon, the basin is overflowing. Sewage lines your floor. Your confined to the comforts of your rusty spring metal mattress. And then when you do manage to dry the weed enough to smoke, you feel like an idiot for smoking something that’s been up someone’s ass. Needless to say, Emilio himself felt like a dope. Bottled up in the confines of El Alcatraz with nothing to do and no one to beat up, Emilio was beginning to feel a little under the weather. Granted, it had been only three days since he last stepped foot on free soil. He remembers it well: Basking in the glow of thousands of roaring fans as he prepared to put Aneglo Deville away for good. But then that bastard, Nelson, had to make his appearance… You see, the game Emilio was playing with the former president of PCW was a dangerous one. And Nelson had decided to raise the stakes. As strong as Emilio’s wrath was, and as resilient as a competitor he was known to be, Nelson was not one to back down from the opportunity to screw someone over. Thuroughly. Even if it meant him losing a little of his own blood later…. It had been nearly a month since that shocking night when Tyler Nelson revealed himself to be the CEO of Nelty Investments, the firm that was helping PRIME "get back on its feet." And although they gave PRIME a blank check to carry out its upgrades, Emilio wasn’t the only one with the distinct feeling that no strings were attached. Because if anything rang universally true around PTC, it was that Tyler Nelson was the ultimate master of puppets. Emilio, however, had known all this was coming for quite some time. He was forced to bite his lip when he heard the news. Laid up in the house for six months, recuperating from a broken back, broken ribs, and a concussion, he was in no position to launch a holy war against one of the most insidious men in the history of professional wrestling, let alone return to the ring. But that damned conscience. Sometimes Emilio just wished he could find the damn thing and kick it in the balls. Whenever he seemed to be on the right path in life, the logical path, the path that brought him happiness, that damn conscience managed to get the best of him. This time was no different. So he packed up his begs, said his goodbyes to his daughter, Sylvia, and sent her off to live with her family in Florida. He didn’t want to let her go. No. Not after being without her for fifteen long years. But he was without options. The idea being, as dangerous as a man Nelson is, better not give him the opportunity or the opening to hurt you. Because he will. So Emilio protected her. He didn’t leave the opening. But in the process he furnished another opening in his heart. One of pain. But what did he receive for his sacrifices, his troubles? After coming back to win the Trick or Treat match, ensuring him a match contract that could give him any match he wanted, anytime, anyplace, with any opponent…After defeating Danny Ferguson…After taking the fight to Nelson…Right on the brink of ending Deville…Were did he end up? In jail. Deported, actually. And the sad part was, he wasn’t even a Mexican citizen! But as such is the structure of power. With Nelson’s power came access. And with access came the call to the President of the United States. And so when that dumbass got the call –"Hey, George. Yeah, it’s me, Tyler Nelson. Hey, I have this little problem on my hand with some Mexican…I was wondering if you could just…Oh, you can! That’d be great!"-he opted not to check into it and do something as petty as pull up the file with Emilio’s green card. So, because of one quick phone call- and probably a line of coke- Emilio somehow ended up rotting in a jail cell some one-hundred miles south of El Paso, Texas. (Emilio is from Nicaragua, ironically. Much better cocaine. Go figure.) There you have it. ~ DAY ONE. Emilio now stood with his back to the small hole they drilled out of the concrete wall that dubbed "a window" –well, "la ventatana," but, whatever…- trying to get a guard’s attention. He managed to warrant a prolonged belch by a fat mat wearing a sombrero in the corner. The guard’s belly protruded gently under his ragtag uniform as he lay back in deep slumber. He let out a slight cough, but turned on his side and went back to sleep. Close, but no cigar. Emilio began to utter obscenities under his breath. He knew that if he could simply get in touch with a lawyer –or some representative from PRIME- he’d have the unfortunate situation rectified. Immediately. You see, the good thing about the Mexican penal system is that you can bribe your way out of the death penalty. No joke, my friend. But although Emilio wasn’t exactly on the lower-end of the momentary ladder, he struggled passionately with the prison clerk for five hours his first night here, trying to convince him of the money he had accumulated from professional wrestling. The effort had been fruitless. But Emilio caught the warden peeing drunkenly in the corner earlier in the day, and he had promised Emilio another hearing by the clerk tomorrow. Now, sitting on his bed, slowly digesting what the cook had told him was a "taco," Emilio just hoped the drunken warden would (A), remember the conversation at all, and (B), act upon his promise…before Emilio died of food poisoning or a hernia. Possibly both. At the same time. ~ DAY TWO. His promise had in fact been kept. But the ensuing conversation didn’t quite go as nicely as Emilio would have hoped for… "No, senor. I’m serious! …I’m filthy frigin rich!" The prison clerk merely chuckled, however, pouring himself another glass of tequila. "Ah, yes. I see…" And he poured one for Emilio as well. "Here. Take it." Emilio tossed the metal cup handed to him down to the floor. The clattering noise caused a stir throughout the fine institution. "Look! I am not a drunk," Emilio pleaded, his chest heaving angrily in and out. "I am real millionaire, I’m a former PTC Global Champion…and for Christ’s sake," he pleaded, "There’s a scorpion floating in the water covering my floor due to the flood from the toilet. It’s inching closer….I think it wants to eat me." The clerk shrugged, whipping out a pistol and promptly firing at the scorpion. The bullet ricocheted off from the surface of the water and zinged across the room where it took out a light. A roused guard got out of his seat, quickly surveyed the scene, shrugged, and went back to sleep. Emilio was curled up in his bed with his hands covering his ears. "God help me…" "Of course you’re not a millionaire," the clerk responded, drunkenly. If you were a millionaire, that’d make me Rick James, bitch!" Despite his poor impersonation of Dave Chapelle, it somehow tickled the clerk’s funny-bone, causing him to fall back on the table and knock over a bottle of tequila. Emilio chuckled. The clerk got back to his feet and wiped off his knees. "What are you looking at. Gringo!? At least I ain’t got no fantasies like you, you crazy man!" The clerk undid his belt, ripped off his underpants, and placed them over his head. He took another shot of tequila before running up and down the rows of cells, yelling, "Look at me! Ooh! I’m a professional wrestler, senor! Look at me! Ohh!," at the top of his lungs. Emilio sighed. He curled up in the fetal position on the bare metal mattress as the springy bed continued to assault the muscles in his back. He froze his balls off in the cold desert night with no blanket as a coyote howled off in the distance. His stomach was empty and his head hurt. Finally, Emilio drifted off to sleep as the drunken clerk sang "Hit Me Baby One More Time" softly at his desk. ~ DAY THREE. "Essa?" Emilio gripped the rusty bars and leaned his out into a barren hallway. "Hey, essa? Can I get some water, please?" "Shaddup!" the guard retorted, beating the side of the wall with his billy club. "I’m trying to get some sleep here." The walls were painted white but appeared yellow. Years of smoke from the cheap cigs and cigars of both the inmates and guards left their ugly stain in place. When the guard’s club made contact with the surface, a thin layer of paint ripped off, exposing the rotten wood on the other side. "Oh, shit…" said the guard, as he looked up at the wall, pieces of sheetrock falling onto his face. "Eh…what can you do?" he asked, taking a seat and drifting back to sleep. "Just askin’, dude…." Said Emilio, returning to sit on the edge of his bed. "…If I don’t get out of here soon," Emilio said, putting another joint in his mouth, "I’m gonna have to bust out before I go mad…" Emilio’s train of thought was erupted, however, when a small procession of guards made their way into the hall… A short, black haired man in tow. Hin See’s personal lawyer! Emilio quickly rubbed out the joint and sat cross-legged on his bed, staring out the window. "Emilio…" He pretended not to hear the Japanese lawyer. "Emilio?" "Oh!," said Emilio, pretending to be startled as he turned to face the lawyer. "Why Mr. Toshinka, what are you doing here!?" The lawyer rolled his eyes. "Took you guys long enough, huh?" said Emilio, no longer hiding his dissatisfaction. "Well, aren’t you happy I’m here now?" asked the lawyer. "Why you little…" Emilio reached through the bars and grabbed the lawyer by the collar, shaking him violently. "That’s enough," said the guard, stepping up to the cell with a key in his hand. "Mr. Rage, it appears you are free to go." And with that, the door was unlocked and Emilio was again a free man. "Just one more thing before we release you," the warden said. "It smells like weed in here," he said, nodding in Emilio’s direction. "Mr. Rage," he said, pulling out his handcuffs, "I’m afraid you’re under arrest." Emilio’s face went pale as he slid away from the door and back onto his crappy cot. "…Just kidding. But dawg, give me a hit of that shit!" Emilio let out a sigh. "No, really, dude" Somewhere in the background, the drunken clerk misfired his gun again. Everyone laughed. ~ Emilio didn’t say much on the way home. Whenever he and Mr. Toshinka conversed, it was in Japanese. The Mexican escorts –possibly on the payroll of Nelson- were left in the dark on the two hour trip back to the good ol’ U.S.-of-A. Emilio had little patience to converse anyway. For when he got back home, he knew what he needed to do. And to whom. After Billy Blackwell had been knocked out of the picture, bringing Emilio to the second of GTT5, the former president of PCW would be next…
