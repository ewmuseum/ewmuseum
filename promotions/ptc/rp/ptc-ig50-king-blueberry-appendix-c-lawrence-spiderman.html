Dave Hurst versus King Blueberry

5 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(King Blueberry) Appendix C :: Lawrence Spiderman Appendix C :: Lawrence Spiderman If I had to wager a guess, Lawrence Spiderman, my agent, would punch me in the face if given the chance. I realize that he pronounces his name ‘speeder-min’, and that’s all well and good, but c’mon. How can a guy with a name like Spiderman expect me to do anything but give him a hard time for it. It’s just funny, at least to me, though I realize that sort of thing tends to go against any conceivable definition of the word ‘professionalism’. I got his number from the guys at Stracker’s, the school where I got my start in the ring, and our first few conversations were pretty good. I know he’d represented a few of the other guys to come out of Darren’s place, and he had a pretty good reputation from what they told me. “Hi Jared, nice to talk to you,” this was the first thing he said to me on the phone, and it was probably the only time he’s ever had that thought. “Darren told me you’d be calling. So, what can I do for you?” For the record, this is not a question that anyone should ever ask of a man who’s planning on dressing up like a blueberry, especially if he hasn’t yet told you that he plans to dress up like a blueberry. It was two weeks of business by phone before we met in person. Spiderman’s office was in New York, but seeing as I wasn’t gainfully employed at the time it was easy enough for me to hop on a train and rocket on down. The train ride gave me time to think about whether I was making the right call or not. I’m pretty sure we know how that turned out. The first time I met him I did it as myself, Jared Sykes, and not under the mask of “King Blueberry”. I sat alone in the waiting room, since Merv’ and Lloyd had stayed back in Boston. The girl at the desk – I think her name was Stacey, was kind of cute, and I couldn’t help but notice that she was shooting the occasional glance my way. Maybe it was because she thought I was attractive, maybe it’s because I was reading a copy of Highlights for Children. That Goofus is such a douche bag. Larry smiled a lot that afternoon, at least at first. I think he was excited about the prospect of a new client, as anyone in business for himself would be. “So tell me about yourself,” he asked, a question that I think he’d regret in, oh, say, seven seconds. “Well, I’ve been a professional wrestler for the better part of 9 years, held a few titles along the way, and represented the National Wrestling Council as its J-Crown – that means cruiserweight, champion for the second longest reign in history both in days and defenses.” Larry just nodded along for a while, but I wasn’t done. “My closest associates are a nerd and a man who thinks he’s Jesus. I used to carry around a porcelain lawn ornament, a gnome, that I called Earl. He was great for bonking people in the head with.” He stopped nodding, kind of looked at me funny, but because I’m me my mouth just kept on runnin’. “I had a highly-publicized relationship with a hermaphrodite once, had a trans-dimensional experience involving some guys who called themselves the Monks of Funk, and then Gandhi fought Frank Sinatra on the Astral Plane for the rights to my soul.” Larry just kind of stared at me. It probably took an awful lot of willpower for him to keep his jaw from swinging open like a rusty gate. “I’m pretty sure they did it Street Fighter style.” “Uhh,” his response was about par for the course. Here’s the thing about me: most people would pause at the ‘uh’, but most people probably wouldn’t have confessed all the nonsense that I did. I’m not most people. “But there’s more, and it’s pretty awesome…” I have a serious problem shutting up. “I’m thinking of coming back under a mask. Now I realize that it’s kind of clichéd, and I hate clichés with a passion, but I don’t think anyone has ever done anything like what I plan to do.” Larry actually responded to this one, which kind of surprised me, though admittedly his response was pretty short. “And that is?” “Well, I’m going to dress up like a giant blueberry.” You’d think it was a miracle that he didn’t kick me out of his office right then and there. “Yeah, I’m going to start calling myself King Blueberry, ruler of the fruit kingdom. And since I figure that since every king needs a court I’m going to pull in two other people, Mervin my personal nerd and Lloyd my personal Violent Jesus, and stick them under masks as well.” While I was talking I noticed that Larry would sometimes glance at one of the drawers in his desk. I figured it was probably where he kept the magic button that called security, or maybe opened the trap doors. Hell, if Larry had trap doors I would have immediately made him my hero for life. “I’m going to put Mervin under a mask and call him Blueberry Dragon. I think I’m also going to put Lloyd under a mask as well, except I’ll call him Blueberry Mask 14. I don’t know who’ll get the joke, but the whole thing is basically a send-up of the history of masked wrestlers.” Larry swallowed hard. I think he was wondering what he got himself into. He could have fired me as a client, could have had security escort me from the building, but he didn’t do either. Part of me thinks he was honest-to-God curious about whether this would be the magnificent train wreck he imagined.
