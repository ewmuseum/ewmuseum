Garbage Bag Johnny WILL win GTT6 (Maybe not THAT improbable) Part 5

18 November 2006

Date: 2006-11-18 Apparently, when Agent Dash was all high on ecstasy, but before he started rubbing soap on himself, he urinated on his discarded shirt and suit coat. I hadn’t noticed the odor when I was carrying the articles of clothing, but he refused to wear them afterwards, so instead, I fashioned a poncho for him by cutting a larger hole for him to fit his neck through in the ghost disguises. Nova still claimed to hear some phantom noise haunting his engine while I explained the situation with Julius Jones to Agent Dash. "Basically, Dash, Julius Jones is a rival paranormal investigator who’s been looking to put us out of business. You see, paranormal investigation isn’t the most reputable of endeavors yet, so there are a lot of bad seeds in the business who don’t play by the rules. Julius Jones may even be after us as we speak." "And you said he shot one of our old team members?" "Yeah, he’s the guy you replaced. But you knew all about it before the whole amnesia thing kicked in, so there’s no turning back now. He’d kill you no matter what, so it’s better that we stay in a group." Dash nodded solemnly before Nova tried adding to the story. "Some people say that Julius Jones himself is possessed by an evil spirit. How evil, you ask? So evil that the spirit inhibiting the body is in a line of business where he kills other spirits and apparitions in a somewhat cannibalistic, or at least genocidal, nature. I don’t know if his war is on mankind or his own fellow specters. I heard even that he wants to be the only one left…damn it! Did you hear it that time?" Both Dash and I shrugged at each other as Nova angrily hit his steering wheel. We weren’t yet out of farm country, and Nova was growing ever the more paranoid no matter how many joints I rolled and passed around to try and calm him down. My thumbs were tired from all that rolling! "Alright, so long story short, Julius Jones is probably after us, and if he catches us, he’s going to try to kill us. So if you…what the fuck was that?" This time I did hear a noise, something that sounded like metallic sickness as the motor moaned out in distress through the empty night. Then a few scrapes wheeled in rotation as a heavy friction roared out bringing the van to a halt. Nova tried to restart the van several times to no avail before pounding his fist on the dashboard and cursing under his breath. "We’re going to have to see if we can get a jump. Did you see how many miles it was to the next town?" "Yeah, it’s still twenty three miles away," I said. "There’s no way we’re walking that far." Dash chimed in with an ignorant suggestion. "Maybe we could just walk to one of these farmhouses." We had kept him nourished with weed, but he seemed completely oblivious to the farmers in the diner when he was on ecstasy. I looked at Nova, "Well, if we stay here, either Julius Jones or the mob of farmers is bound to catch up to us, and walking won’t do us much good either. That might be our only hope. We should all be fine as long as we stick together." Nova tried thinking of a safer idea, but he knew our choices were very limited. The three of us were forced to get out of the van and walk about a half mile down the nearest drive until we could even see the farmhouse. It was very secluded, protected from the wind and from sight by a wall of trees with a square clearing in the middle just around the house. There was a pickup truck parked at the end of the driveway. "Hopefully he has some jumper cables," I said. There was an air of eeriness that surrounded the place. The cows mooed in the night, still out at pasture- just standing there swaying like they had all been lobotomized. Some of them slept, but the ones that didn’t mooed through the night with croaking reverberation. They sounded more like men imitating cows. Nova and I were hesitant to knock on the front door, but Agent Dash showed his ignorant bravado by rapping on the door rather forcefully. Nobody answered for a while, and I figured the farmer was already asleep and hadn’t heard the knocking. I figured that it was best to not disturb him. "Come on, let’s go. He’s not answering." I indicated that I was in kind of a rush to leave. "Hold on," Dash said, and since he was slow to move, I furrowed my brow and cursed quietly because then we all heard several latches being unlocked and several locks being turned. The barrel of a shot gun nuzzled it’s way through a tiny crack in the door, preceding the man holding it while the door opened a little more, shining some porch light on his face. "Sorry to disturb you, sir," Agent Dash politely greeted, "but our van broke down out on the road in front of your house and we were wondering if you could give us a jump." The farmer sniffed around, apparently to detect us by smell. He could smell the way our scents affected the smell of his crops. The odors mixed together faintly and fomented sourly in his long nostrils. He had some kind of face about him. His hair was matted and had kept its brown though the lines and spots on his face showed his age. He had the same color brown mustache that shagged a bit over his upper lip along with a pointy nose and eyes that were too close together. He wore a white, sleeveless undershirt and sweatpants to bed, but he smelled like the soil and the souls of his crops. He retracted the gun a bit. "I can give you a jump in the morning, but you’ll have to stay in the guest room tonight and prove you’re good people." The three of us looked at each other. This could have easily been some kind of trap, but it was too obvious to be a trap. That’s what confused us. Neither Nova nor I noticed this man at the diner or seen him driving around the small town. There was a difference between him and other farmers. I could smell it too in the air- some strange odor coming from what he had planted. "I guess that’s alright. How can we prove that we’re all good people?" Nova asked. I was taken aback that he was the one to answer given his paranoia issues. "It’s simple," the farmer said. "All you have to do is not sleep with my daughter." The man opened the door letting us in, and I have to admit being a little bit confused when the girl behind him was tugging at the pants on the back of his thigh. She barely came up to his waist. If I was an estimating man, and I’m not, I would have guessed she was about six years old. "Daddy? Can I play with the shotgun?" "Not tonight, honey. It’s past your bedtime. Go on and get back to your room." The little girl, who was clad in pink foot pajamas, skipped back up to her room while the farmer showed us to the guest room. It was a pretty plain room with a very large bed that took up the most of it. The bed was large enough so that Dash, Nova, and I could all comfortably fit in it without feeling gay. We closed the door behind us and shut off the light. It was easier to fall asleep than I would have imagined. It must have been the smell of the whole place. It had a very pacifying fragrance. *** The next morning, or should I say early afternoon, when we woke up, I noticed a strange amount of space in the bed. Nova was gone, and the door, which I could have sworn was closed when we went to sleep, was wide open. "Dash, wake up!" I started shaking him by the shoulder, prompting him to open his eyes. "Do you know where Nova went?" Dash rolled over and shook his head, coming to the same realization that had hit me earlier. The strange thing was that Nova was sleeping in between us, so if someone had come to drag him away in the middle of the night, we would have heard it. Nova, then, must have gotten up under his own power. Perhaps he had just woken up already, but it was unlike Nova to wake up earlier than necessary. That lazy, bearded motherfucker was always sleeping. We decided to venture out of the room, only to see the farmer in from the fields already. When it wasn’t growing season, he’d go out to spend time with the land, but he’d always be in by the afternoon. His little daughter was on his lap as he sat in his rocking chair, staring straight forward. He looked to be in a trance or out of his body. He just rocked there, stroking his daughters head; infuriation bubbling up to push at a falsely calm-looking exterior. I approached him with caution. "Excuse me," I started with a pessimistic politeness, "but have you seen our other friend around today?" He stared for a while, getting the rest of his staring finished off before issuing his delayed response. "I seen him. He’s out in the field." I found this a little strange. Nova really had no reason to be traipsing about in a field, so my fears were not completely assuaged. Still, I figured I’d go look for him. I thanked the farmer for his information with a gesture and headed towards the door to look for Nova. "You’re not going to see him out there," said the farmer, now standing up as he realized I was going for the door. "He’s buried." My heart stopped. Buried? I couldn’t think of anything but that word, and catching a glimpse of Dash out of my peripherals, I could see that he too was in a state of near shock. Buried! I knew we shouldn’t have trusted these farmers. I could see genuine guilt arching in Dash’s facial expressions. It was the way he swallowed all at once. Still I couldn’t hate him like I wanted to hate him. I could tell he never meant for any of this to happen. But buried? "I caught him in bed with my daughter when I woke up just before sunrise." The farmer continued speaking, not looking at me in particular, but just looking there inches in front of him. His voice was frank and direct. It cut through the air like a sharpened knife hacking. This set my dormant mind spinning. His daughter was a young child, and as long as I had known Nova, I couldn’t detect any pedophilic tendencies in his behavior. He had always worn a full beard, but never just a mustache. Sick sex charades weren’t among his many vices, and he had enough vices already. Still, that cold voice left no room for question. It wasn’t spoken like a lie, and as hard as it was to believe, I found myself unable to defy the farmer’s claim- I choked on my defiance!- even with my severe distrust for farmers as a whole. "I knocked him over the head with my shotgun barrel on account of that I don’t have any bullets and buried him alive in the field somewhere. He’s probably long dead by now. There ain’t much room for breathin’ if you’re buried in a field." I immediately ran out the door, holding onto any hope that Nova was alive. Dash followed behind swiftly as I ran to the field just behind the house. In daylight, it was sickeningly clear. It looked like more of a mass grave than a field. Patches of dirt in shapes resembling rectangles dotted the field between the remaining trails of grass. Several looked fresh. There were too many to count, and we didn’t have a shovel. I doubt the farmer would have let us borrow one. "I don’t even remember which one’s his. It’s best you two get your belongings and get on out of here." The farmer had appeared behind us from out of nowhere. Dash and I both began to freak out as unnoticeably as we could before booking it back towards the van. Then I remembered the drugs. "Hold on, Dash. I think Nova left his stuff in the room. I’m going to go get it. His van keys might be up there." Dash nodded and decided to wait. Luckily, he followed me into the house at a distance. As soon as I had gotten back to the front room, I was startled to see not the little girl, but a young woman standing there invitingly in black negligee. She had an amorphously alluring form with perfect curves that seemed to slope and glide off of each other in beautiful oscillations. Her eyes froze me, and as she moved her arms, I was beckoned to follow as if she was guiding me by telekinesis. She began to lead me up the stairs, but my concentration was broken when I was physically jerked from behind. Agent Dash had me by the waist, and both of us went tumbling backward. I snapped out of my trance as soon as my line of sight was ripped from her eyes. "Thanks," I groaned in pain to Dash, who had just rescued me. I could tell he was in pain, too, taking the brunt of the fall. "Don’t mention it." "I still need to get Nova’s stuff. You’re going to have to let her pull me towards her, and while she’s distracted with me, you have to go run in and get that wooden box on the floor in the guest room. Then, you’re going to have to pull me away from the trance again- just don’t aim for the stairs." I would risk anything to get my box. Without it we had nothing. No money. No drugs. And we probably wouldn’t have Nova’s keys either way. I’m pretty sure he kept them in his pocket. Dash nodded and took off as fast as he could as I caught my eyes on hers again and she began to pull me towards a door further down the hallway. I saw her back into the door, her motions blindly practiced, and I began to try and call out for Dash, but when I tried to form the words "help me," I was surprised to hear my own voice spewing out dirtier sex talk than I’d ever imagined myself capable of imagining. It was something about licking my prostate or some funny business like that, but it was much more colorfully described. Whatever I said, Dash had heard me and rushed to my aid, knocking me stiffly against the wall and dragging me down the stairs with the wooden box cradled under his arm. When I caught my bearings at the bottom of the stairs, I grabbed the box, and we both ran out of the front door and a long way towards the road. I slowed down a bit, unable to keep up with Dash due to our differing lung capacities and overall physical conditions. I took this time to open the box and look inside. Everything was there- not the keys- but everything that should have been there in the first place. *** With Nova’s conversion van now useless, Dash and I walked on with our thumbs in the air calling for rides. Unfortunately, there were many things working against us as far as hitchhiking was concerned. Neither of us looked like the safest of passengers to pick up, I having grown well used to my look of homeless mimicry and Dash with his bed sheet poncho. Also, it seemed as though the road wasn’t used very often. Even the cars that passed us by were rarities, and it was still miles and miles to the nearest town. We walked on, though, with no other option, and I started to feel a bit bad about removing Agent Dash from his occupation and his home to drag him along on this catastrophic series of events. After walking began to grow futile, I broke down. I didn’t see the point in walking any further, and I sat down by the side of the road, seriously contemplating telling the truth to Agent Dash. Two of my friends had already been killed, Julius Jones was still on my trail, and as far as Dash knew, there was nothing he could do to help. Maybe turning myself in would be for the better. "Why’d you stop, Johnny? We still have a ways to go before we’re away from the threat posed by farm country." "I can’t keep running like this. Just leave me here. They won’t come after you unless you’re with me." I spoke nobly, but really, I only spoke this way because I was in a temperamental disposition. I was acting gloomy because I hadn’t eaten. "I’m not just going to leave you here to die," said Dash in an equal display of disgruntled nobility. Oh, how pride would get us both killed. He sat down next to me. "I feel bad enough as it is that it was my suggestion that got Nova killed. Having that on my conscience is bad enough as it is." The moment may have been more beautiful if the sun was shining and some orchestral score was hovering above us, but it was silent and overcast and I felt as ugly as the sky. Perhaps if the weather was nicer, I wouldn’t have said anything, but I really just wanted to be alone right now. Julius Jones would have never gotten to me. I could have cleared out that box of drugs in no time and overdosed my way out of this situation. Then again, that was probably just the hunger. "Look, Dash. I made up all that shit about us being a team of paranormal investigators." "Yeah, I kind of put that together when there weren’t any weird gadgets in the van." "Then why didn’t you say anything?" "It was the drugs. At first when I woke up in the van, tied up with all of those bungee cables, all I could think about was my problems. And even before the amnesia kicked in, I’m sure I had just as many problems plaguing my thoughts. That’s not something the amnesia made me forget; I just sensed it. And while I was high, they disappeared." "No, they were just hidden, but you can’t obscure them forever. And the more you ignore them, the worse they are when you finally have to face them." He frowned a bit, and I opened up the wooden box and started digging around. Strangely, I passed over some of the more interesting hallucinogens that we had yet to use and grabbed the bag of pot. But then, I thought about how hungry I was, and how much hungrier I would be if I smoked, and how there were a couple ounces of mushrooms in there, and how at least they were something to eat. "Magic mushrooms?" I offered Dash, who despite my warnings, was still looking into the box salivating as I went through it. He nodded fervently, and we ate the mushrooms raw, ignoring their staleness. "Dash, I know you still want to stick around, but there’s more I haven’t told you." "Yeah?" he asked, chewing. "I was the one who gave you amnesia. I threw a rock at you and it hit you pretty hard in the temple. You’re an FBI agent, and you were looking for me. I panicked." He shrugged. "Sorry, man, but I don’t remember any of that." His lip curled. He looked more so hurt than betrayed, but I could see he was flexing his elastic powers of forgiveness, and I just simply hung my head and wondered why. "In any case, I’d feel a lot better if you just went on and left me here." He shook his head stubbornly. "I can’t, man. Maybe things will come to me, but I’m not an FBI agent right now. I’m just as screwed as you’ll be if we split up, so we might as well salvage what little chance we have at getting out of this unscathed." I probably ate a whole quarter ounce of mushrooms, just because I was that hungry. I made sure Dash didn’t take as much as I did, because I didn’t know if he’d start freaking out or something. With his refusal to leave, I felt a little better, and maybe he’d appreciate my honesty even after his memory started to come back. That modicum of hope was enough to get me back to my feet. I pulled Dash up to his feet again, and we both started walking and waiting for the mushrooms to make the trek a bit more enjoyable. Before the drugs kicked in, though, we saw dust rising back along the road, and a car raced into view. It was completely black from the chassis to the windows- tinted visually impenetrable. I shouted out the first four and a half words that came into my head. "Fuck! It’s Julius Jones." Dash and I began running as fast as we could, but we both knew we couldn’t outrun a car. We veered off the road and began tearing through fields. Still, we could hear the engine roaring louder and louder as it approached us, and fifty yards away from us where the car had stopped in line with our escape, we heard a gunshot ring out in the air. Instinctually, we both dropped to the ground. I looked back. A man in a black trench coat had fired a shot into the air. He was light skinned, though, and I could tell he was old because of the way his sleek hair shined gray even with the loitering, threatening storm clouds blocking out the sun. These were not Julius Jones’ men. "Agent Jim Cooley, FBI!" the man who had fired the shot shouted, trailed by his partner, a brown haired man of similar stature. "I want you both to stay down on the ground. We’ll interpret any movement as a threat and open fire." Dash and I whispered to each other as the two federal agents approached, guns drawn and aimed without the slightest hint of unsteadiness. "Dash, you’re going to have to get us out of this one." "How?" "Just tell them you’re an FBI agent. You still have your badge in your pants pocket?" "I think so." I could tell he was beginning to reach for it. "Don’t move now! They’ll shoot! Just explain to them that you’re an FBI agent and that your badge is in your pocket." "But I don’t remember the first thing about being an FBI agent." "That’s fine. The badge should prove it. Just tell them that I drugged you. Tell them that I kidnapped you and that I’ve been slipping drugs into your food to keep you sedated." He nodded because they were close enough so that we could hear their footsteps now. The FBI agents cuffed Dash and I, but Dash played his part so beautifully I could tell that secret agency came naturally to him. After they ran his badge and checked him out, they removed his handcuffs. I smiled at him, still on the ground under the weight of the brown haired agent. He was a bit heavier than the gray haired agent. Apparently, I was being brought in on the grounds of kidnapping, drugging, and assaulting an FBI agent. I still didn’t know why they had come after me in the first place, but I assumed it had something to do with the investigation of Adam Dick’s death. They needed someone to pin the death onto in order to keep their bribes coming in from Julius Jones and his underworld empire. It just so happened that I would be convenient for both sides. I should have figured in that the FBI would go tracking me after Dash inexplicably went missing. He was one of their agents, after all, and I suppose that things like this didn’t happen too often. They had a way of finding their own. But at least now, I had Dash on my side. He was in a position now to get us both out of danger and set things straight. We’d just have to nail Julius Jones (the one part of the story I fed him that I hadn’t made up), and I’d be a free man. The two agents read me my rights and did a purposefully careless job of directing me into the back seat. Cooley sat next to me, insisting that Dash take time to recover in the passenger’s seat while the larger man with the brown hair drove. I heard Agent Cooley refer to him as DiBenedetto, and then later as Mike more conversationally. I didn’t know where they were taking me, but I didn’t much care at that point. The drugs were really starting to kick in. *** They took me to a small police station in the nearest town that had one. The town itself was still small, and really, the cells were only used as drunk tanks for the most part. The agents caused quite a ruckus when they walked in, flashing their superior badges and demanding that the cops either cram all the drunks into another cell or send them on home for the night. It was more important, apparently, that I be confined to my own cell after some questioning in the back room. The FBI agents left Dash to be attended to by the local cops until he came down from the mushrooms. Looking back at it, it’s kind of funny. I heard that the two agents had tried to interrogate me while I was peaking, and every time they asked me a question, I’d just burst into hysterics. It all culminated when Agent Cooley asked me if I thought this was all some kind of joke, and I laughed so hard that I peed myself a little bit. Then Cooley smacked me in the jaw pretty hard and told DiBenedetto to lock me up until I was in a more cooperative state. Being stuck in a cell wasn’t so bad at all when I was tripping because I was so lost in the detail of the place that I would’ve been content examining every crack in the floor and every piece of dirt and all the stale cocktails of breath that lingered around the place. But eventually, I started feeling in that awful, angry way that you feel when you know the drugs are losing their effect. The comedown was excruciating, and I quickly learned how dull and lifeless the place was. The claustrophobia began to sober me, and I too felt buried. Agent Dash had come down, too, and the three agents were all there. I shouted at all of them generally, but I was really just trying to get Dash to pick the other two feds for information. "I want to know what you all were originally after me for. I swear I didn’t do anything. I didn’t kill nobody!" I began slamming my fists into the bars and trying to rattle the unmoving cage. "Let’s let him cool down in there a little longer," Cooley said cleverly. Damn him! And then the clever bastard tilted his head and signaled DiBenedetto and Dash into the back room. They were in there for a while, and when they came out, I was still raging in the cell, asking what they came after me for in the first place. "I don’t think you need to know quite yet. We’ve got twenty four hours until we’re violating habeas corpus laws. Isn’t that right DiBenedetto?" DiBenedetto grunted, and Agent Dash, feigning anger walked up to me; bars between us. He reached through and grabbed my neck with both hands, choking me violently as Cooley and DiBenedetto laughed at his zeal. I was a bit angry at first, even if he was just acting, because it didn’t feel much like Dash was acting. But then I noticed a piece of paper scratching against my neck, so I grabbed his hands to try and free myself while really just clandestinely accepting the note. I kept it folded up in my balled fist from the time Dash released his chokehold to the time all three agents went outside for a cigarette while I rotted in there a while longer. The note said that Dash had found out that he was originally after me for racketeering and organizing underground fights between the homeless. Truly, I was quite the racketeer, but it was Adam Dick who organized the fights. That’s what managers do. I only fought in the homeless fights. Surely there’s nothing wrong with pretending to be homeless in order to beat up homeless people for their money, and until I see such legislature in writing, I refuse to believe otherwise. The note also said that these charges, along with the ones against me for kidnapping him and all that business, could probably be easily dropped in exchange for my cooperation or some other deal that could be worked out. It said that punishment may even be avoidable if he could drop the charges against me, but that such a course of action would be a bit tricky. It seemed like he was suggesting that cooperating with whatever Cooley and DiBenedetto were going to ask of me would be the most favorable thing to do. By the time the three of them had come in from outside, I had already swallowed Dash’s note. They took me into the back room again to try to interrogate me again, and this time I complied as Dash had suggested in that note. "Alright, Johnny; we can make this really simple for you if you make it really simple for us," Cooley said, tapping his fingers against the table. "I’m listening." "Why don’t you tell us everything you know about a certain underground fighting ring you organized with the homeless?" "Alright," I started. "First of all, I was only one of the fighters. It was a guy named Adam Dick who organized the whole thing. I only went to a couple of the fights, but there was this whole betting thing going on with some of the big names in the drug trade; the biggest being Julius Jones. One night, the first night I fought, Julius Jones was there. I’m sure you’ve heard of him. He probably keeps you guys pretty comfortable." Cooley started scowling, but he let me continue. DiBenedetto didn’t seem too amused either. He basically sat there stone faced the whole time, getting easily distracted by flies that had somehow not succumbed to the colder weather yet. Dash listened intently for any signs of information I wanted him to look in on. "Anyway," I continued, "Julius was there that night, and apparently, Adam had given him a bad tip that I was a dark horse to win. Long story short, I lost. When I regained my consciousness, I saw Julius shoot Adam in the face at point blank range, multiple times. He aimed the gun at me, but there was all this commotion with the hippie protest and the hobo ghost train, so we got away." Cooley and DiBenedetto looked at each other, shaking their heads. "This guy isn’t making a lick of sense, is he DiBenedetto?" "Yeah, Jim, he’s about a few fries short of a Happy Meal." DiBenedetto chuckled to himself, and Cooley looked away with half of his lip upturned in a snide grin. "I think maybe he needs some more time to think things over, don’t you?" "Couldn’t hurt. Let’s leave him in overnight. I’ll get some cots called in, and we’ll have ourselves a sleepover party." And then DiBenedetto turned to speak to me for the first time. "I’m sure you’ll be comfortable enough on the cell bench though." They brought me back into the cell, having one of the local cops there guarding me at all times while the three agents waited in the backroom, probably smoking cigars and playing cards while talking about the case. Hopefully, Agent Dash was soaking up all kinds of valuable information in there. A couple of hours after I had fallen asleep, I was woken up by Agent Dash, who was trying to wake me up as quietly as I could. I opened my eyes to see the room was empty except for the two of us. He had sent the night guard away, DiBenedetto was in the bathroom, apparently for a while, and Cooley had nodded off with instructions for someone to wake him up in fifteen minutes. Dash was pressed against the bars so much that it was hard to tell which side of the cell I was on when I looked at him, but I came in close so he could whisper to me. Whatever he had found out seemed like he’d been waiting to tell me for a while. "Johnny, I looked into things a little bit, stealing some peeks at Cooley’s file, and Julius Jones doesn’t exist in our records at all. And trust me; there are bigger criminals who are bribing us that have been plentifully documented. It checks out that there is no Julius Jones." I was confused. I didn’t see what Dash had to gain by lying to me. Even in good cop, bad cop, no cop is this good. "Are you sure you didn’t just make him up?" I thought for a while and tried to rationalize the nonexistence of Julius Jones. "Well, I was on mushrooms at the time. I suppose he could have just been a hallucination." And then something else hit me. "But if he didn’t kill Adam Dick, then how’d he die? The newspaper even claimed he died of several self inflicted gunshot wounds to the face. That’s what it said. I read it myself. Now you can’t tell me that this doesn’t reek of a cover up." Dash looked very sad when I told him this. "Johnny, which newspaper did you read this in?" I couldn’t remember. It hit me like a brick that I couldn’t remember, and I suddenly couldn’t picture myself sitting there at the hotel with the newspaper right in front of me. It sounded now like something I had read that I had done- reading that newspaper- rather than an actually formed memory. "I don’t remember." I began to doubt myself, and then I was sure of my own self doubt. "Because there are no records of someone named Adam Dick ever existing like you’ve described," Dash sympathetically informed me. "Is it possible that he was just a hallucination, too?" "Then, Nova?" Dash sighed. "No, the whole Nova situation was real. I swear that’s the first thing I’m going to investigate once this whole fiasco is over." "But Nova can vouch for me. He saw Julius Jones shoot Adam Dick. Just ask him." "I can’t," said Agent Dash, and he looked like he was going to crack from the pressure of having to break so much bad news to me at once. "You know as well as I do what happened to Nova. And I’m sorry, but dead friends can’t help you now. I don’t even know how much I’m going to be able to do. I know you didn’t mean to come off like you were playing mind games, but that’s how Cooley and DiBenedetto are taking it. I strongly advise you to ask for representation, and don’t say anything else until that lawyer comes. When that lawyer comes, I advise you don’t say anything he doesn’t first tell you to say. Meanwhile, I’m still pushing for them to offer you some sort of deal if you cooperate. I haven’t found out what it is yet, but I’m almost positive that the FBI has a use for you still." I didn’t say anything. I think I nodded, but that could have just been the room moving up and down as my eyes rolled around in their sockets. Dash grimaced. We heard DiBenedetto groaning from the poorly insulated bathroom. It sounded like he was conducting his final movement. I think Dash wished me a good night, but that was hard given my shaken circumstances. If only I could think of the name of that newspaper, but I couldn’t. It didn’t have a name. And I couldn’t just invent a name where it had been so easy in the past; a name like Julius Jones or Adam Dick. And since I couldn’t just invent a name, I was absolutely certain that it never existed in the first place. And neither did Julius Jones. And neither did Adam Dick.
