Date: 2009-12-02 He used to listen to wrestling podcasts in the car. She’d make him switch things up and listen to her iPod shuffle through songs while he pretended it was a form of torture. Even though he’d never have admitted it to her for fear of losing another opportunity to poke fun, they liked enough of the same music that her knack for commandeering the stereo wasn’t entirely unpleasant. Still, Darcy hated that Amy Campbell continued to be the reason he couldn’t keep up with his podcasts. One mention of that name and his day was shot down in an angry, depressed haze. Local radio would have to do the trick – unless she’d just been arrested, his wife’s name would never come up. Sadly, that meant reaching for the buttons when one in a rotation of another thousand irritating R&B songs came on. He’d later remind himself not to look at the station number, even doing something something as pedestrian as pulling out of his cul-de-sac for the second time that day. Take Cover. ''Malcolm!'' Amy grinned from ear to ear when she fake-stumbled to the table. It was over the top, even for her, but she hoped he didn’t notice. She had already had a couple of shots to loosen up and make acting her (coincidentally, herself) a little easier, after all. ''Amy. To what do I owe the… whoa there.'' Malcolm had nodded, then came out of his seat to place a guiding hand on the little redhead’s arm. ''Don’t trip yourself up, now. What’s the urgency?'' ''I di’n’t sign the… that stuff. I’m sorry. The… uh… those waivers Dan cut me with.'' ''Here, sit.'' He waved the approaching waiter off. The last thing the girl needed was another drink. ''The what?'' ''The papers! Papier. Those… yaknow—'' ''Oh, the release forms. All right. Why might that be?'' He asked. ''And how much have you had to drink?'' ''Just a… a beers!'' If she was red-faced, it wasn’t truly the alcohol – she blushed her embarrassment over her own terrible acting. ''Al said to see you again so we could… so I could… so I needed to figure out if yer legit. Like, you can’t lie to me and shit. People hate me.'' ''You really shouldn’t be telling me all of this if the plan is to test me,'' He chuckled. ''That’s a byproduct of the liquor on your breath, no doubt. Are you planning to sign the papers once I’ve passed, or did your girlfriend talk you out of it entirely?'' ''Ohno, I’ma sign them and everything… I just, she says… she says I should confront you first. Make sure you’re okay. More than just drinks one time before I let a guy into my bank account, y’kn—'' Just then, a ringtone (Blondie’s ‘Touch Myself’) jingled its way out of her pocket. ''Fuck, it’s totally Al. I told her… shit, I’m makin’ a scene, huh. Fuckin’… stupid Becca was supposed to hold my phone for me before I came in!'' He was a gentleman, so he ignored the drunkenness and made an offer instead. It was an offer she’d been told he’d make if he was a half decent man. ''Would you like to take that? I don’t mind.'' ''Reception’s fuck awful in here… I’m gonna step out.'' In truth, the call really was Pierce – it was also Amy’s cue to step away before she gave herself up and let Alexandra take over the play a little earlier for safety’s sake. ''I’ll be juuuust a sec, don’t want her pissed off an’ all.'' The idea was that ''Becca'' would come up from there. Poor Becca, frazzled by having to make sure Amy Campbell of all people was on the straight and narrow. Malcolm would press on her insecurities and pry out a little information he could easily verify. When he then raised his rate, he would what part of Alexandra Pierce would always believe – people are generally horrible. Once Amy was off-stage, the other girl followed, pushing out of the restroom as she adjusted her eyeglasses, little round things that made her face look smaller, more pinched. Amy called Alex's look ''Hot Daphne'' – she looked like the girl from Scoobie-Doo, with wavy brown hair and clear blue eyes. Only she wasn't nearly as awkward (''I'd never have a dumpy assistant,'' Amy said). It was the kind of thing Alexandra Pierce spent hours on; she knew Becca's favorite food and color, and could work up a decent lie about her history if she could. Alex didn't succeed at what she did because she was crazy (though she was) or because she was pretty (which she was), but because she was prepared. All that preparation went by the wayside when she made eye contact with the man at the table, and a frown etched across her features. Girard matched it with a smile, a touch of amusement on his lips. ''Malcolm.'' It wasn't Becca or even Alexandra – that was Desade. ''So she was playing me. I should have known, she usually can’t stand beer.'' He chuckled. ''So who are you supposed to be for this? Not a seductress, that’s for certain. I’m honestly surprised I got away with meeting the girl once without my cover gone.'' Girard could only smile and lean back in his chair. The woman across from him was suddenly stiff, obviously angry – though only someone with years of experience in Alexandra Pierce might know that. ''How are you, Alexandra? It’s been ages.'' ''Not the seductress. The fed-up employee.'' ''Ah.'' He grinned. ''You're much better at that.'' ''Why are you here, Malcolm? What business is this of yours?'' She leaned across the table, one elbow the fulcrum. ''How dare you involve yourself in my life by latching onto her.'' ''I’m simply an observer.'' He said, ''If I wanted to involve myself in your life, I would have chosen an actual cover – one that I didn’t fully know would be pointless the moment she told you the name I gave her. So… we’ve established that your play is blown, what now? Wouldn’t you like to know my purpose here?'' ''I would like to know your true purpose here, which I doubt you intend to reveal yourself. I can find that much elsewhere.'' Malcolm sighed. ''Then we’re through chatting, I take it? I really would appreciate you not mentioning to her that I’m with the Order, and I think we’d all be better off without her knowing you’ve slept with me.'' With a smile that he knew would annoy her, he added, ''Several times, if I recall correctly.'' The people who knew her well would tell an observer that that little twitch beside her right eye was an indication Alexandra Pierce was in a bad mood. The fact that it progressed past the twitch, to a clenched jaw and her palms pressing her to a standing position made this fact quite clear to nearly everyone else. Her whisper was a cold, cold thing. ''There was a time where I would have killed you where you stand.'' She turned away, trying to yank ''Becca'' on as a shield, but it was too thin, too frail to work. ''I'm leaving now, Malcolm. Whether your ruse survives the night will not be the question if I stay.'' ''Then I look forward to seeing you again.'' He smiled. ''Have a wonderful day, Alexandra. Tell Amy that her acting is lovely.'' For that, he almost got backhanded. Girard sighed a small sigh of relief when the woman simply turned and walked the other way, leaving him alone. Amy would not return to him. Alexandra Pierce, however, was a different story.
