A new outlook

16 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-04-16 “Just what in all the world are you doing?” The voice of David Hughes rang around the locker room home of Ian Fetzer, the man known to all and sundry as The Fool. Fetzer looked up from his printer, and spied his friend walking through the door. “Well, you know I said I had a couple of ideas?” “Yes” replied Hughes. “Mister Action Figure here has been helping me with the first of them. Well, when I say helping he’s been completely against the idea, and he’s been telling me that it’s utter nonsense and wouldn’t work, even in a ridiculous science fiction kind of way.” The Fool looked down at the plastic action figure in his right hand, which would often dance and shake as if it had a life of its own. Of course, there was always some part of The Fool’s mind directing the movements of the toy, but the translation of the movements often made more sense than his own thoughts. “You’re trying to get your memory back via your computer?” asked Hughes. The Fool rolled his eyes. “Don’t be ridiculous. A home computer wouldn’t be able to do that! No, instead I am working on a principle similar to that of getting hit repeatedly over the head.” Hughes looked at his friend questioningly. The Fool had tried concussive therapy the previous week to no avail, and really could have been said to have been receiving that kind of ‘medical’ attention ever since he started entering the ring without any notion of how to defend himself. “You see,” The Fool continued. “I think that the beatings over the head aren’t working because humanity has passed beyond such primitive measures. There are whole new areas of medicine these days, so I’ve gone out of my way to try and get two of these methods to work on my particular problem. Are you impressed?” Hughes pulled a face as if to say he doubted that this would work properly. “No, seriously, it’s going to be fantastic. Medical science has worked wonders in recent years, so the first part of my plan is based around high technology.” “Hang on, a home computer isn’t exactly the latest technological breakthrough” interjected Hughes. “Really? That’s not what the man in the shop said.” “Yes, but you get taken advantage of quite easily Ian, and I think we've covered this ground before." “Well, I’m not too bothered. I just need to make one more minor adjustment and I can try it out.” The Fool slowly prodded the buttons on the printer, one by one. Hughes walked up for a closer look. “What are you doing?” “I’m looking to see how I can set my laser printer to stun.” “…” It took a few seconds for this to sink in, before David Hughes playfully slapped The Fool around the back of the head. “Seriously, what are you doing?” “I’m serious! I think it’s in Settings… Printers… Disruptor Energy…” Hughes laughed at the earnest look on the face of The Fool, as he searched in vain for the setting. “Hey Ian, maybe you shouldn’t be using a HP Laserjet.” “Why’s that?” “I’m not sure. Maybe a Canon would work better?” “Are you making fun of me?” “Very probably.” Hughes skipped back and easily avoided the hurled action figure that flew through the space his head had previously been occupying. The toy rebounded off the wall and landed on the unmade bed of The Fool. Ian muttered to himself as he wandered over to pick up his fallen plastic companion. “I’m sorry Mister Action Figure. I just… ummmm… slipped, you see. It was his fault!” The Fool pointed one accusing finger at David Hughes, whilst the action figure was made to do a little dance by his other hand. “What’s that Mister Action Figure?” Hughes buried his head in his hands as The Fool’s illusory friend started to take over his master’s attention. “You think that David Hughes, also known as The X Factor, is a really nasty man, and should be forced to apologise for being horrible to me and making fun of my attempts to regain my memory. These attempts, you might add, are those spurred on by the motivational efforts of said David Hughes, and so his mocking them means he is ultimately mocking himself? Is that what you were saying, Mister Action Figure?” Under duress, the action figure nodded its head. “Okay, okay, I’m sorry… but seriously, that’s not going to work.” “Bah… so much for a bright new era of technology.” “Look, if you went to see a doctor then I’m sure that they…” “Doctors know nothing, David! I once went to see a doctor, but all he did was swear at me in German.” “He wasn’t called Curiosity was he?” “Yes, but that’s not important right now! Modern medicine has failed me. The technological advances of our space age era have fallen by the wayside, leaving me with only one option left to regain my memories and become a dominant force, able to brush aside Syko Arsonist as if he was an errant hair in an otherwise perfectly styled haircut.” Hughes looked at The Fool, wondering what the next ridiculous scheme was likely to be. “My next ridiculous sch… I mean my next fantastic plan is to go into the remedies of the New Age. I saw an advertisement for the Amazingly Mysterious El Fantasmo in the newspaper the other day… he’s a hypnotist!” The enthusiasm in the voice of Ian Fetzer was so strong, and his personality so overwhelming, that David Hughes found himself actually starting to believe that this could work. “Yes, that’s a… NO! Ian, what is he, some sort of stage hypnotist?” “Yeah, how did you guess?” replied The Fool, looking genuinely surprised. “The name gave it away a little.” This didn’t worry The Fool in the least. Before he had become a wrestler he had worked for a while in a circus, where he developed his fantastic sense of balance and his acrobatic skills. During his travels, his company had for a time worked with a stage hypnotist, and Ian had always marvelled at the way he could get anyone to do absolutely anything. It was as if the hypnotist had complete power over everyone and everything. Amongst the field of stage hypnotists there was one man though, a special man who was said to be better than anyone else. His power was legendary, and said to be derived from an amulet said to have been found in the middle of a jungle in deepest Africa. No mortal man could withstand his steely glare, and no mind could help but to give up its secrets to his probing. This man was the Amazingly Mysterious El Fantasmo, and he was going to help Ian Fetzer get back his memories. … There was a knock at the door. From behind it came a voice, saying “El Fantasmo, he be here!” The Fool’s face lit up even further. “He must have known that my attempts to overcome my predicament using technology would be confounded. He truly is both amazing and mysterious.” Hughes shook his head at The Fool, whose voice was fading into hushed, reverential tones. There was another knock at the door, and Hughes walked over to it. With one swift tug it was opened, and there he stood. El Fantasmo. The Amazing El Fantasmo. A short, fat man. The Mysterious El Fantasmo. A short, fat man wearing a tuxedo. The Amazing and Mysterious El Fantasmo. A short, fat man wearing a tuxedo, with a large green amulet hanging around his neck. Hughes raised his eyebrows, one at a time, which takes practice, and spoke to the short man standing in the doorway as if he owned it. “Who’re you then?” “I am El Fantasmo, and I own this doorway!” “Fair enough.” The short man breezed in to the locker room home of Ian Fetzer, and let his gaze rest for a few seconds on each important part of the surroundings. The Great Sombrero poster and bed linen got a few seconds, as did the computer and laser printer. The doorway got no more than a cursory glance, but then his gaze fell upon the man who was to be the subject of his hypnotic skills. Ian Fetzer, the man better known to the world as The Fool. “I can see our subject already. Please, Mister Fetzer, take a seat.” “I’ve been sitting down for hours, other than when I briefly stood to pick up Mister Action Figure a little while back.” The short, fat hypnotist dabbed at his brow with a spotted handkerchief, and then lifted himself to a seat opposite his subject. The Fool looked nervous, his legs shaking as he came face to face with the most impressive of all stage hypnotists. The wide eyes of El Fantasmo bored into the skull of Ian Fetzer (metaphorically, of course) and The Fool felt himself going under before a word was uttered. “You are going to fall in to a deep trance. You’re eyelids are heavy already, and…” El Fantasmo stopped, as a noise cut through the locker room like a hot knife through butter. “SNOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOORRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRE!” Fantasmo glanced at Hughes, and then back to the sleeping Fool, a disapproving look on his face the entire time. “Mister Fetzer… Mister Fe… MISTER FETZER!” The Fool woke with a jump. “Mister Fetzer, please do not fall asleep until I have hypnotised you!” “Yes master.” “Knock it off. I’ve barely started!” The Fool acquiesced, and sat back, awaiting the instructions from the hypnotist. El Fantasmo fingered the green, glowing amulet around his neck, and carried on. “You are falling in to a trance. You can hear nothing now but the sound of my voice, as it takes you on a trip down a spiral staircase. As you go down this staircase, picture it in your mind, and see the cold, hard stone as you walk past it, leaving the light behind and entering in to a world of darkness. You are still stepping downwards as you continue to a place beyond light, to a place beyond sight, to a place where you are whole, and you can reflect on anything and everything.” El Fantasmo looked across the room. David Hughes appeared to be fast asleep, but The Fool was wide awake, his eyes pointed directly at the hypnotist, and his face truly attentive. Now was the time to bring back the memories, to work the magic and leave The Fool as a competitor to be reckoned with. “You are travelling backwards through the times you have lived in. You are sitting in the previous week. You are defeating Robb Hardy and Justin Walker. It makes you feel good, but we are going further back. You have lost matches, you have lost a lot, Ian, but how does it feel.” “I feel hollow.” “Yes you do Ian. Move past those times, move to the times previous to those losses.” “I am… a winner?” “Yes Ian, you were a champion, a great champion who had no fear of anyone he faced. Do you remember this?” “It is… it is vague.” “Ian, listen to me. Your times before the vagueness. The times before the mists, what do you see?” “I see… a circus!” Outside of the trance, El Fantasmo shook his head. To penetrate through the memories that Ian Fetzer has locked away would take many hours of therapy. The problem with that lay with the fact that El Fantasmo was a stage hypnotist, not trained in the patience of repeat visits and general therapy. He was barely able to resist the impulse to tell Fetzer to act like a chicken and then sing ‘Like a Virgin’ at the top of his voice. “You remember your opponent, Syko Arsonist?” “Syko Arsonist…” repeated The Fool. “You remember your opponent.” “Syko Arsonist” muttered The Fool, again. “You remember who you are” added El Fantasmo, hopefully. “Syko Arsonist” “No, you remember who you are!” “Syko Arsonist” repeated The Fool again, dreamily. The hypnotist shrugged and stood up. “Yes, that will do. When I count down from three you will be free of this trance and you will be refreshed, thinking that never have you ever felt so fresh before… Three… two… one… zero!” With that, the hypnotist stood up, and took his coat from off the back of the seat. As Ian Fetzer opened his eyes and started to smile, David Hughes also stood up and looked El Fantasmo up and down. He paid particular attention to the amulet around the neck of the famous hypnotist. “I recognise that. I got one just the same in a Christmas Cracker about four years ago. It was…” “Silence! I found it in the Amazon!” “I thought it was in Africa?” “Ummm… yes, that too. Now, I am a very busy man, and I must flee before you realise what I have done.” “Oh, okay.” Hughes let the man go, and turned around to face his friend, Ian Fetzer, the man better known to all and sundry as The Fool. Fetzer’s eyes were slightly narrowed, and his face looked as if it were more decisive all of a sudden. Hughes stretched out one arm, trying to help Fetzer back to his feet. “Are you okay? Can you remember anything?” “Yes.” The Fool moved to his feet, and looked around the locker room. His eyes seemed to have a certain amount of disdain for the surroundings. Somehow the Great Sombrero poster was not good enough any more. Somehow the computer seemed irrelevant, especially compared to the relative splendour and Spartan perfection of the Del Fuego Wrestling Gym. “So, you’re back to your old self?” enquired David Hughes. The Fool looked around again. There was no way he could ever be right against this nonsensical backdrop. He didn’t want to fight, to wrestle against these PTC types ever again, but once more he was drawn back into it. It was wrong, and no matter what the circumstances, the bets had been finished, the winnings taken and the next round altered. The odds might have favoured one man or they might have favoured the other. It was immaterial. He was not fighting in another round of this tournament. “Come on Ian, are you better?” No more words from The Fool. He was busy trying to work out who this man was talking to him. Who was this person that thought they held sway over a man through to the second round of the PTC Cruiserweight Tournament? Why did they think the tournament was simple? Just because there were smaller people in it? A midget, was that it? Surely they could not have objected to a woman being there, especially after the impressive but ultimately futile efforts of the Fallen Angel. Youngsters, they think they own the world, when they’ve never done anything but wrestle a few practice matches in the gym. “Ian?” This fool asking the questions could wait. There were more important matters at stake here. Who would run the gym if he were to wrestle? Why would he even want to wrestle any more? “Ian! Seriously! Answer me!” The Fool turned his head towards the man that he used to recognise as his dearest friend. “Why am I holding this action figure?” Hughes was stunned. How could Ian reject the action figure? He was even closer to The Fool than anyone. Mister Action Figure would be… wait… he’s an inanimate object… “Tell me your name.” The Fool swung his head up. An imperious look over his former friend told him all he needed to know. This man was a better wrestler than David Hughes could ever hope to be. In one fell swoop, the hypnotist had both blessed and cursed his subject. So he spoke. “My name....” The Fool smiled, his confidence returning suddenly. “My name is Syko Arsonist.”
