"Influential(s)"

25 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-25 * * * * * Over the years, I’ve come to the conclusion that each person walking this great planet of ours will come into contact with one influential person, and that person will change his or her life forever. People often associate a radical change being for the better, but regarding life, it’s a door that swings in both directions. There will be those who meet that person, and their life is sent in a different direction that enables them to do good towards others, as well as themselves. Yet, on the other end of the spectrum, meeting that one person could send an individual on a very drastic, manipulative turn. At one point in is life, Saddam Hussein was likely loved by those who knew him as a young boy, but if you trace back through his life, I’d be willing to bet one person in particular was the forceful reasoning behind his convoluted path through life. In my 32 years on this planet, I’ve met three of these influential people. The first was a man who gave me the ability to use my hands as weapons within the confines of a wrestling ring. The second was a man who took my love for wrestling, my abilities, and replaced them with an assassin’s rifle. Finally, the third was a boy who saw the rifle and convinced me to cast it aside forever. * * * * * Nestled deep within in the rolling hills of California wine country, Victor Arlington’s mansion can be spotted miles away. It isn’t necessarily overly in gigantic in size, or luxury, but when the only other man-made object within a three-mile radius are the roads snaking through the hills – in actuality his driveway heading towards the mansion -- it sticks out like a sore thumb against the grassy hills and acres upon acres of trees. While the care of Victor Arlington is generally beset upon the band of mercenaries he hires, the care of his mansion falls upon the shoulders of one man, Gordon Hart, the butler of the Arlington Estate. Aged much like some of the bottles in the great Arlington wine cellar, Gordon Hart exudes the charm and confidence you would generally associate with a college professor completely in love with his job. His salt-and-pepper hair is slicked back each morning when he rises, only after he dresses himself in the stereotypical black butler outfit he sees fit for the daily chores he does around the Arlington mansion. His chores are generally preparing the meals for Mr. Arlington, as well as small upkeep around the expansive mansion, itself. He is well aware that his employer is one of the most powerful men west of the Mississippi River, but it doesn’t impede Gordon’s ability to work for, or question the judgment of, Mr. Arlington. On this wonderful day, where the breeze often catches the scent of the growing fruit in the nearby vineyards, Gordon Hart was dusting off the marble busts decorating the main hallway of Arlington mansion when the doorbell echoed throughout the vast corridor. A moment later, after peering through the peephole, Gordon Hart opened the twin oak doors and stood face to face with a man he had never seen before. With the profession of Victor Arlington, Gordon had become accustomed to this occurrence, since the men who are generally thought of as the ‘scum of society’ often show up at this very doorstep, usually in need of work. ”May I help you, sir?” Gordon Hart said with a perfect smile, keeping one hand behind his back in a perfect welcoming posture – but also close to the concealed 9mm Beretta at the small of his back, “The Arlington Estate wasn’t expecting any visitors today.” As Gordon watched, the man stood before him and smiled. His teeth were crooked and filled with what appeared to be rot. His nose held a very foreign appearance, the bridge protruded farther than it should. A few strands of hair covered his balding head, and his face was covered in wrinkles. Crows feet reached out from the corners of his eyes, and his forehead started to show the same small sections of discoloration that Gordon’s was beginning to go through. The tell tale signs of aging. Instead of responding, the man simply reached up with one finger and poked at his right eye. What appeared to be a contact lens, brown in color, slid to the side and revealed a pale-white iris. ”It’s been awhile, Mr. Sail. Victor has been expecting you.” Gordon said, welcoming the often-disguised mercenary into the mansion. “Gordon,” Jonathan said, sliding the brown lens back into place, “Please refrain from using my real name.” He took a step into the mansion, ”Of course, Legion.” Gordon replied, his left hand crossing his chest apologetically, “This way, Victor is just finishing lunch. To my understanding, he already has another job for you, as always.” “It would have been arrogant of me to think otherwise.” Legion said, a disgusting smile reaching across his face once again. Opening another set of doors at the far end of the main hallway, which expanded the corridor into what appeared to be a large study filled with books and topped by marble archways, Gordon Hart announced the arrival to the man himself, “Legion has arrived, Victor.” From behind the desk, the mountain of a man stood up, a brilliant white smile breaching the dark beard that covered Victor Arlington’s face. * * * * * Since there was a bit of downtime between the first two rounds of this tournament, I decided to head back home, to Philadelphia, for some rest and relaxation. It wasn’t a very long trip from the arena where Itagaki Freeze once again ended up suffering his second consecutive first-round expulsion from a wrestling tournament, so I decided to pay the small fare, taking a seat at the rear of the bus. This late at night, when most people are at home snuggled in bed with a loved one, I was taking the red-eye bus back towards Philly. I don’t see any influential people that might change the course of my life on this particular bus, although I don’t plan on talking to anybody, either, influential or not. Instead, I’ll share the short ride with only a handful of people, most of which will be sleeping in a matter of minutes, and I can live with that. My mind kept racing back to the match against Freeze, where we each had our momentum shifted by the other on numerous occasions, but in the end, I managed to pin him to the mat. I hope he isn’t discouraged, after all, this was my first-ever tournament victory. Now that I think about it, it was my first tournament…ever. Nonetheless, he was a good competitor, and much like the roller coaster that this business is, Itagaki is in a valley while I’m resting atop a peak. Peaks and valleys, peaks and valleys. Contract-killing, on the other hand, is just valleys. Permanently resting between enormous mountains you can’t even fathom climbing, valiantly attempting to escape. * * * * * Victor Arlington is an intimidating man, both in physical presence, and the way he goes about conducting himself. Standing well over six feet in height, his shoulders as broad as the Grand Canyon, his presence is only matched by professional athletes or bodybuilders. His personality could compared to a Pitbull pup. At one moment, he can be the most charming and adorable person in a room, yet not more than a second later, his unmistakable ferociousness is unmatched and expected. Arlington’s face is covered in a perfectly-trimmed black beard, which is only penetrated by his two emerald-colored eyes, or the occasional smile. When Victor Arlington stands, people take notice and listen. When he silences himself, people are often left wondering why. Gliding around his desk, as light-footed as an apparition, Victor Arlington took two great strides before both Legion and Gordon Hart were standing before him, “Excellent work in Florida. Your planning was perfect, Hurricane Charlie hit only a day later, and the news began covering that story instead of yours. The murders won’t be making newspaper headlines for weeks…and it appears that another Hurricane is already forming in the Caribbean.” Throughout the years, Victor Arlington had grown accustomed to the mercenary known only as Legion standing before him in different disguises. He never second guessed who was behind the makeup, standing in his magnificent study, knowing that his butler provided enough security to qualm that fear. ”In fact,” Victor continued, “The authorities are looking for a man with a scar down his face, a red streak in his long, black pony-tail. People in the park that day said they saw a man matching that description watching the birds on a park bench. Tell me, why the red streak in the wig?” ”The same reason as these atrocious teeth, Victor,” Legion started, only to pause and smile. He turned his head side-to-side so Arlington could catch a glimpse at the mock dental nightmare, “Because people remember things out of the ordinary. Give them one, and they’ll often forget what the face surrounding it looks like, and only remember the red streak in the hair, or the scar etched across his face.” “After all these years, you still amaze me.” ”After all these years, I bet you still remember what I look like.” Legion said, hinting at their long-standing working relationship in which Arlington has only seen Jonathan Sail without make-up on two occasions, “Gordon mentioned another job. Does it fit in within my three rules?” ”It does. I had one of my newcomers do the reconnaissance, for fear of Gordon being recognized. The file is right here,” Arlington mentioned as he turned away from his prized mercenary, and reached for the manila envelope atop his desk. “Where is the mark?” Legion said, without opening the file. Instead, he tucked it into his jacket. ”Your neck of the woods. Philadelphia.” “Age?” “Not as young as you’ve gone before, a little older than usual. While you often take the jobs others won’t, this one isn’t out of their reach. But when this contract came to my attention, personally I wanted you to have it. You know the area better than anyone else out there, and to be honest, I don’t trust anyone with this work as much as I trust you.” ”Has the money been deposited in my account for the Florida job?” “As always, Jonathan.” Victor said. Legion’s stare shot over towards the butler, but unlike the his correction of Gordon, Legion would not be correcting Victor Arlington. In fact, the only person who can continually get away with calling him by his birth name is the raven-haired mercenary boss standing before him. “But Jonathan, this is time-sensitive,” Victor started, then stopped for a moment. Legion noticed that other things seemed to be on his mind, “This isn’t an exclusive contract once 48 hours expires. If the kid isn’t a headline before the sun-rises in two days, then it’s open season. I know you like exclusivity…but I could only get 48 hours for this one. After that, it’s a free-for-all.” ”It’s never been a problem before, Victor. I’ll be in touch.” Legion turned away from the man who has been hiring him to murder various people for the better part of the last six years, making his exit towards the main entranceway once more. As he left the study, Legion could hear Arlington take a seat behind his desk, once again starting to rifle through paperwork or perhaps the lunch Gordon had prepared for him. Gordon didn’t accompany Legion down the corridor, to the doorway, as he never did. Instead, the butler went back to doing his duties with the love and admiration he held for the job. As Legion made his way through the gently rolling hills, getting farther from the Arlington Estate, something was tugging at his attention. Something felt amiss, although it shouldn’t. Arlington has never tried to outwit or deceive him, and Legion’s “Three Rules” were intact, which alone should give him enough confidence to complete the mission. Yet, he could only keep hearing one phrase in particular being repeated by Victor Arlington over and over in his head: ”I had one of my newcomers do the reconnaissance, for fear of Gordon being recognized.” * * * * * My initial thoughts on the reception I would receive were correct. A few fans cheered, simply because I was entering one of the biggest tournaments the wrestling business was putting on this year, and a few booed, more than likely because they are overweight drunks, and don’t care about the hardships a wrestler has to go through to earn a living. They would all boo me, however, if they ever found out the life I used to live. Occasionally, I have nightmares about my previous career as a contract-killer. I’ll wake up in the middle of the night covered head-to-toe in a sticky sweat, after having a dream where I’m watching a young boy, who I assume to be my son, being shot to death by an unseen assassin. As I’m standing beside his frail body, his very life leaving it, I look up and I see myself standing up from the bushes, a smoking rifle in hand. When I killed for a living, I wore disguises…but when my nightmarish doppelganger stands up from behind the brush, there is no disguise. I can see my own ghostly eyes, a colorless gaze shooting right back at me. Now that I think about it and count the empty seats, you could practically fill every vacant seat on this bus with a person I’ve murdered for money. Dear God, I hope I didn’t just create a new nightmare to wake up from. But I’ve given up that life. I’ve once again started the life I should have ten years ago, when Danny first started training me. After years of training under his tutelage, I came to the conclusion that he was the lone influential person I would come across in my lifetime. He took a humbled ex-Marine, and molded him into an aspiring professional wrestler. He molded me into Jonathan Sail, the wrestler. I never guessed two others would follow, each equally as influential. When Danny was murdered, another man immediately entered my life. The second of my trio of influential people, Victor Arlington, gave me the ability to seek revenge on the person who had taken Danny’s life. Oddly, never in my nightmares do I ever cross paths with the kid who stabbed Danny as part of a gang-land “introduction ritual”. That boy, only 12 at the time, never haunts me…and I think it’s because I believe his death at my hands was justified. He was also the only person I’ve killed in close-quarters. When Victor proposed working as a contract-killer -- which I freely admit he took advantage of my emotionally fragile state-of-mind, at the time -- I devised a set of three rules to abide by: 1. I must never know the mark(s) name or identity, only requiring a photograph for reference. 2. Proper research must be done ahead of time, so I know exactly when and where the mark(s) will be. 3. Age is of no consequence, just as long as the first two rules are strictly enforced. But that horrible life is behind me, simply because the third, and so far the last, influential person in my life was also the final contract Victor Arlington asked me to complete. * * * * *
