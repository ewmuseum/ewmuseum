The Politics of Family

17 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-17 "Welcome back to Eye on Washington." The stately, gray-haired host of the long-running political magazine show looked a bit uneasy as his live, Sunday morning broadcast came back from commercial. He usually never looked as uneasy as he did right there and then. The week prior, he moderated a pre-election debate-turned-argument between George Stephanopoulous and Karl Rove with the adept skill of a seamstress manually threading a needle. He stared Rush Limbaugh right in the eye and threw hardball after hardball at him about his painkiller addiction. Never before though… "Of course, I'm Bill Gravers, and with me right now is one Gordon Oliver Powell…" …never before did he have to interview a professional wrestler. "…who actually moonlights as a professional wrestler." Moonlights, Bill? he thought. The man came on his show wearing the Phantom Republican mask. If anything, he was a wrestler who moonlit as a political booster. He wanted to keep the political decorum from completely shattering though. "Mr. Gravers, actually, wrestling is my profession, one of my two true loves. Of course, that's not why I'm here though. I'm here for my other love, and that's politics." A masked wrestler, talking politics. Have ratings gotten that bad? Bill Gravers knew that Powell was one of the biggest boosters of the Republican Party. He spoke at several campaign rallies, mostly for losing causes as the Democrats swept the GOP out of control of Congress for the first time in 12 years. As the President himself said, it was a "whuppin'." The subject matter itself wouldn't have been so bad if he had not insisted on wearing the mask. Such a stigma. "My apologies Gordon. Anyway, let's get started. You're currently in training to wrestle for the New Era of Wrestling World Championship… did I get that right"? Gordon nodded. "Okay then, that's at an event called Battle… Brawl. Also, you're wrestling in a tournament for the wrestling conglomerate Primetime Central in their annual Golden Turnbuckle Tournament." Bill stumbled over his speech, almost like each wrestling inference was a four-letter word. "However, it could be argued that your biggest challenge was helping keep the Republicans in control of Congress. To put it quite bluntly, those efforts failed. Why do you think they did?" There was trepidation in his voice when he asked the question. Normally, Bill never lets anyone intimidate him, but this was different. This was an almost 260 pound man, possibly on steroids, someone who could very much break his heartily stout but aged frame in two. "Well Bill, I'll be honest with you when I say that I was a little disappointed in the results. I thought that there were a lot of close races, and I also felt that if the press would give the War in Iraq a little fairer coverage, maybe some of those races would have gone our way. Now, I understand that our boys are over there dying, and I wish they could come home tomorrow, just like anyone else. But I also know that we went over and started something, and we have to finish it. We can't leave the Iraqis in the lurch. The press would have you believe that my party is over there just because we want to be, and that when they take control, the war will magically go away. There are real problems over there that need time to fix, and the Democrats swayed voters to think otherwise. That's unfortunate, but now, we have to live with that reality and hopefully work together to get something done." There was no doubt in Bill's mind that Gordon was eloquent, a worthy guest had he had another profession. "Well, it's good that you're adopting a bipartisan attitude now, but that's not the tune you've been singing in the days leading up to the election. You've spewed more vitriol at the Democrats than most of the candidates you spoke for combined." "Of course I did, Bill. It's all part of rallying the troops. Did I mean a lot of what I said towards the Democratic challengers? Well, yes, in some respects. I didn't mean any of the ad hominem attacks. Those were just in the heat of the moment, and of course, due to my nature as a professional wrestler. But the things about policy, their inane promises and blatant disregard of the issues, I meant all that." "Wouldn't that make it harder to work with them though?" "To a point, yes, but that's where compromise comes in, Bill. We're in a position of weakness, and as much as we'd like to stalemate things and carry on in belligerent mode, there are things that absolutely need to be worked on. We need an exit strategy in Iraq. We need to find a way to fix Social Security. We need to get in congress with them and dissuade them of their more radical ideas, we keep them close. Then, in 2008, we attack furiously and regain the House and keep the Presidency. We need to stabilize first, and by keeping our enemies closer to us than our friends, we can do that." Yes, the confrontational nature was coming out in spades. "That's quite the belligerent stance you're taking, Gordon. In one instance, you're preaching cooperation and now confrontation. Are you flip-flopping?" He cringed at fear of inciting the large wrestler. Instead, Powell's voice remained rather calm. "No, no I don't think I'm flip-flopping here, Bill. Look at it this way, if we and the Democrats fought all the time, then they're just as good to us as the terrorists we're fighting all the time. But if we got along all the time, then bad ideas would get passed. There's a time for war and a time for peace. You just have to pick and choose which times are for which, but it's not like the friction is going to come unnaturally, like we're just picking fights with the Democrats for picking a fight's sake, and vice versa. We all have our own ideas of how things should be run, and we all want to be in charge. When we're not in charge, well, things get a bit testy. It's as simple as that." "Alright… moving on, you were a key speaker for several candidates. Rick Santorum was one of them, as were Kerry Healy, John Faso, George Allen… you were even one of the only high-profile campaigners for John Spencer. People usually distance themselves from losing candidates, but in your case, you've personally visited everyone you campaigned for. Why is that?" "Well, I'm not someone who doesn't mean what I say, and in prior speeches, I've likened the Republican party as a family. When you're a family, you're there for them whenever they need you. People need to understand that, and they need to understand that the compassionate conservative is not dead, Bill. We Republicans believe in the family structure, which is why I've done my damnedest to promote throughout this election." Very eloquent, Bill thought to himself. "Well, that's all the time we have for today, but I'd like to thank you for coming onto the show, Gordon." "It was my pleasure." Bill shook his hand and thought to himself that he wasn't that bad of an interview. If only he would have taken off the mask. He could hear Greta Van Sustern ribbing him in his head. He never should have agreed to have a carny on his show, even one who seemed that he wasn't in character all the time. The next day, GOP's red Explorer pulled into the driveway of a rancher home with white vinyl siding under a willow tree. Powell stepped out of the SUV and walked in the door without knocking. He looked around the living room; a television was blaring with cacophony of Oprah Winfrey and whatever vapid celebrity she happened to be entertaining at the time, but no one was on the couch watching it. He looked around the somewhat messy room and then called in a loud voice. "Mooom! I'm here!" Next thing that Powell heard was the sound of slippered feet creaking down the stairs. Within a minute, his mother, dressed in a terrycloth bathrobe and a foamy neck brace, came down, looking as if she just rolled out of bed. "It's about damn time you showed up." She hobbled down the stairs, looking like a woman of 60 instead of 40. "And take that damn mask off in this house." "You know I can't, Mom." "You're my son, even if you haven't been acting like it. I don't care how you look." Gordon gulped and pursed his lips. "How… err, how have I not been acting like your son, Mom?" Her face lit up with the shock of a virgin being served a paternity test. "Do you want me to rattle off the list?" "No… I…" "Too bad." She straightened up her back and pointed her finger out at her only son. "One, I got into a car accident and you only called me once, at 6 AM, nonetheless, and left a short voicemail like I was some cheap barslut you were letting down easily…" "But Mom, it was elec…" "Shut up! Then, when I finally got a hold of you after a week of trying to call you, you blew me off and told me that it was payback for me being a harlot. A harlot! You called your own mother a harlot! And then, I saw you on that stupid political show, you blathering on about family and unconditional love. Talking about a bigot like Rick Santorum being your family, but your own mother, who gave you life?" Gordon stared silently at his raging mother, her eyes staring a hole though him like lasers melting through steel. He composed himself and straightened his posture. "Well, you're the one who wrecked your marriage with Daddy. You're the one who went through husbands like Roseanne Barr goes through Whoppers on an all-night Burger King bender! You… you need to earn my love again." Her mouth went agape. "Gordon…" "I'm sorry." She shook her head. "No you're not…" She turned to walk away from him to hobble back upstairs. AS she placed her foot on the bottom step, she turned around and looked up at her son with tears in her eyes. "Unconditional love, Gordon. That means you should love me no matter how fucked up I am. That's family." She hobbled back up the stairs as Gordon stood shellshocked at the previous exchange. He looked at the TV and shook himself out of his daze. "Ugh, how can anyone watch this?" He plopped on the couch and clicked from Oprah to Fox News.
