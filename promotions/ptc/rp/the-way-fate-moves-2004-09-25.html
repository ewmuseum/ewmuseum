The Way Fate Moves

25 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-25 “Life is suffering. Buddha said it, I believe it. It is not something that any of us can truly deny, not if we look at the daily events that hang over us like a pall. Wars, bombs, blood, death, they are all around us—the real fallacy would be pretending that they do not exist, pretending that suffering is a foreign concept that is only real for people in war-torn nations or third-world countries.” .:: She sighed. How many times was it, that she had heard the threats, or as they were often referred to, ‘the promises,’ that she wasn’t going to make it out of her match without being severely injured. The number would have been legion, had she actually bothered to count. But there was no need in Shadow’s mind to count such things, for in the end, she would have had only a number that would have done her no good. ::. “I burn… I pine… I perish… I suffer. Don’t we all? The question is not whether we win or lose, it is whether we learn anything in the process—and that is where I disagree with you, my friend Shaman. Suffering is of no consequence to me, so long as another part of the puzzle which is my purpose is revealed to me.” .:: She paused, a very slight, but noticeable smirk dancing across her features like a sudden shift in the slant of light through a window. ::. “I should have known I’d be mistaken, hm? I’ll attempt to clear my name, so to speak—last week, I told a story. I told my story. As I sat down and read through the pages and pages that I had written on nothing save my own humble life, I realized that mine has been a life full of uncertainty. Something seemed right, then the wind would suddenly shift, and I would be left scrabbling for meaning once again. Reading over my own words made me realize one thing—for quite some time, I lived my life as an uncertainty. I didn’t know what I wanted, didn’t know who I was, and I certainly didn’t know how to make sense of anything around me. But in the telling of the story, I think that you somehow missed the moral of it. The point is… all my life I have been standing in stasis, waiting for someone else to fill me in on the meaning that I was craving. What I never realized was that I knew the meaning all along. It doesn’t matter what it is—it’s different for all of us, I suppose. What matters is that I found it. What matters is that I give myself what I need, instead of sitting around and waiting for someone else to tell me how to handle what’s been set in front of me.” .:: Her smirk widened. All in all, the attitude that Shaman was taking towards this match was really one that amused her, despite herself. ::. “You seem to have a few notions that I cannot seem to understand. In the first, I do not understand where you could conceivably have gotten the idea that I run from my past. Again, it seems that you have missed the point—what I do is not running from my past, Shaman; what I do is called growing. I accept the mistakes that I have made, and I move past them. They are a part of me as much as anything else that I do or say is a part of me, but there comes a point in time where holding on to your past becomes harmful, something that you cannot conceivably do without wrecking your head. I can’t be what I used to be, because I’ve changed. Every creature does it—where would we be if the world did not evolve, my friend? Yet somehow, you seem to suggest that I should be embracing my past with open arms, as though it is my own offspring, were it possible for me to have any… That is folly. I accept what happened in my past, but that does not mean that I cannot pin my mistakes for what they are—mistakes. That does not mean that I cannot call myself disillusioned. That does not mean that I cannot remember a betrayal and still call it as magnanimous a betrayal as it was the first time that it happened. Only I have lived it, Shaman, and you don’t know me as well as you seem to think that you do, so please do not presume that you can attempt to understand what is in my head regarding anything, especially my personal past. .:: She sighed. For a moment, her surroundings became clearer—there wasn’t much to see. The room was dark, although there were a few lights placed rather sporadically that caused shadows to dance across the empty space at crazy angles. One fell half across Shadow herself, but her eyes were still visible, eerie and icy, green as Ireland. ::. “Another point I must differ with you on, sir, is betrayal. You seem to think that it affects me much more than it does—your words belie ignorance. As I said, I was telling my story last week—introducing myself to PTC, if you will. I’m not a known name around here. Halfway purposefully so—I have been content to stay in WMW and garner a few successes there. The other half are personal reasons—things that I needed to set aside before being able to step on a PTC stage and succeed. Such as this recent betrayal that I highlighted in my last appearance. Oh yes, I realize all too well that everyone is betrayed at some point in their life, whether by themselves, someone else, or maybe even their own expectations are what betrays them. It is a fact of life, but that does not make it any easier to deal with, unless one is so incredibly callous that nothing matters anymore. I prefer not to be so—betrayal may stab more, but success is always sweeter as well.” .:: Shadow paused, to think. There was much on her mind regarding Shaman, whom she actually happened to find rather curious. ::. “I do not know where you have found the idea that I do not take you seriously, or do not see you as a threat. Please, Shaman—I’m not like most other people in a tournament like this. I’m not feeding off of my own ego in order to pretend that I’m greater than I am. My own greatness doesn’t matter to me. In fact, that’s about the last thing I do in any situation like this—sit around and think I’m better than everyone else in the tournament. Odds are I’m not. Odds are even if I make it past you, someone else who’s better than me will knock me out of this tournament, maybe even in the next round. I don’t care about ego-tripping, and I don’t make a habit of sitting around and deluding myself by thinking that my opponents are all idiots. Granted, perhaps some of them are. Perhaps even you are. I don’t know you well enough to say. But I think you’re going a little far to take such pains to remind me that I should take you seriously. Obviously you are not as well acquainted with me as you think, monsieur, because I take all of my opponents seriously, whether they remind me to do so or not. I look at each match as a battle, a test that I must endure in order to prove to myself. You are no different. Never once did I insinuate that I did not take you seriously—in fact, I did not even speak before you decided, not once, but twice, that I wasn’t taking you as seriously as you needed to be taken. You are a bit presumptuous, monsieur, and I am no psychologist, but I have to wonder what exactly your issue is, bringing this up so vehemently before I have even spoken.” .:: Her brow furrowed. Truth be told, Shaman had not only puzzled her, but irritated her slightly as well. And that was never a good thing. ::. “You hold your research on me over my head, Shaman. Yet I know that it is pointless for you to go searching for information on me because you will find very little that is of use. If you actually managed to run upon someone who had information on me that would be even remotely helpful, I assure you, they would sooner kill you than provide you with any information. You say that you have done such research, and yet you nullify that claim by the way you speak of me—saying that I run from my past, saying that I don’t take you seriously, and whatever other such nonsense you managed to come up with. What are you playing at? It is no use dangling your supposed knowledge of me over my head when you make it plainly obvious that you have no clue whatsoever who I am, or what I am about. In short, what I hear is ‘a tale told by an idiot, full of sound and fury, signifying nothing.’ Words are powerful weapons, monsieur. At this point, you’ve shot all your arrows—but the problem is you shot them at the wrong target, and here I am, still standing.” .:: She smirked, folding her arms over her chest. This was no time for being smug, and yet she still found it quite amusing that Shaman could claim to know so much about her while still completely missing the point of her essence, of who she really was. ::. “Perhaps you should listen to those around you, Shaman. They might offer you advice—for instance, the fellow who advised you to think a little more heavily on my use of the nom de guerre Shadow. Let me talk philosophy with you for a moment.” .:: She took a deep breath, uncrossing her arms, and leaning forward, her eyes gleaming with something that was entirely unpleasant, and rather unsettling to the pit of one’s stomach. ::. “You call yourself Shaman, you should know what I’m about to talk about… To everything, there is a balance, a light and a dark. Without the light, there would be no dark. Without the bad, there would be no good. There are thousands of different examples I could give you of the dichotomy of this world, and how, one way or another, we are all affected by shifts in this balance. There are those of us who would say that we are born innately good—I disagree. We are born hanging somewhere in between on that scale, and it is only as we grow and learn that we choose to align ourselves with one side or another. I say that we make this choice, because there is no human on this earth who stays completely neutral and unaffected by the world around him for his entire life. Most people err on the side of caution, and, fearing for their souls, choose to play at being saints. But we all have a bad side—there is no person on earth who is infallible, no matter what they say. I believe in shamanic belief, they call this side the ‘shadow self.’ Is it becoming a little clearer now? If not, I will be happy to enumerate—there’s no point in pretending that you’re Mother Theresa if you’re not. I realize fully that there are too many people in this world who see things as either all white or all black, and never realize that there is gray in between. And yet it is that gray area that we should be striving for—we know this simply from natural facts. If there were too much water, the earth would be overrun by it, and everything would die. But if there were not enough, every living thing would perish of thirst. Yet still humans choose to delude themselves by thinking that they can be a saint, thinking that there is actually a conceivable way to do no wrong and still learn what they’ve been put on this earth to learn. To get to the point, monsieur—I embrace my darker side as a part of myself. I realize that without it, I would not be who I am. Whether that dark side is negative or not, I make no claims to know. Perhaps my dark side is the better half of me, perhaps not. Whatever the case… it is just as much a part of who I am as anything else. I don’t waste time pretending to be a saint because I’m not, nor do I want to be. There are too many people running around in their robes of white trying to join the choir invisible already. I’m not one of them, and I won’t ever be—by choice. Someone has to try and tip the balance back the other way, n’est-ce pas?” .:: For a moment, she paused. There was still something else that she wanted to get off her chest before this was over. ::. “And yet there is something else—people have taken, through my career, to calling me Shady. It’s not particularly an affectionate nickname, but one that denotes my nature just as much as Shadow does. At times, it seems that I switch alliances with the wind as it blows, but always I stay true to myself. People don’t trust me, and that’s why they call me Shady. Really, the problem is that they claim to know me when they don’t—and even if they do get an insight, they don’t understand it. I follow the path that is given me, and it’s not always obvious to others why I do it. To put it simply, there are those who would view me as a mystery, as a force that cannot be controlled. And those would be the people who have finally gotten the point—aside from childish hate games, or kissing up to me to gain favor… these are the people who understand that I am neither good nor bad, neither black nor white, but only myself. I fill whatever role you need me to fill, Shaman. Only you can choose what it is, but please spare me the annoyance of having to listen to you assume that you know why I fill it. Because you don’t, and you never will.” .:: She paused again. Over and over again, Shaman’s words repeated in her head, an innocuous rhythm that, rather than lulling her, frothed the brew inside her to its boiling point. ::. “Your charming threats to haunt my nightmares are fallacy. My dreams, just like my waking hours, are not something that are controlled easily by anyone else, and it’s a pity that you must resort to such cliché in order to make yourself seem strong. It’s simple, Shaman: no matter what you say, I will not be intimidated, because I am not easily intimidated. What I will be instead is focused. That is what I always am, especially in regards to something as important as this, something as magnanimous as GTT4. “ .:: She stood up from her seat, the shadows encompassing her face now. She moved about the room, seemingly in preparation for this to end, but it was impossible to know. She continued speaking, although since her head couldn’t be seen, it was more the effect of a disembodied voice, echoing from the walls. ::. “I offer you nothing but the respect of an opponent who takes this tournament very seriously, and will do so until she is knocked out of it. Whether we are alike or similar is of no consequence—what sort of semantics games we play at is of no consequence as well. What matters is that we will step into the ring to do battle with one another; one of us will be the victor, and the other will be defeated. I don’t make predictions about who will win or lose, because that is completely inane. I will warn you only once: do not take me lightly. What do I mean by that? I mean doing what you are doing now. Pretending that you know something about me, and pretending that stereotypes and rumors will get you through this match. Listening to rumors and pretending to gather information from stereotypes will get you nowhere. I am not your typical opponent. In fact, I’m the exception to the rule—whatever rule you’re using. I am an elusive creature, purposefully so because there are not many worthy to know the depths of my soul, and you, monsieur, with all due respect, are certainly not one of them.” .:: She paused in her movement. ::. “I’ve issued my warning. If you choose to take it seriously or not, I cannot truly know, nor can I waste much time caring, to be perfectly blunt. While you sit around wondering whether or not I take you seriously, I have a tournament to prepare for, and that is what I will do.” .:: Her voice was strong, resolute. Having said that, there was nothing more that she wished to say on Shaman, or her match. It would all take care of itself when the time came—if she won, she was amicable, and if she lost, she would go back to WMW to prepare for whatever Fate had in store for her there. The lights in the room flicked slowly out—it was time to sleep now. ::. .:: ::: ::. —The hot air of Persia breathed around her, sticky-sweet with incense and oils. As she awoke, she stretched her limbs, basking in the almost unpleasantly hot Persian sunlight. She stretched out across the wide expanse of bed, expecting to find Amir’s form next to her, but he wasn’t there—and the bed was long cold as though he hadn’t been there at some time. She opened her eyes fully now, a distended frown on her face. — —She pushed the covers back, milk white skin spilling into the light. It seemed like no matter how long she spent in the Persian sunlight, her skin never changed from that snow-white color. But Amir had always told her how beautiful she was. Still, she frowned. Why was he always gone when she woke up? If there was one thing that she really hated about him, that was it. Rising from the bed, she slipped on the silk wrapper that lay in the chair next to her side of the bed. It was an opulent purple design—Amir had picked it out, insisting vehemently that purple would match her eyes splendidly. She would have chosen something a bit calmer, but she couldn’t help enjoying the exotic flush that it seemed to give her. — —She pushed open the doors onto the balcony. The marble tiles were warm against her feet, almost too hot to walk on, but not quite there—it was cooling slightly for this time of year, and it seemed that today was a rather nice day, not at all as abysmal and sweltering as most Persian days were given to being. The sunlight poured onto her form, almost instantly warming her ebony hair, but not at the expense of her comfort, as sometimes happened in the summers here. — —With an easy assurance, she leaned on the balcony, looking over the edge down into the precipice below. She’d never understand how anyone could build in such hostile conditions, but in any case, she never failed to appreciate the beautiful surroundings. She and Amir spent almost every summer here—so many summers to spend.— —Sometimes, she failed to understand him, even in the slightest. He was always up earlier than her, always brooding over something, yet he often didn’t tell her what. Amir was one gigantic puzzle to her, and instead of getting frustrated, she would have happily spent a lifetime trying to figure him out. It was this thought that made her smile at the moment, even though she was looking over this miraculous beauty alone, when she should have been doing it with him.— —Her thoughts were interrupted by the voice of Nasim. She turned around to greet him as he spoke, a smile on her face.— “Salaam, Madame-selle Liana.” —She laughed.— “Nasim, no one has called me Mademoiselle in years. If you want to address me with the French formality, it’s Madame Asha now.” —He looked confused—poor Nasim. All his life, he’d served Amir quite faithfully, but adjusting to having a French woman in the house was almost more than he could understand. In fact, her adjusting to living in Persia had been rather uncomfortable as well. He held a tray out to her, which held a glass of tall titian juice—mango juice, she surmised—and she made a face.— “I’d prefer not to drink any of that.” —Nasim bowed his head respectfully.— “Pardon, Madame Asha, but Master says you must. He says your immune system—“ “Isn’t yet strong enough not to get dehydrated by the Persian heat, I know, I know. But how long has he said that? And how many years have we been coming here?” —Nasim looked troubled.— “Please, Madame, Master will be displeased if I don’t carry out his orders for today.” —She thought of being annoyed for a moment, but she did hate to see Nasim so upset.— “I’ll drink it, Nasim, but only for you.” —She smiled at him and took the glass off the tray, taking a gulp of it and grimacing at the thick sweetness. Nasim did not smile—rarely did Nasim smile, but she often wondered why—if it was something she had done, or if there really was that large of a culture gap between them. She thought about it for a moment, before taking another sip of the juice, which was already warming in the bright Persian sunlight. Grimacing, she took another sip. As she finished the glass, she handed it back to Nasim and sat it on the tray. Normally, he would have turned and gone back into the complex, but he stood still staring at her, as though expecting something. He bowed respectfully.— “Begging your pardon, Madame Asha, but did you hear me?” “Hm?” —She had turned her attention back to the breath-taking view that sloped about in sharp corners around her. Nasim spoke again.— “Master says he will be back tonight and that you should be prepared for him to be at dinner. He wishes to spend some time with you out and about, I believe—he asks that you be dressed nicely.” —Her brow furrowed. It was all well and good that Amir wanted to spend time with her, but why was it never on HER time?— “And what if I do not wish to dress nicely this evening?” —Nasim bowed again, as though speaking his mind might cause him to get a sound knot on the head. But in her mood, it well might.— “I would not advise it, Madame Asha. You know how he gets in a fearful temper sometimes… Today seems to be one of those days if you will forgive me for saying so.” “What is the purpose of his orders, Nasim? Why am I constantly waking up in bed alone with only cryptic messages explaining away his absence and telling me to wait his return? I tell you, c’est merde!” —Nasim looked troubled.— “I do not understand what you mean, Madame Asha.” —She often ranted in French when she was not thinking about it—and she had already gotten herself started. Nasim’s confusion hardly registered in her mind.— “I am not his little puppy! I’m not interested in sitting through another stately dinner this evening with so-called important dignitaries whom I will never see again, and whose names I will never remember!” “I apologize for disturbing you, Madame.” —Nasim bowed, and made a hasty exit—but she wasn’t quite finished yet.— “I am not the possession of some elegant Persian dignitary, Nasim! I am not to be paraded around as though I am the most impressive toy in his collection!” —It was no use—Nasim never responded to her rantings. No one did. Sometimes, she felt as though no one truly understood her. She dropped her enraged fist, letting her fingers slowly unclench. There was no use being angry. She would have done anything for her Amir, and she knew it, no matter if she found it aggravating that he was always gone from her when she woke up or not.— —From the shadows slunk another figure—Amir’s sister, Vashti. She put a hand on Liana’s arm.— “You must not be so cruel to him, Liana. He loves you so…” —Before she could respond, something changed—the fabric of the world around her ripped, her eyes fogging, and then coming back to life on a scene entirely different than the exotic locale she had been visiting.— —Snow dappled the grass at her feet. She was sitting in the midst of the countryside, a few miles out of Rouen. That was all she knew. At her side, a young woman looked perturbed, so perturbed.— “He doesn’t understand me as well as I’d like.” —That was the simple truth, or so she thought. The young woman at her side had been clutching vehemently at her hand, but let it go, with a sigh, a frustrated sigh.— “Mere, when will you quit clinging to these restless dreams? You are not in Persia! You are in France! Remember? Your cottage, just outside Rouen! Armand and I brought you here because you were ill in Paris, and we thought the country air would do you good, and help you start writing again.” —Liana looked down at this woman addressing her, remembering—her daughter. Her daughter Coralie Cendrille, grown into a woman, and married. Yes, she remembered that much—but she remembered Persia too. She reached for her daughter with the shaking, withered hands of an old woman.— “Coralie, where did he go?” —Her daughter sighed, and sat down.— “Oh, Mama… Must I really tell you again?” —Her mother’s eyes seemed to show that she did not comprehend being told this story before. Coralie sighed, and began.— “Mama, you and Amir never got married. He went off to fight in the Napoleonic wars, and he never came back. He died in Russia. Remember?” —Her mother’s eyes filled with tears. Frustration grew in her daughter.— “Mama, you make me tell you this story almost every day, and it always upsets you! Why can’t you remember? He went off to the war, and you found out you were pregnant with me, and you named me Coralie Cendrille—Cendrille because I was from the ashes of your love with him, remember?” —Still, Liana looked at her as though she did not understand. The tears in her eyes spilled over.— “Please leave, Coralie.” —Her daughter burst into tears and stormed back in the house. Liana stared out across the frozen landscape as she heard her daughter inside talking to her husband Armand.— “Armand, it happens every day! I always have to tell her that she never experienced these things with Amir, and she always gets upset and sends me away!” —Coralie broke into sobs and for a moment, that was the only sound audible, before Armand spoke up, in a deep and comforting voice.— “Coralie, there is no sense getting so upset over it. She is an old woman now, and she can’t get over the trauma of her youth. The best you can do is just let her have her delusions in peace.” “I won’t lie to her, and let her live in a fairy tale!” “Shh… she’ll hear you. You’re not letting her live in a fairytale, amour… You’re making an old woman’s last months happy. Can’t you do that for her?” —Liana shook her head. She knew what she was talking about—she remembered. She and Amir had spent the summers in Persia and the winters everywhere else. She hadn’t imagined it. She knew that she hadn’t.— .:: ::: ::. “LIANA!” .:: Shadow almost screamed as she sat up in bed, snapping to attention. She half expected to see Amir there standing over her, screaming her name like that—but that was impossible, she told herself. Amir was dead and gone, Amir wasn’t a part of things anymore. It was Riven. Her brow furrowed. ::. “Liana? Why did you call me Liana?” .:: He tilted his head at her. ::. “You told me to call you that if I ever needed to get your attention quickly. Do you not remember?” .:: She nodded, bringing a hand to her forehead. God, how her head was pounding—and she still had not only the WMW Darwin’s Revenge tournament to worry about, but her Round 2 GTT4 match with Shaman to be concerned about as well. She rubbed her temple. Riven was silent for a moment. ::. “Did you forget that you had something to do tonight, Shadow? Julian Falcon’s contract.” .:: She gasped—a silly, rather mortal gesture, but she sprang out of bed almost immediately, yanking open the closet of Lakota’s room, which was now filled with her things. She knelt down, pulling out a small chest at the base of it. It creaked open, and Shadow began rifling through the belongings inside, pulling out the clothes that were most professional and befitting of a hit—dark clothing, simple and slim-fitting, yet easy to maneuver in, and easy enough to hide weapons in as well. Riven stepped out of the room, satisfied that he had done his utmost to alert her to the time, so that she didn’t get herself in a lot of trouble with this Falcon fellow. ::. .:: Shadow dressed quickly—the shirt that she threw on was a black tank-top, and paired with it was a pair of sleek-fitting black pants, tucked hastily into black boots that laced up almost to her knees. Her hair she pulled into a loose, but practical braid, a long tail that trailed all the way down her back. For a moment, she paused in front of the mirror. Her smooth ivory skin never needed makeup, but she often chose to line her eyes in black, which she did now. Her lips she traced in blood-red crimson. There was no sense in having this fellow’s last memory be unpleasant after all. Once she was satisfied with her looks, there were only two things left: the tools of the trade, and her robe. ::. .:: She stepped over to the large wooden dresser in her room, and pulled out a holster which she clipped around her slim waist. She slid a perfectly shined silver revolver into it—but that was only if worst came to worst. Around her other thigh was a clip, into which she slipped a long, ornate dagger—her typical tool of choice. The one last touch was a poison ring on her left pinky finger—that was just in case the bastard was tough to take down, but from what Julian Falcon had said, it would be just another hit for her, nothing too complicated in the least. ::. .:: The robe was last—her ceremonial robe, although she figured it was more like a tunic than anything else. She slipped it over the attire that she was wearing—long slits ran up the sides of it so that she would be free to move about as she needed to. With a moment’s hesitation to look at herself in the mirror, her nimble fingers buttoned the frog on the high collar, and smoothed down the ornate black silk—just like he’d taught her. She opened the cylindrical door of her room and pulled it shut behind her, stepping out into the common room without any noise whatsoever, save a slight rustling of her robe. Riven looked up from his reading—he’d never seen her in full Assassins’ garb before, but it was most striking. ::. “I’ll be back.” .:: Even her voice had changed—low, predatory and dangerous. ::. “Good luck, Shadow.” .:: She turned to him, and a smirk darted across her face. It was slightly ironic to think that something so beautiful was about to go out and do what she was about to do, but he supposed that was the irony of Assassins sometimes. ::. “I don’t need luck, ami. I’ve got talent.” .:: Like so many other situations in her life—this hit, Darwin’s Revenge, and GTT4. What use was wishing on a star or praying for victory when you had skill on your side? With not another word, she slipped out the exit door of the compound, leaving Riven to his silent contemplation. ::.
