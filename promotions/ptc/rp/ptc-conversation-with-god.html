Conversation with God [Yes, I know this piece is a little out there. I've written four RP's in the last week and my mind is a little out there! Hope you enjoy it as I actually enjoyed writing it. Apologies for any errors that are in the piece. Thanks for the chance. Amazing to be competing against fine people.] “Here are your keys, Mr. Maloof,” the young sounding voice came from above with an interesting accent to it. Craig, who was sitting down in a bench outside of an airport, was reading a magazine that he wasn’t particularly interested in. As he looked up, he saw the young, radiant girl holding a pair of keys in her hands. Behind her was the vehicle he had requested while staying in Melbourne, Australia, the latest stop on the PRIME World Tour leading up to Culture Shock. Craig pulled himself up off the bench and took the keys from her hands. “Thanks,” Craig said with a smile on his face. The woman just simply nodded her head as he started to walk away from her. “Oh, Mr. Maloof,” the young woman yelled out after him. Craig turned around and she was still standing in the same spot as before, but now facing him. “Good luck in your match tomorrow,” she told him and he just nodded his head and smiled at her again. A man of few words, he turned around and walked towards his Black 2006 Mustang with its top down. He got inside of the car and breathed a sigh of relief. After a few delays, he had finally reached Melbourne, not only the sight of the next PRIME event, but the sight of a huge match for him. A shot at the Extreme Title. As he drove away from the airport, he started to bury himself in his thoughts. The officials for PTC thought it would be best to hold this match while PRIME was in Australia, the home country of the PTC headquarters, because of the number of PRIME stars taking part in it. For Craig, he was glad at this suggestion and gladly accepted it. He had yet to be in a bigger match than this, and he wanted to be at the top of his game. Around him, the sun was starting to set as he pulled onto one of the highways. He had been booked at a hotel an hour outside of Melbourne, away from most of civilization. It was the way he liked to travel. Generally, no other PRIME stars would stay at his hotel, but he needed the quiet and the clean air so he could be one with himself. It was different, but it was who he was. Away from the wandering eyes to do what he needed to do from city to city. If he was being watched, it made it rather difficult for him to disappear and take care of business, especially one of such importance. Saving his daughter couldn’t bring him the spotlight. Not here and now. Not when he was so close. The minutes clicked away as Craig sped down the highway, ignoring whatever rules there were put on him, and just letting the speed and the fresh air take him away. Things hadn’t been easy for Craig as of late, and they weren’t going to get any easier on him with the path he had chosen for himself. Most people liked to take the path of least resistance. Unfortunately, Craig was one who craved for the confrontation at every corner. It was what made him his own person and had also made him so many enemies. It had only been a few short weeks since he seemed determined to end his life. However, he had been given purpose. To save his once thought dead daughter, Sarah. He was growing closer and closer by the minute, but knew that even with her safe, it would be hell to keep her that way. But, he was resolute in his determination to have her back in her arms, and to win her trust back after being gone for so long. She deserved better than him, but she was stuck with him. They shared the same blood. Craig then saw his exit, which would lead to another highway, one much quieter then this one was, and he would be at his hotel in no time. Another few minutes passed by him as he felt the roar of the engine take over him. He closed his eyes for a brief second and re-opened them to see that the sun was disappearing faster than he had thought. Before he knew it, it was night out and he was getting closer and closer to his destination. He desperately needed a peaceful night of sleep. He hoped he would get it. Fate wouldn’t be so kind to him though. POP! Without notice, his car started to swerve out of control. He fought with the steering wheel, trying to calm it down, but it was no use. It was almost as if the car had its own mind. He had seen what happened almost instantaneous as the rubber went flying into the air. He had blown a tire. Craig continued to fight with his car, but the car was quickly winning. With one giant yank of the steering wheel, the car went flying into the air, flipping over and over with such grace it was sickening. As he felt the car jerk into the air, he could only think of one thing. He had already accepted death by this point. With his path in life, he had come to terms with it. However, it was too soon. Finally, he had a purpose in life and so quickly, it was being taken away from him. And the person to suffer because of it? Sarah. --- White. That was all Craig saw. Oh, and a pair of pearly gates. Can you figure out where he is? As his opened his eyes and sat up, he was a little surprised to see him in a location that didn’t resemble dirt, debris, and destruction. Instead, it was a rather large white room that looked like someone had fallen in love with that color. After a few moments of looking around, taking in the scene, and overcoming the shock at not being in scrap of metal dying to death, he saw that there was a black man standing behind a white podium with a rather large book on top of it. Craig slowly pulled himself up off the ground and walked over to him, lost as to where he was. As he got closer, he noticed that the man looked exactly like Morgan Freeman. Actually, it was Morgan Freeman! “Morgan Freeman?! What the hell are you doing here?!” Craig asked, extremely perplexed as to what exactly was going on. Freeman looked at Craig with a smile on his face and patted him on the shoulder. “Ah, my child, this is my side job. You see, I get to make you wait here while talking to you before your meeting. It was something that I actually applied for. Something just seemed fun about it. Especially after Bruce Almighty,” he said in this rich baritone voice. Craig just stood there dumbfounded, not a second wiser as to where he was or why Morgan Freeman was here. He tried to ignore the Morgan Freeman part, and focus on about where he was and why. “Where is here exactly?” Craig asked, finding it rather hard to look at all the white. “Here is relative, isn’t it?” Morgan Freeman responded with a question of his own. “No, not exactly. Physically, at least, it’s not,” Craig said in response, hoping this wasn’t going to become a game of random questions that got him no further in his quest for truth. “It is, but in this sense, it’s not. Do you remember the last thing that happened to you?” Freeman asked, and Craig nodded his head. It was the freshest thing in his memory, and for a man with no memory, that’s saying something. “Yeah. I remember the car accident. Am I dead?” Craig asked, trying to get answers, and still not getting any no matter how hard he tried to ask straight forward questions. “Aren’t we all? In some form or way, aren’t we all de--,” Freeman began to respond in his usual, quisitive self. Craig’s patience was quickly growing thin with all of this. He just wanted answers. He needed answers and he was far from getting them. At least from Morgan Freeman. “Listen, enough with this. This isn’t one of your movies, this is real life. Just tell me, where I am?” Craig asked, hoping that Freeman would finally knock it off. It was no use. Instead of responding in his vague, philosophical way, he flipped open his book and started to read it. Craig just sighed as he turned away from him, hoping that maybe someone else was around here. Unfortunately, unless the person was wearing white and blended in with all of the other white in the room, he was alone with Morgan Freeman. “So, Craig, I see you’ve tried to kill yourself before,” Freeman said ominously and Craig swiftly turned back around. Freeman than looked up at him and just smiled at him. Craig was rather shocked that he knew anything about this, and wanted to know why he knew this. “How do you --?” he began to say before Freeman raised his hand to cut him off. “I know everything. Your entire life story is here. Well, at least, it should be. There are many pages missing here. Now, that’s odd,” Freeman said, perplexed as he looked at the book and saw pages ripped out. “I lost my memories,” he said, hoping that Freeman might be able to explain that. Yet, the look on his face told him otherwise. “Yeah, but it’s almost like someone came in here and ripped the pages right out of your mind and the bo--,” he began to say, but then he was stopped. Behind him, there was a creaking noise and as he turned around, the pearly white gates were opening behind him. Craig looked on, wondering if he was supposed to go forward. “You said something about a meeting. Who is my meeting with?” Craig asked, hoping that maybe there was an answer this time. However, precedence took place once again. “That shall be all revealed. He’s ready for you,” he said, motioning towards the white gates and Craig knew he was to walk forward into them. Slowly, Craig walked towards there, not knowing what was waiting for him. For Craig, he was not used to this. He hated unfamiliarity. It took him out of his element and this was definitely a place where he didn’t belong. As he reached the threshold, he looked back at Freeman who was looking right at him. “Who is waiting for me?” Craig asked, already knowing the answer. “Now, I can’t give you all the answers. That you will have to find out yourself,” he said and Craig just shook his head. As he turned around and walked through the white gates, he didn’t know what to expect, but it was surely not this. --- Red and blue lights flashed rapidly. Sirens soared through the night. Two police cars, an ambulance, and a fire truck pulled up to scene as fast as they possible could. Before them was a considerable amount of damage. A car, damaged beyond all unbelief, and a person inside of it. A few firemen and an EMT jumped out of the ambulance and immediately rushed to the car. The only noise in the air was the sirens. Everything else was silent. The firemen looked on in horror at the shape of the car as it was wrapped around itself a tetherball on a chain wrapped around a pole. The started to look inside, hoping for some sign of life or something. Unfortunately, he wasn’t moving. They immediately thought the worse. “We need to get the Jaws of Life out of here, and now! We don’t have much time,” one of the firemen yelled out. The two police officers looked around, trying to figure out if this was an accident or a hit and run scenario. As they looked on, they both remarked to each other that they had never seen anything like this. They immediately rushed over to the fire truck and started helping the men get their tools so they could save the man inside of the car. “Haven’t seen anything like this. Have you?” one of the firemen asked the two police officers, and they immediately shook their heads no. Whoever the man inside was, they already figured he was dead. However, they needed to make sure. He deserved more dignity then this. As they rushed over and started to pry the metal off, they realized they had a long night ahead of them. If he was alive, he would be dead before they even finished. He would need a miracle. --- Craig couldn’t move. Before him laid a man dressed in a white robe with a sword through his chest. The blood on the floor around him though was dried. It looked as if he had been there for quite a while. He almost figured it was a statue as he moved towards it, but saw it was no such thing. It was a human being. Or so he thought it was a human being. “I’ve laid here for two years, my corporeal body here, void and vacant. I’ve been waiting for you,” a loud, booming voice came out of nowhere. Craig looked around, looking for some kind of answers, but just as before, he was left in the dark. “Where am I?! What the fuck is going on?! I’m tired of being treated like this. I want some damn answers,” Craig yelled, frustrated with what’s been happening to him. “Yes, answers. You deserve those,” the voice boomed once again. As Craig looked around, he wanted to see a face to this voice. He was disappointed though as there was nobody in the room with him except the dead body. “I shall just make this short and simple for you. You’re on the road to death and this is your final stop,” the voice said, chilling Craig right to his core. Why this moment? Why did he have to die now when so many times he tried to end his life before and it was impossible to do so? “This is the end of my life? How convenient,” he said, disgusted with everything. “No, this is your time for a second chance. A chance to redeem your life,” the voice echoed once again. “How so? Why do I deserve a second chance?” Craig asked, lost as to how this was even possible. He was almost certain that Garbage Bag Johnny had slipped some weed into his coffee and he wasn’t even actually here. “To right what you have wrong. You’ve done good. You’ve done bad. You still have much to accomplish in your life, and I need you to see that. Before you lays the body of God,” the voice said. Craig could only look at the body, wondering if the voice was speaking the truth. “Okay, now this sounds like a joke to me. What’s going on? I’m tired of this,” Craig said, growing tired of the theatrics. “If I’m dead, then let me die already! This is just some cruel trick you’re playing on me? So be it! But, stop toying with me. Stop playing me like I’m a puppet, st--,” Craig managed to get out before being interrupted. “Fine. You want answers? How about I hit you with this answer. The reason my body lays there before you, dead and empty? Because you killed me,” the voice said with a seriousness to it that Craig took it as truth. He just looked at the body, lost as to what happened. How could this be? He felt like this was just one weird hallucination, but it felt so real to him. Too real. “Now, are you ready to listen? Because, this part is good,” the voice said and Craig just stood there, listening and waiting to hear what is going on. --- Two hours. That’s how long it took the firemen, EMT’s, and police officers to get the metal unwrapped and to get the man out of his car. As they dragged him out, the EMT’s immediately started to check his pulse. To his amazement, there wasn’t a single scratch on Craig’s body. No blood either. Yet, he was lifeless. Not moving. Unconscious. As he started to check his heart rate, he realized something rather odd about a man with seemingly no injuries to him. There was no pulse. “This guy… he’s dead,” the EMT said. One of the police officers walked over and checked him for any kind of identification. He found a brown leather wallet and pulled it out. As he looked at the ID, he was floored by who it was. “Do you know who this guy is? This is Craig Maloof. He’s a superstar wrestler,” the officer said and looked down at Craig’s sullen face. “My kids love this guy.” “Well, I guess you’re going to have to tell his kids that he’s dead. Let’s load him up and take him to the hospital for further tests. Let’s keep this from the media as long as possible,” the EMT told the officer and the other EMT started to get out the stretcher as quickly as possible. It was a sad sight by all. No injuries. He seemed fine in every possible way. Yet, he was dead. --- “Three years ago, you stood before, bereaved with grief from your daughter’s death. You wanted answers. You needed them. You blamed me for what happened to her and you took it out on me. To this day, I haven’t the slightest clue as to how you did it, and I need answers. I need you to give me the answers I so desperately seek, because without them, I’m just a spirit without a body. A God without a body is a sad, pathetic existence to be,” the voice said, finally identifying itself with God. Craig, however, could not move. Before him was the body of God and he had killed him, a memory he didn’t even remember. “I—I don’t remember,” Craig said, his memories blank. “What?” God said, wondering how this could be true. “My memories were gone. I woke up seven months ago with no memories and having been gone for two years. I don’t remember this. I don’t remember killing anybody!” he yelled out, frustrated and at wits end. He collapsed to his knees; needing answers for questions that he didn’t even know existed. This was not the world he knew. A world where he could kill Gods. A world where he could kill period. It drove him crazy. It almost drove him to death last time, but this time he had a purpose. “I’m searching for these answers everyday, and I can’t find them. The only thing I have left is my daughter, and then you drop this on me. I don’t know myself. I don’t know my past or barely even my present. All I have left is a future and I’m not even sure if I have that anymore. I need a second chance,” Craig said, bewildered and lost. “Then, you shall get that. These answers are vital to us, Craig. You need the answers for the same reason I need the answers. To move forward. You will be granted a second chance,” Craig was told and he was shocked by this. He would be given a second chance. “You have much to live for. Your aura is unlike anything I’ve ever seen before. Unique in its own way. There is a destiny to you that not even I can read. Most people are like an open book. Your mind is a like fortress that I can’t penetrate. I only fear that the worse hasn’t happened to you,” the voice finished saying and Craig pulled himself off of the ground. “I fear that too,” Craig said before turning around and seeing the pearly gates behind him, waiting for him to walk out of them, and take his second chance. He had so many questions he wanted answer and as he stood there, he thought about asking them. Yet, he was silenced before he had a chance. “I know you have many questions. We shall see each other again, when you have the answers I need to answer your questions,” the voice said and Craig just nodded his head. He then walked out the pearly gates, knowing that this time around, he had to right his wrongs. He intended to do just that. --- “Time of death--,” the doctor began to say, but he was shockingly interrupted. “Let’s… let’s not do that quite yet,” Craig said, finding himself lying flat on a cold metal table. His red eyes looked straight up and he figured immediately he was in a hospital. It was the logical choice. As he slowly sat up and looked around, he saw stunned looks on everyone’s faces. Craig couldn’t say he blamed them. But, he wasn’t dead. He was very much alive. “What the…” the doctor said, immediately checking him out, and making sure this was all real. Surprisingly to him, it very much was. The body he was ready to announce dead was actually alive even though he hadn’t had a pulse for well over an hour now. Craig just smiled, trying to calm the nerves of everyone in the room. Yet, it was no use. They had literally seen a man rise from the dead. Frankly, if he hadn’t just had a conversation with God, then he would be shocked that he was alive as well. But, speaking with God trumps, well, everything in life. “Listen, you can run tests, but I’m alive. I also don’t like hospitals, so I’d love to just get out of here as soon as possible,” Craig said, trying to get someone to actually talk to him as if he was a human being and not a corpse. After a few moments of people checking on him, he was taken to the lobby of the hospital and after another hour, he was discharged after hearing the word miracle uttered a few dozen times. Yet, he had things to take care of. His career and saving his daughter were the forefront thoughts on his mind. He had to find the answers to the questions he desperately wanted to know as well as the questions he wanted to avoid. But, he no longer had a choice. He had to answer them. Fin.
