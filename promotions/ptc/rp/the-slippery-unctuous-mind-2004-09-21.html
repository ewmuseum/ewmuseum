The Slippery, Unctuous Mind

21 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-21 Delight. A 59 year old man defeated a young, physically fit, and previously undefeated superstar in Avatar William Gwynn. Who did it? Joe Black did, that’s who. Even though Gwynn dominated the match, even though Gwynn threw Joe Black around the ring like a sack of potatoes, even though Joe Black found himself icing down both his knees and his left shoulder after the match, even though Joe Black woke up the next morning feeling like he’d been hit by a train, even though Joe managed to win by a combination of eyerakes, cleverly concealed shots to Gwynn’s groin, and a pin assisted by the ropes, Joe Black was the winner. It was a cloud nine feeling. Approach the brackets for round two shall we? Yes, let’s. George English. For approximately two minutes, Joe Black rubbed his goatee staring at the name. George English. Finally, he remembered something. A match in round one. George English in the middle of the ring, standing there, looking incredibly confused as Doctor Curiosity ran around him in circles screaming at the guy on the outside with the fake hunchback. Joe Black’s swagger and confidence wavered, as he tried to understand this information. If he had to use one word to describe George English, he would use the word “old”. “Oh no.” At first Joe didn’t know who said those words. “Oh no.” By the second time he heard them he realized they came from his own mouth. Suddenly, Joe Black found himself feeling just a little bit tired. His knees were aching again, and he walked over to a nearby bench and sat down. Joe slumped, his eyes downcast at the floor between his boots. They’d paired him up with another old man. They want to see the geriatrics battle it out. Maybe throw some special stipulations in there, “Orthopedic Cane On A Pole” match. He’d entered this, expecting a degree of mockery being at the age he was, but he’d imagined once he’d proved himself that it would change. He’d come through the first round victorious against a young man who was stronger, faster, and more athletic than himself. That was worth some degree of credit, wasn’t it? He’d proved that he could be a contender to anybody in this tournament; that was worth something, wasn’t it? The fact that the brackets now said, “Joe Black vs. George English,” proved otherwise. “This isn’t right.” Victory wasn’t supposed to make you feel like a loser. Depression was opening below him like some great creatures drooling maw, waiting to devour him, render him completely helpless. == The secret of men like Joe is simple. They believe in themselves, and accomplish what they want through refusal to settle for less. This is not the same as determination. Determination means more of a struggle in the face of adversity. To men like Joe, adversity is inconsequential. They reach for more than they deserve because they believe they are simply entitled to anything they desire. This is how assholes convince otherwise decent young women to have sex with them just by asking for it. This is how people convince you to open charge accounts at stores when you don’t really want to. This is why telemarketing continues to turn a profit though it is one of the most obnoxious interruptions a person who owns a phone has to deal with. Somewhere, on the other side of that counter, on the other side of that phone, across from the young lady at the bar, is someone like Joe, who reaches out and grasps hold of anything they can get, and come up with something more often than not. They take advantage of people’s kindness. They take advantage of people’s good manners. They take advantage of the benefit of the doubt. They take advantage of impulsive decisions. These people are pimps, who have a stable of twenty women who get paid to have sex with men, and give all the money to the pimp, in spite of the fact that at any moment all twenty women could grab the son of a bitch, and do anything they wanted to him if they worked together. People like Joe achieve more through strength of personality than any other trait. Because of this, their self confidence is essential to their success. == “This isn’t fair.” == There were times this summer where Joe was feeling a little less than confident. The day to day activity in tSC, the fact that all he’d ever be to that company was a road agent in spite of his experience with the business, his cunning, his clever means of getting anything done with the least amount of effort. As much as the Avalon’s liked him initially, and wanted him to be a part of their wrestling promotion, as time passed they found that Joe’s method of approaching matters to be a little less progressive than what they looked for. Brad Robinson was the head road agent, and while Joe had a better handle on the talent, Brad was far more organized than Joe was, and presented himself better. Joe on the other hand, prevented bad situations before they started which unfortunately meant when he was doing his job well, it looked like he wasn’t doing anything at all. But underneath that was the truth that he wasn’t doing that much. The Squared Circle’s wrestlers for the most part were very happy to be working there. There were times when Joe went to sleep in his hotel room, wondering how much it would matter if he didn’t wake up in the morning. Not necessarily to the other people that he worked with, he was sure they’d all suddenly find a million things that they really liked about him the moment they heard he was dead. It was more regarding how much it would matter to Joe if he died during the night. The best beer in the world was Coors Light, the ole Silver Bullet. The finest cigars were from Cuba. The best sex he ever had was with a ring rat named Delores in 1983. The greatest song ever was Johnny Cash’s “Ring of Fire”, followed closely by “I Walk The Line,” followed by the Man In Black’s cover of Nick Cave And The Bad Seeds’ “The Mercy Seat”. The finest food in the world was undoubtedly a T-Bone steak circa 1962, back in the day when you’d finish a steak and suck on the bones because the flavor was so good. Every time he ate steak in recent times it tasted like it’d been made at a cow factory. It amused him to think that hardly anyone realized that all the real steak was gone, that the finest steak money could buy from the top quality of Angus Beef was incredibly poor compared to the average cut you got from the old butcher shop back in the day. While it was upsetting to think that he’d never taste a cut of steak that good again, at least he knew that it was good once. In counting of the list of the finest things in life he’d ever known, Joe would find little reason to care much whether he lived and died from then on. With one exception. The best match he’d ever had was … == “This isn’t fucking right.” == All throughout his wrestling career, Joe had been careful. If there were rough bumps in the tag team days with his brother, it was Ron who took them. Protective of his own body’s longevity, Joe was aghast at hardcore wrestling and gimmick matches that shortened careers. Saving himself for the long run, Joe was absolutely agonized by the mutilations people were performing on their own bodies, particularly upset over the steroid use that plagued the industry for so long. While he and his brother concentrated to train the most talented wrestlers they could at the Black and Black Technical Wrestling Academy, other men were injecting poison to make chiseled bodies suitable for Vince MacMahon’s vision. That was certainly the most frustrating time. Young men left the school, bright and confident, and after a while would drop by again, with an extra thirty pounds of muscle mass, unable to remember things from one moment to the next, and unable to perform how they were taught. Tom Billington, the Dynamite Kid, would be spending the rest of his natural life in a chair, his sacrifices made completely in vain. For what? Those who wouldn’t inject didn’t get a chance; those who would inject were destroyed by their choice. He was sure he was right about his views on safety. But on the other hand, he couldn’t recall a night where he had given it everything, made just a little bit of those sacrifices so that … Joe remembered being at an airport as Tom Billington was wheeled through, and was waiting for a connecting flight. A young man approached the disabled Dynamite Kid and burst into tears, talking about how much he admired Tom, and gushing over his matches against Sayama, the original Tiger Mask. No one was ever going to do that to Joe Black. Ever. == Joe stood back up from the bench, feeling the pain in his knees. He deliberately rolled his sore shoulder. If his time was running out, and he knew that it was, then he had little recourse but to make his life as complete as possible. He needed to keep wrestling until he found his best match. “This is fucking bullshit!” Marching towards the field desk for the Grand Turnbuckle Tournament, Joe treasured every bit of pain in his knees. Slamming one hand on the desk, and pointing towards the posted brackets with the other, he shouted, “What the fuck is this shit?!? Grandpa English? I am one of the finest wrestlers in this tournament. This is a fucking outrage! Do you people consider me to be a joke? Do you?” The confusion was rampant as they tried to figure out what the angry old man was complaining about, but not urgent. As tournament officials, they heard these kinds of outbursts all the time. Usually though, they came from losers who wanted to complain about poor officiating, or more recently, a tirade from people who demanded that they receive a play-in instead of another competitor, which initially was what they thought Joe was complaining about. “Sir, choices regarding play-ins are decided by the Grand Turnbuckle Tournament Administrative Board. The waivers you signed upon entry into the tournament are binding and they put the bracket decisions at the discretion of the Administration. If you weren’t selected for a play-in then I’m afraid complaints like these aren’t going to bring you any satisfaction, so if you could please step away from our table we have other-” Joe grabbed hold of the speaker by his tie and yanked him over the table close to his face. “You don’t even know my fucking name, you worm. What’s my name?” The glasses fell from the poor man’s face as Joe shook him by the collar, “Tell me what my name is, you son of a bitch!” The unfortunate man’s eyes bulged. He’d been so busy with organizing and paperwork he didn’t even get to watch any of the tournament’s first round matches. He swallowed, desperately trying to put a name to the older superstar with a large mouth and a tendency to shout. Older… that was a clue. He’d overheard something earlier today. What was it?” “Uhhh… uhhh.” “Well?” “Black!” realization came, “Joe Black!” “Legendary Joe Black. Say it!” “Legendary Joe Black!” he shouted, thrilled at the lucky recollection. Unfortunately, his curiosity took over as he blinked, staring at Joe, unable to perceive the reason for the outrage, “But … but, you won. Why are you-” Joe shoved the man back across the table, who stumbled backwards a few steps, backing into his co-workers, who prevented him from falling down. As a reflex action, he loosened his tie. “I came to this tournament to beat the ever loving snot out of punk nosed kids like you that are stupid (Note: To be fair, this person wasn’t stupid) enough to consider themselves wrestlers (Note: Also he was yelling at someone who didn’t even really like professional wrestling, much less dreamed of being a wrestler, much less thought he was one). I came to beat men younger than me, faster than me, and stronger than me (Note: Though Joe sure as hell hoped they were stupid enough to fall for his tricks). Why the fuck did you play-in that freaking old fart English? He’s a loser, he doesn’t belong in the ring with me. I defeated Avatar William Gwynn, fair and square, one two three in the middle of that ring in the first round (Note: Gwynn dominated the match, Joe cheated, and the pin was with the ropes for assistance, making the only truth to Joe’s boast the fact that it actually was a pinfall), and I deserve an opponent at my freaking level (Note: Though Joe sure as hell hoped not too much beyond his level). Now why the fuck was he played-in against me?” The man, who was handling this with a great deal of dignity and respect in spite of the fact that he’d just been manhandled, honestly didn’t know as he was several levels removed from the people who actually made these decisions. So he said so. “Look, I have no idea wh-” For all the good it did him. “BULLSHIT! It’s because I’m freaking fifty-nine years old that you threw the old fart at me you miserable motherfucking vultures. You sick fucks think maybe one of us will have a heart attack in the ring and die, won’t you? Well, I’ve got news for you, sonny-boy, it won’t be me that dies in the ring, and it definitely won’t be a god damned heart attack. You sick fucks want to make a fool out of me? Well, I’ll have you kno-” A hand clamped down on Joe’s sore left shoulder, interrupted his tirade with an undignified, “Ow~!” Joe yanked his shoulder away from the hand, and spun around looking in the face of three security guards. Joe sized them up. They were fat, probably not in as good shape as he was, but they also had walkie-talkies and those damned long black flashlights. While Joe had no respect for rent-a-cops, he had plenty of respect for those heavy Mag-Lites, knowing from first hand experience that those things hurt a hell of a lot. The one who grabbed his shoulder smiled. The old man couldn’t take them on, and he knew Joe knew. Joe sneered back, knowing that they knew Joe knew. The other too smiled as well, knowing that Joe knew they knew Joe knew. The fat one in front said, “Time to go,” Grumbling, Joe replied, “Fine, I’m going, I’m going.” They followed him out, snickering to each other as Joe glowered. As soon as Joe exited the building, he spat on the ground, and gave one last glare to his security guard escort. As Joe left the building, his steps were light, his knees didn’t bother him so much. He couldn’t stop himself from smiling, and starting to whistle the bars to “Ring of Fire”. It was a good day for a walk. Maybe he could find a dingy little bar someplace, with Johnny Cash on the jukebox, a bottle of Coors, and big fat woman in the kitchen who knew how to properly cook a steak. The angry outburst had made him feel like his old self again. And it was good.
