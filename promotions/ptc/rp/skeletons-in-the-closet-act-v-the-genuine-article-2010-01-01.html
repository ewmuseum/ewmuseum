Date: 2010-01-01 "Beneath Her Web: Pro Wrestling's 'Spider' Sheds Her Disguise" By William Levine, Staff Writer Miami, Florida. It's just after six on a rainy Thursday morning, and the unassuming redhead across from me at a Miami-area International House of Pancakes doesn't seem nearly as threatening as her fearsome reputation claims, but then that's the idea. Alexandra Pierce, who wrestles for Las Vegas-based Sin City Championship Wrestling under the pseudonym "Desade", certainly lives by the adage that "looks can be deceiving." "I learned a long time ago I would never be the biggest or the strongest, so I resolved to become the smartest," she explains as our waitress deposits her plate of eggs and bacon before her. "In this industry, sometimes that means playing dirty." For many wrestling fans, Pierce's actions since her February 2007 return to the sport have completely rewritten the definition of the term "screwjob," including nearly six months under a mask as plucky heroine "Charlotte Ramone." Her often-intricate plots have earned Desade a myriad of unflattering nicknames over and above the growing list her employers prints on t-shirts. "They have called me a 'menace', even a 'monster'," Alexandra's smile is tight as she carefully cuts her breakfast into bite-sized portions. "I think my favorite, however, is still 'plague bearer', as if my very presence makes the entire locker room sicker. They give me too much credit, I think." It's a surprisingly matter-of-fact admission from a performer in an industry known for eccentric, sometimes even delusional stars. "Make no mistake," she continues. "It is not that I have been unaware that the things I have done could be considered wrong. It's simply that I have only recently been given cause to care." These "things" Alexandra Pierce refers to extend far beyond the chicanery she uses to win matches on SCCW's flagship "Temptation" program (Sunday nights at 10PM on Cinemax). Pierce first rose to national prominence in the wrestling industry for her part in the closure of the much beloved National Wrestling Council in the winter of 2004. "I wish I could say that wasn't my idea, or that I could distance myself from it, as the others have." "It was a business decision, nothing more," she concludes. "Just one I'm uncertain I'd ever make again." Five and a half years later, Pierce's actions at the final NWC event are still all that many fans associate with her name, and she has come to Miami this weekend with a chance to give them something new to talk about. She is one of eight competitors who will face off up the road at the American Airlines Arena in the bracket finals of the seventh edition of the GTT Tournament, promoted by the PrimeTime Central Group (tickets on sale at the arena box office and through ticketmaster.com). "It is quite an honor," Pierce admits. "Only one woman has even come this far in any previous tournament, albeit with many, many more people as her competition, and none have advanced further. To be one of two to have this kind of opportunity to make history is... a bit frightening, in all honesty." It's the other woman in the tournament with her, former SCCW Universal Champion Amy "Aimz" Campbell, that most people associate with Alexandra, and the mention of her name is the first thing that draws a real, honest smile to Pierce's lips.
