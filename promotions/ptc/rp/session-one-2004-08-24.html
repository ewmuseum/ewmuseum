Session One

24 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-08-24 "So, why are you here today?" Eddie Sanders looked up at the man from his position on the couch. He furrowed his brow a little bit, thinking that the question was kind of redundant. Eddie: "What do you mean why am I here? I have problems, why the f*ck else would someone be here? Unless this is a burlesque house in disguise? It isn't, right?" The other guy on the chair at the foot of the couch was a psychologist, a man in his late thirties, maybe early forties. A plaque resting above his head on the wall behind him told his name, Dr. William Kasey. He was the epitome of a typical doctor, in Eddie's mind that was. Wearing a green sweater vest, khakis and a pair of loafers, one could agree with Sanders. All that was missing was a pipe and a pair of glasses. Dr. Kasey: "No, of course not. But there are many reasons as to why people come here. There is obviously something on your mind, or else you wouldn't be here, right?" Eddie: "Yeah..." Dr. Kasey: "So my objective for today is to find out a little bit about you, about your life and your goals for the future. Let's start off with a few easy questions." Eddie nodded his head a little bit, resting it back against the arm of the couch. He didn't have to lay down, but he saw people on television do it, so he thought that he should. Besides, it looked more comfortable than that old, dark brown, semi-disturbing looking chair. It looked like something out of a 70's horror movie, he thought to himself. Dr. Kasey: "Alright, first question...how old are you?" Eddie: "Twenty-four." Dr. Kasey: "Twenty-four? Hmm, you looked a little bit older than that..." Eddie: "Huh?" Dr. Kasey: "Nevermind...next question. What is your chosen profession?" Eddie: "I'm a professional wrestler, for this company called Neon Wrestling, based a few hours away in Albany." Dr. Kasey: "Oh, professional wrestler...that must be exciting. Tell me a little bit more, how do you like your job?" Eddie: "Well I love my job, and if you knew anything about wrestling, you'd know that I am pretty damn good at it too." Dr. Kasey: "I don't follow wrestling." Eddie: "Well you're just going to have to take my word for it. In fact, I have a pretty important match coming up soon, where I'm representing my own federation and we're competing against like ten others." Dr. Kasey: "Hmm...that sounds pretty important. Are you nervous, anxious, excited...?" Eddie: "Actually, a little bit of all three, among other things. I'm not really worried about myself, but I am worried about my other teammates. I'll try to explain since you don't follow the company. I'm on a team with three other guys...Phil Botwin, Tony Stecchino and Kuff McSlade. And these guys are supposed to be the "upper echelon" of the company along with me. In fact, I have more wins than anyone else on the team, yet they get more press and attention. One of these guys hasn't even won a goddamn match yet, and he's supposed to be my equal? I'm six and one, and the only time I lost was because the referee wasn't doing his job!" Dr. Kasey: "Sounds to me like you're a little bit upset about the whole situation. Correct me if I'm wrong, but you're worried that your team will lose because the guys aren't that skilled?" Eddie: "Yes, you are exactly right! I mean, one of them is a Nazi that relies on underhanded tactics, another guy used to be the champion but he lost his belt, along with his skills and the other guy....hasn't even won a damn match! I'm going to have to carry this team, I just know it. Good thing it's an elimination style...I think." Dr. Kasey: "What's elimination style mean?" Eddie: "It means that every person on my team has to be pinned or made to submit. So it's four people per team, and the last time with at least one person left wins. Easy enough concept, and we got lucky that our opponents aren't really all that good either." Dr. Kasey: "And who are your opponents?" Eddie: "Four guys from this place called EPW and four more from UPWA. They're feds with lesser quality wrestlers, and I've seen the competition, I'm not all that worried." Dr. Kasey: "Aren't you afraid that your cockiness might be your downfall? Always remember to expect the unexpected. What if something unforeseen happened, like an injury?" Eddie: "Man don't say that! You can't jinx these things!" Dr. Kasey: "Sorry, sorry...so I take it that you're superstitious?" Eddie: "No, not really, just don't like to look at things with a pessimistic view point." Dr. Kasey: "But aren't you doing that by doubting how your teammates are going to perform?" Eddie, for the first time, looked up and over at the doctor. The doctor was right about that, why hadn't Eddie himself seen the truth? He thought about it for a few more moments, but honestly, Eddie didn't like giving anybody that much credit. Who was this doctor person to try and outsmart Eddie? Eddie: "Man, f*ck you buddy." Eddie suddenly just got up from the couch, knocking one of the couch's pillows onto the floor. He stood in front of the doctor for a moment, then just made an odd motion with his hands for no absolute reason. With that, Eddie turns around and heads for the door, while the doctor just kind of watches with some wonder at these actions. Sanders opened the mahogany door and walked out of the room, shutting the door without looking behind him. After a few more moments, the door opens from the outside and Sanders just walks back in and heads over to the couch like nothing happened. He leans down, picking up the pillow and placing it at the foot, then just layed down once more. Eddie: "I'm sorry I probably shouldn't have done that. I guess I don't like when people point out things that I should have realized days ago. I am being pessimistic about my teammates, but I am never pessimistic about my matches. You have to understand that I was raised to be a winner...in the ring, in the lottery, just life in general." Dr. Kasey: "You won the lottery?" Eddie: "Yeah, a few years back...nice amount too, a little bit over a million dollars." Dr. Kasey: "Wow...and how do you think that has changed you?" Eddie: "Well I live in a bigger house now, have nicer things, but I don't think my personality has ever really changed...." For a moment, Eddie thinks back to just a few months ago, when he was acting black just for the amusement of most of his black friends. It wasn't really who he was, but he was surprised just how many chicks out there were into white guys who acted black. Crap, if he'd known that back in high school, he would have been wearing those jeans half sticking off his ass and those hundred dollar sweatshirts. And those bandannas...those ridiculous ones that people who are gang posers wear. But other than that, he was mostly right about his personality not changing. Eddie: "...yeah, I'm going to have to say that only material possessions. I still have most of the same friends, and the ones I don't just pissed me off. Had nothing to do with the money I won." Dr. Kasey: "Well that does say something good about your character. So back to wrestling for a minute, because that's where we seemed to lose you for a few seconds last time. How did you get interested and how did you break into the business?" Eddie: "Well my father showed me some old tapes, and I've always enjoyed watching it as a kid. Couldn't figure out anything else to do with my life that I liked. College was just a big waste of time for me. The only thing I learned there was that Liberal Arts is a pointless degree." Dr. Kasey: "Yeah, I'll have to agree with you there. So Mr. Sanders, may I call you Eddie?" Eddie: "Sure." Dr. Kasey: "OK Eddie, did you have to go through a lot of training?" Eddie: "Oh about eight months maybe before I got my first match. This older guy named Sully trained me, good person, tough as nails though. At least he wasn't like those other trainers I've heard about...you wouldn't believe some of the horror stories I've heard about bastard trainers." Dr. Kasey: "Like what?" Eddie: "Like this one guy actually broke one of his student's arms for messing up a move. That's just brutal...I mean, maybe bruising them up a little bit is one thing, bruises heal. So do broken arms eventually, but you know what I'm getting at." Dr. Kasey: "That's absolutely horrible....and here I thought wrestling was fake." Eddie looked up from the couch again, raising an eyebrow at the guy. Alright, maybe making Eddie feel stupid was one thing, but this guy had just insulted everything that Eddie had worked for in his life. That wasn't acceptable in his mind. Eddie: "Man, f*ck you buddy." Eddie got up from the couch again, this time taking the pillow and tossing it at the man's framed diploma. It fell from the wall, the glass breaking as it hits the floor. The doctor suddenly got to his feet, raising his voice for the first time since Eddie had met him. Dr. Kasey: "What the hell's your problem!?" Eddie: "I don't have a problem, your frame isn't broken. It's all fake, remember?" Eddie flipped the guy off, opening the door and heading out of the room. Sanders walked on down the hall, quickly walking out of the office, via the open door. Screw therapy, he thought to himself as he headed to the elevator. He decided that he was just thinking about it too much, and that everything would be fine. Yeah, that sounded good to him. Screw this guy and his diploma, Sanders was the one who entertained thousands, maybe even millions, all over the world in his young lifetime so far. This guy couldn't hold a candle to him, so he can shove his diploma up his ass. Eddie Sanders was finally ready for his match and he just hoped that his teammates would come through. He couldn't do anything about it, so why bother to worry? It was time to focus, and it was time to win. And that's just what Eddie Sanders was going to do.
