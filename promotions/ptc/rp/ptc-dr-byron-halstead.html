Final PTC Extreme Title Match RP Thread - post'em here

12 February 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Dr. Byron Halstead The following events take place in December, 2006. ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ The patient's name is Easton Hall, a prisoner incarcerated in the Baltimore City Correctional Center for the time being. I've been asked to examine him on a bi-weekly basis in regards to his involvement with the Rita Sammael murder case. Specifically, it's been requested of me to see if he might have any motive or psychological imbalance that would make it possible that he committed the crime. Seeing him at first, his presence slightly unnerved me; he is a man of slightly less-than-average height, yet his hair and beard are as long and bushy as a lumberjack's. He looks crazed, which I don't believe will help him in any court of law, but I'm not a legal advisor and it isn't my place to say anything about such matters. After exchanging greetings and informing Mr. Hall of our purpose – as a point in his favor, he seemed cooperative – I asked him to give me his history from the beginning so we might establish where any problems might have started. He hesitated at first, probably resenting the fact that I asserted he had problems, but eventually complied. Born in Toronto, Ontario, Canada to Christopher and Tiffany Hall, has an older brother in investor Isaac. Grew up a relatively normal life. Upon graduating high school, he became a firefighter – which he noted was his childhood dream – and began training in professional wrestling as a hobby. Eventually, he gave up firefighting to wrestle full-time. He recalled excursions to Japan and Mexico before joining High Impact Wrestling's "farm federation" – it's a term I'm not familiar with, admittedly, but he tells me it's sort of like a tryout organization where the best of this group get moved up to the "big time" – Championship Wrestling Federation. Here, he recounted a successful tag teaming with Bret Masters, capturing the CWF's version of the tag team championship, and formed a group with Masters and another man, Seth Hudson, which were given the overdramatic name of the Knights of Victory. Easton left the CWF to go back to Japan, and when he returned signed with Only the Best Wrestling. In Only the Best, he was modestly successful, joining up with his old friends Masters and Hudson as well as a woman named Katrina and another wrestler in the Canadian Kid to form the Allied Forces, as well as capturing OTB's version of the United States championship. Eventually, he left the Allied Forces, for reasons concerning the relative success of Bret Masters and Seth Hudson in relation to his stagnation; he asserted that they were doing their best to keep him locked down while they gained in prominence. I asked him if he possibly was simply displacing his anger at not being able to make it onto his ex-teammates and Easton calmly responded that since then, he's clearly been the most successful of the group. From here, he recalled the rest of his tenure in OTB, which wasn't occupied by any events of major importance except for a continued feud with Bret Masters. This was all we were able to accomplish until he was called back to his cell. All-in-all, I found Easton to be rather typical as a person. He's experienced the gamut of emotions, and although he appears to view many things through a lens of bitterness, it isn't a real psychological problem. I expect he's hiding many small, relatively important failures that nevertheless weigh on his mind. I find him an interesting subject, but not unduly unstable. -- Dr. Byron Halstead ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ In our second meeting, I had Easton pick up from where he was before, and he began by telling me of his marriage to his girlfriend and manager Jeanne Harley. I asked him why he hadn't mentioned her in the first session and he didn't respond adequately to the question, but in the interest of moving on, I just asked that he skip that part, at least for now. He described the wedding as one of the happiest moments he'd ever experienced, which is a standard way to describe one and really gives me no insight on how he views his relationship with his wife. He moved on then and talked about his return to High Impact Wrestling, highlighting his victory over Bret Masters at the first show, which set the pace for Easton Hall's star rising in HIW while Bret Masters remained stagnant. He described this with some pride. He then moved on to talking about his competing in the first Inferno match – he explained this as a match involving fire along the outside of the ring – and his winning of the HIW's version of the Television title. He spent a long time relaying to me the story of HIW's Ultimate Destruction event, which he had led up to by winning a spot in the second Bloodbath match, being placed on Arlen Emerson's Team Untouchable. The match would decide the winner of the as-of-then vacant HIW World Heavyweight championship. He quickly brushed over a loss of his Television title earlier in the night, which is obviously not a topic one wants to talk about, before getting to the Bloodbath match itself. He was animated in talking about it, using hand gestures and the like; his eyes lit up eagerly as he recalled everything. "Sturm had me in the corner, choking the fuck out of me, right? The ref comes over, he's countin' him, and Sturm lets go, then BAM! BAM! BAM! Forearms to the face. I jump up, try to hit him with a spinning heel kick, and he just grabs me and throws me back against the buckles. This is a monstrous guy, man, real powerhouse. So he whips me into the other side and I hit the ropes, but Stanton knees me in the back! Sturm struts over and grabs me and just BAM! HYOOGE lariat. Felt like my neck had been fuckin' cut in two. Sturm picks me up and gets me in a torture rack, right? Just tearing me in half, this guy, pulling me all apart. Then he goes for this move, but I get out and WHAM, spinning neckbreaker! The crowd went insane, buddy! Lost their fuckin' minds." He relayed to me the fact that he outlasted every one of the other competitors – eight of them – until he stood with "the man to beat," Axis, and came out on top with a daring and suicidal move that he called the Face of Oblivion; with a smirk, he told me that the subtitle should be "Laws of Physics Be Damned." I asked him how it felt to have finally won the top prize in the company and he admitted to me that it was a happier day than when he had gotten married, but not without some shame. The sense of pride he had felt then was enormous. He then recapped the next month, which was unfortunately highlighted by the loss of the belt he had worked so hard for in a match against Axis. He told me that at that point, he felt especially worthless and broken. Most champions in HIW didn't last more than a month, he recalled, but even so, it was a personal failing that he should lose so quickly. He recalled feeling distant from people in the days following it, even from his wife. Still, he expressed pride in the match itself, saying that he thought he had done something of particular note and established himself as the premier technician of HIW in many minds, even through losing; Axis was always more of a flyer than he was a technical wrestler. At this point, Easton was called away. Easton is a curiously arrogant man, yet in some ways is still reserved. He does his best to gloss over his failings while highlighting his strengths, which is typical of any person. Still, he seems rational; his relation to this case is curious. If he did commit the crime, at this juncture I'd have to assume that he didn't make the decision in haste or as the result of insanity. -- Dr. Byron Halstead ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ I began this session by asking Mr. Hall about a man mentioned in some statements, Makhpyia Sapa. Hall said that he knew him in a dark manner that hinted at something deeper. I said that Hall had asserted this Makhpyia Sapa was the killer of Rita Sammael and he confirmed that he had said that. In the previous statements, he described Makhpyia Sapa as an elderly but tall and straight-backed Native American with ruddy skin and an "obscenely-wrinkled" face – I believe this choice of words reflects his current feeling about the man – who dressed only in black robes, at least in the time that Easton says he was associated with him. I should take this time now to mention that we have never had a record of a Makhpyia Sapa or anyone fitting this description. Easton recalled hearing about the man from someone, but he cannot remember who it was exactly. At the time, he said he was suffering from a curious depression; his career had been on a downswing after his title loss and he feared he was losing control of himself. He traveled to Baltimore in search of Makhpyia Sapa, but upon exiting a bus he had taken, despair took hold and he entered a catatonic state, eventually either falling asleep or unconscious. He was retrieved by Baltimore PD in an effort to keep that part of the city free of the homeless and was released to Makhpyia Sapa, who Easton said had been there waiting for him. While we have records of Easton being taken in, no record was made of the mysterious man. I once again reminded Easton of the disparity, but he shrugged it off, saying that it wasn't important; the man was a mystic and Easton had not much idea of how he moved or operated. Easton recalled feeling an immense amount of faith that Makhpyia Sapa could help him regain the control he had lost. At that point, he wasn't interested in the mysticism that Makhpyia Sapa was involved in. He only wanted a route to getting back on top and in charge of his life and career once again. This reaction is similar to what is noticed in many celebrities, but sensing that Easton would likely take offense to such a comparison, I didn't make mention of it. He told me that Makhpyia Sapa told him to leave everything that connected him to his previous life, including his wife, and he bitterly recounted his last encounter with his wife. Since then, he has only had sporadic contact with her. He paused here, obviously heavy of heart. When he began again, he recalled joining Makhpyia Sapa in his abode and being instructed on a bit of the purposes of some items and symbols. Before he could tell me more, he had to return to his cell. Easton Hall is an interesting subject, but for the first time I am afraid that he might not be as rational as is necessary to stay functional. Seeing this figure Makhpyia Sapa is an odd and persistent vision; I don't think he can be real, especially as he doesn't seem to exist in any record at all, though I'm not ready to contradict the department's assertion on him quite yet. An insanity defense would help Easton Hall avoid jail time, but I am aware that the department wants to use him to draw out this real killer. He still appears rational, however, so the fact that this Makhpyia Sapa is real is still a possibility. I will need more time to make a verdict. -- Dr. Byron Halstead ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ The slim-shouldered and professionally-dressed Byron Halstead closed the door of the office behind him, manila folder in hand. He lifted his eyebrow, glancing to his right and seeing the imposing, bald form of Detective Stan Hepton. "So?" Hepton asked. "So what?" "So what've you found out about him?" "He might be a tad bit unstable," Halstead said. He frowned a bit. "But we'll have to continue the sessions for me to make a decision." Hepton's eyes flickered to the folder. "Let me see your notes," Hepton said, reaching towards them. Halstead twisted, putting his body between the detective and the folder. "No," Halstead snapped firmly. His eyes blazed with indignation. Detectives were so headstrong, so muscle-headed. They couldn't appreciate the intricacies of his profession. "They stay with me until I make a decision about him." Hepton scowled, but he brought his hand back slowly. "You hurry up and make a decision, then, doc," Hepton said. "I need the bait out, understand?" "Yes, Detective." Stan Hepton eyed Halstead a moment longer before turning and stalking away. Halstead watched his back, fingers tightening on his folder. He had always thought of Hepton as a sadistic brute. Why he was on the detective force, Halstead couldn't figure out. ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ I began this session by asking Easton to give me an example of the rituals that he performed with Makhpyia Sapa. Easton consented, relaying to me the first ritual that he and Makhpyia Sapa enacted. Easton said that the ritual was intended to find the direction Easton's spiritual compass in hopes that it could help discern what exactly was wrong with his sense of control. It began with Easton being instructed to draw a large pentagram on the floor in white chalk in a unicursal fashion, which, for this shape, is rather easy. Once done, he was instructed to draw a circumscribed circle about the previous figure. At this point, Makhpyia Sapa was apparently blessing candles and incense. When Easton was finished with this circle, he was told to draw another larger one, with about a foot of width between the two. Makhpyia Sapa then took the chalk from him and inscribed several symbols along the outside, which Easton was told spelled out "sophia" or "wisdom" in an ancient language. After this was done, Makhpyia Sapa set five candles out, one at each point of the pentagram. Taking a censer in hand, the mystic walked the space between the two rings, stopping at each letter to whirl the censer around his head three times exactly. He then took his place at one point of the pentagram, facing in towards it. Easton was instructed to sit in the center pentagon, facing Makhpyia Sapa. Easton sat as he was told, cross-legged and eyes closed. At this point, Easton closed his eyes as he was telling me this, as if summoning up the memory from somewhere dark in his mind. His fingers seemed to tremble and I can only assume that this must be a difficult memory to recall for him; he has expressed his incredible distaste for the mystic on more than one occasion. Mr. Hall recalled that Makhpyia Sapa chanted something in a language Easton couldn't even begin to place, but was told later that it was Enochian. He said as the incense began to consume him, he experienced a vision. I goaded him to tell me about it, but before he could, Easton was called back to his cell. I have to admit being frustrated by the rules of this facility; every time I'm on the verge of a breakthrough, Easton has to leave. It is a strange ritual, though, obviously occult or the construction of something occult-sounding. I don't think it would be easy to validate the authenticity of such a ritual, so for the time being, I simply have to trust Easton's word that this actually happened. Still, I have my reservations about his mental state, and he perhaps could be seeing himself in a dual capacity, serving as Makhpyia Sapa and himself when necessary. If this is true, he is suffering from multiple personality disorder and will obviously have to be committed. -- Dr. Byron Halstead ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ I began quickly by asking Easton to recount the vision he had mentioned in our last session. After he oriented himself to the discussion once more, he began in on the telling of it. He cautioned me by saying that it might appear strange but that I would have to trust that this is what he saw; I had no ideas about doing anything different. Easton said that he saw himself as a green-and-gold Oriental-style dragon in the sky, similar to the one tattooed on both of his arms, except that he had two heads that were slightly disconnected from one another; that is to say, there were two "necks" and not just one "neck" that sprouted into two jostling heads. He told me that he was flying through the blue sky, his form undulating to power him forward. He recalled that the path he followed was a hexagram. I asked him how that could be, because a hexagram cannot be drawn smoothly in one motion. He explained to me the concept of the unicursal hexagram, which is a symbol that is connected to many occultist principles. I then inquired how he knew that the path he followed was a hexagram and he told me that he didn't know how he knew, only that he knew it had to be true. I didn't pursue it further; it was his vision and I couldn't very well inject normal logic into it. A lance began to strike up from somewhere at one of his heads; Easton couldn't tell exactly where it came from, only that it came from below. At first, his heads bobbed and weaved away to dodge the spear, but eventually it came close enough that it apparently became necessary to strike at it. His heads took turns snapping and butting the spear off-target, making sure that it didn't strike home. This went on for a while, with the dragon growing more frantic and desperate as time went on. Easton himself relayed the vision calmly, but his hands fidgeted a bit, restless. Eventually, the lance pierced under the jaw of one of the heads and through the cranium. That head fell away cleanly, leaving the other head solitary, the dragon flying unhindered and almost unconcerned about the loss of a "brother." This is where the vision ended. I asked if Easton knew what it meant, and Easton conceded that he didn't. He had asked Makhpyia Sapa, but the man had apparently only taken it as a matter of course and hadn't given him an answer back. Before I could ask further, Easton was called back to his cell, ending our session. Easton's vision is curious, but standard as far as visions go; the fact that a dragon was a part of it rather than a conventional animal is likely a product of his environment at that point. Still, experiencing a vision is rare and I wouldn't use this as any sort of validation that Easton isn't mentally ill. He still appears rational, but he may be suffering in any case. -- Dr. Byron Halstead ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ Byron Halstead climbed the flight of stairs to his apartment and sighed, shaking his head. Easton was an interesting patient to say the least; he wasn't sure how this would take the man. At this point, however, it looked like he might have to be committed. Halstead resolved to at least visit if Easton was put in a mental hospital. The story was fascinating. Halstead blinked as he stood before his door. On it was etched an ovoid shape, like an eye, but with nothing inside. He leaned in, squinting a bit. The cuts were so clean they had to be done with a laser; a knife or chisel would have cut irregularly. Halstead straightened, scratching the back of his head a bit. He didn't remember any of the other doors bearing such a mark. He shook his head and decided he might as well not think about it too much right now. He unlocked the door and stepped inside, reaching to his right and flicking on the lights. Home sweet home. He closed the door behind him and turned around to lock the door when his eyebrows shot up. On this side was the same ovoid shape, but here was etched an iris and a pupil with incredible detail, the eye open in a wide, accusing stare. Byron stepped backwards, his mouth working wordlessly. Had someone broken into his house? Nothing looked amiss, but… he glanced around, trying to see if someone was there. "Come out!" he called, his thin voice barely carrying across the room. No one answered him. Shaken, he rubbed over his forehead. He'd have to call the police. Uneasily, he tottered across the room towards the phone, glancing back often to see that eye staring back at him coldly. He couldn't escape its gaze, it seemed. He felt goosebumps rising all over his skin. ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ I decided that we should turn the table back to Easton Hall's career at this point. Easton consented and began to tell me about a man named Kael Stanton, a man that Easton described as his greatest rival in wrestling up to that point. The man was a stellar martial artist, a debonair Englishman who led the group known as the Institute; it wasn't just a wrestling stable, though it was primarily such. Unlike Makhpyia Sapa, Kael Stanton undoubtedly existed; there are records of his work in the HIW and other places. Kael Stanton was a megalomaniacal man who blamed Easton Hall for his own downswing in luck, which had coincided with Easton personally eliminating him from the Bloodbath match. He began to attack Easton's family and friends, which provided an interesting play-in to how Easton had been acting in his personal life. I asked him how he felt about these occurrences and Easton responded that they began before he was taken in by the mystic, but he still had some care for them and felt an obligation to do his best to defend them. However, Easton also noted that he wasn't experiencing much success in the ring. I questioned him about how this could be in light of his tutelage at the point. Easton said that he had been focusing most of his energies on his spiritual advancement under Makhpyia Sapa and not as much on his ringwork. By this point, he had accepted the fact that the control he wanted would possibly not be a result of or be reflected in wrestling. Easton bitterly told me that he must have been an idiot at this point, which was the end of our session. I feel as though Easton is growing a bit more erratic and distant as these sessions wear on. The contradiction between his success in the ring and his continued affiliation with Makhpyia Sapa is curious at the very least, perhaps a symptom of growing insanity. Still, I don't know what to say about this exactly; I'll have to continue to speak with him. -- Dr. Byron Halstead ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ Byron opened his mail box and pulled the envelopes out, closing it as he shuffled through them. Bills, bills, bills… whoa. Byron lifted up a black envelope with his name written on it in chalk with a halting script. He glanced about surreptitiously before opening the envelope and pulling out its contents. Three cards were inside, painted. Tarot cards; these obviously weren't playing cards. One was numbered XVI on the top and depicted people leaping from a flaming tower. The second was numbered XVIII and showed an odd scene with what looked like a face in a yellow sun looking down on some weird animals. The third was numbered X and featured a man lying prone being stabbed by a number of swords in the back. Byron looked at them for a bit longer before sliding them back into the envelope. What exactly was going on here? ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ I had Easton recount for me the various teachings he was learning and Easton began telling me about meditative techniques and rituals that he was being taught. Several of them included incense and candles and they focused on alternatively centering oneself and expanding one's mind to include everything possible. I pointed out the contradiction to Easton, but he simply dismissed it; even now, he apparently holds stock in these teachings, even if he now hates his mentor. He remarked that a girl by the name of Fortuna began to appear in his dreams. I remembered that Fortuna was the name of the Roman goddess of luck, amongst other things. I asked Mr. Hall is he didn't mean a woman, but he asserted that she was a little girl, perhaps of elementary school age. He said that Fortuna would often be cryptic yet childish, telling him of things he might experience soon in an ambiguous way. "The first time I saw her, I knew her name instantly. She… asked me if I was keeping the Sabbath holy, and I said I wasn't. I'm an atheist, see? Anyhow, she said I should, 'cause I was gonna die. Kind of an exaggeration, looking back on it, but anyway… that Sunday I was slated to face Carl Sturm. I grabbed her by the shoulders and shook her real hard, asked her what she knew about Sturm. She made like she was gonna cry and then… then in a real quiet voice, she said 'He's a killer.' Sure enough… guess who ended up seeing ceiling that weekend?" Before I could inquire further, Easton was called away. Easton shows no knowledge about Roman mythology, yet he summons the name of a goddess in a capacity somewhat akin to her actual one; she was not exactly a prophetess, but the future and luck are inextricably tied. The prophetic nature of the dream is curious, but could very well be accidental. Its validation has seemingly bolster Easton's faith in such things, however. Such things are subject to intense scrutiny. -- Dr. Byron Halstead ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ Byron Halstead hurried down the hall, folder in hand. "Hepton!" he called. "Hepton!" The bald detective stopped and glanced over his shoulder. He and another policeman had been leading Easton Hall towards his cell again and Stan Hepton didn't look glad to be disturbed. "What?" Hepton called back. "May I see Mr. Hall for just a minute?" Halstead called; he still stood several feet away from Hepton, making no effort to come closer. "I have a question for him." The guard glanced at Hepton and Hepton shrugged. "Go over there, Hall." The bearded man nodded and then turned, walking down the length of the hall towards the psychologist. "But make it quick." Dr. Halstead glanced over at Hepton as he reached into the folder, sliding out the cards. He displayed them towards Easton. "Have you any idea what these are?" Easton looked over them carefully, his green eyes scanning them. "XVI the Tower," he began, "XVIII the Moon. The Ten of Swords." Halstead blinked. "The Moon?" he asked. "But that looks like…" Easton shrugged helplessly. "But what do they mean?" That shrug again. "I have no idea," Easton said. "I can recognize the cards, but as for their meanings…" "You done?" Hepton shouted. Easton lifted an eyebrow and Halstead nodded. Easton turned on his heel to go rejoin the detective and the officer. Halstead looked over the cards again. Tower, Moon, Ten of Swords. Curious. He would have to look into this. ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ I inquired about the tattoos hidden under the gauze that perpetually wrapped Easton's forearms and asked how he got them and why he hides them. To the second question, he replied that he doesn't like to be asked about them overmuch. As to how he got them, Easton said that Makhpyia Sapa had decided that he was what was known as the World-Dragon, a peculiar figure in the mystic's brand of occultism. Easton admitted to not knowing too much about the role he was supposed to play, but it was one of utmost importance to him. Easton then unwrapped his arms, showing off the brilliant tattoos there; on each arm, a green-and-gold dragon wound up with its tail at his elbow and its mouth opening on the back of his wrist. Inside the jaws was an eight-spoked black wheel, the dharmacakra, a Hindu symbol that Easton said represented the path to enlightenment. He described the process of getting the tattoo as one that was as ritual-based as anything else he had experienced under Makhpyia Sapa's tutelage. The tattoos were hand-tapped with sharpened bamboo that was tipped with dye and the precision was incredible. I'm convinced that this had to be the work of a modern tattooist and that somehow he's deluded himself, because if he was Makhpyia Sapa, it would be hard to believe that he has the dexterity in both hands to complete such works of art. He recalled that the room was almost choked with incense while this was being done and candles burned at the points of a hexagram with Easton at the center. Makhpyia Sapa had actually gone without his hood for this, something that the man rarely did; Easton recalled that the man had very straight and orderly jet black hair when he – and, I'll admit, I – was expecting a snowy white or at least gray. Once again, before much more could be said, our time was up. I think Easton might be losing himself in this occultist fantasy. What bothers me is that up to this point, most of the imagery used has been Western in nature, derived from the teachings of Aleister Crowley and his ilk: the use of the pentagram and hexagram, the chanting, the word sophia, etc. This blending of Eastern occultism is rather hodgepodge in fashion. Still, I need a little more time to make my decision. -- Dr. Byron Halstead ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ Byron Halstead opened the door to his apartment and stepped inside, shrugging off his coat. Immediately, his eyes widened a bit as they always seemed to lately. He stepped inside slowly, looking over the scene. Every piece of furniture in his living room – the couches, the television, the stands, the coffee table, all of it – had been arranged in a rough circle in the middle of the room. "What is going on here…?" Byron breathed, approaching the circle hesitantly. It was a regular homemaker's Stonehenge, and Byron had similarly little idea of what it meant. He leaned over one of his couches and spied what was in the center of the circle. Singed into his carpet with a detail that marked that this could be no accident was a vescica piscis. At one end was a stylized eye inside a triangle. In the center was a dove displayed as if diving wings-out, its head pointing away from the triangle and pointing towards what was at the other end, a chalice that held a bough of leaves and an equal-armed cross. Byron stepped backwards, rubbing his forehead with the heel of his palm. This was too much. Was someone trying to scare him? What did they want? Hurriedly, the psychologist rushed to his room and fetched his camera. Returning to the living room, he took several pictures of the scene. Byron Halstead sighed a bit and glanced around. Well, it was obvious he couldn't stay here. He kept his camera and his folder close as he edged out of his apartment, locking the door behind him. The best course of action would be to take this to the police and then ask Easton Hall about it in the morning. ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ "Hm," Easton said, looking over the pictures. Byron Halstead watched him intently, lips pursed. The shaggy-haired prisoner looked up at Byron. "It's the lamen. A symbol of Ordo Templi Orientalis." "A symbol of the what?" Dr. Halstead asked. "It's a magickal organization," Easton explained. "Dangerous if you're on their bad side… did you do something?" "Nothing." Byron Halstead was exasperated. What could this… Ordo Templi Orientalis want? He nodded as he took back the pictures. "You should take care of yourself," Easton said gravely. "Please, Mr. Hall," Byron said. "Let's… let's start the session, shall we?" Easton nodded, studying Dr. Halstead. The psychologist swallowed hard. He didn't like the way Easton seemed to know something. Byron was supposed to be the one with the answers here. Easton Hall… well, Easton Hall was insane. ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ I asked Easton to recount what exactly transpired between himself and Makhpyia Sapa to engender such a hatred in him for the mystic when they had apparently shared such a close student-teacher relationship. Easton said that he was betrayed in a final manner, but I goaded him to continue; after all, there could be no advancement in my reading of the patient if he simply shut up. Easton sighed and began, saying that it was a pay-per-view event where he was to fight for High Impact Wrestling's International title; after asking, Easton explained to me that this was not the same thing as the World Heavyweight title and counted for a little less. His opponents were Jack Logan and Matt Essex. He told me that the match had been fairly even between the three of them until his mentor made an appearance. Easton said that he noticed a shift in the man, but he didn't register it then. He tried to shoo Makhpyia Sapa away, asserting that he could handle himself, but the shadowy figure – Easton told me that he viewed Makhpyia Sapa as a great shadow in this instance rather than a benevolent teacher – continued to make his way forward. Before Easton could think further, his world was shut out in complete blackness. When lights began to twinkle in his vision once again, he knew that his chance at the International championship had been lost and that his teacher had sabotaged him. He was red at that point; I suggested that he meant red with fury as a metaphor, but he countered by saying that he was composed of the color itself or, rather, the essence of the color. He flew out of the ring then – this is how he described it – and sailed through a gray purgatory in search of the black that was Makhpyia Sapa. He cornered the shadow in an open hallway and they engaged in some form of combat, or at least that is what I gather. How he described it was that he and Makhpyia Sapa melded together, but rather than trying to blend, they were struggling against each other, to consume the other. Each pushing against each other created new hues. Easton recalled to me blazing blues, subdued oranges, and intense yellows. Easton said that he felt as if his soul was being driven apart in two, one half towards a heaven realm and the other towards a hell. I asked him about his previous assertion that he was an atheist, but he ignored me. He said that in the end, his mentor was inert, but not utterly beaten. Eventually, the shadow fled. I asked Easton why he still bore a grudge against Makhpyia Sapa. He responded by telling me that the man had ruined what shards of life he had had and that while Makhpyia Sapa still existed, Easton would not feel as if he had defeated him, would not feel whole again. Also, he confessed that he believed Makhpyia Sapa was at the same time trying to kill him, so he could not be off his guard. At this point, our time was up and Easton was taken back to his cell. Easton Hall is a curious person, but I don't have any faith that he is completely sane any longer. No useful information will come from Mr. Hall concerning this case, in a courtroom or otherwise. This will be the end of it, the last session, and a fitting end. These notes may serve as some guide to further caretakers in what he has been dealing with. I am hereby recommending that Easton Hall be committed to a mental institution. -- Dr. Byron Halstead ~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~ Byron Halstead ascended the stairs towards his apartment, shaking his head. This was ridiculous. He couldn't stay away from his house on the advice of a madman. There were things that needed to be done. Specifically, his notes needed to be revised to be submitted to the department. He expected that Hepton would want his head for this, but he couldn't help it. The man had already proved himself a menace by killing Rita Sammael. Byron unlocked his door and took a deep breath before pushing it open. Instantly, smoke flooded out and he shielded his eyes, coughing uncontrollably. The smoke seemed to clear quickly and he took a step inside, lowering his arm to look about. The circle of furniture was now consumed by a brilliant fire, all of his possessions dumped on top. The tops of the fire rose up to the ceiling, but rather than catching that on fire, seemed to slide across it as if the ceiling were glass; indeed, the flames acted like this when they came in contact with the walls and ground as well. The smoke similarly refused to leave this room except for the initial gust outwards. Bewildered, Dr. Halstead took another step inside, looking around. Everything was burning and grimy, but the house wasn't falling apart. If anyone knew how Moses must have felt, it was Byron Halstead in the year 2006; an odd person and time to be chosen. Byron shook his head, coughing a bit more. He couldn't go any further. He would have to go downstairs and call the fire department or something. Byron turned towards the door and his jaw fell slack, clutching his manila folder to his chest like a safety blanket. A tall man clad completely in black robes stood in front of him, his frame filling the doorway. Under the hood, Byron could see a bit of a craggy, weathered face. The man towered over him in a way Easton Hall couldn't have. He fit the description perfectly. "You are a fool," the man said gravely, his voice like rumbling asphalt. Byron turned away with a breathy shriek. An intense foreboding filled every nerve of Byron's body. Easton Hall was right. He was the only one who knew about this man, well, except for Dr. Halstead. Now, Easton was the only one who could stop him. All Dr. Halstead could do now was make sure that he didn't go to the mental hospital, make sure he was free enough to find this man, to prevent him from doing whatever it was he planned. He lunged and flung the folder edge-first towards the flames. A roar filled Dr. Byron Halstead's ears. His eyes drew towards each other and he watched the bullet sliding forward through the air, burying itself in the ground. Byron followed it down, landing with a thud on the ground, a tunnel through his brain. The folder crumpled in the pillar of flames, curling up until it and its contents were nothing.
