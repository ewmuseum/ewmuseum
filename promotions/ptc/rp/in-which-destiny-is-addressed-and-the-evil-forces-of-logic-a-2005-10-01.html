In Which Destiny Is Addressed And The Evil Forces Of Logic And Common Sense Are Slain

1 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-10-01 March 6, 2005. XUW: My First Big Wrestling Show Pay-Per-View. The lights. The crowd. The utter and absolute silence as one man’s words sends shock and disbelief ripping through the very fibers of XUW: “I love wrestling, and I love all of you Fark-O-Maniacs… which makes… Which makes… Which makes what I have to say so difficult. Basically… tonight… tonight is my last match. After tonight, whether I win or lose, I'm… I'm retiring… from XUW. You see, another thing happened when I came back to wrestling last June. I know a few of you may have heard rumors, and I'd just like to say right now that those rumors are true: I met a woman while I was preparing to make my return. We started seeing each other, and after a couple of months, well… she became pregnant. I'm going to be a dad. I'm going to be a dad. And I realized recently that as much as I love wrestling, I love my unborn child even more. It's one thing for me to come out here and risk my body and my life when I have nothing to lose. But it's another thing for me to risk those same things, when I have everything to lose. I have a woman who loves me, and a child on the way. I need to be with them. I need to stop taking risks. I need to leave XUW before I get seriously hurt again. The bottom line is, I need to stop being a full-time wrestler, because even more than being a wrestler, I want to be a full-time dad. So tonight… tonight is my own ‘Final Huzzah.’ Tonight is the last match in the history of Fark-O-Mania. I'm either going home with the XUW World Title, or I'm going home after passing the torch to another guy who's worthy of carrying it. I don't have anything else to say other than… Thank you. Thank you to all of my fans, all of my fellow Fark-O-Maniacs, who saw me through every battle I've been in. Thank you for all of your love and support, in all of my triumphs and tragedies. Only a truly, truly blessed man gets to do what he loves, and love what he does. Thanks, XUW, for more fond memories than I could ever hope to have in a lifetime. God bless you all. Now Latch, get yer farkin' arse down here, and let's give all of our fans the farkin' World Title match of a lifetime!!! HUZZAH!!!” The applause and adulation for the hero they thought they were seeing for the last time, fading away into history… The irony of life is inherent in the paths we choose. The twists and turns we take lead to many destinations, yet destiny is inevitable and unavoidable in spite of our best efforts. It is not something that we forge on our own, but something that is dictated for us. We all have hopes, dreams, aspirations… plans, intentions, goals that we make for ourselves… all built on top of one other, stacked precariously like a house of cards. And all we can do is watch helplessly, knowing all of it shall be effortlessly scattered on the wind, torn asunder by forces beyond our control, leaving us only with the truth—that each of us is at the mercy of who we are fated to be. Each of us are men and women of destiny. Scott Jeffries had plans of his own. He planned to settle down and raise his family. He planned to get away from the everyday risks and dangers of the business that had nearly destroyed him, though he loved it dearly. The man known throughout all of wrestledom simply as “Saij,” who had quickly become a living legend in spite of his youth, chose a path that led him away from XUW. It was a path he thought would lead him away forever, to the safety and comfort of home, to a place where he would never again have to worry about nursing injuries or fighting off enemies—and indeed, he’d had many in his time. He found a woman he loved, and his child was soon to be born. He had no more scores to settle with anyone. The days of Fark-O-Mania, he decided, had come to an end. But Scott Jeffries… but Saij, too, was a man of destiny. And his destiny was not to live a quiet, peaceful life. His destination was retirement, but his destiny was XUW. His destination was fatherhood, but his destiny was Fark-O-Mania. His destiny… was to be a hero. Saij was the only one who knew the details of what happened between his departure and he subsequent return to XUW months later. Leanne, the woman who he had given up his career for, the woman who was carrying his child, was no longer with him. He never spoke about why, and if someone asked him about it, he never answered. Understandably, being left a single father with a baby to raise alone was not a particularly pleasing topic to discuss. Indeed, Saij refused to discuss it at all, even with his closest friends, the Boomer Sooners. His stubborn silence on the topic made it difficult to gauge how little or greatly it weighed on his mind, but those who knew him could tell something about him was slightly off. Of course, being “slightly off” was Saij’s modus operandi—after all, this was a man who was a self-admitted dunderhead. Saij was as much of a clown as he was a hero. Logic almost didn’t seem to apply to him or to the rest of his fellow Sooners, all of whom had a line of thinking that was anything but linear. He prided himself on fighting with what he called “Super Farkness,” a style of wrestling that wasn’t really wrestling at all, but rather pulling childish pranks on his opponents in the ring. Wedgies and wet willies were the norm when it came to this. His sidekick was a mute midget given the moniker of “The Cockjockey,” for the fact that he rode around on a giant inflatable penis like a child would ride on a toy horse—that is, until the giant inflatable penis turned against its former master, leading to their inevitable showdown in the ring at XUW’s biggest show of the year, Reborn (and in case you’re wondering, the penis won). He befriended a life-size cardboard cutout version of former stablemate Chase Wilson, which somehow managed to eliminate a man with a genius-level IQ in a battle royal. Saij’s world outside the ring—and sometimes even inside—was a veritable circus of absurdity and nonsense, where it seemed almost anything was possible. It was almost as if his life was being scripted by a madman—or, at the very least, a college student with a wild imagination and entirely too much free time on his hands (and occasional blatant disregard for the proverbial fourth wall). Nobody would ever have guessed that this man would have to face such harsh trials and tribulations. And no one certainly would ever have guessed that this man would grow to become one of wrestling’s greatest heroes. Nobody would ever have guessed that a fun-loving simpleton with the common sense of a rock would defy all odds become XUW’s longest-reigning World Champion, let alone capture the prestigious PTC Global Title by toppling a behemoth that the entire wrestling world thought to be unbeatable. Nobody would ever have guessed that he’d return just a few short months after retirement, his life having been completely turned upside down, and lead XUW into battle against PRIME. Nobody would ever have guessed. But that’s destiny for you. Fark-O-Mania had returned. Saij’s legacy picked up where it had left off. Now, the worst thing anyone could do to their self in Saij’s presence, second only to royally pissing him off, was to let his train of thought (“thought,” if you can call it that) run on… Because once Saij’s train of thought built up enough steam, there was no telling where it would go. --- “Whaddaya mean Jack’s keepin’ CK locked up in the Domicile!? Doesn’t that farkin’ bastard know we have a match coming up here!?” Saij looked particularly frazzled as he stood in front of a changing table that he’d set up in one corner of the Boomer Sooners’ locker room. It was empty for the time being, save for Saij and his infant child. The Master of the Huzzah looked understandably tired. He was sporting a healthy five o’ clock shadow, and his sleeves were rolled up as he was simultaneously talking on his cellular phone while trying to change his baby’s diaper (with great discomfort, as he was practically tilting his head ninety degrees to hold his phone between his ear and shoulder). “Hey, I tried to reason with Sirius,” came the voice of Scotty Spalzcutoff on the other end of the phone, “but he’s downright scary! I mean, he swung his freaking cane at me! Swung it like a madman pumped with steroids while having a serious case of road rage! Not only that, he was angry too! Angry and pissed and ticked off and what have you! He also seemed kinda mad.” “Dude, my five-month-old kid could whip his farkin’ arse,” Saij replied, as he struggled to unfasten the diaper without dropping his phone. “I’m surprised the slightest gust of wind doesn’t knock him over, or crush him, or something. What kinda wuss are you, anyway?” “The kind that runs away from scary old men, that’s what!” Scotty came back. “But anyway, this is a serious problem! We need to get CK outta there, or else we’ll be a man short at Super Series and PRIME will surely kill us all in nasty ways!” Saij finally managed to get the diaper off, and was immediately overtaken by the rancid stench of infantile excrement. “OH, FARKIN’ CRAP! DEAR MOTHER OF GOD, THIS IS THE WORST THING EVER!” he wailed, holding the offending object out at arm’s length and looking desperately around for the trash bin. His attention was no longer on his phone conversation with his XUW teammate. “No kidding, we’re in a dire situation here,” Scotty continued. “We finally have the opportunity to claim the title we should have won a year ago at Survival, and we’re short a man! What the hell are we gonna do!?” “Must… throw… away!” Saij managed, trying not to breathe in what he figured to be fatally toxic fumes. “Throw the match away, are you mad?” asked Scotty unbelievingly. “I can’t believe YOU just said that! YOU! You’re Saij, damnit! You never back down from anything! You’ve built your career on defying the odds! Hell, I should know, I’ve seen it first-hand! And now, because we might be going into a four-on-three situation at Super Series, you’re going to just give up? You, sir, have just thrown away any respect I had for you!” Finally, Saij found the trash bin he was looking for, and disposed of the dirty diaper. “Phew,” he said with a sigh of relief, “glad I managed to throw that away. What a stinking pile of crap that was!” “Wh-what!?” Scotty cried indignantly. “How dare you, sir! I am insulted! Never in all my life have I been so offended! People have always spoken highly of you, but you’re nothing more than an unruly jackanapes!” The baby was becoming restless with the slow pace of the diaper changing, and was beginning to whimper. “Oh, please don’t cry,” Saij said as he pulled a wiping cloth out of a dispenser. “It’ll be over soon!” “Don’t talk to me as if I’m some sort of child!” said Scotty, anger rising in his voice. “Just because you’re a former World Champion and in the XUW Hall of Fame doesn’t give you the right to talk down to me! And don’t act so casual about things, either! ‘It’ll be over soon,’ honestly! We have a lot of work to do and increasingly less time to do it in! The way you’re treating me, I’m not so sure that I want to continue teaming with you anyway!” Saij finished wiping and powdering his baby, and was now searching the room for diapers. “Damnit,” he said, “I need those Huggies.” “WELL I’M NOT GIVING YOU ANY HUGS, YOU INSENSITIVE CLOD!” Scotty yelled. “I’ve had enough of your ornery cantankerousness! Forget it, I’ll figure out a solution to our problems myself, and you’ll be groveling at my feet and thanking me for doing so! Just you wait and see! You’ll rue the day you crossed Scotty Spalzcutoff, mark my words! I BID YOU GOOD DAY, SIR!” And he hung up. “Alrighty, all finished!” Saij smiled as he picked up his baby, who was now smiling and giggling. Saij walked over to a playpen in another corner of the room and set the baby down. “You go ahead and play with your alphabet blocks while Daddy talks to his teammate,” he said. “Alright Scotty, now what were we talking about?” Silence. “Scotty? Are you there?” Beep. Beep. Beep. “Well fark, that was incredibly rude of him to hang up on me like that! Honestly, the nerve of some people!” Saij snapped his cell phone shut and stuffed it back into his pocket… which also contained a pacifier and half a pack of tissues. “Well, if Scotty’s gonna be that way with me, then I guess I’d better think of a solution to this problem on my own. Now let’s see…” He stooped down next to the playpen, resting his folded arms along the top, and watched his child play with the alphabet blocks, almost transfixed by them. “Let’s see,” he thought aloud. “So we have no contact whatsoever with CK, and there’s no telling what Jack’s doin’ with him. Fark, I guess it’s possible that we really might be a man short at Super Series. That would like, suck and stuff. What can we do about it?” Saij furrowed his brow and closed his eyes. The final boarding call had passed, and nobody could stop him from thinking what he was going to think next. He opened his eyes and made a painstaking effort to formulate his thoughts aloud. “Wait a second… if… if we’re a man short… then surely we’d be allowed to have a substitute! Yeah, we’d have to be allowed to do that! I mean, they let PRIME get a substitute for Youngblood after he got a shot in the main event, so… it’s only fair! That’s it! I need to find someone to replace CK! But like, who?” Saij rubbed his chin thoughtfully, as he gathered steam and was getting dangerously close to full speed. “Well, the obvious question is who would be a suitable replacement for CK? I honestly don’t know too much about the guy. I mean, whenever I’d see him cut a promo or something, he never made any sense. He just babbled like an incoherent simpleton.” The baby giggled and made a few babbling noises. “Come to think of it, I bet he has the IQ of a rock. Or paper. Or scissors. I mean, he has elementary knowledge, I guess, but otherwise not too bright.” Somehow, the baby had managed to take the alphabet blocks and spell out the letters “H-U-Z-Z-A-H” in succession. Saij didn’t notice this. Instead, he reached into his pocket and procured the pacifier, gently putting it into the baby’s mouth. “Plus he just plain farkin’ sucks,” he finished as the baby happily suckled. “Fark, this shouldn’t be hard to figure out, but I can’t think of anyone! Who could…” He paused. It hit him. “Hey, that’s it!” he exclaimed. He joyfully picked the baby up out of the playpen, and lifted it up into the air with a beam of pride on his face. “You can replace CK in our match against PRIME!” he declared with glee, yet quite seriously. “Sure, you need some training and stuff, but wrestling knowledge has to be like genetic or something, right? So it shouldn’t be any problem! This is gonna be the greatest farkin’ experience of my life! And yours, too! C’mon, let’s go to the gym and I’ll teach you a shooting star press!” Saij made his way out the door with the baby, his voice fading down the hallway as he continued to talk excitedly. “This is going to be great! You and me in the ring together! A family team! That’s right, father and…” There was no stopping the train now. The evil forces of logic and common sense had once again been slain by XUW’s legendary hero. That’s destiny for you.
