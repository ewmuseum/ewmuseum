Connecting

15 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-03-15 Two mud stained sneakers move side by side in a hurried cadence, up two steps and into a bustling bar. The man who wears them is attired in a backwards Yankees cap, Knicks Jersey, baggy jeans and more gold jewellery than P. Diddy. He orders two Miller Lites, slaps some coins on the bar and turns on his heels, through the crowd. “Yo homes!” He spies an old friend, handing him a frosty beverage. His friend is similarly attired, except he is wearing a Knicks cap and a Yankees T-shirt. “You da man!” They exchange high fives, locking grip in some form of secret handshake. “No, you da man!” They pull each other in closer, then away from each other. “No man, you da man!” They slurp on their beers as they finally release their grip. “So what you been up to homes?” “Nuthin’, you?” “Nuthin’ much.” Both men wipe the suds from their lips, enjoying the watered down taste. They savour their silence, the noise of the crowd, staring into nothingness, the bustling crowd. After a few more slurps, the Yankee Cap man slams his beer down on the counter. “Dawg, I gotta split, been great catching up.” “Damn straight!” And with that, Yankee Cap man disappears through the crowd, reappearing near the bar where he passes a pensive looking Mal Somers. Mal samples a handful of beer nuts as he sits on a bar stool, observing the goings on. “Funny isn’t it, how disconnected our lives can be, even in close proximity to one another. Here I sit, in a bar with maybe two hundred people in various stages of drunkenness, and I wouldn’t know one of them from a bar of soap. I would find it hard to speak to anyone here unless they approached me first. It’s a bit of a sad indictment on our society how we can come across so many people in a day and yet, rarely connect with any of them.” Somers nods towards another section of the bar, this time three males around their mid twenties, drinking shots of vodka. Several empty glasses and beer bottles adorn the table as a shrine to their inebriation, and as they slam their drinks down they yell and holler, having a great time. “These guys meet here every Tuesday night to catch up on things. Sure, they connect with each other on a weekly basis, but it makes me think, when was the last time they all spoke to each other sober?” One of the men tries to make a point, throwing his arms out and smashing a few of the bottles to the ground as the others laugh at the top of their lungs. “Sure, they are having a great time, but I wonder if they will remember half the stuff they said in the morning? I wonder if they can really stand each other? Perhaps they are just going through the motions?” Mal leans back to let a couple past, and takes a sip of his beer. He surveys the bar once more. A well dressed man in his early forties, obviously a few sheets to the wind ambles into the bar, stumbling into a waitress, spilling the drinks on her tray all down her shirt. He shrugs his shoulders and keeps walking as she looks at him in disgust. Elsewhere, a young woman sits at a table, alone, shouting into her mobile phone. “Listen here you bastard, didn’t you get my email? I dumped your sorry a$$ on the weekend! …. It’s over you no good pig! …..Look I know that some of the context is lost when you are reading words on the screen, and if I had the time I would have dumped you in person, but rest assured you moron, it’s OVER!!” She hangs up the phone, throwing it down, before picking it back up and furiously typing in a text message. Mal simply shakes his head. “I’m convinced, the world is going mad. Technology, globalisation, all the things that are supposed to bring us closer are actually driving us apart. No-one takes responsibility for anything, no one wants to connect with anyone anymore.” He grins, rubbing his mottled beard. “Well, almost no-one. Soon, very soon, I will have the chance to connect with three other human beings, and I don't think they are going to enjoy it very much." He takes another sip, savoring the flavor for a moment before swallowing the amber ale. "In fact I know that Nick Worrell and Emilio Rage are not going to enjoy it much at all. Despite beating them for the chance to take on the Latch Key Kid around a month ago, here they are again, involved in this match. I know the top two guys were going through, but seriously, all three of us?" He takes a deep breath, before pulling his long hair back from his eyes as he exhales. "But what's done is done. Worrell and Rage, I have entered the fray against them before and emerged triumphant. I know...deep inside that I can do it again. The big question here is the X-Factor, the Latch Key Kid. He is the unknown for me, as I have never faced him before." Somers finishes the bottle, wiping the residual foam from his lips. He'd like another, but does not give in, so close to the big match. "He is the Champion, so I know to be wary of him...very wary...but I think I am ready to make the connection with him in his Global Title defence." He holds his hands to his face in a steeple formation, takes another deep breath, and closes his eyes, consciously exhaling. "Latch Key Kid...Nick Worrell...Emilio Rage....at Seven of the Best, unlike many others, I will be ready to take full responsibility for my actions, be they good or bad. With the support of all the MALcoholics in attendance, and those at home, I know I can do it. My whole career, in fact my whole life has just been a precursor to this one event. To go that one step further than my nemesis Dave Harley and take the Global Title to where it belongs...the World Wrestling Alliance! At Seven of the Best...I will be ready to make the connection!"
