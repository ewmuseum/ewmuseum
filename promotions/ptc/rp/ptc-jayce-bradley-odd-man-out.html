Odd Man Out? - Jayce Bradley Uni title match RP

24 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Prologue Clinton Sage. Dusk. Jason Snow. Jay Phoenix. Digital Mortality. Five names with so much clout, that you have to wonder why names like Jayce Bradley, Bobino, and Cassandra Paragon were even considered to be mentioned among them. Cassandra Paragon may have been the Infinite Gauntlet champion, but she had virtually came out of nowhere to win the coveted spot that many had clamored for. Bobino was a member of FUSE up until the point when they decided they no longer were in need of his services, then he magically appeared in XUW and was just handed their open spot. How generous of them, right? Jayce Bradley can say the same thing, because in his mind... Well, in his mind he doesn't think he did anything to deserve the honor of representing Global Championship Wrestling. He's not even too sure that he really wants the honor, considering what happened the last time he was chosen to represent the company in a huge match. Then again, he may not have a choice in the matter. * * * * Las Vegas, Nevada April 5th 2007 War. As far as Jayce Bradley was concerned, that was exactly what he had just been through. A man twice his size, twice his weight, and twice as strong had pushed him to limits he had never been to before. He put it all on the line as he tried to reassert himself as a mainstay on the Global Championship Wrestling roster, but even with a win he ended up losing. Now, as he stumbled out of the alley behind the High Spirit Casino his High Stakes opponent, Shaman, owned and operated, he could feel his knees buckle just before he crashed shoulder first into a row of trash cans. The stench singed the tiny hair follicles on the inside of his nose, as bile rose up and lodged itself in the back of his throat. “There he is,” he heard someone call out, though even that sounded a bit muffled as his throat loosened and released the first wave of vomit through gritted teeth. His eyes struggled to focus, but there was no mistaking what the thick crimson syrup that left a tangy taste in his mouth was: blood. Mixed in with the grilled chicken breast, rice, and broccoli he had for dinner, the mere sight of it sent another wave coursing from his gut to his throat. This time, aside from tasting it, he could feel it burning the buds on the tip of his tongue, before the combined scent of the trash and vomit nearly caused him to collapse face first into the vile puddle. “Pick him up, Rich,” a familiar voice said as he felt hands tuck beneath his arms and lift him away from the putrid mess his face was inches away from slapping against. He was beaten and bloodied in his match with Shaman, running more on adrenaline and gut instinct than any type of game plan he and Mario had concocted before the show began. Yet, he still managed to find a way to win the match. It didn't matter if it was because of Shaman getting disqualified, because that was a clear sign that Shaman already knew he was beaten. Why else would Shaman have his security team beat him to within an inch of his life? “We need to get him to a hospital.” “I'll go get the car.” He heard the conversation between his trainer and confidant Mario Trellis, and Mario's assistant Richard Hartman, but his mind was focused on not passing out from the pain in his chest, that pounded against his rib cage like a native beating on a drum. “Don't worry, kid,” He was reassured by Rich. “We're gonna take care of ya.” Then the pain became too great to bear, and a quick flash of white was the last thing he saw before everything went black. * * * * San Antonio, Texas April 7th 2007 “How ya doing, kid?” Jayce opened his eyes slowly, his eyes taking more time than expected to adjust to the sudden flood of light. “Hey, Rich.” “Brought you a couple of magazines, figured it would keep you from rotting your brain watching the crap they show on the television here.” Richard Hartman had worked with Mario Trellis at the Anywhere But Your Back Gym for the last seven or eight years, and never had he seen someone with as much natural ability as Jayce had. Ever since the first day he had seen him walk through the door, he knew that there was something about this kid... Something that made you want to take a seat, lean back, and enjoy the show. Of course, Mario did not tell him much about Bradley's history, just what the man needed to know in order to do his job and that was it. He didn't need to know anything about Jayce's past in order to instruct him on the basics of submission wrestling, which just happened to be Rich's field of expertise. Richard Hartman had been a professional wrestler for most of his life, mainly regional independent promotions around the Southern states of the U.S. “They at least have sports,” Jayce said in regards to Richard's earlier comment about the television, though Rich had been lost in his thoughts and completely forgot what he had said. “What's that?” “Hey,” Jayce said jokingly. “Who got hit in the head, you or me?” Richard laughed it off, not particularly fond of talking about concussions; he had plenty during his tenure in the sport. “Did Mario tell you the good news,” he asked, as he tried his best to change the subject. “That I'm getting out in a few days, yeah.” “No,” Rich said with a shake of his head. “About the Primetime Central Title Unification match.” “He didn't mention anything about it,” Jayce replied with a shrug of his shoulders. “Well, Caldera chose you to be GCW's representative.” “He did what!?” Hartman was a bit surprised in the anger of Jayce's tone, it was as if he had just heard that he wasn't going to be the representative. “Caldera picked you to fight for GCW in the match.” “Why would he do that,” Jayce asked, a bit perturbed about the whole situation. “All of the talking he does about GCW being the best, and how it would dominate over all the other Federations under the umbrella, and he throws in the towel by sending me... Is he a damn idiot?” “Are you?” Richard turned around to find Mario Trellis standing at the door, his arms folded across his chest as he leaned against the open door frame. “What the hell is that supposed to mean,” Jayce asked with even more fervor than before. “For the last few weeks you've been complaining about not being noticed, and you haven't helped yourself much by doubting yourself at every turn.” Mario made his way toward Jayce's bed, the scowl on his face enough of a sign of how angry he was. “Now you're given this great opportunity to stand out and prove yourself on a National stage, and you want to bitch and moan about it. Suck it up, kid, because this is just the tip of the iceberg.” “I heard who was in this thing already,” Jayce replied with as much fire behind his words as Mario had. “You know more than I do that I cannot compete with those guys. Hell, I can hardly win a damn match in GCW, and Caldera chose me to represent him on an even grander stage. Even you should be able to see that this is all a part of something bigger, so don't give me any shit.” “Excuse me,” a feminine voice called out from the open doorway. “You guys are going to have to keep it down before we call security.” “Sorry,” Richard apologized for the trio. “We'll be sure to keep it down.” “Thank you.” Through a haze of rage, Jayce and Mario never took their eyes off each other as the nurse spoke, and hardly twitched as Rich apologized and she left. “You're always looking for an excuse Jayce, always looking for some big conspiracy theory to explain why you do or don't get something to go your way.” He shook his head before he turned away. “Maybe you need to go ask Caldera why he chose you, because I only see one reason.” “And what's that?” “Because you deserve it,” he replied, before he nodded to get Rich's attention and motioned for him to leave the room. “I know you don't see it Jayce, but you're a lot better than you give yourself credit for.” He never turned back to face Jayce, but he paused for a moment when he got to the door. “Imagine what you could do if you believed in yourself too.” Jayce watched as the man that has been training him for the last few months walked out the door, then watched as it shut behind him with a slight click. “Damn,” he mumbled under his breath, as if being careful not to let anyone hear. Mario was right about him. He never truly believed he had what it took to make it in this business. When he first started out, this had all just been a clever way to travel without anyone catching on to what it was they were truly doing. Following the Prophecy of Fate's code of honor, fulfilling the prophecy's wishes to cleanse the World of the 'unpure', was what he had been trained to do. It was all he knew how to do. But it was time to move on, to let go of the past that died with Braun more than a year ago in that motel parking lot, and focus on the future – his own future for a change.. * * * * California April 8th 2007 The sun struggled to rise over the valley but still managed to cast an ominous glow over the trellises of grapes grown to make wine that blanketed the ground below it. With a yellow notepad and a folder full of information that the man known as 'The General' had requested tucked neatly under his arm, Brian Himner stared out the multi-paned windows of the marble-walled pool house that had been outfitted as 'The General's' private office and conference room when they arrived at the compound. A short, husky man with a discreetly concealed firearm holstered along the side of his chest, raised his arm to his chest and bowed his head slightly as a woman walked past him and entered the room. “Brian,” she said with a smile, as she rushed over to give him a quick hug. “It's been so long.” Himner gave a curt smile in return, as he pulled the woman away and started at her feet to get a good look at her. “Sam, you've grown up since the last time I saw you.” Samantha Arden had long blond hair that framed her shoulders and floated innocently above her ample chest, which was emphasized by the low cut top that struggled to keep the twins from coming out to play. She had been recruited into the Prophecy of Fate at the age of twelve, and has grown up following the Prophecy's faith for the last seven years. “It has been seven years,” she teased him. “Did you expect me to still be wearing braces and pigtails?” The two shared a laugh as Himner shook his head. “It's just different, you know?” “Well don't get too worked up about it,” she replied as she took a quick glance at the watch wrapped taut around her wrist. “We still need to get situated before he get-” A side door opened with a hiss, and the couple that stood there waiting quickly turned their attention to the gentleman that walked out from that same door. He was a tall, thin man with a narrow face that looked as if someone had grabbed onto his chin and hung there for a few years. “General,” Sam called out as she made her way toward 'The General'. “Nice to finally see you away from the buffet table.” “Excuse me,” 'The General' said as he stifled a laugh. “The last time we met sir,” Sam began. “I accidentally bumped into you just before you scooped a spoonful of pinto beans onto your plate, and they ended up spi-” “Yes, you're the kid Hastings raised,” He acknowledged as he took her hand in his and shook it. “Yes sir,” she replied with a smile wider than the broadside of a barn. “And I must say he did a very good job, sir. Your words are followed religiously in our household, and I consider it an honor to have you summon me.” The General gave her a curt nod before he turned his attention to Himner. “Brian,” he said like the two have been the best of friends since birth. “I'm glad you could make the trip. It's been so long since we've spoke, and I wish it could've been under better circumstances.” “As do I,” he replied with the same genuine excitement. “You were very vague on what was going on, so I'm guessing that is why you called me here.” “Yes,” The General replied, his face a bit distraught. “We've decided to begin Operation: Slither sooner than expected. It turns out we may have a mole in our midst, and we have no clue as to who it is or what information they gave to the DA's office.” Sam and Brian have all of their attention focused on The General and his words, never losing track of all that he said and what the implications were. “Brian,” he began “I asked you to come here because you are the one I want heading this operation to begin Operation: Slither. I trust you and your judgment, and know that you will not startle Bradley when you make contact.” “And me,” Sam chimed in out of turn, something that brought her the devilishly glare of doom. “You have been brought here because of your level of expertise in handling people that choose to rise up and stop the love that we share among all of our people.” “I don't follow,” she replied with an innocence about to be shattered. “There's a District Attorney in Texas that has dug up more information than should have been allowed, and now we need to have her taken care of before she learns too much.” Before Sam can ask any more information, she feels a quick sting as the security guy had walked in with a syringe and plunged it into her neck. He cradled her in his arms as her legs gave way and she nearly collapsed onto the floor. “When she wakes up,” The General said with an uneasy smirk on his face. “She'll be a totally different person, and she won't even remember half of the stuff she did, but the time has arrived and we must move on if we are to succeed with our goal to fulfill the prophecy.” “So then,” Brian handed the folder full of information to The General. “I guess I should be on my way.” “No,” The General shook his head as he pointed toward the fresh fruits and juices that had been served at the table as The General spoke. “We know exactly where he's headed next, so there is no need to rush.” He flipped open the folder and started to look over some of the information. The name Keisha Anderson was labeled across the top of the folder the General thumbed through. “Have breakfast with me until Samantha is able to rejoin us.” “It would be an honor,” Brian replied without hesitation, as The General placed the folder down on the table and began to serve himself a plate of food. “Perhaps we could discuss the intricacies of the plan.” “Of course,” The General replied, as he grabbed a link of sausage and took a bite out of a slice of bacon. “There's nothing like working out the intricacies of plotting someone's murder over breakfast that I cannot get enough of.” Brian laughs out loud at the horrible bout of sarcasm. All in a day's work. * * * * GCW Headquarters April 12th 2007 “I put him in the conference room,” the young blond hired from the temp agency to cover for Steven Caldera's normal secretary said as she saw him walk through the door. “And why would you go and do that,” Caldera asked with slight surprise, since none of the chairs in the waiting area to her left were occupied. “There was just something about the way he looked at me,” she replied, as she pressed a hand to her chest. Caldera had no reason to reprimand her, it wasn't like she had done something wrong. It was just something he would've preferred her not to do without consulting him first, but what was done was done. Jayce Bradley was seated just beyond the double doors to his right, and he still wasn't sure exactly what Bradley wanted to hear. “He said he had an appointment,” she said while she thumbed through a few portfolios of wrestlers interested in joining the ranks of GCW. “Who's Sero Blake?” Caldera pushed the door open and walked in to find Jayce seated at the table with his fingers interlocked in front of his chest. The room was narrow and dark, the blinds had been pulled down to keep out the sun this early in the morning. “Sorry to keep you waiting,” mock apologies aside, Caldera strolled over and shook Jayce's hand. “What can I do for you today?” “I want to know why you signed me up for this PTC thing.” Jayce remained seated with his arms in front of him. “It's not like I've been the best member of the roster, you know.” “No one said you had to be,” Caldera replied. “I chose you because I'm confident you have the ability to bring the new title home to GCW. Back where it belongs,” he said as he stared toward Bradley with other problems fresh on his mind, most importantly being the challenge he accepted from Miss Troy. “Then why didn't you ask someone big like Mayhem, or maybe Shaman,” he just couldn't take that as an answer. He knew that there had to be a reason as to why he was chosen over some of the favorites. “I asked you because I felt I could trust you to be able to perform in a different type of situation. You did it at GTT5, and I felt you could do it again.” Jayce shook his head, there was no way he wouldn't want to be in this match, not when so much was up for grabs. He could become the first ever Unified Title champion, and proudly wave the GCW flag for all to see as he awaits his crowning moment. “Of course,” Caldera was far from over though, and Jayce was about to find out what he felt Caldera's angle was. “I expect you to be able to make it back on time to be a part of the Warpath Tournament.” “So that's your angle,” Jayce questioned the GCW President. “How do you mean,” he replied with a genuine innocence, as he pulled a folder out of a stack on his desk and opened it up. “You're trying to buy me off.” Caldera nearly burst out laughing by the ridiculous insinuation. “I'm merely rewarding you for your ability to persevere against the odds set before you. You just beat Shaman a week ago, and have been making me take notice of you for the last month. I know you don't trust me, I wouldn't trust myself either.” Caldera stood from his chair, as he closed the folder he had just looked through and shook his head. “But you need to believe me when I tell you that I feel you are the best choice for me to have in that match.” “Fine, if that's the way you want to play it.” Jayce stood up as well and looked deep into Caldera's eyes. “Just be ready for me to take the number one contender spot coming up for Bryan Mayhem's title, because I can tell you right now that I'm going to go out there and try to prove you right... Then I'm going to come back and shove it down your throat before I go out there and beat whoever it is that happens to have the title by then.” With that, Jayce turned on his heels and walked out of the room, so he failed to hear Caldera's last words. “I hope so, kid... I really hope so.”
