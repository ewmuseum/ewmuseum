Falling Off the Wagon: From the End to the Beginning (IG 34 - Tchu)

28 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The wet blacktop slid beneath his arm, grating his flesh like a block of cheese; and, for the first time in his life, he found himself wishing the city took better care of its alleys. “Shit” He cursed, but the profanity was muffled by the thud of his younger brother’s body landing beside him. At least, he assumed it was Scott. It was hard to say for sure. “Scotty, that you?” “Ughgh… I think.” Matt reached out his hand and felt for his brother. There were no lights in the alley, and the rain that had moved through brought with it a cover of clouds that blocked the moon and left the night as black as the tiny chunks of asphalt embedded in his skin. “You ok?” “They threw us out, man. Just thew us out.” “Yeah.” Matt pushed himself off the ground and clutched the back of his cranium, relieved to find his brain wasn’t spilling from a hole in his head. “I noticed.” “I’m sorry, bud.” “C’mon, get up.” Matt locked his hands under Scott’s armpit and pulled him to his feet. His little brother brushed the gravel from his jeans and the two trudged down the alley towards the street. “Seriously, Scotty… you ok?” “Shit… yeah, I’m ok.” As they left the alley and rounded the corner, Scott looked over to his brother. “You’re bleeding.” “Huh?” “Your lip… it’s busted open.” “Really?” Having not noticed, Matt reached up and felt with his fingertips. Pulling them back, he saw a few dark spots of blood that flashed a brilliant red as they passed under the nearest street lamp. “Well, the arm hurts worse.” “Jesus. I’m sorry, man. This is all my fault.” “Yeah. It is.” He responded as if a stranger had asked him if it were a Tuesday. Scott took notice of the tone and kept his mouth shut. The two continued down the sidewalk in silence, occasionally searching out leftover bits of alley, and ripping them from their flesh. Matt wondered how long it had been since his little brother had last gotten him into a world of trouble. Scott wondered how long it’d be before Matt blew up at him. Before either could come to a conclusion, their thoughts were interrupted. “Damn!” The homeless man next to the postal box sat up as the two brothers passed. “Looks like you found the party.” “Right where you said it’d be.” Matt just kept walking, not even turning his head. “You two know each other?” Scott wrenched his neck to look over his shoulder, as the homeless man slid back to rest against the mailbox. “Just helped me find you.” “So he’s technically to blame for that busted lip you…” “Shut up, Scotty. I’m not in the mood. I can’t believe you pulled this crap. And what the hell were you thinking… putting all that pressure on me?! Dragging me into this! When are you gonna just keep your fuckin’ mouth shut?” Matt stopped at his car and opened the door, but paused before getting in, resting his forearm on the roof to look his brother right in the eyes. “And you better drop the sarcasm fast. Your fiancée won’t be in the mood for your stupid ass jokes either.” “She’s pissed, huh?” “Worse.” Matt slid into the car and slammed the door shut, Scott doing the same. “She’s worried.” “I figured.” Scott let his head drop to the floor mats for a moment, then quickly lifted his gaze and fired a smirk towards his brother. “Guess it’s a good thing I’ve got you to bail me out.” “Don’t get comfortable, cuz I’m through with this.” Matt started the engine and pulled out into the street, his tires slipping a bit on the rain-soaked roads. “The next time you screw things up, you’re on your own.” “Yeah, right.” Scott forced an abrupt laugh and leaned his head back against the seat. “The next time I fall off the wagon, you’ll stop on the side of the road to pick me up. That’s what brother’s do.” “No.” Matt flipped on the windshield wipers. “Next time I’ll just run your ass over.” * * * “I’m worried.” Laura let herself collapse into the leather couch, resting her elbows on her knee, and burying her face in her hands. She had a right to be worried. Sometimes, he wondered what Laura saw in Scotty that made her want to marry him. It sure as hell wasn’t stability. Scott had been as up and down as any human being over the last decade. You never knew exactly what you would get with him, but you could count on the highs being high and the lows being in the fucking ground. Laura knew all this. And that’s why she was worried. She cared. It was the core of her personality. That’s why Matt liked her. She saw more in Scotty. And she knew guys showed up late to things every day. She knew they lost track of time, got stuck in traffic, overslept … but those things just annoyed… they didn’t cause a man’s fiancée to sweat with fear. A past like Scotty’s caused that. “He’s an hour late.” It was 9:35PM, Scott was supposed to have been back at 8:30 so he and Laura could catch a nine o’clock movie. That plan was already a half an hour down the drain. Mary placed a hand on Matt’s shoulder and said something about things being wrong. He said he was probably just wrapped up in some baseball game at one of his friend’s place. No one believed him, and he didn’t expect them to. “Maybe you should go look for him.” He told her maybe Scott ought to just keep his cell phone on him so they could just call, but he grabbed his car keys and headed for the door anyway. * * * “What the hell are you doing, Scotty?!” “You’ve got a deal.” Darren smiled, satisfied with the agreement. “No way.” Matt took a step towards the three gentlemen, holding out his hands. “Hold on just a sec…” He didn’t finish the objection. A loud ‘Crack!’ rang out, bouncing off the walls of the room. His momentum was halted abruptly by a right fist to the mouth, and the force of the blow sent Matt crashing onto the poker table, scattering chips across the felt. As he tried to pull himself up, he felt a Loafer snap across his cheek, and he was right back down to the floor “Jesus!” Scott rushed over to help his brother off the ground. “I think we should call it night.” Darren took a step towards the brothers, each of the men with him following his lead. With a bit of help, Matt felt himself jerked to his feet by the shirt collar; Scott received the same treatment. “You shoulda stayed home and let Scotty deal with his own problems.” His words didn’t do much to push through the ringing in Matt’s ears “Darren, c’mon… I..” Scott attempted to plead his case as he and his older brother were forced down the hallway. “You better keep close your mouth before you cause more trouble, Scott.” As they approached the door at the end of the corridor, Darren freed up a hand to push it open. “Best of luck to you, Matt… you’re gonna need it. The way I see it, I’m 80% to win. I’ll take those odds every time.” Matt saw Darren’s grin just a moment before he was tossed out into the warm summer night with littler concern as to how comfortably he was going to land. The wet blacktop slid beneath his arm, grating his flesh like a block of cheese; and, for the first time in his life, he found himself wishing the city took better care of its alleys. “Shit” He cursed, but the profanity was muffled by the thud of his younger brother’s body landing beside him. At least, he assumed it was Scott. It was hard to say for sure. “Scotty, that you?” “Ughgh… I think.” * * * Scott’s issues had started fresh out of high school. His problem, as he self-diagnosed, was that he was too much of a fun guy. Matt thought it was a lack of self-control. It’d begun with gambling, then quickly moved to too much gambling. The partying soon followed, until it go the point where all Scott was doing was wasting away the night playing poker and living it up with any crowd he could slip into. Soon, he found himself without a steady job, and his habits did nothing to help him find new work. So, over the course of a few years, Scott spent his time holding down a job for anywhere from five or six hours to five or six weeks, partying on the weekends, and finding countless ways to bet, and lose, what little money he was bringing in with his sporadic paychecks. He had been a connoisseur of casinos, card tables, and all things gambling. It was his biggest crutch, and he’d battled with it more times than he’d seen the flop. Finally, it looked like he’d actually overcome it. Thanks to Laura, as he put it. But Matt knew it was just a matter of time before he slipped back and fell into old habits. Chances were, that fall had come tonight. * * * The sound of his sigh replaced the roar of the engine as he slid the key out of the ignition. Rubbing the back of his neck, Matt leaned across the car and grabbed the picture from his dashboard. He stepped out onto the street, looking to his right, then his left, before heading towards the lone living being he saw out on a rainy night. Matt wasn’t part of the poker scene, legal or otherwise. The extent of his interest in the hobby went no further than the occasional game of Texas Hold ‘Em with a few of his buddies. His lack of knowledge on the darker side of the addictive hobby left him with little direction to aid his search. He had already been to a couple of Scott’s friends to check with them, and he had called to see if the Riverboat was running. It wasn’t. That left only one place to check. “Excuse me, sir…” Matt approached a man sitting on the sidewalk, leaning against a postal box. His hair and beard were unkempt and the clothes he wore smelled of sweat, rain, and old age “…have you seen this guy around here this evening?” “Yup.” The homeless man leaned closer to the picture in Matt’s hand. “Just a couple-few-hours ago.” Matt wasn’t surprised to hear. His brother had once mentioned to him that High Street was a hot spot for some of the bigger cash games in town. Where exactly those games went down, he wasn’t sure. “Do you know where he might be?” “Probably. Just up the street here, turn and head down the alley. Go in the side door to the old hotel. Follow that little hallway. He should be in the last room on the left.” “You sure?” “They hold poker tournaments and cash games there a couple nights a week… in one of the conference halls the city rents out since they re-furnished the building.” “Gambling, in the old hotel?” Matt folded up the picture of his brother and stuck it in his back pocket. “Pretty much all they use it for now. Hasn’t been used as a hotel in over forty years. But, they converted the first three stories to conference rooms for corporations and what not… use ‘em for big business meetings during the day. And, a couple of different circles rent out rooms for their poker games. The heavy hitters can be found in the last one on the left at the end of that first hall. Just use the alley way entrance.” “Sounds like you know what you’re talking about.” Matt dug a twenty-dollar bill from his wallet and handed it to the guy. “Yeah.” The man took the money and nodded his head in thanks. “How do ya think I ended up here?” Matt had nothing to add. He just tried to make a sort of sympathetic smile that seemed fitting, though he didn’t know exactly what was appropriate. With his new information, he headed up the street, passing the castle-like old hotel. At its corner, Matt turned down the alley, found the side door and entered. The hall was well lit, nice carpeting, with a few simple paintings lining the walls. It wasn’t quite the dark and dingy atmosphere he was expecting, though given the fact that the building was used for business conferences by day, he shouldn’t have been surprised. At the end of the corridor, he could hear arguing coming from a room on his left. With a few long strides, he was down the hall and pushing open the door to find Scott pleading his case to three gentlemen. They were the only four in the room, though the various poker tables set up across the floor seemed to indicate that there had been several others at one point. “I can get the money… I just don’t have it on me.” “You shouldn’t bet what you can’t pay up immediately. C’mon, Scott, that’s Poker 101.” The man shook his head in disgust, while the two gentlemen behind him just stood with their hands in their pockets. “Darren, Just give me some time. I’ll get you what I owe ya.” “What’s going on?” Matt stepped out of the doorway and into the room, announcing his arrival with a booming voice. “Matt…” Scott started to say something, but was cut off. “Matt? you must be Scott’s brother… the big-time wrestler.” Darren smiled, apparently amused with Matt’s profession. “Yeah. Who are you?” “Name’s Darren… one of the regulars here in this room. And a helluva Seven Card Stud player, if I do say so myself.” “And them?” Matt nodded his head to the other two gentlemen. “Just a couple of buddies of mine. Decent poker players themselves.” He looked over his shoulder to the men. “What… you guys broke even tonight… maybe pocketed three or four hundred?” Darren turned back to Matt. “That’s more than your brother can claim.” “Scotty,” Matt shifted his gaze towards his sibling “how much?” “I owe him fifteen.” “You owe this guy fifteen hundred dollars?!” “Fifteen thousand.” Darren clarified, the smile from earlier no longer present. Given the kind of money he was owed, it was understandable why. “Tuesday night’s the big game. Hundred dollar minimum bets.” “Christ, Scotty! Fifteen thousand? What the hell were you thinking? You’re a twenty-dollar buy-in kinda guy. You don’t have fifteen thousand!” “I know.” Scott looked at the ground. “Look,” Matt refocused his attention to Darren, “why don’t we have Scotty pay you what he can now, then he and I will see about…” “I’m not interested in some sort of payment plan. We could take that twenty-pound gold title of yours, though, and hold onto it until your brother can come up with all the money. Kinda like a deposit, champ.” The words seemed almost amusing, though Matt didn’t detect any humor in Darren’s voice. “Wait.” Scott’s interjection grabbed everyone’s attention. “Double or Nothing.” “Scotty, wait a min…” “My brother has to defend his title at IG 34 against four guys. If he wins, clean slate… I owe ya nothing. If he loses, to any of ‘em, we’ll make it an even $30,000… paid in full.” “What the hell are you doing, Scotty?!” “You’ve got a deal.” Darren smiled, satisfied with the agreement. “No way.” Matt took a step towards the three gentlemen, holding out his hands. “Hold on just a sec…” He didn’t finish the objection.
