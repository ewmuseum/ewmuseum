"Err"

19 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-08-19 * * * * * I’ve never had a problem admitting that I’m an alcoholic. The absurd and dismissive looks from strangers at the bar during those blurry, vodka-filled nights, or Heather’s tear-filled eyes when she dumped me the first time so long ago because of my drinking have basically numbed me to the fact. I can admit it, without any sort of anguish or shame, because I’ve already had my fill of both feelings over the last few years, more so since my mother passed back in 2001. I, Archibald Allen MacGregor, have a drinking problem. It’s a problem that has been with me longer than Heather has, and has cost me more in my life than I’ve spent on the alcohol itself. You’ve been reading these memoirs for a long while now, and I only make mention of this again for one particular reason: Since Heather’s accident, I’ve barely had a sip of it. It sickens me to swallow a tumbler of Scotch or a shot of whiskey and feel the liquid burn my throat on its way down. Every time I feel the heat of the alcohol, I can’t help but think back to hearing Heather’s pained screams as she lay trapped in the burning van. Fire water, indeed. So, unlike most alcoholics out there who try to numb the harsh realities of life with glass after glass of liquor…I can’t seem to tolerate the stuff. In an attempt to separate myself from the urge of alcohol, I’ve been trying something new. I’ve opened a bottle of vodka and poured a full glass of it on the kitchen counter. The glass is glued to the counter and is impossible to lift, but the bottle itself isn’t secured. I figure…if I can pour a glass and let it evaporate, then refill the glass, once the full bottle is gone I should have no problem keeping my distance from it. So far, the warm summer days have swallowed a quarter of the bottle, rather than me. I have an infant son, barely half a year in age, who hasn’t had a mother to tend to him during the night for what now amounts to 80% of his life. A month after Aaron was born, the accident occurred. In that time, Heather has had numerous operations to graft skin back onto her upper torso. I’ve taken Aaron to see his mother at least three times a week, when the doctors permit, in hopes of keeping that mother-son bond present. Thankfully Heather’s family, especially her sister and mother, have more heart in them than I could have ever asked for. During the days when I’ve needed to leave town to wrestle and make the money to pay Heather’s health bills, they’ve been selfless and immeasurably helpful. I wish I could say the same for Heather’s father, Rear Admiral Daniel Davis. He visited for two days shortly after the accident, but his naval duties pulled him back out to sea rather quickly after his emergency leave. He put in for a leave soon after, and had been granted his request once the aircraft carrier returned to port and a replacement was found. For someone who commands and demands the respect of hundreds and thousands of naval officers, I find it highly ironic that he can’t earn the respect of his future son-in-law, nor show any sort of admiration for what he has done for his daughter. * * * * * "Open up, here it comes!" Archibald said, pretending to fly the bottle filled with baby formula as if it were a miniature airplane. Aaron MacGregor stared back, his blue eyes not necessarily focusing on the bottle, but perhaps more curious as to why his father was acting so ridiculous. At almost six months of age, Archibald was sure that Aaron could understand when he was being silly and when he wasn’t. He was being silly, but Aaron didn’t care. Aaron was hungry. On the hospital bed, Heather Davis slept. The staff was preparing her for another round of surgeries to graft more skin onto her flame-ravaged body. Even though he knew she wouldn’t wake from her sleep, Archibald had insisted on visiting and bringing along their child so the family could be together more often. Especially since he would be leaving to wrestle in a few days, he just wanted to keep everything as sane as possible before he had to go off and fight the insanity known as Primetime Central. He piloted the bottle into Aaron’s mouth, and the child sucked on the plastic nipple. Archibald smiled and ran his finger across Aaron’s brow and pushed his hair back away from the infant’s brow. The door opened, and a nurse stepped in. Archibald recognized her as Nurse Meadows, who always snuck him some of the Jell-o that they feed the patients. She smiled at first, most likely out of courtesy, and then spoke quietly, "Mr. MacGregor, may I speak to you out in the hallway?" "Sure, let me just grab the little tater-tot and we’ll be out in a second." Nurse Meadows nodded, and disappeared behind the door. It clicked shut shortly after. Archibald picked up the infants seat and made his way to the door. He adjusted the bottle so Aaron could continue to feed while he spoke with the nurse. Before leaving the room, he glanced back at Heather and stood quietly for a moment, then finally opened the door and stepped out into the hallway. Even though he would be gone for only a minute or two, he hated leaving her side. A million things could go wrong, more than they have already, and Archibald just wanted to be there for her. "What’s up, doc?" Archibald said as he chuckled to himself, trying to lighten his own mood just a little. He looked down at Aaron as if the child would understand such a juvenile joke, only to shrug his shoulders as Nurse Meadows smiled and shook her head side to side in mock disbelief. " That’s Registered Nurse, but I don’t suppose that has the same ring to it that Doctor does, right?" "Not really. But hey, the doctor treating Heather seems to be uptight and has probably never even seen a Bugs Bunny cartoon, so you were the next target. I figure it’ll get me a few more servings of Jello-O." "I feel honored." She said, smiling. "So what did you want to talk about?" "Well, Mr. MacGregor…it’s about Heather. You see, I wanted to tell you about what’s been going on while you aren’t here, while she’s alone." The nurse paused a moment, visibly upset to be delivering the news to Archibald, "She won’t stop crying." The words hit Archibald like a sledgehammer to the ribs. The air left his lungs in such a force, he had to put an arm up against the wall for support. He stared at the nurse for a moment, finally responding, "What? Why…have you talked to her about it? Now I’m not saying I want her to…but why won’t she cry in front of me? She’s been nothing but the personification of upbeat and optimistic!" Below them, still sucking on the bottle, Aaron nodded off into a sleep as innocent as they come. Archibald knelt down and gently pulled the bottle from his mouth and placed it between his legs. "Mr. MacGregor, she cries because of what she’s been through. She thinks everything is her fault, and she weeps nearly every night. Once you’re gone. Once her mother and sister leave. It’s just an endless sobbing that occasionally I’ll hear as I pass by the room. I’ve tried to console her whenever I can, but I think it’s a state of depression that’s just taking over. She doesn’t want to show weakness to you, and I’m sure she doesn’t want to cry in front of Aaron." "But none of this was her fault, none of it." Archibald replied. To the nurse, it was a typical response, but Archibald knew more than the nurse, and he knew exactly what was going on with Heather’s accident. There were stronger forces at play. "She tells me that she’s ashamed to be Aaron’s mother, even if the skin grafts will take. Afraid to marry you, because she doesn’t want her wedding photos to show her damaged face. She absolutely loathes the fact that we won’t let her continue to wear her engagement ring for fear of infection, Mr. MacGregor. That’s why she keeps it taped to the wall above her head." "What can I do?" "I’m not sure, but right now…she thinks that everything is her fault. We need to figure out how to make her realize that it wasn’t her own, but someone else’s." Archibald sighed and thanked the nurse, who quickly left to tend to her other duties. She had no idea that the weight of responsibility was crushing him, and now that he heard this news, it was becoming unbearable. It was his fault that Heather’s nights were filled with tears and guilty anguish. Because of what he meant to God. Because of what he meant to the Devil. It was time to summon one or the other, he figured. Let’s see who was listening. * * * * * God stared out at the small town of Oxford, Pennsylvania through glowing eyes of fractured glass. The reds, blues, yellows, and greens of the giant stained glass windows composed nearly half of the front facade of Oxford’s only Catholic Church, St. Mary Magdalene’s. Her steeple, the highest point in tiny Oxford, scratched away at the low-laying clouds on the overcast night. The sky was bruised and swollen, ready to burst. Archibald stood in the parking lot, dressed in jeans and a black hooded sweatshirt. Aaron was safely with Heather’s sister back at his house, because while bringing his son into the house of God might be a good thing, he didn’t want Aaron present for what he was going to do. There would be minimal churchgoers at this hour of the night, but he knew the doors would be open. One thing that Oxford took for granted was the fact that the church never closed, and was always open if anyone should feel the need to seek guidance. Inside, Father Thomas Lloyd was undoubtedly asleep in his quarters at the rear of the church. As Archibald entered through the large oak doors, he made sure to quietly shut them so the noise wouldn’t echo within the arched ceiling above. He sought no guidance. He needed only Holy Water and the hope that someone was listening. Quietly and cautiously, he walked up between the rows upon rows of pews, atop a crimson red carpet that led up to the base of a great stone carving of The Crucifixion. Jesus stared down at Archibald with marbled eyes and a crown of thorns. For a moment, it occurred to Archibald that the red carpet might have a double meaning, but he quickly tossed that thought aside when he saw the saucer of Holy Water a few feet off to the side. He stared at it for a moment, took a deep breath, and pulled out a pocketknife. He held out the palm of his left hand and glanced at the scar that crossed it. A jagged milky-white tendril of hardened skin where the glass had carved months ago. He held the knife in his right hand, and slowly pressed the blade up against the meat of his palm. With a quick slice, he could feel the warmth start to flow. But he clenched his fist with all his might, and walked over to the Holy Water. Holding his hand above it, he spoke, "I know both of you are listening. I’m pretty sure that I’m going to end up in Hell because of this, so right now I want to ask God something. Where are your miracles? How could you let this happen to Heather when it’s my soul that you’re fighting for? WHY DID YOU LET THIS HAPPEN!?!" As the emotion took over, he quickly hushed himself as to not wake Father Lloyd. The blood started to seep out from between his fingers. It began to slowly trace its way down his forearm, but never dripping into the Holy Water. "And to the Devil…I know you more than likely can’t show yourself within these walls, but I want you to know something. I want you to know that you can stop torturing my family and friends, because it seems evident that I’m all yours. There have been no miracles in my life to show me that God cares about my place in Heaven, so I give you this gift: the blood of a sinner within the holy water." Opening his hand, the blood ran freely and quickly dripped from his palm down into the Holy Water. As each drop hit the surface, they evaporated into a fine red mist, but never actually mixing with the water itself. From behind Archibald, a voice spoke, "It’s not that he doesn’t want you, young MacGregor. It’s the fact that you need to want him, too." Turning away from the saucer, Archibald MacGregor and came face to face with someone he hadn’t seen in nearly a year. The man for whom he was named, and who had given his life for his country in Vietnam nearly decades earlier, the ghost of Archibald Matthews. The angel took a step forward, and pressed a finger against the wound in MacGregor’s palm, which quickly vanished. He then wrapped his arm around Archibald MacGregor, whispering into his ear, "Come now, we have much to talk about." Above the church, the charcoal gray sky roared to life with a window rattling thunder. * * * * * Standing perfectly straight, as if his spine were made from the same steel beams the great vessels he commanded were wrought from, Rear Admiral Daniel Davis stood with his hands clasped at the small of his back. He peered out through the window at the rain filled night, with tiny gray shadows of rain snaking their way down his face. The darkness hid the twin trails that ran from his eyes to his chin. The tears came quietly and sparsely, but they came. "Daddy, is that you?" Her voice was soft and fragile, and sounded as though an impending return to sleep was suffocating her words. Daniel Davis turned from the window to face his daughter, who lay bandaged from waist to forehead atop the hospital bed. Without a lamp to light the room, it was bathed in the silver and gray of nightfall. "I’m here, darlin’…" he started, voice heavy with a southern accent, "Everyone else has left for the night. I said I’d stay and hold down the fort." Beneath the bandages across her upper lip and chin, a smile formed, "Fort? Isn’t that the army? I thought you were in the navy, Dad?" He smiled in return, "Always the smartass. Definitely got that from your mother’s side." Far away from the hospital, the drumming of thunder followed the trumpeting rain. Nature’s orchestra was playing a tune of it’s own. Daniel walked over to the bed and placed his hand atop his daughters, grimacing at the bandages that kept her burnt skin from touching his. Rear Admiral Daniel Davis felt helpless, but for every ounce of inability to help, there was presence of anger equally as strong. His nears in the Navy had trained him with the ability to control the showing of emotions. He wanted to bend down and kiss his daughter on the forehead and brush her blonde hair away from her eyes, but the fire had taken both. In their place were bandages and mottled skin. Her eyes were still radiant, if masked by pinkish skin, and they stared up at him, unblinking, "Daddy? Where’s Archie?" "He’s at home taking care of my little grandson. Your mother and myself will be watching Aaron while he goes off to wrestle in that god forsaken match he’s been entered into." "Daddy, please…" Heather said, trailing off as she noticed the displeasure in her father’s voice. "I’m sorry, dear, I know he’s making the money to support your stay here. I know that." Daniel Davis paused a moment, his voice soon crushed to a diminutive whisper by the pressing gravity of the situation and his own disdain for Archibald MacGregor, "I just wish it were something else. Some other profession, built upon honor. There is no honor in wrestling, dear." Heather looked up at her father and said nothing. The rain continued to speak its mind against the window. "I just want you to be happy, honey. His problem can’t give you a life filled with anything more than grief." Heather’s eyes began to shrink behind their lids, and Daniel Davis could tell his daughter was exhausted and quickly falling back into a sleep. He bent down and gently kissed the bandages on her forehead, barely pressing against them with no more weight than a falling feather might have as it landed. "Thank you, Daddy." She said, her eyes closed but obviously aware of the kiss, "Can you find Archie and make sure everyone is here in the morning?" "Sure thing, sweetie," Daniel Davis started, quietly backing away from the hospital bed and returning to his position by the window, "I’m going to find him. That’s for sure." The stern demeanor of his voice was lost to Heather as she slept, but it brought a rare grin across the face of Rear Admiral Daniel Davis. * * * * * The first-person excerpt from "100 Proof Life" by Archibald MacGregor was printed with permission from his estate.
