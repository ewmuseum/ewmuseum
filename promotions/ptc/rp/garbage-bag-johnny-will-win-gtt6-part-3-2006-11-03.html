Garbage Bag Johnny WILL win GTT6 (Part 3)

3 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-03 We had made it to the fence by the tracks after a route filled with accidental detours. It was almost by chance that we had made it there at all, but I feel as if a far greater hand than chance had been played on our route. Call it fate or what you will, but the events that took place that night are so surreal that I even question the validity of my own memory when recalling them. Adam was there already. When Nova and I arrived at the fence, Adam was finishing off a makeshift entrance with a huge pair of wire cutters. He was peeling one side of the slit fence to fold it open. He released it when he saw that I had brought Nova with me. "Johnny? Who the hell is this?" I looked back at Nova, who was standing behind me to let me do the talking. "This is Nova. He’s my dealer. Don’t worry. He’s cool." I spoke in short, choppy sentences. Staccato sentences, when pounded evenly like drums, carry with them a sense of frankness. I looked at Adam, and I could tell that he wasn’t listening to my response even through the mask. Instead, he was surveying Nova, and I realized his question to me was rhetorical and that I had even misheard it. He said, "Johnny? What the hell is this?" Adam Dick pulled the fence back again and walked through. I had to walk fast to catch up with him, and he made an effort to keep the pace up to a speed where Nova would have difficulty catching up with us. "Johnny, do you know how risky it is to bring outsiders here?" "I know him, though. There’s nothing to worry about." "So? I know him, too. He used to be my dealer. I still owe him some money, so keep him away from me. Besides, isn’t he a hippie? Hippies wouldn’t condone homeless men fighting each other. They’re all into world peace and all that bullshit." "I don’t think Nova’s a hippie." "But he still lives with all those hippies, right?" "Yeah, I guess so." We walked down the decline as we talked with Nova a good ten meters behind us. As we came to the railroad tracks, they looked more decrepit than I had first remembered them. I could smell the dusty peel of the wood slats. There was something ancient and knowledgeable about those slats. They weren’t like any other railroad slats I had seen. "The Veteran’s been down there for a while. We’ve got a few people with money on this fight. Show them what you got. You impress a few people with the right money for a few weeks, and suddenly you’re the favorite. We wait for a fight where the odds are good, I place a bet against you, and you take the fall. We make off like bandits." "That’s quite the plan. And you’re sure we’ll be rich off of this?" I asked in regard to Adam’s masterminded plan. "You won’t ever have to work another day in your life," Adam assured me. "But you’ve got to beat this cripple and beat him good. You’ve got to make a point, and the right people- the people that are betting big money on these fights- have to see you make that point." "Who are these right people? Are they here tonight?" "Keep your voice down," Adam admonished me. We had been talking back and forth in an escalating whisper, and my excitement had gotten the best of me. "Some of them are, but they don’t know you. Most of the bets are going towards the Veteran, but you can take him down." "Sure I can! He’s a rickety codger with a stump for an arm." I threw a few punches into the air with the gusto of some scrapper you hear about on the tube in black and white. Fighters back in the day had gusto. Nowadays it was all a load of cockamamie showmanship. The Veteran was already down in the pit, riling up the audience by bouncing around and throwing punches into the air with his good arm. I could hear the audience laughing in synchronized bursts even though I couldn’t hear the verbal barbs the Veteran was throwing at me. He was working the crowd over like a comedian on how he was going to work me over. I could barely make out his features from here, although I could tell the difference in length between his arms. And then there was the audience, a blur of colors meshed together in humanlike forms. They were like a quilt with heads stitched on and the same blurry faces. There were not too many of them, but there were enough to convince Adam that these fights had the potential to become both popular and profitable. "Am I supposed to go fight him now?" I asked. Adam nodded. "Rile him up a little bit first. Give the audience a show; even the ones that bet against you. At least give them their money’s worth." I trudged across the field towards the Veteran, and as I trudged, the field felt long even as the grass became more sparsely and eventually it was just an old lot. I was almost winded when I had walked the whole length of it. When I finally arrived, I was breathing heavily, but over my gasps of air, I could hear the Veteran hooting at me while swinging his crippled arm barely overhead like a stump tornado. "Son, I’m going to raze you over like a gook in a rice paddy!" I coughed a little bit and raised a middle finger while teetering back and forth on dizzied legs. "Down in Antietam, I bayoneted a whole battalion of Union boys. That’s where I lost my arm. I took some damned shrapnel from a cannonball, but I kept marching on until I could see the whites of the enemy’s eyes. Then I went ape shit. You’re just a bead of sweat on my right ass cheek, boy." When I had finally caught my breath, I started dancing along and shouting at him, but I couldn’t think of anything good to say because I was never quick with that sort of thing, and I was on magic mushrooms. I couldn’t, after all, make fun of him for being homeless. That would have been at least perceptibly hypocritical under these circumstances. I stuck with the safest, most obvious route. "You armless prick! I have one hundred thirty three percent more arms than you’ll ever have!" "You talk a mean game, Fancy Jack, but let’s see if you have as many arms as you say you do!" I have no idea why he called me Fancy Jack just there, but he beckoned me with his stump arm to come at him with a charge. I was tired from the walk, but I hobbled at him, and he limped at me- again, his limp an illusionary tool to exaggerate the amputation of that arm. What a smug bastard he was, trying to milk pity for his stump arm by milking it with a limp! There was a slight rise in the fans as we sized each other up, walking in arcs across from each other on an imaginary circle in the ground. It was like I was staring into a rotating mirror that followed me along a platform because all I could see was his eyes, and his eyes were just as good as mine. All eyes were the same to me. And speaking of eyes, I could feel all the pairs of them stuck on my back. I caught a glimpse of his knuckles clenching up slightly on his one hand, and that subtle sign of movement was enough to rouse me from these games of the psyche and into battle. I could hear faint war drums beating their way towards me as well as the flares of the battle’s consummating bugler. I charged forward and clenched his throat in between my hands. I squeezed tightly, but I found it hard to concentrate on strangling this slippery shit while he was jabbing at my ribs with his full arm. I gnashed my teeth together to withstand the pain and eventually took him down to the ground. There was a small rise in the crowd as we started rolling around on the ground. I ended up on the top with my hands still tightly around the Veteran’s neck. I was pressing my thumbs into the spots where I imagined his arteries would be, but the feisty bastard was still flailing beneath me. I released my hold and smashed a palm hard against the side of his head. His head instantly turned over, and I thought I had him, there. I stood up to breathe and he stayed on the ground, collecting himself as I diverted my attention to the sounds of the drums and the bugle. They were growing stronger, and with them, came an odorous vapor of sweat and sweat plastered to clothes and incense plastered to sweat. As I noticed the silhouette of a front line gathering at the fence, the Veteran was up and he stung me with a blinding, one-handed eye rake. The son of a bitch had gotten up, and he was relentless like a pit-bull as I staggered backward. He wrapped his arms around my waist, and I could feel the unnatural end of his half arm digging into my hip. The discomfort put me off balance and he was able, then, to take me to the ground. I shielded my head defensively and curled up, expecting him to slam at me with his good (and only) fist. I felt a few pounds try to penetrate the barrier of my advantageously numerous arms, but the frequency of the pounding slowed as the fence rattled in the distance while the group made their way towards us down the decline. Still, the oncoming group wasn’t enough to peel the Veteran off of me, but it gave me time to look through the scissor crack between my arms. It was the hippies, making their way down the hill, and they were still playing those infuriating bongos. The homeless trumpeter had come with them, and he was still at it as well. The blasphemy of butchered rhythms pulsing towards me, I gathered my strength and pivoted my hip to shift the Veteran to the side. I rolled to face him, both of us lying sideways on the ground, and I grabbed at his head, but he scurried back to his feet. The hippies were still up there, trying to chant messages of love and brotherhood along with the drums, but none of them could settle on the same beat, let alone find a beat to settle into, so the whole puddle of noise sounded like gargled words and arbitrary slapping. The trumpeter didn’t know what the fuck he was doing there, and with no real ardor for the cause being protested, he just kind of wandered around still playing his damned licks. Both of us back to our feet, the Veteran clumsily tripped towards me, trying to grab me around the waist and tackle me down again. He was successful on the first account, but I was able to stand my ground and maintain my base. I slammed at his back with balled up fists as he leaned against me, but I was caught off guard when the son of a bitch lunged his head to the side and bit me in the bicep. He took out a little chunk of skin, it seemed, because I was bleeding from it and wondering what diseases I would contract if I didn’t wash the bite out in time. I could see the hippies now in the last stretch of dim light that kind of hung on the ground so that I could see things near enough to me. The Veteran threw a punch at me, but I was able to catch his good arm in time to catch it in both hands. I tried to bend the good arm back, but the Veteran was relentless. He tried to hold out long and hard, and I saw him try to bite my wrists, so I tightened my grip before he could. He started to yell out, and I thought I was going to get him to quit right then and there, but I was brought down to my knees and forced to release my grip on him by a sharp pain that had unexpectedly stung me low. "That fuck!" I wisped in a breathless tone. "That dirty, rotten fuck!" He had hit me in the balls with his stump arm. It felt like he had just swung a tube of packaged lunchmeat- a stick of salami, perhaps- at my groin and connected squarely. All I could think about was the misshapen arm stump swinging like some disfigured flesh loaf towards my most intimate of areas, so I was in another world completely when his whole arm swung at me with his full power plus the power from the amputated section of his arm being summoned from beyond the grave. The next thing I remembered, Nova was standing over me in the eye of a hurricane of commotion. "Johnny! Get up! We need to get the fuck out of here!" I took a look of round while I still had the ignorance in me to fully gauge the heaping pile of steaming shit we were in. A loud shrill whistle pierced the air as a train appeared out of absolutely nowhere down those old, haunting tracks. It literally materialized from nothing before my very eyes, and chugged to a halt that blocked us off. "Shit!" Nova yelled out as I still looked around to gain my bearings. "It’s some sort of ghost train!" And true to Nova’s observation, the spirits of hobos long passed started flowing out of the train to avenge their restless souls that had been drifting in this locomotive limbo for ages. The hobo ghosts began attacking the protesting scourge of hippies, and the hippies were forced to fight back out of necessity. The defenseless flower children, in the tumbling throes of failure at the revival of their dead movement, were only delaying the inevitable. I looked back over my shoulder, and Adam Dick was lying prone under the ground. A long, black dress shoe dug into his neck. The shoe belonged to Julius Jones, the city’s most renowned dealer. He was a big money player; so rich, in fact, that he was able to pay off most of the city’s crooked cops and write it off as an expense on his taxes. He had his run on the town, and unbeknownst to me, he had also placed a formidable sum of money on me based on a bad tip that Adam leaked to him. In a certain way, Julius himself was a ghost. Everyone had heard of him, but not many had seen him. Of those select few, an even smaller percentage was still alive, and I feared Adam was awaiting a similar fate. It hadn’t yet occurred to me that I was next in line, but that would set in soon enough because Julius was one sadistic mother fucker. I had heard of his extravagance and flash as well as his legendary affinity for fat chicks. The fatter they were- the better, and Julius’ appetite for it was insatiable. There were nights when he’d bury himself in a blubbery sea of eight or nine of them land whales. I was sure those numbers were slightly exaggerated, but that wasn’t important to the issue at hand right now. Julius had a pistol aimed directly at Adam’s pinned down face. Julius was a ghost, alright, and Adam looked to be one soon because in addition to being a ghost, Julius was one of the right people that Adam had been going on about. "Adam!" I yelled out. I started back, but Nova pulled me by the arm with such immediacy that I was spun completely. I only heard the series of shots that Julius fired off as Nova and I ran through the mess of hippies and ghost hobos, back up the incline- the one that was a decline from the other direction- towards the blocked off fence. I just ran though, because I was being pulled. I don’t know if Nova was still pulling me or if it was something else. All I know is that I heard six shots. It was hard to hear anything. The hippies were screaming and the ghost hobos were making these terrible noises that sounded forgetfully human. In between the screams and the ghastly hobo moaning, a bongo hit would surface here or there, and that goddamned trumpet was still blasting off somewhere. But behind us there was a silence, and the ghost train that blocked the way left us walled. In the silence, across from the field, I turned in time to see Julius’ gun, the barrel smoking and pointed at me. I swallowed hard and heard a click. The damned extravagant mother fucker had wasted his whole round shooting Adam. I didn’t have time to look at him though, but I knew I wouldn’t want to for fear of it making me feel even sicker. Julius began to reload his gun, and the train started to slowly chug on. There were boxcars as far as I could see, though. "Shit, Nova. What are we going to do?" "I don’t think we have any choice. We have to get on the train." I nodded, and we waited for an open boxcar, running alongside the door when it came to try to jump on at an even pace. I jumped first, being in front, and I crashed right through the ghost train, landing in the grass on the other side of the tracks. Nova followed soon after me, and the train chugged on a bit before it started to speed faster into some vanishing act. Still, we ran as fast as we could back through the fence and then on until we couldn’t see anything but the empty streets. It set in there how much energy we had exerted, and gravity started to push a little harder. "That was Julius Jones," Nova said gravely. "And I think we’ve just made his shit list." "Should we go to the cops?" "It wouldn’t do us any good. They’re in his pocket. Besides, I buy some of my stuff from his guys when the prices are decent." "Alright, then what the fuck are we supposed to do?" "I have no idea. Do you have a place to stay other than your apartment?" Nova already knew the answer, though. I could see him calculating in his head. "No." "I’ll pay to put us both up in a hotel, then." As he caught his breath, I could hear the tone finally start to return in his voice. We were screwed, but as long as neither one of us came out and said it, we could pretend we weren’t. Nevertheless, neither of us said anything to each other as we walked to the closest place we could rent some beds. The next morning, Nova and I enjoyed the complementary continental breakfast. I read the newspaper as I horsed down an egg bagel that was lavishly lopped with cream cheese. The article claimed that Adam Dick was found dead from six self-inflicted gunshot wounds to the face. "Nova," I said, prompting him to look up from his bowl of apple cinnamon oatmeal, "I think we’re going to have to lay low; maybe get out of town for a while." He nodded solemnly as he stirred his oatmeal.
