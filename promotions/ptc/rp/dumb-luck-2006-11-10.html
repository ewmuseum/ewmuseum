Dumb Luck?

10 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-10 Brooklyn, New York October 29, 2006 The slow rhythmic hum as rubber met asphalt echoed through his mind, the lack of conversation from his wife a deafening silence that he wished would end. He knew better than to spark up the conversation, especially after what had happened. What do you say to the woman that you love, when the two of you were abducted for a shady deal that you never should have been a part of in the first place. "I'm sorry," he thought to himself. It would be a start, but the crooked grin that flashed upon his face briefly was why he could never bluff at poker. He could never keep a straight face when he became nervous, further proven by the beads of sweat that would eventually make their presence known upon his brow. He was a terrible liar, and after thirty years of marriage his wife knew it all too well. He knew full well that she deserved an explanation, and he was the only one that could give it to her. Something he was not looking forward to at all. The vehicle slowly came to a halt, but before the cab driver could turn around and quote the fare… Isabel Stevenson was already halfway up the steps they were parked in front of. "That'll be fifty bucks, pal." The driver had a thick accent, but it was something a Brooklyn native would consider normal. Bradley Stevenson turned his attention away from his wife, nodded to the dark skinned, bearded, man with the New York football Giants cap flipped backward, then fished for his wallet. He leaned to his right, propped his elbow on the seat, and caught a quick glance of his better half slam the door shut behind her as she entered their home. "Damn," he muttered under his breath as he opened up the bill fold, where he found three twenty dollar bills. "You got some change?" "Sorry, pal," the driver replied as a half grin wrinkled up the left side of his face. "All I gots is twenties." "Figures." Stevenson slapped the three bills into the thief's hand, then slid out through the still open door. "Thanks for nothing, pal." The emphasis on the word pal dripped with sarcasm, as the smile took full form on the cabbie's face. With a slam of the door, Bradley looked up at the door to his home. It was as if Saint Peter himself was waiting for him atop the steps, and he knew his name would be nowhere to be found on the list. He still managed to drag himself up the stairs and turn the knob. Of course, he should have expected what happened next. *SLAP!* The sound of flesh smacking against flesh echoed through the foyer of the home, but even before his skin began to redden… the screaming began. "What the hell did you do, Brad!?" Her eyes were welling with tears, the beautiful crystalline blue of her eyes tinted a slight shade of violet from how bloodshot they had become. "What did you do that almost cost me my life!?" He didn't know how to answer her. How do you tell someone that you tried to rig a wrestling match in your favor, when; for the most part, everyone thinks that wrestling is fake. Why would you rig something that is expected to be planned out days in advance? He had no clue how to explain it, but he had to say something… anything. With a forced swallow – probably his pride – Bradley Stevenson opened his mouth. "I was just trying to make you happy." And started off with complete and utter bullshit. "To do something that would let me retire early, so we could spend more time together, do all the things we've always wanted to do." His wife continued to cry, and he could only imagine how distorted he must be to her as she stared at him through tear filled eyes. "I could've paid off all of our debt, paid off the house so we would never have to worry about anything else for the rest of our lives. Lives that we could spend in each other's arms, instead of through telephone conversations, Isa." He could tell that his lies were going nowhere, as she turned her face away from him. "I thought we left all of this behind us, Brad," he heard her choke on the words, in between sighs and whimpers. "All I wanted was honesty… love… I didn't need money, Brad. You knew that." "It was foolproof, Isabel," he continued to plead his case. "All Sammy had to do was lay still for three seconds. Money in the bank." "Foolproof," She turned to face him, a maze of streaks upon her face from the dried up tears standing out on her usually soft skin. "We were kidnapped, Bradley. We were held against our will, our lives in someone else's hands, and you want to tell me that your plan was foolproof." He could hear the anger build within her, the words coming from her mouth filled with spite. She had him backed into a corner… no answer would seem right, but he knew she was expecting one. "It can still work, Isabel. All I nee-" "ARE YOU SERIOUS!" She lost it. He watched her charge in his direction, then everything seemed to slow down around him as he watched the back of her hand swing toward his face for the second time tonight. And all he could do was stand there as the stinging sensation grew, much like the purple welt that slowly started to rise. "I can't do this anymore, Bradley." Her sobs were almost gone, the tears once free flowing… now trickled. Anger had replaced the once worried and scared woman he called his wife. "I can't." He rubbed the side of his face softly, the bruise hurt more than he let on. Watching her as she walked away… That almost killed him. ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ Portugal, Spain October 29, 2006 "You wanted to see me?" Reginald Jenkins lifted his head in Sammy Brown's direction, a curt smile on his face as he flipped a sheet of paper over and closed the folder he was looking through. "Yes, please," he motioned toward the chair on the other side of his makeshift desk. "I know you have an important match coming up, Mr. Brown-" "Please, call me Sammy. Everyone does." "Very well," Jenkins adjusted the salmon spotted white tie around his neck, as his lips curved into a somewhat forced smile. "Sammy, I really don't know how to explain this to you…" Sammy could feel his throat tighten slowly, every breath restricted slightly. "They know about the money," he thought to himself. He was going to get disqualified, possibly banned from ever competing again with his luck. And yet, though he still had the money with him, he had turned down the offer. It wouldn't matter though, not in a tournament like this; where who you are and what you have done in the past will not guarantee you success. This tournament carried a certain prestige about itself, where being chosen to participate was more of an honor than a privilege. "It seems that we made a mistake when we chose you." He was the one who had made the mistake, now he knew he would have to be punished for it. He had tarnished the reputation of GTT by even thinking that the money would ever mean more to him than being able to compete in something as grand as this event. He felt as if he should be taken out back and beaten to within an inch of his life, only allowed to heal in order to inflict even more pain for his betrayal. "We had actually intended to accept a man by the name of Samuel Browne; with a double you, and we definitely apologize for you having to come all this way." "What?" "We're really sorry this had to happen to you, but we're not sure if we can allow you to continue competing when we had you billed as someone else." Jenkins could tell that Brown was having trouble understanding what was going on. "It's just that Samuel Browne pronounces his name the exact same way as you do, and while he wrestles under an alias down in Mexico… Well, we heard a rumor that he was going to enter the contest this year, and when we saw your application we naturally assumed that it was him competing under his real name." "You're not kidding are you?" Sammy didn't know what was worse, the fact that he was mistaken for someone else… or the fact that Jenkins is talking to him as if he is being sent home. "Unfortunately," Jenkins said apologetically, "I am not joking with you at all, and I really wish we could figure out some way to make all of this work." The surprise slowly started to fade away, as Sammy realized that this did not seem to be headed in his direction at all. No, things were taking a turn for the worse, as if he did not have enough of that in his life already. "But there is too much at risk." He knew this was coming, and yet he wasn't prepared. "So we ask that you bow out gracefully." "You want me to quit?" He could hardly believe it. This was the second time someone had asked him to just give up on something he loved, to walk away. The only difference, Jenkins was not offering him money. "We would never ask you to do that, Sammy." "Call me, Mr. Brown." He practically barked, as Jenkins' eyes grew wide as saucers. "Mr. Brown, I'm sure you understand that a man with your history could tarnish what the GTT tournament stands for." Sammy just shook his head, still in disbelief that he had been asked to walk away. Again. "This tournament was created to bring together the best of the best. To be honest, we wouldn't hire you to set up our ring." The comment pierced his heart, the pain started at his toes and traveled a slow and painful course up into his throat; coming out in what could be best described as a dry heave. "I understand that my words may be harsh, Mr. Brown, but you of all people should know that I am not lying. I'm not trying to hurt you here. I just don't want you to embarrass yourself." "But I won," Sammy finally muttered. "Yes, you did, but you can just chalk it up to dumb luck." "Dumb luck…" Sammy could barely talk. He could barely keep his head up for that matter. "I mean, you were lucky to get here… lucky to win your first match… but your luck eventually has to run out, and we cannot take the chance that it ends up blowing up in our faces as well." Sammy wanted to hop over the desk and wrap his hands around Jenkins' neck, but he knew that would not solve the problem. "So please, Mr. Brown," he pleaded, "just walk away. That's all we ask." Sammy shook his head from side to side, as he slowly lowered his head to stare at his shoes. "One more match," he whispered. "Excuse me," Jenkins responded, as he leaned forward to hear better. "Just let me fight Easton Hall," he repeated. "Win or lose… I'll walk away and you'll never hear from me again." Sammy lifted his head, and as he stared at the well dressed man sitting across from him, he could almost hear the gears turning inside of his head. "I would have to speak with my superior, but I think this is something we might be able to arrange." "You know where I'll be." Sammy stood from his seat, and as Jenkins extended his hand for a shake… Sammy just turned and walked out of the room. He didn't bother to look at Jenkins, or bother to wait for him to pick up his phone and make the call. He just walked out hoping he would he have one more match… Hoping that his luck had not run out.
