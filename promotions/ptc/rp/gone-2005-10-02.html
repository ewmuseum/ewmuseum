Gone

2 October 2005

Date: 2005-10-02 I am aware now… Of how everything’s gonna be fine… One day… Too late… I’m in hell… I am prepared now… Seems everyone’s gonna be fine… One day… Too late… Just as well… I am… Prepared now… Seems everything’s gonna be fine… For me… For me… For myself… - Seether *** The PTC Global Title. A championship. The championship. Pure and simple. In the PTC world, it gets absolutely no bigger than the Global Championship. It gets no bigger than winning it. It is the gateway to going from mortal to legend. Of achieving a status only a few have the ability to approach. Ironic to say or think when you see the list of people who have hoisted that title on high. Chris Storm. Do I need to even go further? Needless to say, it's a belt that has been passed around like a joint in a frat house. From one talentless half wit to another. A constant, steady stream of waste that is so foul that it forces one to cringe as it fogs up their glasses. A belt that has all the prominence of the Los Angeles Clippers. But in the world of PTC, PRIME is a big deal. An organization that is in essence carrying this dying alliance of federations. On our back this organization has garnered some level of acclaim. But the Global Title has been a punch line. A joke. But something changed that for me. Karina Wolfenden, kicking the hell out of an injured Emilio Rage. Hurting him to win a title. And then, vacating it. As soulless as I am, as callous as I am, that moment showed me that the worst human being in professional wrestling was far and away the Negasonic Lupine. A riot broke out after all that was said and done, on a PRIME show no less. It was fun having to run out there after watching some of the most disgusting minute of my life pass as she toyed with an injured Emilio to defend the honor of my organization as the fire starter herself walked out. Not caring. I see the vacant Global Title and see one thing. A wrong the must be righted. A vindicator to put to rest moments like that. When it comes to Karina Wolfenden, I am the prevailer of good. The PTC Global Championship will be mine. Not only for this, but because I am the only man who can bring prominence to it. I am the only man who can give the title a reputation that matters in the professional wrestling. I am, after all, the single Greatest Wrestler Alive. I don't see BANDANNA MAN as someone who can make a title matter. I can't see some Role play Game loving freak making a title matter again. I can't see a walking joke with Uber in his name making a title matter. I can't see some goth bitch making a single person care outside of her death poetry jam friends. I can't see fat ass, Captain Ride-My-Father's-Coke-Snorting-Hillbilly-Jim-Wannabe ass making a title matter. Just look at PRIME. He has sat on the damn Universal Title for over two months and is too scared to have anyone face him for it. He's like Black Angel, but only longer, and with a beard. I am the best damn thing going today. Just ask Mike Randalls. Just ask Karina Wolfenden. Nova. Angelo Deville. Dave Gibson. Ask Jeff Garvin. And I'm just getting started. PTC, hopefully your prayers will be answered. Hopefully I will be your new Global Champion. *** A tumble weed, blowing on by in the desert expanse. The white hue of a powdery, half crescent moon is visible from above the desert, lighting a trail in the beige sand. A lifeless area filled with dunes and far away mountains that had been beaten by the winds for centuries. It was a beautiful sight to behold, just before the look upon the neon glow of the city. Las Vegas. The city that might as well be the sports capital of the world. A place where you pay to gamble and the house almost always wins. Put your chips down and watch the house bleed the college fund to nickels. Bet on the National Football League and on red thirty-seven because no matter what, the house wins. He looked through the window as he sipped on some sarsaparilla on a bar stool. The time before was a blur, something he could not even remember. But here he was, looking out the window of this bar that seemed to overlooked the desert. Was he even in Las Vegas. He didn't know. The bar was made of brick, the inside having a pub feel as smoke and conversation hung in the air. The bar stools were empty save him. He sighed and rested his arms on the dark brown varnished bar counter top, his eyes closing for a moment. He felt distant, almost unattached to the world surrounding him. Probably a thousand different conversations, laughs loudly shouted in the air for all to hear, but it was silent to him. The white t-shirt he wore clung to his body like a second skin as he took his left hand and went through the pocket of his khaki shorts, digging for his cellular phone. 'It's funny, ain't it?' A rough voice, somewhat familiar but he can't really put his finger on it. 'What's that?' A response. He looked over, seeing an decently built Asian man wearing a pair of worn denim jeans along with a black t-shirt. On his head was a white Reebok knit stocking cap with a small New England Patriots logo in the lower middle with strands of unkempt black hair coming from under it. 'The fact that whenever you come into a movie theater, or you sit at home in your easy chair to watch a movie, you get thrust into the middle of a story. Most instances, you do not know the people to begin with.' He took a sip of his sarsaparilla before responding. 'You know the actors that play the parts, the cinematography style from director, you have those…' 'Not what I am talking about.' He smirked as he pulled a cigarette out from his pocket and lit it up. 'I'm not talking Spider-Man with Tobey Maguire and Kristen Dunst, I'm talking Spider-Man with Peter Parker and Mary Jane Watson. I'm talking you're thrust in the middle of the story, and you do not know these people. You don't know what they want in life. What their aspirations are. You don't know what they did at that fifth birthday party…or for THAT matter if they had one.' 'But Spider-Man has source material.' Was the response as he took another swig of his drink, having a look pained expression on his face after this one. 'Okay…bad example…come at it from the perspective that you've never heard of this Spider-Man. You floated over to this country on a door. Let's come from that perspective.' 'What the hell does floating on a door have to do with Spider-Man?' He laughed. 'Just shut the fuck up and listen.' The tone was serious, unchanging as the Asian man continued. 'Point is, you walk into a story and there is a face. Just a face. The audience is given essentially nothing. Not a reason to care. But they do. Most of the time.' 'So you're telling me someone walked into Gigli and cared for those characters?' 'Most of the time is the key there wise ass. Good films get you to care the second you walk in. They don't need a reason to care. They don't need an overview of the character they are watchings’ life.' The Asian man said as he took a drag of his cigarette, having never looked at the man he was conversing with. The face of Brandon Youngblood cringed into one of a quizzical manner. 'What's funny about that?' 'The fact that people need a reason to give a damn about you coming into this one.' The Asian man looked at him finally, looking right into his eyes. 'They need to know everything about you. What you are doing. What you are going through. All that crap. But I don't see these people walking into feature films then getting up to leave when they don't get the Cliff notes version of the lives had by the characters on screen. But in a written story, they need that sort of stuff.' 'I guess the reader has to decide if I am worth investing that sort of emotional investment.' Brandon smiled as he took another drink of sarsaparilla. Smoke blew through the Asian man's nose and he looked away. 'By the way, how's the marriage going?' An eyebrow cocked. 'Excuse me?' 'Marriage. Yours. Chiming bells, love at first sight, all that stupid shit. How's yours holding up?' The joking manner the conversation might have had before had changed dramatically with that little dash of information. 'Wait a minute here…' 'What? Too personal?' The Asian man smiled as the cigarette dangled loosely on his lips. 'You just ranted for a good minute right there about the hypocrisy of storytelling. How an audience can go and invest emotion into the characters and story right off the bat without knowing much about them. How you can't do that with written story to much success.' He was drawing back, trying to show he was a good listener. 'So you actually listened in-between those polite interruptions.' The Asian rolled his eyes as he said polite, taking a quick drag off his cigarette. 'You know I can't answer that then.' 'How about your father?' 'Not that either.' 'Your family?' 'Enjoy the silence.' 'So what, I'm not worth the effort?' 'For someone who disappeared off the face of the Earth, I don't think you really are. Especially since the last time I saw you you couldn't even put on a pair of jeans you hadn't pissed all over.' He'd realized it the second he looked over. The man he broke into the business with. His former tag team partner in the developmental leagues. The man who lost his Action! Wrestling job as well as being involved with the infamous fWo Survivor Four competition as a member of the Black team, being one of the men who tried to break away with the conspiracy to start Orlando Championship Wrestling. Alastair Nagatsuka. Neko Case. 'How's the tumor? Must be hard to hear the term inoperable.' Brandon got up from his bar stool immediately. 'What did you just say?' 'Am I getting too personal?' Typical old Al. Answering questions with questions. And then, a realization. 'This…isn't real.' 'Of course it is. That sarsaparilla that was in your hands? Real. That comfy bar stool? Real. That four hundred you dropped on USC not to cover? Real.' Smoke bellowed out of his nose as a smile beamed on Alastair's face. 'It can't be.' 'Don't make me point more things and ask you about them then respond to said questions with 'real'.' Alastair was showing is flare for the sarcastic at the moment, and it was starting to annoy Brandon Youngblood. 'No…you don't know about the brain tumor.' 'Everyone knows about dad's brain tumor Brandon.' Brandon downed the last bit of sarsaparilla and slammed the glass onto the counter. 'This is a dream.' 'Took you that long to figure it out…damn…you're slippin' on me Brandon. You really are.' An evil cackle and then everything pinned. Nobody moved except for the two men locked in conversation. 'Why am I seeing you…why the hell are we even talking?' 'Because you know damn well what is going to happen, don't you?' 'What are you talking about?' 'It's falling apart. Right before your very eyes. All going away. All that you thought you were fighting for, all you thought you were, all you ever thought was right and what was wrong. It's all falling apart before your eyes and every day is a test. A test to see if you can make it.' 'I don't drink sarsaparilla. I don't even know why I would dream drinking it.' 'Wha…what? Calling Cloud Nine, I'm sick and tired of weaving through the bullshit. You're going to lose everything. Everything. And all you will have is your…wrestling.' 'And that is a problem how?' 'I don't know Brandon. Maybe it's because there are more important things going on in this world then whether or not you will be winning championships and putting on five star classics.' 'It's my job.' 'And nobody lives the job quite like you do…oh wait…old Benjamin did!' 'My father was a damn moron who was obsessed with his work. He had absolutely no vision beyond it…no matter what he says.' 'And you say that with such vigor! I love it! I absolutely love it! You are the perfect man!' 'I want to know why I haven't woken up yet…Al…Alastair…you aren't Alastair…what do I call you?' One huge drag and Alastair ashed his cigarette onto the floor. 'Does it even matter. I'm a figment of your imagination. I'm nameless, lifeless. When you open your eyes I die. Everyone here dies. You see…you don't need a back story, yet you care.' 'This isn't a written story.' 'And how can you truly be sure of this?' 'I'm just here to tell you one thing. That's all.' 'And that would be?' 'What was it about your father?' 'I can't remember everything I said…' 'I believe it went: 'My father was a damn moron who was obsessed with his work. He had absolutely no vision beyond it…no matter what he says.' Pot kettle black.' 'I do my job.' 'And you let it absorb you. Your marriage is on the rocks and your family life is nonexistent. Your father is going to die. Yet all you really care about is your job.' 'Wrestling is my life.' 'Wrestling is what you do, it ain't your life.' 'I beg to differ.' 'And I beg to say you are full of shit. But that's just me.' 'But listen to this. Over the next couple…whatever…days, months, years…whatever…just think about your job. Think about how you let wrestling consume you. And hopefully, you will realize what truly is going on before you lose everything you have.' 'I'm not going to change!' 'Just remember that when you have a bunch of accomplishments and nobody to share it all with.' And with that, Alastair Nagatsuka got up and walked away, just as a white light began to fill the entire bar. *** Sun screen. A necessity. The white plastic bottle with the flip top that he could do in a simplistic manner with his thumb. A huge squirt into the palm of his hand, a white glob of sun burn protection that soon spread to his other hand as he slightly rubbed his other hand with the substance. He began spreading it over his shirtless upper body, making sure to get it on each part of his skin that he could reach. The Mojave Desert train regimen was great for stamina training, which was paramount to Brandon Youngblood's style of wrestling. So many people simply lift weights, or try hitting moves over and over until they feel they have perfected the movements and mannerisms of the craft. However, there was a lot more to wrestling then having big muscles, knowing every possible move in book and being able to execute it. There is always a question of desire in all of sport. At the college and professional levels of athletics, it does not necessarily become a question of speed or leaping ability. Having physical gifts is great, but it does not guarantee success. Fundamentals and desire are items that determine just who is good and who is great. In a football game, you see two teams hitting each other, and as the game goes on, teams get tired. The audience watching will scream about having heart (or the lack of it) if their defense is letting up positive plays. But the questions of heart is mostly false. If you have ever played a sport, you know what it is like. You lace up your shoes and want to do everything it takes to win. Nobody enjoys losing. It is stamina that can make or break anyone in professional sports. In wrestling, it is a paramount item as it allows you to do more. It is the ability to have some semblance of the energy you had forty five minutes into a battle as you did when the bell rang. Brandon Youngblood's wrestling style is predicated on stamina. He drains his opponent of their energy and then slaps the Gridlock on them. It is a game plan that is simplistic, yet is ultimately effective. And that is why he constantly works out. Why he does as much as he can in the blistering Mojave Desert heat. Running the dunes is a monumental task for any man. But it is a training tool that is literally flawless. The burning that the legs get as you pump them uphill in a sandy landscape is a hellish proposition, but in the heat of battle the run might have strengthened the muscle so you could get a little more spring out of the legs when executing a hip toss, or lifting someone up for a suplex. The legs do not get the same treatment as the upper body. The reason is because the first day Brandon went out and did the training out in the desert, he came back so burnt on his upper body that he literally could not move. However, as burned as his upper body was, his legs were fine as could be. It might have been that the shorts pretty much allowed him some semblance of protection. However, there was still the question of the small of his back, a place which he rarely could get covered on his lonesome, and absolutely never covered well enough as necessary even when he was able to reach it. This was where his wife came into play. A beautiful wife she was too. But a woman who undoubtedly masked her current mind set from her husband. It became somewhat clear around the time of Colossus II that she was less than happy with him. However, he read it as simply her not liking his job, which was an irony as she was the one who approached him about being his manager. Little did he know that her real disdain was associated to his feverish and unhealthy obsession with wrestling. Something that Brandon had slipped into like a former cocaine addict who just gets one tiny sniff of the white powder. And as time went on, his training regimen evolved into an almost all day job. He could do nothing else but focus on his upcoming opponent or train. Brandon slipped into this because the more he did, the more he won. The more he trained, the success he had rose. He was literally breathing on the neck of Hoyt Williams, teasing him into a lull before he would knock him down and take his place as Universal Champion. His training regimen allowed him to conquer Karina Wolfenden, Angelo Deville, Dave Gibson, and Nova. It was what won him the PRIME 5-Star Championship at Colossus II against Nova. It was what helped him outlast five of PRIME's top stars and stake claim to the number one contendership of the Universal Championship. But before then, his success was lukewarm. He was barely breaking even. He was watching great opportunities slip through his fingers. But it was a control issue. A Youngblood curse, if you will. His father had become a workaholic and did not realize exactly how much of his soul it had cost him until it was way too late. "Melissa, can you lend me a hand here?" She came to him, in her white robe trying to rub the sleep from her eyes. She took the bottle of sunscreen from the bed and put a little bit on her hand before rubbing the cool substance into the skin of his lower back. Brandon got up from there, putting on a pair of black Nike running shoes. Melissa just walked away, asking Brandon a question as she was leaving. "What do you want for breakfast?" "Don't care." Was the response. "There's a lot of that goin' around now a days." Melissa mumbled as she walked out. Brandon left the bedroom, making sure to grab the blue tackling dummy as he did. The sun was just coming in. It was a blinding yellow, almost seeming to sear the landscape. A quick crack of his neck and he threw the dummy over his shoulder. He took off, running out into the blazing hot oblivion, hoping to come back a little closer to true professional wrestling purity. After all, he had titles to win. *** Dear Brandon, I'm done. Sick of it. Put out. You know…it isn't what you do. It isn't who you are in there or anything you have said to me like you might think. When I met you in that bar after that Circle show, it was different. It was magic. Teaching was really getting to me and you were there to boost me up. But then, something happened. I let you steal my heart again Brandon. For real this time. And I took you for everything you were. You were a good man, a person with ambition and drive. Someone who I knew would never go out and harm me. Would never cheat on me or let me down because of immature behavior. You had an eye on the ball. You were drinking then Brandon, but I overlooked it. It was you who came to me and said you had a problem. And we resolved it. Together. I loved you Brandon. I loved everything about you. How you wanted to wait. How you respected me. How you always seemed to care. You were the perfect man. A whole man. Someone I dreamed when I was growing up that I would spend my life with. But something has changed inside you. You have gone from being this person to being this…this…obsessed crazy man who only cares about one thing. All you do is train Brandon. You did it for a couple hours everyday, and I understand why you do. But Brandon, every waking second of your life is absorbed into professional wrestling. When you aren't doing the suicidal things you do in the desert, you are watching tapes. I don't even know why you watch the tapes Brandon, because you never change your strategy in a match. I can't stand that. I can't stand living with someone who is so absorbed in what they do that they have no idea what is going on around them. I'm your wife, but now I'm just an accessory. It's always wrestling wrestling wrestling. And I'm sick of it Brandon. You changed. And the man you are now scares me. It scares me because all you are going to do is get yourself hurt. You've changed into this monster that I don't even know anymore. You're cold Brandon. Ever since Vetra…ever since then all you have done is train and work so you can hurt people. I don't know what it is, but what happened to Nova, how you hurt his neck…I knew then that you had completely changed. Brandon, I'm leaving you. You aren't the man I married. And when you decide to become the man I loved that night in the bar, then I don't want to hear from you. I'm going to be living with my sister, until I can find a new job teaching somewhere. Hopefully I can get some substitute work for a while. Brandon, you need to change. I don't know what happened to you, but you need to deal with it. And until you do, I can't live with you. Goodbye. Love, Melissa The funny thing about it is, you never know what you have until it is gone. Slipped through your fingertips and runs off into the sunset. Leaving you with this unfilled hole in your stomach that it seems nothing can fill. She left me. What does she want? What the hell does she want from me? What does she expect? What the hell does she want me to do? This is my life. My life. And I'm never going to change that. I can't compromise. Not now. Not ever. Compromise is weakness. She's the love of your life and you can't possibly feel the need to change yourself. To right the wrong you created. Wrestling is my life. It has been since I was a young lad, on the mats that first year of amateur wrestling. I'm an athlete for the love of God. What I do is built specifically for performance. To be the damn best that I can possibly be. This is a damn joke, isn't it? I go and wrestle Duke Williams. That's bad enough. But coming out of all that, I come home to this letter. She said she had to take care of her affairs. She did alright. I picked up my cell phone, punching in her number as quick as possible, and here a singular ring. Then, a voice telling me to leave a message. Beep. "I ain't changing Melissa. And if you can't take that, don't you ever fucking come back here! If you aren't with me, I don't give a flying FUCK 'bout you anyway." Hang up. And the funny thing? I do miss her. Even now, I am starting to miss her. You never know what you have until it is gone. Slipped through your fingertips and runs off into the sunset. And then, the phone rings. Main phone for the house. Let it ring a couple times, and I let the answering machine pick it up. I didn't feel like talking to credit card companies vying for my business. Beep. "Brandon…Brandon…it's mom…your father is in the hospital Brandon. He's had a heart attack." And my throat just fell to my stomach. I instantly pick up the phone. *** Beep. Beep. Beep. Beep. So I see him. Laying there, a sort of white glaze overcoming his skin. It's not a good sign. The loss of color. It means something is really really screwed up inside the body. It's that sort of skin you see just before someone does pass away. There is the hospital smell which you can not get out of your nostrils. Not necessarily of anything at all, since the air is basically sterilized. But it's the air. There is nothing to it, so it would seem. She looks at me through her red, beaten eyes trying her best to put on a brave face. A smile is feigned as she looks over, trying her best to put on said brave face and show some measure of composure. 'It was…the stress…they say it's from the headaches. He's been having these headaches recently, they are so bad that he can barely move. He's gone to the doctor twice in the past month to get something to do it. They put him on this medication, I don't know…they said it was a reaction. Mixing and whatever.' 'Did they say what it was exactly?' I ask. But I see her head lower again as she responds. 'I can't. I can't remember what it was. It was headache medicine. The stuff for real bad migraines. He had an accident where he fell and bumped his head at work apparently, and he hasn't been right since.' I think about it right there. No. She doesn't need the truth right now Brandon. Even if she wanted it, you can't give it to her. You can't tell her your father has an inoperable brain tumor that he only told to you. No chemo or nothing. Said he didn't want to live in complete agony in his last days. But he was in agony, wasn't he? Numbing headaches that got worse and worse, making it almost impossible to move. Yet you put on a smile and fight through it. How long could he have been fighting through it? How long have the headaches been ripping his brain apart before he really had to do something about them. It must have been absolute torture. But you know inside that you can't tell anyone. You don't want that sort of grief. You don't want everyone around acting like the next second you live might be the last second of life. But it is. He just did his job. Did it well. Let it consume him. Thought he was doing what was right. Maybe he was. Maybe he wasn't. He made me hate him for more than this, but I can understand this decision. I can respect not making your life a mockery and have to look into everyone's eyes as they look back at you having labeled you dead man walking. Nothing. No surgery, no chemo, no sign of a cure even though it might have been possible. The firing must have killed his will. Losing the job he had worked for over many years only to get canned because of in house politics. And this ain't wrestling where you can go to another organization and do your thing even if you have a bad reputation. In that world, you can never really get that back unless you are willing to totally rip apart your current way of life. Without wrestling, I don't know what I would do with myself. I'm sure it was the same thing with him. That must be why so many old people die soon after retiring from their job. There isn't a reason to wake up in the morning. Heart attack. Mixed medications apparently. I really don't believe it. I think he mixed with the intentions of suicide. For someone who had was roughing it out, roughing through the pain, it must have become too much to carry. Must have driven him mad. I look at mother. I look at my father, the slow hum of machinery in the background the only telling sign that he is still alive. I just don't know for how much longer. I get up, and don't even look at her as I leave. 'Brandon…Brandon please…' 'I've got to go to work.' 'There are more important things that your job honey…we need you here.' I turn around for a moment, looking at her. She had stood up, her legs shaking, the tears once again coming out in full force. 'He needs you here. The whole family needs to be here. They say…they say he might not come out of it.' 'I need to work.' 'Brandon…' I walked away. If only she knew. *** What happens when you lose everything? I don't know. But I'm finding out. Keep going even though right now, your father is on his death bed and your wife left you? There isn't a feeling that I need to do anything. I need to go and wrestle. That's it. That is all. Does that make me a bad man? Does it even matter? All I need to do is win. Win the Global Championship and take it back, saying I did it for them. Yeah. Turn the guilt around on them. Guilt. Am I feeling guilty? Wrestling is my life. I dedicated my life to this. I'm the best thing this sport has had. Might be the best to ever lace up a pair of boots. I'm twenty six. Not even reached my prime yet. I'm good at what I do. So why this lingering feeling of sadness? This guilty feeling that I am a piece of shit? I'm not a bad person. I'm not a good person. This is your moment to solidify your standing in professional wrestling. Another accolade. I say that, but when it happens, it's not a simple accolade. I'd kill for it. Fight to the ends of the world for it. My body count says it all. I will be the PTC Global Champion. Now I got a reason. Win it for father. Prove the wife that she is wrong about you. As I've said before… Just try and take this from me! *** (Lyrics: Fine Again by Seether) OOC Note: Not proofreaded, not enough time. Sorry. :-(
