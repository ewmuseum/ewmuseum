Kill... ummmm... Collette

9 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-09 It was a bright and sunny day in Washington DC, which annoyed Doctor Curiosity no end. Murders should be committed in dark alleyways in the dead of night, preferably with some mist or rain to obscure the harsher edges of the deed being carried out. The GTT entrant sat in a café in town, looking around and waiting to see when his wife would appear. She had called him back, eager to meet up again with Doctor Curiosity, and hopefully just as eager to terminate their marriage. The doctor toyed with a stick of French bread, and restlessly drummed his fingers on the table, nervous that his plan could go wrong. Nearby stood Eegor, dressed as a waiter, and more deformed than he had ever been before. In fact, he’d deformed himself so much that he even looked attractive. His waiter’s uniform suited him well, as did the latex covering over his face that made him practically unrecognisable from his usual subservient self. The plan was laid out in perfect form for the brothers to rid themselves of Collette. Eegor hoped that by killing her, his brother might be freed from the mental imprisonment he has confined himself within. A neurotic Dr Curiosity had done well enough in competition thus far, but a confident one would truly be a force to be reckoned with. The good doctor looked down at his wrist, and his Curious Watch. It was useful in a multitude of ways, allowing him to check the local air pressure, temperature, and strangely enough the amount of pregnant women within a twelve mile radius. Alas, whilst fitting in such levels of gadgetry, he somehow neglected to actually put a chronometer in there. Cursing under his breath, he called over to the nearest waiter, the man in disguise, Eegor. “Vhat time is it, good vaiter?” Eegor checked his cheap, but functional, wristwatch. “It’s ten minutes past one.” “Call me MASTER!” yelled the doc as he swung a small stick of French bread and smacked Eegor in the gut with it. The doctor repeated his blow, and was about to hit a third blow, when she arrived. Looking resplendent in a long blue dress, hitched in at the waist and bulging in all the right places, the Goodman brothers immediately remembered why they had been attracted to this woman. There were at least two very good reasons staring them in the face. “Do you always treat the staff at restaurants this way, Chris?” The doctor flinched at the mention of his real name, and he sat back up, smoothing out the creases in his tweed jacket (complete with leather patches on the elbows). The guilty look on his face was saved as Eegor spoke up. “Ah, the gentleman here was just helping me, as I was choking.” “By hitting you with French bread?” asked Collette. “It’s like the Heimlich Manoeuvre… but a bit different,” explained Eegor. “Well… just get a glass of white wine.” Collette dismissed Eegor with a wave of her hand, and took a seat opposite Dr Curiosity. She drew her seat up close to the table and leaned forwards, revealing even more cleavage, which drew the eyes of the doctor downwards. “I’m up here, Chris.” “Jah, vhatever. Now, vill you be signing zese papers and letting me be getting on vith killing you?” “What?” “Divorcing you! Divorcing you! Jah! I meant zat… not killing,” spluttered the doctor, trying to hide his fiendish grin. Collette sat back in her chair and her fingers instinctively went to the ringlets of auburn hair dropping down over her ears; a nervous reaction she had tried to cure herself of, but could never quite fully master. Doctor Curiosity was not the only person at the table who was uncertain of the next few minutes, and was about to take a very risky move. “So, spit it out, Chris.” Curiosity shivered again, and Collette loved it. That something as simple as a name could upset the man she was about to try and control made her smile inside. She could actually tolerate the ridiculous fake accent and the eccentric clothing to get what she wanted. “Jah, vell, ve need to talk. I got ze documents from your solicitor… is zat ze right word, jah?” “Yes.” “Okay. Vell, we just need to sign zese tzings, jah?” “No.” “Nein?” “No.” “Vhy?” “Maybe I don’t want to get divorced any more.” “VHAT?” Collette just sat back and smiled, fixing Curiosity with a look that could be best described as predatory. It wasn’t that she loved him any more, far from it. It’s just that the trappings of fame, the glitz and the glamour, the… well, the money. It was all attractive, and since she’d put in the leg work as the wife of a poor scientist, she figured she should reap the rewards of a modestly successful celebrity. Well, maybe not celebrity, but if Chris could keep up this Dr Curiosity thing, and continue to progress as he had, then the money and the sponsorships couldn’t be far behind. This was a big tournament, and big tournaments usually have big rewards for the victors. The mind of the good doctor was whirring at ten thousand revolutions per minute, whatever that means. She didn’t want to leave him any more? Was she in love again? Did he want her to be in love with him? Should she still die a horrible death? Decisions, decisions… “Your wine, madam.” The waiter came to the table, and tried to disguise his glee as he placed a large glass of white wine in front of Collette; wine laced with a deadly poison. He stood to the side of the table and waited in rapt anticipation. Collette raised the glass to her lips. “I said I don’t want a divorce.” “But vhy?” “I have my reasons,” she said, winking conspiratorially at the doctor. She moved the glass forwards towards Curiosity; a gesture of goodwill as she tried to hoodwink him in to believing she wanted the relationship to succeed. It worked. “NEIN!” Curiosity dived across the table and snatched the glass from her hands, throwing it to the floor, where it sizzled suspiciously. “What the hell are you doing?” yelled Collette. “It vas… ummm… a dirty glass.” “What?” “Or, a bad vintage!” Collette’s eyes narrowed. “I have to be leaving… and so does zis vaiter. It’s urgent. I need to educate him… jah… educate him on ze quality of vines served here!” “But…” “Nein! I must go! I vill call you and ve vill meet again!” With that, Doctor Curiosity grabbed Eegor by the throat and ran from the café, leaving Collette sat in something of a daze. There was clearly something going on, but as hard as she tried to think, it was just out of her reach. This deserved further investigation. ---------------------------------------- “She loves me again!” Curiosity literally spat it into Eegor’s face. His eyes were wild with rekindled passion, contrasting with those of his assistant, who was highly dubious of the whole affair. “She loves me! She vanted to stay married to me! Jah! Ze vorld is suddenly a happier place! Zat is vhy I stopped you givink her ze poison! “No master, she is playing you for a fool!” “You call me a fool? You call ze genius zat is Doctor Curiosity a fool? You know nothing, Eegor! She loves me!” Eegor considered these words. He knew Collette, perhaps better than his own brother did. She had been a sweet enough girl to start with, seemingly overawed by the doctor’s American ways. When he literally swept her off her feet and took her down to San Diego, she was charmed beyond belief. Yet when she had found those feet again, her claws had come back out, and she had shown herself to be shrewd and calculating. Holding their eventual wedding in England had been a smart move, confusing the doctor’s legal approach to a divorce. When the marriage is registered thousands of miles away, everything seems to be more of a chore. So, this new Collette didn’t seem to be fitting in with the old version he had known, and had indeed slept with. The very thought repulsed Eegor, bringing on the feelings of guilt once more. This couldn’t be for real. “She doesn’t love you.” “Vhat vould you know!” “More than you’d want me to.” The stark admission of his guilt silenced Curiosity. Eegor was right. “Jah… but I choose to not understand your argument… she loves me! I vill contact her and organise another meeting between us, and ve vill consummate our love once more.” Eegor sighed inwardly. He was facing up to too many things at once. Getting Curiosity ready to face Alex Grey would be hard enough, and then adding in all this was a bridge too far. This could not be allowed to continue. “Master, do you not remember your Plan B?” The good doctor wracked his brains, and suddenly remembered. To be absolutely certain that his wife would die, he had placed two back-up plans into effect. Should the initial mission fail, the second one would be activated. “Oh no. Ze Virtual Doctor.” Eegor nodded. The droid was on the loose. ---------------------------------------------- To make sure that nothing would go wrong in his mission to kill Collette, Doctor Curiosity had brought a special friend with him to Washington DC. It was an android by the name of Ze Virtual Doctor. He had spent many hours in the Curious Laboratory working on this device and it worked to his satisfaction. The only problem with the droid now, was that it was programmed to do one thing. Meet Collette at a specific time, and kill her. As such, the lab coat clad automaton walked through the streets of Washington DC, to the appointed bar at the appointed time. The walking motion was somewhat on the jerky side of things, and indeed the face was mostly moulded using old Halloween masks and cut-outs of the man from the ‘Weetos’ cereal boxes. In fact, the entire thing looked less like Doctor Curiosity, and more like what an artist’s impression would be of him if it was to be built in clay… by a blind sculptor… with no knowledge of anatomy… or indeed of clay. The walking, creaking, box on legs entered the bar, one thing on its simple computer mind. To identify and kill Collette. There she stood, looking immaculate in a red dress, changed from the earlier meeting so as to arouse more jealousy within her husband, to string him along further and increase the chances that she might end up not needing to return to work. She had laughed when Chris had suggested not one but two places to go to should anything go wrong. With his fastidious nature and attention to detail there should have been no problem with meeting at a specific time and place, but since she’d left him many things had changed. So there she was, at the bar, waiting and waiting and waiting some more. The automaton spotted her seconds before she spotted it. Simple electronic impulses dashed through its brain; it’s really amazing the things you can do with an old fuse box and the remnants of a broken Gameboy. Collette turned round and thought it was something of a joke. In her waiting she’d had a few drinks, and evening was closing in on the city, but to see some grotesque mockery of her husband in a bar was the thing she really least expected… and if not least expected, then it was right up there with a herd of giraffe singing the ‘Macarena’. “Jah. You are Collette. You must die.” The machine spoke briefly, fulfilling the programming given to it by the bitter scientist. It raised one arm up, and underneath the lab coat there was no arm. Instead, there was just a gun. It levelled itself at her head, and with mechanical precision, but without remorse, the machine fired. ------------------------------------------------ The doctor has rushed through the city in search of the bar. In theory he should have been able to reach it well before the Virtual Doctor did, but a mistake in navigating himself around a particularly fiendish one-way road system had left him stuck in traffic heading out of town, and by the time he had found himself in an area he recognised, it was getting late. For once, the occasional training schedule he lived by found a use outside of the ring, as he ran for blocks before coming to the bar. He just hoped he was in time. Entering the bar, the words found themselves on his ears, and he increased his pace again. “Jah. You are Collette. You must die.” The doctor sprinted, and then emulated Mike Powell in a jump of monumental proportions. He skittered into the Virtual Doctor at a tremendous speed. The gunshot flew above Collette’s head and smashed the light fitting located behind her. There were screams, and the more squeamish, and indeed the more sensible occupants of the bar decided that this would be a good time to flee the scene of such an incident. Collette looked down, aghast, to see Doctor Curiosity wrestling with a poor approximation of himself. “Jah, zis vas a surprise. Fireworks, jah?” The doctor bowed his head, before sticking the automaton under his arm and fleeing with all the pace left in his tired legs. Inside the bar, Collette just stood there, dumbfounded by all the insanity. Surely there were easier ways of making a little bit of money? ------------------------------------------ Back at the hotel, a curious hotel indeed, the doctor sat, planning his next move, and idly kicking the deactivated robot. Eegor was nowhere to be seen, doubtless returned to the Curious Laboratory in shame that Collette was once more in love with the doctor. Curiosity smiled, because he only had one more of his own plans left to foil before he could be reunited with his wife forever. This one would be the easiest of all to stop. One simple phone call and the hit would be called off. One simple call and… …the doctor stopped in his tracks. Eegor had the number. ----------------------------------------- In the event of a second consecutive disaster, the final back-up plan was to meet at the original café at the exact same time, just one day later. At the time everyone had considered it being over-protective, but just twenty four short hours later, it was the final hope of several people. To find the hit-man in the first place had been simplicity itself. For some reason wrestling tournaments would often find death following them, wrestlers and their associates somehow becoming rife for the mafia, murderers and other such undesirables to infiltrate. A strange phenomenon, but in the world of Doctor Curiosity, many things were strange. After finding the hit-man, picked up on a street corner where there was actually a line of them waiting, it was easy to set the target, time, method of dispatch and other such grisly details. If Curiosity had been paying attention instead of being giddy with excitement, he might even have remembered some more of them. As it was, all he could recall was the time and place, and he was even sketchy on those. So it was that the three of them reconvened a day later at the safe café. This time Doctor Curiosity was even more nervous than usual, and Collette was just plain bemused. Luckily for the pair of them, Eegor was still there, and he lurked just out of their sight, his eyes flicking to the rooftops on occasion to see what kind of shot the assassin would have. “Collette, my dear, zis is such a pleasure.” “Chris, what the hell is going on?” Doctor Curiosity saw the cushion she was about to sit on, and brushed it from the seat to the ground, worried that it might contain some crazy poison. “Chris? This is getting too weird for me.” “Jah! I am sorry… I mean, yes dear. I’m sorry, but I need to…” She was raising the glass of water to her lips, and Curiosity swatted it from her hands, an apology already on her lips as he stood over her, a protective shield that could only be breached by going straight through him. “Zis is going to sound strange, but…” Flipping his cell phone shut, Eegor approached the table and interrupted the doctor. “It’s all my fault.” All eyes turned to face the assistant, who had been a busy man. The hit from the rooftops had been called off, and indeed had been done in style, police being alerted to the presence of a murderer on the loose. ‘One down, about fourteen thousand to go’, they said, as they escorted the assassin into custody. “Collette, Chris…you’re going to have to settle your differences later on. For the moment, he needs to prepare for his match against Alex Grey, and you… well you just need to take a break, Collette.” Eegor took control of the situation, and amidst the confusion, some sort of understanding passed between the brothers. Curiosity knew that his wife was no longer in danger, apart from the remote threat of a misplaced bullet from one of the other numerous handguns in the city. Collette decided that perhaps it would be better to let Chris get the anger out of his system quickly, or whatever it was that was making him act so strangely. Eegor led his brother away from the café, and away from Collette, but not before she could offer one last request of him. “Beat Alex Grey, Chris. Beat him for me.” She smiled, and inside maybe her heart weakened just a little bit. Doctor Curiosity himself didn’t even flinch that time at the mention of his real name. His heart was set. He’d defeat Alex Grey, and return to his wife, and they would live, as the story goes, happily ever after. The only doubts remained within the mind of Eegor. His time had not been wasted, and he had quickly found his way to the bottom of Collette’s plans. A simple hotel phone tap, simplicity itself compared to maintaining the Curious Laboratory, had shown that the doctor’s wife had nothing on her mind other than money. With the evidence safely stored on the tape, Eegor had gone to the café to witness the end of her life, and to prove to his brother than she had deserved it. The actions of his brother had surprised Eegor, and the risk of Chris being hit by a bullet had forced him to call off the assassin, but now he carried a weight in his pocket, a weight that was burning a hole in his jacket and also in his heart. Revealing the truth would break Doctor Curiosity, right before the time he needed to be strongest. Perhaps it would be for the best to let the doctor defeat Alex Grey, for the sake of Collette, and then reveal the news that would hurt the man he served afterwards. Eegor smiled. The truth could wait.
