Ooame ("Downpour")

5 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-11-05 One week ago... My journey as a professional wrestler has taken me through even harsher downpours than what I'm experiencing right now. Truth be told, I was a pretty unlikely guy to make it in this business as it was. Were it not for Joey Malone taking me under his wing, I probably would have flamed out early and just been another "also ran" who couldn't cut it in this business. People have long accused me of having little charisma or talent with a microphone. I've weathered such harsh criticisms and became a pretty good wrestler... I'm pretty big with those niche audiences that you get in the independents. Trouble was, though, I could never make that much money in the independents. It's already a lot of work, setting up the dates and schedule I could work and then following through with it. Sometimes I would work six shows in a week and maybe get paid for the same price I would've gotten paid for a third of the shows when I still worked in the national scene. If I was lucky, anyway. I'd been stiffed on more than my fair share of paychecks, which actually hurt me a lot towards the middle of this year when I bought an engagement ring and had it eat so far into my funds that I was literally living off whatever I was making on the shows for a few weeks afterwards. That was probably the biggest reason why I turned to Sin City Championship Wrestling, and the mask of Baron von Blackberry. To be able to support my future wife, I discovered, was far more important than my dignity. Yet, in a cruel twist of fate, I had become more popular than I could've possibly imagined playing the role of a delusional mad dictator fruit who spoke in rhyme, alliteration, both, or just acted really weird. I'd main evented pay-per-views and gotten less reaction than the stupid "Retcon Punch" I do every night I wrestle as Blackberry. The gimmick was so unlike me and so thoroughly annoyed me that I always stayed in-character and in the mask even backstage. With the exceptions of about four other people, nobody could possibly know that it's Coral Avalon under the mask. That being said, two people close to me did know my little secret, which I kept even from some of my closest friends. One person was obviously my fiancee, Annabelle Summers. Another, unfortunately for me, was Inoue Doi. I don't know how my relationship with Inoue got to the point that I trusted her with the important information that I was Baron von Blackberry. When I first met her, she very kindly threatened to kick the crap out of me. Then, when I met her in the ring, she laid down a beating so severe that I swore that I'd never be able to eat solid food ever again. That's just the type of person Inoue was, though. She was a "hit hard, hit fast, to hell with everything around me" type of person. She not only gave no regard for her own physical well-being, but she gave no regard to the physical well-being of others, as well. In this profession, it didn't lead you to getting very many jobs on the national level in the United States if you had an unsafe wrestling style. Sometimes, you had to know when you're really hurting the other guy, and for as long as I've known her, Inoue had no such restraint. As ill-tempered as she was in the ring, she was even worse outside of it and regularly belittled everyone around her. Especially if they couldn't understand her. My relationship with her was established late in 2004, even though I'd worked with her throughout that year, when she befriended my girlfriend, Annabelle... the only person I knew that Inoue has never said a bad thing to. Inoue and I had our own grudging little friendship ever since then, where she would regularly belittle me and I would take it. I don't really let a few words bother me, and Inoue was far too immature to really dig down and say something that would actually hurt me. It's with that tangent about Inoue that I consider why I was in Memphis, Tennessee on a cold, severely rainy day in October. Tracking down Christopher Declan Sheffield was a whole hell of a lot harder than I thought it would be. As one of the stars of the Fans Wrestling Organization, Sheffield, better known under the ring name of "Alias", kept a busy schedule. This, combined with my own recently busy schedule, ensured that I'd have a tough time getting in touch with him. On top of that, I learned that he was teaming with Karina Wolfenden, a woman who invoked some decidedly awkward and difficult memories in me, so I had to make sure that I could meet him without seeing her. I had seen Chris once since I left All-Star Championship Wrestling at the end of 2005, and it was such a quick meeting that I didn't even get a chance to talk to him. So, it had been four years since I last saw him. We met in a hotel lounge somewhere just inside Bartlett, Tennessee. Apparently, I had lucked out today. Chris was visiting family in the city just as I was passing through for one of my last tour dates with the Squared Circle. This was the one free moment I would have to meet with him, even if it was coming down in such buckets that I worried if I would have to get back to Dallas in a rowboat. The man who stood before me and called out my name looked nothing like the Christopher Sheffield I had remembered. Gone was his spiked hair, his head shaved bald. His skin felt akin to natural leather when we shook hands. He had a scar over his left eye that wasn't there when I was still acquainted with him, and from what I can tell of his body language and the way he was looking at me, he could no longer see out of that eye. Speaking of body language, it was obvious from how gingerly Chris was moving around that he was nursing some kind of injury to his abdomen. It begged the question... what had happened to Chris Sheffield in the last four years? "Crazy rain today, huh?" Chris said with a grin. Well, his voice was still the same, anyway. "A little bit." I said in response. Truth be told, I was happy I brought an umbrella with me, because it was coming down so heavily that I would have been drenched had I come without one. The two of us stood in the hotel lobby for a bit, an awkward silence filling the space between us. It's during this time that I almost focus more on how I'm breathing rather than what I really came here to do. Sometimes, I really wondered about myself. Chris spoke up, "Been a while, Coral. How've you been?" "Can't really sum it up in any word other than 'alright'. It's not like I'm wrestling while on fire, anyway." I responded. Chris chuckled in response, "I wouldn't say that too loud. You might actually have that happen to you." If that came from anyone else, that might have sounded like something vaguely like a threat. Coming from Chris Sheffield, though, it was good-natured in tone. The man who stood before me had weathered a storm unlike anything I had ever endured, and here I was complaining to myself about the rain. He still had enough in him to smile and joke. That same storm he'd endured would have broken much lesser men, possibly even myself. "You're probably right," I said in response. I shuffled about where I stood. I admit it. Being in Chris's presence invoked a nervousness in me I didn't feel very often. Had it really been this long since I spoke to Sheffield? Did I really let myself get put up to this by Inoue? Man, I'm a pushover. "You look like you've been through a lot. Last time we spoke like this, you still had hair." "Not to mention peripheral vision," Chris said, before he tapped the orbital bone over his left eye, which drew my attention towards it, "A nice little gift from an old 'friend'." "Sounds rough." I said. When the hell did that happen to him, anyway? Chris looked at me as though I was wasting time. I had called him days ago, but he didn't get back with me until right in the middle of filming a vignette with Sykes for Sin City Championship Wrestling. By the way, never call me during a promo with Jared Sykes, he has a tendency to start making farm animal noises in the background. Do I just naturally attract the immature ones, or am I really just actively seeking them? "So, what did you want to discuss with me... in person, of all things?" Chris asked me. Well, at least he didn't beat around the bush. "Ah, right." I said. I admit that I was still getting over the culture shock of seeing Alias again, so changed that I barely recognized him, "Do you remember Inoue Doi, by chance?" Chris looked up at the ceiling for a moment, and I could see that little hint of recognition in his one good eye. He remembered her. Then again, Inoue Doi was the type of person who was not so easily forgotten, "Yeah, of course I do." Chris's expression was a little grim. Just because you remember someone didn't mean that your memories were entirely happy. Sometimes, a memory can be bad. Sometimes, a memory can be awkward. In the case of the weathered man standing in front of me, it was the latter. He seemed uncomfortable, or at the very least, he didn't seem eager about having this conversation, "What about her?" The million dollar question. Should I take the center square for the block? "Well, she'd been asking about you lately." I said. I decided not to mention the part where nobody was allowed to mention either the name "Chris Sheffield" or the monicker of "Alias" around her without risking being beaten to death with that woman's downright Amazonian kicking strength. I usually tried to warn the greenies in advance about that. Along with the kicks, the inevitable beatings, the inevitable hardway blood, and the various other liberties Inoue liked to do to new people. And then she wondered why nobody liked to wrestle her. "She was... rather insistent. Oh, um... I'm apparently her buddy now. Or at least, she asked me nicely, and I'm not even being sarcastic when I say that, to talk to you." I said. I still remember that hug and cheerful tone she gave me when I agreed to talk to him. To be honest, it still kinda freaked me out. "I... see." Chris said. The pause in his statement gave me some cause for concern. I'd known him for being straight and true, a guy who didn't hesitate about a lot of things. I couldn't help but think that there was something on the other side of this fence that I didn't know about. There was an uncomfortable silence between us before Chris spoke again, "So, you don't talk to me in years and now this." I didn't like where that line was going, not one bit. I didn't want to sound defensive, but maybe I came off that way in my response, "Sorry. I was... busy, I guess. Spent a lot of time in Japan." "Sounds like it." Chris responded. His tone wasn't icy cold, but rather, it had a hint of disappointment in it, "I guess we've all had a little change, huh?" "This, coming from the guy who had hair and full eyesight just four years ago?" I responded. My own tone was nervous, but I meant the comment in jest. The comment earned me a nervous chuckle from Chris, who kind of just waved me off as he did it. "Alright, look. Inoue had a crush on me back in the days of the Squared Circle, alright? But it didn't work out. It couldn't have worked out. She was 17 back then. Hell, I wouldn't feel right trying it, now. I'm even older, now." he said to me. As I remembered, Chris had been wrestling since about 1993. That's more than twice the amount of time I'd been wrestling. He had to be in his mid-30s by now, but given how haggard and weathered he looked right now, I wouldn't be too surprised if somebody suggested that he was 40. Did Inoue even think about this before she asked me to talk to him? Or did his new appearance not matter to her? Nevertheless, I did make a promise, and I'm just dumb enough to stick to it, "Chris, all I was asked to do was to get you to talk to her. Even if it's over the phone. She wants to hear your voice. I can't imagine that the age difference means anything to her." Chris sighed and shook his head, "Even still, you know that she's the type of person who'd keep trying until she got what she wanted, right? She's not the sort of person who'd understand what the word 'no' means." "Of course not, she doesn't speak English." I retorted. Actually, she understood English, but for whatever reason, she never spoke it. Maybe it's just that she'd turn all of our "R" sounds into "L" sounds, and she'd beembarrassed by that. I don't really know. Be that as it may, Chris just shook his head at me. "Not really what I meant." "I know what you're talking about, Chris," I said. It occurred to me right then that Inoue was only ever nice to me when she wanted something from me. I guess after I get Chris to talk to her, she'll go back to being cranky and hostile with me. That just makes me wonder more just how the hell Annabelle ever tamed her, "I just... I dunno. Sometimes, I think she'sunnecessarily hostile and that part of her, I understand. I grew up with three siblings, I know all about unnecessary hostility. It's the parts about her where she acts like a real girl... that's what drives me up the wall so damn much. I could take it if she was one or the other. I really could. Both at the same time means I don't know which Inoue Doi I'm going to get at any one time." Chris looked at me for a few seconds, and then came that chuckle again. "Yeah, that's what put me off, too." he said, "She's a polarizing person. Sure, she'd make my life a lot more interesting than it is now, but on the other hand, my life's already pretty interesting." Oh, I bet it is, Cyclops. I shifted my weight from foot to foot. Dammit, I've only talked to Chris for maybe ten minutes and now the only thing I wanted to do is leave. I talked to him, he didn't want to talk to Inoue, that's it. End of conversation. You know what stopped me? My stupid, foolish pride and my inability to intentionally break a promise. "You could at least tell her how you feel. Keeping her hanging isn't right, even if you might regret talking to her." I told him. I knew Chris well enough to know that he didn't get into screaming matches with people. "Hm," he said, thinking about it for a second, "Perhaps... you're right." Wait, did I just win this argument? "Look," Chris said, before he looked at his watch, "Just give me her number. I'm gonna run late on meeting with my folks if I don't get out of here. I'll talk to her when life gets less hectic, alright?" Hallelujah. I reached into my pocket and pulled out a slip of paper with Inoue's name and cell number on it that I had prepared for such an occasion. Chris looked at the paper and nodded, "Alright. Guess I'll let you know how this turns out when I can." "Yeah. I left my number, there, too." I told him with a shrug, "In case we decide to start talking regularly." Chris nodded and raised the number towards me in a gesture, "Yeah. Don't be a stranger, Coral Avalon." With that said, Chris walked past me and left the hotel lobby, right into the pouring rain outside. I watched him leave for a minute, wondering what could've happened to the man I once knew. Then I thought... could the same thing happen to me? Could I change so much that nobody could really recognize me? And... have I already changed in that way? I had a downpour of questions to ask myself, just from an encounter as brief as this one.
