You Never Loved Me

30 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-30 Sixteen Years Ago "Everything’s going to be all right," Wolfgang comforted his wife. Abigail nestled her head in his chest as they stood just outside the door to Dr. Erin Haskell’s office. Wolfgang embraced his wife tightly as if it were for the last time. They had tried everything they could to conceive a child, but nothing had worked. This was their last hope and Wolfgang couldn’t shake the nagging feeling that something was terribly wrong. The inside of Dr. Haskell’s office had a unique Mediterranean look to it. The Moriarty’s were impressed by the exquisite taste of furniture and framework around the office – the mahogany carpeting, redwood wallpapers, and the overhead lighting that gave the room a rich amber glow. It was the kind of richness they expected from a hospital hailed as the best in New York City – Presbyterian Hospital. Dr. Haskell definitely had exquisite taste and it showed. They hoped the same could be said about her doctoring skills. Wolfgang led his wife to the cream-colored seats that were smooth to the touch. He would sleep so much better if his bed mattress were made of this! But he would trade the sweetest dream for a chance to start his own family. That was the reason they were here. That was the reason Abigail hardly ever talked. "I bet it’s just a small matter of medication," Wolfgang began to explain. Desperation hitched a ride with his words. Wolfgang didn’t know what to believe. Looking down at himself, he couldn’t help but notice that maybe they were overdressed for this diagnoses. He was in black dress pants with a light blue, and Abigail was in a peach dress. Their colors were so pastel compared to their surroundings that Wolfgang felt as if they were dressed for a funeral. Abigail merely nodded and muttered a meek "I know". It was the kind of answer that any husband would give his wife and she accepted it. She had been through a lot. For two years they had tried to conceive a child and for two years they had tried everything anyone ever advised them. They made teas from herbal remedies, sought the advice of sex specialists, booked appointments with eastern medicine specialists, and even took some notes from the Kama Sutra. The last was especially entertaining, if only to add some spark to their bedroom lives. It was especially true in the case of Abigail who had always been very timid. Wolfgang kept taking deep breaths. There was nothing in his young life that had ever prepared him for this. He was still only twenty-two years old despite the rugged features he began to develop. With each chest-inflating breath, he could smell the faint odor of lilacs over his wife’s citrus shampoo. He smiled. Lilacs were Abigail’s favorite flower and their home in upstate New York frequently smelt of it. Perhaps it was a sign of good fortune. "Good afternoon," Dr. Haskell greeted them as she stepped out of the backroom to her office. Wolfgang and Abigail quickly rose to their feet and properly introduced themselves, though they had already met once before. That was the very first meeting between the three and Dr. Haskell had instructed both of them to undergo fertility exams. Now they were here for the results. "It’s nothing severe, is it?" Wolfgang asked. Dr. Haskell pushed the rims of her glasses back with the side of her thumb. "If we were just talking about you, Mr. Moriarty, then yes it wouldn’t be severe. You’re in good health, Mr. Moriarty." "What’s wrong?" Abigail quickly jumped in, her voice shaking. Dr. Haskell sighed. She could see that Abigail was not ready to receive the news she had for her. "Can I ask you a question?" "Of course," Abigail answered. "You haven’t had your period for a while, am I right?" Dr. Haskell questioned her. Abigail looked down, a brief moment of chagrin swept through her body. She nodded. "That’s good, isn’t it?" Wolfgang chimed in, assuming that a late period meant pregnancy. "Not if you haven’t had your period for months," Dr. Haskell informed him. Wolfgang blinked. He turned to his wife. "Why didn’t you say something about this?" Her lips trembled, but Abigail remained strong. "I thought maybe I was pregnant. So, I waited. And I waited. And then I just got scared, I didn’t know what was going on." "But you could have said something!" Wolfgang yelled. His emotions were getting the best of him. "I didn’t know what to do!" Abigail snapped back, her voice cracking. "Mrs. Moriarty…" Dr. Haskell began to tell her the full diagnosis. "You’re not getting your period because of an advanced case of amenorrhea bought on by hyperprolactinemia. You’ll probably never have another period and therefore won’t be able to conceive a child." Abigail burst into tears. "There’s got to be something else, doctor," Wolfgang begged her. You said she wasn’t receiving her period for the past couple of months, but we’ve been trying for years." "I know," Dr. Haskell began to explain. "But in her case, she’s had an excess of pituitary hormones, which have grown into a tumor. Her eggs have gotten to the point where they can’t be fertilized. Even if she were to conceive a child by some miracle, her reproductive symptom is completely out of whack, that I would highly recommend against it. I’m sorry." "You’re sorry?" Abigail scolded her. "You’re supposed to be the best fucking doctor in the city, and you all you have to say is you’re sorry?" "Madam…" "Don’t give me any bullshit!" Abigail screamed, her tears flowing. "You were supposed to be the best doctor here. They told us you could help us. You were supposed to help…help…" But she couldn’t finish her words. The tragic news of never being able to have a child of her own was too much for her. "If you really want to have a child," Dr. Haskell replied, "there are other ways. You could always adopt-" "Fuck you!" Abigail retorted. "I’m not going to fucking adopt a child. God damn you! Why the hell did we pay you –" "Calm down, Abby" Wolfgang tried to comfort his wife. He gripped her in a tight hug, trying to keep her calm as she tried frantically to get loose. After a while, she succumbed to his power and to her own helplessness. There was nothing she could do. "I’m terribly sorry." Wolfgang glared at Dr. Haskell in a cold stare. "Just don’t," he warned her. Abigail was muffling her cries in his chest and Wolfgang was helpless to comfort her. All he could do was hold her tight and breathe for her. Suddenly, the day seemed a bit less bright, the colors a bit more faded. Everything in the world around him seemed to lose its luster. Even the smell…that smell! That smell he was so fond of now made him nauseous – lilacs. "Let’s go," he whispered into his wife’s ear. It was time for them to leave. He couldn’t stand being in the office again. Moreover, he couldn’t stand the smell of lilacs anymore. They would forever be a reminder of his inability to have a family. He hated lilacs. A Fiery Affair (Present) He’d done it. The Sentinel had bested Jason Snow and sent the masses into a silent stupor. Their collective cheering was hushed into an overwhelming sigh of disbelief as the Sentinel had defeated Jason Snow. Who was the Sentinel? Who was this athlete who, at ten years older than Snow, had been able to completely overwhelm him? Underneath the blurring lights, the Sentinel had countered everything the four-time PCW World Champion had thrown at him. When the match was nearing its end, Jason Snow became more and more frustrated. It wasn’t supposed to be this way. The Sentinel whipped Jason Snow into the patriotic-colored ropes and stopped him with a kick to the midsection. All that was left to do was finish Snow off with his patented finishing maneuver, The Law – a jackknife power bomb. He’d connected and then pinned Jason Snow for the one…two… No, that wasn’t the way it happened. Wolfgang – alias, Sentinel – looked out the window of his truck. Days after the loss to Jason Snow, the agony was still unbearable. The PRIME superstar had been as advertised and the Sentinel was at a loss to amount any kind of offense. When it was all over, Jason Snow had won and the Sentinel had been merely another statistic. Everything he heard about Jason Snow was true. Wolfgang even swore when he walked down the aisle, that he could faintly hear the climatic moans of the women who were foolish enough to look directly into Jason Snow. I’ll get another chance, Wolfgang reminded himself as his eyes darted around looking for road signs. The lady from several nights ago had urged him to see her at her place, promising him extraordinary news. Wolfgang knew that she was a manipulating woman. He had fallen prey to her advances before. But it was the subject she touched on that caused him to second guess himself. Wolfgang would have felt much more at ease trying to find the woman’s location without the help of Map Quest if he didn’t have thoughts of his first round match still fresh in his mind. He had lost in the first round – much to the dismay of loyal AIWF followers – but had still earned a spot in the next round due to some favorable reviews. This time, he wasn’t going up against a 4-time world champion, but against someone who had a nomadic career – Brian Arson. He didn’t know much about Brian Arson, but he suspected that he was fearsome. Arson’s round one opponent had called in to forfeit, perhaps out of sheer fright. With a reputation like that, how could he be stopped? Wolfgang didn’t have time to conjure any plans of victory as his black Motorola cell phone rang. Ignoring safety regulations, he picked up. "Hello." "You got a lot of nerve sounding calm," Chuck responded. Wolfgang rolled his eyes. "Don’t roll your eyes at me." "Right," Wolfgang replied, smiling. Chuck had been like a father to him for fourteen years. It was only natural that Chuck knew every nuance about Wolfgang. "Losing in the first round doesn’t give the AIWF a good name." "I wasn’t aware the AIWF had a name." "Don’t play jokes with me," Chuck warned him, though he knew Wolfgang was joking. "You did well, but we really need to get your name out there." "I can do it this round," Wolfgang replied, his eyes still darting for that ever elusive Exit 40 that he was supposed to exit on. "You better do it this round. I can’t have-" "It’s against Brian Arson. From what I’ve heard, he’s good, but not great. Besides, we both know I don’t lose twice in a row." "There’s always a first." "Not this time," Wolfgang replied in stern determination. "Not for this." "Good man. That’s why you were always my favorite." "Thanks dad," Wolfgang replied jokingly. "Now if you don’t mind, I really need to get back to the road." Wolfgang hung up just in time. The words "Exit 39" beamed brightly under the night sky on the green background. Exit 40 was just half a mile away. He went over the directions the lady had given him the night before. He was to stay on the street after the Exit for a few minutes until he saw a black and white house. As he approached his destination, he began to wonder if the news the woman had for him had anything to do with his daughter, Angela. It’s Not Kool-Aid Angela sat groggily in her full-sized bed. She listened to her downloaded music on her Sony VAIO laptop with headphones one. Next to the bed, her suitcases laid open on their sides. She hadn’t bothered to unpack any of her clothes because of her father’s tradition of moving quickly in and out of places. Angela had become very aggravated of it and sought to escape. Her personal exodus was short-lived and her father was able to recover her. However, her father had not been able to do it on his own. When Angela woke up, finding herself back in her father’s black Land Rover that she had stolen, she saw that her father was talking to a woman she had never seen before. Angela had stepped out of the car to ask what was going on, but instead was embraced in a tight bear hug of joy and relief. Her father later assured her that he had never met the woman before, but that he was grateful that she had helped him find her. What puzzled more about that night was the fact that Angela could see the woman looking at her in an almost longing fashion. "Fuck," Angela screamed out, and slapped her headphones down on her laptop. She closed the laptop and set it aside next to her. The headaches were starting to come back. Every night since the incident, Angela had been vomiting and experiencing headaches. She didn’t know what was wrong her. She couldn’t recollect much from the night in question. The only thing that kept resurfacing her memory was the mention of one particular name – Cassie. But even that, she wasn’t sure was accurate. "Dad," Angela called out, stepping down from her bed. She walked out of her room and into the hallway. She called out once more, but silence continued to reply. Where the hell is he? She asked herself. Her father had told her that he was going out on a routine drive for his work, but that was hours ago. If he wasn’t here, she was just going to have to take care of herself. Angela eyed the opened door to her father’s bedroom. It remained a mystery to Angela why her father always kept his bedroom locked in their home in Albany, New York. What was more bewildering was the fact that he always kept his bedroom door closed everywhere they went. Angela was no doubt surprised and assumed that something drastic had to have happened for her father to forget this habit. She tiptoed inside the bedroom as if she were a burglar afraid to trigger any alarms. The room was empty. She looked around the dresser for any medications. Her father was always taking pills for headaches. Her eyes didn’t find any headache pills, but they did catch the corner of a black box sticking out of the closet. Angela didn’t need an invitation and pulled the box out. She sat herself on the floor and opened the box. A moment of relief and joy swept through her body as she found the box was a photo album of sorts, almost like a time capsule. Her father had obviously kept this black box as a treasure chest of sorts, filled with photos and memories. Angela smiled as she searched through the photos and papers, still wondering why her father kept something as beautiful as this a secret. Then she saw why. Amidst all the photos of her parents and herself, she saw another set of photos that disturbed her. Her father was standing next to a woman who looked very demanding. Angela picked up on particular photo and squinted, thinking she had seen her before. Then she realized where. It was the same woman she had seen several nights before! The very same woman her father assured her he had never met before. Angela’s thoughts began to scamper at a million miles a minute. She wanted to know who that woman was. A Spiritual Reunion The door to the black and white house stood ajar. Wolfgang couldn’t explain it, but he felt a strange force pulling him towards the house ever since he got off on exit 40. It was still apparent to him – after fourteen years – that the woman still trusted him. He let himself in and did the hostess a favor by locking the door behind him. The inside of the house looked more like a greenhouse than anyone’s house. Wolfgang unbuttoned the top button to his teal shirt when he felt the tropical warmth inside the house. Lined up against the walls were dozens and dozens of plants, most of them ferns. There was no furniture as far as Wolfgang could tell. The very first room had nothing but curtains and rugs with no sign of a television set or a place to sit. "I’m in the back room!" the woman called out. Wolfgang walked slowly towards his hostess’s call. He couldn’t tell if he was awed or amused by her choice of decorations. He always knew that she was a nature lover and her tastes were very exotic. When he began to near the back room, several overlapping scents began to fight for domination under his nose. He flinched. The scents were a bit too strong. "Were you expecting someone?" Wolfgang asked, walking into the back room. Standing with her back towards him, the woman remained silent. She wore her wavy red hair loose. Wolfgang looked around the room. There was no furniture here either. There was also the absence of plant life which had filled every room he had seen, including the hallway. Instead, the edges of the room were lined up in what appeared to be white petals and different colored candles placed at several intervals. He guessed the scents he smelled came from the candles. "Not someone, something," the woman replied turning around, in her revealing dark green dress. "Hello, Wolfie." "Hello, Karla." Wolfgang introduced himself. It was obvious in his voice that he had no love lost for the woman he had an affair with before losing his wife. "You look good after all these years." "Cut to the chase," Wolfgang replied. Karla smiled at him. "You always were in a hurry to finish." "It never bothered you before." Karla sat herself down on the floor and patted the carpet, inviting Wolfgang to take a seat. Wolfgang declined. "Do you know why you’re here?" "You have something for me." "No." Wolfgang shrugged. "Why then?" "Because I want you to be here," Karla explained to him. "Did you know that each person has a spirit that they can control at will?" Wolfgang seemed perplexed at the question. He knew he shouldn’t be as this was vintage Karla – always asking questions nobody gives any thought to. Still, he began to recollect his Catholic school teachings. "I suppose when you die-" "No," Karla interrupted. "I’m talking about having a spiritual form with you right now. Something you can control at will. When you go to sleep, the dreams you have…they’re not you, they’re the spirit form of you adventuring in the other plane." "Of course," Wolfgang replied, mockingly. "I bet the candles here are to welcome the spirits from the other people. You can’t be a bad hostess. I know how much you hate being a bad hostess." Karla glared at him, smirked. "They have their own uses. Some of them are for love, some for power, some for truth, and some for money." Wolfgang nodded. "You sure know what you want." "I know what I like. Do you know what I really like?" "The sales they have on scented, colored candles down at Walmart?" Wolfgang asked, half joking, half seriously. "I like knowledge. I like knowing." "So do I!" Wolfgang quipped. "Now tell me what I need to know." "Sure thing," Karla replied. "Sit down next to me," Wolfgang took a seat next to Karla rather than protest another time. "What is it I need to know?" "This," Karla said. She cupped Wolfgang’s face in her hands and leaned in for a kiss, closing her eyes. Wolfgang quickly snatched her hands away from his face and turned his head. "You have some nerve." Karla laughed. "What’s the problem? You’re wife is-" "Don’t you dare fucking talk about her," Wolfgang warned her. "You don’t have anything to tell me, do you?" "I do." "Then get on with it." "What do you think I love the most?" Karla asked. "Well I know it isn’t sales at Walmart," Wolfgang started, "maybe this time it’s sales at the local plant shop?" "You," Karla answered. "Too bad I only loved parts of you." "You never loved me." "You caught me." "What do you love the most?" Karla questioned. "Easy: my daughter." "Bingo!" Karla exclaimed. "What do you know?" "Men," Karla exclaimed playfully. She leaned towards Wolfgang for another kiss. "They’re always trying to get to the end." Wolfgang denied her advances again. "I don’t have time to waste here." "Why?" Karla mused. "You don’t have anything else to do. You think you’re daughter’s important? You lie to her all the time. Are you worried about Brian Arson? He’s going to lose." "You know he’s going to lose?" "Yes. I can see the future. He’ll pose no threat to you." "Why couldn’t you tell me about my match with Jason Snow," Wolfgang asked, mockingly. "That would have saved me some medical bills." "Funny. It’s as funny as the black box you keep hidden in your bedroom and how you always lock your room no matter what." "How…" Wolfgang stammered, having difficulty finding the right words. "How do you know all this stuff?" Karla laughed. "I told you already – spirits. You control your spirit and you can do all sorts of things." "Is that a fact?" "You can discover just about anything – time travel, mysteries, and truths. That’s how I helped you find Angela." "Yes, Angela," Wolfgang reminded her. "What does she have to do with this?" "Give me your hand." Wolfgang hesitated. "Wolfie, I’m not going to bite," Karla quipped. "I’m sorry you’re a bit edgy right now. Actually no, I’m not." Wolfgang offered his hands. Despite his bulky physique, Karla gingerly grabbed his hands as if they were the hands of a small boy. Wolfgang noticed that Karla’s hands were unusually soft. When he first saw her, he noticed that despite nearing forty, she looked like she hadn’t aged a single year. Maybe there was something to this spirit thing she preached on about. "You look so young." "The miracles of science," Karla said. "Here have some tea." Wolfgang took the cup from Karla which had been placed besides her on the floor. "What is this?" "It’s just some herbal tea." Wolfgang gulped the tea down. He found it refreshing. Karla looked Wolfgang straight in the eyes. "Now I want you to close your eyes and relax your mind." "What?" "Please," Karla begged. "I promise you it’s going to be worth it." Wolfgang did as he was told. "What do you want me to do now?" "I want you to pretend there’s a white light surrounding you and just breathe relaxed." "I don’t understand." "You will in time." Wolfgang did the ritual but couldn’t help feeling a bit foolish. He decided it was enough after a few minutes. He started to speak, but then fear swept through his body. He realized that he was paralyzed. He couldn’t move. Inside, he trembled as he fought with every ounce of his body to attempt to move a muscle, any muscle. But he couldn’t. He was finally able to open his eyes, but it did not do anything to calm matters. The entire room was a blur and the white light he was imagining now seemed real. It began to engulf the entire room until Wolfgang could feel the warmth of it. Then he passed out. Sitting across from Wolfgang, still holding his hands, Karla opened one eye and smirked. She watched Wolfgang fall back onto the floor. Wolfgang wanted to know the news about his daughter, Angela, and she was in full control. Who Am I? Angela’s heart raced. She ruffled through the photos she found in the black box in her father’s bedroom. With each photo she picked up, she began to feel more and more bewildered. She thought she knew her father; she thought she knew who she was. Here, hidden in the depths of his own personal closet, Wolfgang had hidden a part of his past. What disturbed Angela most was not the papers she found that she could not understand – in particular, one with the name Brian Arson on it – but the numerous photos of the lady she and her father had met several nights ago. If Angela wasn’t angry enough, she was about to erupt like a volcano. A slightly faded piece of paper caught her eye, jumping at her amongst the sea of photographs. She gingerly pulled the paper out from underneath the photographs and read the title aloud, "Presbyterian Fertility Clinic." Her eyes darted through the paper. It was a diagnosis of her parents. There wasn’t anything startling until she read the diagnosis under her mother, Abigail. A sharp, intense pain ran through her body when she read the report. Patient Abigail Moriarty, twenty-three years old, was diagnosed with advanced amenorrhea and is unable to bear any children.
