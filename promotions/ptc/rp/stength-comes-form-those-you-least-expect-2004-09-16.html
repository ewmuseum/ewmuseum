Stength comes form those you least expect

16 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-16 “You can’t be a wrestler.” Those words kept going through my head. Everywhere I went, those words were told to me. I couldn’t win a match because I was so small. I couldn’t hang with the boys because I was a female. I couldn’t take the punishment because I was to weak. Those words kept ringing in my head like a bad cold that won’t go away. Yet in the dark ride of these days, I tried to stand tall. But my mind kept wondering to the words that my father and mother kept saying to me. “You don’t need to wrestle. You need to work and make a living for a future in college.” Yet at the same time, I didn’t need college. College was far from my eyes. They were stuck on my deepest challenge ahead of me. That was getting to the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament. But I might have been ready on the outside but inside, I was scared and confused. But I wasn’t alone. As I finished up my workout, running the hot morning street’s of Dallas, standing at the top of my porch was none other then my ex-boyfriend and best friend still, Thomas. I opened the white gate to the house that I was renting, pulling it behind me before walking towards a man that I hadn’t seen in weeks. “Hey Elia,” Thomas said, standing up to both his feet with a smile. His eyes glimmed from the hot sun shining on his face. I could say I still loved him but he was just the same as my parents. Forceful and had his own mine set of what I should do. “What do you want Thomas?” Looking up at his face, he didn’t seem happy about my attitude. Not that I cared. He was one of the few that said I couldn’t do it. I walked up to the porch, stopping right in front of Thomas as a cool breeze came past the house. “Well I just wanted to come by and see how you are. I Haven’t seen you around in a while.” Seen me? He’s words almost felt like he and I were still together. I gave a small giggle as I walked passed him. But it was good to see him. Though in this day in age, I didn’t get to see many people. I had moved away from my parents. Feeling the pressure of all their words, I just couldn’t see living next to them and trying to do what I felt was the right thing to do with my dreams. Though, with Thomas here, it made my mind go at easy. But the feelings and pain that he brought came back with every words he spoke. “I’m ok. Moving back home in a couple of weeks.” I didn’t want to do it but I had been feeling lonely. I loved my mother and father so much and I knew that me doing this would hurt them even more then the way they hurt me. I walked over to the oak chair that I had in the corner as I flopped down into it. “But why…?” His voice was soft at this point. He turned around, looking right at me with his pretty blue eyes. “I can’t do this anymore. I can’t concentrate on wrestling and besides, I can’t live with the fact that my parents hate me for even trying.” I brushed the tears that I wanted to cry away with my right hand. Looking out at the sun rising. There was a small breeze of fresh air, almost as if it was on cue. I wasn’t really moving back home. Deep inside, I knew I’d be close to there forever. It was my destiny to live there. Take care of my parents till they die. That was what I was born to do and that is what I’d die doing. Nothing was going to change that. Not even a dead dream to begin with. Thomas walked over, placing the other chair close to me, his eyes locked with mine as he sat right down in front of me. “They only hate you because you do what they think you can’t. But I think you can…” My eyes lit up. Maybe he was trying to change. But if he only knew how I felt now. I wasn’t strong anymore. I wasn’t the fighting person that I used to be. I looked up at him, grabbing my head with my hands before I pushed my long dark hair out of my face. “Well I don’t…” I pushed myself up off the chair, feeling a little sick as I walked over to the side of the porch. It seemed that over the last few weeks, I just lost all joy in my little dream. I couldn’t think straight and everything reminded me of their hard cold words. I just wanted things to be simple. I just wanted to be happy again. And if that meant listening to their words, I’d do it. “What do you mean you don’t?” Thomas never understood me very well. He never knew what I meant when we were together. Maybe that was why we couldn’t be. It wasn’t enough to say I couldn’t but to know I couldn’t, that was the hard part. The last few days just seemed like the same thing. I couldn’t get my mind off the man, the best, or whatever he was that I had to face. Jackson Namenlos-Duisternis. Six-seven, three hundred and thirteen pounds. A giant to me. I kept telling myself that I could beat men like that but it always lead back to the words of my parents. The words that just stated simply, “You can’t do it.” “You can’t do it?” Thomas said, grabbing me from behind with his soft and gentle hands. “That’s what they always said. Now, it just doesn’t matter. I can’t be what I want to be.” My lost all faith of being myself. I felt tided up and tossed away in a jail. Never to be seen as myself again. I turned around, facing Thomas, as I looked up at his eyes. “You want to know something Elia. You can do it. I was the one who was afraid that you’d leave me. That is why I said you couldn’t.” As I heard those words, it felt as if my life was confused. Was he really saying this or was I dreaming it? Or was he just trying to get back at me? Did it really even matter what he meant it by. It wouldn’t change the fact that I couldn’t do it. “How can I do something when I can barely stop crying inside? How can I beat a man that’s two times bigger then me?” At that very moment, I remembered what my father said. You are a disgrace to the Smith name. You will fall flat on your face when you first face a man. I couldn’t take the pain anymore. Mt eyes began to leak out water, tear’s began to fall down my face as I pushed Thomas out of the way. My life had turned upside down. All I wanted to do was be happy. But selfish people changed my thinking. I felt insecure about my looks, about my entire life, but most of all, I felt insecure about everything inside my head. I just wanted to be left alone. “Elia. Come back.” I could feel Thomas coming after me. His voice wasn’t far and he splashed through the puddle that I had passed through just a few second’s before. I stopped at the tree in my front yard. Grabbing the branch that was hanging down from the storm that had hit just days before. Thomas got to me, pulling me around to face him as he put his hand on the side of my face. He to looked sad. A lonely cry from his eye came out as he leaned down, pulling me in closely towards him, as his soft and caressing lips began lift mine up. As he kissed me, I didn’t feel anything . What was going on? It felt as if all the problem’s that were around me disappeared. We slowly pulled away from each other, looking deep inside each other’s eyes, as to see what was under the mask that we held up. “Wh…What was that for?” I said, my voice feeling a little different. I couldn’t believe he would do that. Maybe he really did believe in me. “I love you Elia and I always will.” I couldn’t believe it. He said it. The words that I longed for in months. To have someone beside me to make all the pain, all my doubts of being insecure of whether I’d be a great wrestler, a great woman, or even a great human being. I just stood there, looking up at him before I wrapped my arms around his chest. Just feeling his body as he held me close. The wind started to stop and as I stood there, thinking of one thing in my mind, everything went away but one think… “I can do it.”
