Goodnight, Prince

22 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-22 Gil Lavenstein is stammering on the phone, afraid of what I might do if he lets me hang up the phone. "Chandler, you need to listen to me for once! Stop being such a goddamn fool!" This is the most stern the little pecker’s ever gotten with me. He’s a shrew; rarely does emotion come into our dealings. I take it for granted that he’s a Jew who knows money and he takes it for granted that I will insist on being difficult. And usually, Gil leaves to my own devise. Normally, I’m free to burn all the bridges I like. So, for the cagey Lavenstein to put his foot down lets you know that I’m being more of an asshole then usual. "Gil, you listen here. I have personal fucking issues that need tending to. Comprendo?" Of course he doesn’t. He sits behind his lacquered desk all day, waiting for me to present a problem to him. "I need to see my shrink. Plus, I like training in my hometown gym." Lies. I grind my teeth as I lie into the phone, anxious to get away from Gil’s pressing, prying questions. "You’re terrified of something." I want to ask him if he’s ever seen me fly in the ring; I don’t fear any man or thing. However, as a human…I’m not invincible. In the real world, you can’t merely grind down your problems. Chisel away for as long as you like, but your real problems never become dust. "I’d just like to know what it is." For the record, I’m terrified of poisonous snakes, burning alive, and the past. And that’s it. "I’m afraid of nothing. This is your bullshit attempt to dictate my life. Thanks, mom, but I think I’ll be fine on my own. Just because I’m getting exposure from GTT5 doesn’t change things." Gil is ecstatic about my win last round against Jimmy Bong. He’s already set me up for interviews and this and that, all of which I will skip. "We’re not those guys who do strict business together then go to ball games together. We’re permanently formal, you and I." I crack my knuckles loudly and I can just see Gil’s face cringing, hypothetically whining that I’ll give myself arthritis like that. From the drinking or the sheer exhaustion of being a professional wrestler, I hope to be dead before I’m old enough to have arthritis. "Tell me this isn’t about Deville." The implication is as clear as the mordant tone. "Tell me this goes beyond Chandler Tsonda and Angelo Deville and some petty grudge." I hate talking about Deville and PWC, but if you let me play the martyr, I will. I lost two years of my life due to Ron Starr and dozens of other people who mishandled my talent. "Fuck Deville with a steel dildo. The guy won’t even show up this round. I hear he’s zonked out on heroin." Truth or not, I have heard the rumors. Deville’s finally gone off the deep end. I even hear that it may all be part of some effort to have Deville relocated in witness protection after he testifies against some mob buddies. "You didn’t say it." "I don’t need to say it." "Tell me the truth then," he begs. His voice is rickety and delicate, imploring me to face this alleged fear. Because he’s afraid of the Deville, I have to be too. Hell, there are two other guys in this match too. I’d be the last one to point it out, but the universal revolves around more then just trying to catch up to the majestic Angelo Deville. "Tell me what this nonsense is about. You can’t really expect me to believe that you need to be in San Diego to absolve your psyche." "It’s not about Deville." * * * * * It was the most impressive match I had ever seen. PWC had gone overboard, for a "Sleeps With The Fishes" match. Of course, New Jersey was going crazy for their two princes, Angelo Deville and Clay Vendetta. Vendetta was "a horrible little pygmy," according to Deville and Deville was a something that you couldn’t say, according to everyone, for of being nastily dispatched in an alley somewhere. But it was no small thing to the roster that Ron Starr was sparing no expense on his two prized Italian cadets. Electric eels swimming around outside the ring were not only lavish for our down-home operation, but this was for no more than our regular programming. Everyone was getting ring ropes with no give and unpadded turnbuckles, while these assholes were getting aquatic ecosystems implanted at ringside. And this was what led most of the roster to watch the match from a small TV backstage. You could see the divisions in the roster between who was cheering for who. Considering the state of things now, it was laughable that Karina Wolfenden wasn’t present to cheer on her future ex. Sebastian Toomes, a hero if ever the world has had one, nonchalantly watched from a director’s chair, loudly flipping dip between his gum and lips. Toomes was also cheering loudly for any possibility of injury to either competitor, but other than that, he ignored the PWC roster around him. Every thing that Vendetta could muster up, Deville had an answer for. Using his reach advantage, Deville kept the shorter Vendetta on the defensive. He was perfectly fluid in every one of his movements within the ring. As only a two month veteran of the wrestling business itself, I was awed at the flawlessness of their exchanges. In spite of everything that the fans could spit in his face, Deville was unmoved. His face was obdurate, uncaring in the face of sins too numerous to name. As Vendetta tired, Deville remained stubborn, unflinchingly repelling all advances from Vendetta. But the little guy kept coming because he knew, even in a shithole near Piscataway, that beating Angelo Deville happened rarely in a career; when that win is within reach, you do everything you can. So I wasn’t surprised when Vendetta stood up, weary and probably in need of some oxygen, but victorious. Because when Angelo Deville is across from you in that ring, if you think you’re good enough to hang with him, you don’t just go through the motions. If you’re good enough to hang with him, then you fight a war of attrition with Deville. And then you summon up all you’ve got to strike. Maybe…just maybe, you can penetrate that mindset of an assassin that he’s got…or the one that he had. Now…there’s no killer instinct, no inborn knack to rip at the jugular. He’s no longer a symbol to be reckoned with- he’s just another man who can be beaten with his shoulders pinned to the mat. Dare I say, he’s not the old Deville. * * * * * In the dying dusk, a tall figure in a brown trench coat swept through the streets, his feet barely touching the ground. His strut was ferocious and in time with the hasty setting of the sun. His loose dress pants swayed with every calm zephyr around him, the figure passed the bright warning lights that society had given to those dark places. He was tucked among those dark places, where the real soul of the city lay. In these corners, human nature still lived. And him? He straddled the line between true animalistic nature and refined swank. Past a burlesque and several liquor stores, the young gentleman, dressed to the teeth in black under the coat, continued to walk. Down here in the slums, he was a beacon of prosperity. Among the destitute, his riches were enhanced even beyond their usual level. It was hard to figure out why this princely man was traversing the central city. He was no humanitarian, but he was here. And the adage was that if you didn’t have business here, you stayed out. He had business to take care of. He turned a corner, his coat billowing in the growing, whisking winds. The streetlights were coming on and he avoided the light that they showered onto the sidewalk, preferring to cling to the edges of dilapidated buildings. A vagrant appeared in his path, lying underneath a blanket, situated over a heating grate. As this tramp saw the approaching businessman, he began to call out. He lived off the alms of the bleeding heart rich. The Salvation Army had stopped coming into this brutal neighborhood, but food was still funneled in. The problem here was that the food was coming in with men like the one who was walking towards him; criminals were handed the aid and trusted to distribute it. In a town this rotten, there was no other way to do things. As the lone man on his promenade got to the wanderer, he finally heard what the other man was saying. "Sir, can you spare some change, please?" The shepherd passed him by, ignoring these words. But the hunger was almost unbearable for the homeless man. He latched onto the man in black’s shoe, in the hopes that his target would fling change in his direction and flee, as the others did. "Get off me, filth," was the stolid response from the businessman. "These shoes are worth more than your life." It was true. The sickening truth to this comment was not lost on its author. In this city, his material possessions were far more important than the inconsequential lives of stragglers. "Sir, I don’t ask for much. I’m just a guy who’s hungry." Rarely did a man with so much apparent wealth come down this far into the central city and the vagabond was distraught that he might not be able to take this many for some money. "If people like you don’t help people like me-" "Then you’ll become extinct. It’s called social Darwinism. And it states, specifically, that your inability to survive in society stems from a genetic flaw." There was silence, a familiar result from the cold matter of fact tone. "Who am I to interfere with natural law?" Flabbergasted by the abhorrent nature of this businessman, the sans domicile fixé merely stared into that face, hidden partly by shadow. A smirk crept out of the twilight and for just a second, this man thought that the devil himself was here to collect his soul, à la The Devil Went Down To Georgia. The homeless man began to scramble the other way, when the glint of a chrome Beretta M92 flashed from the businessman’s pocket. His eyes were glued to the gun and the shepherd, noticing the intersection of the man’s gaze, pulled out the weapon. He swung it wildly in the air, finally focusing the minute spread of the eyesight directly onto the vagrant’s forehead. "Do you understand now?" The homeless man nodded, truly comprehending the power that a man like the one in front of him possessed. The power to fuck with anybody and everybody could not be bought nor could it be won. It was earned by strong-armed tactics and never backing down. In this part of town and in every other, this was the rule. He looked up, but the businessman was already gone, the Beretta tucked inconspicuously back into the tidy suit jacket. * * * * * I’m sitting in the apartment, doing whatever I can to get the fifth round out of my head, to no avail. The shrink had no time for me today and I just got back from the gym. There’s nowhere else to hide from the thoughts that keep assailing me. The inescapable conclusion keeps coming to me that even if he’s not that Man In Black, not the Deville of old, he’s still the toughest challenge this tournament can offer me. He’s an inescapable entity himself. When I see a bridge, I return to Jason O’Neill’s tragic end (or so I thought) off the Brooklyn Bridge, as orchestrated by the black maestro, Angelo Deville. When I see petty thieves slipping pock-marked fruit off of vast stands, I’m reminded of his criminal syndicate, the way that he inspired fear in FSW and PWC. And when I notice a man in a suit walking behind me, I make sure to lose him. But who is new Deville? Who is this compassionate, fan-friendly sports entertainer? What happened to the human death sentence for all who opposed him? My mental response is automatic. The first person who could awaken the emotional zombie? Of course. Her. * * * * * The businessman was back, but something was wrong. This scene was entirely new. He was sitting in a café, his hand at his waist. Obviously, he was about to have a meeting with a client, which meant that someone was going to end up with a bullet inconveniently deposited into their cerebellum. Or…it would’ve been obvious in another lifetime, but this was almost a different man. His appearance was different; his face had been vacated of the congenital hostility. There was an air of happiness- his suit was tan, not black. His red-haired lover slinked into the metal chair, carrying a coffee. They stared into each other’s eyes, but this was not the familiar businessman. Where he had been inherently demonic…now he was human, and by that token, effable. He wore no trench coat and now the slick dress was corny, not intimidating. He had cleaned the dirt and dried blood out from underneath his fingernails. Were those frosted tips in his hair? Across the picturesque harbor was the reality of the central city, where corruption still ruled and men were killed over nickels and dimes every day. However, these men didn’t work for the businessman anymore. He had forsaken this lifestyle (or had he?) to be accepted, to be…loved? He looked into his love’s eyes and saw his future, dangling precariously like the necklace on her subdued, but immaculate neckline. There was no certainty to this business of love and he still abided by it, hoping for salvation of his human condition. Could that be what this was about? Was the devil himself asking to be let into the gates of Heaven? He held the amorous hand of the red-haired woman and they walked off in the direction of the sunset. The businessman mindlessly flipped a quarter to one of the panhandlers outside the café. His response was meek, but thankful. "God bless you, sir." * * * * * The assault over, the cub stumbled across the unclean plains. A gash across his left front paw flowed freely, giving him gory footprints to mark his final steps on earth. He had fought with bottled ferocity, unleashing all that his (relatively) small frame could unleash. The mourning and the anger powered spirited, breakneck blows. On heart alone, he had taken down two hyenas, one of whom already lay dead, covered in a pile of his own entrails. The second hyena had resigned himself to halcyon breathing, as a gossamer veil slowly floated down over his eyes. The cub shook his head from one side to another, trying to clear his muddled vision. The battle had taken him far from the corpse of his mother. Only now did he begin the somber march back towards the ghastly congregation. He felt flies squirming over the fresh wound on his right hind leg and tail and swung his head around to bite the vile creatures. It was futile, though, to try and rid the wound of this corruption, for every time the flies returned. As he neared the edge of the clearing, the cub walked more sluggishly. He saw movement on his right side, but his peripheral vision was compromised. Swinging his entire frame around, he tried to find the source of motion. Wary of his surroundings, he slowly pressed towards the shelter offered by the enveloping canopy nearby. Having turned his back on the remaining hyena, the cub had sealed his fate. Seeing the cub in the weakened state, the hyena displayed uncharacteristic patience. Of course, he had gorged on the flesh of lions and his own pack earlier. But his appetite was enormous; he had traveled many miles to find a bounty such as this one. Running his appetent, coarse tongue over ochre, rancorous teeth strong enough to snap a wildebeest’s leg, the hyena crept up on the lion that, in dying, was beginning to lose control of his senses. The hyena’s nature was not to scavenge, but to kill. His steps were muted by the dusty plains as he closed in on his prey. The cub staggered now, struggling to hold himself up due to the pain in his injured paw and his haunches. He was still king, though, and with a few more regal steps, he would reach his unassailable territory. Lunging at his prey, the hyena began the agonizing bloodletting, as powerful canines stripped through several veins in the cub’s neck. Sputtering, the cub collapsed onto his stomach. Immediately, he was back on his paws, but his neck squirted, ensanguined and opening wider with every rigid movement he made, frantically trying to jerk his head so that he might bite his attacker. These futile efforts would not stop this heroic butchery. As the cub collapsed, his breathing labored and a soft film coming over his eyes, the hyena knelt down next to him. He watched intently as the cub’s chest oscillated, but he avoided the cub’s eyes. He would not shame the king, but instead would make a noble meal of his enemy. With the last heave of the cub’s form, the hyena went about opening the royal rib cage. With ease, he was upon the soft meat. The cub’s eyes were frozen open. When the hyena had eaten his fill, he trotted off into the lion’s jungle to sleep off his feast. The plains were quiet except for the chatter of vultures, now descending upon the macabre landscape like a gaunt night cleaning crew.
