"Audience" -- Archibald MacGregor

27 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
* * * * * Heather Davis was walking around the kitchen when she felt the baby kick. She stopped and pressed her hand firmly against her tummy, knowing that any day could bring the birth of her first child into the world. She stood there for a moment, a smile across her face, and then pressed on with the breakfast she had been preparing. Underneath her dress, Heather felt a warm liquid start to run down her inner-thigh. Instantly she froze, and waited a moment. The flow suddenly got heavier, and soon her dress was starting to stick to her legs. Her water had broken. Any day became any minute. “Archie!” Heather yelled, not a scream in horror but more laden with urgency. He didn’t respond right away, and the most horrible thought shot through her mind. Dear God, please make him be sober. Heather felt horrible for thinking such a thought, but for the last year of her life, she had learned to cope with Archibald MacGregor, and the figurative demons he battled. She loved him, his devotion to her and their unborn son, but she also knew that he loved scotch and vodka and anything that could take the edge off of his painful life. Six months ago, when she first told Archibald she was pregnant, Heather had hoped that this life-altering news would steer him away from the bottle. A fool’s hope, perhaps? Archibald MacGregor rushed into the room, already throwing on a brown leather coat. Heather studied him for a moment, her eyes hovering from his mouth to his eyes to his hands to his pockets. He didn’t appear to have a flask with him, and at the last moment, Archibald caught her going over her visual checklist. He smirked, which transformed into a full-blown smile, “I’m saving the alcohol for tomorrow, babe. This is one day I never want to forget.” Heather smiled in unison with Archibald. Secretly, she hoped today was the start of two lives – the birth of their son, Aaron, and the rebirth of Archibald MacGregor as a respectable father. * * * * * A year ago, I started writing down memoirs because the events in my life started to leave the realm of ordinary, taking up permanent residence in the realm of extraordinary. Mostly written in notebooks, I took them seriously enough to save them for someone to read years down the line. While moving into a new house with my fiancé, Heather, she stumbled across these notebooks and asked me what they were. I had never told her about them, or why I had started writing in them. Even then, with her leafing through a few pages, I lied to her about what they contained. If she saw that I claimed to have talked to a man who had been dead for three decades, she could have taken our unborn son and fled to another part of the country, forever wallowing in the misery that the father of her child was insane. But I told her the truth about why I write in them, and their purpose. I left out the extraordinary parts, and left in the ordinary, and she thought the idea was brilliant. She probably only encouraged me because I know she hates my alcoholism, and was hoping that perhaps my writing would soon replace drinking as my everyday hobby. So I started over, and this is what you are reading right now. A year after pens, pencils, and notebooks…I’m starting fresh on a laptop PC and a word processor. My story will be told, and whether you choose to believe it or not really is of no concern to me, because I know the events described in this book are factual and true, even though they may seem impossible. In fact, I’m willing to bet that as you read this, I’m dead and buried. I’ve left instructions for this memoir to be published only after my death. Typically, your generic writer will try to lure you in with a hook. Something monumental happening within the first few pages to get the reader involved in the storyline, and care about the characters involved. Usually it is an event that dramatically changes the lives of those involved. My story is no different, as it will start with the birth of my son, Aaron. But I guess if I wanted to suck you in right away, I could sum up my life with a simple quote. A year ago I was confronted by the aforementioned ghost, with whom I share a first name, and told something very important about my life and the role I’ll play once it ends. Burdened with the news that I was a mere pawn in the deadly game of the afterlife, I asked him a simple question: “Does God really want me that badly?” The ghost of Archibald Matthews – a casualty of the Vietnam War who served alongside my own father – replied quickly and with the most sincere voice possible: “No, he doesn’t. What’s more important to Him is that you don’t fight against him. He fears your presence in Hell much more than he approves of your place in Heaven.” So there is your proverbial carrot, dangling before you. Teasing you. Now I’m going to lead you on a tale that you might discard as lunacy, or you might preach as truth. Everything in this book relates to that quote, the impact it had on my life, the life of my late wife, and the life of our son. Heaven and Hell went to war over my soul, and I should have known better on the morning that Aaron came into this world. I should have known that a small farming town, with only two traffic lights and one main street, should never have a high-speed collision. * * * * * Quickly escorting Heather to the minivan, Archibald frowned slightly at the sight of the vehicle. He had traded in his Nissan 300ZX sports car and bought a used minivan a few months ago at the request of Heather. She had said that safely driving a family around was more important than being the fastest car on the road. She was right, as always. “You ok? How are the contractions?” Archibald asked, surprisingly calm considering what he knew about their son, and his sought after future. Breathing in tune with the contractions, Heather simply gave a thumbs-up as she gingerly got into the passengers seat of the minivan. As soon as Heather was secured, Archibald hustled around the vehicle and started it, backing out of the driveway. The drive to the hospital would be simple. They would need to go through downtown Oxford, and out towards the nearby borough of Nottingham, before heading south towards Maryland. Union Hospital was only 15 minutes away, a simple drive from their home. “Be careful, sweetie, it rained last night and the roads look sli—“ Heather started, before being abruptly cut off by the sound of skidding tires. Caught up in the heat of the moment, Archibald had backed out of the driveway and halfway onto the road before he realized a car was coming. The oncoming vehicle, a large pickup truck, slammed its brakes and skidded a few feet before steering around the hospital-bound minivan. Heather looked at Archie, who looked back at her with the widest of eyes. She could have sworn that he saw a ghost, or realized how close they had come to death on the eve of something so wonderful. His face looked pale and ashen. ”Don’t worry about it, baby…that’s why they call them accidents. You didn’t do it on purpose.” She said as she reached over and rubbed the back of Archibald’s neck with her hand. Archibald noticed her wince and place her free hand across her swollen abdomen. He continued to pull out onto the street, and headed for downtown Oxford. Archibald couldn’t stop thinking about the truck that had almost t-boned them, and what it could have possibly meant. He knew that Hell wanted his soul, as it had the previous four generations of his family. Each generation grew stronger in power, and in a war against Heaven…Hell wanted to have the most powerful soldiers to help attain victory. With the birth of Aaron, a sixth generation would be sought after. Archibald knew the consequences, and tried to lead a better life that would earn him a ticket to Heaven rather than Hell. He had purchased his late grandmothers home, and started refinishing it for when he and Heather would marry, sometime later this year after Aaron was born. He could remember continually picking the weeds from the base of the large bronze statue of Archibald Matthews that stood at the center of Oxford. His winnings from the Cruiserweight 2 tournament went towards paying for the house, his medical bills after Cereal Killer tore his bicep clean off of the bone, and the rest was donated to charity. He was trying to become a better man. Protecting his son was Archibald MacGregor’s ultimate goal, yet he couldn’t shake the addiction to alcohol that seemed to be the seed of all the problems in his life. As the minivan passed through the first of two traffic lights that bookend the main street in Oxford, Archibald glanced both ways making sure that no traffic was coming at them from any direction. He could see no cars, and very few pedestrians. Only a few hundred feet ahead, the green light was turning yellow, and then red. As Heather groaned in pain with another contraction, Archibald was tempted to mash the gas and speed through the last traffic light. But that is what they wanted. A mistake on his part, so Hell could capitalize. The van coasted to a stop, and the happy family waited. Once again, Archibald quickly glanced around the surrounding area to make sure that nothing out of the ordinary was occurring. To the left, he saw the church, it’s spire the tallest object in Oxford. There was a pair of children standing next to each other, their hands clasped in friendly unison, waiting for the light to change as well. To his right, across the street from the church, was the cemetery. There was a funeral procession in progress, and he quickly felt eerie staring at someone being put into the ground, especially when he feared that something was hoping to put him in that very same place. Across the intersection, another minivan was idling. In the side windows, Archibald could see children playing with each other, and their parents were having a laugh-filled conversation in the front seat. The light turned green right as Heather let out a yelp of severe pain. Rather than pressing the accelerator, he quickly turned to his soon-to-be bride and looked in horror as her face tightened in agony and became flush. Archibald didn’t hear the roaring engine, nor see the car it belonged to until it was too late. * * * * * Holding your son in your arms for the first time is the greatest feeling in the world. Even if he is sleeping, wide-awake, or crying…it won’t ever matter. You know that you helped create life, and it is right there in your grasp. And it fills you with the most unmatchable, indescribable feeling of joy this world could ever offer. You might owe thousands in college loans, or your mortgage payment might be late. Your parents could have died the previous week, or your pet could have died that very morning…but it all gets dulled down to the most painless moment of your life when you hold your child close that very first time. When you meet someone, it takes quite some time for you to fall in love. Be it a girlfriend or boyfriend…lust at first sight is often mistaken as love at first sight. But when you hold your child in your arms, you instantly love him or her. I loved Aaron the moment I laid eyes on him. Aaron Christopher MacGregor, born into this world on February 26th, 2006, was asleep when they first gave him to me. But after about 5 minutes of crying alongside Heather, he finally awoke and stared at his father for the first time. I wonder what he thought, if anything. When I first spoke to him, I wonder if he recognized the voice that had sung to him countless nights while I lay next to his pregnant mother. I don’t have the greatest singing voice in the world, so I know Heather must have been in agony as I sang to my unborn son. As I write this, a month after his birth, I realize I might have sang to him because I had read somewhere that a child can recognize their parents voices, starting well before they are born. Perhaps I wanted my son to love me as soon as he saw me, because I knew the hate he might have for me later on in life as a result of my undeniable faults. But in that moment, it was just Aaron, Heather, and myself. She cried, I cried, and soon thereafter, little Aaron was crying as well. One happy, tear-filled family. Holding Aaron was the greatest feeling in the world, and I can’t express that enough. It was also hard having to let him go and give him to Heather, knowing the dangers that would certainly come his way during the next few years of his life. I’d be damned if I would let the sins of myself, my father, and those before him doom the future of my son. The ride to the hospital was proof enough that Heaven and Hell already were preparing for our arrival with eager eyes. * * * * * Archibald just about had the accelerator mashed when he quickly slammed on the brakes. Out of the corner of his eye, he saw the sports car enter from the right and blow through the red light at a high rate of speed. The minivan that was waiting across the intersection had already started through, and was directly in the path of the speeding car. The collision sounded otherworldly, as tons of metal slammed into each other and went flying in different directions. Archibald stared at what happened around him, soaking up the details as if it were happening in slow motion. The other minivan was tossed into the air by the force of the impact, sending it cart-wheeling towards the cemetery. The vehicle was airborne for a second before it came crashing down to the asphalt in a roar of screeching metal and sparks, sliding on the pavement until it came resting against a tree at the edge of the cemetery. The funeral party stood and watched, mere feet away from the mangled vehicle. Birds flocked from the tree as the minivan came crashing to a halt against the trunk, hovering above the scene in a constantly swirling circle of dark feathers. The sports car lay in a heap of metal in the center of the intersection, it’s front end bent upward at an unnatural angle. Its front wheels were actually off of the ground, still rotating on the bent axel. The pair of children standing at the intersection remained frozen, perhaps paralyzed by such a tragic event. And then the church-bells tolled 6 times, signaling the new hour of the early morning. Heather screamed once more, this time out of horror for what had just occurred, causing Archibald to mash the gas and weave through the mess as he sped off towards the hospital. He picked up his cell phone, and dialed 911 to inform them of the horrific accident they had barely avoided. * * * * * Accidents happen so quickly, you can barely realize the severity of the situation. The moment is so surreal that you are actually soaking up more details than you know. Do you remember the first time you got into an auto accident, and the feelings that initially followed? Confusion, then rage. But hours after the accident, you start to remember more details. Little things here and there, and sooner than later you can re-live the entire ordeal over again and realize exactly what happened. The accident that occurred while I was driving Heather to the hospital is no different. But what scares me is that time has given me more insight to what actually occurred, rather than what was initially perceived. Looking back, I can still see the minivan being smashed into and sent flying through the air. Spinning end over end, I can still see the shattered windows and bent framework of the vehicle, but time has given me new details. I can see the six faces, pressed up against the shattered glass, staring down at my vehicle. I can think back to when we were waiting at the red light, only moments before the accident, to when the funeral procession was taking place. At the time, I didn’t realize that every single person in the funeral party was glancing towards the intersection, as if they knew what was going to occur. And when the mangled van came to a rest against the tree, only feet from where they were standing…all of their eyes weren’t on the van in the accident, but rather, on mine. The birds circling overhead of the accident each had their gaze fixed upon the van I was driving. And the two children, apparently waiting to cross the intersection, weren’t waiting for that at all. I can remember that, at first, it had looked like they were holding hands as they stood next to each other, but now I can clearly remember that their hands weren’t touching. It appeared that way, but their hands were maybe a half-inch apart, at best. One child had silver hair, and wore white from head to toe. The other child wore a black suit with a crimson tie, and stood beside his counterpart. Their hands never touched, only flirting with contact with one another. Both were smiling. Both were watching. Their gaze never moved away from our minivan, and more importantly…their gaze never left the stomach of my beloved Heather, and our unborn child. Aaron already had an audience. * * * * * Excerpts from "100 Proof Life" by Archibald MacGregor were printed with permission from his estate.
