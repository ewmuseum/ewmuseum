Ember - IG24 - The razor's edge

24 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The razor's edge The cold water lapped around my waist, causing me to shiver, as my body silently protested the frigid assault against my naked flesh. Sitting in the darkness, my upper body held taut and rigid, I ignored the chill just as I ignored the harsh steel of the tub beneath me. Two hours ago the water had been hot, scaldingly so, and I recalled that my traitorous body had protested then – fickle creature that it was – when the nerves in the soles of my feet had first realised that they were immersed in liquid hot enough to turn even skin as alabaster white as my own a fiery shade of red instantly; the ‘joke’ – if there was one – was on my body, though, for wasting its energy in shouting out the shock of the pain to the feet. … it should have waited until I sat down fully and the near boiling water engulfed my testicles. That would have been worth ‘shouting’ about for that was the only moment that even I thought that I would lose control, that I would unclench my jaws and actually scream out, I didn’t, though. I won that battle and my body lost. One of the few battles that I could claim victory in; as hollow as that victory was. An autonomous system that was obviously schizophrenic as one moment it was complaining that it was too hot and the next it was complaining that it was too cold, ludicrously thinking that it had any say in what I would do, what I would put myself through. Does the body make the man or the man make the body, after all? At that moment, however, it didn’t really matter as I realised that I could hardly feel any sensation from the waist down anyway. My body had numbed, either due to the coldness of the water or the fact that I had been sitting there, immobile, for two hours. Perhaps, and most likely, both reasons. A pyrrhic victory, after all, for my body, as it no longer really felt the cold anyway. As my hair hung limp and wet against my back, the last four of five inches floating like a mass of Sargassum seaweed in the Sargasso Sea in the water that surrounded me, I felt the muscles in my right shoulder starting to cramp. It didn’t really surprise me, of course, even though I felt it to be a betrayal – another one – of my body. After all I had been sitting in the same position for a long time; my arm raised in front of me, elbow bent at over forty-five degrees, and hand clenched – oh so tightly – around the straight-edged, surgically-sharp, razor that I held against the tender skin of my throat. Why? The thought echoed around inside my head, alone in the otherwise unbroken silence of my stoic reserve; as if it was spoken by someone else, I ignored it just as I would have ignored a drunken bum on the street who slurred plaintiff cries for money through a broken toothed maw, one that no doubt stank of cheap booze. As inevitable as the tramp following me down the street, no doubt spitting as he spoke, with yesterday’s McDonalds still staining his grimy beard as he tried to persuade that any money given was only for ‘something to eat, honest’ the thought wasn’t going to just give up that easily. Why? My college psychology one-oh-one class was, admittedly, a few years back but even the limited recollection that I had of it was enough to know that the sub-conscious was called just that because it was meant to be subsumed inside my mind and not, most definitely not, vocally present. This voice, however, had obviously failed that class - probably out enjoying itself somewhere - or just didn’t care for what was meant to be and what wasn’t, for the question echoed around my head, as impossible to quell and as all-consuming as an inferno. Without meaning to ... and inadvertantly giving the sub-conscious question the influence and acknowledgement that it 'desired' - I tried to figure out what it meant, how I could answer it. What was it asking me, this rising voice inside my own skull? Was it simply asking me the same questions that I had asked myself time and time again over the past few weeks? Why had I been reduced to this? Once on top of the World and earning more money each and every week than I had even dreamed possible to suddenly be yesterday’s news and reduced to the basic salary ... the agreed, and insultingly low, downside ... I had first been contracted to without the ‘bonuses’ that had been thrown my way when I had been the World Champion – when I had still been the ‘draw’ that they had wanted. A millionaire's lifestyle on a pauper's income. Why had my much-vaunted abilities deserted me? An ego that was only beaten in sheer scale by the confidence that I had in knowing that not only could I talk the talk but that I could – and did – also walk the walk. Inside the ring that so many men called their career I was master of my own fate, King of the domain that some called wrestling, others called sports entertainment and I simply called ‘home’. Whether flying high above the stunned crowd that watched in jaw-dropping amazement or going toe-to-toe with someone on the mat until the opening came to twist their bodies in ways they thought impossible – to give pain that they hoped would never reoccur – until they quit; I could do it all. Then, like cherry blossom blowing in the wind, it all faded away and I was left stumbling where once I flew and fumbling where once I was sure. Why … when once I knew I would win before the first blow had even been struck … was I suddenly losing each and every match that I took part in? It had only taken one year to go from nothing at all within the WWA to the man that everyone wanted to be – and to beat; the World Champion. Just as everyone, rightly, feared to step foot inside the ring with me – despite the fact that most of them towered over me – I always felt supremely confident that I would win. The parable of David and Goliath called out to even my heathen soul and was something that I paralleled each and every time I cut the bigger men down to size. Of course I had one up on David, for I didn't even need to use a sling. In another life I could see myself walking the wild plains lands as a lumberjack – but in my current life I simply took my opponents down at the roots of their pride and cut them deeply with humility. … until the day when I felt the sting of loss myself. Until the day when I found myself looking up at the lights and hearing the cheers of the crowd as some other man walked away with the prize – the title – that I had worked so hard to make my own. Until the day – the ever-increasing days – when I began to lose. Perhaps it could be asking me why I even bothered doing this anymore, why I put myself through the anguish of stepping foot inside the ring that was once my hallowed ground, why I kept on chasing the Holy Grail of accolades that were seemingly too far out of my reach? Like Daedalus before me had I flown too far to the Sun and was now doomed to fall eternally, never again reclaiming the glorious heights that only angels and eagles dared to fly? At least within the WWA I had taken a leave of absence ... though the question could be asked if I had walked or had I been pushed? The answer to that question didn’t really matter, as either way I was ranked as inactive with them and the sigh of relief from the powers that be was audible. They wouldn’t have to wonder what I would do next; they wouldn’t have to prepare their contingency plans for when next I choose my own path instead of following their scripted one. … but still I couldn’t seem to stop the ever-increasing circle of self-abuse. Tournaments called my name and like a sheep following blindly the crook of the shepard I willingly – eagerly – answered their call. I didn’t need to cut my arms in thin slices to salve my wounded soul, I didn’t need to use the soles of my feet as an ashtray to stub out cigarettes, and I didn’t even need to plumb the depths of depravity and submissively bend over and let an overweight slob of a woman whip my back and leave stripes upon my skin. Instead I could put my name forward for things like the Infinite Gauntlet and get my fix of humiliation that way. The name, I knew, was very apt indeed because it seemed that it would never, ever end – the Siren called me and I leapt from the safety of the ship into the cold and dark waters, never knowing – never caring – what lay beneath the surface. … or maybe, just maybe, it was even asking why I even tried at all. Not wrestling – not everything had to be about wrestling after all – but living itself. Why even bother living? WHY? The ‘shout’ inside my head … reminding me that it was still there … was like the tramp suddenly grabbing me by the throat and pulling the folded dollar notes directly from my wallet with a mad gleam in his eye or the subconscious mind suddenly becoming aware and taking over in some twisted parody of Doctor Jekyll and Mister Hyde, leaving me trapped and desperate inside a cage of my own broken mentality. Impossible to ignore in its intensity the shouted question managed to break through my concentration and – my control shattered – my hand twitched. In the great scheme of things it was an almost insignificant movement; earthquakes had shattered mountains, had lifted the oceans and washed away towns, had even buried civilizations. My hand only moved a fraction of an inch but still – for me – it was enough to set a series of events in motion that, domino like, would all fall into place. The tug at my throat happened only slightly before – no more than a split second – I felt the sting of pain there too. I had been dropped on concrete, I had fallen from the top of a metal cage to crash into the unforgiving canvas of the ring more than twenty feet below, I had been driven head first through a table and had even once been hung by my own hair. So pain was nothing new to me, in fact it was as close and familiar as a family member. … but, even so, as my hand twitched the cold steel of the razor bit into my flesh and I winced. My sudden understanding of my own mortality made the scratch ... two centimetres up, and two millimetres deeper and the jugular itself - the wellspring of my life - would have been severed ... feel like the cut of a sword, made the small droplets of blood that ran freely down my neck and onto my shoulders feel like a tsunami. They say that when you die – in the last few seconds before the darkness takes you as its own – your life flashes before your eyes, but that didn’t happen to me. Why would it, I was scratched not dying! In the darkness of my bathroom, in the chillness of the water that surrounded me, I chuckled to myself as the simplicity of the answer to the question came unbidden. With my own blood coating me, outside rather than in, I allowed the muscles of my arm to relax and dropped the razor to the side of the bath, still holding on to it. The lectures of the past came back to me and I recalled something about the simplest answer usually being the right one. If all extraneous information is removed from two similar hypotheses, and all other things being equal, then the easiest answer is – normally – the correct one. So, when I was trying to figure out just what the unconscious musings could be about – could it be about my abilities, could it be about my accolades, could it be about why I was losing so many matches, could it be questioning why I was trying to win the infinite gauntlet again … could it be … could it be … could it be … blah, blah blah? It could, easily, have been about anything of those things, it could easily have been about so many more as well. My life did not resolve around wrestling, no matter what it looked like to those outside and as such I did not live and breathe it twenty four hours a day. I am sure that anyone watching my life – Truman like – from the outside would have been as bored as I would have been had I done nothing but think and talk about wrestling. Thankfully, it seemed, I was much more complicated – screwed up even – than that! Just as I knew what it definitely wasn’t about I suddenly – laughing to myself again – knew what it was about. I knew that the question was not about me ending my existence, I had already been offered that route, given that choice, and I turned it down then just as I always would. I had fought too hard to take this life for myself, had sacrificed so much to make sure that I would not be stillborn like the others, to throw it away with a late night jump, or even a razor’s cold kiss. Lifting the razor up, almost as if the thought had reminded me that it was there, I realised that the answer was the simplest one to the question – what the ‘why’ meant - that there was. Simply, why hadn’t I started shaving yet?! Two hours ago I had filled my tub with hot water and lowered myself into it, the sun long sunken into cloud-filled night, and lay there alone in the darkness, just me and my thoughts. I had raised the razor to my face, confident through practice that I could shave clean and close without the use of a mirror – I had never used one, never would – and then had stopped and forced control over my body – testing it, punching it, to stay still and quiet for as long as I wanted. Just as life was not all about wrestling, training was not all about throwing punches; the competing, whether it was against an opponent or against the protesting muscles of ones own body and the mind that wanted to move was just as important in all areas and control was paramount always. The shaft off moonlight shot through the small window high up in the wall of the bathroom, sneaking in – thief like – through an area of glass that I must have missed when I had painted it black. It hit the edge of the razor and reflected off and – before I could stop myself, before I could regain control – I looked down, my colorless eyes widening. Barely a glance. … but it was enough. The reflection stared back at me, the single eye ..verdantly jade ... that was caught in the polished steel filled with fire and vehemence and pain … not the nick of a sharp edge, not even the crunching of vertebrae as a neck contracted, but all-consuming agony ... ripped through my synapses as my brain filled with white, hot, light and my mouth open in a scream that I never heard. As the darkness took me I realised, too late, that Ockham was not always right - sometimes the simplest answer is wrong ... horribly, horribly wrong. “… what?” Sounds broke through into my consciousness – traffic in the distance, the buzz of voices many floors below and the cacophony of birds that never ceased to circle the building – competed for my attention with the question and it took me a few seconds to realise two things. The first was that I was lying, face-down, on the cold tiles of my bathroom; light from outside in the hallway streaming through the partially open door … hadn’t I closed it before I entered the bath? … informing me, as if the sounds weren’t enough, that it was daylight. The second was that the question, this time, wasn’t internal, it wasn’t as if spoken by someone else entirely, it was a voice that I knew, a voice that I recognised. It was my own. Harsh and croaking, I still recognise that I had spoken the one thought that was prevalent in my mind. What had happened? One moment it was dark, it was night, and the next it was daylight and I was waking up facedown in my bathroom! Starting to get up I suddenly realised a third thing – something that was brought home to me as a wave of pain rushed through my body and I felt a heat, something like sunburn, on my chest. Reaching one hand to the burning sensation I used the other to lever myself off the tiles and then stopped as my fingers traced something that refused to be interpreted by my mind. Ridges … lines ... were felt under my fingertips, like Braille and incredulously I looked down. My gorge rouse and vomit, joining the small pool of blood on the tiles, spewed forth from my stomach as the shock hit me as I saw, lightly and no more than a couple of millimetres deep, but carved none the less – branded almost – was two words that were traced into the skin. I’m back.
