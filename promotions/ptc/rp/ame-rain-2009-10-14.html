Ame ("Rain")

14 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-14 I hate the rain. No matter what, the rain just brought down the mood that much more. Walking in it made me soggy and wet. Driving in it made it difficult to see what's in front of me. Listening to it made me drowsy. Just the veryambiance of the rain made me want to curl up in bed and go to sleep until it was gone. If accompanied by lightning, I would then have to worry about any electronics plugged in and maybe even the occasional freak accidents. So you have to understand that when I woke up this morning to the sound of Mother Nature weeping her little cloud tears on the city of Dallas, I was a bit miffed. But I'd have to persevere it for at least another day. Perseverance, after all, was a trait you always needed as a professional wrestler. Without it, you could never succeed, you'll always give up right after you start the minute you begin falling onto your back. There's no such thing as "do no wrong" in this business. There're going to be times where you'll fail. You'll bottom out, be faced with a situation where the only way you're going to survive would be through your ownperseverance. Mine is a cautionary tale, a story wrought with questionable business decisions, relationships that I wonder how in the hell I developed, and nearly a decade of wrestling. You can think of my career as being like the weather, always changing, with patches of sunshine coming after the patches of rain. I've had bright spots, and I've had spots that I'm less-than-proud of. I've been in the main event of a few pay-per-views, and I've been stuck as the straightman of a group of wackos. Most people are the same, of course, their circumstances can change as quickly as the weather. I don't think there's a weather forecaster on the planet, though, that could accurately predict the weather that hung over my head. My name is Coral Avalon. And this's just one part of my story. I made my residence in Dallas, Texas, although I'm really from New Orleans. That being said, I'm often away from my little apartment because I'm always travelling from place to place. When I began my training, I had a trainer that was more interested in the money I was giving him than he was in actually teaching me anything. So, a guy named Joey Malone took me under his wing. Or at least, that's what happened after he subjected me and fourteen other guys to the harshest, most brutal professional wrestling training on record. Joey had this "survival of the fittest" mentality with how he worked. He built a wrestling ring in his backyard in Mesa, Arizona, and we did our training in the summer. I liked to see the expressions of disbelief on people's faces when I told them about how I was trained. Just in the first day of training, we had half of our group quit. They all just said "I can't take it any more" and didn't come back the next day. Another few quit within a week, saying the same thing. By the end of about three months of training, I was the only one of two guys left. We wanted to be wrestlers, and nothing that Joey Malone could say or do to us could change that. We persevered, you see. It was a downpour on a October afternoon, and I was preparing for what would be my last date in the Squared Circle. The venue was just a small convention center on the outskirts of the city. I had kept on wrestling for the company even after I took up a silly mask and began working for Sin City Championship Wrestling. Unfortunately, they had nothing left for me after all these years, and although Sin City had allowed me to keep taking outside bookings, I realized that I had nothing left to prove any more. At least, not in the Squared Circle. I walked into the locker room, surrounded by people I shared the ring with. Many stood up and gathered around me, giving me handshakes and hugs and wishing me well. Yet, there was one person sitting in the corner who didn't approach me. Unfortunately for me, I felt compelled to approach this solitary soul. Her name was Inoue Doi. She was the single most unpleasant existence in my life, an violent Japanese girl that either liked me or hated me, and I was never sure which it was. I have no idea what possessed me to walk over to her. If I'd been smart, I'd have just ignored her. Just gone along with my day and got to talking with the man responsible for ultimately ending the career of Coral Avalon in the Squared Circle in tonight's "Loser Leaves Town" match. On the other hand, she did look like she wanted to be talked to. The chivalrous side of me willed the rest of me towards her, "Hey,Inoue." "Bakayarou." she said. It's a harsh Japanese word, which effectively meant "dumbass". She always used that word when addressing me, rather than my name. It actually bugged me a little bit. On my end, I referred to her as a friend at times when the Asgardian walls of my sanity start to show cracks in it. Inoue Doi didn't speak English, but she understood it. Conversely, I didn't speak Japanese, but I understood it. Well, most of it. I'm still working out some of the little details. I'd been acquainted with this woman since the end of 2003, during the dying days of Action! Wrestling, the promotion where I had first made my name. Back then, she was seventeen years old and already a frickin' prodigy. Her manners were atrocious, she was incredibly blunt and rude to anyone she didn't simply kick the crap out of, and since I'm the first guy that's ever beaten her in a match, she had this particularly special amount of loathing reserved just for me. If Inoue wasn't a close friend of my fiancee, Annie, she probably wouldn't even talk to me with anything more than a swift kick to something vital. "Nice to see you, too." I said in my usual manner. I sat down next to her. Inoue wasn't wrestling, tonight, so she was wearing casual clothes. She wore this purple hoodie with that damn "Hello Kitty" thing right on the front and a pair of black pants and ankle boots. The hoodie rose questions in my mind as well as my eyebrows, but I wasn't going to start asking. She'd just hit me. "Why do I sense deja vu?" I asked. I was in a situation like this ten minutes before a match that Inoue and I wrestled in Japan two months ago. I was leaving the Japanese tour and she confronted me about it. This time, she was a bit calmer. "It's just your imagination, idiot." By Inoue's standards, anyway. Inoue looked away from me, as though she wanted to pay attention to someone other than me. I tried not to say anything, though the awkward tension between the two of us could reasonably be cut with a knife. Despite the chatter among the other wrestlers in the locker room, I almost couldn't hear them over the battering drums of rainfall over our heads. After a few seconds,Inoue spoke up again, "What do you want?" "Who, me?" I asked. "Most of this locker room of idiots can't understand me, so of course you." Inoue said. One thing I had to give credit for with Inoue... she didn't exactly know how to soften her blows, either with her feet or with her words. I stared at Inoue for a couple of seconds, not sure how to respond to her question at first. My mind's a jumble of words, kind of like a big crossword puzzle. "You just seemed like you wanted to be talked to." I told her. She snapped back at me, and I swore that every head in the locker room turned towards me and Inoue as she spoke, "Well, I didn't. Especially not by you. Bakayarou." You know, I survived Joey Malone's training. I'll be the first to admit that it sucked. Yet, I sometimes think that I'd rather spend another three months in that rickety, rundown old ring underneath a summer Arizona sun than try to casually talk toInoue Doi. Even the rain seemed tolerable in comparison. Deciding that I wanted no more part of Inoue's abuse, I stood up and started to walk away. Being the highest-paid star in the Squared Circle, even if it was only for one more night, gave me the right to not take this kind of abuse. At least, the kind of abuse that I didn't inflict upon myself by being a professional wrestler, anyway. The rain got louder, and I didn't think that was possible. I almost didn't hearInoue from behind me because of it. "Matte." Or "wait", if I were to hear this in in English. I turned to see Inoue standing up, her hands on her hips, glaring at me. She had one of those stares that either conveyed seriousness or a wish to fire laser beams out of the eyes to incinerate whoever she hated most. Every time she stared at me, I thanked whatever deity I needed to thank thatInoue couldn't actually shoot eye beams. Inoue didn't say anything. She just grabbed me by the wrist and started to tug me away. I couldn't very well just pull my arm away since everyone was staring at us, andInoue had this talent for beating the hell out of you if you didn't do precisely what she wanted you to do. At the same time, though, I hated being pulled along by a girl who was half my size, too. Oh well, I've endured worse humiliations in my life than this. I letInoue drag me out of the locker room and into the hallway. Maybe it's just a coincidence, but the hallway was virtually devoid of human life outside of ourselves. Somehow, this felt just as intimidating as being in a locker room filled with my peers. Inoue didn't say anything at first, but I couldn't really see her face because of the hood of her shirt. She looked down at the floor instead of up at me. I'd knownInoue long enough to know that she was always straightforward about whatever she wanted to say. The fact that she could struggle to say something... well, let's just say that it never happened for as long as I'd known her. Ever. "You." she said to me. Perhaps I wasn't taking in the atmosphere too well, but I had a quip at the ready, "Inoue, it's been six years, I think you should know the truth... my name is 'Coral', not 'you'." Either they didn't have sarcasm in Japan, or Inoue was ignoring me, because she continued onward. "I'm in love." ...Wait, what? I tried to process what she was saying despite the Japanese, but the scenario before me felt like a confession to me. And, well... thanks to my fiancee, I've seen more than my fair share of scenarios like this. "Uh, what?" I asked. Rational thought was foreign to me at this juncture. Talking to girls did this to me all the time, come to think of it, so this's looking more and more like business as usual for me, "Inoue, you know that I'm about to be married, right?" "Not you, bakayarou." she said. I'd never known the sound of every brain cell I had simultaneously breathing a sigh of relief up until now. It's a soothing noise, I'll give it that. "Oh. Well. So, why are you telling me, then? You tell Annie about this stuff, not me. I'm just the guy you beat up every once in a while." "Shut up, bakayarou." she said, just a bit more irritably than before. The rain made me feel a little drowsy, but maybe it's just the fact that I didn't really want to stand here and talk to Inoue, "Okay. I'll bite. If this isn't about me, then... who's the unfortunate sap that you've got your sights set on?" "Who the hell do you think? It's that Sheffield idiot." she told me, crossing her arms and looking away from me as she said it, as if that was supposed to make her point. Did I die and wake up as someone else? I could have sworn that she was willing to kill people for even mentioning the name Christopher Declan Sheffield, the man known to the wrestling world as "Alias". Inoue never told me much about her relationship with him, but from the pieces I could gather, she had a thing for him five years ago. WhenInoue was technically still underaged. I guess she took offense that Chris wouldn't do anything with her out of concerns of... you know. Public decency and the need to not be served with a statutory rape charge. I heard those tend to ruin people's days. Either way, Inoue took the rejection pretty hard. Now, she spoke of him again. It's a little disconcerting. "You threatened to rip out my heart if I spoke his name again, you know." I said. "Yes." Inoue said, simply. The scary thing was that Inoue usually kept her promises. To the letter, in fact. The one thing I hated more than anything when talking to Inoue were the awkward silences we'd have. It was either because of our language barrier or the way Inoue acted around me, and to be honest, it bugged me that I wasn't sure which one it was. Finally, Inoue broke the silence, "I want you to talk to him." "What, me? Are you serious?" I asked, almost rhetorically. That woman's stare was all it took to realize how serious she was, "Why me?" "You know him, clearly! You're both emotional pieces of stupidity, clearly, you mingle amongst each other. You have... mating calls. Or whatever."Inoue said. After I just stared at her for a few seconds, slack-jawed from how strange the statement sounded coming from Inoue, I merely shook my head at her. "Okay, first of all... NO, we do not have mating calls. Second of all, I haven't spoken to Sheffield in months. You want me to just call him up and say 'Hey, Chris, I know this girl who has a crush on you and she can crack a wooden baseball bat with her feet'?" "Shut up, bakayarou." she said, again, managing to make something that looked like a pout. At least, it was a pout from a girl whom I thought wasn't capable of such a thing. I guess it's true that you learned something new every day, "You are supposed to help me, not make fun of me." "Sorry, sorry." I apologized, sighing as I did it. How did this girl manage to make me look like the bad guy, anyway? "So, what do you want me to do?" "Tell him to call me. I... I really want to talk to him again." Hesitation in Inoue's words? Well, damn, we're just running the gauntlet of things I've never seen her do. "Why don't you call him? I'm sure I've got his number somewhere." "No, no no!" Inoue waved her hands at me like she was trying to put out a fire, "I... I can't talk to him over the phone! This has to be face-to-face." Now that raised all kinds of different questions in my mind, but I practically blurted out the most glaring question, "You're not going to kill him, are you?" "No!" she quickly denied. In English, no less. I shook my head and looked down for a second, before I looked Inoue in the eye, "Alright, Inoue. I'll get him to talk to you." Something unexpected happened when I told her this. She lunged forward and gave me a big hug, and I heard her say a very excited "Arigatou!" when she did it. It caught me so off-guard that I nearly lost my balance and came tumbling backwards with Inoue on top of me. I briefly wished someone had been around to take a picture of the scene, because I swear to God this was never going to happen again. After the hug,Inoue let go of me and seemed genuinely happy. Usually, when I saw her smile, it was this mischievous "I just kicked the crap out of you" sort of smile. I'd seen that particular smile enough times to know when it was different. Inoue backed away, and nodded towards me, "Thanks, Coral. I mean it." I watched her walk away, more surprised that she called me by my name than anything else. I almost forgot that it was raining outside. I almost forgot that I had a match to prepare for. I almost forgot that I already had more troubles than I needed without having to cater to Inoue Doi's. Sometimes, I think I'm just too nice for my own good.
