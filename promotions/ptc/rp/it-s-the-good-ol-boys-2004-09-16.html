It's the Good Ol' Boys...

16 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-16 The camera cuts to a picturesque two-story red brick house, located beneath a beautiful arbor canopy of oak that serves to shade it from the harsh midday sun and flanked also by a number of pines. On the driveway that wraps around the left side of the house sit two utility vehicles—a weathered black Jeep Wrangler and a seemingly brand new garnet Ford Expedition. The automobiles are being fervently washed by a group of young men, buzzing here and there with undivided attention to every detail. The house itself is brick, and the white trim work stands in stark contrast to the deep red of the structure itself. The lawn is very well kept, with nary a single blade of grass overlapping into the driveway or street. Moving in closer to the house, we see someone sitting on what appears to be a black leather recliner on the right side of the front porch. With a white railing bordering its perimeter and partially obstructed our view, the porch spans the entire width of the house, breaking only to allow access to the two front brick steps. On the aforementioned black leather recliners sits a truly imposing man. Beside him sits a keg, with the Greek letters Delta Upsilon Iota emblazoned on the front in black lettering and a polished chrome tap with the image of a skull and crossbones emblazoned on both sides. The behemoth has shaggy dark brown hair that falls down across his forehead almost to his eyebrows and is currently refilling his black 64 oz. Black Carolina Panthers cup with fresh beer from the keg. Although his exact measurements are difficult to approximate with his being seated, he appears to be somewhat constrained by the recliner, thought it is quite oversized itself. His white wife beater holds taut across his massive frame, while his cuffed khaki shorts reveal a portion of his gigantic quads and all of his massive calves. Reaching down to sweep a couple daring ladybugs from his brown boat shoes, the man takes another sip from his mug before speaking. “Maybe I shouldn’t a’ done that. Momma used to tell me how ladybugs bring good luck, and with this big ol’ tournament, I need all the luck I can get.” He takes another swig from the cup before continuing. “So let’s talk turkey people. Ya’ll didn’t come out to hear me talk about ladybugs or momma or even beer…...but it would be kinda fun to talk suds for awhile. Anyway, GTT4, god damn this is gonna be big people. We got samurais, knights, doctors, the whole nine yards. To be honest we’re about two hound dogs short of huntin’ season. Now I could talk the ear of a hedgehog, but I’m gonna try to get to the point.” But before doing that, he gulps down a healthy amount of beer. “Holy smokes, where did my manners go? Well I’m Henry Cobb, but most people just call me Hank. My brothers throw a ‘the Tank’ on the end in honor of the silver screen’s finest—Old School—but we really ain’t friendly enough to be starting that just yet. You may have seen me around the LOW, my boy Korver and I just won the tag team straps over there a few weeks back, and we have a big title defense coming up so I got that to worry about too. As you can see, a got a lot of stuff coming at me, thank god big ol’ Ivan headed elsewhere, or I might a’ gotten swept up, up and away for good. So I got to thinking, how the hell am I gonna pull this off? I’ve just been here a couple weeks. I’m up against an accomplished veteran from the Far East. What if he starts in with that kung-fu stuff? Man, this has really got me sweating bullets. I mean, I gotta stay on him. I know I should stay humble, but if I can just lean on him, I know I can break him. I’ve blocked some of the best athletes in America, big as shit too, so I shouldn’t let this little guy get to me. But sometimes, ya know, those thoughts come creepin’ around.” Cobb pauses yet again to finish off his brew, then continues with a much more focused tone and demeanor. “Well I ain’t gonna let him come around here no more. I’m taking this boy out, plain and simple. And you know I ain’t gonna back down from nobody. So bring it buddy, and we’ll see who the better man is. Cause when I hit you with the Deacon Driver, there ain’t gonna be nothin’ left for you to do but wait ‘til next year.”
