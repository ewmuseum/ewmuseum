Deadly Sins: Exiting Eden

2 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-10-02 .:: ::: ::. Sequitur pars secunda .:: ::: ::. On the road Somewhere in between Toronto and Las Vegas The early morning sun was beginning to spill over the Nevada landscape. The outline of Shadow’s slim panther-like form was ringed with a golden halo; her sleepless jade eyes narrowed as she pressed on. She raced the sun across the horizon in an endless battle to keep the day from dawning on her without her learning the knowledge that she sought. The bike had been humming under her for what seemed like an eternity now, steady and constant, driving her forward into the unknown—or really, this week, it was just driving her towards Las Vegas again. The path was familiar, but the way the journey had been made was what mattered: without any responsibilities or other personalities intruding on her thoughts. Of course, if she’d been trying to get away from everyone, she hadn’t exactly done it in the best way possible. The bike, the helmet… well, they were constant reminders of Dawn. And as far as Virgil went, she needed no physical reminder of him, because there was always that tenuous, tense, knotted feeling in her chest that grew with the distance between them. She wondered what they were doing. She wondered if they were okay. She knew that there was no reason to sit and worry about it, that worrying about it was only keeping her from finding out what was wrong with herself. She’d come to the conclusion while she was driving that one week wasn’t going to do it. She needed more time to figure out what was wrong with her—it went much deeper than Emoticon trying to set Virgil on fire. It stretched all the way back to WMW, following Corvus there on false pretences, believing she’d had a life with him, a daughter with him… all lies. Believing that she’d been an Assassin, forgetting all the disciplines that she had been painfully taught over her undead years… all lies. She couldn’t remember her past. She couldn’t make up her mind who to care about. She could hardly function without putting herself in situations where she’d end up betrayed, either by herself or by someone else. The bike shuddered under her slightly. The wind seemed slowed, was not pushing her backwards off the seat anymore. That, of course, was when she looked down at the fuel gauge, and saw it lapping lightly at the little red E that signified that she was in a shitload of trouble. She guided the bike over to the side of the road and narrowed her eyes, grimacing. “Fuck.” It was the most eloquent expression of irritation that she could come up with at this point, after five days of not having spoken to anyone, and five days of not getting anywhere at all with herself. Resigning herself to a long walk, she slid the helmet off and balanced it carefully on the seat. Her platform boots pressed into the gravel. Their resounding crunch was the only echoing sound in the gradually creeping morning aside from the arid breeze howling around the corners of the mesas and rifling its fingers rudely through her long hair. .:: ::: ::. Ding-ding! The tinkly little bell sounded rather pathetic, as though it would have been no more than a whisper had you taken it outside and rung it in the ears of the sprawling flat desert highway. Eddy Cook’s eyes flickered up, and then practically popped out of his head. The woman who had entered was like no woman he’d ever seen before—paler than he thought skin could be, with a crop of metal studs littering her face, dressed all in skin-hugging shiny black, with an accenting ring of black around her battle-hardened eyes. He flinched, standing behind the counter. “Uh… Can I help ya ma’am?” She looked up at him, and her eyes carved slices into his soul, he felt. Something inside him begged him to run right now, but he told himself that was silly. She narrowed her gaze, and it was mercilessly unwavering. “I’ve got a bike out there that’s out of gas. Your pumps are locked. You want to help me out?” There was something that he didn’t like about her. He told himself that it was her tone, as though he owed her something. He shook his head. “Yes ma’am, we’ve had to lock it up to make sure that people come in and pay after they get their gas. Pumps are still old-fashioned out here, an’ with gas prices bein’ what they are, people have just started fillin’ up and boltin’ from the pump so they don’t have to pay.” He had expected a sympathetic shake of the head. But she only hmph-ed absently, and kept staring at her fingernails. “So are you going to help me out or not?” Eddy Cook felt again the urge to revile this woman. He swallowed it, with difficulty and held his hands up in a gesture of innocence. “I’m sorry, ma’am, I can’t open up the pumps until my uncle gets here. One of us’s gotta watch the cash register while the other goes outside to the pumps.” She looked at him for a moment, great aggravation washing over her fine, eerie features. “You have to be kidding me. I just walked about three miles wheeling that fucking thing, and you’re going to make me stand here and wait?” Her palms slammed down on the counter a little louder than Eddy Cook felt necessary. He might have bent the rules for her if he liked her a little better. As it was, she was lucky he didn’t toss her out on the side of the road and tell her to wheel that little bike ten more miles down to John Thurston’s station. “I’m sorry ma’am, there’s nothin’ I can do.” The little clock on the desk beeped insistently as the hour rolled over to six. She glanced at it for a moment, then up at him, like she could have torn his throat out without a second thought. Eddy remembered the shotgun that he had behind the counter, and felt around for its familiar muzzle, just in case. “Fine,” she snapped. She flounced out the door with an angered heaviness in her step. As it swung closed, he heard her farewell to him. “Rotten bastard.” She stamped across the poured concrete slab. Eddy Cook watched her take a seat on her bike and fold her arms over her chest as she waited for the proprietor of Cook’s Petroleum to arrive so she could fill up her tank. Eddy hoped his uncle arrived soon. Los Altos had no place for people like her. .:: ::: ::. On the road Somewhere in between Toronto and Las Vegas The midday sun was burning into the parts of her skin that were exposed—really only her hands as she gripped the handlebars of the bike tightly. She’d been flying at ungodly speeds ever since she got out of that little shithole with a full tank of gas, and she didn’t intend to stop until she got into Las Vegas, dammit. Of course, Lia had a tendency to not think about how hot it got in this part of the United States. Being from Europe, weather in America had never particularly concerned her—even more so because until she’d drank Soma’s blood, she’d never experienced the hottest parts of the day, because she hadn’t been able to wake until the sun went down. She was parched. She was absolutely starved for some way to cool down, and on top of that, she was sweating out most of her blood supply. Her face was veiled with a light swath of pink, occasionally forming droplets at the end of her nose. She could taste the salt mixed with copper when an errant drop slid onto her lips. She swallowed, feeling the unfamiliar and unpleasant burn of thirst in her throat. She needed something to quench it—blood. Or, if none could be had at the moment, water. That was another surprising consequence of drinking Soma’s blood—food still made her ill even to smell, and other beverages the same, but she could drink water. It would have the effect of patching a hole in a dam with a piece of tape, but it would have to do. She’d ridden far enough from Los Altos that she’d hit suburbs. She was about two hours’ drive from Vegas, but she’d put this off long enough—she was to the point now where if she didn’t put a stopgap measure in place, she was going to lose consciousness. And she could only imagine what was going to happen if she showed up neither at Evolution nor Super Series. Desperate thoughts racing through her head, she turned down a little side-road that looked like a shortcut, headed with a painted sign reading Las Colinas Verdes Road. Looking at the sparse scrub grass, she managed a roll of the eyes. These people weren’t optimists, they were just plain lying to themselves if they thought they were living in some lush oasis. A mile or so down the road, she was beginning to doubt the truth of the “short” in this shortcut. Up ahead, the wavering illusion of heat evaporating from the roads obscured slightly her vision of the car broken down on the side of the road. A young father threw his hands up frustratedly to a young mother who was balancing their young daughter on her hip. Lia’s brow furrowed, and she gently pulled the bike to the side of the road. She wasn’t sure exactly what made her feel like she could help—certainly, she was no genius with fixing cars, but something about their helplessness called to her. The engine of the bike slowly purred into silence, and she pulled the helmet off. She was slightly dizzy, and stumbled with her first step, but made her way over to them nonetheless. She took a deep breath, and her voice came out husky and dry. “You look like you could use a little help…” Their eyes snapped up at the same time to stare at her, and give her a critical once-over. She resisted the urge to shudder, feeling in that glance the appraisal of witch-hunters. The female’s lips pressed together in a narrow-lipped pout. Chillbumps washed over Shadow in a quick wave. “Uh… just a little car trouble. You any good with cars?” The father was speaking to her—the mother refused to meet her gaze. The little girl sucked on her thumb and looked at Shadow with deep pebble blue eyes. They reminded her of some distant little girl past, but it could not have been Angela, because Angela had green eyes… She snapped out of it, switching her eyes from the hypnotizing gaze of the child to the father. “I’m as good as I need to be to keep this thing in shape.” Here, she jerked her thumb backwards in the direction of the bike. A lie, of course, but spoken with the veracity of the truth. The young father looked at her with eyes squinted through sweat dripping down his forehead. She self-consciously wiped her own blood-streaked forehead with the back of her hand. “Guess it couldn’t hurt if you had a look at it,” he said, gesturing for her to come around the front of the car, “Just a little battery trouble, not sure if much can be done for it without somebody finding some jumper cables.” The two of them peered into the car’s splayed innards. They were a maze to Shadow, but at the very least, she knew where to find the battery—and she also knew that she could more than likely get it running again, but she couldn’t afford to have anyone looking at what came from her fingertips when she did it. “Hmmm,” she said, her brow furrowing, “I think I see the trouble. Go around to the front of the car and see if you can’t get it started when I tap on the hood and give you the signal.” The young man grunted his assent and slid into the driver’s seat. Even as drained as she was, she closed her eyes, and pressed her remaining energy towards her fingertips. A tiny spark passed into the air and into the battery, and she tapped on the hood. She heard the young man turn the crank, and the engine roared to life. She peered around the side of the hood long enough to see the shocked expression on his face, before there was an irritated noise. “Pete!” The young man slipped out of the car and went to his wife, as though she had bid him come on a most important journey. Their conversation was heated, but low enough that even she could not hear it over the spinning engine. She stared down into the car for a moment, and dizziness covered her perceptions. She gripped the edge of the car for balance. “Um…” The young man Pete’s voice intruded upon her. She shook her head to try and clear the fog that was her blood rushing inside her veins. “Look, I dunno how I can thank you for doing that. I dunno how you did it either.” “Just a couple of loose cables that I jiggled back into place,” she murmured, coming up with an answer out of reflex. He looked at her like he didn’t believe her. “Pete!” his wife hissed. The young man started, and shot a glance at his wife. He looked back at Shadow apologetically, but at the same time shifting from foot to foot, like a deer about to bolt from a staredown with a mountain lion. “Sorry, um… my wife says she doesn’t want you around our kid. Says she uh… she doesn’t—“ Shadow held up a hand. “Nothing I haven’t heard before,” she said half-weakly, swallowing halfway through the sentence. He shook his head. “I’m sorry, you… you really did us a favor fixin’ the car and everything, but I’m not in a mood to listen to her bitch, you know?” He thrust a bottle of cold water in her hands. “Here, take this. You look pretty pale.” She nearly dropped it stumbling back to the bike. The wife turned her nose up at Shadow when the two of them passed one another, and the pebble blue eyes of the child looked at her, slightly unfocused. The car roared off, leaving her inexorably alone, and wondering what she could have done to make them hate her so much. Greedily, she uncapped the bottle of water and poured it down her throat, discarding the empty vessel on the ground when she was done. Her body gratefully lapped up the tiny bit of energy that it gave her, but she felt, for some indescribable reason, that she was unclean, that she had sinned. Reflexively, she crossed herself. .:: ::: ::. The Strip Las Vegas, Nevada Sometime after nightfall She’d gotten to Las Vegas in the foulest humor possible. The bike was driving her bananas, her skin was cold-coated with sweat, she was tired, hungry, and pissed off at the world. It was one of those nights when people seemed to sense the darkness radiating off of her, and kept their distance. The look on her face was sour. The look in her eyes was that of a desperate, hungry animal, casting to and fro for its next meal. And in some sense, that’s exactly what she was—but it was not just blood, a rather base meal, that she hungered for. She hungered for a little retribution, a little payback on the human race for treating her like a pariah no matter how she tried to help them. She was just a freak to them, wasn’t she? Just somebody who didn’t deserve any consideration because she was intrinsically different. They could smell it on her like the dogs that they were, bloodhounds that scented out those who were attempting to be something they were not. The thought chafed at her. What had she done to them? She’d tried as hard as she damned well could to take care of Dawn McFarlane and make sure she didn’t kill herself with her recklessness. She’d kept Alexis Key alive when she could have just as easily killed her—Alexis, after all, had been hunting her. Trying to kill her. Alexis would have deserved death—she was hunting Shadow and Virgil without knowing why. At least Shadow knew why she killed. She’d tried to let Emoticon in, too. He’d needed a friend, and she’d let him into her private life. He’d turned on her like a rabid dog. They hated her for what she was, once they discovered that she was not smoke and mirrors, but a flesh and blood animated corpse. Perhaps they hated her because one day, they would turn into what she was, only they would not have the chance to live again. Her shoulder thudded dully into another body. “Watch it, grim reaper!” sneered a cleanly dressed male in a suit, on his way into one of Las Vegas’ many casinos. She bared her teeth at him, in a gesture of challenge, but he had already turned away, sucked into the digital addiction that so many of these children of Adam fell prey to. She was rejected because she was not a child of Adam, but a child of Cain. She was rejected by the children of Cain as well—Corvus reminded her of that any time she needed evidence that she didn’t fit in with vampiric society such as it was. She was no longer the same as the race of beings that referred to themselves as “the Kindred”—not after drinking Soma’s blood. Now she stood apart from even her brethren. A scream split through her musing. Her alert senses detected not the human danger, but the potentiality—she was, after all, hunting. She slipped down a side alley, and found what she would have expected to find in the seedy area of town: a hood trying to rip off a twenty-something young woman who’d managed to wander off The Strip. He was bigger than her—he held her pinioned to the wall. She was too close to frenzy to resist a scene. Her eyes flamed with fury and she threw herself in between the warring parties. “Back off,” she hissed. Her fingernails were curling into claws. “What are you gonna do about it, broad?” he said, a wide grin sweeping across his face. She didn’t wait for any more invitation than that, leaping upon him with her fangs bared. She drank deep. It wasn’t the best decision in the world, but she got what she wanted—a meal. He staggered off into the darkness, cursing and half-crying in spluttering terror. In the morning, he would wonder how much meth he had shoveled into his half-senseless body. A full two or three minutes later, she came back to her senses. She rushed to her victim without thought to what she looked like, skin dug under her fingernails, and blood running down her chin, with that wild look in her eyes. She took the young woman by the shoulders. “Are you okay?” she asked. The deafening shriek brought her back into reality with one sound, like a lighthouse through the fog. “Get the fuck away from me!” The young woman shoved Shadow brutally against the wall with surprising strength. Her spine slammed into it, knocking what breath she’d been holding in past her lips in a rush. Before she could apologize, she looked up into the dim, blearing details of the alleyway to find that the woman she had just saved had made herself quite scarce. She was alone, and all she could see was the spires of the cathedral stabbing whitely into the darkness from some distance away. She groaned under her breath, more from the emotional distress than anything else. She’d done something wrong again. Her shaking hands drew away from her face stained with blood, the blood of that woman’s attacker. She could feel it even now coursing through her body, invigorating her with a stinging rush. It was the reality of her existence. Somehow, she was to blame for all this, in their eyes. She was to blame for what she was, which was nothing short of an inhuman monster. A bell tolled above her in the Catholic church. It tolled the tocsin, heavily and with finality. In her eyes reflected the fires of the torches that destroyed her master’s castle Vysehrad. The fires that immolated Virgil’s mansion in Napa Valley. The fire Emoticon had used in an attempt to purge Virgil from their match. It was all around her. The flames licked at her no matter where she turned. She had a choice, now: she could leave their world willingly, or they would rout her out by consuming her whole. .:: ::: ::.
