Dreams-A Back Alley Brawler Noir

11 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(Note: the daughter of the Back Alley Brawler was given incorrectly in my last RP. Her name is Carmen, I hope this clears up that error for those looking for continuity, my apologies. Enjoy the RP) “We’re now in the 46th minute of this World title contest and Ric Flair has that headlock locked in tight on Ricky Steamboat…..” A father can appreciate these moments with his child. As much as The Back Alley Brawler ate, slept, and breathed the art of professional wrestling…his true passion was nestled in the utmost comfort against his chest, watching on in wonder of the definition of “wrestling classic”, Steamboat/Flair for the NWA Title. As much as he admired the titans clashing on the modest television before him in a battle he had seen at least a million times, it was hard not to be mesmerized by the greatest thing Joe Balboa had ever offered the world. Carmen Erica Balboa. His daughter. Her frail 8-year old arms wrapped around the sturdy oak that was the Brawler, watching on as Steamboat proceeded to make his comeback against the ever-cunning Ric Flair; the two sitting on the floor of a typical 8 year-old girls room. Usually a hardened man, the Brawler would never dare to rebuke a loving hug from his daughter, gently curling a strand of her auburn hair with loving care. Truth be told, he always wanted a boy. A daughter could never carry on the legacy of wrestling for “dear ol’ dad”. However, it was hard to argue with the outcome when he finally held her in his arms, his monstrous forearms able to eclipse her those 8 years ago. A lot of things had changed when Karen and Joe divorced. The Brawler became a more callous person then many thought possible. His drinking and smoking reached an all-time high. His ring work became stiffer then a necrophiliac in a deserted morgue. He married four and divorced more times, to four women he could have cared less about when it came down to it. However, the constants always remained. The child support checks never faltered. He had yet to miss a bi-weekly visit since the court mandated it. And Carmen always remained the #1 woman in his heart, with her mother being a solid #2. Oh, and the Virgin Mary too...but that goes without saying. “Daddy?” She titled her head back so it rested against his broad chest, Joe looking into her cinnamon stick eyes and losing himself in the innocence within. Whatever the question, he was ready to answer it for her with not even a hint of lying. “Yes, Carmen?” “Why don’t they stop fighting when they get all bloody and stuff? Like when me and Jamie play, if we get a boo-boo, we always stop to tell a mommy, then she puts a Band-Aid on it and sometimes we get a Rocket Pop! How come they don’t tell a mommy?” 8 years old and already has better sense then her father. Carmen never ceased to amaze the Bronx Barbarian. He leaned his head down, kissing her on her smooth forehead. A giggle escaped her when his stubble brushed against her, drawing another smile from her paternal figure. “Dey cain’t stop hunny. See, da people don’t wanna see dem’ stop and da winner gets all da goodies.” “Like what?” “Money. Respect. Title belts.” “Oooooooooooooooo, okay. But do they at least Band-Aid each others boo-boos after?” “I’d like to say “yes”, hunny-bunny. Not always tho. Some boo-boos needs mo’ den a Band-Aid, baby. Like dis one hea.” The Brawler pulled up the sleeve on his navy blue Dickies worksuit, rolling up the sleeve until it made a stop at his elbow. He placed a stubby index on the inner part of his arm, revealing a gash where skin never healed back to its ebony glory. Instead, the scar was Sahara sand golden, the edges of the gash jagged and erratic. He looked on as Carmen traced the outline of the scar with her index, her gentle caress being the first to ever touch that particular scar. “I got dat in Japan. Mah opponent stabbed me wit a fork right there and gave me a boo-boo I’d neva forget, hun.” “Why would you let someone stab you with a fork, daddy? Besides, if Mommy won’t let me pick my nose with my fork, nobody gets to have fun with their forks.” She folded her arms in a haughty fashion, the Brawler soaking up the personality that was his little Carmen. She seemed to be the only one he could talk to without feeling like a complete idiot. For once, he could be the genius of the conversation and have all the right answers for a change. He knew that wouldn’t last when she hit 10, maybe 11, but he'd seize these moments while he could. Holding her tightly to him as Steamboat delivered a VICIOUS chop to the reddened chest of Flair, the Brawler explained himself. “Ya mommy is a smart woman, let me tell ya. As for WHY he stabbed me wit da fork, well…it had to happen. We had to give da fans dey money’s worth. It’s da only way I can keep da checks comin hea so you can have those nice lil shoes yous wearin.” “AND MY BRATZ DOLLS?!” “Yes…even ya Bratz dolls. Daddy hurts, so you don’t have to.” She pressed her lips to his wound, placing her head against it as she closed her eyes. Watching her clench her eyes so tightly, Joe remembered the days when he was younger and dreamed of facing Terry Funk in the main event of an NWA super-show. Hell, he remembered when he had dreams period, those same dreams scattered to the wind with no hope of ever being reclaimed. Whatever it was Carmen dreamed of, he would clasp his hands together in whatever locker room or dive he inhabited that night and pray she would receive them ten-fold. “I miss you daddy, I wish you were around all the time so I could hold you just like this.” Soon, hope started to creep into her voice. A smile developed as she kept relating her fantasy to her father, the right hand usually used for pounding opponents into submission now a gentle comfort to his daughter. “That’s why you’re here right? You wanna be home again! You wanna be with mommy and me all the time! WE’RE GONNA BE A FAMILY! I don’t have to tell the kids at school that my dad won’t be there for the school picnics and stuff! Tell me you’re comin’ home, dad. Please? Is that why you’re here?” God she was smart, she knew it had not been two weeks since the last visit. He was hoping she hadn’t been counting, but she was, why wouldn’t she? She loved her dad. Better then other fathers could say about their kids in these situations. She must have been thinking about this for days since he called to tell her he would be by. All the nights she slept under her Tinkerbell sheets, drifting into Slumberland with a smile on her face with total faith in her family being whole again. How do you destroy the dreams of a child? There is no right way. There was no easy way to relieve the burden saddled on his back, but he had no other choice if he loved his daughter. Joe would have to break her heart. He moved his mouth to form words, fighting back tears and the inability to speak knowing fully this task needed to be done. “Carmen…d….dadd” As Joe looked up, the poster plastered on Carmen’s door of Scooby-Doo running from The Creeper was replaced by an open doorway…. ….With the Brawler’s only love standing in the frame now. There she stood, home from work earlier than expected with her arms crossed and blue eyes fixated on the pair that was Joe and Carmen. It was the same look she had given him since the divorce. He knew Karen still loved him, but her anger towards Joe always kept the Bronx Barbarian at a distance, always in fear that letting in Joe would cause her another round of pain. While the Brawler let aging chop him down more than once, Karen was like a fine wine aged in Queens, New York. The years had never been kinder to her, still as petite and voluptuous in all the places you need to be. Any good that came for Carmen in the looks department as far as DNA went, Karen would be reason for that with no question. “Hey you two. Carmen, I hate to take your daddy away, but mommy needs to talk to him for a little if you don’t mind?” Carmen politely stood up with a smile, having no objections to her mother borrowing Joe. Pushing himself up, the Brawler planted a kiss on Carmen’s cheek before reaching his feet with a grunt. A love tap followed from his daughter with Joe smiling back affectionately. “Hurry back daddy, we’re gonna play dollies in a minute!” “Yea….dollies….riiiiight. I cain’t wait.” Carmen proceeded to dive for her bed, the tower that was her father making an exit from the room with her mother closing the door behind him. Holding her pillow tight, she let her dreams run rampant, hoping that her prayers for a real family would be answered when Joe returned to play dollies. In The Kitchen…. “I can’t believe this shit, Joe. You come all the way over here, completely outside your scheduled visit might I add, to tell me this?! What kind of man are you?” She definitely did not take the news the way he had hoped. He expected “mad”. Definitely not “vengeful Lord and Savior” mad. Joe was leaning against the counter table, the buffer between himself and his ex-wife at the moment as she held her head in her hands, a complete bundle of anger and sorrow. The kitchen was crowded already with a relic of a refrigerator, gas stove, and other little knik-knaks Karen was so fond of collecting. Both parties prayed for more room so they wouldn’t be forced to be so close to each other, Joe’s announcement reminding Karen of the bitter reasons why the two parted in the first place, and his presence only strengthening the thoughts. The lonely nights. Constant worry about his health. Never having the support of her mate at home when she needed it. For what? To chase a dream that Karen always knew consumed the man she so desperately did not want to love, not anymore. But how could she stop it? As much as she hated Joe for the roller-coaster of emotions he put her through, that never could change the fact that he was a loving man and a good man…when wrestling did not exercise its grip over him. “Let me tell you something Joe. I was scared SHITLESS for you when you had that pay-per-view with Nash. Yes, you did it Joe, you got to the main event. But look what Nash took out of you! He made you bleed! That match took so much out of you and TOO much out of me! You told me that was it….that it would be over….now you tell me this? Did WRESTLING ever give you a child, Joe?! WRESTLING HASN’T GIVEN YOU A FUCKING THING, BUT YOU LET IT DESTROY YOUR FAMILY AND YOUR HEALTH!?!?! This time…you better believe this time….” She opened the drawer under the counter, removing a pack of Newport menthol cigarettes. Scrounging for a lighter, she was determined to take the edge off this painful conversation. Unfortunately for her, the Brawler would not let her. A catchers mitt of a hand from across the counter snatched the Newports from her grasp, the Brawler tossing them into his pocket as he coldly spoke for the first time since he told her his news. “There ain’t gonna be none uv dat.” “And why not?! You smoke more than a church barbecue grill!” “BECAUSE YOUS NOT ABOUT TO BECOME A SHIT LIKE ME!! You got Carmen, you got a good job, you got a LIFE Karen, I don’t! All I got is da sauce and mah smokes.” BANG~!! The same hands that would caress the sandpaper skin that was his face turned into a mighty fist, rattling the salt and pepper shakers on the counter as she rebutted back with flame in her aquamarine eyes. “YOU’VE GOT MORE THAN THAT JOE. At least you used to. You had me and Carmen, and if you just let wrestling go FINALLY, you would have had us again. YOU FUCKED THAT UP….not me.” If the issue was not as serious as it was, the Brawler would have been quite the horn dog at the moment. Karen could turn him on like a light switch whenever she got angry. The passion she had for issues was undeniable, especially when coupled with the Queens accent she would lightly suppress. The Brawler took in a deep breath, looking Karen deep in the eyes. Her cherry lips trembled with an erratic anger, Joe having no warning signs of what would set her off next. With all the patience he could muster, the Brawler tried to explain his stance the best way he could. “Karen, I didn’t expect all dis to happen. I got a call about bein’ in sum open invite for PTC. I didn’t expect to do as well as I did. And befo’ ya kno it, I got a title match against 3 otha bustas for a title. Den’ I get a call from sum GW suit and dey wanna give me a tryout for a rosta spot. Karen…it hit me like a ton of bricks, but these are da best times to chase mah drea…” “GOD DAMN YOU, JOE! YOU CAN’T HAVE IT ALL!! You can’t keep being half a father to our child and I’m not going to have you being half a husband to me again! You’re 42 years old! This dream of yours….if you didn’t claim it 20 years ago, it’s not happening now….and if you’re going to keep chasing this fickle little dream….” Her breathing slowed, her lips tensed. The issue had taken her on all kinds of peaks and dips in her emotions. Karen never recalled this much pain since sliding the divorce papers to Joe so many years ago, crying a river as she asked for her freedom. Tears began to course down her face, her emotions a total mess as she hugged herself tightly. The hand of her former lover reached out to her, attempting to brush away a salty tear from her face. SMACK~!! “DON’T TOUCH ME. DON’T YOU FUCKING TOUCH ME.” The slap did not hurt. How could it hurt a warrior who had the privilege of going through flaming furniture? It was the feelings behind it that stung the most, that feeling of being unwanted and therefore…unloved. “You make a choice, Joe. Right fucking now. I need you to just GIVE UP. Don’t you see? Look at me. LOOK AT ME!!” He did as she asked, witnessing the display of sincere, painful tears that rushed from her now reddened eyes. This was the most vulnerable Karen has ever been since the divorce, always a bastion of strength when she confronted Joe, now a mess of tears. “Don’t you see I love you Joe? I still fucking care about your sorry-ass! Even through it all, even when I left you, you’ve still been good to me. If you were like any of the jerks I’ve dated, it would be easy to write you off….but when you’re here for us, you’re wonderful. WHEN YOU’RE HERE, remember that. Carmen and I need more then what you gave us before. If you ever want to see Carmen more then just every two weeks and you EVER want me as your wife again, you will stop hurting us with being on the road and wondering how badly you’re hurt match after match.” Who knew a dream could hurt so many. Dreams are what drive most human beings, an idealistic goal we journey and quest to fulfill. Was the Infinite Gauntlet worth it? Was a spot with one of the most celebrated federations in the PTC circuit, Global Wrestling, worth it? What good was fulfilling his dream without his daughter and the women he loved sharing it with him? But what good would it be having a family and waking up everyday in quiet torture? Knowing you deferred a chance at immortality against your will. There would be no winners no matter the choice. Pain would saddle this broken family today no matter the door he decided to walk through. “If you decide to go after this title…that fucking GW job, leave me and Carmen out of this. I don’t EVER want to see you again. You can keep the child support, all of it. All you’ll do is make me hurt, make me cry like this. You can’t have a family and be some warrior god too….pick Joe, pick.” Silence befell the kitchen. Discomfort replaced the oxygen, making every breath a harsh one to swallow. This choice was different when Karen divorced him. She made it, she was sure of what she wanted to do. Now, the ball was in his court and Joe never wanted to choose between the two most important women in his life and his dream from childhood, the sport he was convinced he was built for. “Karen….when I leave dis house, I’m getting on dat plane ta Las Vegas. I’m gonna do dat tryout, show those suits I still got something left to give and start cashing some checks with Global Wrestling on em. As for the Infinite Gauntlet…I’ma crush anything that moves plain and simple. I love you, I love Carmen. But the fuckin’ fact is, I can’t be a good father or husband knowing I let this drift. You wanted a choice, I made it woman.” She bit her lip, the fresh set of tears cluing in that she wished for the opposite choice. She stroked away a loose strand of her brown hair, nodding her head in begrudging agreement with the choice. Karen rounded the counter, the angel who fell from Heaven and made her home in Queens, as beautiful as the day he met her. Her hands clasped Joe’s head, those precious moments reminding him of the days when their love had no limit but the marshmallows called “clouds” above them. After all these years, the foreign feeling of her lips pressed against his. Years of hatred, confusion, and frigid feelings melting away with the fire in the kiss. Karen’s kiss could have been forever for all he cared, it mattered not, it would be this moment that would wake him up on those lonely nights….The Brawler questioning himself when he would hear the wind call her name. Her kiss had not changed. It was still a promise of something more to give, something deeper if you fell just as hard in love as she did. It was her way of dragging you deeper into the cyclone that was love with her. And then it ended as abruptly as it started. She mustered the strength to wrap her arms around him, Joe doing the same as he finally let a single tear emit from his eye, crashing onto her dress shirt from the office, already smattered with her own tears. “I love you, Joe. Now say goodbye to Carmen, then get out. And never come back again.” Karen released her hold, trying to hold back her tears as she walked away as calmly as she could, closing her bedroom door behind her with the sound of a lock twisting behind it. “Daddy?” The Brawler turned around, Carmen standing against her door with a saddened look on her face. She had a good idea what had happened, but her young mind was still confused about everything she had taken in. All 6’7, 329 pounds of the Brawler felt as small as his little princess at that moment as he lumbered over to her, coming down to one knee so he could speak to her. “You’re leaving again aren’t you?” “Yea baby….for a long time dis’ time. Daddy had a few more battles to fight.” Her little arms tried their best to wrap around the Brawler, Carmen quite the strong little girl in spirit as not a single tear refused to budge despite the situation at hand. This was not the time to cry, this was the time to show her father she still loved and supported him. It would be in this two-bedroom apartment in the Bronx where Carmen made her greatest statement of love to who other wrestlers knew as a beast and a monster. “Daddy…you beat the bad men, and you make sure you take care of your boo-boos. You won’t forget about me will you? Will you daddy?” He gripped her tight to his broad chest. Every two weeks was never enough to be with his daughter, so he knew indefinitely would be a knife in his gut. He questioned his decision a million times, wondering how smart it had to be to leave something so precious. “How could I eva fahget you, sweetness. You are mah everything…you ARE me. I’ll die befo’ I fahget you…yah hea me! I LOVE YOU.” “I love you morer, daddy.” “Morer”. What a wonderful adjective. At that moment, he felt something slip onto his wrist. Not exactly learning the art of stealth as of yet, Joe looked to his wrist and found a set of bright blue beads on it with Oriental calligraphy, certainly not an accessory meant for a tough guy like the Brawler. “We made them at art. They mean all types of stuff like love, caring, health…just a bunch of good things. I want all those things for my daddy.” A daughters unyielding love for her father, it was enough to keep him going another 20 years. The two shared a hug that could melt glaciers with the warmth, taking in each other the best way they knew how before they parted. A loving kiss on the forehead punctuated this moment. He began to take the bracelet of beads off his wrist, his daughter’s face ready to turn to a frown. “You don’t like them…I understand.” “No no, nothing like that. I just want them on the hand I use the most.” The bracelet was on his right hand now. The same hand he relied on for bringing down opponents, would always bear the reminder that he was loved, even in a world dominated by cruelty. One more embrace by the two could not be helped, once again bringing warmth to each other in this moment before father had to finally relinquish daughter. Those steps to the front door were long and heavy, Carmen watching part of the reason of her existence walk out the door while doing his best to keep his head held high. The door opened and he looked to Carmen, a long lingering look so as to take a mental image of the best thing he left on this earth. She did the same, the two looking to hold strong to their bond no matter the cost. The door finally closed, beginning what would be a hard journey for the Brawler. For wrestling, he left behind the purest loves he ever had in his life. Behind that door was a loving, yet confused and daughter with a broken mother. He never would have made such a painful decision had it not been for these opportunities being once in a lifetime. For the pain and anguish he had to intake as he began to set foot on the harsh Bronx streets, bones would be broken in volumes and blood would flow like rivers. He did not forsake the chance to have a family once more to be a loser. Emoticon, Drake, and Darkstar would know the definition of “suffering” for making him break the dream of a family for his daughter. If Global took him in, there would be no stopping until he came back the conquering hero, proving to his family their sacrifice and pain was not for an empty dream. There were new constants in his life with this decision. No stopping until he reached maximum success. There would also be no way he would return to Bronx without something to be proud of. But still, some constants stayed the same. The checks would always be on time and in the mail. And he would always love Carmen and her mother, the #1 and #2 women in his lives. And we need not forget the Virgin Mary. The dream of a family man would have to wait just a little while longer. The legend of The Back Alley Brawler would need to be cemented.
