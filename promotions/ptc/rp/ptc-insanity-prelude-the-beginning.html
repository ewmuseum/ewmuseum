Insanity Prelude: The Beginning (IG 23 Open Invitational)

13 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Another night, another fight. Tonight it’s a tag team match in Seattle. The other day it was a singles affair in Tacoma, the day before that a six man in Vancouver. From my spot on the mat, half-conscious, I look over at my tag team partner, trading right hands with the GCW World Champion, LeStatt Knight. Shadow’s giving as good as she gets, as per usual, and they tumble over the top rope together, leaving Brandon Youngblood and I both mostly out of it in the middle of the ring, courtesy of stereo chair shots. I can’t see it yet, but I can feel the sticky warmth of my own blood trickling down my forehead. Again and again the bell sounds, as if it’ll do anything. Referees have been ringing bells after double disqualifications since the beginning of time, but it never helps. At last, officials come down to ringside to separate Shadow and Knight. Youngblood groans in pain, rolling over and out of the ring. Thankfully, it seems the vengeful bastard’s done for the night. A quick glance confirms he’s in agony, too. He screams at me from the floor, but I can’t process what he’s even saying. No doubt the usual about how he’s going to kick my ass. He’s so…predictable. Youngblood and Knight walk back up the aisle, side by side but not together. They receive an ovation from the fans, Youngblood almost limping as he disappears behind the curtain. Shadow rubs her head thoughtfully on the floor, rolling back inside the ring. For a moment, I wonder why, until her slightly less graceful than usual manner of moving brings her to a kneeling position above me. “Are you okay, Seymour,” she asks, and I merely groan in response. After a moment or two, I nod weakly, and she helps me up to my feet. With the aid of the ring ropes, I can stand, long enough to bask a bit in the jeers of the crowd. I almost stumble trying to get out of the ring, and she shoots me a reproachful look. She and I both know the real answer to her question. “I’m fine,” I insist, walking under my own power. Sure, everything hurts like Hell, but it always does after a match. Nothing's different tonight. Shadow’s not convinced, though, and I can feel her eyes on me as I make my way behind the curtain. Whether her gaze is borne out of personal or professional concern, though, I can’t quite tell. Thankfully, the walk to the Dies Irae locker room is a short one, and the others have finished showering. Silently, Shadow gives me permission to go first, and I step into the room’s bathroom, taking a quick glance at myself in the mirror. I’m bleeding. Again. Just like last night. Just like the night before. Whatever opened me up that night before did a pretty damn good job, because the wound keeps re-opening. Last night, a right hand from one of Youngblood’s taped fists did it. This time, the chair shot. Maybe that’s a good sign, though. A chair’s more damaging than a right hand, though with Youngblood punching, not very. Silently, I disrobe, while listening to them talk through the closed door. Strategy, most likely. Or Dawn hitting on Lia. Again. I wonder sometimes why Virgil puts up with it, how he hasn’t tried to strangle Dawn yet. Shadow must have him well-trained. Like she does Vivica. And Mayhem. And Dawn. …and me, as much as I hate to admit it. I’m not as well trained as the others, though. I’m still too independent for her tastes. That Childress fuck that got into her head deserves a swift, painful death for being ultimately responsible for my reduction to a lapdog. Once, we were a group of equals. Now, I’m a subject. Four legs good, two legs better and all that. Of course the others put up with it. Dawn and Virgil love her. Vivica’s her apprentice, the heir apparent. And Bryan…well, my personal theory is Bryan Mayhem is on enough drugs to kill a Malboro. I’m still too ambitious, though, the potential Kefka to her Emperor Gestahl. Turning the water on, I step under it. It is scalding hot, just the way I like it. The pain is excruciating at first, but it distracts me from the deeper pains, loosening exhausted muscles and washing the blood from my face. It occurs to me as I soap myself down that I forgot shampoo. A towel even, for that matter, such was my haste to get in here. With a shake of my head, I reach down and pick up what has to be Vivica’s shampoo. You know, girly and pink. I lather my head and rinse as quickly as possible. A quick sniff makes me sigh. Lovely. I smell like lilacs. Disgustedly, I turn the shower off. At the very least, one of them left the first aid kit in here while tending to their wounds. I step out of the shower and pull a pair of large butterfly bandages out of a case, slapping them both over my head wound. A quick glance in the mirror confirms that it is sufficiently covered. And so I simply stand there, drip-drying, wondering how the fuck it all got this bad. Wondering why I can’t ever do anything right, by her standards. I’ve been fighting Youngblood all over the country for a month. Spilled his blood. Had my own blood spilled. And still, she’s not satisfied. “Destroy him,” she says. “Now,” she says. “You go try to drive Brandon Youngblood out of professional wrestling! Think you could do it in a day? Do you?!” That’s what I WANT to say to her. Instead, I nod and apologize, and promise to do better next time. I’m a former two-time PTC Global Champion, for God’s sake. One of the best known wrestlers on Earth. And I’m Shadow’s bitch. I joined Dies Irae because I was lonely. Because I wanted to dominate Global Championship Wrestling, hold it under my thumb. And, if I’m honest with myself, because I didn’t want to fight them anymore. Because I couldn’t beat them. Because I couldn’t even beat Shadow once. But now….now I may as well be on my own, because all I get is abuse. I’m the ugly duckling. I’m Cinderella. Suddenly, a knock comes at the door. Probably Lia, I think, wanting her turn. “Yes?” “You left your bag.” The voice is Virgil’s, and the door opens just wide enough for my duffel bag to be slipped into the bathroom. “Thanks,” I reply, weakly, and the door closes. Virgil’s not one for ‘you’re welcome’s most of the time. Still no towel, but I’m mostly dry now anyway. I pull a pair of black leather pants out of the bag. Don’t know where I’m gonna go, but Yevon knows there has to be a club in this city somewhere. As always, the leather’s like a second skin. I find a suitable shirt to go with it, throw it on, along with some socks and shoes. It takes me only a minute or two to gather my wrestling attire and throw it in the duffel. Seconds later, I’m out the door, duffel in hand. “Headed out on the town,” I say, blowing by the group before they can react. Not that they care, probably. The door to the locker room opens and closes quietly as I exit, leaving them where they sit, plotting their diabolical plots. I’ll get briefed later. After a night like this, there’s only one kind of healing that helps, and that’s hopping on the back of a chopper and hitting the clubs. It’s strange, ever since Laura died; the clubs, bars and fangirls are a lot more appealing than they used to be… ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ One of the advantages to being the bad guy is you get bothered less. Fewer fans coming up to you asking for autographs. Though, believe it or not, I still sign them. Especially for the kids. Telling off 12,000 people in an arena is easy. Telling off an eight year old with stars in his eyes is anything but. The quality of fangirl is also better. Maybe chicks really do dig bad boys, after all. This breed is less shy and stammering and “Oooh, it’s Seymour!” with requisite giggles. This breed tends to wear black, or enjoy having their hair pulled, or know rather wicked things to do with their tongues. As I nurse my Yuengling, I can see one of them watching me. She’s being coy about it, but she obviously knows who I am, and is obviously appreciative. I’ll go over to say hello later. For now, though, I’m going to sit on my ass, drink my beer, and figure out what the fuck I’m going to do about GTT6. After a year off, I’m the center of fuckin’ attention again. The Tournament Director’s decided to make Infinite Gauntlet Round 23 the Almasy sweepstakes. Winner faces me round one, with the assumption that said person can take me out right off the bat. Make my last PTC tournament a total failure. Well, you know what I say? Fuck that shit. I slam the now-finished beer down, ordering another. Another of many that’ll likely go on my tab tonight, before I go chat with that girl, take her back to my hotel, and end up tying her to the bedposts. Sometimes it’s good to be Seymour Almasy, even if I don’t show it much lately. The next beer comes, and I pop it open, a grin on my face. All apologies to PTC, but I’m not going gently into that good night. Not by a long shot. Because, for as smart as the Tournament Director is, as good as he’s gotten me in the past with bracket shuffles and Archibald MacGregor in the first fucking round of CW2, he’s finally made a fatal mistake. He’s let me in the Gauntlet. And with my own reward if I win. I can’t help but damn near salivate. If I win, I get to make ANY first round match for myself that I want. Anything! I can book myself against Darth von Doom if I want. Or Noah Hanson, if he ever got back in. Mr. TD’s given me the chance to OWN this tournament. And all I have to do to hold GTT6 in the palm of my hand is win the twenty-third round of the Infinite Gauntlet. An open invitation…meaning anyone can show up. Under most circumstances, this would be a difficult task…but again, the Director’s fucked up. Simply put, who’s stupid enough to WANT to face Seymour Almasy in the first round? That’s not a prize, that’s a GTT6 death sentence. It means that you’re going out first round unless you’re the crème de la crème. And let’s face it, people that good are smart enough to not want to risk their tournament on a first round coin flip. So I expect turnout to be sparse. And those fools who do show up I can crush easily, and in doing so hold the destiny of any man or woman who wishes to enter in my dominion. That fool in charge must be slipping. This is almost too easy… I will become the greatest GTT champion ever. Even Rollins, mighty as his performance was, only beat eight people. I will beat an entire FIELD in the Infinite Gauntlet, and then go on to crush all in my path in GTT6. My dominance will be assured, preserved for all time. My legacy unassailable, my name unforgettable… Suddenly, I feel a hand on my shoulder. Almost instantly, my body tenses, until I see the hand’s owner take a seat on the bar stool next to mine. He is a strange looking fellow. I should know. I’m usually a strange looking fellow myself. The guy might be the only man other than me who considers shoulder-length white hair a fashion statement. There’s something odd about him, though, beyond that. Maybe it’s the suit he’s wearing, the sort of suit that probably costs what the average man makes in a week. Maybe it’s his overly friendly manner with the bartender as he sets up his tab. Maybe it’s the smile he shoots my way, a strange half-smirk that makes me quirk a brow. The one thing that I DO know is that he’s potentially trouble. That’s confirmed when he extends a hand and says my name. “Mr. Almasy,” I hear him say, as my hand almost automatically reaches out to shake his. “I’ll be brief. I have a proposition for you.” The last time I heard that line, some sleazy Hollywood director was trying to get me to star in a gay porn movie. “Not interested,” I say, in the most disinterested tone of voice that I can muster. My eyes turn back to my beer, which suddenly is the most fascinating thing in the world. I take another long swig, a satisfied glint in my eyes. “What if I told you I could grant you any pleasure you wanted? You know that mouthful of beer? It ended up somewhere in the vicinity of the door. It’s not every day someone makes you an offer like that. “I take that back,” I say, trying to regain my composure. “I’m interested.” “Of course you are,” comes the all-too-smug reply. “I can promise you something that no one else can, for the proper price, of course…” “And that would be,” I ask right back. Just because this guy’s in a suit doesn’t mean I’m going to let him push me around. The other man suddenly gets too friendly. He stands up from the stool, wraps an arm around my shoulders, and leans close. “I know you, Seymour,” he says, conspiratorially. “I know how difficult it’s been for you lately. What I’m offering you is a chance to interact with the best elements in this world. A chance to enjoy yourself in total privacy. I can absolutely guarantee you that the only people that will know what you do are our fellow members, and they won‘t say a word.” Shit. He DOES know me. He knows what the paparazzi have done to my life. How I’ve never been able to get a moment’s peace. How my marriage was ruined. In spite of the foreboding feeling in my gut, my curiosity is peaked. “Keep talking,” I say, though my confidence is frazzled. “You used to live in New York City, yes?” I nod, and he continues. “I own a club in the city,” he says. “A club so exclusive that even you won’t know where it is. Only I do.” I have several questions about how such a setup works, but I swallow them. His offer has taken root in my mind, and I can’t help but ask what I really want to know. “Any pleasure,” I ask, my eyes just a little too wide, my heart pounding just a little too hard. The smile on his face is nearly sickening. “Anything,” he says, with a casual wave of his hand. “Some patrons come simply to enjoy the finest in food and drink. Others come to dance. Others to see film or art…and of course, others are into more…shall we say, debauched pleasure. Admittedly, the latter is the vast majority of our clientele...” As if that’s a surprise. It’s New York City. No different than Rio de Janeiro. Tokyo. The world’s nightspots are all places where people with cash go to jerk themselves off. I know that all too well. Had a tryst with a Japanese hooker before GTT4. Long story I’d really rather not go into again. “I understand,” I say. I’m still intrigued. Debauchery at this point of my life is one of the few things that keep me going. That and RPGs. Strange combination, sure, but it works for me. “So, assume I’m interested in going to this club. How do I get in?” He whispers a figure to me, and I whistle. No small amount of money, that. I can afford it, sure, but it’s still no small amount of money. “That’s per month,” he says, watching my jaw drop a bit, “in cash. Cash is less traceable. No way for anyone to track either of us.” That definitely makes sense. Still, I’m a bit bothered by something. “So, I’m supposed to hand you that amount over sight unseen?” Before the question’s even out of my mouth, he smiles. “Of course not. We’ve got a gathering two weeks from tonight. Come out and see what we have to offer. Believe me, when you experience this place, you’ll have no problem forking that over…” He seems sure. Confidently so. That both excites me and gives me pause. “So,” he says, a grin still on his face, “do we have a deal?” Once again, he extends his hand. Once again, I shake it. “Excellent,” the man responds. “My name is Ichiro Roppongi. A pleasure to make your acquaintance.” With that, he stands as if to leave, before suddenly turning around. Snapping his fingers, he gets the bartender’s attention. “This conversation,” he says, throwing a wad of what have to be $100 bills on the bar, “never happened. If you say anything to the contrary…” “Never happened. Got it.” Apparently, the barkeep’s either been through this sort of thing before, or a couple thousand is more than enough to buy his silence. Either way, Mr. Roppongi leaves the bar and I’m left alone to contemplate a lot. For example, how the fuck did he find me here? I wasn’t telling anyone where I was going, Hell, I didn’t even know this bar existed! He must have followed me…which is slightly frightening. Ah well. There’s nothing I can do for two weeks about it. In two weeks, I’ll find out what Roppongi has up his sleeve. Those two weeks should fly by, after all. I have much to do. I must deal with Brandon Youngblood once and for all, to spare myself Shadow’s wrath. And I must crush Primetime Central’s spirit in the twenty-third rendition of the Infinite Gauntlet. I must seize GTT6 in my hand, and squeeze it tight in my unforgiving grasp. I must state in no uncertain terms from BEFORE the beginning that the tournament is mine. Not mine to lose, just mine. Period. End of sentence. I call back over the bartender, and order another lager. That thought is definitely one that I can drink to. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ I thought my night was over then, but after an ill-advised half-drunk motorcycle ride back to my hotel, I couldn’t have been more wrong. Because there, sitting on the king-sized bed of my suite was a black robe, and a rather strange looking mask. “The uniform,” I mused. Now I knew how he could guarantee my privacy. Everyone would be masked, and thus no one in the club would recognize anyone else. On the nightstand was a bottle of champagne. Cristal, I recognized, with a box of fine chocolates next to it. For a few moments, I stood, confused, until I heard something that sounded a whole hell of a lot like a purr. I whirled around, only to find a woman standing there, wearing nothing but black lingerie. A long, lingering glance confirms it’s the girl from the bar. “Welcome to Club Plaisance,” she says. At least that confirms in my head why Roppongi didn’t pay her to keep her mouth shut, too. As she approaches, I can only smile, arms opening invitingly. Still, I can’t help but have this feeling in the back of my head that I’d be much better off kicking her out of my room. Yeah. Right. Like that’ll happen. I’m Seymour Almasy, soon-to-be conqueror of GTT6. PTC, your fate’s in my hands.
