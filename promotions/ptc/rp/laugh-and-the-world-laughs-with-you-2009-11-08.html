Laugh and the World Laughs With You

8 November 2009

Date: 2009-11-08 Laugh, and the world laughs with you: Weep, and you weep alone; For the sad old earth Must borrow its mirth, It has trouble enough of its own. Sing, and the hills will answer; Sigh, it is lost on the air; The echoes bound To a joyful sound, But shrink from voicing care. Rejoice, and men will seek you; Grieve, and they turn and go; They want full measure Of all your pleasure, But they do not want your woe. Be glad, and your friends are many; Be sad, and you lose them all; There are none to decline Your nectared wine, But alone you must drink life's gall. Feast, and your halls are crowded; Fast, and the world goes by; Succeed and give, And it helps you live, But it cannot help you die. Tempest!” Voices shouted over each other attempting to get the attention of the small, nervous woman who sat fidgeting behind the conference table emblazoned with “GTT7: Crushing Mediocrity since 2001.” She twirled a strand of hot pink hair around her finger and sighed, looking out over the throng of reporters as if they were the opponents she least wanted to face today. She was still dressed in a tight black bodysuit with pink glitter undertones that caught the light of flashes as they went off in her direction. She nodded to one of the shouters in one of the front rows accepting his question. “How does it feel to have passed through to the second round of the tournament?’ “Uhh… well, it’s great and all. Would be even better if I had actually had a match,” she answered with a shrug and waft of her taped left hand. Who knew athletic tape came in hot pink? “Tempest! What’s it like being back in the ring after such a long time away?” Tempest’s eyes narrowed as she gazed at the questioner with a look of mild concern for his sanity and said, “Um… well, I haven’t been back in the ring except for training. Did you just miss that fact or something?” She spotted a woman with her hand up, chuckling at the last question and that seemed to satisfy her that the reporter might have an intelligent question, so she pointed to her. “Tempest, what would you say to your opponents who no-showed your match? And do you think they were afraid or overcome by the power of your legend?” Tempest seemed taken aback, but grinned and puffed out her chest comically as she repeated, “The power of my legend… wow, that sounds good, can I hire you to write my biography?” This brought some laughter from the assemblage, but seemed to get their attention as well. “Well, I can’t really speak to what was going on in their minds, although I’ve heard that Darek DeAngleo is in the middle of a pretty heated custody dispute. So, I would certainly have to respect him putting his little girl first.” She smiled and gave a little nod to a whirring camera nearby. “Hang on to her Darek; you have no idea what it will mean to her one day.” She shook her head a little seeming to recover herself a bit. “As for Mask of William, I never knew anything about him and I guess the only thing I can say is that I still don’t, and neither does the rest of the world. So that’s a missed opportunity, my friend. Not smart.” “Tempest!” The shout came from a well-rounded man at the back with an array of cameras and equipment, who looked like he might be covering this and eight other events, possibly a safari and a hunt for aliens. He palmed an earpiece in his left ear and nodded at her meaningfully. “Word is that you’ll be facing Desade in the next round, and…” His question was interrupted by a clamor of ‘ohs’ and the abrupt clicking of dozens of texting devices, but he raised his voice a bit and continued, “Any comment on that match up?” “Well, it’s a great match up! And nice job getting the drop on the news there,” Tempest said making little swirls at her ear with her finger. She seemed amused, but quickly went on with the subject at hand. “That’s a fantastic match actually; any chance I can have it now?” She laughed and the reporters stopped in mid-text, gawking at her in apparent disbelief, all except for one. Darren Stearns, who stood in the back with no camera, no notebook, and no Blackberry, gave her a quick thumbs up, which she responded to with a wave. “Hi Darren! What’s the matter; these guys don’t read your magazine? They’re not into doing their homework anymore?” she asked and chuckled as all eyes turned to Darren, some with recognition and not just a hint of jealousy; others were clueless. Tempest stood and said, “Sure, I’d love to face Desade. Here I am all ramped up and no place to go, so to speak. I’d rather fight the most fearsome names in the business than just go home. But I guess that’s where we’re at, hmm? Thanks for coming to my great non-return to the ring. But stay tuned, it’s coming! And sounds like it’s coming in fine fashion next round.” She jogged around the table and down the few steps, headed for the exit, with the cameras clicking and the flashes popping around her, seemingly ignoring it in her own frenetic energy and desire to be anywhere but here until a voice piped up from the throng. “Tempest! What about Ivan?” Tempest stopped and turned slowly, her eyes narrowing to focus on the source of the question, the same young, blond woman who had asked the smart question of her earlier. Tempest straightened and the room went quiet as others listened for the answer to the question they simply hadn’t dared to voice. So there it is, the question I’ve been expecting, if not dreading, ever since I popped back into this little rat race. What about Ivan? What happened with him? Is he coming back too? Did you escape your former enemies you earned as The Devil? Will your even older allies stand by you? Where are they now? What about all those other people? You should be asking about me, because that’s all I know about. It’s all I’ve ever really known about. Looking at the woman who asked the question, I can tell she’s excited. Bold, I like that. She’s got that look like she’s on the edge of a great breakthrough in her own career, like this is the scoop she’s been waiting for. Or maybe just something she’s been dying to know for years. What’s your angle on this one, sweetie? Did you miss me? Are you my biggest fan? Did I give you an autograph once or did you sit in the front row and watch me fly from the top rope to deliver a System Crash to Sebastian Toomes? Have you got a Tempest shirt gathering dust in the bottom of a drawer that you’re just dying to break out? Maybe it’s none of that and you’re just one of the throng who would use me as a means to an end, to get something you want and make a name for yourself. But maybe you’re a fan deep down. Maybe that’s what brought you this far in the first place. I’d like to think so. And I’ve always hated to disappoint the fans. So get this one down… The tension in the air hung thick and still like the static buildup before a lightening strike. The reporter swallowed hard, for the time seeming to grasp that she might have overstepped her bounds. But then, the corners of Tempest’s mouth turned up in a mischievous smile and she gave her the tiniest of winks. “Ivan who?” And with that she turned on her heel and skipped from the room. ~~~ Darren Stearns handed Tempest a menu and said, “I recommend the meatloaf. I’m sure a lot of people just go straight to fear with her, but not me. I see too much that’s familiar and it makes me curious. I wonder about the how’s and why’s. How much worse did you make it? Why did you have to be so stupid as to try and kill me? And why did I show mercy the one time it was so pointless? This is the cloth from which the Devil was cut. These are the nightmares from which the Tempest stormed forth. So, who am I to look at someone like Desade and judge? Who am I to say a thing except to ask, “How is it with you, lady?” For me, strangely enough, it’s also where that desperate desire for happiness came from and the determination to have it and be it. Life is short and precious and so incredibly fragile. And in that brief and crystalline time in my childhood that I shared with my father I knew what it was. I was determined to own that and hang on to it with all my might. And if possible, I would find it again. He died, but my spirit did not die with him. And so now, after destroying that which those streets and pits made me, this is what it’s like having my soul back. That’s how I went about retrieving it. It took years. It took walking away. It took giving up everything I ever knew and loved. It’s only when you have nothing that no one has any more power over you. Then you are safe. And then you are empty. But then… you might as well be dead. Slowly I live again. I breathe. Then I stand. Then I walk. Now… I run? “Why do I do it? Well, you know… maybe no one will care about this piece of floor they’re walking on or that wall or whatever. But I know that I’ve been a part of something, that I built something and preserved something worth saving. And I didn’t just let it fade into nothing and be forgotten without a fight,” Troy Rivers said with a smile as he looked up at the grand façade above the stage of the Prospero Playhouse, now nearly restored to its original 1920 glory. “It may be just an old theater, but I believe it’s worth it.” He turned to the young intern who seemed satisfied with this as he nodded, but then found himself in the path of an oncoming Troy as he moved toward Tempest. “Rachel?” The question startled Tempest from her reverie and she looked up at Troy from the great handful of red velvet spilling from her hands and forced a smile onto her quivering lips. “What’s the matter, hmm?” he said with a quiet concern reserved for her ears and designed not to draw attention as he stepped closer and put his hands on her shoulders, rubbing them gently. “You looked a little far off there.” They stood on the stage, still lit with work lights during the ongoing renovation. “Um, Skeeter was just asking you about the curtain there. Is it ok?” he asked, drawing her attention back to the present and to the huge center curtain with the mask of comedy grinning forth in purple and gold from just to the left of center stage. Tempest stared at its frozen, glittering smile, with empty eyes, the work of her own hands and nodded mutely. Her mouth was open slightly, still caught in her own thoughts as she gripped the other curtain and looked up at Troy, trying to shake off her thoughts which had been elsewhere and said, “Yes, it’s fine. Looks great if I do say so myself.” “Ok,” he said with a small nod. He turned to the catwalks above the stage and shouted to the unseen worker, “OK, looks good!” Turning back to Tempest he said quietly, “Listen, you all right? Seriously?” He peered at her with concern. He has no idea, once again. Well, he knows that I came from Hong Kong and that I worked in sweat shops and endured poverty. Not the rest. But even that’s a far off concept to him. Poverty is something you help out with by doing charity work for Habitat for Humanity, by contributing to the local food bank. It’s something you see on TV in those Save the Children commercials. He has some idea that I’ve suffered some amount of privation, but no real idea about what that means. He’s willing to listen, but not one to pry and I like that. That look on his face right now is just, “I’m here.” He’ll listen anytime I’m ready to talk about it. I just don’t know that I ever will be. “Hey, Troy!” A voice carried across the darkened seating section of the theater and both he and Tempest turned to the now familiar voice of Charles Bentley, the project’s foreman as his shadow came closer with another male shadow of similar size. “Hey I finally found an electrician to tackle these ancient lights and shit!” Troy laughed and smiled at Tempest, knowing the frustration they had all gone through as a train of electricians had thrown up their hands or mysteriously stopped showing up after a shocking experience or two in the old building. “Troy, Rachel, meet Frank Valetta,” said Charles, finally stepping into the small circle of light put forth by the work lights with the new electrician. He had dark hair and brown eyes that lit up with recognition upon seeing Rachel. Her eyes widened in horror and she opened her mouth but not in time. “Tempest!” he blurted out in the same boisterous tone with which he’d congratulated her the night before and wished her luck in GTT7. Troy’s eyebrows arched curiously as he looked from one to the other, hands on hips, but Tempest barely noticed. Her gaze had strayed down to the mask of tragedy on the red velvet curtain clutched in her hands and she merely shook her head slowly.
