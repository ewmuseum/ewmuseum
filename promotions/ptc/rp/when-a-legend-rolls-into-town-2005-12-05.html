When A Legend Rolls Into Town…

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 Prologue Now. New York City. An Abandoned Warehouse on the Lower East Side. I backed away from her lifeless body in horror and slumped downward to the floor, back pressed up against the wall, my head buried within my hands instinctively. I found myself attempting to hold back a solitary tear, but quickly realised the futility of such action and, in the end, allowed it to haphazardly cascade down my right cheek. It seemed as though I’d spent my entire life shielding my emotions from those surrounding me, but at this moment in time, I can’t seem to care about it; I can’t seem to care about anything. I can’t stop my eyes from washing over her bloodstained body again, and sure enough, my mind reattached itself to the utter tragedy of it all. Aeryn - my angel - is dead. The woman I have - no had - feelings for is no longer alive and there is nothing I can do to remedy the situation in the slightest. She’d been the only tangible link to my previous life, before the cold, vulnerable, uncertainty of the amnesia swept over my very being like an untameable force of nature. And in that previous life - although she’d neither confirm nor deny it - I am convinced that our love was even more intense. Now I will never know. I glanced over toward the body of Nathaniel, the self-proclaimed ‘legend’, and noted his chest moving up and down. He was still alive, more to the pity. To hear him talk the thought of such an occurrence was almost unimaginable, but the truth of the matter is: even legends can be hurt and, some can even be defeated. But I knew that much already. Chapter One Eight Hours Ago. New York City. A Bar on the Lower East Side of the City. Nathaniel inhaled the night air plentifully and smiled; he always had liked night time the best. The mysterious beauty of the night had always held a prominent place within his soul; its dark veil covering his actions - and the actions of those like him - from the average citizen. He very rarely came out during the daytime anymore. Nathaniel parked his Harley next to the other choppers, by the front of the bar and made his way over to the heavy wooden doors, noting to himself just how much the place looked like a dive to him - and he’d seen a fair share of dives in his time. Nathaniel fished in the breast pocket of his shirt and quickly withdrew a small notepad. He flicked through a few pages until he came to the address he’d already written down three town’s back; there could be no mistake, this was the place alright. He delicately placed the notepad back his breast pocket, shoved open the heavy wooden doors and took a step inside. His first reaction, upon entering the establishment, was that the cosy interior more than made up for the completely rundown exterior. Hell, it was almost impressive in comparison. However, when all eyes of the bar’s drinking patrons immediately darted in his direction, it became a decidedly uncomfortable hostile environment. Nathaniel had read the instructions of how to make contact with the man very carefully, just to make absolutely sure he’d ignore every one of them with great proficiency. He glanced to his immediate left. There were two booths there; the one closest to the door he’d just walked through was completely empty and the one along had one grim-looking, solitary figure sitting inside it. The man looked big enough to be the contact Nathaniel had come here looking for, but the large black cowboy hat and his black leather pants made him reconsider that. His mind race; is this really the guy? He looked like one of the Village People, the one people don’t talk about anymore, but it had to be him, hadn’t it? Regardless, he casually strolled over to the booth and wedged himself into the seat opposite the man. Nathaniel leaned back, getting himself comfortable, and glared at the man, waiting for him to make the next move. All eyes in the establishment were still fixed on Nathaniel and the tension was palpable. His contact name was Vandal and he very much doubted that anybody had ever come looking for him before, completely disregarding all the correct protocols. Everyone who’d ever sought out the assassin’s services knew of the correct protocol; you couldn’t even mentioned Vandal’s name without the subject cropping up. Vandal had his head lowered concentrating on the glass of whiskey he nursed before him. Then he spoke, in one of the grimmest voices Nathaniel had ever heard, never raising his head, "What the hell do you want?" Nathaniel contained his chuckle. Talking grim doesn’t make you tough, it just makes you grim. Besides, Nathaniel had taken apart grimmer men in his time without too much difficulty. "Information." Vandal raised his head and looked him in the eyes. Nathaniel noted the anger spread across his face like a neon warning sign and held back a smiled. He wanted him angry. "Do you have any idea who I am?" Nathaniel grinned. "Yeah." Vandal rose from his seat extremely animated, his hand quite clearly fishing in the back of pants, undoubtedly for some kind of weapon. He was fast, but Nathaniel was much faster, and with unyielding focus, he rose from his seat, grabbed Vandal’s head and yanked him down hard, putting him face-first through the table and shattering the wood as though it was thin air. The sidearm Vandal had reached for fell to the floor, harmlessly, as his body went completely limp - one of the most telling signs of unconsciousness. Nathaniel stepped out of the booth, a little disappointed. He thought Vandal would’ve at least put up a decent fight. Nathaniel heard a rustling of chairs and turned to see ten burly guys standing there, menacing looks etched into their grizzly features, each one with a weapon of some kind. Nathaniel grinned, he may get a decent workout out of this after all. "That wasn‘t particularly smart, friend," The particularly dangerous-looking one, stood at the forefront of the pack declared. "You’ll be paying for that!" "I very much doubt it," Nathaniel replied, unbuttoning his shirt. "I just want information. None of you need be hurt. Just tell me what I want to know and I’ll leave peacefully." The one stood a little ahead of the others chuckled at this and the others quickly followed in suit. "You’re not going to leave here at all" Once unbuttoned, Nathaniel slipped of his shirt, folded it over his right arm and then laid it down gently in the booth where he previously sat. Now, he just had a plain white vest covering his torso, which, despite his age, perfectly emphasised his muscular body. Nathaniel noticed the confusion expressions smeared across each face within the angry mob and felt an explanation was in order. "I don’t want to get blood on my shirt." The crowd remained silent, but Nathaniel heard a mummer emanate from the both behind him. Vandal was beginning the unpleasant journey of regaining consciousness. The blow to the head will hit him immediately, Nathaniel considered unable to keep the smile from his face. Nathaniel strolled over to Vandal’s large, helpless frame and gave him a short, sharp kick to the ribs. "Wake up!" Nathaniel yelled. "I want you conscious for this - you’re about to see a legend at work!" Nathaniel turned back to the mob, now with a look of determination and focus clearly etched into his face. This was nothing, just a little warm up before the main attraction, but after this, each of the men before him will know the simple truth for what it is, without reservation; a legend had rolled into town. Chapter Two Four Hours Ago. New York City. The Stakiri corp. Towers. Downtown. As soon as he stepped out of the elevator, Mr Stakiri’s receptionist, Sheila, knew that something was wrong with him. The man walked with a limp as though he’d been run over in the parking lot, his clothes looked like he’d feed them through a shredder before putting them on and his face was as bloodied and pounded as a slab of meat resting upon a butchers table. Obviously something was wrong him, she understood, but that did not explain precisely how the man got past security on the ground floor. She didn’t have time to dwell on the subject any longer; he was getting closer now. Sheila nodded at the security guard posted just outside of Mr Stakiri's office to gain his attention. He nodded in response and wandered over. "What is it, ma‘am?" He asked, politely. Sheila nodded at the man heading their way. "That man over there…" Before Sheila could finish her sentence, the security office had already set off to intercept the man, wondering just how he hadn’t noticed him before. "Can I help you sir?" The security guard asked, placing a stern hand upon the mans chest to halt his advance. "I…I…I need to speak with Mr Stakiri immediately!" The man demanded, making an conscious effort to keep himself calm. "Looking like that?" The security officer asked, rhetorically. "I don’t think so." "Look, the security people on the ground floor called Mr Stakiri whilst I was down there," the man explained, his words loaded with urgency. The security officer developed a confused expression. If what the man said was true, then why didn’t they tell him as much over the radio system? That was the standard procedure with Mr Stakiri’s normal business associates. Unless, of course, this wasn’t one of Mr Stakiri’s normal business associates; it could be one of his other associates. The security officer had seen a few of them in his time, but he didn’t recognise this man. However, in his current state the security officer very much doubted whether his own mother would recognise him right now. In any case, the security detail on the ground floor would not inform him of such a visitor over the radio system, by Mr Stakiri’s own standing orders. "It’s okay Eli," the voice behind him assured. The security officer whirled around to see Mr Stakiri stood in the doorway of his office. A grim looked carved onto his face. "This is a special case. Something important has come up." The security officer stepped aside, allowing the bloodied and battered man to pass. "Yes, Mr Stakiri." Mr Stakiri extended his arm and ushered the visitor into his office, leaving the security officer and Sheila to go about their business once again. "Sheila," Stakiri said, before stepping into the office himself. "Hold my calls." Sheila nodded in reply. "Yes, Mr Stakiri." Stakiri closed the door behind him and gestured for his guest, the man he knew as Simon Bogan, to sit in the seat opposite his own. Bogan took Stakiri up on the offer and made himself comfortable. Bogan worked in one of Stakiri’s subsidiary organisations in the Lower East Side of the city. Technically speaking, Stakiri didn’t own the property - or at least the paper trail could not be traced back to him - but he sure as hell ran the operation. It wasn’t exactly a legal business he had operating over there, so protecting himself was something of a necessity. Around four hours ago one of his agents headed over to the establishment - a little dive Stakiri had various assassins-for-hire operating out of - to pick up Stakiri’s cut of the takings for the week, and his agent found the place burnt to the ground. There were many survivors of the incident, but the man sat opposite him was the only one who Stakiri‘s agent found conscious at the time; he was the bartender. The police picked the others up a few minutes after Stakiri’s agent left the scene. Needless to say, Stakiri was pissed off. One of his most profitable earning ventures evaporated into thin air over night. This also posed quite a difficult problem; someone had decided to make a move against the drinking establishment, and what’s more, they succeeded. Stakiri was dealing with someone, or something, extraordinary and obviously dangerous. "What happened?" Stakiri questioned, slumping down into his luxurious leather chair. "It…it…it happened so fast! I’ve never seen anything like it before…I mean he took them all apart…" the bloodied and battered man mumbled. Stakiri rolled his eyes. Obviously whatever the man witnessed was traumatising, but Stakiri had to act fast; he sure as hell didn’t want any more of his ‘outside interests’ destroyed in the same manner as this. "Come on, Bogan!" Stakiri yelled. The poor fellow was obviously in shock, but Stakiri didn’t have the time to molly-coddle the man. Someone was making a move against him and he wanted to know precisely who. "Snap out of it!" That didn’t do much to help matters. "He…he…he just came in and sat with him. We thought the guy was mad, but he took down Vandal as though he wasn’t there…" Stakiri’s eyes widened. Vandal was the very best - and toughest - assassin in the drinking establishment. Whoever it was couldn’t have taken him that easily…could they? "He took down Vandal that easily?" "He wanted to wait until Vandal woke up, ‘cause he wanted information," Bogan continued. "He took down everyone within five minutes, then when Vandal finally did wake up, he made him talk…" He made Vandal talk? That just wasn’t possible. Nobody could make Vandal talk…could they? "Who?" Stakiri questioned, out of his chair with both hands on his table, aggressively leaning over toward Bogan. "Who did this?" Bogan raised his head and looked Stakiri directly in the eyes. "The legend." The legend? Stakiri had heard of him before, in myths and legends told or known by the majority criminal who‘d ever been somebody in this world. The kind of stories that you neither believed nor doubted at the same time; the stories about the man couldn’t be true, everyone knew that much, but there was so much detail and truth in them they couldn’t be false. The foundation of every good legend. Stakiri’s face became pale and he slumped back in his chair, eyes wide with disbelief. "The legend? Are you sure?" Bogan nodded. "Yeah…he told everyone as much." Stakiri quickly reached for the phone and hit the third speed dial button down on the list; the genetics department of the Stakiri Corporation. Despite the late hour, the call was answered within seconds. "This is Swanson," the man answered. "Swanson, this Stakiri," He replied. "I need you to prep Project: Lazarus for immediate deployment." "Project: Lazarus?" Swanson questioned, hardly believing his employer’s words. "That’ll take days to adequately prep!" "We have an emergency. I want it done within an hour. Just the bare essentials, but you may need to amp up the strength level," Stakiri responded. "Can’t get the strength level much higher, but we’ll try. An hour is pushing it, but we’ll see what we can do," Swanson replied. "Get on it," Stakiri commanded, slamming his phone down. Stakiri didn’t like this one bit. Project: Lazarus was nowhere near ready and he had to wonder whether it would be much of a match for this legend character at all. "He…he wanted something," Bogan spoke up, all of a sudden. "What?" Stakiri asked. "When…when he came into the bar, he wanted information," Bogan replied. Stakiri’s eyes widened. It didn’t even dawn on him that there was more to this. He thought the bar was the first hit in many of his less than legal earning establishments and perhaps this legend character was hired to do the job by one of his many ‘competitors’. "What did he want to know about?" Stakiri questioned. "I…I heard him question Vandal bout it," Bogan began, still shaking with the shock of it all. "…About something - or someone - that roams the city at night." Stakiri raised an eyebrow. Interesting. "Go on." "The…the Legend said another contact of his told him of Vandal and that he might know of this creature of the night," Bogan explained. "But…but Vandal told him that it was no creature. He told the legend that it was just a man in a black Kevlar suit and he knew because he had him bang to rights one time…" "Wait," Stakiri questioned, beginning to finally understand what all this about. "Vandal was hired to assassinate him…and failed?" "Ye…yeah. He said that it was your daughter, Alexa, who stopped him in the end. The legend didn’t care much about that, he just wanted to know how to find him," Bogan continued. He was referring to his ‘daughter’ Alexa Kendericks. He treated the young woman as if she were his own daughter, but truth be told she was created in a lab. Another one of his organisations less than legal dabbling in the genetics industry. He’d heard about her encounter with Vandal a few months back and remembered being quite impressed, but he never counted on her mission of mercy coming back to bite him in the ass. Of course, Stakiri had only allowed her to go along with the aid, in order to protect another one of his genetic investments - Jonathon Winters. "Did he say why he wanted to find this man?" Stakiri asked. He had to play this one cautiously; he didn’t want Bogan to know more than he needed to. "He…he wants to fight him," Bogan replied. "Fight him?" Stakiri enquired. "Why?" "He…he told Vandal he’d heard stories coming from the city about the man," Bogan explained. "And needed to know." Stakiri developed a confused expression. "Needed to know what?" "He…he needed to know who’s better," Bogan responded. "He…he said it was a matter of honour." Stakiri leaned back in his chair, trying to wrap his head around it all. This legend character wanted to find Jonathon Winters just to fight him? To see whom was better? For honour? None of this made any sense to Stakiri at all, but in the end it didn’t need to. Jonathon Winters, one of his most valuable assets, was in danger from this thing and so the deployment of Project: Lazarus would still go ahead. Perhaps between the two of them, this legend could be defeated. Perhaps. Chapter Three Three Hours Ago. New York City. Jonathon Winters Apartment. Location Undisclosed. I approached my apartment, with a large brown paper bag full of groceries resting in my right hand, and I realised almost immediately that something was wrong. I couldn’t tell what it was, but I sensed something out of place, something that shouldn’t been here. I carefully turned the doorknob and entered my place of residence, placing the grocery bag on the table by the door. I heard a noise coming from my lounge area, it didn’t take me long to recognise Jerry Seinfeld’s very distinctive voice; the noise was coming from the television. I felt a wave of relief sweep over me, until, of course, l I realised that I didn’t leave the television on when I went to the store earlier this morning. I carefully, made my way into the living room, expecting to have some moron jump out on me, but instead I find a middle-aged man casually sat on my sofa, staring intently at the TV. I can’t believe my eyes. At first, he doesn’t show whether he’s noticed me or not, but when he found one Kramer’s scenes particularly comical, he pointed toward the screen and glared at me, laughing. "You gotta love Kramer!" "I guess," I responded, still trying desperately to figure out just what in the hell was going on. "I’m not much of a fan of the show." The man reached for my TV remote and clicked the ‘off’ button. "Truth be told," He said. "Neither am I. I take it you‘re the great Jonathon Winters?" There is something about the man I can’t put my finger on. A gentle ease about him that belied his clearly muscular frame and his aggressive body language. Not to mention the fact that he was actually in my home and knew my name; hell, my neighbours barely know that much. "Maybe," I replied, coldly. "Who are you and what in the hell are you doing in my apartment?" The man chuckled to himself and stood from the sofa. "Straight to business…I like that. I‘m Nathaniel." "I don’t much care what you think or who you are," I told him. "Get the hell out of here." "I’m afraid I can’t do that," He responded. "We have a little issue to discuss…" "I think not," I told him. "Out!" "I know who you are and what you can do," He stated. "Still want me to go?" Did he actually know anything? Or was he just goading me, hoping that I would be careless enough to let some vital information about my life, beyond the wrestling industry, slip out? I don’t plan on falling into that trap. "You know nothing," I informed him. He grinned. "So you don’t spend most nights jumping from building top to building top in a particularly intimidating black Kevlar suit?" He had my attention and he knew it. Though, I decided to make at least some effort to deny the fact. "I have no idea what you’re talking about." "Of course you don’t," he replied, with a smile. "My underground sources tell me that you are the best, a formidable opponent by anyone’s standards. I want to fight you." "Fight me?" I asked, disbelief spread thickly throughout my words. Then I remembered to keep the pretence up for my own benefit, more than his. "But why? I’m just a professional wrestler…" "Sure you are," He answered. "I want to fight you, because that’s what I do. I need to know whose better. I need to know that I am still the man to beat. I need to know that I’m still the legend that people speak of…" "The legend?" I questioned. "Yes," he answered, obviously not wanting to get much into the issue. I forced the matter. "Why do they call you that?" His grin now had a more reflective, if not, nostalgic underlying tone to it. "Because that’s what I am," he paused to correct himself, "what I was." "Okay," I told him, wondering for the millionth time that month precisely why all the lunatics seem to always find me. "You’re better than I am, I’m sure. Happy? Good. Go." He turned to me, disappointment painted so clearly across his face. "I don’t need you to believe me," he told me, "I just need you to fight me." "Well I won’t fight you," I responded, making sure he recognised the substantial thread of defiance interwoven with my words. "Look, I don’t know just who you think I am, or what you think I can do, but I assure you, you have the wrong man." "Denial up until the end, eh?" He said. "Anyhow, I know whom you are whether you admit it me or not. I followed you home last night when you were moving quickly across the rooftops just south of here." Followed me home? I didn’t sense anybody who could be construed as following me last night, at least not within a one-mile radius. It seemed impossible, but how in hell else would he know where I live? That meant he was good…very good. "I know you’re him," he continued, "and I know that you can be difficult if you want to be. I don’t like this, but if you don’t meet me at this address," he handed me a piece of paper, "I’ll kill her." He headed toward the door, leaving me completely confused, vulnerable and shocked all at the same time. But I had enough of my senses about me to ask the question, "You’ll kill whom?" He turned to me, with a stern expression now distinctly painted across my face. "Aeryn," He told me, attempting to gauge my reaction to the name. Despite my underlying feelings for the woman, I managed to keep my facial features as indifferent as I possible could. "I took her last night. Be there in three hours, prepared to fight." He walked out of my door and all I could do was to glare after him, not exactly sure of what just happened. I brought myself around by focusing on his last words about Aeryn. He had Aeryn? How did he find her? Had he hurt her? Many of questions do need answering, but I know one thing for certain; he knew that by abducting Aeryn I would have no other just but to go after him. He already knew all the right buttons to press in order to manipulate me into doing whatever he wanted and I knew nothing about him. In essence, I was at a distinct disadvantage with severely limited options. Sounds about right. This is the last thing I need playing on my mind now, with Primetime Central’s GTT5 tournament looming around the corner and particularly difficult opponent to overcome. It will be the pinnacle of my career, where I will have the opportunity to make the ultimate mark on the very face of the wrestling industry itself, and I have deal with this bullshit on the side. Fantastic. I mean, it’s not even as if I have to face just some average wrestler in the first round; I must go up against a veritable legend of the wrestling industry; Seymour Almasy. The man other men can only hope to emulate within the squared circle. He isn’t like Nathaniel. That man has been all talk so far and because of this I can’t verify whether he is or isn’t the legend he says he is, with Almasy I can. Almasy is a man of substance. A man that has proven himself to be the legend he is now considered to be…and he sure as hell didn’t pronounce himself a legend. That was done by those around him; his peers and his critics alike. Any fool can simply dub himself or herself a legend, but not so many can actually back that claim up. Perhaps this Nathaniel will prove to be more of a legend than I give him credit for, but on the other hand perhaps he won‘t. Chapter Four Forty-Five Minutes Ago. New York City. Location Undisclosed. She knew from the very moment they reawakened her that something was different; first something inside and then upon noticing her reflection in a particularly shiny piece of equipment in the medical labs of Stakiri Corp Towers. It felt as though her mind was not her own. It was more animalistic in many ways; although she couldn’t understand what the feeling was, she just instinctively knew that there was no logic in her anymore, there was no reasoning. Perhaps she was always intended to be this way, perhaps it was something the lab technicians did during the reanimation process that gave her the need for logic and reasoning. She will probably never know. To hold intelligent thoughts together became difficult and often taxing. She felt stronger, faster, and even bigger than before, though the memories of before were fading from her, inaccessible for some reason. Nothing felt right to her, as though she didn’t really belong in this world, as though she was some of abomination to nature itself. She tried to form the words to say something to one of the lab technicians, but failed to form her thoughts into words. Something she took for granted then, in a previous life, that seems so long ago now. The memories seem distant now, circling into the black recesses of her mind, allowing the animalistic portion of her mind to grow stronger and to conquer everything else. Then she saw herself again. Though this time she appeared as how she once remembered herself to be and despite her quickly diminishing intelligence she realise that this was no longer a mere reflection. This image of herself was real. Looking at her with a clear expression of disgust painted across her face. Was it disgust? Her double mentioned something about a project and a word that her word would no longer recognise. Laz..la…lu. She no longer knew, nor cared. Animals do not care about such things. But that was hours ago and the memories were already beginning to fade away, getting sketchy and muddled every time she accessed them. Now, she watched him from the distance. The distance her masters told her to keep, though they needn’t have. She liked hunting from a distance, waiting for the right time to pounce and destroy her prey. As he moved through the concrete jungle, just for an instance, she thought she recognised her prey, but these thoughts evaporated into the air as quickly and as crudely as they formed. She no longer cared about any of that; she just wanted to hunt. She set off to follow him. Chapter Five Thirty Minutes Ago. New York City. An abandoned Warehouse on the Lower East Side. I entered the warehouse via a broken skylight window directly from the rooftop and dropped down, silently, onto a raised walkway that spanned the majority of the interior of the large warehouse. From my vantage point I could see the whole of the warehouse and it seemed as though it was all just one big room now; someone had decided to knock the interior walls down and some of them had only been half completed. "Prompt," the voice bellowed, echoing throughout the massive room. Then the self-proclaimed ‘legend’, Nathaniel stepped out the shadows of one of the half-demolished walls into the vast open space. I couldn’t see Aeryn anywhere. "I like that." "I’m not going to fight you," I told him. "I’ll just take Aeryn and leave." Nathaniel grinned. "You‘ll after get past me first!" Then I realised that I couldn‘t actually sense Aeryn within a one-mile radius. Whenever I’m in the presence of Aeryn, I sense it because I have some sort of connection to the woman that I can’t adequately explain with mere words. I should’ve sensed it as soon as I entered the building, but where Aeryn was concerned my judgement has always somewhat clouded. I sighed. "She isn’t even here, is she?" The ‘legends’ eyes widen. "Of course, she is!" "I guess your informants forgot to mention the fact that she and I share a special bond with one and other," I told him. "I could sense her if she was here." "Okay," he finally admitted. "You got me!" I was angry earlier because this Nathaniel fellow manipulated me with the greatest of ease, now that I realise he really hasn’t got Aeryn captive I’m even more angry. However, I don’t allow this anger to take over my mind. If I engage him in battle through the anger he has caused, then technically he is still manipulating me to do his will. I’m nothing, if not disappointing. "If you don‘t have her," I said, not allowing my anger to show upon my face or within my words, "then there really isn’t anything to keep here, is there?" I noted the shocked expression splash over his face, like the sea onto the shore and smiled despite myself. He hadn’t counted on any of this and I could now almost see the cogs turning in his head, trying desperately to think of some reason, no matter how feeble, to further compel me into fighting him. I decided not to wait for him to put the idea into words and so set off toward the nearest broken window. Then, without warning, something crashed through skylight windows and dropped down to the warehouse floor with a decidedly heavy thud. Nathaniel looked at the beast in shock, so I knew right then he had nothing to do with this, so who in the hell sent this thing? Actually, the better question is: just what the hell is this thing? To me, it looked like some kind untamed beast trapped inside the body of a humanoid, possibly female. Whatever it was, it composed itself rather quickly considering the height of the drop and then sets its sights upon Nathaniel with great interest. Alarm bells literally rang in my head, my senses were reacting with beast’s presence and that’s when I realised just exactly who this beast actually was. It was Aeryn. "It can’t be…" I murmured to myself. The beast seemed to recognise my voice and glanced up at me for a second, before concentrating upon Nathaniel once more. It was definitely Aeryn, but she had obvious irregularities. She was much bigger now and her body language seemed to mimic some sort of predatory animal. "What the hell is this thing?" Nathaniel asked, backing to the wall. "Aeryn," I answered, unsure of what exactly to do. "Aeryn?" Nathaniel questioned, in disbelief. "You sure can pick ‘em!" "She’s obviously gone through some changes," I told him, "transformed in some way…I don’t know." Now, turning his attention back toward the beast he asked, "What do you want?" Aeryn, the beast, whatever the hell it was, seemed to have trouble forming a reply to the ‘legend’s’ enquiry. It was a struggle, but she managed to form the words nonetheless, "K…i…Ki…ll…Kill!" "Did she just say what I think she said?" I asked Nathaniel. He nodded. "Pretty much." "Good," I responded. He glared at me disapprovingly, as though his well being was actually supposed to mean something to me. "Look, I can see the irony in the situation; I tell you I’ve captured Aeryn, when I actually haven’t and then she turns up nonetheless, looking to kill me…" "…yeah?" I interject. "But come on!" He almost pleaded. Real legends don’t plead, do they? "You’ve got to help me out here!" I really don’t want to do it; my head’s telling me just to walk out the door and let the two of them go at it, then worry about the survivor afterwards. But the beast was Aeryn, at least in one form or an other. I sighed, grabbed the railings and then propelled myself over the edge, to the lower level of the warehouse. "Aeryn," I called to the beast, hoping that at least a small part of the woman I knew was still in there. The beast turned around upon hearing the name, though I could clearly see the confusion spread thickly across her face. "Stop this madness!" However, before she could respond, Nathaniel tried to attack her from behind. Do legends understand the meaning of honour? Anyhow, before he could actually connect with anything, she sensed his approach and threw left arm in his direction, sweeping him away as the sea would sweep away an extravagantly created sandcastle. The sheer force of the blow knocked Nathaniel flying into the wall on the other side of the warehouse. He crashed into the wall with a sickening thud. The beast then turned back to me and growled in the most horrifying fashion. "Stop this!" I urged her for a second time. She growled again and hurled her right arm at me haphazardly. I ducked the blow and forward rolled toward her blind side. Her excessive bulk meant that her speed was not even in the same league as my own and I was back on my feet before she’d even turned around. Once she did, I caught her with a power right hand, connected with the side of her head, quite pleasingly. She rocked backward, but despite my strength, the blow barely affected her, much to my dismay. It was clear to me now, whatever this thing actually was; it was not Aeryn anymore, despite the striking similarity. This creature was something else entirely; a clone gone wrong perhaps? It didn’t matter, she needed to be stopped one way or the other. Realising that I was too fast for her, she changed her tactics and charged at me this time, with equal reckless abandonment. I vaulted upward, placing my hand upon the very top of her head as she came closer, I flipped forward and landed on my feet with considerable grace. However, her momentum propelled herself forward, slamming into the wall with a sickening thud. To my surprise, she did not fall immediately. She stumbled around for a few seconds and then finally fell on her ass. Though she appeared to be dazed by the blow, she was still conscious and appeared even angrier than before. Just across the way from her, Nathaniel finally stirred, spluttering blood all over the floor. "Hey ‘legend’," I called over to him. "I thought you were supposed to be good?" He grumbled something under his breath, then with his right arm carefully cradling his obviously injured ribs and scuttled away behind the partially destroyed wall that he emerged from when I first entered the warehouse. I shook my head, hardly believing his cowardice and I pose the same question to myself as I did before: do ‘legends’ actually have honour? Now, the animalistic Aeryn was back on her feet and charging toward me again. I tried the same manoeuvre as I did before, but this time she was expecting it and caught me in mid air, before I could forward flip to safety. She bear-hugged me tightly around the waist so that I could hardly breathe and then ran forward and slammed me into the wall. I fell to the ground in a heap, wheezing for breath and attempting to resist the urge to slip into the dark comfort of unconsciousness. I heard Nathaniel reappear from behind the wall, with a clatter of metal and heavy footsteps. I struggled to see past the beast, but when she eventually turned to confront the ‘legend’ I could see the two samurai swords quite clearly, sunlight glinting off each of them majestically. I watched on, as Nathaniel swung both swords at the beast, with little effect. The animalistic version of Aeryn managed to move out of the way just in time, the blades skimmed her skin and clunked heavily against the warehouse floor. Then Aeryn stood on the blades, leaving Nathaniel wide open to attack and followed up with a powerful uppercut that sent him flying backward, hitting the floor hard upon impact. Nathaniel lost consciousness immediately and so Aeryn turned her attention back to me. I forced myself to one knee, despite being in no real condition to do so, and when Aeryn stomped close enough toward me, I slid myself through her legs and crawled over to the fallen swords with pace that surprised even myself. The creature was quickly on my case and charged at me again. I was able to raise the sword just in time, as she drew within mere centimetres with me and the blade impaled with gruesome beauty. I allowed the blade to slip out of my hand, and the creature fell backward, the life quickly slipping away from her. My eye’s widened in disbelief and I found myself momentarily breathless. I killed her. I actually killed Aeryn, or some mutated version of her. During the attack, the finalisation of it all didn’t sink in. It was life and death, and I didn’t want to be on the death end of the deal. Quite understandable, but that doesn’t mean I can’t feel guilty about issuing the final blow. I backed away from her lifeless body in horror and slumped downward to the floor, back pressed up against the wall, my head buried within my hands instinctively. Epilogue Later. New York City. Jonathon Winters’ Apartment. Location Undisclosed. A day after the her death; after leaving the unconscious body of the ‘legend’ in the warehouse with Aeryn’s lifeless frame, the knock on the door finally came. I opened it and was not in the least bit surprised to see Nathaniel stood in the hallway, a grin plastered across his face. I sighed. "You…again?" "Yes me…again," Nathaniel replied. "I just wanted to thank you for…" "Don’t," I told him, "I neither want, nor need your thanks." Nathaniel nodded, understanding. "Nevertheless, you have it." "Is that it?" I asked. "Will you go now?" "When I’ve healed," Nathaniel began, gently tapping his ribs for effect. "We’ll meet again, Winters. And next we will be seeing who is better." "I already know the answer. It’s me. I’m better. Deal with it and move on," I shut the door in his face, made my over to my way over to my sofa and slumped down into its comforting embrace. I was glad that he didn’t want to press the issue about going into battle with him so soon after our little experience. I had wounds of my own that needed to heal (not all of them were physical) before I could even begin to think about the notion. Besides, I have another legend to face this week; Seymour Almasy in the first round of the GTT5 and I was already behind on my preparation for the epic battle. Besides, it would be a stretch, even for me, to defeat two legends in the same week. The End
