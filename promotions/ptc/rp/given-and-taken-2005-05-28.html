"Given and Taken"

28 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-05-28 * * * * * Furious, Typherion paced back and forth on his balcony – which overlooked the conjoined battlefield of Hell and Heaven – completely irate at what his counterpart, The Almighty Himself, has done. “She’s bearing a child? His child?” Typherion barked at the three men standing a few feet away. “It would appear that way,” They replied, all in monotone unison, “Although MacGregor was unaware of the situation until we had already started bringing him to us.” Typherion, The Devil himself, stood there and seethed with anger. Like previous, the area around him started to split and crack with electricity. Where normal humans turned red and clenched their fists, the Devil’s anger ripped apart the atmosphere of Hell. “I can’t believe he would risk something so great.” Typherion said, his gaze shifting across the miles and miles of rolling hills, towards the far-off Heaven. His demonic gaze intensified on the Palace of God, where his heavenly counterpart and nemesis was undoubtedly staring back with a smile peeking out from behind his white beard. “In essence, our enemy has potentially given us a more powerful weapon.” The Three said together, their voices overlapping so well that it barely sounded like more than one voice, let alone three. The devil nodded. He cupped is hands behind his back, and looked out over the battlefield. Millions and millions of troops were lined in camps, ready to fight. He could see his Generals, taller than normal soldiers, built more for battle than the soldiers themselves, barking orders at the minions. He only had to think an order, and the Generals would convey it to the troops. “I’ll give Him credit, I didn’t think he would play this hand,” Typherion started, a smile crossing his face, “But you said MacGregor was already walking down the path you’ve laid for him, and into our arms?” The Three replied, “Yes, m’lord.” “Then we’re going to up the ante. MacGregor will be ours, but lets make him think that he’s invincible. The higher he rises, the more tragic his ultimate fall will be. And his child will be witness to it, just like MacGregor to his father.” “What are you going to do, sir?” “He’s already a gifted grappler. He’s found his passion in life. I’m going to make him one of the best that ever lived, giving him all the talent he’ll ever need. In time, greed will overpower his senses, and he’ll be ours.” The Three smiled in unison, matching the grin across Typherion’s face. The Devil doesn’t play cleanly. Typherion snapped his fingers, “So it’s done. God may have given him an heir…a direction in life to keep him on the right track…but I’ll give him something no son or daughter could ever give him. And that’s fame and fortune, and the sinful future they bring.” * * * * * I guess you could say, amid the chaos of the worst night I’ve ever known, that my life took a new direction. On a night when I searched back through my family history and found out that the four generations above me – my father, my grandfather, my great grandfather, and my great great grandfather – had all committed either sin or suicide, you would assume that my outlook on life would have become undeniably grim. And on that same night, when my grandmother was taken from this world by a violent heart attack…you could assume that I would drink myself into oblivion. Well, I did. That was a nearly a month ago…but also on that night, my ex-girlfriend Heather told me that she was bearing a child. My child. Even though the events of that night have made her keep her distance over the last few weeks, she’s allowed me to talk to her on mild occasions via the telephone and described what she thought happened. She assumes that one night when we made love, the condom broke and she became pregnant. But two months of menstruation made her think she wasn’t carry a child, and all was well, but it appears that sometimes (to Heather) that women can get their periods while in the first trimester of a pregnancy. The night she told me, she was two months along. As I write this, she is entering her second trimester. She hasn’t learned the sex of the baby yet, and I doubt she will until she’s in the hospital holding our child in her arms. But I know what really happened. I know that a tug-of-war is still taking place. I’m aware of my importance in the events of all things here and there (mostly there), so I’m fairly sure of what is going on. So I decided to better my life. I entered the Cruiserweight 2 Tournament, and have advanced to the finals. I’ve pleaded constantly with Heather to take me back – although she’s insanely reluctant after viewing a letter and roses in my home that I didn’t write. She’s willing to talk to me on the phone, and hasn’t built up the will to see me in the flesh. Since that night, I’ve tried to give up alcohol again. But it’s not working, and my body seems to weaken to near-hospitalization levels when I try to stop. I think its Hells bidding, keeping me addicted to the bottle. But I made a promise with Heather: The story of Archibald MacGregor is going to go on for sometime, and I want her to be a part of it. I want our child to be a part of it. I want to give my child the life that I’ve long desired. A life where my father wouldn’t have shoved me into every possible sport in an attempt to honor his fallen friend. A life where father and son (or daughter) can co-exist and accept each other for who they are. I have my faults, and am working on them. I’m sure my child will have his/her faults, and we’ll work on them together. The Devil continues to whisper in my ear, but I think God is the one pushing the earplugs deeper into my skull making it impossible for me to listen to the demonic seduction. Like I’ve said before. My life doesn’t stop with this chapter of my memoirs, and it will keep going. I’ll keep writing. I’m sure there will be battles to be won and lost in my future, and the future I might possibly have with Heather and our newborn child. But I’m going to fight. And I can only hope that it leads me where I’m supposed to go. I can drink myself stupid, and accept the power that I know I’ll be granted. I can’t deny that this is enticing. But to lead a life of sin isn’t anything compared to watching your own son take his first step, or graduate from college, or marry his long-time sweetheart. I want to be around for a long time. I want to watch a possible daughter celebrate her Sweet 16. I want to watch my son try out for his first sports team. As I write this, I can honestly say that I have but one fear. I’ve never been afraid of death, but now that my bloodline will continue for another generation, I know that they’ll be after him, or her. I know that things will happen to test my patience, my resolve, and my devotion. I fear that my defiance will only be the ultimate downfall of my child. But it’s a war that I’m willing to fight. And I’m going to do it with Heather, and our child. As a family. * * * * * On the morning before leaving to face off against Seymour Almasy in the first round of the Cruiserweight 2 Tournament, Archibald MacGregor once again made a visit to the park in the center of Oxford, Pennsylvania. For years, his father had wanted him to “Honor the fallen” by becoming great at something. To take his name and make it a prominent fixture in some fashion, somewhere. Looking up at the bronze statue of his namesake, Archibald Matthews, the 29-year-old Archibald MacGregor knelt down on his knees and started picking the weeds away from the base of the statue. An hour later, after the weeds were all picked clean, he started to clean the statue itself: removing all of the bird-droppings from the dark brown metal. He took an old rag and cleaned out the layers of dirt that had accumulated over what appeared to be years. When he was done, he stood up and smiled at the statue; content with his way of honoring the man he was named after. It might not be a World Series Trophy, or the Presidency of the United States…but as long as Archibald Matthews’ statue remained in Oxford, the boy who was named after him would tend to it twice a year. Archibald was confident his father would approve. And perhaps a few weeks later, he could tend to the statue once more: victor of the Cruiserweight 2 Tournament. In time. * * * * * The Palace of God was cut into the side of a mountain, with magnificent spires and arches of white marble shooting up and out of the rocky face. Hundreds of thousands – perhaps millions – of open windows lined the palace. This palace was home to those in Heaven, and at the top, a magnificent balcony stretched out and hung in the air. Standing at the end of this balcony was the Lord himself, God Almighty. Much like the Devil chose a look that created envy and lust and desire, the Lord simply chose to look like a figure that everyone loved. In fact, he resembled what most humans would describe as Santa Claus. Aged, overweight, and a brilliant white beard and rosy red cheeks. He wore a white robe, and his balding head glimmered in the brilliant sunshine. From behind him, a messenger approached. He turned to greet his messenger, tearing his gaze away from the rolling hills of Heaven, away from the battlefield that his angels would soon go to war on, and away from the Palace of the Demon, which was far off in Hell. “Typherion has matched your play,” the messenger said with a slightly musical voice. Not musical in the sense that bells and strings accompanied it, but the melody of the messengers voice was pleasing to the ear, even when delivering bad news. God’s gaze hovered back across the battlefield, and landed on the far off Palace of his nemesis. “What has he done?” “Where we gave MacGregor a child, something to love and cherish and keep him away from the dark path that his family has taken for so long, Typherion has given him incredible talent in the thing he loves second most: his passion for wrestling.” Stroking his pale beard, God said nothing. The Almighty stood stoic, and thought. “Archibald has been given enough talent to easily defeat those in the tournament he’s entered in, and enough talent to do it so easily that his confidence will soon turn to cockiness, and ultimately be followed by dominance, greed, and deception. Even with his child on the way, his vision will become blurred.” The messenger stopped for a moment, thinking about the words he wanted to use, “Even with the memoirs he has been keeping, with the talent awarded to him…he will eventually burn them as nonsense.” The Lord spoke, “But even when that happens, he will still be a noble father. A loving father.” “Yes, m’lord.” “How do I weigh the loss of Archibald to Hell, against the gain of his child into Heaven?” The Lord asked, rhetorically. “It’s a question that I can’t allow to be asked.” “What are you saying?” the messenger wondered. Far below them, God’s angels were preparing for battle as well. Camps of millions were stationed, and ready to be sent off into the rolling hills of The Battlefield. “I can’t allow that to happen. We’ve got to prevent the unborn child from being lured into Hell and Archibald, himself.” “But how, sir?” The Lord sighed, and turned to his messenger, “By taking away the devilish gift he’s been given. Where Typherion gave him talent, I’ll take it away. He can’t be given something that he’s unable to control. And I’m sure we’ll feel the consequences of my actions as The War wages on. But it must be done.” The millions of angels in Heaven all stood at once, and started their march towards The Battlefield that stood between Heaven and Hell. On the opposite end, Hell’s minions suddenly rose in response, and started their march as well. “Are you sure you’re willing to do this, m’lord?” “I don’t have a choice, anymore. I must take it back. Whether he wins or loses will now be on his own shoulders, and not the Devil’s bidding. It will be all Archibald Allen MacGregor. I just hope it works.” “Are you saying it’s too late, m’lord? That you might not be able to intervene? How is that possible?” “Typherion and I are of equal power. You know this. I don’t know if I’m able to take away what he’s bestowed upon Archibald.” “I see, m’lord. I’m sorry for questioning your resolve.” “Forgiveness, I do with ease. It’s ripping away someone’s dream that I’m no good at.” God said with a sigh, waving his hand away from his own heart. The messenger took a step forward, and put a consoling hand on The Lord’s shoulder, “It appears we will have to wait and see.” God sighed, watching the armies march closer together. Soon, the first volley would be sent flying, and The Great War will have started. It will last many millennia. “That’s what I fear.” A sole tear fell from the eye of God. Giving hope was his specialty, not removing it. * * * * *
