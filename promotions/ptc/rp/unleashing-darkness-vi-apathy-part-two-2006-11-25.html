Unleashing Darkness VI: Apathy (Part Two)

25 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-25 Comfort Zone The Hole. The Bronx, New York. Today. Despite the disgustingly sour twinge of the ale, McKail managed to finish his flagon in something of a quick fashion and, against his better judgement, ordered a second when the waitress finally came around to collect the insect-laced empties. McKail winced at the ants nest clearly nestled in the bottom of one of the flagon’s, but noted, with detached interest, the waitress glaring at it as though it were the most natural thing in the world. McKail eyed the man sitting opposite, noticing for the first time the man squirm uncomfortably in his seat. McKail grew concerned; a lot was at stake and the last thing he needed was this clown to fuck things up. "What the fuck’s up with you?" The hollow man gazed back at him, his hollow face placid and his eyes still showing no signs of life. "Nothing." McKail fixed his eyes upon him and leant back in his chair; it wasn’t the most convincing of responses and he wanted the hollow man to know it. "Fuck that. If there’s something wrong, I need to know about it." "There’s nothing wrong, man," the hollow man began, breaking eye contact with McKail and gazing away toward the old jukebox lodged in the back corner of the room. "You’re one crazy fucker and this is fucked up, is all." McKail frowned, ignoring the crazy part; it’d been noted many times before. "Fucked up? What I’m paying you to do seems fucked up to you? Seriously?" "Yeah," the hollow man confirmed. "This job you want me to do; it isn’t what I’m used to, it’s--" "What the fuck!" McKail exclaimed, seemingly unconcerned by volume of his own voice. "Are you getting a conscience on me all of a sudden?" The hollow man shook his head. "That ain’t it at all. I lost my conscience a long time ago. I’m just not comfortable--" "Comfortable?" McKail interrupted. "You’re having second thoughts because this is out of your comfort zone?" McKail shook his head in bewilderment and then added, "You’re a hired killer for fuck’s sake!" "Shhhhhhhhh," the hollow man warned, glancing around the room, desperately trying to gauge how many people had heard the outburst. If anyone did hear them, they didn’t react; hell, it was probably a common occurrence in a place like this. McKail had already noted the various stains of blood deeply embedded within the flooring. The waitress returned to the table, chewing gum as though it were a suitable pass time. McKail nodded a thank you at the woman, who promptly ignored him and he noted that her face padded with entirely too much make up; so much so, you had quite a difficulty making out the fresh bruising behind the thickly laid foundation and unnecessary mascara. She promptly left and McKail realised that the hollow man had been glaring at him for the entire time, his eyes no longer dead; now they flickered with fire. "You do that again and I’ll snap you’re fucking neck here and now," he warned, cooling himself off with gulp of his beer-like substance. McKail remembered himself, precisely where he was and with whom he was dealing. "Sorry. I just can’t have this fucked up…" The hollow man nodded, presumably accepting the apology at face value. "I know you can‘t, but rest assured, I am a professional. Mark my words, it’ll get done right." McKail took a mouthful of his ale and allowed the sour alcohol sear his stomach lining right off, then added, "Make sure that it does." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Good Intentions Taken from a letter penned by Mary McKail. Original time of writing: six months ago. I just want you to be happy after I’m gone, but I know you will be anything but and I need to stop you from taking things too far. I didn’t know how else to do it, Jacob and so I chose to manipulate you as I only I ever could. You are undoubtedly working your way up the hierarchy of the wrestling industry at a rate too fast to be measured, without a care for anything but success and I know you are doing it to make me proud. But, sadly, such was not my intention when I made you promise me to fulfil your dreams; I just wanted you to move on without me and to build a new life for yourself. I hate to tell you this, but the meaning behind the promise was empty, Jacob. My goal is not to see you conquer the wrestling industry, it is much more simple than that. I simply want you to live. I want you to move on, find yourself another woman and build a family for yourself; the family we always wanted to build together. You must start anew, Jacob. Mourn me if you will, but please start anew. This is the true intent of the promise, Jacob, and no matter how much you may dislike it, you’ve already given me your word to see it through. I need you to keep your oath, Jacob, for both our sakes. I love you now and always, Mary. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Manipulation Some Back Alley The Bronx, New York. Three Days Ago. They pulled up opposite of the suspect alleyway close to midnight. For hundredth time that hour, McKail found himself wondering just how he got himself into this mess and he was promptly reminded, as Kate lurched forward in the passenger seat, peering out of the window, desperate to catch a glimpse of her ex-boyfriend. Apparently, he liked to spend the majority of free time working out of a dark alley in a bad part of town. Beautiful, McKail silently exclaimed, noting a good number of unsavoury characters lingering around its entrance way. Just fucking beautiful. Kate had maintained her nervous mannerisms throughout the entire journey and had been prattling on about how her ex-boyfriend did this to one guy and that to another. McKail idly suspected she was trying to intimidate him, in order to better siphon the money out of him and truth be told, it could’ve worked too. That is, it could’ve worked if he’d been listening to her. He caught bits and pieces here and there, but his mind wasn’t focused; he just couldn’t get that damned letter out of his head. Why did Mary do that to me? His bewildered mind raged. Why did my own wife have to deceive me in such a way? She could’ve just been straight with me; she could’ve just told me outright what she wanted for me! Quickly, he calmed himself and his rational mind regained control, Couldn’t she? "There," Kate announced, effectively snapping him out of his reverie. She pointed to a heavy-set man, wearing a sleek brown leather coat, black trousers and expensive Italian shoes. If that didn’t broadcast his wealth, the hefty medallion hung around his neck hammered home the fact. "That’s him." McKail eyed him warily. Working out of a back alley late at night in this part of the neighbourhood was a transparent indication of what this fool was capable of. McKail sighed -- this wasn’t going to be easy. "Are you sure about this, Jacob?" Kate questioned. "Are you sure you want to go through with this?" McKail shook his head. "I never wanted to go through with this, but we don’t have many other options, do we?" "We have one," Kate muttered under her breath. McKail heard her and shot her a cautionary glance. "I’m just saying." McKail directed his attention back to Mr. Bling, the ex-boyfriend, across the way, whilst she decided to push the issue, "You could save yourself the hassle and just loan me the money, you know." McKail ignored her and she didn’t dare push the issue any further. A wise option as far as he was concerned. "Let’s just get this over with, shall we?" He said, opening the car door and stepping out into the bitter night air. He shivered some, before wrapping his entirely too thin jacket closely around him. He closed the door behind him and Kate came over to joined him. "I’m just going to tell you one last time," she whispered, as her ex-boyfriend locked eyes upon them both. "This is a very bad idea." "Well," McKail began, setting off in the direction of their target, "nobody ever did accuse me of being an intelligent man." Kate sighed and stayed close behind him. "And nobody ever will either…" The ex-boyfriend eyed McKail up as he approached and ignored Kate completely. McKail stopped a relatively safe distance away and made sure to establish eye contact immediately. The ex-boyfriend produced a toothy grin and glared at Kate as she popped over McKail’s shoulder tentatively. "I see you’ve managed to manipulate somebody else into bailing you out again, Kate," his imitation Bronx accent embarrassingly transparent. Trying to sound meaner than he actually is? McKail considered, desperately grasping for an answer. Just trying to fit in with his surroundings? Or something more… "Don’t you have any pride?" "Don’t listen to him, Jacob," Kate whispered directly into his ear. "He’s just trying to turn you against me." McKail grunted and shot them both a look of disgust. The last thing he wanted was to be stuck a fucking domestic disturbance. Fuck that… "I don’t know who you are, man," the ex-boyfriend began, "but she’s a user and a troublemaker. You’re best off away from--" McKail raised an open palm and the ex-boyfriend became silent. "Enough posturing, please. Bottom line is, she doesn‘t have your money." Mr. Bling produced another toothy grin. "Direct and to the point -- I like that." His grin faded and he developed a more sinister expression, as he nodded them. "Let’s see if I can’t reciprocate." McKail heard a squeal and turned to see a burly individual seize Kate under the arm and cart her off toward her ex-boyfriend, a smaller man dressed in a knee-long black leather jacket and completely unnecessary sunglasses stood just to the right of him, shooting him a vicious grin as eye contact was established. McKail was hardly surprised and cared even less. He turned back toward Mr. Bling, his face undecided, just as Kate was propped upright in front of her ex-boyfriend. She was shaking like a leaf, but McKail couldn’t bring himself to care. His mind was still transfixed upon that damned letter and he could tell it wasn’t going to let up any time soon. Mr. Bling the ex-boyfriend caressed Kate’s chin, despite her best efforts to pull herself away, and at the same time kept an ever watchful eye on McKail. "I want my money," he told her. McKail couldn’t tell whether he was addressing him or Kate for definite, but assumed it was the latter. "And I want it now. You remember what I said I’d do to you if you didn’t get it, huh? Do you remember?" He leant back and put his weight behind a backhand slap that sent Kate sprawling backwards onto the floor. Their reactions told McKail everything he needed to know. Her expression was of shock, not at the pain, but at the fact that he’d actually hit her. Whereas he stood there looming over her for the longest time, with a hint of regret briefly flickering across his face. He composed himself quickly enough, but by then it was too late. It could be that they still had feelings for each other and the exchange reminded them both of that, even if neither of them were willing to admit it. Could be something else entirely. Could be he was getting played again, just like his wife had played him, and he’d fallen for it again, or partly had. Fuck this! He silently cursed. Truth was, he didn’t actually care either way. He’d had enough of it and had his own problems to deal with. McKail shook his head, still disgusted with himself, and turned to leave. "Fuck this, I’m gone." "Where the fuck do you think you’re goin’?" Mr. Bling’s voice boomed. "Jacob…" Kate trailed off, in between sobs. The leather clad fool with the sunglasses stepped out in front of him, preventing his departure. "You’re not going anywhere." "Really?" McKail asked, flinging the much smaller man aside with relative ease. He hit the unforgiving brick wall with a thud, knocking his glasses clean off his face. It was only then McKail realised precisely why the fool was wearing the damned things in the first place; it was to hide the fear deeply embedded within his eyes. McKail allowed a sneer as he continued onward. "Jacob, please no! He’s going to kill me!" Kate wailed. The last flicker of McKail’s conscience called in its marker and forced him to glance back briefly, as Kate’s ex-boyfriend had now produced a nine-millimetre gun and had it pointed at her head. "I’ll fucking do it, man!" "Jacob, please…he’s going to kill me!" "Yeah," McKail responded, as he continued toward his car, regarding their little performance with indifference, "sure he is." Inside his car, just as he’d inserted the key into the transmission, he heard the gunshot. Somehow it didn’t come as a shock to him and it didn’t let it stop him from firing up the engine. He fired into the air or the ground or something, he reassured himself. Yeah, that’s what he did. Just part of the show, that’s all. But the thoughts quickly evaporated into the bitter night air. Nothing mattered to him now. None of this. Nothing of what he’d done for the last six months within the wrestling industry. He was living a lie, had been since he’d made the fraudulent promise to his manipulating wife. Now, the ties he’d once had with the rest of the living world were well and truly severed. He had nothing to life for anyone, so what was the point of living at all? He drove away. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Arrangement The Hole. The Bronx, New York. Today. "Look, I feel as though I should explain myself a little better," the hollow man began, already beginning to slur his words. They’d finished their second flagon’s of ale in silence and were each three quarters of the way through their third. McKail couldn’t even taste the sour tang anymore. "It’s not that I won’t do it or that I’m incapable of doling it correctly. It’s just something that’s not asked of my kind very often. So, you can‘t blame me for being a little uncomfortable about it." McKail nodded in acceptance. After all, his request was slightly out of the ordinary for an assassin of his alleged calibre. "So you’ll do it then?" "If the money’s right," the hollow man agreed. McKail stood, deciding not to finish his beer after all; he’d already gotten what he came here for, but the hollow man unexpectedly continued, "But I just have to know one thing…" McKail retook his seat, glaring at the hired gun warily. "Look, I thought we agreed no questions asked?" The hollow man finished off his beer and nodded. "We did. But I have to know why you want this and that is not negotiable." McKail’s filled with rage. "I do not intend to explain myself to anyone, least of all you…" "You’re right, no question about it," the hollow man said, his dead eyes making McKail a little uncomfortable all of a sudden. "But wouldn’t it make you feel more comfortable, if you told me the reason why you want this done." McKail sighed. "No it wouldn’t. Besides, even if I did tell you, you wouldn’t understand." The hollow man grinned and somehow that put McKail even more at unease. "Try me." McKail shook his head. "I’m doing this for my late wife." He allowed the sentence to hang in the air for a moment, as he shuffled out of the booth. Upon seeing the hollow man’s now confused expression added, "I told you, you wouldn’t understand." "You weren’t wrong," he replied. McKail turned and headed toward the door, stopping mere centimetres before it. "Promise me one thing." This time it was the assassin who eyed him warily. "Okay." "When you come for me," McKail began, finding it difficult to get the words out. "I don’t want to see it coming." "I‘m a professional," the hollow man assured him. "Of course you won’t see it coming." McKail left. The End
