Just Another Night: The Father/Son Talk

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-10 Like a child piecing together a jigsaw puzzle, Detective Pride stared at the Gutierrez file for hours, uncertain how the few pieces he had collected would connect together in the end. He had a gut feeling that there was more to it than just a random murder, but he could find nothing to prove it. He needed some sort of proof that this was something far more serious than someone taking the life of a cross dresser, even if it meant that he had something far more serious on his hands. A buzzing sound, occurring simultaneously with a vibration on his left hip, snapped him out of his daze. "This is Pride," he answered. It was a detective from another precinct, a Jason Mandel. "Detective Pride…we have a body you might want to come see." "Why would I want to do that?" "Because he’s holding your business card in his hand." "Son of a bitch," Pride mumbled softly to himself, almost dropping the phone from his hand. "Give me the address and I’ll be right there." Pride grabbed a pencil he had been doodling with earlier, and started jotting down the address. Suddenly, just as he heard the name of the street, he dropped the pencil. "Sapphire." =¤=¤=¤=¤= =¤=¤=¤=¤= There was a reporter sitting on the steps leading up to Sapphire’s apartment, a laptop computer resting on his knees, his fingers gliding across the keyboard as he started on the following days top story. Apparently, the murderer had e-mailed a few photos of the female impersonator that laid lifeless in the apartment upstairs, along with a few choice photographs of Miguel Gutierrez; the skinless body Sapphire knew as Lacey. The reporter was all over the story, enticed by the murderers choice to send him the information; he had a scoop on a story that no one else even knew about. Michael Jenkins enjoyed reading True Crime novels, almost as much as he enjoyed mystery and fantasy stories, so this story was something that could possibly make his career. No longer would he have to slave over a deadline, jotting story after story about the weekly gossip that never turned out to be true. This was his chance to have people whisper his name, as they talk about the man who broke the story on ‘The Skinner’, as he decided to name him. "What are you doing here, Jenkins," Pride asked as he made his way up the stairs of the apartment. Jenkins took the time to lift his head from his laptop to address the detective. "Just piecing the story together for tomorrow’s early edition," he said, his fingers sliding across the keys as if he had been doing this for years. "There’s no story here, Mikey." "Is that so," Jenkins solicited with a raised eyebrow. "Then how do you explain these?" Jenkins turned the laptop for Pride to catch a glimpse of a man dressed in a dark purple sequined dress, a dress that had been blue at one time. The amount of blood that covered not only the floor, but the walls as well, was almost too much for someone to bear. With the amount of time that Pride had been on the job, he managed to hold it back. "Where did you get those?" Pride grabbed Jenkins by the collar, lifting him to his feet as the laptop collided with the cold gray concrete of the stairs. "Hey, that cost a lot of mon-" "I asked where you got that from?" "He e-mailed them to me," Jenkins replied as a single tear trickled down his cheek, watching in agony as his laptop continues to fall down the steps on it’s way to the sidewalk. "Do you have a return address?" "All I had, was on that laptop." Jenkins turned to look Detective Pride in the face. "And you just broke it." "Tell your new boyfriend to get you a new one then." "I have copies of these at home." Detective Pride smiled as he opened the door to the apartment and walked in, "We’ll see about that." The officer at the front door took a glimpse of Detective Pride’s badge and allowed him to walk right in, a simple nod of the head as he stepped back to give Pride ample to room to maneuver past him. Pride was greeted by Detective Mandel. "How are you this morning," Mandel asked. "Not as good as I’d like to be." Mandel did not waste any time, getting right to the topic at hand. "How did you know the victim?" Pride did not hesitate to answer. "He was the only lead I had, on a murder case I’m working on." "Well, it seems the murderer knows who you are." Pride was a bit shocked by the comment. "What makes you think that?" "Because your business card was stuffed into the victim’s hands…along with a note." "A note…" Pride asked suspiciously. "What sort of note?" Mandel started walking, Pride not that far behind. "We sent it over to our forensics team, just in case the suspect left any prints on the paper, as well as doing some blood work to figure out if it all belonged to the victim." "I doubt the guy got sloppy after all of this." "Well, no one could ever be that thorough." "Then you don‘t understand just what you’re in for. I don’t see this guy making a mistake after being so thorough with everything else. He doesn’t even leave any hair behind, which is strange considering that is the one piece of evidence we find the most at a crime scene." "You’re impressed with him." "No, I just don’t want this to turn into a trend. We don’t need a serial killer running around loose in the city." Mandel just shook his head, that was something the two of them could agree on. "Any idea what the note said?" "Yeah," Mandel replied. "It said for you to stop pursuing this case, or Little Boy Blue won’t be the last queer you find with pieces missing." The murderer had made this personal, and Pride was not one to run away from a fight. Especially not one as serious as this one. "Guess I better find the bastard quick then." =¤=¤=¤=¤= =¤=¤=¤=¤= "What in the hell did you do?" Jayce Bradley was furious, Russell Braun had shown up unexpectedly; with good news. "Killing that cock sucker is going to have them running around in circles." Russell Braun was beaming with pride, not only had he cleansed the World of yet another who was unworthy to live past the rapture, but he had given Detective Pride someone new to pursue. "They’re going to be focusing their attention on the fag bars around the city, following around a serial killer that doesn’t exist." Jayce sat directly across from Braun, his elbows propped on the table as his head rested in his palms. He couldn’t believe what he was hearing, and he could not believe that Braun would come all this way to just let him know what he had done. There was more behind this visit than what Braun was letting on, but it wasn’t something that he was going to be easy to find out. "You’re getting sloppy old man." Contempt dripped from the words as they rolled off his tongue, anger released with every breath taken. "You’re angry," Braun began, "you’ll understand that this was all for the better once you sleep on it." "Sleep on it!?" Jayce sprang from his seat, the sudden jerk tossing the chair a few feet through the air before it came crashing down with a thud. "You didn’t kill some spic or nigger, Russ! You killed a white man…one of us!" "One of us…" Braun stood in disbelief, shaking his head in disgust of the words that he heard spewing out of Jayce’s mouth. "That queer was not one of us, he swallowed cock for Christ’s sake. How could you ever consider his type one of us…it’s blasphemy!" "You’re going to get us caught, you stupid son of a bitch!" Jayce shook his head as he paced to and fro, the rage turning his face a slight shade of crimson. "I did this so we wouldn’t get caught," Braun yelled in response. "I did this for you!" "For me…" Jayce stood dumbfounded, pressing his forefinger hard against his own chest, jabbing it repeatedly. "When have I ever asked you for anything!? When have I ever told you to do any fucking thing for me!?" "You don’t have to ask, son, I just -" "Stop calling me that!" Jayce’s words stopped Braun mid-sentence, taken aback by Jayce’s sudden outburst. "Stop calling you what," Braun stood there staring at a man that he once considered to be his own flesh and blood, raised as if he was produced from his loins. "Son…you want me to stop calling you son?" Jayce turned away from the old man, a bit surprised he had yelled out in such a way. "Yes." A single word response, but one that would rip the small dime sized blood vessel known as Russell Braun’s heart. "Fine, I understand." A knock on the door breaks up the awkward silence that just seems to hang in the air for a few seconds. Seconds that seemed to last an eternity. "Who is it," Jayce asked as he made his way toward the door. Braun started grabbing a few things he had brought in with him, as a voice echoed from the other side of the large wooden slab. "Hotel security, the neighbors reported some yelling." "Son of a bitch," Jayce mumbled to himself, unlatching the chain that secured the hotel room door. His demeanor seemed to change within a blink of the eye, as a grin spread quickly across his face. "What can I do for you?" "Like I said," the blonde haired man in the light brown uniform peeked in to check on things before he walked through the door frame, his hand almost hovering over the service revolver that was holstered at his hip. "The neighbors reported some screaming coming from the room." "Everything okay," he asked as his eyes surveyed the area. "Everything’s fine," Jayce said with a calm grin. "We were just arguing over who was the better team, San Antonio or Detroit." "That shouldn’t even be an argument," the once nervous security guard seemed to calm down a bit, his hand slowly moving away from the revolver. "Everyone knows it’s Detroit." "Yeah, that’s what I say." Braun simply shrugs his shoulders, playing along. "Do I know you from somewhere?" The security guard gives Jayce the once over, his eyebrow cocked as he tries to figure out where he knows him from. "I doubt it," came Jayce’s quick and rehearsed response. "Yeah, I do." Braun slowly reached his hand into the black duffel bag that he had brought with him, his grip tightening over the handle of the twelve inch serrated blade he used on a regular basis. With a grin, the security guard realizes just who Jayce is. "You’re one of those wrestlers here for the big tournament, aren’t you?" Jayce smiled, nodding as Braun released his grip on the knife. "Yeah, that’s me." "I knew it…" The security guard started fumbling through his pockets, "I know I had a paper in here somewhere." "How about I just get one, and a pen while I’m at it." "That would be swell." The security guard was filled with glee, as Jayce walked back with a paper and pen in hand. "Wait ‘til the guys hear I met Joe Miller!" Jayce smiled; the pen and paper still in his hand, then he slammed the door shut in the security guard’s face. Guess Jayce isn't as popular as people think. "Now where were we?" Jayce spun around, but Braun was nowhere to be found. "Russ…" The blinds hanging in front of the sliding glass door were swaying from the slight breeze coming through the now open door. Jayce made his way closer to the door, peeking out onto the empty balcony. "Damnit!" Jayce pounded his clenched fist onto the steel railing, no sight of Braun anywhere.
