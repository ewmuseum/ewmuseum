A Bucket Full of Kryptonite

10 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-10 With four successful matches under his belt, and a burgeoning reputation as a tournament wrestler, Doctor Curiosity was facing his life with confidence and vigour. He had his wife wrapped around his little finger, and nobody thought that someone as young and green as Sebastian Moraco would cause him any sleepless nights in the run up to the bracket finals stage of GTT4. Life, however, was about to continue its annoying habit of throwing a spanner in the works just when you think that everything is fine. Rarely has a spanner looked as fine as Collette Goodman, but armed with the knowledge that she had been exposed, she intended to strike back against Doctor Curiosity and his brother, Eegor. Complacency can be a killer in many situations. It can cause people to lose their concentration for a fatal second when driving, and it can lead to experienced fighters letting their guard down for a split second too long, allowing an unexpected blow to strike them. Doctor Curiosity was about to take a fall… but would he learn enough from it to avoid making the exact same mistakes against Sebastian Moraco? ------------------------------------------- It was night outside the Curious Laboratory, though not one of the dark and stormy variety. It was also night inside the lab, proving that the doctor’s new Curious Time Shield was, as Eegor had suggested, nothing more than a cereal box wrapped in tin foil. At such a late hour, was the doctor sleeping? Resting up for the challenges ahead of him? Was he perhaps preparing a new gadget or gizmo to bring about the destruction of Sebastian Moraco? “Hold ze piñata still, Eegor! I can’t hit it if you duck all ze time!” No. Doctor Curiosity was still celebrating his splendid trickery of Collette by bashing the huge piñata he had built in the shape of Sebastian Moraco with a baseball bat. The object of the doctor’s vengeful bat seemed to be either badly made, or very dented. Beside the doctor’s feet rested four broken bats. “Eegor, I am beginning to tzink zat I should not have made zis piñata out of titanium.” The doctor laughed as he put down the current baseball bat of choice, which was starting to look a little worn around the edges. Eegor peeked over the top of the titanium piñata, and breathed a sigh of relief. Some of those bats had come dangerously close to being broken on his head, as opposed to the head of the fake Moraco. At least Eegor thought it was the head end; Curiosity’s metalworking skills had left a lot to be desired. It was the day after Doctor Curiosity had let Eegor in on his secret; that he was in control of his actions, and that he knew Collette was a scheming witch with nothing on her mind other than bleeding Curiosity dry of his every last penny, though perhaps the doctor used a few less words. Everything had been going well during the following day. Eegor had not wanted to disturb the doctor before mid-afternoon, as Curiosity was a lover of sleep, and interrupting him before he was done was often met with a most curious wrath, which usually caused bleeding. Curiosity had apparently slept well, for his demeanour had been cheerful all day. Whilst he had confided obliquely in Eegor that all was under his control, the metaphorical mask that he wore throughout the day remained in place, and all the formalities of the mad scientist and deformed assistant dynamic were back in place. Eegor even went as far as to mould himself a latex ‘stump’ to attach to his left wrist, before it fell off whilst he was cooking their evening meal, leaving him looking positively normal. “So, master… will you now be preparing to fight Sebastian Moraco?” asked Eegor. “Maracas? Vhy vould I need to do zis? He is a young fighter who has fluked his vay to zis round. My training vith ze big metal box vill stand me in good stead for anything he can throw at me.” “Master, he has reached this point in the tournament. Do you not think you should be taking him more serio…” said Eegor, before Curiosity silenced him with a glare. The assistant wanted to change the subject. He wanted to talk about Collette, and about the doctor’s true feelings. He wanted so badly to talk to his brother, and not the man who was wearing his skin and talking through his lips. He wanted all of this, but he knew that Chris Goodman would only come out when he really wanted to; in his own time and in his own ways. In the silence, the doctor looked around and surveyed the room. It was the main laboratory of the building, stretching out many metres in both directions. The floor and walls were all made from large stone slabs, giving the impression of constantly being in some grand gothic castle. The centrepiece of the room was slightly off to one side; a huge monolithic slab of oak turned in to a workbench for the curious doctor. Atop it lurked hundreds of pieces of scientific ephemera. Bunsen burners rubbed shoulders with beakers of frothing green liquid, and jars containing body parts almost literally did the same with dried out animal carcasses. As a physicist, Doctor Curiosity was quite concerned over the abundance of biological apparatus, but Eegor insisted it was within the doctor’s milieu. Curiosity had just nodded and smiled, hoping it didn’t show that he had no idea what a ‘milieu’ was. Eegor walked over to the grand fireplace, his battered shoes echoing sounds on the stone floor, like a man slapping a wet fish with a sock full of mud. To either side of the large hole in the wall were two large canisters, each full of chopped wood. Needless to say, Eegor was very familiar with the wood, having been the one that chopped it in the first place. With a weary sigh he grabbed a couple of chunks and threw them on to the glowing embers, coaxing yellow and orange flames to leap back to life, enjoying the new fuel they had been given. “Good vork Eegor,” said the doctor, adjusting his cracked spectacles. Eegor raised one eyebrow as he turned. Praise from his brother was as rare as a round passing in a wrestling tournament with everyone turning up for their match. Their eyes met, and something passed between the brothers in a glance. “Come over here,” said the doctor, plainly. ------------------------------------------------ The road to the Curious Laboratory had not been in the best of repair when Chris Goodman had used the last of his money to buy the old building. A few years later, and with nobody willing to pay for the necessary upkeep and repairs on it, the journey to the lab was bumpy to say the least. Collette comforted herself in the knowledge that she could buy herself a lifetime supply of shock absorbers for her car should all her plans come to fruition. Her recent discoveries had left her with something of a quandary. Her husband, and probably his despicable brother too, had found out about her schemes, or so it seemed. This made things dangerous for her. They probably wouldn’t go so far as to harm her, she thought, but he could finalise the divorce quickly, and whilst she would still make money, it would be nothing like what she’d make if she was still married to him when he won the tournament… …which brought her to the other point. In her current position, with access to his laboratory and to ringside, she had been able to assist the doctor in getting this far. If he were to be spending his time extricating himself from a marriage instead of plotting the downfall of his next opponent, then he might not progress any further in the tournament. She had to work out a way for him to remain wrestling, whilst also remaining married to her. Luckily for Collette, she’d always been the resourceful type. ---------------------------------------------------- Doctor Curiosity was sketching out something on a large sheet of plain paper. At various moments he showed the drawings to Eegor, who nodded appreciatively. After a while, the doctor started to chew on the end of his pencil, and Eegor tried broached the only concern on his mind. “This is all very well, master… but do you think perhaps that instead of plotting ways to dump buckets of water on Collette’s head, we should plan something for Sebastian Moraco?” “Vell… zat is an option, Eegor. Neverzeless, I vould prefer to have fun vith Collette. I have many years of torment to get her back for,” replied Doctor Curiosity. A few seconds of silence ensued, before Eegor broke it again, thinking out loud. “Have you ever considered jacking it all in?” “Vhat do you mean?” enquired the doctor. “Well, there are bookies giving your opponent twenty to one odds of beating you.” “Tventy to von?” asked the doc, incredulous. “I know I’m a legendary megastar who vould never lose to a charlatan like Moraco in a million years, but zose are long odds…” “Yeah, they are. You know, if we mortgaged the lab, sold most of the stuff we had, got a couple of loans out and pooled all our money… we’d have quite a bit of cash,” commented Eegor. “Jah, ve vould. Vhat are you trying to say?” “Well, if we put it all on Sebastian Moraco to win, and you lost intentionally, then we’d have twenty times the amount of money we put on. That’d be twenty times a lot of money.” “Tventy times a lot of money,” repeated Curiosity, in an awed tone. “Just a thought. If you were willing to lose, we could be rich. You could retire.” Doctor Curiosity scratched his head, messing his shock of white hair up further than it already was. After a quick patting down, so as to best conceal the receding line of the hair, Curiosity turned to face his brother. His face hardened, and as he spoke, his accent faltered, hanging on for some words and not for others. “Jah, it is a lot of money, but, really, vhat kind of loser gambles on wrestling? You know zat is not what I’ve been doing this for… you know that’s not what all this is about. It’s not the money, it’s the achievement. It’s the fame that comes with proving everyone wrong. You know better than anyone else, Eegor. You know that… zat I have never been accepted in ze scientific community properly, just as I was never accepted as a kid. There’s always… alvays been someone holding me back. Even vhen I became zis man, zis curious doctor, I have been beset by failure. Zey never saw me coming in ze Universal Title Tournament… but I didn’t vin it. I lost three matches short of a title. Zen my name vas in lights for ze fWo Survivor… but I didn’t vin zat either! Rana Venenobody came and took my spot! Right in front of ze vorld, ve teamed to defeat everyone in zat federation. I vas on ze cusp of greatness, Eegor! I vas von little step avay from joining ze elite. Ve von zat match, Eegor, we WON IT!” The last words came out in a screech. Everything that he had said was true. When it all came down to it, the wrestlers from the Survivor contest had decided the winner of the competition, and who would join the federation they were all striving to enter. The wrestlers, his compatriots had done what everyone had always done to him. They turned their back and chose the better looking man. They chose the more personable character. They chose the man who wasn’t Chris Goodman. Curiosity’s eye held a tear in it. For all the success that he had gained, for everything he had achieved, Doctor Curiosity considered himself a failure. Every time he threatened the gates of victory, they were unceremoniously slammed in his face. This tournament was the chance to show the world that it was wrong, and that he could be the final man standing at the end of possibly the biggest tournament the wrestling world had seen. “You still showed them though,” interjected Eegor. “You went on to their show and you won their belt from them.” The assistant pointed over at the fWo Cruiserweight Title that he had won fairly, if in unorthodox fashion. It was scant consolation to his brother. “I don’t care about it, Eegor. I really don’t care. Vhere do I defend zis title? Does anyone even know I have it? Vhen vas ze last time I wrestled outside of zis tournament? No, Eegor, I do not care about zat. Everything, for me, is about zis tournament.” “So, shall we prepare for Sebastian Moraco then?” asked Eegor, placing his arm around his younger brother. The doctor turned and smiled. “Vell, Eegor, I have…” The doorbell rang. “Collette?” asked Eegor. “Jah,” replied the doctor. “And I never got ze bucket ready either.” -------------------------------------------------- The journey had been as long and arduous as ever. Had she been American she might have been concerned by the cost of the constant travelling out into the desert, but having been raised in England she was still constantly surprised by the comparatively low cost of fuel. Slamming the door, she walked to the little buzzer on a stick that comprised the entry system to the Curious Laboratory. Ever since Eegor had changed the locks and made just the one set of keys to open them, Collette had been having to ask permission to enter the lab. This worried her, not least because she was now in grave danger of being expelled from it permanently. She pressed the button, and the drawbridge eventually lowered itself. The intercom remained silent, apart from a brief crackle of static, and what sounded like a voice saying to fetch a bucket. Collette shrugged, and when the drawbridge landed down, strode across it. She gave a quick glance to the moat that surrounded the laboratory, making it look more and more like a castle from the English Middle Ages, and then she was inside. --------------------------------------------------- She walked through the door with confidence, grace and poise. It was at times like that that Doctor Curiosity wondered how he had ever ended up married to such a woman. Still, the moment passed and his mind was whirring again. He couldn’t tip a bucket of water over her head this time, but he could probably have some other kinds of fun with her. Of course, she still believed that they actually slept together! “Can we talk?” asked Collette. “Jah! Vell, of course ve can, my dear. Vhat do you think ve are doing right now?” replied the doctor, with a laugh. “I meant in private,” insisted Collette. “Nein! Vhatever you have to say can be said in front of ze both of us. Ignore Eegor, he is beyond contempt,” said Curiosity, gesturing towards his assistant with indifference. Collette shrugged, and then changed the mood in the room with just five words. “Chris… I think I’m pregnant.” It was a bold move for Collette. She knew it wasn’t true, and she also knew that when her husband thought it through, he would also know it wasn’t true. For the moment though… “Zat is fantastic news! Pregnant! But… but how do you know after only von day?” “A woman knows, my darling,” she replied. “A voman knows what?” “She knows herself.” “Oh,” replied Curiosity, with nothing really to say to that. His mind was starting to go through the gears though, and Eegor was signalling to him out of the corner of his eye. “Just give me von minute, Collette…” he said, before dashing off to join Eegor in the corner. “I’m so overjoyed zat… zat I must go to ze other room!” Curiosity flung the nearest door open, and let himself into the Curious Kitchen. Pots and pans lay discarded seemingly at random. Eegor, never having enough time to clean up his messes, kept it this way. The demands of being his brother’s assistant were too much to keep the entire place in immaculate condition. Curiosity’s attempt to criticise Eegor died on his lips though, as the assistant spoke. “She knows!” The doctor tried to think it through, but… “She must know! There’s no way she’d try that bluff with you! Not after how you broke up!” Eegor insisted. “Ze whole not having babies tzing,” replied Curiosity, biting back a comment about who else had been involved in the end of the marriage. On reflection though, Eegor was right. Curiosity mentally applauded Collette for showing she knew in such an imaginative way. “Jah… but now ve know she knows ve knows about her.” “We do?” asked Eegor. “Jah, ve do. Observe!” He led Eegor from the Curious Kitchen, back out into the main laboratory. Collette was idly filing one of her nails, smirking on the inside, but determined not to show anything of the kind to her in-law family. “So, Collette, my dear… who could ze father be? I’ve just remembered zat ve used protection last night,” lied the doctor. Collette had expected something like that. She reflected that he knew that she knew that they knew that she wasn’t being entirely above-board. So now that she knew that he knew that she knew that they knew, she was left with only one card left to play, and it would only work if he was unprepared for it. It would only work if he had underestimated her. It would only work if he had been complacent. He smiled at her, his confidence… his arrogance even making her feel sick with nerves. He thought that he had every card at his disposal. He knew that she knew that he knew that she knew that they all knew she was being duplicitous, but he also knew that if anyone was going to know anything about anyone else then he was going to start getting very confused indeed. So she tried her final gamble. “Okay, so you know I was lying.” This elicited an arched eyebrow from the curious one. “You know I was lying… so I want the divorce.” Curiosity’s mouth dropped open. Of all the tricks he had expected, this was not amongst them. He had expected her to become bitter or spiteful. He had expected her to beg or plead for him to go easy on her. He had expected something entirely different from what he was facing. He’d been complacent and he was paying for it. Why does she want this? There must be a reason, he thought. She must know something that I don’t. I’ve not done my research and there must be a loophole. There must be a reason for it. There must be… If she did it for a reason, then it must be stopped. “No. Collette, you cannot have the divorce.” His words were music to her ears, and she had to restrain herself from dancing where she stood. Her bluff had worked. Doctor Curiosity had been undone by his complacency. Somewhere else in the United States of America, unaware of these events, Sebastian Moraco may well have been praying that the doctor’s attitude would not change.
