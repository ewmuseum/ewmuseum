Skeletons in the Closet (Act VI): Burning Bridges.

3 January 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2010-01-03 Like any five-star hotel worth its salt, the Viceroy Miami had its own hotel bar, a quiet, well-appointed place for its guests to enjoy a stiff drink before risking contact with the unwashed masses. Though it was only two in the afternoon, after the day Alexandra Pierce had, she needed about seven. There was a reporter waiting for her to continue his story on what life was like for someone in the wrestling industry – the very same interview she'd spent the last seven hours unintentionally avoiding, and not one she was eager to see continuing after the half-fight, half-romp she'd had with the woman she loved. So when Alexandra slipped out of the room she shared with Amy Campbell and made her way for the lounge, she'd donned one of the Red Raver's collection of zip-up sweatshirts, a black one featuring the backwards-S Bizarro logo, using its deep hood to shadow her face. "Welcome to the Diamond Room," the bartender said with a quiet smile. "What can I make for you?" "Vodka tonic, hold the lime," Alex muttered, levering herself into a barstool, her head down. One long-fingered hand touched the mermaid's tear at her throat thoughtfully. SCCW's Spider in the Web wasn't one for introspection – when one had done the kinds of things that Desade did, crises of conscience could be crippling, days-long events. She just wished she'd known where it all went wrong. She had found something – someone – who didn't want her for her skill at infiltration, who wasn't seeking her dark mind, who saw her as more than the monster used to frighten children. She had magic in her hands, and now it was slipping through her fingers, and she couldn't determine why. No, that wasn't quite true. She just didn't want to admit why. The bartender was a thin, Hispanic man (her well-trained ears caught his accent, one that bespoke his Cuban upbringing) who knew enough to leave her the hell alone, and she'd charged the first drink (and its two brothers, who met a similar fate) to the room. Let the fucking GTT people pay her to wallow in her misery. If the last two days of 2009 went the way she imagined they'd go, they might just have to find a replacement for her, anyway. Quiet, sullen, and by-God lonely, Alex's razor-sharp instincts dulled in the half-hour of alone time she allowed herself. She knew she should be alert, she just... couldn't be bothered. The world was coming to get her and she'd just decided to let it. Which was how she missed the arrival of the last person she probably wanted to see, just as the clock ran out on those thirty minutes of brooding and boozing. Perhaps he was not the last one, but Jared Sykes was certainly on the list. Sykes, SCCW's "Black Sheep" and reigning Gateway Champion, was Amy Campbell's best friend in the business – a relationship that stirred up a hornet's nest of controversy on both sides of the federation's locker room. And that was before he kissed Amy. Sure, it was a drunken mistake, an error in judgment on Amy's part that she'd had the good sense to confess, but that didn't change the fact that the night Alexandra learned of the indiscretion, she'd punched her speed bag so hard it came flying off its hook. It was one of the weights the Spider felt crushing their relationship, one of the things that resulted in the bruising session she'd come from. Sykes didn't look like much – just a hair under six feet tall and only two hundred pounds if you made him hold a set of barbells, with kind hazel eyes and light brown, flyaway hair. He was average-looking, at least among the male models that dotted the professional wrestling world. Sykes jammed his hands in the pockets of khaki cargo shorts as he trundled up to the bar, about to make (another) very bad mistake. Uncharacteristically, Pierce had her back to the entrance, so all Jared saw was a woman in a hooded sweatshirt at a bar, slamming down a drink. Given Amy Campbell's rather extensive history with liquor, it was an understandable mistake to make, and Jared's steps quickened as he neared the bar. "Hey, what's—" Sykes came up short when she turned to fix him with her medusa's gaze. His face screwed up in a sudden wave of fear, guilt, and concern, his right hand lifting to scratch at the scruff on his cheeks as he tried to remember how to speak. "Jared." Her greeting was neutral, calm. On anyone else, that was probably a good thing, but she'd already imagined jabbing her finger through his eye socket. Pierce straightened as she shook away the red haze, pulling the hood back, to expose her loosely tied mane of hair the color of clotted blood. "Feel free to sit," she said, twisting as she slipped off the stool. "I was just leaving." "Wait!" he blurted, extending a hand – and then quickly retracting it for fear he might lose a finger (or worse the whole hand). Sykes had first-hand knowledge of the kind of vengeance Alex Pierce could bring when provoked, and his most recent offense was certainly worthy of it. "I, uh… I think we kind of need to talk." "We really don't," Alex whispered. "I am not mad at you, Jared. I'm not going to hunt you down or exact any kind of crazy plan to get you." She lifted her eyes to meet his again, her smile tight. "You don't have to worry." Her own hands found the front pocket of the Bizarro sweatshirt, her fists hidden there. "Please?" He arched his brows. "I mean, you’d be well within your right to do all that stuff, but… like… I feel like I owe you an explanation, an apology, a something." Jared swallowed hard. "Even if it does eventually lead to the part where we smack me with something heavy." "No." She lowered her head, pushing past him. "It's my fault. Not yours, not hers, and I'm having enough of an impulse control problem today without listening to some puling apology." "Alex..." Jared raised a hand, but he didn't know what to do with it, didn't know what to say. "Just... like, one drink. That's all I'm asking." She spun on a heel, a single, stabbing stride bridging the distance between them. Unlike many of her male colleagues, Pierce was the same height as Sykes, able to meet his gaze – at least, until the fire in her eyes forced him back a stride. This was a man who spent fifteen minutes dry-heaving after their very first meeting, what seemed like ages ago. "Do you know what the saddest thing is?" she asked before roaring into the answer. "You have the answer I have been dying to find, but I don't really care. I left Amy upstairs, and we wrecked the entire goddamn room, but I'm somehow more miserable than I was when I went up there, and now I have to look at you." "You wrecked the room? Is she... oh!" Jared paused, half-turning. "Ohhh." He swallowed, hand reaching to scratch the back of his neck. "It's fine." His smile was a weak one, the nod that came with it even weaker. "Maybe later, or whatever." Alex stared at the man, her breath escaping in a long burst. "I'm sorry," she said finally, even if she didn't mean it. "I just... I can't deal with all this, not today." Sykes didn't answer or turn, and Alex's left hand tightened in the pouch at her waist. "One drink." "Thanks." He pulled a stool away from the bar with his foot, losing a sandal in the process. Jared took his seat, and while he spoke, his toes went about searching the floor for his shoe. "And you should probably know that I’m pretty horrible at this, even though I seem to have a lot of practice, so… Yeah. It might suck."  Jared closed his eyes and inhaled deeply through his nose, slowly exhaling in an attempt to calm his nerves. It didn’t work. "I want you to know that I take full responsibility for what happened. Like, you shouldn’t even think it’s on you, because it isn’t. At all. Not even a little." He forced himself to turn and face her before his willpower crumbled and his eyes snapped back to the floor. "I don’t want to make excuses… Things got out of hand because I’m a dumbass, and it kills me to think that I took advantage of the situation. It’s like… I dunno… Like I’m genetically programmed to find a way to fuck up every friendship I have." "It is me, though," Alex said, straddling her stool. "I can't be what she wants me to be, and I feel like I'm holding her back while I try. She wants a nice person, a kind soul, someone who won't..." Pierce's breath came out in a long sigh. "I don't know how to be a kind person – I see nice people and I think, 'Lord, what fools these mortals be.'" The Spider nodded for the bartender, who mixed her another vodka tonic. This was the first one she didn't down immediately, withdrawing just her left hand from her pocket, idly spinning the glass. "I don't even know where to start." "To be honest, I’m not sure I’m the guy to ask." Nervously Jared tapped his thumbs together, his eyes still drifting to all parts of the room except where Alex sat. "You know, all things considered, and everything. Besides, for what it’s worth, I don’t think you’re all that bad." A sudden chill flew the length of his spine, and Jared shuddered as a result. "Okay, that came out wrong." "You don't even know me," Alex muttered bitterly, lifting the drink again to touch her lips. "My indiscretions extend far beyond what I've done in a wrestling ring, Jared." Both of Pierce's elbows rested on the bartop, a particularly rebellious strand of rust-red hair slipping across her frozen gaze. "I'd venture to say I'm the worst person you've ever shared a drink with." She shifted just her eyes to Sykes, who jammed a straw into the chunks of ice floating in his cola. "But I'm working on that." “Hrm.” Jared mindlessly stirred at his drink. “Then I’m willin’ to give you the benefit of the doubt. Have been for a while, actually.” He took a quick drink from his glass, noting that the balance of soda to syrup was a bit off. “But, uh, how exactly does one ‘work on that’? What is there to work on?” She tilted her eyes down to the drink. "This morning, I had... let's say I had someone pinned down on a hard tile floor, and I had every intention of smashing their head until the ceramic went red." Alex snorted a chuckle. "I probably should start with not wanting to do things like that anymore." “Right,” he laughed in spite of himself. “Because good people never have bad thoughts.” "I wouldn't know, seeing as I was probably sixteen the last time I would qualify." Alex turned unsteadily on the stool; three vodka tonics in short order was enough to tear through anyone, even, apparently, the Spider. "How do you do it? How do you stay so... nice? This is not a nice business that we are in." “I never thought of myself that way, really. And, if we’re comparing notes, I was a monumental dick for a loooong time.” He finished his soda. Definitely too much syrup. “And then, I guess, one day I just… wasn’t. There’s a story in there, too. Maybe one day you’ll hear it.” "Just..." She crossed her eyes at the errant strand of hair, a long-fingered hand brushing it back. "I don't want to press. But... there has to be a trick, a way to get there. I am aware of the problem – that's the first step, right? All the bullshit self-help books say that." She gestured to her glass, and the bartender gave a thoughtful before he went to work on another. "Guess I just have to find 'How Not To Be An Evil Cunt In Three Easy Steps,' right?" “I wish it was just a three step thing, or easy, or even both. That’s why it sucks, because it’s none of the above.” For the first time all conversation Jared braved a prolonged look in her direction. “Here’s the thing… is this a ‘you’ thing, or a ‘her’ thing, ‘cause there’s a difference. And it’s a pretty sizable one if dramatic, life-altering changes are suddenly on the menu.” "You think I'm just doing this for her, and that when she's leaves me, I'll go back to the way I was before – maybe worse." There was some heat in her voice that she couldn't cool, even with a long gulp of the drink. "That's what everyone else says." “Kind of. Well, no… not exactly.” He paused, taking a minute to choose his words carefully. In light of his own indiscretions it was imperative that he tread very, very carefully. “I mean, there’s really no way to predict all that. I can’t even count the number of days – especially recently – that I just want to snap-out and hit someone or something. It’s just that doing all that really doesn’t accomplish anything. Yeah, you might feel great for about six seconds, but when that’s over you’re right back to where you were, you know?”  Jared rested an elbow on the bar and cradled his chin in his hand. “I don’t even know if this makes any sense. Isn’t really the kind of question I expected to field, you know?" "If 'snapping out' and hitting something hasn't accomplished anything for you, you just haven't been doing it right," Pierce concluded. "But... no, I'm not doing this just for Amy." She wasn't sure how much of that was the truth. "A few months ago, I helped a friend of hers out of a jam, and... and I keep thinking about moments like that. It felt good to help her, but..." "But?" "But it also feels good to hurt people, sometimes. I guess I'm just looking for some guidance, for someone to point me in the right direction." She drained the rest of the glass. "I just don't want her to cry anymore when she thinks about what I might be doing." Jared bit his lip and sighed. A thousand thoughts flashed through his mind, but none of them seemed prudent to mention. At the forefront was how hard it had been for him to actually watch one of those episodes not too long ago. It was, after all, what led him here. "Yeah," he said at last. It was the safest answer he could conjure. "It would be easier to just give up," Alex said, looking down. "To just... let her free. To be bad again." The strand of hair swung down before her eyes once more. "But I can't imagine it. I can't..." Her face came back up, briefly meeting the bartender's, and she shook her head softly. "I can't keep burdening people with my problems. Especially when they refuse to share the answers." Pierce hopped unsteadily off the stool, weaving as she fought to keep upright. "I said one drink. I've had two. So... I'm going upstairs now." Her first few steps away were winding ones, and she offered a tipsy grin over her shoulder. "If you see a scrawny guy with a netbook, don't tell him you saw me, okay?" Jared was quick to his feet. He was fairly certain that she could make it back to her room under her own power, though given her current state of mind it seemed best to make sure that she got their safely. "Hey, hold on a sec." Once again he lost his sandal, though this time he was quick to recover it. "No offense, but you're kind of a mess, so… Congratulations, Alex. You have just acquired a designated walker." The Spider nodded a little against his side. "I'm fine, Jared," she protested. "Everything's swell. I have a kitten now – how can people have a kitten be in bad shape?" Her words aside, Pierce didn't pull away or fight the man as he led her towards the bank of elevators. She only stopped when they were about to emerge into the lobby. "The hood," she mumbled. "Can't let him see it's me." She lifted her eyes to him, smiling a little. "Last thing either of us want is an implication I'm cheating on Amy, y'know?" Sykes reached around her as best he could, tugging the black cloth over her mop of unruly red ringlets, swathing her face in shadow. It was far more difficult than it should have been, but she was leaning on him quite a bit heavier than he expected. As they approached the uniformed security guard, Alex tugged her keycard out of the deep pocket of the sweatshirt, and he let them pass without comment. When the chime sounded, he helped her into the elevator. "I can make it the rest of the..." Her feet tangled, and if not for the elevator's handrail, she probably would have taken a header. "Okay. Maybe I can't make it the rest of the way." The Director tucked her keycard into her slacks, and the moue her lips twisted into would probably have reached "adorable" on any other woman. For Alex Pierce, it just looked weird. "Would you mind?" "Not a problem." Jared followed Alex into the elevator, leaning into a nearby corner. He was close enough to react in the event she lost her balance, but far enough away to keep a respectful distance. "So, how far up we going?" Regardless of what she said, the thought lingered in Jared's mind that the idea of throwing him out the window would suddenly become appealing should they go high enough. "Nice view? Lot of windows?" "Uhm." Pierce screwed up her lips in a moue of concentration. "I'm not really... not really sure, actually." Her smile was an embarrassed one. "I'll figure it out." If he knew her better, he might have recognized the signs she wasn't being completely honest: the way her eyes tracked the car's progress up the floors, her careful positioning against the wall, and, most of all, her hand lifting to tuck a lock of dark hair around her ear. "You're a really... nice guy, you know that?" When her hand fell back to the rail, her grip there was a solid one. "Like... I know I say that a lot, but it's true. Or at least I thought it was true." Using that arm as a pivot point, she turned, two fingers jabbing the stop button. The elevator shuddered to a halt, and Alex turned her grin over her shoulder. Jared looked suddenly frightened, scooting further back into the corner as the Spider skittered towards him in the same lazy line. "Not gonna lie, Jared. I should be really mad at you. And I was. I was really mad. I mean, you kissed her. You kissed my Amy." "I did." His whisper was low, as if even his voice was afraid to come out and show itself. "I did, and it was a mistake. I know that. It was dumb, and… and God, you have no idea how sorry I am, Alex. How much I've been beating myself up over it." He swallowed, his throat suddenly dry. "You both deserved better from me." "We did." The fact that she doesn't disagree only serves to make his expression more crestfallen. "But you're a nice guy. Girls like nice guys." She crossed her wrists grabbing the hem of the Bizarro sweatshirt, pulling it over her head in one smooth motion. "I'm a girl, too, you know. All evidence to the contrary." In a black tanktop, her ring name spelled out in the letters of a spider web, that evidence was quite apparent. Jared Sykes used to have nightmares about Alexandra Pierce coming to murder him in his sleep, but, as it turned out, the opposite was a good deal more frightening. "Alex..." Briefly, he wanted to turn two-dimensional – it was the only way he could be any closer to the wall. "Alex, you're drunk." She stepped between his legs, somehow able to smile up at him even though they were the same height. "Maybe a little." She wrinkled her nose in a grin. "I have no idea what you're doing, but it's freaking me right the fuck out… Amy is upstairs and..." "And you didn't seem to mind it when she was drunk." It was like a switch had been flipped, and all the sweetness and light was gone from her voice. "I figured maybe that was what pushed your buttons. Be a hero, steal a kiss, and make her miserable. Isn't that what revs your engine?" "No, I… I mean it's not…" He tried to duck to the side, to find a way to get away, but a hand fired fast against the wall cut off his exit. "I didn't plan that to happen, but it did, and… and I can't take it back."   Jared contemplated ducking in the other direction, but a hand slammed against that wall put that thought to rest, as well. He bit his lip again as he stared into his shoulder, feeling as though she had somehow hardwired herself into his brain. "I could apologize a thousand times over, and it wouldn't help, would it?" "Eventually, it may," she admitted. "Just not today. Because I don't believe that it 'just happened'. Maybe for her, but not for you." For the second time in just a few hours, she hissed a threat at someone who'd only ever tried to be her friend. "I'm trying to be a better person, because she deserves it – because I deserve it. Then I realized... I finally got what's going on inside your head. And it's hard for me to shake the image, to keep myself from doing very bad things to you." "I probably deserve them." "No one deserves what I can do to them, and that is the lesson I have learned." She stepped back a hair, so they were no longer breathing the same air. "I have more than enough ways to screw up this with Amy... I will not tolerate someone else speeding the process. If you try to steal her from me..." "That's not at all what I want, it really isn't. I mean, I know I've said it before, and I doubt you'd believe me if I said it now, that is not my intention. At all." Between the Miami air and the stress of his current predicament Jared questioned whether his deodorant would hold. He tried to collect himself in between shortened breaths. Then he did something remarkably stupid. "And, I mean, it's not like you had to watch it on national television, or anything." She could formulate no words to respond, and without logic to shield her, Alex's temper get the best of her and her knee came up between Jared's legs, hard, sharp, and without mercy. Sykes' strangled groan was followed immediately by his collapse against the wall, both hands going to his bits. He looked up into her extended finger. "Don't you dare compare the most magical fucking night in my life to some tawdry, drunken kiss," she seethed, jamming the heel of her hand into the stop button, releasing the elevator for the rest of its trip up to their floor. When she wheeled back around, the Spider nearly wobbled, and hooked the sweatshirt over her arm. Pierce dug a small, circular tablet out of her bag, grimacing as she popped it into her mouth. As the bronzed-over doors parted, she put her back to the fallen man, painting on a pleasant smile in case there was someone waiting for it. There was. "Mom?" Quinn Gregory's brows lifted over the rims of her cat's-eye glasses. "I was just coming to find you. I tried the room, but I guess no one's in there. That Levine guy even called my phone looking for you, and... and where's Amy? Did you guys have another fight?" "Ah..." Alex swallowed, the faintest color coming to her lips, this time for real. "It wasn't really a fight, no." She offered an arm to her daughter. "Let's go." "Wait." Quinn rocked up to her toes, peering over her mother's shoulder. "Is that Jared? Is he okay?" Pierce did not look back. "He's fine, doubtless. Just seems that something disagreed with him." Alex stepped to Quinn's side, guiding her back with no small amount of emphasis. "Come on. I just had the most brilliant idea for this evening." "Uhm." The girl looked back over her shoulder to Sykes, who waved her away wordlessly, mustering what he could of a smile. "Okay, I... I guess." Inside the elevator, Jared pressed his forehead to the floor. “Fuck my life,” he muttered as the doors whooshed closed. Interlude. Last night. She slept through the shouting and the fights, but it was the sharp, hollow bang on the metal door that kept her locked away from the world. "Nngh." Her head hurt, the effort it took to force open eyes gummed with sleep far more than she cared to spend. "What?" The word was hollow, her vocal cords feeling like they'd been dipped in molasses. "Maddox." The toneless voice on the other side of the door sounded the same it always did – as far as they were concerned, Detective Helena Maddox was a crooked cop, in league with monsters for the benefit of her bank account. "Visitor." "Tell them to fuck off," she whispered disconsolately, turning her face into the thin thing the jail had the audacity to term a pillow. "I'm not interested." She'd been sleeping more the past few days, but she'd excused it as a byproduct of depression brought on by the fact that the charges were complete and utter bullshit. Maddox wasn't corrupt – far from it, actually. She'd just gotten in over her head. It was supposed to be just another case, just a girl who turned up in the hot tub of a house she didn't own with the probable culprit unconscious beside it. It should have been open and shut, but the trouble with that was that she didn't think Donovan Bell did it, so she'd dug deeper. That was when it really got weird. Her fevered mind, half-dreaming, conjured up the image of a small pimple that, when squeezed, shot out a much bigger spray of pus than she expected. But she'd still popped it, and what she saw beneath the surface scared her. It all sounded so crazy – ancient secret societies acting behind the scenes to ensure a balance only they could see, a whole culture built on espionage and intrigue, a cell of murderous sociopaths who also happened to be professional wrestlers. It hadn't made sense, hadn't sounded real. And then... then she got in a gunfight with a tattooed man on a backstretch of Bay Area highway, and she learned the truth of it all – that the woman she thought responsible for the crime had been set up, that the whole thing was part of some game of real-life chess being played with Alexandra Pierce. Still, it wasn't until she'd been arrested that it seemed possible – when the apparatus of the Order of St. Whoever-The-Fuck destroyed her good name in a single stroke, she had to believe it all. Helena just didn't know what to do with that knowledge. She couldn't tell her colleagues – they'd likely never believe her, and, even if they did, they'd be at risk for the same things that happened to her. The media wouldn't believe her, either; it just didn't sound feasible, and the "evidence" Maddox had amounted to no more than anecdotes whose credibility were at best questionable. So, over the last few weeks, Helena withdrew into herself, hardly eating, sleeping more than was healthy (especially on a rock of a cot). She didn't say a word at her arraignment, refused an interview request from some rag, and stayed in her cell, working over the problem in her mind. There was an answer to every riddle, a solution to every problem. The banging man hit the door again. "Convict!" It wasn't the first time the man stationed at her door raised his voice. Crooked cops don't get proper treatment. "This ain't optional. Wake your ass up!" She dug the heel of her hand into her eye, sitting up as best she could – which wasn't very well at all. In the end, she had the pea green concrete wall to hold her in place as the door buzzed loudly before it opened. The man who came through first had never given Helena his name. Fat and bearded, he was the type of man who would probably be jolly in any other line of work. As it was, his double chins just made his scowl deeper. "Courts decided you needed a psychological evaluation," the Fat Man said. "Because a crazy piece of shit like you might not be capable of standing trial." "Nngh," she repeated. It was the only word her tongue knew, apparently. He loomed over her, his mustache catching the spittle from his shout. "Don't gimme any shit, convict! You tell this man you are calm and rational, and then you go down for your sins, are we clear?" "Guess so," she managed. The Fat Man pulled his hand back as if to deliver a backhand blow, but it never fell. Instead, he grabbed her by the front of her prison-issue jumpsuit. "You'll stand trial if I have to throw you in a hole and forget where I put you." "That's enough of that." The new voice was quiet, neutral, and slightly bored, but, even so, Helena lifted her chocolate brown eyes over the Fat Man's shoulder. The first word she'd use to describe the man was "well-dressed" because nothing but his fine, double-breasted blue suit stood out. He was like a blank slate poured over his skull, with dull, cornflower blue eyes hidden behind horn-rimmed glasses. "Mr. Hodge," the blond man gave the Fat Man a name. "I don't like to repeat myself, sir, so if we could just move to the part where you cease threats and I do my job?" "Sorry." Hodge straightened, digging his beady eyes out of the bags they cowered behind. "I keep telling you she's fine." "I am afraid I must insist upon making that decision myself." The man stood aside, allowing the guard to waddle through the door. "She so much as looks at you cross-eyed, you give me a holler." Hodge didn't turn, pulling the door firmly shut behind him. The man in the dark suit crossed the distance to the bed as soon as he heard the door slam. "Helena, do you know who I am?" He drew a silver penlight from the inside pocket of his coat. "Yeh," she croaked. "What is my name?" "Jo... Jonas." Maddox's eyes wouldn't focus on the nondescript man. "What the hell's... the hell's goin' on?" Elijah Hawke tipped an invisible cap as he turned his back, but Roderick Ashe waited long enough that Hawke had to hit him with the cane before he turned his back as well. "What... what just happened?" Amy's glare moved away from her would-be accountant as he pushed the chair out of his way. "I'm free," Alex said very quietly. "What does that mean?" Amy asked, her hand finding the Spider's. For the first time in recent memory, Alexandra Pierce had no idea how to answer.
