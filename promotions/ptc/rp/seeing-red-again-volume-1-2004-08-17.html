Date: 2004-08-17 Seeing Red Again Volume One: Prelude to Primetime They say that fate, or destiny, you make your own choice, only beckons upon you once. If you don’t answer the call, you have to live with the guilt of not doing something that will be looked upon with valiance for the rest of your days. But, for the selective few that do answer the call, this story would be for you. I thought my days in the ring were over, I thought that I had no business in the squared circle ever again after being humiliated by the hands of Hessian for the PTC Global Title. It seems so far ago, but for me, I remember it as if it just happened yesterday. Trying to climb the ladder rung by rung, with every ounce of my strength going up with me, just to have it cut short at the last moment. The belt snatched away from my grasps, and nothing I could do to stop it, as I lied upon the mat looking up at my foe, the foe that celebrated his victory that day. You never get over a memory like that, in fact it still haunts me every night, to the point that I wake up in a cold and shivering sweat from it. Psychologists couldn’t diagnose me with anything; they couldn’t find the motive behind my madness. They put me into test after test, and finally gave up after all the results came out the same. However, they didn’t dub me as insane or obsessive in the least bit, but that’s what I think I am. I am cursed to remember that day for the rest of my times in the ring, that is, unless I do something to avenge that loss. Sometimes when you step away from the spotlight for so long, as I did, you would think that after a due amount of time, everyone would start to forget about you. But, for some reason that wasn’t the case with me. It seems that to this day, my name has stuck around the business. It is almost as if I were a workhorse that everyone looked up to. Why would somebody chose to remember me? Surely, my acts in the ring weren’t that spectacular, and surely some of my wins weren’t that impressive, at least I didn’t think they were. Nevertheless, I received my second invitation from destiny itself, as I was put on a very talented roster in honor to defend my right to be a participant of the company. The motive this time was simple, survival. It would be a hard course of training to take, especially with the months I’ve spent away from my regimen, but I was still determined to make the cut. I wanted to prove that even though I’ve been beaten in a main event in the past, this time would be different. This time, I would hold up my own and be victorious, no matter what the cost. You may say that my kind of talking sounds very un-rational, perhaps even borderline insane, but I say that this match is too important for me to fail again. The rules are fairly simple too, especially with the experience that I have in the ring. And I must say, my partners know this stipulation far too well as well, so we clearly have an advantage. I won’t become too pompous though, I can’t become to full of my own strengths, they could become my weaknesses if I don’t keep aware. And the title of the match couldn’t be more to my liking, fatal four way. Our opponents were three other fantastic organizations, all of which have sent out their best in show for the match. The problem was, only one team would survive this match and go on to claim top dog in Primetime Central. Only one team would be able to boast that they were the best until the next survival came around. Hopefully, if all the cards dealt our way, we would be that team, but we needed a lot more than skill to bring us to the same level as our opponents. And that reason is why they called me into action. Determination, passion, and gut instinct. Throughout the live and times of my professional career, I’ve learned to master these three intangible skills, the kind of skills that could turn any ordinary person into a superhero. These were the skills that didn’t let you throw in the towel, no matter how hard you had been beaten. These were the skills that wouldn’t settle for second best, and these were moreover, the skills that made you better than any other wrestler out there. Seems arrogant? I think not, especially not when you are dealing with the odds I would have to put up with in a very short amount of time. Thinking this way is natural in the course of any wrestler, especially those who are what they term as “pushed around”. Pushed around is a psychological condition caused when more physically fit wrestlers pick on a not so fit wrestler for being small, and more often than not, the bigger wrestler gets beaten at his own game because of the pride and courage of the smaller opponent. This is what I was hoping for when the survival came around. Think about the humiliation that could be served if a cruiserweight was to step up to a heavyweight and rise to the occasion, think of the madness that would spread throughout the building. It has been done before, and it sure as hell could be done again, but of course this time, it would be on a grander stage and at a bigger expense. And if all the luck came my way, I would be involved in the final climb. Yah, that was the prize for winning the survival matches. Each organization that won (and the top two that didn’t) would participate in a final fatal climb up Mount St. Hells. What exactly was Mount St. Hells? A five-level mountain that gets harder with every step, and if one member of the team fails to make it, the whole team suffers. It’s about trusting each other, and sometimes I have a hard time giving a task for somebody else to perform. Don’t ask me why, but I think a lot of people have a fear of leaving a job for someone else to do. It’s a mix of wanting to be a leader, topped off with the slightest pinch of jealousy that someone else may be better than you at a certain thing. The question was, did I have it in me to trust my companions? I didn’t know, especially since at times, I couldn’t even trust… myself… End of Volume One
