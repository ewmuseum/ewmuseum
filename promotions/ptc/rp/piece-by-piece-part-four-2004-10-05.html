Piece by Piece: Part Four

5 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-05 Notoriety. Something that most famous people, to an extent, have. Let’s face it, celebrities aren’t always the nicest and fluffiest people in the world. They have darker sides. It is perhaps no surprise then, that some of the most notorious people around (mobsters) enter another of the most notorious professions in the world (professional wrestling). Men with questionable pasts in GTT4 include Angelo Deville and Jonathan Sail, among others. But most important of this group to Seymour Almasy is his opponent in the next round, a member of Brooklyn’s Vendetti crime family. Joseph Marciano Vendetti. Man, it sure doesn’t get any easier, does it? ---------- Fresh off of his victory over the 4WW North Wind Champion Darek DeAngleo, Seymour Almasy was already back on the publicity circuit. The PTC Global Champion sat in a radio station, headphones attached firmly to his ears, being interviewed by a disc jockey in his home town of New York City. The look on Seymour’s face indicated a combination of exhaustion and sheer boredom. DJ: Alright everyone, this’ll be the last call for Universal Wrestling Federation superstar Seymour Almasy. Joe from New Jersey, you’re on WXYZ. Joe: Thanks man, how you doing? DJ: I’m doing fine. Got a question for Seymour? Joe: Yeah, I do. Seymour, are you worried about your next GTT opponent’s Mafia connections? Seymour shot the DJ a look, who could only look back apologetically, realizing that the Final Fantasy found the question somewhat silly…as he did the whole procedure of appearing on radio. Still, his natural manners (and the threats that Tournament Director Argyle von Smackdown left on his voicemail) led him to answer the question respectfully. Seymour Almasy: No, Joe. Not at all. Were I taking Joey Vendetti on in the middle of Bensonhurst, then yeah, I’d probably be more than a little bit nervous. But this match takes place in a wrestling ring, under normal rules. None of Vendetti’s hardcore crap is legal. So if he wants to beat me, he’s going to have to outwrestle me. You can’t whack someone in professional wrestling, as much as he might want to after I outclass him in every way possible. DJ: Thanks for your call Joe. Joe: My pleasure. The caller from New Jersey was disconnected, and Seymour smiled. Just a couple seconds left in the interview. The jockey would ask for a strong closing comment, Almasy would give it, and he would then be free. DJ: Any final words for your opponent? Just as he expected… Seymour Almasy: Vendetti, if you’re listening to this somewhere in one of your restaurants or garages or something, listen well. So far, you haven’t been tested in this tournament. You got a first round bye, and your opponent no-showed in round two. Meanwhile, I’ve beaten two men, one of them a champion in his federation. I’m doing what I do better than anyone else, namely PROVE that I’m worthy of the Global Title that I wear around my waist. I’ve proved it twice in GTT4, and when I meet you in the ring, I’m going to prove it for a third time. And if you or your goombas think otherwise… Almasy paused, for dramatic effect, as he always did before delivering his catchphrase. It was a bit silly, perhaps, but the phrase was his. It wasn’t good enough to make a t-shirt, or even have 10,000 people in an arena saying it along with him, but he liked it, and that was enough for him. Seymour Almasy: You’re living in a fantasy world. DJ: That was the UWF’s Seymour Almasy, live on WXYZ. Don’t miss him taking on Joey Vendetti in round three of Primetime Central’s GTT4 tournament. Right now, we’re going to commercial, and when we come back, today’s top nine at noon! The DJ sent his show to commercial, and nodded out a thank you to Almasy. Seymour nodded back in return, and removed his headphones. He didn’t even look back as he left the studio, and breathed an immense sigh of relief. That is, until he looked out the window and saw the groupies. Seymour Almasy: Yevon… There were at least twenty of them, outside the station, carrying signs. Others had their hair dyed to match his. To Almasy, it was insane. To the girls, it was simply fandom. Seymour Almasy: Alright…doesn’t look like I have any choice in this matter. It’s time to go greet my adoring public. He half-smiled to himself, reached into the pocket of his jeans, and pulled out a Sharpie marker. Fans usually DID want autographs, after all. Crossing the hall, he stepped into the elevator, and pushed the button for the ground floor. Seymour Almasy: When Laura was around, I didn’t have this particular problem. The female fans were usually afraid of her… The memory made him chuckle despite himself, and despite his current feelings in regards to his estranged wife. Her protectiveness had gotten her into more than one altercation with an adoring fan, and usually managed to resolve the situation without violence. Usually. Seymour Almasy: Ready or not, here I come… As the elevator opened, Almasy could see the crowd through the station’s clear glass doors. Screams came from the mob of groupies, as Seymour advanced, steadily but tentatively, like a small child walking to the dentist’s chair. He opened the door, and pandemonium was immediate. Groupie A: It’s him! Groupie B: Oh my God, it’s Seymour! Groupie C: He’s so hot! Being a naturally shy person, Seymour could have died. He nobly raised his Sharpie high, to indicate that he would be willing to sign things. It was the best approach, he reasoned. Make these twenty happy and send them home before their number increased exponentially. Seymour Almasy: I don’t know how long I have until I’m being picked up, but I’ll sign what I can until then. The Final Fantasy’s salvation was on its way, in the form of the Lincoln Town Car driven by his paternal grandfather, Cid Wilson. Also in the car was the Wilson family attorney, Mr. Jacob C. Fields, Esquire. Both men were dressed to the nines after their respective days at work. The pair were to pick Seymour up, and then the important business of the Seymour/Laura divorce would be discussed. For Seymour, they couldn’t come soon enough. Seymour Almasy: Okay, okay, no shoving….everyone’ll get a turn….yes, you can get a hug with each autograph… The next ten minutes were a blur of Sharpie meeting photos and other paper, and warm embraces from generally pretty women that Seymour didn’t even know. Hands were everywhere, he realized, as he felt them move over his body. Two were cupping his rear end tightly, others were sliding down his chest. It was one of the most distinctly uncomfortable things Almasy had ever experienced. He felt as if he was being somehow raped… *BEEP!! BEEP!* Thankfully for Seymour, the horn of the Lincoln Town Car sounded. Almasy could see the vehicle pulling up to the curb. Prying hands off of him, Seymour apologized. Seymour Almasy: I’m sorry, but I have to go now! Bye! As much as he wanted to sprint for the car and slam the door, once again, his inner sense of decorum took over. He walked at a medium pace, but still did not look back until he had entered the car, and was safely inside. Grandpa Cid: Enjoying the single life already, are you? He chuckled, but the look on Seymour’s face was not an amused one at all. Seymour Almasy: Drive, grandpa. Drive! Grandpa Cid: Okay, okay…those fans can be a handful, can’t they? Seymour Almasy: YES! They can! Now please! Chuckling even more, Cid Wilson put the car into drive, and pulled away from the curb, driving back out into the streets of New York City. Grandpa Cid: It’s not just that you know. One of the wrestling publications has pictures of you at the Gaia with a pretty young lady. Mr. Fields: Not too bad, if I do say so myself… The lawyer tossed the magazine back to Almasy. Sure enough, pictures of Almasy talking to and dancing with the mystery woman covered pages twelve and thirteen. Seymour Almasy: Would you believe that I don’t even know her name? Grandpa Cid: Considering the fact that you were at the Gaia, yes, I do. Enough about that though, how’d the radio appearance go? Seymour Almasy: Not badly. I’ve had better…and much worse too. The typical questions. It’s boring, but they’re easy to respond to, and I have the answers practically programmed into my head by now. Mr. Fields: Vendetti this week, is it? Along with being an excellent family attorney, Mr. Fields was something of a wrestling buff. Seymour Almasy: Yeah. I’m surprised Misfit no-showed, but something must have come up. Mr. Fields: It happens. Seymour Almasy: Indeed it does. Grandpa Cid: Vendetti…is he part of the Vendetti family? Seymour Almasy: Yes, he is grandpa. Grandpa Cid: Shit…the Vendettis. They’re supposedly every bit as powerful as the weaker families in New York’s Big Five, but they’ve been kept out of the fold due to their insistence on drug trafficking. Some of the older time Mafioso don’t believe in dealing in drugs. Mr. Fields: While the new time ones don’t give a damn. I’ve done some cases for various soldiers of the Vendettis, and I’ve seen and heard some things I’d really like to be able to forget. Seymour Almasy: Eh, I think everyone overrates the guy’s Mafia connection. This is going to be a wrestling match. I’m not some paisan he’s extorting on a Black Hand deal or anything, I’m the PTC Global Champion. It’s not an extreme match, so he can’t use any of his blackjacks or tire irons, or any shit like that. Mr. Fields: If he really wants to advance, all he’s got to do is put out a hit on you, and you don’t even make round three. Seymour Almasy: He’s not going to do anything like that. These guys are all about honor and respect. But at the same time, I have to show Vendetti that I’m not afraid of him…or his connections. Grandpa Cid: Seymour…what are you going to do? Seymour Almasy: Nothing that’s going to get us killed, don’t worry. Take us to Brooklyn. We’ll discuss the divorce proceedings over lunch. My treat. Almasy smiled and focused on the road ahead, his destination already firmly in mind Seymour Almasy: Anyone in the mood for Italian? Mr. Fields: Where are you going to take us? Seymour Almasy: Pagliachi’s. It’s a fine Italian restaurant in Bensonhurst. Mr. Fields: You’re not exactly dressed for fine dining though… Almasy looked down at his jeans and t-shirt clad body, and frowned. The attorney had a point. Seymour Almasy: True. We’ll go shopping once we arrive. There HAS to be a decent tailor nearby. Cid looked at Seymour, a touch of nervousness still clearly visible on Cid’s face. Something wasn’t right here… Grandpa Cid: There has to be a catch to this. How does going out to eat show the Vendettis you’re not afraid? Seymour Almasy: Ah, of course. How silly of me to forget. Pagliachi’s is connected to the Vendettis. It’s rumored that mob business is conducted there daily. What ISN’T rumored, however, is that the place is one of Joseph Marciano Vendetti’s favorite dining spots. Grandpa Cid: Lovely. Seymour Almasy: Hey, if nothing else, you can’t argue with the man’s taste. Pagliachi’s is one of the best Italian restaurants in town. You can’t get in without a reservation, so I should probably make one. He reached into his pocket, and pulled out his cell phone. As the Town Car continued to drive through New York to reach the Bensonhurst section of Brooklyn, Almasy punched in several numbers on his cell phone, and waited, as the phone rang. Finally, someone picked up. The voice on the other end spoke with a strong Italian accent. Vincent: This is Pagliachi’s, Vincent speaking. What can I do for you? Seymour Almasy: You have any tables for three open for an hour from now? Vincent: Yeah, we’ve got one or two open. Name? Seymour Almasy: Almasy. Vincent: Almasy? Seymour Almasy: That’s what I said. I’ll spell it if you need me to. A-L-M… Vincent: No no, that won’t be necessary. Your reservation’s for a table for three at 1:30, under the name Almasy. See ya then. Both hung up. Cid gripped the wheel noticeably tighter, and even the attorney looked more than a tad white-faced. Seymour Almasy: What? Grandpa Cid: You’re going to get us killed, that’s what! Seymour Almasy: Why, because I’m going to an Italian restaurant with some associates of mine? Shit, if I thought like you guys do, Vendetti would be able to beat me! All this guy’s got going for him is his extreme fighting ability, and the aura that being a member of the mob gives him. Other than that, he’s no better than Darek DeAngleo or Ash, both of whom I’ve defeated handily. Mr. Fields: You yourself said Vendetti shows up there a lot! What are we going to do if we run into him? Seymour’s answer comes quickly, without the slightest bit of trepidation or fear. Seymour Almasy: I walk over to him, I shake his hand, and then I look Joseph Marciano Vendetti, feared member of the Vendetti crime family dead in the eye. All I will say to him will be four words. You can’t beat me. And in his heart, he’ll know I’m right. Grandpa Cid: Is this before or AFTER we all get fitted with concrete shoes? Seymour Almasy: Damnit Grandpa, we’re NOT going to get fitted with concrete shoes! The Global Champion’s exasperation was evident. Like a poker player or a chess grandmaster, he has made a read of his opponent. In Almasy’s mind, Vendetti was a man who kept business and pleasure separate. Seymour Almasy: Yeah, it would look real good to the cops when the opponent of a member of a well known crime family ends up being found dead in the East River. You don’t even need an investigation then, you just go round up every member of the Vendettis until you find who ordered the hit, and who carried it out. Mobsters are intelligent people. They’re not going to do anything stupid and vain like that. It’s bad for business. Guys like Albert Anastasia who ordered vanity hits ended up hit themselves, because they bring undue heat onto the organization. Both Grandpa Cid and Mr. Fields were silent for a moment, before the lawyer responded, surprise evident in his voice. Mr. Fields: Wow, I didn’t know that you knew so much about organized crime. Seymour Almasy: I might have been in my own fantasy world for most of my life, but I grew up on Long Island, and when my parents WEREN‘T being neglectful, we‘d take trips to the city. Of course I know about the mob! My family and I were two blocks from Sparks when Castellano got whacked…stop the car. The reason for Almasy’s order loomed outside the window. A fine men’s store, seemingly very out of place amongst the poorer homes surrounding it, was no more than one hundred feet away. Seymour Almasy: Here’s a tailor. Pagliachi’s is down the road a block. Park the car over there, I’ll get my suit, and we’ll walk on over. Grandpa Cid: Alright. I’ll put my faith in you. I really don’t want to end up as part of that new apartment complex on the East Side though… The Town Car was parked near several other cars, partially in an effort to protect it. Grandpa Cid gingerly got out of the car, and though he needed no cane, was in most other ways a typical lovable (and at times ornery) fifty-five year old man. Grandpa Cid: I swear, if any of those Vendettis ruin my car… Seymour Almasy: They won’t, grandpa. Don’t even think about it. Now, c’mon. Let’s go get me a suit. The three men walked across the street, and soon were inside the store. Suits, sports jackets, and slacks of all varieties were hung neatly on hangers and racks all over the store. An Italian man approached the trio. He carried a long measuring tape, and looked knowledgably at each of the three, sizing them up. Considering that Seymour was the only man of the three not already clad in a suit, the salesclerk’s mission seemed obvious. Salesclerk: Something for the white-haired man? Seymour Almasy: Yeah. Something a little less antique than that… He gestured to his grandfather’s perfectly acceptable, yet thirty-year-old suit. Seymour Almasy: …and somewhat less expensive than that. He pointed to Mr. Fields’ immaculate black tuxedo. The salesperson nodded, and began to estimate Seymour’s size as he walked circles around the Final Fantasy. Salesclerk: I think we can do something for ya…let me take some measurements. The clerk’s measuring tape flies out, as he moves around Almasy with practiced speed and accuracy, taking measurements of everything needed to determine suit size and style. Salesclerk: Any preference on color? Almasy prepared to speak, but Cid cut him off. Grandpa Cid: Something decent, please. I’m not having my grandson run around in one of those powder blue contraptions. He’s not a gangster, nor is he a pimp. The salesclerk laughed, and turned to go wade through the store’s numerous racks, reassuring Cid before he left. Salesclerk: I’ll keep that in mind. As he left, Almasy decided to take the opportunity to get to know his lawyer a bit better. Seymour Almasy: Mr. Fields, how do you know my grandfather? Mr. Fields: We’ve known each other for years. Went to school together, fought in the war together…and have both lived in New York City our entire lives. Grandpa Cid: There’s no one I’d rather have at my side, at wartime or peacetime, than Jacob. He’s a truly good man. Mr. Fields: Same goes for you, Cid. It means a lot to have someone to talk to when you’re attacking Monte Cassino in WW2. The war conversation was broken off as the salesclerk returned, carrying a single suit in his hand. It was pure black, with matching slacks, and a connoisseur of such clothing would be able to note its fine craftsmanship. Salesclerk: I thought something simple would be in order. It’s basic, but very well made. You’ll be happy with this one. Go on, try it. Taking the suit from the salesperson, Seymour headed across the store, and into a changing room. As he changed, Mr. Fields decided that a preview of the business to be discussed was in order. Mr. Fields: Do you know anything about why Seymour and Laura separated? Somewhat sadly, Cid could only reply in the negative. Grandpa Cid: I’ve got no idea at all. He’ll tell us in a little bit though, and I hope whatever it is, you can work with it. Mr. Fields: I wouldn’t worry if I were you. Laura really doesn’t want that much in the grand scheme of things. Seymour Almasy: Hey guys, how do I look? The talk of divorce is dropped as quickly as the discussion of war was, as Seymour modeled his suit. Somewhere along the way, Seymour managed to locate a dress shirt and tie, which he wore as well. Grandpa Cid: Not bad at all, Seymour! You wear it well. Mr. Fields: Indeed you do. Seymour Almasy: Excellent. I’ll take it. Can I pay for it and wear it out of the store? Salesclerk: Of course. Got somewhere to be soon? Seymour Almasy: 1:30 reservation at Pagliachi’s. Salesclerk: Ah! Enjoy, that’s some good food. Seymour Almasy: We will. Pulling his wallet out, Almasy removed a credit card, and slapped it down on the counter. He winced noticeably as he saw the amount ring up on the register. Seymour Almasy: Hey, I thought this was supposed to be cheaper than his suit! Mr. Fields: It is. What I’m wearing right now costs more than you do. He gave a small laugh at his own joke, as the transaction was completed. Putting the credit card back in his wallet, Seymour led the group out of the store. Seymour Almasy: So, everyone ready for some fine Italian dining? Grandpa Cid: I’d be more ready if I was sure that I’d live to enjoy it. Seymour Almasy: Oh, come on! Stop worrying! It’ll be an adventure. An adventure into the heart of the enemy. I’m not afraid…and I’m the man Vendetti wants to beat. You certainly have nothing to be afraid of, and Mr. Fields has been a lawyer for some of the Vendettis, so he’ll be fine too. This is more than just a meal for me… Seymour nodded, seeing the sign for the restaurant off in the distance. Seymour Almasy: This is my chance to see what Vendetti surrounds himself with. To see his natural environment. To learn about who he is, and what he does. I will get insights from this I could never get by studying tapes. I’ve learned all I can by seeing how he wrestles. Now, I must see how Joseph Marciano Vendetti lives…how he thinks. Before Cid or Mr. Fields can protest, Almasy is off, walking down the street to the eating establishment. Grandpa Cid: …ah well. At least we get a meal out of the deal. Mr. Fields: Besides, we need to discuss the divorce proceedings. The two older men follow Seymour, hearts pounding, as they prepare for their date with destiny…or at least some good breadsticks.
