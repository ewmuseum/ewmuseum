Date: 2004-09-16 Choking smoke gathered near the third-storey apartment’s ceiling like a roiling thunderhead of a miniature internal weather system. Beneath this, a lighter shade of black haze hung thick in the air; an all-smothering lung-coating blanket of darkness killing off the fine oxygen-exchanging alveoli that were so vital for aerobic respiration. Out of reflex, the occupant’s cardiovascular system immediately constricted to jettison itself of the noxious fumes. The follow-up gasp was to intake a more favourable alternative, but it resulted in an even more concentrated inhalation of smoke and a full-blown fit of rib rattling coughing and convulsions. Like life itself, the retching coughs were a vicious cycle: each duplicate as mean and nasty as the original. This was life as metaphor in action. To impede vision further, quite perversely, as if the smog wasn’t doing that amply enough, the billion billowing atoms of carbon bombarded his delicate white eye tissue with the stabbing sensation of a thousand tiny scorpions. The resultant tears blurred reality quicker than any political spin-doctor. But there was more, the smoke alarm’s clarion call joined the cacophony of chaos. It’s piercing wail tore at the mind like a Chechen child under gunfire, in retrospect the rejected option to upgrade - for a mere five dollars more - to the psychologically torturing Camp X-Ray special that blurted out the Sesame Street theme tune may have been hastily taken. So what if it was produced by the Children’s Television Workshop...cut out the ‘Television’ part and Nike had been using the economically viable five rupee a day workforce in India since the seventies. Not that you could give a damn about the infants. If they had any smarts they’d have learnt from their Viet Cong cousins; dug a load of stake pits and pissed on the points, give Uncle Sam a real good communist kicking... He got off the couch in a newly awakened fug, a quiet death by asphyxiation was no longer an option. ...after all, if you’re not prepared to shit on a stick for freedom, then enslavement by capitalism is justly deserved. Choked, deafened, blinded, Ian English, or Sammy Wong if you looked at the tenant’s lease on the apartment, fumbled - as if faced with a bra strap - on a haphazard route through the furniture “shitting” and “fucking” at every knee-banging hurdle placed in his way. With all the mayhem his place had become Hell in a bubble. He was probably going to die. But that would be a crappy way of ending a story in a Dallas-esque ‘it was all a dream,’ way, especially after the use of such descriptive verbs in the flowery prose as to be almost borderline homosexual. Not that the Narrator is, in fact he’s all man…I mean a man’s man, well not like that. For Christ’s sake stop conjuring up images of Steven Regal in a hard hat you sick fucks! Anyway, he was probably going to die (when writer’s block sets in). Back in fictional reality the Englishman stumbled into the epicentre of the plume, the bearded lady’s hairy armpit itself. A beacon through the murk; winking red digital letters lifted twenty years on from the set of Battlestar Galactica and transferred to a Kenwood electric cooker guided Ian in. With a renewed urgency to avoid carbon monoxide poisoning, the knobs and dials were randomly banged and fiddled; one-by-one the instruments shut down, the bulb at the back of the grill pan died - the cooking cavity was black. The remaining whoosh of captured dragon’s breath seared Ian’s moist eyes bone dry when the front was tugged open and grill pan pulled out. Wafting his hand backwards and forwards resembling a magician’s apprentice - cooking was certainly more arcane that scientific - the smoke parted for an instance. Mmmmm. Waffles. Other people cooked by the labour intensive appearance method, others by time, Ian English went for the low maintenance approach and cooked by the smoke alarm: thus far it never failed to deliver well done (or ‘burnt’ in the Collin’s Dictionary) cuisine. KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK. Arse. That would be the fire department, four days in a row. He’d be due a fine, then, like flies to crap, they might smell the whiff of bullshit in his cover (the lack of Chinese parentage perhaps) and come to investigate - discover Sammy Wong’s workers permit was a fake. He’d have to go on the run...again. That was the problem with living in an apartment block, it was like Cheers, everyone knew your name, admittedly it was your Chinese Buddhist-religious-exile name you’d paid fifteen hundred dollars for. And they weren’t your friends. Everyone made it their nosy, suspicious, business to know you because they were looking out for Number One. If he’d been living in a trailer park then nobody would’ve called the fire brigade, they’d have roasted marshmallows on his trailer more likely, but an apartment block is different. You’re all interlinked. Not by the values of society or community spirit but by bricks and mortar, living on top of each other like battery hens, if English went up in flames then he took floors two, three, and four with him. Maybe the whole building. It was a comforting thought. Before 9/11 if you had a strange accent it meant you were a shoe-in with the chicks. Nowadays you were a terrorist.
