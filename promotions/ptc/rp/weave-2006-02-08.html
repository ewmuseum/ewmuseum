Weave

8 February 2006

Date: 2006-02-08 Shanty rubbed the scar tissue that covered the lower half of his face. "Paul? What the hell are you doing here?" "Relax, Shan. Nobody saw me come in," said Paul Goodman, standing up and walking across to crouch in front of his friend. "Now be quiet or you’ll wake up Artemis." Most people would have asked how Paul knew who Artemis was, or how the convicted murderer ended up in his hotel room, but Shanty knew Paul Goodman too well for that. In the year they had spent together in prison, Paul had taken Shanty under his wing, and as a man with connections that was no bad thing. At the time, Shan had been an outsider, a quiet but violent man. Everyone thought it was just because he was serving his first ever sentence, for assault. Paul noticed something different though, and saw that Shanty had been through something that had changed him. He’d never guessed at the time as to the true scale of what Shan had been through, and was not finished with. "So, why are you here, Paul?" he asked after a few moments. "Well, I was missing your company!" said Paul, taking a couple of steps back and turning around in a carefree manner. "Prison can get so boring sometimes. I just needed to spread my wings and see how you were doing. You’ve come so far, my friend…" The man more commonly know to the world as Eegor, Doctor Curiosity’s assistant, fell silent, his voice trailing off as he returned to his seat and studied the face of Shanty Jones. "You want to know what I’m really here for, don’t you Shanty?" The wrestler nodded. "What do you know of Jayenne Industries?" Shan’s mind reeled. The one thing he’d seen constantly during his first time in the factory had been the name stamped all over the products; Jayenne Industries. He’d never even had a clue as to what it signified, not even after returning to civilian life and having unfettered access to the internet. No search turned up anything that could be linked to the company that seemed to run the factories. It was as if they didn’t exist, but Shanty knew he had not been dreaming. His entire life since then had been spent with it worrying the back of his mind. What had it meant? What had he seen, and was it really, truly real? "I thought so," said Paul. He stood up and looked out of the window. He was trying to appear calm, but Shanty knew that he was slightly on edge. The normal placid nature had been replaced by one that was slightly prone to fidgeting. There were little ticks and nuances that Shan didn’t recognise as being those of the man he had come to know. "Shanty… I don’t have much time here. You need to tell me all you know about the factories." *** "Mister Goodm…" "DOCTOR!" "Doctor Goodman," said the police officer, trying to placate the angry Doctor Curiosity. "We’re very concerned about these developments, and we highly recommend that you remain here or under police supervision until such a time as your brother can be located. He could be armed and dangerous." "Dangerous? To me? You do not know Eegor like I know Eegor." "Sir, people can change whilst in prison. You haven’t seen Pau… your brother for a while now, and in those months the reports stated that he was acting more and more irrationally. He was talking to himself and at times it was as if he was fighting with himself. He was going to undergo some heavy psych evaluation when he disappeared." "He disappeared? I thought it was supposed to be a maximum security place. One zat ze A-Team would have had misgivings about breaking out of?" said Curiosity. "We can’t explain it. One morning he just wasn’t there." "Could he not be hiding? Or attacked by the other inmates?" "Attack Paul Goodman? Your brother was the closest thing to a god in that place. The place has been searched time and again. He’s gone somewhere, and our best guess is that he’s coming for you. When he was talking to himself, he sometimes mentioned your name…" The doctor looked over his cracked glasses at the officer. "I know that our relationship might seem strange to you, but he’s my brother, and we have a bond between us. Blood is thicker than whatever kind of gruel you serve in your prisons." "Gruel?" "It vas an analogy!" snapped the doctor. "Now if you vill let me leave, I can go and see for myself if anything is amiss, and you can trust me, officer, zat if I see any sign of my errant brother, I vill be back on ze phone to you as soon as I can be. Ve can’t have murderers roaming ze streets, jah?" "Yes, sir," said the officer, a model of professionalism. He let Curiosity out of the door, and sat down on the plastic chair he had been eschewing. The doctor’s laboratory coat swept a few dust bunnies from the floor as he left, swishing and gliding across the hard floor like a phantom’s cape. "Dawkins," said the officer. "Yes, sir?" "Make sure that he’s followed. If his brother isn’t going to kill him then the odds are that he’s still going to make contact. When that happens, we’ve got to be there, ready to get him back." "Yes, sir." *** Elsewhere, the man of darkness had left the medical room and was waiting for his protégé to recover sufficiently to join him. The shadowy figure looked around at his domain; a place where he had returned time and time again to create his plans and see them come to fruition. His large hands cradled a glass of what observers would surely hope was just red wine, and his eyes scanned bookshelf after bookshelf. There was nothing in his kingdom that he did not know, that he did not have catalogued in his prodigious mind. Everything was in place, and out of the sight of any prying eyes. It had been quite the task for Sirius, but he was up to the task, as he ever was. A good servant, thought the figure of darkness. The location of this building had been a closely guarded secret for years, with only a select few allowed to find it. It seemed to live off maps somehow, staying in the wilderness, miles from anywhere and yet just twenty minutes from everywhere. It had been his crowning achievement, the one thing with no flaws, no humanity attached to it. The figure believed that it was somehow artistic, but sadly there was nobody else to appreciate the art. The giant shadow drew the glass to his lips and took a long draught from his bulb shaped glass, before placing it down on an ornate gothic coaster. Glass hit ceramic with a high pitched note, but it was overcome by the creak of the nearest door. The heavy oak opened ponderously, and from behind it stepped a ragged figure. Emaciated, he looked like a cross between the Backstreet Boys and a famine relief poster. Spittle hung down from his lips in translucent, fragile chains that extended and shrank back with the motion of his head. The wretch’s arms were cast in darkness, but the track marks and scars along them seemed to catch the moonlight that spilled through the bay windows in an almost unnatural fashion. Hair once cropped close and the envy of models across the globe was unruly, part afro and part joke. The patient, the prisoner turned his eyes to his mentor, his captor, and they spoke without words. The pupils were shrivelled up so as to be almost negligible. He was living in darkness now, no matter what the conditions in the room. An unspoken plea reached towards the larger man, who waved it away without a second thought. "Oh, the little killer has awoken." The voice was perfectly enunciated; light in tone but with a heavy sense of finality, like cemetery gates clanging together. The younger man staggered into the room and collapsed in one of the ornate, crimson, high-backed chairs. He seemed to be half dead still, but to him this was the most alive he had felt in a long, long time. Something was happening, and he was being allowed back in to the world that he had once inhabited. "You notice the difference, don’t you?" The man tried to nod, but his muscles were still not obeying him fully. More drool hung down from his lips and chin, and he wiped them away with the sleeve of his medical green smock. "Time must not be wasted though. We leave in one hour precisely, whether you are ready or not. And with those words Jack "The Ripper" Nobbledy left the room. *** Paul Goodman sat and listened intently to the stories told by Shanty Jones. Some were surprising and some he suspected, but all were horrific. "You can see a thousand people riddled by bullets or come across a hundred corpses that have been burned and charred by missiles or bombs, but nothing can prepare you for seeing a needle slide through the skull of a living fifteen year old boy. That wasn’t the worst part, either," said Shanty, running a hand nervously over his shaved scalp. "The worst was that you knew the stuff they were injecting into him was going to fuck him up completely. I’d seen the ones who’d had the first set and it was like stuff from a bad dream. Just nightmarish, but you knew that these were real people, and no matter what you thought of the war or the Iraqi people, this was wrong. They were changing them, Paul. They were changing them into other… into other things. I couldn’t stand by and let it happen. I thought Steven was the same, but I guess not…" His voice trailed off, and Paul came over to him, placing a hand on his shoulder and trying to comfort him. "No matter what happens, Paul, I’ll always have it on my conscience. I’ve paid back my debt, I know I have, but I still can’t get rid of the past. I could be a saint and those visions would still haunt me. There’s nothing I can…" Shanty stopped as he heard a key card unlocking the hotel door. He looked up, taking his head from his hands, but by the time the door had begun to swing open, Paul Goodman was nowhere to be seen, vanished like a ghost into the night. "Shanty! I have some important news!" declared Doctor Curiosity as he walked into the room. "You must keep it quiet though, because I have a feeling zat I vas tailed back here. Vell, I say ‘tailed’, but zey just gave me a lift to ze hotel and zen zey didn’t leave afterwards!" Shan looked at the doctor, his eyes red from the tears he had been unable to hold back. Curiosity didn’t know what to make of the stoical, emotionless Shanty in this state, so he just continued on as before. "So, ze police told me a momentous event has occurred!" "Hello," said a voice from behind the doctor. "Quiet Eegor, I am explaining to Shanty here zat Eegor has escaped from prison and is at large. He could be…" Finally the neurons kicked in, sending the chemical impulses to the right place. Doctor Curiosity turned around quickly and saw brother there, nonchalantly resting against the door frame. "EEGOR!" cried the doc, rushing forwards to embrace his brother. "You’ve got to be careful! The police are here and they…" "Don’t worry about it. As long as they don’t come in here I’m fine," said Paul Goodman, the wrestler’s assistant formerly known as Eegor. "Jah! Vell… vell it’s about time you started pulling your veight again! I have been through several lackeys since you left. Ze regular standard of lackeys leaves a lot to be desired zese days!" declared Curiosity, effortlessly slipping back into his persona. "I’m sorry master," replied Paul, looking genuinely contrite. "Master?" asked Shanty. "Jah! Eegor is my servant! He is my lackey! He is ze person who does vhat I say!" "Paul? What the hell is this about?" asked Shan. "Well, it’s sort of complicated. I was quite different before I went into prison." Jones shook his head and stood up, walking to the window and seeing a couple of police cars outside. The traffic came and went, but the two cars remained stationary, looking at the hotel building. As Shanty looked outside, a third, unmarked car pulled up and joined them, presumably their relief for the time being. Shanty didn’t care though. His entire world was being turned upside down. Just a week ago he had felt like the most powerful man in the world. He had held, for not the first time, the very soul of a man in his hands. He had been given a responsibility from God to pass His judgement on to people. They had to be given a chance, a fair decision as to whether or not they could choose the right path, but if they chose wrong, it was up to Shanty to absolve himself by doing the hardest job he could ever imagine. Now he was in the very same hotel room, the odd smell of burnt rock and gun smoke lingering unnaturally in the air, but everything was different. The only man he had respected or looked up to since he returned from Iraq was acting obsequiously towards a man who was a bona fide idiot. It seemed like some insane variant on the childish game of ‘rock, paper, scissors’. Paul councils Shanty, Shanty intimidates Doctor, Doctor exerts power over Paul. "Tell him it’s different now," asked Shan, looking towards Paul for some sort of assistance, for some way in which the man he had done all of this for would become himself again. "Some things you can’t take back," said Paul. "I promised my brother a long time ago that I would never repay my debt to him. I had my reasons at the time, and even though he’s forgiven me, I am still who I am." "Sssshhhh!" insisted Curiosity. "You’ll wake Artemis!" "Oh, don’t worry about that. I drugged him senseless before you got here. He’ll be out for another twelve hours," said Paul, with a devilish smile. "So, you’ll just let him walk over you?" asked Shanty, incredulous. "Jah!" "That’s not important right now," said Paul, flashing his brother a glance, as if to say that he needed just a minute off the hook. "What’s important is that you’re ready for your next match." "What?" "Your next match. You left prison and I got you a job as a wrestler. I know that you’re still in the tournament." "What?" said Shanty again, his refusal to understand the situation starting to test he patience of Paul Goodman. "Your match against Masquerade! You’re on of the last eight people in this damn tournament, and you have to win!" "It’s just a wrestling match," said Shanty. "A way for me to make money and get me back on my feet after prison. A way for me to be more visible to complete my task, my repayment to God." "No!" cried Paul. "That’s not it at all. There are greater forces at work here, Shanty. Remember Jayenne Industries? They’re not done with you yet, and they’re not done with me. They were in the prison, Shanty, don’t you understand? They’ve become more subtle, but they were experimenting on us too! They’re tied up with this competition somehow, and I set you here knowing that you could get this far. Your determination and strength would only take you so far before you needed help, but here I am!" "Vait! You talked me out of defending my title so zat zis man vould be involved?" "I’m sorry Chr…" said Paul, changing tack very quickly under the stern glance of his brother’s personality. "I’m sorry master. It had to be done this way to reveal everything for what it is. There’s more at stake here that a strap of gold or a trophy. There’s more to all of this than talk of renegades, wrestlers and cult leaders. It’s coming to a head here. It’s attracting more and more of the most depraved people, and it’s doing so for a reason. Some of the people you defeated, like Kasidy Drake or Damien Ryan, they had plans for this event, they had desires and schemes that were beyond just bragging rights and a hefty cheque. They’re all coming from miles around, like a beacon. They don’t even have to be involved in the tournament itself to be caught along in its slipstream." "I don’t see any of this." "You don’t see it because you’re caught up in it. You’re in the eye of the storm. It’s the people around you who can tell that something isn’t quite right. What has brought us three together here, not to mention Artemis over there? There’s more going on than any of us know, and that’s why I had to get out. I had to come and warn you that the storm is moving, and that you’re no longer going to be safe unless you can move with it and stay in the eye. You can’t get eliminated from this tournament, Shanty, or you’ll get torn to pieces in its wake." Paul stopped speaking, his chest heaving as he breathlessly watched Shanty’s face for a flicker of recognition; some spark that would let him know that his words had found a mark. "Paul," said Shanty. "I don’t even know what to think any more. I thought my time here was done with the bomb blast in Iraq, and then when I had finished the judgement. Anything beyond that just confuses me. I must be here for a reason. This isn’t all random… that much I believe." Paul Goodman was about to reply when there was a sharp rap at the door. Three knocks, delivered with strength and purpose. The men inside the room looked to one another and were instantly unsettled. There was nobody else that they would have wanted to see. Three more knocks, and Curiosity ventured as far as, "Who is it?" "Room Service" came the reply, but it was the method of delivery that caused the intakes of breath within the room. The voice was clear and authoritative, but contained within it a hint of menace, and of fate. It reminded the men in the room of cemetery gates.
