Self Doubt Part 1

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 "Doubt is the father of invention." - Galileo Doubt is the snake squirming inside of us constantly reminding us of our biggest fears and our goals that seem beyond reach. It’s the demon we loathe that speaks the words that we don’t always want to hear. It’s that little voice deep down inside of your mind that prevents you from doing what you once thought was right. Doubt is a great weapon that we use against ourselves. It flickers through our minds, colder than shadows and pain. Doubt is like a weed, spreading and destroying the flowers, creating a garden of gripping vines and horrid creepers. Once doubt spreads within, it can’t be stopped. "You’ll never be anything in life." My father hated me. He never believed that I would be able to succeed in life. He always doubted me, telling me that I’d never be able to spread my wings and achieve my dreams because I simply wasn’t good enough. He told me that I was too afraid to step forward and make the right decisions in life… and that I’d probably be working retail, living check to check, not having enough money to support my family. His cold words would always seem to get to me, striking fear in places I didn’t even know I had, and for that I hated him. But in a way, I sort of understood the ‘advice’ he had tried to give me. It was motivation in disguise, and being the strong man he thought he had to be, he made it seem as if he had no sympathy for his words. That’s what made me love him all along. You see, what my father really wanted me to know was that he had loved me deep down inside. Beyond his witty remarks, and exaggerated feelings towards life, he wanted me to blossom into the man that he always wanted to be, not making mistakes that I’d later regret in life like he had. Here is our story. California. The glitz and glamour of Hollywood. The land of opportunity. The land of milk and honey… the sunshine state. The place that I had always dreamed of going. Ever since watching favorite stars like Lucille Ball and Desilu Arnaz, I was destined to become an actor, so I thought. I loved everything about it; the fame, fortune, and happy ways of life. The many women drooling over your body yearning to get their hands on you as you strutted the immaculate red carpet. Most importantly, the money. I could picture myself staring up into the marquee through thick black sunglasses, watching my name scroll by. Chris Collins. It always had that ring to it. Saying the words over and over to myself brought a sparkle to my eyes. Ah… I could smell it. The sweet smell of success, and boy did it smell good. Snapping back into reality, I lifted my head back from outside the window and begin to watch the birds fly by. Watching as they passed through the clouds high in the sky. It brought a tear to my eye, in fact I didn’t want the moment to end. I reached out grabbing a daisy that had just bloomed within the garden smelling the sweet pollen a bee would die for. "What are you doing now boy?" My father asked from behind. His voice was stern and I knew that I was in trouble. Slowly I eased to a turn. Placing the freshly picked daisy behind my back, not wanting him to see what it was. I swallowed spit and took a deep breath. "Nothing." I mumbled. "Just looking." "At what?" He asked. "The sky." "What for?" He asked sarcastically walking over to a recliner taking a seat. "Is it falling?" "No sir." "Then what’s so interesting about it? I don’t understand." "I just see things." I earnestly replied. "Bright things about my future." I tried to persuade. He laughed. "I see opportunity! I’m going to become a movie star!" "Heh… Your future? Kid you have no future." He reached for the table grabbing a can of beer which had been sitting there for a while popping it open with a grubby thumb. "Movie star? R-right." He took a sip from the can and leaned back placing his feet upon the yellow stool. He had just arrived home from work – he was a mechanic for an automotive shop he owned- and wanted to watch the tube as he patiently waited for supper to finish. He unbuttoned his one dirty one piece mechanic suit and allowed the sleeves to dangle at his side revealing a yellow wife beater underneath. "Sure I do!" I struck a tone. That caught him off guard, and I’ll always remember the look he gave me. He rammed his can back to the table and slowly lifted from the recliner. "What did I tell you about talking to me in that tone of voice?" He asked. "I’ll give you something to scream about you little twerp." …SMACK… He removed his belt from the loops and all I can remember from him that night, was the worst beating of my life. I never spoke to him again in that tone and my dreams of becoming a movie star has been shattered ever since. Which brings me to the point of my life today. I could’ve really been something if I would have never doubted myself. Why did I listen to the words he had to say? Why couldn’t I follow my own mind and achieve the dreams that I wanted so desperately. I know it may seem a little weird but I can be in Hollywood now, sipping a nice cold glass of lemonade kicking back in the shade. Snapping my fingers demanding my butler to bring me another bowel of caviar. Instead I’m here. A wrestler who has achieved quite a lot his few years in the business. I’ve traveled all around the world and like it or not women are still drooling over my body. Sure I’ve made quite a few mistakes in my life, such as having a baby too soon but I’m learning from them. I’m also understanding the easy way to winning a wrestling match, hence the reason why the weasel inside of me was born. So what, I’m a liar… …I cheat. Live with it. I do everyday. I’m a weasel… the sliest trickiest person in the game. GTT5 and Saloak is just the beginning. Maybe my dad knew that I was going to become a wrestler all alone. To be continued.
