The Fall of the House of Almasy A Tragedy in Two Acts Act One .:: ::: ::. Disclaimer: The following story is not necessarily the same one that Seymour Almasy OR his publicist will tell you. .:: ::: ::. Early 2006 Click! Empty blackness surrounded the single circular spot created by the weak beam of a bare light bulb hanging some ten feet above. Its chain swung sadly, as though it had been forever abandoned after this one last touch, and knew so by premonition. The grain of the floor was faded, obscured under years of waxing over scratches caused by careless shoes, dropped bags, and dragged chairs. But the floor, as weary and worn as it looked, did not seem as bone-exhausted as the young man who took two slow, plodding, staggering steps into the glare of the light. He squinted his red-rimmed eyes, as though even that weak light was far too much for him to bear. His clothes looked rumpled, but not stained- they gave the impression of a man who was trying desperately to fool everyone who saw him into thinking that nothing was wrong, but they were plainly recycled. Dug from the depths of a bag, buttoned carelessly, and sprayed with Febreze to hide the strong scent of unlaundered poly-cotton blend. He fingered the locks of his white hair as it lay, greasy and unwashed, lank and clinging together in strands against his head. He pushed it out of his eyes, looked squinting into the light, and gave a gusty sigh, one that sounded as though he was breathing out the remnants of an old, tattered soul. Despite the deep lines etched in his forehead, though, it seemed that he could scarcely be more than twenty-eight. What sorrow could befall someone of that age was an untold horror, hanging somewhere back in the darkness behind him, claws and fangs bared. "I'm at the end of my rope," he said, breaking the silence with a voice that was wobbly and uncertain. He seemed as surprised by the sound as anyone else who might have heard it, but he gathered his courage, and went on, "no, I mean it. I really don't know what else I can do with myself right now." There was no answer, and he held his breath tight in his chest as though waiting for one. Disappointment flooded his features. "I know there's no easy answer to this... I can't just snap my fingers and make it go away, though by Yevon, if it were a possibility, I would have already pounced on it. I know they always say life's not easy, nobody's got it easy, but I thought I was different. I had it all—I got the girl, I was on top of the world at my job, I was raking in the money, living the charmed life. I had what everybody dreams about when they enter this sport, and I had it because I was a golden boy—I had more than the mind to dream about it, I had the rare talent to pull it off.” Something in his face contorted, twisting those fine elfish features into a caricature. “But for some reason… nobody’s happy to see me succeed! I mean, there’s one thing—there’s people thinking they’re better than me, that I’m nothing but hype and stepping up to take a shot, falling by the wayside with their clumsy, slow blows… there’s people who hide in the dark and take potshots, but never had the courage to face me… but there’s only one person who’s made up his mind to take it beyond that, one person who decided that the only way to beat Seymour Almasy was to ruin him.” It was as though his blood was boiling from within, raging through his veins as his face twisted into an enraged scowl. “Rich! Fucking! Rollins!” Those shouts came from deep within, each staccato burst boiling up with bile. And then it was as though the floodgate broke. “That man single-handedly took it upon himself to make my life miserable! To take away everything that I cared about with one swipe of his greedy little hand! I was the King that he wanted off the chess-board, and he knew that the only way to do that was by taking the Queen.” His fists clenched at his sides, the very mention of the idea acting like a 30-06 shot to the gut. The rage broke, blanched with an expression of unbearable sorrow. “God, Laura… I’m so sorry.” A gut-wrenching sob broke from deep inside him, and his palms jammed heel-first into his eyes, trying to stem the tide threatening to break forth from his already burning eyes. It was no use. The pain was too fresh, and the wound too deep. He was reduced by it again, and again—every time he mustered the rage to stand up and fight back, she would return to cut him down. “I should have done more,” he murmured, more to himself than to anyone else, “I should have done more, but what the fuck could I have possibly done? He wraps her around his little finger, puts this crazy shit in her head, and now she’s—” He stopped. Couldn’t say the words anymore, he was tired of saying those words, so bone-tired. And for a moment, all he could do was let the tears come, unabated, because it was no use trying to stop them. No matter how hard he had tried to pick up the pieces and go on, the pain of losing her had not lessened. “Why,” he said, his voice broken with emotion, and it was probably a question that he had asked innumerable times since her death, “Why isn’t there anybody out there who understands what it’s like? Why do they all just want to see me fail, to wallow in this agony? Can’t anybody… help me?” He had asked that question so many times in the past weeks that he felt like a broken record. And as before, it went unanswered, bounced off the wall and echoed mockingly back at him. Then, the darkness came. Click! .:: ::: ::. Early 2006 A Hotel Lobby He’d known from the moment he saw her walk through the hotel doors that he was in trouble. The black corset-style top she was wearing hugged those curves that he remembered so well from his Global days, and the skirt she was wearing with it made absolutely no secret of her long, shapely legs. Her strangely piercing eyes were already zeroed in on him from across the lobby, as though she knew he was looking—the smile quirking her crimson lips said as much. Suddenly, he wondered why he had bothered to meet her here. A strong impulse inside him told him to get up right now, and walk away before he did something stupid. But it was too late for that. With lithe, panther-like grace, she slid back the other chair at his tiny café-table and sat down, leaning on her elbows so that she was a mere foot from his face. He flinched. She smiled. “Seymour,” she said, in that purr he’d gotten to know so well in the past, “I’m surprised to see you here. After everything that’s gone on lately, I thought you’d just chalk this up to a trick and stand me up.” She was toying with him, and it made his stomach lurch with nausea. He had every reason to stand her up, and they both knew it—Dies Irae had made a show of their war against him. They had stopped at absolutely nothing to destroy him in the ring, and make him look like an idiot outside it. And yet he knew that the greatest weapon they had at their disposal, they had not used. Had they wanted to make the war personal, it only would have taken one mention of his departed wife’s name—one scathing comment from Dawn, one little aside from Lia, and it would have erupted into an all-out Armageddon. But there had been no such word—and he wanted to know why. He was already exhausted from this mental gambit, and he sighed. “Look, I said that I’d hear you out. So instead of rubbing it in, why don’t you start talking?” She was unaffected by his brusque response, and leaned back into her chair, the barbarian queen on her throne. “As you wish. I think there’s been a little misunderstanding between us these past few weeks. A lot of things have been said that, well… I don’t really think either of us means— ” “Oh I mean it,” he cut in, “I don’t know what you’re up to, but I know you’re up to something. And you better not think I trust you to be honest about that for a minute.” She nodded to him in concession of that point. “We’ve given you no reason to believe otherwise. But you said you’d listen, and I’m trying to explain—you see… we had one goal in mind when we arrived here, and that one goal had absolutely nothing to do with you. Or at least it didn’t until you put yourself in our way—until you decided that you had to play the hero, Seymour, and tell everyone that we were planning on killing GCW from within. It simply wasn’t true, you just knew you’d get some credibility based on name value alone.” “But I haven’t,” Seymour started indignantly, sitting up straighter in his chair, “Nobody’s listened to me, it’s all been about me and my crazy conspiracy theories, meanwhile the lot of you—” She held up a hand for him to cease, and cease he did, clamping his mouth shut in awe of the force of her gesture. “Because you’re not preaching what they want to hear. Think about it, really. People like Knight, people like Sam Wolack, Aiden Jackson… you preach to them about how they need to save GCW, and all they can think about is how they need to save their chance at the spotlight. They got complacent. They want it easy, and with us around, they can’t have it easy. Now why do you think they would want to listen to you who is telling them to mount some great ideological war for a cause that is outside themselves?” She watched his face fall. He hadn’t thought of that. He had assumed that his voice would be enough to unite a wildly dissonant troop of old-timers to rally against the new blood and remind them that they had not earned any attention. Remind them that one group held the cards, and snatching them away was strictly forbidden, no matter your skill or enthusiasm. “…ridiculous,” he growled. It was the last defense of a desperate man, and he folded his arms over his chest and looked away from her to shut her out, to make her words meaningless. “Seymour…” “You know you just don’t get it,” he rejoined, “You walk into GCW, and the first thing you do is inform everyone there that they’re not fit to kiss your boots. Why is it so fucking surprising that someone might get offended by that? Why is it surprising that interfering in matches and playing mind games and taking over a tournament that you didn’t earn a space in might get old really fast?” She returned to him a flat, unbelieving look. ”Are you going to tell me that you think Lestatt Knight deserves his spot? Or that you would have liked to see the most complacent of them all, Rich Rollins… would you have liked to see him atop the tournament, holding GCW’s World Title belt high?” “WHY do you have to bring him up!” Seymour snapped, drawing attention from the other patrons in the bar with his indiscriminate roar. In response, she simply shrugged. It was an infuriating gesture, one that made him want to reach across the table and strangle her, but he couldn’t bring himself to do it. “Don’t defend them if you don’t even believe they deserve defending. You sound like you’re trying harder to convince yourself you’re telling the truth than you are me.” His anger deflated, without even the dignified violence of a popped balloon. It was flaccid before he could mount a response. “What do you want?” he said, looking away from her again, only this time it was because he was so tired of fighting this. “I don’t want anything at all,” she said, her voice taking on the cool, soothing tone of a mother, “I’ve got something to offer you.” His stomach twisted into a pretzel. ”What,” he said flatly, with no intonation whatsoever. She leaned forward, her eyes flashing like dazzlingly brilliant emeralds at him, all the more passionate now that he was subdued. “Join us, Seymour.” “No,” he returned evenly, threatening to stand up. She froze him with a look. “Sit down and listen to yourself before you say that. What have you got right now? Your life is in shambles, and before you get mad about me saying that, think about how often you’ve said it yourself. You’ve got nothing left. You’re fighting a losing battle trying to make allies out of people who hate you more than your supposed enemies do. You want someone who cares. I care.” It seemed like such a blatant lie that he clenched his teeth trying to swallow it down. He leaned forward, eyeing her furiously. “You care? You tried to kill me back in Global, or did you not remember that?” She raised her eyebrow, still managing to keep her cool somehow despite his violent rebuttal. “The past is past. I’ve moved on from that. I’ve forgiven you for your wrongs, and if you’re as righteous as you seem to think you are, you’d be able to forgive me mine.” He bit down on his bottom lip, knowing without a doubt that he was slowly being pinned against a wall by a tiger with a penchant for playing with its food. “You want to make it out like we came in here and assaulted you, but there was no action taken in any vein but competition until you picked a fight.” If he thought about that way… it actually made sense. He’d been on high alert ever since he’d taken off the mask in Global, and all of them signing with GCW had been a nightmare come true. He hadn’t given them a chance to speak before he mounted a paranoid campaign against them, convinced that the past was coming back to haunt him—he perpetually lived under the shadow of the past now anyway. He swallowed, gulping down the sick feeling that was rising dizzily into his head as he thought of them all—Dawn, Virgil, Vivica, Shadow… they had all hated him at one point. For all he knew, they might still hate him now; even Shadow as she sat before him could be concocting a clever plan for his demise. “What’s in it for me?” She’d been prepared for the question—it was a natural one for him to ask. “I slew your demon,” she responded, her eyes digging holes into him, pitchfork holes. It was true. Rich Rollins had never set foot into the tournament finals at Rampage, because Shadow had cold-cocked him before he ever got there. “God,” he said, leaning forward and dropping his forehead into his hands. This was unavoidable, this ugly truth that she was forcing him to look at. “And if you’re worried about the rest of them… Loyalty can be proven, even where it seems like a hopeless case. They trust my judgment implicitly; they trust that I’m making the right decision by talking to you about this. All you have to do is prove that you’re with us.” “How?” he asked, feeling like some thread within him had been forever broken simply by his asking the question. He could have sworn that he saw her smile. “Simple. Take on Tessa and destroy her.” It was like she’d driven a knife hard into his gut. His ex-girlfriend, the woman he might have asked to marry him, had not all this blasted complication ruined everything. But, he had to admit whatever he might have had left with Tessa was so much burnt foundation, of what used to be a house. Her hand reached across the table and pressed on top of his, the one that was clenching the edges of the wrought-iron for dear life. “Seymour… it’s not a difficult proposition. You’ve been fighting this for so long, but you’ve said yourself, all you really want right now is for someone to understand you. Somebody to care. And I’m offering you me right now, with the eventuality that others will follow after you join. Is it so hard to say yes or no?” It was the touch that did it, the touch that seemed to bite him in half and send him spiraling down the throat of the beast with one gulp. “Yes,” he whispered hoarsely, looking up from where their hands touched, up into her eyes, “Is that what you wanted to hear?” “Yes,” she said, smiling at him with a dazzling and unreal kindness, “Yes, it is, because there’s no more reason to be enemies—it’ll be like that first connection we had in Global, only stronger… better…” He didn’t quite buy that. Not yet. His eyes dropped down to the table, taking in the finery of its grate, but the details didn’t matter. His head was spinning too quickly to find any purchase, his mouth gone cotton dry. “Would you rather discuss the particulars in private?” he found himself asking, before he even knew he was asking it. Her fingers laced through his, sending cold shooting up his arm. He was beginning to feel deadened and numb, but fought it off—he’d made the decision that he needed to make. A sudden surge ran through him, electrical. This was an affirmation, not a defeat. He tightened his hand around hers, got to his feet, and pulled her the first couple of steps. He turned back to her with an almost rakish, grim smile. “I hadn’t expected company, but I’ve got a suite anyway,” he said, “and in any case… I think we’d probably better be able to get to the grit of things alone.” ~~~~~ He hadn’t waited much longer than it took for the elevator doors to close to pin her in the corner and plant a kiss on her. It was not the touch of a self-assured man—rather the touch of a man who needed assurance. There hadn’t been much talking once they had reached the suite. Seymour’s idea of sealing a pact wasn’t about talking over nuances, not in this case. Her eyes shifted over to the side, across the wide expense of bed. Seymour lay curled on his side, breathing lightly. He seemed in sleep like a soul at peace, and not the conflicted being she had seen a mere half hour earlier in the hotel lobby. What she might have gotten out of their little tryst didn’t seem to bother him much. He was lost in his own little world right now, and that world included only what could pierce through all the pain that was hazing his every waking perception. Seymour had shrouded himself in it, and begun to see his world through jaded lenses. This pact would only be complete if he could somehow go back and erase what had happened in the past, in Global. She smirked to herself, knowing she was only part of that equation—and she didn’t think Dawn would take too kindly to being the subject of Seymour’s amorous ambitions. In that case, she’d just have to warn Dawn that Seymour was a little… unstable… right now. Shadow sat up slowly, so as not to wake him. She knew that he wouldn’t much mind seeing her gone when he woke up, if only because agreeing to something like this would more than likely give him quite the guilty conscience for a few weeks. She shook her head with a gentle, barely discernible motion. Seymour had a great deal to learn, and she didn’t think he was going to take to the lesson heartily. He was terrifically guilt-ridden at the moment, stuck in the past, and obsessed with the idea that if he could have only hopped in a time machine and gone back, he could have made it all right for himself. He could have untied Laura from the tracks in time to save her from the freight train that was Rich Rollins’ influence on her life. Understandable a viewpoint though it might have been, it was naïve. And Seymour would have to realize that—he’d have to realize that the fantasy was over, that his new allies expected him to realize that not everybody got to live like he’d lived. He’d have to learn that being part of Dies Irae didn’t involve using starpower to your advantage. It involved bludgeoning people over the head with the truth, and then using it to brutally flay them. He’d have to stop feeling sorry for the unbelievers. He’d have to get real. She pulled taut the laces on the side of her shirt and stood carefully, quietly. A few stealthy steps led her to the door of the suite, and back out into the hallway. Seymour slept on inside, blissfully unaware of anything outside of his own relief. He’d gotten what he wanted out of this—the assurance that someone cared about him. And she would not have called it a lie. His happiness concerned her. She cared a great deal for Seymour. However, he would have to return that favor, and she wasn’t quite sure how he’d take it when he found that out. .:: ::: ::. The Present McFarlane Estate Toronto, Canada Shadow let the drapes fall back into place, her eyes rolling almost instinctively. “He leave?” Dawn hadn’t even looked up from filing her nails as she asked the question. Shadow folded her arms over her chest and leaned against the window frame. “Yes, that beeping was the cab. I was wondering if he was actually going to hang around here moping for the rest of the week before this match or not.” Dawn snorted. “It figures he would run out without so much of an apology for screwing up like he did against Ciel. You know, how he’s been acting lately, I wouldn’t be surprised if he just left because he can’t stand the idea of most of us hoping you’ll retain the Global Title against him. What does he expect, anyway? After he basically told you to piss off a couple of weeks ago when you told him he was out of line?” Lia’s lips set in a thin line at the reminder of that situation. It made her very blood boil to think about it, and it had been playing in her head over and over again ever since it happened. Plainly, Seymour Almasy did not remember his role. He did not remember what he had agreed to when he’d joined Dies Irae, and now he wanted to twist the rules to make himself look like the victim. She shook her head. “I don’t know what’s wrong with him. He’s spent the vast majority of the past month in the basement screwing around on the computer, or on that stupid PS2 of his… skipped or been late to every house meeting we’ve had, missed promotional dates…” “Not promotional dates for PTC,” Dawn reminded her, glowering darkly from underneath her bangs, “He’s been to every one of those.” Shadow tapped her long fingernails against her forearm. “It would be an understatement at this point to say that we’ve got a problem.” Dawn nodded her agreement. “It’s a shame that Ian couldn’t take him out when he had the chance in IG 23,” Dawn said, pausing in her motion for a moment to look up at Shadow, “Do you think I need to step in somewhere? I mean, I could go to your defense with you, try to talk some sense into him before we go on air…” Dawn trailed off as Shadow shook her head vehemently. “Absolutely not. This is a lesson he needs to learn without any more reminders. We’ve been patient… I’d even say we’ve been indulgent as far as his relentless glory-hogging goes. The time for discussion, even if it’s yelling, is over.” “I still plan on giving him a piece of my mind,” Dawn insisted, “He’s got to come back to the mansion and get his things some time.” Shadow felt a neat smirk slide across her features, and she couldn’t help but chuckle under her breath. “What?” Dawn asked, sitting up a little straighter in her armchair, “What’d I say?” Shadow’s fingertips gently ran over the heavy brocade of the draperies. “You didn’t say anything, it’s just that… well, I think you’re wrong on that count. He left with his bags.” She jerked her finger towards the window with a wry smile. “What?!” Dawn was on her feet in a split second, and the two of them parted the curtains together, peering out into the twilight that was setting in over the lonely sprawling gardens of the Estate. In the distance, just turning onto the main road from the gravel drive, were two pinpricks of red light. The ember-glowing taillights of the cab glowed for only a moment longer before fading into not much more than insignificant dust in the distance. Shadow doubted that any of them would ever see Seymour Almasy outside of a wrestling ring again. There would come a time when he’d be out of breath, when he’d find himself cornered. Finally, he would have to turn and look into the face of the sulfur-eyed demon he had been running from all this time. .:: ::: ::. “Destruction, hence, like creation, is one of Nature’s mandates.” -Marquis de Sade
