[IG 27] Lost Hope Part Two

[An honor to be facing The Poet and Winters. Even if I lost, I gave it my all against two brillant writers. It's been a blast. Now, I need to go kill myself as I wrote 4 RP's. Blame Argyle. He wanted to kill me. He told me so.] An entire day had passed since he went through the roller coaster of hell. Around him, people were talking amongst themselves about the different subjects of life; religion, politics, and romance. Yet, for Craig, he was disinterested in anything that they were talking about. Above him was a clock that slowly clicked off minutes in an hour. For each click, he felt like he was one minute closer to death. In all reality, he was. Before him sat your standard drink glass that you’d find at a bar. By this point, he must’ve had five or six drinks in the past hour. Frankly he had lost count. He could no longer care how many drinks he consumed, as long as they lead him to his eventual destination. Death. The bartender slowly walked over to him to check on him. This was the third time in as many nights that he had seen Craig in his bar and he truly wondered why he continued to come here only to get plastered time after time. For the bartender, he saw people of all sorts of nature with their own, unique problems. It was obvious to him that Craig was depressed, but Craig hadn’t reached that point of opening up as most of his customers had done in the past. Part of him was grateful for that. However, the other part of him felt for him because of whatever pain Craig was going through it must be something truly terrible. “Another drink?” the bartender asked and Craig just looked up at him. He took a minute to form his words, having been lost in his own thoughts once again. “Yeah. Get me a Rum and Coke, please,” Craig said before looking around him some more, admiring the people who were deep into their conversations. Craig found human life rather interesting, mainly because he didn’t have your typical life. Amnesia, framed for murder, killing people, and deep depression was the complete opposite of a typical human life. At times, he didn’t even consider himself human. Instead, he felt like he was someone just observing the life that was going on around him just so he could suffer more and more. Unfortunately, it was working. As the bartender set his drink down before him, Craig could feel a sharp pain in the center of his forehead. He had felt it before, last night to be exact. He moaned as he dropped his head slightly, and the bartender knew something was wrong. He figured that Craig had drunk too much already and was ready to expel the poison in his system. He quickly walked around the corner, since he didn’t want any vomit in his bar, and helped Craig out of his bar stool. “I’m taking you to the bathroom, give me one second, and don’t blow it all in here,” the bartender said softly, making sure that only Craig could hear him. Craig just groaned, trying to find the strength to actually form words. “L… listen, just… just… let me… d-d-…. die,” Craig finally said, his words spilling out of him as if he had been drinking all night long. Unfortunately, that wasn’t the case as the pain in his head was becoming worse and worse. He could feel the life slipping away from him and he wondered to himself if this was death. He wondered to himself if he was dying because he couldn’t take the pain anymore. Instead of lingering on those thoughts, he just let go. He didn’t want to live anymore and he saw this as his ticket out of here. The bartender glanced over and saw him passed out, and knew it wasn’t good. Yet, he continued to take him to the bathroom anyways just to get him out of the plain view. The pain worsened for Craig as he imagined that death had to be painful for it to be death. Yet, as he felt the pain worsen and him slipping into unconsciousness, he knew it wasn’t death he was experiencing. It was another memory implanting itself into his brain. --- Balloons filled the entire room as people looked on with delight on their face. Craig looked over and he saw a little girl with long brown hair just sitting in her seat and smiling. He slowly pulled himself out of the seat and walked over to her, and smiled at her. “Happy Birthday, Sarah!” he said excitedly, pride exploding from his throat. His little girl looked up at him and just smiled back. She had yet to start speaking, but he could see the recognition in her beautiful blue eyes that she knew that it was her father speaking to her. Slowly, she raised her arms, wanting to be held by him, and he did just that. There was nothing greater than being able to hold her in his arms and he just caressed her arm before walking back to his seat. Around him, people were deep into their conversations, all there to celebrate Sarah’s first birthday. From the kitchen, which was across the room, came Samantha who looked as beautiful as ever. In her hand was a phone and she slowly walked over towards Craig. He knew from the look in her eyes that it was for him and he sighed, not wanting to take away from this time with his daughter. “It’s Rick,” she told him, referring to the owner of the wrestling business he was with. He took the phone, not wanting to in the least bit, and place it to his ear. “Hey, Rick. Can’t I spend the day alone with my daughter without you calling me? It’s her first birthday,” he told his boss, irritated at the fact that he had been interrupted in the middle of the happiest day of his life. Twelve months ago, he didn’t even know if he would have a family. As he held his little girl in the hospital twelve months ago, he remembered the trauma that Samantha was going through. She had just gone through surgery due to both of her fallopian tubes exploding on her. Thankfully, he had married a strong and resilient wife. She lived through it, but lost the ability to ever have children again. For the two of them, Sarah would be their only child, and for Craig, he couldn’t imagine trying to raise her with the love of his life. “Listen, I know you’re supposed to have today and tomorrow off from the shows, but we need you out in Chicago by tonight for a match. I’ve got a ticket waiting for you,” Rick said with a rough kind of voice. That was the kind of person Rick was though. He was a tough son of a bitch who had been trained in the military. At the sound of those words though, Craig could care less if he was from the military or was a Care Bear. This was his daughter’s birthday and he was being asked to basically skip out on it. “No, Rick. I’m not coming. I told you in advance that I needed this weekend off. I’m spending it with my daughter. You already don’t pay me enough, so what makes you think I’m going to get on the flight and go wrestle for you? Forget about it,” Craig said, exploding into the phone. The people around him were watching him. Samantha stood before him and reached down to take Sarah out of his arms, but she held on for dear life, refusing to let go of her daddy. She just smiled at her and kissed her on top of her head, touched by the love she had for him. “You get on that plane Craig, or you’re fired,” he said, and before Rick could say another word, Craig had hung up the phone. He had just lost his job, but he could care less. He would find another job with a promotion somewhere else. This was something that you could never replace. The love from a daughter was priceless, and seeing her on her first birthday was a once in a lifetime experience, especially for Samantha and Craig. He then looked at his daughter who just had the same smile on her face that she had when he picked her up out of her chair. “Daddy loves you Sarah,” he said as he leaned in and kissed her on the cheek. As he did that, she did something amazing. “Daddy,” she said, and the fire in Craig’s eyes just exploded as he looked at Sarah, then at Samantha, before looking at Sarah again. She had spoken her first words, and the two parents couldn’t believe it for a second. Not only did Craig’s red eyes turn into a fire, but he could feel the love exploding from his heart. He loved his little girl and this just made him even happier to hear the first words come out of her mouth. “I’ll always be here for you, Sarah. Always,” he said, the happiness just spilling out of him. He then looked up at Samantha who was beaming bright with a smile on her face. “I guess it’s time to get the birthday girl her cake,” she said before walking off. He just sat there with Sarah in his arms. He was as happy as he could be and nothing could take that away from him. --- Koff. Koff. Koff. Craig could feel himself coming back into the realm of consciousness and his lungs were scratchy as hell. He continued to cough as he felt like someone had brought him back from life. As the cough subsided, he looked around and noticed that he was in the bathroom. He then realized he had passed out again as the headache he had experienced last night hit him once again. Slowly, he groaned as he pulled himself over to the toilet, wondering if the alcohol in his system was going to expunge itself. “Who the hell leaves their customer in the bathroom when they pass out?” he said to no one in particular as he sat there, feeling the pain in his heart. For him, the pain in his heart was even greater then the headache he was experiencing from the memory implanting itself into his brain. This time, it had been a memory of his daughter’s first birthday and of her first words. Every time he had a memory injected into his brain, it split him in half so horribly that he wanted to end his life. This time was no different. It had been the reason he was drinking himself into oblivion. He wanted it to be over. He had no reason to live. He sat there for a few minutes, lost in his thoughts, but felt confident in the fact that he wasn’t about to throw up or anything. Slowly, the pain in his head started to vanish as he wondered to himself again why these memories were coming back to him out of nowhere. As he pulled himself up, he just wanted more alcohol. Thankfully, there was a bar right outside of the bathroom that would help him with that. He dragged himself out of the bathroom stall and towards the sink so he could clear his mind for a quick second before he walked back outside. As he looked at himself in the mirror, he saw a shell of himself. If Sarah or Samantha had ever seen him like this, they would be disappointed in himself. For Craig, he was disappointed in himself. He hated who he had become, but couldn’t find the strength to move on with his life and live. At one point in his life, he knew he had found that courage and motivation, but here and now, with nothing to grasp hold of, he just couldn’t move forward when he had everything he needed once upon a time. As he looked at himself, he had an idea. A plan of sorts. Without any kind of warning, he slammed his fist into the mirror and shattered it. He knew he didn’t have long if he wanted a chance to do things his way, and picked up one of the shards from the mirror. This was it. He knew he could do it now. He knew just how he had to do it, in a fashion that was definitive and wouldn’t allow him to live. Slowly, but surely, he started to place the piece of glass by his throat. As he did so, he could feel the thoughts rushing through his mind, but he ignored them out. He just shut them out and focused on what he had to do. His family was waiting for him and he needed to be there with him. He could feel the sharpness of the glass against his skin and he was ready to do it. He started to apply the pressure, but the pain in his head returned without any kind of warning. Immediately, his body collapsed to the ground and he started shivering. He was unsure of why he was shivering. Then, it hit him. He wasn’t shivering. It was a seizure. His body was fighting against the pain in his brain. It couldn’t take it any longer. Then, blackness covered him. He had passed out again. However, just as the blackness swallowed him, he heard a creaking noise. A door was opening. Someone was coming in and would see what happened. Then, he was gone. --- Knock. Knock. Knock. The steady beat of someone knocking on a door echoed throughout the entire house. Craig stood at the door, knocking at it, and waiting for some kind of response. Unfortunately, he wasn’t getting one. His right hand then grabbed at the knob and slowly twisted it, and he pushed the door open. Before him was a nice sized room, decorated as if a girl had done it. Interesting enough, a girl had decorated the room. In the corner of the room was a bed with a pink blanket on it. Craig’s feet carried him over there and in the bed was a small girl, with beautiful brown hair and blue eyes. “Good morning, Sarah,” Craig said softly, trying to wake his daughter up out of her peaceful sleep. Slowly, her eyes fluttered open and the first thing her eyes landed upon was her father. She smiled as she started to wipe the sleep out of her eyes. In Craig’s left hand were two presents for his daughter. “Good morning, daddy,” she said, glad that the morning had come. She started to sit up in her bed as Craig sat down next to him, looking as if he was worse for wear, but still happy to see his daughter. “Happy Birthday,” he said, the joy escaping from his voice. His daughter was his entire world, and just being able to talk to her brightened up his day. “Thanks,” she said as she wrapped her tiny arms around him. Just the love he received from his daughter was enough for him. The fact that he got so much more from her was a blessing for him. He then placed the presents on her bed and she smiled before pulling her hair back from her eyes. Today was her fifth birthday, but it was a bittersweet one. Someone was missing from the picture. “First off, this isn’t all that you’re getting. But, I wanted to start your morning off a little better then some of the mornings have started as of late. This first one is something that your mother bought for you last month,” he said, his voice starting to constrict on him at the mere mention of Samantha. Sadness washes over Sarah’s face as she takes the present in her hands. It had been a month since her mother had been brutally murdered on her way home from the grocery store. The news had hit both of them rather hard as she had been a wonderful wife and an even better mother. Sarah took it hard, but Craig took it even harder. That was the person he loved and to have her ripped from her life so traumatically was rather hard on him. She took the present and started unwrapping it, feeling the pressure in her chest, and trying to hold back on the tears. As she opened it, she saw that it was a book and looked at it for a moment. She thought about her mother buying her this book a month ago, not knowing that she wouldn’t be able to give her that book on Sarah’s birthday. Sarah was intelligent for her age and had gained her father’s way of thinking about the big picture. It made her shiver as she flipped through the book and noticed it was a blank. It was a journal. “Your mother always talked about how your thoughts were plentiful, and more than anything, she didn’t want you to forget them. When she was your age, she always wrote in a journal. Even when we first met she was writing in a journal. She considered a person’s thoughts to be the most important things, the most valuable objects, which we have as human beings,” he told her, trying to fight back his own tears. She just simply nodded her hair as she looked it, lost in her own thoughts about her mother. She then looked up at her father and saw the sadness etched in her face. He could feel her eyes upon him and it warmed his heart. “Now, open the other one, sweetheart,” he said, and she did just that. She opened the small, felt box and looked inside to see a necklace with a ring on it. She remembered the ring rather distinctly. It was her mother’s wedding ring. No longer could she hold back the tears. She had tried to be strong for her father, but she just couldn’t do it any more. This was too much for her. She missed her mother more than life itself. “I--- I c-can’t take this,” she managed to say to him as he wrapped his arms around him. He just simply nodded his head as this was always meant to be her ring. It had been that on her sixteenth birthday, her mother would give her the ring. Yet, Samantha hadn’t made it that far. Craig felt like it was the most important thing her mother could give her besides her wisdom and love. “You will, Sarah. I want you to wear it to remember your mother. I know you’ll never forget her, but this will be a reminder that you have a purpose in your life. That you were and will always be loved by your mother. It’s tough dealing with a death, and you feel like you have no purpose any longer. This will remind you that you do. That you have not lost your mother, because she’s there with you everyday, and she wants to see you become the amazing woman she knew you would become,” he told her as she just held on to him. The days had been rough. It would only get worse as time moved forward. “I miss her so much, daddy,” she told him, the tears getting caught in her throat. “Me too, baby. Me too,” he responded, no longer able to hold back his tears. He hated to see Sarah cry, but they had lost so much. Unfortunately, they would lose so much more. --- “Craig, come back to me,” a mysterious voice whispered into his ears. As the light started to return to Craig and he opened his red eyes, he realized that someone was holding him up. The throbbing in his head was starting to cease much quicker then it had before, but still he felt the pain in his ears. Next to him laid the glass shard he had held in his hands before he blacked out once again. Yet, he hadn’t the slightest clue as to who was trying to help him, and why they knew his name. Slowly, his eyes scanned the room against him and then they landed upon the person holding him. It was Melody, a woman he had met while training with Master Thibaut so that he could get his memories back. He started to recoil from her, but realized he didn’t have the energy to do so. The two of them were sitting upon the tile in the bathroom as his head rested in her lap. “What’re you doing here?” he asked, the tension in the room growing considerably. She just smiled back at him as there was no love lost between these two. The first time he had met her, she jammed a metal electrical object into his back and proceeded to shock him, causing a considerable amount of pain to him. Ever since, they had been at each other’s throat, and she blamed him for the death of one of the students at the temple that she was teaching. Yet, here and now, she was trying to help him, and he didn’t have the slightest clue as to why. “Master Thibaut sent me. He had a premonition that something was going on with you. Sure enough, he was right. Some of your memories are coming back to you, am I right?” she said calmly. He nodded his head as he broke his sight on her and looked around the bathroom. He hated the position he was in, but decided to let that feeling go so he could work on getting better or ending his life. “Why are these memories coming back to me?” he asked her and she just remained silent for a moment before responding back to him. “I have a theory, but you need to explain to me why there was a glass shard next to you when I came in here. Tell me that you’re not trying to end your life,” she said, knowing what Craig’s answer was going to be. He flexed his right hand and felt the pain traveling his entire body. The memories were doing more harm than good to him, and he wasn’t a fan of this. “I was trying to kill myself,” he told her bluntly and she just shook her head with anger. “Trying to kill yourself? How pathetic of you. I thought you better than that, but I see I was wrong. You have so much to accomplish and instead, you’re trying to end it all. Pathetic,” she said, obviously not happy with Craig’s intentions, but he could care less. It was his life and he wanted to end it. “So, do you want my help with this then?” she asked and he immediately told himself yes. If she was going to end the pain and end his life, he was game for that. “Yes, please, end my life. I can’t take it any longer,” he told her and she just shook her head again. “No, this will be much worse,” she told him before placing her fingers on his forehead. The pain this time was blinding. Instead of a black light, he saw a white light, and then he was out once again. --- A body lied in his arms. As he looked at the body, he saw that it was Sarah. The tears were streaming out of his eyes, not able to believe that his daughter was dead. Yet, her blood stained his arms by the second. As he slowly looked up, he saw the person who had killed her. Jocelyn. In the worse way, he wanted to get up and just kill her, but it was his child in his arms. He refused to let go of her. He could never let go of her. “You pushed my hand, Craig. You pushed it and now you have paid for it,” Jocelyn said before turning away from him. She was cold in her tone and her movements, and he wanted her to feel his pain for what she had done, but he couldn’t move. He was paralyzed with the sadness in his heart. Sarah lied in his arms. It shouldn’t have come to this he told himself. Three years ago, he had lost Samantha. Today he had lost Sarah. It just wasn’t fair. Life had dealt him a horrible hand. “You’ll pay for this!” he yelled out, the anger eating him alive. Jocelyn stopped in her tracks and turned around. There was no happiness on her face. Just despair. “I hope that day comes, Craig. For your sake, I hope it does,” she told him, before disappearing in the fog that was surrounding Craig and his daughter. He lost all focus as he held on to her, the tears dropping from his face and onto her lifeless body. --- “No!” he yelled out as he sat up, ignoring the pain in his body. The image of him holding his daughter, the memory of it all, was too much for him. He pulled himself away from Melody and pushed himself into a corner so he could just be by himself. His body was fighting him in the worse way. It was punishing him for the mistakes he had made in the long run. He deserved every ounce of this, but he was a coward. He wanted it to end. He couldn’t take it any longer. “Yes, Craig, you revenged her death. Now, you feel like you have no purpose left. Everyone you’ve cared about is dead,” Melody said as she stood up and looked down on Craig, physically and mentally. Craig could care less. She was right. He had no purpose left. “If I have no purpose left, then what am I to do?” he asked her, wanting to honestly know. “You came to Thibaut and wanted your memories back. He’s doing that, but he warned you of what could happen. People don’t understand, but sometimes, ignorance is truly bliss. You were given another shot at the world around you, lord knows why, and yet you rejected it. You needed something more. Now that you’ve gotten it, you want it all gone. You rather be dead. You can’t have your cake and eat it too Craig,” she told him, and then she kneeled down before him. “Your life has a purpose. Yet, you need something tangible, so I’m here to give you that purpose that you so desperately need. You think your daughter is dead? Wrong. She’s alive and she needs you Craig. You need to save your daughter,” Melody finished telling him, knowing that would wake him up. He looked at her, stunned by her words. He slowly pulled himself up and looked her straight in the eyes. “She’s alive? You’re speaking the truth? How is that possible?” he asked her, wanting the answers that he knew that she more than likely didn’t have. She just remained silent. “I can put you on the path, but you have to find the answers to yourself. You’re looking for purpose? Then there’s your purpose. Sarah’s alive and she’s waiting for her father to save her. The question is, will that purpose lead you to another chance, or will it lead you to death?” she asked him, but he could care less. If his daughter was alive, he would do whatever it took to get her back. Even if that meant his death. Fin.
