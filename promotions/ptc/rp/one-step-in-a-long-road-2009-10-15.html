One Step in a Long Road

Date: 2009-10-15 Just the Start He felt the tremendous weight of millions upon millions of people on top of his mighty shoulders. At times, Senator Sean Kelly could hear their screams and cries, begging him for help. There were days when the job felt overwhelming, but, for the first-time Senator from Delaware, he loved his job nonetheless. The midnight oil was burning on this particular night and he sat behind a large, majestic oak desk with a stack of papers on his desk that he had to read and proof. Life was never easy for a man of his position, especially when so many people relied on him. From the first day he had stepped into his office after taking his oath, he began to work. Morals and pride that had been engrained into him from an early age had driven him to a crazy workload within his first few days, and quickly, the news organizations began to take notice. Kelly's approval rating in the miniscule state that had elected him was a shocking 93%, and there were already rumors of him taking a swipe at being President of the United States. All of that was talk to him though. Sean just wanted to work. His pen flew furiously against the paper, making adjustments and amendments to the bill that sat highest not just on his desk, but in his mind as well. Poverty had stricken the nation like never before, and he was determined to find a way to curb the rising tide before it took everyone under for good. In the corner of his office was an hourglass with beads of sand trickling down until it hit the bottom. Sean used this as a barometer for the day, when he should wrap it up. When it emptied out, he knew his work was done. Not a moment sooner though. His blue eyes, puzzling and endearing, fluttered towards his door as he heard a shallow knock against it. "Come in," Sean's gruff voice welcomed the visitor that awaited him. The door slowly swung open and through the threshold entered his Senior Aide, and the person most responsible for him being here, Heather Gower. Her long blonde hair flowed effortlessly to the middle of her back while her hazel eyes caught the attention of every man around her. "You have a visitor sir," she spoke in this smooth, silky voice. Sean's right arm immediately shot up and he pushed back his shirt to reveal that it was nearly half past eleven. His mind instantly went to work as to figure out who could possibly be disturbing him at such a late hour, knowing full well that he was easily the only person in D.C. who worked this late outside of the President and his staff. "Let him in, Heather, and then you get home to get some rest. We've got a busy day tomorrow." Heather flashed Sean the smile that had the press in love with her. A moment later, she ushered in an older, balding man who was bereft with good looks, a pale comparison to the two he shared the room with. Heather quickly shut the door closed and for the first time, Sean noticed a large, manila folder in the man's right hand. It took a moment for Sean to realize that the man was a congressman out of Kansas. "Congressman, to what do I owe this pleasure tonight?" The man cleared his throat for a second. "It is not with pleasure that I come here tonight. Honestly, Senator, I wouldn't be here if it wasn't that I had been told by several congressman to approach you about this issue that has seemingly arisen in my home district. I almost laughed at their faces for suggesting a freshman senator, but there was a serious tone in their eyes which just tell me I had to come see you." Sean had become accustomed to this. "Congressman..." Sean then realized that the man's name was lost to him. "Arnold Packard, Senator." Sean nodded his head. "Congressman Packard, let's try to shorten our sentences about your surprise at my stature in D.C., and try to focus on the reason that you're actually here." "You know I was just elected to my fifth term here?" "I did not, sir. Please, tell me what it is that brought you here tonight." The manila folder opened to reveal several glossy pictures revealing different symbols that had been carved, burned, or etched into various buildings, farms, and rooftops. A serious chill was sent up the Senator's spine. "Several of my constituents have woken up over the past week to find these symbols over their magnificent town. Fear has overtaken my people, Senator Kelly, and it seems like due to your status and position, you might be the man with some answers to these symbols." Unfortunately, the Congressman from Kansas was markedly wrong. "No, sir. I don't have any answers, but I'm seriously trying to find some. Do you mind if I keep these pictures? As soon as I have an answer as to what they mean, I would be more than happy to share with you my findings." "Of course, of course." With that, the Congressman eagerly rose from his seat and shook the Senator's hand. Sean did it without even thinking about it as his mind had started to wander towards the meanings of these symbols, and the impact they had on his life to this point. Before he knew it, Arnold was gone, and he was picking up his phone. From the moment he had arrived at the Capitol, he had been selected and elected onto the Senator Intelligence Committee. He considered it an honor. A few days went by though before he realized it was a curse. Within the Senior Intelligence Committee, there were a few select members who considered themselves a Cabal, and Sean had been made a member of them. A few people from the CIA, FBI, and NSA were part of this Cabal. It was something Sean regretted almost instantly. They wanted him for his experience with these symbols. They played with the strings of his heart to make it happen. His right hand slowly pulled open a drawer and he pulled out a few more pictures. The one on top was the most disturbing of them all. A young man laid in the middle of the street, dead, with the same symbol on his chest. That young man was Sean's son. *** The Little Girl Lelah Stearns would have to be described as a major cutie. Even at the age of ten, she was the kind of girl that turned heads. The thing that endeared her most to her friends and family was her heart that never seemed to stop giving. Each night, he tried to find the strength to curl up in his bed, alone, and get some sleep. Each and every night though, he failed. He just needed more time. *** A Dream A barren wasteland. Craig took a careful step, the ground littered with countless dead bodies. His red eyes scanned the horizon carefully, ready for the next attack to strike without warning. He moved effortlessly through the bodies though, his body trained for this kind of fight and war. Craig heard a loud buzzing coming from behind him, and swiftly dropped to the ground as a wave of arrows flew past the very spot he just stood. He knew he had to move fast. Craig pulled himself up within seconds and ducked behind a nearby wall. Footsteps echoed throughout the hollow buildings, and Craig began to dash down an alleyway. As he reached the end though, his body collapsed into the ground as the man he was trying to get away from knocked him to the ground. The sheer speed of this man was too much for Craig to handle. Craig tried to get up off the ground, but was met with a powerful fist that nearly jarred a few of his teeth loose. The ground spun beneath him, but he fought through the fog and narrowly dodged another fist from the man before he slammed his knee into the mysterious man’s midsection. For Craig, it felt like slamming his knee into steel and he felt the pain as it coursed his entire body. Craig refused to go down though as he ducked a wild punch and drove his foot into the man’s chest, finally knocking him down, on top of bodies that were victims of a war that Craig was unable to stop. As Craig began to walk up to the man, a forearm collided against the back of his neck and within seconds, Craig found himself on his back with a shin across his throat. Another one, he thought to himself while his arms thrashed against his assailant. “Stop moving,” the voice from the man thundered down around Craig, but Craig refused as he drove his elbow across the man’s jaw, breaking the hold in the process. “Stop fighting,” the other attacker speaks in this monotone voice. As if without warning, a deafening noise overcame Craig, who rolled over onto his stomach clutching his ears. A large hand reached down and yanked him up by the shirt before Craig was tossed effortlessly into the nearby wall. The air left his lungs, painfully, and Craig struggled just to stay on his feet. His instincts took over completely as he dodged the fist that came for him and heard the crumbling brick over his head from the impact of fist against stone. Craig connected a punch and then another one. The feeling of iron against skin didn’t slow him down though. Just human, he told himself, regardless of their strength. His left leg shot out and caught his other attacker in the ribs before he spun out of it and his right leg connected with a roundhouse kick to the back of the other attacker’s skull. The man collapsed in a slump as Craig grabbed the second assailant around the throat and forced him to the ground. This is the hell that Craig wanted to prevent. Sadly, he failed. Instead of wallowing in his own pity though, he continued to fight, not wanting to give up. He could still see the faces of the people he let down, and he refused to let their deaths go without fair recourse. Craig’s knee collides across the man’s jaw and he could hear the crushing bones. Hands came around his chest, but this time, Craig was too fast for his original foe. He spun away and wrapped his forearm around the man’s neck before snapping it. Victorious at last, this battle might be done, but the war was far from being over. Not until he could find Alexandra Pierce. He began to take a step through the war-torn landscape, but stopped. A horse, seemingly alone, wandered into his sight and took a long look at him before it looked down the opposite way. A lone white horse, majestic in sight, and mighty in strength. Craig was seemingly frozen in time as another horse approached. This one is pure black; a stallion. Before Craig could even process this information, a third horse, silver appeared, and it was quickly followed by a fourth horse, red in color. They all looked at him for a second, and Craig didn’t know what was happening as they did so. Then it all went to black. “Wake up,” a voice overpowered him and his eyes immediately shot open and his body stiffened in pain and shock. His torso came up, tense, and he looked around for an answer of some sort. A dream, he told himself, and he found his breathing finally start to slow down. The voice had some power over him, and he wasn’t certain how it could do that. Craig swung his legs over the edge of his bed and found the comforting cold wood underneath him. No longer were there any bodies littered there or this overwhelming sense of death as it lingered in the air. He began to stand when a voice stopped him. “Please, if you’re not wearing any clothes under that sheet, throw these on.” A pair of shorts landed squarely on his face. If it hadn’t been for the familiarity of the voice, Craig would’ve killed the man already. Instead, he found some comfort and looked over at the hidden man who stood in the corner of his bedroom. “Sphinx?” He could almost see the faint creases in the man’s face. “The one and only. We’ve got some talking to do. What do you know about this thing called the GTT?” Craig wanted to laugh. He really did. Sadly, this was not a laughing matter. *** A World Lost South of the Altai Mountains and the grasslands of Mongolia, there lies a desert that is of sheer importance to the survival of not only humanity, but of this world. She smiled back at him and grabbed his hand as he began to sit down. “Doing very, very well this morning.” “Enjoying the morning sun?” “Oh, yes.” The two sat there in silence for a moment, letting the sheer beauty of this morning overtake them. “You know,” the man began, “today is just another step on the long road to healing.” He had made a point to tell her this each and every morning. She had quickly grown old of it. Instead of responding to him, she just nodded her head. Without wasting another moment, the man stood up. “I’m going to make you some breakfast.” “Thanks.” As he began to walk away, he froze in mid-step, and turned back towards her. “Sabrina?” She looked at him over her left shoulder. “I love you.” He didn’t wait for a response, and Sabrina turned her head towards the rising ocean. Under her breath, she whispered, “I love you.” Yet, it wasn’t meant for him. It was meant for another. - Credit goes to the Bible for the Scriptures used in this piece.
