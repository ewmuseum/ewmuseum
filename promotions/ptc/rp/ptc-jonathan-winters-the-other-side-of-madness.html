[IG 27: Jonathan Winters]: The Other Side of Madness.

11 February 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Infinite Gauntlet. It was all Jonathan Winters had ever wanted. A genuine shot at glory that couldn’t be snatched out of his grasp at the last minute by some damned thief with delusions of capability. It was his dream. And now? Well, now round seventy-seven of the Infinite Gauntlet was the last thing on his mind. What’s more, whenever such thoughts threatened to linger in his head, he forcibly removed them without hesitation; this was no time for distractions. This was important. This was about Aeryn, the woman he thought he loved. This was about the woman whom he thought loved him. Regardless of how this one would play out, there was one thing he knew for sure; his life would be dramatically changed forever. There was nothing he could do to stop that now. *** Winters stood on the raised balcony of the desolate and rapidly decaying warehouse, observing his helpless prey positioned on the lower level intently. To the best of his knowledge, the building hadn’t been in full use for the better part of seven years, but the majority of its original heavy machinery still littered the factory floor, gathering dust and nesting a plethora of vermin. Handy things to have around when conducting an interrogation, though he very much doubted he would need such apparatus to get the job done; he was a very persuasive man. Casting his gaze downward at Rune Wallasey, tied to a plain wooden chair, gagged with tape and blinded by a black velvet bag, Winters almost felt sorry for the poor bastard. Almost. At one time, Winters had regarded him as a friend, but that was during less complicated times. Now, he felt nothing but hatred and pity and Winters had quickly chosen to disregard the pity; it would become something of a hindrance for what he had in mind. He made his way toward the stairway, leading downward in an elaborate spiral toward ground level, stopping metres away from his hapless victim and idly contemplating precisely how all this began. It all started three months ago. At the time, Winters worked for a man known as Cornelius Stakiri, the owner of a legitimate organisation that dealt primarily with genetic research, with a sideline of arms manufacturing for profiteering purposes. Over the years, Stakiri’s ruthless nature transcended the unnecessary confinement of legality and soon found himself to be a major player in criminal underworld. He carved himself something of a substantial territory throughout the east coast of the country, using his legitimate business ventures to front, and ultimately streamline, his underworld operations. It gave him an edge and an edge was all he needed to beat away the competition. Stakiri was a powerful man, who governed his territory with an iron first. As cliched as it sounds, people either obeyed him or died -- there was no third option. Also around the time, Stakiri’s primarily field operative, a beautiful brunette Winters knew as simply Aeryn, came back into the picture, bringing chaos back with her. She’d been sent to assess one of Stakiri’s legitimate business rivals, an unknown genetic engineering organisation up in Salt Lake City. Turns out, they had ties to the underworld too and didn’t much like Stakiri’s attempt at corporate espionage. War was declared. The rival organisation bankrolled a number of lower standing crime lords enough to be a threat and set them loose on Stakiri, thereby weakening his grip on power. Then, In his primary base of operation; a skyscraper in the center of New York City, an unknown assailant breached security and made sure he knew that they could get to him. The final straw came when they somehow managed to turn a number of Stakiri’s most trusted general’s to betray him and he hasn’t been the same since. He reached new levels of paranoia, believing someone else within his inner circle had been feeding the enemy information regarding his operation. The finger was pointed directly at Winters from the outset and he couldn‘t really blame them. Winters was a loner at the best of times and often considered his illustrious employer’s orders as optional. And so, Stakiri had him closely followed. Then, he tried to torture any relevant information out of him, or so he thought. After escaping the torturer’s grasp, he headed directly for the leader of the team assigned to take him down, Rune Wallasey, looking to inflict some serious harm. Wallasey broke almost immediately, his potent ramblings getting to Winters more than he expected something could. Winters shook these thoughts from his mind. Wallasey was just covering his own ass, there was truth to his words. Perhaps he though it would be better to have Aeryn as an enemy, rather than Stakiri? Winters already knew that was a mistake, but then Wallasey was never the sharpest knife in the draw. Yes, he silently concluded, his mind unwavering now, that’s precisely what happened. Wallasey may very well be a fucking idiot without the brains he was born with, but he is well trained to lie effectively this kind of situation. Winters silently surmised. He’s playing you, you moron! And you’re playing right into his hands! Winters pulled the black velvet bag from Wallasey’s head, causing him to blink wildly as his eyes attempted to adjust to new levels of the light. When the flickering ceased, Winters noted the fearful recognition wash over Wallasey’s face and he ripped away the tape from the mouth of his prey, grinning at the twinge of pain that accompanied it. That’s nothing, fucko, he silently warned. I haven‘t even started yet! “I--I--I told you everything I know!” Wallasey pleaded. “D-don’t kill me! Please! “Thing of it is,” Winters began, shaking his head, “I don’t believe you.” “What? Why?” Winters smiled. “I think you’re playing me. I think you’re telling me precisely what my illustrious employer wants me to hear.” “But it’s the truth, I swear!” “Sure it is.” Winters smile evaporated. “I’m going to give you five seconds. After that five seconds is up, if I don’t hear the truth, then I’m going to start getting violent.” Wallasey shook his head wildly is disbelief, struggling for all his worth. “But I am telling you the truth!” “Five.” “It was Aeryn!” “Four.” “She was the one made the call!” “Three.” “She told me you’d gone AWOL, said she needed you taking down before Stakiri found out!” “Two.” “Aeryn made the call!” “One.” “No! Don’t! It’s the truth I swear!” Winters lunged forward at his helpless victim, implanting both thumbs into Wallasey right eye. He pushed until he heard an oddly satisfying wet pop and agonising screams. Winters felt the blood stain his hands and he liked it. He smiled. *** The snow was thick for this time of year and seemed to have something of untameable glint to it, Jonathan Winters silently observed, carefully stepping out onto the building rooftop. He made it to the edge of the building and glanced outward into the horizon, finding the bleak grey black skyline to be something of a comfort. He’d had enough and he had to get away. Seems like there was only so much screaming Winters could withstand, before it finally became just plain annoying. But Wallasey’s pain was further from his thoughts right now that his glittering wrestling career. It was true, Stakiri’s henchman was trained well, but not that well trained. He’d maintained his story right up until Winters had place both of his thumbs in his other eye socket. It could mean only one thing; Wallasey wasn’t lying. Aeryn did give the order. She betrayed him. “Oh god, Aeryn,” Winters muttered. “Why? Why did you do this to me?” His mind raced, compiling a number of excuses in to somehow understand why she would do this to him, each was more ridiculous than the last and soon it became very obvious to him that there was no reasonable explanation available. “Fuck!” He cursed under his breath, smashing his fist down hard upon the snow-laced protective wall. “Now, now,” the voice began, audibly drawing closer. “Temper, temper.” Winters produced a fierce sneer. He’d heard this clown coming a mile away. Also spotted him desperately scarmbling for cover just as he stepped out onto the roof. If he wanted to make some kind of grand entrance, he failed. It was Rich Langton, Stakiri’s new right hand man. “Fuck off, Langton,” Winters warned, not even bothering to face the man. “I’m not in the mood.” Langton sighed. “Stakiri said you’d be like this.” “He knows me well,” Winters responded. “Well enough to realise I won’t be taking any shit from you.” Winters shot him a cautionary glance as he took a step closer. Langton stopped in his tracks. “Stakiri doesn’t hold any ill will against you, you know that don’t you?” He paused, but not long enough for Winters to answer. “In fact, he wants to help you. He’s just as concerned as you are about the Aeryn situation and wants me to officially offer you aid in uncovering this debacle.” Aeryn may have been the one to give the order to Wallasey, Winters silently reasoned, but only because Stakiri told her to. There’s no way Aeryn’s alone in this. No way... But when Winters glared into Langton’s eyes, he knew then that the offer was a genuine one and then it struck him, like a well placed punch to the gut. What if Aeryn betrayed both he and Stakiri? It’s not possible, is it? No, of course not. Aeryn has been loyal to Stakiri, longer than she’s know me. It wouldn’t happen, it couldn’t happen... “Cut the bullshit,” Winters told the little weasel of a man. “If Aeryn did do this, it was only on Stakiri’s order and we both know it.” Langton sighed. “You’re wrong. Aeryn did this alone and we want answers from her as much as you do. You need our help.” Bullshit. No way, no fucking way. Winters silently screamed. He’s playing me. Just like Wallasey. Just like all the others. He allowed the information to roll around in his head for a time. Isn’t he? Aeryn couldn’t have betrayed Stakiri, could she? Winters sneered. “I’ll do this alone.” Langton smiled. “That’s not advisable.” I have to find out. I have to know for sure. Winters walked away. “Like I give a fuck.” *** It could take days to find her, Winters realised that much, but he didn’t have the time to waste. He had to know the truth and he had to know now. So, he allowed her to find him. Since his illustrious employer, Cornelius Stakiri, had his world turned upside down by the ongoing whole territory war, each of the upper echelon of staff, of which Winters was formerly a part of, received a state of the art panic button, straight out of Stakiri Tech. The device wasn’t on the open market yet, so the risk of device tampering was severely limited. Although, with Stakiri’s recent security problems that didn’t mean a whole hell of a lot. It meant that if Winters pushed the button, Aeryn would inevitably come running. If Aeryn didn’t betray him, then it would be for love. If she did betray him, then it would be for information. Winters winced in agony as the thoughts of the latter option remained rooted in his mind. However, with great effort, he shook it away. Now is not the time! He silently screamed. It also meant that Stakiri would send a crew also. Either to assist, or to take him out. Or both. But that was not Winters greatest concern. Not now. Within ten minutes of pressing the button, Aeryn found him on the roof top of his apartment building. His hands dug deep into the pockets of his knee-long black business coat and his face unnaturally calm. That was how she knew something was wrong. She drew closer to him, her vibrant green eyes piercing his steely exterior the way they always seemed to and he was forced to look away. She rested a gentle hand upon his cheek and forced him to look at her. Her skin was smooth to the touch, she smelt of coconut and her jet black hair was left flowing in the wind. It was his perfect vision of her and somehow, she knew it. She’s come ready to play you, He silently warned himself. Be strong! Resist! “Jonathan,” She greeted, with that little half-smile she always did that seemed to exaggerate her full pink lips. No, He feebly corrected himself. She had nothing to do with this. She couldn’t have... “What’s wrong?” She asked. “Are you okay?” He smiled, weakly. “I’m fine.” Aeryn looked confused. “Then why...” Winters held up a hand. “I just wanted to see you. I just wanted to see how you were.” She grinned and embraced him. “How sweet.” She allowed a minute or two before pulling away and developing a stern expression. “But you used your panic button. You realise Stakiri has access to the signal too, right?” He nodded. “Then we’d better get out of here, before he shows,” she warned, grabbing his hand and leading him to the stairwell. Winters allowed his body to become dead weight and soon they came to a stop. She glared at him, looking for answers and he provided them, “I’m tired of running. I’m waiting until he shows.” Aeryn’s eyes widened. “But why? He’ll torture you again. Is that what you want that, Jonathan?” And that’s when Winters knew. He saw it in her eyes and heard it in her voice. She didn’t want to be here any more than he did, when Stakiri’s team inevitably showed up. Langton was telling the truth. He silently exclaimed. Stakiri didn’t betrayed me, Aeryn did. He backed away from her, his face etched in a colourful mixture of anger, confusion and pain and upon seeing this, Aeryn’s face morphed from concern to bitterness in the blink of an eye. “Why?” He muttered, barely able to splutter the words out of his mouth. She produced a vicious grin, but did not answer him. Instead, she simply turned her back on him and headed down the stairway. Winters shook his head in disbelief and slumped to snow laden floor absently. He leaned back against the cold brick stairway wall, his eyes wide and his mind racing. “Winters,” the voice came, from somewhere behind him. Winters didn’t hear him coming and the voice was too distant to properly put a name to the words. It wasn’t like him to be so sloppy, but he didn’t really care anymore. The man stepped into his line of sight. It was Langton. “Are you okay?” Winters ignored him and a wave of understanding washed over Langton’s face. “She was here, wasn’t she?” He asked, his eyes darting around the immediate area. Winters didn’t answer him and when he was sure Aeryn had fled the scene, Langton calmed and produced a knowing smirk. “You know now, don’t you?” Again his question was met with silence, but it didn’t stop him from persevering. “Aeryn betrayed you.” Winters grunted something under his breath and pushed himself up from the frosted rooftop surface. He walked past Langton and stared out, at the grey black horizon. This time it brought him no comfort. “It wasn’t Aeryn,” Winters told him, heading for the stairway. Langton followed, visibly dumfounded. “What the hell are you talking about?” Winters sighed. “It may have looked and sounded like her, but that woman was not Aeryn.” “Fuck!” Langton exclaimed, running hands through his hair. “You’ve gone crazy! Aeryn betrayed you! Accept it!” Winters persevered. “Believe what you will. I’m going to find out the truth.” To Be Continued...
