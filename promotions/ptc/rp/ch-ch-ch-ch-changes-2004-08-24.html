Ch-ch-ch-ch-changes!

24 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-08-24 Smoke curled up from the ashtray, making its way up to the slowly rotating fan, which pushed it around the room. The ashtray was full, numerous extinguished cigarettes lining the base of it, with one final burning ember finally running out of material to fuel the flame. The room itself was hard to see across, both due to the poor lighting and the smoke itself. The source of illumination was an antique table lamp, its forty watts of power hampered by a lampshade coated with years of grime and neglect. It shone weakly across the hotel room; from the bed, complete with a flowered pattern that was last fashionable in the 1920s, over to the television, tuned to fifty four different brands of static. The tattered chair that sat in the corner of the room was occupied by the man who had set fire to all twenty of the cigarettes, but who had smoked none of them. Jack “The Ripper” Nobbledy. He looked on with interest as the final cigarette burned itself out, a thin tendril of smoke making its way to mix with the swirling miasma above. Smoking had never been a habit that he had picked up. Even when the members of the Army of Darkness were doing their best impressions of chimneys, getting through several packets a day, smoking was never a vice picked up by The Ripper. Looking back on his life, this struck The Ripper as unusual. Given the very nature of his demise, crushed to death underneath a hail of rubble, metal, glass and other such debris (or so the story goes), it seems almost ridiculous that Jack never found himself attached to the slow motion equivalent. The man who self-destructed in the most spectacular fashion never took up smoking for that very reason. The irony of it all was definitely not lost on him. Given a second chance at life, and given the means to do what he pleased, The Ripper had decided to consider everything he had discounted the first time around, and perhaps try to see the other side of it. He had been mocked as he left Hell, told that he would certainly find his way back there in the end. He’d been told that his soul was too tarnished to ever find salvation. As Jack looked at the cigarettes he realised how true those words seemed to be. It was if a prophecy was controlling his fate. Everything he did was what he would have done before… before his little accident. The Ripper looked on as the smoke moved heavenwards, a direction he seemed fated never to go. The same mistakes were beginning to repeat themselves, presuming that being condemned to Hell for a second time would indeed be a mistake. The Ripper tried thinking his way out of that one, but it was inescapable. Hell was not a place where anyone could prosper, not even one as cold-hearted and emotionless as The Ripper. That was the key to it. Emotions. The Ripper had never been one to get in touch with his emotions. It was enough to deny others their better ones, and see them crumble. Denied of hope, of love, of anything resembling success, men had crumbled in the strong hands of The Ripper. Following the teachings of what he called ‘the darkness’, Jack had never been too concerned with anything of his own. He’d been known to laugh from time to time, and he’d shared many a great adventure with the multitudinous people in XUW, but emotions had never been the driving force in his existence. Hell is hard to fear, when fear is something alien to you. All the intellectual knowledge of pain and suffering is hard to weigh up against the desires of the here and now. Even if you know that Hell exists, that doesn’t mean you can avoid it. The smoke ceased travelling upwards as the final cigarette burned its last. The Ripper stood up and walked to the ashtray. He inhaled deeply of the fumes, but he felt nothing. There was no rush, no feeling comparable to crushing the hopes and dreams of another innocent in his bare hands. Nothing here could hold a candle to feeling an opponent’s life blood ooze through his fingers, another victim on his way to the hospital, courtesy of The Ripper. Smoking would never be a vice that would appeal to The Ripper. The vice that he was a slave to could be described perhaps as sadism; the pursuit of pleasure from the suffering of others. Was it pleasure though, or more some sort of physical necessity? The Ripper seemed to achieve some sort of grim satisfaction through the failure of others, but it was not so much an emotive state, as the alleviation of something else. Just as an addiction leaves a craving, the relief of which is not inherently pleasurable, but still desired, The Ripper needed to see others flounder and fail around him. It was not in the pleasure he derived from their physical pain, it was the alleviation of the suffering he felt while others prospered. Misery truly does love company, and bringing people down to his level was always something Jack “The Ripper” Nobbledy had sought to do. So, what did this mean for The Ripper as he stood in the hotel room, looking around himself at the kind of bed used by prostitutes and businessmen in the pursuit of a few minutes of pleasure. Cheap sex had also never been high on the agenda of The Ripper, though he had known several women in that sense before. It was something in giving himself up that put The Ripper off the whole idea, and gave his dwellings in the Domicile of Darkness a faint monastic edge to them. Perhaps not a monk in the usual sense of the term, there was always a certain ritual, a certain self-control within the frame of The Ripper that reminded some people of the highly disciplined men of God. If God had ever needed an order of monks for smiting the unrighteous, he could have done worse than to cast a glance over the Domicile of Darkness. The Ripper picked up the keys to the hotel room, and held them in the palm of his right hand. He considered them for a moment, before discarding them to the floor. He would not have any further need for them, so they would stay where they fell. The Ripper strode to the door, and pushed it open. The smoke within the room burst out like a prisoner being released after a decade inside the cells. It grasped at the clean, fresh air, only to dissipate to nothing after a few seconds. Freedom was fleeting before society could swallow you whole again. The Ripper stood on the walkway that led to the stairs. He looked around to see if anyone else was around to share the strange, melancholy evening, but he was truly alone. The Ripper took the steps down to the courtyard slowly, savouring the air that entered his lungs unpolluted by the taint of nicotine, tar and carbons both monoxide and dioxide. On nights like this he sometimes felt it was all pointless. Nothing was achieved under the blanket of stars, under the watchful eye of a low summer moon. As The Ripper walked across the courtyard and out on to the street, he realised that all this now was just killing time before the show would begin. The show was the only thing that could conjure up genuine emotions for him. Hell, though real, was less immediate than the return to what The Ripper had done so well for so long. A smile crossed Jack’s face as he walked down the street, realising how absurd his story must seem to an average person. A man gets a second change to change his life, and he not only fails to change his attitude and outlook, but he fails to change his lifestyle. He remains a professional wrestler, a job fraught with danger and incident. The Ripper could only laugh at this, and not just because most of the incidents and danger came from his own self. The Ripper walked on until he reached an old bar. It had definitely seen better days. The sign that proclaimed that this was the “Pier Tavern”, but with no piers for miles in any compass direction, it could have been named for anywhere in the world. Jack sat down at the bar and motioned for the bartender’s attention. He needn’t have bothered, as a man of his size and demeanour is always spotted immediately by any self-respecting barman. This one obviously knew The Ripper, as he uncorked the bottle of Scotch and poured The Ripper a large shot of it, on the rocks. The glass was pushed across to within the reach of the wrestling star, and he accepted it with one hand, the other delving in to his pocket for money. The offer was waved away without a word spoken. The Ripper smiled, knowing that this routine happened every time he went for a drink here. His money would never buy him anything within these walls. Having old business associates with hands in more than just a few pies sometimes did have the odd advantage here and there. “So, what’s up now then, Jack?” The Ripper raised an eyebrow to the question from the barman. He was not used to interrogation in this of all places. “What do you mean?” “You look different. I don’t know what it is, but there’s something different about you.” The Ripper looked down at himself, and failed to spot anything different about his appearance or indeed his attitude. It is hard to bring on a convincing change in personality or demeanour suddenly, and when such a thing happens to you without your knowledge, it can be most disconcerting. For a man who has faced down Hell though, such things are not matters to lose sleep over. The Ripper just shrugged and drank from his glass of single malt Scotch whisky. He knew he had changed though. It was the obsession with this show that was the trigger in it all. It was the obsession with having to win at all costs that made him go to such great lengths to make sure that Fatbadger would be able to participate effectively in the competition. It was the obsession that had made him spend the time with the other team members, be it visiting a movie studio or wandering aimlessly around the arena where Survival would take place. It was an obsession indeed, and it burned so brightly inside The Ripper that even those on the outside could see there was a difference. Jack smiled, and then realised he had been doing so a lot recently. In the old days, a smile on The Ripper’s face was rare, coming only in moments where he had broken the dreams of another young wrestler, or taken a title from a babyface wrestler who had never come close to realising the power of the force he was grappling with. This obsession was bringing with it something that The Ripper had thought might never happen, but from what had gone before, was inevitable. The Ripper was feeling a little emotional. He slugged down the remnants of his whisky and looked around the bar. The barman refilled his drink quickly, with an expert hand, as The Ripper took in the pool table, dart board and assorted battered old furniture. If his eyes had not deceived him then he also might have seen a cockroach scampering over in the far corner. This was not a glamorous bar for a movie star or a big Prima Donna, this was where people came to drink, worry themselves stupid, and set the world to rights. The Ripper drank some more. Truth be told, it really had all started with Chryste. The kiss he stole was more from curiosity than desire, but it had brought to the surface more feelings. The Ripper had suppressed many of them, but with the Survival show just around the corner, there was no denying them forever. The Ripper was, for the first time in living memory, excited. The agitation that grew in his limbs was infectious. First his right hand started drumming on the bar, and then his left followed suit. Soon even his legs were moving rhythmically, priming themselves, coiling like a spring just waiting to explode. The targets were to be, initially, the Universal Wrestling Federation, Global Wrestling and Next Generation Wrestling, but after they all fell, The Ripper felt he would have plenty left for the rest of the entire group of federations. None would be spared his wrath, his fury… his excitement. It finally struck The Ripper why he wanted to win so much. It finally dawned on him that Fatbadger had been right all along. The foolish one had spotted a difference within The Ripper. He really did want to win more than before, and not just to see others broken in front of him, their bodies scattered to the four winds as he climbed to the top of Mount Saint Hells. The excitement was mounting for The Ripper. Everything in his new life was wired to emotion, and there was one emotion that could never be felt in Hell, and had never been felt since. The Ripper cast his mind back, and was unsure if he had ever truly felt this in the way he would if Team XUW were to win the Survival I-PPV. This was the force driving The Ripper, and in an instant he wondered if this could be even stronger than the darkness that had motivated him in the past. Now The Ripper was not fighting merely for the pain of others. He was fighting for triumph. He was fighting for achievement. The Ripper was fighting for joy. He finished his drink and laid down some money on the bar. They could do whatever they wanted to do with it. Anything at all, it didn’t matter any more to The Ripper. This was a beautiful night, and there was plenty to be joyous about. And when PTC Survival would happen, the joy would manifest itself in the defeat of thirteen other unlucky teams, and find itself distilled in the heart of one man, atop Mount Saint Hells, and a lost soul would finally find some brief respite.
