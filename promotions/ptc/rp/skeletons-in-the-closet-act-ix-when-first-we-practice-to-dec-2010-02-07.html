Skeletons in the Closet (Act IX): ...When First We Practice to Deceive.

Date: 2010-02-07 Daydreams & Distractions, Pleasant or Otherwise 93 Hours From Now Staten Island, New York. It was just before five in the morning, and the sky was overcast – the fourth day in a row that threatened rain. The office park was one of a hundred like it in every city, maybe a hundred-hundred like it in the Big Apple. The centerpiece was the Orpheus Tower, seven stories of reflective glass just starting to shine with the first rays of the morning sun. In about two hours, the office on the fourth floor will hold a meeting for a particular group – one most people have never heard the name of, but whose fingers have been in quite a lot of pies over its many, many centuries of existence. They were called the Order of St. Julian, dedicated to maintaining a balance only they understood. A push here, a pull there, a new element introduced into the mix, and equilibrium was restored. No one won, no one lost; they existed in a world where the desired result – the goal – was a tie. This meeting will be important to the future of the Order. Old faces return, and new order is restored to an organization dedicated to it. This is not the story of that meeting, however; it is a tale of what happened before. It's the story of what happened in the building down the street. Unlike the fancy architectural marvel that was the Orpheus building, this was a one-floor, slate gray building currently serving as a call center. On the asphalt rooftop of the center laid a woman with a rifle, covered in a dark blanket. This wasn't just any rifle, but rather, a KAC SR25 sniper's rifle with a massive, free-floating, center-fire barrel just a hair over two feet in length. The gun fired NATO-issue 7.62x51mm rounds, and while the woman hadn't hand-loaded the rounds, she'd been present when they had. This was not just a gun, this was a Gun – the kind of weapon that would punch a hole through a car door or carve the top of a person's head off with a single shot. It was an effective, if final, solution to the problem she faced, a true last resort, but she was sure it was the only thing left. Alexandra Pierce had been here since right after midnight. It took ten minutes just to put the weapon together, and she'd been lying there for hours, the barrel flush with the roof, waiting. Soon, they would be here. She looked away from the scope, her infamous, steely gaze gone bloodshot. Her brows came together – it would be more correct to say that they came more together. Pierce's copper-red hair, mostly hidden behind a black ski cap, was stringy and greasy, unkempt. Her cheeks were drawn and scuffed, her lips chapped. She looked like she hadn't slept for a week, but it had only been two days. Her slender fingers clutched at an amber, teardrop-shaped piece of sea glass at her throat, and she sniffed softly. Sadly. "Still at it?" the voice came from behind her, airy and lightly accented. Alex touched her forehead to the asphalt, shifting only slightly as she fit her left eye back to the scope. "Not now," Pierce breathed in exasperation. "They'll be here soon." "Afraid it doesn't work that way." The soft scuff of sneaker sole announced Amy Campbell's approach. "You can't just invite me along and then just lie there like a log." "I didn't invite you along," the Spider breathed. "I'm not having this conversation." Campbell dropped into a light crouch nearby, small hands dragging across the asphalt. Alex's gaze slid to the side, her lips pursing in annoyance. "You're still mad?" Amy asked, perfectly sculpted brows arching a little over her famously mismatched brown and blue eyes. The Spider didn't respond, blowing out a cleansing breath. Security for a meeting like this would be tight – the parties wouldn't arrive for nearly an hour, but their protection would be here shortly, and it was important that she controlled herself if she was going to get this shot. "Oooookay," Campbell said, settling down onto her jean-clad derriere at Pierce's side. "Guess I'll take that as a yes." "Of course I'm still mad. I have reason to be mad." "You have reason to think you're mad," Amy corrected. "I was out of this. I was done." Amy shifted to lie on her stomach, perpendicular to Pierce. She rested her chin on her hands, folded the small of the Spider's back. "No, you weren't," Campbell said, her voice quiet and not a little sad. "No matter how much I want you to be, you'll never actually be out of this business." The view through the scope was the same as it had been most of the night. There was an occasional car passing on its way to the corner store, but the City That Never Sleeps seemed to be going out of its way to prove the name a lie. "Well, I was trying to be, at least." Alex's eyes were half-lidded, kind of puffy. She smelled peaches in the air, Amy's signature scent. "I just want a chance – just a chance – to be normal." A new trail of wetness etched a line down her sharp cheekbone. She stopped it with a curled finger, unwilling to look behind her. "Is that so wrong?" "No. It's not." Campbell rolled her head to the side, her smile small and serene, strangely comforting. "I'm just not sure the best way to go about that is to shoot everyone. I feel like I should mention again how much I hate that thing." "I know." Pierce pressed her eyes closed, swallowing the frog in her throat. "I'm really sorry you have to see me like this." "'Sokay," Amy mumbled. "I'm serious about the 'don't kill everyone' thing. We've talked about this." "I'm not going to kill all of them. Just her." "Oh, that's right. Margaret. Because killing her once wasn't enough to screw you up." Campbell shifted again, rolling up to a seated position, her legs crossed at the ankle as she rested back on her hands. "I wish you wouldn't." "I have to." Alex's whisper was half a sob. "You don't understand. It's her fault." "Is it?" Amy's brow crooked, like a thunderhead building steam. "She made you do all these things? She made you a liar and a thief and a murderer? Wow, maybe you should kill her." "Don't talk like that." Pierce croaked. "I don't want you to—" "To know the truth?" There was an unsettling nastiness to the cast of the smaller redhead's smile when Alexandra looked back. "Or did you mean you don't want me to leave you to die alone, without me, without your daughter, without anyone you care about." "I'd rather not die at all, if that could be arranged." The ill-fated attempt at humor made neither woman smile. "Maybe then I'd have time to make up for it all." "People who hole up in sniper's nests usually don't have much of a desire to live, Al." Campbell smirked, sitting up. "What happens if you succeed? All that security you're worried about... do you even have a plan to get off the rooftop?" "Not really, no," Pierce admitted. "So you'll just die? Leave me all alone?" The Red Raver's expressive features shifted from bitter anger to quiet sorrow in the span of a heartbeat, without a stop in between. "And Quinn? You'll leave abandon her for two seconds of retribution? Is the joy of squeezing that trigger really worth it?" "If I can redeem myself, stop her from doing it to anyone else... it would be worth my life." The words were noble, but her hand was shaking as she shifted her grip on the rifle. "I'd rather die than face a mirror." "Oh, so you're a coward, too?" Amy looked away, eyes shining. "Good to know." "You don't mean that." "You don't know what I mean. I couldn't take losing you. Not like this. Al, please..." Campbell swallowed hard, touching the heel of her hand to her cheek. "You don't have to do this thing for me." "I've already lost you." The Spider's whisper was nearly inaudible, the words choked off before they reached her lips. "I know this doesn't... doesn't change what I did. Believe me, I know that. But someone has to pay." "No." Amy reached out for Alex, but her hand dropped away before it touched the other woman's arm. Her fist balled at her side. "They don't." Pierce's stormy gaze had threatened rain all morning, too, and they finally started to sprinkle. "It's all I have left." Campbell shook her head as she stretched out at the taller redhead's side. "It will never be all you have." Her head pillowed by one hand, neon, stoplight red hair in her face. "Someday I hope you'll finally understand that. How does it go? 'An eye for an eye makes the whole world blind.'" "Gandhi?" Pierce's chuckle caught in her throat. "As if there was any doubt of what this was." "'This'?" Amy's grin was wide, wry, and cocky – it was Aimz' grin, the one on the cover of the SCCW press book. "So I'm a thing now?" Alex put her eye back to the scope. Still nothing outside Orpheus Tower. "There isn't anything you can do to convince me not to go through with this." "Oh, yeah?" Amy rolled over, pressing herself into Pierce's side. Campbell nipped at the other woman's earlobe, her fingers finding the smooth skin between the waistband of Alex's pants and the hem of her blouse. "Because I think I might know a way..." The smaller redhead's fingers rode the taut flash of Alex's side, slipping underneath her stomach. Pierce's hips rose to accommodate them. "C'mon..." Amy's voice was husky in Alex's ear. "Pack up your big gun and let's go cause an unreasonable amount of damage to a hotel room." The Spider closed her hand around Amy's wrist, stopping it but not pulling it away. "What are you doing?" she hissed. "N-no, Amy. Please." The nastiness returned to Campbell's smile, even if Alex could only hear it. "Oh, so you want to play?" she asked, rolling Pierce away from the gun and onto her back. "I know how you like it when I make t—" "No! You can't—we can't do this." "We can't?" Amy straddled Alex, unbuttoning the fly of her torn blue jeans. "Oh, you mean on account of the fact that this isn't really happening, because you've been up for almost three days straight and you're dreaming? Or maybe because I gave you that nasty little cut on your cheek?" Campbell reached down, tugging up the hem of her Social Distortion tee, exposing a Hello Kitty bra. "Welp, I'm willing to look past all that if you are." She bent again, pinning Pierce's wrists over her head as she lowered her lips to the spot just below where her jaw connected. The spot that always made Alex hum. "Tell me again about this gun?" Amy murmured, lips right against the skin. Pierce's eyes rolled up into her head, but that didn't keep a fat tear from slipping through her eyelashes to splash on the asphalt. * * * "Oh, fuck!" Alex was just barely able to keep from sitting bolt upright, resting back on her elbows. She touched the home button on her phone, bringing up the clock. 5:39. She hadn't been asleep for long, thankfully. Pierce rolled onto her stomach, adjusting the scope. The others were starting to file in, a half-dozen or so nondescript sedans peppering the street. To the untrained eye, it would just be morning in the big city, but to Alexandra Pierce, it was the first sign of a mounting security threat. She'd tossed the thermal blanket off in her sleep – the same one that would mask her heat signature from prying eyes. Inch by inch, Alex tugged the thing over her shoulders, blowing out a breath. "Okay." The voice came from behind her again. Alex turned a hair, wrinkling her brow. "Not gonna lie, I was pretty sure that would work." Amy Campbell pushed off a pipe, jamming her hands into the front pocket of her sweatshirt. "Am I—" "Still dreaming?" The little redhead shrugged her shoulders. "Maybe? I don't know. Not really my department." "Then why are you here?" Alex rolled up to a seated position. "To keep you from doing something stupid because you're all wrapped up in this... this stuff. You can't go around shooting people, Alex." "You're not real." Pierce turned back to the rifle. "Maybe a leftover from the sedative, maybe a byproduct of my guilt. I can't let myself get distracted, not if I'm going to stop all of this. Not if I want to fix—" A size-eight canvas sneaker made a soft thokk sound as it was driven into the back of Alex Pierce's head. The Spider saw stars, and this time, when Amy rolled the taller redhead over, it wasn't for frisky foreplay. Campbell put her shin across Pierce's throat, leaning down. "Maybe you'll be more reasonable when you wake up," the little redhead sighed out as Pierce finally passed out, visions of four days ago playing in her head. The Smoking Gun Today. Oakland, California. She couldn't sleep. Every time Amy Campbell closed her eyes, tried to drift off in the arms of Alexandra Pierce, she'd wake up to the sound of gunfire, the pop-pop jarring her back into consciousness. She saw the muzzle flash, heard the loud spang of the bullet ricocheting two feet to her right. "Fuck this," Amy murmured, tossing off the thin cover and rolling to sit up. Though they'd begun their ill-fated attempt to rest spooning each other, it wasn't much of a surprise that her partner had rolled away. Alexandra Pierce was a fitful sleeper, too many years of too many horrors in her life denying her solace. Campbell glanced over her shoulder, rubbing the back of her neck. There had been many days she would have given anything to make those ghosts go away, to give her just one night – one night where the weight of being Desade wasn't threatening to crush her. When Amy moved, rising unsteadily to her feet, the shift of the mattress was all it took to stir the Spider. "Nng." Alex's tongue tasted like paste. "Where're you going?" "Just need some water," Amy whispered. "Go back to sleep." "'Kay." The little redhead scrubbed at her eyes as she staggered towards the bathroom – she'd get her water in a minute, but Campbell was in all honesty crying out for a little pick-me-up of her own. Her own habits were numerous, but the tiny canister on the sink in the master bathroom, mixed in with the haphazard pile of Campbell's cosmetics housed the only one of which she was ashamed. Amy's shuffling steps across the carpet would have gotten her to the bathroom without waking either the woman in the other room or the girl down the hall – if they hadn't kicked Alexandra's shoulder bag on the way. "Fuck!" the little redhead hissed. She didn't remember where they put it when Donnie Bell brought it back from the site of Pierce's kidnapping, just that she wanted the annoying, motor-mouthed Brit gone, and Alex didn't even take the time to make sure nothing had been stolen first. "They kidnapped me," Pierce had said. "I doubt it was because they wanted to steal the cash out of my wallet." Campbell hadn't wanted a fight about it – of late, Amy didn't want to start a fight about a lot of things, but that had become increasingly difficult. In bed, they were magical, with a chemistry that bordered on the dangerous. Out of bed, they'd always been... different. "'Different,'" Amy grumbled, gathering the contents of Pierce's bag back together. "One word for it." The redhead's sullen murmur stopped when her gaze flicked over a violet envelope buried among the makeup (really, who needs this many shades of lipstick?) and knickknacks that meant God-knew-what to the enigmatic woman in the other room. The envelope was new, and it didn't just stand out for its vivid purple shade, but because the name on the envelope caught her eye. "Ms. Amelia Campbell-Markson," it read – a sure sign that it wasn't a surprise gift Alex bought for her, since the Spider rarely called her "Amelia" anymore, and certainly wouldn't have brought up the memory of the man she'd betrayed to try to find happiness. The handwriting wasn't anything Amy recognized – not Alex or her daughter, or the fine hand of Elijah Hawke. (The scarecrow had written many breakdowns of the complicated plans the Dead Man's Hand would be engaging in on SCCW's Temptation broadcast, and even though Amy wasn't really a member, it behooved them to have her aware of what was going to happen). The first thought she had was that it came from Darcy's lawyers, that Alex had somehow intercepted her mail. That didn't explain why it was purple, though. Amy tossed the Bizarro sweatshirt Quinn had worn that night – the one Alex had a strange fascination for – into the hamper, taking a seat on the wicker thing as she tore the envelope open, her sluggishness dissipating in the face of the odd mystery of it all. Campbell didn't know what she was expecting – but a DNA test wasn't even on the list. But there it was, along with two separate vials, and nearly forty different photos. She spent the better part of the next hour piecing everything together, migrating from the hamper to the floor to spread it all out. It quickly became evident that there were two different mysteries at work here: one concerning the DNA test, and one having to do with the pictures – photos of her, of Alex, of a night she regretted more than most in her increasingly troubled life. The night she'd fallen off the wagon was not a night Amy was particularly proud of. It had been at some bullshit party, a meet-and-greet for the investors – the kind of thing that had occupied Alexandra Pierce (well, Elisabeth Pierotti) in the weeks before they'd become involved. The pictures put a new timeline into place, one Amy was just coming to understand. So intent was Campbell on organizing the pictures, solving the mystery of the violet envelope, that she didn't hear the other woman's steps – not that she would have otherwise. "Hey, I was thinking maybe we could—" Pierce stifled a yawn, her eyes flickering across the photographs that surrounded the smaller woman in that hallway. "Oh." "Oh?" Amy lifted her mismatched gaze to the other redhead, brown-and-blue eyes red, wet with tears. The hurt in that gaze took the wind out of Alexandra's sail. "I can explain. It's... it's not what you..." "What is this, Alex?" Campbell's arm had goosebumps as she pointed to one particular photo. "There. You're talking to the guy who gave me coke that night..." She swallowed nerves quickly, trying to force herself not to jump to conclusions before Pierce could speak. "And these papers... why would you have my DNA, and what does Malcolm have to do with any of this?" Amy shook her head, blinking back those tears. "What the hell is this, and why do you look like something terrible just happened here?" "I... I wanted to tell you." Alex began tremulously, half-turning away. "I've done something horrible." "Al... y-you're scaring me a little." Pierce stepped to the window, her voice quiet. "Malcolm is your father," she said, staring into the street. "He doesn't have anything to do with this. He... he's just a father who was scared of someone coming for his daughter. I could've been him, could've been... just a pharmaceutical sales representative who sent an email to Quinn once a year, on her birthday. Don't... don't blame him for it." "Did they drug you? Have you lost your fucking mind?" The confusion had clumped with anger, and Amy's voice raised. "You're not even making sense. He's—" "He's your father, Aim." Two fingers opened the blinds, but Pierce flicked her eyes back to Campbell. "It's all there, but it's... it's not why you were given that envelope. It's a cherry on top of the sundae, leaving you no one to rely on when you learn the worst of it." "What the fuck are you talking about? Does this have something to do with... with the night..." "I should have said something sooner." Alex shook her head softly. "I was selfish. I thought I could make it work." Her hand fell away from the blinds, which closed with a snap. "There is no easy way to say this, no way I can dodge this blow. I owe you the truth. I... I was losing control... there's no excuse for what I did. What I ordered to be done." "What happened." It wasn't a question, but Amy couldn't meet the other woman's gaze. "I never meant... I knew, of course. Everyone knew that you'd had problems in the past. The whole party... it wasn't about the sponsors or the advertising or the jeans. It was about you. About reintroducing..." She swallowed hard. "Lauren drugged your drink, made your buzz worse. The rest was... please don't make me say this." Alex's first tears came quickly, her head against the doorjamb. "I'm so sorry. I didn't know – couldn't know – what would happen in Vermont, and... and beyond. If I had... I've carried this around for months. Every time you touched it, every little snort, I'd just... die inside. It's my fault that your marriage fell apart. It's my fault that you're an addict. I thought... I don't know what I thought." Pierce swallowed again, her back turned, her hands miserably tangled at her waist. "Say something." "What do you want to hear? That I forgive you? That it's okay?" "No." Another sob spilled from her lips. "I know it's not. I should have said something. I should have—" The quiet, the sudden stillness of Amy Campbell was the scariest thing to see. She was suddenly so cold, lips pursed. "What was the goal? Exploit the weakness, hand-feed me what I needed to break myself so you could swoop in?" "No!" Alex's eyes flew open. "It was nothing like that. I didn't want to 'swoop' anywhere. I... I wanted to have something I could... could guide you with." "Control me with." Campbell corrected. "Because an addict would do anything for what they wanted. You... you pretended to be my strength. I was miserable because I felt I'd fallen, I felt like I'd fucked up all by myself, like I'd embarrassed myself in front of you..."  Amy wasn't sure how she could stand, but she did. She was dizzy, dazed... "You rigged drug tests for me, helped me fucking get it, and I went all this time thinking I was damaged fucking goods – like you deserved more than a junkie, some cheater... someone you fucking used and helped—"  The sudden, fitful strike to Pierce's cheek was unexpected. "—create. You... I ruined my life, you made me a charity case for two fucking YEARS, you—no." She wound up to lash out again, but stopped, face screwed up in pain, tears and anger as she staggered back, towards the door. "No, don't cry. Don't you dare," she snapped. "Poor fucking Desade. You got attached to your pet. Enjoy the laughs when I crawl back out and admit that I got played like everybody said I would." Alex cried out, but the smaller redhead shrugged it off. She was determined not to let the real pain show, backing out the doorway. "My failure's kept you warm for a year... I hope you freeze without it." "Amy... please, I didn't—I love you!" Amy's Cadillac's tires squealed as she whipped out of the parking lot, unsure where she was going, but wanting to be anywhere else but there. Alone in the hallway, surrounded by proof of her crimes, Alexandra Pierce sobbed. They were not quiet, no subtle, and not ladylike – they were deep, heaving, slobbery things. It was the most undignified anyone would ever find the Spider in that moment, spindly arms curled around her legs, and her daughter – awoken by the shouting – wouldn't risk worsening the situation by disturbing her. Crawling on the floor, Alexandra gathered the pictures, but she didn't throw them away or destroy them, and when Quinn finally poked her head out nearly a full hour later, her mother was gone, fled into the early afternoon and lost in the lunchtime traffic. Facing the Past 94 hours, 15 minutes later. Staten Island A part of Alexandra Pierce knew the slaps were imaginary – like the kick to the back of her head.
