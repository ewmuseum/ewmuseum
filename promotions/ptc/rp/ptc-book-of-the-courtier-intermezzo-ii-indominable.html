Book of the Courtier - Intermezzo II (Indominable)

23 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Somehow, a park seemed to fit Emoticon. The little known Global Wrestling superstar, from what precious little he exuded, seemed to be a man of chivalry and affection, of love and poetry, of tenderness and mirth. Therefore, a park, green with the leaves of mid-summer, seemed to be the perfect place for him. And so it was there that he sat, on a wooden bench overlooking a small, clear pond, just being. Dressed in his full ring regalia, a skin-tight black latex bodysuit, white face paint, and a ninja-style half mask, he drew stares from most passersby as he sat, pondering over a pad of paper and a fountain pen in his hand. The only thing written on the paper as of yet was his symbol. An animeish emoticon, that many used in internet instant message conversations. Most people threw it around meaninglessly, but to Emoticon, it was the world. ^_^ “Perfect.” He smiled as he stared at it, the two carat eyes were, to him at least, one of the cutest things in the world. They were innocent, somehow. Innocent and trusting. In the face of the emoticon, he could see himself and all he knew. The man who referred to himself as Aphrodite’s Right Hand sighed. He was distracting himself again. He was here to think about the Infinite Gauntlet, to ponder his career and the potential if he were to win, not a silly symbol that he happened to enjoy. As often happened however, he was once again distracted from his task by a vision of loveliness. Not the sort that he was romantically attracted to, however. THIS particular vision of loveliness was a young girl, seemingly a straggler from the playground equipment a few hundred feet away from the man on the bench. She wore her long hair in a ponytail, and stood before him dressed in a pretty pink dress. Before Emoticon could quite figure out why she had come, she spoke. “Hi there!” she chirped, in the excited voice of a child. “Whatcha doing?” His train of thought was immediately derailed. Still, he turned to the little girl, and answered her truthfully, the response slightly muffled by the face mask. “Drawing. Writing. Watching.” “Oh!” she said, seemingly placated as she hopped up next to him on the bench. With curiosity shining in her eyes, she looked into Emoticon’s, his green eyes gazing back gently at her brown, as she formulated her question. “Why do you dress like that?” she asked, showing the candor that children are seemingly born with. “It’s not Halloween.” Emoticon had to control his sigh. The question was asked of him at least ten times a week, and no matter how much he tried to explain, no one understood his explanation. Or if they did, no one accepted it. But those were adults, people set in their ways. This was a child. Young and innocent to the ways of the world. Perhaps she would understand. “Why do you dress like you do?” he asked, answering her question with one of his own. “Why do you wear that pink dress?” Her response was immediate. “Because my mommy got it for me. I like pink, and it’s comfy.” Fair enough, really. He nodded along, showing that he understood her point. “Just like you like that dress, I like wearing what I wear. To me, this outfit is like your pink dress.” “Ohhhh…” It was as if she hadn’t thought of that, due to how odd his attire looked. She blinked at him twice, processing the information. She was just about to ask him a question when a high-pitched shriek came from behind the two. Emoticon whirled, looking at a soccer mom who was practically running in place, waving her arms all around and screeching frantically at her daughter. “Jessica, get away from that creepy man! Come back here!” “Sorry,” Jessica said, “but I’ve got to go. Bye mister!” She jogged off towards her mother, who cradled her in a warm, loving hug upon her arrival. Emoticon watched the scene a bit sadly, allowing his sigh to form and deepen. “If she didn’t want her daughter to talk to strangers,” he whispered, turning back towards the pond, “it would be okay. But her choice of words…” The mother had called him a ‘creepy man’. Undoubtedly due to his attire. So, if he’d have been wearing jeans and a t-shirt, he would have been fine to talk to? It was the sort of thing that utterly infuriated Emoticon. People looked at him, and formulated their opinions. Made snap judgments on who he was based on his attire. When he really thought about it, the Infinite Gauntlet was nothing more than judgment. The Uber Judge had performed his own brand of judgment, keeping a stranglehold on the championship until Rich Rollins had successfully beaten him for it. Each contest was in and of itself a separate judgment. Thus, the Infinite Gauntlet was a never-ending series of judgments, stretching onward as far as the mind could fathom. “God, I’ve got to stop thinking about things like that. They’re depressing,” Emoticon said, continuing to gaze out upon the clear water, which reflected his image back at him. “Wow…” It had been some time since Emoticon had looked at himself. Really looked, that is. Even to his eyes, the latex and paint looked odd. It had just been habit for him to wake up every morning and put it on. This was the image he was used to now. The man behind Emoticon’s attire had been minimized, somehow. The face that stared back at him in the mirror when he showered was less real than this. Perfectly ludicrous, really. He rose from his seat, leaving his pad of paper and pen on the bench as he approached the blue water. Emoticon wanted a closer look at himself, and so he knelt, lowering his face close to the liquid. “Ah yes,” he said, smiling to himself, “the mask.” That was the source of contention for most people. Why did he wear the mask? It was another question that he was asked nearly constantly, and one that he hated answering. “Sometimes, I wish I were a luchador. That way, no one would ask why I wear my mask. It would be assumed, and I could wear it in peace.” As he looked at himself, he came to two conclusions. Number one, he liked who he was. Number two, he was tired of being looked down upon for it. With a quick glance over his shoulder, he saw the mother and daughter still standing there. She was saying something that he couldn’t quite make out, but he assumed it to be derogatory. Before he quite knew what he was doing, he stood up, and turned around, beginning to half-walk, half-march towards the mother. She looked at him, confused, backing up a few steps as he approached. He spoke to her in a soft, questioning voice. “What’s wrong, ma’am?” The woman’s face screwed up with a combination of surprise and disgust, almost as if she was shocked and embarrassed that Emoticon was talking to her. She didn’t reply, instead allowing her mouth to open and close, open and close. He pushed harder. “Why am I creepy, ma’am?” For the first time, she looked into Emoticon’s eyes, and saw the pain etched there. Her response came in stammers, but the look in her own eyes made it clear that she thought the answer to be somewhat obvious. “Look at what you’re wearing,” she finally spat out. “It’s not appropriate.” “Not appropriate for what,” Emoticon asked, in response. The mother sighed. This conversation was becoming exasperating. After all, she was clearly right. “It’s just not appropriate,” she finally concluded, turning on her heel to go, and taking her daughter by the hand. Under his mask, Emoticon frowned, before pushing yet again. “Ma’am, I am not your child. You can’t answer me by not answering me. That’s not how conversations work between two adults.” She turned back around, seemingly growing more annoyed by the second as Emoticon stood before her, calm as a cucumber. He hadn’t yet raised his voice once. The mother, on the other hand, was reaching the endurance of her patience. “I don’t need to be having this conversation with you!” she screamed, trying to figure out exactly why she felt compelled to stand here and have this argument with a man she had never met before in her life. “Perhaps not,” he admitted, taking a step to the side, “but at the same time, I think you owe me an explanation. No matter what I’m wearing, we’re both adults here. We’re the same, you and I.” “WE ARE NOT!” Emoticon looked at her dubiously, as she covered her mouth. The outburst had even surprised her. With a casual toss of his head, Emoticon smiled, surveying the situation she had gotten herself into and pondering the proper response. “So, the fact that I consider us equals as adult human beings offends you?” The daughter tugged at her mother’s hand, looking up at her inquisitively. “What’s wrong with him, Mommy?” Mommy sighed for a few moments. Effectively, she was trapped. The only answer she had was no answer at all. It was instinctual bias, but instinctual bias that she rather liked, and thought that her daughter should also have. After all, staying away from oddballs in black latex generally seemed a good way to stay out of trouble. At the same time, however, she knew that really wasn’t an answer that meant anything. The man standing before hadn’t done anything to warrant being blackballed. “As I told your daughter,” Emoticon began, “I wear these clothes because I like them. I’m not trying to make a grand statement about the state of the world, or anything silly like that. I happen to enjoy white makeup and black latex. Is that really so bad?” Not REALLY, no. But at the same time, to the woman, his attire was an affront to much of what she believed in. “You’re not one for conformity, are you?” she said, with a little uncomfortable chuckle. Emoticon looked at her conservative attire, and shrugged in response. “No, not really. But then again, I’m not out here trying to defy convention. That’s not my intention with this outfit. Personally, between you and me, I find those who intentionally try to be non-conformist quite silly.” She smiled at him, a smile that lasted about five seconds before he continued. “Of course, the only thing sillier than that to me are people who intentionally try and conform.” Emoticon ran a hand through his hair as he watched the woman’s face tighten for a few moments. He couldn’t help the small chuckle that tore through him at her discomfort. “I believe,” he went on, “that everyone has something to offer the world, just by being who they are. When we change that, for whatever reason it may be, we are doing ourselves and the world a disservice. At least those who exaggerate themselves to “fight the system” have a legitimate purpose, a goal for change. Those who are changing themselves to fit in are doing nothing but perpetuating the way things are. They seek to make life easier by destroying themselves.” The little girl blinked at his big words that she didn’t understand, but was affected by the conviction in his voice nonetheless. The mother, for her part, looked somewhat stunned. “I never thought of it that way,” she admitted. “But don’t you want life to be easy too?” “It would be nice,” he responded, “but it doesn’t work like that for me. Being who I am has led to some, err, complications.” Complications indeed. The woman whom he loved with all of his heart seemed to despise him. The only person he was friends with was among his next opponents. To top it all off, he took constant abuse from commentators who mocked everything that he stood for. To put it mildly, being Emoticon really wasn’t much fun at all a lot of the time. “But I persist,” he continued. “I am who I am, and as easy as it would be to change, I would regret that decision until the day I die. So I will continue to be myself, and take whatever comes my way because of it.” Once again, the woman found herself gazing into his eyes. They burned with conviction and pride in who he was. Finally, the woman had little choice but to give in, at least partially. “I admire your steadfastness,” she replied, “if not your attire.” “That’s all I wanted,” said the Emoticon, returning to his bench to pick up the pad of paper and a pencil. “I can go now, and leave you two to your day.” Satisfied with her admission, he tore the piece of paper with his symbol on it off of his pad, and presented the little girl with it. “Take care, Jessica,” he said, with a smile. “Remember, you can be anything you want to be. Just be true to yourself.” “Thank you!” she said, excitedly, even as she realized something important. “Err, hey, I don’t even know your name!” Almost immediately, the Soul of the Poet replied. “My name is unimportant. But if you want to call me something, call me Emoticon.” With that, he was gone, beginning the walk back to the parking lot. He realized, with a sigh, that he hadn’t really gotten accomplished what he came here for. As he approached his vehicle, however, he understood that in a way, he had. Emoticon had stood trial for being who he was, just as he did every day of his life, and he had been victorious, at least in part. In the battle of wills and bodies that was the Infinite Gauntlet, self-awareness was key. Rich Rollins didn’t seem to have it. He seemed to have lost his mind, from past trials and tribulations. But Emoticon did. The man who wore the latex and face paint knew exactly who he was. Behind the slight, 5’7”-5’8” exterior and odd clothing beat the heart of a man who would not yield, even under the most difficult circumstances. Compared to the emotional Hell that his life could be at times, the judgments of the Infinite Gauntlet were trivial. Victory would be nice, of course, but Emoticon had triumphed in the mind of one of his peers, who had believed him to be a miscreant. That was a victory far sweeter than any in the ring. Emoticon smirked as he reached the bike rack, unlocking his Mongoose mountain bike and helmet. He immediately put the headgear on, before hopping astride his bicycle and beginning to pedal away. One could certainly say that Emoticon danced to the beat of a different drummer. That was, however, exactly what made him dangerous. After all, when the world pre-judges you, what do you have to lose trying to prove them wrong? ---------- 7/23/05 Dearest Diary, One day, I will take the mask and the face paint off, and let everyone gaze at Emoticon’s face. One day I will give in and let them see what I look like, to satiate their curiosity. But until then, I will hold out. I will continue to wear what I wear and do what I do because I enjoy it, and because it is who I am. Rich Rollins and Ember come into the Infinite Gauntlet confident men, no doubt. Both have had far more success than I in this sport so far. But I come into the Gauntlet unashamed and ready. I am set to face judgment, by all who would give it. I will survive Ember. I will survive Rollins. I will endure. And, if all goes well, I will become the Infinite Gauntlet Champion. If that happens, I will become a part of the never ending spiral of judgments that characterizes the event. I will lose one day, that much is certain. Whether that day is tomorrow or a year from now all depends on how the chips fall. C’est la vie. What happens, happens. - Emoticon
