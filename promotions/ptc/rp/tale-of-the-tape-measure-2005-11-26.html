Tale of the Tape Measure

26 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-26 I remember when I first met Rich Rollins. Immediately I knew he was a skyscraper above his peers, a superlative talent the likes of which the Academy hadn’t seen since I, the present-reigning Universal Champion, washed their ring with crimson glory. He wasn’t a diamond in the rough, he was a centre-jewel finely cut and sparkling, the Koh-i-Noor of his time. I believed his reign would begin right then, so I took him under my wing and guided him, prepared him for the mountains he would climb. It was too much too soon, I reckon, for he drowned in the ocean of expectations and hid from every eye. Years passed and he was forgotten, his stardom never realized, the victim of his inhibitions, plucked prematurely from the sky. But greatness can’t be silent forever, especially not in a heart akin to mine. It seeks out the most daunting challenges it can, measuring itself against others of its kind. He returned this year a supergiant, bleaching every other star that dared to try to shine. Seymour Almasy, Shadow, The Uber Judge, Hoyt Williams, and too many more to name, all of them fell when their name was called. He ruled the Primetime Central ratings and conquered the Infinite Gauntlet. He did what few elite others have by seeing the Global Title and staking his claim. Now, as a former three-time World Champion, the purveyor of countless other titles, the reigning Global Champion, he has set his sights on a new goal, one to put the rest to shame. With the incomparable GTT5 looming, he sees the chance to cement his legend for eternity. Through his dominance he earned what no other man in history has, the opportunity to name his opponent for the first round. Every other combatant understood, and prayed that they would be spared, prayed that he’d pick the bye. But they needn’t have worried, as I well knew, for Rollins has too much pride. He could have taken the easiest road, but there he’d find no glory. He could have picked one of the many he’d beaten, but that was a tired story. With that heart of his pounding like an army of war drums, he scoured deep inside, and there he found the ultimate showdown, the fate he could no longer deny. The world says he has conquered it all, but his soul sings another rhyme. When he asks himself whom he could beat, the absent name is mine. He thinks he can, but he doesn’t know, and that doubt glistens in determined eyes; the champion in him demands that he find out, even if it means his demise. As sure as he wants to measure himself, to prove he can’t be tamed . . . he made the first round his final – it was Angelo Deville he named. Now do you really think I need hype?
