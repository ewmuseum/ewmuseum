The Hollow Man

20 October 2006

I am the stuffed man. For thirty one years now I have walked this earth, a shape without form, a man with no recognizable identity. I have been a child. I have been an artist. I have been a killer. I have been a lover. I have been a sumo. I have been a professional wrestler. I have been feared. …I have been a coward. The first time I was a puppet. Nothing more than a tool, an instrument of destruction wielded by a diabolical madman overcome by his ego. As a pawn I was powerful, liberated by fear and doubt, but ultimately hollow; void of what drives us to succeed. The second time I was a monster. Fueled by my anger I tore into my competition without pause or mercy. As a beast consumed with vengeance I was powerful, free of pity, but ultimately stuffed; filled with those things that harm those without, but harm us within even more. Who am I this time? What have I learned? I have learned that there is only one man who can defeat me, and I see him every day when I look in the mirror. I still look at that face and cringe at the sight of my conqueror. Between the conception and the creation, between the emotion and the response, lies the Shadow. The shadow of failure, of coming so near only to collapse under the weight of my own spiritual impotence, that is perpetually cast over my name. The shadow of doubt, of fear, of anger, of vengeance; the type of shadow that can only be cast by a shape with no form. The type of shadow that follows me always, in darkness and in light. The Shadow that rests over me, obscuring me from sight; me, that paralyzed force. Who is this man Kimbusa? ---------------------------------------- I’m drifting again… Kimbusa forced his eyes open, squinting even in the dim light. An intense drowsiness had overcome him and he’d plunged into a restless sleep. It had been happening more and more often lately; he had been sinking further and further into the depths of despair, to the point that sleep was an escape welcomed at any opportunity. He was particularly tired after his trip, but it wasn’t the travel that had brought about his slumber. It was a year of unresolved personal issues, of spiritual emptiness; a year of idleness. His lethargy had been disturbed by the ringing of his phone. It was a sound so foreign to him that he’d almost forgotten what to do about it. Eventually he came to his senses, but still made the mistake of answering. Twenty-one hours later, here he was in the back of Steven Caldera’s limousine on the other side of the planet. The world was whizzing by outside. Columbus, Ohio was having another of its typically gray and gloomy autumn days, though the rain had stopped for the moment. Still, the tires beneath him sloshed across the wet pavement as they sped towards their destination. Kimbusa did his best to shake the cobwebs from his head, but found himself to be in a daze too thick to penetrate. "Oh good, I thought you’d died. I was wondering how the hell we were going to get your corpse out of the car." The voice was stiff but masked a subtle humor. Glancing over, Kimbusa saw the face of Steven Caldera looking out the window, idly watching the landscape pass by. Caldera finally looked back down, smiling with a look of both fondness and pity. Caldera normally kept his guard up around the unpredictable former sumo, but somehow he got the impression Kimbusa was in no condition to threaten him. Kimbusa’s head was swimming. "What am I doing here?" he said with more than a little impatience in his voice. Caldera smirked. "If you’d managed to stay awake more than five minutes, I’d have told you right away. We’re just taking a little ride, that’s all. You’ll find out when we get there." Rolling his eyes, Kimbusa straightened himself and eyed his sponsor suspiciously. Steven Caldera. Founder of Caldera Enterprises, the media megacorporation that ran Global Championship Wrestling. A brilliant and ruthless businessman, Caldera had always been successful in his career. Caldera himself was once a successful professional wrestler, but his body wasn’t built for the constant wear and tear of the business, and he’d retired to run things himself. But his drive to be the best was only intensified. Caldera had managed to be the best at nearly every endeavor he’d undertaken, except one. GTT. Caldera, unable to participate personally, sponsored and mentored Kimbusa during GTT3. It was Kimbusa’s first real test in American wrestling, and he’d been dominant. He tore through the field, but a falling out with Caldera led to him falling short against Angelo Deville. Furious, Caldera and Kimbusa engaged in a bloody war over the next two years, during which Caldera attempted to take matters into his own hands and enter GTT4 himself. His body gave out quickly, and he fell short of matching Kimbusa’s success. By the time GTT6 had come around, Kimbusa and Caldera had forged a fragile truce. In a show of good faith, Caldera had signed Kimbusa to a GCW contract that was purely ceremonial. Kimbusa appeared on GCW television only a handful of times, in exchange for a handsome monthly salary that allowed him to retire to the Japanese countryside and live in isolation. Kimbusa entered GTT5 on Caldera’s behalf, once again finding a way to dominate. But again he fell short, this time to a man less than half his size. Kimbusa hadn’t forgot the man who defeated him, but upon returning to his homeland he did his best to put his failures out of mind. The moment the phone rang, he knew what was coming. It was that time of year again. So, even as Kimbusa asked the question, he already knew the answer. Caldera was obsessed with winning GTT, and with his own body too worn to ever compete in such a format again, Caldera called in his old weapon to return to the battle. "Answer me," Kimbusa said, his dark eyes leveling at Caldera. "I’ve long since guessed my purpose here. So just tell me." Caldera, still staring out the window at a gray Ohio day, seemed to be ignoring him. Kimbusa grunted, and finally Caldera glanced over his shoulder. "I don’t know what you think you’re doing here, but you’re probably mistaken." Chuffing in disbelief, Kimbusa leaned forward. "I know what time of year it is. GTT is about to begin once more. Why else would you call me like this?" Slowly turning, Caldera seemed genuinely impassive. "No. That’s not what you’re here for." With that, he turned back to the window, losing himself in whatever thought happened to be plaguing him at the time. Kimbusa was taken aback. No? Then why the hell had Caldera dragged him all the way over here? "I… I…" Kimbusa struggled for a response, but he had been thrown for a loop. From beneath his stupor rose an odd burning sensation in his gut. It was a reaction Kimbusa hadn’t expected; he actually found himself feeling slighted rather than relieved. "Why not?" Kimbusa managed to say at last, looking hard at Caldera. Caldera shrugged, his eyes still diligently watching the city as it passed by outside. "I’m not going to ask you to do it this year. I’ve got someone else. Things are well under control; Rollins is already gone. I don’t know who the hell Clinton Sage is, but I’ve already sent him a fruit basket. Almasy will be taken care of. Relax, we’re just taking a little trip." Snarling, Kimbusa slumped back into his seat again. Suddenly he found himself feeling drained; that was as much energy as he’d expended in a month. His eyelids felt heavy and began to droop, and he felt sleep taking him once more. His face was still twisted with frustration as he began to drift off, when Caldera’s sharp voice roused him again. "You want to know why?" Caldera barked, now giving his undivided attention to Kimbusa. "That’s why. That… look on your face. What have you got to be so damn morose about? I gave you what you wanted. I’ve left you alone. I brought you here to see Kimbusa, but all I got was this dreary, shell of a man. You look terrible, you’ve become an insipid, insufferable grouch and I have no interest in putting someone like you back out there. So just sit back and try not to hurt yourself." Flashing Kimbusa a condescending frown, Caldera turns back to the window. Drooping in the corner of the limousine Kimbusa stared blankly at Caldera. It had been a long time since anyone had dared berate him like that, and yet for some reason he had neither the will nor the desire to respond. Instead he remained expressionless and dispassionate, his body almost as weary as his mind. The next few minutes passed in silence, save Kimbusa’s labored breathing. Looking at the window, Kimbusa saw his pallid, thin reflection staring back at him. He looked dreary and cold, as if whatever passion that once burned in him had expired. His eyes were pale and empty, his face tired and worn. To himself he looked to be a man devoid of all life. A great secret of success is to go through life as a man who never gets used up. Albert Schweitzer said that. Kimbusa was used up. He began to feel a bit claustrophobic in the confines of the limousine, and very soon he found it difficult to draw air into his lungs. His depression got the best of him, and soon he found the situation to be positively unbearable. "Stop the car," Kimbusa wheezed, eyeing Caldera intensely. Caldera turned, giving Kimbusa a confused look. "Stop the car now," Kimbusa insisted, and finally Caldera called out to his driver. The car turned into a grocery store parking lot and Kimbusa instantly leapt from the car, sucking in a deep breath of cold, open air. The Ohio day was cool and dull. The sun, lowering on the horizon, was unable to warm the penetrating wind blowing from the west. Looking over his shoulder Kimbusa saw dozens of people trudging through the parking lot, heads down, going unnoticed about their business. Once again Kimbusa found himself longing for that life; a life of quiet obscurity, free of the burden of power. The door on the far side opened in response, and Caldera circled around to his side. Adjusting his tie, Caldera glanced about, surveying his surroundings. Caldera had plenty of fans in Columbus, but had plenty of enemies as well. "What the hell is wrong with you?" Caldera demanded, his voice still strict but tinged with pity. Kimbusa bent over the back of the limo, closing his eyes and slowly drawing a breath into his lungs. "What’s happened to you in the last year?" Caldera prodded, not giving Kimbusa a moment’s reprieve. Kimbusa turned back to Caldera. Caldera had always kept his wrestling physique, though he had dropped some muscle in the last two years. Still, Caldera was a well-built six-foot-four man who carried himself well. Clad in a fine charcoal suit and impeccably groomed, Caldera seemed to shine even under the gray skies. And though Kimbusa was nearly twice his size, he seemed to shrink next to the confidence and power of Caldera, and he felt ashamed. "I… I don’t know," was all Kimbusa could manage, unable to look upon his employer. Caldera shifted his weight and crossed his arms. "You don’t know? What do you mean you don’t know?" Finally glancing up from a corner of his eye, Kimbusa snapped, "I do not remember. I simply do not remember." Rising to his full height of six feet seven inches, Kimbusa looked down at Caldera with desperation. "It’s as if it’s all been a dream from which I could not wake; I’m still in that dream, even as we stand here. I have been watching a play in which I am but an actor, and I am powerless to do anything but sit here and wait for the play to reach its conclusion." Caldera shook his head, sighing audibly. "What on earth happened to you?" Glancing around the parking lot, Caldera noticed a number of people watching them from afar. Suddenly feeling quite conspicuous, Caldera motioned Kimbusa back towards the door. "Look, get in, we’ll talk about it on the way." Kimbusa hesitated, but finally slid back inside. Caldera slammed the door behind him and climbed back in the other side, and once more the limo sped off towards its destination. Removing his jacket, Caldera stared at Kimbusa for a long while before speaking. Kimbusa was wearing what Caldera referred to as the film noir getup; gray shirt, gray pants, black trenchcoat and fedora. Kimbusa favored the look for the feeling of anonymity it gave him; though no matter what he wore, he could not avoid standing out. For his part, Caldera thought it somehow appropriate; film noir suggested that there are no heroes, and that was precisely Kimbusa’s indictment of the world. Without being prompted, Kimbusa began to speak. "Last year, after I was beaten by… Almasy… I went home with the intention of staying there for good. But when I left, some part of me stayed in the ring. I do not know what it is, but it is something I have been lacking for some time now. I feel… hollow, as if I am an empty shell with no substance." Sighing, Kimbusa met Caldera with a look, and for the first time in Kimbusa Caldera saw vulnerability. "I have no identity." Looking hard at Kimbusa, Caldera examined him for several silent moments. Kimbusa’s face was a mask of despair, his eyes utterly despondent. He seemed almost transparent, as if he might fade away before his eyes. Finally, after what to Kimbusa seemed a lifetime, Caldera responded thoughtfully. "I know what you think about me, Kimbusa," Caldera began. "I’ve done some horrible things to you, and you have no reason to trust me completely. But for all my shortcomings, I know my business very well. So I hope you’ll take this to heart." Kimbusa nodded, and Caldera continued. "When I first met you, you were out of control. You were angry, sure, but that’s not what was powering you. You were a freak of nature, a physical phenomenon, but your anger was blinding you. Say what you will about my own motives, but I did know that if your anger could be harnessed and guided, you could be the most dangerous man in professional wrestling. "Now look at you. You’re emotionally chaste; sure, you’re anger is gone, you’ve purified yourself. But it’s left you hollow. You’re a damn paralyzed force; your power is unrivaled by mortal man, and yet you’re devoid of passion. You’re afraid of who you are, Kimbusa, and rightfully so. You’ve inflicted unspeakable harm upon everyone you’ve ever encountered. Though you may have forgotten it, you’re one of the most purely evil men I’ve ever encountered. That’s why I thought you had the potential to dominate the business. Unfortunately, your malice was too powerful for any many to handle, and it’s left you with this choice; abandon it and live peacefully but empty, or embrace it and live in exciting, exhilarating hell." The energy and passion in Caldera’s voice brought about a rise in Kimbusa, as if a spark of that old flame might ignite him again. But as quickly as it came it had gone, and Kimbusa was left feeling even more wretched than before. He could never match Caldera’s love of professional wrestling; it was Caldera’s life, all he knew. Kimbusa’s life? I don’t have one… Caldera shook his head, seeing that his speech had little effect. "Whatever. We’re here." The limo pulled to a stop, and immediately Caldera stepped out. The door slammed behind him, and for a moment Kimbusa was alone. It was like a weight off his shoulders, the isolation; he found he could breathe again. But the door beside him opened, and reluctantly Kimbusa stepped out into the open. A graveyard. Caldera had brought him to a damned graveyard. How fitting… Maybe they’d come to finally put him out of his misery. Caldera was already flanked by several men in suits, signing paperwork and talking quickly and quietly. The man brought his work with him everywhere. Try though he might, Kimbusa could not think of a reason why Caldera would have brought him to a graveyard in Columbus, and soon the strain of it grew tedious. He gave up trying to solve Caldera’s riddles and wandered away from the limo, walking into the graveyard alone. The smell of fresh grass bombarded Kimbusa’s senses immediately; grass and roses. It was a lovingly maintained facility, though all about things were beginning to fade. Beds of roses lined the fence as far as Kimbusa could see, but the buds were fading and wilted in the chill fall air. The grass was green but pale, clinging to the last rays of warmth that could penetrate the winter clouds. Walking down a meandering gravel path, Kimbusa’s eyes darted between the modest gravestones lining his way. A light rain began to fall and a stiff breeze rattled the dry brown leaves in the trees. Over the hill ahead the sky was growing darker; a distant rumble of thunder crept towards him, hinting at the brooding storm to come. Kimbusa walked towards the storm. It was quiet save the rustle of the trees and the sound of the rain pattering against the soft ground. After penetrating nearly three hundred yards into the graveyard, he began to hear voices. Further still he began to hear the odd sound of machinery, and Kimbusa adjusted his trenchcoat and headed towards the clamor. Summiting the hill, Kimbusa looked down to find four men, one in a small crane, hovered above a recently opened grave. The crane was slowly hoisting a mud-covered coffin from the ground as the men carefully guided it to safety. It was an odd and unexpected sight to see, and Kimbusa began to wonder if it had anything to do with his presence there. And then it hit him. It started slowly; like the faint inkling of a memory just out of recollection’s reach. Slowly it grew on his mind until he began to recall the place in which he was standing. Glancing about, he realized he’d been to this very place before, but in his stuporous torpor he’d nearly forgotten it entirely. As the memory slowly returned to him, he felt something odd welling within him, something he hadn’t felt in quite some time. Rage. To his amazement, he found himself furiously angry. The pallid tones of his skin were washed away by a wave of red. He felt himself flushed with energy again, and his lax muscles began to tighten. He knew this place very well, indeed; he knew the occupant of the coffin that was being unearthed. His old sensei and master, Yoshi Kimawa. Whistling casually, Caldera rose over the hill, studying a packet of papers. A livid Kimbusa caught him by the jacket and pulled him in close, snarling at him angrily. Startled, Caldera lost his grip on the papers and they scattered in the wind, blowing across the grounds. "What is the meaning of this?!" Kimbusa shouted, the sadistic tone of his voice actually bringing the workers to a standstill. "Why do you disturb his grave?!" Caldera did not look afraid, but rather incredulous. "You asked me to!" Kimbusa’s face twisted in confusion, but his grip on Caldera remained taught. "What?" "You asked me to do this. Remember? You called me a year ago and asked me to move him home to Japan. I finally got around to it. Just… let go of me, will you?" Caldera spoke calmly but with a certain air of annoyance, glancing nervously at his paperwork as it continued to fly away. Bewildered, Kimbusa slowly loosened his grip and released Caldera. He racked his brain but could find only vague memories of the conversation. It had been a rough year. Caldera adjusted his tie and cleared his throat. "I just wanted to surprise you. In retrospect, it might have been a bad idea to sneak up on a sleeping grizzly bear. Next time, I’ll just send you a damn fax." Caldera shook his head and walked away to collect his scattered forms. His fury at seeing his master’s rest disturbed began to subside, and soon he found himself realizing just how little of the last year he could directly recall. This had been the first time in many months that his emotions had risen beyond despondency. Dropping to his knees, Kimbusa finally saw how far he’d fallen. A voice from above spoke to him; a faint voice little more than a whisper, so distant that it could be mistaken for a light breeze. It was barely an echo on the bitter wind, but Kimbusa knew precisely what it was. "Rise, Kimbusa. Rise off your knees and stand." The voice was distinct, but it could not be true. Rising slowly, Kimbusa’s eyes darted about in bewilderment. Finally he spotted a thin and fragile man, cloaked in black, perched lightly atop a nearby tombstone. He was slight and narrow, and thin in presence as an apparition. Timidly approaching, Kimbusa lowered his head in an attempt to see the man’s face. Slowly the man drew off his hood and looked up, and at once Kimbusa stumbled back. There was his old master, Yoshi Kimawa, seated plainly before him. Kimbusa shook his head, struggling for an explanation. His first instinct was that he’d fallen asleep again; dropped off in the middle of his walk and began to dream. But a stiff breeze blew in and chilled his spine, and his shivers were too real for a dream. Finally he concluded that he had perished, finally lost the will to live and dropped dead on the ground to be met in the afterlife by his master. "You’re not dead, Kimbusa-san," said Yoshi, as if he were reading his mind. "Nor are you sleeping. You are quite awake; more awake than you have been in quite some time." Growing bolder, Kimbusa stepped forward in disbelief. "But… you are dead. Your body, it’s-" "…Being taken back to our homeland, yes," said Yoshi thoughtfully. "Thank you for returning me home, my son. They buried me here because it was quite convenient for them; but I cannot rest quietly here." Kimbusa drew in a deep breath. "So you are… dead. By what device are we communing now, then?" Yoshi smiled fondly at his pupil, though his eyes were black as death. "I am the sum of everything you have been hiding within yourself, Kimbusa. I am all those things you have repressed and rejected out of fear. Fear of who you are. But you cannot hide from them any longer. You have one final task, my son, before you can lay this matter to rest and be at peace. Before we may both be at peace." He’d gone mad. Totally mad. Looking around, he saw that he was alone; the work crew had gone back to their task of unearthing the casket. Caldera had his back to him some distance away, muttering to himself as he chased down his paperwork. But there was Yoshi, sitting light as air on top of the gravestone; his dangling legs obscured the name, but Kimbusa could just make out the inscription: ignis fatuus. "What… what do you want from me?" Kimbusa queried, still unsure of the situation, or what had broken in his mind to drive him to such delirium. "It is not what I want, Kimbusa-san. It is what you want. You have spent the past year in isolation, hiding from the truth. But the truth cannot be suppressed forever; eventually it will come out. You must not hide from it any more," Yoshi replied, his voice growing more serious as he spoke. Kimbusa nodded. "I know… I know," he stammered weakly, his eyes dropping to the ground in shame. "I have grown weak and pathetic. I am lost, wandering alone without purpose. But… I cannot go back with him," Kimbusa said, gesturing towards Caldera. "I cannot go back to that life." Yoshi began to speak with surprising forcefulness. "Why do you run from your destiny? It is who you were born to be. Sumo could not contain you. It could not give you what you seek. The release, Kimbusa-san… do you not remember it? How it felt to be unshackled and free to unleash the full scope of your abilities? You did that in sumo and they drove you out… and it drove you to this country, to American wrestling." Shaking his head, Kimbusa still could not face his master. "And what did that release earn me? Failure, ridicule… fear. I have no desire to return to that insipid occupation." "What then?" snapped Yoshi, with such ferocity that Kimbusa’s eyes rose and stood at attention. "You will flee back to the country and hide out until time and age withers you beyond recognition. You will haunt the hills and the valleys, wandering forever, pining for your squandered potential. You have not yet reached your peak, Kimbusa, you have not yet shown your full talent. Deep down inside you, Kimbusa, is the means to become the most dominant force this country has ever seen." "I cannot conquer those demons," Kimbusa muttered desperately. "You must face them, and face them now," Yoshi ordered stiffly. Kimbusa looked up at Yoshi defiantly. "He who fights with monsters might take care lest he thereby become a monster… And if you gaze for long into an abyss, the abyss gazes also into you." "Nietzsche. A wise sentiment. But, Kimbusa-san, it does not apply… if you are already a monster to begin with." The thunder roared louder now, slowly crawling closer. The rain began to pick up slightly and the wind began blowing a little harder and a little colder. Kimbusa struggled for a reply, but he could not resist any further. Yoshi had always been able to see deep within him and find the truth; and this was a truth Kimbusa had been running from all his life. "I-" Kimbusa started, but Yoshi cut him off. "You are a monster, Kimbusa. Those demons within you that you have fled from for so long torment you because you allow them to. They can work for you, Kimbusa; they are what drives you. But you cannot be a complete man, and you cannot reclaim your life until you accept who you are." Kimbusa shivered again, but this time it wasn’t the breeze. He was feeling his hollowness for the first time, suddenly aware of the void within him. And yet that spark, that spark of rage, was kindled once more, the lone faint feeling in his empty heart. "You remember your Nietzsche. I have another for you: No price is too high to pay for the privilege of owning yourself. Accept the truth of who you are, and anyone who is harmed in the process will be little more than a necessary sacrifice. And it can start immediately," Yoshi said, glancing over at Caldera. Kimbusa raised an eyebrow. "What… what shall I do?" "Show him you have mastered yourself. Show him your true nature. You are a contained chaos, an infinitely powerful instrument of destruction in complete control of yourself," Yoshi said, leaning in towards Kimbusa. Yoshi’s eyes had begun to glow with a dull blood red; and immediately Kimbusa recognized it as the same lust for destruction that used to overtake him. "I have harmed too many people. People… people I cared for…" he said, his mind trailing back to the one woman who stood with him… "You will always bear the scars of a life of combat. But the scars of others are not your concern." Kimbusa subtly reached for his left pinky, the top half of which was missing; the mark of the Yakuza that he bared always. "What right have I to dictate their fate?" he demanded, even as he felt himself filling with that wretched feeling of hate. "It is your right as their superior. It is the power you were blessed with to rise above them all!" Yoshi shouted, leaping off the tombstone. To Kimbusa’s surprise, Yoshi did not fall to the ground, but rather floated above him, rising higher as the storm clouds rolled in behind him. A bolt of lightning tore through the sky, answered by a horrible crash of thunder, and suddenly Yoshi was encased in golden flame. "They think you to be weak, they think you to be a coward! Do not give in to melancholy, and do not fear your power!" The thunder roared again, and the rain began to fall. As the water poured over his soaking body, Kimbusa could feel his despair washing away, and soon he began to feel a warmth inside him. His heart began to race as if it might explode, his breathing intensified as his timid exterior gave way to a monstrous frame. His memory returned in a stinging wave of images flashing through his mind at once; the failure against Angelo Deville, the failure against Seymour Almasy; the loss of his beloved, the death and chaos of the streets of Tokyo. They had all feared him at first, but further inspection proved that he was little more than a shell, a man too terrified of his own power to ever dare to use it. But he would be manipulated no longer. Releasing the last threads of doubt and caring, Kimbusa opened his mind to the truth of which Yoshi, the embodiment of his own true self, had spoke. He had come so close before, but he had always fallen short. As his power grew, he had grown afraid of the seemingly limitless pain he could conceivably inflict. As he realized the scope of his abilities, that no mortal man on earth could hope to stand against him, he had cowered in the face of his own infinite rage, his insatiable lust for greater dominance. But no longer. The burning flames before him grew nearer, until his very soul was set fire. He felt the burning within, searing his heart and blackening his mind. And it felt wonderful; to feel the fantastic pain again, to give in to all the anger and fury and stop fighting. He was tired of running, he was tired of hiding for the sake of lesser men. He would enter GTT one final time, and unleash his full potency upon the world. His eyes were burning crimson as Steven Caldera approached, his wet papers tucked under his arm. Seeing Kimbusa again he was taken aback, startled at the obvious change in his appearance. He had risen to his full height and seemed larger; not larger in size but in stature, as if he had been inflated by some unseen force. He ceased to look like anything recognizable as man; he was strong and fell, and for a moment Caldera himself seemed to shrink in his shadow. Thunder crackled behind him, and Caldera began to speak with a sudden urgency. "We’re done here… Let’s… Let’s get in out of the rain," he said, tentatively motioning towards the exit. Kimbusa stepped forward, towering now over Caldera. "GTT. I am in." Suddenly summoning his courage, Caldera shook his head defiantly. "I will not let you screw this one up. This MUST be done. Almasy must be eliminated, by any means necessary. And I don’t think-" Before he could finish, Kimbusa lifted him off the ground by his lapels. Pulling him in close enough to smell his sour, brimstone breath, Kimbusa unleashed a hellish growl. "You will let me in or I will destroy you along with everyone else! I will not be remembered as the disgraced sumo who fell to a pathetic, insignificant miscreant! I will have my vengeance on Almasy, as I will have it on them all!" Startled but somehow unconvinced, Caldera shook his head again. "You’re not cut out for it, Kimbusa. You’re not up to the task. You’ve been away too long, you’ve been hiding for fear of being embarrassed. And when you step into that ring again-" Kimbusa suddenly thrust Caldera through the air, sending him into the trunk of a thick oak tree. Caldera bounced off the tree and landed hard on the ground, coughing in agony as he lay in a puddle of dark water and pain. "I am going to GTT. I am going to destroy Seymour Almasy.
