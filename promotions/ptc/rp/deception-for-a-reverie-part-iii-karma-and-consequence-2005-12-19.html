Deception for a Reverie, Part III: Karma and Consequence

19 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-19 'When odds have been stacked against me, I've never buckled. When it seems as if nothing could turn a match around in my favor, I've managed to pull something from my ass. I've never been one to look at the odds, advantages, or disadvantages of a match-up and take them to heart. I've always thought of myself as the constant underdog, and just assume keep it that way. I'll never give up. I'll never hand a match to anyway. Oh, and I don't care what kind of odds you stack against me, I'm still giving it my all.' -------------------------------------- I've never felt more proud of myself than I did on this day. I was in the backseat of the cab, and on my way to the airport. I had left my house significantly early to avoid any confrontation with my father. It was a couple hours early to be exact. Mom was still around, and she wished me the best of luck with my life. Then again I had to reassure her I wouldn't be gone for good, and that I'd be able to visit eventually. The smell of pine air freshener filled the cab, but was accompanied by the ghastly scene of what could only be the cabbies personal hygiene, or lack there of. It was overpowering, to say the least, but I huddled up next to the window, and watched the scenery go by as I waited to arrive at Athens International Airport, hours before flight 133 left for New York, where I would connect, and switch planes, and board flight 175 from New York to Atlanta, Georgia. America, a place I couldn't wait to see for the first time in my life. The place my mother hailed from. The place where I would likely call home for the better part of the rest of my life; thanks to one Mr. Justin Michaels, and Havoc Entertainment, Inc. My wildest dreams were going to come true. To be honest, already have. Competing in Round 1 of GTT5 had already fulfilled more of my dream than I ever thought possible. Then when you consider the fact that not only did I get to compete in round 1 of GTT5, but also obtain a victory to move me to round 2. I could have never asked for more from my life. That is saying a lot considering I'm only twenty-three years of age. I can only imagine what kind of things I'll be able to look back at in twenty-five years or so, and see what I have accomplished in my career. 'Whacha thinkin' bout back there, huh kid?' The cab driver asked, keeping his eyes on the road. I wasn't really paying attention to the smelly guy much, but I heard him ask. I decided to be nice, and reply. 'Not a whole lot, just the opportunity of a lifetime.' I said, keeping my attention out the window of the cab. It was quiet for a few moments before his interests seemed to be intrigued. 'What kind of opportunity?' He inquired. Still not paying much attention to him, and keeping my focus out the window, I continued to humor the guy. 'I finally get away from my abusive and over zealous father to do what I love.' I retorted as I continued to keep my eye on the bleak and sunny day in Athens through the backseat window. This continued the silence that was the cab ride for me into my life of freedom. Something I was looking forward too. I had been looking forward to for the last four years of my life. Long overdue was this trip away from my initial homestead, and away from my father. Despite the underdog status I have seemed to have kept with me, even in a situation where I'm not known at all, I'm not worried too much about a win or a loss. After all, despite being in the match with another particularly unknown wrestler, my opponent, Masquerade, did out Global's Shadow in round 1. Something you have to look up to in a competitor. So if I happen to lose to this more than worthy opponent in Round 2 of GTT5, then so be it. At least I've given it my best shot, and gone down by the hands of someone more deserving of moving on than me. I smiled, and nodded to myself. All the while wondering how I could have grown up with such good morals considering the fact my father figure was, well, who he was. I can't even imagine how hard Mom had to work to bring me up to be who I am today with someone like Alec, my father, around. The gruff voice of the cabbie once again rang through the inside of the vehicle. He couldn't keep himself out of my business. 'So what exactly is it that you love so much?' He meddled, trying to get more information out of me. I rolled my eyes, with them shut so he couldn't see, but decided to keep the small talk between us going. Just as long as he didn't go into asking me about my father, I'd be happy to keep the guy company until he dropped me off. 'Sports entertainment, you know, the sport of professional wrestling?' I stated. I looked at his expression in the rear view mirror change from indifferent to down right vulgar. By this time, we had arrived outside of Athens International Airport, but I was far from the door I needed to be dropped off at. The cab driver slammed on his breaks in front of what seemed to be the closes one for him to stop at. 'You have got to be kidding me? You punk little kids and your fake wrestling drive me crazy. I swear that good for nothing soap opera you all call sports entertainment wrestling is just a waste of time for everyone on this Earth. If you want to wrestle, why don't you just stay here in Greece and do the real thing. You could even become part of our Olympic team if you are good enough. Winning an Olympic gold medal is surely a lot better than those fake title belts they give out in those other piece of shit federations!' He exclaimed. I looked at him with a slick cock in my neck, and a questioning look on my face. He kept his mean demeanor. 'What the…' I was unable to finish. He hit the button to unlock the doors to his cab. 'Just give me the fare, and get the Hell out of my cab you lowlife. I swear I hoped to never run into anymore of your kind the rest of my life, and here I'd been riding with one for the last thirty minutes. Get out!' He yelled. I threw the cab fare money into the front seat and grabbed my bags from the trunk before I watched as he spun his tires, and sped off. I turned and looked at the airport entrance, still holding my bags. 'What in the Hell am I going to do for the next three hours here?' I asked myself before proceeding inside. I already couldn't wait to get to America, despite the fifteen hours of flight I'd be endeavoring and the hour and a half delay in New York while I board a different plane. I couldn't wait to start the rest of my life. -------------------------------------- 'I never want to leave Athens, Daddy. I love it here. I can't wait till the day comes where I grow up and be just like you. I want to be big, strong, and a possible Olympic athlete supporting Greece in the games one day; just like you almost did. I can't wait!' -------------------------------------- That's what I used to think, back when Alec had me so brainwashed. I was too impressionable as a child, and I hate the fact, now, that I was back then. 'Don't you remember what that little bastard once told me?!' Alec had blurted. He and my mother were going toe to toe in the middle of the living room, verbally that is. My father had obviously remembered the day I told him I wanted to be an Olympic hero for Greece one day. I didn't know any better at the time. The verbal fighting continued with my mother defending me. 'Yes, I heard what he told you that day. You realize how long ago that was Alec?! You can't expect the boy he used to be to still be the man his is today. He's moved on, and he's got the opportunity of a lifetime. Just leave him alone, let him do what he wants to do, and not make him do what you want him to do!' She finished. He wasn't up for fighting a fight he knew he wouldn't win, so he just huffed and puffed a little more before sitting back onto the couch. Mom walked into the kitchen where she would continue cooking dinner. 'Fucking punk little kid.' He mumbled to himself as he grabbed the remote, and started flipping through channels. -------------------------------------- 'Stupid little good for nothing prick son of mine. Who fucking cares anymore? I don't! Ungrateful fuck! He'll never even make it to the States! I hope his fucking plane crashes or something. Kills him, and only him. That'll show him. Karma is going to bite him in the ass, and when it does, he's going to be sorry.' -------------------------------------- He stopped on the news, and plastered up on the screen were shots of a plane crash. Flight 133 from Athens to New York had gone down just north of Madrid, Spain. Alec had turned the volume up and Mom came from the kitchen to look at the news. Both of their faces filled with fright. 'We are clueless as to the cause of why this flight went down, and the only thing we really know at this point in time is that there are likely no survivors.' The women broadcasting the news said. Both of my parents’ jaws dropped instantly, but Mom's face quickly turned into anger. She walked up to my father and just stared at him. 'You realize what you've done?!' She asked with an evil tone, but yet her voice seemed to crack. He was almost speechless. 'I… Well… How was I to know? I wasn't being serious.' He answered back. Mom's face filled with tears as she ran to the back of the house, but quickly reemerged with her jacket, and the keys to the vehicle. Her eyes were flooded with tears now, but none had escaped the rim of her bottom eyelid, yet. 'Just… You stay here you evil son of a bitch!' She screeched. Before he could even stand up, or say anything, she was out the front door. -------------------------------------- 'Don't worry about a thing Kaden. I couldn't see anything bad happening to you. You may endure some hard times, but you'll make it through everything. I have faith in you, and only hope for the best for you and for your life. I love you.' -------------------------------------- What would be Mom's last words to me before I left for the airport earlier that day? Little would she know that my flight would go down a few hours into it. Why she rushed so fast to the airport, it may never be known. She had come in through the entrance closest to the Delta Air Lines terminal, and frantically looked for me. Hoping I wasn't on that flight for one reason or another. She hurriedly ran up to the nearest desk servicing Delta, and began her questioning. 'What in the Hell happened to flight 133?!' She asked, almost hyperventilating. The lady behind the desk got a worried look on her face. 'Ma'am, maybe I'm not the best person to be talking to right now. We are so very sorry for what has more than likely happened to you, but not even we know what happened yet. You should probably go to a police station, or back home. It would be the best place for you.' She said, as nice as she could be for the probable woman beginning to mourn for the possible loss of her only child. Tears fill up her eyes. 'Were all passengers aboard that flight?' She asked. The lady sighed, but went into the computer system. After some searching she looked back at my mother. 'Yes, ma'am, all seats were taken, and accounted for on that flight.' She said lowering her head, sorry for her. Mary, my mom, couldn't handle it, and began to break down. She covered her face with one of her hands, and walked to a nearby seating area to bawl her head off. She wouldn't care that people walking by were wondering what was wrong, or anything of the likes. She had "lost" her only child, her son, Christopher Kaden Calix. -------------------------------------- 'Don't worry, Mom, I'll be just fine!' -------------------------------------- It had been the last thing I would have said to her for a while, myself, on my way out of the door. Then again, you never know what might happen. My mother continued to cry in her seat, in the Delta Air Lines terminal of Athens International Airport. After a few moments of her continuing to cry, a soft tap came across her right shoulder. 'Leave me alone, I don't need your pity.' She said, her face still buried in the palm of her right hand. The tap came once more. 'I said leave me alone. I don't need anyone bothering me right now!' She blurted out. A few more moments went by before a voice spoke out to her. 'You may not want anyone to bother you right now, but I think I've got some good news for you.' The voice claimed. The voice, however, was not just any voice, but yes, of course, my voice. Mom darted directly out of her seat and latched onto my neck. 'Oh my God! Kaden, you're alive!" She shouted. Thus causing more people to stare than who already was. I smiled to myself and hugged her back. 'Why didn't you call?!' She asked. I nodded my head. 'I just got off the phone with my sperm donor not long ago. He told me you had taken off in a fit, and would more than likely come here.' I told her. She kind of chuckled; however, it was muffled by her face being buried in my left shoulder. 'Is that all that was said?' She asked, picturing the conversation. 'No.' I said back to her. 'It was far from being a pleasant conversation. That was also far from being all that was said. All that other shit was just nonsense though.' I explained. She pushed herself away from my shoulder and looked into my eyes as she wiped the tears off of her cheeks. 'Why are you still here? How were you not on that flight with the rest? They said the flight was full, and everyone boarded.' She said, asking how I was still alive. I smiled. 'Well I was here long enough I realized a few things. I sold my ticket back to the air line, and got a later flight. Then after this happened, I sold THAT ticket back as well, and got a flight through a different air line as well. They kind of canceled all flights out of the country for a day or two because of this, so yeah.' I said to her, the smile on her face getting bigger as the conversation continued. She then kind of got curious. 'What had you realized, though, that made you get the later flight?' She asked. I smiled to her, and picked up her purse off the seat next to where she had just been sitting. I rummaged through it for a few seconds before finding what I was looking for, and pulled it out. I showed it to her and smiled. 'I forgot my passport.' I said. She smiled back at me, and let out a few short breathed laughs that were more like whimpers from crying, and put her arms back around me. I put my arms back around her too, and we stood there. The last time we'd see each other for who knows how long. But it will be a moment I won't forget. A day I won't forget. The beginning to the rest of my life. The scene fades to black.
