Forgotten love

2 February 2006

Date: 2006-02-02 "You mean pretty boy? Bah, he was lucky to have pinned me the other night but this round’s going to be different." Salaok responds to the question regarding his next round in the Primetime Central tournament. "That pretty boy smashed your ass Salaok, you can’t call it luck when he destroyed you in the middle of the ring." The questions coming from the young reporter’s mouth clearly weren’t well thought through, or at least the wording wasn’t as he soon found Salaok’s hand wrapped around his tiny next. "Listen son, I had and still do have other things on my mind these days so if I tell you I wasn’t fully focused in the ring that night you sure as hell had better believe me, clear?" Salaok’s voice that of a demonic warrior, gruff as hell and full of fire. "Okay, now let me go ya big…." The young man’s voice trails off not wanting to finish what he was saying. "Now this is the first time you’ve made it this far in the tournament, most people think it’s a fluke, what do you think." "I think that it’s time for you to shut up or ask a question that I’m not going to find insulting." The young man pauses for a second as Salaok removes his hand from around the reporters neck, allowing him to flick through his tiny pink note book. Smiling to himself, the young man looks up at Salaok and riffles out the next question. "So any truth to the rumour that you and Bubba have spoken about you returning to Global?" Finally, a non-insulting question from the young man. "We’ve spoken however the outcome isn’t what the fans were wanting. I will never be setting foot into the 8-sided ring in Global again according to McCoy." "What, wouldn’t you suck his dick or something?" With that Salaok’s hand returned to being around the young man’s neck, only for a brief moment before through him across the hallway and through a closed door. The above were the happenings just before Salaok Davis went to seek tournament director Argyle regarding the level or reporters that he’d hired for this years tournament. Wayne was a simple man. A grounds keeper for a private cemetery he saw nothing exciting happen most nights as his small run down television set was enough to keep him company when he wasn’t at the local bar. His hands had long since seized up making it hard to do his day to day working but he kept at it regardless as there was simply nothing else in his life to pass the time. Each day was spent picking the weeds from the headstones, removing any old flowers that were left and trying his hardest to keep the gardens looking alive. Day after day he did the same thing but it never bothered him because his own family had either moved away or pass away, leaving him on his own. Some would think that living at a graveyard would be a haunting experience but that was life for old Wayne as he watched individuals turn up on random days to pay their respects. Wayne was never alone and yet had no one to talk to for the ghosts who walked the grounds never said a word. While sure, Wayne believed that some of the ghosts he saw were of the dead, most of the ghosts were actually of the living. A live human being who had lost everything and since had become a ghost themselves as their emotionless faces peer off into the nothing which surrounds there lives. The void in which their dearly departed had left. There was however one man who had been coming into the graveyard recently who he hadn’t seen until a month prior, who would come in and place white sheets over some of the headstones. Every night without fail this gentleman would walk through the graveyard, placing the sheets of pure white over the headstones, only to then have another man appear shortly after. The second man Wayne knew, and knew well as Salaok Davis. The reason for knowing him as well as Wayne believed he did was due to the deaths of his wife and child, which had brought him to the cemetery every day for a year. Every day for a year Salaok had visited the graveyard and then one day he simply didn’t come, not just that day, but every day after that for nearly two years. The word on the breeze was that Salaok had lost his mind, which in truth it was that Salaok had lost his memory and these new trips to the graveyard with the other man was a reminded of who he was. Having nothing to remind you of who you used to be other than the headstones of those who you once loved couldn’t have been an easy thing and Wayne watched on with worry for the remembering man. Tonight however there was something not quite right about the situation which played out in front of Wayne’s eyes as Salaok arrived on his own and the other man hadn’t returned to put the sheets back up. The covers, they’re not here. Salaok thought to himself as he moved faster through the graveyard towards the area which had continually been covered from his eyes, hiding those who had died from him. He stopped dead in his tracks, close enough to see the headstones were indeed uncovered but not close enough to read the names of those who were remembered by them. Was this truly what he was wanting, what he was needing? He peered over his right shoulder, through the old desolate trees and looked at Wayne sitting in his one room building, lit only by the light coming from his only friend, television. Wayne however wasn’t paying attention to the television, he gaze was fixed on Salaok as he could see what was about to happen. The two men exchanged a nod, both of had different meanings not that it mattered at the time. Salaok’s nod was to acknowledge the old watched, while Wayne’s was a nod of luck and understanding of what Salaok was about to put himself through. His heavy steps through the soggy leaf covered ground brought him closer to what he needed to see with every squelch underfoot. Each name, like a knife driven into his heart only to then vibrate up into his mind where the memories flow and the tears run like a river to the ocean. While there were many a name in which he saw and that hurt his very being, there was but one name he had to see. One headstone that meant more to him that all the others combined and that was the one between his false headstone and that of his son’s. The headstone which had been kept from him since he had begun to remember who he was, he believe to be that of the woman who he wore the ring for and the woman who had given birth to his son. Her name until now had escaped him. Salaok dropped to his knees in front of the headstone as the heavens above opened up with tears of their own, quickly turning the dirt under Salaok’s feet into mud. The name that had escaped from for the past two years was there; cold as the stone it was carved into. Lisha Davis Salaok broke out into a sob while his hands reached out and hugged the headstone, at one point tracing the name on the pillar that was the only reminder he had of his late wife. While up until now he hadn’t known for sure if she had been alive or dead, there had always been a feeling in the back of his heart that this was her resting place. Resting there next to the headstone in the soaking rain, he became silent and still while his eyes opened wide like an owl’s during the hunt for prey, all the time shaking uncontrollably. As the sky cracked open with a bolt of lightning, there she was in all her former beauty with he red hair falling down over her shoulders and draping over her perfect breasts. Her hair while striking in it’s own right, it served as the perfect frame for a face of pure amazing beauty and love. Her skin that of silk with a radiant glow of a pregnant mother which is how Salaok remembered her as she carried their second child inside of womb. She was love, in every way he knew the word to be. Walking on air as she moved towards him, Lisha smiled a shy smile and reached down, touching Salaok on the side of the face with a care that only a life long companion could. "I’m glad you remember me now my love." Her voice while as angelic as he remembers, it also echoed slightly, bouncing from the heavens above. Not able to fully comprehend the situation, Salaok’s mouth opens and closes, trying to speak but only sounding like a mute at first. "It has been two years since I last visited you my dear, for without your memory of me, there was no point to my visit to you." "I wanted to remember you, I really did Lisha but I couldn’t." His voice, almost pleading for forgiveness but there is no need for such a sound as Lisha nods knowingly. "Relax my dear Salaok, I know of what happened and while remembering everything again is indeed a painful process for you, all of this had to happen, including my death. For in the end there will be only one and this is the path that you must travel but there are still things you must relearn as painful as it will be." Her words sound that of one who knows the future already, as if the guiding words of an angel or one of the overseers which Salaok had been told about. "Tell me what it is that I must learn and how it is you know of what is to come?" He questions his late wife, still not completely in belief that he could be talking to her, here in the cemetery in which she lays. He watches as the air around her turns into a white light, almost blinding, but still he listens. "You don’t remember what happened to me do you Salaok? You don’t remember the war, which left me laying six feet under where you sit and that you must understand again. You need to understand for what it is you have left to fight for my love and you will not understand until you relearn." Extending her hand to her husbands to which he excepts, pulling Salaok to his feet, Lisha plants a kiss as soft as a piece of freshly picked cotton upon his cheek. The light, which had originally surrounded Lisha, seems to grow, slowly at first as Salaok hugs his wife and enjoys the feeling of her soft lips against his corse skin, then the light grows fast and consumes all. White Squinting and shielding his eyes with his right hand while his left holds onto his wife, Salaok tries to make out the images through the burning white light but it is impossible until it gradually begins to fade away again. Fade into the images that he had been struggling to see, and now were as clear as the summer sky in July. Parts of the images he could see were clear while others were blurred which confused Salaok at first as he knew the location so well. His wife who stood by his side had brought Salaok home and yet together they looked at a group of people sitting around the dinner table and he knew each and every one of those people, two of which was himself and Lisha. "Do you remember this night Salaok?" Lisha questioned, knowing full well that at this point in time he did, otherwise his reaction to it would’ve been less pleasant and calm. "Look out there on the small grassy hill my love and it may just job your memory." Lisha points and Salaok’s eyes follow her hand that directs his attention out of the window to the small grassy hill in question. Salaok’s eyes narrow and fill with hatred and there, standing atop the small hill was his half brother, Mani and partner in crime, Jude. Salaok knew that he loathed these two men but for the moment, didn’t know why until the next scene began to play out in front of his eyes. The shattering of the glass sounded clearly as it feel to the ground, but that wasn’t all the feel to the ground as he watched the image of his wife fall to the ground with a bullet wound to her chest. The crimson oil pumped fast out of her body as Salaok watched himself order people to help in different ways. He watched everyone run around in a panic doing what he or she could and yet the only one with calm on her face was Lisha. She lay there knowing that this was going to happen and pulled Salaok close to speak to him but as imagines of them began to speak, the white light blinds once again. White This time his eyes adjust faster and it’s clear to see where they have arrived at this time but Salaok remains silent and watches. The image of Salaok steps into the room and there she lay, awake now and changed into clean hospital clothes as all of the external bleeding had been contained. There was nothing to show that Lisha wasn’t going to pull through except for the knowledge that the internal bleeding wouldn’t stop and her body wouldn’t take the blood on. Her face was that of an angel. Although on death’s bed, her face was always strong and beautiful. She would never let anyone other than Salaok know that she was hurting, but even now, she stays strong for her husband. He moves over to the side of the bed and takes a hold of her hand as she smiles. It wasn’t a forced smile, but one that was heart felt and one that she was pleased she could give him. "I’ve excepted it Salaok and you must now do the same." Her voice of calm reason was one thing that Salaok could not bare to do without. He brings her hand up to his lips and plants a soft kiss on her tender and now pale skin. "I will never accept what they have done to you my dear." "In the end Sal, you will see that everything that has ever happened, happened for a reason. Every action has it’s purpose whether you like it or not Salaok." Salaok lowers his eyes, not worthy of being in the presence of such a woman. Although not a lot of strength is left in her body, she nudges Salaok’s chin with her hand to make him look at her. "Make sure that Darrell remains safe at all times. And in doing so, I will make sure you are the same. I will watch over you Salaok and be there when you ask for guidance. When you need me, just call out my name and I will be there." "You didn’t fight this did you? You gave up back at the house." Taking Salaok’s hand in both of hers, she holds it tight. "I didn’t give up Sal, I accepted that which had been given to me." As soon as Lisha finishes the door opens and Sage walks into the room, behind Darrell who still has tears streaming down his face. "Mumma!" Darrell runs across the room and throws his upper body over the bed. Lisha’s hands let go of Salaok as she wraps her arms around Darrell’s body and squeezes him with everything she’s got. She looks up at Sage and smiles. "Thank you Sage. Now make sure you look after my boys now." Sage stands there silent as a tear runs down his cheek before opening his mouth. "I will Lisha. And thank you." Lisha nods in understanding of what Sage meant and then turns her attention to Darrell in her arms. In the calmest voice she could possibly muster, she begins to explain things to Darrell and Salaok can do nothing but stand there in silence and in awe of this beautiful person who he is about to lose. "Darrell my dear I have something to tell you. I’m going to be going away to a place you will never be able to go. My life is coming to an end, but your life is only starting. Remember everything I have told you and you will grow up to be a great man." Darrell, now completely overwhelmed by the hurt inside his little heart, talking through his cries. "I don’t want you to go Mumma. Why do you have to go?" "I’m dying, Darrell. The doctors can’t stop the bleeding inside of me. But don’t worry. I will be all right. Wipe away those tears and be strong for me Darrell. I need you to be strong." Darrell’s cries don’t stop, however they do reduce in volume as he tries to be strong. But for a child losing his mother, there is nothing in the world that is harder to try and be strong through. "I don’t want to be strong Mum. I just want to be with you." "I know dear, but you can’t be with me. One day we will be together again, I give you my word on that." Suddenly Lisha visibly shivers as her body begins to go cold and she grimaces with the pain, which luckily Darrell doesn’t see, but Salaok and Sage certainly do. "I will forever be with you in your hearts as long as you remember that I love you." With that said, Lisha closes her eyes and lets one last breath exhale from her lungs as Salaok drops to his knees next to the bed. "I will never stop loving you Lisha. Never." As the white light begun to consume all again, Salaok lets a single tear fall down to the floor, unable to believe that such a time had been wiped from his memory until now. White The light fades and Salaok is back in the cemetery and so is Lisha, only now the image of her isn’t as he had remembered as the bullet wound seemed to have had reopened and blood trickled down her chest, soaking into the pure white dress that she wears. "Remember Salaok, that in the end, there can only be one. Remember what has happened to me and of the warnings in which you were told as a youth and you will find what you need to fight on." Lisha’s voice begins to trail off as the sentence ends and Salaok realises what’s going on and calls out after her. "I’ll never stop loving you Lisha. Never!" "Why thank you Salaok, but my name isn’t Lisha." The voice was changing, as it echoed until finally Salaok’s eyes snap too and there in front of him, soaking wet is Wayne. His grey hair saturated from the rain hangs over his face and his clothes hug is thin body, but still he tries to get Salaok up. Salaok pushes Wayne away, not sure what is going on and the old man falls backwards into a puddle, splashing water up into Salaok’s already wet face. "No need to get angry young Salaok, I just didn’t want you sleeping here tonight in this weather otherwise I’d be rolling you into that false grave of yours. This isn’t heaven’s waiting room pal, you’re going to have to move on for the night." Salaok was sure that what he had just been through had been real, he couldn’t have dreamt it, could he? The message she had mentioned was so clear. "There can only be one." While he knew that it wasn’t the tournament she was talking about, it was something far bigger. This was about his family. It was time to see his brother again and find out the truth this time.
