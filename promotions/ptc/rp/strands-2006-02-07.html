Strands

7 February 2006

Date: 2006-02-07 There is a house that is not quite a house, and in it lives a man who is not quite a man. The darkened corridors are empty of life around the clock, and the cavernous halls and libraries in this most imposing of buildings gather dust as they lie derelict. Gothic arches seem to lead nowhere, as if construction work was stopped before everything was ready. Secret passages lie behind bookcases and paintings like a 1950s movie villain’s paradise, but down one of these there lies a room where there are two men and a machine. BEEP… One lies prone, his consciousness long ago shrivelled and gone. Two drips feed into his arm, one containing all the essential nutrients and nourishment that he needs to survive and the other containing the drugs that have gradually brought him in to this comatose state. BEEP… The only other sound in the room is from a ventilator, as it pushes air in and out of the patient… the prisoner’s lungs. The slow process repeats itself over and over, and other than the rising and falling of his chest, the man’s body doesn’t move at all. BEEP… Then there is the other; wreathed in shadows, standing back and watching his handiwork finally come to fruition. He has been biding his time, staying away from the spotlights and completing his masterwork. BEEP… He has been patient. BEEP… He has been waiting. BEEP… But the time for waiting is now over. BEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEP… *** "So, what the hell happened to me?" asked Artemis Stevens. Shanty Jones looked across at Doctor Curiosity, who just shrugged. This was going to be a hard sell. "You were… taken ill," said Shanty, in his soft Welsh accent. "Taken ill? Where the hell am I?" "You’re in Washington D.C." said Curiosity. "I’m… what the fuck?" Shanty looked across the room again to silence the meddling doctor. It was going to be hard enough to explain why Artemis was so far away from his home in London, why he might be feeling slightly drained, and perhaps most importantly, why he had no memories of the previous week. "Have you kidnapped me?" asked Artemis, suddenly looking around at everything in the room. They were back in Shanty’s hotel, with the wrestler sat on the bed next to the dazed Artemis. Curiosity was sat in a chair across from them, sniffing the air and wondering when the smell of cordite would go away. It had lingered ever since Shanty had gotten rid of the ‘dybbuk’. Artemis rolled across to the far side of the bed and started to shiver a little, his eyes darting from one man to the next. "What do you want with me? I’m not rich… I don’t have much." "We don’t want anything," reassured Shanty. "Have you taken a kidney? Please don’t take a kidney!" "You want to know what happened to you?" asked Shan. Artemis nodded, though deep inside he wasn’t sure if knowing would be a good thing. "The short version is that I killed your brother in Iraq, along with a whole bunch of other people. Only thing was, his soul didn’t go to either Heaven or Hell, it stuck around. In fact, everyone who died there came back one way or another, and then they had a choice. They could accept what I did when I planted the nail bomb, and forgive me. Others were too consumed with hate, and they would stop at nothing to see me dead. Your brother, Steven, I thought he was a good man. I thought he was as upset by the factories as I was…" "Factories?" asked Artemis, but Shan ignored him. "So he needed a way back, did Steven. He found you and took control; brought your body all the way back here to get revenge on me. He didn’t know that I had been waiting for him the entire time. I had been waiting for them all, and one by one they made their choices. Steven was the last one, and he chose… he chose the wrong path." Shanty sniffed the air. No amount of air fresheners or bunches of flowers would ever rid the room of the smell. "When he left, you were dropped back here, back to life. It’s taken a few days but you’re awake again. That’s what you’ve been doing for the past week." Artemis mulled over the words carefully. "Okay, what really happened? How about you tell me the truth? Are you drug runners?" Shanty laughed. "No, it’s worth than that. We’re professional wrestlers." *** "Do you think he’ll be any trouble?" asked Doctor Curiosity as he walked down the street with Shanty Jones. It was a couple of hours later, and Artemis was again getting some rest. Whilst there was no way he believed what Shanty had told him, there was little that he felt up to doing, and fatigue had claimed him again. "He’ll be fine. The dreams he gets for the next few nights will not be pleasant, but he’ll leave us and go back to his life and it’ll be as if nothing even changed." "How can you be so sure?" "I told you… it’s not the first time that I’ve done this." "Jah… I suppose so." "This kind of experience messes your mind up for a while, but he’ll forget it quicker than you think would be possible. Give him a fortnight and he’ll be sure that it was all just a bad dream." "I see," said Curiosity. "You’ve still not given an answer though." "About what?" "The factories." "I don’t want to talk about it." *** The body didn’t even have the time to start cooling before the man of shadows moved forwards and took action. The chemically induced coma had brought his patient to the brink of life and death, and then flirted somewhere between the two states like a terrifyingly real Schrodinger’s Cat. His body looked alive, even if it was emaciated, a far cry from his days as an athlete. Blue veins bulged at the edges of his skin, but no longer pumped life through the body. A simple turn of a switch was all that was needed to get the electricity flowing, and to replace the flow of one drug with that of another. This was the start of the recovery, and as the body jolted on the table, sparks coruscating down on to the cold stone floor, the man in the darkness smiled. This was the part of the plan where things could have failed, but his technicians and physicians were very, very good at their jobs. They had practiced for many years to be able to run these techniques. The man on the table came back to life as easily as switching on a light bulb. Everything was in its right place, and the endgame was about to begin. *** "Tell me about the factories," insisted Curiosity. "You don’t want to know," murmured Shanty. "Zen vhy did I ask?" said the doctor, stopping on the sidewalk. Shanty paused to look around at his companion. It was strange how they had somehow stopped being at each other’s throat the entire time. Was this really the same person that he had considered getting a restraining order against? Was he genuinely having a conversation with someone who had conned or drugged a judge into signed a restraining order forcing the two of them to remain within twenty yards of one another at all times? "Vell?" asked Curiosity. Ah, what the hell. Maybe this maturity from the doctor should be rewarded. Well, that’s if he could consider this a reward… *** We were never meant to even see the factories. We knew that there were certain buildings that we were forbidden to enter. That’s not exactly something that stands out when you’re in the military because there’s always a dozen or so no-go areas, and we post guards everywhere just in case. We just happened to be going past it when the mortar shells landed on the Andrews Factory. It was another insurgent attack which was again nothing new; in Iraq you have to get used to being on guard at all times. Basra is certainly one place you can go to if you want to be on your toes the entire time. With one hundred dead British soldiers there though, you might end up off your toes for good. This attack was slightly different because it seemed to be more sustained. There were eight or nine shells that fell on or around the factory, and there was a difficulty in ascertaining where the fire was coming from. Normally the insurgents will fire once and then leave, but these guys kept on going, and for some reason we just couldn’t find them until they had done some serious damage. I was busy trying to help our guys in the factory, but I was told that the attackers just stayed in the middle of the road, firing mortars at the Andrews Factory. Even when they were faced with guns, they didn’t seem to acknowledge the presence of our troops. They didn’t even acknowledge the existence of any of the other factories, like Brown, Cartwright or Denver. A few bursts of automatic weapon fire, and the mortars stopped falling. By then the damage was done. The Andrews Factory had been a plain building, a testament to cement and corrugated iron, but after the bombardment it had proven to be weaker that the people inside might have hoped. One exterior wall collapsed and it brought down half of the ceiling. A further load-bearing wall fell, and most of the building followed it down. I saw it, and my reaction was instant. There were places that you weren’t meant to go, but when you could save your colleagues, other considerations took over from just how private a building was meant to be. I was standing next to Steven Atkins, you know the one, Artemis’s brother, and together we went in, moving rocks and rubble to get through to the people underneath. Eventually we got through a crack, and though it was dangerous our adrenalin was pumping and we wanted to do something. The lights were gone so all we could see was from the light filtering through a few little holes in the carnage. I guess we should have waited for the other crews to come on over and the construction guys to tell us which bits were safe to move, but we’d not fired our guns in anger for so long, and being on the edge in Iraq for so long was getting to us. We had to have action, and this was it. Steven spotted it first, a sort of trap door in the floor. It had metal handles at the side, two bars shaped like pi, but the wooden face of the door was covered in pieces of the wall. We cleared it quickly and then pulled up on the handles, hard. I remember quickly glancing around as we pulled away the rocks and something seemed odd, but it never occurred to me until the door was open to realise that there was nothing in the building at all. It was completely empty. Not just the room that we were in but the entire building. Various holes in walls showed us the whole space, and there was not a single thing inside the Andrews Factory. I guess our minds were still processing this information when we dropped down to the floor below. It wasn’t much of a drop, and there was a ladder there to help us, but what we saw took our breath away. They probably hadn’t even noticed that they were under attack. We found ourselves on some sort of gantry. It was a metallic walkway, hard beneath out feet but perforated enough to let you see all the way down below. We looked down from this walkway and saw so many different things. There were cells, there were tables, there were bodies. The bodies, I can’t… they weren’t… it wasn’t right. I’d made my mind up without even realising it. I knew that if I lived beyond those moments that I would have to take some action in the future. There was no way that I could be a party to what was down there. They were prisoners. At the time I didn’t know how they were arrested or why, but they had all been taken down there and shackled to something. It wasn’t far enough down for me to have trouble seeing their faces, and their mouths. One woman looked up at me with an unspoken plea, her mouth opening wide, trying to say something in a language I couldn’t understand, but being prevented from even doing that by the burnt, cauterised stub of the tongue that was just visible at the back of her throat. The whole place looked like a cross between a medical laboratory and the Temple of Doom. I half expected to see Steven swinging down on a vine like some kind of modern Indiana Jones, but he was just staring, as I was. From there on in it only got worse. … No. I don’t want to talk about it any more. *** Shanty and Curiosity walked along in silence, towards the hotel. The time out in the open air had been good for Shanty. He had gotten something off his chest, though by no means everything, and he was feeling reinvigorated. He would soon be facing Masquerade in the biggest wrestling match of his short career, and despite everything that had happened, he still wanted to wipe the smile from behind the mask and reverse the result of the previous week. He’d been distracted, he told himself, but this time it would all be different. Shanty’s train of thought was derailed violently as the scream of sirens interrupted the quiet afternoon. Three police cars soon came into view, and drew up to Jones with a screech of brakes and a faint smell of burnt rubber. "Ah crap," said Doctor Curiosity, as the cops got out of their cars. "Artemis must have said something. "No," said Shanty. It doesn’t work like that." The first cop walked over to Doctor Curiosity, completely ignoring Shanty, and drew the smaller man away to one side. "Doctor Goodman," said the officer, ignoring the doctor’s wince at the sound of his actual name. "We have to request that you come with us for the time being." "Vhy?" "There has been a situation, but we strongly recommend that you come with us for the time being. It is a matter of your safety." Doctor Curiosity, the consummate coward, nodded his head and then turned to Shanty, who had walked on a few yards to put distance between himself and anyone who could feasibly arrest him. "Shan! I vill be going vith zese men for ze moment! I vill come back and see you before ze match!" Shanty shrugged. Despite having grown accustomed to the strange man’s company, he had always been one to favour solitude and silence over company. Without regret, he turned and walked off towards the hotel. "Now, can you tell me vhat you vant?" asked Curiosity of the police officer, as they walked towards the nearest car. "Well sir, it’s about your brother." "My brother? Eegor?" The cop was confused for a second, but carried on anyway. "About your brother, Paul Goodman." "Jah! Eegor!" "Well, sir. Paul Goodman was, as you know, in prison for the murder of your wife, Collette." Curiosity nodded, but he knew somehow what was coming. Paul had changed while he was in prison. It all started during GTT4 when the doctor had inadvertently caused a riot whilst visiting. Somehow in the aftermath Paul had become important in the prison, and from there it all snowballed. "Jah," said the doctor, quietly. "Well, nobody knows exactly how, but he’s escaped. His cell was still locked and there was no feasible route out… but he’s gone." "I see." "So, sir, since he killed your wife, and we know that you haven’t been to visit him in the past few months, it’s been suggested that he might be trying to come and visit you." Curiosity nodded his head. He didn’t feel any fear towards his brother. Over the last few years he’d never had any trouble manipulating his elder sibling, and the murder had been committed by Eegor trying to save his brother. No, there would be something else. "Jah, I vill come vith you for now, and find out more." Another mystery to solve, thought Curiosity. Another mystery to solve. *** The key card entered the slot as usual and the door gave its beep of approval as the locking mechanism shifted tumblers and let Shanty Jones back into his hotel room. The faint whiff of smoke remained in the room, as it always would, and Artemis slept on the bed, regaining his strength. Everything was exactly as he had left it. The door clicked shut behind him, and after hanging his old leather jacket on the stand, Shanty sat down on an armchair and closed his eyes. Now was a good time to relax; the calm before the in-ring storm with Masquerade. He had almost drifted off into the realms of the subconscious when a voice woke him up abruptly. "Hello Shanty," it said, bright and breezy, as if it didn’t have a care in the world. Shan opened his eyes, and sat on the other chair, right across from him, was Paul Goodman. A smile played on the escaped convict’s lips, and a devilish glint was twinkling in his eye. "So… did you miss me?"
