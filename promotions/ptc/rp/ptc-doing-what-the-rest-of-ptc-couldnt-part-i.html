Doing What the Rest of PTC Couldn't, Part I (Extreme Title Match)

19 December 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Inside a tan brick house with a white picket fence, in the heart of Suburbia, Middle America, three months ago… “Awww, gimme a fuckin’ break!” Kernels of popcorn bounce off Archibald MacGregor’s face… …well, the image of his face, at any rate. On-screen, the PTC Extreme Champion lords his defended title over the seething crowd, ignoring the debris that blankets both the ring and the body of Vivica J. Valentine so thoughtfully tenderized by MacGregor with a steel chair moments before. The hatred of the crowd is reflected by the group of boys gathered around the big-screen watching PTC vs. Archibald MacGregor on Pay-Per-View. One of the boys leans back in a bean-bag chair, tossing another handful of popcorn at the visage of PTC’s Most Wanted. “I can’t believe it,” he proclaims, disappointment etched on his acne-pocked face, “seven of the best from the rest of PTC, their only mission to beat one guy, and this asshole still manages to walk out with his belt.” A round of bitter laughter circulates through the room. “He’s a lucky bastard,” comes one opinion from the voice. A chorus of agreement echoes the sentiment. One of the kids seated on a hassock to the far left says nothing, his eyes locked on the screen where the image of MacGregor remains, the PTC Extreme Title gripped in his contemptuous hands. Almost to himself, the kid whispers softly: “He’s unstoppable.” Doing What the Rest of PTC Couldn’t, Part I Leahi Hospital, Honolulu, HI, August 30th, 2005 A skinny, balding man waits in an elevator, eyeing the digital floor number display impatiently. He adjusts the tie of his suit as a bead of sweat trickles down the side of his face and soaks into his collar. It’s still very much summer in Hawaii. Ding! The doors open and he steps out briskly, walking over to the first receptionist’s desk that he sees. The woman behind the counter looks up at him inquisitively. “May I help you?” “Yes,” the man replies, offering a thin, cordial grin, “I’m here to see Caesar Vega…checked in I believe two days ago with neck trauma.” The woman swings around in her chair, typing at her computer for a moment before turning back to the man, cocking an eyebrow. “Goes by ‘Nova,’ right?” “Um, yes, I believe that’s right,” the man says with a nod, the briefcase at his side tapping against the frame of the desk. “Hmmph,” she snorts, “you family?” “No, no,” the man replies, dismissing the notion with a wave, “I’m, ah, a representative of one of his employers.” “Well, when you go in there, tell him to take it easy,” the woman says, obviously exasperated, “He’s been a real pain in the butt these last two days.” Down the hallway, two lefts, straight through the double doors, past the ice machine, in Nova’s room… “I’ll do it. Don’t test my resolve…Mary.” Nova stares up at his nurse, his eyes darting over to the tag on her enormous hooter in order to tack her name onto the end of his declaration. He’s stretched out under the covers of a hospital bed, and looks like shit, with dark circles under his eyes, a pallor to his skin, and a neck brace that pushes his bearded cheeks up like a chipmunk’s. “Mr. Vega, you should be resting, not staring into this box all day,” Mary advises, “I’m turning the TV off.” “I’m telling you, you turn that TV off and I’ll shit my pants right here, right now,” Nova warns, the corners of his mouth curling into a devious grin, “and they’ve been loading me up on pudding and apple sauce.” Mary pauses with her hand on the set’s power button, her eyes scanning Nova’s face to determine whether or not he’s bluffing. With long blonde hair strewn about behind his head, a woolly beard beginning to poof out over the chin-line of the neck-brace, and aquamarine eyes currently possessed by a wild glint, she deduces that he probably isn’t. Shit, he looks like one of the hobos you’d see taking a dump under a bridge on the interstate. She backs away from the TV, giving him a stern scowl of disapproval. Nova’s grin broadens. Another victory. “I love you, Mary,” he declares, his voice muffled somewhat by the brace’s fastening effect on jaw mobility, “you’re taking great care of me here.” “Yeah, yeah,” she says, rolling her eyes as she moves towards the door. “Mary,” Nova says, managing to raise his left arm a couple of inches off the bed, “can you…hand me that?” She follows his gaze over to a table where the PTC Extreme Title lays, gleaming in the afternoon light that shines through the window blinds. Her eyes soften and she nods, walking over and scooping the belt up before bringing it him and setting it gently on his stomach. She walks to the door, turning back before opening it. “You’re going to be fine, Mr. Vega,” she offers hopefully, but the words don’t register to Nova, whose eyes are locked back onto the TV screen where clips of PRIME’s Tropical Turmoil Pay-Per-View play over and over again on repeat; Nova leaps off the top rope with Shooting Star Press intended for Aimz, Brandon Youngblood collides with him intentionally, and he lands head-first in the ring, instantly going limp. Impact. Impact. Impact. The reflections of his fall dance in his eyes, which flinch with every rag-doll accordion fold-up of his tiny televised body. His fingers slowly grip around the black leather strap of the title in his lap. Mary gives him one last look before opening the door and stepping out…directly into a man in a suit carrying a briefcase. “Oh!” he cries, jumping to the right to avoiding colliding with her, “so sorry! Excuse me!” Mary gives him a cold once-over before turning to Nova. “Mr. Vega, it appears you have a visitor.” Nova’s eyes finally peel away from the TV and fall on the man walking towards his bed. Mary shoots the man a dirty scowl before walking out the door, letting it shut behind her. He walks around to the side of Nova’s bed and lays his briefcase on the table, taking a seat next to the Fallen Star. “Hello, Nova.” Nova turns his head slightly, tired eyes falling on the suit. “I don’t think I know you.” “That doesn’t surprise me,” the man says with the same thin, mirthless grin, “but I know you.” “What’s your name, cowboy?” Nova inquires, sizing up his new acquaintance. “It’s not really important, you’ll probably never see me again,” the man replies, dismissing the question, “but I’m a representative of Primetime Central, and…I’ll just get right to it…I’m here for the Extreme Title.” “No.” The man nods, expectant of the reaction, and continues. “I understand this is a very difficult time for you, but PTC has big plans for that belt, Nova. There’s a interfed-Pay-Per-View approaching, Super Series, and in the wake of your injury, the guys at the top have decided to make the main event a Scavenger Hunt for the, ah, vacant, Global and Extreme Titles. It’s really gonna be a huge event, you should see the lis-” “The belt’s not fucking vacant,” Nova practically spits, little fires spontaneously combusting in his eyes. The man pauses at the interruption, his eyes going from Nova’s head down to his toes and back up again, blatantly making his point without words. “I can’t fucking believe this,” Nova growls in frustration, “I wait all summer to be booked against the Uber Judge, to defend this strap, and two days after I get shelved they send a shark…” “Uhhh, excuse me…” “…to tell me about their grandiose plans for a PTC Pay-Per-View with this glorious main event that, unfortunately, has nothing to do with me,” Nova finishes, “were you fuckers waiting for something like this to happen?” “Nova, the bottom line is, we need the Extreme Title, and you have it,” the man says, looking at his watch. The reality of the suit’s purpose finally sinks in for the Fallen Star, and the anger leaves his face, replaced by desperation, “Can’t…can’t you guys just give me a little time? Maybe I can defend the belt at this Super Series or something…maybe I can…” “Nova,” the man interrupts, his voice emotionless, “we have to look at the facts here. You’re bedridden, unable to walk at the moment, and we don’t have a prognosis for when that’s getting better. Meanwhile, the PTC belt divisions are stagnant. The Global and Extreme Titles went all summer without defenses…” “Not my fault!” “…and I recognize that, but people are clamoring for title match-ups, and we can’t wait around for you to heal up,” the man says, dropping the metaphorical guillotine on this conversation, “so we’re gonna have to vacate the Extreme Title.” He clicks open his briefcase, revealing a foam-padded interior with the shape of a belt cut out in the center. He turns to Nova and extends his hands for the belt. “Come on, Nova.” The Extreme Champion’s hands don’t leave the belt’s rim, and his eyes narrow. “Nova, the belt, please,” the man says, reaching forward and grabbing a hold of the title. “Don’t do this to me,” Nova grunts, feeble hands attempting to hang onto the belt, hands that, were he not injured, would probably be turning this dick’s face into cranberry sauce. The man wrenches the title away and places it into his briefcase. He snaps it shut quickly and adjusts his tie before standing up. He turns his unaffected gaze back to Nova as he moves to leave. “PTC offers its condolences, and wishes you the best of luck for a speedy recovery.” “Fuck you.” Nova shuts his eyes and takes a deep breath, attempting to combat the sense of helplessness that’s washing over him. “Shit.” All is quiet in the hospital room, save for a few birds chirping outside the window. Their gaiety goes unappreciated. “Shit.”
