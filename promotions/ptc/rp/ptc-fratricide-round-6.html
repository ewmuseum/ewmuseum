Fratricide (Round 6)

7 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“Mr. Moraco, are you with us?” Professor Smith asked, looking up from the roll call sheet on his desk. Mr. Smith was a middle-aged man whom had been teaching at the University for nearly twenty years now. Teaching was his passion and had turned down the opportunity to retire several times. Sebastian Moraco shook the cobwebs off, and answered. “Yeah…I’m here.” Mr. Smith checked off Sebastian’s name on the roster sheet. Without looking up he responded back. “I trust everything is all right. You don’t want to add another class to repeat, do you?” Sebastian straightened himself up in his seat. “No, everything is fine,” he lied. He didn’t feel the need or desire to talk about his concerns. He couldn’t get the idea that something happened the night before his Gauntlet invitational. When he had arrived home with the take out dinner, his mother had appeared indifferent. She was quiet, shaken up, almost as if something had happened during his small absence. But she had not wanted to talk about it, despite the obvious visibility of it, and Sebastian was succumbed to just let it go. Sebastian continued the rest of the class mostly head down in his books. He feigned interest in reading and searching for answers that Mr. Smith was undoubtedly asking. It was a ruse he had learned before. Most teachers –as he came to find out – often call on those who present appearances of nervousness, hoping the teachers won’t call on them. Rarely do they call upon those who appear firm in their answers, or seek the answers so they can be called upon. It hadn’t always worked, but this time it did for Sebastian After what seemed to be a lifetime, the class finally ended. Sebastian was in no hurry to get packed up and move on to the next period, as so many of the others seemed to be. He lazily stood up from the desk, throwing his books into his bag and tossing it over his shoulder. A hand tapped him on the shoulder outside of the classroom. Sebastian turned around to see that it was his good friend, Melissa Roberts. Melissa was one of those girls who found it their social duty to befriend everyone they saw. You know the kind that is always the first to talk to the exchange student, the new kid, the kid that always stays in the corner pretending to be cool because he wears black. She was twenty years old, just like Sebastian, but a month older. To Sebastian she was cute, had a slender body and hair that seemed to gloss in the light. But he had no interest in her. In fact, he didn’t have any interest in any of the women at his University. They all seemed lacking to him. “Are you okay?” she asked. It was obvious that was going to be the question of the day. Sebastian forced a grin. “I’m okay, really.” “You don’t look okay, Sebastian,” Melissa answered, calling alongside Sebastian. “You’re never that out of it in Mr. Smith’s class. World History is your favorite subject.” Sebastian sighed. “I’m just worried about a few things back home. It’s really nothing.” “Is it your mother?” Melissa asked. She had met Mrs. Moraco a year ago and the two had become very fond of each other. Maria Moraco often toyed with the idea that Sebastian should date her. Sebastian picked up the pace. He didn’t want to talk to anyone. “I said it’s nothing, Mel. Don’t worry about it.” Melissa picked up her pace to match Sebastian and the two were now outside the main building of the University and walking down the stairs to the parking lot. “I’m sorry if you don’t want to talk about it, Seb,” she answered. “Anywho, I’m having a party this weekend if you want to come.” Sebastian stopped at the foot of the steps. “This weekend?” he asked. “Yeah, it’s going to start Saturday night.” Melissa explained. “You will be there, right?” Sebastian nodded, but didn’t answer immediately. Saturday was the day of his match against Uber Judge, Clinton Sage, and Rich Rollins. “Uh…sure, I’ll be there. But I might be a little late. I got something to do Saturday night.” “Don’t worry about that,” Melissa answered, “I said the party starts Saturday, not that it was on Saturday.” II Maria worked in a seamstress factory. You know the kind that was supposed to be illegal for their sheltering of illegal immigrants among other hazards. This particular factory in downtown Queens – which was only a 40 minute commute – had been raided once when Maria had worked there. The agents seemed more interested in locating illegal immigrants than finding any reason to close the shop. They did, but took no such matters. About a half-dozen of the workers had been found without any papers, unable to present their case, and had been escorted out of the factory and to the immigration center in Manhattan. Maria, although she spoke broken English at the time, was able to present her Visa documents and was allowed to remain at the factory. It was the first and only incident to occur at the factory, but it was enough to make Maria wary and she took night classes in English for the first few months in the United States. Now nearly a year later, she spoke English well, though her strong accent combined with her broken English made it hard for her to be understood sometimes. ”Moraco you got a phone call up front,” Kelly Su spoke out on the PA system. Su was the owner of the factory, a Korean lady who had been naturalized five years ago. Maria got up from her desk and talked towards the office up front. She picked up the phone and answered. “Don’t tell me you are out of school already?” “School?” the voice on the other line answered. “We both know I graduated a decade ago.” Maria looked as pale as if she had seen a ghost. “Are you okay?” Su asked her. Maria feigned a smile. “I’m sorry. Can I have some privacy?” Su nodded and walked out of the office. “Porque estas llamando aqui?” Maria whispered loudly asking Miguel why he was calling her. “Why? I was your damned husband for twenty years. I raised all our children with you. Don’t you dare tell me I can’t talk to my own wife.” “You lost that privilege a long time ago, Miguel,” Maria explained, pulling out a chair to sit on. “Don’t give me that shit. I provided for our family. I did everything a father was supposed to do. Do you know how hard it is not to be able to see your own kids? Do you know how hard it is to not fight back when I hear of how the others back home speak about me?” “You should have thought about that, Miguel,” Maria explained, wiping her red eyes dry. “Every court we went through found you unsuitable to be with the children. Not only that, but Sebastian made his own decision to stay with me. Maybe if you had paid more attention to us and not to the whores you see every night, you might have prevented this.” “I’m going to see Sebastian.” “No!” Maria exclaimed, standing up from her seat. “Don’t you dare go near Sebastian. You know the court ruled that you cannot be anywhere near us. So leave us alone.” With that she hung up and took one some tissue paper from the desk to dry her eyes. She hoped that Miguel wasn’t serious about going to see Sebastian. If he was, there was going to be more at stake than just the violations of the court rulings. III Sebastian Moraco lived in the residential area of Manhattan just south of Central Park. A few blocks from his home was the only park for miles, with the closest being the actual Central Park to the north. It was always filled with pick up games in hockey, basketball, touch football, and soccer. Everyday Sebastian would walk by there on his way from school and get involved with a game or two. This time, however, he walked through the park, but didn’t have any plans to get involved with a game. There were other things on his mind. “Yo Sebastian…we need another player!” Sebastian turned to see Junior, a neighborhood friend of his, calling him from the playground. There were a couple of men there all playing around with a soccer ball. They were about to play a pick up game of soccer, but needed one more player. Sebastian reluctantly walked through the opening of the fence. “I don’t think I want to play,” Sebastian explained. Junior nodded. “Come on. It’s going to be a quick game. We all know you can play, so you know it’s going to be quick.” “Nah…I’m just not feeling up to it,” Sebastian replied. Junior saw a glimpse of a bruise peeking out of Sebastian’s collar. “That looks like a pretty bad bruise, bro.” He pointed out. “Oh…um…” Sebastian stammered, “It’s really nothing. I got hit by a truck while I was trying to save the new Pope.” Junior laughed. “Don’t lie about it. Some of us know what you do.” “What would that be?” Sebastian asked, wondering how much they knew. “That you fight for Prime Time Central,” Junior answered. “It’s all right, though. Maybe you can put in a good word for me.” “I don’t think so,” Sebastian replied, shaking his head. “I’m not involved like that.” “Then let’s do this: if your team wins, you stay quiet. If we win, you talk.” Junior offered. “Whatever.” Suddenly, Sebastian felt like playing a pick up game of soccer. The one constant about Sebastian was that he was addicted to competition, no matter what the sport was. It was one of the main reasons why he sought to compete in Prime Time Central’s many events. It was also the reason he was looking forward to compete in Saturday’s match. In a span of a week, that match had evolved from a great possible one on one fight, to a four way of tremendous talent. At least that is how Michael Sloan had explained it to Sebastian. Sebastian was already aware of how good a fighter the Uber Judge was. Though his methods were a bit unorthodox, he got the job done. He was unsure of the abilities of both Clinton Sage and Rich Rollins, but was warned not to underestimate two who had every talent available to win the match outright. The game was set for match point at five. It went on without incident as Sebastian’s team moved up to 4-2 on the score board needing one more to win the game. However, the score had been held the same for over twenty minutes. In the last dangerous effort by Sebastian’s team, the ball bounced off the post and Junior kicked it far ahead – out of the park and onto the street. Sebastian trotted towards the ball. Sebastian looked up ahead to see that the ball had been stopped on the other sidewalk by a man whom had perched on leg on it as if he had conquered Everest. The man didn’t seem to make any effort to pick up the ball, much less offer it to Sebastian. Sebastian used his hand as a visor to keep the ferocious sunlight from his eyes. Under the sun’s shadow, the man’s appearance was hard to identify. Sebastian could see that he wore raggedly clothes. The man’s shirt was stained with dirt; his black pants were wrinkled, leading down to a pair of shoes that had been worn out years ago. Sebastian couldn’t see the man’s face, but saw that his skin was dark with areas that seemed darker than others. At first glance, they seemed like burn marks but were actually just dirt marks. “Can I get that back please?” Sebastian asked, remembering to keep his manners. The lean man did not move or talk. After a brief moment of awkward silence, the man stepped off the ball and picked it up with one hand. “Thanks a lot, sir,” Sebastian said. Yet, the man did not give the ball back. Instead he just tossed it aside. “Porque juegas con tonterias, hijo?” Miguel asked his son, wondering why he was always wasting his time. Sebastian was taken aback. He stepped onto the sidewalk and the sun was no longer bothering his eyes. He stared at the man who had dared call himself father to him; the man whom had neglected his entire family. “What the hell are you doing here?” Sebastian asked. “I wanted to see my son.” Miguel answered, attempting to put his hand on Sebastian’s shoulder. Sebastian tossed the hand aside and shook his head. “What makes you think that I want to see you?” “Because I’m your damn father…” Sebastian stepped back. “No…You lost that privilege a long time ago, Miguel. I’ll make this simple for you. Just go away and I won’t have you arrested.” Miguel hiccupped and in his first attempt to walk towards Sebastian stumbled. He was obviously drunk, having been drinking the day away. Miguel Moraco was never much of a drinker, but since he lost the court rulings and custody of the children, it was the only thing he did. “Don’t do this, Sebastian. You have to idea how hard it is not to see your family.” Miguel pleaded him with. Then it hit Sebastian. Sebastian finally realized what the exactly was wrong with his mother the day before the Gauntlet Invitational. “What the hell did you do to mom?” Sebastian asked, demanding answers. “Nothing…” Miguel stammered. “She is still my wife. She still has to do her spousal duties.” Sebastian didn’t wait for an explanation for that remark and knocked his father down with a punch that would have even knocked out his round six opponents much more an elderly man. Sebastian stared down at the man he had knocked down. In that brief moment, a glimpse of his life flashed before his eyes. Sebastian stood there, shaken up, trying to sort through a million of questions that were rampaging through his mind. Fortunately for him, Junior and the others who had seen the incident held him back. “Sebastian, don’t even go there man,” Junior said, dragging Sebastian back across the street. Sebastian pushed himself off. “I’m fine, man. I’m fine.” “Hey, you going to finish the game or what?” another player cried out. “No,” Sebastian answered and picked up his bag. IV Maria arrived home from work and went straight to her room. She was trying to find the phone number for her lawyer, in an attempt to get her relocated or any other answer for Miguel’s continued violations. She simple tossed her purse on the bed and began to frantically search her shelves for that piece of information. Maria heard the door open, and then heard it slammed shut pretty hard. She raced towards the living room just in time to see Sebastian toss his bags on the couch. e you ok?” she asked. Sebastian didn’t answer. “What happened?” Maria asked him, walking towards him. Sebastian didn’t answer. “Did something happen?” Maria asked him again, hugging him. Finally, Sebastian spoke. “Why didn’t you tell me that Miguel was here?” he whispered into her ear. Maria stepped back. “How did you…” “So it’s true?” Sebastian asked. “What the hell did he do?” Maria shook his head. “Don’t worry about it, Sebastian. I’m going to call the lawyer and have him arrested.” “Enough with that bullshit,” Sebastian cried out. “You’re always downplaying everything he does. Stop that!” “I…I…” Sebastian shook his head. “I’m going out.” “Wait a minute…” Maria stammered. “Where are you going?” Sebastian opened the door half way and turned back. “There’s just something I have to do.” “Don’t do it, Sebastian,” Maria pleaded. “Tell me you’re not going to do anything you’ll regret.” “I won’t regret it.” With that said, Sebastian walked out of the apartment. V Michael Sloan grudgingly got up from his couch. The knocking on his door had turned to banging and kicking. He opened up the door to see Sebastian invite himself in. “What the hell are you doing here?” Michael asked, rubbing his eyes. His clothes were all wrinkled from having fallen asleep on the couch. “I just want a favor from you.” Sebastian answered. “If this is about round six for the Infinite Gauntlet, you got plenty of time,” Michael said, turning off the TV. “No,” Sebastian explained. “I’m not worried about Rich Rollins, Clinton Sage, and certainly not the Uber Judge. This is more important than them.” “All right, what do you want?” Sebastian took a deep breath, trying to decide if this is what he wanted. It was. “I want to know…” Sebastian began, “what are your policies on offing someone?”
