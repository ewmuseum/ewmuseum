Part 3A: Know When To Walk Away...

2 December 2009

Date: 2009-12-02 They say that no man is an island. He can build walls. He can destroy the land that surrounds him. But the connections run deep, and one day he will be confronted by others' humanity. Or worse, his own. # # # It took a lot to make Wyatt Connors feel uncomfortable. It took even more to make him look uncomfortable. Unless, of course, you were at the Wildcard in downtown Chicago. Then, all it took is walking through the door. Upon the moment of entry, Wyatt scanned the crowd and unconsciously scratched his arm. The bar was at a little more than half capacity; not quite enough people to cover a quiet escape. "I should go," he muttered. Deacon Dale rolled his eyes. "We just got here. Christ, are we going to go through this every time?" They had made several appearances in the Chicago bar scene in recent weeks, and it had been roughly the same experience every time. Trevor chose the bar--usually seedy, but not enough to prompt a hipster takeover. Once there, they had a few beers--well, Deacon had a few beers. Ratigan had a few too many, and Connors might finish one. Then, somewhere around 11:30, T.J. would start making an ass of himself, and Deacon had to make sure Connors didn't bolt for the door in the confusion. Tonight's locale was no different. The Wildcard was a pub with a half-assed casino theme; a few more neon lights than other places, playing-card coasters, and a large table with green felt in the center of the room. There was a small group at that main table, and a few small groups standing by the bar or the stage. "Where should we--" Deacon started to ask, but cut himself off as Wyatt immediately headed for the unoccupied table in the back corner. "Of course," he muttered, and followed suit. Connors rested his overcoat across the back of a rickety wooden chair, checked the seat for anything that might stain, and carefully sat down. He reached up to straighten his tie, but found he wasn't wearing one. T.J. had been adamant about that, stating that people only wear ties to the bar if they plan on hanging themselves in the bathroom. "Isn't Trevor here yet?" Wyatt asked. "Ain't seen him, heard him, or smelled him," Deacon answered as he lowered himself into the chair on the opposite side of the table. He peeled off his brown leather jacket and dumped it into the empty chair. "I'm gonna grab our beers. The usual?" Wyatt nodded, and turned to inspect the crowd as Deacon left for the bar. Occasionally, his gaze would settle on a face, and he would instantly start judging-- measuring --them. Spending his parents' money. Spending money he doesn't have. Married--and that's not his wife. Gay, and afraid to admit it. Beats his kids. That guy's falling off the wagon tonight. And so on down the line, for every man in the bar. Which is not to say he wasn't doing the same for the women; he was. There just wasn't any variety in his conclusions. With every woman, he thought the same thing. Before long, Deacon had returned with two bottles of beer. He took a long drink from a Coors Light, and set the Guinness--still capped--in front of Wyatt. "Next round's on you." Connors nodded and reached into his pocket, but frowned after a moment of rummaging. "Damn. Must have left the bottle opener behind." "Wouldn't be a problem if you let the bartenders open it for you. They can do that, you know." Wyatt shook his head and answered, "Not a chance. Way too easy to slip something in while--" "We've been over this," Deacon groused. "Jesus, man. Can you not relax for one fucking second?" "Not if I want to stay alive." As Wyatt looked for alternative means for opening his beer, someone approached their table. He was of about average height, and in fairly good shape. His light brown hair was cut short and spiked up in that sort of "00's Douchebag" look, and his goatee was neatly trimmed. His clothes were plain but new--black longsleeve shirt, blue jeans, white Nike sneakers. In fact, if he hadn't opened his mouth... "About time you fags showed up!" ...they might never have guessed that it was Trevor Ratigan. The other two looked at him, succumbing to a sense of cognitive dissonance. A man they had known for years, who had never once tried to change his general aura of shabbiness--who was, at times, proud of it--now stood before them, attempting to look like a respectable human being. Well, "respectable" might have been a bit strong, but still. "Nice hair, Teej," Deacon said, barely able to contain his laughter. "Don't wanna hear it." "You gonna join Rascal Flatts now?" "Rasc--you fuck off," Ratigan fired back, his face turning slightly red with both anger and embarrassment. Deacon burst out laughing, and even Wyatt let out a chuckle or two. "Yeah, yeah, laugh it up. Look, I hate to interrupt your little make-out session, but the party's goin' on at the big table." "I'm fine here," Wyatt told him, suddenly less amused by the situation. "There's girls over there, bossman. Women. Humans with lady-parts. I'm sure it's a lot of fun sitting in the corner and talking about your knitting projects to fatty here--" "Hey!" "--even he has to admit, he'd rather talk to girls." Deacon shrugged. "Clay Aiken's got a point." "Hey!" "Go ahead, then," Wyatt told them. "I'll be fine." "No, no. You're not getting off the hook that easy," Trevor answered, and grabbed his boss by the arm. "If you wanted to sit in the corner, you should have stayed at the office." "I wanted to stay--" But of course, Trevor was having none of it. "Goddammit, it's not going to kill you to enjoy yourself once in a while. C'mon Deke, help me with this load." Not wanting a chance to put his boss on the spot, Deacon stood up and took hold of Wyatt's other arm. Together, the two flunkies dragged Connors toward the large table in the middle of the room. There, they were greeted by three young women, all of whom were at least reasonably attractive, and probably had low self-esteem (judging by the fact that they had been talking with Trevor Ratigan.) As soon as they arrived, T.J. shouted, "Good news, ladies. When you're done fighting over me, the ones left out will still have some lovely parting gifts." The girls tittered as Trevor began the introductions. "Fellas, this is Mandy, Candy, and Andi. Ladies, this is Deacon and Wyatt." As Ratigan sat down, he leaned in close to the one he'd identified as 'Andi.' "And just so you know...Wyatt here is rich, and he can..." He looked around in an exaggerated manner, then whispered something in Andi's ear. Her face turned bright red, while Ratigan leaned away and said, "Without using his hands." "What are you doing?" Wyatt muttered behind an obviously fake smile. "Trying to get you laid, man. I figure you're in medical need of getting your dick wet." There was a round of handshakes, and some slightly more formal introductions. "I'm Melissa, not Andi." "Jen." "Actually, my name really is Candy." Then, the men sat down. Wyatt ended up being next to Andi--Melissa, rather. She had shoulder-length brown hair, brown eyes, and a slight tint of yellow to her teeth. She wore a tight blue top and white mini-skirt, and filled them out rather well--definitely pretty, and could probably start fistfights if she dropped a few pounds. That was how others saw her, anyway. Wyatt saw her like this. Impressive work on the makeup. Hardly notice the padding. Smoker's teeth are a nice touch. If I'm anyone else, I never see it coming. "For fuck's sake!" Ratigan shouted. "Is your beer still capped? The fuck is the matter with you?" All eyes at the table turned towards Connors, putting him on edge. "I don't suppose one of you has a bottle opener." "See, if you drank a real beer, you wouldn't have this problem." To emphasize his point, Ratigan picked up his bottle of Pabst Blue Ribbon. "Not all of us like to drink things that taste like stale urine, Trevor." "Drink a lot of stale urine, do ya?" Ratigan quipped, and took a long pull from the bottle. Wyatt rolled his eyes, and snatched T.J.'s lighter from the table. He wrapped his thumb and forefinger around the cap of the bottle, then wedged the lighter into the space between his fingers and the bottle cap. With a quick motion, he used the lighter as a lever, prying off the cap and sending it clattering onto the table. He set the bottle onto a conveniently placed coaster, and tossed the lighter back to Ratigan. "You better not have fucked up my lighter doing that," Trevor whined, ignoring the fact that he could get one just like it at a gas station for a dollar. "That was a neat trick," Melissa cooed. "Where'd you learn that?" Wyatt cast a suspicious eye at her. "Just something I picked up." His eyes darted around the table, looking for possible assistance or escape routes. He found none of the former; Deacon was smiling awkwardly at Jen, while Trevor was engaged in heavy conversation with Candy's breasts. Escape routes were also a non-starter, thanks to Wyatt's own refusal to sit with his back to the door. "So, what do you do for a living, Wyatt?" she asked. "As if you don't know," he muttered. Really. She doesn't think I see through it? I'm insulted. "What?" "Don't play innocent, Alex. I'm onto you." "...What?" Confusion reigned on the girl's face, but Wyatt was not fooled. "I'm a little disappointed in you, Alex. I didn't think you'd be this foolhardy; not with the evidence in my possession. I might expect this from Drusilla, but not you." "I have no idea what you're talking about." Melissa slid her chair back a few inches, trying to put some kind of distance between her and the ranting Connors. "Admit it; I've figured you out again. So unless you plan to do something in a very public place, in front of all these witnesses, I suggest you pack up your poisons and try again tomorrow." Melissa's face went white, and she stood up. With a quick word to her friends, she headed immediately for the bathroom. Jen and Candy followed closely behind. "So I said to him, 'If she's your girl, then why is she holding my--wait, where are you going?" T.J. stood up, as did Deacon--both oblivious to what had just happened. "Just doing some girl stuff, cutie," Candy responded. "We'll be right back." Ratigan sat back down, somewhat despondent. "That was my best story, too." He leaned back, had another drink, and only then noticed Wyatt Connors, looking around nervously. "Are they gone? Can we go now? We should go." T.J. narrowed his eyes at Wyatt. "What did you do?" "Saved your life, most likely," Connors answered sharply. "I can't believe you were stupid enough to sit us at a table with Alexandra Pierce." "Whoa whoa whoa what now?" "It was a good disguise; I'll give you that. But it was her." Ratigan tried to answer, but the words had obviously escaped him. For a moment, he just sat there with his mouth open, while pointing an accusatory finger at Connors. "That Melissa girl?" Deacon asked, only slightly less bewildered than his associate. "There's no way. They don't even look the same." "Disguise, Deacon. Did I not just mention that?" T.J.--having finally put his brain back together--piped in, "She's good enough at it to make her face look that much bigger? What'd she do, fill her cheeks with acorns?" "You'd be surprised," Wyatt said, although Ratigan's comment did give him some pause. Now that he thought about it, there were a few details that nagged at him. The face was too big...and, come to think of it, the ears were too small. Eyes were the wrong shape, and the way she carried herself...she seemed confident, but not overtly so. Alex could do that, but usually chose one extreme or the other--depending on the target, she would either be bold or timid, figuring most men would respond favorably. Faced with his mistake, Connors refused to back away from it. "Doesn't mean anything. She could have been one of the other two." "You son of a bitch," Trevor groaned. "Did I just get cockblocked by your fucking paranoia?" Wyatt tugged at the collar of his gray dress shirt and stared at his beer. Trevor grumbled a few curse words and headed for the bar, leaving Deacon behind to ask the questions. "Did you seriously believe that woman was Alexandra Pierce?" Connors tried to meet his lackey's gaze, but was appalled by the mix of puzzlement and pity on Deacon Dale's face. In the end, he could only nod...but he still wasn't admitting the whole truth. How could he? They'd never understand. He believed every woman was Alexandra Pierce. T.J. Ratigan returned with a few fresh beers and even more fresh complaints. "We're never taking you anywhere again." Deacon tried to keep the bad situation from becoming worse. "Calm down, T.J. Maybe the other two will come back." "You wish. I'll tell you exactly what's happening. They're in that bathroom, talking about us. Candy's gushing over me, Mandy--" "Jen." "--is talking about how you're probably gay, but she bets she can save you. And then the other one's gonna talk about how bugfuck crazy he"Perhaps if you stopped thinking with your penis for a moment, you'd see things a little more clearly." "Would you fucking listen to yourself?!" Trevor shouted, loud enough to get a few looks from the other patrons. "I swear, she better have been the best fuck you've ever had. That's the only excuse I'm taking for you being this goddamn crazy. Now, I want you to listen to me. Alex Pierce is not fucking here. Not in the bar, not in the city, not in the fucking state. Right now, as we speak, she's probably engaged in the Olympic qualifier for muff diving. She's doing a reverse double-somersault pike with a half-twist, right into Amy Campbell's muff. It is even hotter than anything you've got in your pictures--which you still won't let me look at, you cocksucker--and is far more important to her than whatever the hell you're doing. So would you please, please pull the stick out of your ass? Just once?" Wyatt scowled, refusing to accept that Ratigan was probably correct. "You don't know that for certain." Trevor's face twisted into a look of extreme displeasure, just before he brought his head down on the table. "I give up," he said, his voice muffled by the table. "I'm done." Connors stared down at his bottle of Guinness. He couldn't have been wrong. Pierce was bound to be here somewhere. Or if not her, one of her underlings. Right? "Deacon, you're the smart one. Tell him I'm not being unreasonable." Deacon coughed nervously, unwilling to speak out against his boss...but that hardly mattered. The words, though unspoken, were as clear as day on the big man's face. "With all due respect, sir...I think you're out of your goddamn mind." Deacon was the first one to notice the bathroom door open, and watched as Candy, Jen, and Melissa walked back into the bar. "Trevor! Girls!" T.J.'s head shot up, just in time to see two of them walk past, headed for the exit. Only Candy stopped long enough to talk. "Hate to do this, sweetie, but we've got to go. Jen's got finals in the morning, and she's our ride. But it was nice meeting you and your...friends." Trevor nodded and smiled pleasantly. As soon as Candy turned her back, Ratigan scowled at Wyatt and mouthed the words, "dead mother." Wyatt Connors turned and saw the other two, hovering by the door. For a moment, he caught Melissa's attention. He thought about saying something, even though he knew it would do no good. In the end, the words stayed in his head. Sorry. I...I thought you were someone else. # # # At the opposite end of the bar, a small woman with large boots was busy tapping at her phone's keyboard. You are not going to believe who's in here tonight.
