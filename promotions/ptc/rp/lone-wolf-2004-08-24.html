Lone Wolf.

24 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-08-24 Survival of the fittest. A dog eat dog mentality in which the biggest, the baddest, the brightest come out on top, thus continuing the evolutionary chain of the best of any species. Based on Charles Darwin's theory, this lends itself to the notion that only the cream of the crop shall survive, eliminating the weak and making the species continue at it's peak. The weak must be sacrificed for the good of the whole. It is their role, their fate, their destiny. Thus, the motto of the lone wolf remains. ***** The screen is black, typical, not exactly foreboding with imagery. The silence is deafening, ironic, allowing the mind to wander. The screen clicks on, only to find nothing but static, the black and white pixelated sprites moving around in some sort of rabid frenzy. The noise associated with it comes a few seconds late, but is halted when the screen turns blue, and we notice a reflection on what must be a 32" television, or perhaps larger. Although the image is a bit altered because of it being a blue reflection, it is easy to note the sheer size and frame of the reflection, as it sits perched on some sort of seating arrangement, pointing towards the tv set. The screen cuts towards a title menu, the same kind that you would see in a DVD's opening, providing options as to what to view. The cursor moves down, hitting the "play" option. The title screen dissipates into nothingness, and the words "Interview, September 7th, 2001" appear in the center. A man, anywhere between 6'0" and 6'2" stands in a room, a black cloth hung behind him. An interview is scheduled, but he refuses to sit in the chair to his left. The interviewer, a short, bald man with circular focals, nestles into his own chair, straightening the papers in his lap. He look at Shawn, whose arm is still in a sling, as Shawn continues to shake his head. "Shawn...it's only been four days since your near fatal accident. Why return to active competition so soon?" he asks, unsure how to approach the then-259 pound specimen. Shawn stands there, shifting his attention from the camera to the interviewer, the black t-shirt he's wearing is wrinkled. "Shawn..." the interviewer continues, reaching towards him, trying to make sure he's still all there. Almost as if challenged, Shawn pulls his arm back, a thick grimace coming across his face as the pain shoots up his right side. "Don't touch me," he says, his deep voice booms with bass from behind his long brown hair, which sits in front of his face. The interviewer draws his hand back, frightened by the response. "I'm sorry, Shawn. Did you hear my..." he begins. "I don't take time off. Injury is no reason to falter along the path that one must travel. This business is how I eat, it's how I provide for myself, it is my passion. Now, you tell me...why shouldn't I come back to the ring?" he says, unapologetic. "Well, and correct me if I'm wrong, your right shoulder is fractured, yet you insist on having a match in the upcoming card. Why?" he questions, making marks on his paper. "This business is cutthroat. There is no such thing as sympathy. You insult me with the very notion that there is. No one has asked me a damn thing about my well-being, and I expect no such questions. This business is based on the principle of kill, or be killed. If I show weakness, whether it be injury or a mis-step, then I stand the chance to be swallowed up. I take no such chances. If you've done your work well, you'd know that I have a military background..." Shawn says with force, before being interrupted. "...I know, the Marines," he intervenes. Shawn stops in mid-sentence, and flips his hair back with his left hand. His eyes, the color of blue that fire reaches in it's center, are cold, almost as if they're looking a hole through the interviewer. "You ever cut me off again, and I'll break your body into segments. Are we understood?" he says such with a certain force, yet his tone remains calm, as if it means nothing to him. The interviewer nods his understanding. "My background has taught me that pity is an emotion of the weak, and to expect such is as bad as displaying it. I use that knowledge, the physical preparation, and that mentality, and it keeps me in a position where I can thrive. Wrestling is not life or death, but if you treat it as such, you can not fail. You ever heard of Charles Darwin?" he continues, still refusing to sit. Again, the interviewer nods, unsure if he was given permission to speak or not. "Of course you have. His theory of survival of the fittest is one that I lend my own beliefs to. In this industry, we are all lions, all predators. We don't prey on other species, we prey on one another. Any one of us who shows weakness must be eliminated, taken out. I refuse to be taken out. Weakness, as well as failure, is unacceptable. Survival is my only option, and it is what I must do," Shawn says, his voice never wavering in volume. The interviewer sits for a moment, taking in what he has been told. "Um, well, I don't know how to ask this question, but what about the other man in the car...The Hunter?" he asks gingerly. Shawn stares at him, his breathing changing, going from normal to heavy. He rips the microphone from his shirt collar, knocking the chair from beside him. He walks off the set, leaving the interviewer by himself. ***** The screen is turned off, and the camera pans back from the large tv set. The room is bare, except for the television, a couch, and Shawn Singleton. All 6'1", 238 pounds of muscle sits in the center of the couch. Veteran of wars, both in the ring and out, a man carved out of stone. You tell him he won't survive. When the chips fall... ...my money's on the lone wolf.
