Heaven & Hell ETD Match Roleplaying

13 May 2006

"The Calm" -- Archibald MacGregor * * * * * With a gust of wind accompanying him, Typherion snapped from the Earthen realm back into his study high atop the carved fortress that overlooked Hell. A moment earlier, he had been in a rural hospital, whispering the rites of death into the ear of an elderly woman. The last time he crossed back onto Earth, the world was still believed to have been flat. The revelation of the MacGregor child disturbed Typherion to such a great extent that he had trouble concentrating. The doublewide doorway in his study, which leads out towards an expansive porch, framed the distant and undulating mountains and meadows of the Great Battlefield. The devil himself might favor death and agony and unrelenting malevolence, but he admired architecture. His palace was carved into the highest mountainside that Hell offered, and his plateau of a porch speared out from the summit of the mountain towards Heaven. It remained the closest that Typherion would ever get to Heaven, as he and his Heavenly counterpart were confined to their palaces while in the after-life. He stared mindlessly at the battle being waged. He thoughts fluttered around like the flaming arrows that his demons launched towards the angels as they defended the realm of Heaven. The MacGregor child bore the mark across his infantile back, and it meant that the prophecy outlined in the poem would come to pass. He had a war to win, The Great War, and suddenly his fate as ruler of the Underworld was thrust into its final stages. It might be a week, or years in Earth time, but sooner than later he was giving up his throne. The prophecy foretold it, after all. The pressure was building tenfold, and had no crack from which to escape. Typherion clenched his jaw in anger, his lips momentarily peeling back to reveal a perfect set of teeth. Had he been in his natural form rather than the current embodiment of a perfect male human, they would have appeared as long, arched, and jagged teeth in multiple rows. The Devil glanced down at the poem, tracing his finger over two lines in particular that alarmed him: Bearing a mark across the infant’s back, Allowing entrance to hell with memories intact. When someone enters Hell, their memories are wiped clean and they know only to obey the devil himself, Typherion. There have only been a handful of souls to enter the realm of Hell with their memories intact, and each one of them has ascended to the throne he now holds. If the prophecy within the poem holds true, the MacGregor child will pass from the Earth realm with every memory he has fully in place. Such is the practice in Heaven, not Hell. Every single one of the angels passing into Heaven are allowed to keep their memories from their days on Earth. Much like how Heaven and Hell are polar opposites, a soul entering Heaven without any memories intact is known to be the successor to the throne of God. Where angels can walk the Earth and interact with it’s people, the demons of Hell can only manipulate objects and observe through other beings. While God can never set foot back atop the terra firma, Typherion can. Where the Devil keeps his memories of the tortured days on Earth, God loses all memories in order to be a fair and unbiased Lord of the Afterlife. Typherion was now aware that his time as Lord of the Underworld was numbered, and he glanced across the battlefield to the Palace of God, wondering if his Heavenly counterpart knew of his inevitable demise. Typherion sneered with displeasure, hoping there was a prophecy foretelling God’s demise as well. It would only be fair. * * * * * Archibald MacGregor got into the drivers seat of their minivan and buckled up, then turned around and looked at his future wife, Heather, as she adjusted the car seat for their newborn son. He could see the crooked frown across Heather’s face as she buckled Aaron into the seat, knowing full well that she would rather be holding their son the entire ride home rather than trusting him to some seat that someone else had built. The thought made Archibald smile, her devotion to their son. But after seeing the horrific accident on the way to the hospital, it was only appropriate that Heather sat beside young Aaron on the way home, rather than holding him. Two days had passed since Aaron was brought into this world. While getting some snacks during Heather’s 2 hour labor with Aaron, Archibald had asked around the hospital about the accident they had witnessed, and perhaps wondered if there were any survivors. He distinctly recalled seeing the minivan, filled to capacity with some random family, tumbling through the air. Chills formed at the base of his neck and toppled all the way down his spine to the small of his back like dominoes when he thought of the van tumbling through the air and noticing every single member of the family staring down at his own van. The hospital had said the impact killed the family, and the occupants of the car that struck it. “Alright babe, lets go home for the first time…as a family.” Heather said, hunching over and kissing Aaron on the forehead. He continued to watch her in the rear-view mirror. Perhaps it was the emotions running high from the birth of their first child, or perhaps it was simply because he hadn’t drank any alcohol in two plus days, but Archibald MacGregor suddenly felt filled with love for the mother of his child. She had been through tough times with him over the course of the last two years. From the break-up on the day that the ghost of Archibald Matthews visited him, to the discovery of her pregnancy, to the death of Archibald’s grandmother 8 months prior…Heather Davis had been there the entire time for him. When his shoulder was ripped to shreds at the hands of Cereal Killer in the Cruiserweight Tournament finals, to the birth of little Aaron…she believed in him. She believed that he could fight his inner demons, and quit drinking. That the hype surrounding his wrestling prowess was built on a solid foundation. And he loved her for that. They decided to wait until after Aaron was born to marry, because since both he and Heather had hardly any family left, they might as well include their firstborn in the procession. In a month’s time, Heather Davis with those green eyes and spiraling blonde hair would become Heather MacGregor, and the family would be complete. The thought alone filled Archibald with so much hope and enthusiasm that his eyes often started to glaze over with tears. As she pushed Aaron’s sandy blonde hair away from his face, which was as soft as the worlds finest silk, Heather looked up at Archibald and made a simple request. “Babe, take the longer way home. Head out past the park in the center of town, I want to see if the trees are in full bloom yet.” “Sweetheart, it’s February 28th. I think the medicine they put you on might have knocked you a little crazy. Well…crazier than you already had been.” Archibald said with a sly smile as he flipped the turn signal and took the turn that would grant Heather’s wish. “It’s warm for this time of year. I’d be willing to bet that I’m right and you’re wrong.” “We’re almost out of gas.” “The park is a half mile from our house,” Heather said, theatrically over-exaggerating the rolling of her eyes, “I think we can make it.” Archibald grinned, and momentarily turned his gaze away from the road and back onto his family, “Fine. We run out of gas, I carry the kid and you push the van.” ”Deal. But I’ll expect you to cook from now on.” Archibald let out a hearty laugh at her comment, knowing damn well she hated his cooking. The rest of the drive through the backcountry of Southeastern Pennsylvania went without conversation, and without incident. As they grew closer to the small town of Oxford, Archibald started to take notice of the trees that grew beside the road, and smirked to himself as he saw small buds of flowers and leaves starting to sprout out from their branches. He half expected to turn the corner and drive beside the small swatch of grass known as Oxford Park, seeing the bronze statue of the man he was named after, Archibald Matthews, covered in the pink petals of a blossoming tree. What he did not expect to see, however, was a statue beside it, standing equally as high and atop a matching white marble base. Where the statue of Archibald Matthews stood, kneeling and peering through binoculars at some unforeseen danger, there was now an accompanying statue next to it, the man cut from bronze was holding out his right hand, pointing at perhaps what the Matthews statue was gazing at. His other hand held a walkie-talkie to his mouth, which was open and looked to be yelling a command to some following troops. Archibald slowly pulled the van to the curb beside the statues, and slowly got out, his mouth agape. He didn’t hear the sliding door of the mini-van open, nor did he feel Heather rest her head on his shoulder, but he certainly heard what she had to say, “I arranged for this to be erected when Aaron was born. It had been completed three months ago, but I wanted it to be installed when you weren’t around.” Archibald continued to stare up at the twin statues in awe, unable to speak. ”I figured…you always talked about how you were named after Archibald Matthews, and how your father named you in his likeness. They were forever separated when Archibald was killed in Vietnam, so I got a little money together and felt like they should be re-united.” It was a perfect likeness. The statue looked exactly how Archibald remembered his father, and oddly, was placed in the exact same spot where Christopher MacGregor had committed suicide many years earlier. But aside from giving birth to his first child, Archibald MacGregor came to the conclusion that this was perhaps the second greatest gift he had ever been given, by anyone. He looked at Heather and tried to form some semblance of thanks towards her, but she just kissed him on the lips and smiled, returning to the van where their newborn son slept silently. Numb and paralyzed with an overjoyed shock, Archibald MacGregor stood perfectly still and took in the moment. As a breeze rustled through the naked branches of the surrounding trees and across the grassy sliver of park, for a split second Archibald thought he heard a few strings of a guitar being strummed. It was soft, but familiar, and out-of-place just enough to rip him from his moment alone with the statues of his father and his namesake. He quickly glanced around, and then came to the conclusion that what he heard was simply in his head. It had come and gone with the soft breeze. Shaking his head and grinning from ear to ear, he returned to the minivan and drove his family home for the first time. * * * * * Not many things shock you once you actually see a ghost, understand that what you are seeing is impossible, but know what is happening is real. So it’s easy to say that the months after the ghost of Archibald Matthews visited me, informing me that my place in Hell was virtually secure, that the strange occurrences wouldn’t have surprised me, but they sure as hell did. At first, it was the I-pod screaming warnings at me in my late grandmothers house. Then it was the birds circling overhead all the time, never once cawing. Just circling in a predatory fashion, their oily black eyes never once spying on mice, but rather my family. On the day my son was born, it was the van barreling end over end while the supposedly dead family stared out through the spider-webbed and shattered glass of their mangled vehicle. Or the funeral procession watching my family, rather than the loved one they were putting into the ground. So you could say that once Aaron was born, I was ready for the ante to be upped significantly. I expected the worst possible scenarios, and oddly, got absolutely nothing in return. Weeks went by, and there were no more birds hovering overhead. There were no trees growing towards the ground, or ghosts walking the streets of Oxford prepared to tell me that my fate was ultimately in someone else’s hands other than my own. The MacGregor/Davis family settled into a routine, and two months after Aaron’s birth, I was awarded a championship match for my outstanding efforts in the Infinite Gauntlet Open Invitational. Never one to brag about my wrestling (which is vastly superior than my first person narrative, by the way. And my cooking.) I shrugged off the award and debated on never actually entering the match, because of its dangerous nature. Where the normal person, completely unaware of my circumstances dealing with Heaven and Hell, would simply view me as being protective of my career or possibly being afraid of entering a match of such violence, it was actually that I was scared that this would be the time when the Devil decided to reestablish his presence in my life, and send my world on a topsy-turvy decent that would ultimately lead me to a face-to-face confrontation with him. But Heather thought otherwise, always the optimistic soul. She told me that this was an offer that nobody had ever been given before, and because of my talents, I should take this chance and try to seize it. Usually these shots are only awarded to those members of the wrestling community who are active on a regular basis, and not a spotty “shows up when he needs money” wrestler like myself. Well, I needed to support the cost of diapers and bottles (of the baby variety, non-alcoholic of course) so I took my loving fiancé’s word and entered into the match. Passion is a powerful thing. I have a huge passion for wrestling, but my passion towards my family comes first. And it seems that both Heaven and Hell have a passion for winning the war to earn not the right of just my soul, but the soul of my newborn son as well. Those quiet months leading up to my Extreme Title match, looking back as I write these passages, I should have known that they were quiet for a reason. Some people always say that the anticipation of an act is always worse than the act itself, like going to the dentist or getting a broken nose set back into place. Well I can say this: the quiet lull before the storm is always peaceful for a reason. It’s a reminder of just how perfect and serene life can be, because it will suddenly be ripped away from you in the blink of an eye. Never again will I be able to hear the melancholy sound of guitar strings gently being strummed without the accompanying agony of reliving the pained screams of that afternoon in May, or feel the searing pain of the scars forever etched across my palms. * * * * * The first-person excerpt from "100 Proof Life" by Archibald MacGregor was printed with permission from his estate.
