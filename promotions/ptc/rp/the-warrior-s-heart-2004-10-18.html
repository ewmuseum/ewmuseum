The Warrior's Heart

18 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-18 I woke still feeling the handle of my sword, the treaded leather grip seeming to mold itself against my palm; at one together. It was shortly after I realized I was not holding a sword that I realized I couldn’t truly be awake either… I didn’t have that dried sticky taste in my mouth, and my head didn’t hurt as it did after one of those ‘visions’ as I’ve dubbed them. And along with the absences of pain and discomfort there was a lack of, well, anything. There was darkness, everywhere and all around me. There could have been a floor, I was standing on something even though I couldn’t see it. At first I assumed it to be dark, but there they were, my own hands. I could see the boxers around my waist and my bare feet, realized then I was nearly naked, but I wasn’t cold. I didn’t feel naked. Then, just as I was feeling that I must be about to wake up, that this was some sort of limbo between a dream state and full awareness, it turned on. One of those bright white lights in a hooded lamp, like you’d see over a smoky pokers table or in an interrogation room. It swung side to side with an ominous accent, taunting me. Beneath that baleful glare was a table and a pair of chairs, for a moment they seemed unreal, and by that I mean featureless. Generic brand, not the normal curves or grooves you’d see in a real set. But that feeling passed smoothly, and without hesitation I started walking towards it… but it didn’t get any closer. My legs were moving but I wasn’t, and I got this horrible feeling that time was somehow running out, that I was doomed to stay in that spot forever. I remember looking at my feet and begging them to work, and as I looked again to the light I was looking at Emperor Riley. He smiled. We were seated across from each other at the table, the light still swayed from one side to the other endlessly though. On the surface between us was a board, a chess board to be more exact. All the regular pieces were accounted for, lined up perfectly in their timeless formation, waiting to be directed into war. But then again, all the pieces were the same, there weren’t the bishops and rooks, they were all… me. At least they looked like me, on Riley’s side I was portrayed as a warrior, my sword gripped in both hands and my body suited for combat. But on my side I was dressed as I always dress, right down to the nike sneakers and rolex watch. I asked Riley how we were supposed to play with the pieces like that, but he must not have heard me because he went right ahead and moved his first piece, not a word uttered. Perhaps it wasn’t chess we were supposed to play, now that I think of it, because he didn’t move his piece as you would move a pawn. At the time I didn’t notice this though, and I went ahead and moved my first piece, and then he moved his and I moved mine. I didn’t question my choices and neither did he, and when he tipped my first piece over in death I didn’t see it as wrong. But I see it as wrong now. Now it curls my stomach in knots and makes my heart pound against my ribs, looking at the board brought on anxiety. Not the anxiety like I get before a match, like I got before climbing into the ring with Riley for real. That flashing moment where you ask yourself if this is the last one, if this is the match that’s going to end the run. Because it’s coming for all of us, it’s coming for Riley and it’s coming for me, it could be years down the road or it could be this week against Wolfenden. Riley moves his piece and I release a silent moan of protest as another of my Lexicon’s perishes from combat and fades into nothing. Seven of his warriors remained, their carved blades prepared and ready against my two remaining. The warrior against the wrestler, the person… The warrior that is me, the animal that had savagely slaughtered Equinox, the warrior that had forsaken humanity for the sake of glory. Against me… The person who had to live with that animal inside of him, the one chosen to cage a dangerous beast next to my family, my daughter… “Johnny…” What? Did Riley just speak? “Johnny…” He’s staring at the board, waiting for my turn, and then he’s gone. Fading into a headache and a gross, sticky taste in my mouth. “Johnny wake up already!” Blinking the world into reality; a groan is the only response I’m able to give, even if I told me to say something more I wouldn’t listen. I just wanna roll over again and dream about my glorious conquests, be alone with my warrior’s soul. “Johnny!” Claire is more insistent. Leaping into the bed she plants both of her diminutive hands into Johnny’s arm and gives him a shove, pig tails bouncing about wildly. “I’m up.” Johnny says groggily and rolls onto his back. His room is dimly lit by streams of muffled light sneaking in through the curtains, little white things floating lazily along the warm beams. “Hurry up! Grandma made pancakes then we’re going to church.” “I don’t wanna go to church…” Johnny moans as Claire hops off the bed. Turning to look at her father with mock severity she plants her little fists into her hips. “If I have to go! You have to go!” She waves a menacing finger at him, barely holding her smirk in. With a rough chuckle Johnny pulls the blankets aside and swings his legs out. Settling his bare feet on the soft carpet as Claire makes her way from the room and disappears. Palms find themselves rubbing at tired eyes as he smacks his lips and tries to rid that horrible taste from his tongue. Standing up it’s only a few steps until he’s reaching inside a dark bathroom, clicking the light into life and closing the door. I sometimes wonder what it is I’m looking at in the mirror, if that’s really me looking back. Is that Johnny Lexicon, the warrior, the World Champion, or is that Johnny Lexicon the dad, the friend and son. Is it the person who takes a little girl to play mini golf, or the person willing to twist an ankle until it breaks. Johnny goes through his entire routine, brushing his teeth and washing his face, taking a piss and changing from his boxers into a pair of blue track pants. Pulling a sleeveless shirt just a shade lighter of blue than his pants he heads from his room, feeding his arms into the slots and then stretching the fabric over his head and shoulders. The cotton settles across his broad chest comfortably and he descends the stairs from the second level. Walking into the kitchen he takes a wiff of the air and grins. “Smells good.” “Morning.” His mother says cheerfully. Claire looks up with a half smile and continues to munch on her breakfast. Pulling a chair out Johnny settles into it and rubs his hands together, as if on cue a plate full of pancakes moves in front of his face. “Oh yeah, thanks mum.” He gives her the obligatory kiss on the cheek “You’re coming to church with us right? Pastor Dave has missed you.” “Yeah about that…” Johnny begins. With eyes on his pancakes, on the syrup he’d just poured, at the way the light reflected it. That shaded lamp hanging from the ceiling, swaying side to side gently in the cool breeze from outside. Watching the way it reflects on the syrup and butter, looks like checkers, the kind one would see on a chess board. “About that what hun?” His mother asks, the sounds of frying eggs making her think she’d missed something. I could tell her that I really need to train, that this tournament wasn’t ‘just another match’ as she would say. That I had a real shot in this thing, that I could be crowned the best. The biggest goal of my career realized, and perhaps after I could maybe, just maybe, relax a little from the wrestling game. Do some travelling where I wasn’t preparing to get my head bashed in. “Nothing.” “So you’re coming?” “Of course, it’s church!” He says. Claire smiles and gives him a wink, Johnny winks back and gives her the ‘Dad’ smile. Of course I couldn’t tell the two of them any of the above, I was the parent and the provider. It was my role here, my resources and energy are expected to be boundless, my compassion without flaw. It was one of several masks I wear without complaint, here I do what I must as a father, and out there I do what I must as a wrestler. I am expected to defend my title as if my very life depended on it, I’m expected to walk into a tournament against a person I’ve never even heard of until last Friday and perform on par with my damnable reputation. These are the things I am expected to do without fail, and fail I do not, will not. But still… Tis a delicate balance between a warrior… and his heart.
