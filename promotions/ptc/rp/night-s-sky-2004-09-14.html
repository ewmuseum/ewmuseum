Night's Sky

14 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-14 (The pure black nights sky, perhaps one of the most beautiful sights in nature. With millions of twinkling stars burning like pin pricks in black satin, the sky is a sight that has enthralled men and women for millennia. Men have seen all manner of shapes and images in the night’s sky, to the point that travellers use it for comfort, grown men seeking solace in the shapes that their minds form. How apt is it now then; that the night’s sky is attempting to ease the mind of a man. A man who needs some serious solace.) “I don’t see it.” (He has been laying here for the past two hours, making a permanent dent in the hood of his rented Olds as he stares into the sky trying to find the shape of the Great Bear that he has been assured is there, and will help clear his mind, but to this point, no calm has been forthcoming.) “My eyes are open, but I do not see.” (Curse, failure, truth, three words that have been going through this mans head for the past few months, words that don’t mean anything as individual expressions of language, but as a whole come together to mean the possible end of a short yet glittering career. Begun two years ago, the rising star of this man reached the very pinnacle of the sport, and yet now, here he is, lying depressed on the hood of a beaten up car, his eyes staring straight up.) “What have I become, what happened to the man I was, the man I grew to be?” (Each time there is a flicker in the sky, his eyes dart to meet it, but it is never anything more than a brief explosion from one of the stars, a light that would have begun its journey thousands of years ago. Each spark could trigger the answer in his brain, the answer to the questions he is asking himself, but instead he is just left with more disappointment.) “Mother Earth, can you not help me? Surely I have been one of your most faithful sons, why don’t you answer me?” (His eyes shut tight, his lips pursed tight together. His fists clenched together and they slam down on the hood, denting the admittedly thin metal.) “Goddamnit, what the hell am I supposed to do about this.” (Rolling off the hood, the man crouches on the floor next to his car and rests his head on the door.) “It’s not like I actually have anything that could be classed as talent for pities sake, all I seem to be able to do these days is lose.” (The voice that joins his is quiet, yet forceful.) “Why say that? I don’t see much to pity right now.” (Turning to face the origin of this second voice, our man in question turns from a look of self loathing to a look of disgust.) “Go away, NOW.” (Out from the shadows steps a woman dressed in an outfit that could only be described as business gothic. A smart black suit is topped off with a leather collar and pale makeup.) “I really don’t think you want me to do that, yes?” (To understand quite what the dynamic is between these two people, you have to look at their pasts and you have to learn just who they are. The man we have been pitying is the man known as Kash, a former WWA champion and a man with a large cult following, one which is a total mystery to the man himself. The woman is someone who has been in and out of the picture for a while. Her name is Victoria and she is an integral part of the coven known as the Thirteen that Kash is a member of. She is the mother figure, the ear to talk to, the shoulder to cry on and the gentle touch that is needed at certain times. However, recently, the connection between Kash and the members of his coven have been strained.) “I don’t know what I want, leave me alone.” (You can almost hear the self loathing in his voice, the sound of a man destroyed internally.) “Why would I want to talk to you of all people, Madam Victoria?” (A smile crosses the beautiful black lips of Victoria, and she lets out a small murmur of laughter.) “It’s quite simple really, you have a match coming up, and I just thought I would let you know.” (Rolling his head to look at her, Kash gets a quizzical and slightly arrogant look his face.) “The WWA has seen fit to give me a leave of absence, why would I have a match?” “Because, oh wise one, when you gave me managerial powers all those moons ago, I retained the ability to sign a match for you, so I entered you in the GTT over at PTC.” (Anger blooms.) “You ... did ... WHAT?!” (Clenching his fist, Kash raises to his full six foot four frame and stalks over to Victoria.) “You bit... you booked me in a match? What the hell were you thinking?” “Simple, you need to get back in the game and this was the easiest way.” (Wheeling away and walking to the car, Kash simply and plainly punches his hand through the passenger side window.) “Well, that was worth while you big idiot.” (With a smirk, Kash has no choice but to agree.) “I know, but you have no idea what you have done. Maybe a couple of years ago I could have matched someone in that ring, but not now, not after all that I have been through, I am spent, and you know that.” (Walking over to her friend and ally, Victoria places her hand on his shoulder.) “The only person who believes that is you, I know I don’t, and the rest of the guys don’t either.” (Kash falls to the ground and rests against the passenger door, sitting in the broken glass of the window.) “It’s amazing to think that nearly two years ago, I had never held a WWA title, and then I won the US belt, what a buzz that was, and then to go on and win the WWA title, I didn’t think that life could get much better, but then, I started to fade away. Sure, I won back the title from Shaman, but at what cost, where along the line did I leave my soul, my heart? Right now I couldn’t win a match against a training bag, let alone anyone with talent.” “You beat Tracer Bullet, yes?” “Only by luck, he had an off night, nothing he could do about it, no way that’s going to happen against ... who am I fighting?” “A guy named Gonzalo Rey, a free agent.” “No way will it happen against him, I will go the same way I went against Shaman, against Damian, straight down.” “Without going down, you cannot get back up.” “It doesn’t work that way, my WWA career is over, you should realise that by now, I am all washed up, as for this match, and I don’t see why I should even bother.” (Victoria takes Kash’ face in her hands and looks into his eyes.) “You bother because you are Kash, and that’s all you need to know.” (Kash closes his eyes and turns his head away, only for it to be buried in the neck of Victoria who holds his head tight.) “You are Kash; you just don’t believe it anymore.” (As we leave them, one thought comes up. When does a man who had such a glittering talent lose it all? When does a man who once had the world at his feet end up with the whole world on his shoulders crushing him? Kash does not believe in what he can do, but everyone else seems to think so. Time will be our judge, but in the mind of the Wicca Man, he has already lost.)
