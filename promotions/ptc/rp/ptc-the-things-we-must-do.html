The Things We Must Do--->A Back Alley Brawler Noir Final Notice. Payment of $20,000 due before August 5th, 2005 at 7:00, or the property resting on 2020 Memorial Avenue will be seized by the state of New York. Payment is due immediately or vacate all belongings within the premises. Sincerely, Jack Rucker. Everyone has had those moments. The type where you bite your tongue, swallow the acidic words aching to be expelled from your mouth, and do the task needed to survive. Office workers slump into their cushy chairs and slave away on their keyboards, finishing some 10-page report that will just decorate the office of some upper management member. Janitors bite their lip when some inconsiderate prick spills a drink all over their pristinely cleaned floor, said prick leaving the mess to be cleaned up with little respect for the work put into the glistening linoleum. Even a stripper has to deal with the wandering hands of a drunk patron who refuses to respect the unspoken rules of a gentleman’s club. In the midst of the violation, the stripper is surprised to find that she can be treated lesser then her job already demands. Why do these three do it? Because human instinct is to survive. In America, survival is dependent in most cases on how much money lines your wallet. No longer does the proud, muscular lion rule the Serengeti. Far from it. The meek toucan can easily run it with a large enough bank account. Which leads us to an aged lion on his last legs, still prowling the Serengeti, but lacking the strength and majesty of his wonder years. This lion from the Bronx is Joe Balboa, otherwise known as the Back Alley Brawler. Mere months ago, by some chance in God, the down and out “never-was” managed to main event a Jolt pay-per-view against Carson Nash, easily becoming the highlight of his 20+ year career of mediocrity and light counting. He survived the odds and proved he still had it, that something was left in the “ol’ Brawler”. That was to be the end. He would walk out on top and settle down to his meager existence in his native Bronx, able to look back on that moment and say he had made it to the top of the mountain. Hell, the publicity would pull a few more students into his training school…. …But how could he train them if there was no training school. “FUCK! I cain’t believe I gotta do dis. I…it wasn’t s’pposed to be like….GOTDAMMIT~!” Joe knew he missed a few payments, but it’s not like the Jolt paychecks were rolling in anymore. Besides, what company really wants to invest in a 40 year-old who PRIDES himself on his lack of moves? In a workrate dominated wrestling scene, Balboa was a dinosaur that should have drowned in some tar pit decades ago. It became easier to agree with the fact that he was allowed to keep living just to piss off the masses out there. Somebody had to do it. And speaking of doing something, it was what he’d have to do tonight that had the Bronx barbarian in such an uproar. Sprawled out on his dingy mattress, lacking the extras like sheets and a spread, the Brawler had taken the liberty of guzzling down 1/3 of Ripple. Nothing drowned out sorrow like cheap liquor. Rubbing on his ill-kept stubble, he looked over to his right and stared aimlessly at the phone lying next to him. The phone looked to be the only thing in its proper place as lamps, magazines, photos, and furniture in his meager bedroom had been thrown around in a drunken fit earlier. A bottle of Jack and a hard day do that to a man. Damned if it rang and damned if it didn’t. If it did not ring, there would be no money for the Block. It’d belong to New York. His beloved training school would face the wrecking ball, all for the greater glory of the ten-millionth Starbucks in New York, as if there were not enough already. Any attachment he had to the sport would be erased with the signature of Jack Rucker and a wrecking ball. If the phone did ring, it would be a return to the bad days. To the days when Balboa would have to take hour long showers, trying to get all the blood off of his 300+ pound, 6’7 frame. The nightmares would continue all over…. The nightmares of his life before his daughter. Before wrestling. Before he had a soul. BRRRRIIING~! BRRRRIIING~! The phone finally rang and he wasted no time answering. Not even a second ring emitted from the phone and the receiver was to the Brawler’s ear in no time. The man on the other end was not the type to be kept waiting 2 rings. That man was Troy DeNucci, the most vicious “boss” the Bronx had seen in the last 30 years. Still a rolling stone after those 3 decades, none had yet to surpass the mean streak and ruthless aggression which DeNucci had mastered. Just hearing that hoarse voice, DeNucci’s throat sounding like it swallowed a straight razor, brought back memories when the Brawler would break fingers and backs so he could make child support every month. Sadly, the bills had to be paid, and the money could not come any faster unless it was from the boss himself. “Joe, it’s been awhile buddy. Saw dat pay-per-view ya had wit dat Nash fella. New York was proud of ya, but I guess da gate wasn’t enough to keep ya school up, eh? I told you…you’d be back soona or lata. Wrestlin’ was neva your callin kid…now breakin necks, God. You’re like a dike’s tongue in wet pussy, just meant for it ya feel?” Talking to DeNucci was like talking to that successful ex-girlfriend/ex-wife you broke up with a few years back. They always manage to catch you at your lowest and they can never just outright insult you. It has to be that sly, condescending manner so they can savor every moment of your weakness, but seem eminent at the same time. And all you can really do is take the force fuck and smile. In this case, the force fuck from DeNucci meant keeping his school. If he had nothing to offer in the ring, the school was his only attachment to it…losing that would mean death, not that he never thought of it before at his age. Knocking back a swig of his now half-emptied Ripple to sooth his nerves, the Brawler took to a tone he only used with his daughter, Olivia. He respectfully replied to his grim savior. “Thanks for callin, Boss. Jus’ tell me what I gotta’ do, I gotta get dat paper by tomorrow mornin’.” “I got one question for ya, Joe.” “And dat’d be?” “You still got it in ya ta kill? If you can’t do dat, don’t waste my time on this one.” The Bronx Barbarian had hurt a lot of people. Enough to encircle the Earth three, maybe four times. Killing on the other hand, only enough to hold up 3 stubby digits on the wrecking balls he dubbed “hands”. He had his share of nightmares about those three and tonight, if he took this job, it would be four. This lead to the important question, biting his thumbnail as he contemplated it with the patience of a scholar, a rarity for such a savage man. The question? “Was wrestling worth killing for?” What else did he really have? Wrestling, even on the independent “get paid 10 bucks to work a high school gym” level, always managed to sooth the raging tumor that was his life. Even with nothing to show for it, his 20+ years in the business were certainly better then his bitter divorce and his tenure as a shylark. Wrestling was Valhalla compared to those moments. It had been a long time since a life had perished under his hand, but tonight, if it meant saving the school he had worked so hard to erect. Someone would just have to be a dead motherfucker. A sip of liquid courage and his reply turned cold, gruff. Joe Balboa had crept into a hole to hide and the Back Alley Brawler was in full effect. “You tell me where and who. Tomorrow mornin’ you bring da headstone and your choice uv’ flowers ready. I’ll bring da corpse.” “I always did like you Joe. Everytime I know we’re gonna talk I always cut da heater on…you’re just such a chilly muhfucka and I’m not up for frostbite. Hehe.” He clenched the phone with his neck, keeping it tight to his ear as his instructions were delegated with surgical precision. The bottle of Ripple was down and his fingerless gloves were being slipped on, the Brawler never being a fan of skinned knuckles. There was no question that blood would be spilled tonight. It was all a matter of how much. As long as the check cleared tomorrow morning, he’d find a way to sleep. Later That Night….. “Well, it looks like da broads are out ta’night.” He surveyed the area around him, his lungs taking in the cigarette-tarnished air of “Patty’s”, a Bronx institution since the 40’s and still responsible for sending husbands home drunk. His lungs and his senses chose not to rebuke the foul odor, but instead embrace it, the scent taking him back to days when his temper was a raging flame with no hope of putting it out. The Brawler would need that motivation again to get the job done tonight. As mentioned earlier, Troy DeNucci was quite the ruthless man. Even worse then his ruthless aggression, was his jealousy. It had been a few weeks ago that DeNucci discovered that his wife, 20 years his junior, had been having an affair with a man named Charlie. It was no secret that DeNucci’s relationship with his wife, no matter how vile the man himself, was sacred to him and anyone with the guts to ruin that would pay with their life. Joe was here to collect on that promise. DeNucci did his homework too, using any and all informants he had to get information on Charlie’s whereabouts. It was up to Joe to seal the deal by matching a name to a face….then the rest was up to fate and if he could plunge the butterfly knife in his back pocket in the right place. In his younger days, the Brawler would simply walk up to the bartender and ask for his prey. Once they were identified, he would come up with some smooth way to buy them a drink. The brash New Yorker would be your friend for a solid two hours while discussing family, religion, the latest game, the whole nine yards. Then it would come time to pay the tab. Which was Brawler code for “beat the shit out of him now.” Wash, rinse, repeat. However, age and lack of patience lead the Brawler to a new way of tracking his prey. Looking about the seedy bar as the patrons enjoyed various beverages on classic wooden stools and high bar tables, the Brawler was able to bring all the interplay to a hush with his dominant tone. The alpha male had made himself apparent. “I’m not getting paid by da hour. Fact is, I work for DeNucci and I’m sure ALL OF YOUS know he ain’t to be fucked wit. Now the sooner I find dis cat named Charlie, da sooner ya’ll can get back to drinkin’. Charlie, show yaself dammit! I ain’t got all night!” The hush continued as now eyes slowly laid themselves upon a booth in the back of the bar, covered in a light mist of cigarette smoke, much different then the Marlboro’s and Newports in the air. No words needed to be spoken; the stares in one uniform direction were enough. He took deliberate steps towards the back, cracking his knuckles in anticipation for the beating he was prepared to lay down. He had images of who this Charlie might be. Maybe some suave fucker from around the Manhattan area, who liked to get a little dirty in the Bronx. Probably the type you’d see on the cover of one of those grocery store romance novels or “written orgasms” as the Brawler liked to call them. Maybe the artist type or a novelist, it’s always those types that fuck “the boss’ wife.” Point is, Charlie crossed the line and now it was time to feel the wrath. That whole bar or the Four Horsemen, biblical or wrestling-wise, would be unable to stop the onslaught that was to come. Anything for the paper. Then, the smoke cleared just enough for the Brawler’s mental image to be shattered on the rocks of reality. DeNucci’s wife was a dike. The Brawler knew this, because Charlie was a woman. A very beautiful woman at that. All the Brawler could do was curse to himself mentally, saddened that a woman of such beauty preferred to bury her face in carpet. She calmly held her place, by no means fazed by the imposing sight of the Brawler as she continued to smoke her cigarette. Perplexed by the matter, the Brawler took a seat across from her, doing his best to keep his eyes on her and not her voluptuous body. Joe could understand how DeNucci’s wife chose to cheat with a specimen this perfect. “Surprised,” she beckoned in a soft manner. It was women like her that made smoking look appealing. The full inhales and exhales she would take, the way her lips softly nestled her cigarette, even the careful touch when she removed it from her mouth so she could speak. She was quite a piece of work to say the least. “Lets just say I’ve seen a lot…yet I’m still amazed. I think ya kno’ why I’m hea.” She smiled, surprise seemingly a foreign emotion to her as her eyes gazed off to the poorly lit ceiling lights, prison to dead flies and roaches. She then looked back over to the Brawler before placing her cigarette back between her cotton candy pink lips. “Yes, I know. I’ve known someone would be coming sooner or later. I’ve made peace with my decision for some time now. I got what I wanted.” The Brawler looked on, putting his original task to the side for a moment so as to delve further into this subject. Intriguing was not even the word for the prey across from him as curiosity gripped the Brawler about Charlie. “And before I do DeNucci the service of making you a stain on this bar wall, why don’t’cha tell ol’ Joe what ya wanted? I’d offa ya a cigarette but I’m afraid I can’t offa ya none of that insen….insen….DAT FRESH-SMELLIN SHIT yous smokin’. Excuse da fuck outta me, my big words ain’t so good. Charlie smiled, amused by the Brawler as she leaned in to continue the conversation. Joe was expecting some sob story to explain how justified she was in her actions. Actions The Brawler wish she had on tape for those lonely Friday nights if you catch my drift. Instead, he was the Catholic priest and she was the confessing sinner…hoping for forgiveness before he impending fate. “DeNucci offed my parents 18 years ago. I’m 20 Mr….I didn’t catch your name…” “Joe. Most call me Brawler.” “Brawler. I like that. Well, me being 20, my parents died when I was two Mr. Brawler. Over a loan my dad took out with DeNucci…a loan he was two weeks late on. As I’d guess you know, DeNucci is the HARBOR of patience and virtue. So he killed both my parents and I was adopted by a whorehouse he jointly ran. As soon as I was old enough, he wanted me turning tricks. I would be the return on his lost investment. I notice he likes that. When you do business with DeNucci, he always ends up owning some part of you…I’m sure you know this.” Joe knew it too well. It wasn’t just wrestling that cost him his wife and daughter, it was the shylarking and drug-peddling to put food on the table that did in his family as well. The streets were all Joe knew, nobody wanted him for a 9 to 5. But his wife wanted more for her child and her husband then being slaves to the mob…so she had the guts to do what the Brawler was unable to. Leave. “Ya got a point lil lady. A good one at dat, so why are ya telling me dis?” “Because, I figure you would get a laugh about the end of my story. Besides, what good is a master plan if you have no one to gloat about it to?” If Brawler were 20 years younger, he was staring ex-wife #6 in the brown eyes. “Perhaps I could buy you a drink, Mr. Brawler?” The Brawler politely shook his head with a chuckle. Digging into his beaten up coat he unearthed the unfinished bottle of Ripple from earlier in our story. Grabbing it about the bottleneck, the Brawler placed his focus back on the siren across from him. “I bring mah own lil lady.” He managed another hearty laugh from Charlie as she leaned back in her chair, playfully recollecting her story in her head as the aged goliath that was the Brawler took another swing from his drink of choice. Her cigarette burning slowly in one hand and playing with her raven locks in the other, she continued. “I had no real childhood, Mr. Brawler. My child hood was fucking, sucking, and making sure my pimp had his money. My parents were fortunate enough to be killed and have it left at that. I’ve been tortured by the life of whoring for years upon years…over a petty loan.” She paused, her voice rising to slight anger. A drag of her cigarette calmed that flaring nerve. “Dick to me was kind of like having the same dinner for 10 years straight. You get sick of it, loathe it, never want to see it again. So, I got curious and I found out I just like women. We know what we want and in most cases, we bend to a man’s needs, never the other way around. I had my first taste and never looked back….and I got to wondering, with all the rumors that go around, how satisfied was DeNucci’s wife with the old Boss? He never struck me as the type who knew how to satisfy a woman.” This woman was beautiful and perhaps, more ruthless than the Boss himself. The Brawler had a good idea where this story was going, but he was too enthralled by Charlie to cut her off. She could read a recipe for a cake and make it interesting. “It was all a matter of timing, Mr. Brawler. I met her at the right club on the right day. I said all the right things. Before you know it, she started screaming and crying things that would make a Tourettes victim blush. I knew after that fourth orgasm that I had her…I had DeNucci’s most prized possession and made her mine. He can kill me, but she’ll always remember that night with me….and it’s going to kill him when he tries to make love to her and she screams MY name.” Brilliant. She made herself a martyr. This night and this woman were just full of surprises. “And that’s the story. I suppose I should be asking you if a girl gets a last request?” “Anything you want Charlie.” “Don’t become a slave to DeNucci. I can tell you’re an alright guy in a fucked up situation. Do what it takes to get out of it.” The Brawler nodded, unable to argue with the valid point she had made. Charlie definitely gave him a different perspective on the way his life worked. She smiled, putting out her cigarette knowing exactly what was to take place. “I guess you have to do what you must?” “I do. It was nice meetin ya Charlie…you’re a hell of a woman. I’m sure his broad enjoyed every last minute.” “She did.” Silence overtook them both, this moment something spiritual despite their lack of knowledge about each other. It would be a moment she would remember for the rest of her short life, and a moment that would echo forever in his. “Goodbye, Mr. Brawler.” “Nice meetin’ ya, Charlie.” She leaned in and placed the softest kiss on his forehead, one only eclipsed by the kisses his wife would give him before he fell asleep in her arms. FLLLPT~! The silencer worked like clockwork and she slumped down to the table without even so much a thud. Nobody would know she was dead until clean up later tonight. Hopefully the check would clear by then. There are things we want to do, then things we must. Tonight was a must. We can’t all be Charlie.
