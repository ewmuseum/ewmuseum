IG - Jayce Bradley (GCW)

11 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Charcoal clouds inch their way across the drab mist-gray skyline, as dew kissed blades of grass dance gingerly to a low whistle carried by the slight breeze of the early morning hours of the day. Beyond the weathered iron pickets of the old graveyard gates, and past the gently sloped field, the stumps of ancient trees stand at attention in the misty shadows of the lake. It doesn't appear as if the day is going to be gentle for the mourners that will assemble to say goodbye to some unfortunate soul that has recently lost his life. Jayce Bradley stands alone next to row after row of light blue folding chairs, his back to them as he focuses on the headstone next to a freshly dug grave. The mound of dirt that has been dug up is conveniently covered by a sheet of astroturf, but he still had no problem finding the grave of a man he knew he would never forget. He had been such an intricate part of his life, and yet he regrets ever meeting him in the first place. ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ The last few weeks had been so empty, and he had often times thought about taking his own life. All of his parents attention had been pulled away, focused entirely on a son they lost instead of the one that still remained. What is the point in being here, he often asked himself late at night as he listened to the mournful weeping coming from his parent's bedroom. It was as if he didn't exist. That was until he had met Russell Braun. An older gentleman with a kind and caring face, his features were soft and inviting, the perfect mask to cover the face of a bloodthirsty murderer; although that wasn't something Jayce would realize for quite some time. He had approached Jayce one day after school had let out. Damnit, there's that creepy guy again, he thought to himself as he pulled his jacket taut over his shoulders. He had seen the older man before, he often stood across the street from the school and watched as the children exited. For the last few days the man had begun to follow Jayce as he walked home, though he never got close enough for Jayce to get a good look at him. He turned down an alley, because he knew if something happened that he could run through it blindfolded and not miss a beat. He just never expected a garbage truck to be parked at the exit. When he turned around, he had come face to face with the man that had been shadowing him for the last week. “Hello,” the man said in a chipper tone. “I don't know who you are old man, but I'm not gay.” The man replied with a hearty guffaw, and shook his head while he wagged a finger in front of Jayce's face. “I didn't mean to scare you, kid. I'm not looking to hurt you in any way.” Jayce stood ready for anything, though he had yet to figure out how he would get around the man if the need arose. “I heard what happened to your brother, and I offer my condolences.” He seemed genuine, and his words were ones he had yet to hear from anyone else. “I know what it's like to have someone you love taken from you because of the violence of the streets. I know about others just like you, and I think you need to meet people just like yourself, son. People that have gone through what you're going through, and can help you get through this rough time.” “I don't think my parents would let me,” Jayce replied. “Ever since the murder, they haven't really let me go anywhere.” “So they watch you constantly now?” “No, they usually just make me stay in my room.” A mischievous grin curled onto the corners of Braun's lips. “So they would never know you were gone, would they?” Jayce shook his head, and that was all the answer Russell needed. A new member of his army had been recruited. ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ The occasional passing of a car or truck on the Interstate breaks up the grave silence that fills the air, as a dew filled breeze carries the soft conversation of two men dressed in denim coveralls to his ears. He watches as they lounge around a backhoe covered in mud, going through cigarette after cigarette like they were going out of style. The wind begins to pick up slightly, a few gusts of wind slapping him on the forehead like he had just sucked down a large amount of ice cream. He thinks back to something his brother had told him. How sometimes their football practices would run late into the night, and the darkness was so cold they would jam their hands down into their pants to warm them up, fondling their sacks just enough to not get an erection. It made him laugh, because his brother always said they would still scream at the coach to put them back on the field, even though the gusts of wind snatched at their skin with razor sharp fingers, because it was even colder if they just stood there and let the chill freeze the perspiration that had soaked their jerseys. The cemetery was deserted for the most part, the family of the deceased still cuddling beneath the warm hug of their blankets. Though something manages to grab his attention, and he turns to find no one or nothing there, just a large tree. My mind must be playing tricks on me, he thinks to himself as he shakes his head. Stress again, he reasons as he opens his eyes. Regardless, he walks among the dead, heading toward the tree and thinking that even if there is no one there, the walk will do him good. He navigates his way past the graves of children, that like his brother, had been taken too soon. He walks past the headstones of soldiers who gave their lives for wars they never knew the true meaning of. Sorrow seems to float in the air, each burial plot holding it's own story of pain and loss, the headstones the final punctuation mark of the story, a finalization of memories faded away as those that could remember also passed away. When he reaches the tree, he only finds a few cans of beer, piles of cigarette butts, and a few dried up condoms. The place in the center of death, was certainly full of life. The sound of rocks being crumpled beneath tires draws his attention to the graveyard's entrance, where a white four door Ford pulls in and parks behind Jayce's two door rental. He could easily identify Mario Trellis as the man behind the wheel, and a quick wave grabbed Mario's attention as well. ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ Mario Trellis had been working late, the fan on the edge of his desk caused a low rhythmic hum to tickle the inside of his ears; he had to do everything in his power to keep from falling asleep. Being the owner of the Anywhere But Your Back Gym, Mario had to make sure everyone was paying their fees, that way he could pay the bills and keep the doors open to anyone and everyone that needed his help. He enjoyed training men to become champions, but he never expected what awaited him. “Excuse me.” He heard someone call to him from behind, and as he glanced over his shoulder and found a man he had never met standing in the doorway to his office, he replied with a puzzled look on his face. “I'm sorry, we're closed for the night.” “I know,” the man replied, “and I'm sorry to bother you. It's just that I need some help, and I've been pointed in your direction.” “Well,” Mario replied, “I'd be more than happy to help you out if you came during normal business hours.” He tried to be as courteous as possible, but how the guy had even managed to get into the gym was a question he was afraid to ask. “I...uhm...” he stumbled on his words a bit. “I don't have anywhere else to go.” Mario felt no remorse for the kid, but there was something about him that he couldn't turn away. “I don't usually offer room and board, and I'm kind of busy at the moment. If you-” “Just hear me out, sir,” the man interrupted. “All I want is a few minutes of your time, and then I'll leave it up to you on whether or not you want me to come back.” Those words, along with everything else that had already happened since the man had spoken, grabbed his interest by the throat and pressed it up against a wall. “Okay, I'm listening.” “I don't know where to begin,” the young man said nervously, almost as if he still had much he did not want to reveal. “How about your name?” “Jayce,” the man said under his breath. “Jayce Bradley.” ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ Mario slams the door of his car shut, then waves as he makes his way over toward Jayce. “Thanks for coming,” Jayce says, walking up to meet the man halfway. “It was an unusual request,” Mario looks at him sideways. “But I already told you I wasn't going to turn you away.” “I know,” Jayce replies with his head staring curiously at his feet. “I just figured you'd still be pissed about me ignoring you and going to wrestle on WorldWide after you told me not to.” “Your decisions are yours to make, Jayce,” Mario says with a reassuring grin. “I'm just here to make sure you don't make any grave mistakes. He looks around, chuckling softly as he remembers where they are. “No pun intended.” “I know that,” Jayce laughs. “I just didn't think you'd help me after I yelled at you the way I did.” “You were pissed because you thought I was trying to hold you back. I just wanted to make sure you weren't going to get hurt. I saw the show, and you didn't seem to get up off the floor as quickly as you would have if you weren't hurt.” “Yeah, Shaman really worked me over.” “Good to see you got a bit of payback though.” “Yeah, but at what cost?” Mario nods, as the two continue to walk toward the lake. “That isn't why you called me to meet you here though, is it?” Jayce shook his head. “No... I've been having some weird dreams lately, and he's in them.” “Braun,” Mario asks, as his face contorts awkwardly. “Yeah.” “But he's dead, Jayce,” Mario says with a slightly agitated scratch in his throat. “I know, but it just seemed so real.” “You need to let it go, Jayce.” Mario stops walking, turning to face Jayce, who doesn't do the same. Instead, Jayce starts kicking at the grass like a five year old playing center field in a tee-ball game. “You can't let that bastard control your life from the grave, it's not healthy.” “I've tried to explain it to you, Mario.” Jayce never lifts his head when he speaks, he just keeps staring at the blades of grass that get trampled by the sole of his boot. “But even I don't know how he does it. It's like he's always around, watching me.” “You were there when he shot himself, Jayce. He died in your arms.” “Did he,” Jayce asks in a high pitched squeal. “He was carried away with his heart still beating... How do I know he really died? “Is that why we're here?” “Not right now,” Jayce says with a shake of his head. “Braun is buried here, or at least that's what I was told. So I asked you to meet me here because I want to know if I can trust you.” “I told you that the night we met, Jayce.” “Good,” Jayce smiles, as he finally lifts his head and turns to Mario. “I need to bury something with Braun, something that I knew I couldn't put in his casket with the cops watching.” Jayce pulls a large military knife from inside his coat, and Mario's eyes grow wide. “Is that,” Mario hardly knows what to say. “Yes, this is what we used to kill all of those people.” Jayce stares at the knife, carefully pulling it from it's sheath to get a good look at himself in the mirror finish of the hand polished blade. “I need to bury it in order to bury my past completely. Then I can stop looking over my shoulder and focus on the future.” “Is this what's been troubling you?” Jayce nods, the lump in his throat shifting as he swallows. “I just hope Braun's really in there when we dig him up.” “He has to be.” “Mario,” Jayce puts the blade back beneath his coat. “There's one other thing I'm going to need you to help me with.” “What's that?” “You ever hear of the Infinite Gauntlet?”
