He had GTT6 to think about. Fuck it. An empty bottle of rum lay in shards next to Jason on his bathroom floor. Beneath him, maybe. He couldn’t feel anything anyway. Another bottle was in his hand, half empty, and spilling out wastefully every time he brought it to his lips. He lay there with his head propped up against the cabinet beneath the sink, slipping in and out of oblivion. There was vomit on the floor next to him. There was vomit on his shirt - in his hair. His body had started rejecting the alcohol sometime after those first forty ounces, but it wasn’t enough to deter him from starting a second bottle. Now every drink was followed by a gag and a heave. There was nothing left in his stomach to come up, save for most of the drink itself. He’d lost all sense of time. Alyssa was just there. An hour ago? Two, maybe? He couldn’t be sure. All he knew was that she triggered an avalanche of Raven, and it had all been too much to bear. "You know you’re killing yourself, don’t you?" Familiar high pitched tones. Painfully, he turned his eyes and found his wife sitting next to him on the floor, somehow unsoiled by the mess he’d made. "You’re not Raven," he slurred. "Depends on what you want me to be, I guess," she replied with a shrug - Raven’s shrug. He shut his eyes tight. "Why won’t you just leave me alone?" he asked desperately. "I can’t keep doing this. It hurts so fucking much!" "Don’t swear," his wife scolded him. "As for leaving you alone, I don’t think that’s really possible. I’m you, after all." When he opened his eyes again, he was no longer in his bathroom. He wasn’t sure where he was, really. He could still see his bathroom clearly, but his consciousness had dipped somewhere into the drunken chaos of his mind. That’s where the image of Raven was, after all, and sometimes, even the hint of her seemed better than the vast emptiness she left in her wake. "Raven’s gone," he said; or maybe he didn’t. "You’re just me." "Yup," Raven’s voice from Raven’s lips. But it wasn’t Raven. His head was spinning, his eyes were hurting, but he couldn’t stop staring. She looked every bit as beautiful as she had in life, even if she was only a figment of his imagination. Of course it wasn’t actually Raven! If Raven could see what he had become, she would never visit him! She was better than that… better than him. "You don’t know what it’s like," he went on anyway. The longing was so torturous that perhaps he was clinging to this artificial version of her. "Every day… It’s supposed to get better… it’s supposed to get easier. Three years! Three fucking years!" She didn’t tell him not to swear. He closed his eyes again and shook his head - an artificial version of her, as it turned out, only made the longing worse. "My handsome man," he heard her say, some bitch-demon from the bowels of his imagination that was hi-jacking his wife’s voice. He could feel (actually feel!) her hand on his arm, pushing the fine hairs to one side as if styling the hair of a child. Her hands were so soft… so fucking soft… Raven used to do this when they were teenagers, the same as he could feel this drunken illusion doing it now. "You’re killing me," he was on the verge of tears, or perhaps he was crying already. His entire body shook save for his clenched teeth. "You’re going to fucking kill me." "My handsome man," she repeated. He didn’t open his eyes. He wouldn’t open his eyes! "My handsome man…" He was shaking in his pile of glass and vomit, his eyes clenched as tight as his jaw - a drunken hallucination of his dead wife stroking his arm. And all he could think was: How did I get here? "My handsome boy." What? He forced his eyes open and turned them on his imagination. It was no longer Raven. Instead, his hallucination was in her middle thirties, with blond hair the same color as his own, save for a little more honey. She had blue eyes like his too, but the similarity stopped with the color. Her face was kind, and he’d always resented that, because he knew that her kindness was only on the surface. He could not say why she was here. She was his mother, or at least, the image of her. "What are you doing here?" "You know you’re killing yourself, don’t you?" Her words cut him far worse than the glass that he couldn’t feel in his back, not for what they said, but because they mirrored Raven’s. "You’re one to talk," he spat at her before defiantly taking another drink. It was no surprised when the majority of his effort ended up coughed up on his chest. "What? Are we back to you being fifteen again?" His mother asked. "Trying to shock me?" "Fuck!" he cursed long and from his gut, and then he brought his hand to his face. Was this what insanity felt like? "Stop being so dramatic!" His mother snapped. "You know, I told that girl when you were sixteen that you had too much of your father in you - that someday, she’d end up just like me, sitting in an empty house waiting for you while you were in prison. I told her that." "She told me," he gave her a hateful grin. "Well, of course she told you! How else would I be telling you now?" She rolled her eyes. She was so real. "Well, it’s not like you really did all that much waiting." "That tongue of yours," she shook her head. "What was I supposed to do? Sit around the house and stare out the fucking window? Waiting for my Romeo to come back to me so we could live happily ever after? Honey, there is no happily ever after, and your father was no Romeo." "I wouldn’t know," Jason replied. He had last seen his father when he was five - Haley’s age. "And you’re better off for it," she replied. "Believe me, whatever problems you had with the people in my life, none of them were anything compared to that son of a bitch." He’d heard her say these words a hundred times growing up; a thousand. She looked so like his mother that there were moments he forgot that she wasn’t - that she was an illusion from hell, or at least, the hell in the back of his head. He had to remind himself of that, thinking that his mother would be near to fifty years old now, where this image was still young. He remembered her this way from when he was a teenager - he remembered her younger still from the days of his childhood. He could even remember his father - the way he walked… the way he moved. While his mother’s skin was softer than her interior, his father’s skin was like stone. He had no idea about his father’s interior. "Where are you now?" he didn’t know why he asked and yet there was desperation in his voice. "How should I know?" she shrugged, and suddenly there was a cigarette between her fingers, burning down slow. He could smell the smoke. He could feel its heat. "I’d come see you, maybe," he said in a soft, cracking voice. "I’d bring Haley. Raven always wanted to." "You’re lucky you had me though," she ignored him, blowing a cloud of smoke into the air of oblivion. "If you’re this much of a mess now, God only knows what you’d be if not for me." "You’re not real," he sobbed. "Well duh!" She rolled her eyes again - the bitch. Have I finally gone too far? Is this what dying feels like? Things got less tangible from there, and Jason lost himself somewhere between consciousness and chaos; sanity and insanity. He slumped downward, letting the bottle roll gently out of his hand and across the floor until it finally stopped dead against the bathtub. Emily Jamie’s hand brushed Emily’s for the third time that day as they strolled side-by-side along the white picket fence of Jason’s house. She supposed that she should start thinking of it as her house now too, and it was even starting to feel that way. It gave her a creative outlet; trying to make the house pretty again was a lot of fun, and she even thought Jason appreciated it. She was getting used to reading him, as Danielle had. It was a pretty necessary skill; there were times that he kept himself closed up like a book. Haley was ahead of them a few paces, her face still painted like a kitten from her day at the local October Fair. She’s so small, Emily thought with a smile. It was a good day - a fun day. After picking Haley up from her daycare at the school, Jamie was the one that suggested the fair. That gave Emily a strange tightness in her stomach - a good tightness. She doubted Jason would mind them going. In all likelihood, he wouldn’t even be home yet. As they approached the house, Emily found herself continually glancing at Jamie out of the corner of her eye. He was tall - probably as tall as Jason, but much thinner. His hair was golden brown and worn down to his eyebrows. He needed a haircut, but it wasn’t really her place to tell him that. What is my place? She asked herself. For weeks, she and Jamie had been getting closer and closer, now to the point that she probably considered him her best friend. But there was something more than that - something in the natural way he accompanied her on walks, and the way he’d save her place in line at the cafeteria at school. She was comfortable with him. "Your brother’s home," Jamie pointed out. She hadn’t yet gotten around to telling him about Raven - he just knew that Haley was her niece, so he assumed Jason must be her brother. Well, that wasn’t exactly true… there had been that first day when she actually lied to him and said Jason was her brother, rather than her brother-in-law… She supposed she had to tell him now, before they met, so she stopped at the door. "Haley, go upstairs and unpack your bag, okay? I’ll be up in a minute." Emily nudged her toward the door. When Haley was gone, she turned to face Jamie. "About that… he’s not actually my brother…" "He’s not your husband is he?" Jamie had a quick wit and she rewarded him with a playful punch in the arm, but then her face went serious again. "He’s actually my brother-in-law," she told him, her eyes darting from the interior of the house to his. "I didn’t know you had a sister. Is she home?" "No," Emily shook her head. "She…" "Left?" "Died." "Oh." They shared a very long silent moment; neither of them knew what to say until finally Jamie spoke up. "I’m sorry." He could sound so sensitive sometimes. "I didn’t know." "It’s okay," Emily forced half a smile as she looked up at him. "It was a long time ago. Three years ago." "So she was Haley’s mom?" "There are pictures in the livingroom, if you want to see her," Emily wondered if it might be appropriate to take him by the hand inside, but in the end, she settled for leading him in without contact. "In here." Together, they went into the livingroom where the pictures were. Haley’s kindergarten artwork was mingled among them now, something that Emily thought cheered the place up a bit, even if sometimes it was hard to look at. They showed Raven in happier times - easier times. Jamie inspected several of them thoughtfully, giving a smirk at a few - most notably the shots that depicted her comedically pregnant. "Is this when she was having Haley?" Emily peeked over his shoulder. "No, that was… um…" she looked away. "She was pregnant when she died." "Oh." He turned back to the photographs, letting his eyes linger on each before moving to the next. He said something then that truly surprised her. "You look a lot like her." "No I don’t," Emily giggled. She moved in for a closer look at Raven’s wedding photo; her sister in a modest white gown as befit her nature, standing next to her new young husband. She was only a few years older than I am in this, she thought. "She was beautiful." "I know," Jamie replied without taking his eyes off the pictures - so casually… as if the words weren’t going to punch Emily right in her fluttering stomach. Boys could be so weird sometimes. And then he only got weirder: "Oh my God! Is that your brother-in-law!?" "Yeah." "He looks exactly like Jason Snow!" "I know," Emily shrugged. "That’s him." Jamie froze for a moment, and then his eyes darted from Emily to the photographs. He leaned in close to inspect one of Jason and Raven at a family function a few years back, right before Jason went to prison. Jamie looked back over his shoulder at Emily with saucer eyes. "You’re saying your brother-in-law is Jason Snow!? The Jason Snow!? The guy that just showed up in PRIME!? The guy that threw his hat into GTT-fucking-6!" "You shouldn’t swear," Emily furrowed her brow, and then found herself under a hundred of Raven’s smiling eyes. She didn’t know why she said it, and now she felt embarrassed. "What?" "Lets go check in on Haley," Emily changed the subject. "Jason’s probably asleep, but if he wakes up, you can meet him." "You’re fucking kidding me!" Jamie’s eyes went saucer again, and even though Emily didn’t say anything to correct him, she had never been very good at disguising her emotions with her face. "I mean - you’re kidding me. Are you sure it’s the same guy?" "Well, that’s his name and he’s on T.V. a lot," shrugged Emily. Together, they went up the stairs toward the second level of the house where the bedrooms were. It was also where the second bathroom was, and Emily noticed the door was open and the light was on. She didn’t think much of it, so when she opened the door to turn the light off as saw what was inside, she was horrified. Jason was there, lying on the floor in a pile of broken glass. Someone must have broken into the house and attacked him! "Oh my God," she stammered to herself. That’s when she saw the vomit - when she smelled the vomit. Above all of the other fowl things mingled with the scent, the most prevailent was alcohol. It was on the floor, his clothes… My God, it’s even in his hair! His eyes were open, but only half, rolled up, and fluttering - like an open door to an empty house. His right hand was shaking on the floor beside him, more than a little, beyond his control. His mouth was wide open. There was an open liquor bottle next to the bathtub, nearly empty and sitting in a pool of its own contents. He’s drunk, she realized, though she had never seen anyone in this state before. She was suddenly very aware of Jamie’s presence, behind her and a little to the left, and the situation became embarrassing. "Maybe you should go," she said with tears in her eyes. Why am I always crying!? Jamie was frozen behind her, staring at the mess that remained of Jason Snow, a man he obviously had idolized at one point in time. She could only imagine how white her face was, a chaotic cocktail of embarrassment, anger, and worry. "Do you think we should call an ambulance or something?" he asked quietly. "I’ll handle it," she replied. "But-…" "I’ll handle it!" Her voice was firm; her eyes were hard. Jamie looked from her to the scene in the bathroom, and then reluctantly went back downstairs toward the door in the kitchen. Emily turned her attention to Jason - or, what was left of him. How did you let yourself come to this? She wasn’t sure if she pitied him or hated him. Both, perhaps. She probably should have been terrified. His eyes would open from time to time and he seemed to look at her, but then he would sink back into whatever state he was in. His hands shook all the while, the right one especially, bleeding at the knuckles from scraping against shards of glass on the floor. "Get up," she heard herself say in a more commanding tone than she thought she possessed. He turned his head to her, and finally, she saw a hint consciousness in his eyes. He babbled something that she couldn’t understand. "Get up!" She repeated, even more harsh this time. "Get up now!" The words came out slow - demanding. "Emily…" his voice trailed off and then he turned his head so not to face her. "Get up before your daughter sees you like this," she told him, drawing a knife with the words. He responded by looking over at her again; there was something so desperate in his eyes. "If you don’t get up I’m calling an ambulance." "No," he managed to say, and then pushed himself into a seated position. It took far more effort than it should have, and when he leaned forward she could see blood stains on the back of his t-shirt. "What are you doing to yourself, Jason?" There was no empathy in her words - only heat. He didn’t respond. He just stared up at her with those blue eyes of his. They usually looked like they were about to burn people with laser beams, but right now, they looked like big empty oceans. Carefully, Emily stepped into the bathroom, trying her best to avoid the glass and puke. She closed the door behind her, in case Haley happened to wander by, and then grabbed Jason by the arm. It was funny how people never looked as heavy as they really were. Emily pulled with all her might, but all she could do was pull his arm away from his body. What now? Instinct took over. She turned the shower on, cold, and directed the shower head outside of the bathtub so it completely soaked him - that got a response. He jumped as soon as the water hit his face and began trying pathetically to scramble to his feet, but all he did was slip and fall. He was so graceful in the ring, at times looking like an acrobat, but here he was like a newborn calf learning to walk. Finally, supporting his weight on the sink, he stood up. "You’re disgusting!" "I know…" He held his head low and turned the water on to soak the puke out of his hair. "What are you trying to do anyway!?" She couldn’t remember being this angry with anyone in her life. Perhaps, in truth, she really was terrified, and not that angry at all. "Haley could have walked in here! Wouldn’t that have been a nice memory for her!?" He didn’t say anything. He didn’t have to. She could see the self-loathing on every inch of his face. "You’re bleeding," she pointed out. The back of his soiled white t-shirt was stained in red-black blood from the glass. He instinctively pulled it off and inspected the cuts. Luckily, no glass seemed to be embedded in the skin. "Alright," he said, turning back to the sink and wetting his hair again. "I’ll clean this up." "You can barely stand up," she snapped at him. "Just… just go to bed. I’ll clean it." "That’s ok," he said, and that only made her angrier. "Jason!" She felt her teary eyes harden and her mouth purse, and she glared at him harder than he’d ever glared at anyone in the ring. "I said I’ll clean it." For a moment, he only stared at her with some sort of soft recognition in his eyes. He nodded reluctantly, letting his gaze linger for a moment in submission. He didn’t seem so tough at all now. She couldn’t be sure if she felt sorry for him or if she just hated him. For now, she just wanted him out of her sight. He turned and carefully started making his way to the bedroom, where he would confine himself for hours. Emily spent those hours cleaning the bathroom, and luckily, Haley spent most of the time in her room, looking at the her painted face in the mirror and drawing cats in the backs of her coloring books. When she was finished with the bathroom, Emily set herself onto another project in the kitchen. She took each and every liquor bottle from the refrigerator, emptied its contents in the sink, and left them all in the trash. Perhaps it was feeling a little more like her house after all. *** *** *** October 18, 2006 *** *** *** Jason "Hello?" "Hey kid," Johnny Silver’s familiar voice was on the other end of the phone.
