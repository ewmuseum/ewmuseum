Trial Series: The Great Scopes Scandal

27 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-27 The Olsig legacy was not supposed to die. His name was Vangelus Olsig, son of the royal Czar Olsig. Royalty in what respects, you ask? Well, Czar was the heralded arch bishop of the city of Bogotá, Columbia. In fact, the last three Arch Bishops had been all been within the Olsig family. With the death of Czar, it only seemed fitting that his son take over his reign. His son, Vangelus, did just that. But there was something different about Vangelus. His appearance…his demeanor…it all seemed rare to the citizens of Bogotá, all of whom believed in God, might I add. Speculation was brought to light, however, on one faithful evening when Olsig dropped a bombshell. He dismissed God Almighty as the sovereign power causing an immediate uproar amongst the citizens. Bogotá was not the fairest of cities, so much so that it had established a law that would severely punish all of those who dismissed God as the sovereign ruler. Now you had the Arch-Bishop, the very figure who was symbolic with religion, breaking that law. The city rebelled. It was literally a mob scene as the police dragged Vangelus away in chains. Fires, violence, cursing…the city felt betrayed. They locked Vangelus up until his trial approached…a trial that would prove to shape the cities very foundation. --------- The providence in which he’d been made to tolerate was fanatical. Pessimistic. Akin to an impulsive crucifixion brought on by a mere distinction in belief. Amplified by a mere boasting of said belief. Growing tensions arose in the tri-states of the city, Bogotá, giving dawn to a new wave of rebellion. A violent wave. Assembled outside of his cell stood masses of Bogotá citizens, all aligned with the common purpose of expressing their immense discontent with the governing figure whose feet they once bowed before. Gathered, faithfully, in an upstage against the tyrant whom they'd once declared sovereign. Vangelus Olsig was that tyrant. That governing figure. The citizens of Bogotá were his sheep. His crucifiers. Their loud, inapt chants were weaved with undying passion and fervor, accompanied by their full-sized banners, all of which swayed with the evening-sky’s winds, stained with words of revulsion towards their ruler-turned-tyrant. It all joined together to create a surreal landscape located on the very outskirts of town. His heart bled with sorrow. Their minds had been completely swirled and toyed with, leading them to bite the very hand in which fed them. He and his family shielded those very people from every plausible threat for several years, and this was what had become of the result. His heart bled for them. They knew not what they were doing. Creeek… A ray of light illuminated the otherwise dark cell, drawing his attention to the passageway. In sauntered two brawny officers, both of which took the liberty of snatching his limp body up from off of the padded flooring and shackling him with a pair of rusty, corroded chains. Neither said a word…Olsig followed suit. The sound of the clattering chains pierced through the otherwise lifeless ambiance, providing a visible sense of substance that was formerly imperceptible. Shifting, heaving a sigh, they braced the gray hallway. The stretch seemed to continue on forever, convoyed by the clattering of imprisoned criminals and the smell of death, all of which he disregarded with the utmost exertion. His sweat-drenched hair stuck to his pale face as he struggled to progress forward despite the bounding of the shackles. His fate seemed all but sealed. The conference room door was kicked open, leaving him to be tossed in with little disregard for his health. A stay the fuck here escaped the mouth of one of the exiting security guards, followed by a authoritative slamming of the door which, besides leaving him in a state of darkness once more, left Olsig on the floor. Abandoned. Seeking a way to fight through the shackles in order to reach a vertical base. Chills. The room maintained a dastardly aura of wintriness to it, causing me to bundle himself on the floor in an effort to counter the threat of pneumonia. Not long after, the door swayed open once more. No officers this time, but instead a visitor…one in which he’d least expect of all. Plaided with a black dress and equally black shoes, she entered the room…eyes sheltered under a dark shade of glasses. His Sister…Angie. Upon entering the room, she immediately rushed over to his aid, lifting him from his paralyzed position on the ground and leading him over unto a wooden chair. He found himself yanking his arm away from her grasp, implying that her help was not needed. When he was younger, he was treated as an outcast because of her. His parents rebuked him in favor of their precious daughter, creating within him a thick hatred for his sibling. Remorse, even now, was definitely possible…but not probable. "You have two minutes…" He stated. "Given your usual morbid self, you're practically being a box of chocolates." came her response. "Minute and a half…" A deep sigh escaped her. She placed herself closer to the wooden desk which separated the two, placing her hands on top in a folded position. The expression on her face was a mellow one. Olsig couldn't tell whether she wanted to laugh or cry. Olsig couldn't care much, either. "Why are you even going through the motions? Why in hell are you trying to fight the law? You of all people should know that no man, no matter how exalted, is above the Columbian law." His head hung low. Even behind the veil of dark shades, he could not stand the mere sight of her. His response came slow, weaved with every amount of emotion he could muster. "I am an Olsig." "And what's that supposed to mean?" She queried. "Olsigs fight for what they believe in. It's implanted within our DNA." "That analogy couldn't even begin to be any more false." "Just because you skipped the bill doesn't make it false." Head still bowed, he could feel the tension in her voice. She didn't want to be here anymore than he wanted her to be. Through the restraint, however, he could also feel a sense of urgency in her voice. One in which remained absent through her words. "Whatever. The point is, you're guilty as charged." She replied. "Is that why you're here? To reiterate the obvious?" Her tone amplified to that of a more serious one. "I'm here in an effort to push you past your selfishness so that you won't spend the rest of your god damn life trapped in a cell. You're guilty, Vangelus. Any average joe could see that!" "If fighting for what you believe in is a crime, then yes I am guilty. If proudly laying claim to that in which you have built your beliefs off of is a crime, then yes I am guilty. If mere distinction from the rest of Bogotá is a crime, then yes I am guilty. None of those justify themselves as crime…therefore I'm an innocent man, and because of such, I trust that justice will rule in my favor." Slowly, she shook her head in shame, taking the time to run her hand through her hair. "…stubborn…just like your father." For the first time, he swiftly lifted his head in order to face his counterpart. "Don't ever compare me to Your father. And what do you care whether I am charged or not? You've hated the mere mention of my name for years, which leaves me to believe that his persecution would do you more good than harm." "It's doing me more harm than even you could comprehend." He shook his own head in shame this time. "And you call me stubborn." Then the unexpected happened. Without a word, she removed her shades revealing a face costumed with bruises and red cuts. Peels of dried blood were left, stained upon her battered face. His heart sunk at such a gruesome sight "W-wh…who did this to you!?!" He responded, utilizing every limb in his body in order to fight through the chains in which held him shackled. "Boxers Rebellion." He took a moment to relish on the name. Nothing. "Who?" "They're practically a religious mob. As soon as word got out that I was your sister, they lynched an attack on me, punishing me for having any relation to such 'blasphemy' as they labeled it. Blood was everywhere…thought I'd broke an arm." Silence, for a moment at least as he glared upon her face, mesmerized by such a gruesome sight. A desire for more information came with his next response. "And the police?" "Didn't do a god damn thing about it. This is why I never wanted to get involved in politics with you and dad. You've stained the Olsig name, and just because I am forced to wear that name, I've become a victim as well." And then there was complete silence. For a moment's time, all of the prior hatred had been placed aside in a somber manner. For the first time during this entire scandal, he as if he was on the brink of tears. Then she added more fuel to the fire… "That's not even the worst par-…nevermind." "Wait…what?" "Nothing." "No, tell me." "It's noth-…" "TELL ME!" A silence fell upon the room once more as she went to wipe the tears from her eyes before granting him the information in which he desired. "Th-…They kept beating me. It was the only way I could get them to stop…I'm so sorry." "Tell me what you did, Angie!" "I…I told him everything." "What? Told Who?" "Corbin Esposito…leader of the Boxer's Rebellion." "Please don't tell me you told hi-…" "Yes…the secret you told me, and now he's struck a deal with Essex to testify against you." He erupted. "WHAT!?! HOW THE FU-…" "It's not my fault! If you you would just quit with this whole charade, I wouldn't even have to be in this predicament!" "But I told you that secret with the utmost confidentiality! The court is going to crucify me when they find that out, all because YOU couldn't keep your big mouth shut! " "Are you really that inconsiderate? I was beaten within an inch of my life, and all you can think about is yourself!? Screw you, Vangelus. Why don't you just give it up…confess. Tell them it was a joke or som-…" "NO! That's what they want, can't you see that!?" "STOP BEING SO DAMN SELFISH! YOU'RE NOT THE ONLY ONE SUFFERING HERE! The judicial doesn't give a DAMN about you! They want you to suffer…they want me to suffer…they want this entire family to suffer, and your selfish pride is giving them just that!" He took a moment to regain his composure before continuing on. "A confession of guilt right now would be nothing more than a blatant betrayal of my beliefs and the cult in which practices it…" "SCREW YOUR BELIEFS! Where's your cult now? HUH? They're nowhere to be found because they don't give a DAMN about you! Open your eyes and see that, you selfish son of a-…" "Get out." "I'm not done ta-…" "GET OUT!" A ray of sunshine illuminated the room as the security officer stepped inside of the room with a look of bewilderment planted upon his face . "What's going on in here?" He questioned. "Get her out of my sight." He hesitated for a moment, but progressed with the removal of Olsig's sister. He attempted to gently snag her wrist, but she quickly snatched it away in a raging fit, forcing him to grab her by the waste and drag her out of the room. Olsig simply watched as she kicked and screamed, yelling out her inane comments as she exited. "YOU'RE NOT THE ONLY ONE BEING HURT HERE. YOU'RE GUILTY! ADMIT IT! GUILTY AS CHARGED!!" And with that, the door slammed. Darkness once more as he surveyed the events in which had just unfolded before him. A lot of what she was saying made great sense. She wasn't right, though. She couldn't be. He was innocent. He knew it in his heart, and those who prosecuted him knew it as well. He was innocent…He assured himself of it. Innocent. -- Morris Essex was chosen to defend the city of Bogotá in the trial. He was notorious amongst the city and considered a hero. Parades were thrown in his honor, banners were hung, citizens practically fell before his feet. He was the good shepherd, in their eyes…the prosecutor who would rid the town of the blasphemous cancer that had corrupted it. Walter Ellis III, on the other hand, got the complete opposite treatment. He was chosen as the defense attorney for Olsig and was notorious for being a man who many considered to be an advocate of Satan. He'd acquitted two young boys who had murdered two younger girls in addition to countless other cases which lead the public to view him as demonic. The governor, even, attempted to devise a plan to stop him from entering Bogotá, but Essex stepped in and stopped it. He knew that a defeat over someone of Ellis' caliber would make the victory truly undisputed. There was another factor present in the case, however…arguably the most vital of them all. His name? Corbin Esposito. Corbin was the leader of the religious mob group known as the Boxer's Rebellion, the very group in which had beaten Olsig's secret out of his sister, Angie. Only Corbin knew the secret…and he assured Essex that the secret alone would bring Olsig down. The entire city anticipated the revealing of this secret. Olsig feared for his life because of it. -- Serenity as he sat upon that padded cushion of a floor, totally bleak to the rest of humanity. Meditation through silence served as a method of escape for him during this process…an escape that would find itself rudely interrupted at the sound of his cell door being kicked open. With little time to retaliate, two figures charged his room, one taking the liberty of planting the bottom of their solid boot in the bridge of his face. On all fours, he found another boot placed in the back of his head, driving his face into the cushioned grounding, a shot which wasn't so bad when compared to the rest. The same could not be said, however, for the grinding of that very boot into the back of his skull which followed immediately afterwards. Barely on the brink of consciousness, he found the two men yanking him up from off of the ground with blood dripping from his cracked nostril. The only thing he could see of the two men were black uniforms, akin to a mob squad, and the knight sticks in which they held a tight, firm grip unto. He would go on to find himself becoming quite acquainted with those very knight sticks, because immediately after driving his head into the steel cell door, they proceeded to tackle him to the ground and brutally cave into his helpless body with those sticks. One even took the liberty of turning him unto his back, choking him with the stick until the very second in which his life could be spared. A few boots to the ribs followed, causing blood to flow from his mouth as the two yanked him up once more and literally dragged him across through the corridor. He struggled to remain conscious as the prisoners laughed and spat upon his battered body. He was Jesus and they were his gentiles. At this point, He could only hope that they were not going to nail him to a cross, which didn't seem too far-fetched considering the beating he'd already taken. He fought to release himself from their grasps, prompting them to pause in the middle of the corridor and slam his head into the steel bars of the jail cells in which contained the prisoners. One time…two times…three times. He passed out. Shift Corbin Esposito was a made man. The prosecution had paid him a vast amount of cash for his secret concerning Olsig. He'd basically went from rags to riches overnight. He entered his newly decorated home with a smile on his face that seemingly lit up the world. He'd just quit his job, figuring that the government's finances would be enough to keep him stable for atleast a span of ten years. Right then, all he wanted was a nice hot bath…a chance to soak up the good life. Shift Slowly, He regained consciousness, feeling as if he'd just been run over by a semi-truck multiple times. Immediately, he felt one of the guards yank him up by his hair and toss him inside of a much larger and better lit conference room than before. He coughed up the last remaining gulps of blood present inside of his throat before one of the guards took the liberty of kicking him in the face for good measure. He rolled over unto his stomach, desperately seeking some method of salvation from the pain in which he'd been made to endure. A clicking sound arose. He looked up to witness a figure practically hovered above him. That figure…Essex. Shift Esposito pulled off his clothing, allowing it all to drop to the ground as he stepped into the pool of hot, steamy water, iced with crisp bubbles. He allowed himself to relax. Life was good. Suddenly he felt a slight breeze brush against the back of his neck. The breeze continued to brush, leading Esposito to believe that the window behind him was slightly open. He was too comfortable to close it, however, and the warm water in which he soaked in was enough for it not to be a major problem. Or so he thought. Shift "I'd prefer you dress a bit more casual next time." His statement came traced with the utmost amount of sarcastic wit. Assembled behind him was an alignment of trustees, all of which stood perfectly in a formal position. Smirks on their faces. Thousand dollar suits on their body. Sooner or later he'd come to find two of them lifting his limp and lifeless body off of the ground, slamming it against the cold white wall and pressing it there all the while Essex paced the floor, hand in pocket, taking on every task possible in order to portray himself as the executioner he so desired to be. His chilling voice pierced through the atmosphere. "Sir Olsig, I have requested your presence here not in an effort to boost his own ego, but to help out a man I once respected in his dire attempts to save his families legacy." he stated, retiring from his pacing in an attempt to look me straight in his battered face. "Unless you have a .22 magnum and a henchmen willing to pull it, you couldn't possibly help me." came his arched response. He was willing to go toe-to-toe with him verbally, if need be, even if his physical stability could not keep up. "You're certainly in the wrong position to be running off at the mouth, wouldn't you say?" He refused to respond. Shift While on the inside Esposito was soaking up the good life, on the outside something wicked was brewing. A dark figure, cladded in black ski mask and equally black clothing, loaded his chrome magnum with it's final two bullets, taking the time to click the gun completely shut. Shift "I am here to extend to you one final opportunity to plead guilty. Look at it from his perspective, Vangelus, we have all of the weapons necessary to lock you up…more than necessary, even. We have the jurors on our side, the judge on our side, a darn good lawyer if I have to say so himself, and most of all…your secret. As soon as the world heres Corbin Esposito's testimony, you'll be extinct. So why go through the motions." He asked, crafting an evil grin upon his wrinkled face as he juggled with change in his pocket. "Because I am innocent…" "…and justice will prevail, right? You're more transparent than even I would of have thought. Who decides Justice, Olsig? Judges, jurors, prosecutors, all of who are citizens of Bogotá, Columbia…and you know better than anyone else that not one soul of Bogotá thinks you're guilty. Not…one." Came his reply, his tone shifting to that of a more serious one. "Because theres not a citizen in Bogotá who can think for his or her self. Otherwise, all of them would recognize his innocence." "You broke a law! What part of that is not clicking for you!? Your beliefs don't matter…screw your beliefs!" He attempted to fight through the grasp of the advisors, but was only met with failure. He wanted so bad to tear through Essex, though…so bad. "Don't get upset with me, Olsig. It's the truth. And since you're too stubborn to accept my gracious offer of a guilty plea, I guess I'll just have to prove that in court. After all, Justice will prevail…right?" Shift Esposito whistled to himself, totally unaware of the gun in which the figure inserted through the opening of the window. Shift Essex chuckled once more, giving a nod to the advisors as they released their grasp, allowing Olsig to slither down the wall and unto the ground below. As quickly as they'd entered, they'd left, but yet his parting words still lingered throughout the atmosphere. After all, Justice will prevail…right?. Shift He pulled the trigger. Shift "Damn right it will." Shift Blam! Blam! Blood turned the bath water crimson red. Esposito had been killed. -- The Olsig legacy was not supposed to die.
