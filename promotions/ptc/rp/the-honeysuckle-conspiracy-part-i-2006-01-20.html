The Honeysuckle Conspiracy: Part I

20 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-20 If ever a child were born and raised without having experienced the succulent pleasures of a honeysuckle bloom, then they’d find themselves deprived of an integral childhood memory. Running barefoot along a fence full of saffron petals that envelop their sweet pink antenna-like pistols, or watching butterflies scatter in a kaleidoscopic cloud of beating wings as the child plucks a flower at random. The agonizing care taken to preserve its saccharine payload as the stem is pulled out, revealing a dewy treat that clings stubbornly to the flower’s innards. That first indescribable taste as the child discovers that flowers aren’t just for the birds, after all. As one of the most varied and resourceful plants in the vegetation kingdom, Lonicera japonica, or Japanese honeysuckle, has inseparably burrowed itself into the subconscious of American flora. First introduced by Asian traders in the mid-1800’s, the honeysuckle phenomenon began as an ornamental fetish for bourgeois southerners. Every cottage getaway and plantation estate saw the aesthetic value of the budding vines; before long it was hard to find a home that didn’t have the damn things snaking up its pearly colonnade. The irony of it all? It’s just a weed. They grow like green cancer, sprawling in every direction as the vine entwines everything from chain link to chestnut tree. With a vibrant exterior full of pastel whites and yellows and pinks and full of nectar sweet enough for a hummingbird, the vines themselves are harder to kill than an Ebola outbreak in Times Square. While they look and smell more beautiful than homemade apple pie, it’s the dirt that does the dirty work. The soil hides the plant’s nefarious rhizomes as the rootstalk expands, suffocating every flower and floret unfortunate enough to be caught up in its honey-fueled offensive. I would know. I’m the fucking floret. ~<>~<>~<>~ Five days ago… "Shut up, it’s back on!" Jason Hood inhaled a handful of popcorn as the television wandered back from a commercial break. "Oh man, this is so awesome!" Sitting cross-legged on his father’s favorite couch, Jason passed the buttery bowl on to his buddy Will, who immediately attacked the kernels with fat and greedy fingers. Jason knew his father would be beside himself if they got popcorn crumbles on the carpet - let alone his precious golf-themed couch - but the occasion at hand called for at least a little leeway in the rulebook. The chances of his parents interrupting their party was slim to none anyhow. They were too busy upstairs watching CSI: Miami to notice their child’s obsession with pro wrestling, and besides, it wasn’t a pastime they usually allowed him to indulge in. "There’s too much violence in the world already without you having to watch men beat the living snot out of each other," his mother was fond of saying. This was usually followed by a stern "don’t make me get your father" as she turned around to watch her precious CSI. Jason thought he knew what a hypocrite was, but even at the ripe old age of eleven and a half he was sure that it described his mother right down to the white polish on her French manicure. "Who do you think’ll win Match of the Year?" Will mumbled around a mouthful of greasy snack food. An avalanche of half-chewed popcorn spittle fell to the carpet as he spoke. "I’ll bet the IronMaze takes it. I’ve never seen anything as crazy as that." "Nah," Jason shook his head. "The IronMaze will probably win Innovation of the Year, but no way was it the best match." Digging himself a cushy nest amongst the caddy bags and golf clubs that adorned his father’s favorite couch, Jason stuffed another fist full of crumbly goodness into his mouth. "Deville and Rollins, now that was a match." "You’re such a poser." Will rolled his eyes. "That’s what everybody says. Rollins’ match against Almasy at NC-17 was better. They blew up a ring and wrestled to the top of a freaking mountain!" Jason shrugged. "So? They do that kind of crazy stuff in Japan all the time." "How would you know?" Setting the popcorn on a nearby coffee table, Will turned around to face his friend with a cruel accusation: "Your mom barely lets you watch wrestling at all." With an indignant sniff Jason snatched the popcorn off of his father’s coffee table. "Well, we have the internet." Trying to ignore his friend’s attempt to get under his skin Jason turned back to the television. The 2005 PTC Awards Ceremony had just begun. ~<>~<>~<>~ "Live" from the Kodak Theatre in sunny Hollywood, California… Droves of fans had been screaming themselves hoarse since sunrise, creating a living, breathing barricade that sprawled around the entire multi-block perimeter of the illustrious Hollywood & Highland Complex. A strange world where limousines outnumbered taxis and tuxes rented for more than a man’s mortgage awaited the brave few (thousand) who had made sure to secure a curbside seat for themselves, eager to catch the parade of wrestling royalty as they arrived with their various entourage in tow. It was a day that would teach Rich Rollins a lesson that he would remember for the rest of his life. While he may have loathed the elitist, self-serving nature of awards shows in general, goddamn if the Renegade didn’t love walking a red carpet. Women swathed in outrageous regalia of every shape and size looked at Rollins in a way he wasn’t used to. It wasn’t every day you could catch Rich Rollins in a tuxedo; he wore a finely tailored suit and tie about as gracefully as a bloated boar, but by the reaction he received as his feet touched red velvet he could have been President of the United States of Everything. Hefting his PTC Global title with a lopsided grin smeared across his rocky face, Rollins walked his first red carpet as if he were a Greek god. Rich Rollins, a true wrestling messiah. Why the hell not? he mused as a hundred flashbulbs bathed him in the dizzying glow of immortality. Kurt Loder tried catching his attention for the MTV audience, waiting on a stage set not far removed from the theater’s ornate two-story entrance portal. A photographer from the Chicago Tribune solicited an impromptu pose as he took his first step up the elaborate inlet staircase. Various journalistic dignitaries brushed elbows with the owners of Primetime Central’s many federations, offering their worldwide audience a glimpse into the lives of the men who made it all possible. So many eyes, yet for Rollins, there weren’t nearly enough. Usually backlit for the Academy Awards, the multitude of Best Picture plaques that lined the red carpet had been replaced to fit the occasion. Instead of Gandhi and Schindler’s List, pictures of Jesse Jamester and Tristan Belmont greeted guests as they walked the red carpet. Saij loomed instead of The Sting. Almasy instead of Amadeus. Chris Storm instead of Chariots of Fire. Rollins smiled when he saw his own likeness had replaced The Godfather Part II. It immediately disappeared when he noticed that Angelo Deville had taken up residence over the original. When a tall radio host for an off-beat show in Atlanta planted himself in Rollins’ way, all the Renegade could do was toss him a confident nod. The host had other plans. "Inside sources are saying that Angelo Deville won Wrestler of the Year by a landslide," the cocky little fuck butted in. "I hope this loss won’t negatively affect your performance against O‘Shea, Bradley and Winters." Trying to keep up appearances the Renegade refused to slow down for the man. Keeping his tongue in check, Rollins shrugged as he walked by. "Everyone has an inside source," he said. "I’ll believe it when I see it." The radio host began side-stepping his way up the stairs to keep up with the Global Champion’s quickening pace. "Better believe it, Rich." His smugness was suffocating. "I was on the ballot committee." Having only been half listening to the man’s insinuated threats, Rich Rollins now turned to confront him. Sucking back a biting retort, Rollins’ jaw locked in frustration as he watched the man mix himself back into the sidelines, disappearing into a crowd of media moguls and camera-happy news hounds. His sudden irritation was already gone by the time he reached the theater’s grandiose foyer. A suit-and-tails usher was waiting for him by the golden spiral staircase, standing with hands folded under the glittering eyes of a grand chandelier. "Let me escort you to your seat, Mr. Rollins." This was what his limo driver had referred to as the "Tom Hanks treatment." Rollins thought it was more along the lines of needless pampering. Then again, who was he to complain? ~<>~<>~<>~ A sparkling silver tiara of lights loomed above a crowd comprised of over three thousand celebrities, wrestling journalists and the wrestlers themselves, every one of them dressed like an ostentatious bureaucrat. The audience chamber itself radiated with the heat of the moment, a true modern miracle of plexiglass and cherrywood finish. An ocean of plum upholstery covered every seat in the house, every eye turned to the spectacle that was slowly unfolding on the stage. Australian conductor John Foreman called for the attention of his fifteen-string orchestra. Three separate podiums lorded over three separate sections of stage, planned accordingly to aid in the continuity of presentation. And me without my Funyuns, thought Rollins as he leaned back in his second-row seat. The usher had originally placed him up front in the same chair that Drew Barrymore had occupied at last year’s Oscars. He asked to be moved on general principal; Jack Nicholson seemed to suit him just fine. And so it began. After the applause died away and the opening orchestral swell faded into the rafters, the owner and operator of Primetime Central Entertainment took center stage. A warm welcome rose from the crowd as a faceless announcer introduced [name withheld] R. Guyle to the podium. "Hello and welcome to the first annual Primetime Central Awards Ceremony," the proud owner gushed with a distinctive Aussie drawl. "We’re here to honor the men and women of the wrestling world who have proven themselves to be the best. Without hesitation I could ramble off a list of legends that have passed through our doors in the last twelve months. We’ve watched as the greatest heroes of our time engaged in battles of both muscle and wit with some of the most hated heels the squared circle has ever seen. We’ve seen men fight for titles, for freedom, for their future… but most importantly, for you. For every fan who has given our sport the respect and admiration we in turn offer to you, thank you all." Cue the thunderous ovation. A warm smile tickled the corners of [name withheld]’ lips as he listened to his audience’s own unique thank-you, drinking in their acclaim and unabashed gratitude like a bottle of fine champagne. When the roar dulled down he finished with a triumphant pump of his fists: "P7C r0x0rz 73h h0u53~!" No truer words were ever spoken. The evening played out like a marathon frat party that refused to turn down the music, filling the theater with a kind of fever only a wrestling community could cure. Diva of the Year became the first order of business. A broadcast scrim screen projected live face shots of Karina Wolfenden, Eva VanLorne, Shadow, and Dawn McFarlane as the presenter - a lingerie representative from Fredericks of Hollywood - cracked jokes about the apparent lack of apparel on today’s top female grapplers. Survival winners were given their fair share of the spotlight as a special presentation of personalized PTC Survival trophies were awarded to Team XUW. Saij, woof woof, Scotty Spalzcutoff and Jack "The Ripper" Nobbledy proudly accepted the accolade, but after an emotional three minute acceptance speech woof woof was drummed off the stage by an overzealous orchestra. Feud of the Year was called on account of tea withdrawal as the ballot card was eaten by its presenter, Pete the PG Tips Chimp. The world wept along with Eleanor Kannon-Hall as the one and only Phantom Booker presented PTC’s first ever Lifetime Achievement award to an overwhelmed Xavier Kannon. Scientologists everywhere rejoiced. Whether it’s the Oscars or the Aussies, it occurred to Rollins that no single commercial entity gets more awards show airtime than the Almighty Himself. No Pepsi spot can compete with an endless string of bleary-eyed acceptance speeches thanking God and the faceless names who helped them become the athlete or artist they are today. They stroke the Lord like a lucky rabbit’s foot, hoping that their liturgical lip service keeps their name on His good side until the next ballot cycle runs its course. Listening to the Manager of the Year’s meandering tribute Rich Rollins began to wonder just where exactly gratitude ended and sincerity began. The audience fell into a reserved silence as the 2004 Wrestler of the Year winner Saij finally took the stage, holding in his hand the ballot card for the 2005 PTC Wrestler of the Year. The Renegade fiddled with his lapel out of nervous habit as he felt the reassuring crease of his acceptance speech that was tucked tenderly in his left breast pocket. A wizened old man sitting in the row at Rollins’ back leaned in close, offering a firm pat on the Renegade’s shoulder as he said, "Knock ‘em dead, champ." Clearing his throat, Saij milked the moment for as long as he could. He read the nominees as a video package played out on the scrim, highlighting a year in the life of the best and brightest Primetime Central had to offer. Small cheering sections erupted throughout the theater as select favorites appeared in the montage, lending their support as they let every nominee know that win or lose, their work wouldn’t be forgotten. The montage faded away. Saij pried open the envelope with an obligatory: "And the winner is…" ~<>~<>~<>~ -CLICK- ~<>~<>~<>~ "Aww MOM!" Jason threw up his hands in frustration as his television blinked off. "That was it! That was the last award!" "And it’s the last of that wrestling smut you’ll be watching for a good long while," his mother scolded. "And how many times do I need to tell you not to eat on your father’s couch? Do I need to go get him?" "No." Shuffling his feet, Jason scooped up the remains of his popcorn bowl as he started tromping up the stairs, head down. "I’ll clean it up. C’mon Will, let’s go to your house." Will jumped to his feet as Jason’s mother planted both fists on her hips. "You’d better not be running off to watch this crap elsewhere, Jason. I’ll know. Mothers always find out." Jason winced. She always used that threat when he was about to do something wrong. "We’re not," he promised. Satisfied, Momzilla wandered back upstairs. Jason and Will took off running as soon as they hit the front door, hoping to catch the post-show interviews before dinnertime. ~<>~<>~<>~ Back at the Kodak… Rich Rollins was already halfway to the stage when he noticed the queer expressions on the faces of his peers. His head rang with the anticipation, his pulse pounding pure adrenaline, urging him up the stairs and to his unavoidable destiny. It was faint, but the one name he didn’t need to hear kept echoing in his brain. Angelo Deville. The odd looks from the front row began to bother him, but this was a moment he wasn’t about to allow a few skeptics to rain on. Angelo Deville. Saij coughed awkwardly as the Renegade arrived on stage. Rollins’ pace began to slow when he looked at the podium and the name on the opened ballot card that sat in plain sight. Angelo Deville. "The fuck…?" He stopped. It was all the Renegade could do not to kick the damn thing over. Angelo Deville. A light turned on behind the confusion in Saij’s pupils as he leaned into the microphone. "Angelo Deville couldn’t be with us tonight, so here to accept the award on his behalf… PTC Global Champion Rich ‘The Renegade’ Rollins!" Polite applause filled the theater. Rollins head suddenly filled with the desire to strangle a small child. Angelo fucking Deville. Rollins took the seven pound statuette that signified one of the greatest endowments PTC can bestow, his mouth suddenly devoid of moisture and his eyes devoid of humor. Saij took his leave, strolling out stage right as the orchestra played the sad soundtrack to Rollins’ crushing humiliation. Millions of eyebrows perked with interest as he hovered over the podium, looking at the name on the award’s faceplate as a sick sort of irony began using Rollins’ appetite as a punching bag. The orchestra ceased its melodic consolation. Rollins finally looked out over the glaring footlights of his pride. "Are you serious?" His words bit like a shot of whiskey as they reverberated throughout the theater and into the grand lobby. "In case you haven’t noticed, Angelo Deville doesn’t give two shits about his fans. Then again, neither do I… but at least I bothered to show up. "Now I know a wrestling fan’s memory is all but nonexistent, but him? This is the man you want to deify?" He shook the statuette above his head, contempt dripping from every pore his body could pry open. "Let me save you all some trouble and fill you in on the facts ‘they’ do indeed want you to know. On his best day Deville couldn’t hope to conquer my cardboard cutout, let alone a wrestling ring while I’m standing in it. You want a champion? You’re already fuckin’ lookin’ at one. And where is your precious Angelo Deville?" A low rumble began swirling throughout the audience, but Rollins wouldn’t let it build. "That’s right, where the hell is your unstoppable phenom now? Oh you didn’t hear? I fucking sent him back to Little Italy. Or wherever the hell he spawned from. So yeah, I’ll accept my award. I’ll accept the fact that Deville has his fingers in the pockets of every critic from Tokyo to Timbuktu, but I will not accept something that he’s too busy masturbating over to claim for himself. Say hello to the real Wrestler of the Year, folks. Unlike some, I won’t leave you in the dark." It took far longer for security to descend on Rollins’ podium than he had figured, but by the time they were within reaching distance he was already on his way down the stairs. Heads turned in unified abhorrence as he made his way back up the aisle, clutching the 2005 Wrestler of the Year statuette in one white-knuckled fist. ~<>~<>~<>~ I can hear them all now, already clutching the rocks with which to stone my career back to the indies. "But it’s just a stupid statue." Sure. And the Mona Lisa was just a deaf man’s painting. A statue it may be, but I’ll be damned if that one moment didn’t taste sweeter than the sweetest stem on the honeysuckle vine. If Deville is a weed, I’m the goddamn pesticide. He can plot and swerve under the surface until he has everyone’s roots all tied up in a knot, but I’ll still be there to root him out with a eulogy and a shovel as I bury him right along with his fucking statue. Can I get an amen? To be concluded…
