Revelations (II) - Faith Balks To A World Gone Mad

8 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-02-08 "They have all fallen. I can see the gates of Heaven already, Maury. The promised land awaits us!" Dawson grimaced at Warrick's voice as he barked his pleasure from across the dinner table, others mumbling their hearty agreement through spoonfuls of soup. Back now at the estate of Avery Shields, the guilt-stricken banker could still hear the peal of the revolver as it punched a hole through the chest of Claude Metcalf. The other man's voice was a ceaseless catalyst for the memory, his every word igniting Dawson's fevered mind into recalling that fateful day in the outlying wilds of West Virginia where the ruthless ex-Marine had gunned down a man for sake of claiming his own redemption. Whereas the faith of Ellis Warrick has seemed to increase exponentially after his murderous deed, Maury Dawson's own conviction in aiding their faceless benefactor had grown distant. He believed in sacrifice, but not a senseless murder the likes of which he'd been an accomplice. "Chaney's opposition?" queried Dawson with a hint of hesitation. "Yes, he seems to be faring rather well doesn't he?" The remark was met with more of the same from his colleagues, an assembly of persons of refutable character that he suspected were, on the whole, equally unscrupulous when examined against Ellis Warrick. Another of Chaney's zealous followers rose, a thin-stemmed wine glass in one hand. Gregory Marin didn't have the rough and grizzled posture of one such as Warrick but Dawson had come to suspect his mind capable of infinitely more wicked, and clever, machinations. With piercing brown eyes like daggers and a grin innately sly, he nodded agreement. "As our trusted guide, Mr. Chaney, has told us all, we all must sacrifice. All of us have accepted the responsibility in guiding the world to its nirvana and all of us also have to bear the burden of responsibilities we may consider…'questionable'. But we all have persevered and shall reap the rewards of our loyalty." Dawson suspected a background as an executive in Marin. Between the refined, professional bearing and candy-coated morale speeches he'd delivered in days recent, he could see no other viable background for the man he viewed as a wolf in sheep's clothing. Expensive sheep's clothing at that. Thunder rolled in the distance, a booming herald for the rain that began to pelt the roof and course down the windows of the expansive dining room. Pausing in his speech to refill his wine glass from the expensive selection their host had provided at the onset of the dinner, Marin smiled at the weather before averting his eyes to Maury. The icy gaze of his partner made Dawson uneasy, but he did well to hide his discomfort. "Who would have thought a professional wrestler, of all people, to possess such grand knowledge? A truly remarkable man, this Chaney, and undoubtedly a man with capacities far surpassing such a lifestyle. Tell me, Dawson…you've been at his side longer than man in this dining hall. What do you know of him? Did he happen to divulge anything to you beyond what we may already know?" It hadn't been the first time Maury had been prodded for knowledge of their magnificent savior and his identity behind that odd pallid mask, and it wasn't the first time he had to reply in the negative. He was beginning to consider fabricating some minor tidbit for the wolves to feed on; this lot's hunger for information wouldn't be slaked until he told them something, outlandish or no. He was saved from that internal debate as another of the motley gathering broke the silence. "Our success still rides entirely on his ability to keep winning. We musn't ignore that fact." Preston Thatcher reclined in his chair, sipping scotch from a crystalline highball glass. He seemed a product of the same mold as Marin, equally devious perhaps, if not more eager to suppress the sort of hollow fallacies that Marin often pronounced to them all. "We can act as lapdogs for his cloak-and-dagger designs as long as we please, what matters is whether he can continue his march to victory. Unlike some of you doddering fools, I haven't staggered blindly through the dark with only a ray of hope as my beacon. Shanty Jones may prove too much for our esteemed 'guide'. This man is rather convicted in his own right. And the ones directing him have the credentials one would want in the staff managing their career." There was a shifty tone to his words that Maury picked up on immediately. "It isn't our business to worry over that part of the plan," replied Marin over the lip of his glass. "I occupy myself with what we can control, nothing more. A company producing tires doesn't give insights to car manufacturers in regards to assembling their engines." A coy smile said that he was satisfied with the analogy. "Therefore I'll go about gathering the rubber for my tires and leave the final product to Mr. Chaney. I'll still profit from the partnership." Thatcher grinned in kind, lowering the cigar occupying his other hand as tendrils of smoke billowed from his nostrils. "I fear you missed my point, Gregory. So I'll be more clear. Let me put it this way…let's say your company only provides tires to one particular manufacturer. Now, wouldn't it be in your best interest to see the stock of your partner's rival take a dive in the market?" Catching the expression of depraved glee that crossed Marin's face at the not-so-subtle suggestion, Maury instantly felt queasy. He shifted his attention to a tapestry near the crackling fireplace, plagued by remembrance of the package he had sent to the one called Shadow many weeks prior at Chaney's request, the package bearing the macabre mask and the accompanying letter penned with its bizarre glyphs and cryptic closing. The puzzle was beginning to come together for Maury Dawson and he only wished he could rend it apart, and scatter the pieces across the corners of the Earth before its vile image could take form. Chaney knew the order of men he had assembled under his beguiling eye to carry out these things; the merciless type who reveled under his guidance would see glory and power for themselves even if it entailed the bereavement of innocents. Warrick was comparably a neonate in the cutthroat arts being so casually devised by Marin and Thatcher, his tendency for outward violence paling in comparison to the unscrupulous tactics they suggested to be employed. Had a similar fate befallen McFarlane, as rumor held so strongly? Bile pitched in his stomach at the thought, its bitterness rising as the first plans were proposed to sabotage Shanty Jones' aspirations. "A message from Mr. Beckett for you, Mr. Dawson." The house-servant's words drowned out his thoughts, mercifully. He really needed a hobby to keep himself from dwelling on such thoughts more than necessary until he figured a way of escaping what was becoming a nightmare. The other's company wasn't a proper substitute, sadly. "Beckett?" Maury sighed, fingering his glass nervously. Christian Beckett had grown even more anxious in the past couple days and he hadn't been surprised to hear he declined the evening's dinner invitation. Not considering the fragility of his mind, the wild theories he ranted over whenever they were alone. "What is the message?" Maury couldn't suppress a wince. He was at her grave. Damn the man for drawing him back there again. * * * Dawson felt like he was looking into a mirror as he found Beckett on that cold night. Like his distressed associate that had been locked away only weeks ago to confine the knowledge he possessed, Maury felt the desolation and loneliness of championing a cause he lacked faith in. Kneeling at the foot of a smooth tombstone, eyes lolling to and fro as the chill winds of winter tossed his coat about in the storm, Christian Beckett clutched a bottle of bourbon in one hand as the other rested on his lap. Regardless of being placed in an older section of the cemetary, the grave was fairly new; the polished gravestone was yet untarnished by the eroding winds that would mark it a monument of any formidable age. Threading his way between newly erected tombstones and crumbling markers of decades long past, Maury Dawson stood over Beckett, his eyes pressing on his troubled comrade. His reddened eyes were the evidence of a man forlorn and unafraid to shed tears over it. "Fine wine with devils or cheap whiskey with only my guilt for company. I went with the latter." With a swagger echoing intoxication, the monotone Beckett pulled himself up, taking a step toward Maury and offering the bottle. "Drink?" "I'll pass. But thank you." The other shrugged, dropping into a seated position in front of the gravestone. "Suit yourself." Kindred spirits they were, in a sense. An auspicious trust in their benefactor had faded to sorrow and regret in light of what they had been ordered to do to further his enigmatic schemes. The resentment Maury once harbored for his unsolicited colleague, this man he now watched emotionally deteriorate before his eyes, had dwindled in light of things they'd learned about one another. Maury, however, still maintained a monicum of hope for what lay ahead, whereas the other had forsaken all. He was a prisoner now as much as he ever was in that padded room, just minus the bi-daily injections of sedatives. "Kinda funny, huh? Just when you think you've hit rock bottom it always seems like the bottom falls out and there's still another level to spiral down to. At least that's my experience. But hell, my talents have always seemed to bring me more hardship than fortune." His delivery was remarkably stoic for one so visibly pained. Another swig from the bottle did nothing to change his tone. "Now I've not only worked to doom myself, but the whole world has to pay for our crimes. Well, our crimes. Sorry, Maury, but you fucked this one up too." An impassive sigh brought him further pause. "I dunno if there even is a bottom where we're going. If there actually is a God, he's going to have our hides for this one." Dawson, taking a seat on a nearby gravestone, locked his eyes on the other. Christian was distraught and his condition did nothing to stir his own optimism. Judging by the frantic gleam in his eye, he was an episode away from being thrown back in that asylum, this time rightfully. "I know who he is, Maury. God and Heaven help me, I figured out who he is. The man is nothing short of a ghost from my past, diabolical in every sense of the word. And he's come to exact his revenge on me. Hell….on all of us!" The gaze he directed at his friend was accusing, baleful in spite of the pity he placed on himself. Maybe everyone had become the enemy to him in that drunken and self-contemptuous state. Hopefully it really was all the booze. "This is lunacy, Chris. I may not know who Chaney is behind that mask and this damn wall of secrecy he's fortified around us--" "Fortified to detain us, not to protect us. Why can't you open your eyes and see what's happening? You know this is wrong, let go of your hollow hopes and believe me!" Beckett's words never lost that tinge of contempt and he accentuated his comments by pointing toward the grave he sat in front of. "None of this will bring her back, Maury. She's gone. But don't desecrate her memory by giving a madman the keys to Armageddon!" Only the howling winds of February pervaded the silence shared between Dawson and Beckett. And in no way could that breeze be as cutting as the memories that invaded his thoughts while his eyes rested on that grave. Years did nothing to lessen the pain of his transgressions. Sage Zimmerman Dawson Maury almost reached for the bottle now but he restrained himself. He was waiting for her corpse to dig itself out of that coffin and through the worm-filled dirt to slap some sense into him, tell him the fantasy was over and to get out before he damned himself and everyone he touched. As clear as it was, he wouldn't let go. He couldn't yet. Metcalf had said… "He knows that I know who he is! To him, it's a fucking game." Beckett clutched his head, running clawed fingers through hair matted down by the frigid rain. "I can't believe I was so stupid. And so careless. I can't even feign ignorance now, Maury." "Tell me who he is." "I can't." "Why?!" "Because I have family. This bastard isn't as kind as those greedy sons of bitches who had me put away. I won't be the only one who suffers next time." Tears had begun to trickle down the cheeks of Beckett. Dawson wasn't sure if the alcohol had contributed so much to his speculations or if they were really that severe before he had gotten himself drunk. He was either genuinely mad or of such outrageous paranoia that the most hardened conspiracy theorist would be dismayed. "I know what you and Warrick brought to him, Maury. And damn you for going through with it." His hand clenched around the bottle and for a moment Maury thought he was going to shatter it against the gravestone or a tree, if not with his white-knuckled grip alone. "Wha…what is it exactly? The bookseller was too cryptic." Christian's look was death. "It's a demonic thing. Part of it is a lexicon used by evil men to decipher the language of devils. The rest….histories no man should fancy as any concrete past, and formulas to conjure things never meant for a world living in the light of God." Nothing more instructive than what he'd gotten from Claude antemortem, but when issued through Christian's lips it jogged more than he wished. More memories flooded the banker's consciousness. Flashbacks of the society he had worked for and the battles he'd fought, a fragmented torrent of broken titles and harrowing encounters. Everything, just a web of shadows and lies spun amidst a fractured crusade orchestrated by unseen puppet masters. He was reminded of that tragic period of his life where he had embraced his awakening to the world he didn't know existed, and ruined all that he cherished. Sage was the crown jewel of his disastrous past, the embodiment of his fool venture. "I thought we destroyed them all. Your people and mine. I never dreamed one was still hidden in the world." He didn't need to state any names or titles. He already knew something of Beckett that he thought the other wasn't aware of about him but such a revelation, though vague, was enough to shatter that self-imposed illusion. For Dawson, it was like being blindsided by a truck. "You know?" A laugh, casual and nonthreatening. The banker took nothing implicit in its alcohol-fueled tone, unlike he did in their more suspicious colleagues. "About you? Yes. I know all about your affiliation. And I know what you did for our organization." His lips formed a thin smile, but at this point a smile was a smile. "Surely you don't think I decided to befriend you because of your charming personality, eh? Truth be told, I thought you were a stodgy asshole when we first met." At least a degree of humor remained in the man; he hadn't completely succumbed to the dark side. Not yet. It was Maury's turn to laugh. But a phantom voice cut that short. "So….we were right. You are part of his society. If only you knew what you've unleashed…" A gaunt figure stepped from behind a tree, a thin outline in the light of a distant lamp reaching above the gravelly road. Draped in a slick raincoat with hood drawn, he appeared harmless at first, albeit a face still concealed in the inky darkness. The gun in his left hand quickly altered that perception. Maury sprung to his feet in horror. Events had just stacked atop one another and he was sick of people brandishing firearms as matters escalated. Too many deaths, too much blood. Beckett's reaction was contrary to Dawson's as he spotted the weapon that jutted from the newcomer's dripping sleeve; alcohol had drained him of any fear, if any remained in him alongside that oddly malevolent gloom. "Another trigger-happy maverick to join the fold. You'd be right at home with us, my gun-toting compadre." The new arrival breathed something that could be considered either a pleased sigh or a bemused chuckle as he lowered his hood. He had lowered his defenses remarkably fast; perhaps the comments Beckett in his stupor had worked that quickly. The downpour had subsided in the moments since his appearance and now came like a steady trickle from the clouds veiled by night. Wiping water from his brow, the young man lowered the weapon and slipped it back under his glossy raiments. Dawson immediately suspected he was as well-versed in concealing the sidearm as wielding it; the man's movements were quick yet deliberate, far from the clumsiness of a common mugger. Moreso he didn't strike Dawson as unsavory, at least not in the class of madmen like the violent Warrick and the conniving Marin. "Perhaps," he answered. "I consider myself versatile that way. Truth be told, though…" A pause as he tapped the grip of the .45 magnum outlined inside his coat. "…I really prefer a blade to one of these." With blond hair that seemed meticulously styled to perfection and eyes shimmering a sapphire blue, the youthful aggressor first seemed out of place in the position of a would-be killer. But the cruelty etched across his face, a stark heartlessness burned into the lines and curves of his sinister visage, told instead of an reformed innocent that knew malice as one of his most familiar bedfellows, a life baptized in violence. It further upheld Maury's suspicion of their visitor's identity as a soldier. "So…what the hell do you know about this guy anyhow?" The rain did nothing to wash the aroma of a brewery from Christian, or bind his tongue for that matter. The bottle's contents were dwindling fast and touched no gullet but his own. "He's pure evil. I want you to help me stop him." The man was blunt in his intentions. It was a refreshing change from what Maury was used to between Chaney and his roommates back at the estate. Christian echoed his sentiments with a wily laugh before the other reflected his own eased mood in the form of a meager smile. "From what you seem to allege of your reputed 'mentor', I'd say you're swinging in the right ballpark. But you can keep your suspicions to yourself for now. I mean, hell…I just walked out from behind a tree and pointed a gun in your direction. Why would you trust me with theories?" His jovial laugh immediately dissipated any hesitance on the drunken Beckett's behalf. Maury still had to mull over how much to entrust their equally-evasive contact, but realized they may need all the help they could muster in this war. He was amiable enough, not to say he wouldn't need to be observed until they were sure of his virtuous intentions. "I understand he goes by an alias. 'Chaney'." The dark-faced youth chuckled as he recognized the irony in the title. "You can call me Brutus. It seems fitting enough, considering many things. I'll let you in on more when we're not so subject to monitoring." Dawson liked the man. It wasn't a sensation he could readily place a finger on, and really an odd stance to take so soon after their meeting, but he felt an instant rapport with the even-tempered gunslinger, regardless of him being in a similar vein to the inscrutable Chaney. That beacon of hope had reignited for the banker-turned-mercenary; if there really was a network of operatives undermining whatever Chaney planned, there was a fleeting prospect that he'd be stopped before more were 'sacrificed' in the name of his dark designs. "You're all connected. Everyone you've shared dining and accommodations with as of late, in some way, has a thread twined about in the web. This 'Chaney' has woven an elaborate network and it spans factions and organizations that would never affiliate otherwise. And whether you realize it or not, he's already injected his venom into all of you. Even the righteous are unknowingly serving forces infinitely darker than you could fathom." Shadows danced as the trees wavered under brusque currents of wind, chilling Maury through the thin jacket he had wrapped around himself on the way out of Avery's manor. Beckett's eyes darted about wildly, anticipatory, as if he expected some fanged denizen of Hell to pounce on him at any moment. He didn't suspect that Brutus had tried to instill more fear in them then was already present; surely their visible apprehension was formidable enough that it needed enriched no further. Unfortunately Dawson had little doubt that his comrade's waning sanity would eventually be depleted, and when that happened, he would be a dangerous wild card in what was going to amount to a guerilla war against a resourceful villain in the masked Chaney. It had taken only a couple of catalysts to solidify his decision toward treachery. As if reading his mind, Brutus turned that frigid gaze toward Maury. He was deathly somber as he leaned forward to speak in a lowered volume. "He cannot know. For your sake, for the sake of my own people. Cliche as it may sound, countless lives may depend on maintaining secrecy until he's thwarted. A lot is riding on our success in this fight. It may get violent, more may have to die. But that's the bleak truth of the matter. War isn't pretty. And this is a war being fought for the souls of innumerable people who haven't the faintest notion of what forces may lie beyond our world. Understand that their continued ignorance is what keeps our civilization so tranquil. If it's shattered, say farewell to what we know and hello to a new dark age spawned by fear." New alliances or not, Maury wasn't sure this made affairs any easier. If anything, matters had become much more complicated. How many roles could he juggle in this great play before he muddled his lines? Could he still save Shanty Jones from his conspiring peers? Rescue all the innocents in Chaney's way before he could summon whatever he was undoubtedly after? Prevent him from reaching whatever maniacal goals he had hidden from him all this time? "We're going to need you, Maury Dawson. We know about your background and your expertise. You'll be invaluable to us." Maury Dawson didn't need masks to join the masquerade. He now filled too many roles even without a veil. This feeling was all too familiar. Yet another new development to curse. * * * "How could such demons encroach on this holy ground?! Be gone, servants of Hell! In the name of Jesus Christ I demand that you depart this sanctuary of God!" Father Yuri Romanov almost stumbled as he backed down the aisle from the advancing duo in their jet-black cloaks, clutching the silver crucifix dangling from his neck like it would invoke a shield to defend he and the bastion of the Lord he had been deputed to protect. Through thick spectacles he stared, quivering, at the pallid mask of the fiend on the right. He'd prayed for courage had the agents of the abyss ever come in the flesh to his own church. It was unfortunate that he now felt nothing but dread and despair. That initial cry had completely sapped him of valor. They had come to be known as Masquerade and Kendall, specters that materialized out of the night to walk the streets in hopes of obtaining whatever hellish goal they may be seeking. The priest in Philadelphia had warned many of his brothers about their inevitable coming. Romanov simply didn't think it would be his chapel, not so soon. He hadn't reinforced his church yet. Not against the unspeakable evil he feared manifest in these two wraiths. They couldn't have what they'd come for. Romanov spun, turning his back to the trespassers as he made a bee-line for the tools he'd spread out in quasi-preparation, the implements he'd need to repel this unholy attack. The bell tolled the hour in the belfry as Romanov fumbled with his tools, casting a frantic glance back at the phantom in the mask and his stone-faced companion that had paused in the center of the aisle. This was the witching hour. The masked one spoke, calmly and cordially as if addressing an old acquaintance. Romanov was aghast, the flask in his hand falling to shatter on the floorboards. "We have no inclination to hurt you, Father Romanov. On the contrary. We've come to save you from the treachery that has poisoned your mind all these years. Deny the temptation that has poisoned your altars for so many blood-soaked centuries. Come now, join us, take in communion with the truly righteous lords of humanity." Masquerade punctuated his fervent invitation by kneeling at the center of the altar, casting hateful eyes toward the avatar of the Virgin Mary and the cherubs embellishing the lofty ceiling's vast mural. "That voice….it cannot be…." Romanov shivered behind his garments, head shaking woefully as his eyes transfixed on the form with the mask. He abruptly snapped from whatever revelation had shaken him, his eyes taking on a fiery new resolve. "You cannot have what you've come for, dark one! In the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost, I command you to leave this place!" His ultimatum had none of the effect he hoped for. The stone-faced one's expression was marked with disdain while a quizzical gaze flickered through the mask of the other. As the black-clad pair exchanged looks, Romanov heard a creak at the head of the dais. Goblets overturned and candles spontaneously sprung to life, their minute flames casting broad and sinister shadows. A vast umbra washed over the front of the cathedral as the smaller shadows shifted and danced about, the laughter of bodiless apparitions reverberating across the the great structure. Loathsome faces took shape amongst those distorted shadows, the many painted angels choked into concealment by lashing tentacles sourced of amorphous monstrosities now obscured in the background of the murals. "Our Father which art in heaven, hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come…" "Father, why plea to a God who lacks compassion? Surely you must realize the vanity of such an appeal. Father?" Still the blasphemy continued to take shape on the altar, tendrils of blackest night that unfurled from the recesses of shadows cast by flickering candles. The wind howled an unearthly scream beyond the threshold of the chapel, the darkness through the windows twisting and warping to form hunching demons duly borne of the most demonic planes. His voice took the form of a fevered appeal as his sanity waned in the face of incarnate evil. His training couldn't never prepare him for facing the real thing, not to such a hideous extreme. Masquerade's words barely registered as Yuri struggled to hold onto his diminishing sanity. "…Thy will be done in earth as it is in heaven…" Kendall and Masquerade appeared oblivious to the horrors manifesting around them, watching like gargoyles as he recited the invocation in a panic. More shapes had formed from the shadow and a great chorus of reverberant growls had overwhelmed the inside of the church. What Romanov had deemed a fortress against the Devil had quickly become an edifice reigned over by Hell's most powerful titans. "…Give us this day our daily bread…" "Another wretched ritual. You can recite any hollow litany you want, friend, but your pleas are falling on the ears of an idol both voiceless and deaf. Let go of this madness." Yuri had to ignore the ghouls that seeked to quiet his prayers. The shadowy masses blossomed into repulsive caricatures of the saints, the faces of God's most celebrated servitors imprinted on vaguely humanoid bodies that pulsed with mutation and the corruption of the abyss. The nightmare parade strutted around the pews as they echoed high-pitched screams of torment, their piercing cries causing the windows to erupt into clouds of glass shards. The horde of things that lurked beyond the walls skittered inside, their multitude of eyes locking ravenously on the frantic priest. "…And forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive them that trespass against us…" Blood gushed from the pores of his hands as he regarded them with terrified awe, the wetness beginning to slick his throat and trickle into his ears. He couldn't weather this kind of witchcraft, not even in His house. Something monstrous had taken hold of these grounds and consecrated the Lord's refuge with its infernal power. The enemy's siege had broken his stronghold; defeat was imminent, and defeat brought death. Still he uttered the words that had always brought him shelter against the storm, trying to ignore the beasts that snarled menacingly as they encircled him at the foot of the ringed pulpit. Masquerade and Kendall stood among their number, impassive and dismissive of the horror. Romanov couldn't help but tremble at the thought of them leading Hell's legions as he suspected may be more than rumor. "…And lead us not into temptation. But deliver us from evil…" Something formless plunged from the rafters and hurtled straight for Father Romanov, serrated talons outstretched as a black maw yawned open to engulf its prey. Eyes of radiant hellfire locked hungrily below upon descent just as a savage shriek rattled the church to its foundation. The abomination brought death on wings of wispy smoke as it pitched. The Lord's prayer ceased. "Amen." Kendall finished the blessing for the fallen priest. He'd never utter the word again. Moving quickly for the first time, Masquerade surged forward to kneel beside the Catholic agent. What was obscured beneath the flimsy pallid mask was made all too evident by the whitening of his fists. "Damn. We were so close…" Checking for a pulse, Masquerade found just what he anticipated. Another of the Lord's retainers, dead, the victim of a hysteria rooted in superstition. No blood, not so much as a bump on the pale flesh of the clergyman. A bad heart had spelled his end. "We're trying to help them, Kendall. Why this resistance?" He had seen the terror that had washed over Romanov's wrinkled countenance and felt only disappointment. Not pity or sympathy for the dead, merely diluted frustration. He much preferred cooperation over this. "More delusions? Maybe we can't see what is assaulting the men of the Church, my friend. The horror on his face was a deal more than fright over two unwelcome guests." Kendall surveyed the walls, the bold decor and brilliantly hued stained-glass windows pitching the chapel into a lavish aura he felt no religion worthy of openly flaunting. In the mind of Father Romanov, that environment had surely taken on a life of its own in the brief moments before his passing. There was no other plausible explanation for the fear that Romanov exuded in his final moments. "You'd think the old man had peered into the depths of Hell itself." "Vicars of a dogma gone mad, promoters of fear," said Masquerade. "Can they really be saved now? Is this a fruitless effort?" He was truly vexed at what was happening around them. Strongarm tactics hadn't worked in the past and bureaucracy proved to be an even greater failure. Mediating technique had done nothing to alleviate their efforts. "Perhaps it really isn't the world that's gone crazy, friend. Maybe we have. Perhaps we should stop consoling those who hate us and just let our actions speak for us. When they're saved, everything will change. We're going to remain exiles until judgment is passed." He had wanted to say that their past had condemned their future. Masquerade was perceptive enough to pick up on it through his friend's paltry assurances. Uncanny for a man ordinarily so candid. "Nonsense. They'll all bow. They must." He gestured absently toward a side door near the rear of the chapel. "We have precious little time. Retrieve the chalice and make haste about it. I have no intention of standing over the corpse of a holy man when someone walks in the door." He couldn't relent, not after so much progress. His name had brought venom to the lips of many and reverence to that of others, but in light of the veneration held globally for the man under the pale shroud, he'd rarely found himself at this crossroad. All that he had done in the past several years had been building toward what loomed ahead. In Shanty Jones, in Doctor Curiosity, in his enemies worldwide that would come together to strike down his ambition, nothing would prevail in this struggle that he didn't license in light of the circumstances. The man called Masquerade wasn't evil and he didn't long for a world that writhed on the cusp of its own slow death. There was a line between black-and-white; he was that line. Touching on the fringes of both had presented their vices and it was a cold fact that light needed dark as much as dark needed light. The stars were finally right. His fate was non-negotiable. The fires of perdition awaited his enemies while he was destined for the spoils of fidelity and absolute servitude. A phantom whisper echoed in his head, praising his efforts and his victories, foretelling of his ultimate revelry as it offered council against what would oppose him ahead. Infinite wisdom at his command. Dire devastation for whatever sought to usurp his inevitable conquest. He was the spark that would set the world ablaze, the savior of a devolving species gone astray. He couldn't relent. The stars wouldn't let it be.
