Like Pebbles on a Beach...

15 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-04-15 It was the soft, rhythmic sound of the waves sucking at the beach’s pebbles that lulled Ian Fetzer to sleep. It was the loud noise created by the snapping of bones under the impact of a large family saloon car that shook him from his reveille, and brought back the first of his memories. ------------------- It wasn’t a particularly warm day, and as such the beach was fairly empty. More to the point, the end of the beach surrounded by rocks and covered in more pebbles than sand was completely barren of humanity, save for Ian Fetzer. He sat there, listening to the sound of the waves dragging the small rocks back and forth. Almost hypnotic in tone, the sound was one of the reasons that this beach was the place that Ian went to when trying to think back, to reflect on recent events. Normally Ian would be able to think past his recent loss, and conjure up a smile, sure that soon his memories would come flooding back, and his winning streak would return. This time his face didn’t seem too prone to a smile though, which is unusual considering he just picked up his first wrestling victory in well over a year. In fact, as Ian Fetzer sat, cross-legged on the cold, hard stones, a single tear traced down his left cheek. The liquid traced a crooked line down his cheek before waiting at his upper lip. The Fool’s tongue lazily turned upwards and a rueful smile finally creased his lips as he tasted the saline. Ian Fetzer had defeated two men in the ring at once, both Robb Hardy and Justin Walker. He had taken the first step along the road to redemption, along the road to becoming a wrestling star once again. Despite all this, The Fool was not happy. He looked down at the beach, at the expanse of pebbles and sand, and he saw his mind there. Every time he thought it was coming back, that the pattern of it all looked familiar, a wave would come and change it all again. Ian felt like he was trying to solve a ten thousand piece jigsaw puzzle, but one where the shapes of the pieces changed every minute, and threw one another out of place when your back is turned. He was not so much looking for a needle in a haystack… he was looking for the haystack. He sighed once more and looked at the sand that poked through the layers of detritus. Those were his memories, the grains of sand hidden from view, always moving, never staying where they were for long enough. To get back to where he had been, The Fool would need to stop the waves. He would need to be able to stop them long enough to take away the pebbles from the stony beach, and find his memories again, intact, unharmed… complete. He knew this was impossible. Against Walker and Hardy he had not been able to stop the waves, he had just learned to surf them. This was no long term strategy, as there are waves that cannot be surfed, and one of them lurked on the horizon, a tidal wave named Syko Arsonist, just waiting to crash down and destroy both Ian Fetzer’s tournament chances, and the hope of regaining his memories. There are times when surfing won’t do, and when faced by an insurmountable problem, one natural instinct can be to just ignore it, to seek somewhere quiet and just let life pass you by, whilst you sit there; silent, alone and miserable. Such was the lot of Ian Fetzer, and that was the reason why his fist victory in over a year has not been celebrated with champagne, but rather with solitude and tears. As ever, he sat on the beach and reflected on his life, before the sound of the waves slowly took him away from his problems and into blessed sleep, or some close approximation of it. And so he stayed, until that idyllic respite was taken from him with the sound of an automobile accident occurring on the nearby street, an accident that caused dormant neurons to fire, synapses sparking after a gap of over a year, and a few precious memories returned to the brain of ‘The Fool’ Ian Fetzer. -------------------------- The sound was unmistakeably organic; there’s something about hearing an impact that makes you just know, deep down, that it involved the flesh of a living creature. It startled Ian Fetzer, and he jumped to his feet immediately. The sounds changed to include the screeching of tires and the crumple of metal on metal. Spying the stairs as the quickest way to the road that overlooked the beach, Ian ran to them and jumped up three at a time until he could survey the wreckage caused by the crash. It was hard to tell what had caused the incident, but the aftermath appeared to be a young girl lying on the floor in a truly unnatural position, and a black saloon car with a crumpled line right down the middle of it, where the lamppost had been struck. Bent metal pointed in all directions, and the sound of the collisions ran through The Fool’s mind time and time again as he walked slowly to look at the body on the floor. “Are you okay?” In retrospect, they were foolish words, as anyone with a leg bending to a right angle somewhere halfway up the shin bone is clearly not in the best of health. It was all Fetzer could think to say though, as his mind raced, and the smells of burnt rubber and blood mingled, bubbling up into his brain and bringing back the memory of the eighteen wheel truck that caused the amnesia in the first place. The girl on the floor started to move, her hands reaching down towards the horrendously broken leg. Soft, whimpering cries escaped from her mouth as a crowd started to gather, brought on by the evident chaos and trouble. From out of this group stepped forwards a man, one who recognised The Fool, and walked towards him in a purposeful manner. This was the man who is the closest thing to a friend that Ian Fetzer had. He was known in XUW arenas as either The Great Sombrero, or The X Factor, but here, outside the world of wrestling, he was simply David Hughes. “Ian, are you alright?” The Fool nodded, without taking his eyes off the poor girl, who was now being comforted by a few people that had approached her. Some of the others had gone to the car, looking to see if there was anything they could do for the driver. Ian blinked twice, and then started walking away, his mind still churning. “What’s wrong?” asked David. The Fool didn’t reply. His mind was now cast back to the fateful day that he had been crossing a road and Mister Action Figure had slipped from his grasp. He hadn’t even looked to see if anything was coming before darting back to pick him up. This bond with a plastic toy was his undoing, as the vehicle bore down on him, and even his extremely heightened reflexes couldn’t get his body out of harms way in time. Memories had returned to The Fool, but only bad ones, only ones that spoke of his demise, not of his previous glory. Dejected, hurt even more by this discovery, he slunk off back towards the beach. David Hughes looked around at the carnage and checked that the authorities had been notified before following him. He caught up with Ian at the foot of the steps back down towards the beach. Ian was sat there on the stone, his shoulders bobbing up and down, his eyes tearful through sorrow. It was as if a dam had been holding back his disappointment and sadness for the last year, and it had all come bursting through with the memories of his injury. “Ian, listen to me.” Nothing could help him now. He’d forever be a jobber, someone opening the shows, never recapturing the heroics that he craved, never being noticed as anything other than a figure of ridicule… “Ian, stop sitting around feeling sorry for yourself and listen!” The Fool raised his reddened eyes and looked at the man stood before him. The man in the cut-off jeans and leather jacket was trying to appeal to something within him, and as a friend, he really had to listen. “Ian, what are you going to achieve sitting here? Do you really think it’s all going to come back one day while you’re sat here, and you’ll magically become a fantastic wrestler again?” The Fool sat there, his chin resting in his hands, his eyes still tearful as they looked up at the man trying to save him. “Even if your memories came back, Ian, what’s to say it would be enough? You never fought anyone like Syko Arsonist before. Just about everyone knows who he is, and you’re just… well… you. Even at the height of your popularity you’d still be an underdog.” “I… but I…” stammered The Fool, trying to speak through his sniffling, but being unable to; his heart breaking at the thought of his victory being nothing but a false dawn. “Ian, you can win this match. You can beat Syko and move on, and there’s nobody you can’t overcome here, but you won’t do it by sitting here and looking at the pebbles. All the metaphors in the world won’t do you any good unless you make it all work for you.” “How can I do that though? Look around for more car wrecks? I feel so bad for the poor girl up there; she looked so hurt, so broken. It makes me feel worse, as I end up thinking about myself again instead of helping her” “I don’t know, Ian, but you’ve just got to keep on trying to turn it around, or you’ll end up here forever.” The Fool looked up at David Hughes again, an unspoken plea for help on his face. “Maybe if I stay here, maybe it’ll just come back to me.” Hughes shook his head again and looked in his pockets for something. The Fool turned away, staring into the distant sea, his face a picture of melancholy and hopelessness. Hughes shortly found a piece of paper, a receipt for something or other, and then produced a marker pen from his other pocket. He scribbled a message on the receipt and walked down to where Ian was sitting. “Here you go, maybe this will cheer you up?” The Fool looked at the paper, which had ‘XUW WORLD CHAMPION’ untidily scrawled on it. “What is this meant to be?” “You’re World Champion now. Doesn’t it make you feel better?” asked Hughes. “Is this some sort of a joke?” The Fool’s demeanour was turning dark for the first time in many, many months. David Hughes, in contrast, was smiling. “Oh yeah, very funny. Why don’t you make me a badge saying ‘World’s Biggest Loser’ too. Would that make you laugh?” stormed The Fool. “No Ian, but think about it. Why doesn’t this make you happy? Why isn’t it an achievement?” The Fool looked shocked, as if Hughes had asked him to explain how to walk down the street. “Why isn’t it an achievement? It’s not the real belt! The real belt is made of leather, and it has gold on it, lots of gold. It’s ornate, and it looks and feels brilliant. That’s why it doesn’t make me feel happy!” “So,” continued Hughes, “if I was to go out and steal the belt from the real World Champion, and then I gave it to you, would that make you happy?” “Of course not!” “Why?” “I wouldn’t have earned it! The belt means everything to the person who has earned it, but to me it would be nothing. It could never make me happy, just being given a title. You’ve got to earn things in this life, David! I know you used to be a thief, and I’m sure it’s not beyond you to steal the belt, but what will it prove? It’ll prove I couldn’t earn it myself. Nothing is worth having unless you have to work for it.” “Nothing?” “Nothing!” “Not even your memories?” enquired Hughes, politely. The Fool stopped, looking up at his friend once more, and starting to understand. “I worked for them the first time around. It’s someone else’s turn now.” Hughes moved closer to his friend and put an arm around him. “Nobody else can work to get your memories Ian. I’ll help you if you want the help, but to get them back you’ll have to think of something, and you’ll have to make an effort.” The Fool smiled, knowing he was defeated, but not wishing to admit it. “So, if I try hard enough it’ll all come flooding back?” “No Ian, and there’s no guarantee that they’ll ever come back, no matter how hard you try, but it’s got to be worth the effort, rather than coming down here and moping all the damn time.” Hughes held out a hand, and The Fool took it in one of his own, pulling hard to bring himself back up to his feet. His other hand went to his eyes and brushed away some of the moisture that was lingering. “You’re a good friend, David.” Hughes had the decency to look embarrassed as he pushed The Fool lightly on the shoulder and turned away. “No David, you are. You’ve been around for me in one way or another for quite some time now, and if there is a way for me to get back to my best, then I’m sure you’ll help me find it.” “I’ll try” replied Hughes. “Good, because I’ve just had an idea… no, make that two ideas!” The happy-go-lucky look returned to the face of The Fool, and at that time he didn't have a care in the world. Everything was an opportunity, and everything was going to end happily ever after... at least if he had any say in the matter.
