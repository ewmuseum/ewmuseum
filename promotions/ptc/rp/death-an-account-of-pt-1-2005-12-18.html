Death, An Account Of (Pt 1)

18 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-18 I’m sorry, you’ll have to leave… Leave… There seems to be a problem, and we’d appreciate no family members… Everything’s okay, isn’t it… Please, sir, we’d like you to leave now… Is she okay… We’re trying our best… Trying your best… Please, sir, this is the last time I’m asking… Now I’m stuck for words. For emotions. So, I leave. Walking away, I take a quick look back. Maybe the nurse was joking. With a giant grin, she’ll usher me back towards her. Nope. The doors are already shut, and that dreaded red bulb above the door is back on. It is her second operation today. Three in total if I count the one she had last week. The one she was scared about, the one I laughed at her for because "it’s nothing but a stomach ache." They’ve opened it up twice now, and they still don’t know what’s wrong. They said they’d find out soon enough; three times’ a charm, you know. "How is she?" I don’t have the energy to talk to anyone right now, so I’ll head straight for the toilets. "Armaan? Armaan? Did the doctor say anything? Armaan?" The toilets smell of badly fragranced soap, the kind specially designed for public toilets. The hot-water tap never really is hot, just like the cold-water tap that never really warns of the icy fountain it is about to unleash. Hot or cold, my hands still feel heavy. I wash them continuously, but it doesn’t help. I don’t remember the last time she smiled at me. Usually, I don’t mind staring at my face in the mirror. Apart from the regular scars I get working, I consider my face moderately handsome. I don’t think anyone would turn around for a second look, but they might appreciate the first glance. But this mirror seems to be quite critical of my appearance. Granted, I haven’t slept in three days, but my eyes shouldn’t be so red. I haven’t cried all day, all the tears have pretty much dried themselves out. I haven’t shaved in a week, so I’ll grant the mirror for its poor presentation of my stubble but, still, I didn’t think I’d look so bad. So I’ll check my face in the mirror beside this one. Then, the other one. Same expression. So I’ll check in all the mirrors. A man just walked in, and I can see him giving me a strange look. If I had more strength in my arms, I might have pushed him up against the wall and punched him twice in the stomach. Doesn’t he know that I’m allowed to do this? His mother is not lying in a hospital room, close to death. I am allowed to do any single thing I like. I don’t punch him. I’ll stop analysing my face in the mirror. That’s what people with no mothers dying do when they realise they are doing something stupid. Maybe now, he’ll think I’m just crazy. Fortunately, I leave the smell of bad soap inside the toilets when I leave them. Sujata is still sitting down where I told her, bless her. I don’t know why she doesn’t stop crying, she always has a tissue in her hand. I’ll sit beside her and tell her that everything is going to be okay, but it won’t work. Hasn’t. It’s only making her cry more, so I have to fuddle around for more tissues. During my search, I’ve found my mobile phone. It should have been off but then, well, my mother should be at home. Sujata has a fresh tissue now, so now I’ll probably go and check my text messages. I don’t like when my phone is blinking unnecessarily. I don’t like a lot of things right now. mumz ill. txt bak. sujata. It’s a pity I got the message after Sujata phoned. Reading a message would have been less painful than hear a nine-year-old crying down the phone. Though flying to India would not have been on my mind had I read only the text. I am here, though. hey, hope evrythings ok. just wondering when youre coming back, bb nxt week. let us knw whats going on so we can work around it. take care and best wishes for your mum. vik. BB? Birthday Bash. I don’t think I’m going there anymore. I think they can cope without me. Some people here can’t, and I’m here to help them. I say some, and mean three. My father’s at home, ill himself. He’d have wanted to be here like I am, but I’m carrying out the duty of two men right now. Wrestling can wait. GTT5 rd 2…you drew Tsonda…good draw, dude, should be a good fight. If ya need any tips, Im here as usual lol I’m going to delete these messages now, because they’re pointless. I don’t want to know about Birthday Bash or GTT. I don’t want to know anything. Maybe they’ll let me fly back to America by tomorrow, when my mothers doing okay, and I can talk to her. But I’m a logical person. Logic doesn’t connect with my mother getting better by tomorrow. I could sit here every day if I was allowed to, but I have to go home every night. They don’t seem to understand that I stay awake at night as it is. I’ve been staring at the ceiling for the past six days, which would explain the highly-exaggerated bags. There’s plenty of time to sleep, but not now. My phone rings on rare occasions. When it does, it is a momentous occasion. Now, it’s just a terrible disturbance. Hello? "Hi, Armaan, it’s Alec." No time to talk right now; mother dying. Hello, Alec, a surprise to hear from you. "Well, I’ve especially made a long-distance call to talk to you." I’ll put the phone down now. Was there something in particular you wanted to talk to me about? "Yeah…GTT5 is a week away." Yes, I know. "Great, you know. Because people are a little worried because we haven’t had a peep from Armaan yet." I see. "Any reason why you’ve stayed low these past few days?" My mother is fighting for her life in an intensive care unit. She doesn’t seem to have long left to live. A family problem. "Oh right. So, when will you be flying back to the States to get ready for the match?" I’m sorry? "For your match…against Chandler Tsonda. You know you’re wrestling Tsonda, right?" Yes. "Great…well, as you know, I deliver the words of the guys up top, and they want this match to happen. They’re looking forward to it. But we need you in America for that." I’m afraid it’s a little difficult. When having to choose over a wrestling match and my mother’s life, I usually tend to take the latter option. I’m afraid it’s a little difficult. My mother’s condition is quite serious. "Oh…" They pause when they’re thinking of something valid to say. "…so, will you be back within the week?" I’m usually quite reserved on the phone, pretty much as I am in person. I feel like shouting now, however, and I may have done, but I’m distracted by a running Sujata. "Armaan! Armaan!" What is it, Sujata? "The doctor’s calling you."
