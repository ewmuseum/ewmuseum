The Search Begins

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 The sun began to set on the deserts surrounding Las Vegas, sending the spindly shadows of the scrub and wind-blasted rock formations across the still-warm sands. There was a great cacophony of caws and screeches of various kinds, as the birds that were active during the day found places to nest, out of reach of the coyotes that were now howling in the distance. Another predator awoke with them, as the last rays of light disappeared behind the rocky hills. Virgil Ambrosi had been wandering the desert for nearly a week now, his search for his beloved having eliminated the city some time ago. For days, he’d awoken with the night, despite the fact that he was able to move about during the day to some extent these days, because he knew that if she was, in fact, out here somewhere, she’d likely keep to her habit of sleeping at night, and thus would become a stationary query. However, he reflected as he climbed the steep rock incline that lead out of the cavern he’d been using for basic shelter, that did little to change the fact that she was seemingly leaving behind no tracks at all, of any kind. The only force he found himself able to rely upon at all (however little) was a nagging sensation in his mind that seemed to lead him in certain directions, a sort of gut instinct that had, as of yet, not been very successful. Had he a limited time span to search, hopelessness surely would have set in by now. However, being immortal had its advantages. ---------- The dusty ground crunched lightly underneath his footsteps, as he left his little outpost to search blindly once again. All he could see was the vague movement of the coyotes and nocturnal rodents, scurrying about on their own missions, and the occasional rattlesnake, coiled up in thick patches of weeds. They wouldn’t even make proper meals, so he ignored them. Even the coyote’s sensitive nose was no match for his tracking ability, so even the off chance of one of them stumbling upon her hiding place was miniscule. Lia, known to most of the Primetime Central world as Shadow, had gone missing nearly a month and a half ago. Originally, it had been on her accord, and Virgil understood her reasons for it. There was simply too much for her to deal with in the little world that was Global Wrestling, and thus, she had decided to leave it. Perfectly understandable, Virgil had thoughts of doing so reasonably often as well… but the problem had come when rather than finding herself, she appeared to be losing it. A rat fled before his resolute walk, dashing off into the shadows to watch him pass in fear. He wasn’t certain by any means where he was going, but it had been that way for several nights, now. Eventually, were she anywhere in the area, there would have to be some sign of her presence… or so he hoped. It had all started when she began showing up to her matches in Global, not quite seeming to be herself. Her face had lost the cold, appraising gaze that she set upon most people, but more importantly, the comforted, pleased look that his presence seemed to elicit. It had simply confused him at first, but quickly, it had begun to worry him. She showed no love, no hate, no sympathies of any kind, and never spoke a word to anyone. She simply showed up, fought her match (with what seemed like increasing fury each time), and left. Even waiting for her to leave by whatever transportation she’d used to get to the arena proved worthless, as she always seemed to slip out undetected somehow. Every time he spoke to her, or anyone spoke to her, she wouldn’t react, wouldn’t even look in their direction. It was almost as though she was in a trance of some sort, walking the halls of the Global arena, pulled by some force beyond her control. And that was the reason that his trust had begun to wear. She was a very conscious and attentive being, and the apparent loss of this trait made Virgil wonder if, in fact, she was under someone else’s control. The thought seemed paranoid, and very foolish. A creature of her power was not one to be easily manipulated by anything, be it man, machine, vampire, or force of nature. The simple existence of one more powerful than her anywhere in the area would have set the local packs into a panic, and they likely would have attacked said being en masse. Such things had not occurred, obviously. He trusted no one to accompany him. Dawn McFarlane had expressed some interest in doing so, but Virgil had dismissed the request as absolute folly, having no desire to carry the extra weight of a human on his shoulders. Thus, he had gone alone. The desert surrounding Las Vegas seemed like an incredible amount of space to cover, and in truth, would be, save for the fact that Virgil knew at least one thing: She was still getting in and out of Las Vegas by motorcycle. That left only so many routes to take, and he had wasted his time with two of them previously, his inquiries turning up not so much as a memory among anyone in the area, much less any kind of tangible evidence of her presence. That had left the lonely two-lane highway that he now walked, the only reminder of civilization to be seen, save a small building around a mile away. He expected to find little once he got there. Even the question of the building actually being occupied was up in the air. But, searching was the only thing he could do, even if it was fruitless. Waiting in despair would leave him with nothing. GTT5 would keep him distracted from misery long enough to find her, he hoped. ---------- The red neon sign buzzing above the dark-red, chipped door read "Hell’s Mouth", and occurred to Virgil as a reasonably appropriate title. After all, what better place for Hell to be than in the middle of a lonely desert, where there was no comfort, no sustenance, and no companionship? He looked around the side of the building from the opposite shoulder of the highway, and only saw a run-down pickup truck parked on the side of the building. Dirt had collected around the bottom of the wheels. Obviously, this truck hadn’t moved in weeks. He crossed the empty highway without fear of oncoming traffic, as he’d become convinced that none could exist in this place, and stepped up to the door He turned the doorknob, as many others had done before, shown by the brass laquer having been completely peeled away, and opened the door, immediately blasted by a wave of cold air from inside. There was no one inside, save a man Virgil assumed to be the bartender, polishing a glass that had likely seen no use in years, if this place had always been so remote. He beamed a wide smile at Virgil, which Virgil did not return as he sat down on a squeaky barstool. "Good evening, stranger." A thick Siberian accent surprised Virgil, causing him to turn his eyes up at the still-grinning man, raising an eyebrow. "Is it not a little hot for a Russian here?" The bartender looked slightly impressed. "Ah… very few recognize me as more than ‘a foreigner’ at first." "I’ve heard my share of accents, I suppose." The bartender nodded. "An educated fellow, I see. We rarely get those types here." Virgil glanced behind him briefly, at the racks of liquor on display- an oddly large amount for such a tiny bar. "I’d assume most know better than to wander about in the desert long enough to find the place." The bartender held out his hand. "Excuse my rudeness, I forgot to introduce myself. I am Dominik." Virgil shook his hand, an old alias coming into play once again. "Gideon." "Well, Gideon, can I get you a drink?" Virgil shook his head. "I rarely drink, no offense." Dominik raised an eyebrow. "Then why are you out here, of all places?" Virgil blinked, glancing around the bar once again, mostly out of curiosity. "I’m looking for someone." Dominik looked puzzled, and set the well-polished glass underneath the counter. "In the middle of the desert?" "I never said I expected to find her anytime soon… though perhaps you’ve seen her." Dominik shrugged. "It’s possible, but we get a lot of folks in here." As Virgil turned back to look at Dominik, he noticed a sign carved above the bar: Abandon all hope, ye who enter here… He couldn’t possibly imagine this being true. No liquor in existence was worth the time it took to get here from Las Vegas, just to drink it in a miserable and run-down building with a suspicious-looking bartender. "She is a reasonably tall woman, very fair of skin, with long, black hair, and bright green eyes. She probably would have arrived here by motorcycle, although that’s simply my conjecture." Dominik scratched his chin, and looked up at the ceiling, in what Virgil considered to be a slightly showy way to think, almost as though he recognized her immediately, but was attempting to appear strained to recall. Why this could be, however, Virgil was not sure. "You know, I did see a woman that looked like that about a month ago." Virgil’s attention was immediately drawn from the bar to Dominik’s eyes, searching for some kind of disingenuous gesture. "Yeah, she came in here and ordered some water, stayed and chatted for a few seconds, and then left. I don’t know what it was, but something about her seemed a little weird when she was on her way out. It was like she was in a hurry to get away from something. I figured it was the cops, we get a lot of those types out here." Virgil nodded, intrigued. "Did you, perhaps, happen to hear where she might have gone? Or, possibly, what direction she headed in?" Dominik shook his head, and Virgil’s expression soured. "Sorry… that I don’t know. We were busy that day, and I didn’t catch her leaving." "I see." "I’m sorry, I wish I could help you out a bit more. I hate seeing a man chasing after a lady he can’t find, or who doesn’t want him to find her." Virgil glowered, and Dominik held up his hands. "Hey, hey, I didn’t mean anything by that." "I know, I’m just a bit discouraged at the moment." He pushed up from the barstool, and turned toward the door. "Thank you for your help, though." He started to head out, before he heard Dominik’s voice once again. "Here, you might need this. It gets hot out there, even this time of year." Virgil turned just in time to avoid being cracked on the back of the head by a plastic bottle of water, catching it, and eyeing it warily. "It’s just water, no need to look at it like that." Dominik smirked, and Virgil nodded. "Thank you." And now, unimpeded, he headed back outside, noting the hollow echo of the wooden floor underneath his feet. There was almost certainly something underneath this building, and to Virgil, it also seemed almost certain that Dominik had not told him the entire truth, despite his overly sincere appearance. He looked at the bottle of water again, and decided against drinking any of it for the same reason that he didn’t believe Dominik's story. Shows of real kindness were rare these days, and he wasn’t going to take chances. He’d be making another visit to Hell’s Mouth very soon…
