The Test of the Curse

3 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-03 Walking up to the grassy intersection of two familiar but infrequently used roads brought back memories flooding Megumi "Butterfly" Hamada's mind. Traveling east, you cross a hill and in the distance you will see the misty lake of Akino, as well as the little town that shares its name. Travel the road west, and you are walking toward the base of the Nagano Mountains where another road heads up into the cloudy treetops where Butterfly once called home. But there was nothing there anymore, so now she called Akino her new home. She headed west down the road. From a distance, Akino's small fishing boats could be seen pulled from the water. Around the lake there were festival paper lanterns lit. Butterfly had to squint to see through the mist and the afternoon sun, but she could just make out the rocky green island that stood in the middle of the lake. The closer Butterfly came the better she could see the little bridge that connected the island of Miyashina to the shore of the lake. Skirting rice paddies and ditches in the road, Butterfly approached the village passing under old fishing nets where vineyards of grapes grew. A lonely shrine to the Buddha sat by the roadside, its oranges moldy and its incense long burned out. As she reached the village square, decorated with white iris flowers and paper lanterns, she noticed that no one was around. The place was strangely desolate. There were no signs of life, but then Butterfly's attention was spurred by muffled voices coming from the village meeting hall. She walked toward the hall through the mists, which even at midday never completely lifted from Akino. Butterfly thought back to the days she would come down from the mountain in-between those rare moments when she got a break from training. Akino was a second home to her in many ways, this little village on the west bank of a sacred lake. And once a year they would have the Iris Festival, and Butterfly remembered those days the best. For two weeks, all the fishing boats would be pulled up on land, and iris petals are thrown into the water to keep the nature spirit of the lake appeased. For two weeks no boat can enter the water, as the lake belongs to the nature sprits alone. Butterfly found this very romantic and traditional. The lake looked beautiful during that time of year, covered in the white iris flowers. And it was a peaceful and happy time in her past celebrating along with the villagers in traditional and time-honored festival. Butterfly entered the meeting hall, which was also the home of the village spokesperson, Yasu. The pungent smell of herbs met her nostrils, as well as the sound of muffled coughs and the quiet whispers of conversation. She was met with a low bow by an elder village fisherman, who took her hand and led her across the meeting room and toward a pair of sliding doors. Old, weary, gray eyes looked up at her and she felt their desperation. "What is going on here?", she asked to herself. The elder led Butterfly through the sliding doors into a bedroom. In the center of the room a man lay on a futon, attended by his wife and two elderly women. His eyes were closed until his wife whispered into his ear; then he opened his eyes and sat up. His cordial bow and smile did not mask his pain from Butterfly, and she winced a little as he lay back down and began to speak. "Welcome child. Are you the little girl who used to live on the mountain?" the man asked her without flinching. "You remember me," proclaimed Butterfly in surprise. "It has been almost seven years since I last visited your village. I do not recognize you, however." "Nor should you, child.," said the man's wife. "It is but a coincidence that the Hamada clan's closest relative would be the one he saw in his dreams." "The daughter of Nikkou," the man said in a dry whisper. "Who would have known. The spirits here and very wise and knowledgeable." "What spirits? And what dream? I came to Akino for a small vacation before I go back west. It is like my second home here. I thought I would come and pay a visit, remember the old days. My first home atop the mountain was destroyed." Butterfly thought back to the smoky ruins of her house high up on the Nagano Mountains, seven years ago. "I am still studying the art of wrestling. But now I am working in America." She smiled, her travels a big accomplishment in her eyes. "And I was privileged to be picked to participate in a wrestling tournament, Golden Turnbuckles Tournament 5. It is a great honor. I did not know I was expected here in Akino. I am only here for two days before my plane leaves. In fact, I am surprised you would know that I was coming here at all. It has been so long. And I did not tell any--" "The spirit of the lake told me you were coming, someone like you. And here you are," said the man weakly. "I don't understand," said Butterfly. "I am Yasu, myoshu of Akino. Consider ourselves at your disposal. I am sorry to have bothered you on your way back home, but I have a situation that only you can help me with." He nods his head and his wife and the two village women bow and leave the room. "It is the curse," he said with a whisper, "and I am beyond being cured." "Curse?" she said aloud. This man so sick with fever he believed he was cursed, she thought. The people of the mountains and valleys still believed in spirits and luck and superstition. But to be made sick by them? There had to be a simple explanation. "Our family founded this village," the sick man continued, "and our household has always led the people who live here. Thirty years ago…" he closes his eyes. When he opens them again, they stare blankly at the white walls of the bedroom. "Thirty years ago, my grandfather Yoshi banished Black Genki from Akino for killing a merchant named Fuuka. Genki had been a loyal villager in the past, while the merchant was of a dubious character, but Genki refused to explain why he had killed Fuuka. We all heard the terrible scream that night." "When we found Fuuka, he looked like he had been mauled by an animal. Genki stood over the merchant, staring at his blood-soaked hands with a crazed expression and repeating that he had been shamed, over and over." "The Curse of Miyashina?" Butterfly said under her breath. She had heard of this old wives tale, but she knew it as a way for the elders of the village to keep their children from wandering off into the forest jungle or try to head to the island that stood to the east of the village. "You know of it then, girl?" "It is but a myth, a bedtime story." "If only that were true. Genki was tried immediately for the murder of Fuuka. The people were afraid that perhaps he would use the same magic he had killed Fuuka upon them. Black Genki was given a choice. Death or banishment. He chose to be banished from the village. No explanation, nothing. Black Genki gathered up his work, his equipment from his shack, took his family marker from the temple and left that night. He said nothing, not a word in his defense. Among our people, death is preferable to banishment in those days. But Genki chose to go to the island of Miyashina. And he was never heard from again." "So this is where the curse of the island story came from. A banished murderer. It did well enough to keep me from going to that island," said Butterfly with a smile, trying to cheer up the sick old man. "You think this is a story. You think this is a tale we use to frighten our children. 'The curse of Miyashina will get you if you do not eat all of your watercress!' Ha ha, I wish it were so, but no." The old man looked over to her and smiled, trying to meet her halfway, but the truth in his tone led him back down a serious path. "The curse is as real as you are, here in my home, as my dreams told me you would be." "What dreams do you keep speaking about?" asked Butterfly curiously. "The dreams that began one week before my grandfather fell ill and died. Two months ago. We thought it was old age, that his time had finally come to leave this world.. But then the dream came again, and I saw Black Genki, twisted like a half-man and half-animal. And he stood over my father's bed. My father was sweating and calling out to his family, and Black Genki reached out with a clawed hand to break his neck." His voice rasps. "But then, a shadow fell upon the room, one like that of a great moth who might get too close to the flame of a candle. In the window stood a young warrior, a woman. And she had gossamer wings and she looked at Black Genki and he knew, without saying a word, that she was here to end his curse upon my family. Three days later and my father died. This was but last week." "Oh no!" gasped Butterfly in shock. "I am so sorry." "I contracted the fever but two days ago. In my darkest hours, I have the same dream now as before. But the heroine, the one who is supposed to stop the curse of Black Genki who has hated my family for so many decades, has her own obstacles in her path. To become stronger, to gain experience, she has to defeat her own test. It is like her own curse. A curse for a curse. And the farther she travels, the harder her test becomes." "A test?" says Butterfly quizzically. She becomes tired of playing this guessing game, only because it continues to hit closer and close to home. "You must break Black Genki's curse. Our village needs your help. My family needs your help. Avenge my family and stop this curse, I beg you. I have two sons…" Yasu sighs and his eyes close but the pain doesn't leave his face. One of the elderly villagers reenters the room. "Now you know our plight," he says. "This is only a humble fishing village, but it seems that its fate and yours are somewhat intertwined, eh. My name is Kiyo, and I am the majordomo of the village. I remember you, young Butterfly, when you would come down from the mountain as a young girl." "You remember me?" asked Butterfly, with a shy smile. "As I got older I started to come less and less, but I always visited during the Iris Festival. The lake looked so beautiful with all the flowers floating upon it." "Maybe this is why Yasu dreamed of you," said the old man as he led her out to the village square. "The Iris Festival is to honor the spirit of the lake, Akino. Perhaps it was Akino that sent these dreams of you to Yasu. Perhaps the spirit remembers the young girl who came down from the mountain to visit during her festival. And perhaps it is your fate, your destiny to save the village from the curse of Black Genki." "This all seems very far-fetched. Besides, I am only here in Akino for one day. Then I am off to the west where I am going to have my first match in a highly regarded fighting tournament." "Then perhaps that is the first test that you must pass in order to become prepared for your battle against the curse. For in order for you to defeat Black Genki, you must travel to the island of Miyashina by foot through the jungle along the foot of the mountain." The old man pointed out over the trees, into the mists surrounding the countryside and off toward the green island in the lake. "Your journey must start as soon as you pass your first test it would seem. Yasu will live through the week, perhaps. His father died in barely three days. Yasu was touched by the spirit of the lake, however. He dreamed of the hero who would come to stop the curse. Perhaps Akino sent him the dream-warning to show him the way to defeat the curse. Regardless, his life and the fate of his family is in your hands." "There has to be some mistake. Yes, I used to live up in the mountains with my mother where she taught me the ways of the fighting styles she had practiced all her life. And yes, I had come to Akino many times. It was the closest town to our home, the only one within many miles. And as a little girl, Iris Festival was my favorite time of year to spend near the lake." Butterfly looked down at the ground for a moment, as memories stirred up emotions of her childhood and her mother. "And as much as I believe that all things in this world have a spirit, such as a tree, or a bird, or even a lake, I don't believe that these spirits can talk to people or send them dreams. I also do not believe in curses." "So then you do not accept your destiny? You do not wish to help us?" "I want to help you, but I do not know how," she said with a guilty look on her face. "But chasing superstitions into the misty jungle won't help save a sick man. And as far as my destiny, it is now held in neutral judgment until my first match in GTT5. The level of my honor depends greatly on how well I do in this tournament. Akino is a special place for me, always. But I have already cast my lot." "It is as the dream said it would be," piped back the old man. "You have your first test. Pass it, and you can come back to us and help us defeat Black Genki." "How is Doreen Pepperpot my first test," Butterfly said more to herself, thinking about the GTT5 tourney ahead. "What does Pepperpot have to do with this? A test? This is confusing the heck out of me. There's no doubt that this woman is a monster inside the ring. She outweighs me, she is taller then me, has the reach advantage, and is a bitter woman who seems to enjoy inflicting pain on others. This would be a test of my abilities regardless." "This match will make you stronger," said the elderly man with conviction. "Focus your abilities, call forth your inner-strength. Defeat your opponent and you will learn. Lose, and you will learn, but the price is higher. Lose, and our small village here is doomed." "Everything to lose," said Butterfly to herself. She shook her head, turned, and smiled at the majordomo. "I will see you right after my match in the tournament is over. Win or lose, I will come back to end the curse of Black Genki." Butterfly looked out over the lake. She felt the spirit out there, somewhere. Just like she remembered when she was a little girl. "You have my word."
