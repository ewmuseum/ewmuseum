Prologue to the Great Game

29 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-29 "They want a statement." He turned from the window, one hand resting atop the windowsill as his fingers drummed rhythmically on the lacquered frame. A grin passed across his face, veiled under the pallid mask that concealed his face. "Of course. Did you not grant them a timeframe as I requested?" The servant nervously clicked his fingernails together as he offered a meager shrug. His mannerism spoke of a man who knew little beyond meekness and subservience, respectable yet lamentable at the same time in the eyes of the masked beholder. Even though undoubtedly accustomed to the company of his eccentric employer and the many colorful guests he commonly hosted, the young man was obviously a tad unnerved in the presence of the enigma behind the veil. The one by the window expected to see beads of sweat form on his brow if this audience was extended much longer, a coronary if he so much as raised his voice at an inappropriate moment. "They're rather persistent, sir." Apprehension radiated from him as strongly as the cheap cologne he'd seemingly taken a bath in. "We've tried but they just won't stop. They're a relentless lot. I told them two days and you'd release a statement, but they just won't quit…." He silenced at a composed nod from the other. The 'wrestling paparazzi' had flooded the estate from the moment Avery Shields made the announcement; it wasn't every day that an eccentric millionaire provided asylum for a masked wrestler requesting anonymity in a high stakes competition. Even now he had been watching as Shields' flock of manservants impatiently shooed away a small pack of their overbearing kind from the front expanse of the grounds, just as they had all morning prior. "They will be patient just as I must be. I only regret to know that they may be rather disappointed when all is at an end." The downtrodden expression on the servant's face wasn't one that Shields' mysterious guest was unused to. He'd seen it often and knew well of the sentiments it echoed. "Shields will be well-compensated for his generosity. As such, so will all who serve him in this house. Believe me when I say that all of your efforts won't go in vain or without reward." The enigmatic guest grinned behind the pallid mask as he offered his promises. Alleviating stress in his benefactor's overworked retainers was a must at such an early stage of his planning and he could not have them feel deceived or neglected, lest all could fall apart. "Trust in me, friend. I offer you my pledge as one of the blessed blood." He rounded his gaze on his own faithful associate, a middle-aged man with a round face who supported all his statements with a consistent nod. Maury Dawson was fervently loyal, sometimes to a fault perhaps, but he had already proved invaluable to the designs of the man now bearing the mask. One of many to come he was sure. "I can assure you of the same," concurred Dawson. He'd likely endorse anything the the other would, knowledge or no, just as he did now. In truth, even he wasn't completely knowledgeable about his mysterious patron, but he felt obliged to help him. "Our gratitude will be felt ten-fold when this is all over." Striding across the room, Masquerade pressed a handful of bills into his caller's hand. He could not have these people fearing him, scorning him for the riddle he was to them. Only time would buy him the luxury of trust and understanding, though a few greenbacks always seemed a ready substitute, if however temporary. And judging by the smile on the face of their nervous visitor before he rushed off to aid in fending off the investigators invading the property, it did its job for the time-being. Dawson set his eyes on the clouds rolling across the horizon, one hand caressing the bushy mustache on his lip. "You've never told me your goal exactly……Mister Chaney." The name he had assigned Maury to address him as, a reference to to the celebrated Lon Chaney Jr., the Man of A Thousand Faces from times past in early Hollywood. "I'm not a man who likes to pry, but that's not to say I don't have my own curiosities I'd like to have satiated." An educated man as well by the language and manner of his speech, traits Masquerade held with reverence in his closest allies. In this game where secrets and diplomacy could prove to be the foundation of one's rise or ruin, he would need his accomplice more than any other could know. For time, for anonymity, for the attainment of his ultimate objective. Maury Dawson was his lifeline. He rued the fact that his answers were required to be so bare at this point. The masked man fingered the range of masks he had aligned in his wardrobe. They all illustrated varying emotions and mentalities, as would he as GTT5 progressed. Even now as he pondered over what he had seen of Shadow, he considered his first course of action in the great masquerade. What guise would he don as he approached the inaugural battle? Which would be an appropriate exhibition for one so easily dismissed and omitted amongst the tournament field? "We all have our secrets," came the solemn reply as his hand came to rest on one his favorites. "I'm just a man trying to do good. Trying to do good in the only way I know…."
