Dr Faustiosity - Chapter 2: Contracts and Cells

8 December 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-12-08 Unseasonal rain began to fall in the desert. Something or someone seemed to be playing tricks on the world. Above the ruins of the Curious Laboratory there was a hole in the clouds, where the force of the calling had vaporised the… vapours, and cleared the drab night sky. The ragged circle of clarity was slowly eroded, the heavens clearly intent on dampening the ground below. Intermittent spots fell on Doctor Curiosity, blown by the wind, as he stood in his laboratory. The roof and ceiling had gone from above him, and the wall directly in front of him was also missing. The doctor stood opposite a ragged figure, grotesque in nature. Burned flesh hung off his body, as wretched a being as ever existed. “I called you here to make a deal,” were the words spoken by Doctor Curiosity. The lipless face of the creature sneered at him. It spoke, the insect nature of the voice dying away to be replaced with a smoother timbre, a human voice. “A deal, you say? Who are you to make deals?” “Who am I? I am someone strong enough to have brought you here,” said Doctor Curiosity, hoping that his fear didn’t show through in his voice. The creature paused, and the torn and tattered flesh over its body seemed to start healing, as if the very air were giving it nourishment… or if perhaps the words of the doctor were doing likewise. “Do you know the price?” it said. “I’ve heard the stories.” “They were true,” replied the creature, which now seemed almost perfectly human. The doctor blinked his eyes, and in the flicker of his eyelids, clothes appeared from nowhere and wrapped themselves around the creature’s body. Curiosity was naturally impressed, and also a little scared. When playing with forces such as this, a mistake wouldn’t leave you with just burnt fingers and the knowledge to no longer pick up Mommy’s frying pan. “So,” continued the doctor. “You’re a demon?” “What did you expect to find lingering around the depths of Hell?” replied the demon. The doctor was surprised, so much so that he tried to make a step backwards. The large oak desk at his back prevented him. He looked around nervously, before remembering that the demon stood within a cage, a pentagram that he had drawn. His confidence boosted, the doctor started to walk around the floor. It seemed to still be steady enough to take his weight, which was another relief for him. “You’re pretty smart for somevon who can’t move more zan a couple of feet, demon. Now vhy don’t you just leave ze tzinking to ze clever people?” The demon inclined his head, the smile never leaving his face. Curiosity was behind it now, but the demon stood perfectly still. “You see, I’ve been tzinking, and I’m not entirely sure about giving avay my immortal soul. I’ve become fairly attached to it over ze years.” The demon’s voice was still changing, from that of a normal man to that of the slickest of salesmen. Every syllable was alluring, every sound coated with honey and sugar. “Of course you have, and why wouldn’t you? It’s a great soul you’ve got there. You could do a lot of things with a soul like that…” Doctor Curiosity stopped pacing. The pause at the end of the sentence had left him hanging on. He wanted to know what the demon would say next. “Of course, you’d be getting a good price for a soul like that. None of that ‘three wishes’ crap for you, sir.” The doctor nodded, and the demon’s smile grew even wider. Curiosity was hooked. “Jah! I vill be able to vanquish Dave Hurst and Craig Goat, jah!” “You’ll be able to do loads of great things,” said the demon, choosing his words carefully. “It’ll be a fantastic deal.” Curiosity was stepping forwards to take the proffered hand of the demon, when a small part of his mind told him to stop. His eyes rolled heavenwards as he tried to work out what was going on. Ah yes, the demon had been clever, and tried to sway him from his plan. Doctor Curiosity smiled and stopped just short of the pentagram. “Nice try, demon.” “I prefer to be called Mephistophilus,” interjected the demon. “Vhatever your name is, you vill not fool me.” “Is that so?” “Absolutely. Now, you listen to me and you listen vell. I have studied your kind, and have found zat demons are notoriously bad at keeping zeir ends of a deal.” “Really?” replied Mephistophilus, arching one jet black eyebrow over a deep red eye. “You’ve read that demons are less than honest with some of the fools that call us up? Well, we’d best never let that secret get out!” “Zere is no need for sarcasm, Mesi… Mephli… demon!” “Well, what did you expect?” “Demon, you vill listen to me! I studied customer complaints vhen I vas a student. I know my rights!” “I don’t think that any of those laws apply to demons.” “Vell, zey apply to telemarketers… you can’t be much vorse zan zem…” “Touche.” “Now, vhat I vant is some sort of trial.” “That normally comes at the end of the affair,” remarked the demon. “Qvit interrupting me vith your snide comments, Mepi… Murray… Merrick.” “My name is Mephistophilus.” “No longer!” declared the doctor. “You are now renamed Merrick, for it iz much easier for me to say… and before you got dressed you looked just like ze elephant man.” The demon rolled his eyes. He was now looking almost as human as Doctor Curiosity himself. His wounds had healed and he was smartly attired in a dark suit, with the collar of his white shirt unbuttoned. The only thing that gave him away was the red sheen of his skin, still unable to mimic humanity completely. Still, the name that he was called by did not matter, so long as the contract got signed. “So, as I vas saying,” continued the doc. “Jah, I vant a trial of ze promises you give.” “What do you mean?” “Vell, I vill give you my soul in return for ze power you promise, but only if I am allowed to test it out first. Give me a few days of vhat it vould be like, and if it is something zat I enjoy, zen my soul is all yours.” The demon looked puzzled, but soon recovered. He had not been tempting the darker sides of humanity for millennia without learning a thing or two. All he had to do was give the doctor what he wanted for a few days, and then when the contract came to be signed… well maybe it would have a few extra clauses in it. Doctor Curiosity studied Merrick’s face. He looked confused, and that was the doctor’s plan. He had read all the books, and nobody ever came out ahead of a demon in these kind of negotiations. Well, he was going to change all that. Perhaps there would be one of the ‘poetic justice’ type put in there, mused the demon. The publicists in Hell loved those. He’d string poor Merrick along with promises until the match was over, and he was the GTT4 champion. He’d have his miracle and then Merrick could be sent packing. The demon smiled. He would, of course, be sure to cancel all promises right before any important dates his victim… no, his client might have. Doctor Curiosity stepped forwards, extending a hand to the demon. It was all going to be almost too easy. Merrick stretched an arm out and laughed. It was all going to be almost too easy. --------------------------------------------------------- Some miles away, Frank was rubbing one hand across the scar on his lip. He sat in the passenger seat of a moderate sized van, and awaited the return of one of his colleagues. Behind him sat eight of the toughest men he’d ever come across. Some were good with knifes, and others with fists. Some had experience in bar-room fighting where a broken bottle might be your best hope of immobilising an enemy, and a couple of them even kept guns on their person. Frank turned and sighed with relief as the man he had been waiting for opened the door and slipped into the driver’s side. He had with him a large sports bag, which he threw over the back of the seats. It landed with a loud metallic clang on the floor of the van. The thugs in the back started to smile. They were in the presence of a professional here. Most of them had never been provided with weapons before. Some of the less mentally agile had never even thought to use weapons before. Before they could get too excited, Frank turned round and fixed them all with a steely glare. “No, I don’t want any of you to fuck this up. We don’t want to kill this guy… yet.” The men in the back nodded their assent, grudgingly. “His wife borrowed quite a bit of cash off us. Now, she’s dead, but all is not lost. Turns out she was actually married. Now, her husband’s a bloody strange one. He’s a wrestler by trade, but he doesn’t look like he should be.” One of the hoodlums in the back tried to stifle his laughter, but failed. Frank’s eyes were on him in the smallest fraction of a second. The laughter died down. “Why do you laugh, Eric?” asked Frank. “Well…” stuttered the hapless hood. “He’s a wrestler, yeah?” “Yes he is.” “Ummm… I don’t know if you didn’t get told… but it’s not real.” Frank closed his eyes and tried to count to ten. “Yeah, they’re not strong or tough or anything,” continued Eric. Frank got to five before he cracked. “Look, I fucking know that those fancy shows they do on television are scripted, but do you know anything about amateur wrestlers?” “Are they the ones not good enough to get in the… aarrgghh!” Eric had started to make a joke, before a slim man in a trench coat, and wearing a balaclava, grabbed his arm and twisted it. Eric was down to the floor in an instant, and the smaller man commenced on wrenching Eric’s shoulder joint in an unusual direction. “Ahhhhh! Help! Get him off of me!” yelled Eric. Frank nodded, and the man in the balaclava sat back down on the side with the other men, who were all looking at him with a newfound respect. “Eric, you just met Anthony. He’s a former amateur wrestler who was doing very well for himself before a certain drugs scandal. Now, would you care to repeat your assertion that wrestlers are weak in front of him?” Eric looked down at the ground and meekly shook his head, all the while rubbing his sore shoulder. “I thought not. Now, gentlemen, tomorrow we’re going to pay this man, Chris Goodman, a visit. He calls himself Doctor Curiosity and he lives in some stupid mansion out in the desert. He’s an unorthodox fellow, so we’re going to be very careful. Don’t take anything for granted, and this will go off smoothly. You can never be too careful.” The men in the back nodded. “We’ll all be going, and once we’re done with him, there will be handsome rewards. Don’t drink anything tonight, but tomorrow we might crack open something bubbly,” Frank finished, with a crooked smile, accentuated by his scar. The men in the back nodded once more, and opened the door on the back of the van. One by one they melted into the night, wary of what Anthony had showed them, but still thinking one thing. Ten of them, all armed… up against one of him. It was going to be the easiest money they ever earned. -------------------------------------------------------------------------- “So, you vill give me vhat I vant? A free trial of your powers?” asked Doctor Curiosity, eagerly. “Of course,” replied Merrick. “What would you like?” “Vell, I vould like to see my brother again… but I don’t vant to be an inmate at ze prison, before you go and try to trick me!” cautioned the doctor. “It never even crossed my mind,” replied the demon, acting the innocent. In truth, he was saving the tricks for when it really mattered. “So, perhaps I could visit him zere, but I vould be invisible and able to go anyvhere I vanted to.” While the doctor was thinking whether he wanted to add any further additions to his wish, the demon clicked his fingers and with a flash and a bang, he and Doctor Curiosity were transported to Cell 143b in the State Correctional Facility. “I might also vant to… vhat ze hell?!?” “You wanted to come here.” “I vasn’t ready! Do not be up to tricks, demon!” ranted the doctor. He suddenly fell silent and calm as he looked around and saw his brother, Paul Goodman, lying on a thin wooden bunk. Like this he looked much smaller than usual, and was wearing none of the get-up that transformed him into Eegor, the freakish assistant to the mad scientist that Doctor Curiosity strove to be. The doctor turned round to the demon and raised a quizzical eyebrow. “He won’t hear you if you talk,” said Merrick. “It’s just like he can’t see you. I’d be wary of trying to touch him though, things can get complicated.” “What do you mean? Am I really here? Am I invisible?” The demon stroked his chin. “You’re not precisely invisible. It’s pretty hard to change the physical manifestation of things.” “You can’t do it?” asked the doctor, aghast. “I can do it, but it requires a lot of effort… and I get migraines. No, instead it’s much easier to change perceptions. You’re here, it’s just as if everyone’s ignoring you.” “Ignoring me?” “Yes, and doing it really well. They won’t be able to notice you. You’re like a ghost. No matter how much you wave at people or yell in their ears, it won’t register. Just try not to bump into anyone. Things can get complicated.” As Merrick spoke, Eegor got up off his bed and walked disconsolately around the room. He even swerved around the doctor and the demon as he did so, without noticing. Eegor let out a soft sigh, disturbing the rest of the enormously fat man on the bunk opposite. “Shut up, you idiot. I’m not a woman, so I can get up there and slap the smile right off your face. Don’t think I won’t do it!” Eegor slumped back down on his bunk. He’d not even seen his cell-mate get up in the past day, so he doubted if the obese bastard would try and slap him, but he thought it better to play it safe. “Can I just tell him von thing? Maybe it vould just sound like it was a voice in his head, jah?” asked Doctor Curiosity. The demon nodded, and Curiosity walked over until his mouth was scant inches from his brother’s ear. “What would Chris do?” he whispered. “He wouldn’t take this from some fat idiot, would he?” Chris Goodman stood back and watched to see if there was any reaction. At first nothing seemed to have happened, but soon Eegor was nodding to himself, and he stepped off the bunk and walked around the room again, slamming his hands down across the bars every time he approached them. “What the hell do you think you’re doing, bitch?” yelled the fat man. “Whatever I want to,” was the reply. “You sit the fuck down before I kill you.” “No, I won’t be doing that,” said Eegor, calmly. “Oh, tell me you didn’t just say that!” screamed the rotund gentlemen, eliciting calls to shut up from around the whole wing of the jail. The fat man struggled to his feet and moved towards Eegor, but the assistant was nimble and he danced around behind him and pushed the fat man in to the bars, smacking his head into them and dropping him down to the floor. To the side, Doctor Curiosity smiled. He wouldn’t be able to look after his brother all of the time, but he’d done… uh oh. The fat man was getting up, and he didn’t look happy. One arm shot out with surprising speed and attached itself to Eegor’s throat. He tried to scream, but the fingers were tightening around him. “Hey, knock it off!” shouted the guards. Two of them were there, and one turned the key, entering the cell and smacking the fat man on the back with a night-stick. The man let go of Eegor, who landed, coughing and spluttering, on the floor. “Fatboy, you get back in your bed! You,” the guard said, turning his attention to Eegor. “You just stop pissing him off and get some damn sleep.” “I shouldn’t be in here with him,” pleaded Eegor. “I’m not that kind of person, really!” “You’re nothing but a number here.” “I am not a number, I am a free man!” “Shut up prisoner 24601, and go back to bed.” Eegor got up quickly, frustrated, but unable to do anything about it. On the other side of the room, Doctor Curiosity did the same thing, annoyed that his attempts to help his brother had backfired. Unfortunately, Curiosity backed in to the guard who was about to lock the cell door, knocking him over. “Nein! Zat vas a mistake.” Merrick was a picture of disinterest, pretending to file his nails in the far corner of the cell. “What the hell happened?” asked the guard, standing up. “Let me help you up, you dropped your stick… NEIN!” said the doctor, forgetting himself for a minute, before stumbling back and throwing the stick over his shoulder. “What was that?” said one guard. “Did you see… it flew…” replied the other, almost speechless. “Mein Gott!” yelled Curiosity, banging the palm of his hand into his head, and following his usual tactics of fleeing from the face of danger. He smacked into the guard again and knocked the keys from his hand and in to the cell next to Eegor’s. Looking back on the incident later, people were never sure if the so-called ‘Haunted Night-Stick Fiasco’ or the fumbling of the keys was the primary cause… …but they all agreed on that being when the riot started.
