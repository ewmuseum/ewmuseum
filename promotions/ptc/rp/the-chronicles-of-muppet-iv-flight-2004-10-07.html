The Chronicles of Muppet IV - Flight

7 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-07 The Muppet Man burst through the doors of Muppet Manor, visibly exhausted. Actually, 'burst' is very much of an overstatement; it was little more than a feeble push. That was really all he could manage. He moved slowly and wearily through the entrance hall, completely and utterly drenched. It had clearly been raining... and how. Slipping his pack off his shoulders, he couldn't help but smile a little. One less weight off his shoulders, albeit a literal one. Tossing it somewhere over to his left (it didn't really matter) he took his trenchcoat off, lobbing it in the vague direction of the coat hangers. It missed, but he didn't notice, or care. Muppet couldn't stand press conferences, interviews, photo shoots... and all that. Unfortunately, he had just had to go through a healthy dose of all of those. He had been sure it wasn't in his WWA contract, until his 'manager' King Muppet had decided to take a 'detailed look' through it. Looking at things in that light, the Muppet Man realised that King was definitely doing him a lot more bad than good. Then again, he was sure that he had always known that. He didn't really know anything these days. He worked all day, trained himself, and then went out and wrestled a busy schedule. Did he enjoy it? Sure. Or was that just an unfounded reassurance? "Why, why, why..." he asked himself out loud. It was really meant as a rhetorical question, still... he couldn't help but wonder. With an overwhelming sigh of relief, the Muppet Man collapsed into an armchair, letting himself sink deeply into it. He hadn't realised quite how tired he was. His muscles ached, and he was dripping with a mixture of sweat and rainwater. He never got this tired from wrestling. He realised this with a chuckle, leaning back and closing his eyes. For a few moments, he just sat there, letting the soft textures of the chair silently massage him. For a few moments, he was one with his surroundings, and he was at peace. For a few moments, he was in his own personal heaven. He was home. "ANDY! HEADS UP!" But that wasn't how things worked. "What the..." was all that the Muppet Man had time to utter as he turned his head... to be nailed right in the face with a heavy ball of some sort. He did his best to stop himself from losing his balance and tipping backwards, but it was no use, and the armchair fell backwards with an almost comical delay, sending him toppling over into a sort of backwards roll. The ball bounced off to the side somewhere, and a crash could be clearly heard... but no one paid attention to that. "Frenzy!" came the voice of King Muppet from high above, on the top of the staircase. The Muppet Man, now supporting the whole of his body weight on his neck, and in a minor state of shock, rolled to the side onto his back to witness his partner sliding down the banister with another ball in each hand. Momentarily panicking, Muppet scrambled to his feet and began to run, stumbling after each couple of steps. King let the second ball fly, and it rocketed straight towards Muppet's head, only to be avoided with a perfectly timed diving roll. As Muppet got back to his feet to see what he had just done, he couldn't help but grin. He hadn't intended anything of the sort... he had just done it to look cool. Still, he had managed to back himself into a corner, and King noticed this, still sliding at a decent pace down the banister. He began to take aim, as Muppet foolishly backed further into the corner, not knowing what the hell to do. "Shit..." he muttered, forgetting completely about all of the exhaustion pulsating through his body. King locked on to the target, and was just about ready to fire. Muppet ducked, covering his face. ... The Muppet Man looked up just in time to see King Muppet meet the end of the banister rather earlier than expected, and be hurled across the room, skidding across the floor. The ball slipped out of King's hands, rolling towards Muppet as he sprinted out of the corner, seeing his chance. Instead of picking it up, Muppet simply booted the ball as hard as he could (met with a severe pain in the toe) and sprinted on. He could only wince as he heard another crash from the direction he had kicked it. All of the exhaustion was now back, and it was holding him down severely. He was properly panicking, now, too. This was just like everything else in his life; fiercely competitive, just like the Muppet Man's upcoming match against Horazon in the GTT would be. That wasn't his main concern, now, though. He made for the first door he could see... any door. King Muppet got to his feet, glancing around to witness it all. He dived to his right, landing into a roll, grabbing a ball half way and throwing it with all his strength as soon as he found his feet. It was pure John Woo, and it almost worked perfectly. Almost. It all happened so fast, but to the two Muppets, it was in time-honoured slow motion. The ball hurtled towards the same door as the Muppet Man, who kicked it open milliseconds before he noticed the ball and leaned backwards. The ball missed him by a good half inch, hurtling through the door, and connected hard with the first object in it's path... which just happened to be King Muppet's rather lavish purchase of a month or so ago, the gargantuan statue of the Penguin of Death. Slowly, the humongous penguin teetered. Muppet dived away as far as he could, and the statue fell forwards... There was a momentary silence. CRASH! The almost mini-explosion reverberated through the entire house, making it shake to it's very foundations. Muppet felt a great tremor beneath him as he lay on his stomach, hands over his head like a scared little child. King Muppet, stunned at the whole thing, was flung off his feet and slammed down on the floor. For a good minute, things remained like that. Again, Muppet Manor was perfectly silent. Finally, raising his head from his hands, the Muppet Man sat up, surveying the damage. It was not a pretty sight. At first, he had to shield his eyes from the thick clouds of dust in the air. The massive statue had created a rather large dent in the floor, sending plaster and shards of tile flying everywhere. The doorway had been completely decimated, and the door lay crushed and helpless under the head of the penguin. The statue had not managed to come out of the encounter unscathed, either. Climbing to his feet, Muppet rubbed his back, in mild pain from the shock. He heard rapid footsteps, and turned his head to see King rushing... right past him, almost knocking him down again, and over to the statue. King went down to his knees, examining the underside of the statue with an apprehensive expression on his face, on tenterhooks. After a few agonising (to King, at least) seconds, he sighed, turning around to face his compatriot. "I'm sorry, Andy... the penguin didn't make it," he said, spectacularly anticlimatically. There was another slight silence, Muppet in a state of mixed shock and disbelief. King knew what to expect, and braced himself for the oncoming onslaught of verbal abuse, even smiling a little. He was so used to it by now, it was almost second nature, just another factor in his less-than-ordinary life. Nothing came. King looked on in disbelief as the Muppet Man turned away, his expression unreadable, and began to climb the stairs. He reached the top, after what seemed like an eternity, each step taking more out of him than he thought possible, and proceeded without a glance backwards. King simply stood there, watching his friend walk out of sight, without a word. Not even a "what... the fuck... was that?" or a "you... fucking... retard". It was unnatural, almost... wrong. Yes, something was definitely wrong. For the first time since he had settled down again, King Muppet was worried. For a moment, at least, the damage was forgotten. The destruction of the room and the immense cost of repair had been forced out of his mind... for he was scared he may be losing something a hell of a lot more important. With an overly elaborate sigh, King Muppet glanced up to the top of the staircase, before turning around and walking away. Nothing he could do would help now. Something was wrong, and Muppet had to figure it out himself. ---- The Muppet Man walked slowly down the corridor, his feet dragging behind him slowly, and leaving marks on the would-be perfectly clean carpet. He didn't notice. His steps and his steps alone were the only sound disturbing the quiet demeanor. He didn't care. He was too absorbed in his own thoughts, too deeply concerned with his own situation, too worried as to what might come. Just like old times. Reaching the end, he firmly grasped the doorhandle, turning it and walking in, shutting the door neatly and quietly behind him without a second thought. This was his room, his study, whatever you would call it. This was his own personal sanctuary, this was where he came to relax, to be alone with his thoughts, to plan out his schedule... his life. Little snippets of his life were scattered around this room, telling more of a story about him to anyone who looked in than he would like to reveal. This room was the Muppet Man. It's good to be back... Finally. GTT4 was advancing as planned. He had come out on top in both round 1 and 2, and he was confident that he could do it again if given the opportunity. In round 3, a man he knew little about named Horazon awaited him. He assumed that Horazon knew little about him, either, but he also realised that assumptions are rarely correct. Horazon had come through rounds 1 and 2 with flying colours, defeating Phil Botwin and Caitlin Fairchild, and was probably the favourite going into the match. Could this possibly work in Muppet's favour? No... Probably not. That's a naiive view. Nothing is ever that easy. It's an unwritten rule of life. Flinging himself on his bed, Muppet reached out and grabbed his CD player from his desk, putting on his headphones and pressing 'play'. He awaited the soft, melodic tones of A Perfect Circle's lead singer, Maynard James Keenan, one of his main sources of calm and relaxation. Music soothed him, and stimulated his brain, almost subconsciously guiding him. Music had always been a major part of his life, and he saw no reason why he should change that. Nothing came, though. The player stuttered for a few seconds, and he heard a little jolt electricity, quickly throwing the headphones off before one of them blew. Just my damn luck... With a sigh, he grabbed his headphones and half-ripped them out of the CD player, tossing them aimlessly across the room, hopelessly calculating the ever increasing bill for the damages caused today. It was very untidy. Actually, untidy doesn't really fit. More like... craphole. Yeah, craphole sounds about right. Cleaning was one of his pet peeves, he hated it with a vengeance, almost as much as shopping. He wasn't about to change that now, though, lying on his back and staring up at the blank, lifeless, white ceiling. He could almost feel the confidence draining out of him. As he stared up into nothingness, flashes of previous disgraces appeared before his eyes, letting him see for long enough to make him feel horrible, before snapping smoothly into another... and another. His decimation at the hands of the Devil's Own over a month back. He could still remember the fine details, still feel the horrible trickle of blood inside his mask, blinding him, still feel his neck slam hard into the mat on the end of the expertly executed Vertebreaker... His defeat at the hands of Brian Calvin two weeks ago on Monday Night Meltdown. He had been so utterly confident, so convinced that he was going to win, to once again prove to everyone watching, and the Devil's Own, that he was truly untouchable. He didn't. Brian Calvin, in the upset of the night, came out after a fantastic performance to pick up the victory. Muppet had thought that he was cool about it. He had thought that he was fine, it was just a minor hiccup. He wasn't, and it wasn't. Last week. He was back at Meltdown, back in the UK, and ready to prove a point. Whatever that point was, he would never get to prove it. The Devil's Own put a stop to that. Once again, at the hands of the stable he used to be such a key part of, he fell from grace... and fell through a good few tables as well. He grimaced, right there, a sharp pain suddenly shooting up his spine. He still hadn't fully recovered from the second humiliating beating at their hands within two months. He had to beat Horazon. He had to advance in the GTT... for himself. No one else. He had to satisfy his own damn selfish desire. He was fine with that, but... The Muppet Man was scared. He was scared of losing what he lived for, what he spent every hour of every day poring restlessly over inside his mind, losing what he had dedicated his whole life to for over a decade now, losing everything he had, and everything he probably would ever have. He was scared of losing his passion for wrestling. It had always been the predominant feature in his life, it had always been what Andy Scrine did. It was a massive, life changing commitment, but he had trusted himself to stick to that all of those years ago. By now, it was just second nature. He couldn't imagine his life without it. But, now... now, he wasn't really sure he wanted it at all. As he had come back in, totally and utterly shattered, and witnessed part of his house demolished, something had clicked. As he lay there, among some of the only possessions that meant anything to him, he wondered where he was going... what would become of him. Could he continue living like this? ...And would this stop him from defeating Horazon? I'm sorry, Will. I have to work this out on my own... or I'll never recover. For now... I have to go. I'll see you again when I figure all this out, and if I don't, then... well... Goodbye. Getting slowly to his feet, he glanced around his room, sighing. He picked up his United States championship, making a mental note to pick up his bag on the way out, and walked out of the door without a look back. The room lay as he left it... quiet, desolate, and predominantly fucked up... and that was how it would remain. ---- With a yawn, King Muppet headed up the stairs, feeling more weary than he would have liked. He had sorted out all of the rebuilding payments for the doorway he had destroyed earlier, but now, he was knackered, and need a good night's sleep. As he walked down the corridor, barely able to keep his eyes open, he noticed the Muppet Man's door open a crack. His curiosity overpowering him, King decided to take a look. Taking a peek inside, he realised there was no one there, and entered. At first, there was nothing particularly wrong. It was as untidy as ever... the Muppet Man wasn't known for being particularly organised. On his bed was his CD player, with one earpiece blown out. The immediate explanation that rushed into King Muppet's brain was that Muppet had gone out to buy some new headphones. Well, it was a perfectly plausible explanation. Then, he glanced over to the right, to the place where the Muppet Man's United States title should be. There was no belt. A momentary panic shook him as he walked over to it. He looked around the room... there was no note, no sign that Muppet had left... but he had. King slowly placed his hands on the table, closing his eyes, his brow furrowed with concern. "Where the hell has he gone?" To be continued...
