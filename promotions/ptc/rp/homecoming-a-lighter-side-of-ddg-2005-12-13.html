Homecoming….A Lighter Side of DDG

13 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-13 The fierce winter overcomes the environment and atmosphere of a holiday fever flowing through the veins of New Yorkers, as they dig deep into their pockets to please their loved ones. Everywhere he looked, greedy young children leaving mucus from their nostrils on windows locked onto the train set and Barbie dolls each boys and girls beg and plead for. Trying to break free from the collision in front of each toy store along the street, Graves runs along a young boy holding onto the hand of his father that caught his eye stopping him dead in his tracks. Looking at the boy staring at a small robot that virtually does everything a ten year old would want, and all he was hearing was… "Maybe next year son…maybe next year." Learning that harsh reality that money does not grow on trees, the boys head cannot only go but down staring at the worn down sidewalk. Turning his head away from the pair, Devil D. Graves has a quick flash back of his childhood, remembering times where his father would come home late on Christmas Eve nearing the dawn of Christmas itself. All his father would carry was one small bag in one hand, and a cheap bottle of wine in the other. Closing his eyes he could almost feel the half working fireplace keep him warm while he’d watch wrestling tapes before it was bedtime. Every Christmas Eve his father would lurk towards his mother with the smell of whiskey coming from his breath after a courtesy drink with his boss, and each time Graves would ease drop hearing the same words each year. "So…did you get you’re bonus?" "No… (Taking a deep breath) Just like last year, just a cheap glass of whiskey as a thank you for the Christmas rush. All I could afford was a remote control car for Deville missing a wheel, and a pink teddy bear missing a button eye for Marie." "Well…as long as the kids have sometime, and I take it you’re still going to wrestle tomorrow." "We need the money…you know that. Is it my turn this year to tell the kids Santa doesn’t fit into apartment chimneys" Suddenly Graves found himself back in the present, still being crowed by children pulling their parents towards the large window panel. Looking back for the child with his father, he realizes they are gone and he was out of it for a few minutes when a man wearing a large black overcoat approaches him. "Are you okay? You were just standing their not even blinking staring onto the street." "Yeah, I’m fine just a little dazed." Graves turns his body and begins to walk in the opposite direction of stranger pushing through crowd after crowd of people. While walking all Graves could concentrate on was his childhood each Christmas before his father’s unfortunate accident in the ring that cost him his life. Each time another defective toy from the department store his father worked at wrapped in the same blue wrapping paper with a red bow attempting to add some Christmas cheer to Devil’s Christmas experience. Even his much younger sister found herself disappointed receiving the same bear but different color with an ear or eye missing each time. Christmas became a repetition; Devil hated it as much as traveling to Manhattan to see his grandmother every odd month. Each year I was disappointed… Each year I would look at my father and see the sorrow in his eyes and the look of failure that he couldn’t bring a proper Christmas to his family. Each year I would get up with that very toy he worked so hard for and sit on his lap and give him a hug and then kiss my mother. I’d never say anything…my sister would cry but that was expected. But my grandparents would make it up for her with makeup and real dolls. I’d only get five dollars from my grandpa in a cheque for my savings account. And that was how Christmas was in my family, bleak, dull, and always ending up in an argument on how my father couldn’t run a stabilized shelter for my mother. We’d always leave and in the car my mother would cry and my father would stare out on the road not saying a word till Boxing Day. One year my grandfather told my dad he was better off dead…that was the same year he died in the ring. That was the year both my sister and I turned our values upside down and made our mother’s life a living hell. My father was the glue in our family; he kept it together and could put a smile on our faces without saying a word. Graves continued to walk back to his apartment still visualizing memories from the past turning quite gloom while surrendering to the absolute cold that fills his veins with ice and forces himself to keep all bare skin kept enclosed in clothing. After a difficult walk Graves finally reaches his apartment, but this time not heading for his penthouse sweet but this time underneath the building into the parking lot garage where all that echoes through the large area is the sound of water leaking from a pipe. Removing his hood from his head Graves takes out his set of keys and opens the door to his black Ford 500 and sits down in the driver’s seat holding his hands on the steering wheel looking on out of the windshield. Has it been to long…? Would she even want to see me again…? I left almost ten years ago and no word spoken between us since, only words between Marie that just let us know we were both alive. I wonder if she has ever come across me on T.V… I wonder if she even cares. Graves starts his car and slowly pulls out of parking spot "A-54" and heads out onto rush hour traffic preparing to visit his mother for the first time since he left home ten years ago. Growing up Grave’s wanted to wrestle just like his father did in NYCW, but after witnessing one of the most horrendous accidents ever his mother forbid Graves to even watch the sport on television. Going against her demands, Grave’s trained under Gregory Alio, founder of NYCW and the man who knew his father better than anyone. It was only a madder of time before his mother discovered why Grave’s was taking Tylenol more and more and coming back home unable to move sometimes with bruises scattered across his body. She told him to leave and never return…and he never did, he stayed with Gregory until he earned enough to live in a rat hole apartment on his own barley making ends meet. After being accepted into the Chaotic Wrestling Alliance earning a contract from CwA owner Eric Withrow, Deville’s life changed completely. His was able to move into a larger home and help support his sister when her husband would accept his offer. Now all that was left for Grave’s to accomplish in life outside of wrestling, was to speak at least once more with the very person that brought him into this world. Stepping into the lobby of her building Victory Mansions, Graves approaches the front desk. "Can I help you sir?" "Yes, I am looking for the room number of a Mrs. Janice Graves." "I’m sorry unless I have written consent from Mrs. Graves to allow someone in I cannot let out that information unless you were a family member." "Does her son qualify for that right? "I believe Mrs. Graves only has a daughter, I have never heard her speak of a son nor seen you here before." Graves reaches into his back pocket pulling out his wallet showing his driver’s license to the bellhop. "Deville D. Graves…that sounds just like Devil D. Graves from the CwA." "No shit." "I had no idea; I am terribly sorry Mr. Graves, her room number is 718, on the seventh floor" "Thanks" Graves folds up his wallet and places it back in his pocket and heads for the elevator. As he watches the numbers light up as the elevator progresses up, his heart begins to pound faster and faster, even a line of sweat falls from his hair causing irritation on his nose. Whipping it off he looks back up and reads 7 as the elevator shaft slowly stops reaching the seventh floor. Taking a deep breath Graves walks out of the elevator walking quite slowly while his hands start to shake as he gets closer and closer to his mother’s apartment. Reading the golden numbers on a white door, Graves slowly raises his hand forming a fist and proceeds to knock twice with a long period of silence between each knock. Hearing laughter from what he believes to be his mother, his heart begins to race even faster than before as his stomach feels like a bag of cement. The door swings open and the vision of a woman appears in front of Graves with her mouth opened slightly appearing as if she had seen a ghost. "Hi mom…" A long awkward silence sets the tension as the two just stare at each other for a few moments before Graves breaks the period of silence. "It’s been a while…" "I…Why…I…I don’t know what to say" "It’s okay, I understand. I came here because I needed to speak to you, weather it be the last time or the start of something, but it’s been on my mind for a few years now and I just had to come." "You’re back…I never thought you’d ever come back. The way Marie talked about you, the way I treated you…this is a big surprise Deville." "I know, I never thought I could build up the courage to come back either." "Come in…there is so much to talk about." Graves walked in looking around the room finally smiling for the first time all week, sitting down and enjoying a beer and a cigarette, Deville and his mother began to exchanging information dating back to the date when Devil D. Graves left his home and his mother’s life until today. Graves had finally reached out and overcame a challenge much tougher than any opponent could offer in the ring, the challenge to regain his mother’s love and friendship. But as things appeared to be better than ever, it took a turn for the worse as Deville’s sister walked through the door with an older man Graves had never seen before. The two strolled through the door laughing but suddenly stopped as they saw Deville sitting on the couch looking confused. "Marie…who’s that, you’re not cheating on Frank are you?" "No…of course not this…this is…" Once again an awkward silence filled the room as Janice, Marie, and the strange man look at each other very confined. Graves speaks trying to find out what is going on. "What the hell is going on here…who is this guy?" "Honey, about three years ago I met George, and well we connected and well…we tied the knot last year. I’m sorry Deville, I didn’t want you to find out." Graves stands up and looks at Marie. "You didn’t tell me? I have a right to know if something like this is happening. Especially when somebody is banging my mom." "Hey don’t speak like that in front of my wife" George attempts to shove Graves but is brushed off by a right forearm from Deville. "Don’t you fucking touch me…you’re wife?…my father is ‘his wife’ she’ll never be you’re wife…just hope I don’t see on the streets anytime soon…and none of you talk to me…just when I thought I could speak to my mother again, get back into the family somehow, but no…I get lied and fucked around and not as if I hadn’t enough in the past ten years." Graves walks out the door and rushes through the hallway heading for the elevator as calls for his name being shouted from his mother and sister, but he does not look back and only finds himself back in the elevator no longer nervous or worried…but infuriated with such rage he does not speak at all. But the man locked in his head is not the man who Deville recently discovered was his mother’s new husband, but the vision of his next opponent in round two of GTT5…Michael "Spitfire" Stevens. The scene slowly fades to complete darkness with a short last message said by Devil D. Graves. "And the crusade continues…"
