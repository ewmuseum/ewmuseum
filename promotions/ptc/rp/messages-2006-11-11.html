Date: 2006-11-11 Four and a half days is a mere 108 hours; but it can feel like an eternity when you are stranded inside a Super 8 motel in Paramus, New Jersey. Steve Watson stewed in abject boredom. The Super 8 didn’t boast quite the amenities that Steve had become accustomed to in his business travels. In fact, if it weren’t for the cable TV and the "Magic Fingers" vibrating bed, Steve might well have thought his escape from the police over the weekend had gone far less successfully than he had remembered it. But most county jails didn’t have quite so much blinking neon out (or a cruddy, emptied swimming pool) out front. Frank Hainesworth would call at least once a day to update Steve on his "progress". Things were always going "well" and Frank would assure Steve that he would be bringing him back in as soon as was humanly possible. It seems a few of the conspirators were proving tougher nuts to crack in this regard that Hainesworth had anticipated. And from what he was able to from their conversations, Steve had determined that Old Man Bagley with his "traitors should be taken out back and shot" mentality – was proving the most stubborn of chestnuts. Although Frank had warned Steve against using his company credit cards or making any outgoing calls to people they might be able to trace back to him, Steve was dying to hear his wife Elise’s voice and how she was holding out. He wondered if she was blissfully enjoying here visit with her folks. Or had she caught on that something had gone quite wrong back with Steve and that he was not just ties up in an extended business trip from Hell like the daily email she was receiving from him kept telling her. Steve wished he could have sent said emails himself; but – without internet at the Super 8 – he has to trust Frank that he was doing all he could to ease her concerns. At least once a day, Steve would walk down Colonial Street to the local Walgreens where he would buy up every local paper he could find and a Sugar Free Red Bull, and spend a few minutes chatting with Millie, the morning cashier. The rest of the day tended to be divided between Sedukko, sit-ups, and watching a whole lot more TV than he had at any time since he was a kid – especially a few hours a day with his newly discovered guilty pleasure, PTC 24-7 (a channel which – if we are being honest – Steve did not know even existed before his arrival in Paramus.) And it was there, between the Prime and Global updates - and shortly before the ACW Rewind show - that Steve first saw himself on the GTT6 Report. Or rather that is were Steve first discovered the fate of the ersatz Masked Marauder and were he kept abreast of his travails. The Marauder had been arrested and booked on Saturday night – an event that the Late Night comics had been sure to note in their Monday and Tuesday monologues, especially after the rumor that a joint had been found in his boot had been presented to explain possibly why he had run in the first place. What the comedians never got around to mentioning was when he was releases the next day for lack of evidence. Steve just had to shake his head at the marijuana thing as he was probably one of only a couple of people that he knew that had never even chosen to defile his bodily temple with a glass of beer, let alone an illicit herb. He was not quite so amused though by the reports that the Marauder was planning to drop out of the tournament. This wasn’t right. Steve wouldn’t let them do this to him. And not just for his own sake either. His scheduled third round foe, Tigera, had already been advanced through once when her assigned foe failed to meet his booking commitments; and Steve had far too much respect for the FUSE Universal Champion to see her tournament credibility damaged by a second free pass - not even considering how dropping out of an event like this would damage his own future in the career. In his worst nightmares, Steve had never imagined it ending like this. If he was going to go be eliminated from GTT6; it should be in the center of the ring, not with him sitting on his assets in a room of some low-rent chain motel. He had entered this tournament exactly so he could test his meddle against the likes of Tigera and the thought that he might already be written out of the match just killed him. But these things were far beyond his control right now. And Steve knew it. So he waited - and waited - and hoped that somehow fate would allow him to get out of Paramus while there was still time to rescue the match. In the mean time, Steve spend his time playing out the match over and over again in his head as he exercised his body incessantly to pass the days; not knowing, if in doing so, he was engaging in an act of match preparation or just daydreaming an event that would never come to pass – although that was the fate that seemed more and more likely with each passing day. Then suddenly it was Thursday - the day before the Round 3A bell time. Steve had awoke that morning and followed what had started to become his Super 8 routine: a thousand push-ups, followed by a thousand crunches, followed by a shower and his jaunt down to Walgreens. Mabel was especially chatty today – her calico had given birth overnight – so he didn’t arrive back at the motel until after the 1 pm GTT6 report had already started. When he arrived at the motel room door, he found it already unlocked and slightly ajar. He found this curious as Estella was typically done cleaning the room by 10:30 am. Tentatively, he swung the door open to see Lisa Somer from HB&T’s marketing department sitting on his bed, reading a magazine. Now now, Steve," said Lisa, "You know it’s not polite to keep a girl waiting." "Lisa? Why are you…?" "Frank had some business to finish up," Lisa explained, "He asked me to come get you." "Oh," exclaimed Steve, "So it’s all clear then?" "Sure, I guess, "said Lisa, "I’m just doing what I was asked to." Steve moved towards his dresser to start packing until Lisa interrupted. "I already took care of the bags while I was waiting for you" "Um…thanks," Steve responded. "So you want to grab a bite of lunch on the way into the office?" asked Lisa. "No, that won’t be necessary." Steve said as he hefted up his bag and onto his shoulder and headed out the door and into the motel parking lot. Steve tossed his bag into the back of Lisa’s Camry and climbed into the passenger seat. He then turned to Lisa as she joined him on the driver’s side. "Sorry about not responding to your email, Lisa," said Steve, "But as you can see, I have been otherwise indisposed." "Sure," Lisa responded, quizzically, "What email?" Steve’s mind raced briefly. How could she not know what he was talking about? Unless… "Uh…nevermind," said Steve, "Let’s just get going. This place is getting on my nerves." The ride back to the city did little to deter Steve from his preconceived notions about Lisa Somer. Conversationally, she was little more than a bundle of office gossip wrapped in a hint of flirty innuendo. Steve guessed he could see what some of the guys in the office might see in here, but he tended to prefer a bit more substance in his daily social interactions. And the longer they rode together, the more Steve became convinced that there was no way she could possibly have been the mind behind the Manning Joke email and its attendant mysteries. Finally, Steve could take no more, He needed to get out the car. He looked directly at Lisa and said, "You know, I think I might want to grab a quick bite before hitting the office after all. And look, I know a great little café just a few blocks from here that serves the best focaccias." "Sounds fun," purred Lisa. Steve smiled to himself as he guided Lisa down the narrow one-way streets to the café and – just as he expected – once they reached the building there was not a parking spot in sight and probably wouldn’t be for blocks to come. "Damn," Steve feigned dismay at their plight, "I was really looking forward to a tomato and roasted garlic." "Yeah. Bummer." Said Lisa. "Oh, I know." Steve thought aloud "Why don’t you take a couple of trips around the block while I run in and grab the grub? I’ll only been a minute or two, they’re pretty quick here." "Well, I…" But Steve was already out the door. "So what do you want?" asked Steve. "I guess the tomato and garlic sounds good," replied Lisa. "Tomato and garlic it is then," and with that Steve was through the door. Alone. Steve walked up to the register and placed their order. The café was fairly empty at this time of day (only three or four other patrons plus the clerk) and Steve saw that three of the four courtesy internet workstations on the back wall were empty. He decided to take advantage while he waited. Steve sat down at the far right booth and logged on using his personal code. He brought up his Hotmail accounts and shot off a quick email to the GTT6 promoters to let them know that The Masked Marauder – despite the persistent rumors - was indeed going to be in attendance this week. He then opened another window and logged onto his "Garbage Fed" account. That same address he has provided to the now mysterious sender of "The Manning Joke". When his In-Box came up it was filled with 8-10 emails sent from various, seemingly random ISPs. They were entitled things like, "Who to trust and who to not trust", "The money trail", "The Conspiracy Thickens". Then later emails came with subjects like, "are you there" and "Please Respond"; and, finally, "DELETE IT ALL NOW!!!!" Steve clicked open the "Who to trust…" email; but, before he could read it he felt the touch of cool steel through fabric pressing into his back and warm breath on his cheek. "Crap Steve! I really wish you hadn’t done that." Steve glanced back over his shoulder, but he already knew who it was pressed up against him." "Yes, Steve" said Lisa, "that certainly is the Browning 9 in my jacket pocket that you are feeling there. I think you had better come back to the car with me right now." Steve had no reason to doubt her about the gun. But he also knew that they were in a public place. He would call her bluff. "And if I don’t?" "I don’t think either of us really wants to consider that option, Steve." She replied, "But to be completely honest, I would rather be the only surviving ember of your unexplained murder/suicide than have to explain to my Uncle Norbert how you got away." "Your Uncle Nobert?" Steve phrased it as a question, but he knew exactly who Lisa was talking about. For the only Nobert he knew was Nobert Bagley, the Senior Partner of HB&D, LLC. No. This wasn’t good at all.
