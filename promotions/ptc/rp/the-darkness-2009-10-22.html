Date: 2009-10-22 - 1 - CLINTON The feeling of dread lasted no more than a second, an ominous surprise that startled Clinton Sage as the nurse placed his infant daughter into his arms for the very first time. The fear wasn’t the sudden realization that he was now a father, but rather an unbearable worry that his sinister past would ruin the happiness that had changed his life over the last 18 months. Clinton drowned in that feeling only briefly, before the apprehension was replaced by the greatest feeling of accomplishment that he had ever experienced. Arya Elisabeth Sage didn’t cry immediately, she only stared up at Clinton with a pair of brilliant pale blue eyes. She was still covered in smears of blood and afterbirth, but tears began to well up at the edges of Clinton’s eyes when he looked at his daughter and saw nothing but unrivaled beauty. A full head of black hair, no doubt Clinton’s own trait being passed down, contrasted the eyes Arya had inherited from her mother. Her skin was pink and warm to the touch. When Arya finally started to cry, Clinton bounced her gently and hushed her. Clinton didn’t know how much time had passed, but the nurse finally stepped forward and opened her arms. Clinton – as part of the deal he had made – reluctantly placed Arya into the nurse’s arms and let the hospital staff do their usual post-birth cleaning and check-ups. Nobody could take that moment away from Clinton, though. He had begged and pleaded for it – the first person to hold Arya other than Dr. Roebling would be Clinton himself. Callie had carried her for 9 months, bonded with her while she wrote her novels and short stories, laughed with her when she kicked and moved inside the womb. But Clinton wanted that first bonding moment, and right away. Putting up with Dr. Roebling’s stand-up at a local comedy club was worth it. Clinton looked down at his hands, noticing they were covered in blood. How many times have my hands been coated in the blood of others?, he shook the thought from his head and quickly wiped both of them clean on the hospital scrubs. “She has your lungs,” Callie whispered. When Clinton finally turned his attention away from Arya being cleaned and examined, he saw his wife lying atop the hospital bed. Shoulders slumped low in exhaustion, her face terribly pale and waxen, her face still held the smile that Clinton cherished. “She has your eyes, Cal.” She didn’t reply, only smiled. Her body looked as though it had been through a war. Callie’s light brown hair had been pulled back into a pony-tail, which had been thrashed loose as Arya pressed through into this world. Normally her hair hung loose far past her shoulders, a subtle spiral that curved down around her face and across her chest. Sweat had stuck some of her hair to her scalp, while clumping other sections in small knots. Her eye-lids hung halfway closed, but she continued to grin as the nurses wrapped Arya up and walked her over towards the hospital bed. Clinton felt speechless as Callie lifted her arms to welcome their daughter. The tears finally fell from his eyes when his wife placed a kiss on Arya’s forehead. The doctor’s joyous voice was filled with warmth, “Mr. Sage, it appears our quick examination of Arya has turned up nothing of concern. You’re the father of a healthy baby girl.” Dr. Garrett Roebling, a man of average height but rounder than most of the women he helped deliver babies for, over-exaggerated the signature he had been placing on a series of forms. When he looked up at Clinton, his bushy brown eyebrows bobbed with excitement as he spun on his heels (once again, over-exaggerated) and snapped his fingers and pointed towards where the Polaroid camera was resting across the room. There was something about Dr. Roebling’s smile that put Clinton at ease. Through all his years in the wrestling business, seeing the fake personalities and emotions that others would portray as truth, Clinton came to the conclusion that Dr. Roebling just loved life. He loved bringing new life into this world, and he earned Clinton’s trust and respect with that unmatched enthusiasm. The uneasy feeling returned. Never a man of faith – good or bad – Clinton suddenly had tightness in his chest that something might be wrong with either wife or daughter. Something that wasn’t immediately noticeable. He had to ask again, “So they’re ok? Arya and Callie?” “We will do more tests in a little while, but right now everything appears to be perfectly normal,” Fumbling with the camera, Dr. Roebling waddled back across the room. He looked up at Clinton, placed a hand on his elbow and quickly flicked his entire head twice in the direction of Callie and Arya, “In fact, the only thing missing from your beautiful family…and this is my official medical opinion…is you, by their side.” Dr. Roebling loved stand-up comedy, one of those odd quirks about the man that Clinton welcomed, “Doctor’s orders?” “Yes, yes. Go. Get over there, doctor’s orders.” Roebling said as he flicked his hand towards the bed, “Also it should be noted that I’m an excellent photographer, or so the other families tell me. 100% approval rating, not one family has complained about my skill bringing their children into this world or taking their first picture after entering it. So you’re required by law to proclaim that fact if anyone asks.” Clinton stared at Dr. Roebling – who preferred to be called Garrett – and didn’t say a word. “I’m going to have a wing of my own at the Louvre someday.” Clinton’s black eyes didn’t move off of the doctor, didn’t blink. “We need to work on that sense of humor, Mr. Sage.” Clinton continued to stare, zero emotion across his face. Dr. Roebling pretended to loosen a tie that wasn’t there, and then nervously chuckled and fumbled with the Polaroid camera. Once it was powered up and ready to shoot, the doctor looked up to still find Clinton staring through him. “Clint, stop it. You’re scaring Garrett.” Callie said, the left side of her mouth curled up in a smile. She was still gazing down at Arya, but she clearly knew what trick Clinton was up to. Clinton Sage finally smiled at Garrett, put an arm around his shoulders and shook him lightly, “Gotcha.” “One big, happy smartass family, I see. My 'Professional Medical Opinion', if you will.” Clinton erupted in laughter, “You know you love it, Doc. I had to put up with your stand-up, so you had to put up with a little ribbing yourself.” “I almost had a heart attack, Mr. Sage. You’re a man of considerable…uh, presence.” “You brought me my Arya, safe and sound. You’ve earned a pass for life. Thank you, by the way, for letting me hold her right away. I can’t tell you how much that meant.” Doctor Garrett Roebling sighed with relief, wiped his brow with the back of his hand and hurried Clinton to the side of Callie’s hospital bed, “My future in professional wrestling just took a turn for the better! A free pass for life! I hope you heard that, Mrs. Sage, I’m going to hold it against your husband should he ever venture back into the ring.” Now it was Callie Sage’s turn to stare at the chatty doctor. Clinton had no intention of ever stepping back into the ring, and he knew Callie loved that about him. She wanted him at home, helping raise Arya and not on the road so many days of the year. Clinton couldn’t possibly imagine a reason to ever end his retirement. “I better take the picture.” Garrett said as he framed the Sage family in the viewfinder, “Now smile and say ‘Happy Birthday Arya!’” There was no flash, only one jovial doctor who would probably rather be delivering a punchline rather than a daughter. Roebling smiled as he waved the photo, and finally after the ink had dried and the picture had set, he nodded at the picture in admiration, “Beautiful.” Clinton took the picture from Dr. Roebling, leaned over and showed Callie, then placed a kiss atop Arya’s forehead just like her mother had done, “Perfect.” “Told you I was good, Mr. Sage.” “You also said you were a great comedian.” “Ouch.” “Thanks, Garrett. For everything.” Clinton knew what he did, and he watched Dr. Roebling’s eyes widen with surprise, then drift down towards Callie’s – who shared the emotion, “So your husband does know my first name!” Callie swayed Arya in her arms, trying to lull their little beauty to sleep. The doctor’s smile stayed as he spun away and attended to other matters, a newfound spring in his step as he did so. Clinton Sage knelt down beside the bed and stared at the Polaroid picture of his new family. In time he knew Callie would hate the picture, looking so disheveled and exhausted after hours of labor. He knew in many years that little Arya would stare at the moment and wonder how anyone ever thought she was a cute baby, so pink and odd shaped. He knew he would keep this photo forever, a reminder that June 3rd would come and go each year with a celebration of life and love, and how much his life had changed over the last two years. It was an easy picture to stare at. Clinton couldn’t remember the last time he saw a picture of himself smiling. - 2 - CALLIE The day Callie told Clinton that she was 3 weeks pregnant, he gently kissed her on the cheek and smiled.
