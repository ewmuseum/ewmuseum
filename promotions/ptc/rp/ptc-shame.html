Shame

7 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
10:30am 4th June 2006. Memphis, Tennessee. Some Backwater Hotel. It began and ended like Jacob McKail always remembered it did. The light beckoned to him, from far upon the horizon, compelled him closer, out of the cold and to embrace the warmth. The offer was too good to resist - it always was - and McKail couldn‘t hold out for long, but then, he didn‘t really want to. The result was inevitable and it always had been. The journey was only there to soften the blow to his system, to ease his passing back into the conscious realm, the destination was not in dispute. Click. Considering the amount of pain he was in, there could be no question that he was conscious again and it happened as quickly as someone flicking on a switch. His head pounded, felt like someone had decided to spend the evening smashing him over the back of the head with a rather sizeable lump hammer. Now, he was beginning to wish he’d been strong enough to resist the overwhelming allure of the light. He knew an hangover when he felt one and instantly he came to the conclusion that he must’ve hit the bottle pretty damn hard. He couldn‘t remember when he’d ever felt this bad. He groggily opened his eyes, and taking his mind off the pain, he decided to take stock of his situation. He’d been asleep, or passed out, he knew that much. Either way it was a surprising, if not welcome, change. He glanced at his watch and raised his eyebrows speculatively. Ten-thirty. Nice. He’d managed at least a couple of hours sleep and that was a hell of a lot more than he was used to. McKail had been a glorified insomniac since the death of his wife, only getting an hours sleep every night, if at all. For some reason he just couldn’t seem to get any sleep without Mary lying next to him, her warm body not being there, pressing up against him, denied him an unparalleled sense of comfort and stability. McKail’s focus was drawn away from his maudlin problems for the time being, as a vicious twinge in the back of his neck registered for the first time. Instinctively, his right hand shot up to gently rub the area, to somehow ease the pain, but his hand stopped long before reaching his neck. Something was in his hand, he painfully turned his head and looked down at the half-emptied bottle of Jim Bean resting so comfortably in the palm of his hand, almost as though it belonged there. Wait. Looked down? Something wasn’t right and it didn’t take him long to figure out what. He realised that he wasn’t actually lying down. He’d spent the night sleeping in a seated position, on the thick carpeted floor, propped up against the wall. No wonder his neck hurt so damned much. McKail pushed himself up, rather painfully, from the floor, immediately stretching his arms out wide to their extent as he rose, not even noticing, nor attempting to halt, the various files of paper fall from his lap. “What in the hell...?” He muttered to himself, scrunching up his face, confused. He tried desperately to piece together the occurrences from the night before and perhaps then he’d figure out just what in the hell had...Oh. Of course. The Infinite Gauntlet. The organisers had Fed-Ex’d him the information regarding the event yesterday and god knows how they managed to track him down. He hadn’t stayed in one place long enough over the past three months, to have any kind of permanent address and he didn’t even bother leaving forwarding addresses anymore. He figured it didn‘t really matter much now. How could he forget something so important? He painfully bent down, his bones creaking all the way, to pick up the files and caught another glimpse of the half emptied bourbon bottle. That’s how he could forget. Being soused in a powerful liquor had that effect on him, whenever he chose to abuse himself in such a way. From what he could recollect, the file contained information about the location of the event, directions to get there, long standardised rules that everybody could regurgitate at will and also in-depth biographies for each opponent he was pitted against. He’d spent the night sifting through the information, desperately attempting to contrast it against what he had firmly bolted into place from the last time he’d fought Joshua Taro Freedom and Jake Hix in round eighteen of the event. McKail couldn’t quite remember why he was doing this, but he guessed he was just trying to sift out anything he could use in his match, some little snippet of information that had eluded him before. The thing of it was, he couldn’t remember whether that line of thought actually bore fruit or not. He figured that throughout the course of the day, when last nights antics slowly return to him, piece by piece, he’ll get his answer. McKail sighed. He placed the files on the bed and, still stretching, made his way over to the window. He felt the stagnant warm air cling to him as he moved and for a second found it difficult to even breathe properly. He pushed aside the curtains and opened the heavy, clunky window open, watching as the sun intensified the vision of the numerous miniature dust particles that were thrust into the air majestically. Glaring at the dust particles, something from across the way caught his eye. His room was on the second floor of the hotel complex and the building resembled a kind of giant, but narrow, horseshoe from a distance. McKail could see directly into the room opposite him with perfect clarity and noticed the door ajar, with the greasy, long-haired Hotel manager he recognised from downstairs when he first got the room three days ago. With him was a short, stocky man who McKail guessed to be a janitor. Now why the hell had he brought a janitor up here? McKail idly wondered. Shouldn’t it the maid be cleaning the room? From the body language and raised voices, it didn’t take much of a genius to figure out they were enthralled in some kind of heated debate. Interest piqued, McKail remained at the window and honed in on the remainder of the conversation the best he could. “Can you believe this?” The manager said. “Can you believe someone would leave their hotel room in such a state?” McKail rolled his eyes. Precisely how long had this guy been a hotel manager anyways? He thought back to some of the things he and the rest of the boys used to do whilst on the road in his last wrestling federation. It was mostly horrific and too appalling to repeat, but that didn’t mean he regretted it. Hell, back in those days he’d do anything to take his mind off being away from his wife. Now, he envied such inconsequential problems. The janitor nodded, with an expression on his face that told McKail the guy had been around and seen this sort of thing before, despite his relatively young age - McKail figured him to be around his mid twenties at most. “I can believe this,” he confirmed, “some people just don’t have the house training they were born with.” “Just deal with it will ya?” The hotel manager told him. “I’ll go see if the fool left an home address I can bill all of this to. I swear when the guy checked out this morning he had a grin on his face the size of Gibraltar and now I know why!” The hotel manager took a step toward leaving the room, but the janitor placed a firm hand of his shoulder to stop him, a confused expression plastered across his face. “Me? Deal with this? Are you out of your damned mind?” The hotel manager whirled around, his eyes wide with anger. “Yes, I expect you to deal with this!” The janitor gestured toward whatever mess the two were arguing about and said, “Explain to me precisely how this is my job. Who am I, your maid? Why the hell did you even bring me up here?” “Look,” the hotel manager began, sighing in synchronisation with his words. “The maid can’t clean this mess up because the damn thing is blocked, it needs to get unblocked before she can do anything. Ergo, it falls into janitor territory…and guess what? You‘re the janitor!” “And so I’m supposed to do it?” The janitor replied, shaking his head in disbelief. What did he expect? McKail observed, almost bemused. He’s a janitor. It’s his job to do the things nobody else wants to do. “You will do it,” the hotel manager barked, walking away now, before the janitor could throw another objection at his employer. “If you want a pay check at the end of the month that is.” The distance was getting so great, he was beginning to shout now. “Just get it unblocked, that’s all. The maid can clean it after you’re done.” The janitor had no answer to this, instead he just shook his head, took another look at the mess made and cursed something under his breath McKail couldn‘t quite make out. Poor bastard! McKail shook his head and smiled. The guy didn’t have the worst job in the world, but it was damned close. McKail walked away from the window and slumped down upon the bed lazily. He thought he had it bad four months ago, when he was running around in a clown suit in his previous job and now he had to reconsider the notion. The memory at that period in his life brought back pain that he expected, but didn’t even try to avoid. The memories containing Mary always seemed to have that effect on him. 11:30pm. 23rd January 2006. New York City, New York. The Former Residence of Jacob McKail. McKail had been sitting in the dark for almost an hour before Mary sleepily wandered into the room, wearing nothing but one of McKail’s old t-shirts that hadn’t fitted him for years, and switched on the lamp in the corner of the apartment. The sudden influx of light emanating from the lamp was not great and McKail barely reacted to it, but did shoot a sympathetic glance over toward Mary, although he couldn’t quite bring himself to look her in the eye. “Hey, honey.” Somehow he could tell that she noticed this small inconvenience, his wife always had had the ability to cut to his very core, and so quickly, he turned away from her caring gaze, hoping that she wouldn’t want a conversation about it. “Hey yourself,” she said in return, idly rubbing the sleep from her eyes. She made her way over to McKail’s chair and gently eased herself onto his lap, resting her head upon his chest. “How long have you been home?” “About an hour,” McKail answered, desperately trying to hide the shame in his voice. But he knew she’d already detected it. Mary removed her head from his chest, glaring up at him and McKail still couldn’t bring himself to look her in eye. “Why didn’t you come to bed?” “I just wanted to stay up a while,” McKail told her. He wasn’t lying, not really, he just left out the part about not wanting to face her. “That’s all.” “Come on now,” Mary began, her face scrunched up, concerned. “Spill it.” McKail glared at her, supplying an obviously fake expression of confusion. “Spill what? What are you talking about?” “You can’t fool me, Jacob,” she told him, with a knowing smile. “I know something’s wrong. So just stop dancing around the subject and tell me.” McKail sighed. He knew he’d have to get this over and done with sooner or later. He was a fool to even consider the plausibility of keeping this bottled inside, to deal with himself, when Mary knew him so well. It was not the first time he acted like a fool and considering his current gimmick within his home wrestling federation, it definitely wouldn’t be the last time. “You remember this new gimmick I’ve been throwing around backstage? I told you about it last week.” He asked, still not looking at her directly. “Yeah,” she replied. “That clown thing?” McKail rolled his eyes. “Yeah. That one.” “What about it?” She questioned. “Well, the bookers bought it.” He laid his head backward, resting it upon the comfortable leather cushioning of his chair. “And so did the damned audience.” Mary’s concerned expression quickly morphed into that of confusion. “But, isn’t that a good thing?” “You’d think,” He said. “But after my match, the bookers told me that my gimmick wasn’t plausible in a wrestling capacity. They want me to be a manager. A manager. Can you believe that? I’m in the prime of my career and they want me to be a damned manager!” “Well it’s not all that bad,” Mary told him. “They’re still employing you at least, you weren’t so sure whether you were going to get axed or not yesterday.” “You’re right.” Mary was trying to put a positive spin on things, trying to make McKail look on the bright side of things and he could see the logic in her argument, but he really was in no mood for it today. “It’s just that I’m no manager. I’m wrestler. I want to wrestle. I want to be inside of the ring, not hanging around the side of it. It‘s just not me.” “Well perhaps in time...” Mary trailed off as soon as McKail shot her an angry look. “Perhaps in time?” McKail asked her. “As soon as they’ve used me in this gimmick, they’ll get rid of me and what will I have then? Nothing. No career. No status. No hope. Just a half-assed clown gimmick with a really limited shelf life.” “Nothing?” Mary asked, eyes wide, obviously hurt. “No, no, no, baby.” He gently cradled her head. “I didn’t mean I won’t have you...” She pulled her head away and he put up no resistance. There were tears in her eyes now, but McKail didn’t think he hurt her that badly. “I didn’t mean...” Mary placed her hand over his mouth to stop him. “You didn’t say anything wrong, Jacob.” She wiped the tears from her eyes and just glared at him for the longest time. “Then what is?” He asked. “Come on, you’re scaring me now.” “I’ve been to the doctor today,” she spluttered. “I have cancer, Jacob. The doctor told me I have cancer.” McKail’s eyes widened upon the reluctant realisation of this. “What...?” “I found the lump about a week back,” she explained. “I‘ve been putting it off, but I got up this morning and forced myself there. I kinda wish I hadn’t now...” “But, but...” She smiled. She’d spent the entire day getting her head around it, of course she hadn’t really succeeded in doing so, but she had managed to put a brave face on it. At least for the time being. “Don’t worry. There are treatments for this kind of thing now, I’ll be fine.” “But what if you’re not?” McKail asked, tears welling his own eyes now. He was making things worse, but he ignored this rationale. “What’ll happen then? What’ll I do then, without you?” Mary grabbed his shoulders firmly and developed a stern expression. “If I go, I want you to promise me something.” “Anything.” McKail couldn’t focus. The news had hit harder than a fourteen wheeler, doing a hundred and twenty, and the shock was still working him over good. “I want you to do what you‘ve always wanted to do.” Her eyes were intense and McKail had trouble looking away from them. “I want you to promise to follow your dreams to their logical conclusion, no matter what.” “I’m being selfish, I...” “No.” She pushed herself off McKail’s lap. “You’re not. I don’t really want to think about it for now, at least not tonight.” “But...” She smiled at him. “I know your unhappy at the moment, but don’t let that get you down. If you don’t want to do this clown gimmick, then don’t. Go somewhere else and fight with everything you‘ve got. Promise me you‘ll do this, Jacob.” Her smile faded. “Promise me.” McKail couldn’t help, but smirk. Mary being her usual self, thinking about him rather than herself, even under the circumstances. Despite it all, she was right. If he didn’t want to stay and do this stupid damn manager clown gimmick, then he didn’t have to do. He could be anyone he wanted to be, go anywhere he wanted to go. Granted, it could take him a while to get something going, but that didn’t really concern him. Especially not now. Now, he was going to put his career on hold, no matter what Mary thought of it. She needed him through all of this and being on the road, away from her for such long periods of time, just wasn’t going to cut it. She would be set against it, but she’d see it to be the correct choice. Given enough time. “I promise, baby.” For the first time that evening he put together enough courage to look at her directly. “I promise you, I will.” 10:35am 4th June 2006. Memphis, Tennessee. Some Backwater Hotel. McKail fought back the tears. The harsh reality of memory was too much for him to take, especially considering the delicate shape the bourbon from the night before had left him in. He reached over to that bottle of Jim Bean again, hoping that the alcohol would take away the painful memories. He wanted to crawl back inside the bottle to get a little peace, to forget, at least for the time being. He glared at the bottle in his hands for the longest time, his mind at war, desperately trying to make some kind of decision. No. He couldn’t do it. The shame he felt then, paled in comparison to the shame he felt now. These memories are all that he has left of Mary now, no matter how painful they were and he was actually considering forcibly removing them? Even just for a temporary period? Disgusted with himself, he hurled the bottle of Jim Bean across the room, watching it smash against the far wall in delight, the resilient shards of glass and dark brown liquor dribbling down the wall in some kind of strange formation. No. These memories could be used to his benefit, with the right frame of mind. They were painful for a reason and they stuck with him for that same reason. Mary wanted him to do all that he could to be the wrestler he’d always wanted to be and the Infinite Gauntlet was just such a platform for him to utilise in order achieve this goal. Yes, last night he’d taken to going over all of the relevant information again, but he done so heavily intoxicated. He didn’t even know whether he’d found something of use. He grabbed the files from the side of him and opened them up, with a new determination, unparalleled by any other. The promised he’d made to Mary still meant something to him and long ago he’d vowed to keep every promise that he made, especially those ones he’d made to Mary. Come the Infinite Gauntlet, he’d be ready. He’d make damned sure of it. It’s all that he had left.
