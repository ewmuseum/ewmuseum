It's All Up To Me

26 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-26 October 8th, 2003 The birthing rooms at the hospital were significantly nicer than the regular rooms, which was something pleasant in a time of uncertainty. About the size of two normal rooms, sporting hard wood floors, an earthy color scheme, and a stereo to play soothing music, it did its best to provide a calming effect on expectant couples. Kami Axtell wasn't due to give birth for another six weeks, but the baby inside of her had decided that the time was now. Her contractions had started two days ago at two minutes apart, which is fairly close to begin with, but now they were getting closer and closer together. She lay in the bed at about a forty-five degree angle, light sweat glistening on her forehead as the mid-morning sun fought its way through the blinds covering the windows, her legs up in stirrups. "Are you feeling any pain, Mrs. Axtell?" the nurse asked as she checked the monitors next to the bed. "Looks like we might be seeing another contraction in a moment. It'll be time to push soon." Kami finished the last few bits of crushed ice in her mouth, shaking her head as she replied. "Not really. Thank God for epidurals!" She squeezed the hand of her husband, who stood next to her by the bed. Jim Axtell was as nervous as his wife, and equally excited as well. They had both been so excited when Kami first became pregnant. It had taken them several months and a few doses of fertility medicine to make it happen, but it finally did. When the first home pregnancy test came back positive, Jim immediately went to the store and bought another one, a different brand, just to make sure before they really began to celebrate. In fact, he still had both tests in the cabinet in his bathroom at home. Jim looked down at Kami and smiled lovingly, dabbing her forehead with a cool sponge as the next contraction came surging through her body. The intensity was increasing, he could tell, as she clenched his hand a little harder this time than previously. She winced in pain and grunted an exhale of the breath she had been holding. "I think it's about time," Kami huffed, laying her head back down on the pillow to wait for the next contraction. The nurse looked over some readouts and checked the monitors once again. "Okay, on the next contraction we'll give a little push to see what happens." "Are you ready for this, baby?" Jim was trying to maintain his composure, but his voice crackled a little bit as the adrenaline level in his body increased. Kami laughed at her husband. "Are you?" The nurse smirked at the father-to-be. "If you like you can sit down in the chair, Mr. Axtell. We've had many fathers fall and hit their heads either on the bed or the floor." 'The Axphyxiator' puffed his chest out indignantly. "I'll be fine. I can handle it." Soon the next contraction arrived and the nurse ordered Kami to start pushing. Mrs. Axtell winced in a mixture of pain and effort, clutching Jim's hand tighter than ever. When the contraction subsided, Kami panted and moaned a bit. "Kinda felt that," she said with a forced chuckle. Jim fed her a few more crushed ice chips and wiped some of the sweat from her brow. "Here comes another one," called the nurse. Kami sat up a bit in the bed and readied herself for the next go round. "PUSH!" As Kami strained and pushed, Jim started to see the crown of the babies head. His eyes widened and he felt a little buckle in his knees. "I see the head!" he exclaimed excitedly, looking toward the nurse for a response. The nurse was calm as she finished looking at some of the readouts on the monitors. "Okay, we'll get the doctor up here in a little bit." "But I see the head!" Jim couldn't quite understand why there was such a nonchalantness on the part of the nurse. "It's right there!" He even pointed for emphasis. Kami looked at her husband, then at the nurse. She was somewhat puzzled as well, but at the same time trying to recover from the last push. "The baby is fine right there, Mr. Axtell. The doctor will be here soon." The nurse went about her business and soon the doctor was in the room. Along with him came a bevy of neonatal care doctors and nurses since the delivery was so early. It would have almost felt uncomfortable in the room except for the excitement that was taking place. After a relatively short amount of pushing, roughly one hour, the baby was finally born. Jim was so pleased and excited when he saw for the first time that he and Kami had a son. "It's a boy!" he shouted, tears welling up in his eyes. An exhausted Kami layed back on the bed, a tired smile on her face. Dr. Cummins, a grey haired man in his sixties, held the baby and offered Jim a pair of scissors. "Would you like to cut the unbilical cord?" A nervous and excited new dad reached out and took the scissors, taking a couple of cuts to get through the tough cord. Almost as soon as the cord was cut, the neonatal intensive care doctor came over and scooped the baby from the doctors arms, taking him over to a table for a quick examination and rub down. Jim moved over and kissed Kami gently on the forehead. "I love you." "I love you, too." Her voice was faint, but her eyes sparkled. After a few moments, one of the NICU nurses brought the baby over and handed him to Jim. Wrapped in a receiving blanket, his eyes were closed and he didn't hardly make a sound. He was calm, allowing Jim to just look down at him in awe for a second before walking over and handing him to Kami. With tears of joy rolling down her cheeks, Kami cradled her newborn in her arms, a huge smile on her face. "Hello, my little Jaxon Ryan," she cooed. The love in her eyes was so evident. They had wanted a child for so long and thought many times that they wouldn't be able to. To finally see their son and hold him in their arms was like nothing they had ever felt before. Unfortunately, the feeling would be short lived. "We have to take him down to the NICU for examination," interrupted the neonatal doctor. "He looks fine, but this is standard for premature babies. You can come down and see him shortly." The doctor reached for Jaxon, and Jim was half tempted to knock him on his ass when he saw the look in Kami's eyes. She didn't want to let her child go, and neither did Jim for that matter. But he was the doctor, and they had Jaxon's best interests in mind. "It's okay, honey," Jim said as comfortingly as he could. The father gently reached for the baby. Kami placed a kiss on Jaxon's forehead and looked at him once more. "Mommy will come see you soon. I promise." Jim lifted Jaxon from Kami's arms and handed him over to the doctor. He watched carefully as the doctor placed Jaxon onto a cart and exited the room, taking about six nurses with him. They got to hold their son for approximately one minute. One minute that at the same time seemed like the longest and shortest moment of their lives. Jim leaned over and gave his wife a hug, kissing her on the cheek and holding her tight as she sobbed in his ear. Such an exciting moment for the two of them, giving birth to their son, had suddenly turned into a feeling of emptiness. They knew the feeling would end as soon as they could hold their child again, but it felt like shit. He didn't want to feel like that again. ********** The trans-Pacific flight from Hong Kong to Los Angeles was excruciatingly long for some of the passengers on board flight 632, especially those who didn't exactly favor flying in the first place. For some in business class it was just another in a long line of commutes, almost second nature by now. To most of the other passengers it was just a long ass flight, broken up by either the in-flight movie or a restful nap. For Jim Axtell, however, the seven thousand mile trip had seemed like mere seconds in the eternity that had been the last week of his life. Just a few days ago Jim had been getting ready for the GTT6 tournament, preparing to get back into the sport that he had grown to love. There as a small void within himself that he wanted to fill. He felt that if he made a good showing and got through the first couple of rounds, someone might be willing to offer him a contract. Jim's wife, Kami, wasn't sold one hundred percent on his returning to professional wrestling, but she loved her husband and saw that he missed it. She agreed to let Jim see where the tournament took him and then go from there. Unfortunately, Jim's world would be turned upside down just days before leaving for Hong Kong. His son, Jaxon, was kidnapped. No reasonable explanation was given. He didn't know who had done this terrible thing. All Jim knew was that his heart and soul had been ripped from inside of him. His wife was on the verge of breakdown. His life was in turmoil. His precious son was gone. And to top it all off, the tournament that had started off as a 'show up and see what happens' type of thing, had now become a 'win the tournament or never see your son again' thing. It took every fiber of Jim's being to pull himself together and get on that plane to Hong Kong, knowing that if he didn't win he might not see Jaxon again. The amount of emotion and pressure Jim was feeling almost overwhelmed him, but he had to stay strong in order to save his family. Luckily, Jim was coming home a winner. After a hard fought match against Kerry Owens, Jim had his hands raised in victory. It was a victory he couldn't enjoy, however, as the road ahead was still long. He would have to beat many more opponents, none of which would have sympathy for his plight. Next in line was a man who called himself High Flyer. Rumor was that this High Flyer was borderline psychotic with thoughts as myraid as his technique in the ring. For Jim Axtell, however, High Flyer was nothing more than another step toward winning the most important prize he had ever won…. His son. ********** Trapped in the tight confines of his coach seat, Jim Axtell stares blankly through the window of the airliner as it speeds through the night sky. The white hood from his sweatshirt covers his head as it rests against the hard plastic interior, offering only minimal relief. The stewardess had offered Jim a pillow several times during the flight, and had she not known better she would have thought he was a deaf/mute from the lack of response. He didn't want to be comfortable. He didn't deserve it. With the recent events on continuous replay in his mind, Jim failed to notice the plane begin its decent. He tries to block out the pain with fond memories of his son, but they always lead back to the horrified look on Kami's face when she showed up at the gym that day. How could I have let this happen? I get paid to protect people and I can't even take care of my own family? "Sir," the stewardess begins quietly as she approaches down the aisle. "The captain has started our decent into Los Angeles. You'll need to fasten your seatbelt, please." Jim's thoughts keep him in a trance until the middle aged stewardess with short brown hair softly touches his shoulder. "Sir?" Jim startles and snaps his head around to look at her. She is taken aback somewhat but still offers a sympathetic smile. "Your seatbelt?" "Oh. Sorry." He regains his faculties slightly and fumbles around with the seatbelt, finally clicking it back together. Jim returns his gaze toward the window. The stewardess frowns, obviously sensing that something isn't quite right with her passenger. "Is everything alright, young man?" she queries. "It's not very often that someone spends the duration of a long flight, like this, simply staring out the window." If you only knew, lady. Jim looks back to the stewardess and forces a half smile across his lips. Under the dim reading light we can see a small mouse under his right eye and a cut on his lower lip. "Just have a few things on my mind, ma'am." His voice is quiet and calm, hiding the turmoil going on inside of him. She looks over Jim's face, taking in the minor injuries along with the lost look in his eyes. After a few moments of processing information and reaching a conclusion about this passenger, the stewardess offers Jim a wink of encouragement. She is a mother, afterall, and mothers have seen looks like that before. "Don't you worry," she says warmly. "Whatever's gotcha bothered will work itself out for the better. You just have to have a little faith." Jim only offers a simple nod as the stewardess moves back toward the front of the plane. Not so sure that faith is going to have a whole hell of a lot to do with this one, lady. It's all up to me. **********
