Final Confession (Part Two)

9 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-02-09 Staring out the window of his penthouse flat, Kurokame couldn’t shake the notion that something was wrong. The normally glittering, brilliant Tokyo skyline was muted and gray; somehow, the entire city seemed to be moving a bit slower than usual. The sun was weakly penetrating the thick haze that had settled about the city, though it did not seem inspired to burn it away. An uneasy feeling took up residence in Kurokame’s gullet, an unusual experience for him on the day of a match. Somehow he got the feeling that, though the sun had risen, it had not brought daytime with it. He felt as if he were dreaming, even as he watched the city slowly rising from its slumber before his waking eyes. His throat was dry and his stomach was sour; his body ached, his mind still dull from sleep. The morning had greeted him cruelly, yet it was not of fear or anxiety. Tonight was to be the biggest night of his life, and yet it seemed far from his mind. No, there was something else at work here. The past several weeks had been leading to this; Kurokame had slowly but surely fallen into malcontent, had grown restless and listless from the tedious repetition of routine. Once the match was announced, the whole city, and indeed the entire nation, had begun to buzz with anticipation. "It’s the biggest match ever," they said, and they meant it. Kurokame had become the greatest yokozuna ever, achieving the high title faster than any other in the ancient history of the art of sumo. He had since been undefeated, his dominance becoming so complete that none would face him, for fear of embarrassment. So the past months had been dedicated to tireless promotion, as his sponsors greedily whored him out to whoever met their price. Public appearances, photo-ops, endorsements; they had made Kurokame rich and wildly famous, but the constant presence of the spotlight was wearing on him. He finally thought he might earn a reprieve when they announced they had found him his greatest challenger ever; Myoseki, the horror of Hokkaido, as they called him. He had won several tournaments in a row, and in impressive fashion. Even more intriguing was his size; though Kurokame was an intimidating 6’2" and 500 pounds, Myoseki towered above him at near seven feet, and was rumored to be pushing 600. It would be a battle of two undefeated warriors, in a contest that would draw attention from the entire country. And so Kurokame had been dragged from appearance to appearance, allowed only a few precious hours of sleep each night before rising early to continue once more. He had spent countless hours standing on display in his mawashi, with his bored expression and bared ass equally visible. His promoters had promised him a record-setting gate, though publicly they continued to spout the historical importance of the match as their greatest concern. Kurokame had broken into sumo because he enjoyed the idea of being both and athlete and an artist, taking place in one of Japan’s most revered traditions. But now, just a few years later, he had grown disillusioned and decidedly disinterested. No, it wasn’t fear or anxiety that had curdled his innards. It was simply a deep desire to get the day over with. If he could simply get through this last match, surely he could disappear for a while, relax and unwind, and come back to his beloved trade refreshed and ready to continue. The door to his flat flung open, and a short Japanese man in a white suit breathlessly rushed in. "What are you doing? Get dressed, we have to go!" he commanded, rushing into Kurokame’s bedroom. Kurokame rolled his eyes; it was his agent, Doug. Doug had Americanized his name a few years back in order to be friendlier to overseas media. Of course, Americans didn’t pay much attention to sumo, nor would they appreciate it should they actually be presented with it. Despite the frenzy Japan was in over the match, there was virtually no foreign media present. But he still called himself Doug. Doug hastily snatched a modest ensemble from Kurokame’s closet and tossed the clothes into his arms. The entire process was so degrading; Kurokame wasn’t even allowed to select his own clothes any more. From dawn to dusk today, Doug and his entourage of personal assistants would micro-manage every moment of Kurokame’s time. Kurokame had long ago accepted this as the price of fame; but today, his patience was already wearing thin. His head began to throb with impatience; he longed for the old days when he could prepare in peace through meditation, rather than being trotted like a showroom animal from photo-op to photo-op. He suddenly found himself wondering, with some amusement, if a fall from his thirtieth-floor window would liquefy Doug, or merely shatter him. Kurokame begrudgingly pulled his clothes on, though Doug didn’t even pay him the courtesy of leaving the room first. The moment Kurokame finished tying his robe, Doug grabbed him by the arm and began pulling him towards the door, as best a 98-pound weakling can pull a sumo. On their way downtown, Doug ran down the details of the match for the hundredth time; the match had sold out the Tokyo Dome, an unprecedented venue for such an event. Kurokame had been in front of some large crowds before, but this would be something new altogether; television stations from around the country would be broadcasting the match, countless celebrities and prominent businessmen would be in attendance. There were even rumors that the Prime Minister himself would be in attendance, though Doug was having trouble confirming that. Either way, Kurokame had somehow allowed himself to be sucked into a veritable media circus, and for the moment he saw no way out. Seated in the back of his sedan, Kurokame stared idly out the window at the passing city, having tuned out Doug’s frantic rambling. On the ground the city was much as it seemed from above; the colors seemed bland, as if the streets were nothing but shades of gray. The air was dense, enveloping him in a sweaty cloud of smog. The streets were nearly empty; it seemed as if the entire city had ceased operation in order to hold its breath, as if bracing themselves in the calm before a storm. Kurokame shifted in his seat, wholly uncomfortable with the attention he was receiving. Fame was a heavy price to pay for reaching the heights he’d climbed to. At first he had been taken by the novelty of his newfound celebrity, but it quickly wore thin. Kurokame had grown to hate the bright lights and the constant interviews, being made to eat dinner with high-profile citizens just for the sake of being seen with them. But after reaching the title of yokozuna, the only currently active yokozuna of his time, Kurokame abandoned frustration and anger for apathy. He felt as if he’d been sleepwalking for the past year and a half, having chosen this rotten day to wake. He’d started out so young, so idealistic; now jaded, Kurokame found himself disregarding his opponent and his art, wishing only to get away from it all. "Are you listening to me?" he caught Doug say. "You’d better, because I don’t think you understand what’s riding on this!" …No, explain it to me again… "This is the match legends are made of. If we win tonight, we will have defeated every legitimate threat in the country. This is everything we’ve worked for! Every dollar we made in the last year and a half will be doubled from now on! Sponsors will pay ten-fold just to have their picture taken with you! Kurokame, I suggest to summon… whatever it is you run on, and get yourself ready to go. Now, let’s go get some photos done." Kurokame could remember little of the next eight hours. He was shuttled in and out of the car, driven through every corner of Tokyo, watching wealthy businessmen eat their meals and gauge their investment. But somewhere in this gap in his memory, Kurokame had begun to heard interesting whispers; they were all betting on his opponent. Despite his high stature, they had become enamored with his opponent, possessor of unrivaled size and ability. This strange notion caught Kurokame off guard, and forced him to recede even deeper into his stupor; something truly strange was brewing inside him, that odd feeling in his gut that had never faded away. It had started that morning with that sour feeling in his stomach and had worked its way through his entire body; a lingering numbness, an ethereal transparency that left him feeling distant and hollow. He had mastered the ability to achieve inner pacification before doing battle, but this was different. Not passive but empty, as if his soul had departed, leaving his vacant body to wander the earth. A thunderous roar woke him from his daze hours later, and briefly he thought a storm had erupted overhead. But it was no storm; it was the roar of sixty-thousand buzzing fans, restlessly awaiting the match of their lifetimes. Blinking a few times in an attempt to return to reality, Kurokame found himself surrounded by his entourage. The entire crew was there; Sukoshi Yamamura, his publicist; Nakashima Suzuki, his personal trainer; Tashito Sakora, his manager. And, of course, Doug. The locker room was awash with activity; his entourage chatting excitedly together, a deluge of reporters and photographers bustling in and out. The room was not particularly large or well equipped to handle a crowd, and Kurokame found himself growing a bit claustrophobic. He stealthily began inching towards the exit, hoping for a breath of fresh air, but the ever-vigilant Doug was right on him. "Where do you think you’re going? You’ve got twenty minutes, and you’re not even dressed yet! Your mawashi is back there in the corner, get going!" Kurokame sighed, slowly walking a few yards to the back of the room. His mawashi was resting carefully on a bench, and next to it his robe and shoes. He picked up his gear and glanced around, realizing the room was packed and there were no private areas in which to change. Flashing a furious glare back at Doug, Kurokame motioned for him to clear the room. But Doug simply shrugged and held up his watch, tapping it impatiently. Turning his back to the crowd, Kurokame’s eyes glazed over, a distinct wave of defeat rushing over him. Without another protest, Kurokame undressed and began to change, while the photographers continued to snap away. Tying his robe about his massive chest, Kurokame closed his eyes and knelt to the floor. He hadn’t given a moment’s thought to the match itself throughout the course of the day, nor did he plan to now. He didn’t care about the money, he didn’t care about the fame, he didn’t care about the prestige; he had entered the art thinking the only requisite to success was a pure soul. Now, he realized, one was required to sell his soul altogether to get ahead. The art he loved so much had been wholly corrupted, and there was nothing he could do to change it. He spent his last moments of inner peace reflecting on his career, a career he suddenly decided to be trite and void of meaning. His meditation was broken again by Doug’s frantic, rasping voice. "What the hell are you doing down there? Get up, it’s time to go!" he cried. His entourage circled him, each barking breathless orders into his ears simultaneously, as if they expected him to listen to a word of it. Slowly he followed his strutting agent out of the room and down a long dark hallway, while the rapacious murmur of the crowd grew ever more present in his ears. A voice, barely audible in the back of his mind, warned him of the approaching danger, of the consequences his lethargy may bring. But the voice was quickly drowned out as Kurokame stepped into the ready area, and the full roar of the crowd washed over him. Peeking out through the curtain, Kurokame saw countless Japanese restlessly milling about the stadium, breathless now as the moment drew near. Kurokame still felt as if he were dreaming, as if at any moment he would wake and find himself back in his bed. But a colossal cheer from the crowd proved how real the situation was. At once, every spectator rose to his feet and thunderous applause followed. His entourage were still hammering last-minute instructions and advice into his ear, but he pushed past them to peer into the arena. Across the vast expanse there was an awesome figure; towering over the spectators, wider and more imposing than any man he had ever seen before. Myoseki appeared to be everything he’d been touted to be, and yet Kurokame found himself underwhelmed. He felt no sense of awe, nor intimidation, nor competitiveness. The indignities he’d suffered that day, and indeed the past years combined… He’d been anesthetized with apathy, and there would be no waking from this nightmare. Myoseki stepped into the circle to await the champion, and presently he appeared. A gasp of reverence rose up as Kurokame slowly began yet another walk to the ring. His expression bordered on contempt as he stared up at the ring, wishing with all that was left of his heart that the whole ordeal would be over quickly. The routine had become monotonous and stale; he stepped into the ring and greeted his opponent with a bow, then turned to the gyoji and bowed again. He then turned and placed his hand in a shallow box, producing a handful of salt. He idly began tossing the salt about the ring, "purifying" it before the bout. Somehow these ancient and revered traditions seemed laughable compared to the capitalistic corruption waiting for him backstage. The buzz in the arena settled, and the Tokyo Dome fell silent. The gyoji began his pre-match incantation, as Kurokame removed his robe. Myoseki did the same and began the traditional pre-match gesturing. Kurokame suddenly felt himself sickened by the theatrics; he was being made to take place in an elaborate stage play, with novel eccentricities painstakingly acted out for the amusement of their paying spectators, masked as ancient ritual. Myoseki halted, flashing a confused frown at the motionless yokozuna. The gyoji continued, but looked appalled at Kurokame for refusing to complete the ritual. Astonished whispers rose in the crowd, as a truly unprecedented display of disrespect was taking place before their eyes. Finally, rapidly growing aggravated, Kurokame crouched and indulged them, with a sickening feeling in his gut. Finally the gyoji was finished. The buzz began to rise as the two massive sumos crouched to the ready position. Myoseki still towered over Kurokame, his face hard as stone and burning with competitive fire. Finally the gyoji shouted, and the match began. A roar rose from the crowd as the match of the decade began. At once the sumos charged. The approach seemed to last a lifetime to Kurokame, as all the thoughts and feelings of the past two years suddenly began to boil within him. His apathy, his emptiness, it all began to fade away as the monster Myoseki approached; every indignity, every moment spent in captivity, it all rose and seized him. He felt a ferocious inferno explode within him, and rapidly his body was seized by the unadulterated desire to destroy. To destroy the charade, to destroy those who sought to profit from his imprisonment. To cast down the treachery and the corruption, to breathe the free air and unleash the feral being within. Kurokame and Myoseki finally collided, and almost instantly Myoseki was rocked. Kurokame met him with such vicious, vile intensity that the massive man was nearly floored immediately. A gasp rose from the crowd as Myoseki struggled to keep his feet in the circle, eyes wide from the shocking impact. Kurokame did not relent; he met Myoseki with a brutal palm-thrust directly to the face, and immediately his nose was bloodied. The crowd leapt to its feet in shock, crying out in terror as the assault continued. Kurokame grabbed his stunned opponent by the mawashi and stepped to the side, wrapping his fingers about his opponent’s throat and tossing him violently from the ring with a brutal Judo throw. Myoseki flew across the circle and landed squarely on his head upon the floor below. A wail of horror rose from the Tokyo Dome; the match was over. The losing wrestler in a sumo match is known as the shini-tai, or "dead body." In this case, it was the literal truth. Immediately, all hell broke loose. Anarchy ruled the aisles as the capacity crowd cried for Kurokame’s head. Standing alone in the center of the circle, staring down at his motionless opponent, Kurokame felt an unexpected rush. Even as furious cries of Kinjite! Kinjite!* poured over him, Kurokame took a deep breath and relished the sensation. For the first time he could remember, he felt alive! A primal lust for violence had seized him, and he had struck down his conqueror with impunity. It was the most satisfying victory of his life; he had just brought down the most monumental sumo event in history with one bloody motion. Then, just as soon as it had come, the sensation was gone. It gave way to an overwhelming sense of fear as reality began to set in. Glancing about, he realized security was beginning to advance on him. Immediately Kurokame leapt into the crowd, where angry but terrified spectators dove from his path. Amid the sea of Japanese fans, Kimbusa stood out clearly, but the speed with which he ran carried him through the teeming masses and safely out into the street, where he promptly disappeared. What became of Kurokame started as rumor and evolved into legend. Some said he was secretly arrested and put to death for his crimes. Others insisted that whatever demon had possessed him was wandering the streets, searching for a new host. But there could be no denying it when a man matching his description reappeared two years later at an American professional wrestling event. The monster had fled the country, banished forever from his outraged homeland. The gap in between is still talked about among those that were in attendance that night. Whispers began to surface about an evil force walking the dark places of the Entertainment District, but as none would ever confess to having traveled there, they went unsubstantiated. Needless to say, the true fate of Kurokame, and his rebirth as Kimbusa, went unnoticed by a nation that still fears him. ------------------------ The room sits quiet for a few moments, before Steven Caldera realizes he is suffocating. He’d been holding his breath since the tale began, and he finally sucks in a desperate gasp of air. Sitting back, body shaking, Caldera draws his hands to his face. Kimbusa had gone into an odd trance shortly after beginning his final tale, and there he sits, quiet and motionless, eyelids quivering from the power of some terrible vision. Caldera clears his throat nervously. He opens his mouth to speak, but no words come out. Finally he stands and walks a few paces to his left, and with a trembling hand pours himself a glass of scotch. He takes another deep breath and drains the glass, relishing the warm, comforting sting as it slides down his throat. He briefly considers offering Kimbusa a glass, but somehow he didn’t think he would partake. Caldera quietly steps back to his chair and seats himself, leaning forward and starting at Kimbusa in awe. Finally breaking the silence, but keeping his eyes closed tight, Kimbusa begins to whisper. "I… I never did find out if he lived or died. Either way, it doesn’t matter. I disgraced myself, I disgraced my art, I disgraced my ancestors. In that one moment, everything I knew, my entire life, it left me. I wandered the streets that night, lost, confused and desperate. My actions were seen by the entire nation, there was no way out. There was no one I could turn to for shelter. I had no friends to stand by my side, no family to take me in. It is difficult to explain how lonely a feeling that is, to be trapped in a city of ten million people all eager to exterminate you." "But you found a home," Caldera said, trailing off, already knowing the rest of the story. "Yes, but even the Yakuza could not shelter me forever. They offered me sanctuary, but it came at a price. And after time, I had to escape again. I fled to the countryside, where I met a man, a man you knew once. Yoshi Kimawa, my sensei… he knew of another solution. He brought me to America and introduced me to professional wrestling, entered me in a tournament unlike any I had ever taken part in before. The genius of this… the simple perfection of this business. In wrestling I found a business where my extreme nature, my terrible past, it was embraced and celebrated. I did not have to hide it, and I did not have to compromise myself for it. In the American ring I was able to release my anger without fear of retribution, and in fact I was highly sought after for my unique talents. It has taken me two long years to find the balance in it, but now I have reached that point." Kimbusa sighs, finally opening his eyes, his voice firming up. "I have confessed my sins, Mr. Caldera. I have told my story. But in order for the story to come full circle, I must win this tournament." Caldera flashes Kimbusa an inquisitive eye, and Kimbusa nods. "I reached the pinnacle of sumo, I was the greatest yokozuna of my time. But I was disgraced. I reached the pinnacle of the Yakuza, but I was corrupted. I have been to the top, but each time it has brought me great failure and desperation. But now, Mr. Caldera, I am near the pinnacle of wrestling, I am very near the end. If I can reach this goal, if I can claim victory in this tournament, I will have reached the pinnacle without sacrificing my humanity or my morality; I will have achieved greatness in a true, pure form." Again Caldera clears his throat, coming to a sudden realization. "As a matter of fact, your next challenge happens to be an employee of mine, and a formidable one at that." Kimbusa stands, suddenly heading for the door. Caldera frantically chases after him, still not satisfied. Finally Kimbusa turns and places a kindly hand on Caldera’s shoulder. "And I will meet this challenge with the respect it deserves. But I will not fail this time, Mr. Caldera. This is my last chance to set things right." With that, he nods softly and turns to exit. Caldera calls after him once more. "Hey… Good luck," he offers nervously. Slipping his five hundred pound frame through the doorway, Kimbusa quietly calls back. "Don’t worry, Mr. Caldera. This time, I’m not out for blood." --------------------------------- *Kinjite is a term for "illegal maneuver," though it was spoken here with considerably more malice. A mawashi is the traditional sumo-garment worn in the ring.
