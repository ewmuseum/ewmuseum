Legacy: The Universe is Waiting Legacy: The Universe is Waiting Suddenly his eyes opened, and Jared Sykes leaned forward in his bed. Resting height weight on his elbows, he craned his neck to the right to get a better view of his alarm clock. The time read 3:14am, still a good six hours away from going off, assuming he'd remembered to set it the night before. His eyes scanned the room and a faints sense of disquiet slipped over him. He flopped back in his bed, his head denting the pillow, and sighed. He closed his eyes again and rolled to his left, then his right, and back to his left. He opened his eyes again, slower this time, the disquiet growing in him. From under the door came a faint orange glow. A sense of panic struck him like a bolt, and at once a flood of images poured into his mind: it could be a burglar, looking to make a quick score; it could be Mervin stopping by because of some unknown emergency; it could simply be that he forgot to turn off a lamp before going to bed. Through all those thoughts there was one that he clung to, regardless of how foolish it seemed. Ever since a child he had held on to the irrational fear that aliens were going to steal him away from his bed in the middle of the night. Throughout his formative years he'd done his best to manage fear this by diving under the blankets and pulling them tight over his head. The rationale behind this, however flawed, stemmed from a belief that if he couldn't see the monsters then the monsters couldn't see him. Even now, at 30 years old, he had to push the thought from his mind before succumbing to its pull. With his fear momentarily compartmentalized, curiosity took over. He quietly left his bed and slid his feet into a pair of sandals that happened to be nearby. Though he realized that boxer briefs and flip-flops were far less than the ideal equipment to fend off any potential intruder, the desire to go downstairs and explore pushed the thought out of his mind. From his vantage point at the top of the stairs he could tell that the light was coming from his living room, and so he decided to creep down the stairs and stealthily as possible, so as not to alert any would-be intruders to his presence. The plan was moving along flawlessly until the fifth stair from the bottom, which creaked ever so slightly under the weight of his foot. “Shit,” he cursed himself under his breath. The rest of the stairs gave him no trouble, and as he reached the bottom and his front hallway it was clear to him that he had not left the light on by mistake. Before him, and with his back to him, sat an unknown figure in an armchair. Jared’s eyes quickly scanned his surroundings looking for a weapon, ultimately having to settle on a ceramic greyhound statue that he pulled from a nearby hall table. He continued to creep towards the living room, raising the statue over his head with each step, but when the man spoke and a jolt of fear shot through him, Jared found himself unable to strike. “You can put that statue down now, Jared, if you don’t mind. I’m not here to cause a fuss.” He spoke with a faint Irish brogue, and glanced over his left shoulder. “What? Surprised, are you? You made so much noise coming down the stairs that I could have heard you if I was mute.” Frozen in fearful apprehension, Jared said nothing, though he took a step back as the man leaned forward and rose to his feet. The intruder wore a pinstriped white shirt and a navy blue suit that he smoothed as he stood. His hair was short and dark brown in color, while his eyes were a blue of equal intensity to the clothes he wore. Once on his feet he offered his hand, but the gesture was not met. “No need to be alarmed. Like I said I’m not here to cause you any problems. I hope you don’t mind that I let myself in. Didn’t want to wake you, you see. Figured that I’d let you find me when it was best for you.” Finally able to work beyond the lump that had formed in his throat, Jared nervously addressed his uninvited guest. “Look pal, I don’t know what your deal is, but if you’re not out of here in three seconds, I’ll…” “You’ll what? Whack me over the head with that statue? You won’t do that. It’s special to you. Your mom gave it to your after you had to put your dog to sleep.” Jared’s eyes widened, surprised that his unwelcome guest would know a personal detail like that. “Dewayne was his name, yes?” The stranger smiled as he withdrew his hand. “How did you…?” “Sorry, did that freak you out a bit? I’m not trying to startle you either, just need to make sure I have your attention.” “Oh, you certainly have it, chief. That’s why I’m calling the police.” Still holding the greyhound statue, Jared moved towards the kitchen, edging closer to the nearest telephone. “You’re welcome to do that of course, but you realize I’ll surely be gone by the time anyone arrives.” Jared said nothing. He picked the phone up out of its cradle and began punching in the keys: 911. Though the phone had power, evidenced by the soft glow of the keys, it produced no sound as each key was pressed. “Something wrong with the phone?” His tone was playful, but the implications severe. The notion didn’t deter Jared in the least, and he continued to feverishly mash his fingers into the keys. Frustrated, he placed the receiver to his ear checking for a dial-tone, but heard none. “Might be the weather, I find it seems to be interfering with quite a bit lately. Phone, clocks, you name it.” “Yeah, well maybe the cell will work. And why the fuck are you still here?” “Come now, there’s no need for harsh words.” “Are you insane? You’re in my goddamn house!” “I know, and as I said I don’t intend to be a burden, however there are some things that I’d very much like to discuss that I think might be of interest to you.” “There’s nothing you can tell me that I’d possibly care about.” “No? I told you about your dog. You seemed to care quite a bit about that, judging by your reaction.” Again Jared fell silent, unsure of what to say. “What? You don’t really think you’re as expert as hiding your emotions as you’d like, do you? I can read you clear as day, Jared. Now put your phone down and have a seat.” “Okay, fine, so you know one little detail about my life. You could have picked that up anywhere.” “What else shall I tell you then? Would you like me to tell you about the little episodes in your hotel room both in Canada and Las Vegas? Would you like me to recount in detail how you tried to drown out your feelings with whiskey and pills, two incidents spawned from your feelings about one woman.” Jared scoffed, his courage beginning to build. “You think that impresses me? So you scored some info from a bellhop, big friggin’ deal. This is really starting to get old, so either get the hell out of here or get ready to throw down.” “Fine then, and you tell me if this is irrefutable enough for you…” The stranger slipped his hands into his pants pockets and stepped gracefully into the front hall. “Would you like me to tell you how a few months ago you stood here, in this very spot, and started to make peace with yourself after what happened with your friend, the one named Mervin? You prayed that night. You prayed to God and you thanked him for the chance to make things right with someone you love like a brother. You thanked him for the opportunity to start anew, and you swore that you would never put someone you cared about in harm’s way again.” As the color drained from Jared’s face, his fingers slowly went slack, loosening his grip on the statue. It slipped from his hands, falling intact to a gentle stop on the carpet at his feet. “So I have your attention then?” “How could you possibly know that? I never told anyone – anyone – about that.” The stranger stepped back into the living room, stopping to place his left hand on Jared’ shoulder. With his right hand he gestured towards the couch, and then made himself quite comfortable in the chair where he had originally been found. “Please, sit with me. As soon as you hear what I have to say I’ll be out of your way.” “Just who, or what, the hell are you?” “You’re a curious person, Jared. I know this to be the truth. I appreciate your apprehension, I do. Please, sit.” With great trepidation Jared stepped into the living room, taking a seat opposite his uninvited guest. Though his hands trembled noticeably it did not prevent him from trying to gain control of the sense of dreadful anxiety brewing in him. “Okay. You win. Talk. What’s so important that it had to take place at 3am?” “I’m here to tell you about the life you’ve lead, the life you’re leading, and the life you’re going to lead if you don’t take what I say to heart. I’m here to help you choose your path, and build the foundation of your legacy.” “How do you figure to do that?” “Do you remember when you met your dear friend Mervin, or rather when it was that you became friends?” “Yeah, of course. Why?” “Tell me about it.” Again Jared scoffed, this time annoyed at the stranger’s question. “You’ve admitted to knowing things about me that I never told a soul, and you want me to tell you something that you probably already know?” “Humor me.” ** ** ** ** ** ** ** “Seriously, Dale, you’re acting like a total fucktard.” Marcus Dale had been terrorizing the students of Peabody Veterans Memorial High School since he was a freshman. Now, as a junior, he had established something of a rather nasty reputation. Few students were brave enough to stand up to him and his gang of miscreants, and those that did usually ended up bloodied for their troubles. “What the hell does that even mean?” Marcus shouted back at the young freshman who dared challenge his authority over the school yard. Jared Sykes did not back down. “It means you’re fucking retarded,” he shouted back, earning some laughs from the circle of students that had gathered around them, “But I wouldn’t expect you to get that seeing as how you’re fucking retarded.” “You shut your mouth, Sykes, or you’re next!” Marcus tightened his grip on the collar of much smaller boy in a plaid shirt, jeans, and glasses. Marcus was furious. He lashed out again, this time swinging wild with a right. It was ducked, and much to his surprise countered with a shot to the ribs. Even more to his surprise the crowd around him cheered the strike in between their chant of “Fight! Fight!” “Ah! What the hell, man?!” Jared let the change in dynamic go to his head, and was two steps into his worst Muhammad Ali routine before a very large, very angry Marcus Dale wrap tackled him to the ground. You can help explain this young man’s side of the story. I can’t promise it’ll help his case, though.” As the teacher led them back into the building, Jared turned his head towards his unexpected ally, looking at him with eyes that had started swelling shut. “Thank you,” Mervin whispered. “Any time, dude. Any time.” ** ** ** ** ** ** ** “And you’ve been friends ever since?” “Did you really come here to ask me questions you already know the answer to?” “No, I didn’t.” The stranger took a deep breath, and crossed his legs. For a moment Jared found the contrast in their dress - underwear and sandals against a suit, to be slightly ridiculous, but he showed no sign of his amusement. “I want to ask you, Jared, why do you think I mention this event in your life? I’m not here to outline actions, I’m here to help you come to grips with the motives that drove those actions, and what those motives say about you and the life you lead.” “Okay then, what’s special about this event? What makes this stand-out over anything else in my life?” The stranger cleared his throat, and leaned back in his chair. “By your own admission you were sometimes quite obnoxious as a youth, yes? You were known to poke fun at your fellow classmates as well. You even had a laugh or two at the expense of the very person you ended up protecting, and yet you took particular exception here when someone else did it.” “They crossed the line. I just joked around, but these guys… I thought they might actually hurt him.” “So you did what you thought was right.” “Totally.” “Without any regard for you own well-being.” “Well, no, not exactly.” The stranger raised an eyebrow in expectation of more from Jared. “Go on then.” “You don’t get into a fight without knowing that you might get hurt. It’s just how it is. Nobody honestly believes they’ll walk away in the same condition as they go in, and anyone who tells you otherwise is either a liar or completely fucking retarded.” “So you were afraid you might get hurt?” “Of course.” “But you pushed beyond it. Did it anyway.” “Yeah, what…? Why are asking me these questions that you clearly don’t need me to answer?!” “Because I want you hear you say it. You need to hear you say it, too, even if you don’t realize why quite yet. Everything I ask of you, Jared, is done so with a clear purpose in mind. You don’t have to understand that purpose right now, but throughout the course of this conversation I hope it should become clearer to you. “You were willing to put your own well-being aside, to push past the fear of getting hurt to help someone else. The tells me that you are not afraid of physical pain, or that at the very least you are able to look beyond it and see the bigger picture. Is this not accurate?” Jared did not answer. His brain had yet to fully process the speed and conviction with which his uninvited guest had spoken. It was difficult enough to allow this man to stay in his home, let alone know that he had intimate knowledge about him, but Jared now felt small under what he perceived to be an onslaught of information. “Yeah, I guess it is,” he said at last. “Good. Keep that at the forefront of your mind for now, because there’s another event in your life that I’d like to discuss. It also holds a rather fascinating insight into who you are.” ** ** ** ** ** ** ** At twenty years old, Jared Sykes was not the best student of Stracker’s School of Professional Wrestling. That honor fell to a classmate of his, Gabriel Simon. Ever since their instructor Darren Stracker, the son of the school’s owner, had scored Gabriel his first professional engagement it was not uncommon for the two to be seen conversing privately in a corner of the gym. “What do you think they’re talking about?” “I don’t know, Calvin. Probably how awesome Gabe looks in his new tights.” “Don’t say that too loud, Jared. They might hear you.” It was also not uncommon for them to be taunted from a distance by the class’ resident “married couple”. Jared Sykes and Justine Calvin had never met before signing up for the same sessions, but the two had formed a fast friendship that extended beyond the ring. After a break in the afternoon’s drills, Darren had once more pulled his prized pupil aside. Jared and Justine decided that it was as good a time as any to hang out in the ring and whisper jokes at their expense. “Oh shit. Oh shit,” Justine whispered nervously, “They’re looking at us. I wonder if they heard you!” “They are no… oh. Yeah, they are.” “We are so screwed.” “Nah, we’re fine. Watch, I’ll take care of this.” Jared Sykes at 20 shared some of the same defining personality traits as the 14-year old version, namely a striking inability to keep his mouth shut. “Yo! Heavy-D! Gabby Gumdrops! What’s up?” Understandably embarrassed and suffering from a case of the giggles, Justine tried desperately to hide her face. A smiling Jared met the gaze from his instructor across the room. “Sykes!” he bellowed, immediately bringing Jared back to the real world, “Say goodbye to your little girlfriend, and get your ass over here.” “She’s not my…,” Jared started to say, before a look from his instructor suggested otherwise. “Yes, sir.” With a concerned look from Justine, he stepped through the ropes and onto the gym floor. It’s amazing: he’ll be the first Stracker in four generations not to get into the wrestling business.” “Good for him. He was always a bright kid. I remember when he was little, and you’d bring him into the gym after school so he could do his homework. It’s a miracle he didn’t pick up a bad habit or two from the boys. Kind of makes me feel like an old man.” “Makes you feel like an old man? Christ, Sykes, what the fuck does that make me? You know what, don’t answer that. I’m going to have to listen to a night’s worth of your smartassed acceptance speech, so let’s spare me the jokes now, eh?” Confused, Jared cocked his head. “What acceptance speech?” “The one you’re giving, Mr. Hall-of-fame-inductee.” “Oh.” A wave of disappointment crashed over Jared, and he slowly slipped back into his seat. “I’m sorry if my message confused you, D’, but…,” his voice trailed off. Few people had as much influence or held as much respect in the eyes of Jared Sykes as his former trainer, and at that moment the thought he might disappoint that person was overwhelming. “I’m not being inducted tonight, actually. I’m doing the inducting. Bobby Irish, if you can believe it: professional wrestling hall of fame’s class of 2023. I mean I guess it makes sense for me to induct the prick, after all. We did kind of have a history, but it’s not like it was a good history from where I stand. Pretty hard kick to the ego, you know?” Jared picked up a small stack of note cards from the table, showing them to his mentor before casting them aside. “Oh, man. I’m sorry. But hey, you’re still young, you know? Forty-four isn’t as old as it used to be, kid. Plenty of fightin’ left in you.” “Actually…” “Jesus, don’t tell me you’re going to hit me with something else. You know I’m not good with surprises.” “I know, and I’m sorry, but I figured I should tell you.” Jared unbuttoned his shirt, sliding it off his right shoulder, and a pair of wide surgical scars became suddenly visible. Darren thoughtfully ran his nails through the hair on his chin, his gaze shifting between his friend’s eyes and scars. “The second operation on my shoulder,” Jared continued, rebuttoning his shirt, “it might have sidelined me for good. The doctor said that I might have another two or three years if I take it easy, but anything beyond that and I risk screwing up the use of the arm.” He stood from his chair, reaching around back and lifting the jacket to his tuxedo. He slipped his left arm through, but stopped as he struggled with the right. Reluctantly he cast a glance at his mentor. “I hate to ask, but I’m not through the rehab yet. Mind giving me a hand?” “’Course.” “Thanks, D’.” ** ** ** ** ** ** ** “It’s real, isn’t it? What you described… it’s going to happen. No family, no career, and nothing to fall back on.” “And what makes you say that?” “I don’t know how, I don’t even understand why, but the things you said earlier… I hate to admit it but they’ve got me believing that you know a lot more about my life than you’re letting on. I honestly can’t help but think that somehow, for some reason, you’ve got knowledge of my future.” The other man said nothing as he folded his hands together on his lap. Jared rose to his feet, running his hands through his hair as he nervously paced the room. “This is all just so goddamned creepy. I’m either out of my mind, or I’m hallucinating. Maybe both.” The stranger didn’t answer, instead curling his lips into a half-smile. Jared found this to be quite disconcerting, but he stopped his pacing and turned to face the suited man. “It’s true, isn’t it? You didn’t just make this all up?” Still the stranger said nothing. Unsure of his future or his sanity, Jared collapsed back into the couch. “Yes, it’s true. A Jared Sykes will have his life determined in the manner that I’ve mentioned.” It was an answer that he’d been expecting, though he was nonetheless ill equipped to hear it. Again he pressed his palms into his eyes and grit his teeth as if to will it all away. “Wait,” he answered finally, pulling his hands away from his face, “what do you mean ‘A Jared Sykes’? Is there more than one me?” “I came here to tell you all this for a reason, and to be quite honest I’d hoped you’d have caught on by now.” The stranger rose from his seat in the armchair and took up residence next to Jared on the couch. “Tell me something, and I want you to really think about this, do you assume that I would tell you all this, confess to you a future you’re on the path to experiencing, if I expected you to do nothing about it?” “But, wouldn’t that screw everything up? There’s a word for this, umm… ‘parallax’, I think. Wouldn’t telling me all this create some sort of problems?” “I believe the word you’re looking for is ‘paradox’.” “Oh, well still…” “You’ve been reading too much science fiction.” “So no paradox?” “No. No paradox. You see, Jared, time is not a fixed construct. Hours, minutes, seconds – think of these as a means to measure a distance, but not from any fixed origin. There is no clear path through time from one point to another. You’d be better suited to think of time like a river flowing downstream. Just like its corporeal counterpart it has to work its way around obstacles. You’ve seen it yourself, sometimes a rock or a branch will get in the way and the water has to adjust, has to flow around it. The same thing happens with time. “In some cases, cases like this here, time branches out. Off the river come streams, each one sourced from the same point. Water from the same source flows in two directions, but while the source is the same the paths it takes are unique. Those streams, they may spawn streams of their own. “Through it all, Jared, the universe adjusts.” Confused, Jared rose to his feet and again began pacing across his living room, trying desperately to wrap his mind around the notion suggested by his uninvited guest. “So, the version of me that you described… he exists, but… I’m sorry, this is way over my head.” “He does exist. He exists somewhere else along the stream. There are a hundred, maybe a thousand Jared Sykes floating through the river of time, each one born from an event, a decision, a desire to go in one direction with his life against another. The man I described exists in time, further down the river. Whether your own stream will join with his is something left entirely up to you, but he exists regardless. “The universe is waiting for you, Jared. It’s time for you to make your decision.” The stranger raises his right hand, pointing across his body at the clock display of a cable television receiver. The reading is the same as when Jared first woke – 3:14am. “The decision you make tonight will alter the rest of your life. You can continue to live in fear and apprehension, or you can come to terms with the things you’ve experienced. Keep in mind that whatever you decide the consequences affect more than just yourself. You’re building a legacy, Jared, every day that you live. You’re deciding how you want to be defined, and how you want those you care about to remember you. “Now, if you’ll excuse me, I have somewhere else that I need to be. This was an absolute pleasure for me, and I do apologize if I’ve kept you awake longer than you’d hoped. You’ve been quite a gracious host, despite all that I’ve told you.” The stranger stood from his chair, pausing for a moment to straighten his suit. He quickly brushed away a few pieces of lint that had gathered. He smiled once again at his reluctant host and stepped towards the hallway, moving into the foyer and close to the door. Jared, still trying to come to terms with the information he’d received, stepped into the hallway after him. The visitor removed a matching navy fedora from a coat rack adjacent to the door, and extended his hand in friendship. “There is no wrong decision here, Jared, take some comfort in that. But, if I may be so bold, the Jared Sykes who came to the aid of his friend, who put aside his fear and did the right thing regardless of the consequences, I don’t think his journey is quite finished.” His smile was warm, and as Jared took his hand he found his mind quieted and somewhat comforted. “I have no doubt I’ll meet him again someday.” The stranger placed his hat atop his head, and with a wink of his eye he opened the door, stepping out into the darkness. He slid his hands into his pockets as he walked away, his footsteps echoed in the cool night air. Jared remained at the door. He continued to watch until his uninvited guest had disappeared out of sight and only then did he close the door and step back into the foyer. He stepped back into the hallway that led to his living room, turning his head to check the clock on the television receiver that continued to read 3:14am. He stared at it for what seemed like minutes until it at long last ticked ahead to 3:15. Smiling and content he turned to the stairs that would take him to his bed and some much-earned sleep.
