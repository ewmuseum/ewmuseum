Ordinary World (Part Two of Two)

9 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-09 As the soles of her sneakers slapped against the tarmac, the melting rubber was ground deep into its texture. Each pace caused the molten, tacky rubber to 'string' as it was torn from the floor. Choking, toxic fumes masked Karina's feet in thick, dark grey smoke, through which the slightest hint of fire could be made out. She was running so hard that the world around her couldn't take the strain. Her lungs were already dry and burning, limbs already aching with the sharp discomfort of lactic acid, and a trail of perspiration followed Kari from her saturated skin. Clothes stuck to her sizzling body, where sweat had turned to steam, and her pink fringe was matted down against the sides of her face. Eyeballs had to endure the salty sting of her perspiration, while her tongue dealt with the taste as it dripped down over her opened lips. Karina Wolfenden was a raw, untamed force of nature. Her straining effort was the power of combustion, and in her wake a pressure-less void that ravaged all she left standing. Sprinting down a tree-lined avenue, her mere passing caused leaves to wither in the heat, drying to an amber-tinged brown, before flames licked their edges. A mixture of ash and foliage fell all around her as the fire spread to the branches, leaving a charred leaf litter scattered over tacky, smouldering footprints. Hanging low in the sky, the sun was more than just a source of hazy light... it was a target. Paces were stretched out, head was raised, and eyes closed. Her arms, which had been harshly pumping back and forth with every stride, relaxed, then hung loosely out to her sides. Strides added spring, turning her sprint into long, bounding paces. The sweat that had slickened her skin and drenched her clothes was evaporated off, the steam eventually subsiding to leave her arid dry. Eyes opened once more as eyelashes began to singe, and skin started to bake. It was glorious. Ring, ring... Eyes shot further open, and where before she was running on air, Karina suddenly felt gravity grab a tight hold of her. Ring, ring... Plummeting, Karina reached up, her fingertips trying to grab any grip on the clouds to halt her fall... but she felt the heat diminish. Ring, ring... Panicked, emerald eyes watched on helplessly as sky bled into cityscape, a change that repeated itself over and over as she spun back down to earth. Ring, ring... Crusty leaves tickled her cheeks, while asphyxiating smoke clogged her lungs. Through the dull, all-engulfing grey, she could no longer make out the world that she was soon to plough into. Ring, ri- "Huh?" Karina exclaimed, bursting from her sleep into an alert and upright position. Out of instinct, her right hand shot out, grabbing the ringing phone and snatching the receiver to her ear. "Hello, Karin-" she automatically began, until the sights, sounds, and smells reminded her of the world she existed in before the desire for slumber overcame her. "Sorry, I mean... Wolfenden and Associates, how may I help you?" It took a few more seconds for the startled K-Wolf to adjust herself back into her surroundings. The waste of space in the reception area that vastly surrounded Karina astounded her at first sight. The desk at which she was confined looked out onto square foot after square foot of featureless, carpeted floor. A distance - that seemed almost too far to walk - away, there were a pair of leather sofas, each flanked by a matching chair, where nobody had sat for nearly 2 hours. "Putting you through to him now," Karina continued on with the call, finally getting the hang of the switchboard. If any of her close circle of friends could see her, they'd rub their eyes in disbelief. It just wasn't Karina Wolfenden... on any level. The overly polite phone manner, the words delivered with a false, ever present smile, and even the way she sat pert and upright in her chair. As her head was thrown back into a deep, wrenching yawn, Karina's observant emerald eyes caught sight of the clock behind her. It wasn't even midday, and Karina Wolfenden had felt like she'd aged months and years, as opposed to minutes and hours. All morning, she'd been twiddling her thumbs, kicking her heels, and to sum it up neatly... been bored out of her mind. "Kill me..." she groaned, again shuffling in her seat to find the elusive position of comfort. "Kill me now..." After a while, even the shrill ring of the phone seemed to lose any vibrancy, instead blending into the dull murmur of the office's interior. Everything was a shade of grey, and all the wood was a pale, unremarkable pine. Everything was uniform, from the PC monitors, to the wire mesh wastepaper baskets. Only a few little photos that were stuck with pride onto screens seemed to break the conformity. The conformity imposed by her father. "Morning, Nina," one worker offered as he rushed through reception, late... and not even noticing the different young woman sat at the desk, spinning a pen like a baton between her fingers. Even Karina had now blended into the background of unremarkable office life. It was Monday, and her thoughts had already drifted past the rest of the working week. Tuesday she wasn't even thinking of, Wednesday was long forgotten, Thursday failed to hold her interest, and Friday was dismissed as just being another day of boredom. At the end of the week, Saturday shone like a celestial beacon. Saturday was a day when she didn't need to rise with the sun. Saturday was a day when even a split second didn't seem to stretch on forever. Saturday was when she would once again take to the ring. The release the match would give Karina would likely be felt by all at ringside, such was the extent to which she'd been tightly wound. Every kick would be a liberation, every flip a stretch of her freedom, every twist a flexing of her independence. When she was an RPW member, wrestling was just another day of the week for her. Just another pastime in a life of leisure. But now, with no weekly RUSH Pro fix, Karina Wolfenden was reminded of just why the action between the ropes had become so captivating... such an addiction. Even after one morning, she'd already began to understand why white-collar boxing was becoming an underground hit. "Can you sign for this?" Everything about the show helped create the unique feeling. Even though she rarely arrived on time, Karina remembered the looks of expectation on the fans' faces as they waited outside the building. Even confined to backstage, their energy seeped through the brickwork and plaster, letting the workers behind the curtain feel the emotions and excitement emanating from ringside. Every entrance whipped the fans up into a frenzy, either rousing cheers or seething jeers. Every hand slapped on the way down the aisle made someone's day, every pop created a memory, and every chant showed how fans were at one with the action. It was a feeling she'd tried so hard not to get used to... not wanting that feeling to ever lessen. "Excuse me?" She wanted to wrestle. She loved to wrestle... and although there were many, many better men and woman in the business, people wanted to watch her. The showmanship appealed to the normally shy woman. In the ring, she could look out into a crowd of strangers and not feel nervous. She could be surrounded by shouting and chanting, while not feeling the slightest bit intimidated. It didn't matter to the fans that she had little in common with them... and in the normal world, they'd live lives far detached from one another's. She was accepted as the K-Wolf. Nothing to prove, no reason to feel she had to. "Excuse me, Miss?" the courier asked a third time, raising his voice just enough to penetrate the K-Wolf's daydream contemplation. "Oh, yeah, sorry," she snapped out of it with, having to blink a couple of times in quick succession to re-adjust her vision. "Was off somewhere..." Not even looking up, Karina scrawled her untidy signature, then took the parcel and dumped it on the desk beside her. "I haven't seen you here before," the courier enquired, taking a closer look at Kari. "First day?" Finally raising her vision, Karina noticed the man was a little more appealing to the eyes than most of those who'd walked past so far. Deciding that a chat might actually help to pass the time, Karina engaged him in conversation... the tone in her voice betraying the boredom that was creeping in. Wait, make that cascading. "Yeah," she replied with a sigh. "Shame it isn't the last." "Boss a bit of a slave-driver?" he enquired back, resting his elbows on the desk's highest point as he leant forward. "Boss is my father," Karina answered. "Ouch," the man quipped, wincing to exclaimate his point. "Wouldn't catch me working for family..." "Normally, you wouldn't catch me working..." was the K-Wolf's quick and true reply. "Ah, well," the courier commiserated as he backed away to make his exit. "Welcome to the working world, kiddo." InDEED. Sluggishly, the hands forced their way around the clock face. Trying to find anything to fill the time, Karina had already resorted to surfing the internet on her work station... and wasted a good five minutes grumbling about how all the e-mail sites were barred. Thoughts of Saturday had even invaded her web surfing, as Karina tried to remember the correct spelling of her opponent's name. After a rapid flurry of key striking, followed by a satisfying slap of the return key, Karina was presented with the info she desired. "I must be so bored..." Kari half-moaned, half-sighed as she slumped forward onto her desk, looking out of the tops of her eyes at the screen. "Wow, this is one of the guys who beat Gabe... I think I like him already." Although not a real follower of any wrestling scene, even those she was involved in, the K-Wolf remembered RPW's resident loud-mouth - Invincible Gabe - losing a PTC Tag Title shot to a team called the Souls of the Black Lotus. Well, everyone in RPW remembered it, as Gabe even created a few new swear words to express his opinions on it the following show. Either Karina was paying her opponent a very, very high honour, or she was very, very bored. It was rare for Karina to even enquire as to who she'd be facing before the show. Unlike many in the business, she cared little for verbal sparring, or trying to hype herself up with overly bias self-praise. She'd even cringe every time she heard guys backstage mouthing off to the camera about how they'd beat this guy, that guy, and the other guy. Words that seemed to be forgotten when they walked back through the curtain, defeated. Even with the internet to vaguely amuse her, time continued to drag for Karina. Trying to scoop info on her opponent had only seemed to caused her sense of anticipation about Saturday to swell... and her thoughts about outfit colour and arrival times had evolved into move sequences and finishers. But having the weekend on her mind only caused to deflate Karina when she looked over her shoulder to the clock, which blatantly mocked her as it showed just past three. Dropping her hand back onto the mouse, Karina hovered the pointer over the Internet Explorer icon... then paused. If she managed to kill a few more minutes, there would still be over four days of it left, and she just couldn't face it. It wasn't her, nor would it ever be her. With a regretful, yet decisive sigh, Karina picked up the phone's receiver and hit her father's extension up. As always, he snappily answered. "Yes," his hurried words spoke. "Dad, I'm sorry," Karina offered, "I can't do this... Sorry..." "What?" a confused Lawrence asked back, before covering the receiver with his hand so he could ask whoever he was with to give him a few moments. "Karina? Are you there?" Having lowered the receiver away from her ear, Karina could still hear her father's words emanating from it, before she dropped it back down into it's rest. Swivelling her seat to the right, Karina hopped up, before freeing her mane from its tight ponytail. Away from the desk she walked, watching the lights above the elevator count down, and hoping they'd reach the 4th floor before she heard the doors behind her fly open. "He's going to kill me for this..." she whispered, knowing how her father was always angered by broken promises. Karina just hoped he'd understand. The usual ping sounded out before the hissing sound that accompanied the opening of the elevator doors. With a determined couple of paces, Karina walked in, turned back to face the doors, and patiently waited for the repetition of the hiss. Watching nervously to see if her father would storm into reception, Karina's fluttering stomach was eased slightly as the doors thumped shut before there was any sign of him. Attentive eyes watched the floors count down to the ground, where she immediately strode out of the elevator and through the foyer. Attracting even more odd looks with her hair flowing, Karina just blanked them all as she confidently paced towards the exit, where the man who greeted her that morning remained. "Are you leaving so soon, Miss Wolfenden?" he politely enquired, opening the door for her to breeze through. "I never truly arrived," she offered as a confusing answer, before sliding off her suit jacket and casually tying it around her waist. After descending the steps awkwardly, Karina just gave up and kicked off her heels, leaving them strewn across the sidewalk. Feeling its smooth texture underfoot, Karina took a few swift paces, which then threatened to break into a slight jog. Refreshed by the feeling of real breeze as opposed to air conditioning, Karina opened her eyes widely and invitingly to the world, remembering the vibrancy of colour that occurred outside the sterile workplace. The slight jog slipped up a gear to a light run. Undoing a pair of buttons under her collar, Karina felt the un-recycled air replenish her lungs, while the sun tickled her pale skin. The light run turned into a full, releasing sprint... her feet slapping against the pale grey paving slabs. With her blonde and pink mane flowing freely behind her, Karina's entire body was thrown into every stride. Odd looks were ignored, and deep breaths added fuel to her fire. Uninhibited, only the limits of her body could contain Karina as she ran freely, wanting to make up for the hours of inactivity... of mental sedation. She tried to run through the week, stride past Tuesday, sprint though Wednesday, bound over Thursday, and plough through Friday, all to get to the weekend and her next match. Never before had she been so ready. Never before had there been such a sense of anticipation. Never before had Karina felt so free in feeling like a wrestler.
