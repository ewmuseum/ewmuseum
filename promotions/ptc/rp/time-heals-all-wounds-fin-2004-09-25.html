Time Heals All Wounds... (fin)

25 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-25 “Darkness and the chirping of crickets, those were my only companions at this time of the night. I found myself wandering aimlessly through the dark streets, like a lost soul without purpose, my gait rather unsteady. It wasn’t the alcohol I had consumed… it was the emotional maelstrom I was attempting to fight that made me falter. A familiar sight gave me pause in my nocturnal wanderings… not the gym… as I had originally intended… this was a place more familiar. A safe haven of sorts. Perhaps my place was here, among the would-be-angels?” The confessional was dark, shielded by a heavy curtain that blotted out the light. Moonlight bathed the floor in a silvery, almost ethereal glow. Candles flickered in their votive jars, decorating the altar below the statue of the blessed virgin. He sat there, with his head back against the wooden wall, feeling the cold wood against his back. His long leather jacket was puddled at his feet, deeper darkness among the shadows. He took one deep breath after another, strangely comforted by these surroundings, even though he hadn’t set foot in a church in ages. A strange paradox this was, indeed. He held his hands out before him, thankful that the darkness concealed their trembling. He was afraid… terrified of what he had become. “I guess the shock had finally set in… after how long, I wasn’t really sure. Weeks? A month? I’m sure someone knows… perhaps Gabriel. I’d been lying to myself, and now I wondered, just how far I would go to maintain that fantasy. I wanted to go back and undo the damage, to restore their lives. I wanted to confess my sins. To be free of this burden of guilt… to be free of this pain. Instead, I was handed something else entirely… an opportunity.” There was a sudden bump against the wood, and a rustling beyond the thin partition that startled him from his reverie. Before he could speak, a small, plaintive voice spoke from beyond. “Father?” Jackson stiffened, and in the process bumped his head against the low ceiling… there were certain disadvantages to being six-foot-seven. The voice came again, louder this time. “Father? Are you there?” Jackson moved aside the sliding panel before him, revealing the mesh screen that connected the two halves of the confessional before retreating into the darkness. He could see the boy that sat on the other side clearly… it was like the contrast between night and day. The teenager was pale, his moon face streaked with the dried snail tracks of tears, his lower lip swollen, and trembling with emotion. After a moment of studying the man-child, he spoke, his voice low. “Yes, my son. I’m here.” “Bless me father, I have sinned. It’s been ten days since my last confession…” Jackson chuckled softly. “Ten days? Doesn’t seem like much, does it, my son? Ten days can pass by in the blink of an eye… ten days to our Lord in heaven is nothing more than a nanosecond. Time means nothing, and everything, have you ever noticed that, my son? Some wish for more… children like you, would love to have more hours to play… to do what children do… and then there’s the other side of the coin, the terminally ill person, begging for death to come…” He seemed to catch himself, and laughed again, this time with more good cheer. “Forgive me, my son… the hour is late, and I have been lonely… what is it that weighs so heavily upon your soul?” “I…” Tears filled the boy’s luminous eyes, turning them from slate gray to a dazzling shade of silver. They fell silently down his cheeks as Jackson watched impassively, unmoved by the display. He could have reached out and touched the child’s mind, discerning the nature of his soul, but he chose to remain where he was. He was tired, and more than just a little annoyed at the intrusion. “My son, do get on with it… the Lord God doesn’t have all night to wait upon your tears. Out with it… what have you done?” The words had an undesirable effect, making the boy only cry harder, sobs accompanying the tears now. Jackson felt a twinge of revulsion that made him curl his lip in distaste, and then he pressed his hands against the wood, letting his eyes fall closed, letting his head hang low. The child saw nothing more than a flash of reflected light off the sunglasses that covered Jackson’s eyes, and then he suddenly felt better, overcome with a feeling of peace. It was almost a pleasant high, like the one he got when he smoked that joint out behind the high school the other night. Soothing. The tears dried in his eyes, and he looked up, trying to see beyond the heavy screen. Jackson brought his head back up, slumping against the wall, his energy drained for the moment. When he spoke again, his voice was raspy, filled with weariness. “Dry your eyes, my child… the Lord works in mysterious ways… I’m sure nothing is as bad as it seems in the radiant Light.” “Yes, but… you… who are you? I don’t recognize your voice… you’re not Father Kilpatrick.” “Very astute. Who I am doesn’t matter, my child. I am here for you, now… that’s all that really matters. Let me be your Father, please.” “My father,” the teen’s tone was one of bitterness now, a sneer curving his bruised mouth. “that’s mostly the reason I’m here.” “Your father? Go on, Billy.” If the boy noticed Jackson calling him by his given name, he didn’t call him on the slip, instead he leaned against the wall, and replied, his voice strained with emotion. “Yeah. I disobeyed him again… I’m always doin’ that… and like the Bible says, you’re supposed to honor your father.” “And mother…” Billy shook his head. “Just me an’ my dad. My mom died when I was a kid…” Jackson nodded, continuing in that same patient, almost patronizing tone of voice. “Yes well, the Good Book’s not always right, my son. What did you do to get that fat lip?” Billy squirmed in his seat, averting his eyes, one hand rubbing the back of his neck. “I was… uhh… wrestling with some buddies… and…” “Lies, Billy. Don’t speak the garbage he taught you to say in this house of the Lord… tell me the truth. He did it, didn’t he?” “Yeah, but I was askin’ for it… he told me to clean my room, and I didn’t… I was reading the new Maxim magazine… and I lost track of time. I lipped off to him… I said…” Jackson leaned forward, finally visible to the boy as he removed the sunglasses, peering through the mesh with fierce intensity, his impossibly blue eyes glittering strangely in the half-light. “What did you say?” he whispered, his tone matching his expression for intensity. “I… I can’t remember…” “Bullshit!” The expletive was explosively loud in the confines of the wooden confessional, making Billy wince. He looked through the mesh, seeing Jackson for the first time, seeing him in a different light. All he could really see clearly was the priest’s face, and the startlingly blue eyes, filled with something close to pain. Billy sighed, and then haltingly repeated the story. “I told him it was ok… I would clean it right after I was done. There was still lots of time, I mean, I have late classes this semester, my spare period is first thing in the mornin’… but… I guess I was givin him attitude. He… smacked me… the same way he always does… I swear, Father… sometimes I just wanna…” “Kill him?” Billy lowered his head, nodding in shame. “Yeah. Father Kilpatrick told me that was a sin… that even thinking about it was like you’d already done it. That’s why I’m here. Cuz I wanted to kill him, so bad… sometimes… I dream about it… I’m an evil person, Father. Forgive me, please.” “Forgive you? Don’t be absurd!” Billy blinked a few times, utterly taken aback by not as much the words, as the vehemence behind them. He shook his head, and put his hand against the mesh, gripping it with his fingers, a pleading gesture. “Can’t I just say some Hail Marys? Or Our Fathers? Please, Father… I don’t want to go to hell for wishing my father was dead!” “Billy,” Jackson began, his voice soft, yet vibrating with authority, “Father Kilpatrick is a fucking moron. You’ve done nothing wrong… Christ Almighty, you’re just a kid. You can’t be responsible for his actions. He’d the goddamn adult… and you are not a punching bag. Billy, let me tell you something about wishes. If wishes were horses I’d have a stable full… and wishing someone dead doesn’t damn them, or you. Let me tell you something, when I was your age, I wanted to strangle the bastard I called father with the same belt he beat me with. I wanted to hit him until he begged me for mercy, like he did to me almost every night! That doesn’t make me evil. Thoughts are something you cannot control… actions, however… those you are responsible for.” Billy was ashen, his eyes wide with fear. “I… I don’t understand…” “In time you will, Billy, trust me. No Hail Marys… or Our Fathers… or rosary recitations are going to fix him. He’s the problem, not you. He is evil, not you. Repeat after me, Billy. I… am… not… evil.” Billy obeyed, his voice halting as tears sprang to his eyes again. “I…am…….not……evil.” He looked up, into those strange eyes, and what he saw now was unmistakable. Understanding. Billy watched as the man smiled, feeling the smile on his own face as the darkness receded, just a little bit. “Who… who are you?” Jackson shook his head. “Names aren’t important. I’m just a friend… nothing more.” “Thanks, Father. I…” He looked down for a moment, and when he looked back up, the portal was closed, nothing but silence coming from the other side of the wooden divider. Billy sprang to his feet, and dashed into the church, whisking aside the heavy velvet curtain that covered the little door to the priest’s side. He pulled open the door, and found the tiny compartment empty. His confessor, whoever it had been, was gone. As silently as a thief in the night, as magically as… “An angel,” he breathed, his voice filled with reverent awe. “An angel? Hardly. Last I checked, angel wings weren’t forged in the fiery pits of hell. I saw a lot of myself in that kid; perhaps that was why I’d been drawn here… to make a difference. Serendipity. A happy accident. Maybe, that was my new calling, to change the world. Creating order from chaos… perhaps I was destined to play God after all? Listen to me, getting all introspective and philosophic… maybe that scotch was hitting me harder than I expected. Or maybe, some shred of mortality was still clinging to me… tied to some tiny scrap of morality. Whatever the reason, I followed the kid home. I felt compelled to do something I hadn’t done. I felt the need to confront the bastard. I could feel the rage building inside me, at the thought of my hands around his throat, pulling that scrap of leather tight. My father. Billy’s father. Didn’t matter, they were one and the same. The more I pushed it down, the hotter it burned, a white-hot ball of anger, simmering in my belly. This wouldn’t be pretty.” He stood in the shadows, across the street from the small house, waiting for the light in the upstairs room to go out. Billy’s light. He drew the smoke into his lungs, relishing the sharp bite of the tobacco. Nothing like a Camel. Some vices he would never give up… not like smoking would kill him now. That thought brought a humorless chuckle past his lips. Morgue humor. Depressing really. The light clicked off, and he hesitated only a few seconds, watching the blue glow of the television on the curtains that covered the enormous picture window. His fist hesitated for a fraction of a second, over the door, and then he muttered an epithet, rapping briskly on the wood. “Who the fuck it is, at three o’clock in the morning… I’m coming…” The words were little more than a drunken mumble, but Jackson heard them clearly, the voice so much like his father’s it was eerie. A cold finger of dread trailed up his spine, and if he could have, he would have shivered then, suddenly chilled. Fear nagged at the edges of his mind, but he pushed the doubts away, standing tall at the door. The portal opened, revealing a fat man, a man who was a good eight or ten inches shorter than Jackson. “The fuck do you want?” Jackson grinned, the expression rather unsettling, his eyes hidden behind the dark sunglasses again. He was dressed in black from head to toe, his long leather jacket blowing against his legs in a sudden gust of wind. “Hello. Are you Billy’s father?” The drunken pig in the doorframe swayed, banging against the wood as he squinted at his visitor. “Who wants ta know?” “A friend. Of Billy’s.” “Yeah?” Billy’s father cackled with laughter, his fat belling rolling beneath the thin undershirt he wore, tossing like the ocean in a storm. “You that fucking priest that’s been diddling with my boy? I know you perverts, all alike…” “Believe what you wish, Mr. Anderson. I’m here to talk to you… about Billy… really, about Billy’s fat lip.” Billy’s father opened his mouth to speak, and closed it as Jackson stepped up on the stoop, forcing the fat man to back up. He closed the door softly behind him, and then poked a finger in the drunk’s chest. “Don’t even try telling me any lies, you sick bastard. I know what happened. The thing I don’t understand is why little men like you feel the need to beat on kids… does it make you feel powerful to see your own kid laying on the floor, crying. Huh?” “You think you know what you’re talkin’ about? You don’t know shit… who the fuck are ya? Some do-gooder who thinks he can come in here an’ tell me how to raise my boy?” Jackson shoved Billy’s father, hard, making the man stagger back. To his surprise, the drunk stumbed forward, shoving Jackson back against the door. He roared in Jackson’s face, the stink of his sour whiskey breath bringing back memories long buried. Jackson squeezed his eyes shut, almost cringing, although he had the size advantage. “YOU DON’T KNOW SHIT ABOUT SHIT, BOY! I oughta kick your ass too, just for stickin’ your Bible thumping, altar boy fuckin’ ass in my business!” The drunk’s hand lashed out, slapping Jackson across the face, startling him. “You want me to kick your ass? Huh, Mr. Preacher Man? You think you’re tough?! Come on then!” Jackson shook his head, holding up his hands. “I didn’t come here to fight you, Anderson. I came here to talk…” The drunk chortled with sadistic glee. “Ain’t that rich… he wants to talk. Talking’s for sissies and faggots… which one are you?” The drunk drew his hamfist back, and swung at Jackson, who caught the blow with his hand, easily, without flinching. He glared at Billy’s father, steely blue battling with watery brown as he forced the man’s hand back to his side. With his free hand, he reached inside his jacket, withdrawing the pitch black pistol from its holster. He pointed it at the drunken man’s face, noticing with a sinking feeling in the pit of his stomach, that his hand was shaking. The drunk laughed, mockingly. “You’ve either got the biggest set of brass balls I’ve ever seen, boy… or you’re just plain stupid! Nobody tells me how to raise my fucking boy! Least of all a snot-nosed city boy like you.” Jackson tried with all his might to steady his hands, holding the gun now with both, but it was still jittering wildly. Sweat was pouring down his face, the room seemed to be whirling in slow motion, falling away, until it was just the two of them, surrounded by darkness and silence. The years fell away, the present bleeding into the past… Billy’s father becoming his father. The drunk lunged at Jackson, trying to wrest the gun from his hands, and the two went down in a tangle of arms and legs, each battling for control of the pistol. “Let go, you son-of-a-bitch! I HATE YOU!” Tears filled his vision, further distorting the image, making the past more real as Billy’s father managed to pull the gun away from him. He stumbled to his feet, and held it triumphantly, a sadistic grin twisting his bloated, livid features. “Who do you think you are? Jesus fucking Christ? You can’t save anyone… look at you, boy… you’re a grown man, kneeling on the floor crying like a girl. I think someone’s father didn’t keep him in line enough… if you were my boy, I’d pull out my belt and give you a few lickins… that would straighten you right out.” Jackson’s head snapped up, his lips pulled back from his teeth in a snarl, murderous intent in his eyes. With a primal roar, he leapt at the man, colliding with him in a solid tackle. The gun went flying, spinning slowly through the air as the drunk slammed into the floor, with Jackson landing atop him. The impact left him gasping for air, and Jackson seized his moment, slamming his fists into the drunk’s face, over and over, each blow accompanied by words that were almost sobs. “Why couldn’t you just stop?!” Fist to flesh, spittle spraying. “Why couldn’t you see me?” Bruising, blood flying, bones cracking, teeth shattering. “Why couldn’t you just fucking LOVE me?!” He slammed his hands into the man’s face, until it was nothing more than a pulpy mess, but he was still alive. Blood bubbled out of his mouth, between shattered teeth, his breathing a horrible rattle-wheeze as Jackson drew his fist back one last time, sending him into unconsciousness. He sat back, breathing raggedly, tears streaming down his face as he sobbed brokenly. Pain… all he could feel was pain, tearing him up inside. “The CID is DEAD… and… I’m going to be a wrestler. There’s not a goddamn thing you can do about it now, you fucker. How do you like THEM apples?!” The words fell from his numb lips, making no sense, and making perfect sense at the same time. Two worlds had collided, leaving miles of wreckage behind. The past fell away, and he looked at what he had done, the destruction his own hands had wrought. He stumbled over the man’s legs, and staggered to the door, wrenching it open, tearing it off the hinges in his haste to exit the scene. He made it to the sidewalk before he had to stop… the horror of the scene still replaying behind his closed eyelids. He could feel the impact of his knuckles on the flesh… and he knew the satisfaction all over again. That, above all else was what horrified him, and sent him to his knees, gagging and retching. Nothing came out of his mouth but a bitter stream of bile mixed with top shelf scotch, he hadn’t eaten in days… “Revenge. Vengeance is mine, sayeth the Lord. In this fucked up world, I had just made myself the judge, jury and executioner. It wasn’t my place to make people pay, I didn’t fancy myself as a dark comic book hero, like The Punisher. I was just a man. A demon… a slave to my own dark past. He didn’t die, if that’s what you’re worried about. In fact, I did him a favor. He awoke a few days later, a few synapses in his brain rewired… the sadistic bastard had become as meek and loving as Boo Radley, a character from my favorite novel growing up. To Kill a Mockingbird. Good book… excellent Gregory Peck movie. Alas, I digress. Facing my demons had turned me into one… as surely as running away from the past had robbed me of my life. There would be no catharsis for me, no epiphany. There was nothing to learn by reliving this, nothing to gain by baring my soul to you. I felt empty, broken and spilled out on that carpet, along with Billy Anderson’s father’s blood. I don’t want your pity. I don’t want your hate. Honestly, I don’t care what you think of me. I don't want to fight your battles. I don't want to change the world. I don't want to be feared... or revered. I just want to be left alone. The events since my death have been… surreal. A better word eludes me, now that I am blessed with this abundance of clarity. Reality as you know it didn’t matter one iota. It was all like something happening in a distant place, having no real meaning or impact to me. I just didn’t care. Apathy. I knew now, that this cold, dispassionate monster was what I had become, and there was no going back. I could never be one of them now, one of the flock… the sheep… not knowing what I knew now. Even if some miracle afforded me a chance to reclaim my life, I would be compelled to walk away. Bradley Jackson was dead. There was no going back... I didn't fancy myself as a Messiah... I didn't want to rise again after three days. I didn't consider myself a phoenix. The fire had consumed me, I would not rise again, reborn from the ashes of my funeral pyre. I was damned to walk this earth, forever an outsider. That was my punishment... my lot in this life. But I would endure. I would move forward, perhaps there was a lesson in all of this, if I just waited long enough to read to the end. Life is a journey… and death isn’t the end. Oh no, not at all. It was just… The beginning…”
