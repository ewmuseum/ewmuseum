The Villain (Part 1)

15 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-04-15 The air is hot. The noise is deafening. You can’t imagine what it’s like to be packed shoulder to shoulder into a warehouse on the Chicago waterfront, hundreds of otherwise upstanding citizens all screaming for blood in a nice little diversion from their boring lives before they go off to some party reminiscent of a Gatsby-bash. There are two wrestlers in the ring. The first, Jormungand. She used to work in the independent scene with Ai Mei Montrose (Wild Chylde) in a place called World Hardcore Wrestling, under the name Keres, but left when it wasn’t hardcore enough for her tastes. Since then, she’s been traveling the country, crippling and permanently retiring wrestlers left and right. She’s a walking occupational hazard, to the point that promoters aren’t even covered by assumption of risk anymore. The only reason that she still gets booked is that 1) she works in deathmatch federations; 2) people want to see her get beaten; and 3) there’s always a fresh supply of meat that never pays attention to who else is out there and so fall into the trap of not expecting a lot out of her. Jor is really only 18, which is truly frightening considering how long she’s been wrestling. She passed for 22 when she first started wrestling, but when she kept growing and didn’t have any problems with acromegaly, it was discovered that she was only 13 when she started wrestling -- which brought an interesting dynamic into it because then it meant that Wild Chylde’s lesbian relationship with her counted as statutory rape… not that anyone would pursue those charges. At 6’11” and 254 pounds, Jor is massive, the largest woman to become a wrestler in years. And despite her size, there’s not much sacrifice in speed. A dangerous individual for anybody to face. The second, Bella Donna. She comes out of the Midwest, this being her first match. She signed up for the tournament for publicly-unknown reasons. All that people know is that she’s 43 years old, looks every year of it, and can’t wrestle to save her life. We know that much by how she’s faring in the ring. McCombs: Another hard hit! Bella Donna went down like these girls behind me are gonna do after the show! Loughlin: We REALLY don’t need to hear about that, fatso. While they make light of the situation, I see her struggle in the ring. Bella Donna suffers blow after staggering blow. They probably wouldn’t be so bad if not for the blood pouring from her head. I’m quite surprised that she’s still on her feet; the sound made when her skull cracked open from the thunderous right hand of her opponent… sickening. Loughlin: I can’t believe that this match is still going on. McCombs: I can, and that’s the scary part. We all know that Jor can finish off her opponent anytime she wants. This is just a punishment for poor Donna, and her only crime was getting in the ring with the Sadist. Jormungand; the female wrestler taking her name from the mythical Midgard Serpent, that is supposed to emerge from the sea, causing earthquakes and tsunamis, before it kills Thor at the time of Ragnarok. This woman is pure evil, and that’s why I like her. Right now, though, she should probably lay off. Bella Donna doesn’t need this kind of punishment, especially for this being her first match. Loughlin: And there’s the Spinning Ganso Bomb from Jormungand. And the cover, please let this be over for Donna’s sake. Loughlin: Nope, Jor pulls her up again. She’s really dragging this thing out. I can’t believe that anyone in their right mind would go through with this, but Bella Donna has her reasons. I talked with her before the match. Note that this isn’t verbatim what we said, but it’s close to what went down. “Jormungand? You realize that you’re going to get torn limb from limb?” I was leaning against the jamb of the door, the filthy partition allowing the rusty hinges to keep the door creaked open for an invisible separation between the dusty hallway and the dank locker room. No other women were back there; they were all watching the Xiao Suarez/Nikitress Melendez match from seats around the arena, though I know that Tanya Isama was one to get the aerial view from the catwalks over the ring. Bella Donna, real name Isabella Mazzelli, nodded nervously. She was lacing up her worn-out white wrestling boots, which went nicely with her matching denim pants and blue tank top. White… so sad that her pristine clothes were soon to be soiled with her own blood. But then again the worst thing about white is that everything looks ten times worse as a stain upon white. It’s too pure. So pure that it has to be kept in a vacuum lest the slightest thing horribly mar its beauty. Maybe it was fitting, then, that this Midwestern housewife, who had only a week’s worth of training and forty-three minutes of videotape study leading up to this match was wearing it. “You should back out now.” I was something of Jor’s mouthpiece. Unofficially, of course. She didn’t need me to talk for her; hell, she’d a mouth on her and nobody was safe from her acid tongue. Still, Bella was entirely outmatched and better just quitting from the tournament. “The rules don’t allow for referee stoppage or disqualifications. That means that she can do whatever she wants to you. And trust me, she’s VERY imaginative when it comes to ways to inflict pain.” A little about me. Remember Magnum Wolf? I’m like that, only in luchador form. The Quick, as I am called thanks to the mask and the physical attributes. Small, masked, high-flying primarily is the style. But even though I’m a flashy competitor and a funny guy, people HATE me. I’m cruel. I’m unforgiving. I’m a backstage cancer of the worst kind. I’ve caused the collapse of more federations than Raizo Tenzan, I’ve cheated more wrestlers out of World Titles than anyone can imagine, I make Magz’s feud with Karina Wolfenden look like a love story compared to the kind of shit that I put RANDOM people through on a nightly basis. No one is safe from my mind games and my manipulation, after all, it’s been synonymous with the name ‘The Quick’ for the past fifteen years. So yeah, I’m generally a fun, if despised more than Nazis, kinda guy. But I still have a heart. It’s in there somewhere. Just doesn’t get the light of day too much because I know that my real selling point is not what I do in the ring, but the fact that people come to see me lose, and as long as I don’t, they’ll keep coming because the anticipation never fades and I keep pissing them off, meaning more and more people will pay for tickets and pay-per-views to see me get mine. In the meantime, they’ll be closet fans, buying my merchandise and watching any place or show that I turn up on, but that’s fine. I don’t need or want supporters. They give me all the money and fame that I could ever hope to achieve without me pandering to them, so why the fuck would I play the nice guy? Wrestlers get the best (worst) of me because they usually bring it upon themselves. Thinking that they can get anywhere without my blessing. Dumbasses. But Bella Donna… she was different. She didn’t belong here. Really, she didn’t seem to be in great shape, was entirely inexperienced, and I could tell that she was so nervous that she couldn’t function properly. Her tank top was on backward. It might have just been her ignorance, though; she’d probably never put one on before. Not only that, but her jeans weren’t zipped up and the left boot was tied on the right foot. She was a mess. I noted, “You could probably get by with a throwing of the towel. But there’s no one to do that for you.” She sighed, her sagging breasts not helped by the tight shirt, though her shoulders went square for a second before returning to their original slumped position. “My son would, if he were here. At least, I’d like to think so.” She had a son. And she was doing this. A match with Jor is almost a death warrant for a normal wrestler. She’d certainly get the beating of her life and she had a son? No, no… this wasn’t right at all. What could she hope to achieve by this suicide? I had to know. “Why are you in this?” I asked, standing erect in the middle of the floor having entered from the hallway by this point. She looked up at me with her innocent brown eyes. “I’m trying to find my son.” I adjusted my mask. It was larger than my head; didn’t fit properly, the eyeholes kept misaligning and obscuring the upper half of my view. “There are other ways to find people. Private investigators, for instance; they work longer hours and typically they don’t require you to throw your life away in an attempt to find someone.” Bella shook her head, the brown pools of her soul closing. “I don’t want to locate him. I say a prayer for him every night, but I know that he has to come home on his own. I’m trying to find him spiritually.” My left hand sails over the crown of my head as I make a crackling sound similar to a radio. “Good evening, passengers. We’ve just reached our cruising altitude of ‘What the fuck?’ and if you look below you will see one very confused masked man.” Vintage humor that made The Quick the guy you all love to hate. But that’s how I do and you’d shoot yourselves if I stopped. “He wasn’t always like he is now,” Donna continued, as if she ignored me (and she probably did). “Before he signed up for that Cruiserweight Tournament, Silvio used to be so nice and sweet. Such a good boy. He was humble, true to his roots and his upbringing. Didn’t let wrestling and the people in it corrupt him. But it changed. He changed.” I was getting the picture. Silvio Fiore, winner of the 2005 Cruiserweight Tournament hosted by Primetime Central. He came out of nowhere to win the thing. Well, nowhere in the sense that PTC doesn’t really pay attention to what happens outside of it. While they had their favorites inside of the community, Silvio was quietly building a name for himself in Death Row Wrestling Federation as a young man of infinite potential. He seemed to invent a move every match, and had the special gift of being incapable of feeling pain thanks to some birth defect. Even so, he could be winded and knocked out, and with his small size, he became an underdog anyway. That, along with his babyface image with the people meant that as long as he was constantly innovating and always being the people’s champion, things wouldn’t go wrong for him. “He grew distant,” she said in a tone that indicated that she blamed herself for it. “Maybe it was because people started tormenting him more after he became a big name. I liked it when he was an exhibition wrestler; no one bothered him then. But the wrestlers came after him, the advertisers wouldn’t leave him alone; all that temptation… he became a different person altogether.” I’ve seen it happen. People who can’t handle the very success that comes their way have their lives thrown into turmoil. They can usually see the error in their ways, but it was said about Silvio that he still continued his bad habits picked up after surging through the second annual cruiser tourney to become an instant worldwide celebrity. Anyway, the important thing was that she wasn’t trying to physically locate her son. We all remember the wrestler who popped up starting in 1999-2000, who would claim as their backstory that their girlfriends were murdered by some guy and now they’re a wrestler so they can track the culprit. No, you cumslurps have that wrong. You’re a wrestler because you’ve got bills to pay and/or can’t psychologically handle a 9-to-5 job. The search for the killer is a diversion for the other 6 days a week that you’re not piledriving someone into the mat. At this point, I sat down on the floor with her. “You’re trying to understand why and how he changed?“ You always listen to someone’s story when it might very well be the last one that they can tell for a long time. She gave a weak “Yes.” Bad memories were coming to the surface. She subdued the tears. “When he joined that tournament, he seemed genuinely excited, but in a good way. I supported him in his decision to become a wrestler. Myself and his father. We knew that we had given him a good Christian upbringing and that even by himself in the world, he would make the right decisions. We were always a phone call away and whenever he was in town we’d go to see him.” “Sounds like you were the perfect parents.” “We just wanted our son to be healthy and happy. His father and I, we knew that he had a good head on his shoulders not to get himself hurt if he could avoid it. And we taught him all he could about how to be a good person. It was up to him to exercise the teachings and for a while he did. But there was just something about him that after he won, he did a complete 180. I suppose getting everything that he could ever want and more turned him away from God instead of his realizing that the success was through Him.” “You’re not worried about turning out the same way?” She shook her head, forcing a short laugh. “I’d be crazy if I said that I ever expected to win. But then again, even Silvio said that he didn’t care if he won. He just wanted to have fun, and I believe that he did. I just have to see firsthand what it is about this environment that altered him. I still remember his first round opponent. Sebastian Moraco. He was a good wrestler, supposedly. The first of many big wins for Silvio. He always said that his favorite opponent in the whole tournament was Sebastian, because he was another young man who loved the sport for what it was and that was all he needed to drive him on.” Nevermind that it wasn’t true, Moraco aspired more than anything else just not to be like his father, but that was beside the point. Maybe it was a good opponent for Fiore. Not that he wouldn’t get more or anything. He beat some of the best names in wrestling, period. And all in one fell swoop. Moraco was the best draw to start out with, because once he beat a legitimate mat wrestler and worked that into his repertoire, Silvio was impossible to keep down or keep a hold of. Moraco made Silvio a better wrestler, and an almost unstoppable cruiserweight threat. Isabella Mazzelli definitely blamed herself for what happened to her son. Silvio Fiore went from class act to village idiot to common jerk, and rather than the responsibility being his for losing his way, she took it as her fault for letting him be a wrestler in the first place. I could tell that she regretted her decision. Her last words before the match started were, “And maybe, and I hope that this doesn’t have to be the case, if my son sees me risking my own health to understand him, perhaps it will move him to come home and repent. I don’t want to judge him… I just want him back.” I looked over to my left and saw that Sahda Mehta had left a towel on the single folding chair in the otherwise furniture-devoid locker room. A bright yellow one, nearly the size of a beach blanket. Standing up, I walked over and picked it up, putting it over my shoulders. “You’ll need all the support you can get out there,” I told her honestly. “If it gets to be too much out there, I’ll throw in the towel.” She thanked me with a nod and a silent “God bless you,” before someone passed by and told her that she had to go out for her match. Jormungand has tossed Bella Donna over the top rope in such a way that her head falls back through the middle rope. By tumbling the legs out toward the floor, what has happened is that she is now caught between the twisted ring cables, being choked by the ropes. Jor is on the outside now, holding the legs up just enough so that Bella cannot support her body weight on the ring apron. Meanwhile, there is absolutely no air going to her brain, and the pulling of her body means that she’s being hanged without the noose. McCombs: This is it! Braindead Superstar! Jor saves this for when she really wants to make a point, and she’s making it now. Don’t mess with the best! Loughlin: This kind of format works in Jor’s favor. She can’t get disqualified and the referee can’t stop the match. She can do this for as long as she wants! Who signed Belle Donna to this first round matchup anyway? This has got to be a booking error; I hope the responsible party is made to answer for this! McCombs: It’s good watching, Jack. Just leave it at that. I haven’t seen this in two years, but here it is again and you have to ruin it with your disrespectful words. You can always count on the color commentators to be pandering jackasses, can’t you? Though truth is that I wouldn’t want to be in the ring with Jor; she’s pretty much crazy and would snap me like a twig. I’ve convinced her, though that it is in our interests to be aligned with each other, so even though we’re both in the deathmatch circuit I get a free pass from her reign of terror and am in fact the chronicler of it. Bella Donna, on the other hand, is getting the life choked out of her right now and there’s nothing that she can do about it. A little history about the Braindead Superstar. There have been only three uses of it in the past five years. Every time that she has used it, while the wrestler has not died, there has been permanent brain damage from lack of oxygen to the brain. She’s retired, permanently crippled, with this move. And it’s now locked onto Isabella Mazzelli. Loughlin: Why won’t anyone stop this? Why, indeed? For I did promise to throw in the towel. And this is as good a time as any. But I know that Jor will ignore it anyway. There’s no getting her off of that until she wants to, save for the entire locker room pulling her off. With the representation here among female wrestlers, that is a possibility. But there’s something else to consider. Did I mention that I’m an evil little bastard? I’m not going to piss off Jor to help a lady who shouldn’t even be there in the first place. She brought her beating upon herself. Just like my former trainer Skylar G. when he tried to confront me about my change in behavior and got multiple concussions from me. Just like my former tag partner Vangellus Oxios, who still hasn’t been able to breathe without a respirator since I turned on him. Just like my former girlfriend Sakura Shinsuke, whom I used to verbally and physically abuse on a daily basis because she allowed it to happen and pretty much invited it upon herself. Just like everyone else who thought that they had any business being in the ring with me. Mercy is for the weak. Mercy is by the weak. Bella Donna is the weakest of the weak, but I am one of the strong, and only the strong survive. That she could be so trusting as to believe that an asshole like me could actually care about her problems. Sickening. I can see her clearly. I have the perfect vantage point. After all, while she’s losing consciousness, I’m right there on the apron yelling at her for how stupid she is that she could be so trusting and so reckless. I warned Donna not to get involved in this, but she did anyway and look where it’s gotten her. The police finally move in to remove Jormungand from the lifeless body she‘s been choking for the past five minutes. I‘ll give her slight credit, Bella stayed awake for three. Then again, her eyes were open, but I can‘t tell if she was aware of everything. The tears were in her eyes though. She cried. Oh, how she cried. And I walk away from someone who will most likely never function properly again in her life. And I’m perfectly fine with what went down. Would I be aligned with Jor if I’m not down with the sadism? A fan asks, “How can you live with yourself?” Easy. It’s nothing new. Just a continuation, unfinished business, from the first time that I put my hands around Bella Donna’s throat. The day that I left her lying unconscious on the floor, and never looked back. I can live with myself because it’s easy to fail her as a stranger, since I’ve already failed her as a son.
