A Memory

17 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-17 The streets were all too familiar. Granted, he’d just visited a day ago, but this was different. His visit had been confined to a limousine and a quick stop at the club. He’d gone as a tourist, a dilettante. Now he was back, and this was much more intimate. It didn’t take Kimbusa long to get his bearings and realize he was dreaming. The back alleys of the Entertainment District had always been dark, shrouded in a charcoal pitch that obscured all but the most obvious details. But the tones were somehow more vague and transparent, clearly the products of Kimbusa’s foggy memory. He was standing in a place he did not immediately recognize. It was clearly the Entertainment District; he could tell by the rancid stench of booze and fear. The air was thick and enveloping, warm and stale with the fog rising from the depths of the sewers below. The electric cackle of a neon light flickering in is death throes was the only thing breaking the eerie silence, save the muted sound of traffic coming from the street a block over. Five- and six-story buildings lined the alleyway, buildings that from the street appeared glamorous and inviting, but from their back end revealed their true nature. They were worn and cracked, slipping from their foundations, poorly lit and poorly maintained. The brilliant lights and fresh paint out front belied the fact that they were little more than desolate havens for sin. Wracking his brain, Kimbusa searched for any memory of this place. He’d been to just about every building of significance at one point or another, but he could not place its significance. Why was he dreaming of this place? Why was he dreaming at all? His visit to Shindo has been quite anti-climactic, as it was clear to him that the Entertainment District and its master held no power over him any longer. He had freed himself from Shindo’s grasp, he had evolved beyond the simple mafia henchman of his past life. He was inflicting pain and fear now, but under the bright lights and attentive cameras of the American professional wrestling circuit, and the regard he was winning for his abilities surpassed anything he’d earned in these alleys. So what dark recess of his mind had chosen to return him here? From around the corner came four long shadows, followed by muffled voices. Kimbusa could not resolve the words out of the fog of his dream, but the faces that presented themselves in the alley were all too familiar. In the lead came three young lads, teenage recruits that Shindo had stolen from the bright lights of the city and corrupted for his own use. Shindo favored these kids for his more menial tasks, as they were eager to please him and easy to replace. Kimbusa had never worked with them, though he knew their faces well. Kimbusa had always preferred to operate alone, and certainly couldn’t be hampered by some sniveling child clutching to his sleeve. For the life of him, Kimbusa couldn’t recall their names. He’d seen hundreds of faceless, soulless youths pass through the Entertainment District, delivering packages and passing messages until meeting with a mysterious end. But these three stood out in his mind for some reason; but, much like the alley, he could not place it. Had he actually been to this place? Or was his mind simply imagining something that may or may not have been? Behind the three yammering teens was a dark, slinking shape, so dark that he could barely be distinguished from the shadows. It was Matsu, one of Shindo’s favorite henchman. Kimbusa remembered Matsu well; but his clearest memory of Matsu resided in a different anonymous alleyway, and ended rather poorly for the man that, prior to Kimbusa’s arrival, was the most feared man on the streets. Matsu called ahead to the three teens with a commanding grunt. At once the alleyway went silent, and Kimbusa watched intently as Matsu slinked back into the depths of the alley. Out of view, Matsu whistled ahead, and one of the teens approached a decrepit wooden door. He knocked, and at once the door swung open. A bright light flooded the alleyway, so bright that Kimbusa was forced to close his eyes and turn away. When he opened them again, he found the face of Steven Caldera’s driver leaning over him. "You all right? We’re almost there," he said. Kimbusa sighed. Somehow he’d been roused; the mystery of that memory would go unresolved. Still, Kimbusa was glad to be rid of the sight; the plane was speeding him away from Japan, and he was anxious to leave it behind him for good. Presently Kimbusa remembered the driver, and his dismay at having to tolerate him on the flight back from Tokyo. The driver had apparently been ordered to return back to the States, and had boarded the jet before Kimbusa had a chance to protest. Kimbusa valued his privacy, and the driver was a most unwelcome burden. Still, he had wisely kept his distance from Kimbusa, until he leaned over to inform him of their impending arrival in Los Angeles. Glancing through sleepy eyes out the Caldera Enterprises jet window, Kimbusa sees the lights of Los Angeles glittering on the horizon. In terms of size its skyline was quite like that of Tokyo, but that is where the similarities end. Tokyo’s light was a clean silver, penetrating and pure. Los Angeles had a grungy, hazy overtone, a brown tint to its luminescence, cloudy and dull. Kimbusa had always liked American cities for that reason; it was why he was so anxious to begin his new life in some American metropolis. American cities were impersonal, content in their sprawling anonymity, without any distinctive charms or personality. Sure, there were those awkward gawkers who would snicker and point at him from a comfortable distance, but for the most part they seemed content to move along unnoticed, absorbed in their uncompromising sense of self-importance. Tokyo may have moved faster, but they possessed a certain sense of hope that most Americans seemed to lack. And to Kimbusa, their desperation was empowering. "Productive trip?" the driver asked from across the cabin, doing his best to sound casual. Kimbusa winced at the sound of the driver’s voice. What the hell was he doing there? Kimbusa was usually a pretty quick study, and he’d made fast work of the situation. He could only assume Caldera had asked this man to follow him wherever he went, but Caldera’s motive eluded him. The driver was young, maybe thirty, with a hastily groomed beard and a casual hairstyle that seemed unfit for his profession. He was tall and particularly well built, though to a five-hundred pound, six-foot-nine man it was difficult to accurately size up an average man. Now that Kimbusa spent a moment studying him, he seemed oddly familiar, though he could not place it. Throwing his glance back out the window, Kimbusa tried to ignore the driver, but the driver’s unflinching stare finally broke him. "What are you doing here?" Kimbusa snapped, leveling his suspicious eyes at his fellow traveler. The driver seemed taken aback by Kimbusa’s cold demeanor, but showed no fear of him. "I’m just one of Mr. Caldera’s many underlings," came his austere reply. "Sorry if I’m cramping you, Caldera said you didn’t like to be bothered. But I’ve got my orders, and for the time it seems we’re destined to be together." Kimbusa grimaced and turned away. The plane was smoothly descending onto a private runway at LAX, and with a light bump they touched down. Neither man pressed the other further, riding in an uncomfortable silence as the jet taxied towards its refueling station. As they approached the glittering lights of the airport, the pilot’s voice broke the quiet in the cabin. "We just need to refuel, we should be back in the air in less than twenty minutes." With a sigh, Kimbusa slumped back in his seat and attempted to close his eyes. But he was restless now that his slumber had been broken, and the images of his odd dream began to play over in his head. Fidgeting uncomfortably, Kimbusa attempted to find a comfortable position and relax; but the mystery was too intriguing to conquer, and Kimbusa was suddenly overcome by the desire for fresh air. The driver watched with great curiosity as Kimbusa stood and walked to the cockpit. He swung the door open and barked at the pilot. "We shall be staying here for a while. Find a place to park this thing, and call me a car." The co-pilot began to utter some protest, but Kimbusa slammed the cockpit door and returned to his seat. "Uhm, I think we’d be better off getting back to Columbus, sir," the driver said, glancing nervously at his watch. "Mr. Caldera wanted us back there so you could prepare for your next tournament match." Kimbusa ignored him, staring idly out the window, his mind clearly on other matters. But the driver was persistent. "You should at least call him, let him know that we’re-" "I do not answer to him any more," Kimbusa venomously interjected. "I answer to no one." The driver retreated. "Cool, man…" Moments later they were standing in the temperate Los Angeles air, watching as a black Cadillac sped across the tarmac to meet them. Kimbusa was beginning to enjoy Caldera’s resources. There had been a time when he’d thought self-denial and sacrifice could strengthen him; he’d been taught from a young age to live a simple life. But a man of his power could not be contained by any one place, and Caldera’s long reach was affording him the ability to spread out. The Cadillac pulled up before them and a driver stepped out. Kimbusa slid into the spacious (even for his considerable frame) back seat and closed the door. Outside, the two drivers were in the midst of some debate, which Kimbusa’s companion apparently won. The driver that had accompanied him from Tokyo climbed into the driver’s seat and pulled them out towards the street. Kimbusa sighed. Would he never have some peace? "So, where are we going?" the Driver asked, sounding much too like a teenager embarking on a road trip for Kimbusa’s liking. "Downtown," Kimbusa muttered, staring out his window at a drab Los Angeles skyline. "Take me to the city." The Driver dutifully did as he was told, speeding them towards downtown without another word of question. For his part, The Driver was perhaps even less thrilled with the company than Kimbusa was. This was hardly his idea of a good time, and certainly fell out of range of his usual job description. He’d been asked to babysit some of Caldera’s more important partners in all corners of the world, but following around a disgruntled sumo wrestler was going above and beyond. Still, he had been given specific instructions, and he intended to see them done to the letter. Fortunately for him, Kimbusa didn’t seem to suspect anything out of the ordinary. In fact, Kimbusa was quite suspicious of The Driver, but whatever his motives were, he was quite certain he could handle the situation. For the moment he was content with the peace and quiet, and took a few moments to let his mind wander. He wasn’t sure what had driven him to deplane and take an unscheduled detour through the city. The claustrophobia had set in, and he’d certainly wanted to get out of the suffocating confines of the small private jet. But something was still nagging at him; that dream, those faces. Why could he not remember them? The Driver once again proved his usefulness, deftly maneuvering through the Los Angeles traffic and bringing them downtown rather quickly. Kimbusa cracked the tinted window of the sedan and drew in a deep breath through his nose, savoring the smell of the city. Despite his basic need for anonymity, Kimbusa had always preferred the urban jungle to the empty wilderness. He had spent his time in his first tournament alone, sequestered in a small woodland cabin in Colorado. At first the utter isolation was relieving, a welcome respite from the horrors of the Japanese underworld. But soon he longed for the towering skyscrapers, the innumerable faceless people, the ever-present stench of urban decay that reminded him so much of home. He’d been to Los Angeles before, but he’d never taken the time to get to know the streets. Now, cruising through what appeared to be a more posh section of the city, he realized why; the people looked plastic, dreadfully underdressed, even in late autumn, exhibiting their plastic, artificially constructed bodies in a sickening display of hubris. Women with fake breasts and lifted faces, so made over that they more closely resembled mannequins than humans. Perhaps that was an accurate description of how Kimbusa felt about American society; they were all mannequins, empty and mindless, drawn only to whatever light happened to be flickering the brightest at the moment. After some time, The Driver appeared to take a wrong turn. He began cursing under his breath, his head pivoting nervously on his muscular neck, frantically searching for a U-turn. Kimbusa watched as the streets steadily grew darker, the pedestrian traffic steadily thinner. They were definitely moving beyond the tourist-friendly section of the city, though Kimbusa could not account for The Driver’s worries. He knew there were dangerous parts of Los Angeles, but the streets they found themselves on now looked positively welcoming compared to the Entertainment District of Tokyo. "Stop the car," Kimbusa suddenly said, spying something that piqued his interest. The driver hesitated, letting his foot off the gas but not moving to stop. "Uh, are you sure, sir? I know of some better spots you might rather see, if you’ll just let me-" "Pull over and let me out now," Kimbusa snapped, causing the driver to obediently pull to the curb. Kimbusa slipped out of the car and flung his massive trenchcoat over his shoulders. Pulling the collar up over his face, Kimbusa slowly began walking down the sidewalk. From behind, the voice of the driver called out. "I can’t stay parked here! Where are you going?" Desperate to break away and find a few moments for lonely reflection, Kimbusa simply grunted and kept moving. Shit thought The Driver to himself. He’s not making this easy… From behind, Kimbusa heard the car pull away, and at last he was alone. Two blocks back he located the alleyway that had caught his attention. Darting across the street he stood at its entrance, gazing down the dark gap between buildings. They appeared to be apartment buildings, rather poorly maintained but hovering just above the slum line. Three young black kids were parked on the front steps to his left, eyeing him with great curiosity. Beyond that, the street was empty, save the ubiquitous background of car alarms, traffic and bustling humanity. Stepping towards the alley, Kimbusa felt his adrenal gland kick into action. His body tensed with preparation as he moved into the shadows. "Yo, don’t go in there, man," came the warning call of one of the kids. But Kimbusa ignored them. The shadows were his domain. The alley was hardly lit; only the ambient light of the city trickled in over the rooftops. He was drawn into the alley by the dream; somehow he felt the dreary confines of the urban labyrinth might spark some deeper understanding. Kimbusa moved through the darkness with an expert sureness of foot. Having spent so many years lurking in the shadows of the Entertainment District, his eyesight had been dulled, but the rest of his senses had been sharpened. His hearing was potentially keen, as was his ability to sense objects in space. As such, he didn’t see in the dark so much as feel; it was the sixth sense he’d developed by necessity in Tokyo, a sense that had proven most useful in the wrestling ring. Honed by the Yakuza, it was the skill never to be cornered, to always know what was lurking ahead (and behind), and the ability to use all his senses to never, ever, be taken by surprise. He came to what appeared in the gloom to be the back door to one of the buildings. It was slightly raised from the ground, accessed by a three broken concrete steps. He was at a crossroads; two intersecting alleys populated only by rancid dumpsters radiating rank odors that might have overwhelmed a smaller man. Crickets were chirping with mind-grating incessancy. There was a rustling amid the plastic trash bags overflowing into the street behind him, but Kimbusa could easily distinguish the sound of a rat from that of an approaching human. Most alley-dwelling people carried a fouler stench. At once Kimbusa’s imagination flared to life, his mind’s eye all the clearer in the dark. At once his memory was overwhelmed with images, information pouring out from those deep, dangerous recesses of his mind he so rarely dared to probe. Soon he had isolated the dream again, playing it over in his head. It had seemed so real in his sleep, and perhaps seemed even more vivid now in the shadows of the alley… Matsu called ahead to the three teens with a commanding grunt. At once the alleyway went silent, and Kimbusa watched intently as Matsu slinked back into the depths of the alley. Out of view, Matsu whistled ahead, and one of the teens approached a decrepit wooden door. He knocked, and at once the door swung open. A hideous ghoul of a human being stood on the other side. His grey hair was matted and wild, a two-day beard hung from his rotting features. He donned a black patch over his left eye, and held a cigarette between his rotting teeth. His wife-beater was smeared with a dark brown substance, but whether it was sweat, dirt, urine or blood, Kimbusa couldn’t tell. He began hissing at the boys in his doorway with serpent-like Japanese, snarling at them suspiciously. After a while one of the boys reached into his pocket and produced a full pound of white powder. The old serpent in the door snatched the bundle from the boy’s hands and tossed it to a faceless voice behind him. An angry debate ensued, all while the three boys stood patiently waiting to collect their payment for return to their master. Kimbusa’s blood ran cold as a sudden recollection fell upon him. He had been there that night, standing much in the same place he was standing now. He had watched the entire deal transpire, acting like the boy’s guardian angel from hell. Shaking his head violently, Kimbusa tried to stop the memory. But the vision was too potent. In an instant, the emaciated old drug peddler inside the doorway produced a pistol. Finally Kimbusa was able to end the vision. He had no desire to relive the senseless violence that transpired that night. But he had countless memories like that one, all indistinguishable from one another save the minor details of the cash amount and the body count. Why, then, had he remembered that night? Turning away from the alley he moved swiftly back towards the light. He blew past the gawking kids on the stoop and began marching down the street, doing his best to calm his breathing. Ultimately, he supposed, the memory itself wasn’t important. But the dream had come to him for a reason; his subconscious was drawing out the last of the lessons he could learn from his time in Japan. The senseless violence, the terror he wreaked in the night, they did not disturb him. But they did carry some significance, with respect to the path he’d chosen. He was not all that different now; he was still the same monster he always was. But he was no longer an unspoken fear lingering in men’s minds, a silent malice haunting the dark corners of the streets, casting a shadow of dread upon the city. He had been unleashed; his full splendor on display to those who would dare to stand against him. All his life, all the horrors endured and inflicted, was leading to this moment. All his past failures, all his moment of wretched weakness, all his shortcomings, all were parts of the grand movement of his days. He was in the crescendo of his life, ready to reach the climax with the greatest battle of his life. And that battle would not be fought in the darkness, but under the bright lights in front of the eyes of all with the courage to be witness. A car horn sounded behind him. The Driver had been circling the road and finally tracked him down. "Caldera’s on the phone," came his timid call from the driver’s window. Kimbusa sucked in a deep cool breath and stepped to the sedan. Casting one final glance back to the alley, he slipped into the back seat and closed the door. Closing his eyes now to summon all his patience, Kimbusa slowed his breathing and took the cell phone from The Driver. "What the hell are you doing?" came the irritated voice on the other end. "You’ve got a match in less than 48 hours, and you’re still not back yet?" Kimbusa considered arguing, but opted for a more placating tone. "We are on our way back to the plane now," he replied, with a confirming glance to The Driver. "We?" Caldera demanded. "Yes, the… driver you sent for me in Tokyo has accompanied me for the duration of my travels,’ Kimbusa said with a hint of annoyance in his voice. Caldera sighed audibly. "Look, I’m just trying to keep you on schedule here. Whatever it is you’re doing in LA, you need to start looking towards this next match with The Sentinel. It’s coming up fast and you need to prepare. "If there were one word I would be concerned about, it would be complacency. Don’t take anything for granted. You’re up against the top wrestlers in the world, and things might be coming easy for you right now, but if someone catches you napping this little run of destiny you’re enjoying will go down the tubes before you know what hit you." Sound advice from a true wrestling professional. But what Steven Caldera failed to understand was the true nature of Kimbusa’s evolution. He wasn’t simply becoming a great professional wrestler. He’d grown beyond that, exceeded the boundaries of traditional greatness and created an entirely new definition. He was more than a wrestler, he was more than a man. He was a veritable God of combat, wondrous and terrible to behold; immortal and infallible, a deity whose very whim would decide the outcome of this tournament. "Then I will prepare," Kimbusa said, and snapped the phone shut. Enough reflection. It’s time to start planning for the future…
