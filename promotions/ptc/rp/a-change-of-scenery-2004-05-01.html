A Change Of Scenery.

1 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-05-01 Oh Kari, Where Art Thou? #06 : A Change Of Scenery. 2/2 vs. Diablo The phone rang for the 3rd time in 15 minutes, but it's shrill call was heard only by Betsy and Psylocke, the apartment’s feline occupants. Opening her eyes just wide enough to show the murky green around her pupils, Betsy gave the phone a stare that only a Cat can... The "You're disturbing me. Stop. Now." look. When the ringing ceased, her head lowered back down onto her pillow, merging herself back into a ball of deepest black. "Leave a message if you want," the taped voice of Karina Wolfenden then offered as the answering machine kicked in. "It'll only cost 10 bucks." For a few brief seconds, there was nothing but silence throughout Karina's home. All the blinds were pulled down, masking the whole of her ceiling to floor windows. There wasn't even a red standby light on her television... The only things showing signs of life were the 2 Cats, and they were ready to take a bunk if the phone kept interrupting their slumber. "Beep!" then came from the machine. Karina had realised that less people bothered her with messages if she kept them hanging. For the most part, it worked... Unless someone was very eager to get a hold of her. And given Karina's natural tendency to forget things that didn't interest her, it was prone to happening quite often. "You really need to change that message," a stern, business-like voice declared. "It gives a bad impression." The voice could only have belonged to Karina's father - Lawrence Wolfenden. Although very close emotionally, their outlooks on life could be used to disprove just about every theory on genetic and environmental ideological inheritance. Lawrence was both everything Karina wished she could be, and at the same time everything she didn't want to become. She too wanted to be a loving and devoting parent one day, someone who'd do all they could to let their children blossom. But she could never follow in his footsteps as far as work went, stuck inside and office all day, having to smile falsely to keep clients and investors happy. If she could only ever see the half she admired, then they would have the perfect father-daughter relationship. But every now and then, she'd have to bite her lip and endure the other side to him - the side that cared about impressions, appearances, and making sure strangers went away with a good opinion of him. "You said you would be here at 7-30," Lawrence then continued, aggravation creeping into his well-spoken words. "It is nearly 8 now, and we begin serving at 9." From the mere sound of him, it was clear that he was in business-mode. Karina's life may be adventurous and care-free, but she had no stash of cash under her bed to fund her jaunts. She didn't have a giant share portfolio that kept her bank account topped up. And she certainly didn't have a well-paying job that gave her vast fortunes. She was a trust-fund baby, no matter how many ways she tried to disguise it with different words and explanations. Lawrence never made her be thankful for it, and he even tried never to remind her of it. But every now and then, she'd be needed to help sustain it. He was known in his circle as a hard-working family man, a straight-up guy, and the man you'd go to if you wanted fair and safe, as opposed to ruthless and risky. Men wanting to put money aside for their kids' college funds were drawn to him, as was practically everyone who worked for their family's future well-being. And to keep the image of the family man, he had to show them off. "And please say you've dyed your hair back to brown," his almost-disapproving voice continued, being imprinted on the whirring tape. "This is formal, so no sneakers either." He would open up the Wolfenden residence, a large home that was paid for by 80 hour weeks before Karina's older brother - Max - came along, to prospective clients. It showed them the man away from the office, and saved them to problems of going through a gauntlet of receptionists to eventually ask him a simple question. Each event was a lavish occasion, formal with every rule of etiquette followed... And it brought new business to him every time. And as uncomfortable as Karina was being paraded around as his daughter, she did it for him every time, without question. "Just get here soon, will you?" As the closing beep sounded out, you could almost hear sighs of relief from Psylocke and Betsy, who hoped that it would be the final interruption before their owner returned home with her usual post-dinner grumblings. Lawrence needn't have worried, however, as the second he hung up the phone, Karina's arrival was announced. Through the aged stone walls, the gutsy roar of a Ducati 748 pummelled. Even those tucked away in the centre of the house suddenly picked up a low grumble. As the sun crept down behind the newly blossoming trees that bordered the estate, a red flash wound along the path leading up to the house. Treating the approach as a racetrack, each corner was taken at speed, a fresh acceleration coming at every exit. Having been sufficiently alerted to her arrival, the domineering, solid Oak door opened, Lawrence immediately becoming bathed in the amber light of the beacon-style lamps that illuminated the front of his home. Shaking his head, he watched Karina take the final twist, sending a puff of dust and gravel into the rose beds, before pulling up at the foot of the steps. "About time," Lawrence grumbled, checking his watch when he knew his daughter was watching from behind her visor. Clad in red and black bike leathers, the K-Wolf hardly looked as if she was arriving for a formal function... And Lawrence would be lying if he said he wasn't concerned as to what she was wearing under them. Off came Karina's helmet, before she took in a deep breath of the fresh, evening air, her face hot from the stuffy helmet. A sigh of relief from Lawrence echoed Karina's breath, as he saw her hair had been dyed back to it's 'natural' brown colour. No streaks, no braids, no clips. With a shake of her head, Karina loosened her hair, sending the brunette's mane flowing down over her shoulders. "What, were you expecting pink again?" she shouted up to her father, who was still stood at the door, ready to escort her in. "It's not the hair I'm worried about," he replied, having to almost yell as his words were swept away by the brisk, Spring evening breeze. "It's the clothes." Knocking down her kick-stand, Karina felt the bike's footing secure beneath her before dismounting. Black and navy Vans sneakers embedded in the gravel, not giving the best indication that the K-Wolf was pulling off the formal attire thing. "Don't worry..." she groaned, the delivery making sure to tell Lawrence that his constant nagging had been acknowledged. Unzipping her leathers, Karina slid her shoulders free to reveal a plunge-neck, black evening dress, accessorised with a simple - yet elegant - silver necklace. "If you could look away for a second?" Karina asked, motioning for her father to avert his gaze... More to spare him embarrassment than her. As she slid the leathers down her legs, Karina pulled free the lower half of her dress, revealing a quick flash of black "shorties" underwear beneath. "Done," Kari then announced, and Lawrence turned to see the beautiful dress fall down past her knees. "Not bad, huh?" "You look..." Lawrence began, appreciating the rare sight of his daughter dressing as a female. "Glorious." "Yeah," Kari bit back, wanting to let him know that it was far from her usual attire. "Well it's awkward as hell... How come you get to wear trousers?" While Lawrence ignored her question, Karina reached into the rucksack that was buckled tightly behind her, and pulled out a pair of black, strappy shoes... A perfect match for her dress. "And I want you to know," she continued to moan, striding up the steps while still in her sneakers. "These things are going to send me to a chiropodist." Resting a hand against the deep green doorframe, Karina kicked off her sneakers, then yanked off her short-socks. Within 30 seconds, she'd changed footwear, then, after a quick tug of fabric and flick of hair, she was ready. "There," Kari announced, striking an un-provocative pose. "Let's see what fun awaits." Ignoring her obvious sarcasm, Lawrence paused after taking Karina's arm to lead her in. "Thank you, for this," he sincerely spoke, helping mellow Karina's rather combative stance. "It means a lot, really." Karina Wolfenden could be as bolshie as anybody, but sincere words of gratitude from those she loved always quelled her fight. "And hey," Lawrence than added, not wanting to sound too soft in front of his little girl. "It's a free dinner, right?" "Sure," Karina agreed, smiling warmly back at his attempt at deflecting humour. "Saves me ordering take-out." Proudly, Lawrence lead his daughter inside, where they were immediate dwarfed by the grand hallway. The staircase calved the room into two, the right leading to the guests and the left to the kitchen and dining room. Right away, the voices hit her, overlapping conversations and fake-sounding laughter blanketing her hearing. She could just about pick out her brother, Max, amidst the static, but the rest was just the 'same old'. "You should make the next one fancy dress," Karina jokingly suggested as she was lead towards the crowds. "Or maybe a pool party?" Just from her body language, and need to say something to fill the spaces, it was clear Karina felt out of place. It would be like if Lawrence had joined Karina in Liechtenstein during the week... Apart from the beer, he'd of probably felt he didn't belong. A supposed wrestler, seemingly intimidated by a bunch of middle-aged, tie-clad businessmen - hardly the persona to worry her rivals and opponents. Just for a brief moment, she even wished she were standing in front of Jeff W.'s despised "promo wall", reading hollow threats to Jimmy Diablo from an auto-cue... At least her feet wouldn't have been killing her. "Greg," Lawrence then warmly greeted one of the guests with, always ensuring such business gatherings were done on a first-name basis. "I'd like you to meet my daughter, Karina." Karina just about managed to move her lips into a less-than-enthusiastic smile to greet the man. She'd noticed him walking over from Max as they entered, so it looked like Greg was getting a little 'tour of the family'. "Pleased to meet you, Karina," Greg replied, his voice a little more relaxed and fresh than his stuffy suit would have suggested. "So, are you following in your father's footsteps? Or are you going to give Max a challenge across the court-room in a few years time?" The question was basically "what do you do?", simple enough, yet very hard for the K-Wolf to answer. "Er, not exactly..." she hesitantly answered, waiting for her father to step in. "Um, no, Karina is taking a year out," her father announced, hardly throwing the truth right out there. But then again, what could he say? "She's still deciding what to go for, so is seeing the world until everything comes together." In a way it was true. She had no clue what would fill the remainder of her years, and he crossed borders nearly as much as she crossed the street... But whatever the wording, Lawrence’s answer would have always been anything but, "she's a wrestler." For the first time, a new thought crept into her head - was she a disappointment? She knew her father loved her too much to ever see her as an embarrassment, but that didn't mean he wouldn't wish 'better' for her. If only she'd have come to work for him, he could proudly boast she was part of the company, an offspring ready to step into his shoes. But instead, he had to massage facts and expand interests to make her sound 'normal'. "Ah," Greg acknowledged, accepting Lawrence's words as nothing out of the ordinary. "Wish I'd had the chance to travel the globe before getting tied down." It was a relief to know at least one of the guests envied her. With Karina's introduction over with, the conversation immediately turned to business, and tax credits for partners' wives weren't something the K-Wolf had much of a passion for. Skulking away, her curious eyes scanned for drinks, before settling on Max... Who also seemed to have freed himself from the conversations that saturated the room. "I shouldn't be here," Karina shyly remarked as she reached Max. "It's just... It's another world..." Maximus Wolfenden - the father's son. Lawrence was almost too eager to announce that Max was nearing the end of his legal studies, and didn’t have to stretch even one truth for him. Karina felt she was always in his shadow, a fact that Max didn't take too long to pick up on, and was always first to speak up for his little sister. "Hey, if dad didn't want you here, he wouldn't have invited you," he assured her. "And to be honest, I think he misses having you around... When was the last time you even came here for dinner?" As well a speaking up for her, Max also had a way of grounding Karina when her thoughts were in danger of dragging her off. "I met him for lunch a few weeks ago," she replied, hesitant and guilt-tinged. "And I'm coming next month for grandma Sue's birthday..." "He misses you, ya know?" Max continued, making the K-Wolf seem almost disgusted with herself for drifting into selfish brat mode. "Sometimes I think these things are just an excuse to get the family all together every now and then... Doesn't seem like we see each other all that much anymore." "We don't really have that much in common," Kari curtly hit back, before immediately feeling like an idiot for it. "We have a surname in common," Max lectured back, "I don't think people can get more connected than that." Seeing he'd made his point, Max left a silent Karina alone, and merged back into the conversation that surrounded her. Despite her feet already beginning to hurt, and the feeling of near-nakedness from the less-than-substantial dress that hung off her slender frame, Karina's thoughts were all on what Max had just said. Her father wouldn't have rung with reminder after reminder if it was just some business dinner... And he wouldn't have made sure her room was made-up so she could stay for brunch if she was only needed to create a "family image". "I'm an idiot..." she moaned to herself as her shoulders slouched. But why did he lie? Why couldn’t he just say that Karina wrestled on a Sunday, was a Snowboarder throughout most of the week, and never, ever wore a dress when she wasn't required? For someone who RPW had forced forward to do combat with PTC, the K-Wolf spent an awful lot of time just battling her own thoughts... It's an amazing feat that she even had time to worry about opponents too. "I need a drink..." Kari straightened herself up, as if she was sniffing the air for the scent of Vodka, and began tracking it down. Through the bodies she weaved, doing her best to avoid being sucked into the vacuous conversations. Walking behind Lawrence, she tried to stealthily sneak around him, not really in the mood to be introduced to any more strangers, who’s eyes usually wandered down from her admittedly plain face. "Your son's on the wrestling team?" she then heard Lawrence exclaim while discussing colleges with a hopeful client. "Maybe he'll face my Kari one day..." And just like that, her body froze... Did he really say that? Doing her best, but not exactly a 007-worthy, job to seem inconspicuous, the K-Wolf tried to hear the words that followed. "Yeah, she's on the pro-scene," Karina heard him continue. "She says it's all a hobby, but secretly, I think she's starting to like it..." The K-Wolf didn't know it was possible to be so happy, yet so self-loathing at the same time. For every second's doubt that she gave her father's love and devotion, she'd be angry at herself for an hour. "I had no idea..." she whispered under her breath, and pride washed over her. "I think I've got a photo here somewhere..." Out of his pocket, Lawrence pulled his bulky leather wallet. As he popped it open, Karina expected it to be padded out with cards and receipts... But instead of credit cards, one of the pouched was stuffed full of photo-cuttings. "Here we are," Lawrence said as he pulled out a magazine-cutting of Karina in her wrestling gear, sandwiched between photos of herself and Max from all though their lives. "From her first show." She'd never once actually asked him if he'd watched her... Assuming that wrestling would be the last thing he'd ever follow. Maybe Max was right - they did have the most important thing in common. Much to her frustration, Karina then felt her eyes welling up uncontrollably... An act that caused her to growl with embarrassment, as tears of pride clung to her lower eyelid. Karina Wolfenden would never be the most dedicated wrestler in the business, and she'd never hole herself up in a gym to prepare for the big match. But in the space of just a few short minutes, she'd been overcome with a desire to win that dwarfed most wrestlers' desires. She wasn't a family secret, and she wasn't an embarrassment. She was the daughter of Lawrence Wolfenden, and proud of it. And she was the girl who was going to beat you, while her father watched at home, waiting for her next memorable moment.
