Tchu - IG 35 Defense Conscience Cancer Now The sound of skull impacting drywall was mildly unsettling. Mildly. “Seriously… just shut up!” With two fistfuls of shirt collar, Matt held firm and tried not to spit as he shouted, but a few drops of saliva shot out of control, splattering across the face of his prisoner. Up and down the hall, no one seemed to care about the scene he was making, slamming a guy against the wall and screaming like a nutcase. “I’m sick of this!” “You tell ‘em, Matt!” “Both of you,” Mat turned his attention in the direction of his cheerleader, “shut up.” With a heavy sigh, he released his grip on the shirt. “I think I’ve got an idea.” * * * A little while before 10B… 10B… 10B… where the hell was 10B? And why wasn’t it just ‘11’? A dozen stupid thoughts raced through his mind faster than his feet carried him past the rows of identical looking rooms. Why did they keep the floors in hospital halls so shiny and slick? So many people traveling them were either injured on hobbled legs, or rushing frantically to the side of their ailing loved ones. Just seemed like a disaster waiting to happen. And it was one of the many reasons that Matt hated hospitals… not because, like so many, they made him uncomfortable, but simply because they didn’t make much sense. Their floors were slick tiles. Their layouts were often confusing. The elevators never worked or ran too slow. Just not sensible accommodations. Still, what was he supposed to do when his father called and said, ‘Come to the hospital. Your uncle is dying.’ So here he was. Matt slowed his jog as he reached the point where the corridor took a sharp right. On his left was a large room… two patients. 10B. The tumor on the back of his Uncle Chad’s brain had been discovered about eleven months ago. The family had claimed they were preparing themselves for the worst, but when the results of the biopsy had come in two weeks after they’d found the lump, they were awfully crushed for something they were prepared for. Matt had handled it better than anybody… better than he should have, according to a few of his family members. “Hey.” He stepped forward into the center of the room. A couple of his cousins gave him an uncomfortable look as they moved to the side to clear room. “How’s he doing?” “Hangin’ in there for now.” Matt’s father put a hand on his shoulder and squeezed. “Doctor’s doubt he’ll make it through tonight, though.” “Ok.” His response didn’t seem to go over well with those within earshot. It wasn’t that Matt didn’t love his uncle… he did… but he didn’t understand all the crying and praying. None of it was going to change anything. None of it was going to save anyone’s life. So it all seemed like wasted energy. But, it was the way society expected a guy to grieve, and anything else was considered abnormal and unhealthy. When the doctors had given the unfortunate word… that it was cancer and there was nothing they could do… everyone had immediately been deflated. Matt’s father had even been offered three days, paid time off of work by his employer when the news had broke… a policy usually reserved for the passing of a loved one. For three-hundred-forty days, everyone had been shedding tears over his uncle’s death, only Chad had been alive for every one of them. It must have drove his uncle crazy. How could a guy keep up the fight and defy the odds when everyone around him was crying over his defeat that he hadn’t even suffered yet? Matt could only hope that his family would wait till he actually passed away to grieve his death. “Is Mary coming?” Matt’s father moved his hand off his son’s shoulder and turned his gaze towards his failing brother. “Yeah. She’s on her way… stuck in traffic on I-75.” “She was in a meeting with her publisher?” “They’d just gotten started. She rescheduled for early next week.” Matt stared at his Uncle, lying in bed. His chest slowly raised up and down. He looked more alive than most everyone crowded around him. “ She should be here soon.” For a few moments, there was an uneasy silence in the room. Matt was just about to make a pointless comment to his father, anything to shatter the nothingness, when he heard someone yell out from behind him. “Hey, what are you doing?! You’ve got a plane to catch in just a couple hours. Pay your final respects, say your ‘so-long’s and get going!” Matt spun on his heels and looked towards the other bed in the room. The rest of his family ignored the disturbance, continuing their choked back sobs. With slow paces, he made his way towards the curtain that was pulled around the entirety of the bed. He hadn’t known there was another patient in the room. Leaning forward, Matt squinted his eyes, trying to distinguish a silhouette behind the sheet. “Hello?” “Hello… Hello? I told you to get started on the goodbyes!” “What the hell?” Matt reached up to the tract around the bed and shoved the curtain to the side. Sitting up on the matress, sporting the typical unflattering hospital gown, was… himself. “Are you gonna leave, or what? The Infinite Gauntlet Championship isn’t going to defend itself. If I were you, I’d turn around, smile, wave, and head for the door.” “Who… wha…what’s going on?” “C’mon… don’t act like you don’t know who I am. You recognize your reflection in the mirror, don’t you?” Tchu propped himself up further in bed and ripped the IV from his wrist. “Let’s get out of here. I fucking hate hospitals.” “This isn’t happening.” “Actually, it is. And it shouldn’t be. You shoulda just told your family ‘Sorry… got at title to defend’. I mean… you are a wrestler for Christ’s sake.” “Was.” “Don’t pull that shit with me.” Tchu swung his legs over the edge of the bed and hopped down to the cold floor. Reaching over his shoulder, he grabbed a handful of the gown and tore it off. Underneath, he sported full wrestling gear, from the tights to the boots to the taped fists. “Just because you’ve retired from PRIME doesn’t mean you’re not a wrestler anymore. You are the IG Champion. You’ve got a banked shot at the Challenge Championship in TEAM. And you ought to be thinking about making the charge to take Snow’s Unified Title. Every time you step into the ring with the guy, you crush a dream of his.” “Stop.” Matt ran his hands through his hair, but it did nothing to empty his brain. “I don’t know exactly what’s going on, but I’m in a bit of a situation here. I know I’m supposed to be catching a flight so I can do what I do, but the doctor’s don’t think my uncle will make it through the night.” “All the more reason to just leave. Staying here isn’t going to help anything. Get over yourself. Your presence isn’t going to cure cancer. So let’s get going.” “Maybe you’re right.” Matt turned and looked back to his family, all standing around the room, the salt of dried tears on their cheeks. “No, I think you should stay, Matt.” The voice floated in from amongst his family. A nurse leaning over his uncle stood up and smiled. She was the ugliest nurse he’d ever seen. It was probably the thick goatee and two-day stubble of a beard. That and the tattoo ‘MARY’ on the throat. “Not again.” Matt’s shoulders sunk as he stared across the room at another version of himself. “Y…I… you look terrible in that stuff.” “Yeah.” The nurse-version Matthew slid past the member of Matt’s family. As he did so, he looked down at his reddish colored scrubs. “And just like you’re right about that… I’m right about this… you need to stay and be with your family.” “Ya think?” “Of course.” Matthew approached Matt and placed a hand on his shoulder, much as his father had done just moments before. “You’ve done the wrestling thing… accomplished all a man could dream of accomplishing. You’ve won the Dual Halo, the JitC You’ve won Universal championships… you defeated Jason Snow and a dozen other men to win the Infinite Gaunltet title… be satisfied with all you’ve been blessed with. You’ve fought and won your final battle. Now you need to be hear with your uncle as he fights his last.” “When you put it that way…” Matt rubbed the back of his neck, then sheepishly cast his gaze to the floor. “Shit… you sure do know how to manipulate a guy and make him feel like an asshole for nothing!” Tchu stomped towards his fellow selves, not about to let some punk in a pink shirt come in and talk Matt out of doing his job. “Why don’t you go change out bedpans or something.” “Why don’t you take your Neanderthal ass back to the cave and go strangle a bear.” “C’mon, guys” Matt turned and looked back and forth at each individual. “I’m really not in the frame of mind to deal with this, y’know?” “You’re not honestly gonna listen to this punk, are you?” Tchu pointed his calloused finger towards his other self. “Why shouldn’t he listen to me. While your over there pounding on your chest, telling him about how he should kick someone’s ass… his uncle is lying in bed taking his final breaths.” “Exactly! His final breaths. He’s done, that’s…” “Guys!” Matt grabbed two fistfuls of hair from the back of his head and quickly left through the doorway, opting to pace back and forth in the hallway in front of the room. In unison, Tchu and Matthew followed. “That’s enough, guys! Just give me a few minutes to think.” “I don’t really think there’s anything to think about. A wrestling match is a wrestling match. You can miss it. You might be able to even catch another flight and still make it. But your family carries more weight than any gold belt.” “This guy’s a real fucking poet.” Tchu shook his head and laughed to himself. “He is.” Matt shook his head, then nodded in the direction of Tchu “I just think… Neanderthal… is right. I’ve got a responsibility to defend my title. It’s in my blood.” “It’s your family’s blood.” “Right.” “And I just think it’s a pretty easy decision. You stay with the family, Matt. Any other set of actions would be selfish and foolish. When you retired from PRIME, you were making a commitment to your fami…” Matthew once again attempted to put a hand on Matt’s shoulder, but this time… there was no acceptance. In the blink of an eye, Matt swung away the offer, and exploded forward, pushing his hands against Matthew’s chest and driving. The sound of skull impacting drywall was mildly unsettling. Mildly. “Seriously… just shut up!” With two fistfuls of shirt collar, Matt held firm and tried not to spit as he shouted, but a few drops of saliva shot out of control, splattering across the face of his prisoner. Up and down the hall, no one seemed to care about the scene he was making, slamming a guy against the wall and screaming like a nutcase. “I’m sick of this!” “You tell ‘em, Matt!” “Both of you,” Mat turned his attention in the direction of his cheerleader, “shut up.” With a heavy sigh, he released his grip on the shirt. “I think I’ve got an idea.” “I sure as hell hope it involves you leaving this shit-hole and getting on a plane.” Tchu clenched his first a few times, wearing-in the tape around his hand. “No, it involves the two of you settling things. You guys want an answer… this is how we’ll do it.” Matt smirked, then took a step back and held out his hands to a spot in the middle of the corridor. “Whoever wins, we’ll go with their plan.” “Are you serious?” Matthew straightened the wrinkles in his shirt. “You want us to fight… right here in the middle of the hospital? That’s stupid.” “I’ll do it. I got no problems with that.” Tchu stepped forward, closing the distance between he and Matthew. “Fine. Whatever. If that’s what it takes to get you to do the right thing, Matt” “Good. Then get to it.” Matt stepped back a few feet, giving his selves some room to fight. With a quick charge, Tchu jolted in and threw a heavy right. At the same time, anticipating, Matthew clenched his left hand and swung hard for the chin. Matt folded his arms and leaned against the wall. If he was lucky, they’d knock each other out.
