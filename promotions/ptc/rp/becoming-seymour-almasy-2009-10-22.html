Becoming Seymour Almasy

22 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-22 I - The Hair Seymour Almasy couldn't quite explain why he let his hair get so long. Once upon a time, he'd had the excuse that it was for the ring. He'd had the excuse that his long, flowing locks were part of his persona. Now, though, he'd stepped into the ring only a handful of times in the past year, wrestling several matches for SCCW against the likes of Jay Phoenix and Lance Marshall. Even so, that was no excuse for allowing his hair to grow down to past his shoulders. It was a nightmare to deal with in the morning. Seymour regularly spent twenty minutes trying to wash and shampoo it when it was this long. Of course, there was a distinct possibility that this was simply because Seymour took stereotypically-womanly long amounts of time in the bathroom getting ready in the morning, but still. Spending thirty minutes with short hair was a Hell of a lot more appealing than spending fifty or even sixty minutes there with long hair. All of this was why Seymour was seated in a chair in the waiting area of his favorite barbershop, waiting for Nicos to finish up with another customer. The hassle wasn't worth it, Seymour had decided, as he usually did when he realized he'd let his hair grow out to Almasyian proportions. Again. "I'll be with you in ten minutes, Seymour," Nicos called, as he continued to carefully snip away at his current customer's hair. "Take your time," Seymour called back, reaching into his pocket. He was searching for his PSP, intending to kill ten minutes playing Dissidia: Final Fantasy. He still had to get Onion Knight to level 100, after all. What he came out with instead was his wallet. Shrugging, Seymour opened it up. There'd be time to play Dissidia later. One glance at his bulging wallet told him that there was another task that needed to be taken care of. "Yevon, do I *ever* throw anything away," he asked, incredulously, as he looked through the mountain of paper and plastic cards that had accumulated. There was his GameStop Edge card (keep), a Barnes & Noble membership card (keep), and no less than six hotel room keys (throw away). That was just for starters. There were also receipts from what had to be at least three months worth of video game purchases. "I'm going to have to chuck a lot of this when I get home," Almasy said, continuing to rifle through the cards until he came to the end of the line, a picture tucked behind the stack of plastic. It was probable that nothing in the world made him sadder than this particular picture. Having the woman he loved stare back at him from a glossy photograph was more than he could take sometimes, considering that he could only ever see her in pictures and home movies. Dead or not, though, Laura Winters was as active a presence in Seymour's life from beyond the grave as she was when she was alive. Seymour wore his wedding ring, to the surprise of almost everyone who knew him. Never mind the fact that she'd initiated their divorce proceedings (even if he'd deserved it at the time). That didn't matter. The ring was a tangible reminder for Seymour of her. The picture served the same purpose. Smiling wryly, Seymour knew why he always let his hair get this long every so often. Laura always liked it better that way. Almasy shook his head, unable to stop a sinking feeling from permeating him. Usually, realizations that he had done something because of Laura made him feel happy. Today, though, he felt trapped. Everything that reminded him of her was wonderful -- wonderful except for the fact that it also reminded him that she was never coming back. "Nicos," he suddenly called, as the older man was blow-drying his customer's hair. The balding fifty-seven year old barber turned off the blow dryer, and turned to face a man almost half his age. "Yes, Seymour," he asked, "What is it?" Feeling a bit sick inside, Seymour bit the bullet. Nicos and he had talked about their lives for the decade or so Almasy had been getting his hair cut here. They knew all about one another; Hell, Nicos had cut Seymour's grandfather's hair, when Cid Wilson had been alive. "Does it ever get better, Nicos?" Almasy asked uncertainly. Nicos cocked his head to one side, slightly confused. "Does what ever get better?" Self-consciously, Seymour looked down at his right ring finger. "Loving a dead woman," was all he could manage to say, his eyes welling with tears that he had cried more times than he cared to think about. In contrast to Almasy's reaction, Nicos beamed as he looked down at the ring on his own, time-worn finger. It was amazing to the Greek barber how time worked. He could remember easily the first time the ring had been put on his finger at his wedding, how fair his skin had been, how unmarred by time and work his hands were then. Twenty-seven years later and things had changed, as they were wont to do. "Believe it or not, it does get better," Nicos replied, knowing full well that there had been a long period of time for which he would never have believed that fact. "Isabella died thirteen years ago. I still love her, I always will. I couldn't imagine ever remarrying. But..." "It doesn't feel like you're constantly being stabbed in the heart every time you think of her," Seymour tried, sheepishly. Nicos grinned. "That's close enough, yes. Things will get better eventually. It takes time, as all things do." The barber took a brush to his current client's hair, fixing it just so before undoing the black smock around the man's body, and sending him off in the direction of the cashier. "It's been four or so years," Seymour said, dabbing at his eyes with a tissue retrieved from his pocket. "It still hurts as much as it makes me feel better to think of her." Nicos nodded, and grabbed hold of a long push broom, beginning to sweep the hair clippings from his prior customer into a neat pile on the floor for easy disposal. "The hardest part is being able to move on. That doesn't mean forgetting about her. It means being willing and able to live your life without guilt." The barber gestured at the three-chair barber shop. "For me, it meant setting out on my own after working for someone else for years and opening my own shop. At first, it was very difficult. I had one chair, and I was doing everything myself. Now, I've got employees, three chairs, and I'm making a good living for myself that I'm very proud of." Seymour looked at his elder, and saw the pride in Nicos' eyes. The simple pleasure that Nicos took in running his barbershop was something that Seymour's life utterly lacked. Every day was the same: wake up, eat, work out, play video games, go to sleep. Working out was only still part of his routine because it was so ingrained in him from the days when he would compete five or six nights a week. "Wish I had something I could be that proud of," Seymour said, glancing at Nicos with veiled jealousy. "You'll find something," Nicos replied, returning his push broom to its proper place. "I have faith in you. You've grown into a good young man. Your grandfather would be very proud of you." Generally, Seymour doubted that considerably, but it was a nice thing for Nicos to say. "Now, come on, it's your turn. What do you want me to do? Cut it nice and short again?" Seymour opened his mouth with the intention of saying "yes." What actually came out, of course, was something different entirely. Glancing at the photograph of Laura one more time, he placed it and his cards carefully back in his wallet, pocketing it. Almasy grinned, and headed over to his barber. "Actually, Nicos, let's try something else. I think I'll keep it this length, but would you mind dying it for me?" A slow grin crept its way onto Nicos' face. He had, after all, dyed Almasy's hair for as long as he'd been wrestling. "Which color, Seymour," he asked. "Blue? Purple? Red? Or..." "Let's go with the platinum-white," Almasy grinned. "I've got GTT7 coming up." Nicos turned to head for the storage area of the barbershop, to go get out the dye he would need. Before he disappeared from sight, though, he turned and looked over his shoulder. "Good decision not to cut it," the barber said. "Laura was right. You do look much better with long hair." Almasy couldn't help but chuckle, even if it did seem as if the world conspired with her sometimes. II - The Armor It was a decidedly odd thing to look at yourself in a mirror and see a figure stare back at you that you could barely recognize as, well, you. Seymour knew full well that the man looking back at him, clad in specially-designed foam and plastic armor was in fact himself, but it was a Seymour Almasy from a time that seemed so long ago. "When was the last time I had all of this on," he asked himself. "2004? Maybe 2005?" It had been a long time, whichever the case. The occasion for him putting it back on in 2009 was Sin City Championship Wrestling's "SinCon," a pro wrestling convention centered on the company. While Almasy's contractual status with SCCW was best considered "in limbo," he'd jumped at the chance to appear at the convention, sign some autographs, and generally have a good time before GTT7 started. Turning away from the mirror, Seymour came to face Jonathan Calver, his agent and one of the few good friends Almasy had in the world. "How do I look, Jon," he asked, spinning around for effect. "Pretty damned good, I must say," Calver admitted. "Looks like you never stopped putting the armor on." "It's heavier than I remembered," Seymour said. "Probably just because it's been ages..." He never actually wrestled in the armor. It was always taken off carefully before matches, because, well, it wasn't meant to stand up to kicks and punches and body slams. Even so, Seymour always felt...comfortable when he put it on, as if he really was a warrior headed to do battle against a dangerous foe in the days when three feet of steel was a man's salvation against man or beast. "You're on, Seymour," Jonathan said, glancing down at his watch. "Let's get out there!" As Seymour headed for the door, he slowly got re-acclimated to walking in his armor. His movements were stiffer than he remembered, even with the joints designed to make walking easier. Opening the door, Seymour stepped out into what could only be described as something...different. Sure, Seymour knew that he was in a convention center in Tucson, Arizona, but the center was set up in such a way that it was easy to forget the outside world. SCCW memorabilia dealers were everywhere, hawking shirts, foam fingers, and replica title belts. A regulation size SCCW ring sat in the middle of the room Seymour entered. Children were clambering up the ring steps, and bouncing off the ropes, pretending to be all manner of their favorite Sinners. Just WHY parents would allow their kids to watch wrestling on Cinemax and go to a convention for that wrestling promotion is a question for those parents and those parents alone. All of that, though, didn't fully explain why Seymour felt at home here. Wrestling was in his blood, sure, but really, it was the costumes that did the trick. There were fans everywhere dressed up as their favorite SCCW superstars. And, as it normally went at similar conventions, such as those held for anime around the country, some of the cosplayers were really good, and some were really not. Almasy spotted a King Blueberry that had him wondering if it was, in fact, the real Yahweh of Yogurt. Counterpoint to that was an admittedly overweight woman, with hair dyed red and her frame squeezed into a far-too-tight white pantsuit. "You know," Seymour murmured to his agent, "I never thought anything could be more frightening than the ACTUAL Alexandra Pierce." Agent and client shuddered at roughly the same time. Walking past the commentary booth, where fans of all ages could commentate with either Dave Gibson or Eugene Ware (ID proving one was over the age of 18 was required to work with Mr. Ware), Seymour soon found himself in the autograph section, where lines of fans stretched for many of Sin City's best. Sure, Almasy didn't have the sort of line waiting for him that he would have once commanded, but there were still a respectable number of fans waiting to have all manner of things signed by the Final Fantasy. "Sorry I'm a little late, everyone," he apologized, settling into a seat behind his table. "I sort of got caught up looking at everything. This place is great!" Seymour estimated that he'd probably done over five-hundred autograph sessions in his career. Some were big, like this one, and others were smaller, but several things made them all enjoyable. One, the fans loved to talk. Sure, Almasy had to keep the line moving, but generally he could get in a decent quick conversation with most of the autograph-seekers. Some wanted to know about him personally. Others wanted to know his thoughts on other promotions and wrestlers. Provided the subject wasn't too politically charged, Seymour was more than happy to oblige with commentary. "It's not that I don't *like* PRIME," he told one fan wearing a PRIME shirt, while autographing an 8 x 10 photograph of himself holding the PTC Global Championship, "it's more that it feels too corporate to me. It's considered the big time, sure, but I've worked for lots of big time places that seem less stuffy. Could be wrong, though. Just my impressions." A couple dozen conversations like that and a day would absolutely fly by. Two, the sheer variety of stuff the fans brought for him to sign always made him remember things he'd forgotten. Like the UWF "Fantasy is Reality" t-shirt that he'd gotten a month after joining that company in 2003. Like a program from PTC Super Series, a mostly-forgotten inter-promotional event that had seen him compete in a Scavenger Hunt Match for the PTC Global and Extreme Championships. There was, however, the occasional thing that took him out of the moment. Today's "thing" was presented to him by a female fan who wore one of Seymour's t-shirts. Obviously she was a fan of his, and even more obviously, she didn't know the impact of the photograph that she was placing on the table for Seymour to sign. For the second time in as many weeks, Seymour Almasy saw his wife staring up at him from a picture. This time, though, the picture wasn't just of her. It showed both Seymour and Laura, the PTC Tag Team Championship belts draped over their shoulders. Laura's ring attire left considerably less to the imagination than the candid photo of her in Seymour's wallet did, which sent a whole other set of memories racing back to Seymour's mind that he didn't need while trying to sign autographs. With the exception of the first time that he'd attempted to have sex with Laura, Seymour couldn't remember another occasion that he'd been aroused and teary-eyed at the same time. Through blurry eyes, he noticed something else unique about the photo. Right next to Laura's name under her picture was her signature. "Wh-when did you get this signed by her," he asked, trying not to look pitiful in front of his fans. For her part, the young woman looked equally stunned, as if she hadn't quite expected the reaction she'd gotten. "I got it signed when she was in XUW's development promotion," she replied. "I didn't know it would upset you this badly. I just wanted to get your signature to match..." Swallowing hard, Seymour nodded. "It's okay. I can sign it." With a trembling hand, he picked up his Sharpie marker, signing his name in a shaky script besides his printed name on the publicity photo. He handed it back to her, and their eyes met for a moment. Green eyes. Just like hers. The tears came down in streams, prompting Jonathan Calver to step in front of the line quickly, waving his hands. "I'm sorry, this autograph session is--" "No." Calver turned to face his sobbing client. "It's okay, Seymour. They'll understa--" "NO!" Even as his lower lip trembled and tears poured down his cheeks, there was a resolve in Seymour's eyes that hadn't been there in years. "I'm going to finish this," he insisted. "Step aside, Jonathan. N-next in line, please?" Awed, and slightly frightened, Jonathan Calver stepped aside and simply watched. Fan after fan stepped to the front of the line. Some patted Seymour on the shoulder. Others shook his hand and clapped him on the back. All presented him with an item to sign. He conversed with them as best he could through the tears, making a thousand apologies along the way. His Sharpie hand flew, writing his name time after time on everything from foam belts to calendars. Only when the line was done did Almasy allow his head to fall into his hands. Hunched over the table, he cried in peace for several minutes, before Jonathan finally had to grab Seymour by the elbow. "Let's go back, Seymour," he whispered, gently, tugging at Almasy's arm. Seymour rose, slowly, still sobbing, allowing Calver to lead him towards one of the doors, as if he was a child. It was among the longest hundred yards Almasy had ever walked, but he made it out of the main convention room with his agent's help. Calver looked around for something resembling privacy, but the closest thing he could see was the men's bathroom. It would have to do. "In here," Calver encouraged, half-guiding, half-pulling Seymour into the bathroom after opening the door. Thankfully, there was no one else inside, allowing Jonathan to help Seymour over to the sink. "Are you going to be okay?" The sobs had been downgraded to sniffles. Seymour still looked a mess, though, with red eyes and tear-streaks down his face. Turning on the cold water, he splashed his face, trying to wash away some of the tears. "I'll be okay," he managed, and the next words were harder for him to say than he'd imagined they would be. "Thanks for getting me...out of there." Calver patted his client on the shoulder. "Don't mention it. We all need help sometimes." Really, he had no idea what to say to Seymour. He couldn't imagine everything that the Final Fantasy had gone through. Losing the woman he loved, to suicide, no less... A quick buzz at his hip gave Jonathan an out to his worrying and wondering. Pulling out his Blackberry, a quick series of button presses brought up an e-mail. The subject was "GTT7." The body of the e-mail contained Almasy's opponents. Quickly, Calver shoved the Blackberry back in his pocket. This clearly wasn't the time to bring up such a thing. It was, however, too late. Seymour had already seen Jonathan pull the Blackberry out, and after drying his face with a paper towel, the inevitable question followed. "Anything important," Almasy asked, and Calver couldn't put on a neutral facial expression fast enough. Caught, the agent sighed, and admitted "your GTT opponents have been announced." "Opponents?" That meant more than one. Really, right now, Seymour thought one opponent was more than enough, thank you very much. "There are a few triple threat matches strewn throughout the brackets," Calver explained. "You're taking on Kaden Calix and Jared Sykes." Almasy thought about that for several seconds. "Hrmm. Actually, I don't think I'll be doing that. Go ahead and send a reply e-mail. I'm dropping out." Calver knew that Seymour was upset. *THIS* upset, on the other hand, he hadn't even guessed. "Dropping out," he asked, incredulously, "isn't that a little severe?" The eyes that looked back at Jonathan Calver were almost blank. "I've almost died because of GTT, Jon. Laura killed herself. I stared down Rich Rollins in a cemetery and saw things that I can never un-see. This tournament has taken everything from me over the years. It's raped me of my dignity. No more." Silence reigned for several seconds. Had Jonathan Calver been a man without a spine, GTT7 would have been over for Seymour Almasy before it started. Jonathan, however, had a backbone. "...then why did you even sign up for it?" Seymour's mouth opened to answer, but instantly contorted into a confused expression. Why the Hell HAD he signed up for the damned thing? He struggled for an answer, but meanwhile, his agent had one for him. "You signed up for it, Seymour, because wrestling is what you do. It's what you love. After everything that happened to you in GTT5, you got back on the horse and won the PTC Global Title because that's the kind of person you are! You never give up, no matter how bad the odds are, no matter how badly you've been hurt! You've done MORE in Primetime Central than anyone else, and like Hell am I going to let you drop out of this tournament just because the chips are down." Calver looked down into the eyes of his charge. Not hard to do, considering he was about six feet tall, and Almasy a mere five foot seven. "You are Seymour FUCKING Almasy," Jonathan said, "and you are going to remind the world in GTT7 why that name has been spoken with awe over the years!" Seymour looked at his agent, and took a deep breath. Blowing it out, he looked somberly at the man who was desperately trying to psych him up. The truth, Seymour knew, could be very painful. "Thanks," he began, "but you're wrong. I'm not Seymour Almasy. I haven't been for quite some time now. I can grow out my hair and put on this armor, but that's not what matters." The honesty in Seymour's voice caught Jonathan off-guard. The agent had expected a verbal slap in the face to be enough to wake Seymour up. Obviously, he'd been wrong. "What does matter, then," he asked. "What matters is what's up here," Seymour insisted, pointing to his head. "I haven't been losing for the past three years because I can't wrestle. I can still arm drag as well as anyone. I can still fly with the best of them. But mentally...the motivation's not there. Just competing to win doesn't stoke the fires anymore. I don't know why. Not even the glory of being GTT7 champion is enough." That proclamation hung over agent and client for several agonizing moments, as Calver looked for something, anything to say. He was greatly relieved when Seymour began to speak again. "But you are right about something else. I can't just throw in the towel because of that. I've got one idea left. Something I've been considering for a long, long time." "What would that be," came the obvious response to such a statement. "I can't tell you yet, Jonathan. All that I can tell you is that I'm going to go look into it. If it seems feasible, I'm going to need your help and support." Seymour nodded, his eyes filled with the fire that they'd displayed only minutes earlier at the autograph signing. "Of course," Jon replied. "What are friends for?" Seymour smiled, and quickly embraced his age--no, his friend. "Thank you. I'll be in New York. Call me if you need me." Head held high, Seymour stepped out of the bathroom, headed for the changing area. Once there, he could get out of the armor and put back on his regular clothes. More importantly, he would be able to open his wallet and find one of the paper cards that he hadn't thrown away. A paper card that read "Dr. Eric Evans, Hypnotherapist." III - The Mindset "Run that by me again," the medical quasi-professional asked, eyebrow quirked. "I'm not quite sure I heard you correctly." Seated at his desk, Dr. Evans stared across the oaken expanse at a slender man with long, white hair down to his shoulders. That man, Seymour Almasy, looked right back at him, voice calm, as if what he was suggesting was the most logical thing on Earth. "I would like you to hypnotize me into believing that I am a hero on a grand quest to save the world." Silence ensued for a few moments. "Well, actually, it's a bit more complicated than that, but that is the general idea, yes." The hypnosis specialist looked at his prospective client, absolutely dumbfounded. "I specialize in hypnosis for the purpose of breaking addictions, Mr. Almasy. I've dealt with smokers who want to quit, alcoholics who want to stop drinking. I've never--" "Is what I am asking you to do possible, doctor?" The question floated in the air for several seconds, as the expression on the doctor's face grew increasingly and utterly pained. Finally, he nodded, once. "It should be possible, yes," he admitted. "But the question should not be if it is possible or not. I have serious misgivings regarding such a procedure, to say the very least." Almasy nodded. "I can understand that. Would it help if I limited the terms a little further?" "That depends on what the terms are limited to." A light sweat broke out on Eric's face. This was one of a handful of conversations that he never expected to have in his professional life. "I want to be made to believe that, by winning a professional wrestling tournament, I can make the world a better place, and save it from my opponents, many of whom are violent, angry, and truly evil individuals. I want to believe that I am a representative of all that is good and just. The tournament is my mechanism to do this, the tournament and nothing else." The request was getting more and more ludicrous by the second. Seymour knew it, and the doctor knew it. Even so, though, Evans had to admit that the limitations did assuage his conscience a little. "I couldn't have you running around New York City playing Batman," he explained, something that seemed like a real possibility under the original formulation, "so that does help. Nonetheless, you do realize that this is the request of a madman?" "That's where you're wrong, doc." And damned if looking at Seymour, the doctor didn't believe it for a split-second. The other man seemed too...sane, his eyes alight with intelligence. What he was proposing was seemingly illogical, but it was being constructed in a manner that indicated a great deal of thought had gone into the proposal. "I've never been surer of something in my life, except when I proposed to the woman I loved," Almasy said. "What I really want from you is an edge. To believe that the world operates in black and white when I know it operates in shades of gray. For a good portion of my professional life, I've pretended to be a real life video game hero. I want to know what it's like, not to pretend, but to really be one." This was utterly mad, and yet, Eric knew that the man across from him was anything but. "Nothing like this has ever been tried in the history of my profession," he sputtered. "I think it's possible, but I can't be sure that it is." "Of course," Almasy said, waving a hand. "All I can ask from you is your best effort." "I'm also going to have to build in a few safeguards. Situations and words that will break the hypnosis." The hypnotist was frantic, trying to come up with any and all limitations that he could. "Fine with me," Seymour replied. "I'll also need someone else besides me who knows about this and can break it," Eric managed, the sweat on his face starting to bead. "No problem," Almasy shrugged. "My friend and agent Jonathan Calver can do it. I’ll tell him about this the second I leave your office. Any other requests?" Both men looked exactly the opposite of how one might expect, Almasy perfectly calm and willing to undergo his mad idea, the doctor afraid and looking for a way out that he couldn't quite bring himself to take. If he succeeded at this, he could write one Hell of a professional paper about it. He would be known as one of the greatest hypnotists of all-time. His business would soar. Of course, all of that was dependent on him not being kicked out of the profession for doing something that was arguably absolutely immoral. Ah, there was his reason to say no. "Mr. Almasy, believe me, your proposal intrigues me as a professional far more than I would like to admit. But the risk is simply too great. Should I be found out, my career would--" "I'll compensate you well for your efforts, Dr. Evans." Eric Evans looked across the desk at Seymour, and swallowed hard. Usually, he received decent compensation for his work. But this was a special--no, this was an extraordinary case. He knew that Almasy was a professional wrestler. Many wrestlers made good money. What was he about to be offered? "Should you successfully hypnotize me according to my terms," Almasy said calmly, "I will pay you $50,000. Even if you fail, I will pay you half of that figure for your good faith effort, considering the personal risk you take in this procedure." Fifty-thousand dollars was more than the good hypnotherapist made in six months of work. He willed himself not to gasp, and instead, forced him to shake his head 'no.' "I can't, Mr. Almasy. The risk is too..." "Seventy-five thousand. Half of that for your best effort should you fail." It was slightly ludicrous to Eric how calmly the other man could rattle off five-digit figures for a procedure that might not even work. Evans hesitated; seventy-five grand was a lot of money. Even if he was ever found out, it was enough money to give him something to live on while he found a new line of work. "One-hundred thousand dollars, Dr. Evans. Same terms as before." Apparently, Seymour had taken the doctor's hesitance as a 'no.' Outwardly, the doctor looked about as calm as one could when one was offered one-hundred thousand large. Inwardly, he was flailing about like a four year old, struggling with the impossible decision before him. "No one has to find out about this, doctor. You have my word of complete silence for myself and anyone involved." Seymour looked across the table at the other man. Though he wasn't showing it, Almasy was getting desperate. He needed this procedure done, and he was close. So very, very close. If money itself wouldn't sway the good doctor, there had to be something else. Rising from his seat, Seymour walked the short distance over to the desk. Spotting a framed picture, Seymour plucked it from the wooden surface, and took a closer look at it. The picture was of the good doctor and a young man. "Who is he," Almasy asked, handing the picture frame to Eric Evans. "Your son?" The sudden change of subject helped the doctor open up a bit more. "Yes," Eric said, breathing a bit more regularly. "He's my son, Justin." Almasy nodded. "How old is he?" "Seventeen," was the doctor's reply. "He'll be eighteen in January." "Ah, fun age," Seymour said. "I remember being eighteen. It was longer ago than I'd like to admit. Is he starting to look at colleges?" "He is. He's already been to visit a couple of schools. Columbia, Princeton...he's really bright. Brighter than I am, in fact." Seymour smiled at the doctor. "What does he want to do with himself?" "He's trying to decide between being an engineer and a physicist," Eric Evans said, pride obvious in his voice. "He'll make a great whatever he wants to be.” "I'm sure he will," Seymour replied, voice soft. "Which is why if you do this for me, all you have to do is tell me where he gets in and I'll cut you a check. I'll write a check right now and have the money put in a separate account that my agent will have access to. Perform the hypnosis on me, and if it works, the money's yours when you need it. If it doesn't, I'll give you half that money myself, in line with my other offers." The doctor froze in his chair, moving too great an effort at that moment. His mouth opened and closed several times. Dr. Evans felt like crying. "Why are you doing this to me? Why are you tempting me like this?" Taking a deep breath, Seymour walked over to the side of the other man. "I'm going to ask you to look past the shoulder-length white hair for a moment, and see me for what I really am, doctor." Brushing some of that hair back from his forehead, Seymour spoke, not as a prospective patient, not as a professional wrestler, but as Jason Wilson, a man who had taken much of the worst life had to offer. "Life has taken away from me each and every solitary thing I have ever derived joy from. My father died when I was a child. My mother treated me like a burden on the life she wanted to lead. My grandfather, the man who raised me, is gone. I watched the love of my life shoot herself in the chest. All I have left is my career, which has been in steady decline for years. I have nothing, doctor. I have pieces of paper that let me buy things that I do not care about anymore." Dr. Evans opened his mouth to say something, but Almasy continued to pour out his story, a story that, judging from the passion with which it was told, long needed telling. "I have wished at various times that I could kill myself, but I don't have the courage to do it," Almasy groaned, head falling into his hands, dropping to his knees beside the doctor. "I don't want to sleepwalk aimlessly through the rest of my life. I want something to believe in again, even if my tournament only lasts one round. Give me the ability to believe, doctor." The doctor's mouth was dropped open in shock, as he looked at what now seemed to be a shell of a man gazing back at him, eyes pleading, hands reaching out to clutch at him. "From a man who'll never get to grow old with the woman he loves, or raise his children...please, Dr. Evans. Do this for me. Let me put your son through college for you." The impossibly loud sound of Eric Evans' breathing filled the office. Second after second ticked away, before finally, helplessly, the doctor nodded his head. "...I'll do it. There are a lot of preparations to make, and we'll need to start immediately." Seymour rose, slowly, and embraced his savior. "Thank you. I can't--there are no words I have that are enough to thank you." There were doubts racing through the doctor's mind, thousands of them. Somehow, though, as he and Seymour sat there, discussing what was by most measures ludicrous, he had started to believe that he could do this successfully. His son was the primary reason, but the hypnotherapist himself was beyond curious. He knew full well that hypnosis could do extraordinary things. Just how extraordinary? He'd have the chance to find out. * * * * * * * * * * One day later... "Are you out of your fucking mind?" Seymour Almasy looked at Jonathan Calver, in the waiting room of Dr. Evans' practice, and chuckled. "You know, Jonathan, if I had a nickel for every time I've been asked that in my life, I could probably go buy a Nintendo DS game." In Calver's mind, Seymour Almasy was far, far too calm for a man who was just about to go through with the most ludicrous idea that the agent could think of. Jonathan paced back and forth across the carpet, while Seymour sat in an uncomfortable chair, cool as a cucumber. "I'm being serious, you know," Calver growled. "This is absolutely insane. There's no guarantee this is even going to work!" "But what if it does, Jonathan," Seymour asked, an easy grin plastered on his face. Calver sputtered for several moments, before getting out "that only leads to even MORE problems! You don't know what you'll be like! What you'll do!" "Anything's better than how I am now." For better or worse, Seymour was fully prepared to live with the consequences of the hypnotism being successful. He had faith in Dr. Evans' ability to craft the session properly, and faith in Calver's ability to take care of whatever needed to be done in the aftermath of the hypnosis. Jonathan glanced around. There was no one else in the waiting room, not even a receptionist. That, he figured, was probably because they were here after hours. Positive no one else was in the room, he could voice his real concerns. "But seriously, believing that you can save the world by winning GTT? That's absolutely insane!" "Is it, now?" The tone of voice that Seymour used could be best described as "unnerving." "What the Hell are you talking about," Calver demanded, continuing to wear down the carpet on his pacing path. "Look at everyone who's won the thing. Angelo Deville and Jason Snow are reputed mobsters," Seymour pointed out, a smirk curling one of the corners of his mouth. "Oh, don't start with this again," Calver moaned. "You can't prove that..." "What about the rumors that Doctor Curiosity sold his soul to the Devil to win GTT4," Almasy asked, the smirk only deepening. "Those are...how can you possibly believe those rumors," Jonathan asked. "They sound like something that would be in the National Enquirer!" "Have you ever seen Doctor Curiosity wrestle," Almasy replied. "He sort of sucks! He has six moves, five of which end with the word 'cheat!'" Jonathan didn't even bother responding, simply rolling his eyes at the Final Fantasy. "Fine, fine," Seymour said, holding up his hands. "Those are all just rumors. What about something that isn't rumor? What about Richard Rollins surviving the electric chair?" Calver stopped pacing. The Rollins incident wasn't explainable as rumor. It had been publicized all over the news. "I was there, Jonathan," Seymour stated, his voice quieting. "I saw Rollins shake and writhe in that chair three separate times. There is no way in Hell he should have been able to live through that. But he walked away. He walked out of that shithole prison a free man." Again, no response from the agent. "I know it all sounds absolutely, positively absurd, but I think there's some truth to my theories," Almasy insisted. "Something's going on with GTT. There've been odd rumors and unexplained occurrences surrounding pretty much every tournament. Maybe I'll be able to get to the bottom of it if I go through with this. Nice side benefit, huh?" "I still say you're out of your mind," Calver finally replied, "but it's your choice. I'll help you see it through." Almasy nodded gratefully. He knew that Jonathan had serious misgivings about this entire idea. The fact that the agent was going to go through with helping Seymour anyway spoke volumes about their friendship. The door leading to Dr. Evans' office opened, and the hypnotherapist himself stepped into the doorway. "I'm ready, Seymour." The nervousness from a day ago seemed gone, replaced with a quiet confidence. Eric Evans was ready for the greatest challenge of his professional life, and he would be damned if he couldn't meet it. "Come on, Jonathan," Seymour said, with a grin, rising to his feet. "Let's go make some history." Calver didn't know quite what to expect as he followed Seymour into Dr. Evans' office, but he was pleasantly surprised to find a professional looking office, lined with books of all sorts, and a comfortable looking couch against one wall. "Please sit down, Seymour," the hypnotherapist asked, and Seymour took a seat on the couch. Jonathan Calver moved to a spot against the nearby wall, unable to hide his curiosity as to what was about to take place. "Mr. Calver, your role in this is to watch and pay attention. You will be the only other person besides me to watch what is happening. As such, you will know the proper commands to break Seymour's hypnosis." "I understand," Calver replied. Seymour had informed him of that fact, and it made Jonathan very, very nervous. He wondered just how strong his own willpower could be. Would he have the willpower to not break the hypnosis simply due to his own fears? He hoped that he would have that willpower, but there was no way to be sure. "Very well. Just sit back and watch," Dr. Evans told Jonathan, taking a seat next to Seymour. Almasy was trying to calm and slow his breathing, taking deep breaths in and out. Seated as he was, it almost looked to Calver as if Seymour was trying to meditate. "Concentrate, Seymour," the doctor told his patient, speaking in a soothing tone of voice. "Close your eyes and relax..." Almasy obeyed the command, letting his eyes drift closed. For a few moments, the room was silent, save for the sounds of the three men in it inhaling and exhaling. Dr. Evans allowed himself a small smile. This was the easy part. Getting people into a state where they would accept hypnotic suggestion was relatively simple after his years of practice. The next part, placing this particular suggestion, would be the hardest thing he'd ever done. Sounding convincing was the most important part. It was what allowed hypnosis to work. It was relatively easy to be convincing when encouraging a patient to stop smoking, or to eat less. Such suggestions would enhance the patient's long term health. The suggestion that he was about to make involved something that was, almost certainly, patently false and ridiculous. With his son's college education at stake, though, Eric Evans had rehearsed and practiced the words he was about to say for several hours. If it was ever going to work, now was the time. "Seymour, please pay attention, because what I am about to tell you is very, very important, okay?" Almasy's head went up and down, and Jonathan Calver's jaw nearly dropped. "Good," Dr. Evans continued. "Seymour, you're competing in a wrestling tournament, GTT7. I have to tell you that there is far more at stake in that tournament than simply the title of tournament champion." Almasy's eyes were glassy, but he was leaned towards the doctor, as if paying rapt attention. "The fate of the world is at stake in this tournament, Seymour. There are those in this tournament who wish to use it to further their evil plots. Should they be victorious, the world as we know it will be in grave danger. You can stop this, Seymour. By winning this tournament, you can thwart all of their plans. You can be the world's hero. Do you understand me?" If Jonathan expected Seymour to robotically repeat portions of the statement after Evans, as happened in lots of stereotypical depictions of hypnotism, he would have been disappointed. Seymour simply continued to listen, breathing in and out calmly and peacefully. "Alright, Seymour, there are two circumstances under which you will forget this conversation that I am having with you ever happened. First, you will forget this conversation when your time in GTT7 has come to an end. Second, you will forget this conversation should you hear the words "Game over! Don't tease the octopus, kids!" That had been the hardest part for Dr. Evans to say with a straight face. Seymour had recommended the phrase for its sheer unlikelihood of coming up in casual conversation. Considering how difficult it had been for Evans to get saying it calmly down, the doctor was in firm agreement. With his part virtually done, the hypnotherapist allowed himself a smile. It was just about time to find out if it had worked. "Now, when I clap my hands, Seymour, you will awaken." Jonathan Calver braced himself against the wall, ready for just about anything. Eric Evans trembled with both nervousness and curiosity as he stood, walking over to Calver. The doctor whispered something into Jonathan's ear, and Calver simply nodded. And Seymour Almasy? Well, he just sort of continued to gaze blissfully at the doctor. The sounds of clapping filled the room, and Seymour returned to coherency. Coherent or not, though, he seemed somewhat confused. "Where am I," he asked, and suddenly, Jonathan understood why the doctor had whispered what he did. "You're in Dr. Evans' office," Jonathan said. "We've just finished your pre-GTT7 psychological exam. Making sure you're in sound mind to compete and everything." "Oh," Seymour replied. "That's good. I passed, right?" "With flying colors," the doctor replied. "You're good to go for the tournament." Calver and Evans continued to watch Seymour closely. It was still too soon to know anything, but the moment of truth had to be coming soon. "If that's taken care of, I have things to do," Almasy said, firmly, rising off the couch. "Jonathan, I'll be in touch." Eric couldn't wait any longer. "What sort of things," he asked, heart hammering in his chest. "The usual," Seymour replied. "Tournaments to win. Worlds to save." With that, the Final Fantasy exited the office, his destination unknown. Jonathan Calver and Eric Evans looked at one another. There was a lot of business left for them to conduct. Transfer of money, for one, and Calver had a ton of questions to ask the hypnotherapist. For now, though, the two men simply continued to stare, first at each other, then at the open door. What had just occurred was equal parts science and fiction, and for the first time in his life, Jonathan Calver actually believed that mind could triumph over matter. Through the unrelenting belief of two men had Seymour Almasy been reborn. Epilogue - The Answer Looking out into the horizon, over Long Island Sound, the Final Fantasy clapped a sword to his side, the familiar feel of the scabbard pressing against his left hip bringing a small grin to his face. "Once more into the breach I go," he murmured, eyes still gazing into the hazy unknown. "Once more, I take up the challenge of GTT, with stakes higher than ever." The sword at his side was the Divine Blade Imperial II, the weapon that he had wielded for several years; years that were the most productive of his career. Almasy's feet dug into wet sand. It was only a mile or so from where he stood that his wife had shot herself. On his way here, he had stopped by that spot and bowed his head. Laura Marie Winters was the love of his life. She always would be, and she would always look down upon him from a better place. But his battles were on the Earthly plane, and after a period of stagnancy, the Final Fantasy now had a battle worthy of his name. His hand tingled with anticipation as it clasped the hilt of his blade, pulling it free from its scabbard. Dozens of memories flashed through his mind in the instant he drew his blade. Images of opponents past raced over his mind’s eye, culminating in Craig Coat, the underdog's underdog, the man who eliminated him from GTT4. The horrific events that took place in Resthaven Cemetery during GTT5 were next, flanked by the image of his wife, in his arms, going to sleep for the final time. Rich Rollins' hand raised in triumph followed, as did Kimbusa dropping The Bomb on him to take his PTC Global Championship in GTT6. Almasy's head shook violently, and his right arm swung downwards at an angle, as if trying to cleave the memories of the tournament in half. "Not this time," he insisted, as much to himself as to the world. "It's going to be different this time." It was going to be different because the man who had won the Primetime Invitational Tournament and PTC Cruiserweight Tournament was back. He could not, seemingly, have returned at a better time. Once again, the world stood on the brink of potential destruction...or at least, the threat of having GTT7 won by an evildoer of some renown. "Vampir Nosferatu. Angelo Deville. Doctor Curiosity. Rich Rollins. Jason Snow. The names of those who have triumphed in GTT read as a who's who of evil that has triumphed over these tournaments since their very inception!" To Seymour, it was a streak that was inconceivable in a fair and just world. Raising his sword arm, the Final Fantasy pointed the tip of his blade out towards the horizon. "No more! I will get to the bottom of GTT. I will discover the forces that have resulted in it being captured by evil year after year. I will vanquish them! I will win this tournament! Such is my destiny!" With the wind whipping through his hair, Seymour couldn't help but smile. Somewhere, in a part of his mind that he wasn't aware of, he knew that this was how things had to be. Four years ago, a question was asked of Seymour Almasy in his dreams: "Wilt thou change the world, or wilt thou change thyself?" At the time, he'd answered incorrectly. Now, though, with years to consider the question more carefully, he had come up with the proper answer. To change the world; indeed, to save it, he had to change himself. Meet Seymour Almasy, a man who made his fantasy his reality. This was his story. This is his story.
