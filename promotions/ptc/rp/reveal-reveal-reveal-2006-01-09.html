Date: 2006-01-09 There have been several key moments in the life of Shanty Jones; moments that have shaped who he would become. I’ve seen most of them, and some I was even present for, most memorably the bomb packed with nails that took out half of a police station in Basra, Iraq. He barely survived, with horrific injuries to his jaw and the lower part of his face. I wasn’t so lucky, as one particular nail went through my skull and turned my brain into a couple of pounds of useless mush on its way to killing me. That was a couple of years ago. Some of these important moments in Shanty’s life happened before that, and a couple happened after it. I’d go through the in chronological order, but who wants to know what happened to Shanty when he was a baby? Moving from America to Wales was a large factor in how things panned out, but you could say that about anyone. No, it’s been the last ten to twelve years that have really been the key. It was in those years that Shanty turned from being just another delinquent to what he is today; a very dangerous man. His development went in a very unusual way, really. At seventeen something happened, something I’ve not been able to see yet, and he stopped being on the road to becoming a total drop-out, and he converted. He became a born again Christian. That’s the only one of the big changes in him that I haven’t been shown. This might require explanation. Ever since I died, I’ve been ‘here’. I do not know where ‘here’ is, other than to say it possesses nothing to stimulate any of my five former senses. It’s neither dark nor light, loud nor quiet. It just is. I’m guessing it’s Purgatory or Limbo or whatever those religious places were for the ‘undecided’. I won’t even pretend to understand. I never believed in God, and even now I don’t have a clue, but I feel I’m here for something. I’m here for a purpose, even if it’s just to observe Shanty Jones… and observing is what I do. I see the past, yet that’s not really the right word, because I don’t see just see it; it’s like I’m actually there. I mean, I don’t exactly have any eyes any more, so it’s different. It’s almost like memories being implanted into me, being slammed into my head at high speed. Some weird, funky kind of data download. I don’t know why I’m tied to Shanty so much; perhaps it was because we were in the same unit in Iraq, but I know more about him than I ever thought I would. I was there, in spirit, when he graduated top of his class. Again I was there when he moved to London, for bigger and better things. I’ve seen him rise up the ranks of the London broking industry, and nail people to the floor in his semi-pro rugby matches. He joined up for the Territorial Army, and I was there. He shipped out to Iraq, and I was by his side, and by my own as well, which was odd. The bomb went off and I died, but he just scraped through. Then back home, to find a Welsh town that he had grown out of, and violent tendencies that had lain dormant for a long, long time. A trip to America, my eyes by his side, and then from bar to bar with me acting as a silent witness. Fists flew and blood was spilt, until the inevitable happened. The warnings racked up and a judge sent him to prison for a period of twelve months. I’ve seen Shanty’s past, but of late things have been getting a little weird. I’ve seen his past, and now I think I’ve seen his future, and I was in it. What’s more than that, I also just saw my brother. And as ever, I get to see the present. *** Shanty Jones sat in his hotel room. The second round of GTT5 had been an interesting experience for him, as his first taste of PTC competition. Belial Kahn had declined to turn up in the first round, leaving Shanty to accept a mandatory count in the ring and walk back to the locker rooms unsatisfied. This time it had been different. He’d locked up with Damien Ryan and he’d found that for all the time he’d spent expecting it to be a tooth and claw fight to the death, it was remarkably easy. And so on to the next opponent, Kasidy Drake. He knew an equal amount about Kasidy as he did about Damien Ryan, that being precisely nothing. Planning was not Shanty’s strong point; that was execution. Jones ran his hand over his head, and was almost surprised to find that his stubble had grown out a fair amount. There was just a matter of days until he would wrestle his third round match, and he had enjoyed the Christmas break, though it was not a conventional holiday season for him. The day itself was spent alone in the hotel room, watching re-runs of classic films on TV and then just sitting in the slightly worn chair, thinking God knows what. He enjoyed it though, the solitude. It’s like since he was released from prison and got given his second chance in this tournament, he’s wanted to be left alone. I’ve not been able to see his thoughts, but whatever they were, they seem to leave him in a reflective mood. His solitude was, of course, about to be broken, and broken in the one way he would least enjoy. The knock at the door heralded the arrival of the only person Shanty had been in regular contact with since his release. "Who is it?" asked Shanty Jones. "Room service," was the reply. Shanty got to his feet and slowly walked to the door. "I know I didn’t order room service, and you know I didn’t order room service. We both know that saying you’re room service is the worst ever way to try to get in to someone’s room, so why do you persist in trying it?" There was a stony silence from behind the door. "Can you not just let me in, jah?" asked Doctor Curiosity, for it was he behind the door. "Nein," replied Shanty, setting the latch to deter any possible entry from outside. "I’m your manager! I’m helping you to vin ze tournament!" Jones just turned around, shaking his head, and walked into the bathroom. Maybe if he stayed in there long enough the annoyance would go away of its own accord. Unfortunately for the GTT5 participant, the doctor was as ingenious and sneaky at breaking his way into hotel rooms as he was inept at tricking his way into them. Sliding a credit card from his inside pocket, he fiddled with the lock for a minute, before pulling out a small welding torch and doing things the old fashioned way. Scant seconds later the chain had befallen the same fate as the regular lock, and Doctor Curiosity was inside the room, wheeling behind him what did in fact appear to be a room service trolley. "You’re paying for that damage," said Shanty, emerging from the bathroom. "Ah, you caught me sneaking in." "You weren’t exactly being quiet about it." "Vell, zat isn’t important right now. Vhat is important is zat I caaaaaarrrgghhh!" said the doctor, his voice turning to a strangled cry as Shanty grabbed him by the throat and lifted him off the ground. "Look, you might find this all very funny," said the Welshman, his voice never raised above his habitual murmur. "Perhaps to you this is all a game, but it isn’t. There’s more going on than you realise. Preparations have to be made, and you’re just hindering them." "My broth… my brother is the only reason you’re here!" spluttered Curiosity as he was dropped down to the floor. "What Paul wants is none of your business. Why he even talks to you is beyond me." "He went to prison for me. Some ties can’t be broken, Shanty. Blood is thicker than water." "Little trite sayings like that piss me off. Now leave." "I don’t vant to." "Look, do you want me to get a restraining order against you? Will that stop you? If not, I’ll have to do it myself." "You don’t know what I can do, Shanty," warned the doctor. "Whatever it is, I’ve seen worse." Shanty almost whispered it, but suddenly Curiosity believed. *** And I’m sure he had his reasons. I guess that’s why he left without any trouble after that. The relationship between Doctor Curiosity and Shanty Jones was really less love-hate, and more just hate-hate… and all the hatred was coming from Shanty. You see, when Shan was in prison, in his first week they tried to test him, or haze him or whatever they do to the new people there. Sure he looked like he was tough, but everyone has to be tested, everyone has to find their natural level once they get inside. The two guys, big guys, they were assigned to shake the new guy down. Sadly for them it didn’t turn out too well; various broken bones and a lot of blood loss doesn’t make for the happiest of times. From there it could have gone one of two ways for Shanty, and luckily for him it went the better way. He was taken in by one of the prison ‘dons’, a title which the man himself shirked and really disliked. His name was Paul Goodman, a lifer inside for the murder of his brother’s wife, and one who had seemingly wandered to the top of the heap by accident, fate favouring his every move. I have… I have my doubts about his innocence, about how little he claims to know of the bloody coups that placed him at the top of the tree, but it’s not his life that I’ve been following. He’s a closed book to me, unlike Shanty. So they became friends, in the end. Nothing dramatic happened, at least that I can see, but Paul was the first person in… well, in forever to supply the other half of the equation that Shan had been working on. That’s not to say it was a sexual bond, it was just that Paul Goodman was the first person of any gender to really understand what Shanty wanted from life, and when to leave him alone and let him ponder on the nature of the world. Paul Goodman was the reason why Shanty entered the wrestling tournament known as GTT5. He was the reason why Shanty even thought of wrestling as a profession. Shan had known fights, and he knew how to take care of himself, but it was Paul, formerly known as Eegor, with a background in amateur wrestling and an encyclopaedic knowledge of pro wrestling that brought him to the stage on which he now stands. *** Twenty four hours later and Shanty Jones is in exactly the same place he was before Doctor Curiosity had impinged upon his tranquillity. The same seat in his hotel room and the same look upon his face, one of calm and peacefulness. It’s as if the war never happened, and he was never involved in anything like the firing of guns and the throwing of grenades. Only the scar tissue plastered across his jawline and the lower part of his face bear testament to the horrors that he has endured. I don’t know why I get shown such scenes, as inconsequential as they are. I guess they tend to get interrupted, stopped by someone like… KNOCK KNOCK …stopped by someone like Doctor Curiosity. I’m in these moments as if they happened a second before I was there, but still I experience it all. Half the time I can guess what’ll happen next. It’s weird, as if I’m almost seeing the future. "Let me in," said the Doctor. "No," was Shanty’s plain and predictable response. "You have to let him in," said a third voice, which took Shanty quite aback. "Who are you?" "I’m Chris Goodman’s lawyer," said the third voice, upsetting Doctor Curiosity by using his real name. "Why are you here?" asked Shan, getting worried about the situation. "It’s about the restraining order." Shanty Jones stood back for a second. Hang on, he was the one who was threatening to take out an order on the doctor! Was he coming here to concede? Perhaps this was finally the… no. He wouldn’t have done this, though Shanty. There must be an ulterior motive. "Let us in, jah!" decreed the doctor, and with a heavy heart, Shanty complied. "Zis is my lawyer, Dewey Howe." "I care about that, why?" asked Shanty. "Because of the restraining order," said Doctor Curiosity. Shanty Jones stood there, just smiling with half of his face and wondering exactly what kind of stupid stunt was being pulled . He stepped back and let in the lawyer and the former wrestler that was causing him so much trouble. "We’ve put out a restraining order on you," said Mister Howe. "Hey, no problem for me," replied Shanty, stepping to the side of the room and leaving a lot of space for Curiosity to leave. "No, you misunderstand," said the doctor, holding a piece of paper above his head. "What?" "This is a restraining order," said Curiosity. "It means you can’t go further than twenty yards away from me at any time. **** I guess he was pissed. I certainly would have been, stuck to such a cretin. Apparently there was such a thing as a Curious Mind Bending Device, and it had been used on a judge, but I didn’t believe it. All I could see was the new contract, and on it there were the unmistakeable words that Shanty Jones was now held in a restraining order, preventing him from going further than twenty yards from Doctor Curiosity. Yeah, that’s right, the doctor had somehow gotten himself a reverse restraining order. A containing order, if you will. "Come here, Mister Jones", said Curiosity, waiting for his prey to come closer. Shanty was stunned by the combination of balls and shit that the doctor had obviously used to get such a match signed, and in such circumstances. "This is bull!" proclaimed Shan, just before the doctor grabbed his leg and wrestled him to the ground. "I didn’t get where I am today by letting people go," said Curiosity, his grip tightening beyond that you would expect from someone of his… woah… …I’m getting something. Curiosity relented, and Jones nearly smashed his head in, but there was an unspoken agreement between the two, they were fighting but it was never as intense as.. Woah. I just saw my brother again. I saw him and I saw Shanty Jones. I saw the future and I saw the past. I saw conflicts and reconciliations. I saw myself in there too. It’s all possibilities. None of it is real. I can scream and join them, a flailing mass of limbs and joy that we’re not all dead quite yet. This is too much to process. There’s the past, and the future, and this… Everything is becoming revealed to me. Bit by bit the world opens its wings and lets me fly inside of it. Part by part the feathers are opened and the centre within becomes a fertile ground. The world is our oyster. Us, the righteous! We own the world, the place that the light will turn to! The brightness is our beacon! The pearls are our tears! The blood split is out sweat! The dead are our… No! The dead are not ours to play with. I can see myself as the nails come towards my head. I die, and my friends die, and my entire squad dies. Shanty Jones survives, but it is purely an accident that allows that to happen. But no. I see it all, and I see what has been hidden to me. I hadn’t missed any incidents in Shanty’s modern life… I had been there, I just hadn’t been observant to know what was happening. Oh my God. I see it now. I see it all now. Oh my God. Oh my fucking God. He wasn’t meant to live. It was a suicide bomb. Shanty Jones was the bomber; the terrorist; the murderer.
