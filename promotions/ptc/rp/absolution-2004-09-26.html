Absolution

26 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-26 .:: The night clung around her form as though it were corporeal. Her step made no noise, not even a rustle, almost as though the shadows were coming forth to mute her out of courtesy. At this moment, her mind was clear—one of the things that Shaman had talked about earlier in the week was her lack of focus, or so he thought… but as she stalked down the street, the look in her eyes was not the look of a lost, unfocused woman. It was the look of a calculating monster. ::. .:: Several people brushed by her on this side of town—quite possibly they were drunk, perhaps stoned, maybe both. She smelled Kine all around her, but yet there was one lingering odor that caught her attention, and her senses were immediately alert. ::. .:: Julian Falcon had told her she would find the lair of Alessandro Pazzi in this quarter of the city, beneath a Laundromat run by a Chinese couple. He hadn’t gone much more in detail than that, other than just to let her know that Pazzi was a crafty sort. That hardly registered with Shadow—she was used to crafty sorts, was one herself. She knew how her kind thought. ::. .:: She approached the Laundromat from the side. It looked like it was an easy approach—the place was already closed for the evening, so getting in would be no problem. She almost approached straightforward, but a tinny electronic whirring caught her attention. She froze in place, and scanned the area. After only a few moments, she found the source of it—a security camera. She smirked. Falcon hadn’t warned her that Pazzi was a paranoid bastard too. ::. .:: For a moment, she considered the situation—what to do? She could have just flung her dagger into the screen, but Pazzi seemed like a real tweaker—he was probably downstairs watching the monitors right now. Alerting him to her presence might have made things more interesting, but also would have made them infinitely more difficult, and she wasn’t entirely interested in that right now, although it might have made this little outing more fun. Besides, she was an Assassin—and Assassins were never detected until it was too late. ::. .:: She slipped into the alley of the Laundromat just under the radar of the security camera, following the its wire around the building. ::. Damnit. .:: The wire slipped through a hole in the back of the building and inside. This guy had thought of everything—either that, or he’d really pissed off a lot of people. She guessed it was the latter, considering that there was one thing he definitely hadn’t thought of. ::. .:: A black leather pouch clasped around the waist of her robe held a few small tools just in case the job was more technical than she expected, and she needed to deal with electronics. Silently, she thanked herself for bringing it this time—perhaps Pazzi was an idiot, but he was certainly a prepared idiot, and that was something Falcon had neglected to tell her when giving her the details of the job. Of course, that wasn’t anything too out of the ordinary—people often gave her only half of the information that she really needed to complete an assignment, but that was how it went; people could always give their enemies’ weaknesses, but almost none could give their strengths, and she needed to know those just as well. Normally, she would have cased the place out for a few nights to observe habits, but in this case, it had been a rush job—Falcon had insisted that the pay would be doubled if she did it on the fly. And she wasn’t one to turn that down. ::. .:: Silently, she waited for the camera to turn its lens towards the street again, and then she slipped up underneath its radius again, standing just below it. She’d have to be careful—she didn’t know if it picked up sound too, but given this guy’s preparation, and psychotic attention to security, she was betting that it did. From the pouch she pulled a magnet—a very handy tool to have in these scenarios. She’d seen a few people in her time who had gone to a lot of trouble to dismantle electronics equipment in order to make it look like a technical glitch, but a magnet worked just as well to do that, and took up a lot less space than lugging around a huge toolbox full of the proper tools to dismantle the entire Fort Knox security system. ::. .:: When the camera was again facing out towards the street, she held the magnet up underneath the lens, which should have been enough to scramble the shot that it was getting. Of course, there was no telling how well it would have worked fully; it all depended on the quality of camera that he had. Luckily, whatever kind of camera it was, it had a metal casing—the magnet stuck to it rather conveniently. With her left hand, she jumped up and grabbed the arm that held the camera on the side of the building and pulled herself up on it enough to reach the wire with her dagger, which she had readied in her right hand. A moment later, it was cleft in twain, and the camera whirred to a stop, the red light on the front of it zipping immediately out as the cord dangled flaccidly down the wall. She let herself gently down. ::. “Halfway there, then… Bon.” .:: She always took a moment to speak before going into these situations—once she actually infiltrated the Laundromat, she wouldn’t be able to speak anymore, since she would have to be on her guard. She slipped around the back of the building. It was always easier to break in the back door of these places—this time proved no different, considering the back door had been left sloppily unlocked. The thought went through her head that perhaps this was purposeful, but Pazzi lived underneath this place, and it was entirely possible that the owners of the Laundromat didn’t even know of his existence. She slipped inside the building, knowing that her silent steps would not betray her. ::. .:: For a moment, her eyes adjusted to the dark, but that was easy enough considering that light still poured in from the city outside. She glanced around—a typical Laundromat, by all means, but there had to be an entrance to his haven somewhere. She glanced around the place carefully, ensuring that there weren’t any more psychotic security precautions—she couldn’t smell the heat coming off of any other electronic instruments, so she assumed that she was safe. So Pazzi was that cocky, eh? Figured that no one would be able to slip by his little camera? She smirked. ::. .:: Her steps carried her silently and slowly to the back of the Laundromat, and into the hallway that led to the administrative area of the place—a dim, narrow hallway that was painted yellow. The back of the building as a whole was some distance away—plenty of space enough for there to be a hollow wall somewhere. She ran a fingernail along the wall, until she came to a conspicuous crack in the crusting, fallen-apart molding. For a moment, she observed it—just a regular space in the wall, with a large crack running down it. Space enough to house a stairwell to the downstairs. She pushed on it slightly, carefully, so as not to make too much noise. ::. .:: It slid open with hardly any protest, opening into a dim stairway, lit only by the dim blue light coming in from the Laundromat. Her eyes adjusted easily enough, and she slipped silently down the stairs into Alessandro Pazzi’s haven. ::. Just like that, hm? You were practically inviting me in. .:: The darkness was all around her as the wall slid slowly shut behind her. Her muscles tensed, but she willed herself to be still and silent. The darkness wrapped around her like a cloak again, hugging her form as she slipped through it like a specter. ::. .:: Alessandro Pazzi sat at his desk, going over the reports that had been sent in to him today. The Necromancers were always in business up to their elbows, and Pazzi was no different. The screen glowed a dim green into the room as he looked over the numbers—something wasn’t adding up, and it had been frustrating him for the past hour. Someone had fudged a number somewhere—that wasn’t to say that they had done it purposefully, or rather, that they had gotten caught doing it purposefully. The Giovanni clan was known for running rackets—whatever kind it could get its greasy paws on—so fudging numbers was never too rare an occurrence. But when someone fudged on fudging the numbers—that was when the problem came in. ::. .:: He didn’t hear the slither of the dagger as it inched out of the sheath—but he heard the terrible splitting noise as the skin of his throat burst open like a seed pod. Blood began to spill, and Alessandro Pazzi’s face took on a most unattractive look of shock. How disappointed in himself he would have been if he had only seen what he looked like at this moment. His eyes were wide with fright, and the front of his white dress shirt was quite soiled with gore and blood. Flailing his arms, he clutched at the rosary on his desk, snatching it up into his quickly chilling fingers. ::. “Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with you… Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb… Pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death.” .:: Alessandro Pazzi took a staggering, gasping breath. Some devil, slipped in to take his life from him—he reverted to his old faith, not quite stolen from him by his new life among the Necromancers yet. ::. “Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with you… Blessed are you among women, and bl—“ .:: The moment he’d started speaking, she had started concentrating on Dagon’s Call. It took a moment to call up, but once the power was in full effect, he quit talking—thankfully. What he started doing, naturally, was choking, as his own vitae swelled up in his veins, pushing ever closer to that point where he’d suffocate on his own fluids. That point wasn’t long in coming—Alessandro Pazzi sprawled gracelessly onto his knees, reaching up to heaven and his Blessed Virgin one last time before he toppled over onto the floor and began making a very messy puddle that he certainly wouldn’t have approved of. But, Shadow supposed, that was all water under the bridge now. ::. .:: For a moment, Shadow looked down on Alessandro Pazzi, the victim of Julian Falcon’s ambitions, via a touch of Fate from a very skilled Assassin. She smirked—she did enjoy her work so. With a skilled flick of the hand, she pulled a Zippo lighter out of her pocket, flipped it on, and dropped it onto Pazzi’s chest. Within moments, his skin started to catch—she didn’t have much time to get out. ::. “We shall see how much your prayers have helped you now, Alessandro Pazzi. Julian Falcon sends his condolences.” .:: So it was done. She turned, her robe spinning around her with a fanning sort of grace, and stalked out of his haven, making as little noise as she had when she came in. She disappeared into the night. Below the Laundromat, Alessandro Pazzi disappeared too. ::. .:: ::: ::. .:: The heady scent of incense filled the main hall of St. Andrews’ Cathedral as she slipped in the front door. It had become somewhat of a tradition for Shadow, to go out and do her job, and then go and revisit her past in a nearby church or cathedral, just for old times’ sake. In this case, she was seeking guidance as well—‘Guidance from God?’ she wondered. But that was an unimportant question. ::. .:: Her steps thudded across the wooden flooring—she wasn’t taking care to mute them anymore, because there was no sense. At this hour, there really wasn’t anyone around to note her oddities, and in any case, she only came here for one reason. ::. .:: That one reason made itself apparent as she pushed open another set of double doors—row upon row of pews stretched out in front of her, stained glass windows dark and imposing against the evening sky. In the back, three confessional booths were set up. Shadow, stepping lightly, proceeded directly into one, her eyes glowing strangely malicious in the low light of the church. ::. “Yes my child?” .:: She leaned her head back against the panel, her voice belying trembling emotion as she spoke the familiar words—but the same terrible look was in her eyes. ::. “Forgive me father, for I have sinned.” .:: Her French accent flowed out regularly—there was silence on the other end for a moment. ::. “What have you done?” .:: She took a deep breath and went on—her form very still, her voice penitent, and her eyes still glittering, glittering. ::. “Father, I have taken a life.” .:: She paused. ::. “I took a life of my own will, Father.” .:: There was a heavy silence from the other end of the confessional. ::. “You have done a grievous thing, my child. This is a heavy burden that you bear.” .:: With lightning fast motion, Shadow’s fist shattered the thin wood dividing her own form from the priest’s. She clasped him around the throat, peering down into his eyes, cool, collected. ::. “It wasn’t so much a burden. I enjoyed it quite a bit, to tell you the truth.” .:: The priest was practically a lead weight, but it made no difference to the strength that she had in her own limbs. She pulled him halfway through the slot between the booths, glowing at him and snarling, fangs distended. ::, “I was going to ask you for a penance, but now you’ve upset me. After all, how could something be so bad when I enjoyed it so much? The same old lies, every time I come here. It’s so disappointing!” .:: She jammed her fangs into his neck, and the blood immediately sprang forth, passing through her lips. For some reason, it always seemed sweeter when there was a bit of theatrics involved. She drank until he was dry, then sealed the wound with a bit of her own blood—nobody would even know. Assassin’s never left a paper-trail, so to speak, and they’d think that this old circus seal of the church had just died of a heart attack, being penitent in his booth. Well, except for the fact that she’d splintered the booth. Oops. ::. .:: She kicked the door open with a rough boot, almost sending it flying off its hinges. She spread her arms wide, as she walked through a pew into the middle aisle of the church, looking up into the echoing ceiling as she spoke, her voice shattering the silence ::. “Absolution!” .:: For a moment, her speech was cracked with laughter, but then she paused. ::. “Isn’t that what we’re all searching for? A validation that we’re doing the right thing in life, a validation that we’re going down the right path. I tell you sometimes, these old beliefs really slip around me like a glove, and it’s the only thing that can give my little soul rest. To come here and pray.” .:: She dropped to her knees in the middle of the aisle. She raised her hands to the heavens, and then her voice turned into a shout. ::. “Hey God! Can you hear me up there, asshole? Good news—Father McCormack down here is finally coming up to join your stupid little host of angels, or whatever the hell it is you have up there. He seemed eager to go, but really, I don’t know why. I’ve been having a hell of a lot of fun down here, after all—Alessandro Pazzi just bit the dust, but I have a feeling that you don’t care much about welcoming Kindred through your fucking Pearly Gates, now do you? Well, that’s how it goes, I guess—some people get all the favor, and others just get left out in the cold, waiting for the light of God to miraculously show them the way out of the darkness. YOU NEVER SHOWED ME SHIT!” .:: For a moment, Shadow seemed as though she was drunk, reeling off of some heady energy that she pulled out of the priest’s body along with his blood. But she composed herself rather quickly, stood up to her full height, and returned to the complete and eerie calm that had possessed her when she came in. Except for her eyes—they still glittered with half-madness. ::. “We all turn and meet our maker when the time comes. Problem is, of course, we never know when that time will be, so we never exactly get the chance to prepare. Me, personally… I prefer to give my maker the finger. If he and I do meet, it will be so that I can tell him to his face that I’ve learned more on my own than any of his sheep wrote down in his little Good Book.” .:: She smirked, terribly. ::. “That’s not the point, is it? I apologize.” .:: She seemed to gather her thoughts, and then she went on. ::. “Absolution—do we search for forgiveness, or do we search for validation? Perhaps the cleansing white light of forgiveness is appealing for some, but I have a sneaking suspicion that more often than not, the motives aren’t so pure. The little Kine only want to feel like they’re following the right path, so they go beg for forgiveness from church, from their spouse, from their boss—from whoever they can get it from. And from that, they take the sense that they’re on the right path again, and they go along their merry little ways, without noticing how blind they really are.” .:: She paused momentarily, and then her voice was quiet and calm. ::. “I don’t need validation. I don’t need anyone’s advice on what to do in order to succeed. Not anyone in WMW, not anyone here, and especially not some prematurely spouting infant who can’t even get his own motives straight before he comes attacking mine.” .:: She narrowed her eyes. Darwin’s Revenge had not been a good experience for her. It had been hard fought, but she had still lost to Justin Evers. It only made her more determined to force herself as far through GTT4 as she could. To prove to herself that she could do it—to prove to herself that she was not WMW’s underdog, nor anyone else’s. ::. “I didn’t come here seeking absolution. What I came here to do was to drive another perpetual nail in the weaknesses of my past. To prepare the burial shroud for other people’s expectations of me—especially yours, Shaman.” .:: She shook her head. ::. “You don’t know what you’re trifling with. You play at your little frightening games, thinking that you’re getting in my head, but all you’re doing is offering me a little amusement at your own pathetic efforts. You don’t know what darkness truly is, Shaman. You couldn’t even keep your own definitions straight—first, you were my worst nightmare, and then you were offering me advice on how to purify my soul. So which is it? You can’t have it both ways.” .:: Her footsteps started thudding through the church again—she was leaving now. ::. “I hope you’ve made up your mind by the time you step in the ring with me, because you won’t have time to do so after our match begins. I will give you what you want—focus. I will give you an opponent whom, if you beat her, you will be able to say that you truly won a battle. But I won’t give it to you easily. If I have my choice, I won’t give it to you at all. We shall see whose spirit is the stronger—but I can almost guarantee that you will still be lost in your little Kine confusion. And by the time you realize what’s going on, it will be far too late for you to do anything contrary to my motion. Perhaps I am your eternal absolution, Shaman… will you get down on your knees and pray like a good boy, or will I have to force you to see the light?” .:: Shadow smirked. She was not usually so derogatory towards her opponents, but there was something about a job well done, and a full supply of vitae that got her running in that direction. She disappeared from the church, slipping down the front stairs with a fixed grin on her face. Time to go home, she supposed—she had big things ahead of her, and she had to prepare. She started in the direction of Riven’s compound. ::. .:: She was only alone with her thoughts for a few moments before a long, lithe figure loped up beside her. She looked up into a familiar pair of brown eyes. Riven. ::. “You look happy, my friend. The hunt for Pazzi went well, I take it?” .:: She nodded. ::. “All loose ends are tied.” “That’s good. Falcon will be most pleased.” “I’m not concerned with whether or not he’s pleased, only whether or not he pays.” .:: She flashed him a grin. They walked on in silence for a moment. ::. “Did I see you coming out of the church?” Riven’s tone was most puzzled. “Oui, in fact you did.” “… May I ask why?” “Even my soul seeks fulfillment some day.” .:: She looked up at him, a laugh gleaming in her eyes. He noticed a small saturation of blood on the side of her lip. ::. “Forgiveness, hm? I do believe some of it has dried on your lip.” .:: She wiped it off with one smooth motion, not missing a beat. They walked on, neither of them saying a word. A few streets back, at the cathedral, they could hear someone screaming. Riven and Shadow shared a look, and then the gulf was bridged—slow, easy laughter split the night as they disappeared into the distance. ::. .:: ::: ::.
