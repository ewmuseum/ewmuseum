The Girl: The Hats We Wear

29 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-11-29 "Look down at me and you see a fool, look up at me and see a god. Look straight at me and you see yourself." – Charles Manson * * * Prologue: Hurricane Season The exact date, time, and location is not something you should concern yourself with. To know these things, is to disappear. Instead just listen and observe. Look, but don’t touch. The tattoo parlor was filled with samples of the artist’s drawings; images ranging from Mickey Mouse to the confederate flag, and everything in-between. It was not a big place, just an out of the way stop on the outskirts of a sleepy town. There was but one chair inside, for a customer to get work done. And on this day, that chair was occupied. Bzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzz. The incessant buzzing of the needle did not even register with Kyle Wagner, the owner and sole artist of the establishment. In fact some days he heard it even when it wasn’t going. Like most tattoo artists, Kyle looked like a client. He was a thick man in his early thirties, and most of this thick frame was covered with tattoos. Most notably on his back where was epic scene that depicted a wizard fighting a fire breathing dragon, with a massive dark tower in the background. So yeah, he was single. Kyle typically tried to make small talk with customers, but there was something a little off about the two men that had come in today. He let them talk amongst themselves while he worked on one of their arms. "That hurt?" "Not as much as I thought." "Surprised a tar baby like you doesn’t have thug life already tattooed on him somewhere." Kyle tried not to react to that, he simply pushed his glasses back up his sweaty nose and continued. The man in the chair was an African American. His name was Darren Hayward, and he looked to be an impressive physical specimen. Before he blew out his knee there were much grander plans than ending up in this filthy place. Before he had fallen into debt with the wrong sorts of people, he could have never imagined the lengths he would be willing to go. Darren’s "friend", if you could call him that, watched over the tattoo process with a sort of bored malice. He had seen this before, he would see it again. He was a tall lanky man. His brown hair was back in a ponytail, and his handlebar mustache was peppered with gray hairs. Darren did not know his name; only that he liked to call himself, "Drudge". "When we get out there, when shit starts happening, there’s some things you ought to keep in mind. Number one, Casimir don’t need you," Drudge said, leaning against a drawing of Bob Marley. "Hell, he don’t need me. But you don’t need to be remindin’ him of that by fuckin’ up, ya get me?" Darren had never heard his new boss referred to that way. His employer, an odd man who always sported a black cowboy hat, had previously introduced himself as Bernie Blackcap or Dennis Darkhat. Darren even heard an anecdote about the days before he was a loan shark, and had actually performed at children’s birthday parties under the stage name "The Magic Man". "I feel ya," Darren said, through gritted teeth that could be attributed to the tattoo and the company. All in all, it was one of the best places to get a tattoo. It was on the upper arm, near the shoulder. Problem was it needed to be filled in. It was one solid color. It was black. "Number two, make the assumption early and often that you lack the perspective to fully understand the scope of what’s goin’ down. One day it might make sense to someone. But we’ll be god damned if we give a shit." And as if to punctuate his point, he spat. The owner ignored that also. "Number four, some of the people we’s after…they’re as desperate as you were, before He saved you. You’d be well served to remember that." Darren glanced at his partner for a second, and looked as if he wanted to speak before deciding against it. "You were about to ask a stupid fucking question. Something like…well what was number three?" The Drudge asked. "It had crossed my mind," Darren replied. "Number five is don’t worry about number three until I fucking say so." It was odd to see the former high school All American, a legend of his own neighborhood, obey such a man. But Darren said nothing in response to the verbal abuse. He only kept his eyes straight ahead, occasionally wincing from the needle. And speaking of that needle, the buzzing suddenly stopped. "There ya go," Kyle announced softly, and once again pushed his glasses up his nose. "Well congrats," The Drudge said, irritation still present in his voice, "you are a butterfly, flappin’ its wings." Darren examined his upper arm, and the black cowboy hat that was inked there. "And I can tell you this, there’s gonna be a hurricane somewhere." * * * Chaifetz Arena. St Louis, MO. - November 22nd It was the aftermath of another edition of SCCW’s "Temptation". Pro Wrestling Illustrated writer Jeff Coltz had been given access to Lane Stevens’ life in the previous few weeks, in an attempt to piece together just who this man was, and if he was possibly as bad as they say. Coltz had only had one cover story before this one, back in the days he was assigned to the NWC. Jeff sat quietly by himself in Lane’s dressing room, having watched most of the night’s events unfold on a small monitor in the hall. He was dressed more casually than in the previous interview sessions: a PWI polo shirt and jeans. Coltz took a second to glance around the dressing room. Unopened liquor bottles stood ominously on a small round table, just waiting to cause trouble. And then he heard it, voices in the hallway, getting closer by the second. The main event of Temptation had ended a few minutes earlier, and upon that end he had retreated here to wait for his subject. Jeff figured this would be a pretty candid time to interview The River Rat, and then scoffed at himself for thinking there was ever not a candid time. Jeff cleared his throat, and retrieved the small recording device from his shirt pocket. He sat on a chair in the corner of the room, as far away from Lane’s personal possessions as possible. In the few moments before the door opened, Coltz privately wondered if this story was doing more harm than good. Didn’t the spotlight just make this man act out more? "Jeffrey!" Lane beamed upon entering the room. Stevens was still in his ring gear: long black tights with "DMH" (standing for Dead Man’s Hand) written in red down each side. He was being held up by Miles Cavanaugh, an old college friend of Lane’s who Desade had allowed on the payroll to help enforce his will upon the company. Cavanaugh set Stevens down on a beige couch next to the liquor selection. "Hello, Lane," Coltz said in response. "Ice! We must have ice!" Stevens barked dramatically at Cavanaugh, who did as he was he was told with no hesitation, and vacated the room. Crossing paths with Cavanaugh on his way out of the room was Regan Guest, who entered quietly. Her thin frame was covered by a blue dress, and her black hair had been curled for a change. Before she shut the door again, Jeff could make out quite a bit of commotion in the hallway. "What kind of sport is this…that a man like Jonathan Rhine can’t even get a fair match?!?" Lane asked, and began giggling uncontrollably. "It’s just goddamn un-American!" "Congrats on your victory, questionable as it may have been," Coltz remarked dryly. "Lane operates within the rules, the real ones anyway" Regan finally spoke up, arranging three shot glasses. "How do you figure that?" Jeff answered. She turned to him, taking a moment to compose her words thoughtfully. "Well, if they really didn’t want cheating, wouldn’t SCCW just install instant replay like the NFL and end it? They need his antics just as most reality TV shows needs their troublemakers. For someone who has been around the business as long as you have, I’m surprised this hasn’t occurred to you." Jeff shot her a troubled glance. Not you too, he thought. "Oh, don’t listen to her…I was molded in the depths of hell, sent here by Satan himself to prevent nancy boy faggots like Jonathan Rhine from winning wrestling matches!" His laughter grew in volume, and then abruptly stopped as the door began to open. Stevens knew that there was only one person who would dare open his dressing room door without knocking. "Lane." The whisper was quiet, but so was the crack of a whip. Alexandra Pierce was little more than a silhouette, arms folded. The first thing the light touched was her hair, rust-red like a waterfall of blood from her scalp. Then a single gray eye crept into the light. "Excellent work tonight." The shadow shifted slightly. "Both of you." Lane nodded at her, his smile a modest version of itself. Regan did the same, but was unable to make eye contact. A moment later, Desade was gone and the door closed as quietly as it had opened. They all sat in silence for a moment, as it seemed Alexandra had literally drained the energy from the room. Jeff waited until he was sure Pierce was out of earshot before continuing. "The longer this GTT tournament goes, the greater the chance you have of eventually running into Desade or Aimz in one-on-one competition. Now these are women that have held your leash, so to speak, for the better part of 8 months now…" "I can see where this question is going," Lane cut him off. "Then by all means…" "You want to know if I’ll wear my black ring tights against Alex, or the new white ones that have been designed. Well it’s a close call, just…" Now its Coltz’ turn to cut off Stevens. "I want to know what will happen in the ring," the writer said sternly. "Will you lay down?" "I’m not telling," Stevens offered with a grin. "And why is that?" "Top secret conspiracy!" Stevens boasted. "Because Alex hasn’t told him yet," Regan said, and flashed a grin of her own. Stevens frowned at Regan, who shrugged. "Regardless, your next opponent is Vivica J Valentine, a woman with quite a reputation." Just then, there was a knock at the door. "ICE!" The River Rat proclaimed. "Come in!" The door slid open, and Miles Cavanaugh walked in with a large bucket of ice. Familiar with Lane’s tastes, he stuck a bottle of Bombay Sapphire Gin and Kettle One vodka in the ice. Regan, for her part, poured three shots of Patron. "You want in on this, Hemingway?" Stevens asked. "No thank you," Coltz replied with a grimace. Regan, Lane, and Miles all raised shot glasses in the air. Regan’s words stopped them from downing the shot momentarily. "Here’s to doing what must be done," she said softly, and stared directly at her man. This was not lost on Lane, who produced a subtle nod in response. And down the hatch the shot went. The River Rat licked his lips, while the other two seemed to take a moment to adjust to the alcohol. "So, Valentine," Jeff said again. "Isn’t her finisher some kind of double rotation shooting star press?" Coltz nodded. "Well I can’t very well win this match, can I? Bitch is obviously one of the X-Men." "You don’t seem as if you are taking her seriously." "Show me something in the world worth taking seriously, and we’ll talk," the former Livewire and Gateway champ replied, leaning back into the couch. Jeff thought long and hard about that statement, and wondered for a moment about the consequences of his next question. "It’s interesting you say that. It is no secret in the backstage area that Regan is not with you because of any romantic desires. You are helping her get out of some kind of…trouble. And in return, she does certain favors for you that I will not discuss here." Stevens howled and slapped Regan’s backside while she poured him a gin and tonic. She didn’t even flinch. "Once your end of the bargain is complete," Jeff said, looking at The River Rat, "will she be out of the picture?" Lane made it a point to turn and face her. When he spoke, his voice lacked sincerity. "I don’t know, Snuggy Bear. Will you?" "I don’t think that far ahead," she said, and busied herself with opening another liquor bottle. The journalist didn’t write it down, but he made a mental note of the look on Lane’s face. He would not think of the words to properly describe it until later. * * * Las Vegas, Nevada – Now. A lot of people make off-hand comments about the freak show that is Las Vegas, but unless you’ve walked down the strip at around midnight, you wouldn’t really know anything about it. The ground was littered with advertisements for prostitutes. These ads were in the form of baseball cards, giving the young (or in some cases middle-aged) ladies’ phone number on the back, as well as interesting facts. Here’s one: did you know Jasmine loves your cock? Walking over these advertisements were the rich, the poor, and everything in-between. Look over there: a man with an afro playing the accordion. And there: a tap dancing transvestite. Taking in these sights and sounds were Lane Stevens and Regan Guest. They walked arm and arm, not for the sake of affection, but just to avoid wasting time telling horny assholes to get lost. The River Rat was nothing if not practical. Lane was dressed in a white suit with a purple tie that contrasted drastically with his scarred face. He liked to get dressed up every now again, if only to mock people that liked to get dressed up. Regan wore a skintight lime green t-shirt shirt, and jeans. As they walked past the bright lights of the Venetian casino, the bright lights captivated Lane for a moment. He loved this. "For a loser, Vegas is the worst town on earth," he said, smiling into the insanity of it all. "Hunter S Thompson said that. The fucker did more drugs than me, but he was right." They passed a man panhandling, and Lane gave him a straw wrapper from McDonald’s. The bum was too shocked to even respond. "When you’re winning, the lights are almost angelic. But when you’re down, when you’re really down…you just need to escape it, but you can’t. All of this pomp and circumstance, it has a way of mocking you." "Vegas has never beaten me, cause I’ve never played," Regan said. "You’re always playing, sugar plum, gambling or not" Lane started, he loved nauseating pet names, "this town doesn’t give a shit if you know it or not." "Isn’t that just something you say to sound pseudo intellectual and menacing?" "Eh, maybe." Caesar’s Palace was on the right, and the Flamingo was on the left. And Desade’s WMD was like a kid in a candy store. But he would not be going to any of these places tonight. Instead they took a quick right between Caesar’s and The Bellagio down a side street that was not nearly as well lit as the main drag. There were still casinos and bars, yes, but not with the same flair. "What should I do while you play?" Regan asked, not in the annoyed tone that you might have imagined. She was curious. "Play the jukebox, make a few friends…get hammered, I don’t know." He produced a few hundred dollar bills from his wallet and slid it into her hand nonchalantly. "Desade would have wanted you to have a good time with her money. She’s a giver." "Very jolly," Regan mused. "Kind of an evil lesbian Santa," Stevens said, and nodded thoughtfully. Regan busted out laughing, despite herself. She hated to encourage him but occasionally couldn’t help it. They walked on in silence for around eight minutes before coming to their destination. They went through the doors of a small bar simply called "The Town House". It had a small sign, but seemed to go out of its way to not attract attention to itself. This was to be the side of an off the books poker game than The River Rat had been invited to. According to the information he had gathered, several men who did not care for the scrutiny of the public eye, or surveillance cameras, gathered to have a high stakes poker game in the basement of this establishment once a week. Why had he received an anonymous invite? He didn’t give it a lot of thought. If there was one thing Lane Stevens liked….strike that, if there was one thing Lane Stevens craved, it was action. There were only four men in the bar at this point. They were scattered throughout the establishment, simply staring into their beer or cocktail, taking the occasional sip. This did not look like a particularly inspired group. Lane and Regan approached the bartender, an older man with a bad comb over. "Howdy, welcome to the Town House," he was going through the motions, "what can I get for ya?" "I’ll have a blind Russian," Stevens said. "Amaretto Sour," Regan said a moment later. The bartender nodded, and grabbed vodka, Kahlua and Bailey’s. These were the requirements for Lane’s cocktail, and it should be little surprise that there were no non alcoholic ingredients required. After a moment The River Rat leaned in and whispered," If I’m here for the poker game…" and he let his voice trail off. "To the right and down the stairs," the bartender replied, not looking up from his concoction, "you’ll have to show your invitation to Stew." "Thanks." Regan was momentarily taken aback that he had nothing rude to say to this bartender. But she didn’t want to press her luck. The pointless tongue lashings he gave people occasionally grated on her. "Well, good luck." "Thank ya kindly ma’am," Lane offered with his signature smirk and was on his way to the stairs. The man that sat in front of the doorway was in a wheel chair. He was a pudgy little man with a buzz cut. His face was as scarred as Lane’s, but not nearly as young. "You must be Stew," Lane said in a chipper voice, already imagining the stacks of cash that he was going to be taking off the poor suckers downstairs. "Must be," Stew said. He smirked as well. "Good luck fella." "I must look like a guy who needs a lot of luck." "It’s Vegas. We all do," Stew replied, and spit some tobacco into a Snapple bottle. A few moments later he opened the door. The tattoo of the black cowboy hat on Stew’s arm didn’t raise any red flags for Lane, but days later he will look back at it, and second guess his decision to walk down those stairs. Stew closed the door behind Stevens and grinned at one of the other men in the bar. For her part, Regan sat quietly by herself at the bar. She was eyeing the jukebox, wondering if they had any Pixies, or maybe even Hootie and the Blowfish for a good laugh, when she heard the sound of boots on the wood floor of the establishment. The sound had a certain weight to it that made her suddenly afraid. "Think its bout time to close up shop Artie, what do ya say about that?" a voice behind her asked. "Sounds like a mighty fine idea to me," was the bartender’s response. He did not look up at the voice. She could feel a presence behind her, but didn’t dare move. She took a sip of her drink, and put it back down. The bartender walked over to the window and turned the sign around so that "Open" was now facing the inside of the bar. One of the other patrons got up and walked to the door, guarding it. The sound of the boots against the floor started up again. A lanky man in a ponytail came around the bar. His handlebar mustache would have been comical in almost any other situation. "Hiya Miss," the man said, in a southern drawl. Although unlike his boss, this one was not manufactured. It was the real deal. "Hi," Regan said, holding as still as possible. "I know your name, and you don’t get to know mine. That’s just the way it works out. But you can call me Drudge if it pleases ya." He poured himself a shot of Jim Beam, and polished it off. "Now, I’m guessing you have some idea what this is," The Drudge said, and lifted up the sleeve on his arm to show the tattoo of the black cowboy hat. She nodded. "Good, the less time I have to explain big things to small people, the better. You have met my boss. And you owe him a bit of money. But he’s thought about that…and he’s willing to forget about it, just like he did for my nigger pal Darren. Casimir is generous." In her head, she could see the son of a bitch. He had so many names, but always the same black cowboy hat atop his head. "Now, how do you feel about discussing a little business?" "It doesn’t appear I have a choice." "You’re smarter than you look," Drudge seethed. Unlike his boss, there was no irony to this man, there was no good humor. "Before we start…is Lane going to be alright?" Regan asked, taking an impressive gulp of her drink. "Not fucking likely." * * * As Lane Stevens walked down the stairs, he saw four men. Three of them were holding weapons. One thing he did not see was a playing card of any kind. The River Rat turned up the stairs for a brief moment, before turning back at the men. "If I were to guess…that’s locked." "Yup," one of the men answered. "Hmm." Stevens took a few more steps down. It was in basements like just like this that he had done unspeakable things to many men and women on Desade’s behalf, and if he were to be honest, he enjoyed it every time. There were no windows, just a small concrete square of a room. "Well, fuck me sideways," Lane chuckled, "this is gonna hurt, isn’t it?" In the center of the group at the bottom of the stairs Darren Hayward was handed a lead pipe. "I don’t think it’ll feel good," Darren said, and frowned slightly. Lane knew that this one wasn’t going to enjoy this in the same way he had, but he also knew by the size of the man that he’d probably be pretty effective at it. "That sucks," Lane supposed. * * * "The beauty of us," Drudge started, "is I don’t even know who us is." Regan blinked. "Take tonight for instance. I don’t know any of the men that showed up. But I knew they would. It’s just how He works. It’s a thing of beauty. Now all these men owed something to Casimir, or Mr. Darkhat as you might have known him, and he forgave that debt in exchange for their service. If they were to refuse that kind offer…well, shit, who is to say what might have happened to them Miss Guest. You follow?" "I follow," she said. There wasn’t a lot to be done about this situation. They had all the exits covered, and they didn’t look to be screwing around. Drudge poured another shot of Jim Beam for himself. "This was never about the money," he continued, "if you were wondering. The money is what puts you in a position to be more receptive to his offer. A man like Mr. Darkhat, he could give a shit less about cash. He’s got his eyes on a bigger picture. One you, me, or that piece of shit you been slummin’ with could never understand. But he likes you, Jesus faggot Christ knows why, but he sure does. And he can unlock ALL the doors. Doors you ain’t never heard of." "He’s just a loan shark, some guy who…who helped me start my restaurant when no one else could and…" Something dry escaped Drudge’s throat. If it were any other man you would have called it a laugh, but this is not the sort of man that laughs. "You have no idea what he is ma’am. But he can help you understand. That asshole you’ve been running around with, he’s been putting all kinds of thoughts in your head…like he can get you out of this, hasn’t he? He thinks he can be your knight in shining armor, that right?" "If I don’t know anything about your boss, then you certainly don’t know anything about Lane," her voice quivered. "I know that right now, that boy bleedin’ all over the place." Drudge paused. "And I know he can’t protect you." * * * That was a fair statement. Wap. With two other men holding Stevens, Darren punched him directly in the face, blood from his nose splattering against the wall. "Hngh," Stevens grunted. "You a wrestler, huh?" Darren was starting to get a bit more into his role, "Well I’m an animal, baby." He struck a knee in Lane’s rib cage, and the two men threw Stevens back first against the wall, causing him to hit his head. The four of them stood around Lane, almost begging him to get up. After several moments, they heard giggling. The sound began to grow in volume until it was all out laughter. "Something funny?" Darren asked. "I took so much Oxy, I forgot I was even in a fight, I thought…" he started laughing again, even wilder. "I thought I fell out of bed." And they were on him then, like wild dogs. Stomping, punching, etc. Darren brought down the lead pipe on Lane’s rib cage. And still, The River Rat laughed. When the lead pipe came down again, he would stop laughing. * * * One by one, the four men came up the stairs from the basement. Stew shook hands with all of them. They did not acknowledge Regan as they passed. She saw blood on a few of their shirts, and cringed. "You get that fella of yours an ambulance and you think long and hard about what I said. Mr. Darkhat ain’t gonna force you to do nothing. He doesn’t like it that way." Drudge tipped an imaginary hat. "We’ll be in touch." Regan felt a few tears welling up, but she didn’t dare let them escape, not in front of this man. How is it that she suddenly felt protective of Lane Stevens? Drudge threw an arm around Darren, who looked a little disturbed. "You done good," she could hear him say, "you done real good." As soon as they all were out the door she bolted down the stairs, cell phone already out. * * * Spring Valley Hospital Regan stood outside of Lane’s hospital room, peering at him every five seconds. She was on her cell phone, and her old friend Katsidy was on the other end. Like Lane, Katsidy worked for Desade. "I don’t even know what to do," Regan whispered frantically, "I’ve already lied to the police, Jesus Christ…" She sighed. "No! Don’t tell Alex! You don’t understand what I’m…" She was cut off again by her old friend. "I know you guys have seen a lot and…" ....she was cut off again. "AAAAAH JUST DON’T SAY A GOD DAMNED THING!" Regan finally screamed, and several of the people passing by in the halls gave her an odd glance. Because of the nature of a Vegas hospital at this time a night, most of them had somewhere to be so they did not linger long. She hung up the phone and walked back into Lane’s room, plopping herself down into a blue chair. Regan hated seeing anyone she knew hooked up to this many tubes and things, but it gave her some comfort that at least he was enjoying the morphine before he passed out. Regan scooted her chair closer to his bed, and laid a hand on his face. "I’m sorry," her voice back to a whisper. "You owe me breakfast for that shit," Lane announced, eyes still closed. She shrieked slightly at his sudden statement. "You want the tiny pancakes?" she asked, a tear finally escaping. "Mmm," was all he could get out, before passing out again. There was plenty to worry about. There were plenty of choices. But none of them seemed all that important at this particular moment. * * * St. Louis, MO – November 22nd In the back of a cab leaving the Chaifetz Arena, Jeff Coltz finally found of a way to describe the look. He finally found a way to describe the look on Lane’s face when Regan hinted that she might not be around for the long term. He looked human Jeff wrote in his note pad. He looked human.
