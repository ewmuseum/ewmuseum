Counterpoint

20 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-08-20 Click! Her eyes watered, precariously threatening to spill their contents down the placid lines of her face. The uncomfortable sensation had not retreated, was still as present as ever, but perhaps today she was more sober, and less resistant to whatever lay ahead. Today was the day of her defense. The little dot on the timeline of this farce that would clearly demarcate the relentless impugning of her character from its defense, those words which had been roiling within her since the first moment. How much of it would be useful was, of course, a pertinent question. She didn’t have to examine the situation too deeply to realize that it was just a show trial. She was being paraded out as the prize before being sold on the auction block to the highest bidder. It had thus far been about thumbing through her insides in order to clearly choose what was worthy and what not. She was not sure, as yet, what sort of decisions had been made regarding that, but today, the gloves were off. They would all get more than they had expected. "Ms. Vlaszy," came the somewhat subdued voice of the judge from his stand, "Are you prepared to begin?" She nodded solemnly. In return, she received a somewhat disapproving noise, as though she could almost see him peering down his nose and over his spectacles. "Now… I am aware that you have been provided the option for a lawyer, but you have chosen not to accept such counsel, is that correct?" "Yes, that is correct." There was a low murmur among the gathered crowd. "That is your decision, of course, and I won’t question it. However, there is one thing I want to be certain of before we get started in earnest. Now… unless I am missing documentation, which is possible… you have no witnesses scheduled in your defense, is that correct?" "It is, yes." The murmur grew louder. Bang! Bang! Bang! "That is quite enough. If the members of the audience can’t control themselves, they can be asked to leave, and I hope they can remind themselves of that before I have to ask them to do so." The murmur slowly died off, though she could still feel their electric thrill over the very idea of her daring to speak for herself. The judge cleared his throat. "Now Ms. Vlaszy, you are aware that in cases like these, character witnesses are usually called." "Yes, I am." "Then may I question why, exactly, you’ve chosen not to do that?" She nodded slowly, pressing her hands together as she weighed her words, each one seeming heavy and distasteful. "Your Honor, I could have found people who would take the stand in my defense. I chose not to do so for the same reason that I chose to defend myself. If any of my close associates, those who knew me best, were to sit in this chair right now, it would quickly turn into the same thing it has with me—pointlessly brutal ad hominem attacks. I care for them, and out of that care, I chose not to put them in the position that I have been in. It is my character that is questioned, and it is my words that will absolve it." "Alright… I just wanted to make sure you were aware, Ms. Vlaszy. Since I see that you’ve made up your mind, and for as good a reason that can be mustered… the floor is yours." For the first time, she felt slightly nervous. Perhaps it was being under such a bright spotlight, in both the literal and figurative. How many have ever been examined in such scrutiny before? And of those who have, how many have not, in some way, felt that they were lacking? But now was not a time to be unconfident. She sighed softly, crossed her legs, and launched into her story, her eyes cast somewhere in the shadow off to the side of the light, a thousand miles and a thousand years away. "If you were all to be honest, it is at this point that you would expect me to sit down and prove myself by telling you my life story. Starting from the beginning of everything I know, and presenting a clear and timely recitation all the way through to the end. It’s the details that would be incontrovertible proof, is it not?" She shook her head with a nearly imperceptible motion. "That isn’t true, unfortunately. In awaiting this moment, it was an option I considered, doing just what you expect me to do, but the truth is, it would not present anything of merit to the court to enumerate every event of my life as though it happened yesterday. In the first place, it would hardly be interesting, and in the second…" Slowly, the bite was coming into her voice, the vigor. "In the second, would any of you believe it? These questions have not been about my personal background, but about the present, and problems with how I present myself in them. The details of my personal background are obviously irrelevant, as they have been gleaned from sources that may not even check their facts before presenting them as such. If I got up here and told you a story, I’d be just that—a storyteller, for your entertainment. At best, someone who has a gifted and creative mind, and at worse, a delusional psychotic liar who has to use over-dramatized histrionics to get anyone’s attention." A slight smile twitched at the corners of her lips, further accenting the tenseness of her expression. "I secretly suspect that I’m neither, that I’m nothing so fantastic as all that. We all tell our own stories, and act in our own plays, after all, it’s just that we don’t expect or necessarily want other people to take notice of that." She sighed softly, shrugging her shoulders. "But I digress… To start out, so much of this argument is based on outer appearance. Quotes have been read, evidence been trotted out about my being a so-called ‘goth.’ But… the term has become pejorative, has it not? While at one point, it might have simply been a choice of affiliation to a subculture, now it’s got affiliations with people who pretend they’re vampires and murder club owners, with misguided children who turn to violence to solve their problems, with the great Satan that is Marilyn Manson influencing good white children to become evil, goat-worshipping serial murderers. The truth is it’s profitable to apply the label to somebody who you want to rob of credibility. Call them a goth, and all of a sudden, the term loses its meaning, and becomes a substitute for the word psychopath. How many self-named goths are really serial killers? It doesn’t really matter. The very term brings with it now the idea of mental instability. Call me a goth, and you’ve got your case proven." She smirked slightly. "If it were that easy, that is. Because the truth is, some people are given a pass. Appearance is largely irrelevant to whether or not you are given one—Hugh Hefner wears his pajamas all the time, but that only makes him an authentic eccentric. If he murdered his harem, how willing would we be to turn that proclivity against him in order to prove that there was something abnormal about him?" She paused, and swallowed. "What is it that makes some people authentic, and others fake? While on the one hand, I’m obviously unstable because I’m a so-called goth, I’m also a fake goth who shops at Hot Topic, as though there is a right and a wrong place to shop for your black clothing. I can state, for the record, that I’ve never been in a Hot Topic store in my life, but minds have already been made up—I am an unstable devil-worshipping psychopath while at the same time, I’m faking it. I don’t know how those two can be synonymous, I don’t know how one can be something and at the same time be only pretending to be something, but this is the picture that has been presented. The saddest part of it all is that people are willing to accept this definition." Her nerves had slowly begun to fade, and in their place, there was shimmering warmth along the tips of her fingers that seemed to be slowly fusing into her body—the feeling that she was finally allowed to speak, and speak her truth. "To address the opinions of this ilk that have been trotted out as though this courtroom is some sort of dog show… I see no reason why they should be considered valid proof." "Even if it is the prevalent opinion of the customers of the organization you wrestle for, Ms. Vlaszy?" The judge’s voice interrupted her steady stream of thoughts, but the flood inside her would not be quelled. It bit back with a vengeance, sweeping on. "The opinions of two or three people can hardly be taken as proof of what the entire organization as a whole thinks. It is as shallow and narrow-minded as saying all Americans are war-mongers, or all French are cowards. Take a few examples, and make them into the accepted truth—but this is never done without a purpose, and that purpose, in my case, is to simply make me look like a useless part of the community, someone who shouldn’t be here, someone who should go audition for an Anne Rice movie instead of wasting their time. What I want to point out by dealing with them… is that everyone has opinions, do they not?" "I don’t think anyone would argue that point." "Then why is it so shocking that there are people who dislike me? Is there anyone on this earth who is unilaterally loved? I work in a business where it is considered good attention for the fans to hate the bad guys, so any argument spat hastily out about people disliking what I do is made irrelevant practically from its very start. Not everyone thinks Jesus was a good man. I’m sure someone, somewhere, thinks Josef Stalin did mother Russian some good. But they’re opinions—opinions about whether or not a person is good at what they do are always going to vary. There are plenty of people who enjoy what I do, and have faith that I can succeed without changing to fit anyone else’s paradigm… why were THEIR opinions not considered? Without taking those good opinions into account, the whole argument is just a straw man." "Objection!" The furious voice came from the other end of the courtroom, accompanied by the sound of fleshy fist on wood. The table groaned in protest. "Whatever it is, Counselor, overruled. Now, Ms. Vlaszy, I believe we covered most of this yesterday. You’ve had your chance to combat that reasoning, but the line of questioning was already exhausted, and fairly tossed out. Move on to your next point, please." Something within her burned brightly that she had gotten such a rise out of her tormentors. "I’d like to continue on with some of the assertions about the wrestling business that were thrown around yesterday. Now, while I’m not aware what sort of expertise the opposing Counselor may have, I feel like I, at least, am qualified to speak on the subject. A point was addressed yesterday about my in-ring record… it was used to essentially ‘prove’ that I could not possibly be telling the truth about ‘what I am’ because ‘what I am’ necessitates infallibility." She paused, swallowed. Expanding from previous points might prove difficult, she well knew that. "For the next few moments, I will require that the jury so kindly suspend disbelief, as they did so kindly yesterday for similar purposes. Presume, for a moment, that I am indeed a vampire, in whatever definition of the term you choose to use. Presume that, essentially, this involves immortality, preternatural abilities such as enhanced speed and strength, and the need to feed on human blood for sustenance. Most of these are very basic assumptions about what a vampire is." She sighed softly, knotting her hands together. "It would perhaps be fair enough to assume that by those qualifications, a vampire should by all rights be unbeatable in a wrestling ring. But there are other considerations to take into account… for instance, age. Certainly, you wouldn’t expect someone who had recently been turned to have the ability to pick up cars and throw them across the street. Then again, what reason would you have to expect these enhancements to be unilateral? Would each vampire be of the same strength according to exact age? If you believe that… then you must very well believe that your own life is a video game. Level up, and get enhancements based on the challenge you’ve just passed." A short clipped laugh pushed its way out of her throat, and she shook her head. "Life cannot be simplified so easily. I could lose a match based on having a bad night, based on bad strategy, based on any number of things that are the same reasons anyone else could lose a match." Her spine straightened proudly. "‘Aha!’ you say, ‘this proves my point exactly!’ But it does not and indeed CANNOT prove anything at all. A wrestling contest is a contest of skill. To presume that, because one is a vampire, one must therefore possess MORE skill is fallacious to its end. What of someone who has never wrestled before? Or never HEARD of professional wrestling, but takes it up as a hobby out of boredom? Are these people, providing that they be vampires, expected to be instant experts? Or is it simply that upon becoming a vampire, or any other supernatural being whose existence you wish to concede, you are automatically given knowledge and expertise as regards everything on the earth?" She smirked, shaking her head. "At what point does immortality come to mean omniscience? At the same time that the existence of such creatures is denied, they are endowed with powers beyond the scope of any human understanding, beyond any human’s ability to match. Is it simply human hubris, to invent such characters and disavow their existence? I don’t know… to be frank, I don’t care. But one thing that you must allow is that if vampires do indeed exist, their worlds are inextricable from the human world." She held up her right hand, and began ticking off points, one by one, like bullets fired out of a pistol. "One, every vampire by definition was once a human. Two, by definition vampires must depend upon humans for sustenance, and therefore cannot extricate themselves from human society. Three, in order to facilitate the process, it might perhaps be easier to be among those you once resembled than to live a life of solitude away from them. In this way, you could theoretically explain the existence of a vampire in this world, in a wrestling ring. What they ARE may have changed, but what they WERE has not." Slowly, the ardor faded, and the seriousness returned. She nodded, and continued on, her face grave. "It would be ridiculous to assume that if I happened to be a vampire, I would also coincidentally happen to be unbeatable. The reason that questions of that nature cannot be answered is that they are in fact… idiotic questions." She swallowed softly, her voice now having completely lost its nervousness. It was now possessed of a quiet, vibrating urgency which, despite its relatively low volume was as gripping as her shouting would have been. "Why would a vampire want to wrestle, then? Why would a being like that want to bother with something which is, in the end, so trivial? Perhaps it is fun—perhaps some of us get a rush off the cheer of the crowd, perhaps some of us had always wanted a career in the sport, and chose to pursue it despite the obvious difficulties of doing so after being changed. Perhaps it is an idle amusement to while away the time—providing entertainment for immortals can get quite tedious, or impossible, truly, so perhaps striving to find something different, the lust for variety, leads others to the sport. Perhaps still others feel that they could not have had notoriety in their mortal lives, so they must seek it out now, in a venue where it would be relatively easy for them to be conspicuous characters without anyone caring, because it’s their job to be conspicuous. Perhaps it is simply enjoyable to be a part of a social group, which is usually relatively impossible. Perhaps it is fascinating to study the pain that humans will put themselves through in order to get the adrenaline they crave, and in order to entertain one another. Perhaps it is an interesting throwback to the days of the gladiators in Rome. The simple fact is, I cannot put forth one single reason for a vampire to set foot in a wrestling ring, because there are many of them. Each personality might well choose a different reason, or might be involved for a combination of reasons—in the same way that a human would choose a career in business for different reasons than the ones that made his brother choose a career in engineering." She cracked her knuckles nonchalantly, but the trembling fire was still growing within, and it was evident through the intensity of her speech, the way that it transcended even the difficulty of understanding her heavily accented English. "To refuse to see any reason for a vampire to set foot in a wrestling ring is as ridiculous as refusing to see that the poorest neighborhood of a city is right down the street from its richest. It is thinking that goes nowhere, because it is close-minded thinking engineered to support a viewpoint, rather than thoughts that emerge from considering all possible realities." Stoked again, the passion in her voice rose. "It is this close-minded thinking which creates the demand for ‘proving oneself.’ It appears that in the mind of this court, and perhaps in the mind of our wrestling community at large, that belief will go far enough to accommodate that which doesn’t challenge us too much. Does anyone ask Vivica Valentine to prove that she is a woman? Seymour Almasy to prove that he is a human? Unite to prove they are a tag team?" "Don’t you find that line of reasoning to be rather reductive?" It was the first time anyone’s voice but hers had broken the silence in some minutes. It stunned her out of her rhythm and for a moment, and she paused to recover her thought. "It is reductive, yes. But it is just as absurd to ask me to ‘prove’ that I’m a vampire." The judge chuckled. "I can’t wait to hear the reason you have for this…" She ignored his sarcasm. It was all so much water under the bridge. "I have been essentially asked to prove that I am a vampire, have I not? Yet, at the same time, it has been asked in a way that implies that there are no vampires. Once again, it is a paradox of this argument—there is no such thing as what you say you are, so in order for you to be what you say you are, you have to jump through hoops to prove that you are what you say you are." She shook her head. "It is a senseless argument. Truthfully think about the scenario. How would I prove myself to you? If I were to pounce upon the opposing Counselor and suck his blood, it would not be good enough. You would all say that it is fake, a Hollywood special effects trick. If you wanted me to prove that I could kill someone with barely a flick of my wrist, what would happen then? Certainly, it’s extreme enough to prove the point, but it’s also stupid—to kill someone in the middle of a courtroom? I realize the court believes sincerely that I am delusional, and however true that may be, they will at least grant that I am not stupid." There was a slight chuckle from the judge’s bench. "Would it really work for me to ‘prove’ myself? I have already been told that I am a costume-shop goth wannabe, so what reasoning would anyone have to believe me if I show them what they’re asking to see? Would it be worth my time, or would they just come up with an explanation to conveniently erase it from their memories?" Slowly, she ran her fingers through her hair. "No matter how much of a circus freakshow I made this courtroom into, nothing would be good enough, and I think that is completely obvious from the sarcastic and arrogant tone that the prosecutor’s line of questioning has taken." The fires were stoked, and began to raise still higher, the passionate timbre of her voice still climbing. "It is all reductive logic, hurled in my direction because it is easier to simplify than try to understand. There is a dominant paradigm of what a wrestler should be, and if he or she is not that, then what? This whole ordeal has simply been about attempting to enforce limitations that are applied about what one may say or what one may do which are in fact nothing but limitations to keep people who are uncomfortable thinking out of the box from plunging into their own worst nightmares." She shook her head. "What is PTC ABOUT? Since when did it become possible to say you must do such and such in this manner, or you will never be worthy of acclaim? Historically, it is the people who have dared to go beyond those limitations who are written in the annals of PTC history, NOT the ones who have settled for the norm and stayed there because it is more comfortable." Slowly, her hands dropped back down to the railing in front of her. "At least it’s about no limits for some people. No limits, provided you meet a certain set of criteria. And what those criteria are, I don’t claim to know, but it’s obvious that there are plenty of people willing to fling those precepts at me in order to prove my unworthiness." She felt a surge within her, white-hot flames urging forth into the pommel of the sword her words were wielding. "But let’s talk about realism for a minute. It’s the cross I’m most regularly asked to tote, historically the easiest to lay on my shoulders. But what else is there? People who are stoned all the time? Are they really that one-dimensional, or do you think they play it up for a laugh? What about the people who say they’re bad-ass assassins, or street-tough killer thugs? Do you question whether or not they say it in order to cover up a lack of skill, or do you believe that they’ve just had a rough life?" Her fingers clenched around the wooden banister, her eyes gleaming with the heat of battle. "Maybe they’re debatable. Maybe you don’t know what you think of their overblown stories either. But hey, while we’re at it, why not examine those people who seem to have nothing better to do than spend 100% of their time dissecting the past, present and future of their wrestling career. Who enjoy nothing better than belittling their opponents to parody level because it evidently proves something. But what is it proving?" She shook her head vehemently. "Great realism? The truth of existence as a professional wrestler? Horse. Shit." Wham! Her fist crunched down on the wood, jolting through the silence as an emphatic aural strike to punctuate her words. "Why are THEY not one-trick ponies? Why does anyone believe it is truly normal, truly healthy to be that OBSESSED with your career that you can talk about NOTHING ELSE? You acknowledge their skill, you tout them as your heroes, as the great ones and the untouchable ones of this sport, but put it where it is! Do you like them for their greatness? Or do you like them because they keep you in that comfortable spot where no one challenges your perceptions, and you’re always right?" Like the demon-possessed, she seemed unable to stem the flow of blasphemy coming from her lips. "The bottom line, the end game… is that it doesn’t say a whole lot about me that I am sitting here enduring this scrutiny. No, in fact, it doesn’t say much about me at all. For the purposes of all these accusations that have been hurled at me, I am less useful as a representative of an individual life than I am as a representative of a theme, or of a great monolith to attack with relentless force. I am useful not as myself, and thus all personal details are useless, any truth they might contain negligible. I am a bastion to attack, a representative of what’s wrong with THEIR sport, someone who doesn’t deserve whatever success she gets because it all comes from a parlor trick. My reduction to a symbol in this two-act play full of farce and illogic doesn’t SAY much about me… but it says a whole lot about anybody who’s willing to latch on to that paradigm. A whole lot about anybody who believes whatever they hear, anybody who believes that one way is the only right way. I, as a person, as an individual who strives, succeeds, and fails like anyone else in this sport CANNOT be pigeon-holed so easily, and the REAL travesty of this show-trial is not that someone is pretending or not pretending to be a vampire! The real travesty is that people who wish to remove this speck from the eye of their community cannot see what they try to do because of the great plank of hypocrisy that seals shut their OWN eyes!" Her voice had reached nearly full volume, but by the last time her fist thudded down on the railing before her, the courtroom had erupted in an angry and deafening buzz. Bang! Bang! Bang! But even the judge’s gavel could not silence their clamor. Bang! Bang! Bang! "Order! Dammit, order! Every last one of you calm down or I’ll throw your asses out the door!" The noise was perhaps the sound of preconceived notions collapsing into so much dust. In the midst of it all was Lia Vlaszy, who was unbowed underneath the unforgiving searing spotlight that held her in place. She was still, perhaps the only figure in the entire room that was not physically clamoring at the tumbling walls of a figurative Jericho. In her eyes burned a bright, violent passion, a sea of green and exhilarating strife. The words were spoken, the spell was broken, and for her, the mountain had been scaled. What she might see from the peak of it, racing down its other steep side, she did not know.
