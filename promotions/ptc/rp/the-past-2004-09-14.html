The Past

14 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-14 Looking back in time it seems that I was once just like everyone else. I was once just a young High School boy, nervous, excited and scared. Yes scared, as hard as it is to admit, I was once a scared young man. Nervous, just as anyone else when they step on that bus to Boot Camp. What would the Drill Instructors be like? Did I have what it took to be called a United States Marine? Nervousness filled us all as we stepped into the Recruit Depot. Quickly our senses were assaulted by what seemed to be a horde of screaming, buff men in uniforms yelling at us. We had to get everything right, and quickly. We were still nervous as they took us through the barbershop, cutting our hair with a frightening speed and accuracy. Not to mention the Marine Corps Barbers having no respect for our personal comfort. The rest of the week we were processed. The nervousness lingered spurred on by, having to get shots, fill out paperwork, and being rushed just to set for hours on end. But this was nothing compared to Hell Week. Hell Week started with a full pack march to the barracks, the march was exhausting, and the assault on our senses to come would make our first day look like a five year olds birthday party. We had to quickly unpack our packs, retrieving the items that the Drill Instructors yelled out. The sea bags needed to have been packed perfectly earlier, according to the processing Drill Instructor’s instructions. If not, then well it was our problem. And quite the problem at that. The yelling only got worse, the more a recruit messed up. As the weeks rolled on, the training only became more intense. Each of us was expected to give our all, 100 percent, all of the time. And through this process we became stronger, only to meet obstacles that challenged all of us that much further. And we grew stronger. I would go on to serve in the Gulf War. All the horrible things I would see there would only strengthen me further. It would be there deep in the desert where I would earn a Medal of Honor. So now I face the first round of the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament, against Sinister Steven Lee. Am I nervous? No. The feeling known as fear has been replaced with an adrenaline rush. I’m ready to kick some ass. No fear, no nervousness, just motivation. Hoorah!
