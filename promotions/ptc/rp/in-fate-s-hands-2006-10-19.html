In Fate's Hands

19 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-19 Dear Hoyt Williams, I am writing you today on behalf of my father. We are quite positive you have heard of us. However, we strongly believe that you do not actually know us. For this reason I write you, to inform you of your blasphemy. 'He who is not with me is against me, and he who does not gather with me scatters. And so I tell you, every sin and blasphemy will be forgiven men, but the blasphemy against the Spirit will not be forgiven. Anyone who speaks a word against the Son of Man will be forgiven, but anyone who speaks against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven, either in this age or in the age to come.' (Matthew 12:30-32) These are my words as recorded by my faithful Apostle Matthew, you can find them in my best seller called The Bible, available in bookstores and churches around the world. So, Hoyt I ask of you, are you with me, or against me? If you are with my father and me, then I must request you back down from wrestling, as wrestling is a pure abomination. 'The LORD is in his holy temple; the LORD is on his heavenly throne. he observes the sons of men; his eyes examine them. The LORD, the righteous One, examines the wicked and those who love violence his soul hates. On the wicked he will rain fiery coals and burning sulfur; a scorching wind will be their lot.' (Psalm 11:4-6) My father holds the words of David to be true, as David’s words were inspired in the spirit of my father. So in closing, we request you remove yourself from wrestling as soon as possible, for only bad things will come to your soul if you do not. For I So Loved the World, Jesus Christ ******************** The parchment rests upon a black cherry stained desk, with one lone quill and a brown leather clad bible (New International Version). A soft rustle is heard, as the dim lit desk becomes fully illuminated with the clicking sound of a light switch. Joshua approaches the desk, wide eyed and barely awake. It is early evening, and the sun is setting, thus Joshua must awaken. This new schedule has become rather taxing on his body, but what must be done, must be done. Joshua lowers himself into his desk chair, opening a drawer in the upper right corner. Within the drawer rests a vial (containing a red substance) and a syringe. Filling the syringe with the red substance, Joshua raises the needle to his neck and sticks himself without hesitation. A scowl of pain fills his face, as he empties the contents into his blood stream. Giving himself a shot every day sucks sure, but the alternative would be much worse. It’s been three days into his new treatment, and frankly he’s not quite use to it yet. With a deep sigh, Joshua tosses the empty syringe back into the desk. He’ll clean it out later, but not right now. Feeling a little dizzy Joshua leans back in his leather chair closing his eyes to quell the sensation. His thoughts drift back to the other night standing atop the roof outside the emergency access door that led into O’Malley’s dwelling. It had been a strange sensation that night as well, as though his mind was being probed from across a great distance. Many experts on Psychic Phenomenon speak of this regularly, from what Joshua understood it had something to do with astral planes and some sort of projection. Then again all of this was still rather overwhelming. Joshua forces his eyes open, though O’Malley told him this serum worked instantaneously, he didn’t want to take any chances. A grown man on all fours and covered in hair chasing a motor vehicle was not exactly Joshua’s bag of tea. Then again, chasing cars was the least of his worries. It seemed every time Joshua transformed into a Werewolf someone wound up dead, now that wasn’t something Joshua wanted to do again. With the serum already running its course through his blood stream, Joshua focuses his attention upon the parchment resting atop his desk. A lopsided grin crosses his face when he recalls writing the letter before him. "Wonder if Hoyt has a sense of humor?" Joshua asks of himself trying to suppress a chuckle. Hoyt might not, but Joshua sure did. A sense of humor was a must when playing the role of a pawn in a game of chess. There was no escaping the fact that, that was exactly what he had become. Much like the angels and demons walking the earth, Joshua too was just another piece on an earth sized chess board. Only downfall is if Joshua makes it to the other end of the board he’ll still only be a pawn; probably a dead pawn nonetheless. Maybe that’s what made things so interesting now. However, there was a light at the end of the tunnel. Joshua’s side of the board kept growing in numbers, even now, and more people are becoming a part of the cause. The largest acquisition was also the oldest piece on the board. David O’Malley. O’Malley had once been a druid in a tribe of forest dwellers. They were called the Keltoi. Many texts refer to them as the originators of the Celtic heritage, dating back thousands of years ago. Much like Joshua fate brought him into the fray. In what should have been a fatal injury O’Malley was reborn in a way. O’Malley had become an immortal. Betrayed by a member of his own family he was left to die, but instead of dying something else had happened to him. David himself does not even know how it happened, only that it did. The circumstances surrounding O’Malley’s immortality however are not of importance. O’Malley made sure that was known from the first time they met. What was important was that Joshua knew the stakes, and was made to understand the consequences of any mistake he made from that point on. O’Malley is the one who gave him the serum. O’Malley said that Joshua would need to be of clear mind, body and soul in order to complete his mission effectively. Mission… a word that seemed so odd for Joshua to hear. It may have been a slip of the tongue on O’Malley’s behalf, or maybe Joshua was just reading too much into things. But the word mission sounded so, militant. Then again even Eryc told him this was a war, and Joshua understood that more every day. O’Malley meant mission, and he meant it for that reason. O’Malley was deliberate with these kinds of things. A shock of dizziness begins to overcome Joshua as his thoughts stray away from O’Malley and all of his troubles. It’s hard to think about much else when overcome with such a sense of confusion. Confusion was another side effect of Joshua’s condition. Joshua is startled when a hand falls upon his shoulder, snapping him out of his state of mind. He turns to see who has brought him back to reality to find his wife Angel standing over him. As always, concern fills her face. "Babe, are you okay? You were screaming…" Screaming, he couldn’t remember doing such a thing. He had felt dizzy, like a panic attack. "Huh?" "You were screaming, I heard you down in the kitchen." Angel comments, her concern growing. "I stood in the doorway over there calling your name, but you just sat there like you didn’t even hear me." "I didn’t… and I don’t remember screaming." Joshua states a perplexed look upon his face. "I just felt dizzy, kind of queasy too." "Well you were screaming. You said ‘not again’ and then started screaming. I could hear you all the way downstairs in the kitchen." Angel insists. "I don’t know then…" Joshua trails off, a weird sensation overcoming him. "I feel, weird." "Are you okay? Do you need something?" She asks, positive that something is wrong. "I’m fine, I just need to get my head together is all." He states, forming the trademark lopsided grin she has come to love about him. "You know, a lot has gone on in the last few days is all. It’s kind of taking a toll on me." Angel nods. She knows everything, and because of that can understand. Living in constant fear for his life, and the lives of his family has an effect on stress levels. Hell, his blood pressure has been through the roof for months now. Then again, she can’t blame him. "Well I’m going downstairs to put Bonita in bed, but if you need anything and I mean anything you call me okay?" Joshua nods and gives his wife a kiss. Reluctantly she leaves the room to put their daughter to bed. Joshua waits for her to leave before turning his attention back to the desk and the piece of parchment resting upon the surface. "Well Hoyt, only a few days now." Joshua says to himself, reading over the letter again. "I’ll have to send this out in the morning, hopefully Hoyt will get it in time." Exhaling away what he hopes would be all of his concerns; Joshua stands and stretches. "Well O’Malley said my best defense was a calm and cool head, so I guess I better start lightening up." Joshua yawns as he checks the clock by the bed. "The night is still young, maybe I’ll go out for a beer or two, loosen up and shit." With probably the best idea Joshua has had in a long time he grabs his coat from the closet and heads out the bedroom door. Joshua jogs down the stairs and slips into the kitchen long enough to give his wife a hug and a kiss before he darts right out the front door. Glancing toward the driveway, he frowns remembering his favorite car was wrecked with his own body. "I’m gonna have to get another one. Fucking jackass." Joshua tugs on the corners of his coat, zipping the waist length leather jacket up. "Fuck it, I’ll just walk." And so he did. Joshua walked all night, enjoying the invigorating night air. Truthfully the walk was so relaxing he forgot all about grabbing a beer, he had all he needed to ease his tension. So, Joshua walked. With a potential career making match coming up in one of the world’s biggest tournaments a walk was exactly what he needed. Hours passed before he realized he had still been walking, and frankly he didn’t care. For the first time his mind had been at ease. He was ready, and if beating Hoyt Williams meant making the baby Jesus cry… then let the baby cry. Besides, that’s what babies did anyway. Hoyt Williams was just another opponent. Winning or losing didn’t matter, either way Joshua still had bigger fish to fry. Compared to all the other shit going on, Hoyt Williams didn’t matter. The walk helped him realize that, and for the first time nothing worried him anymore. Fate had forced him onto his current path, and fate wasn’t done with him yet. Fate would decide what happened next, and nothing would change that.
