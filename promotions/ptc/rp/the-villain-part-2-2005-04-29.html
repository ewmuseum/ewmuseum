The Villain (Part 2)

29 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-04-29 The Quick’s mask is a thin latex blend. It’s breathable and see-through, which is good seeing as how the mask is white and covers the entire face -- closed crown, closed chin. That’s his normal mask, the one that we see him in all the time. But this time, for this promo, it’s a little different. Instead, he’s wearing an open mouth mask with eyeholes, revealing the goateed mouth of a Caucasian man and his dejected brown eyes. The eyes of a man who can see into your soul and is always disgusted with what he finds. The camera is at the zoom level that we can see his entire body. His white and gold ring costume is obscure in multiple places by championship belts. Seven of them. There is one on each shoulder, one around the waist, one draped on either thigh, and the remaining two are strapped around his wiry biceps. He truly has earned his moniker “The Golden One.” We enter to the sight of him smoking a cigarette only recently lit. A small cloud of smoke gusts forth from his open mouth. One can only imagine the fine that he’ll incur, now that the Cessation of Smoking Act of 2009 is fully in effect. Public smoking of any kind has been prohibited for 7 months now, even worse the punishment for doing so on television or movies. Quick: I remember a long time ago, five years back when I first got into wrestling a long time ago, that I always wanted to talk face-to-face with people. The whole "insult a guy in promos from thousands of miles away" thing just seemed rather cowardly. If not for the fact that there can't be immediate rebuttal (thus a dumbass comment gets at least a few hours of "out there time" before it's shoved back into the sender's face), then for the fact that you're sitting back and watching the thing, notepad and pen in hand, writing out everything that's wrong so that you can go back and script a perfect comeback rife with zingers and incontestable statements. That's like e-mailing Yo’ Mama jokes; the only person who looks like a retard is the person who's initiating it. A drag from the cigarette. There is but a single eerie sliver of white light coming diagonally from the upper right. It cuts a swath of illumination across his left shoulder, down his chest, and briefly touches the title on his right thigh. Three glints of gold shimmer at the lens. Quick: Nowadays, though, I'd rather avoid it. People suck. Nobody has a brain anymore. It's worse than what happened with the last generation bean… being the first one in U.S. history to have a lower standard of living by education than all others before it; now there's like this regression to Cro-Magnon levels of idiocy. How is it that a little guy like me can so easily defeat comers; all comers? Simple: by outsmarting them, controlling their emotions, and altogether making it so that nobody knows what I'm thinking while I know exactly what they're thinking. Pretty basic stuff, nothing special. He sits there, motionless save for the right hand that gives him the shot of nicotine in between his paragraphs. The cold steel like the touch of an old friend upon his scarred back. Other wrestlers had been known for going through their promos almost mechanically, never stopping, never slipping at the tongue… The Quick truly goes by the seat of his pants, talking as the words come to his head. Hence the occasional Freudian slip or repetition in his words. He took pride in not being “some rehearsed attempt at a human being devoid of any of the trappings that come with such.” Quick: But it isolates you. Being Captain Mindfuck takes its toll on those around you. You're off all the Christmas card lists, no one announces to you when their kid graduates from high school or college, and every time a Girl Scout knocks on your door, she’s not selling cookies, but just there to kick you in the shin and run away shouting obscenities. A smirk comes across the so-far-expressionless face. Quick: And I wouldn’t change it for the world. Am I missing out? Only if elevating myself above the cattle is a bad thing. Truth is that it’s a sacrifice that I make everyday, willingly, to be the kind of champion that I am. No one can match my pedigree. List them off, all the championships that I currently hold. Angels, Demons, and Demigods Title (Dominion Fighting Arena). MidSouth Heavyweight Championship (Southern Wrestling Alliance) The Title (It’s Wrestling, Stupid) Pacific Coast Championship (Ring of Fire) King of Fighting (Warriors) Pride and Glory Championship (Sunshine Combat Syndicate) Condemned (American Justice Underground) Quick: That’s seven major wrestling titles ever since the old guard all shut down and/or turned into something else. And all of those belts I defend actively. Pick any three days out of the week and I’ll be wrestling at least two of them. That’s why my hometown is listed as “Wherever I’m wrestling next.” If I’m not at the show, I’m coming from the last show. Wrestling is what I do. It’s who I am. It’s the only release for all of my emotions and the only escape from the droll mediocrity and sheer insanity that is everyone else. I don’t even like my associates (they know this), but I keep them around anyway because they can at least serve some purpose for me, and if they’re not geniuses (of course they’re not) they at least understand that it is infinitely more beneficial to their careers to let me guide them and to do as I say than it would be to go it alone. So even those who are closest to me are at a distance, never to know the “real” me. Tangential? Not in the least. Because this all ties in to Rockwilder’s comment about me. He said, flat out, to my face that he pitied me. Pitied the kind of man that I am, since he doubts that I could have lived my whole life this way. The times when he did slip up were the times that he didn’t have the emotion in his words. Of course, the mistakes were going to happen when he didn’t care what he said or if he said it correctly. But when he gets going, when his heart is in it, he is in The Zone. Speaking of The Zone, he has entered it. Quick: So in response, Rockie -- fuck you. I don’t need your pity, didn’t ask for your pity, and damned if your pity is even worth having. I mean, maybe if it were from somebody important, then it would at least be worth considering. But you? The lowest of the low? A guppy in an ocean full of whale sharks? A glorified homosexual ring rat? I wouldn’t have said anything about this before, but since you’ve brought this upon yourself let’s compare resumes, shall we? -- YOU have wrestled in hyped-up backyard federations, sometimes in front of crowds of 500 at best. -- I have wrestled in the greatest of today’s wrestling federations, in front of crowds whose numbers you couldn’t even imagine gathering all in the same place just to see you wrestle. But they have for me. Numerous times. -- YOU have visited the same high school gyms and community centers, in your short career, and to date have not even gone outside of the country, let alone the corner state region, to wrestle. -- I have wrestled in countries you’d need help to locate on the map, in arenas that you couldn’t pronounce to save your life. -- YOU have won… let me think… carry the y -- zero championships. -- I have won so many that I had to buy my own wheelbarrow to get them all to the ring. -- You have declared yourself the people’s champion even though 99.99% of America has no clue who the hell you are. -- I am acknowledged nationwide… no, worldwide as a villain in wrestling. Quick: Are you getting the message now? Am I hated? Of course. But I’m KNOWN. That is something that you have yet to achieve, and there are serious doubts that you’ll ever get to be the kind of household name that I am. You WISH you could move people as well as me. The masses here your name, and the reaction is the same all around – “Meh.” They hear my name and even goddamn Pope Innocent XIV goes “Fuck that bitch!” You? Pity me? As regards me, even Mr. T wouldn’t “Pity da foo’.” So here I am, rock YOU like a hurricane, because whether or not you realize it, you’re small-time, small potatoes, and from what Krisma is telling the guys backstage, you’re small where it counts the most. So get off your high horse and take some Enzyte, and maybe you’ll calm the fuck down enough to realize that you ain’t shit, boy. Promo OVER. And with the same simple line with which he always closes out his addresses, The Quick signals the end. The paid cameraman, someone who cannot stand Quick’s presence, turns off the Sony Progenitor high-definition digital camcorder and hands over the memory card. What? Wipe that expression off your face. You’re wondering what that has to do with anything? Oh, RIGHT. I remember. I was telling you’re my story, and apparently that supersedes my own damn life. The FUCK do you think you are that you’re so important you can dictate what else I deal with along the way? Just so there’s no confusion, you sit there and listen because anything I say is the greatest thing to grace your ears. I’ll talk about what I want and continue my story when I’m good and ready because I dictate the pace of this thing, not you. Need I remind you that you came to me, not the other way around. Supply and demand? Screw that, I’m in control. Now, before I pick up where I left off, might as well answer some of your questions. After all, my method of story-telling is so avant-garde that it just flies over your heads. Unforunately for you, I’m not much in the habit of explaining myself, so you’ll get a little something since I’m not a completely uncharitable bastard, but don’t expect much. As of the last part of the story, five years have passed since I won the PTC Cruiserweight Tournament. You’ve probably figured out by now that I am Silvio Fiore, but I would almost say that such isn’t true anymore. First of all, because I changed my name. To walk around as Silvio when I don’t even walk around outside of the persona of The Quick, no point to it. Government documents label me by a different name. And for security reasons, you are not going to hear what it is. So from this point on, you’ll hear me being called ‘Q’ if someone is talking to me in a familiar tone. Also, you’re probably expecting some explanation about why I’m being The Quick. I’ll get to that when I’m good and ready. Not now, so don’t hold your breath or cry over it. My story picks up not where it left off, but rather a year before then. So if it’s 2010 now that I’m telling you all this, we’ll skip back a year to about July 2009. It was the year that Pope Benedict XVI died of some disease that he picked up in Zambia. Despite firmly stating that she didn’t want to become president, a lack of solid Republican leadership (since Delay wasn’t getting anything) resulted in Condoleeza Rice becoming the first woman and first black to achieve the presidency. McCain got her a lot of votes, though. So with gas prices at an all-time high, and network television seeing its first fully-frontal nude sex scene on FX between Jake Gyllenhaal and Mischa Barton (and let me say that I wasn’t impressed with either of them), we have the backdrop for the period in time in which I was just getting fully-settled into the persona of The Quick. This’ll mean a permanent shift into past-tense. Deal with it. I was standing in line at Wachovia. I used to have an account at Regions Bank, but they had to shut down after a massive identity theft ring, combined with the market collapse of 2007, meant that they had no money and owed everybody everything. Even FDIC was stretched to the limit and snapped by it. Luckily, a quick injection of money back into the economy by Bill Gates and other of the nation’s richest people buying up a shitload of treasury bonds got things back on track. In front of me in line there was this one fat lady. No, that’s no graphic enough. Ultramegahypermordibly obese, that’s a better term. She couldn’t even stand, was rolling around in some reinforced scooter that cried out in mechanical pain through creaks and the loosening of screws as it attempted to support what must have been an 800+ pound frame. I always thought that once you got to the size of qualifying for a Jabba the Hut lookalike contest that you were confined to the home, but there she was. Some kids from Taco Bell were standing behind me. Didn’t even take off their uniforms, must’ve been on lunch break. Anyway, they were talking about Josh, who was at one point “American Youth” but at some point started working more overseas in Japan, Germany, and Mexico. Then, he became known, through no doing of his own, as “American Pride” Josh. Moody, apparently would be coming back around to Global Wrestling after an absence of years. Nothing long-term he’d been convinced into some reunion shows or whatnot. The details escape me. So these kids, who knew jack-shit about wrestling, said that they were all cool to see their favorite wrestler Moody back in the states, back under the GW banner, wrestling against guys like El Serpenti, Elia Smith, Fred Grest, and everyone else who chose to stay with one place instead of branching the fuck out. I’m not going anywhere since this one old guy in the front seems to be taking for-goddamn-EVER to move, so I turn around and lambaste these idiots for having the bad wrestling sense to even consider Josh as their favorite wrestler. I had to remind them that back in 2005, in CW2, Moody sucked. He’d been going downhill since he had lost all drive to win anything. Sure, when he won matches he was all cool and shit, but as soon as he loses one big match he falls out of the spotlight and out of sight altogether. When he was first in GW, he had the World Title, but then as soon as he loses it to Reno, poof, gone. Then he turned up in cW. Core worked out for him at first, mainly because he showed up with a tag partner, but then lost the tag titles and he just decided that he didn’t want to stay around anymore. And then at the time of the tournament, he was a “free agent”, though he was still technically contracted via inactive status to GW because he returned so that he could get into the Hall of Fame, but otherwise did nothing. It’s still surprising that he didn’t make a grand return to Core just to do and get nothing and slip away into the darkness of being ignored. I didn’t tell the kids this, but in the second round, Silvio Fiore (me) had about four months professional experience as a wrestler. And that for being trained in some Midwestern wrestling school that really didn’t make great segue way into the professional scene. Meanwhile this Moody guy was supposed to be trained by some major guy from down in Mexico or at least near there. I only found out because there was rumor of him dedicating the tournament match to the guy, who had died about a week before. Sucked, then, that he lost. Didn’t even put up much of a fight. Oh, I’m sure in his eyes that he fought tooth and nail to win and it was really close and could go either way, but from my historical vantage point, for the fact that back then I was actually quite humble, it didn’t seem like it was an even fight. He had too much emotional baggage on his shoulders, whereas I just came in and did the do, you know? And since then, there have been multiple times that he could have avenged the loss. But every time he faced me, I came out on top. See, his problem was that he felt he was “complete” as a wrestler. Didn’t need to learn anything new, didn’t need to rethink his strategies, didn’t need to improve himself in any way. Myself, on the other hand, I’ve got some years under my belt now but I constantly learn new things in the ring. That’s how I continue to be so dominant. Moody, on the other hand, isn’t a bad wrestler, but can’t seem to understand that the same kids who were watching him wrestle years ago are now wrestlers themselves, and they know exactly how to beat him since he hasn’t changed anything about himself. Moron. The Taco Bell employees behind me verbally came to the defense of their favored wrestling superstar, but their linguistic skills demonstrated the terrible condition of the United States educational system (partially the fault of the Bush administration’s new stance on education in 2007, which I dubbed, “You know what? On second thought, fuck the children over from Washington and leave their sorry asses behind.” Or for short, “Fuck The Children From Behind.” Thoroughly finished with the two jackasses who had found their lifetime careers at the tender age of 15, I turned back around. Nothing. I spent two minutes reeducating the young ones and there hadn’t been one bit of movement in the line. I swore to myself that as this old man walked away from the counter, I’d trip him up. In the line next to me, there was this one guy. Had to be about thirty or something. Stocky, but had slim legs. Kept eyeing me. Not in the way that would indicate physical attraction or anything (though that’s happened a few times), but rather like he knew me and didn’t like me. Maybe he did. I fuck with so many people that it’s hard to keep track, though I do my best to try. I kept looking straight ahead. But flipped him off. Come on, you didn’t think that I’d just do nothing, right? Next thing I know, there’s the barrel of a Walther semiauto double-action against my temple and I’m on the ground. But don’t think that it’s just on account of lil ol’ me. Apparently this Sonny Silver looking ass, the old guy at the front, and two women somewhere off in the back offices had the thing all planned out. The bank was only a Wachovia, but then again Wachovia built up after the market crash, so this particular branch in Washington, D.C. had a lot of safety deposit boxes. They must’ve been looking for something in particular, since there were easier banks to hit. The old guy (mask job, he was younger than the guy with the gun to my head) commanded everyone to get down on the ground. The two girls from the back were out a little later, bringing out everyone who had been involved. The security guard was told to lock the doors, which he did. Then he took out his gun and helped to hold up the place. Inside job. Thing of beauty. Everything’s going cool. They were calm, collected, and know what they’re doing. Except for the guy standing over me. He’d been hugging the trigger like that one hot girlfriend from high school that you never should’ve broken up with. The leader came over to cool him off. Some guy in a brown trenchcoat and a full mustache in the old Tom Selleck show Magnum, P.I. “Cool it, Black! Lay up.” “You don’t know him,” Black said. I was still wearing my mask, never take it off in public, but some people still didn’t know who I was. “He ruined my career.” Being the smartass I am, I said, “Get in line, pal.” Double entendre. Awesome. The butt of the pistol came crashing upon my skull. Didn’t know me out, it’s actually quite hard to knock out a human being. And in fact, thanks to my Congenital Insensitivity to Pain with Anhidrosis, I couldn’t feel the pain at all. I lack the short nerve endings responsible for carrying pain sensations, so he might as well have been giving me a love tap because that’s about as worse as it felt. Being a wrestler incapable of feeling pain has its advantages. I can get hormone treatments to fix it, but why? Right now I’ve all of the benefits, none of the drawbacks, of being a wrestler. I have a sense of touch, but don’t feel pain. Submitting me is pretty much impossible since I’m not going to tap out from being hurt and nobody I’ve faced actually has the balls to injure me when they can just try to pinfall. Not like it’s hard, I rarely ever go over 173 in weight. They were all white, so I can separate them by hairstyle. The leader was bald, the guy standing over me had slicked brown hair that he stole from Christian Bale, and there was a blonde and brunette, both wearing big sunglasses that covered their eyes and partially obscured their faces. Otherwise, though, nobody was too worried about being seen. The brunette said, “Ease off, Black. We’ve got a job to do.” “Then do it, Green,” he said, not moving from me for a second. Apparently they’d all worked out code names. “I have to do this.” Brown and White, the leader and the blonde, respectively, shouted at him that this wasn’t the time. Really, it was. I doubted I’d ever see him again. If he was going to take his shot, what better time than that? But Brown said that Gray was already outside with the one police car that was parked outside. If a gun went off, it’d screw up their whole plan. The details of the plan were never fully revealed to us hostages since they didn’t have to go over it once they got inside, but I could figure some of it when the fat lady literally opened herself up to drop a bunch of equipment on the floor. It looked like drilling tools. They were settling in for a spell. Black kept his eye on me, though. In fact, I sat up, squirmed around, complained about being hungry, thirsty, needing to use the bathroom. Just to piss him off. He couldn’t do anything to me, after all, since there was a police presence outside. And besides, I’m a stickler for doing an encore with people. I remembered him now, having the time to reacquaint with his face in the wide-open one-room bank space that handled transactions, new accounts, and everything else under the sun in the same 6400 sq. ft. space with the white-washed walls and green marble flooring. Grant Sutton. He stood 6’3” and last I faced him he weighed about 229. Looked to have put on some weight though, but then again he’d had nothing but time since I took the Florida native and snapped his leg in half at the knee. They had to fully reconstruct the joint, then give it time to set and heal. And after that, extensive physical therapy. 2 years of his life, gone in a flash. I didn’t blame him for wanting to take his revenge on me. I’d be the same way. Especially for the fact that I would never apologize, and in fact I’d break his leg again if I could do it all over. He was a punkass, plain and simple. The plan to get the stuff that was apparently beneath the floor didn’t matter to me. I was fine just sitting there and tormenting Black. It got so bad that the REAL leader of the outfit, the lady in the fat suit named Blue, suggested that I be let outside. They still couldn’t shoot anybody to force a breach before they were ready. And at the same time, to make a show of good faith to the police outside that negotiations would work and breach was not an immediate necessity, they made the call to let some of us go. First, they just let out the security guard, who had already been paid off by them wiring some cash from there into an account for him. I heard that after a debriefing or whatever, he immediately bought a plane ticket out of the country and hasn’t been found since. The perfect crime. After that, they let out three of us. There was a pregnant lady and myself. So yeah, with that one line you know my stance on the whole “is an unborn baby a person” debate that rages to this day. Not many people in the bank at that time, only 15 (which was down to ten since it was a slow day, Saturday). And five of them were the robbers. Couldn’t give away all of their hostages at one time, but they seemed like they were being cooperative. I got out of there scot-free. Yeah, no heroic story. I didn’t fight off all of them and single-handedly save the bank. Why bother? I’d be getting my money back anyway, thanks to the revamped FDIC. All I did was stay cool and be myself. It’s gotten me into trouble, but it’s also gotten me out of trouble, and lot more than you’ll ever realize. I understand that you find the significance of this to matter fuck-all, but hey, I tell you what I want to tell you. Besides, you’ve gotten a very valuable lesson out of this. Evil bastards make for terrible hostages.
