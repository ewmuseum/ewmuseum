On origins of Fae, religion, no-selling...

27 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-03-27 We open, not on Fae as we might normally, but rather on a scene shot on video. It looks to be observation footage, complete with a handy date. The date reads 3/17/01...three years ago...before there had even been a whisper of Sidhe Nimh anywhere outside of Northeast Georgia...and not even there was the name known to more than a few. Seated at a table is a middle-aged lady, well dressed, with the short haircut of a hippie gone professional. She has notes and charts, pen and paper, and a look of genuine concern about her. Across from her sits our point of focus...a figure that looks vaguely recognizable...if one looks past the long, greasy hair, unshaven face, and dull eyes. It’s the eyes that give it away. Despite the exhaustion evident in every facet of this man’s being, despite the glaze of tears, his eyes gleam with intelligence and mischief. Maybe not brightly, but the gleam is there. He rocks slightly in his seat, and then starts to fall forward. As he catches himself with one hand on the desk, the tattoo of a seven-pointed star on the back of his left wrist becomes visible. Sidhe Nimh...before he was Sidhe...before he was the Fallen Fae...was Sam. Therapist: And how are we today? Sam sits, silent, just rocking quietly back and forth. Having regained his balance, he withdraws his hand and folds his arms about himself. Therapist: Anything you would like to talk about? How are classes going? Sam: Okay. Kinda. I’m not entirely sure, since I really don’t go unless I have to. It makes it easier for everyone that way, since... Sam stops talking. It’s a familiar sight...not the end of a thought, or a break while new thoughts gather, but the middle of a thought that’s slipped away forever. When Fae does it, it’s cute, as he allows himself to ramble. It never happened to Sidhe...he was sure to have everything scripted in advance, with cue cards and notes ready. When Sam stops, it becomes obvious something’s wrong. A look of pain crosses his face. Frustration. Self-hatred. Therapist: Why does your not going to class make things easier? Sam starts, and stops, and starts, and stops. He takes a deep breath, opens his mouth, and no words come out. He sits and rocks briefly, tears beginning to form in his eyes. He inhales deeply. Sam: Because...because I can’t stand being there. I can’t stand people. I hate them all. Therapist: Why do you hate them? If looks could kill, the therapist would have died, been resurrected, and killed again in a more gruesome way. Sam: I see them and their friends, their talk of family, their talk of plans for the weekend, their talk of going out and having fun, and I want to be a part of it. Or, more honestly, I have no interest in it at all. And I know that’s not right, or normal, or anything like that. I don’t want to go out, or have fun. I’m happy by myself, other people just get in the way. But I desire some contact, some kind of bond with the world. Or at least one other person. I... Again, Sam freezes, a thought lost forever in a mind filled with unending noise. If only he could make them hear the noise...maybe someone, somewhere would understand. Therapist: What about your friend...Kathryn? Is that her name? Sam: Kat? Yeah. Kat’s nice. Kat listens. Therapist: Have you made any more friends? You said that you’ve been spending more time with Kat at her house. Does she have housemates? Sam: Yep. They’re all wanks. Therapist: Does Kat get along with them. Sam: Somehow, yes. I don’t see how. Therapist: Have you asked her? Sam: Yeah. She said they were okay, once you got to know them. Therapist: Have you... Sam: Nope. Not going to get to know no one new. Therapist: That was a double negative. I see you’re not being a stickler about grammar anymore. Sam: I’m beyond caring about that at this point. Therapist: Have you made any other friends? Sam: No. Just Kat. Therapist: And how long has she been your friend? Sam: Eight...nine years. Ever since we met in middle school. Therapist: Do you recall having any friends before that? Sam: No. Therapist: Any other friends since? Sam: No. The therapist writes some things down. Therapist: What about drugs? Have you stopped using drugs? Sam: I’ve cut back. I stopped using heroin. And smoking opium. And I haven’t done acid in two weeks now. I still smoke like a chimney. Therapist: Marijuana? Sam: Cigarettes. I hate pot. It makes me feel stupid. The therapist makes a few more notes. Therapist: Have you ever had a flashback or any withdrawal symptoms? Sam: Withdrawal...no. And I couldn’t tell you about the flashbacks because I hallucinated periodically before I ever did acid, and... Sam freezes again, mid-sentence, the same frustration coming over him. The therapist makes a few final notes... Therapist: Okay...this may be a little hard to explain...but I don’t think we’re equipped to deal with someone like you here... Scene freezes. Pull out to reveal the video is playing on Fae’s TV. He sits on the floor, cross-legged, his head clean-shaven, his goatee neatly trimmed, dressed in plaid pajamas. Fae: An’ that was the next to last time Fae went to therapy. The next time, Fae had a new doctor-person, an’ Fae told them what they wanted to hear so as they wouldn’t try an’ talk to Fae more. Fae HATES talkin’ to people! Fae rocks back and forth quietly. Fae: An’ even if people ain’t tryin’ to kill Fae...an’ all evidence says they ain’t...people still sayin that Fae’s a cwazy-person. An’ Fae ain’t no cwazy person. Fae jus’ as sane as the next person. ‘Less the next person is Ikke Mighty, ‘cause Fae prob’ly less sane than Ikke. On cue, the world’s favorite Icelandic giant comes through on a unicycle, wearing a thong and bra set from Frederick’s of Hollywood, singing “Happy Days are Here Again!” Fae watches. Fae: So maybe Fae ain’t less sane than Ikke. But Fae certainly ain’t no cwazy man, livin’ on the streets, wearin’ a paper bag hat. At least not everyday. Somedays, Fae gets out the paper bag hat an’ wears it around, but only when Fae can find his crayons an’ make the paper bag hat pretty. Fae nods. Fae: An’, somehow, Fae ended up in this cruiserweight tournament. Fae pretty sure he signed up for it, but Fae not quite sure... Fae gets out a legal pad, covered in purple crayon scrawling of complete and utter nonsense. Not even letters or words, just a bunch of marks. Lots of pictures of dogs. Fae: Yep, Fae sure did sign up for this tournament thingy. Prob’ly had Kat do the paperwork. Last time Fae did paperwork, they sent it back, ‘cause apparently they didn’t appreciate Fae’s picture-answer to the “Sex” question... An’ Fae not sure why not, it a perfectly natural thing that Fae drew. Fae nods. Fae: But I gots opponents in this match. I got two opponents, an’ that’s good, ‘cause Fae gets confused with number above three, an’ two gives Fae a margin of error. Fae understands that one of ‘em is named Primetime, an’ Fae thinks that a silly name. The other one is Christian Crawfish, Fae thinks. Fae thinks that’s a silly name, too. Fae not a big fan of primetime. Fae used to watch Seinfeld, but since that went off the air, Fae don’t watch much primetime anymore. Primetime sucks. Fae usually ends up watchin’ shoppin’ channels for hours at a time, an’ Fae keeps on orderin’ pressure washers an’ steakburgers. An’ the things you can do with a pressure washer an’ some steakburgers! Nothin’ like propellin’ a five-ounce frozen beef patty along a tile floor with a pressure washer while on rollerblades. ‘Specially when you got a couple friends with pressure washers, too, an’ play roller pressure washer meat hockey. That rocks. Fae can only think that Primetime thinkin’ of himself as bein’ like primetime television, an’ bein’ the thing everyone gonna watch. ‘Xcept most people don’t watch primetime no more. Now they watch weird Japanese porn on Direct TV. Well...maybe most people don’t do that, but Fae sure some people do. Fae usually too busy orderin’ more pressure washers to watch any kind of porn these days. An’ Primetime gonna get kicked on a good bit for bein’ the cause of distraction these days. All this wrestlin’ is cuttin’ into Fae’s porn-watchin’ time. Fae ain’t good at managin’ his schedule, so Fae just ain’t got time to do everything Fae enjoys no more, an’ Fae’s gonna hold Primetime responsible for that, ‘cause Fae ain’t in a mood to blame himself, so Fae gonna project his anger an’ rage onto Primetime, an’ it gonna be a source of sufferin’ for Mr. Primetime. Mr. Primetime gonna be all like, “No, Fae, no more kickin’ me in the head!”, an’ Fae’ll say, “Too bad, poohead.” Mr. Primetime gonna mind the projectin’ of anger an’ rage. An’ Fae got lots an’ lots of anger an’ rage, ‘cause of sexual frustration. Fae’ll project his sexual frustration onto Kat, an’ Fae don’t think she’ll mind. Fae’s other opponent is Christian Crawfish, an’ Fae don’t like him ‘cause his name don’t take into account many many things. Like other religions an’ shellfish. Fae used to know a tag team called Buddhist Scallop an’ Ba’hai Faith Blue Crab. They was a couple of alright guys, kinda. They wrestled Ikke in a handicap match, an’ got their butts beaten by the forces of goodness. Prob’ly ‘cause they was all pacifistic an’ stuff. Fae also knew a guy named Zoroastrian Shrimp, an’ he was a bit weird. So was Muslim Clam, an’ Wiccan Lobster. More Fae thinks, more Fae realizes that all the religion-shellfish people is pretty weird. So Fae gonna assume that Christian Crawfish is pretty weird. That where he in trouble, ‘cause he might be pretty weird, but Fae’s MIGHTY weird! An’ unlike most times, it ain’t a Mighty Fae-sized Fae ‘gainst some normal big-sized people. It’s a Mighty Fae-sized Fae ‘gainst two average Fae-sized people. They even a bit smaller than Fae, so if Fae can beat one person twice Fae’s size, Fae shouldn’t have no problems with two people smaller than Fae. Fae can do that much math. Christian Crawfish just gonna pray at Fae, anyway. An’ prob’ly make gumbo. Fae likes gumbo. Fae thinks. This takes a while. Fae: Maybe Fae should be nicer to Mr. Crawfish, so he can get some of Mr. Crawfish’s gumbo. Fae thinks more. The camera crew could probably go out and have a nice lunch somewhere while he ponders his latest problem. Fae: Maybe Fae should ask Ikke ‘bout Christian Crawfish...Ikke knows lots an’ lots of wrestlers! Maybe Ikke can tell Fae about Mr. Crawfish! Ikke kicks through a wall, no longer dressed in lingerie, but rather in his normal winter camouflage, accompanied by the strains of Type O Negative’s “Christian Woman”. Ikke: HAH! Ye need information on Christian Crawfish? Fae: DA! Ikke: Lo, he ‘tis a fierce competitor...but he is far from MIGHTY! He shalt not be a problem for ye! Fae: YAY! That good, ‘cause Fae facin’ him in a Cruiserweight tournament, an... Ikke cocks his head to the side. Ikke: Cruiserweight? ‘Tis this under interdimensional rules? Fae: No...Fae not think so? Ikke: Lo, then it cannot be the Christian Crawfish whom I am thinking of...for he is well over a metric tonne of mutated crawfish-man! Well within interdimensional standards for Cruiserweights, indeed, but nary for this puny mortal planet. HAH! Fae: Let Fae check who he’s facin’... Fae picks up the phone, and after some difficulty figuring out the difference between “9” and “4”, dials the PTC information line. He checks the card for the Cruiserweight tournament. Fae: Seems Fae is facin’ Primetime an’ Christian Crawford, not Christian Crawfish. Ikke: HAH! Indeed! Christian Crawford! Fae: What you know ‘bout Christian Crawford? Ikke: HAH! Not a damn thing! Ikke can guarantee this, however, he is not a MIGHTY Christian Crawford! HAH! Fae: You sure? Ikke: Indeed, Ikke hath a seventh sense about this. Fae: What ‘bout a sixth sense? Ikke: Lo, Ikke hath a sixth sense concerning sexual ability. Fae: What that for? Ikke: HAH! Ikke hath never had a boring date! HAH! Fae stares at Ikke. Fae: You still a big freak, Ikke. But how Faeness gonna deal with a man Fae knows nothin’ about? Ikke: Ye never know anything about ye opponents. What is Joey Vendetti’s finisher? Fae: Fae not know. Ikke: Darkseid’s birthplace? Fae: Fae not know. Ikke: Sheri Dedmon’s bra size? Fae: 44-D. Ikke stares. Ikke: Ikke should have known ye’d know that. Fae: Kat tells me every day! Says it’s important informationals to have. Ikke: HAH! Indeed it is! But ye know not much information about ye opponents, so why would knowing little of Christian Crawford bother ye in the least? Fae: ‘Cause, what if he some kinda good wrestler who gots moves an’ things Fae ain’t familiar with an’ don’t know how to counter? Ikke: HAH! Ye still are not used to being Mighty, are ye? Ye need not worry about counters. Ye know the art of the no-sell! HAH! HAH! ]Ikke produces a lead pipe and conks Fae over the head with it. Fae just stares at him, blankly. Fae: But Fae not think many peoples appreciate the no-sell. Not the most entertainin’ move in existence. Ikke: Have ye ever seen Hulk Hogan and the pop he gets? ‘Tis not a MIGHTY pop, but is well-done for a mortal. As is that of the Undertaker. They get large pops, and they sell nary a move. Fae: So people like the no-sell? Fae didn’t think they did. Ikke: HAH! In the interdimensional ranks, ‘tis a move in everyone’s arsenal. If ye sold every move, matches wouldst be quite short. Indeed, had Ikke sold being hit with a moonsault by Cthulhu, he would now be dead, as Cthulhu is well over five-million metric tonnes and several miles tall, and that is merely in his small, competitive incarnation. HAH! Fae: He that big? Ikke: Indeed, his moonsault killed every person in attendance at Ikke’s last title defense, save the Interdimensional wrestlers, who merely no-sold it. Yea, verily, when Ikke finally defeated Cthulhu, the only surviving witness was Christian Crawfish. The Ikkeknifing of Cthulhu decimated a small town. Fae: Then why didn’t Cthulhu no-sell the Ikkeknife? Ikke: HAH! Because no one can no-sell the Ikkeknife! Fae: But Fae no-sells it all the time. Ikke: Indeed, but ye are mighty in the art of the no-sell. HAH! Most interdimensional stars are not MIGHTY, merely gargantuan in proportion. Ikke is a tiny, tiny man in comparison. Ikke poses in all his 7’5” glory. Ikke: Ho, indeed. The no-sell is the most powerful of all moves, particularly the MIGHTY no-sell. Fae stares at Ikke. Fae: You a damn big freak, Ikke. Fae gonna go talk to someone more normal. Like...anyone. Fae wanders off. Ikke poses some more, the kicks out a wall and walks out of the room. We catch up with Fae in the Bar of Darkness, where Kat sits, enjoying a healthy breakfast of grapefruit juice and vodka, accompanied by a cigarette. Fae pulls up a stool next to her. Lawrence of Darkness, the Bartender of Darkness, slides a root beer down to Fae. Fae: Fae tryin’ to figure out something, Kat... Kat: I’m working on quite a puzzler, too...how do they get the custard inside a stuffed donut? Fae: That simple, they...they... Fae thinks, scratches his head, then shrugs. Fae: Hmmm...Fae don’t know. DDT probably does, given that he do that cookin’ thing. But Fae’s issue is that he’s facin’ a Christian Crawford an’ a Primetime in that tournament thingy that Fae signed up for. Kat: Christian Crawfish? This is under interdimensional rules? That man-lobster has quite the beating coming, what with his tendency to make random ethnic slurs...at least against Asians...if he was slurring Italians, I wouldn’t have a problem with it...especially if they were against Vendetti... Fae: No. Christian CRAWFORD, not CRAWFISH. Fae was confused, too. Kat: Christian Crawford? No idea. Is he mighty? Fae: Ikke says no. Kat: Then why worry? If he’s not mighty, he’s going to get his ass kicked worse than that guy in that S&M video with Nicole Bass. Lawrence: Dude, that one where she slams the guys head in the oven? Kat: Yeah, that’d be it. Lawrence: That’s funny shit, man. Funny shit. Fae: Fae don’t think he allowed to slam no one’s head in no ovens in this tournament. Kat: Ah. So it’s not under interdimensional rules, I take it? Fae: No. Only ways to win match are by pinfall, submission, DQ, an’ countout. Kat: No victory by death of opponent? Fae: No. Kat: Damn. Because anyone with a name like “Primetime” needs killing. I was hoping you could win by double homicide. Kat shrugs. Fae: Fae guesses pinnin’ just as good as killin’. Kat: Not for someone with a name like Primetime. Lawrence: Shit, man, one time when I was workin’ construction, there was this dude who called himself “Primetime”. We kicked his ass on principle. Fae: But what if Primetime is mighty? Lawrence: Dude, a guy who calls himself “Primetime” is NEVER mighty. And he always deserves to get his ass kicked. Kat: And that’s if you’re a kind and generous soul, like Lawrence here. If you’re like me, you think he just needs to die. I’d stab him myself, were it not for the threat of prosecution. Fae: But Fae can’t kill him. Fae’d prob’ly get disqualified for that, an’ then Fae can’t advance in the tournament, an’ Fae can’t win a new shiny! Kat: Yeah...I suppose shiny objects are more important that killing someone for referring to themselves as “Primetime”. Lawrence: He still needs his ass kicked, dude. Fae: Fae don’t doubt that for even a nanosecond. Kat: So, are all your doubts settled? Fae: Pretty much, Fae just gots to go train for this, an’ get ready for Reborn. Fae gonna have to do some beatin’ on some Skel’tons of Suzie, some Christian Crawford, an’ some guy callin’ himself “Primetime”. Lawrence. Now, if he called himself, “Primeteim”, that might be cool. Kat: Or “Prïmteim”...that might be cool, too. Fae: Fae agrees...now Fae jus’ gotta find Ikke again to commence to mighty forms of trainin’. Ikke kicks through a wall, picks up Fae and Kat, and turns to Lawrence. Ikke: HAH! We must train! Lawrence: Dude, what’ve I said about kicking through my wall? Ikke looks around sheepishly. Ikke: Lo, send Ikke the bill... Ikke strides off carrying Kat and Fae. Fade out.
