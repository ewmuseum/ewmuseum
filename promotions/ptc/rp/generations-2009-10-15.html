Date: 2009-10-15 The first thing you need to know is that I am not Jason Snow. Rather, you can think of me as your guide in this particular story. Your host, if you will, or even your Master of Ceremonies. My name is not important, but I’m here to tell you things that are important, and I think I’ve narrowed the list down to the three most: 1. I am not Jason Snow. But we’ve covered that. 2. There will be no wrestling in this story. Or at least, it’ll be kept to a bare minimum. As it happens, Jason Snow rarely thinks about the ring in his off time, and I assure you that what few moments he does would make for a very boring story. I don’t want to tell that story and I doubt you’d want to read it. But if you insist on knowing what Jason Snow thinks of the wrestling business, I believe I can sum it up for you here, in three simple words: He is unbeatable. Lets all be glad that’s out of the way. Now then. 3.There will be no mobsters in this story. Not if I can help it anyway. I’ll try my best to steer you clear of those facets of Jason’s life. And I’ll go one step further too: There will be no murder in this story. At least not any new ones. There might be reflections on murders in the past and there will probably be foreshadowing of murders to come, if you’re inclined to look for it. Some of you might be. But in this story, no one will be murdered. There will be plenty of violence and there will be things far worse than violence, but if it’s murder you’re looking for, I’m sure you’ll get your fill elsewhere. There’s plenty of that around. On that note, I suppose I should introduce you to the people you’ll be following. The first, obviously, is Jason Snow. If you’ve been living under a rock for the last several years, allow me to catch you up. If you’ve ever watched a PRIME show or even an old PCW show, then I can say with reasonable confidence that you know absolutely nothing about Jason Snow. That’s the fake one, but we’ll spend a moment on him anyway. The fake one: He’s thirty years old and he’s currently PRIME’s Universal Champion. When he was twenty-seven, he won GTT, the most prestigious tournament in the wrestling business. While the real Jason Snow still is not quite sure what GTT stands for, both he and the fake Jason Snow are proud of the accomplishment. The real Jason Snow is far less interesting, or so he’ll tell you. As it happens, he’s also thirty years old, which makes him too old to feel young anymore, but far too young for dementia, which he suffers from. All those years pretending to be the fake Jason Snow came with a price, it seems. Whether or not dementia is the right word for what he suffers from is up for debate. Doctors have been working for a year on a proper diagnosis. It looks like dementia and it manifests like post-traumatic-stress-syndrome. One doctor suggested schizophrenia, but that doesn’t explain the smudge on the X-Ray of Jason’s brain. Such is life. Whether or not he’s the main character in this story is for you to decide. A lot of times I don’t think he is. And a lot of his story, much as the rest of our stories, takes place long before he was born. Now. Here are some things you need to know about Jason. First and foremost, he’s at the beginning of a long overdue romance with a striking young woman named Danielle Manderly. Sometimes I think she’s the real hero in all of this. Of course, she also happens to be the younger sister of Jason’s late wife, Raven, so you can imagine how, in the beginning, her feelings for Jason were a great source of guilt and shame for her. But she got over it and so will the rest of you. But you know these people already. You don’t know Harold. Harold is Jason’s father, but they last saw each other in 1984. Harold was born in February, 1951, and he died in that same month in 2007. I wouldn’t get my hopes up about any fuzzy reunions. Now, I suppose you’re wondering how a long-deceased man can play an active role in this story. Well, that’s what you’ve got me for, and I’ll do my best to make sure you’ve got the information you’ll be needing. It could get bumpy and it almost certainly will get complicated. I’ll try to keep my hand out of things, but I can’t make any promises. Which reminds me. 4. I reserve the right to break any rule I damned well choose.
