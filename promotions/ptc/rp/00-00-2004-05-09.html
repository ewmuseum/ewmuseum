Date: 2004-05-09 00:00:12 All that stood between Seymour Almasy and the truth of his existence was an inch thick piece of wood, the door that led to the bedroom that he and Aerith shared. Inside the room was Seymour, brooding about his upcoming contest with Syko Arsonist. Outside the room was Aerith, finally ready to tell Seymour who he was, and even more importantly, what the world was. It was finally time for the Final Fantasy to be revealed as simply that. 00:06 But how will Almasy take it? For his life, his dreams, and his ambitions have all been built upon a set of assumptions about the world. With the assumptions gone, will Seymour collapse like a building without a foundation? Can Seymour Almasy accept being Jason Seymour Wilson? Time’s up, Seymour. Zero hour. 00:00. ------- Seymour was snapped from his thoughts of Syko Arsonist by a quick knock on the bedroom door. The knock was loud, and almost frantic, as if the knocker was pounding against the door again and again. Almasy shook his head to clear it, and then rose to open the door. Of course, Aerith was on the other side. Seymour Almasy: Hi, Aerith! How was your thinking time? Aerith Heartilly: It was fine, Seymour. Even someone with the minimal deductive skills of Seymour could tell something was wrong with Aerith. All the signs were there. She was obviously nervous, lip shaking, face contorted. Seymour Almasy: Is something wrong, Aerith? Aerith Heartilly: Yes, Seymour…I have something to tell you. Almasy fell silent. The grave expression on Aerith’s face told him that whatever needed to be said, it was serious indeed. His eyes met hers, questioning, waiting to see what he needed to know. Aerith Heartilly: There’s no easy way to say this, Seymour, so I’m just going to come out with it. Seymour…you aren’t a hero on a quest. The world isn’t a role playing game. Those games do not depict real events…they’re just games. And finally…your name isn’t Seymour Almasy. The look on Seymour’s face was akin to that of a small boy who had just been told his puppy had been hit by a car. No trace of anger could be found in his features, rather a stark sorrow overcame the Final Fantasy. He sunk back to the bed, desperately searching for a way out, a loophole in her statement. Seymour Almasy: But wait a minute, Aerith! If that were true, how come whenever I take my Elixirs I always feel stronger, like I could take the entire world on? Aerith Heartilly: Have you ever read the labels of your Elixirs, Seymour? Seymour Almasy: Of course I ha--, err, umm, no, I haven’t. Should I have? His wife turns to an ice bucket on the nightstand next to the bed, and pulls a large bottle out from it. Aerith Heartilly: Is this your Elixir, Seymour? Seymour Almasy: Yes. It doesn’t usually taste especially good, but it gives me all the courage and vitality I need in battle. Aerith Heartilly: Read to me what the label says, Seymour. Seymour takes the bottle happily, fully expecting to see the word “Elixir” on it. However, he is dismayed to find other words instead. Seymour Almasy: Jack….Daniels. Okay….so it’s a BRAND of Elixir! Aerith Heartilly: It’s alcohol, honey. It alters your mental state…and for many people, it makes them fearless, which explains the effects you feel after you drink it. Even as Seymour’s fantasy world began to crumble, he had more proof. His mind rapidly began to think of more reasons why he was right, why he had to be. Seymour Almasy: But what about my materia! My Haste and Protect spells always work! Aerith Heartilly: It’s mind over matter. You believe that you can cast spells, so you think that the effects work. Seymour Almasy: Alright then, how do you explain my almighty Firaga? You can’t see the Haste spell being cast, but you can’t argue with a FIREBALL! Aerith shook her head. This was growing more and more painful, but she knew that Seymour wouldn’t be so quick to believe that his entire life was a lie. However, she also knew that she could disprove anything he brought up, as she would now do with his Firaga. Aerith Heartilly: Show me how you throw a Firaga, Seymour. Seymour Almasy: But I thought I wasn’t allowed to cast Firaga in the house! Aerith Heartilly: You are now, Seymour. Seymour Almasy: Woohoo! Almasy immediately reaches into his pocket, and pulls out a piece of flash paper. The Final Fantasy grins, and prepares for his deadliest spell. Seymour Almasy: FIRAGA~! Aerith Heartilly: Without the paper, Seymour. Seymour Almasy: But-but…but the paper’s part of the spell!! Aerith Heartilly: Did Vivi or Lulu use paper? Seymour Almasy: Well, no… The Final Fantasy’s head begins to hang. All seems lost for him. Suddenly, dramatically, however, he brightens. His head snaps up, causing his hair to fly back. Seymour Almasy: But what about you, Aerith! You’re my proof! Aerith Heartilly: Seymour, you can’t possibly think I’m Aerith from Final Fantasy VII, can you? Seymour Almasy: No, but…but…you have to be related to Rinoa from FF8! Your last name is Heartilly! Aerith Heartilly: Seymour…just as your real name isn’t Seymour Almasy, my real name isn’t Aerith Heartilly. The only thing that Seymour could do now was sulk. She had managed to disprove all of his proof. He now had many questions, too many to ponder all at once. The one that came out of his mouth, however, was a followup to his prior question. Seymour Almasy: Then what IS your name? Aerith Heartilly: My name is Laura Marie Winters. Seymour tilts his head to one side, thinking for several seconds, before the slightest bit of realization dawns on him. Seymour Almasy: You know, that name sounds familiar…but for the life of me, I can’t place why. Laura Winters: What is the earliest thing you can remember in your life? Maybe if you can remember that…you will know why you remember my name. Almasy rose to pace, walking back and forth, mind lost deep in thought, before finally offering an answer. Seymour Almasy: I remember…I remember…being at a funeral. There were lots of people there. I had a Phoenix Down in my pocket…err wait, those don’t exist now… Laura Winters: They were feathers, Seymour. Seymour Almasy: How could you know that though? Were you there? Laura Winters: No, but you told me about it. You showed me the “Phoenix Down” you tried to use to revive your father. Seymour Almasy: My…father was the dead man? Laura Winters: Yes. You’ve probably blocked that out. I can’t say as I blame you, either. Your father was a kind man, but your mother… Seymour Almasy: She sent me to live with Grandpa Cid. Laura Winters: Ah, so you DO remember some of this. Seymour Almasy: Yes. Why wouldn’t I? Laura Winters: Do you know why she sent you away? Seymour Almasy: I don’t know. She said it was because she wanted me to see new things…but Grandpa always said it was because she was embarrassed of me. Laura growled deep in her throat, a sound quite unbecoming of her, but perfectly understandable. Winters’ dislike of Seymour’s mother was at nearly unfathomable levels for her abandonment of her son. Laura Winters: She was, Seymour. Although, it was her own fault, really. Seymour Almasy: It was? Laura Winters: The reason that you believed it all to be real was that she was always busy. Always at work, or socializing with her damn gossipy friends…and your dad also worked, so they left you home alone even when you were little, with only the TV and your games to babysit you. Seymour Almasy: So, what does that have to do with anything? Laura Winters: Parents are supposed to teach us about the world, what’s real and what isn’t. They weren’t around to do that, so you naturally assumed that everything you experienced, even in the games, was real… Seymour Almasy: Ah, now I understand…since you know so much, you had to have known me when I was young. Laura Winters: Yes, Seymour. I did. I’ve known you since kindergarten. We went to school together. I loved you then, just as I do now… The Final Fantasy blushed. He realized that Aeri--no, Laura, had stayed with him this entire time, been his wife…even while knowing of Seymour’s ailments. He felt the compulsion to hug her, and gave into it, wrapping his arms tightly around her. Seymour Almasy: I knew you looked familiar when I saw you for the first time, but I couldn’t place you… Laura Winters: You probably blocked out most of your childhood while you were with your grandfather. He used the games you loved to teach you things. He’s the reason you’re as well spoken as you are. Seymour Almasy: I’ll have to thank him them when I see him next. Laura? Laura Winters: Yes, Seymour? Seymour Almasy: What’s my name? She chuckled. It sure had taken him long enough to ask such an important question. Laura Winters: Brian. Brian Seymour Wilson. Seymour Almasy: Ah…is that what it says on my certificate of birth? Laura Winters: Yes, Seymour. Seymour Almasy: Then, I’m going to have to go get that changed. Laura Winters: But why, Seymour? You can’t deny your past anymore… Seymour Almasy: No, I cannot. However, Brian Seymour Wilson, from what you have told me, was outcast by his parents and delusional. Seymour Almasy is who I am now. The great warrior, the man loved by fans around the world. This is who I am, and now with the knowledge of my past…Brian is inside of me, but Seymour is my identity now. Laura Winters: I understand. Seymour Almasy: You know…were we married under the names of Seymour and Aerith? Laura Winters: Well, yes… Seymour Almasy: So, you weren’t married to me under your official name. We need to rectify that. Laura beamed at him, and he smiled back, pulling her close for a quick kiss. Laura Winters: And this time, we’ll invite everyone. Seymour Almasy: Of course! Your parents know me now, there’s no reason to make our wedding a secret anymore. The smile on Seymour’s face comforted her greatly. Everything seemed as if it would be fine. Seymour, however, had a few more questions. Seymour Almasy: So, does this mean I’m not really trying to save the world? Laura chuckled, and hugged him again for comfort. Laura Winters: No, Seymour, but that doesn’t make your efforts in the ring in vain. You fight for what you believe in, and you never give up. Those are qualities of a great warrior. You may not be a great hero, but you ARE a great wrestler. Seymour Almasy: So that’s what I do…I wrestle. And the Battle Arena is a ring. The new terminology is quickly accepted by Seymour. He frowns slightly, asking a question somewhat jokingly. Seymour Almasy: So, does this mean that Chryste really ISN’T the bane of human existence and the root of all evil? Laura Winters: Well, no Seymour…but people like her prove something that you’re going to have to learn. For the most part, there is little absolute good, and little absolute evil in the real world. People like Chryste are shades of gray…some like them, some don’t. We all have good and evil inside of us, all of us are capable of both. Seymour Almasy: Hmm, that concept IS going to take a bit of time to get used to. But I understand. It is why people cheered men like Tsunami Oni and Chris Storm. Laura Winters: There’s something attractive about being a villain, I admit it. Besides, wouldn’t I just look so cute in black leather? Almasy simply smiled back at her as she winked at him. Seymour Almasy: Of course, sweetie…but I get your point. Being the villain sometimes can be as appealing as the hero. For in this world, I’ve noticed, good does not always triumph. A saddened expression appeared on Seymour’s face, and Laura knew that he was right. Good didn’t always win, in fact, it rarely did. People literally got away with murder each and every day. Other crimes went unreported. Good news reports in the media seemed few and far between. Seymour Almasy: Laura, that is why I must continue to be good. In a world without role-models, without people to look up to and emulate…I want to be a pillar of light. I want to be someone people can look up to and cheer for. Chief Executive and Financial Officers cooked the books of major corporations, causing millions to lose money on the stock market. In Iraq, American soldiers who were supposed to be keeping the peace violated international law by abusing prisoners of war. Laura Winters: That’s a very noble goal, Seymour, for anyone. Seymour Almasy: Thank you, Laura. Laura Winters: You’re right though…good is still something that we all really want to be. In wrestling…we can cheer for the bad guys because there’s no immediate danger to us. Chris Storm beating you didn’t bring about the end of the world. It just made him champion. Seymour Almasy: Looking back now, I feel kind of silly…because I approached every match that way. Every battle was one with the fate of the world in the balance. It’s ESPECIALLY silly because even when I lost, the world didn’t end, it wasn’t swallowed up in darkness… Laura Winters: You didn’t know any better though! Besides, it was the way you motivated yourself. Seymour Almasy: I know, I know, but still… Laura gently hugged her husband once more. Despite his growing confidence, Seymour was still frightened. Being told of a world order far different from what he believed was extremely unnerving to Seymour. Laura Winters: Seymour, this isn’t over. We’ll get you counseling, someone to talk to. Professionals will be able to help you get acclimated to reality. And I’ll be here to help you too. This isn’t something I can expect you to come to grips with immediately. It might be a long time until you fully understand. She accentuated her comment with a reassuring pat on the shoulder, smiling to make him feel more at ease. Laura Winters: And every step of the way, I will be here for you. Almasy blushed furiously. She was so nice to him, so kind. And she always had been. Under an assumed name that he recognized now was clearly designed to draw him closer, she had taken him from his shack in the middle of the Bronx into her apartment. She had followed him from UWF to LOW, from GW to the top events of Primetime Central. Never once had she complained, despite sacrificing her relationship with her family for years to do so. Seymour Almasy: Thank you. I’m flattered…more than you could ever know. You’ve done everything for me. I can’t thank you enough, and there is no amount of anything that I could give you to repay you for your kindness… Laura Winters: You don’t have to, Seymour. I owe you thanks as well. Thanks for being as wonderful to me as you are. But, if you do want to do something…let’s get married next week. I know that’s no time to prepare a ceremony normally, but… Seymour Almasy: But? Laura Winters: But my mother’s a wedding planner by trade. If ANYONE can manage to put together a wedding and get invitations to lots and lots of people FAST, she can. Seymour Almasy: Okay, sweetheart! I’d love to. More smiles all around. It had all gone better than Laura had dared dream. Seymour wasn’t saddened. His competitive fire still existed, and soon, they would be married, officially this time. With her confession out of the way, both were ready to look ahead to the future. Seymour Almasy: Two more times, then, shall I step into the Battle Arena on a regular basis, if all goes well. Laura Winters: Two more battles for you to win, two more opponents to vanquish. Seymour Almasy: Syko Arsonist is a man with credentials very similar to mine. A man of great skill, with mental afflictions, who has been psychologically beaten down. But now, I have come free from my problems, and hopefully, he too will be able to free himself from his mental chains. For I want him at one-hundred percent for our contest. Laura Winters: Seymour, the games you played are known as role playing games. You play the role of other people. At home, you are my Seymour. My loving husband…but in the ring… Seymour suddenly brightens, getting the gist of her statement. Seymour Almasy: In the ring, I AM that which I believed myself to be! I am a great warrior, a man who fights for the forces of justice and honor with all of my might. Laura Winters: Arsonist is neither good, nor evil. Seymour Almasy: He is merely my opponent. My opponent in a battle not for the fate of the world, but a battle for pride, and one that will catapult one of us to the finals of this tournament. Mount Gagazet may not be real, but the metaphor still stands. I wish to go out on top. I want to stand in the middle of the ring after the tournament final with my arm triumphantly raised. I want to hear the adulation of the crowd, their cheers for me. But before that dream can be made a reality, I must defeat Syko Arsonist. Laura Winters: Welcome to the real world, my love. Finally, the embrace was broken. Almasy smiled, and looked over at the corner of the room. The Divine Blade Imperial II, his faithful sword, sat in its scabbard. The Final Fantasy rose, crossed the room, and unsheathed his weapon. Seymour Almasy: Thank you, but is it okay if I bring this? I do love it so, and just because I know the worlds of swords and sorcery aren’t real… Laura Winters: Doesn’t mean that it’s not fun to pretend. I know just how much fun it is, because it’s what I’ve been doing this entire time. Bring your sword and your armor, I shall dress as Aerith once again. Now, though, we can do it because we both WANT to. Seymour Almasy: Arsonist shall be the first to experience the new Seymour Almasy. A man in touch with himself, and his surroundings for the very first time. As before, I still fight for all those who believe in goodness, but I can now do so for goodness’ sake. In essence, my quest is over, but yet it continues. Formally, the journey of Seymour Almasy is over…but the ideals I hold will live forever on! Laura Winters: And if he thinks he shall be victorious despite this… Seymour Almasy: Then it is HE who lives in a fantasy world! Both nod to one another. Aerith Heartilly’s task has been finished, and Laura Winters has emerged once again. The union of Seymour and Laura shall soon be officially sanctified. The long journey of Brian Seymour Wilson is over. Seymour Almasy stands triumphant over his past. But can he stand triumphant over the opposition? (Fin.)
