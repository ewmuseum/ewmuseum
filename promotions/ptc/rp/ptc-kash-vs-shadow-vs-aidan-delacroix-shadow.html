Kash vs Shadow vs Aidan DelaCroix

20 June 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
*Sorry about the wait guys, I've been going out with friends a lot lately and working too. But, I got it up, eight minutes before the end of Saturday night...and I typed that monster with a crazy sunburn! Hope you enjoy...* I've said many words in my life. Much were meant to be said, others were not. Much were strong, and the rest weak - but not necassarily a majority of one side. I could tell you a story of such an occasion in which I used certain words that could give you a symbol of something instrumental towards the likes of yourself; but could I really? Would you really understand? Would you really comprehend? Would you really know what I wanted you to know...to find out? You might. But who am I to judge your comprehension skills? Here's a test: read, my friend, read. A story of survival. --- The cold air, it made him tremble as it confronted him and swept down his back. It had already made his eyes dry and the lip under his nose feel frozen and dry as well. He was cold, sure, but not freezing - of course not, he wasn't dead yet. He had started his journey early in the morning, as soon as the sun had risen from it's hiding spot on the other side of the Earth. His companions: none other than that of his two [best] friends, Gibson and Katherine. And then, there was himself, of course - he did have a chat within his head sometimes - but who doesn't these days? They had hiked about a mile and a quarter up the mountain, the name not understood too them, but it was in the Eastern part of the world. They were young, yes, they were in their early to mid twenties upon this trip. They had known each other for years now, since high school in the states. The best of friends, those three - always around each other. And this was their dream group trip, to climb a mountain. To show their strength, as a group; to show their courage, as a group; to show their care, as a group. Little did they know that on this trip, many things would happen. They would show their weakness, as a group; they would show their cowardness, as a group... They made their way from their resting place, they had traveled about a mile or less (maybe more, no one kept track). They had rested and made a fire, the weather wasn't bad - but it was cold. Their canister's were starting to get low on water - but Paul had a lot in his backpack; no worries. The fire had stopped, because of the wind (it didn't last long, they were only there ten minutes). The oxygen was slowly fading, but their minds were set on their dream: to get to the top - to win [against all that could be offered]. And on they went. Too fight - too battle - too win. --- "They got pretty far, they did. They fought, alright. Against that brutal weather, you'd think no one would travel too such a place - just to get a novelty picture. Do you get a plaque? No. A trophy? Doubt it. Now, a belt? Perhaps..." I said with a glistening tune to my words, it sounded like rain hitting the top of a lake, just before lighthing struck. "No, not even a belt. But my battle, this tournement - perhaps I could climb this, 'mountain,' so to speak, and attain such a thing as a belt...a very special belt." The sun glistened in my eyes, just as my words did too your ears. If we can apply such things as sight and hearing...why can I not apply all of my senses - my senses of will, determination, aggression - too this tournement? I can. Simplicity works with me, and I work back - and we work well together. I sat on the bench, near the water - it was much different than that of one of my foes, left unnamed. "In his snow and icy weather - just as the unknowledgable characters in my story. Killing is a weapon, but not to kill, and keep in suffering is a will of mine - and you should try this my friend, but come our dance...you will...whether you agreed to or not... And I am outside, different from another foe unnamed - who'd rather hide in a darkened room, hiding in his own doubt - or perhaps he has as much will as I have... At least he has a sense of feeling or two. Or perhaps, I have none - and everyone else does - but if I had none, could I say such a thing?" And now I sat in doubt, lingering over my question. Too many questions in the world today - and could I win in the PTC tournement was just another one. Answers were my friend, and they have guided me through life. And I need an answer now...let's continue the story, minus the parts where I tell about how they look into each other's eyes - and see what really is behind them. Is it a friend? Or is it a coward or a selfish fiend? Maybe a hero or a heroine, someone who would give their own life...exciting. --- Air: why do we take it in, just too take it out? Because we need too. They were walking along the path, the path made by hikers many years before - or even before them. It went around the mountain, up the mountain, and beside the mountain; it was chiseled, and as long as it was used - it would never erode. They say mountains grow every year, and they do. The stakes get higher when you take that first step upon that unleveled ground - and once you get to where they have traveled - there is no turning back. They were losing breath, almost forgetting to breathe. "Short breaths, guys," Paul tried to say loudly - but it only came out normal (he's luck he got that projection). He looked back to see Gibson and Katherine nodding - and plodding upon the snowy path. He slowed, to let the two catch up - but you can't stop moving up here, your blood will drown you. "Kath, you alright?" Paul asked, trying to start up a conversation; it had been silent for a while, it seemed, except for the few reminders and mutters of anguish and fatigue. "I'm fine, I'm alright," she said. There were many things you could describe Katherine as: beautiful, smart, classy - but you'd never think tough - and that's exactly what she was deep down. Gibson patted the two on the back, and said: "we're going to make it, guys, I can feel it." They all looked at each other with a smile upon their individual face - all different - all the same feelings: happiness and hope, and perhaps a bit more - but unknown. Poor souls. But they fought - and that made them rich and pure - and they fought more - and that made them immortal. --- The sun was still out, and the water of the lake reflected into my crytal blue eyes a bit, but it was bearable. A tree stood beside me, not to far away - enough to keep valuable shade in my presence. And that was all I needed for a wonderful evening of story telling... "You see, Aidan, Kash: defeat can be a wonderful thing; it's nothing to be afraid of. If defeat were given to you, would you take it? Or would you decide to leave it and forget it? Travel on, without understanding why... Sometimes it's hard to do things such as this. It is for everyone, even for the likes of you two. I can admit I have tasted defeat, I have stared to it's eyes, it's varied expressions forever chaning infront of my own - and I did not notice that it had changed as many times as it had - for every loss is not the same - there's always something different to lose, and something to gain..." I sighed lightly, thinking of the story. Almost enough to break my heart, my heart I do let be seen - I can be of sensitivity and I can be of ruthless - but when I decide to travel to another level of each - I cannot be comprehended, and therefore, cannot be defeated - even if loss strikes my face with it's ungreatful force. "Let us continue on, let us see what happens in this match of survival...friend's sure, enemies never...but when your life is on the line - it's all you." I said with a smile, a bright and shiny smile. --- The fright in his eyes, it was horrific. They were bleeding tears, rupturing out and turning to frost upon his cheeks. Paul and Katherine held his arms tight, but they could not pull him up enough for him to climb up. He was dangling like a broken marionette, just waiting for the strings to be cut and it's show-career to be ended. He let out a silent scream, but Paul and Katerhine could hear it without a sound. They gribbed and clawed their friend, hanging onto his slipping arms for dear life...his life. He clawed back, hoping relieved from his position of tempting-death. "Lean down, and grab him by the pack-strap!" Katherine yelled to Paul He did. He leaned over a bit, holding onto Katherine's other arm. One wrong move, and all three would fall to certain death. He leaned carefully and grabbed the strap that was over Gibson's shoulder. He got it... "I've got it!" he yelled, an echo could be heard. He started to pull soft...and then hard. He pulled it to the top, and Gibson tried to lean his leg over the top of the cliff. He failed at his first attempt. But when something as valuable as your life is on the line, first attempt is nothing but a gateway to a second try, and he wasn't going to let many chances get away from him. He tried again, and Paul tried to grab his leg, but failed as he was holding the strap with his left...and tried to cross over, his reach was lessened greatly. Poor souls. --- "Can you guess what happened? I can tell you there was a great loss to their group, and you will know exactly what I am speaking of. Wonderful thing it is: foreshadowing. And so is suspense... Just wonderful tools." I spoke with a tone in my voice, it rang with melody, blending in with my environment.Such a treat, after such a cotastrophe...to some, I suppose. "I've never met you two before, nor have I seen you in person. I haven't studied tapes of you, I haven't asked about you. I saw your names, and that's all I know. I wonder where the forshadowing is in this story of ours? There doesn't seem to be any, my friends, and that is very odd. But the suspense, the suspense exists; it is all around us, or at least me - I cannot wait to see you both in the ring. Competition at it's best: beautiful. We play for the sport gentlemen, and I hope you do too. If we studied tapes, sure, it's using fair hinges, but could it not be considered cheating? It could. And I do consider it to be such. On we go..." --- Days passed - but the thought of Gibson did not. They argued, Katherine and Paul, whether to keep going and dedicate the trip too Gibson, or to turn back, and not risk anything more. But they were already more than half way...it was too late. Do not start something unless you plan to finish... And they kept going. Tears fell, yes; but they had been turned into frost upon their faces...their skimasks hiding their frosted faces. Silence was with them, and really their only hope now. Talking took energy, and that was something they did not have much of. Days passed. The nights got colder and longer. Oxygen was freelancing below them, but playing a swift game of hide-and-seek on level with them. ... They were almost to the summit, their place of pleasure and mark of marvelousity. Though they felt like they were turning a "cold" shoulder towards Gibson, him not being here. They thought they owed this too him. As soon as they were down, they would have proper reservations made. They were running out of food, and most importantly - water. Lucky enough Katherine found a way to start an effective flame (hard to do with the lack of oxygen) and melt some snow into the cantenes.They could go without food, but water they needed; if they had no water, they would be as their friend, but a much less grusome "discovery." They were far; one more day, and they would be to the summit by late evening. A well said statement, but harder to do than say. They had planned to camp two days, to gain rest before taking one final push forward. Disaster is in nature, it is natural - and there is no way to stop it. "Kathy," he called into her tent. But nothing came out, not a yawn nor whimper. He went in. She was dead and white as fresh ice. ... Much like before, he continued on - as they would want him too. He climbed and mustered all the strength he could to reach the summit. At a time of darkness, he reached the summit. A championship for himself? No. A championship for his friends. A championship for all of them. He put a flag in the ground, upon it were the sewed in initials of: G, for Gibson; K, for Katherine; and of course, P, for Paul. Paul died later that early morning. The same as Katherine, but different. He had reached the top. He had won...he had saw, he had lived the dream of them all. But he gave it all back to them, because that's who he was. And now they sit in Heaven, and tell stories of how beautiful their death really was. In a mysterious way, it made them think of death as a championship, to living life to the fullest, and giving all they had. --- "Championships are not won. They are earned." I said, the sun still out and the water still calm. The story had parts left out, but they were not as important as the others. "Aidan, Kash: this is not an easy journey, to the PTC Global title... I am not saying death is upon us, but I am looking for hardships, and for whomever succeeds, and continues on. It looks like we will end up with everything all the same, just one of us will get to see the summit. But I am sure we will hear stories about it..." I looked to the water a little closer now, as a breeze swept upon me. So simple. So simple... There was something shiny in that water, down in that abyss of lost items. It might have been a ray of hope and good fortune in disguise...it might have been a fishing lure. But with a bit of imagination...symbolism, foreshadowing, and hope can get me a long way.
