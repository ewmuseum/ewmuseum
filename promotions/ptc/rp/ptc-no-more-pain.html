No More Pain

6 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Little white saviors fall out in a row. One goes down and the world seems right. The chills warm up, the shakes calm down, and blurred vision returns to normal. 'No more pain.' A finger that was throbbing unbelievably returns to not being there at all, hacked off just after the first knuckle. Jack was told time and time again at the hospital that it would be smarter for them to just take the rest of it off, all the way to the knuckle, instead of just leaving this useless little nub for about half an inch. But he insisted, so they left it. I have to admit that the universe isn't without a sense of humor. An argument with the woman I loved led to a distraction of the mind which led to not paying attention...which led to me losing the finger that the wedding ring goes on. Part of me thinks that fate is trying to tell me something. But of course, that just shuts my ears more. With a choke, the horse pill goes down. A slight bulge in the throat, but it's washed away with a bit of precious water, just as the pill itself washes away years of regret. And then silence. Nothing. Jack, formerly known as Johnny Borealis, hero extraordinare, now stands in his shelter, away from the eyes of people, and looks himself over in a mirror. A head that once had stylish, vibrant hair was now shaved to stubble length, so that it wouldn't get in the way. His body, though somewhat in shape, wasn't toned to sleek refinement as it had been in the past, since working out got in the way. His face scarred, his hands bruised and blistered, his eyes tired, his teeth yellowed, his clothing torn, and his posture bent. All because they had all gotten in the way. Gotten in the way of survival. But then, nobody said that living in a post apocalyptic future would be easy. Terry's going to lead them right to their deaths... Jack's thoughts went to what the young ones, Keith and Toku had told him earlier. 'They're going to try to move against the incoming group of survivalists. And they're going to get completely slaughtered, and then the survivalists are going to move in here and decimate everything that's left.' Keith in many ways reminded Jack of himself from a better time. He was young, strong willed, and wanting to make a difference. A large, proud, warrior breed from the Iroquois clan, Keith's cultural roots seemed to tear out of his skin and break time itself. He was a statue to a great time before guns, bombs, and the power of the atom. Back when one man could, in fact, make a difference. Back when heroes could exist. And he always looked like he was ready to charge into battle and not take any prisoners, but rather overcome everyone in his path by a combination of bulging muscles and swift cunning. But the truth is that this isn't that time. There are guns, there are backstabbers, and there aren't any heroes any more because of them. Heroes died when man became exceptional at killing them. 'They're planning to attack the incoming group before they hit us. Terrence just gave them all this big bullshit speech, and me and Toku were wondering what you might be able to do about it. We have to be able to do something!' And then Jack launched into his speech about inevitability. Hopelessness. How one or two or a hundred people can't change anything anymore. And Keith stared on in disbelief, but Toku...Toku was different the entire time. She just stared. Not believing or disbelieving, but staring right through Jack Fitzwillis' being. Her eyes had torn him apart and she knew exactly what he was thinking, not what he said he was thinking. And now, at this moment, with him staring at himself in the mirror, Toku's eyes spoke more than any words could. He had just killed the last pills from his stash. One that he had been in bad shape until he stumbled across. One of the raids on a hospital to find supplies had led Jack to an entire room full of relief-giving little white angels. They took the distraction out of his life for long enough to formulate what he had to do. And a tired hand touched the part of the mirror that reflected Jack's facial hair. 'Getting gray. Guess maybe I'm getting old after all.' Withdrawing, his hand automatically went for a cigarette. It already seemed to be so much more stable now than it was before finishing off his painkillers, but the cancer stick would make it so much more calm. And right now he needed to be calm. 'I can't just let this entire group of people march to their deaths.' They're doing what they choose to do. So are you. 'They haven't chosen anything. Terrance is filling them with a false hope and dreams of getting out of the horror that is their real lives and they're buying into it.' Are you guilty of any less? Maybe you, in your leadership role, haven't led anyone to die on the field of battle. But you have led hundreds, maybe even thousands to sit here and rot in their own radiation poisoning or starvation. How many have died because you didn't think it was possible to move on? Maybe go to Canada or Mexico or- 'There is no Canada or Mexico! Atomic bombs do not stop at the borders of countries. They don't make their way to the checkpoint and halt because that's not where they intended to go! Canada and Mexico are just as large of hellzones as this place is. And I'm betting that few places in the world are much better!' You're afraid to even look. Two years ago you wouldn't have been afraid. 'Two years ago I wasn't leading a group of survivors. Two years ago it would've been me against the world. It wouldn't have been me being responsible for so many people.' So why are you responsible for them? Why do you and I, Jack and Johnny, have to sit here and be miserable when we could go out there and die trying? 'They need someone.' They have Terrance. 'Terrance is a madman. They have me.' But why? 'Because I can. Because I choose to, that's why.' Silence. It takes Jack a second to realize that the cigarette he just lit a moment ago is already gone. Smoked it before he realized it, and now it's just him standing there with no relief and no one to count on. A shaking hand goes for the bottle of pills and actually grabs it before Jack remembers that he's clean out of them. And then the hand with four fingers comes across and caresses the other arm. The one that has a large radiation burn on it. Acknowledging its existence seems to be enough to make Jack sick again. But his stomach and head only churn for a few seconds before it quiets to a dull roar. These people listen to Terrance because he gives them hope. What do you give them that they should listen to you instead? 'They're outnumbered and outgunned. They're going to get ripped apart and mowed down and I don't think I have to give them anything because nothing will change that.' Then do something that they'll respond to. One last time, Jack's head shoots up to the face in the mirror. The face of hopeless hopefulness. The one that had always wanted to be a hero, but always fell short. Now was his chance to be that hero. To do something seemingly selfless. But in reality, it was nothing short of the most selfish act known to man. All in the name of saving people. But all in actuality to be immortalized. 'You're right. Stupid son of a bitch, but you're right.' *** Safe. It's a word that Terrance Slauson knew very well. Sitting in his office, a veritable sanctuary inside the very strong shelter known as HOPE ONE, with his two highly trained and heavily armed bodyguards, and an entire legion of people beckoning to every order he gave, Terrance sat back in his black leather chair and sipped his gin and tonic and felt safe. After the blast, there were very few things that were left intact anywhere in the country. It took a lot of work and a lot of time, but his raider crews were able to find enough precious specimens of the world before to make his office feel...nice. Oak desk, leather chair, bookshelves (and books, of course), tiles for the floor, a chandelier, plants (by God, actually living plants!), a television that he took many pains to run precious electricity to, a DVD player so that he could watch DVD's of whatever he could find as there were no television shows anymore. This and that, various pieces from all over, it was almost as though life were normal again. He had stood atop a pillar, proclaiming hope to all the people but an hour ago. And now he was enjoying a nice gin and tonic and thinking about what he would do tomorrow. He never actually planned on fighting against the incoming people. There were, as was suspected, too many of them. They were brutally savage, having spent the last two years in a wasteland having to hunt and kill food in a world where even that was scarce. Sometimes what they hunted was people who might have known where food was. They also had weapons superior to the people of HOPE ONE. But Terrance knew this. And he also knew that among his limited choices, his best choice was to take his bodyguards, himself, his wife, and enough supplies to survive, and leave. A fully gassed-up, comfortable, conditioned SUV was ready to take him wherever they could go. To safety. And the door buzzed. 'Yes, Carol. What is-' 'Sir! It's Toku! She's gone mad and she-' The transmission cut short. Terrance's eyes immediately narrowed. That madwoman! Jack must have cut her loose, allowed her to act on her own accord. He must really be desperate. A quick motion to his bodyguards. 'Take care of her. Try not to kill her, as she may be of use to us later. But be careful. She's quite deadly, and don't underestimate her.' With a silent nod, the two bodyguards walked out the door, mirroring each other exactly. Terrance had found them years ago, long before the bombs dropped, on a trip to Korea. They were former bodyguards of Kim Jong Il, and they were experts in nearly everything deadly. Martial arts, weapons, guns, poisons, explosives, driving, flying, boating, and even taking a bullet for their leader. They were some of the finest specimens of man Terrance had ever known, and they were the only people he saw fit to protect him and his wife. What are their names again? I think the one on the right is Lee and the other one is, like...Hakura. Or something. Oh well. Doesn't matter. The doors shut behind the guards and he heard muffled talking for a couple of minutes. But no fighting. Hmm. Sounds like Toku has gotten more reasonable in the past couple of months. Finishing his gin and tonic, Terrance patiently waited for the sound of someone to lose their cool and all hell to break loose in his reception area. After a few minutes, however, he heard nothing. Just the sound of a door shutting. And then someone trying to open his door, but failing. Puzzled, Terrance listened closely. He heard...Hakura, or at least the guard he thought was Hakura, talking, and then the door was pulled on again. But to no avail. And the intercom buzzed again. 'What, Carol?' 'Sir, Toku has left. Leesig and Hakuso talked her down-' Leesig and Hakuso! That's their names. '- but now they say that the door won't open.' 'Hmm...must have locked by itself. Faulty, cheap wiring. One moment, I-' A moment of surprise. His remote door lock should be where his hand currently was. He should just have to push a button and unlock it and go back to being safe and secure in his own little palace. The door shouldn't have locked in the first place, as he had not told it to. If Terrance didn't tell something or someone to do something, then nothing should be done. But that was not the case at this point in time. And even before he heard Jack's voice, a chill of insecurity went up Terrance's spine. 'Looking for something, Terry?' A tired, smoke stained voice caused Terrance Slauson to swivel in his leather chair. He turned to see Jack Fitzwillis, his former co-leader, standing near the window with one hand in his pocket and the other holding a small gray box. Not just any small gray box, but the one that is used to remotely lock the door. But Terrance was one of the best poker players in the world. Especially now that seventy percent of the population was dead or dying. His face was cold as ice, unreadable. 'Johnathan. How are you?' 'Quite the lock you have on that door, Terry. Double plated metal doors. Covered in oak, as that is your favorite look. And three huge motherfucking deadbolts run through in three different places from one wall to the other, literally locking the door to the wall. I'd say there's no way that even two of Kim Jung Il's bodyguards would be able to get through that.' A slight smile came upon Terrance's face, and he gave a polite little golf clap. 'Toku distracts my guards. You lock the door. We have a heart to heart chat. See, this is the Johnathan that i liked being partners with. Does this mean that you'd like to mend our friendship?' But the smile on Jack's face showed the answer before he said it. He came over and sat on the opposite side of the desk from Terrance. In the background, almost unnoticable, the two bodyguards, Flippy and Skippy, were pounding on the door and uselessly looking for a way in. 'You're going to lead a lot of people to their death, Terry. A lot of people who don't need to die.' With closed eyes and a small smirk, Terrance nodded. 'Ah. Still convinced that I'm not the capable leader that so many think I am, Johnathan?' 'You were never a capable leader. You were a master bullshit artist.' Terrance's silence proved Jack right. 'You bullshitted me into thinking you were capable enough to lead people and put your personal interests behind. You bullshitted the people into not listening to me about your preachings of trying to 'lead life as normal,' I.E. retrieve treats and superfluous trinkets for you and others. You bullshitted them into thinking I was useless because I had only been giving them the bare essentials instead of luxuries. Meanwhile, now they have luxuries and are running low on bare essentials. And now you're going to bullshit them into charging headlong into a fight that you know they can't win. And you're going to take off and head someplace else.' 'Always the conspiracy nut, Johnathan. Gin? Perhaps some whiskey. I know how you love your whiskey.' Jack's mouth watered and a sudden headache came upon him. That would feel better than being with Kate one more time... 'I don't think so.' 'Suit yourself.' Turning his back to Jack, Terrance believed that he was cleverly hiding his actions in picking up the Darranger pistol. 'I should have liked to have been friends with you, Jack. You and i would have had some remarkable debates and discussions about philosophies...ideas...maybe even card games. Tell me, are you a fan of Texas Hold Em?' 'Five Card Stud.' 'You don't believe in riding the bet and drawing someone into a bluff?' 'I believe in playing with what you have. Not what you hope to have.' Shrug. 'To each his own.' Smoothly, Terrance turned around and aimed the pistol at Jack. The tiny gun only had one shot. But when you're dealing with a gun versus a man, you only need one. The reaction from Jack was one of expected surprise. He didn't come up here expecting Terrance to play fair. 'Whoa, buddy! What do you plan on doing with that?' 'Oh, this? You've got it all wrong, Jack.' 'That's the first time you've ever called me Jack. Feel a sudden surge of honesty?' 'Now listen, Jack...you've tried to mess up my business for a long time. Now, you quit a few months ago and started just doing whatever it is you do, and you've stayed out of my hair. Why couldn't you stay there?' Shrug from Jack now. 'Can't change who you really are, I guess.' In frustration, visible frustration, Terrance gulped some gin (no tonic), keeping the gun trained on Jack. 'I didn't want to do this. I never wanted it to come to this, Jack. But you've been working through Toku and that...that, Keith. The two of them have been out trying to ruin what I've been building here, and it's time to stop. It's time to stop all of it. And now you show up and you basically tell me that you're the one in charge of all of it. That you've been leading them to make me look bad. Well it's not going to work, Jack! I've got the people here under my thumb!' 'You honestly amaze me.' 'You were always so fucking self righteous. You preferred to be in the streets and preaching from a cardboard box! I showed them by example what a little ingenuity can get you! I used technology that we found to broadcast my messages on TV's and radios while you wasted your words person to person. And now look at you. This is the last time I'm going to offer this, Jack. God knows I've offered it plenty of times before. Help me to rally the people, Jack. Help me, instead of hindering me, to send them in a glorious blaze. Help me to survive this. I'll even take you with me, Jack. It will be just you, me, and my wife. With you around, I wouldn't even need my bodyguards.' '...I see.' 'You and I...we created something good here when we worked together. We can find others. Hell, we can come back and find the people that move into this place after all the people here are run over and killed! We can rebuild what we have and we can build up a better future.' His hand wavering. He was out of gin now, just like Jack was out of pills, and the two of them might as well have been naked, standing there with no comfort at all. And a gun keeping Jack from tearing Terrance's head off. 'I wrote a comic book once, Terry.' Jack stood up from the desk and walked out to the middle of the room. The afternoon sun splashing on him brightly. 'I wrote a comic book about a superhero who could do anything. He had the power of infinite luck...and the cosmic dice just kept ending up in his favor. Except this one issue...I had it so that he lost his powers. And so...to make the story short...he went to his enemy's office and gunned down the bodyguards. And then it was just him and his enemy.' 'Just the two adveraries. That's how you picture this, is it? Well unlike your comic, Jack, I have the gun. And if you're not going to work with me, then you'll find out that I have very little problems using it.' With a stone cold stance, Jack's eyes look out the window to the setting sun. Golden beams shoot through atmosphere, dust, radiation, death, decay, and hope to his eyes. And it seems like his whole life is culminating to this point as he takes a comfortable, last breath out and says the words. 'Do what you have to do, Terrance.' And without another word said, the trigger is pulled. *** It's indescribable, really. Getting shot. It's kind of like the rest of life. You can't really ever describe anything to anybody without saying that it's 'like' something that they have experienced. And if they haven't experienced it, then they have no idea. Being addicted to painkillers feels like you're addicted to painkillers. Being shot is being shot. That's it. And laying here, bleeding, there's nothing else I think about except maybe Terrance, standing over me. Almost looking sorry. 'It really was nothing personal, Jack. But I have to protect my own interests.' It takes everything that I have to answer. 'I understand. Don't you...Keith?' Terrance's face goes stark white. It takes him a moment to turn around and see him. Keith, standing in the window that Jack came in, holding a video camera with cables running out of it, watching the two men in the room. And immediately Terrance knows he's been had. 'You son of a-' Keith stands up to his full height, towering over Terrance. And with the only bullet in his pistol currently lodged inside of Jack, he decides to back down. And Keith's warrior voice booms through the room. 'This camera is hooked up to all the TV's that you use for your propaganda. There isn't a man or woman in all of HOPE ONE that hasn't heard your words.' Unable to turn any paler, Terrance falls to his knees and finds himself short of breath. He looks up pleadingly as Keith walks over to a dying Jack, takes the gray box, and presses a button that releases the door locks. After a moment, the door opens and Terrance's two bodyguards enter slowly. 'I think they heard the part where you talked about leaving with just your wife and Jack. Hey boys! What did you think about that?' Terrance's horrified face turns to the two bodyguards that come over silently and look down, sadly, at Jack's body on the ground, struggling to breathe. Each one grabs one of Terrance's arms and carry him out the door. 'No! NO! You can't! I'm-...I'm a hero! I'm a LEADER! Let me GO!' His screams die out as he's dragged down the hall, towards fate unknown. And Toku comes to the doorway and sees Keith and Jack. And she says nothing. Her eyes say it all. But Keith is different. 'You sure this was the best way, man?' It is with great effort that Jack reaches down to his shirt pocket. Slowly, painstakingly, he pulls out a cigarette and holds it to his lips. Realizing that he won't be able to light it, he looks up at Keith. 'It was the only way...to make them realize what he really...is.' 'You know, those things'll kill you.' Jack gets out two little surges of a laugh before he settles with a smile on his face and Keith lights the cigarette. 'It seems you were wrong, my friend. In this age of guns, there are heroes. They just need to be a new kind. They need to do things a little differently.' '...maybe.' Silence perforates the room. Not able to stand it anymore, and with tears in her eyes, Toku walks away. Jack never sees her again. 'It's...it's how she deals...with it.' 'I know, man. I know. What I don't know is...what are we going to do now? You are going to...you're going to die here. And Terrance will most likely be executed. You've saved us from him, my friend. But what about the nomads? What about everybody here?' That bullet was so much like the little white pill. The world stops spinning. My finger stopped itching. And everything makes sense. 'You...you have to live...each day at a time...Keith. Lead HOPE ONE. You...and Toku. What you do now...will reflect on every...one. Run. Welcome them. You must do...something.' Keith's hand takes Jack's, but there is no feeling. Only the motions. 'I will, my friend. I will get them through this.' Jack's eyes close. His job done. A hero's reward for a lifetime of work. Just like the pills. ...no more pain...
