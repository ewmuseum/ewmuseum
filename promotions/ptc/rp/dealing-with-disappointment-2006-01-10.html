Dealing with disappointment

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-10 How would you feel if you were 200 000 dollars richer? Alec Lancer felt pretty good about it. After he won his match against Aiden Jackson of GCW fame Alec Lancer was now officially a rich man of his own. Still, he was not as financially independent as he would like to be. According to his plan, however, he still had a few good years ahead of him before he needs to accomplish that achievement. The pressure was on for Alec Lancer. The gamble was of unimaginable importance to Alec Lancer, but fortunately everything went well. A few years ago he was even happy to get some work at all. Now he was a successful businessman and a fairly successful wrestler. Right now, it felt good to be Alec Lancer. The New Year had been bringing great things to Alec Lancer and Alec hoped it would continue to stay this way. In his mind this year would be his year. And who would disagree? On this rainy Sunday afternoon, Alec Lancer had no clue of what to do. After a bad night of sleep, he woke up early and ate his breakfast before he went to the sport club. That was almost two hours ago now. There was nothing shown on TV that could really grasp his interest. It was a typical Sunday for Alec. He would have watched foot ball, and maybe some comedy afterwards as well. Due to the programming, since it had changed on New Year’s day, there was no football and comedy anymore. So, he had no clue about what to do next. His opponent in round three deserved some research of course, but Alec couldn’t be bothered to go and look in dusty old archives for some books about German or Norwegian Gods. He already talked with his brother about God last time around and he didn’t leave with the answers he required. His brother acted strangely; somewhat reserved as if he knew that something terrible lay ahead. Alec Lancer had to put out all the stops to defeat Aiden Jackson, but he did it. Something was wrong about Alec’s feeling. He should be happy but he wasn’t. The anticipation for his match with Jorge Samuelsson wasn’t what it should be either. Alec Lancer’s state of mind was at the very least worrying. A phone call interrupted Alec’s train of thought. As he picked up the phone, he immediately regretted it. "Alec?" his brother asked. "It’s me, Thommy." Alec sighed. He had expected this phone call from his brother. Couldn’t the guy just leave him alone? The sickening interference from his brother was starting to piss him off real bad. "Yeah," Alec replied. "What’s going on now?" "Can I meet you today, face to face?" he asked on an urgent tone. "Yeah, sure," Alec agreed after giving it some thought. "I’ll be there over an hour," his brother promised. "Fine," Alec said as he cut off the conversation rudely. | ------------- | A Volkswagen turned into the gates of Alec’s house at high speed. It was his brother first visit in many years to this house. In fact, his brother hadn’t paid a visit to anyone the last year. He always claimed to do God’s work. Alec never liked the church or God. Alec didn’t visit his brother much either. As of late they had met several times. This was remarkable as Alec wasn’t much of a family person. Nonetheless, his brother could help him through the day. He had nothing else to do so he wondered why he couldn’t have a nice little chat with his brother. His brother finally stepped out of the door. Alec saw this and opened the door before there was knocked on the door. "Please, come in," Alec invited. "It’s nice here," his brother said in an attempt to sound friendly. Alec looked at his brother. His restless, quizzical eyes scanned Thommy’s face. "I know. Why have you come?" Thommy Lancer stared back at Alec. "I must confess you something." "Oh really?" Alec asked surprised. "You see," Thommy said, "I’ve done something terrible. I suppose you know about our agreement. I would bet on you so you could win those 200 000 dollars." "Yeah," Alec concurred. "So I went to the bookmakers," Thommy continued. "But I couldn’t place my bet on you. You hadn’t wrestled in a year before GTT5 came around. Father said you weren’t training hard either, so…" Alec narrowed his eyes. He had a particularly nasty feeling about this. "So… what are you basically saying to me?" "I haven’t placed the bet on you," his brother confessed, much easier than he had expected beforehand. He felt ashamed of this betrayal. "You haven’t got 200 000 dollars, Alec. You’ve got nothing at all." For a moment, Alec wanted to murder his brother. His increasing anger made him furious. His eyes were filled with hatred and he seemed to spit fire when he spoke. "You SCREWED me," he yelled to him. "You have the guts to come in and tell me that you have betrayed me for my father and tell me that I have lost it all." "Alec, calm down," his brother protested. "I know you’re angry…" "Angry?" his brother asked furiously. "I’m not angry." His brother knew he was lying, but denied it. "Please, listen to me. I know there is a voice of reason in you…" "Voice of reason? Yes, there is a voice of reason in me. And you know what it is telling me?" he asked. His brother nodded no. "It is telling me to separate your head from the rest of your body." "Alec, please, calm down," his brother said softly. He knew that Alec was going to freak out in a minute if he didn’t calm down. Alec didn’t reply at all. It seemed to Alec that the words came from the devil itself, but not his brother. Thommy had always been honest to him or so he thought. They didn’t see eye to eye all the time, but at least they were honest. No, he was honest to him. He couldn’t believe his ears. Thommy had betrayed him because their father commanded him. Why would his father do that? It sounded like a bad excuse to Alec. "Go," he commanded. "Leave now." And he meant it, every single world of it. The cold, heartless intonation of the words indicated that Alec had no faith at all in his brother anymore. His brother left the room, quietly. Alec knew what to do. The family betrayal came at a bad time, but he had saved some money. An old friend needed to return the favour. His former tag team partner, known to the wrestling fans as DJ Wallstreet, could help him out. After he earned that money back, he would get revenge on every single person that betrayed him. He didn’t care who they were. But first, he needed to contact DJ. He called his number. "Hey Dan," Alec greeted him. "It’s Alec here." "Hey Alec," DJ said. "I assume you still know you owe me some money," Alec said. "Could you bet that money on my match? Bet on me as the winner." Dan asked no questions, since he had known him for years. "No problem," he said. "Consider it done." Alec hung up and laughed. Even though he just found out he just lost 220 000 dollars he felt a burning fire within. It was a mix of anger, hate and passion. He wanted to win his upcoming match. And then, he would really get revenge.
