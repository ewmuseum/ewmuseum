On The Run Pt 1

7 January 2006

Date: 2006-01-07 "Of course it hurts you moron, I was shot." "I wish you wouldn’t…" "Shut up, it’s not important. Right now the only thing that matters is finding Mike. I’m not about to let all my hard work go down the drain. " "But how are we supposed to find him?" "Don’t worry, I think he’ll be easy enough to track down, I’ll find him." **************************************************************************** He was getting to that point. He could feel his body beginning it’s slow revolt. Too much pain, too much adrenaline, too much running. Hungry, tired, cold, he couldn’t remember the last time he felt anything other than misery. Yet, at least he was free, that was the one thing that gave him a shred of hope. At least he’d found some clothes to keep him warm. All he could remember was the gun going off, and running until he passed out. Upon waking he’d discovered he was nearly naked, and their was snow on the ground, he should probably be dead, but more and more he was realizing that death wasn’t going to come for him any time soon, things would never be that easy. Rummaging through some nearby dumpsters he’d come up with clothing, layer upon layer of old ragged shirts, even a winter coat. By the time it was all said and done he was indistinguishable from the beggars on the street corners. And maybe that’s exactly what he was to become. It was times like this he began to damn himself for never taking the time to remember the small details, the phone numbers. Everything had been in his cell phone, and now without it, he had no one to turn to. Briefly he’d considered going to the police, but had quickly discarded the idea. Talking to the cops would only lead them back to the warehouse, to the dead body. To the man he’d killed. And there it was, like a thunderbolt sent from the heavens. He was a murderer, and there was no getting around it this time. At least when he’d beaten Lance he could blame other factors, the drugs in the kid’s system the blackout rage. Now there was nothing, in cold blood he had shot a man, sure he could argue self defense, but he didn’t want to chance it. So he walked, stumbling blindly from street to street, corner to corner. He’d slept in doorways, alleys, wherever he could lay his head for a few hours at a time. Food found in garbage bins kept him alive, that was his only mission now, he’s only purpose for being, survival. It was the only thing he could do after all, he’d killed to live himself, that meant he couldn’t very well give up now. Some part of him knew that he couldn’t do it forever, that eventually his body would give out. Already he was beginning to see and feel the effects of the last few weeks. His always lean frame was growing leaner, his body eating away at muscle tissue just to keep going, and now it was starting to get worse. His legs shook so violently that he could barely stand, only put one foot in front of the other, again, and again, in an endless cycle. He wanted to sit, but wasn’t sure that he’d be able to get back up. He had to rest, had to eat, soon. Resting his head against a grimy wall he thought back, how many times he’d seen them on the streets. Filthy, urchins he’d thought, bottom dwellers all drunks or junkies. Always with their hands outstretched, begging for spare change. At worst he’d sneer and demand they get a job, clean themselves up and do something with their lives, at best he’d simply ignored them. How many others must have done the same thing? When you live in a city, you get used to the vagrants they’re just part of the scenery, something you see so often you fail to notice any longer. You walk a little faster maybe, become particularly interested in something you see in the window of a shop. Maybe you go in, hoping they won’t still be out there when you come out. It’s just something you do, because you can’t possibly understand what it means to be in that situation. The old adage about never knowing a man until you’ve walked a mile in his shoes personified. Despite the situation he had to chuckle. The irony on the whole situation, gone from one of the most recognized faces in the world, the rich and powerful elite, to just another piece of the city, all in less than a month. At the lowest point in his drinking he’d wanted less to die, more to simply disappear, become invisible so he wouldn’t have to deal with the accusing eyes of humanity. Be careful what you wish for. Now all he needed was someone to extend the hand to him, offer him a little help. Spare change for a cup of coffee and a warm meal. The thought of food made his stomach ache, it’d stopped growling days about. It was almost as if his body didn’t even have the strength to produce sounds any longer. Just another sign of the slow revolt. Another system shut down as the soul prepared to vacate. Staring down at the ground, he saw it slowly rise to meet him. A strange sensation light floating and falling at the same time, as his knees slowly began to give. He’d stopped too long, his rest was going to turn out to be the end of him. He sank down onto the snowy ground, involuntarily curling up inside his multilayered clothing. It was time to go to sleep, he was so tired, he wanted to sleep forever. And if there really was a god he’d let it happen this time. Hadn’t he suffered enough already. Pulling his eyes off the ground he stared into the city that surrounded him. Ducked into an alley, curled up and ready to sleep he could watch them pass. All the people busy on their little errands, so oblivious to the things going on around them. He thought that if maybe he were one of them in this moment, and it was another poor lost soul about to die, he’d have known, and done something. Of course that was a lie and he knew it, he wouldn’t have noticed, probably wouldn’t have cared. Just another wasted life, just another casualty of life. For the first time he really looked at the world around him, knowing it would be his final few images of the world. He saw the lights blinking with cheer, he’d almost forgotten, it must be nearing Christmas. These people weren’t just in a rush, weren’t just wrapped up with the normal routine of day to day life, it was the time of peace on earth, good will towards men. Of course they’d be at their most self absorbed, god knows he usually was this time of year. There wouldn’t be any help, he’d given a valiant effort, but now he knew it was time to stop. He’d fought and he’d scraped, but he just couldn’t to do it. There wasn’t anything left in him to stand, much less keep walking. He was done, it was time to finally accept that, and move on. Silently he prayed, and for the first time not for himself. He didn’t pray for salvation. He didn’t pray for forgiveness, he was already damned. He prayed for them, the other lost souls, the one’s he’d ignored. He prayed for the passers by, prayed they’d open their eyes and their hearts to the streets just once. He prayed they’d notice the suffering and put a stop to it, something he never would have. But most of all he prayed for Sheena, and Alex, Mom and Dad, Cindy and her husband with the baby on the way. He wanted them to be happy and safe, and it would be better for them this way anyway. With him done and gone he couldn’t hurt them anymore, couldn’t cause any more suffering. He just wanted them to be happy. Eye lids fell, and the world grew dark, yet somewhere in the distance he saw it. A single point of light, at the end of the long dark tunnel. He couldn’t stand but he knew he moved towards it. He walked without ever having his feet touch the ground. There was warmth in that light, comfort and security. Finally he’d find some peace if only for a few seconds before he was damned for the rest of eternity. That light held the one thing he honestly and truly wanted, oblivion. It grew closer, getting brighter and brighter. He stared into it’s center, yet it never burned his eyes. Soon he could rest, soon he’d have release. For a second he allowed himself to hope that maybe he’d be judged worthy, but he didn’t count on it. Still, either way, at least he wouldn’t run anymore, at least he wouldn’t hear the gunshot to wake him up every time he slept. He was almost out, he could reach out now, his finger tips brushed against it’s core. It felt like a cloud must feel, the stuff of dreams. Everything relaxed, this was it, no more pain, no more suffering. It was just one more step now, and it’d all be over. And without question he knew now, all was forgiven, he was going home. There would be rest. All was light, all was perfect. He could sleep now, forever, until eternities crumbled to dust. Once he’d stepped inside the light faded, growing darker with every second, as ever body part relaxed. Pulling in one long last deep breathe as the last rays faded away he knew it was over… "Mister?…Oh my god MISTER!" "No….NOOOOO" He felt the hand shaking him away, as the light began to grow once again. He was being jerked back, like a safety rope tied around his waist pulling his back to reality. Reaching out he tried to catch hold of the light, begging to be allowed to stay. He heard himself moaning no, tried to tell the hands shaking him that he wanted to stay. He was finally warm, didn’t want to be cold anymore. "Come on Mister, you’ve got to get up, it’s important, come on. You’ll die if you stay here." Slowly his eyes blinked open, and he found himself face to face with a small child. The kid couldn’t have been more than ten, if he was a day over seven. He was all dirty cheeks and small grubbing hands, pawing at Stevens, desperately trying to wake him up. Michael wished he could have seen innocence in those small eyes, but they’d already seen too, instead of a light there was only a grim certainty that the kid would see another person die if he didn’t do something. It was a scary kind of grim determination that filled the child, and that was just enough to jerk Michael back into reality fully. Groaning he pushed himself up into a more seated position, waving the kid who tried to help him rise away. He should have known it wasn’t going to be as easy as all of that. He still had way too much to pay for before he’d ever find his rest. Once his eyes completely focused he took a better look at the kid, he was scruffy of that there was no question, but he had the rosy glow of someone not well fed perhaps, but at least fed enough to get by. He didn’t look starved anyway, not like some of the other gaunt faces he’d come across in his wanderings. Those were the worst, the ones in his old life he would have done his best to not see. The kid for his part simply stared back, before finally extending his hand. An adult gesture that seemed strange coming from the child before him. But another look into the kids eyes told stories of experiences Stevens couldn’t even begin to imagine. The kid was obviously old beyond his years, probably homeless since birth. "I’m Ben, who are you Mister?" He almost snorted with laughter at the question. At least some things couldn’t be torn away by the streets, the bluntness of youth. The kid had probably saved him from dying, and all he cared about was knowing who Stevens was. Taking the child’s small hand in his own, he shook it carefully. "Michael." "Hi Michael, say you know you can’t sleep here, you’ll catch your death of cold." The mention of death stopped any thoughts of chuckling cold. He couldn’t very well tell the kid that he would’ve been happy to have died here. Already the memory of the peace he’d found within the light was fading, sadly he knew that in an hour he’d wouldn’t remember anything about it. It would just be the vaguest memory of having once been some place nice. Similar to the way he looked at his old life, before his "Angel" had come. " I suppose so, but I don’t have any other place to sleep." It was the most diplomatic response he could come up with, surely he could say more but he didn’t want to get the kid too excited about anything. Although his words did seem to perk little Ben up. "Of course you do Mister, but follow me, and I’ll take you there, it’s were I sleep every night." Probably another alley, maybe one that had the burning trash cans for heat, like in the movies. At least it’d be something better than this he supposed. The only problem would be trying to get back up, although he felt like maybe he could do it if he really pushed himself. It’d just be another few steps, no real turning back now. He’d find out where they were going first though, he was too tired to walk far. "Where?" After he asked it occurred to him that he shouldn’t be in this situation. Part of him wanted to tell the kid that he shouldn’t be talking to strangers, that he should run away and find his parents, not be offering some stranger a place to sleep. There was something that wasn’t right about the situation, but he supposed it was just another part of the street life he didn’t understand. Right now he was one of the urchins, and they had to stick together to survive. "Not far, and you can get something to eat too. Follow me." Again it just didn’t seem right there was something more wrong that just the situation, but he couldn’t place exactly what it was. However the mention of food, the promise the journey wouldn’t be too long, was enough to intrigue him. Forcing himself to stand, he placed one hand against the wall for support. Inwardly he winced as his body shrieked with agony, every joint and muscle desperate for rest. "Lead the way kid." That was all the prompting Ben needed as he turned and dashed out of the alley. He stopped, and looked over his shoulder, to see Stevens following behind at a much slower pace. Quickly the kid jogged back so full of energy. He tugged at the ragged ends of one of the coats Michael wore. "Come on. It’s cold out here, I want to get inside." Letting go of the cloth the kid ran forward again, and repeated the process. Like a small dog running forward to scout and returning to it’s master’s side, so did Ben lead the way. What Michael found at the end of their little journey was something he couldn’t have possibly bargained for. All he wanted was food and a warm sleep, what he got, was Caroline. ~To Be Continued~
