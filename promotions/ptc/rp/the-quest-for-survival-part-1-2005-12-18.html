The quest for survival (part 1)

18 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-18 Prologue Patience An envelope fell on the desk with a smack, causing it to flip over to its other side. A hand reached towards the letter and picked it up. The yellow coloured paper was taken out of the envelope in a matter of seconds, as if the letter was being waited for with great impatience. After a few seconds, certainly no more, the paper was put back in the envelope again. Closed and sealed with a new mark, the envelope was sent away again, to a new destination. A tall, sun-tanned man put the envelope in the inside pocket of his brown leather jacket. And then, he walked out of the room. The other man remained in the room. His desk was a complete mess; piles of paper next and up each other. The rest of the room wasn't much better. It was quite clear this room served as his office. Complete closets full of books, papers and notes. All were covered with a nice bottom of dust. As if it was an archive of someone whose life had been documented by him self. Diaries, photo albums and newspapers were all ordered on date and place. The man looked awkward. He hadn't shaved himself for a few days, four at the most as you could clearly see fairly big bristles. His hair was brown and lank. It reached his shoulders with the end curling up a bit. Roughly reaching six feet in height, his appearance was quite frightening. His teeth were covered with the rests of food and the air escaping his mouth was breath-taking. The brown eyes usually came across as if they were able to look inside you, within your soul. Today, his eyes were soft with sleep. The deep bags confirmed his desire for sleep. His strange, tired appearance made it hard to determine his age. Especially since he had this pale skin which made it even more obvious he wasn't in the best of shape. As his hand picked up the phone, his other hand already dialed the number. The phone went over twice before a connection was established. "Hello," grumbled a man. "Are you ready?" A bark which should go through for a laugh could be heard. A short silence fell in the conversation, before the man impatiently continued. "Are you ready or not?" he asked, this time with more urge for an answer in his voice. Another short moment of silence fell. "Of course I am," the man on the other side reacted confidently. "Good," he proclaimed as if that was all he needed to hear. "You will receive all the information you need soon." "I want it in, tonight," the man demanded on a tone that left no room for questions. "And it will be," he promised. "I take it for granted that you will hold up your end of the deal as well." "Of course." "100 percent guaranteed," the man continued his quest for more promises. "Of course." "The deal will be proceeding as agreed," he said. "Of course," he repeated subconsciously. "Very well then," were his last words before he hung up with a slam. A minute later he lit up a cigarette while he mauled over the events of the day.These events were food for thought indeed, but not for the time. As he took another puff, he came more at peace. Tomorrow would be another day. Chapter one Hope As the sun shone in his eyes, the man walked across the street which had been mostly abandoned for the time of the day. But it was Sunday, so no shops were open nor did people go to work. The day God intended to be the day for rest and worshipping had no meaning to this man. As one of the rare few non-believers in Breda, one of the bigger cities in Holland and (most likely) the self-proclaimed "Pride of the South", he was considered an outsider and as such treated. The large church could be seen from all sides, even miles away, since there was nothing to prevent the sight. He walked into the church, which was pretty much empty as well, apart from a few priests. One of them walked up to him and greeted him. Patiently, the priest awaited the response. "Where's my brother?" he snarled at the priest. Not even bothering to wait for the response he immediately walked to him, leaving the priest behind in disbelief. "Hello Alec," the man welcomed him kindly. The contrast between the brothers was huge. Alec Lancer was often described as cold and heartless whereas people thought the opposite of his brother whom was known as a very friendly person. Some even wondered if they really were brothers. Alec Lancer sighed before he replied. "Hello. How are you? I'm fine, how's life treating you? Not too bad. Nice to hear that, what have you been up to lately? Preaching and bidding mostly, but I managed to watch a game of soccer as well," he said sarcastically. He never had much patience and even less today. "Let's cut to the chase, you know why I am here." "You haven't changed one bit, I see," he remarked. "And yes, I know why you have come, but a man is still allowed to hope, isn't he?" he asked rhetorically. "You can dream all you want," Alec said bluntly. "You've been doing that since the day you were born and I've given up all hope you will stop with that. It's bad enough as it is." Alec never liked the fact his brother became a priest and almost hated him for it. "God is no dream, Alec. He's real and He's everywhere." As the everlasting discussion was about to be sparked to new life again, Alec stared at his brother. "Believe all you want, we've been through this shit before. Now tell me, and you should know, why is God important to people?" "God is first and foremost the personification of hope," he began. "To many people God gives hope. But God also tells us how to live, how we should help each other and learn to share." "Great," Alec interrupted seriously. "All I want to know is why my opponent has such a belief in God when God determined he had to go to jail." "God is the light in the darkness, Alec. God gives him peace," his brother stated calmly. "God certainly gave his parents peace," Alec Lancer referred to the death of Aiden Jackson's parents. "Death comes to us all," the other Lancer said. "Even you, Alec, will die some day." "Can't wait to see hell in person," Alec announced sarcastically. He didn't believe in God at all, to the grief of his brother. "So, what do I need to say to really upset this guy? Saying he is a sinner?" "I wouldn't know, Alec," he said. "You don't?" Alec asked surprised. "I might have a few suggestions then. Should I say I fuck with a condom? Should I say I never thank God before dinner? Well, should I?" The most disturbing thing is that he really meant what he said. "I don't think it will hurt him at all," his brother stated. "And I think you know that as well. If I were you, I would train hard and hope you're the better wrestler." "I'm a bit disappointed," Alec confessed. In fact, he was deeply disappointed, but somewhere deep inside of him he found the intelligence to say something diplomatic. His brother's opinion was something that mattered a little to him, as he had known him for years. Although his brother was his opposite in many ways, Alec felt himself somewhat obliged to look after his brother a little. Albeit the main motivation for that was the heritage. Only through his brother he could obtain the other half. "You are expecting too much from me," his brother replied. "I think you are expecting too much of Aiden Jackson's belief in God. If he's such a believer as you claim he is, he would never have been in jail. However, God might be able to forgive him." Alec Lancer smiled, and now the resemblance between him and his brother was there for everyone to see. "Very well then, priest," he spoke as he turned his back to his brother. As he started walking, he added "You will pray for me, won't you?" Ventilating all his anger and disappointment with a slam to close the door, Alec Lancer grabbed a piece of paper out of his pocket. It was yellow coloured. Then he also grabbed a pen out of his pocket. He scrapped something from his list, but what could not be seen. "Brothers are of no use either," he whispered to himself. Chapter Two Dignity Alec Lancer rose ponderously out of his comfortable chair. Seemingly unmoved, he walked towards the refrigerator. He opened it to get his guest another beer. As he handed over the beer, his guest had expressions of concern on his face. After having some thought about it, he decided to tell Alec about it. "I don't want to sound rude, but why are you giving me this beer?" Alec put a smile on his face. "So you have an excuse," Alec Lancer said nonchalantly. His guest blinked his eyes a few times, before being able to mutter out an answer. "Excuse?" he asked. "Excuse," Alec stated firmly. "For the shit that comes out of your mouth." In one moment, the complete atmosphere changed. In what started as a nice and pleasant conversation seemed to turn into a war of words. "Is this a joke?" he asked. "No, you are," Alec said. "Where is the information I asked for?" "Is that what it's all about?" "Just tell me what I want to know," Alec demanded. A yellow piece of paper was given to Alec. Alec looked at it and nodded. "Youâ€™re can't have arranged this yourself, so who did this for you?" "I don't know his name. He had brown hair, and looked very tired. That's all I needed to know," the messenger told Alec. "I've been driving all night to get here. You know how far I live from here. It's all I know." "Right," Alec said. "I believe you." Alec Lancer narrowed his view to the man's face, looking if he could detect any sign of a lie. Although he didn't find anything, he still distrusted the man as if he knew him somehow. It was just a feeling, nothing more. "Great," the man replied. "Now I must leave you." "You know where the door is," Alec said in return. "Go." The man frowned at Alec for his lack of manners, but Alec could care less. He had more important things to do. Although things were going as planned, Alec still detected himself on having a wrong feeling about all of this. As if he was on the verge of being betrayed. Of course, all of his employees had taken vows and signed contracts, but true loyalty can only be obtained through fear, Alec had been able to figure out during the years. Alec sighed. He knew he wouldn't be able to justify the feeling he had. He decided to go to bed, before deciding what to do next. Chapter 3 Surprise And there he stood with a suitcase in hand, awaiting the arrival of his guest. A meeting had been set up a few hours prior and Alec was slightly nervous about it. His heart told him to back out, but his mind told him to continue. A limousine was pulled up in front of him, and two hands pulled him into the car. "Hello Alec," greeted the man. Alec sighed, because he knew who was in this car. "Was this really necessary?" he asked irritated. "Of course it was," Alec was assured. "I thought you were the one so fond of safety of our well thought out plan?" "I still am," Alec said. "Now, tell me, DJ, has the deal been done?" The man opposite of him smiled. He gave Alec the same, yellow-coloured piece of paper. As Alec read the information on the paper, his excitement grew more and more. "So… so…," Alec mumbled, "so the odds that I win are truly eight to one? This is way beyond our wildest dreams." "Your father expected you to be as excited as you are now. He's willing to help you out on one condition," DJ told him. "You must be thoroughly prepared for this match." "What's that for nonsense?" Alec questioned the decision of his father. His father had been his example, his teacher and his mentor. The feeling he had about betrayal grew stronger. "I've been training a lot and you know it. Is this some kind of joke?" The answer was unexpected. "No, he's very serious about this. He expects you to do well, but feels you're not up to the task of Aiden Jackson just yet." Alec's doubt made place for anger. "I want to speak to my father," he notified DJ. "You can't..," DJ started to protest, but Alec interrupted him. "Since when do you decide what I do? You must know your place, DJ, as we all must. I want to speak my father. So what are we going to do?" "We're going to speak with your father," DJ answered reluctantly. "No, I am going to speak with my father while you are going to continue with our plan," Alec instructed DJ. DJ clenched his teeth. On the way through his father they exchanged no words. Alec stared out of the window the whole journey. He had no idea where this tournament was taking him. His father always said to have faith in his son. Alec was always allowed to do things the way he thought was the best idea. For the first time ever, he was being told to obey. Chapter 4 Confrontation Alec slammed the door open. His father turned around and raised an eyebrow. People described him to be as cold as ice. Their eyes met one another's. Alec's eyes were filled with pure anger while his father's contained no emotion. It was Alec who started the conversation. "I've heard you wanted me to do some additional training," Alec hissed, his voice full of anger. "I must confess I am disappointed. You could have told me this yourself instead of shame me in front of half the world." His father sat down upon his chair. "Alec, my son, I can see that your emotions once again got the best of you." "I would rather say that a nasty surprise got the better of me," Alec disagreed. His father put a smile on his face which was characteristic for him. "You must be able to deal with surprises. You have put your own health at risk to compete in a tournament filled with extremely good fighters. You can put up a fairly good fight, but this is GTT5. The best of the best. You're my son, so I know you have taken a look at the prizes." "I know I'm not the best wrestler," Alec started to protest. His father chuckled. "No, you're not," he agreed. "But is a good way to make a lot of money," Alec continued to form his response to his father's claims. "I realise as much as you do that I am not the best of wrestler. But I should be able to win at least one match." "What's your goal?" he asked. "I want to win this match and a shit load of money," Alec said. "How are you going to do that against an opponent who is the far superior wrestler?" he asked as he questioned Alec's answer. Alec answered this question smiling. "I am going to play to his weaknesses," he said cockily. "Ah yes, I almost forgot to compliment you," his father giggled. "It was a good idea to speak with your brother about God, but you shouldn't have expected a strategy from him aside from praying to God." "I could do the exact opposite," Alec replied. "Like Angelo Deville and Doctor Curiosity?" his father frowned. Alec nodded. "It seems to be the only way to win this tournament." "No, it isn't," his father corrected him. "You must know Alec that it isn't your objection to win the whole tournament." "All I need is this match," Alec agreed. "Ka-ching." "Then do what I am telling you," his father said. He handed over a piece of paper. "Go to the address on the paper. There lives someone there, who can help you out with your problems." Alec put the piece of paper in his back pocket. "Very well then," he promised. "This isn't about winning, Alec," his father assured him. Now Alec was really caught off guard. "It isn't?" "No, it's about surviving" Epilogue Three people were standing next to each other. A man dressed in a long black coat, covering his body all the way the neck to his feet. His face was scared, etched with memories from the past for each scar contained a memory. Memories descending from both recent and ancient times. His eyes were glooming yellow as if they were useless. His right hand had no flesh around it. Mere bones were all that was left for that hand. The other man couldn't be really called a man in the literally sense of the word. His unnatural appearance would have caused any man to piss his pants. All he still had left from his body was his skull, a remnant from his rich past. The girl, the smallest of the three, looked the most normal. She wouldn't be considered to be extremely pretty, but she came across as your average day neighbourhood girl except for that freaky glance on her eyes that resembled madness. "Let's prepare for the coming of our guest," hissed the burned one. The other two nodded in agreement as the threesome left, apparently preparing for the arrival of a guest.
