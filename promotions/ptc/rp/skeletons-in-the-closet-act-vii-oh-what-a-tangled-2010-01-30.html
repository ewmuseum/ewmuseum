Date: 2010-01-30 The Right Thing The shopping cart faded to the left, rumbling over the asphalt as the shorter of the two women pushed it towards a small, mom-and-pop grocery store. In all the oddity of their lives, all the strange relationships and nigh-impossible activities they were dragged into on a nightly basis, it was little wonder Alexandra Pierce and Amy Campbell took some time out of their day to enjoy the little things, like shopping at the grocery store. Of course, these days, all they had was time. It had been just seventeen days since Pierce, the woman professional wrestling fans called "Desade", was ousted from the Order of St. Julian, the society that made her into the woman she was. Alex was living in exile, and her preparations for the finals of the GTT tournament bordered on the masochistic. She spent close to ten hours a day in the gymnasium, engaged in a brutal regimen of cardio, sparring, and more, and then she went home to spend another two studying videotape the same way she used to study people. Her sessions had grown more and more intense, to the point that she was having a difficulty finding people willing to take a kick, even at upwards of five hundred dollars a pop. Even then, as they pushed into Keller Market, Pierce's mind whirred through the possibilities of the four-way round robin, and her lover didn't have to look down to know she was measuring steps. "If you karate kick the shelves, I think they'll be kinda pissed," Amy Campbell said. The little redhead never lifted her head to look at the other woman – after some fifteen months together, the pair just seemed to know each other. "I wasn't planning to karate kick anything," Pierce said defensively. But she blew out a sigh, running her fingers through her dried-blood red hair. "Okay, maybe I'd like to see how high I could reach. That's strange, right?" Amy pushed the cart through the door, lifting her big, bug-eyed sunglasses up into a mess of neon, stoplight-red hair to share her grin with Mr. Kwan, the kindly Korean man who owned the place. "A little?" she asked teasingly. "You'll be fine. I keep telling you—" Though Alex had discarded the white pantsuit she wore for most of her wrestling career, Sin City Championship Wrestling's "Spider in the Web" still cut an impressive figure in a just pair of gray skinny jeans and a green button-down blouse, and none of the store's patrons stopped to greet them. "I know you think I'll be fine," Pierce whispered, laying her hand on Amy's as they turned down the bakery aisle. "But this is the biggest thing that's happened in my career in almost a decade, and I don't want to choke." "Still a bit of a sour note, what with me choking and not being a part of the tournament anymore," Campbell grumbled. "You just need to relax. You're pouring yourself too far into this – don't think I didn't notice your shoulder is sore, and I know that's not my fault." "No, but this crick in my neck is." Pierce grinned as she stepped behind the other woman – she'd been smiling a lot more lately, since her obligations to the Order of St. Julian ended. Alex was an affectionate, touchy woman, and her hand skimmed across the small of Amy's back as she reached up to snag a loaf of Wonder Bread from the shelf. "It's fine," the taller redhead continued. "It's keeping me busy enough not to... miss it." Her eyes dropped. "I know you talked to Jonas Stryker, and I know what he probably said, but... I am happy, Amy." By then, Campbell was at the end of the aisle, where the boxes of Little Debbie snack cakes taunted her. "Until after this tournament is over and you realize that daytime television isn't all it's cracked up to be." She looked back over her shoulder with a small smile. "I just don't want you to come back from New York and think you've made a big mistake." "I haven't made a mistake." Pierce slipped soundlessly behind her. "I get to spend time with my daughter, and with you. It's still a busy life, but I don't have to do bad things to keep myself entertained." "I'm not sure everything you do to me could be classified as 'good'," Amy chuckled. "Honestly, though – I don't want to see you pushing yourself too far in either direction." Alex pressed her lips to the top of Campbell's head. "What did he say?" she murmured. "He didn't say anything!" Amy Campbell was a lot of things – perhaps the finest all-around talent in all of SCCW, a veritable rock star in the world of wrestling – but she was certainly not a good liar. "Anyway, come on..." She slipped out of Pierce's grip, stepping down the aisle. "You have the shopping list, and I want to be home for TMZ. What were those things called that Quinn wanted? Uncrusta—" Amy turned over her shoulder, but Alex hadn't moved from the snack cakes. The Spider's lips were thin, her eyes downcast. "What did Jonas say, Amy?" Jonas Stryker was the man that Alexandra Pierce let escape. Formerly one of Pierce's top operatives, the Spider cut him loose from this obligations, and, despite that, Jonas had still been one of Desade's most determined foes. It wasn't a relationship Amy understood – or really one that she wanted to understand, even if the self-styled "Invisible Man" had been of great help recently. "You'll... he said something I believed. That you'll never be truly satisfied as just a wrestler, or just a homemaker, or 'just' anything." Alex crossed the distance between them, but Amy wouldn't look the taller woman in the eyes – not until Pierce gently coaxed her, two fingers turning Campbell's mismatched blue and brown eyes upward. "You believe that?" Alex whispered. "Is that what you're worried about? You think I'll find some reason to backslide, to... to be her again?" Perhaps naively, the Spider considered "Desade" -- the dark, vindictive, manipulative woman she'd been before opening her heart to Campbell -- almost a different woman. "I believe that even I wouldn't be happy as a wrestler and nothing more." Amy squirmed under Pierce's touch. "Can we... y'know... not in public? People are looking at us like maniacs already." The taller redhead glanced through the sparse crowd. "Let them look," she said. "I'm not ashamed." Alex cinched her hands at Amy's waist, dipping her head to press her lips to the smaller woman's, a quiet kiss that quickly risked becoming more. "Alex... Alex, no..." Amy shoved the Spider's lithe shoulders gently, separating them with a sharp intake of air. "Can we... can we just get some groceries and talk about this at home?" "Okay." Pierce managed a smile, sliding her hand down Campbell's arm. "I realize I am a grown woman when I ask this, but... is it okay if we hold hands?" "Sometimes, you're beyond adorable." Amy took the other woman's hand, and -- though it made pushing the shopping cart difficult -- neither of them let go until they got to the checkout register. The relationship these two women shared was often strained, sometimes strenuous, and downright strange. But for a short time, they were like teenagers, laughing, joking, and sharing stories. None of the anxiety was present in that half-hour they spent in Keller Market, and none of the outside forces that always seemed to be pulling them apart could touch them. "I've got this one," Amy said as they unloaded their haul onto the counter. "You sure?" Pierce glanced outside to the parking lot. "I mean, you get everything, and—" "And you're letting me stay with you rent-free." Amy's smile was a dazzling thing when it came out for real. "Well..." Alex drifted to Amy's shoulder as the cashier began scanning boxes of kids' cereal, Rice Krispie Treats, and ramen noodles. "You provide other services in trade." Her whisper was into the smaller woman's hair, but Campbell's cheeks quickly matched the shade of her hair as she fumbled through her small bag for her debit card. "Alex!" Amy giggled. "Stop it!" "Make me." The cashier, an elderly lady named Bea, her hair tied back by a leather strap, offered the pair a small smile as she rang them up. Amy spent the time in the past flipping aimlessly through the Enquirer and US Weekly. "How long have you two been together?" the cashier asked. "Uh... almost a year and a half now." Campbell glanced up, finding Pierce by the door. They shared a smile as the taller woman came back to her side. "It was October, right? When we got together?" "October 15th." Alex said automatically, reaching up to pluck Amy's bug-eyed sunglasses from her hair. "Hey!" The smaller redhead turned, smacking Pierce in the arm. "Those are my favorite glasses! Quit acting like you're two." "I'll bring them back, promise." The Spider hooked a thumb behind her. "I'm just going to go wait in the car. I'm sure Bea will get someone to help you if you need it." "Al... Alex?" Amy turned to follow, but Bea's interjection jolted her back to reality. "That'll be $98.14," she said. "Debit or credit?" Campbell rushed through the transaction as quickly as possible – so fast, in fact that she nearly forgot her PIN number -- muttering all the while. "Stupid, arrogant, annoying, willful..." * * * Alexandra hadn't really meant to palm that pack of Big Red gum while Bea was ringing them up, and she was surprised by the pang of guilt she felt as she strode briskly to her car. Come a long way if a little shoplifting gets under my skin, she thought. Though her path appeared to be direct, she was careful to keep out of the line of sight of a small knot of teens congregated by the cart corral, and used rummaging in her shoulder bag as an excuse to avoid any stray gazes, just another lady in a hurry as she dashed from the market. The clique wasn't outwardly threatening – mostly boys, maybe fourteen to seventeen. But she recognized the signs of a predator, even in children this young. They were definitely surrounding something -- someone -- and once she heard the laughter, she knew her instincts had been right. The laugh was loud and braying, the next best thing to the cackle of a hyena, and it was a sound Alex was accustomed to in her life. It was the laugh of her brothers, the chuckle of her colleagues. It was no different than the laugh of someone like Lane Stevens, just another budding sociopath deriving amusement from inflicting suffering on others. It was no different than her own laugh, really, but it turned her stomach now. Alex slipped in behind the wheel of her convertible, popping open the glove box without looking. A red leather cosmetics case tumbled into her waiting palm, and she whipped down the sun visor to expose the lit vanity mirror on the underside. As she fished through the case, her eyes flicked into the rear-view, trying to gauge how far things had escalated – not far, but she didn't have the time she would have liked. She'd have liked a wardrobe change, and maybe even a wig to stave off the slim chance one of the boys had seen her. But she didn't even have time to hide her signature gray eyes with colored contact lenses. A quick yank freed her dark red hair from its short ponytail, and she styled it over both shoulders as best as she could with her fingers. Reaching blindly behind her, Alex seized the wicker handle of one of the morning's shopping bags. From within, she tugged a shoebox, exchanging her flats for a pair of kitten heeled slingbacks as she applied a coat of glittering pink lip gloss. With Amy's designer sunglasses in place, the woman who emerged from the car was hardly the same who'd entered less than two minutes earlier. She was glitzy, glamorous – she was a celebrity. Even her posture was different as she strode across the lot, thumbs flying across the face of her iPhone. The heft of her shoulders put a little snap in her hips as she walked. It took a moment for the boys to notice her approach, and as Alex's bored gaze drifted over the crowd, she finally saw their victim – a dark-haired boy on the husky side, thumbing a pair of unflattering eyeglasses up his nose. "Guys..." the kid pleaded, bounced from bully to bully with hard shoves. "Mark, tell them to knock it off." The leader of the pack was a tall kid in a long Oakland Athletics jacket, and the kind of hair that took hours to style, meticulously messy. "'Knock it off!'" he whined, exploding into laughter. "Dwight doesn't like it, fellas. Maybe we should—" "Ahem." Alexandra cleared her throat gently. "I'm sorry to interrupt the festivities, but..." Several of the kids turned her way, but Alex didn't look up – she didn't have to, since Amy's shades kept her eyes hidden, even as she lowered her face to the map. "I think I got completely turned around here," she said. "I mean, I've lived here for about a year, so you'd think I'd know where the mall was, right?" "Uh... Marky," one of the peanut gallery members said. "She looks awful familiar." "Shut up. Fuck." Mark pushed through the group. "Sorry. This isn't what it looks like." The kid looked over his shoulder and back with his smile – the one that probably made all the high school girls weak in the knees, Alex reckoned. Pierce let her own grin stretch. "Maybe not. But... I really am lost, so if you could point me in the right direction, then you can go back to... you know, whatever it was you were doing." "Sure thing." He stepped to one side, closer than he probably needed to be. "Which mall are you looking for?" "I have a signing at the... the Bayfair Center?" "Oh, wow, you are out of the way." Mark hooked his arm around her, pointing off in the distance. "You want to take the I-580 to Fairmont. Probably take you about... ten minutes, maybe?" Pierce's smile was a sunny one, fake without looking it. "Great." She rolled her eyes, even though they couldn't see it. "I knew I should have just asked someone and not tried to figure out how to make this stupid phone tell me." "I'm sorry, did you say a signing? Are you famous or..." "Dude, isn't that Desade?" Alex didn't see who'd pointed her out, keeping her eyes on the leader. High school punks or street gangs, it was all the same – sway the leader and the sheep would follow. Mark looked back to the kids, none of whom even had a hand on the boy. "She's who?" "Desade! You know, from that wrestling show. She could kick your ass, man!" He turned that lopsided grin on her again. "Are you really a pro-wrestler chick?" "I guess I am." Pierce's grin was rather unlike her on-air persona, small and even a touch embarrassed. "Though I'm not sure I like it when you say it like that." "Dude, dude..." Another kid, a mop-headed one in a Pantera tee, stepped to Mark's side. "She does karate, and shit. Make her show us a kick." "I'm not really dressed to fight..." Pierce demurred. "Man, all that shit is staged." The third kid to find his voice was Korean, his flat voice definitely the sound of a skeptic. "She raises her foot, the other guy runs into it. It's all show." Alex could have probably taken all three of the under-aged punks -- it would be over in few quick kicks, but only for right then. When they came to, the boys would be looking for payback, and they might not be satisfied with a few shoves this time. "Just one kick," Mark said. "Call it an exchange of favors. We show you where to find your fancy autograph signing, you show us your sweet moves." Alex slowly skimmed her gaze from Mark, past "Dude" and "Man", and to Dwight, who looked like he'd die to be anywhere else. "What about him?" she asked. "Him? He's nothing. He's nobody. Come on, sweetheart, let's see those legs in action." Alex nodded a little, stepping backwards. The roundhouse kick she turned into would have nearly beheaded whoever she was aiming at (and answered the question as to how high she could get her leg), but it missed horribly, and on the follow-through, she artfully crafted a fumble into Mark – an act that took far more control than the kick itself had. "Man, see?" The Korean kid puffed out his chest. "All show." The horn behind them turned everyone's attention to the sea-blue convertible, top down, tattooed redhead in the driver's seat. Alex's hand slipped down to steady herself against the boy. In the car, Amy Campbell's brow furrowed. "What the hell?" she demanded. "Dude," said the mop-headed kid. "We are so fucking boned." From the look in Amy's famously mismatched eyes, though, it was the woman among them about to get the brunt of her ire. Exactly as Alex planned. * * * Two minutes earlier. Amy Campbell pushed her cart out into the parking lot, reaching for the phone at her hip purely out of habit. Her brows knitted as she read the BlackBerry's face. "Alex – Text (2)". She'd missed the chime – not uncommon, but still annoying. A pair of taps brought the message up onto her screen. "Don't be mad," the first one read, and Campbell's lips turned downwards in a frown. Alexandra Pierce apologizing in advance was never a good sign. "Put the top down and come pick me up?" That was when the tiny redhead lifted her eyes to the parking lot, picking Pierce out by her rust-red hair. Amy's mismatched blue-and-brown eyes narrowed, part in disgust, part in concern. The Spider didn't seem in any danger, but she came from a place for which the saying "appearances can be deceiving" seemed invented. The shopping cart shook so hard as Amy darted for the convertible that her hands were stung by pins and needles, and she only had the wherewithal to make sure the eggs were safe before she simply flung the rest bags into the back seat. Some of the groceries might be damaged, but she'd rather have potato chip crumbs than a trip to the emergency room. As the little redhead turned her key in the ignition, she remembered to put the top down – though Amy didn't know what Alex was up to, she'd learned long ago to trust Pierce's plans implicitly. "This had better be good," Campbell muttered as she pulled out of the spot. * * * "What the hell?" Amy demanded, scowling. Alex straightened, tugging on the hem of her shirt. "Oops, looks like I've gotten us in trouble." Desade had very few tells that gave away when she was playing a part, at least to someone who didn't know her very well. She couldn't lie to Amy Campbell, however – a year of intimate contact gave the tiny redhead unprecedented insights into Alexandra's mind. So she knew the grin that Pierce gave the kid – the one with the little wrinkle of her nose – wasn't real. But knowing that and being able to stomach Alex walking – no, fucking sashaying – towards her was two very different things. Campbell's grip on the steering wheel was tight enough to turn her knuckles white as Alex stopped beside the car, bending to rest her elbows against the car. "I'll make this quick," Pierce hissed her whisper. "And you're just going to have to trust me." She grinned over her shoulder at the clique of kids, her lips hardly moving. "You see the fat one in the middle? The one that's surrounded? His name is Dwight, and if I'd been another two minutes, he'd probably be in the dumpster." "What?" Amy blinked past Alex, but Pierce shifted to block Amy's view. "The kid?" "Yeah." The taller woman nodded slightly. "He's just a kid, and they've been bullying him for years." "You need me to chase them off? I'm pretty sure terrorizing groups of teenagers is below even me." "No, they'll just come back, and the next time it'd be worse. If we rescue him, we'll have to be there all the time." Another of Alex's tells was that light touch, her left hand moving her hair behind her ear – a sign that she had a plan. "They don't think he's worth a damn. He's nobody, just someone they can pick on. If he was somebody..." "You know it's probably illegal to flirt with someone his age," Amy's brows furrowed, her head turned to Pierce. She avoided looking to the group of boys to keep whatever plan Alex had intact. "And I'm gonna guess they know who we are--is that lip gloss?" "Yes. And I'm not going to flirt with him – I'm not Kathryn." Alex rolled her eyes, but there was a small smile etched to her lips, just a touch. Like it used to be. "They think we're actors, that the show is all bullshit, coordinated stunts. You saw me whiff on that kick... I'm playing the role. I'm going to step aside here in a second – smile a little, and then double take at the kid, like you recognize him. They may think we're C-list celebrities, but how many celebrities are buddies with them?" "I don't have to pretend that I suck, do I? And what flavor's that gloss? That can't be mine." "It's not. I, uh... I kept the car stocked. You can yell at me later." Alex managed a grin. "You don't have to suck, I've done enough for both of us. I'll be the fuck-up, just... Dwight. Coolest kid you know. Just remember that." Pierce slid to the side almost imperceptibly, allowing Amy to peer past at the increasingly antsy band. "I guess I don't have time to ask what you've got invested in this..." Amy sighed. "Fine. Let's get it done. Gah, they're staring at you!" She reached for the door handle, but pouted at Pierce. "Now I just wanna hit them. Little jackholes, thinking they're gonna get a piece of my..." Campbell trailed off with a grumble, managing a weak smile, and her snap glance back to the chunky kid was certainly not Oscar-worthy. She did rather like the excited arm-grab as she pulled open the car. "I almost want..." the inked-up redhead began hesitantly. "Do it," Pierce said immediately. "Whatever it is, I'll back your play." Amy rocketed up into an embrace, one hand up Alex's back, the other skidding along her waist. It was hard, forceful, no playacting -- angry. Amy retreated with a tug of the taller woman's hair, wiping the back of her hand across her lips as she threw a glare at the boys. "Whoa, dude!" the mop-headed kid exclaimed breathlessly. Her grin was a little nasty as she swaggered forward. "Holy shit, Alex, this is the guy I was telling you about!" Pierce trotted in Amy's wake, and the three speaking kids puffed out their chests – none of them had a clue what the little redhead was talking about, but holy cow she'd kissed the other hot girl! "What guy?" Alex fed Campbell the line, easily taking the back seat in the play. "Wait... not that guy?" A tattooed arm swung over the shoulder of the chunky boy in the middle. "Crazy-ass coincidence. I haven't seen you anywhere since that She Wants Revenge show when that security guard tried busting you for sneaking booze in! What was your name... Dwight! Dwight, right? I was a little buzzed that night... but goddamn, dude, you held your own." "Me?" Dwight tugged at his collar, gulping. "I don't..." "Man, come on!" the Korean kid exclaimed. "You are not serious." Mark stepped up to the two women, unafraid. "Are you high?" he asked. "Dwight is a nobody." "Dude!" the mop-headed kid pulled on Mark's arm. "She's got a temper about that." Amy's attention quickly turned to Mark. "Did you seriously just ask that? I'd tell you to go fuck yourself, but I'd need to wait for your balls to drop and I'm pretty sure it's illegal for me to even say 'balls' to a little boy." She scowled. I gave him a swirly when we were in second grade." "So what, last week?" Amy's brows were livid. "Oh, man!" "Dude!" "Amy-baby..." Alex sighed, a long beleaguered sound, as she put her hand on the other woman's forearm. "Dwight... can you talk her down?" "Me? I... uhh... okay?" He levered himself between wrestling's Red Raver and his nemesis. "Uhm. Guys... come on." Alex stepped behind Amy, whispering without changing her fearful expression. "You can't fight them," she reminded. "Go sit back down, Dwight," Mark shoved the kid back. "In fact, why don't you climb into the Dumpster yourself." "Uhhh, Amy," Alex's voice was louder now. "Didn't you say this was the guy who'd..." "The guy who surprised the hell out of me when he leveled a bouncer with that crazy-ass, half-drunken flying elbow? Yeah. Moves like a maniac for a bigger dude." Amy chuckled, stepping forward to put a hand on the boy's shoulder. It's been fun and all, but... I'm going to the signing in, and you'll have, like, two seconds to catch up. You understand? Two seconds." "Dwight..." Amy nodded, reaching out. "Dwight, calm down..." Quickly (and with Campbell driving, that was very fast indeed), the convertible slipped from the parking lot. The last thing that they saw in the mirror were Mark's two yes-men clapping Dwight on the back. The door didn't close behind them, however, and another person followed them down, a woman gauging from the click-clack of the heel – nothing too high or stiletto-based. They were firm, steady heels. Functional. The woman gestured to the three men, and the black hood was wrenched from their captive's head. Alexandra Pierce tossed her head back, the hair coming away from her eyes as she tried to get her bearings. The room was still pitch dark, so she went off sound. Scent. She heard the wheel of a lighter flick, its orange flame the only illumination in the place as it was touched to the end of a cigarette, which glowed momentarily. The sweet, spicy scent of cloves permeated the room as her captor stepped behind her. "Alexandra..." The voice was dignified, even snooty. Pierce squinted her eyes shut. It couldn't be her, simply couldn't. The woman who'd trained her, who'd turned a scared teenager living off scraps after her family disowned her into the fearsome Desade, the woman who plucked Alexandra Pierce from obscurity and changed everything. "It appears we have much to discuss, my little Rabbit," Margaret Winters sneered. But it couldn't be. It couldn't. It couldn't be her, because Winters was the first person she'd ever murdered. Margaret laughed behind the Spider, blowing a puff of sweet-smelling smoke over her shoulder. For the first time in nearly fifteen years, Alexandra Pierce screamed.
