All the Wrong Moves: Miss Mystique

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-10 An Aching Destiny Phoenix, Arizona Thursday, January 02, 2003 It was not in a blaze of glory that William Gwynn left his office. In fact, William Gwynn actually did have a slight headache. That did not justify his deceit, as he hardly had a migraine. And William had not done what he had intended to do. Instead of driving to the psychics place of business, he went back to his apartment. He took some aspirin and got a beer. He was sipping on it when he placed the plug in the drain of the sink and turned on the hot water. The television was on in the next room, and some politicians were arguing over some unimportant contemporary issue. William had a bit of spare time before the water reached the brim, so he quickly turned the channel to ESPN, where they were playing NBA and NHL highlights. As always, William was only interested in the big plays, hardly the scores. He quickly wet his hands, finding the water hot and soapy. He wiped his hands on a towel by the sink before shutting off the water. He took a fair-sized swig of the Budweiser just feet away before going to work on the stack of dishes beneath the water. Between the dishes and the soap he could see his outline, some more illuminated than others. He looked into his own hollow eyes, down to his pronounced nose and shallow cheeks. His skin, despite the living in Arizona, was a pale white. He had faded from his muscularity many years ago. He had turned from an accomplished, fit high school wrestler to a pale, nerdy accountant. By no means was he as nerdy as many of those that he dealt with on a daily basis, but nerdy nonetheless. He was grizzled. He was unhappy. And again with the swig of alcohol. These were not thoughts he wanted to be having. He wet a rag and pulled a plate out from under the water, creating a rush of swift ripples in the sink, disrupting the reflection, and thus the brooding of William Gwynn. For several seconds he scrubs at the grime on the plate. For several moments more he scrubs and scrapes. He made it shiny, and he examined it. He rinsed it, and placed it away. He had begun brooding again, thinking again. He did so as he was being productive, still washing his dishes. As he placed away his third plate he looked down at himself, his scrawny, thin frame. Some of the muscle mass was still there. Beneath his white undershirt there was a six-pack, though one that needed a bit of uncovering. His arms were not pythons, nor were they sticks. William Gwynn possessed modest muscles. They wouldn't stir fear in anyone, but they were enough to hold his own. William had thought about joining a gym. He had thought that maybe women would dig his physique. But he knew that physique was not his problems. Not even looks were his problem. His problem was personality, and no gym can change that. Nothing can change that. His situation was truly hopeless, he thought. He pulled a mug from beneath the steaming liquid, and began scrubbing away at the mug. It was from the Four Corners, a branch of a small sightseeing tour that William had gone on just months before. It had all faded away in his memory, as though it were forty years ago. William knew he worked too hard. He knew he needed more time to himself. He needed time to enjoy himself. He placed the mug down and dried his hands once more, again taking a sip of his Budweiser. He pulled out another mug, and noted the soapy substance within. The bubbles looked like foam, and the steam coming off of it made it look almost sightly to drink. And yet William tossed the water back into the sink, and went to work with the rag. After four more plates, three bowls, one mug, five spoons, two forks, two knives and another beer, William Gwynn had completed that task. He had barely made a dent in the afternoon. That made Gwynn briefly happy. He still had a lot of time to kill, and he knew just what he was going to do next. He found his keys, and dropped them in his pocket, along with his wallet and cell phone. He thought twice, and withdrew the cell phone. He quickly turned it off. It made a short, annoying sound. Again, he deposited it back into his pocket. He rubbed his head softly. His headache was now near gone. He walked towards the door and swung it open. He stepped through and swung it closed just as quickly. Minutes later, he was in his vehicle, heading for hope, heading for anything. He was on a quest for an entirely new purpose. He had a bit of a drive in front of him, so he turned his radio on. He flipped to his favorite station. It was the big rock station in town, but it was on commercials. He went to the classic rock station, and they were talking. He flipped to the other classic rock station from a different town, and they were playing music, but it wasn't a good variety. Finally he brought in a Tucson rock station. They were playing some good rock and roll, and William spun the volume dial. There always was that edge, that youth. He punched the accelerator and rolled down the window. His hair blew back, adding to his disheveled look by many degrees. Certainly the woman could not afford to accurately judge him by the way he looked as he walked in. Maybe William just wanted every excuse. Maybe he just wanted to keep some small glimmer of hope, even if he is dealt the worst of news. "News," he sneered at himself, "what she will deliver me can hardly be considered 'news', just a guess, a prediction. Hell, you're wasting your time, but you've got a gift card, you may as well at least see what the woman has to say. Hopefully it won't take too long and you can catch a movie or something afterward … " this seemed a perfectly viable plan. William nodded in satisfaction as he blew through a four-way stop in a neighborhood. There were no cars around. William did not give a damn. He was on his way to find out destiny, part of him said. Eventually, he came to the street the joint was actually on. He turned right, which was west. He still had several blocks to go. He had never really seen this place before, so he was interested when he came to it. It was not as ugly as one would expect a place like that to look like. It was probably once a stately shop. It had windows which were probably once used for window shopping. Drapes, crystall balls and other "pagan" accessories (as a sign outside declared) were in the window now, and surely inside. William found a parking spot, and was not particularly surprised that he was the only car in the lot. He got out of his car and locked it. This was not a place he wanted to be leaving an unlocked car, despite how old it might be. William could not help but feel self-conscious as he opened the door to the psychic's domain. As soon as he stepped in, and the bell rang, a voice ran out. "William Gwynn, is it?" she asked. "Did you know I went to high school with you? Victorian, 1999." "Actually," William replied, "I went to Crusoe, and it was 1997." "Oh," the psychic said, slightly surprised. "Anyway," William began, "impressive nonetheless. I imagine you got my name from the pranksters that bought the gift card for me?" William looked around. He finally found the room in which she was sitting. She smiled at him, and signalled at him to have a seat. "I'm afraid not," she responded, "you see, while no one is here, I have time to let this crystal ball wander as it will. And it shows me things, William. It shows me some of the more important people of Phoenix and what they're about to do. And I've determined that your life is about to take a very interesting turn. You're probably about to be one of the most interesting people in Phoenix in the next couple of years." "How is that?" William asked. "Well," she grinned, "that's what you're here for, isn't it? No, actually, you're here to learn about women. That's generally the only time a man will walk in here. I'll tell you right now, son, you've still got a lot of waiting to do for Miss Perfect. May as well keep trying, though, the hunt is often more fun than the kill." "Alright, babe," William said, "you've got me interested." "Let's start with this," Miss Mystique directed, pointing at the crystal ball. With a gentle wave of her hand, the crystal ball actually had figures within it. Gwynn wondered if it was some kind of apparition. Skepticism must have crossed his face. "No, William," she whispered, "this is real. It's as real as you and I. Here's your future. Do you believe now?" For some reason, William was compelled to nod. "Skepticism is a regularity, but William, you've got to see as I do. Let this foresight be your guide." Again she gestured toward the ball, and William focused on the figures within. Into the Ball Phoenix, Arizona Tuesday, January 10, 2006 And there stands William Gwynn. It's not the rugged William Gwynn of the first days of 2003, it's the buff, stern William Gwynn of 2006. This is a man who has grappled with glory and subdued sorrow. It's a man that has overcome so many hurdles, and knows that there are still many more before him. And yet he's not timid. He's not afraid. This William Gwynn rolls with the punches. This William Gwynn isn't out for women. He's not out for anything in particular. He's out for happiness. And then William Gwynn of this day is happy. He doesn't have to seek anything. This William Gwynn could not possibly still be working for the accounting department of a small, local corporation. "Nice work with Ricky Thunder, William," someone congratulates. "Thank you," William responds. "He was a challenge, of course. This tournament is just full of challenges. I've got Red Rock next." "Who'd you say?" the man asks. "Red Robin?" "Red Rock," William corrects, "heard that he's quite the wrestler. I'm going to go in prepared of course." "Professional wrestling," narrates the psychic, "that's what you were born to do, William." "Who the hell said that?" William asks. "I didn't hear anything," the man William had been talking to says. He looks at William strangely. "Are you feeling alright?" "Fine," William says. "Could have swore someone said my name, though." The man decides to take leave of William quickly. "Well anyway," he says, "good luck with Red Robin. You take care of yourself, y'hear?" William nods, a little confused at the hasty retreat. William opens a door in the hallway and steps into the bathroom. He begins to examine himself in the mirror. "William," Miss Mystique narrates again, "you are so sexy. I can't believe you've made it this long without a woman." "That's what I like to hear," the completely oblivious professional wrestler Gwynn says, "I like dirty girls that sneak into the men's room." Gwynn slowly examines his face. He washes it gently, and checks to make sure that he is fairly well shaven. "Can you answer a few questions for me, William?" Miss Mystique asks. "Sure, sweetheart," William answers. "When and where did you learn to wrestle?" she questions. "Stone's Academy, Phoenix, Arizona," William responds, "I think it was early 2003. I spoke to some psychic chick that said I should get out of accounting and get into professional wrestling. I feel bad, I used a gift card to pay her. I probably owe her half my salary. I do send her a Christmas card every year, though. 'Dear Miss Mystique, thank you for helping me realize my potential'. Some bullshit like that. Of course I don't believe in any of that crap, good luck is what I call it." "You naive, insolent son of a bitch!" she thunders. "What?" "Sorry, sorry," she backs down, "text messaging. Anyway, I guess I accidentally got in the wrong bathroom. I'm sure you know how that works." "Oh yes," William says, grinning. "Well, I've got a press conference, nice talking to you." "You too," she replied. William left, and she decided to address the 2003 William. "You get all that? Stu's Academy in Tuscon. And you start training because I tell you too." "Doesn't that, like," 2003 William muses, "cut a hole in the Earth-Space continuum or something? And wasn't it Stone's Academy in Phoenix?" Miss Mystique sighs. "Leave the deep thinking to rocket scientists, William," she grumbles, "I'll worry about the Earth-Space continuum when I get indicted for breaking it. And it's my responsibility to show, your responsibility for listening. If you didn't get the facility, that's your loss." "You'll be indicted for breaking it?" "In about thirty-five years," she says, "but I'll see it coming. I've already got it all planned out. I'm moving to Belize. Anyway, I can't see past this for you. This is a very important match, with this Red Robin. If you beat him, I imagine you go on to glory. If you lose to him, I'm guessing … well, hell, I don't know. I can only tell you so much if you're paying me with a gift card. Let's go to the press conference." William follows her out of the bathroom and toward the press conference. The door says "do not enter or listen in, important press conference going on". Nevertheless, she tries to open the door. Security punches 2003 William in the face. "Are you STUPID? Can't you READ?" William yells as soon as he recovers. Miss Mystique is aggrivated by this. "Look, if I could read I wouldn't be stuck being some stupid psychic that makes jack shit an hour," she bitches, "and I wouldn't have gone into the wrong bathroom either. Alright, let's go back, what do you say?" Back to Reality Phoenix, Arizona Thursday, January 02, 2003 "Holy shit," William Gwynn said. That was all that came out of his mouth. "Amazing, isn't it?" she asked. "And, by the way, I wasn't joking about you being gorgeous - " Her eyes crept down his scrawny frame. "Not interested," William said. "It's all about professional wrestling now! Okay, so Stone's Academy in Phoenix. I've got to quit my job and go there tomorrow, right?" "Right," she said. "But are you sure - " "Yep," William said, "gotta go. See you later." "Bye!" she muttered. "Hey wait, William … could I have that gift card?" "Oh!" William said, cursing himself. He handed it over. "I'll be sure to send you a Christmas card when I'm rich and famous." "Thanks," she said, unappreciatively. But William Gwynn was already out the door. Following Through Phoenix, Arizona Friday, January 03, 2003 William Gwynn was again standing in the office of his direct superviser. Both of them had come in early. "Yes, I'm sure," William said, "this is an opportunity that I can't afford to miss. I just know this is what I was made to do." "What is it, William?" the boss asked. "What could you possibly be better at than accounting? You're built for accounting. You're built for nine to five. You're built for a civilized life with a white-collar salary and - " "No, you're wrong," William said. "I'm suited for something entirely different. I'm not happy with this." "What is it?" And William did not even hesitate. "Professional wrestling," he said. And his boss laughed. William knew that his boss would laugh. "Fine," he said, between fits of laughter, "go and do your wrestling. But don't expect a position when you come back. I can't deal with accountants that fly off for some little tangent. I hope you can find an accounting job with decent pay in a couple of weeks, when you figure out that professional wrestling isn't what you want either. I've never heard of it, an accountant wrestling. What a joke … " "It won't be so funny when I'm tearing up the ranks," William answered. "I'm sure you'll be telling everyone you know that at one point, you knew William Gwynn, you worked with him. He was an accountant at your workplace." "Look, William," his boss had finally had enough, "just go. Have a nice day, life. Good luck." William did not dignify that with a response. Instead, he turned on his heel and walked out of the office. He went down the elevator for the last time. He took his name pin and threw it in the trash. He walked out the door, free of all restrictions and regulations. William Gwynn was a free man. He could grow his hair long if he wanted. He could grow a beard. He didn't have to worry about the company's image or firewalls or numbers. All he had to worry about was getting in shape and learning a new trade, which was almost equally as daunting. William Gwynn was ready, though. William Gwynn was about to take the world by storm. He pulled out his cell phone and dialed those all-familiar numbers. "Mr. Stone?" William asked. "I'll be there in about half an hour. Yes, I quit my job. I'm about to be your full-time personal student. Yes, I'm very excited. Thank you for the opportunity, sir. I won't let you down." And he hung up the phone. And when all was said and done, Salideus Stone could not have been happier with the work that William Gwynn did in his long, glorious and illustrious career. William Gwynn flipped his phone shut. "I'm coming for you, Little Red Riding Hood." Fade.
