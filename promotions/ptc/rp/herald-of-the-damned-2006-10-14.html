Herald of the Damned

14 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-14 This is Doctor Daniel Rourke, chief psychologist, filing my report via audio. Received new inmate today, all staff was briefed as to his background and the dangers he could pose to the unwary. Inmate is Bruce Shanahan, the mastermind of the Sect of Black Wisdom cult in upstate Vermont. Apparently he was apprehended in a raid near the town of Rutland, evangelizing to his followers in the midst of some bizarre ritual that even police present at the scene are hesitant to speak of. I'm not sure as to the specifics and at the present time it isn't important. I'll worry about that only if it becomes necessary at some point during his incarceration. My preliminary interview with Mr. Shanahan was conducted at 2:18 p.m. this afternoon. As expected, he doesn't have the sunniest disposition, but then few in this facility do. The man appears to possess a remarkable intelligence and equally keen perceptive traits. Hell, were it not for his antisocial tendencies and acute derangement then I assert that he would be quite an asset to mankind. But, that's neither here nor there. I'm not afraid to say that Shanahan's demeanor gave me the chills. His way of speaking was strange, decidedly cryptic. His responses were somehow both elusive and direct at the same time, I'm not sure how to precisely describe it. And just as they say the man has an uncanny charisma, the door swings both ways. Almost something of an aura, sinister and carrying the stench of a tangible evil. Something distinctly inhuman. I found myself unable to look into his eyes from the first moment; it wasn't so much the same wicked mystique as an emptiness, a void where that should have been vitality. They say the eyes are the windows to the soul, but…well, I'll stop right there before they wrap me in a straitjacket and toss me in amongst the population. With the interview concluded, my initial diagnosis is that of an acute schizophrenic. Mr. Shanahan appears delusional, severely detached from reality, christening himself the herald of a pantheon of deities set to, I quote, "reclaim this world when the stars are right". He spoke of things no sane man should and his expression never changed, voice never altering. It was apparent that he believed every word he said. Between that, his intelligence, and his inherent ability to sway others and manipulate minds, he is very dangerous. Professional wrestling background aside, of course. He has the physical attributes to incapacitate any guard in this facility, and for that reason needs to be handled with extreme caution. For the safety of the staff, I have advised that he be kept under constant sedation when outside his cell. Although he was reserved during the interview and reportedly cooperative during his capture, there's something chilling surrounding Bruce Shanahan and I fear that he is biding his time. There's no doubt in my mind that he has plans forming at this moment and I cannot stress enough that every precaution must be taken in dealing with him. Slipups in this matter could be disastrous. Given the nature of his connection, I've also proposed that security be intensified, including the hiring of more guards for the explicit purpose of preventing escape. For now, his organization is only suspected of crimes with no evidence to back the heinous things they're said to have perpetrated. But having their leader in custody in a facility for the criminally insane may force his followers into a corner. I want him on a near constant lockdown and under surveillance twenty-four hours a day. I have a very bad feeling about what this man has brought into this hospital. I'll offer more when I delve deeper into what makes this man tick, or as the situation warrants. For now, this is Doctor Daniel Rourke, signing off. * * * * Lounging back against the padded wall, a thin smile crept across his face. Just as his master, Nyarlathotep, reveled in spreading madness and chaos even moreso than death, so had Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan taken on the nature of his diabolical benefactor. He had no qualms about allowing himself to be captured that night on Emerald Hill, not given the sustained memory of seeing grown men fall to their knees and utter prayers to their feeble God ion light of what they'd stumbled on. They'd stared into the abyss and the abyss had stared back, leaving a stain that could never be cleansed. Minds were forever broken and psyches shattered for life, the greatest compromise one of his faith could ask for. Hardened men so stunned that they could only stammer out his rights as the cuffs clicked around his wrists. It trivialized his seizure, and that was another triumph that the Coalition couldn't deprive him. It was GTT time again. This year there would be no intricate quest for immortality, no great masquerade. But that wasn't to say the players weren't assembled and in place, the stage set for an epic tragedy. The Sect would be empowered, the Old Ones would be pleased, the nonbelievers poised on the brink of an oblivion they could never grasp. He would be the general surveying the battlefield, watching as the pawns slaughtered one another below. The Old Ones' arm of justice, courier of death. He quite nearly drowned out the inane babbling of the nurse, some pallid, lanky pseudo-male with peach fuzz adorning his chin. That was his goal, but the sorry bastard wouldn't have it. And that pre-pubscent voice was grating on his nrves. "Mister Shanahan?" The high-pitched voice should have been sourced from a female, not his overly feminine caregiver. But as long as he allowed himself to remain in this damn asylum, he'd find a way to deal. He could always kill the boy when his plans were finished. "Sir, I need more information. Please just cooperate and I'll leave you alone soon." The nurse turned away for a moment, chatter audible through the bars of the little white room he'd call home indefinitely. The exchange was brief, and following a curt and squeaky "Yes, sir" from his puny and weasel-like caretaker, he heard the latch open and the door creak to admit entrance. Into the room strolled a small entourage, all clad in three-piece suits and wearing looks of disdain. In light of their hostile approach, Shanahan grinned widely from his spot on the floor. "Ah, gentlemen. It's been a long time, has it not?" A chair was carried into the room by care of the nurse, the surname of SANDERS adorning his polished name tag. The first of the visiting party lowered himself onto it, legs straddling the back and never removing his baleful gaze from the one known as Violence Jack. His argent-shaded tie was conspicuously clasped with a golden symbol, an intertwined BC. Shanahan knew it well, just as he knew its wearer even better. "I've awaited this day for a long time, Bruce." The voice was hard, raspy with excitement. "Finally you're going to pay for your crimes. For all the deaths, the grief, the tragedies…" "The only tragedy here is that humanity still lingers on Earth, old friend. And how am I to remedy that with you keeping me here?" He uttered a wry chuckle to the chagrin of his visitors. Clearly they's hoped to see him groveling and pleading for release, but those self-righteous worms would get so such satisfaction. "So…how have you been, Marcus? I trust our little misunderstanding hasn't been flushed away yet?" Marcus Stryker glowered down on the man in the straitjacket, desperately aching to land just one clean shot on the maniac's jaw. He only hoped the justice system would finally work and get the bastard behind bars where he belonged. He had no desire for a court-martial. "What misunderstanding, you psychotic piece of shit? Your lieutenant, that fucking Knight…" "Tsk tsk, Marcus. Your mother would take the strap to you if she heard those obscenities coming from your mouth." "…that fucking Knight murdered one of my men and you know it." He wouldn't get overly angry, it would please his old ally too much. "I'm going to see you rot in prison when they finally get the evidence they need to put you away. And I'll see Knight riddled with every bullet I can pump into his scrawny body before it breaks apart from the trauma." "You have a lot of repressed anger, Marcus," observed Violence Jack with that same wicked smile. "Whatever happened to the forgiveness that your religion preaches? Or have the priests in your secret little society been too busy touching children behind the barn to properly tend their flock in the fields?" "Burn in Hell, you murdering greaseball." Shanahan feigned hurt, grimacing and breathing out a hurt moan. "Oh now, Marcus, that really hurts. I may be many things that aren't complimentary, but I'm not a murderer. Where do you have the evidence to make such a terrible accusation?" A thin smile, a dare for Stryker to use that white-nuckled fist trembling atop his thigh. Captain Stryker cleared his throat and unclenched his fist. He wouldn't have the satisfaction. "The Muldoons'. Thorne and Ignalls. Jago. Grant's team. Not to mention the countless ridiculous sacrifices." "You have no proof, Marcus. This blind crusade of your's is destined to end in heartbreak. You know that as well as I do." It was truth, and he could see it reflected in the eyes of the other as well. At least the man held out hopes, false as they may well be. "Truly, it's sad. We could have remained friends had you just disavowed the lies of the Church and taken my hand instead of spitting on it. Look at the pickle we're in now, huh?" "The pickle you're in, Bruce," retorted the brazen captain. "I'm not the one strapped inside a straitjacket, drowned in sedatives and facing investigation for crimes that make Charles Manson look like a choir boy." "You once called me savior, Marcus." The proclamation brought a long silence, even sufficing to soften the face of Marcus Stryker. "'Tis a pity the Blackhelm pups have clawed out your eyes and blinded you from the truth. I could have shown you what lies beyond the illusion you've been conditioned to accept as reality." He paused and looked past his primary accuser, eyes falling onto Doctor Rourke for the briefest of moments before spying another familiar face lingering just inside the threshold. Another visitor whose bitterness was strong enough that it was almost its own tangible entity. Stryker's eyes never lifted from his his bitter foe. "Maury Dawson," sighed VJ, beckoning his old accomplice with a jerk of the head. "Join us, old friend. Surely you haven't been destroyed by hatred like your partner here?" Dawson's expression was cold, his eyes like ice. He hadn't forgotten his betrayal either, the sins he'd committed in the name of the one he'd called Chaney, the one known to most as Masquerade. Bruce Shanahan's identity only a year ago, a despicable puppet master acting under the pretense of a savior. The number of lives that the cult mastermind had built and toppled was mind-boggling, and now two of his greatest supporters-turned-detractors were set to crucify him. "Before we continue this charade, Bruce, I have one question for you. What do you know of the Eyes of Fear?" The circus was about to begin. And he was the main attraction. * * * * Your dreams are tempestuous tonight, friend. Turmoil has come to reign over your life. Such is the burden of a champion, is it not? No, don't adjust your set. This isn't a dream. My nocturnal visits are infrequent these days but reserved for the most deserving. You are one of those few chosen, so be content with that. I offer very few of you upstarts such laurels at this point in my career. Before we meet in the ring, know that I approach these events all the same, Matthew. You are an appeasement to those that I seek to bring back into this world. It's business as usual, but take heed that I mix business and pleasure quite well. They say you're a veritable phenomenon. You've been a sensation in PRIME, skyrocketing up the ladder without a hint of slowing down. I may have become complacent as of late within our own stomping grounds, but this is a far cry from Revolution. For the moment, you may be king of the PRIME universe, and that I can deal with. I'm a patient man and it's been decreed that I'm to bide my time a bit longer. Enjoy the comfort of your throne, jackal. You've entered a world you cannot fathom. For years now I have prowled the tournament circuit, preying on the weak and the powerful alike. I have slain juggernauts and vanquished the dreams of every small-fry worm that has had the misfortune of facing the wrath of Their disciple. This year, the curtain doesn't fall until I say it does. The time hasn't yet come for us to lock horns on our home soil, but it appears Fate requests a glimpse of what assuredly lies ahead. Lady Luck has scorned you, child. Unfortunately for the cosmic principles, I even have defied Fate on this one. It seems I'm incarcerated in a hospital for the criminally insane and cannot make our date. But that's not to say you won't have worthy competition come belltime. You see, I took the liberty of drawing up an interesting little contract with the tournament officials before I was thrown here into Shangri-La. And per my contractual stipulation where I may declare my own stand-in, I've opted to deputize Brother Tully for this one. Surely you know him from PRIME as well? Either way, it's shame we won't be meeting come the tournament. But I'm assured that Horace will dispose of you as handily as I would. I sense your shock. You feel scandlized by being deprived the opportunity to silence me yourself. Matthew, this whole thing was set into motion long before you even knew there would be a GTT6. Long before either of us were chosen to enter the field. And long, LONG before it was ordained that we would butt heads. For that transgression, I apologize. But we're sure to meet again soon. Quite soon. Alas, it fears my time has dwindled. Take care, Matthew. I hope the child's health improves, I'd hate for you to be too distracted when you step into the ring with my protege. In this time of crisis, take heart in one great universal truth. Mortality may be fleeting, but the Old Ones are forever. Pleasant dreams, Matthews, and heed my warnings.
