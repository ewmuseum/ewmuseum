Garbage Bag Johnny WILL win GTT6 (Part 2)

1 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-01 I wasn’t always like this, you know. This whole homelessness thing is just something that kind of slowly grew on me over time. The only part about it that I still can’t get over is the actual lack of a home, but the rest of it really started to appeal to me after a while. There was something about the whole connectivity of belonging to an identifiable group that brought a certain direction to my life; even if members of the group itself were disconnected due to the unsociable nature and individualistic competition that the homeless lifestyle demanded. In the daylight, before the fights took place, it would seem as if the homeless didn’t get along with each other, but they did to a point. Their behaviors towards their peers were no different than the way they would behave towards anyone. Any perceived hostility between them, from an onlooker’s eye, would be nothing more than aggressive banter that harbored no ill will or anything of the like. I assimilated to this manner of conversation fairly easily, and before long, I became comfortable with the bantering where I tried to avoid it before. It wasn’t something that was acquired naturally, though. It had to be learned, and the lessons came without warning and forced me to adapt. I had spent the morning preparing; dressing for the part. I took great care in dressing myself that day because there is an element to haggardness that has to be believable. The homeless don’t care much about what they wear, but to successfully beg, one must be as presentable as possible without being presentable enough to be identified as a phony. The beggars that made decent money were never homeless themselves; rather, they had done their research and learned from experience. Across the road from me there was a talented beggar who made decent wages exploiting war. He kept conversation to the barest of minimums, usually opting to just shake his change cup while holding a sign that marked him as a veteran of the Iraqi war who had lost an arm. Ten years ago, he did the same thing with Desert Storm, but people were always moving in and out, and only the deeply rooted populace could detect his scam. Really, he had lost his arm after he let an infected wound on his palm go untreated. His palm flared up, and his hand began to resemble a small volleyball that was inflated to the point where it was about to burst, and the infection spread up his arm to the elbow. I sometimes question whether his amputation was a result of ignorance or dedication. Anyhow, there I was, dressed in clothes I had carefully selected from the Salvation Army. I wore pants that were too large for me. Most of the pants at the thrift store were too large for me. Either fat people are the only ones to donate their clothes, or skinny ones are quick to buy used pants. Nonetheless, the large pants benefited me. They were nice enough to make it seem as if I was making an effort to look decent, but they were also obviously used. I wore a plain grayish sweater and an old knit hat. The old knit hat was thrown on as a makeshift solution to an exhausting problem. I couldn’t figure out how to wear my hair. If it were too greasy or overly unkempt, I would scare away potential donations. If it was too neat, my scam would be unveiled. The hat covered up this problem quite literally. The next part of my costume was perhaps the most important- the accessories. It was too early for malt liquor. Most people think it’s alright for the homeless to get drunk at night, but if someone sees a bum drinking when the sun is still out, the bum becomes that much more avoidable. People like bums who can spend their change wisely. The Styrofoam cup is also a very important accessory. It is absolutely imperative that one uses a cup bought with his own beggarly earnings. Finding a used cup in a trash can is dirty and perhaps contagious, and nobody wants to put their change in a cup that has been in the garbage. People want to donate to nice clean cups, but if you have a large sized cup, people figure you have enough money to be spending it extravagantly on large drinks. Instead, you have to have the smallest cup available for money- most water cups are too small to hold enough change, but a small soda cup will do. Though it is usually not necessary, scouting local restaurants for the cheapest small sized beverage adds to your believability. I was doing alright for my first day. I had been out there for about five hours, and I was starting to get the hang of things. I had made forty eight dollars and thirty seven cents. It was all about asking clearly and asking humbly. Some homeless people would ask for twenties near the ATM machine or they’d ask for more from someone who had already given money. I only asked for whatever could be scared, and I followed up with politeness. I wanted repeat business because it would be better for me in the long run.. Then, as the sun started its descent and a pinkish hue dimmed over the horizon, I saw him stand up and limp over to me. It was the beggar from across the street. I don’t know why he limped. His legs were fine. Perhaps the limp was to exaggerate his stumped half-arm. He called himself the Veteran, and eventually, everyone else started calling him the Veteran, too. I guess he believed he was in all the wars he said he was and then some. He would go into great detail about battles he thought he had fought. The battles all had names he had imagined, though, and his descriptions paid very little detail to any accurate account of history. He didn’t look to happy, but he never looked none too happy when you looked at him. He just shook his cup and nodded his head towards his cardboard sign. Now, though, he had grown tired of begging, and he was waiting for the pinkish hue to darken to a blackening purple that fizzled out until the purple only glowed in linings around the stars. He was waiting until it was appropriate to buy his liquor, although sometimes he grew impatient; especially if he had nobody within complaining distance. "You there!" he called to me. "Are you new around here?" "This is actually my first day," I replied, dumbly trying to compensate for any grievance I might have unknowingly committed. It felt like I was speaking to some assistant manager, and once I realized this, I changed my tone; but not before he interrupted during a gulp in my speech. "Oh yeah? How’d you do?" "Not bad," I said. "I made about fifty bucks." My face beamed with pride, but as I looked into his eyes, I could see my face reflecting in them. My dirty teeth were still too reflectively yellow to appear truly homeless. I could see his teeth through the reflection in my teeth through the reflection in his eyes. They were spotted like speckled beans with Dalmatian print and rot marks. The beaming, though, was short lived. He responded to my gleeful answer with a gritty chuckle. "One hundred thirty eight dollars and nineteen cents," he bragged. His gimmick was polished, and I had no gimmick. Some homeless men butchered pop radio songs. Others had practiced sales pitches. One played a trumpet in the subway. He wasn’t any good, but he had amazing focus. Every so often, he would unleash a flurry of notes, pressing his fingers on any combination of the three valves. The result would irritate through airwaves in staccato bursts of frenzied, tonal slurry. I needed something that would endear people to my condition, but I couldn’t think of anything I was good or bad enough at to get people’s attention. As I tried, the Veteran interrupted me with a proposition while waving his stump in my face. "Say, Junior. If it’s your first day, you have to be initiated into the order with a fight. It doesn’t matter if you win or lose, but I’m going to be generous and put my whole stash against your fifty. How’s that sound?" I would have normally felt bad about fighting a cripple, but he was waving it in front of me irreconcilably. He was taunting me with that damned arm! Still, I quelled the swelling rage and looked away from the puckered stump that fleshly strapped his forearm shut just above the elbow. "Are you sure?" I asked dryly, trying to do the honorable thing even if I knew he wouldn’t reconsider. "You only have one arm." I hoped he wouldn’t reconsider. The Veteran was easy pickings, no matter what he’d say if you asked him. "I’m fucking aware of that!" He spat to his side and then returned to stare at me with the glaring eyes of ingrained militancy. "I lost it when the damned Iraqis took it from me in my sleep four clicks north of Iraq City. The bastards snuck up on our tents, and by the time those damned Arabs ripped through my tent, it was too late for me to grab my gun. One of them cut my arm clean off while I was fighting him unarmed, but I still overpowered him grabbed his dagger and skewered seventeen of those bastards while veins and tendons and shit were hanging out from my severed limb. I got a purple heart for that shit, but I swear to God. Those sons of bitches took my arm." I stopped nodding enough to indicate I was listening and not just nodding. "Alright," I finally agreed. "I’ll fight you tonight." He nodded, but his nod was one of confidence and argumentative victory. "Alright," he was waving that damned stub, the permanent scarring of starry stitching staring at me like some sphincter winking Cyclops. "Be at the yard across the tracks from the train station after the bars let out." I nodded again (this time in silent accordance), and he walked off the other way. I had my first homeless fight to prepare for, so I started back home to get my mind right. *** I sat on my couch, smoking a spliff all by my lonesome with my cell phone in hand. Another spliff was rolled and waiting on the coffee table, and the room was misted with a light haze that spread with an expanded immediacy. I could barely see across the room as I waited for Adam Dick to pick up the phone so I could tell him that I already had a fight scheduled. I was excited- giddy, even- like a pubescent girl calling her best friend to announce her menarche. I was excited to the point of unnecessarily vulgar simile. "Johnny? What’s going on?" His voice dragged on lazily in a state of advanced relaxation. I could hear him smacking his jaws illustriously on his favorite snack- broasted face; a culinary combination of broiled and roasted face. "I’m just smoking some spliffs, man." I continued on with the good news. "I got my first fight later on tonight, man. I’m fighting with the Veteran, and he’s giving me odds. He’s putting up his whole stash against mine." "Right on, man," Adam answered between bites. "Isn’t the Veteran that lunatic with one arm?" "Yeah, man. I’m fighting a fucking gimp." "Watch his good arm though. It’s probably twice as strong to compensate for the amputated one." I turned the Super Nintendo on and started playing Super Punch-Out, peering through the smoggy mixture of pot and tobacco smoke. "There’s nothing to worry about, man. I’m training as we speak." I lit the second spliff while mashing the buttons on my controller. The cigarette dangled from my mouth as my sixteen bit character dodged the weak punches and retaliated with furious lefts and rights. "It sounds good, man, but I have to go finish my dinner. I’ll see you at the tracks. Be there a little early." "Alright," I said before taking a deep drag and hanging up the phone. I could barely see the screen now as the molecules from the smoke bonded to the molecules in the air, veiling the room with a hazy opaqueness. I fought on blind using only the sounds of square MIDI waves to determine hits and misses. I was in a lucid state of concentration that would later on prove me unbeatable in my first prescheduled fist fight. Someplace else, Adam was enjoying his dinner. At the time, he didn’t know it would be his last. *** I had some time to spare before I had to meet Adam at the fence near the tracks, so I figured I’d head over to Nova’s place to tell him I’d have the money later on; but more importantly, to mooch some more pot off of him and conserve my own stash. I was pretty sure he’d have no problem with smoking me up, and I was in pretty high spirits already- high enough to tolerate the sea of hippies I’d have to navigate through to find Nova and his hospitable business practices. When I approached Nova’s place, though, I was sent into a panic by the surrounding sounds. My heart rate increased noticeably, and my throbbing temple veins beat out of time with the obnoxious sound of bongos. Indeed, there was a drum circle circled around a bonfire, and as usual, the lousy hippies slapped their dirty, stoned hands against the pseudo-instruments as loud as fucking possible. There was no rhythm involved. The sons of bitches just liked to bang on things and feign some musical connection with the earth. The bongos infuriated me! Rage boiled hotter inside as I approached, only to see that the drum circle had invited a special guest. There was a grubby man with them, standing in front of the fire so that his features were dimly lit inside an eclipsed silhouette. His grubbiness was not the same grubbiness associated with the hippies. I recognized him- that black man with graying tufts of hair curling underneath his driver’s cap. He wore a wool vest with frayed threads growing from it like some clothing fungus. It hung a bit loosely over a white button down shirt with the ends of the sleeves opened. The shirt needed a wash, but that did nothing to distract me from his identifiable mark. He was playing a trumpet in warbled bursts of randomized fingerings. It sounded like shit. Naturally, the hippies swayed to it and enjoyed the bursts as if he was some melodious guru who had somehow been able to channel the voice of nature through a beaten piece of brass. "Do any of you know where Nova is?" I asked during one of the pauses of the trumpeting. My voice barely beat out the god awful bongo pounding. Some of the hippies on the far side of the circle kept playing, and dialogue had to wait for the trumpeted palpitations to rest. "He’s in the house, man," answered a husky kid with his flabby pectorals creasing against his tie-dye shirt. He waited for the next round of brassy atonality to quiet. "The door’s open, man, but I think Nova’s tripping pretty hard, so be careful." I turned away with a pronounced statement of my ungraciousness and marched towards the door, swinging it open to find Nova in the living room running his hands against the wall. Occasionally, he pressed his ear against the outdated patterns on the wallpaper. "Nova?" He shushed me with a finger to his mouth that stayed pressed in front of his lips until he was done listening. "I think there’s a vortex somewhere in this wall." He spoke slowly, without looking at me, but something in his voice assured me that he knew I was there and he knew who I was. "I hear these people talking about something in there, but it’s all in Esperanto." Not so coincidentally, Telemundo was on the shitty television, and the volume knob was turned to its maximum level. I walked over to Nova and turned the television off on the way there. "Nova, you alright, man?" I put a hand on his shoulder, but he was still listening to the wall. "They’ve stopped talking. I think they’re onto us." "Take it easy. It was just the TV. It was the Spanish channel." It was apparent, though, that Nova wasn’t listening to me. He had the urgent look of a man with far more important things to worry about; as imaginary as those things might be. "Look, man. We’ve got to find this wormhole so we can seal it off. I’ve got too much shit on my plate as it is to have to worry about getting sucked into a vortex tonight." "You need some pot to slow your mind down a little bit. You got a bowl lying around somewhere?" Nova ignored me again. He was running his palms down a different section of the wall. "Grab one of the rolls of duct tape on the coffee table, there. I think I found it." With reason striking out as a viable option to dissuade Nova’s hallucinatory concerns, I tossed him one roll of duct tape from an awkward, leaning pyramid of them that he had stacked on the coffee table. He went to work taping strips over a random section of wall until the vortex was sufficiently covered in his mind. "You think that’ll hold?" "Sure," I offered while packing one of the bowls arrayed on his table with some of Nova’s weed; conveniently bagged next to said bowls. "Have a seat, man." Nova sat next to me on the couch, and I gave him greens. It was, after all, his stash, and he seemed to need it now more than I did. He exhaled, but it was very clear that he was detached from reality. If I had exercised better judgment, I wouldn’t have tried to explain the monetary situation to him right then, but I was getting high, and Nova was about to unknowingly exploit my arriving, doglike hunger. He stood up without saying anything and headed for the kitchen. He brought out a plate of some light brown chocolates that were perhaps a bit too light brown. The brownness was unsettlingly diarrheic. I preferred a darker looking chocolate, but at that point, I would’ve eaten actual dog shit if it was on a plate. The plate legitimized the whole ordeal. Our insatiable appetites resulted in an empty plate, and I had unknowingly ingested several grams of dried mushrooms that contained psilocybin that Nova had baked into chocolates "This second wave is going to be intense, man." Nova was already out of his mind, but he had full control of his subconscious faculties. "Huh?" was all I managed to squeeze out of my incredibly dry mouth. "The shrooms, man!" "Shit!" I cursed myself for my uncontrollable hunger. In a clearer state of mind, I would have instantly recognized the possibly hallucinogenic nature of anything Nova cooked, but I was too gluttonous. The worst part was that while contemplating ways to control the oncoming effects of the chocolates so that I could at least stay in my head enough to fight a one armed man, I couldn’t stop thinking that I was still really fucking hungry. Eating was survival, and the only thing I could think to do in order to stay the course was to go to the fight with the intentions of completely maiming the Veteran so that I could cannibalize him afterwards. I knew that such an outcome was completely implausible, but at the time it was somewhat motivating as the intrusively loud, uncoordinated bongos fueled my anger from the yard. "You still hungry, man?" The question seemed to perk Nova out of his delusional floatation. "Yeah!" he charged out with some sort of patriotism towards the cause of eating. "But I don’t have any more chocolate, and I’m way too fucked up to cook something." "Well, look. You know that modeling thing I was talking about yesterday?" "Sure," he lied. He was looking at the ceiling untrustingly. "I was way off on the modeling part, but I’m going to make some decent money tonight. I’m fighting this homeless guy with one arm, and if I win, I’ll have most of the money I owe you." "You should fight that bum playing the trumpet in the yard. He’s fucking awful." Then we both started giggling uncontrollably. We giggled until neither of us could breathe, and then after we had gasped for air for a little bit, we started giggling some more. I managed to keep talking in a voice full of laughter, but I don’t know why I kept talking. "Hey, if you’re still hungry, after I beat the homeless guy…you wanna eat him?" Nova nodded, because he couldn’t respond with words since apparently my question was the funniest goddamned thing he’d ever heard. We both started laughing for another ten minutes of distortedly slow time. Eventually, I’d have to get up and start walking towards the old, unusable train tracks, but I figured with time moving so slowly that I could literally feel the time-space continuum ripping like a stomach ulcer, we’d have plenty of time to get there if we power walked.
