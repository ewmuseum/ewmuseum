Butterflies and Hurricanes - The Redemption of Cereal Killer

15 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-04-15 Part One “Change everything you are, and everything you were.” Once upon a suplex, there was a boy born with conflicting destinies. He grew up at an idyllic pace in the middle of middle-class suburbia, USA, yet he was never fully at ease with himself. Something within him was preventing him from achieving his full potential. At times dynamic and at times sullen, young Frank was diagnosed as being a hyperactive child with an attention deficit that would have shamed a goldfish. He showed great aptitude for spatial awareness and all things athletic, but preferred nothing more than to sit in his room and invent a new world around himself. So often as he grew up he’d be picked for the soccer team, or be expected to excel in Little League, but something would hold him back. His interest would seem to drop away, and he’d go back to being alone again. His parents tried to get him to socialise with the rest of the children, and sometimes he would, but just as they got close enough to show genuine affection for him, he’d turn his back and lock himself away in his room. He’d go back to his imaginary friends, and the infinite possibilities of his hyperactive imagination. He got to high school with little fuss, and ploughed through it in the same old ways. The time spent in his room reduced as he discovered girls, and his natural athletic physique started to fill out, the boy starting to become the man. Still, elements of his propensity for being a loner lurked around the edges of his social scene. Nights were the worst, the darkness bringing on a darker frame of mind for him. Seasonal Affective Disorder was a nightmare for him, the winter months bringing on elements of Bipolar Disorder to his fragile psyche. He often joked that he had a different disorder for each season, and people laughed, but in the back of their minds they were worried about Frank. By the time he graduated and scored a place at college, Frank was almost normal. He had a steady girlfriend, a place on both the track and field team and the wrestling team, and his bouts of solitude and depression were getting further away and farther apart. Still, something was wrong. Every time it looked like he’d become the biggest track star or the best wrestler, he’d lay off making an effort. The same happened with his tests, and the discovery of an unlocked liquor cabinet brought some rare discipline from his parents, tied as it was to a spate of bad academic results. When he went to college, scraping in to his first choice by the skin of his teeth, Frank had something of an epiphany. He’d always thought he might be scared of success, and reasoned that was why he never really tried his hardest. Yet one late night out with his new friends he discovered what had been holding him back the entire time. He was simply terrified of failure. To go all-out, to give everything he had and then come up short would have been the final straw for him, no matter how well adjusted he was seeming at the time. Even when he seemed destined to win, to come out on top, he’d hold back and not give it his all. Frank worked out that it’s easier to accept failing a test if you don’t study for it than to fail it after six months of intensive study. With that state of mind anything was possible. Failed to make the team? Well, I was never really trying anyway; I was out drinking until four in the morning just before the try-outs. Frank’s life had been a series of excuses that he’d never even told himself about. It hit him hard, realising that he’d wasted so many chances to do something with himself, but at the tender age of nineteen the world was still at his feet. Still feeling crippled by his inability, or unwillingness to fully let himself go and try to excel at either sports or academia, Frank gave it up and became a college drop-out. His good looks, and uncanny resemblance of Justin Timberlake got him noticed in places, and so when he turned up to an indy show for the fledgling XUW promotion, a road agent clocked him almost immediately. ‘A handsome young kid with a great physique and a background in amateur wrestling? Let’s see if the trainers can make anything of him.’ Frank was offered a chance, and with nothing better to do, he took it. The agents had not made a mistake, as everything the kid did seemed to turn to gold. Before long XUW got a proper television deal, and Frank, wrestling as Cereal Killer, was performing in excellent matches just before the cameras started rolling. The crowds loved him, and soon he was Cereal Killer, King of the Dark Match. ----------------------------------------------------------- “Where’d the room go? Why’s my mouth taste of dried vomit? Why can’t I see?” asked Cereal Killer. Well, that’s what he tried to ask, but it came out as more of a hoarse cough. The mouth that was indeed caked with dried vomit was incapable of speech, abused as it had been the previous night. CK had yet to open his eyes, mainly out of fear, but his mind was swimming with a hundred and one questions and emotions. Sadly, ninety eight of them were being crowded out by the splitting headache that was insisting on stomping around his cranium, bullying the lesser questions into submission. Ah, there had been singing… no, not singing, screaming. There had been screaming, and a row. He’d had an argument with his fiancée. They’d managed their way through so many years without a hint of trouble, but last night it had all gone wrong. CK winced and tried to move a hand up to his head, but it was too much for him. They’d had an argument, and there’d been some screaming, and from the smell of the place there’d been some vodka too. The main question that remained was why? Ah yes, the wrestling… ----------------------------------------------------------- He got his big break at the dawn of the new XUW era. At Mayhem, the first ever weekly televised show, Jack “The Ripper” Nobbledy was given a tag title shot, but didn’t have a partner. The Ripper was an outcast, a shadow of the man he would turn out to be. He turned to another outcast, an untried rookie. The future XUW Prince of Darkness chose Cereal Killer, still wrestling under the gimmick of ‘King of the Dark Match’, for his partner. After their improbable victory, CK affirmed the team relationship by jokingly calling them the Army of Darkness. In retrospect, it was the worst decision he ever made. Shedding his ‘dark match’ stigma, CK found a new hook on which to hang his career. It was a natural progression for him; as a good looking young wrestler with an uncanny resemblance to Justin Timberlake, XUW’s Teen Heartthrob was born. The speed with which the success of the gimmick grew astounded everyone, especially CK. However, he found himself in the most unusual situation. He was teamed with the most evil man in the federation, and they held the XUW Tag Titles together. Frank did everything he could to be more and more sadistic, to get The Ripper to accept him as truly being worthy of his time, but Jack just treated his partner like a joke. CK thought of trying to face up to Jack, but he’d stare up at the behemoth, his eyes frozen, his heart ten times as cold, and the resolve of the youngster would fade away. Talking to The Ripper was like that. Still, the alliance continued. The Ripper seemed to want to keep their tag team going, which had formed into an emerging stable. Sometimes Frank thought he was just being kept around so The Ripper could see him fail. He didn’t want to fail, but the odds were starting to stack up against him. His partner seemed to dislike him, the teams gunning for their belts were more experienced, and there were several of them. It was getting desperate. Cereal Killer turned to the bottle. Ah, the sweet taste of vodka. The taste of your troubles becoming insignificant; the taste of shirked responsibilities; the taste of premeditated failure; the taste of bile kicking up from your stomach, flooding your mouth and leaving you unconscious on the floor of the bathroom. There’s nothing quite like it. Needless to say, Cereal Killer’s drunkenness ended up costing his team the titles. He was eliminated first in an unusual six man match, and from there on in, The Ripper was doomed, fighting alone against four men determined to take him out. CK expected to be torn apart, to have his partner be mad at him, to have elicited some reaction. Instead, The Ripper merely nodded at him, and somehow it was worse. ----------------------------------------------------------- Feel sorry for Valerie Rose Dubonnet. Not for the car that ploughed into her at a busy crossing, robbing her of sight in her left eye. Not for the freak fishing accident that took away both sets of her grandparents at the same time. Not even for having parents too stupid to realise they gave her the initials ‘VD’, forcing her to use her middle name at every opportunity. No, feel sorry for Valerie Rose Dubonnet, for she was the fiancée of Frank, the XUW legend known as Cereal Killer. “By that noise I’m guessing you’re awake now.” Cereal Killer looked up after the voice of his fiancée filtered through the waves of nausea, pain and vodka. “I am somehow still alive. This is both unexpected and entirely unfair.” Valerie smiled at him. They’d argued, but she was pretty confident she could talk him round. “Well, if you’re feeling bad, you should get some help. Personally I always find that it makes me feel better to have a shower, then brush my teeth, and then go to the bedroom for a good long session of hot and passionate amazing sex.” “Amazing sex?” asked CK, incredulous. “I don’t think I could even manage mediocre jerking off right now.” Valerie threw back her head and stormed off towards the kitchen. Frank could be so uncaring sometimes. I mean, she’d only entered him into a little wrestling tournament! ----------------------------------------------------------- After losing the tag titles, Cereal Killer continued drinking, but it wasn’t helping him. He’d dodged another chance at success, and made do with intentional failure. He’d faded in to the background, but he was getting impatient. The crowd still loved XUW’s teen heartthrob, but he felt a yearning for something else. His mind raced through the possibilities, conjuring up new personas as quickly as he had always done in his room, alone, sequestered away from the rest of the world. The Heartthrob went the same way as the King of the Dark Match. He was now the Emerald City Assassin. Two weeks later he was a former soldier from the jungles of Borneo. One week later and he was an old school wrestler. Monday he was the Teen Heartthrob again. Thursday he was the drunkard. Monday he was the assassin… and the young dog with a lot to prove. Thursday he was the Teen Heartthrob… and the Hardcore Legend… and the Next Big Ting… and out of the Army of Darkness. Whether it was a victory for The Ripper or for Cereal Killer is debatable, but if it was CK’s idea to end his tenure in the AoD that way, then it came at a terrible cost. His memories of loneliness had been amplified a hundredfold each time he stared into the gaping abyss of The Ripper’s eyes. The time spent with his imaginary friends was suddenly his friendliest memory. He thought he was doing well, but to everyone else, he was crumbling. One psych referral later and Cereal Killer took a few weeks off to calm down. He came back refreshed. He was eager to please and the Teen Heartthrob was once more back on centre stage. His undoubted wrestling talent was pushed to the limit as he went on a brief rampage, forcing his way into contention, and winning the XUW Cruiserweight Title. ----------------------------------------------------------- “You were a Cruiserweight though, right?” asked Valerie. “Yes and no. I won the title, but then I renamed it to be the TV Title.” “Why?” “Why not? It seemed like a fun thing to do at the time. I had this great new gimmick thought up. I’d be the King of Television. Each time I’d fight I’d come down as someone different from classic TV. It was going to be brilliant...” CK looked down at his feet, and stopped talking. “And…” proffered Valerie. “And I went and lost my first match as champion. I was so hung up on my gimmick that I forgot who I was facing. I didn’t even pay attention. I was pinned in about a minute.” CK fell silent again. It had been his subconscious that had done it. Once more the demons had crept into his mind and he’d not wanted to fall short. The comfort zone expanded and he lost the title. It didn’t count as a personal loss though, as ever, because he’d not been trying. At least that’s what he told himself. “So what happened then?” ----------------------------------------------------------- Cereal Killer had been beaten. He had been given chance upon chance and blown them all, just when it looked like he was going to step out of the gargantuan shadow that The Ripper cast across the federation. In the absence of CK, the Army of Darkness had gone from strength to strength. New recruits had been brought in, and The Ripper had been involved in a classic feud for the Submission Title. In a few short months he cemented his place at the head of XUW, winning the World Championship by defeating the previously unstoppable Sakie. The beginning of Jack’s reign of darkness was the final straw for Cereal Killer. He’d sunk to the bottom while his tag partner had grown out of all recognition. He’d done a Jannetty, and then some. Cereal Killer, once more, quit XUW. Some things are like a drug, though. He had to return. He had to try and prove to himself that failure was not the only option. He had to prove to himself that he was not just a quitter, someone to either give up on wrestling completely, or go to a lesser fed and be a big fish in a small pond. He still had his pride. A couple of months and two dozen gimmicks later, he didn’t even have that. ----------------------------------------------------------- “So why did you enter me?” asked CK. “Well, you always talk about the old days before the injury, and you said you’re healthy now, so why not? I know sitting around here can’t make you happy.” “You know, it really can. Give me a few video games and a bottle of vodka and I’ll be fine, really. I’ve still got the money that XUW gave me.” “Ummmm, about that…” “What?” said CK accusingly, turning around to face his fiancée. Valerie Rose kicked her feet around idly, trying not to tell him. “What is it, Valerie?” “Well, after you parted ways with the company, they didn’t give you the pay off you thought you received. Yeah, I know they still send you money, but it’s because they think you’re still on the roster. Hell, they still think you’re the Cruiserweight Champion!” “How do you know this?” “I have my sources.” Cereal Killer pondered this, his mind racing. Bypassing the voices and the friends waiting on the fringes of his vision, always swimming there, ready to take over at a moment of weakness, he thought back to his last days in XUW. The place had been going to hell, and since then the federation had been run by a couple of psychotics, a giant mute, an alcoholic, a small child and, well, a woman. None of them would be good with numbers or logic, and so paying someone no longer on the roster might not have seemed like a crazy thing to do. “What does this mean?” “Well, they could kill your contract at any time. You’ve not even shown up for nearly two years.” “WHAT?” yelled Cereal Killer. “DO THEY KNOW WHO I AM?” “Calm down Frank, it’s just that…” is as far as she got. “Do they know who I am? I’m Cereal Killer! I’m the Teen Heartthrob! I’m the King of the Dark Match! I’m the Emerald City Assassin!” “I think you should calm down and…” “Calm down? Do you know who I am? I’m the Mombassan Assassin! The Flair from Delaware! The Alaskan Nebraskan!” “That doesn’t even make sense, Frank, you’ve got to…” “Doesn’t even make sense? DO YOU KNOW WHO I AM?” Valerie was starting to think that mentioning the wrestling again had not been a good idea. ----------------------------------------------------------- One last chance. One last effort. One more match. He planned and plotted and he picked it well. The Ultra Violents were taking over the federation. They were taking down each and every team that stood before them. The only ones to not back down were the Lunchroom Patrol. In a vicious, brutal match, the two teams took one another to the limit. The audience couldn’t have been bigger, the stage more important. Cereal Killer made his comeback. No gimmick now, just a man out for vengeance. He took with him a band of his compatriots. The weak, the beaten down, the ones who had been broken by the Ultra Violents or by the grindstone that XUW come sometimes be. He interfered in the match, attempting to do the unthinkable and take down the great Ultra Violents. For a moment it looked like he might have done it, but it was not to be. The one time he really, genuinely tried to win, the odds were too badly stacked again him. It ended that night with a stack of tables, gasoline, thumbtacks and matches. That fateful night in January 2003, the night when his former tag partner, Jack “The Ripper” Nobbledy won his second World Title… …that was the last time Cereal Killer was ever seen in an XUW ring. ----------------------------------------------------------- “Yeah, can you come round soon, doctor. He’s been at it for nearly an hour now.” “An hour?” ranted Cereal Killer. “Do you know who I am? I’m the Hour of Power, the Tower of Flower, the Jawa from Okinawa! People tremble in my footsteps because I’m the King of Bling, I’ll do my thing and I might even sing. I’m the Devil’s Own, the Lost Angel and the Generic Dark Gimmick. I’m the Green Goblin, the Turquoise Troll and the Weak Brown Water Buffalo! Don’t you even know that? Do you know who I am?” She’d opened the floodgates, but this time it was worse than before. The loss to the Ultra Violents and subsequent ten months of rehab had shaken his psyche to breaking point, but he’d kept it in check so long as he wasn’t allowed to wrestle, so long as she didn’t bring back more of the memories than he wanted to be rid of. “I’m Dumbo the Elephant, Bingo the Dog and Asbo the Chav! I’m Terry McBungle, the mild mannered accountant with a doctorate in tallying numbers and Kicking Your Ass! I’m Cereal Killer, I’m the Master of the Cereal Box Effect, I’m the Emerald City Assassin!” “Calm down Frank. You don’t have to do this. You can pull out of the tournament. We’ll find a way.” “Pull out?” laughed Cereal Killer, a maniacal look blazing through his eyes like a million watt light bulb. “Pull out when I’m having the time of my life? Do you know who I am?” “I know who you…” “I’m Cereal Killer! I’m the Italian Stallion, the Nicaraguan Bagman, the Mexican Lexicon! I’m the Australian Alien, the Russian Cushion, I’m the Iranian Albanian!” “That makes even less sense than…” “It doesn’t make sense? Do you know who I am?” Valerie was at her wit’s end. She thought that she had known Frank, but it had turned out that nobody had known who he was, not even himself. “Frank, you don’t need to wrestle Collins. If you just…” “Do you know who I am? Well, do you?” “Yes, but…” “You don’t know who I am! Do you? Do you know who I am?” Barely pausing for breath, the lunatic continued. “I’m the Lost City of Atlantis, I’m the Lost Treasure of the Sierra Madre, I’m the Lost Ten Cents That You Find Down The Back Of The Sofa When You’re Looking For The Television Remote Control!” Valerie started to cry. “I’m going to show these people! Do they not know who I am? I’m the man who’s going to take them all down. Collins? Core Wrestling? He’ll be more like Whore Wrestling When I’m done with him, pimping out his own Mom to feed his crack habit after turning to drugs following his shameful loss to the Swiss Bliss, the Chilean Sicilian, the Light Breeze from the Outer Hebrides! Oh, they’ve fought me before. I know all about their thumbtacks and their flaming tables. I know all about pain and suffering. I know all about the snapping of bones and the tearing of sinews. I’m the Broken Cage, I’m Satchel Paige, I’m Murderous Rage! Do you know who I am?” Cereal Killer dropped down to his knees. He’d kept himself in shape. If he tried with all he had, then he’d have a good chance. It was just about letting go, and allowing the voices, the friends to take over. He’d find strength in them, and he’d be free. “Are you finished, Frank?” asked Valerie, between sobs. “I’m the Burger King,” said Cereal Killer, evenly. “I’m Colonel Sanders, I’m the Pizza Hut. Dah dah dah dah dah dah. I’m loving it.” …and he was… …and somewhere in the deep unknown, a man with eyes colder than liquid hydrogen was sitting back, alone, and smiling.
