Deception for a Reverie, Part I: Fantasy Becomes Reality

2 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-02 'You wanted to see me?' The female voice asked from behind the closed door. 'Mom?' I asked back. 'Yes.' She no more than finished that, and I opened the door to my bedroom, pulled her in, and closed the door, locking it behind me. 'What's the excitement about?' She asked. She seemed to be a bit worried for me at the moment. I could see it in her semi-squinted eyes, accompanied with the slight gap in between her lips. Without explaining myself, yet, I moved over to my bedside night stand, pulled open the lone drawer, and pulled out an envelope that I had already opened. I closed the drawer, and turned back towards her. 'You see this?' I asked, knowing the obvious answer to ensue. She looked at me for a few moments as if to ask, without asking, if I was being serious. 'Well of course I see it Kaden. What is this all about?' She asked, the worried look on her face and tone in her voice accompanying the question. I once again took the silent approach for a few more seconds. My smile, although making no noise at all, broke the silence. 'This is the letter I've been waiting for.' I started to pull the letter from the envelope as I finished my statement. Seeming to not fully understand, the slight cock in her head telling me it all, I didn't bother trying to fully explain it. Instead, I handed her the letter, and let her figure it out for herself. The thing of it was, I didn't have enough patience, and so I couldn't help but bug her while she read. 'Well?' I asked, as if she had finished already. 'Huh?' I continued. 'You done?' She probably wasn't even half way done, but I kept it up. 'Kaden!' She bellowed. My neck shrunk into my shoulders. 'Sorry Mom.' I said in apology. It wasn't but a few minutes later before the worried look that had once graced my mom's older then she looks face turned into nothing but smiles; the typical Mary Anne Jones-Calix that I've known for twenty-three years. 'Christopher Kaden Calix. My lord! Do you know what this means?!' She shrieked as she asked. I just put on a polite smile and nodded my head before answering. 'I read it first mom. I read it first.' I answered back. 'I know you read it first. But you didn't answer my question. Now do you know what this means?' She inched forward. I could tell her motherly instincts were telling me she was about to give me a hug, maybe even a kiss to the cheek would follow too. I sure hoped not. I sighed. I was trying to hold back the excitement I was feeling ever since I had received that letter. After all, I didn't want my father to get suspicious of anything. He would never need to know that I wanted to leave this "beloved" country of his, and the sport which "we" have loved so much, wrestling of the "real" freestyle kind, to pursue a career in sports entertainment wrestling in the United States. Because, that is what this letter did entail. I finally let me emotions go, for a brief moment. 'Yes! Holy shit, yes!' I shouted. 'My dreams of performing inside of the squared circle will finally come true with this.' I said. 'Watch your language.' She said sarcastically, smile on her face. That was then followed by what I was expecting; a hug, and yes, of course, the kiss on the cheek. I immediately wiped it off, like I was still six years old or something. The happiness was quickly cut shot, however. 'What are you going to tell your father?' She asked. I kind of hung my head for a moment before looking back up at her. 'That's the tricky part.' I said a kind of disappointment in my voice. 'It's kind of hard to figure out what words to say to the guy who's basically been holding me hostage in this place for 5 years longer than I should have been forced to live here.' I said, starting my tirade. 'I have nothing against you at all Mom, but you know how the guy is. You've seen what he does. You hear how he threatens me; I've watched him do it to you. And all the while I wonder how you've put up with it. I suppose you are in the same boat as me. Financial security. We are both lost without the guy's money.' I continued. Mom just nodded her head as she listened. 'I mean, "real" wrestling was fun and all while I was still young and impressionable, and it would have never lead me to finding the Athens Underground. Not to mention leading to me sending that wrestling tape to the states, and eventually getting something back in return. No I hope that I can capitalize, and make something of it.' Mom piped in with sort of a whisper. 'You will Kade. You will.' I continued. 'I should be able too, considering what all Mr. Michaels has already done. I haven't even met him, but he's already got me in the biggest wrestling event of the year, the GTT. I should get some kind of recognition for that thing. But it's all useless if I can't make it past dad's force field.' I finished. A few things that were obvious and some that I had been waiting to get off my chest were all out there. 'It's not always easy with your father. I guess my fault is that I love him. Always have. Then since you were born, I've always vowed to stick with it. Good times and bad. I'll probably continue still, even after you are on your way, in your own life, living your OWN dreams.' She said, with emphasis on my dreams. I nodded my head in acceptance. 'I'm tired of living his dream. I'm tired of doing something for him that he was never able to do. If it wasn't for his own dumb mistake, he might have made something of himself. I'm not going to be the same, and continue to make dumb decisions. I'm going to finally do something for myself…' I was saying, before the interruption happened. The door came busting wide open, and the lock on it had failed me. There, in the doorway, stood the ever so popular, need I mention father of the year, Alec Calix. 'What are you two talking about in here that is too important to be telling me?' He asked, as if he suspected something was up. I was in a panic. I didn’t know what to say. I dropped my head and just stared at the floor while I folded up the letter that Mom had handed back to me, and stuck it in my pocket. She took the opportunity to stick up for the both of us. 'Alec! Can't a mother and son just have a friendly conversation without you thinking we are plotting against you?' She asked, switching out of mother mode for a moment, and getting in the character of wife. He kind of smiled. 'Don't give me that Mary…' He was the one interrupted this time. 'Don't give me that Alec. I asked you a simple question, and you can't even answer it.' She paused for a moment, and her hands hit her hips. 'If you must know, Kaden was asking me about what would be some good gifts to get this girl he's had a crush on.' She said. She put a finger up to silence Dad before he even had a chance to open his mouth. 'He knew better than to ask you, because you would just tell him to take her to the bars, get her liquored up, and take her to a motel. Now if you'll excuse us.' She turned her back to him, and he shut the door behind him. However, the smile on his face never once faded away. I just looked at her. 'That… Was… Awesome!' I held up my hand, and the very rare occasion that Mom gives high fives, this happened to be one of them. She rubbed her high five hand after it was given, as if I may have hurt her. I grimaced a little for her, one of those looks that says I'm sorry, and she just nodded to me. 'Now, you know your father has that meeting scheduled for you two and the President of the Greek Olympic Committee in a couple of days, right?' She asked. I nodded my head. 'Yeah, I'm not really looking forward to it. Especially now that I know what I know, and can do what I know I can do.' I said, kind of vaguely. 'I know I could easily make it onto the team, I just don't want to.' Mom nodded her head. 'So, as I asked earlier.' Uh oh. 'What are you going to tell him?' I thought for a moment. 'That's the tricky part.' The scene fades to black.
