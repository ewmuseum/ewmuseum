Manifesto

26 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-26 The darkhorse. That’s a position that I’m used to occupying—especially when it comes to PTC. I came into GTT4 last year a virtual unknown, having been in WMW for only a few months. That was bad enough, you understand, to actually have to claim heritage from that embarrassment, that wretched abortion of a promotion. Fortunately, I’ve always been known for rising above whatever challenge I’m facing. I made it all the way to the bracket finals without batting an eyelash, no matter who thought I wouldn’t make it there. And in those days? Believe me, no one thought I would make it there. It wasn’t an easy road to hoe, getting to this point, that is. I took out a lot of people in GTT4 that were tournament favorites. That tends to make people sit up and go ‘who the hell is she? And why haven’t I heard of her before?’ That’s the essence of being who I am. The name Shadow—most people see it as some overused, trite goth moniker, right? But it’s more than that. It’s all about being the one that nobody expects to win. It’s about the element of surprise, it’s about slamming someone head-on with a freight train of talent that they didn’t even get the chance to hear coming… until it was way too late. That poses a slight problem this year. A lot of people already know who I am, they already know what to expect from me. All that means, though, is that like the shadows that warp when you walk along, changing distance from the streetlamp above you… I’ve got to adapt. That, also, is the essence of being who I am. You see… I don’t have the best of luck when it comes to being in tournaments. Even in WMW, I never won one. We won’t get into the reasons, mind you, but it never happened. I fumbled in the bracket finals of GTT4, I’ll admit that too. I lost in Global’s Master of the Mat semi-finals because I let emotion get in the way of professionalism. So when people predict me to win things? That’s what’s underlying those thoughts. Sure, she’s good, she’s talented… but she fumbles the important plays, to mix a metaphor. In WMW, I held that wretched title longer than anyone else ever had before me. I was the first WMW member to bring home a PTC title. In Global, I’ve only lost one match since I started there. One. Up against eighteen wins, I’m told that’s pretty impressive, considering I got beyond ten wins before I even picked up that one loss. But there’s still that one thing. That one thing that people always keep in mind when they’re considering my chances at winning: she chokes. I’m tired of choking. A few months in Global Wrestling can do a lot to change a person. I’ve found a lost paramour, gained a girlfriend, moved into a mansion, won the TV Title, lost it in a match against said girlfriend (that one loss I was talking about), won the International Title from a Global legend, gained a friend, lost him when he attacked my boyfriend, and in short, I’ve been through hell (though perhaps I shouldn’t try to explain how close to the truth that literally is). For quite a while, choking in a tournament has been the last thing on my mind. In fact, I wasn’t even certain that I was going to enter GTT5, but then Super Series came along, and at the end of the main event, there was this little piece of paper in my hand, this little ticket. Global Title shot… that’s prestigious enough. But there were a handful of words at the bottom of that ticket that drummed into my brain far more loudly that night: "This ticket also allows the bearer to claim a guaranteed GTT5 spot." It was a clarion call, and eventually, I gave in. I had to. You see, even though my name is known (if you’ll forgive the Biblical similarity of phrase), I’ve still got something that I need to prove. When you look at me, you see the almost. The potential that cracked under pressure. It’s time to change that. Oh sure, this is the bracket of death. A lot of people say so, but who cares what people say? I defied numbers last year, and I’ll do it again, because right now, this is what I need. With Global on break for December, and nothing else to do, I’m target-locked. But I’ve still read what that handful of people say. In fact, I’d like to address it. Round 1 upset? Not on your life. I want this. I’ll do whatever I have to do to fight through the odds, as hard as I can. I’m not going to choke because I simply can’t allow myself to do so, no matter who my Round 1 opponent is. He doesn’t matter. What matters is that I do this. I have to do this. This year, I’m not going home until I prove something—to myself, to everyone else, whatever. I’m not walking away until I have an answer. My quest starts here. I can’t go home without a grail.
