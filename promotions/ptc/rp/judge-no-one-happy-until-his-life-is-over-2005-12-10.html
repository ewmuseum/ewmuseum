Judge No-one Happy Until His Life Is Over

10 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-10 I "But that's not how it used to be, when the Jester sang for the King and Queen." - Don McLean. There are no maggots beginning to burrow through my mottled, tort skin. No frozen pupils staring up into the tops of my eyelids. My bowels haven't spluttered to a halt, spilling faeces, bile, and stomach acid into my boxers. Guess that means I'm not dead. Guess that means I won… convincingly, apparently. I decided to forgo the victory party, in case you were wondering where your invite was. I was never much of a storyteller, even when my kids could stand to be within hearing-distance of me. With every awkward stumbling through every page, I was waiting for their eyes to stutter to a close, before tip-toeing away. It's not that I can't read, as putting whatever assumptions you've made of me aside, I'm far from dumb, and am in no way an ignoramus. Maybe my reluctance to fill their heads with fairy stories, happy endings, and overly-optimistic morals was a subconscious way to toughen them up ready for life. Odds are my daughter will never find her Prince Charming. Last I heard, she'd shacked up with someone who played bass in some barely-audible band, who wore more make-up than her. She probably left him when I stopped returning my wife's calls to help pay her bail, since he was always broke. And it's safe to say my son didn't go on to emulate the stories of Knights of the Realm, and brave warriors. He married his life partner, Yannick, over the summer, and last I heard, was trying to rent a womb for their first child. But as I sit, reclining as the low, hazy sun solidifies the floating dusts clouds, one story refuses to cease narrating itself within my throbbing skull. Let me tell it to you. Dionysius had it all. He was the 4th century's version of whoever's rap video has the heaviest rotation on MTV. Were he walking around today, earning hundreds for every stride, there wouldn't be a VIP area in the United States that wouldn't get roped off for him. Cristal would flow a freely as the saccharine compliments lavished upon him by grovelling hosts. No wedding ring on a woman’s finger would mean she'd stay faithful when the rope was opened for her. He was the kind of guy every woman wanted to be with, and every man wanted to be(at to a pulp). People were paid just to hang off him, making him feel good about every aspect of his luxurious life. Mariah Carey would blush at the extravagance, while Jennifer Lopez would probably get a few ideas to surpass even the concept of paying someone to carry her coat around. I believe he was what you'd call "bling". A "playa". But one day, while Damocles was fulfilling his duty of verbally felating his master, Dionysius took exception… as those types tend to do when they're held up so high on their pedestal that paranoia creeps in, and they feel the need to convince people that they're "real". Trust me, if you can afford to release a record and video with muchos promotion just to tell people you're still real, then you're a) an idiot, b) deluded and c) as real as a Unicorn's ghost. He said, If you think I'm lucky, how would you like to try out my life? Primadonna fuck. The lapdog that he was, Damocles leapt that the chance, and was indulged beyond the wildest dreams of both his mind, and whatever lurked in his trousers. New sensations of taste and pleasure ignited previously dormant nerves. He could almost feel the power surrounding him heavy the air so that he could walk upon it. He thought, How could any man want to give this up? He thought, I'm the luckiest man alive. He thought, I don't want to give this up. And then he looked up. The sharpened sword's edge shimmered in the hanging candlelight, while the ever-fraying twine which held it counted down the clock until it was to plummet, embedding steel in skull. A sword hovering perpetually overhead, ready to sever life from body in one blow, was supposedly the price of life's every luxury. A far higher price than Damocles would consent to pay. Scrambling back down society's mountain as fast as obedient hands and feet would propel him, Damocles landed back at the feet of Dionysius. Judge no-one happy until his life is over. While our lives may seem too far apart for relevant comparison, the gun barrel pressed down against the crown of my skull acts as a comparative… for me, at least. Only I'm not going to wuss out. I'm not going to return to the depths of this profession, wading through the detritus of the industry. Not only am I accepting the burden, I'm looking right up at it, taunting it, beckoning for it to split me in two. Damocles would forever be remembered as the man who turned it all down. Remembered as a sycophant, an ingratiator. A nobody. I'm now narrating to myself, no longer a reluctant story-teller. I say, You shouldn't hold the gun to your temple, as the bullet can ricochet off your skull, shoot up, and simply leave you a vegetable. I'd be great at dinner parties. I say, Nobody has the attention span to care about you in a coma. As it nestles into my thick, scruffy hair, the gun drips saliva down into my scalp. A fear of dentists that was never overcome with age means my mouth is a mess, and the chattering of teeth against the metal of the barrel triggered flashbacks of drills and scraping that almost let a nervous fluttering on the trigger blossom into a full-blown compression. A fatal premature ejaculation. With an exhalation of relief, I recoiled the firearm, and rapidly put the safety back on. The frantic metallic clunks mirrored the pounding resonance of my heartbeat, while I felt my brow moisten with a chill. And so as my own spit dribbles from gun to hair, I stand looking into the mirror, taking a sneak peak at how my last moments of life may look. The dramatic gesture somehow seems a little too… humble? I say, You look ridiculous. Although, admittedly, still better than my carpet would look following the event. I say, Is this really how you want to die? Do you really want to go through the bother of cleaning this place? Fixing it up? Because we all know that "Wrestler Found Dead In Home" will soon be overshadowed by "Wrestler appeared to live in squalor, and didn't even clean beer-can halos from his tables, or dust high into the shadowed corners for cobwebs". No offence to myself, but if I were to introduce my brain to a bullet first-hand, I'd choose to do it somewhere a little more habitable than my home. I say, This is the only thing you'll ever be known for… don't fuck this up. You'd think impending death would bring an almost comforting inevitability to things. You already know the ending. You only have a short time to live with the repercussions of your actions. It should be liberating… yet now the means in which I achieve the closing of my life's story has become my current bane. Although my living room has never been the most hospitable of places, nowadays it's just damn near hostile. A noose is draped over the back of the torn, dusty couch, finally freed from the door-handle, the light fittings, or anywhere else I'd surveyed. Tossed in the corner are the splintered remains of a broken stool, which tested my head-withdrawing reflexes when my contorting weight collapsed it. Laid out across the coffee table, presented on top of a years worth of half-read magazines, and today's bleak paper, are enough blades to shave a dozen Norsemen. It's amazing what you can pick up in the back street shops where there's no passing custom, only the specific clientele which seek them out. My left wrist still displays a trail of dashes, tracing the correct path along the veins, rather than across them. It's amazing what you can learn on the internet. All you need to know about killing yourself is there at the click of a mouse. My wife always used to say, The children need to internet for school. Or, All the other kids use it. Or, They'll fall behind. Never, They can talk to social delinquents who want to kill themselves because they only got a B minus on their history report. Knowledge is power. There were even chatrooms where those who're about to kill themselves can say their final words, their last verbal offering to the world, to others of a similar mindset. For a split-second, it may have been tempting. But although I may be meticulously planning my method of death for when I am eliminated from a wrestling competition, I'm not a weirdo. Nor am I that lame. But is that how I really want them to find me? Slashed up, after what I know will be a few unsuccessful cuts, drained dry like Kosher meat. I say, I need a drink. I mean, I'll finish the bottle. As I drop back into my chair, moulded to my increasing bulk, dust shoots up like a mushroom could, while a brain-grating creak sounds. With the adjacent kitchen seeming too far away, I swig from the bottle of medium-priced whisky, not caring to compare the searing taste with anything either cheaper nor more expensive. On the table beside me, accompanied by an empty cigarette carton and half-finished tin of cheap beer, sits my bottle of sleeping pills. Prescription, they are not. If there was ever an exit that was tailor-made to what proceeded it, I've found it. Filling every inch of my chair's groove, bottle in hand, yelling at my ever-disappointing sports teams, I can down pills like Tic-Tacs, and watch life back gratefully away from me. No fuss. No hassle. Minimal mess. Not… not really much of a statement at all. They'd say, Nobody really noticed anything, until the smell. Or, By the time we found him, the stray cats and dogs had finished off most of his leg. And then it comes to me, and I drop the bottle, letting its contents spill out over the dirty carpet. Heaving myself up, I stride across the living room, and yank the top drawer from the sideboard. Opened and promptly ignored bills, take-out menus, payslips, and everything that accumulates over the years is left strewn over floor and furniture, until I find the memory I buried under life's leaf-litter. At the bottom of the drawer, co-habiting the level only with a few old coins and paperclips, is where my story will end. Still in the dented frame, with cracked glass somehow holding together, is a photo of Gold Beach. Where I met my wife. Where I proposed. And where she told me it was over… for the last time. I begin to realise why it was hidden, as the memories crash like the beach’s waves back into my thoughts. I can almost smell the fresh, salty air coming in off the Pacific over the stale odour of my surroundings. I hear myself saying, I love you. I hear her saying, I do. I hear her saying, I don't love you anymore. I hear her saying, I'm leaving you. I feel myself actually care. It's then I know that I'll feel the air in my lungs, first hand, once more. The last sight I'll take in from its cliffs will be that of the Whales creeping perilously close to land, while the Eagles circle ahead, singing their dusk chorus to their audience below. And then, as the sun drops down over the Pacific's horizon, I'll follow. Not to rise the next day.
