Enter the Mother

30 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-30 The sun hid behind the clouds and still it was unusually warm on that late November afternoon. In the comfort of his own backyard, Wolfgang arched his back and cracked his neck. At thirty-eight years old, it was a nuisance for him to be toiling over an extended period of time. Wolfgang looked up at the gray sky above him and hoped that the gloomy cast overhead wasn’t going to spoil his day’s work. He slid the sleeves to his blue Nike sweater past his elbows and resumed his work. He picked up a pair of gold-colored pants – they were his Sentinel outfit – from the basin and, whipping it in midair, proceeded to drain it on the low cut grass. The golden pants were then hung on a wire that stretched for several yards along with many of Wolfgang’s clothes. "Have you heard of a Laundromat?" a voice asked him, approaching from inside the house. Wolfgang turned around and wiped a bead of sweat from his forehead, "Good afternoon, Chuck." Charles Stuart, Wolfgang’s boss and the owner of the Atlantic Indy Wrestling Federation, stood in marvel of his star wrestler. His eyes gazed upon Wolfgang’s humble roots – his low budget wardrobe, the stack of dirty laundry sitting beside the basin, and the three lines of wire stretching across his backyard supporting washed clothes. Charles knew Wolfgang was a hard worker ever since they had first crossed paths nearly fifteen years ago. Charles often remembered that day. He had caught Wolfgang trying to steal some money when he was working as a backstage technician. Being one of the few people to know Wolfgang personally, he opted to offer him a career choice. That was the beginning of the Sentinel. Wolfgang nodded and returned back to doing his laundry. Whenever he would set his mind on something, it was often very difficult to get him change his plans. Charles knew this and didn’t make any attempts. But some things shouldn’t be ignored. He just needed to wait for the right moment. Charles said, "I never got to congratulate you on your win against Kimbusa." "Thanks," Wolfgang replied, his attention occupied with scrubbing down a pair of blue jeans. Charles took a deep breath, taking in the scent of the mountain fresh Tide. He continued, "That was quite an achievement. It’s really going to put the AIWF on the map." Wolfgang turned around and glared. He whipped the jeans in midair and started to drain the water from them. Without a change in emotion, he responded, "It’s really great that the AIWF is getting the attention it deserves." "Watch it," Charles retorted, "You know how much this means to me and how much it should mean to you. That was a great win on your part. That’s all I’m saying." "I’d like to face him again," Wolfgang replied, recalling how the sumo seemed distracted during their fight. "It would be very invigorating to challenge him again, perhaps at full strength." "It’s certainly possible," Charles beamed with excitement. "I’ve been receiving several offers for your contract." "I’m not interested," Wolfgang replied coldly and returned to the basin. Charles nodded solemnly and looked over to the basin. He stared at the golden mask that Wolfgang was now washing – the mask of the Sentinel. It wasn’t very common for the owner of any business to try and sell his best prospect. Usually that practice came in the line of professional sports, but those instances were dependent on the future of the franchise. Rarely did an owner consider the best prospects for the individual. However, Wolfgang was not the ordinary franchise player and neither was Charles the ordinary owner. "It’s probably not the best business decision for me to try to talk you into venturing into greener pastures, but you really deserve this." "I’m fine the way things are now," Wolfgang responded, heavily scrubbing the Sentinel mask with a brush. "The way things are now?" Charles repeated, arching his eyebrows. "But don’t you want something better?" Wolfgang shook his head, "My parents never gave me more than what I needed and I love them for it. I have all I need." "You do know that when you defeat the Phantom Republican the offers are going to increase, right?" "I’ll cross that bridge when I get there," Wolfgang replied. "Right now I have a lot of things on my mind. One of those things is finding out how I’m going to beat Grand O’ Powell." Charles shook his head. He couldn’t understand why Wolfgang was refusing to accept better opportunities. He knew that Wolfgang needed them. Most of all, he knew that Wolfgang knew it as well. "Do you remember the day you became the Sentinel?" "Yeah," Wolfgang replied, emotionless. His decision to become a wrestler was a very rash one. He never cared for the sport before, nor did he believe he possessed the required physique and fighting ability to be successful in it. Instead, his success came on the reins of fighting with emotion. For the first two years as the Sentinel, he often fought without structure, often opting to overwhelm his opponents. Fighting with anger was the only way he knew how to cope with the loss of his wife, Abigail Moriarty. "Do you know what I was thinking when you told me you wanted to fight in a mask?" "That you had enough of the masked man gimmick?" Wolfgang joked, drawing a slight chuckle from Charles. "I was wondering why you wanted to wrestle in a mask. At first I thought it was because maybe you were unsure of what you wanted to do, or maybe you thought it would be interesting. But then I realized why you did it." "And what was that?" Charles took in a deep breath, "You were afraid of something. I can’t say what, but you were running from something. It was as if something was troubling you and you didn’t know how to handle it." "So now you’re a psychologist?" Wolfgang mocked him. "No," Charles quickly replied, "I know what happened with your wife and I’m terribly sorry about that." "How could you possibly know how that feels?" Wolfgang snapped at him. "You have your wife. You have your kids. You have your grandkids. Don’t fucking talk to me about sympathy for my cause when you have every fucking thing that was taken away from me!" Charles became enraged and slammed his fist on the basin and snatched the mask away from Wolfgang’s pruned fingers. "I think you’ve washed the mask long enough." Wolfgang didn’t respond. Instead he stared Charles down. "Maybe I don’t know how you feel, or how you’ve felt for the past fifteen years, but what happened to your wife was not your fault." "I cheated on her." "So?" Charles replied, shrugging his shoulders. "Men make mistakes all the time. You certainly aren’t the first or the worst to do it. Did you fuck up? Yes, I admit you did. But you cheating on her was not the reason Abigail is gone." Wolfgang kept his gaze fixated on the golden mask in Charles hands. Charles noticed it and crumpled the mask and threw it back onto the pile of dirty laundry. "Forget about the mask, Wolfgang. That isn’t you. You were trying to hide from something back fifteen years ago, and now you’re using the mask to hide from something again. What is it?" "I have no idea what you’re talking about," Wolfgang lied. "What are you hiding from this time?" Charles asked walking in on Wolfgang’s personal space. He had no problem playing the father role to Wolfgang after he had assumed that role ten years ago when Wolfgang’s father, James Moriarty, had died of a heart attack. "Are you still denying yourself the truth?" Charles peered into Wolfgang’s enraged eyes. In Wolfgang’s brown eyes, Charles could see the kind of anger that Wolfgang was keeping locked in over the past couple of years, refusing to let it all out. "No," Charles shook his head. "You’re not hiding from the truth. You’re hiding the truth from someone else." "She’s not ready." "Where’s Angela?" "I don’t know." "You don’t know?" Charles snapped. "I don’t fucking know," Wolfgang snapped back. "She never tells me where she goes." "How can you live like that?" Charles wondered. "She’s not ready." "She’s not ready, or you’re not ready?" "I’m…" Charles put his hand on Wolfgang’s left shoulder, "Go find her. Stop being the Sentinel. Stop running away. Leave the mask behind and realize that there are more important matters here. Find her. You don’t want to lose Angela, too, do you?" Wolfgang shook his head, "Of course not!" # I wonder what dad’s doing, Angela wondered as she rested her head on her palm. Her chestnut wavy hair dangled down in front her eyes, hiding her big brown eyes from the world. Furrowing her eyebrows in a moment of annoyance, she stared at the sea of papers in front of her on the dogwood table. She had decided to help Cassidy with Seconda Vita by offering to take care of some paperwork. She didn’t know the first thing about adoption papers, medical reports, or even building by laws, but she figured she could at least help him keep organized. It was the least she could do after all he was doing for the children. Her good intentions went unnoticed as Cassidy, dressed in navy blue Dockers and a Ralph Lauren shirt, walked into his den. After a brief moment of surveying the mess of papers on the desk, Cassidy stared Angela down in a silent rage. Angela had never seen him so upset. She responded, "I thought it would-" "What the fuck are you doing?" Cassidy interjected. "-be a good idea…" Angela replied, stopping in mid sentence to comprehend the situation. "I’m sorry. I promise I’ll fix this up." "Do you have any idea what these papers are?" Cassidy asked, hurrying to pile up all the papers on the desk. "Those kids are getting a second chance and I can’t have just anybody looking at all this delicate information." Angela stood up and helped Cassidy with the papers. Cassidy quickly stopped her. "I’m serious. No one is allowed to see this." "There isn’t anything here," Angela informed him. "I didn’t see anything on Jimmy. Who adopted him?" Cassidy slammed his fists down on the table, sending some of the papers fluttering to the floor. "Are you fucking kidding me? I’m out here trying to give these children the best time of their lives because no one else cares. I have thirty four fucking lives I have to be responsible for and you’re here trying to figure out why I don’t have paper work for one guy?" "Stop it," Angela pleaded. "I just wanted to help. I thought you needed help." Cassidy took a deep breath and calmed down. "I’m sorry. You have to understand that there is a lot of delicate information here. If you are wondering about why Jimmy doesn’t have a file it’s because it’s all forwarded. It goes to his new adoptive parents and to the hospital. That’s what happens with all the kids." "I see," Angela replied. "I’m really…" "If you don’t believe me," Cassidy said, "I can prove it to you." He extended his hand and led Angela out the door. She protested, "Wait!" Cassidy turned around. He said, "What is the matter? You want me to prove it to you? I will. I’ll take you to the hospital right now and show you where all the records are." Angela shook her head. "Really, it’s ok." Cassidy ignored her and dragged her through the hallways by the sleeves of her brown top, inviting stares from the children. "I’ll show you." Angela finally managed to cling herself to one of the door knobs, anchoring herself to the doorway with her sandals. She yelled, "Stop it!" Cassidy dropped her hand. "I believe you," Angela replied, out of breath. "I’m sorry," Cassidy responded. He approached her and embraced her. "I just want you to trust me." Deep in Cassidy’s embrace, Angela responded. "I believe you." Behind Angela’s back, Cassidy smirked. If only she knew what really happened to Jimmy and to all the other children, he thought. # Karla crouched down and pulled the chest across the cement pavement with all her strength. She was used to having others, mostly men, do her work for her. Thus, every little piece of labor was that much more strenuous. The enormous black chest was heavy on its own. It was made heavier by the presence of a dead body. Karla stopped her toiling to roll the sleeves on her green blouse up. It was late November and she wondered where all the coldness was. Maybe it was in her heart. "Can I help you?" a voice called out from the street. Karla was almost startled and slowly turned around. It was the security guard, Watson. He was one of the newest hires, having been hired to a higher status mostly based on his years of service. He was very chubby and jovial – a regular Santa Claus. He found it odd that Karla was dragging a heavy chest from the side entrance of the hospital. He approached her, but not with caution. He was smitten with her. "Good afternoon," Karla greeting him with a seductive smiled. Whether she knew that Watson liked her or not, Karla assumed that every man wanted her. She was usually right. "Good afternoon," Watson replied shyly, tipping his hat. "Do you need help with that?" "Oh yes," Karla replied, pretending to be a damsel in distress. "I’m moving some of my personal things back home. You know, to give the office a more professional look." Watson smiled at her like a buffoon and crouched down to pick up the chest. The chest was heavier than he imagined. He struggled, "Wow this chest is heavy. What do you have in here, a dead body?" Karla laughed and playfully pushed him away. She said, "You’ve been watching too many movies haven’t you?" Watson returned the laugh. He knew that situation was impossible. Karla was beautiful, smart, and sexy. She was the founder and president of the Second Chance hospital. There wasn’t a more perfect woman in the world. "It certainly gets to you." "I’m really glad you showed up," Karla told him. "Oh?" Watson replied, nearly stumbling over his own feet. He chuckled, trying to wave it off. "Yes," Karla replied, gripping his chubby arms. "You’re much stronger than the other security guards." Watson laughed, "Stop it, missus Karla." "No, I’m serious," Karla insisted. "You could do a lot with that strength - a lot of ways to hold a woman." Watson was beginning to feel a bit overwhelmed. "Aye, yes I could…" "I bet you have any woman you want," Karla guessed. "Am I right?" Watson laughed. "It’s not like that." Karla peered at him. "But you seem like such a great guy. You’re handsome and strong – what’s not to like?" "I’m just being very selective," Watson responded, which was more a cover for ‘I’m too much of a loser to get a girl’. Karla leaned in and surprised Watson with a kiss. "I’m sure you’ll find her." Watson cleared his throat and with final lunge, managed to get the chest into the back of Karla’s van. Exhausted, he wiped a bead of sweat from his forehead. "There you go," he said, out of breath. "That was pretty heavy." "I’m meeting someone very important later," Karla explained walking over to the driver’s side. "Who is it?" Watson asked with the curiosity of a love struck puppy. Karla got inside her car and turned the engine on. Over the roar of the engine, she replied, "My daughter." Watson furled his eyebrows. This was certainly big news for him. "Your daughter?" he asked, "I had no idea you had a daughter." Karla smiled, "She’s been living with her father mostly, but now she’s coming home." "That’s very good news," Watson beamed. "I hope everything turns out all right." "It will," Karla replied. She threw him a kiss and drove off leaving him with some encouragement, "You take care of yourself, handsome." Watching Watson and the Second Chance hospital fade into the distance in the rearview mirror, Karla feverishly began to wipe her mouth and cough up a lung into an empty cup. After she had hacked enough spit and nearly vomited, she threw the cup out of the moving van. "My spirits that was gross," she complained. "Thank the spirits that was one stupid fucker." The worst of the day was over for Karla, now came the best part. It was time for mother and daughter to reunite. # The black Land Rover screeched to a halt in front of the Sam Goodies store, inviting stares from onlookers. Wolfgang nearly tripped over his own feet as he ran around the truck and stormed in the store. Inside, his abrupt entrance drew curious stares from the customers, but they quickly resumed their business. Wolfgang ran up and down the aisles, looking left and right frantically. There was no sign of Angela. She isn’t here, he said to himself. Wolfgang walked back into his car and sighed. She has to be somewhere, he told himself. She hasn’t left the town yet. Gripping his steering wheel tightly, Wolfgang closed his eyes trying to remember all the conversations he had with his daughter. Think Wolfgang, think! He urged himself. # Cassidy watched the kids play in the backyard of the Seconda Vita hospital through the window of the nursery. He almost dropped the pitcher of water he was holding when his phone rang. He answered, "Hello?" "Where is she?" "Karla," Cassidy replied, "this is unexpected." "Please don’t waste my time," Karla warned him. "Where is Angela?" "She’s here," Cassidy informed her. "What’s going on?" "I’m right outside the door. I’m going to see her." Now Cassidy dropped the pitcher spilling the water all over the wooden floor, "What?!" "You better get her ready and get everyone out." "No!" Cassidy protested. "You can’t do this right now. She’s not ready!" "Not ready?" Karla fumed. "You’ve had weeks to get her on your side." "It’s not that," Cassidy tried to explain picking up the pitcher from the floor. "This is going to be a big shock to her. You have to let me prepare her for it!" If Cassidy had a video phone, he would have been able to see Karla raise her eyebrows in curiosity. "Do you have feelings for her?" "What?" Cassidy asked, "That’s ridiculous! I’m just saying you need to break the news easy to her." "You better not have any feelings for her," Karla warned him, "She’s only fifteen. Now bring her to the living room." Cassidy hung up the phone and walked through the gray door to the backyard of the Seconda Vita complex. He sighed as he slowly approached Angela who was sitting on the pavement against the wall. It wasn’t Angela’s reaction to Karla being her mother that concerned Cassidy. It was that her reaction might cause her to leave Seconda Vita. He couldn’t let that happen. She was this close to becoming fully enrolled with his society. She continuously spent more time with him at the church than she did back home with her father. "Angela," Cassidy said taking a seat. "I…" Angela began before she was interrupted by Cassidy taking a hold of her hand. "Do you remember why you came here?" he asked, staring off into the group of kids oblivious to their conversation." "Sort of," Angela replied. She barely recalled why she had run to him, but she knew that she was emotional when she did it. "You wanted me to help you find someone," Cassidy answered for her. "That’s right!" Angela nearly screamed. Her eyes lit up, "Are you saying you found someone?" "She’s out front." Angela remained in a silent stupor. She didn’t know whether to thank Cassidy or not. Instead, without saying a word, like a mind controlled robot, she rose to her feet, dusting off the dirt from her blue jeans and palming down her brown top, and walked into the church as if in a trance. Cassidy sighed and slouched against the wall. This is it, he thought. It was definitely over for her. A million thoughts raced through Angela’s mind as she took each jittering step to the front of the church. When she got to the living room, Karla was seated on the couch. Karla looked over at her and immediately rose to her feet. She knew who she was and more importantly knew that Angela wouldn’t know that. Thus, she played the role of being kept in the dark. "Hello," she managed to speak. Angela arched her eyebrows, "I remember you." Karla nodded. "You do look familiar," she lied. Angela slowly approached Karla. However, she did walk towards her but instead circled her like a mountain circling a buffalo, unsure of whether or not she could go for the kill. "You were there with my father." "That’s right!" Karla feigned a sudden eureka of memory. "He found you all passed out. I’m glad to see you’re all right." "Do you know my father?" "I know him," Karla admitted. For once she was telling the truth. "Why did you do it?" Angela demanded of her. This caught Karla by surprise. "Do what, sweetie?" "You know what you did!" Angela yelled. "You’re responsible for my mom leaving my dad!" Karla gasped, and a tear rolled down her cheek. She would have made a great actress if her love wasn’t in science and nature. "What are you talking about?" Angela dug out the photograph of her father with Karla and shoved it in front of her face. "You had an affair with my father, didn’t you? You were fucking him around when he was a married man. How could you do that?" Karla could see that Angela was furious and her own voice began to crack. Karla shook her head, "Please you have to understand something, Angela." "You know my name?" Angela asked, bewildered. Karla assumed that knowing her name would have been a given if Angela already knew that much about her. This sudden revelation gave her new material to work with. "I should know your name," Karla said, her voice cracking. "I helped name you." Angela’s eyes grew wide and she slowly stepped back, stuttering, "No, what are you trying to say?" "I’m so sorry," Karla cried, "I should have told you this earlier." Angela shook her head. She was beginning to fear the worst. Thus, she cried and screamed in denial. "No, no it’s not true." "Your dad did have an affair with somebody." "Stop it! It’s not true!" "Your mother did leave because of that." "Stop it! Stop it!" Angela cried, she didn’t want to hear the awful truth she knew was coming. "But it wasn’t who you think," Karla told her, tears rolling down her cheeks. She caught a tearful Angela in her embrace and held her tightly. "Please don’t say it," Angela whispered to her. "Don’t say it." "The person you think is your mother," Karla told her. "Please," Angela begged, crying. "It’s not true." "…isn’t your mother." Karla started to sob. She couldn’t hold her tears back anymore. She had fought her rage and depression for over fifteen years. For fifteen years she watched in the shadows, watching the girl who was her daughter live her life without her. But no more. She wasn’t going to let her daughter live on without her anymore. Angela returned the vice grip hug and clutching Karla’s green blouse, she whispered into her ear between sobs, "Please don’t do this to me." The truth was she wanted to hear it. But how could she be able to deal with this revelation when she was convinced her entire life that someone else was her mother? "Please…" "I’m your mother," Karla cried out repeatedly, hugging her tightly. I’m your mother.
