The Hustler Returns

14 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-14 The Hustler Returns This bar was particularly smoky. The hazy smell of cigarettes, cigars, and beer floated through the air, infecting the eyes and noses of all who enter. It seems to be the gang of regulars that usually go to every bar such as this. In every bar or pool hall you’ll ever enter, there are the regulars. These are the men and women who spend four nights a week here. They consider themselves close friends, even though they never see each other outside this environment that tends to smell like that carnival mixture of vomit and sawdust. Out there, they are the worker bees, always doing what they are told. Inside, they were the bosses. They were the power clique that would hustle the newcomers out of their money. This was a group that would protect each other if someone got out of hand. They were their own bouncers. In this bar, they have their own laws, where they can be the judge and jury. Four men gather around the two pool tables in the center of the room. A table with mugs and a few pitchers of beer is off to the side; most of the pitchers are empty. At the bar, a lone bartender leans over, intently watching the group’s pool games. This is when the door opens, and everyone in the bar stands and watches, as Snake Eyes enters and takes a seat. Snake Eyes doesn’t look out of the ordinary. There are keys to hustling that he has come to live by. The number one rule is not to look like a hustler. Don’t look too rich, or they will know something is up. Don’t look to poor, or they will know you are trying to play them for all they are worth. You have to look conspicuous, but inconspicuous; you must walk a tightrope with regards to the clothing you wear. Snake Eyes is wearing a pair of dirty old jeans and a white Tee shirt. His long hair is pulled back in a pony tail behind his head, and a pair of sunglasses cover his eyes. The second rule of hustling is to find the right person to hustle. Odds are, you won’t find them in a place such as this. Usually, the goal is to drive past bar after bar until you find one with a cherry red corvette or mustang or firebird outside. These are the cars of the ignorant youth; college students living on their parent’s dime. They drive their nice cars up to the bars in the hopes that when they bring the drunk girl out, she will be so impressed with his car that she will offer him fellatio as soon as they get in. These are the kids who will be trying to impress the girls with their pool playing skills, so when they are challenged, they can’t back down. Their libidos won’t let them. Then, once they’ve lost, they get angry about being hustled and challenge again. Long story short, on a good night, you’ll be driving that cherry red corvette home, while he calls mom and dad for cab fare. For sure, this bar that Snake Eyes is in now; this is the last place he’d try to hustle. On the same note, he is low on gas, and in this area of the country, hustling is easier than finding an ATM. Snake Eyes downs a shot of tequila, then grabs a beer and turns, watching the men play pool. None of them seem overly talented, which means any of them could be ripe for the hustling. One of the men, this one a bald guy with a long goatee and tattoos running up his arm, he misses the eight ball on an easy angle shot, and Snake Eyes takes the opportunity to go to work by smiling at the man and looking as if he is holding back laughter. Bald Guy: Whats so damned funny? I’d like to see you make that shot! Snake Eyes just shrugs, then walks up to the table, when he notices something he should have noticed when he walked in. The look in the men’s eyes; it wasn’t a normal look. It was one of familiarity. They knew him; this song and dance was pointless. Snake Eyes had been seeing this familiar look more and more since he started professional wrestling. Television exposure from IWZ had made his hustling more and more difficult. People knew who he was, now, so hustling became less and less his occupation, while legit gambling became more and more his bread and butter. At this point, missing the shot and looking foolish wouldn’t do anything for him. This was no longer a hustle, but rather a real contest. They already knew he was good, so why fake it. The man puts the eight ball back in the same position it was in, and replaces the cue ball. Snake Eyes looks around the table a bit, then lines up his shot and takes it, sinking the eight and setting up the perfect shot at the nine. The men look at each other and smile, then look up at him knowingly. Snake Eyes: I know you know who I am. Another man, this one with short black hair and a five o’clock shadow with a cigarette sticking out of it, he decides that now is a good time to step up and start the talking. In situations like this, the regulars will try to talk a big game, hoping to get inside the hustler’s head; maybe even scare him off completely. Lackey: Then you should just leave, ‘else we’re gonna take all your money. Hell, you probably won’t have a pair of pants when you leave. The men all start laughing, as Snake Eyes just smiles and laughs along. Aside from the big bald guy and his little lackey, there are two other men at the other table. One is a shorter fellow, wearing a backwards hat and a long leather trench coat. The other is wearing a wife-beater tee shirt with a beer stain on it, along with torn up stained jeans, looking fresh from a stereotypical trailer park. The big, bald guy walks over towards Snake Eyes and hands him a pool cue, then shifts his gaze towards his lackey, who quickly begins to set the table up for a game of nine-ball. Snake Eyes shrugs and takes the pool cue, then walks over and places his sunglasses on the bar. He turns around, and sees the two guys at the other table whispering to each other. Then he returns looking at the big bald guy, who smiles at Snake Eyes. Bald Guy: So, how much do we want to bet on this game? I should warn you, I am undefeated. No one has ever beat me. Trailer Trash Guy: Yeah, we’ve been coming here for years, and there isn’t a hustler that he hasn’t taken all the money from. You’d probably save yourself a lot of time and effort by just leaving your money on the bar and walking out. Leather Coat Guy: Hell, boy, I’ve never even seen ‘ol Duke there miss a shot! He gets the break, and the game is as good as over. The two men at the other table begin to laugh, as Snake Eyes grins a cocky smile and returns to looking at the big man, Duke. In hustling, Duke is what is known as a hustle-killer. They are a hustler’s worst nightmare. In their world, a hustler has to be a jack-of-all-trades. He needs to be good at cards, darts, billiards, and any other game of chance that might come up. A hustle-killer is someone who specializes in one of those events, and someone who will nine times out of ten beat a hustler. They could go on professional pool or poker circuits and make all kinds of money, but they find more enjoyment when they play as hustle-killers. Some do it for the glory, by beating a hustler and winning back the money of the people he hustled, they are gaining respect from everyone around. Others do it for the money; they don’t return it to the original loser, they just keep it themselves. Either way, they are bad news. Snake Eyes doesn’t look worried, however. He grabs the cue ball and begins tossing it in the air, eyeing the big man. While doubt is running through his mind, you’d never know it from his demeanor. Snake Eyes: How ‘bout we don’t play for money, just glory. This, naturally, causes everyone in the bar to bust out laughing. The lackey is nearly in tears on the floor, while the other two guys are holding themselves up against the wall. Duke: What’s the matter? Ya broke, or just afraid? Snake Eyes (with a cocky smile): No, I don’t want to take all your money. Duke: I only play for big money, Mister Hustler. Perhaps you should turn and walk out of here with your little tail between your legs if you aren’t going to be betting cash. Snake Eyes: Fine, how about we make it for one hundred thousand dollars. The grin and look of confidence never leaves Snake Eyes’ face. The snickers and laugher stops, as all of the men turn and look at Duke. His look of astonishment quickly turns to glee, as his eyes light up and he grins a big shit-eating grin, and he extends his right hand. Snake Eyes and the hustle killer shake hands, and Snake Eyes indicates that he will let Duke shoot first. Snake Eyes tosses the cue ball to Duke, who walks over and puts the ball down center on the pool table, as the lackey lifts the rack. Duke doesn’t even line his shot up. Instead, he simply smashes the cue ball as hard as he can into the rack. Pool Balls fly around the table, but not a single one goes in a pocket. Duke stands with his jaw wide open, as Snake Eyes looks around the table and shrugs, then looks up at Duke. Snake Eyes: The problem we have here is that you aren’t perfect, like your boys talked you up to be. Hype can be a powerful thing, but it can be just as ignorant. Snake Eyes lines up and shoots, sinking the one ball in a corner pocket. Snake Eyes: You see, no man is unbeatable. None. The minute anyone starts seeing themselves as the favorite in a match, or they start living into their own hype, they are doomed. Snake Eyes sinks the two ball, and sets up his next shot. Snake Eyes: Your little bitches around this room have been talking up a real good game for you. Hell, even you have talked a real big game, and yet here we are, with you clearly in a bad position. The four men stare wide eyed as Snake Eyes sinks the three ball in the side. Snake Eyes: The minute someone tells me that I can’t win, I become more enraged and more determined than ever. Without even looking, Snake Eyes sinks the four in a corner and lines the cue up with the five ball. Snake Eyes: Go ahead, keep seeing me as an underdog… I love to prove the ignorant masses wrong. Snake sinks the five and six both in one shot, as Duke leans against the wall, his hand over his face, as he sees one hundred thousands dollars slipping out of his hands. Snake Eyes: Now, truthfully, Duke, you probably were the favorite to win this match. In fact, I’d bet that nine times out of ten, you’d beat me. Snake Eyes sinks the seven, as Duke comes two shots away from losing for the first time ever. Snake Eyes: The problem is that I don’t care about the other nine times. When this is over, we could play again, and I’d win. Why? Cause thinking about what could be is nothing more than a distraction. Ignorance of what could be… that is bliss. Snake Eyes downs the eight ball, and eyes the nine. Snake Eyes: Now, I can sink this ball and walk out of here one hundred thousand dollars richer, or I could not… and walk out of here with twenty bucks for gas. I suppose the ball is in your court. Duke reaches back for his wallet and pulls out a twenty, and he hands it to Snake Eyes. Duke: You know, just ‘cause you beat me, don’t mean you’re gonna be beatin that Deville guy. Wrestlin and pool are two completely different worlds. He did win GTT3, ya know. Snake Eyes shrugs, and puts his sunglasses back on and turns back to Duke. Snake Eyes: There are two kinds if ignorance in this world. There is the good kind, that can allow a man to do anything he pleases; and there is the bad kind, that can lead a man to his downfall. I walked in here and found someone who thought he was unbeatable, and three guys who were doing nothing more than licking his asshole and being yes-men. Anyone is unbeatable if they always fight below their level. You boys… your ignorance cost you this match. Then there is the good kind of ignorance. The kind of ignorance that coined the phrase “Ignorance is bliss.” That phrase is true, but only part of the time. Like I said earlier, you may have beaten me nine times out of ten, but I wasn’t thinking about those other nine times. I was thinking about the one time I would beat you, and that’s all I needed… Just remember that. No matter how much of a favorite you are, always expect the unexpected. Especially when it comes to Snake Eyes. By the way, Duke… don’t feel too bad. You aren’t the only guy who is considered “unbeatable” who I plan to beat this week… Snake Eyes shoves the twenty in his pocket, then turns and exit’s the bar, as we fade to black. Fade to Black
