Christopher Camero Cable Pimp Joined: 18 Feb 2003 Posts: 58 Posted: Thu Apr 10, 2003 5:29 am Post subject: Global Wrestling Title Match RP#3 Regrets of yesterday Fears of Tomorrow Rob us of our moment My moment is now Dr. A.J. Hawthorne is still in Britain; telecommunications about the murder, however, are running rampant. It’s been close to twenty-four hours, and I’ve yet to uncover any leads into the identity of our Jane Doe. In light of the murder, Union Corp. has since acquired the services of Justin Marks, a world renowned inspector. It became apparent this morning that he would be directing the inquiries, as he has set up his operations in room 1005, at Union Corp. headquarters. Since his arrival, Marks has assigned Alpha division several dozen slaughterhouses to check out. I spent the morning filling out my first Bravo division report, dispatched it to Union Corp at 0800 hours. Then, armed with a counterfeit FBI badge, I went round to the criminal county morgue. The commotion was at peak levels, men in blue hustling and bustling in and out of the inner sanctum of the edifice. “Good day, officer.” I found myself acknowledging an accommodating female officer as she stuck her left foot against the gliding elevator doors, allowing me ample time to enter into the steel cage. “What floor?” The simplicity of her question offended my neural senses, as I had spent the entirety of the previous night studying the buildings architecture. “3rd” My answer brought a look of bewilderment to her aging face. I watched as her forehead furled, and her hand fidgeted nervously, as her mind spun a retort. “I’m sorry sir, but the 3rd floor is under level 3 restriction at the moment.” Without hesitation my right hand produced the faulty identification, passing it through her limited train of vision, before gently nestling it back into my jacket pocket. “I’m sorry sir, I was unaware of the FBI’s involvement in this matter.” “It’s ok, officer.” And with that said, the elevator shifted to an abrupt halt, the doors slide open, and I exited without so much as a hint of remorse. I crossed the tile flooring, while scanning my memory for the detailed files on the coronary team. As I approached the slender dissection slab, my memory repeated the name “Wayne Baxter” out loud. Baxter was the coroner for the south-west division of the county’s police force. He stood dressed in a dazzling white waistcoat, checked trousers and crimson scarf. I approached from his blindside, thinking of how his outlandish attire mimicked that of a bad 1980’s mad scientist film. To be frank his clothes were quite, how do you say, dandy. Me, on the other hand, I don’t think much about my clothes as long as they’re comfortable. I attribute much of my success as an “agent” to this. I fit like an old glove wherever I go. I took a seat next to Baxter. “Get an identity on the victim yet?” Without distress Baxter, acknowledged my presence, “The clerk at The Red Dragon said the lady registered as a Ms. Amber Daly, however the name didn’t check out. However, it seems that the shiny black straw hat with a cluster of red wax cherries on the brim was brand-new- and only sold at a few shops in the Lower South End. We’ve got men on the inquiry now.” His voice was direct, yet wavered slightly as his eyes fixed on Detective Brown, who had come into the visual field of Baxter. As Brown, closed the distance between him and our position, he slowed taking a moment to smooth his coppery muttonchop whiskers. Within audible distance Brown questioned my presence, “And you are?” “Neil MacDonald- FBI” I responded in a matter of fact tone. “About time you boys dug your heads out of yer asses and got down here.” My ears strained to interpret his thick southern accent. “Well typically we like to give you blue collar guys a chance to earn your pay.” I mused at his sensitivity to the comment. I had read a number of years ago, how Brown had been released from the Federal Bureau after failing to piece together the Mississippi Strangler case in 1990. My comments were met with a disgruntled gruff, as Brown took a seat next to me, smoothed his trouser legs, and faced front. You’d never mark him for a detective. He is generously proportioned and soft-spoken, more the appearance of a bank manager than a murderer catcher. However, despite my preconceived notions, Detective Brown had served the last ten years as a above average inspector. “Cause of death?” I probed Baxter “Ah yes, the cause of death Mr. MacDonald was the severing of the carotid artery. This wound and the others were made with a long pointed blade.” “How long?” “About six inches” “Frontal attack?” “Yes, and from the angle and direction of the wounds, I would hazard a guess that the wielder of the instrument was left handed.” My questioned were discontinued as Detective Brown inquired, “Was there much blood?” My mind phased out Baxter’s response to Brown’s inquiry, as I had examined the site myself, some twenty four hours ago. At which time I found barely two wineglasses full. Returning to the conversation I questioned Baxter, “How do you account for the lack of blood?” “I would say that she was killed somewhere else and then moved.” Baxter’s response had confirmed my belief that the body had been transported after the time of death. “The other wounds?” I questioned “Those were made after death.” “Nevertheless, could you describe them Dr. Baxter.” “Certainly, she had bruises on her face. Her tongue had been cut out. There were two deep cuts in her abdomen…” Brown shook his head and leaned over and whispered. “I studied the site early yesterday. The ground was wet and muddy, yet there were no signs of dragging resistance, no blood trail of any kind. I think he butchered her on the spot.” “I think you’re right,” I said. “Her clothes were clean- she wasn’t dragged. But why so little blood?” “Ah, that is the question,” Detective Brown said. “It happens, I guess. Shock?” Detective Brown’s words lingered in my head as I lifted myself from my seat. “I’ll be in touch.” Came my generic good-bye, as I re-traced my footsteps to the silver elevator cage. Three hours had passed since my visit to the county morgue. Detective Brown and the coroner Dr Baxter had provided significant clues to the cause of death, however, they had failed to shed any light on who had committed or why this hideous crime had occurred. I had decided it to return to the crime scene, hoping that a second cross reference my heed the answers to my questions. My footsteps were slowed by a herd of bullocks that were being driven down Madison Avenue towards the county fairgrounds, followed by a tattered regiment of barking dogs and small boys with sharp sticks; I had to wait while they filtered through the gateway and past the herded cattle. I watched on in disillusion as the cattle bellowed and thrashed about. My surroundings were peculiar to say the least however I gazed on, probing my memory for any feasible explanation for the uncanny parade of populace. It was then and only then that it occurred to me that the Amsterdam circus was scheduled to arrive today, and perform throughout the Easter holiday. The crowded mob forced me to detour through the back passageway. The afternoon sunshine had melted the morning’s frost, softening the unpaved ground into a thick pasty mess. Yet, I trudged on, sloshing my way through the muck. I was twenty minutes making my way to the other side of the gateway, where the wealth of the North End met with the troubled underbelly of the South End. I was five minutes north of the crime scene, and as I travelled I listened to the hushed reverberation of bereavement, while exhaling the awful stench of death. The time passed quickly as I soon found myself standing amidst the 48 hour crime scene. Since my first visit, the police and onlookers had managed to trample any trace of footsteps from my perpetrator. “Is this the place?” an old woman pointed out the small brown stain on the ground inside the police tape. She had snuck up on me, and her questioned busied my memory. I looked round trying to envision the body lying there. Detective Brown had reported she’d been facing east. To the north was an old Jewish cemetery, a crowded nest of aged marble vaults. Just beyond were the county borderlines and TransAtlantic railway embarkments and lines, and, not fifty yards away, the gate to the to the TransAtlantic railway shunting yards. To the west stretched the South End’s companion industries; tanneries, dog food factories, fish plants, and recycling centers, all of which gave forth an effluvium of rotting flesh. The old lady and I were no longer alone, as the crowd had swelled now with children who had come, no doubt out of curiosity for the dead. It wasn’t every day that they could witness the work of an inspector such as myself. Now, while they waited for the drama within to begin, they danced round the spot, rooted there by the sense of adventure and danger. “Is this where he did her?” a ragged hollow cheeked boy said. The same old lady nodded. “Were her eyes really open?” She nodded. The lad shivered and grinned, walked forward and spat on the spot. Only when the animal cries began from the distant fairground- piercing, haunting shrieks, one following another- did the children turn their attention from yesterday’s death to today’s apparent carnival. I took a deep breath; the air wasn’t fresh, it smelled of tannery and distillery, but I was going inside the old run down factory where the air was worse. I pushed past the children and entered the factory. I looked around, my eyes gaping at the sight of murky tile floors, covered in animal blood. The factory had been rented to Hank Summers, who had in turn re-worked the old factory into a gut-shop, where poverty stricken members of society could come to claim the scraps of meat left over from the riches steaks, and roasts. Summers was no fool though as there was a handsome price to pay, even for such filth. As I made my way through the tattered building, Summers himself came toward me, wiping his hands on his leather apron. “I’m agent Neil MacDonald, from the FBI,” I said. “The police have already been here twice, I’ve no time to waste. I’m a busy man.” “Mr. Summers, there was a woman butchered just outside your door. We’re assisting in the investigation.” “What is it you want then?” “I’ve never seen an animal slaughtered” He raised an eyebrow, then nodded and took out a pack of smokes, offered me one. I accepted readily. “Over here inspector.” The ginger calf hung by its hind legs on a chain; it was bleating. Summers grabbed the chain and swung the animal towards the butcher in the bay. The butcher grabbed the calf’s ear, and holding the head steady, cut the throat with one deft stroke from left to right. The calf seemed to scream a long time after. “Notice,” Summers said proudly, “The windpipe is not severed.” The fact hadn’t escaped me; the scream echoed in my head. I watched the butcher cut up the belly and take out the vitals; there wasn’t as much blood as I’d expected, but there was more than two wineglasses full. --- On the street I pondered it. A butcher would know what he was about; he was used to handling frightened animals. And he could walk down the street in his bloody leather apron- even with blood on his hands- and not be suspected.
