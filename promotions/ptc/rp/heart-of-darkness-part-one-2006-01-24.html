Heart of Darkness (Part One)

24 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-24 What does it do to a man’s heart when the only emotion shown to him is fear? I suppose that’s the question I set out to answer. My mind had been racing ever since Kimbusa stepped into my office and began telling me the story of his life. I still don’t know what compelled him to confess his sins to me, but the depth of his tragedy has opened up an insatiable curiosity within me. I don’t know what he’s planning; I don’t know what he means to do. But it seems clear to me that Kimbusa is orchestrating something truly legendary, and he has chosen to include me in this final chapter of his life. He is drawing all the loose ends of his life together for some cataclysmic climax, and yet the details of his method elude me. Kimbusa offered me only a brief glimpse into his secrets, and even then they appear on the surface to be inconsequential. But it is clear to me now that Kimbusa is attempting to tell the world his story, and for the injustices I did to him I am compelled to listen. And if his story is a confession, perhaps mine is to. No words I can offer you could possibly describe the profoundness of Kimbusa’s story. He spoke for nearly sixteen hours without pause, whispering to me a story of great transformation, and of great tragedy that is Shakespearean in scale. I am not a man who is easily impressed, nor easily overwhelmed by my emotions. But the intensity in his eyes, the passion in his voice; they belied the simplicity of his words and rendered me incapable of resisting the power of his narrative. I was drawn so completely into his memory that I felt compelled to experience these places for myself. I retired to bed after Kimbusa left, but there would be no sleep for me. After several restless hours, I had my private jet fueled and ready for my trip to Tokyo. During the flight, I couldn’t help but marvel at my actions. I had abruptly walked away from my own life to better understand someone else's. And yet all my thoughts were bent upon my errand, to the point that I began to feel less like myself and more like a vagrant apparition, passing idly through the thoughts and memories of Kimbusa’s troubled soul. No amount of storytelling could fully explain the extent of his loss, the depth of his fall into darkness. I had managed this man for several years, and yet only now did I feel I began to understand the scale of his story. And this was a man whom I had caused great personal despair, and it was with a selfish sense of trepidation that I approached this adventure, fearing to discover the extent of the suffering I had caused him. If Kimbusa is orchestrating his revenge, surely I will bear his wrath before the story ends. I had been to Tokyo before, but it was not the business resorts nor the high rise buildings I was interested in this time. No, I went to explore the very streets Kimbusa had walked years ago, the very streets he had inexplicably returned to recently. He has unfinished business here, and whatever scheme he’s concocted involved returning to the Yakuza underworld. I am a powerful man, but only within my own means. At heart I am but a simple entertainer, and despite my great wealth and fame, I felt overwhelmingly small and inconsequential when I first pulled into the Entertainment District. It was precisely that feeling that had drawn Kimbusa there in the first place; that no matter who you are, no matter how great your sins, your identity is washed away the moment you step inside. It is a world unto itself, totally self contained, such that those within seem to be living double lives; one inside, the other out. But for Kimbusa, during his time here, he had only one life, only one identity. On the outside he was banished from civilization for his monstrous actions; but inside, they were coveted. Inside, he was still feared, but never misunderstood; in here, things are much simpler. Men come to satisfy their simplest urges, and leave without having left any trace of their presence. Despite my apprehension, I did not feel fear among those streets; it had been many years since Kimbusa had haunted those streets, but I sensed his legend lingered on. The streets were quiet and subdued, and I got the feeling that everyone was going out of their way to maintain the peace… lest Kimbusa should return. To this day I don’t know what I expected to find, but Kimbusa had clearly given me a mission. He had begun the story of his life, the story he hadn’t dared relate to any other man. But there were two major questions still weighing upon my mind, riddles he never attempted to solve for me. Perhaps he wanted me to come to this place, to investigate his life in deeper detail than his memory could provide. Perhaps he wanted me to see for myself, so that I might truly appreciate the depth of his despair. But there is no doubt in my mind to this day that Kimbusa had a plan for me, just as he had a plan for everyone else who had done him wrong. And I was ready to accept my punishment; for if by my actions Kimbusa might have his revenge, then perhaps I might receive some penance myself. The streets were unlike anything I’d ever seen before; Kimbusa’s descriptions were dead on, but could only just scratch the surface. The buildings were simultaneously decrepit and glamorous, though of no shade of glamour I had ever seen. They were modest in scale, most standing about ten stories high. A casual glance at the main drag might suggest that the city was falling apart, but closer inspection yields a sensory wonderland. It was dark; I had chosen to go at night, so that I might see the streets as Kimbusa had. Beneath the dirty spires rising above the street were well-maintained sidewalks, running past a gauntlet of bars, nightspots and gentlemen’s clubs. Each was lit with a unique neon sign, dancing brilliantly overhead, lining the street with vivid shades of pink, blue, green and red. Steam rose from the sewers, and in mixing with the thin fog hugging the street a feint shroud was formed. I could make out the silhouettes of well-dressed Japanese men and scantily clad women of all races passing by, but through the shroud they all looked much the same. It was a cold, lonely feeling; a veritable paradise lost for a man seeking refuge from the world. There were more bars than I could count; all filled with activity, doors flanked with young, troubled youth doing their best to look macho. I could only imagine the stories one might find with the time to visit them all; but I was on a mission, and I knew precisely where I was going. Kimbusa hadn’t given me directions, but the detail of his story had given me direction enough. It was about a mile down the main drag, standing out among the others with its obscurity. There was no exotic neon sign, no flashy Japanese characters on the window promising booze and women. It was a single wooden door, guarded by a single, professional looking bouncer, surrounded by an unspectacular exterior. This was the place where they’d first found him. The bouncer paid me no mind as I stepped through the door and into the tavern within. As he’d described, it was busy and packed to capacity, but this was a quieter, more passive crowd than the others. I tried not to look, but I couldn’t help but pass a glance at the back of the room, searching for the door to Shindo’s office. I don’t know much about the Yakuza, but common sense told me that Shindo would have moved on long ago. Kimbusa hadn’t mentioned where Shindo’s current haunt was… but I knew some careful probing (and some American currency) might lead me there, too. I took a seat at the bar, flanked on either side by well-dressed businessmen. In retrospect, I was fortunate that Kimbusa had stumbled into the fanciest joint on the main drag; I was able to blend in rather well here, despite the fact that I was the only American in the room. The bartender was a young girl, early twenties, slender and pleasant and accommodating. She was impressed when I ordered a warm sake; impressed enough to strike up a conversation with me. Her English wasn’t bad, but she suddenly forgot how to speak it the moment I mentioned Kimbusa’s name. She gave me a nervous smile, spun on her heel, and walked straight away from me. Moments later, she returned with a tall Caucasian man in a tuxedo. "I am told you are a friend of the man called Kimbusa," he said, his voice barely a whisper, though I detected a feint Russian accent. "I’d rather say we were business partners," I replied diplomatically, hoping to remain obscure. But, to my surprise, the man’s face suddenly lit up. "Steven Caldera! That is you, is it not? I saw you on television in America… You were Kimbusa’s manager, were you not?" he gushed, his chiseled, fearsome demeanor giving way to that of a star-struck fanboy. So much for my cover. But it didn’t matter; the man was more than willing to engage in conversation with me. Of course, all he wanted to talk about was wrestling; he was particularly fond of my exploits in Five Star Wrestling, and my unmitigated destruction of Kimbusa’s life. I didn’t have the heart to tell him that Kimbusa currently had the upper hand on me. I eventually managed to steer the conversation away from wrestling; in order to understand Kimbusa’s actions in the ring, I needed to understand where he came from. "Do you… know anyone I might speak to? Anyone that worked with Kimbusa while he was here?" The Russian man’s goofy grin faded, and his somber, serious monotone returned. "You can speak to me, if you like. Though I don’t like to recall those days. They were dark days, for all of us." "Why is that?" I asked, my curiosity piqued. Had Kimbusa the misfortune of stumbling into the underworld during an exceptionally evil time? "Because of Kimbusa," came his matter-of-fact reply. "We were all afraid, especially after… the… incident." He grew noticeably weary, speaking the words with great care not to be overheard. He glanced about the room, then leaned back in from his seat next to me at the bar. "He was uncontrollable. It was rumored, just before his disappearance, that even the bosses were growing afraid of him. And not that they were afraid he might take over, either. He had just become so volatile, so… evil." His choice of the word "evil" to describe Kimbusa didn’t shock me. I, myself, had often labeled him precisely that. "You knew him, then?" I asked, surprised at my luck for having found a source so easily. Looking back, though, I doubt there was anyone on that street that hadn’t at least heard the name. "No. Nobody knew him, at least not as one person knows another," came the rather severe reply. The Russian reached into his pocket and produced a pack of Marlboro cigarettes, and the lovely yet suddenly nervous young bartender swooped in to offer him a light. For a moment the Russian’s face disappeared behind a thick plume of aromatic smoke, and when it reappeared he seemed to have collected himself. "But there are plenty of stories. Some I’ve only heard from others, some I saw myself. But to answer your question, I don’t think there’s a soul alive who truly knows the man… if you can even call him that." Without knowing it, the Russian had more or less summed up what I had suspected. That’s my mission, his task for me… The man’s eyes seemed to glaze over, as he struggled to recall some distant, or perhaps repressed, memory. "It started quietly enough. Rumors of a demon, as the Japanese called it, lurking in the darkness. There is no shortage of dark places in this part of Tokyo, as you’ve no doubt noticed. And the dark places used to be one of the great attractions of this place. Perfectly private, where even the most well-known man could slip away and… indulge his sweet tooth. But once the rumors started, once people began to hear the claims of some monster under the influence of the Yakuza, the whole place just got nervous. Business didn’t slow down, mind you, but people were more inclined to play it straight. Which, for obvious reasons, isn’t good for business. "Then the rumors grew, as more and more people would stumble into bars claiming to have seen some massive black ghost with fiery red eyes. I, myself, only saw Kimbusa three times. The first two on business, while he was running the casino. And the final one… well, let’s just say I was one of the few people unfortunate to have seen him immediately after the incident, and it is a nightmare I still cannot shake from my mind." The Russian stopped and shut his eyes, his bottom lip trembling from the fear of whatever terrible memory he was recalling. "The incident?" He had referred to it twice now, and I wasn’t sure what he was referring to. Kimbusa certainly hadn’t mentioned anything necessarily more important than the rest; I think his intention was just to get me started, as he had, and set me off to find out the rest. At that moment, it became clear to me that there were deeper, darker secrets to Kimbusa’s life I had yet to uncover. "If you don’t know of it already, then I will not be the one to subject you to it. But it remains the most infamous moment in the history of these streets, and in a place made for crime, that is quite an honor." The Russian spoke grimly, his voice beginning to waver under the weight of the images in his head. His eyes were wide now, but he stared past me, through me, as if looking back into another place. "When I met him, I was frightened of him. I knew he was a dark man with an even darker heart; what’s worse, I knew the powers above him were actively seeking to corrupt him further, as if to see just how far down into the darkness they could drive him. But that night… what I saw from him, in the aftermath… I realized that they were right to call him a demon, for he is nothing less." The Russian pulled a long drag from his cigarette, then raised his empty whiskey glass in the air. The bartender, apparently having overheard our conversation, now would not even make eye contact with me; I suppose she was secretly cursing me for bringing the story back to life. But the Russian’s eyes were filled with both regret and the acceptance of the inevitable; just like his presence here, Kimbusa’s absence had left a terrible void, filled only with the memory of his reign of terror. And now he had manipulated me into coming to this place, completing the inexorable circle in which his legend would be remembered. I didn’t intend to cause this man more pain than necessary, especially if he was a fan, but I felt I had no choice… I needed more. "The two times you did meet Kimbusa in the flesh, what happened then?" I asked, trying to sound as comforting and understanding as possible. The Russian took a long pull of his refilled whiskey, and the drink helped to strengthen his voice. "The first time I met him, it was only briefly. He came here to meet with-" The Russian stopped short, realizing what he was about to say. I have always had the gift to quickly gain the trust of a stranger, and the Russian sat upright, realizing that he almost gave away more than he intended. To his knowledge, I didn’t know anything about Shindo or the organization that ran business in the Entertainment District, nor that Shindo was precisely who he worked for. After backtracking a moment, he pressed on. "I went to frisk him, but my partner informed me it would not be necessary. Apparently he had gained… my employer’s trust rather quickly… And, it was said, the man never carried a gun. After taking one look at him, I understood why. I am no small man, but I felt feeble in his presence. Then he passed out of my sight, and I did not see him again until several years later. "By then he had passed out of rumor and into the limelight, though not many men could claim to have actually dealt with him. They say he loved his privacy, that he hated contact with others, and that the only person he would ever talk to was our employer. There were also rumors of a lady companion, but who she was and what became of her are a mystery to me." The Russian finished off his whiskey and shook the glass eagerly, desperate to drink away the pain of the memories. He then turned to me with a legitimate flair of curiosity. "What are you doing here, Mr. Caldera, if you don’t mind my asking? Why do you ask about him? Most people here are content to let the stories die…" he trailed off, with a passing glance at the young bartender. "He came to see me," I offered, really just wanting to see his reaction. He did not disappoint; his jaw dropped, his eyes grew wide with fear and wonder. "You spoke with him?" he asked slowly, his voice incredulous. "I did," I answered, unintentionally sounding a bit smug. One didn’t just speak with Kimbusa; these people were terrified of the name alone. "And I’m trying to find out more about him. You see, he’s… he’s back. I don’t know what he’s planning yet, but he’s entered a tournament in America, and he’s having a great deal of success. But I’m afraid of what his plan might be, and for the sake of everyone involved, I’d like to figure out just what on earth is going through his head." The Russian shook his head in disbelief. "He’s… back?" he stammered, obviously frightened by the concept. He seemed to fall into a state of panic for a moment, but he quickly regained his composure. I decided it wouldn’t be prudent to tell him that Kimbusa had been to the Entertainment District not four weeks ago. "I- I am sorry, Mr. Caldera, I cannot speak with you any more. I must return to my duties," came his sudden and panicked reply. He drained his last glass of whiskey and quickly stood from the bar, turning his back to me immediately. "Wait, I need more information! Who else can I talk to?" I shouted after him. He kept walking, but finally slowed to a stop. Turning gradually, he glanced over his shoulder, his face pallid and weak. "Have you ever enjoyed the company of one of Japan’s finest girls, Mr. Caldera?" he asked weakly. I didn’t understand; was he asking me if I’d ever bought a hooker? "No, no I haven’t… That’s not what I came for. I need to-" "If you decide to, Mr. Caldera, I might suggest the best place in town. It’s reserved only for VIPs, but I am certain a man of your stature could get in. You need only say the password," he said, turning to walk away again. I took a few steps after him, totally confounded. "What’s the password?" He stopped again, turned back, and quickly strode towards me. He walked forward with a sudden ferocity, and I found myself growing tense as the massive Russian stalked to my side. But he placed a hand on my shoulder and gently leaned in, whispering a single breath into my ear: "Kurokame." ----------------------------- What does it do to a man’s heat when the only emotion shown to him is fear? It renders your body uninhabitable by the soul, until you become hollow and vacant inside. I think now that Kimbusa imparted to me these tales because he wants someone to understand; he has lived virtually his entire life without anyone to appreciate his tragedy, and those who have managed to see inside him have been claimed by the demons residing within. And that is Kimbusa’s intention for me. He chose me because he needs someone to understand his story; he needs someone to fully appreciate his depth and his complexity. So that when he’s gone, there will be someone left who knows, who can see the greatness of his triumph and remind his enemies of his victory. And his demons will surely claim me, as well; I have already fallen under his spell, and soon it will consume me. And so he has rendered his vengeance upon me; he has passed the darkness of his memory onto me, and I am fated now to live with them and within them. He has endured them for too long now, and he is orchestrating his escape. If he is successful, should he earn total victory, he will finally be free of the memories; he will be able to leave forever without fear of them haunting him again. But someone must always remember, someone must always be able to tell this tale. I will be the sole possessor of Kimbusa’s life; and this will be my privilege and my curse for the rest of my days. ----------------------------- Kurokame? I had heard that word (that name, perhaps?) somewhere before… But where? Either way, I was fairly sure my Russian friend hadn’t simply intended to point me towards the classiest hookers in town. No, he’d pointed me in the direction of my next source. I felt a thrill as I stepped back out onto the street. Somehow, I felt more connected to Kimbusa, having felt their fear and their loathing of him. If he had really meant for me to come here, then he had planned well. If I was absorbed by the depth of his story, I was now completely immersed in his life. His name could not be spoken without the room growing cold and grim; what would such a thing do to a man’s mind? I had stepped into the heart of darkness; not the heart of the streets, but the heart of Kimbusa's legacy. I had begun to understand the true scope of what Kimbusa was fighting against, the weight of the sins for his he was now trying to atone. But I needed more. I just needed to find the nicest girlie joint in town.
