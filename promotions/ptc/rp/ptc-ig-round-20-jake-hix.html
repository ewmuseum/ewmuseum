IG Round 20-Jake Hix

7 June 2006

Infinite Gauntlet Round 20 Jake Hix “Never Saw It Coming” Interstate Highway 35 Outside Dallas, Texas 12:30 PM The road was blindingly bright, reflecting the unobstructed sunlight from above while he drove out from the desert. If not for the sunglasses, he didn’t think he could handle the reflection or the wavy distortions the intense heat coming off the road caused. He knew without the air-conditioner working overtime in the S.U.V. he would begin sweating like he had during the desert workout. The sand was not inviting like on the beach, but it helped keep him in top shape as he went through the calisthenics. Doing his normal Dozens workout routine ended up wearing him out as designed, but doing the same routine ended up kicking his ass when he did it in sand. The entire idea of being in shape was put to the test when the environment betrayed the muscles, but it kept him sharp and ready. He would need to be in top form to continue holding onto the title he had won in PTC. He had two tough opponents, and both had experience in running through the Infinite Gauntlet. Jacob McKail and Joshua Taro Freedom were decorated, dangerous and a threat to his reign. He didn’t have to be told how important the match was. He knew he wasn’t just representing himself. He was representing the entire Global federation. He wanted the glory that came from not only winning the belt, but also being able to successfully defend the title. Both men stood in the way of him continuing on to the next round for a huge battle with a fellow Global wrestler and an old “friend”. Jake knew what they would say if he lost. He was the champion by a fluke, and he had never deserved it anyway. First it was someone dropping out and forfeiting their title, and then it was his opponents not being in top form. There was always an excuse his enemies and detractors threw out at him, and he was getting used to hearing the same old complaints. None of that mattered at the moment, and poisoning his thoughts with the negativity was simply giving the assholes what they wanted. He refused to play their little games. He was staying just as strong mentally as he was physically. The workouts he put his mind through were just a little subtler than what the critics were used to seeing. He remained strong in his own ways. Besides, he had enough negative problems in his personal life to put his mind working against him without adding doubt about his in-ring ability. Right now, he was obsessed with his sister Alexandria and his sister-in-law Bella. Both had been kidnapped, and he couldn’t do anything about it. If they involved the authorities they would certainly be killed. His only connection to them was the black RAZR cell phone sitting in the cubbyhole of the S.U.V., fully charged and ready to receive a phone call. They would call eventually, but not until they had made him miserable and broken with worry. All of that had to be pushed aside. Jake had to forget about his personal issues and forge ahead to keep his appointments so no rumors would start about him being distracted. Today was easy enough. He had to make an appearance on Live 105.3 Free FM. Jake liked the appearance with Russ Martin because he was what Jake liked to think of as an “intelligent smart-ass”, and it took one to know one. Jake kept driving, letting his mind churn over the demands he had received. If he gave the kidnappers what they wanted, he didn’t know if he would get his family back and he would lose all the data to bring down the man who was threatening everything he had in the world including his family. Then again, if he didn’t give them what they wanted, Sid would make sure he never saw his family again. That was bad enough, but knowing how Sid would dispatch them from their mortal coil made his blood run cold. There has to be a middle ground… Jake considered how to handle the situation as he drove into Dallas. The familiar skyline beckoned, soothing at least a few of the angry waves crashing against his insides as he made his way to the radio station. Jake came around the corner, and saw a couple of patrol cars outside the studio. I wonder who threatened them now? He thought as he took a parking spot out front. He grabbed his black RAZR, keeping it on him at all times. Jake stepped out, feeling the sudden shock sent through him by the thirty-degree increase in temperature. Jake’s boots crunched down on the rocky asphalt parking lot as he approached the front of the building. The eyes of the assembled officers turned to him, and he saw recognition in their eyes. They didn’t just know him from the television, these men recognized him for a reason outside his fame. They’re looking for me. Jake panicked. If he was being watched here then Sid’s men might think he approached the police for help on the kidnapping. The misunderstanding could cost his family their lives immediately. Jake saw another man, this one wearing a beige linen suit come stepping out from behind the patrol officers. He had to be the detective, but why was he here? Jake shook off the apprehension, knowing he couldn’t simply ignore them. He walked forward looking around at each of the men for a moment. He was thankful the dark sunglasses would hide any hint of panic his eyes may have betrayed. Men like these officers on the hunt mistook that kind of panic for guilt. “I knew my appearance on the show was a good idea, but I didn’t know I had so many fans in the DPD. Did you guys come out for some autographs?” Jake smiled, calm to the world on the outside. His mind might be churning through the possibilities, but the flesh had been trained to be calm long before today. The detective stepped forward. There was a direct stiffness in his gait, betraying his military background. He was an older gentleman, the sides of his hair giving way to gray while the top was receding back as the constant march of time betrayed him. The short haircut was clean, tight and high; and the man had no facial hair at all. Jake took note of the white circle of flesh on his left ring finger amidst the otherwise deep tan when the detective took his sunglasses off. “Mr. Hix?” “Yes, but somehow I think you already knew that Detective???” Jake waited, not giving anything to this man. The confidence he saw as the man approached told him they were here looking for him about something important. “Detective Michael Hollister.” The detective took another step forward. He was pressing, trying to make this man uncomfortable. He was challenging the man who was a hundred pounds heavier and eight inches taller, telling him he wouldn’t be intimidated by his physical presence. Of course, he had four patrol officers to back him up just in case. “We wanted to have a word with you down at the station.” “Am I under arrest Detective Hollister?” Jake let the left corner of his mouth creep up in amusement. “No, but we do have some questions for you we’d like to discuss. Can you come with us?” Detective Hollister made sure he included the force behind him. “If you’re here Detective, then you obviously know I have an appointment inside.” Jake flourished with his hand to indicate the radio station. “I’ll be happy to speak with you Detective, after I’ve made my appearance today. I don’t like to disappoint my fans or neglect my commitments.” Detective Hollister wasn’t smiling or backing down. He shifted his right hand to his hip, not touching his weapon at all, but the revelation of the holster conveyed its own meaning. “I’m afraid we need to speak with you right away.” “Am I under arrest?” “No, we just have some questions about an incident you might be a witness to.” Detective Hollister’s voice softened. Jake made leaned left looking over Hollister’s shoulder, then leaned right looking over Hollister’s other shoulder. He returned, cocking his head sideways, “Excuse me? Just some questions? Let’s lay it on the line here Detective. If you just had a ‘few questions’, then you wouldn’t have come here heavy.” Detective Hollister tried to diffuse the situation, “Mr. Hix, we’re just…” “Full of shit?” Jake let his face break into a wide grin, knowing he could make the detective slip up a little playing it this way. Hollister’s face clouded over with anger from the insult, “You keep this up and I will take you in!” “I doubt that. My support of law enforcement is well documented. I could call Chief Kunkle and I bet he wouldn’t approve of this at all. I always work all the charity events for the department, not because I need special treatment, but because it’s the right thing to do.” Jake looked down at his watch, “Now, here’s what I’m willing to do. You tell me where to meet you, and as soon as my appearance on the show is over I’ll come down and answer some questions.” “Is that right?” “Yeah, I won’t even bring a lawyer.” Jake leaned down, “Then it’s not in the papers, it’s not you with an army coming to take me in, and I cooperate. Maybe your momma should have told you that you could catch more flies with honey instead of vinegar. Now, where do you want me to meet you after the show, or do we all start playing games around whatever bug you have up your ass? Because I’ll have my lawyers keep you running around, delaying you and being a pain in the ass just because you annoyed me with this bush league approach.” Detective Hollister reached around to his back, touching the handcuffs he kept there. He could throw Jake over the hood of the patrol car, cuff him and bring him in for questioning. The anger dispelled as quickly as it had built. If he did that, Jake would call a lawyer and he wouldn’t have any answers for the investigation. Michael didn’t like Jake Hix before he met him, and now his opinion was vetted and confirmed. The man was a pompous, arrogant jackass. Unfortunately, he was also a friend of the Chief’s, and a man with enough financial resources to fight anything thrown his way. “I’m not trying to be hostile here, but this is a serious matter Mr. Hix. I’ll be at the Jack Evans building, do you know where it is?” Michael let his arm fall back down to his side. “Been there a time or two, even for a ride along once.” Jake assured him. “Good, then we’ll see you there soon enough.” Detective Hollister pulled out a leather business card holder. He handed one of his cards to Jake. “Just ask for me.” “I look forward to it.” Detective Hollister looked for any hint of betrayal in how the man handled himself, but Jake held onto himself and his motives tightly. My momma liked to say you have to get along to get along, so I’ll play the game for now. Things are gonna’ change when you’re in my interrogation room. Jake Hix slipped the detective’s card into his back pocket. He stepped past the detective, and watched as each patrolmen seemed to lock onto him with accusatory glances. He didn’t know what this was about. Jake had broken many of the laws of civilized society in his secret war with Sid, but all of those crimes were far from prying eyes. He didn’t think they had any information from those misadventures that could come back to haunt him. Detective Michael Hollister watched as the behemoth disappeared behind the dark, tinted glass of the building. The man didn’t seem right to him, and it had nothing to do with his fame or lifestyle. One of the patrolmen walked up to him, “We’re just letting him go?” “For the moment.” Detective Hollister took the time to smile, “Besides, he’s painted himself into a corner for the moment.” “How’s that?” “He agreed to come down to the station after the show. If he doesn’t, I know some judges who might consider that reasonable enough suspicion. If he does come down, he’s stuck in my world then. Besides, he’s already betrayed himself in this.” The patrolman was confused, “How’s that?” “When is the last time you confronted someone and they didn’t ask you what it was about?” Michael saw the patrolman’s face as understanding dawned on him. “If he didn’t do this, then he knows he did something else we might be looking at him for. He’s got a guilty mind, and I like him for this. We’ll play his little game for now. Once I have him inside the interrogation room he’s going to hang himself. They all do.” Inside the radio station building, Jake Hix nodded at the security guard. “I’m here for the Russ Martin show.” The security guard looked out at the cops, “They were asking about you earlier.” “Classic case of mistaken identity. I guess a lot of people look like me.” Jake explained as he signed in on the sheet. The security guard looked Jake over. He couldn’t imagine too many men running around at his massive size, and no one in Dallas mistook him since he was a local celebrity more than even some of the Cowboys, Stars, mavericks or Rangers. Although, at the moment, the Mavericks were definitely bigger. “Sure, mistaken identity.” “Exactly.” Jake took the visitor badge from the guard and walked toward the elevators. His mind was churning over the possibilities. He had done so many things, but his only possible exposure hadn’t come in Dallas. New York City might have something on him if they were very lucky, but not Dallas. Jake felt a vibration in his back pocket, and he realized that was the black RAZR phone. Not a month ago, UPS had delivered the package containing the phone. He had only received one phone call from the men holding his family, and it was just to let him know they were still waiting to make arrangements for the exchange. Jake couldn’t believe the timing of this call was coincidental. He flipped the phone open, “Hello?” “Mr. Hix, a pleasure to hear your voice as always.” The voice was the same scratchy, high-pitched voice from the phone calls he had received from Sid’s men before. The man took a sick pleasure in taunting Jake, but there was nothing he could do about it. “As you say.” “I wonder Mr. Hix, how did your conversation with the police go?” The man asked, no threat in his voice. Actually, he sounded rather amused. “Short.” The man laughed, the cackle on the other end grated on Jake’s nerves. “Oh, I saw. They were waiting for quite some time, and then they just let you pass. I was surprised, considering what they wish to discuss with you.” Jake looked out the front of the building, trying to see if he spotted anyone watching him. He saw no one, “You know why they’ve approached me?” “Of course, it was something we arranged for you Jake.” The man seemed delighted at the turn of events. “We just wanted to remind you of the possible consequences if you don’t do exactly as instructed. Malum felt it was best to remind you of what we are capable of Mr. Hix, and I must say he got very creative here.” Jake knew anything he did in anger could be taken out on Alex and Bella, so he tried to remain professional with this man. “What, no hints?” “And ruin your surprise?” Another annoying laugh came from the other end. The man spoke so plainly and politely; in civilized society you couldn’t call him out despite his constant prods. Here, rules of decorum were different, but there was still no satisfaction to be had with the voice on the other end. “I would never dream of doing that for you Mr. Hix. Enjoy both your interviews, and let’s not forget we don’t want the authorities involved in our little arrangement.” “Understood.” The line went dead. Jake looked back out to where the police were climbing back into their cars. He didn’t know what game was being played, and that meant he was simply a pawn being maneuvered around the board. Jack Evans Police Headquarters Dallas, Texas 7:15 PM Sitting inside the interrogation room, Jake knew he was being kept waiting on purpose. While he was not being held, they were still playing games with him as though he was not free to leave at any time. Of course, both sides could always play games. Jake had been led to the room upon arrival, and once inside he found it could only be called a cliché. The room had white tile flooring, and the walls were a muted beige except for the large mirror on the left side. In the middle of the room there was a table with a single chair on each side. When the officer had escorted him in and pulled out the chair for him, Jake had taken a seat until the officer left him alone. Then Jake began his own games, changing to the seat on the other side of the table so his back was to the two-way mirror. Ten minutes later an officer came back to the room, “Sir, we’re going to ask you to sit on the other side.” “No thanks, for me that side is bad.” Jake smiled at the young officer. “It’s a Feng Shui thing, you know?” “What?” “Don’t worry about it.” Jake leaned back, making himself as comfortable as possible. He wouldn’t give up any of his own advantages here. The officer left, but they had now kept him waiting another twenty minutes. So he just forced himself to ignore his surroundings by going through some isometric exercises. Jake had his arms held out in front of him when the door opened and Detective Michael Hollister finally joined him. “Detective, so nice to see you again.” Jake intoned dryly as he lowered his arms. Detective Hollister didn’t say anything as he went to the opposite side of the table. He set the manila folder down between them before he took a seat. He pulled a tape recorder out of his pocket. “You don’t mind, do you?” He asked. Jake shrugged, “Why not?” The detective turned on the recorder, “This is Detective Michael Hollister, June seventh two thousand and six. I’m in interrogation room six at Jack Evans Police Headquarters. I am in with Jake Hix. Mr. Hix, can you confirm you’ve agreed to this recording?” “Yes, I agree.” “Thank you. I’m not here to play games with you. We have some questions on serious allegations made involving you.” Michael flipped open the folder to reveal two pictures, one on each side. The pictures showed two young women dressed up like they were in posing for graduation photos. Michael tapped the two photos, “Do you know these two women?” Jake looked down, and it dawned on him what this was all about. These were the two ladies he had left the club with after prodding from his brother. Either there was a Kobe being pulled, or they were under age. He nodded, “I know them, I enjoyed an evening with them recently. And, just to be clear, I met them at a club requiring patrons to be older than twenty-one to enter, and I spent a consensual evening with both of them.” “So you admit to knowing them, and that would have been the evening of the twenty-ninth?” Michael asked. “I think that was the night, but there’s been many other late nights since it’s hard to be sure about a date.” Jake joked, knowing this wasn’t going to pull him in. A set-up on rape or statutory rape seemed so pedestrian by Sid’s men. He never saw it coming. “I’m glad we have that on record.” Michael removed the picture on the right, and spread the pictures beneath out to show them to the suspect. That’s how he thought of him now, and as he saw the shock and surprise set into Jake’s features he knew they had him. “Because we found these young ladies, who you admit to knowing intimately a few days after that. Forensic evidence confirms they were murdered the morning of the thirtieth. Mr. Hix, we need to discuss where you were since you were the last person with these women.” Jake never saw it coming.
