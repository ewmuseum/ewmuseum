Angie in Wonderland

10 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-10 When Deville opened his eyes, seeing through them without Satan's demonic tint, he almost crumpled to the ground in tears. As it was, he merely dropped to his knees and pushed his hands deep into the sand all around him, stretching and flexing his fingers, relishing the simple pleasure of being able to touch and feel. Sweet breath, flowing into him under his own power, was like drinking from the elixir of life. He breathed as deeply as he could, holding it and savouring the taste before letting it stream out of him in a fit of lilting laughter. Then his memories came rushing back to him, and the laughter abruptly died. His hands clenched fistfuls of sand as he let his head sag down between them, the flimsy dam holding back his tears once again threatening to burst. The Devil had won. Using the weapon of his eternal enemy, Satan had chased him out of his own mind. Deville didn't know how it happened. When the effects of the crack started seeping into his brain, scrambling it, turning it to useless mush, he panicked and focussed all of his power on being elsewhere, on escaping. He hadn't expected it to work -- didn't know how it could -- and now that he realized it had he couldn't help but think he had made a fatal mistake. Satan had caught him off-guard, and Deville's instincts, usually keen and sharp enough to deliver him from any problem, had failed. The Deville had lost. He couldn't help but wonder if this had been Satan's design all along. To scare him out of his wits about his mother so that he would have none left when the real ambush was sprung. That was how a clever enemy operated, making you focus on a threat that wasn't there while the other crept up behind you unnoticed. But with The Devil, an enemy unlike any other in the history of the Universe, was the first threat truly only a bluff? Or was there something more to it? Angelo didn't have to answer. He knew there was more to it. He knew that even with himself out of the way, his mother was still in mortal danger. And knowing that, he knew he had to somehow get back and save her. But how? Deville screamed in his mind, automatically flinching right afterward, expecting a cutting response. But none came. He really was alone. For some reason, whatever he had done, wherever he had gone, Satan didn't -- or couldn't -- follow. Maybe there was something in that The Deville could use. If so, he couldn't fathom what. Knowing that cowering on the ground wasn't going to solve anything, Angelo climbed quickly and shakily to his feet, looking all around to try and determine where he was. Despite having been to Hell and limbo and beyond, the sight made him blink with disbelief, as if to wash the lie from his eyes. There was no trick, though; what he was seeing, as near as he could tell, really was the reality here. He was in a desert wasteland, but nothing like he had ever heard of before. Sand stretched as far as he could see in every direction, but strangely, that didn't seem very far. The sky all around him swirled with pink and blue and purple. Not like clouds, but rather streaks of light, something reflected. They danced straight across the sky, and some even seemed to disappear into the sand. All were the same distance away, though, as if trapped and running along a smooth sphere Deville was near the centre of. It was like being inside a bubble. Had that been the strangest part about this world, Deville wouldn't have balked. What had him stunned and gaping was the huge yellow monstrosity looming ahead of him, a ridiculous palace large enough to appease even the most egotistical king. But it wasn't the size that was askew, or even the fact that it seemed to be floating on air a good twenty paces above the ground. It was the shape that blew Deville's mind. The palace was a giant bath toy. A beastly rubber duck. With an inward "what the fuck," Deville gave his head a clearing shake and began striding towards it, certain the answer to his problem lay inside. As he made his way over, he idly wondered if this was what crack did to you. The duck looked no less strange as Deville got nearer, and an odd sound began tickling at his ears, an annoying muzak of sorts. When Deville walked underneath the smooth rubber breast, the sound became clear: it was Ernie's "Rubber Ducky" song from Sesame Street, though tweaked to make it sound dangerous and somehow perverted. Trying to shut the disturbing noise out of his mind, Deville scanned the underbelly for some sort of entrance, almost immediately spotting the small hole near the back. He made his way over to the ground underneath where it was and stopped dead. There was no way to get up to it that Deville could see, but he wasn't really looking. His attention was riveted directly in front of him, at a stern statue he hadn't noticed until now. It had been hidden in the shadow cast by the monstrous duck. The statue, a marble replica of The Deville himself, grinned at him mockingly. Only with supreme effort was Angelo able to pull his eyes away from that stone mirror, to read the inscription on the golden plaque at its base. It was a poem of sorts, or a verse from a song, and something about it tickled the back of his mind. He thought he had read something similar in a book once, Lord of Chaos by Robert Jordan, but that thought fled as he read the passage aloud. Give me your trust, said The Father of Lies. I'll show you marvels to dazzle your eyes. Trust me to shelter and protect and to guide, And from transience you'll hide. But trust is the taste of betrayal in blood. Trust is the taste of promises turned mud. Trust is the taste of the soul's last breath. Trust is the taste of death. "Bloody hell," Deville whispered after a moment, and as if that was a secret code, a rope ladder lowered from the opening until the bottom dangled just above the statue's head. He eyed it suspiciously for only a moment before climbing up onto the statue and grabbing hold of the bottom rung. Sensing his weight, the ladder retreated back to the opening, carrying Angelo along with it. Maddeningly, the muzak got louder as he went up. It carried him straight into the duck's belly, and Deville gaped at the emptiness inside. Somehow, he had expected something more. He couldn't say what exactly, but the pure majesty with which the duck was presented gave him the impression there would be something more to it than a bunch of gaunt characters scurrying about without any evident purpose. The ladder disappeared as soon as Deville was inside, and the hole vanished, becoming just another part of the smooth yellow plastic. The floor beneath him felt soft, as if it might give way at any moment, but Deville took the first few steps confidently, looking around with awe. The inside of the duck was one huge room, cut exactly in the shape of the duck itself, with no care given to making the surroundings comfortable or aesthetic. The floor curved with the shape of the belly, bending neatly into walls and ceiling with never a crease. People milled about all around, never seeing Angelo even when their eyes passed right over him. Something about the people struck Deville as odd. It took him a moment to figure out that while there were dozens of individuals immediately visible, there was nothing individual about them; they all seemed to be cast from one of two moulds. Half wore white livery, with identical faces like angels and broad, pleasant grins etched permanently across them. The other half wore purest black, with unshaven scowling faces and fists clenched angrily at their sides. Those in white bore nametags that read simply "Good"; those in black "Bad". In his grey suit with alternating black and white pinstripes, Deville couldn't help but feel out of place. He tried to flag one of the clones down, but they sped by him unheeding of his calls, striding around in dizzying circles as they chased one another for no apparent purpose. Off to Deville's left, one of the bad men seemed to have caught one of the good. The good was tied to a rickety chair while the bad loomed over him and tried to seem maniacal. Deville watched for a moment, eagerly anticipating the explosion, but even though the bad man could have easily had his way with the good, nothing happened. They just stayed as they were, the good looking unnecessarily worried and the bad gloating without cause, dancing around him and uttering threats Deville knew he would never carry out. Wondering if they were retarded, Deville gave his head a shake and began investigating the rest of the strange place. Little wooden boxes like trash bins dotted the floor, one every few steps, each with a cigarette-sized pencil and a stack of post-it notes placed on the flat top. Beside those was a slit large enough for the notes to be dropped inside. Deville walked over to one and smirked when he saw the word "Suggestions" carved just above the slit. Checking two more proved they were all the same. Smirking, Deville picked up one of the pencils and wrote "Come up with your own goddamn plans." then peeled off the note and dropped it in the box. The second the paper touched bottom, a flurry of midgets in strange costumes like servant's uniforms, with ruffled lace lining every hem, ran over and surrounded the box Angelo was at, elbowing his knees to make him move out of their way. "Out of ideas! Out of ideas!" they chanted in impish voices. "Steal. Steal. Steal." It continued on like that as they hoisted the suggestion box up onto their shoulders and carried it away like a platform holding royalty. Deville blinked and stared after them, then rolled his head along his shoulders, letting the tension at being in such an absurd world rattle out of him in a machine gun torrent of cracks. Then he strode off in the direction the midgets went, reasoning that the suggestions must go to whoever was in charge of this madhouse. Despite how immense the duck was from the outside, it didn't take Deville long to get to the front, where the floor sloped up into the breast. An escalator stretched up and out of sight, presumably leading to the head. With a sigh, Deville stepped on. The escalator did indeed lead to the head, as aesthetically empty as the cavern below. It wasn't very large, about the size of a basketball court, but at least none of the generic ilk scuttling about beneath were in evidence. Not even the midgets. In their stead were other uninspired creatures. A bunch of men in black robes with tiny white squares at their necks and beads hanging from their belts watched over just as many bathtubs. Young children splashed around inside, playing with plastic ducks and giggling, though occasionally they shot untrusting glances at their guards. The priests, for their part, licked their lips a touch too often, and something peculiar, something tainted, gleamed in their beady eyes as they leered. The urge to run over and castrate them was overwhelming, but Deville managed to maintain his composure. In the centre of the room there was a large saucer atop a bed of coals. Thick tendrils of smoke tried to rise, but a bunch of dark-skinned men with feathers in their hair leaned forward and sucked it up before the smoke could get very high. A goofy looking man in Sesame Street pyjamas lounged in front of the bowl, one end of a tube pressed between his lips, the other disappearing into the smoke. A long line of slutty looking women with glazed over eyes mooned over him. The man in the pyjamas spotted Angelo staring and skipped over to him, swinging his arms around in an exaggerated motion and snapping his fingers to a tune obviously only he heard. "Hello there," Deville greeted when the man closed in, striding forward to meet him partway. "Hello there," the man replied, though not at all like a response. He greeted Angelo as if he were the first to speak, the delivery of his words exactly the same as Deville's. He proffered a gap-toothed grin and rolled back and forth on his toes and heels, shaking slightly as if holding some great secret that wanted to spill out. Deville eyed the happy faces of Ernie and Bert painted across his chest with almost as much disgust as for the man himself. He tilted his head and narrowed his eyes. "What is this place?" The pyjama-clad man tilted his head and narrowed his eyes in mimicry, then said in the exact same tone, "What is this place?" Taking great pains not to grind his teeth, The Deville took a menacing step forward. "Are you just going to copy everything I say?" "Are you just going to copy everything I say?" The whirring left cross caught the man right in the temple, knocking him sprawling backward and to the side before he fell. Deville wasted no time in looming over him, dark thunderclouds playing across his face. "I have no time for this bullshit. Unlike you," he hissed, "I have important things I need to do." Offering the man a hand up, which he wisely accepted, Deville nodded satisfactorily. "So let's try this again. Who are you?" "Dave 'The Duckman' Burst," the lunatic announced, puffing out his chest. "Emperor and ruler of mighty Duckonia." Duckonia? The Deville looked around with more than a hint of amusement. Mighty?! "What the hell is a duckonia?" A pained expression crossed Dave's face, immediately replaced by that foundless pride. "This place," he somehow boomed in his squeaky voice, gesturing around himself excitedly. "This whole world is Duckonia, the swang-bloody-ankiest place there ever was." "What's with all the paedophilic priests?" Dave shrugged. "They give me what I want, I give them what they want." "I see," Deville said dryly. "And what is it that you want?" Dave eyed Deville as if he was exceedingly dense. "Faith." "Is that what built this place?" The Deville's mouth twisted up disgustedly, and the word came out as if forced by bile. "Faith?" "Sort of. This place came to be after I won a wrestling tournament, as a reward and representation of my victory." Deville eyed the overgrown child askance. "How did you ever win a wrestling tournament?" "Faith." "Fuck off," Deville laughed. "Faith couldn't do that." "Faith can move mountains, mate." "No it can't." "Yes it can." "No," Deville grated firmly, "it can't." "Yes, it c--" Thankfully, the exchange was interrupted by a paper-thin woman appearing suddenly out of thin air. She eyed Deville a tad worriedly, a tad lustily, before bending forward and giving Dave a peck on the cheek. "Hi honey," she bubbled, and he repeated the greeting, then flinched at the memory of a punch. "What's going on?" "Just a wee bit o' business," he replied, "nothing for you to concern yourself with. I'll give you a ring when I need you to fill in a gap." Glowing happily, she nodded and then winked out of existence. "Well," The Deville said, scratching his head. "That was random. Is she your girlfriend?" "One of many," Dave answered with a conspiratorial grin. Turning, he pointed to the long line of zombie women swaying back and forth incoherently in front of the bowl. "See all of them?" Deville nodded that he did. "They all want me." Deville rubbed his thumb and forefinger against his closed eyes. Sarcasm radiated from him. "I take it faith did that, too." "You know it, mate." Deville sighed. "How the hell do I get out of here? Can faith do that, too?" "My faith could," Dave began in a lecturing tone. "But not yours. My faith is superior, somehow, and supersedes that of others. The only way for you to get out of here is to kill me." Dave sounded as if that was a preposterous notion, but then his eyes went wide and he hurried on at the glint that rose in The Deville's evil blue orbs. "But I know you won't do that, mate. I have faith." He grinned and nodded, as if that was the end of it. "Of course not," Angelo said facetiously. "But just for the sake of conversation, if I were to kill you, that would take me out of this place? This . . . Duckonia?" "Aye, mate. Aye it would." When The Deville started rubbing his hands together, Dave cleared his throat and hurried on again. "But again, mate, that won't happen. My faith prevents it." The Deville sneered doubtfully. "Watch. I'll just turn my back on you like so," Dave said, turning around, presenting his back to The Deville, "and my faith will protect me." Without a moment's hesitation, Deville pulled out his serrated hunting knife and buried it deep into the exposed back, right between the shoulder blades. Blood spread across the pyjamas in an ever-widening stain. The world around them shimmered as Dave gasped and fell to his knees, blubbering, reaching behind him with both hands to try and pull out the knife. He couldn't reach far enough. "Faith," The Deville whispered, leaning over Dave as he gasped his last breath and his ridiculous world imploded, "is the taste of death."
