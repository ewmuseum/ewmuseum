Date: 2009-12-02 Scene 15: The Café Day Two, 7:17 AM. Tucson, Arizona. High-dollar resorts like the J.W. Marriott Starr Pass Resort had an image to maintain, from glittering chandeliers in the lobby to gourmet restaurants on-site to the bossy-but-competent staff. Rich people expected the managerial staff would mistreat employees, which simply gave horrible people an excuse to be awful and excuse it as part of the show. Needless to say, the managers at the Starr Pass weren't particularly popular among the staff that actually had to deal with the ear-reddening tirades. Half-hearted apologies did little to cool their displeasure, especially in the face of amused smiles from the clientele. It was a cannibalistic system, designed to keep the staff on their toes for when a whale came to visit. A whale like Elisabeth Pierotti, who stormed through the double-doors, cellphone pressed to her ear. "I do not care, you insignificant little troll," Pierotti barked into the phone. "You will get it done as I have asked or I will find someone who will. Am I clear?" There was a pause as she reached the front desk, and she shared a nasty smile with the hapless clerk. "I rather thought not. See that you do." "Welcome to the Starr Pass," the girl chirped. "My name is Veronica, how can I help you?" Elisabeth turned up her nose at the young woman. "I have a reservation." "Name?" "Pierotti." She spelled it. "A suite for two nights." Veronica nodded briskly, typing the name out as quickly as she could as Pierotti half-turned, her hip against the desk. She brought her iPhone back up, dismissing the woman with a sniff, as if she were unworthy of her attention. The clerk looked up nervously, swallowing as she saw the thin, cat-that-ate-the-canary smile on Elisabeth's features. "Uhm." She cleared her throat. "Miss Pierotti?" Slowly, that head turned to face her, icy blue eyes narrowing briefly. "Yes?" "I don't... I don't show a reservation for you in our system, ma'am." "What?" The woman's brows flew up. "Try it with two E's, rather than an I-E." Elisabeth's finger flew across the touchscreen of her phone. "I am certain I have a reservation." "I've tried every spelling of your last name I could think of, ma'am. I'm sorry." Pierotti turned, palm on the countertop. She set the phone down beside the monitor. "This is unacceptable! I have had this reservation for weeks – it's rare enough that I get time away, I certainly cannot—" "Well, we have a standard room available..." "I will not be downgraded because some simpleton misplaced my reservation! You will get me your manager, and he had best fix this problem." Veronica looked over her shoulder. "Mr. Frank is in his office right now. Perhaps I can offer you a free coupon for break—" "Your manager. Now." Elisabeth dipped her hand into her pocket. "I do not care if you know where he is. You will find him." The clerk ducked her head in her polite smile, stepping backwards and pushing through the employees-only door. Elisabeth kept the frozen smile clipped to her lips until the door clicked closed again, then, with a glance behind her, vaulted the countertop on one hand. "I told you she'd cave," the woman murmured, tugging a short USB cord from inside the pocket of her jacket. One end fit into the back of her iPhone, the other into an empty slot in the back of the computer. "Never underestimate the power of a bitch." "Whatever." Even annoyed, the lilting, French-Canadian accent in Elisabeth's ear brought a smirk to her face. "You didn't have to be that mean to her." "I don't have time for subtlety," Elisabeth Pierotti – Alexandra Pierce – hissed. "Tell Donovan we're on." "Y'know I can hear, too, right? I'm already working on it." Repeat performances were rare for someone in Desade's line of work – after a would-be mark has been deceived, it was generally best not to give them an opportunity to vent their frustration. Sometimes, it was necessary to have a consistent presence, and Pierotti was one of Alex's most frequent roles. A stuck-up, notoriously reclusive humanitarian was easy; she was someone who could pop up and throw the types of parties where the rest of Pierce's people could run smaller gambits. It was Elisabeth who'd thrown a party following last month's SCCW show at the nearby Tucson Convention Center, and it was Elisabeth that Pierce last remembered being, the night before Lauren Fox died. So it was Elisabeth that Alexandra was again this morning in an attempt to retrace her steps. "Downloading the guest lists now." Donovan Bell and Amy Campbell sat outside in a rented minivan; Alex's protégé typing briskly on a laptop, while her girlfriend waited nervously – and not as quietly. "You so owe me for this," she grumbled. "For sitting in the car?" While the file downloaded, Alexandra ducked low (she'd never be mistaken for a desk clerk, and even on a Wednesday morning, there was the chance of an early-morning guest). The Spider walked like her namesake to the door, trying the handle. "You know how much I hate this." Campbell's frown came through the words. "In and out, I promise. I'm not hurting anyone – she'll deal with worse today." Pierce tried the handle, but found it locked. "What are the chances we know what's going on in there?" "I'm havin' enough of a problem making sure I'm downloading the right information. You still should've let me just chat up the girl. Ladies love me." "Ugh," said Campbell. "What? I'm charming enough – dashing, even." "Riiiiiight." Alexandra stifled a laugh as she pressed her ear (the one that didn't have a tiny bud in it) to the door. "Quiet, both of you. I'm trying to listen." Her brows lifted. "How much longer?" "It's at fifty-two percent now." "Shit." Alex stood. "Well, they're coming now." "You're sure?" Bell's voice stretched under the strain of the stress. "Figure... figure we need another minute, maybe two if you want all the files." "I do," Alex whispered, striding briskly around the counter. Despite her heeled shoes, her steps barely made a sound on the tiled floor. She slipped the Bluetooth back around her ear, her voice slipping into the light, breezy cadence favored by Elisabeth – she was more of an idea in Pierce's mind than an actual person. "Sell," she said, loudly enough to be heard as Veronica led her manager out onto the floor. "No, I do not care that the shares are going down in price. I want to be rid of it all." "Ms. Pierotti?" Veronica's question was gentle – whether she was just polite or was afraid of another outburst was a question to be debated at another time. Alex didn't turn the first time, so the desk clerk had to come nearer – a little too close to the iPhone for comfort. She lifted her head as naturally as she could manage, pinning the woman in place with a modicum of the glare she used with such frequency in the wrestling business. It was only after a hair's-breadth that the girl broke away from the medusa gaze with a smile. "Thank you, Donovan," Pierce said to the no one she was speaking with. "What is the percentage?" "Eighty-three." Bell's whisper came into her ear. "Talk to you soon." Alex tapped the button on the side of the headset, rising and turning to face the slight, dark-haired woman she'd first bullied into abandoning her post. A half step behind her was a man Alex immediately named her manager, an effeminate, well-dressed man in his mid-thirties with slick-backed orange-red hair and too many freckles. Her gaze slid past the man (not before she caught "Lyndon" on his nametag). Everything changed when her eyes landed on the plain-looking blond man that stood behind him, hands in the pockets of a long leather coat. Jonas Stryker tipped his head subtly. Alexandra Pierce frowned. "Fuck," she muttered. "What?" Donovan asked. "Thirty seconds." It was Amy who caught onto the larger issue. "What's wrong?" "Is there a problem, Ms. Pierotti?" Lyndon floated forward, allowing his employee to go back. "I am in the midst of an interview right now..." "It's nothing." Alex smiled, both for the three she was facing and the woman who could only hear the words. "I just realized that my reservation was at the Canyon Ranch, not here at the Starr Pass. My mistake." She looked to the clerk briefly, dismissively. "My sincerest apologies." "Of course," Lyndon beamed. "I'm just sorry we couldn't accommodate—" "Now, Elisabeth," Stryker said with a smile. "I'm ashamed you don't recognize me!" Alex squinted, allowing a slow smile to spread across her lips. "Jonas? Is that really you?" The chatter in Pierce's ear immediately picked up steam. "She knows this guy?" Donnie Bell was still whispering unnecessarily. "Who the fuck is Jonas?" Amy demanded. Alex imagined her brows knitting, lips pursed in that way she did when she was worried. Somehow, the Spider was able to block all of that out. "I haven't seen you since... was it Seattle?" "2005." Jonas' smile was a little plastic, but it would have taken someone far more observant than Lyndon to actually see how much he meant. "Feels longer." "You two know each other?" the hotel manager asked. "Only in passing," Stryker said dismissively. "Elisabeth used to be part-owner of our magazine. She—ow, dammit!" He caught his toe on a marble bench, lurching forward. Both the manager and the clerk turned at the exclamation, allowing Alexandra to yank the card from the back of the computer, and then again from the phone. "Are you all right, Jonas?" Pierce didn't miss a beat. The man's smile was sheepish. "I'm fine, Elisabeth." He flicked his eyes to Lyndon. "Would you mind if we continued this after I've had a cup of coffee? I appear to be quite out of sorts." Disappointment flickered briefly in the other man's eyes. "Of course," Lyndon said with a tight smile. "I'm here until one this afternoon." "Alex, are you okay?" Amy's voice was quiet and controlled over the radio – this was very much her I'm-trying-not-to-freak-out voice. "Talk to me." "Ms. Pierotti?" Jonas asked gently. "Since you're booked across town, do you have a few minutes to catch up on old times?" "Al?" A note of desperation crept into that voice in Alexandra's ear. "It's fine." Alex answered them both. "I have a few minutes." "Superb!" Jonas grinned, jogging up to the Spider. He offered an arm as he swung his small smile to the hotel's manager. "I'll be back in an hour, Mr. Franklin." Lyndon didn't respond, turning sharply away as Stryker led Pierce back out through the sliding glass doors, arm in arm. Once they were out of sight, however, Jonas shoved Alexandra up against the building. "You wired?" Pierce shook her head. "Just a two-way." "Cough it up." He extended a palm. "And why would I do that?" Alex asked. "Last I recall, we were not even on speaking terms." "I swear no harm will come to you." He smirked, just a little. "Besides, you want to know what I've learned about Lauren Fox's death, and I do not trust the people who might be on the other end." "Al, don't you dare." Amy's concern hadn't faded, despite (or perhaps because of) Pierce's assurances. "Don't you fucking—" "Fine." Alex bit off the word like it hurt, digging the tiny radio and induction mic out of her ear. "If you screw me, I will have to kill you." "That threat would hold more weight if you hadn't made it before." His smile was remarkably pleasant given the gravity of the threat. "No tricks, though. I promise – we'll sit outside here at the café so they don't think I've abducted you." "This had better be worth it, Jonas." Pierce handed over the earbud. "Isn't it always?" Stryker tugged open the coffeehouse door, holding it for Alex. "Shall we?" Neither could hear the frustrated yell from a van nearby, though it was nearly loud enough that the radio was unnecessary. * * * * * Amy Campbell was not the calm, rational sort about most things – and certainly not about her feelings for Alexandra Pierce. So it was little surprise that her hand was on the car door's handle the moment – the very instant – it became clear the Spider's earbud was no longer in place. "Wait!" Donnie Bell's hurried shout only just held Amy in check. "Just... just wait a second, wouldja?" "She needs me." Donnie had never seen Amy this worried; she seemed at war between tears and the fervent desire to hit something. "You can't expect me to just sit here." "She needs you to be smart," Bell said sharply. "This guy is serious business, and if you run in there, you're both liable to be up against it." "Who is he?" "I..." Donnie sighed. "I don't know." He regarded the screen as if the words were suddenly printed in Klingon. "Only reference I can find for anybody named 'Jonas' seem to be for a guy who was in the cell, but... it's all classified way above my pay grade." "Classified? You people do that?" "Says Alex did it herself. I don't... it ain't like I go poking around here a lot, y'know? But I never seen anyone marked like this – he ain't been hid, it's like he's been bloody erased." Campbell shoved open the door, and this time, it took a hand on her arm to stop her. "I'm going in there now, Donnie," Amy said coolly. "And you're not going to stop me." "What're you gonna do, eh?" Amy opened the glove box, fishing a short wrench from inside. "I'm gonna hit him with this and then play it by ear." "I... just be careful," Bell sighed, but Campbell was out of the car so quickly he'd never be sure if she heard it. * * * * * Meanwhile. "Why are you in Tucson, Jonas?" The scene would almost be quaint. They were seated around a wrought-iron table, along the edge of the coffeehouse's fenced-off patio on a picturesque December morning – if not for the tension in the air. Neither touched the glass of water set before them, but neither of them were precisely looking at each other, either. The overall sense of it was two lions stalking each other, merely pretending they were not. "I could ask you the same question, Alexandra." His brow raise was a slight thing. "I've heard of returning to the scene of the crime, but..." "I didn't do this, Jonas." "Forgive me if I seem disinclined to believe you, Alexandra." He seemed vaguely saddened by the notion. "I have been burned too many times by your lies, which are often quite extravagant." "The last thing I remember of the night, I was in the ballroom here, and I was Elisabeth." "This was after you arranged for a woman to be kidnapped for indoctrination into the Order, yes?" Stryker did not raise his voice, but Alex still flinched. "Yes, I know about that, too," he added. "That is not pertinent to this case," she hissed. "Everything is connected when you're dealing with the Spider in the Web!" His smile was not a kind one. "I learned that long ago." Within the café, a diminutive redhead with a wrench tucked into the back of her waistband waited hesitantly at the server's station. "Table for one?" the hostess asked as drifted back to her podium. "Actually, I think my friends are already here," Amy mumbled, stepping around the other woman and slipping out onto the porch. "...I had to know for sure," Jonas was saying. "Had to know what?" Amy forced good cheer into her voice as she approached. Jonas lifted his head, his cornflower blue gaze meeting her wolfen eyes. "That she truly has abandoned her principles completely." "'Abandoned' her principles?" Amy asked, brows lifting in indignation. "From where I stand, she actually found them." "You must be Amy," Jonas said around a thin, almost reptilian smile. "The Girl Who Would Save the Spider." "And you must be another one of the bottom-feeders." "Not exactly." It was Alexandra who interjected. "Amy, this is Jonas Stryker. He... used to work for me." "Used to?" As she had the day before, Campbell perched on the arm of Pierce's chair; this time, she was less "concerned lover" and more "watchful guard dog." "I'm currently working in the... ah, the 'private sector,' shall we say." Jonas' words were carefully chosen. "I didn't think you could ever really leave the Order," Amy murmured. Ordinarily, her fingers might have twined in Pierce's hair, but the short Pierotti wig prevented that. Instead, she slipped her thumb under the collar of Alex's blouse, a comforting touch. "Oh, you can leave the Order, Miss. It's just that the Order never leaves you." "So... what, you're some kind of cop now?" "Something like that." Amy only glanced to Alex briefly this time. "I already told the real cops that Alex was with me that night, so..." She shrugged a little. "So we three know that is a lie." His even stare was one Amy couldn't meet. "We three know that after the SCCW show, Alexandra Pierce became Elisabeth Pierotti and put in an appearance at a fundraiser in the main ballroom of that hotel right across the street." "I told you. That is the last thing I remember. I woke up five hours later in my hotel." "Really." His humor was dry at best. "I spoke with the catering staff, and it seems they recall someone meeting the description of Elisabeth Pierotti in an argument with one of their waitresses – a girl named Gabrielle, who happens to look like Lauren did the night she was found." Amy's hand stopped drawing tiny circles on Pierce's shoulder, but that was the only sign of her discomfort. "Yeah, because it's hard to look like Alex in your twisted little circle." Jonas spread his hands. 'Perhaps. I also have unconfirmed reports of Gabrielle, Elisabeth, and another, unidentified girl leaving the party less than an hour after it began." "All the way to Oakland?" Alex's brows climbed. "Even if the police knew that I was Elisabeth, your theory is missing why I wouldn't have just killed her here in Tucson. Why shit where I eat?" "I haven't figured that part out yet," Jonas admitted. "But I'm not going to let you get away with this. You have to know that." Amy rolled her eyes. "She's not 'getting away' with anything," she growled. "Isn't it possible that when she says she didn't do it that she actually, I don't know... didn't do it." "Amy." Alex's voice was gentle, quiet. "What? I know you didn't do this." "You're not going to convince him of that." "Why aren't you trying?" Amy turned to face Alex. "Tell him you didn't do it." "I didn't do it." Alex's gaze found her lap. "But I also know you're never going to convince Jonas Stryker of that." The man in the long coat stood, straightening the lapels of the only thing that made him stand out. "I really hope you didn't do this, Alexandra," he said, dropping a pair of bills atop the table. "I may disagree with your methods, but I never thought you were a monster." His smirk twisted. "I imagine we will see each other again before too long." The man didn't say goodbye or farewell, simply strode briskly towards the exit. Amy stood, drawing Pierce to her feet. "Did we get what we came for?" "I think so, yes." Alex still seemed sullen. "That and more." "Then go to the ladies' room and get rid of the stick in the mud. I'll go make sure Donnie hasn't had a heart attack, then we can go back to Oakland and you can quit stressing about this all the time. You promised Quinn some of your creepy alone time, anyway." The Spider sighed out, nodding slightly. "Thank you for coming." Amy rocked up to her tiptoes, touching her lips to Alex's gently. "You bet. Now go," she bade. "Off with you!" As Alexandra Pierce stepped briskly for the ladies' room, Amy Campbell's smile flickered down – just for a moment. She looked back over her shoulder towards the door, hurrying that way. Twelve minutes later, when Amy slid back into the van, Alexandra Pierce was already in the passenger's seat, perusing the information they'd obtained. "An' where've you been?" Donnie asked. "Oh, I... I totally got lost on the way back," Amy said. "You'd be surprised how many silver minivans there are on the streets this early." It was a lie – but if Bell or Pierce noticed it, they didn't show it. Amy restrained her grin as Bell meandered through the streets back to the airport for their flight back to Oakland. Spoiler alert: those twelve minutes may become important later. It was that late that afternoon, and just over twenty-four hours later that Meredith and Jessica Gage snuck into a movie theater. Flash Forward 1: A Night on the Town Day Three, 10:14 PM. New York City, New York. The cab driver was a portly, mocha-skinned man in his late thirties with a gut that hung over his belt and a pair of beady eyes that spent most of their time in the rearview mirror scraping up her form, and for once, Kathryn Shaw didn't toy with him. Ordinarily, she didn't mind the attention – if anything, a dress like the deep red, asymmetrical thing she wore on the plane only served to encourage men like Luis (maybe it was Louis, she couldn't rightly have said). Tonight was different, however. Tonight, she wasn't interested in finding a new cat's-paw. Tonight, Kathryn was coming home for the first time in... Christ, how long had it been? Four years? Five? "Here we are, lady," the man said. She favored him with a smile – just because she was nervous didn't mean that she was rude. "It's not 'lady,'" she said off-handedly. "It's Kat. Could you... would you mind getting the door?" Shaw ignored the man's sigh at having to get out into the drizzly, overcast evening, peering through the rain-spattered window up at the tall building, jutting like an angry middle finger against the sky. "How the mighty have fallen," she murmured as the man tugged the door open. This place used to be something special, a symbol that there were still people worth looking up to, whether you wanted to or not. She handed the driver a striped designer umbrella and waited dispassionately for him to open it. Kathryn Shaw slid to her feet, a little too close to him for his own comfort. Hey, just because she wasn't looking for a plaything didn't mean she wasn't still Kathryn Shaw. "Thanks," she said, forcing a little bit of breathiness into her words. "How much do I owe you?" "Twenty-five... uhh..." He had to peer back in to the meter, the numbers suddenly gone from his head. "Twenty-seven eighty." Shaw's purse was a tiny golden clutch, and she slipped two crisply folded twenty-dollar bills from within it, all without stepping away. "Keep the change, mm?" "Sure. Thanks." His Adam's apple bounced in a gulp. She stepped backwards, trailing her fingers down the hair on the man's forearm as she walked – no, prowled – towards the building. It was one of a hundred on the island of Manhattan, only noteworthy from its architect's obsession with gargoyles and the name one of Kathi's old boyfriends gave it – a name that stuck. Once upon a time, former NWC, SCCW, and PRIME superstar "Peerless" Hunter Sabuani called it "Fox Towers." The lobby was desolate, the overhead fluorescent lights flickering and dying in several places. Graffiti stained the near wall, marking over the notices tacked to the hall. Apparently, KBI Industries was late on their rent – or had been whenever they were served with eviction papers. Kathryn stood, just feet past the doorway, her shoulders slumped. "My God..." "Eh?" The security guard behind the counter looked up from a small television set under his desk. His name was Hugh, and he used to like this shift, but that was before his rabbit ears stopped working. Stupid government was out to get honest, working-class citizens. Kathryn turned, pulling up her tiny purse as she drew her cellphone from within, holding it up as if she were looking for a signal. Guys like this were like putty. "Oh, pooh," she said, pitching her voice a little higher than her usual breathy purr. "I'm not getting a signal in here." She looked over to the desk, widening her eyes just slightly. "Ohmigod," she stammered. "Ohmigod, I didn't even see you!" Shaw's steps over were mincing, more jagged than they should be. They caused all the right parts to jiggle. "Do you have a landline here?" Both elbows rested on the counter, her lean as blatant as any of the prostitutes walking down some of the nearby streets. "Yeah, it's... uhh..." Hugh's seat was a wheeled office chair – the kind he'd have to bring from home – and he pushed off from one side of the desk, swiveling around to face the other. Kathi unscrewed the stone atop her ring, pouring a white powder into the man's coffee mug as he pulled an old, repurposed office phone across from the far side of the counter. "Dial nine for an outside line," Hugh said. When he turned back to the screen (the Nets were fucking losing – again), his attention was split, and she knew it. Shaw dialed her "friend" – a number she'd chosen at random – having a quick, whispered conversation with the dial tone. Once she hung up for real, she turned again with a sigh that caused him to gulp down the remainder of his drink. "My friend will be here in a minute," she said. "Is it cool if I just... wait?" "Yeah, it's fine." When he shrugged, Hugh tried to look indifferent. He mostly failed. "So what's going on in this place, anyway? Seems kind of rundown to have a honest-to-goodness security guard out front." One of Kathryn Shaw's secrets for dealing with the male gender was simple (actually, most of them were simple, but this one was almost facile) – men like to talk about themselves. Give a guy a chance to puff up his own ego, and he is more than likely going to end up as putty. So it was a testament to whoever had hired him that gave that faux-indifferent shrug again. "I don't know," he said, squinting one bleary eye up at her. "I get paid to sit here an' watch the place." Another of Kathryn's secrets (this one just as obvious as the first) was that good drugs always help. Hugh flopped backwards in his leather office chair, head lolling to the side. Kathryn shook her head, tipping her wrist up to look at the face of her watch. "Two minutes?" she grumbled. "Losing my edge." Shaw stepped around the desk, feeling along the underside of the counter until she found a button. She didn't press it, however; just squatted down beside it and clipped the wire that ran from it with her cuticle-cutters. A pair of old-school brass keys from the visitor's box later, and she stood again. The man snored quietly and Kathryn reached under her skirt, tugging down her black lace thong. She tucked it in the man's hand, held loosely between his fingers. Far be it from her to leave the man without something to remember her by. Scene 16: The Deal Day Three, 4:34 PM: Thirty-five minutes before doomsday. The waiting was the worst part. Detective Helena Maddox sat in the driver's seat of her car, drumming her fingers to the machine-gun rhythm laid down by her heart. Perry Mathis sat beside her, the beat cop fidgeting only slightly less than she was. A half-dozen other unmarked cars lurked throughout the Jack London Square parking lot, their drivers all looking to her for guidance. As if she somehow understood all the nuances of this insanity. All she could think about was how badly she could use a beer right now. Maybe seven. Perry looked down at Helena's bouncing knee, and he couldn't help but smile. "Relax. You'll be fine." Maddox's own twist of a smirk was a rueful thing. "I wish I was so sure," she breathed. "I think you may be the only one who thinks that." "Aw, don't mind the rest of them." Mathis shrugged a little, glancing into the rearview mirror. "As long as you're sure we're doing the right thing, they'll come around." "I used to be sure. I thought I had it all figured out." "Did something change?" Perry Mathis had big, bushy eyebrows that were just starting to go gray. They made him look like the kindly grandfather on every bad TV sitcom, and when he lifted them right then, she almost laughed. Helena couldn't tell Perry about what she'd read in that envelope, however – the one she'd been given in a truck stop diner. She couldn't even use it as evidence – her attempts to independently verify the information had been frustrating at best. The reports in the packet were authentic; they were formatted properly, written on the correct forms, and were filed by agents active at the time. They just didn't exist. It wasn't that they'd been classified or lost in a fire – as far as the United States government was concerned, they'd simply never been filed at all. So she lied. "Nothing, I guess." She half-mumbled the words, and they felt like a betrayal, so she snapped her eyes away, turned to the window as Mathis' boisterous partner, Bernie Dixon wandered back to the car. "I don't like this," he said flatly – the third time he'd done so. "I don't trust this... this 'source' of yours." Dixon brushed at the front of his shirt – the mustard stain had been there as long as Maddox knew him, but the glob of cheese sauce was new; it came from the man's recent trip inside the theater for a tray of nachos. Helena glanced down to the small patio dining area of the nearby Starbuck's. Jonas Stryker was the only one among them even making an attempt to be unobtrusive while they waited, flipping idly through the business section of the USA Today as he nursed his coffee. "Hey." Mathis had to lean awkwardly to make eye contact with Bernie. "Helena vouched for him, and that ain't enough for you? C'mon!" "I can't help it." Dixon's shrug was hidden as he lifted his hand, cupping it around his lighter's flame as he lit up a cigarette – at least the third the man had smoked in the forty-five minutes they'd been waiting. "Guy's a spook – gives me the creepy-crawlies." As if on cue, Jonas picked up the BlackBerry resting on the table, tapping it back to life, He regarded the face for a moment, nodding succinctly to himself as he rose. Stryker folded his newspaper carefully, almost robotically, and tucked the bundle under one arm. The phone went back into its holster, and his chair was quickly slid back into its place under the table. Helena watched all of this before she responded. "I'm not asking you to trust him," she said finally. She met Bernie's eyes, forcing her fingers to stop their rapid-fire tapping. "I'm asking you to trust me." Dixon held his frown until he was sure the man in the long coat could hear him. "Whatever, Beanpole – it's your ass when this all goes pear-shaped." "C'mon, Bern." Mathis pushed out of the car. "You could try not being a dick about everything, just this once." "Screw you, Perry." Dixon stalked back towards the theater, his words trailing behind him along with the cigarette smoke. "You know what these kinds of people are like – you know." Perry leaned into the car, his brows at work again, making his expression even more apologetic than his tone. "I'll talk to him, Hel. Maybe smack him upside the head for you – always seems to right his ship." "Only when you do it, Perry." Maddox managed to grin, though she'd probably never quite know how. "Thanks, though."
