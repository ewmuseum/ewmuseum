Past Midnight Madness

26 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-26 Foreword: Enclosed Thoughts When you are given a sure fire antidote to your problems, you naturally expect it to work at its full performance. But, it wasn’t the case with the sleep aid that I took. In fact, I should have read the side effects on the side of the box, one of them being… more insomnia than I already had. So now I was lying on my couch, since I didn’t have a bedroom in my apartment. Well, I did have a bedroom, I just transformed it into a trophy room, you know how that is. My eyes were wide open staring at the ceiling, and I could swear that it was lowering down on me inches at a time the harder I stared at it. It must have been the effects of the medication. I was frightening myself into believing that the “walls were closing in on me”, and I felt like a mime when he did his trapped in a box routine. I always got a laugh out of that, but when it happens to you, you aren’t much into the humor of the situation. I felt trapped in my wide open spaces, and everything was a blur in front of my eyes as well, it was a real trip if I do say so myself. I didn’t really like the feeling of enclosed spaces, especially if that space ended up being my own apartment. So, I vowed to try to get off of the couch and get out and do something productive. Surely, there had to be a select few places open at this time of night. And when I turned to the clock near me on the coffee table, the thoughts of going out and painting the town red danced through my head. Maybe the open surroundings and cool breeze in the air would do me well, and surely, if I kept productive, maybe I could end up getting home and actually falling to sleep. This was pure insanity, the fact that even an invented antidote couldn’t solve my problems. No wonder the doctor’s always wanted me to be a test rat, and no wonder they were also always calling me a loon when my back was turned. So, with the thought of a fun night on the town firmly implanted in my cranium, I tried step one of what seemed to be a multiple step process. I tried to lift my body upright so that I could get off of the couch. Never have I experienced such dizziness and disorderly body motions, my spine seemed to swing about seamlessly back and forth, wobbling about on its way up to face a nearby window. My eyes opened more with a groggy feeling, and it almost felt like I was pulling the lids open with rubber cement trying to seal them shut. It felt so weird to actually open them at full attention, and soon enough I yawned to greet the moon’s light pulsating into my windowpane. I didn’t know what I was getting into… Scene One: Gutter Rats :: Finally arising from his Lazy Boy (and might I add stylish) couch, Iron Lung rubs his eyes profusely and groggily gets up on his two feet. His shirt is wrinkled, his eyes bloodshot (although you couldn’t tell from his red contacts being left in), and his hair in complete disarray. It was a frightening monster movie-type scene, and we were expecting Lung to let out a Frankenstein type groan any moment. :: “Ugh, I can’t get to sleep.” :: Stating the obvious in an emotionless fashion, our hero tries to make his way around the apartment room, finding it hard to catch his footing or get out of the way of common objects he had cluttered about. He almost tripped over his ottoman (ala Dick Van Dyke and his show of the same name), and he almost slipped and slid face first into his door thanks to another wrinkled shirt placed on his floor. :: “Who made this mess?” :: Looking around as if he weren’t the only one in the apartment, Lung yawns again and scratches at his scalp. But he was alone in this place, in fact, he hadn’t had company in quite some time. You would think that he would get used to all this solitude, but deep inside of him, you knew that he was lonely. Hell, he was actually talking to himself while walking around the room at this very moment. :: “I sure as hell didn’t, must be that damn Sasquatch again.” :: In a somewhat lame attempt to mock Billy Madison, IL sat back down on his couch, searching underneath with his hand for a pair of socks. Having no such luck, he lets out a loud and discomforting sigh, he had to go into his ‘laundry room’ in search of some clothes. Barely being able to drag his body up from the couch once more, our subject wobbles his way to a nearby room. This room happens to have two or three piles of clothes in it, all scattered about in various patterns. There were darker colored clothes, lighter color clothes, and of course the subject of his interest, undergarments. :: “Ugh, everything in here is a mess.” :: Stepping inside of the room, we see some more piles of clothing. It wasn’t exactly messy, the fabrics were all folded up in a storefront manner. It was the fact that they were lying about all over the floor that made Lung a bit discomforted. None of the less, he stumbled about until he located a pair of clean socks. His apartment complex was equipped with a laundry mat, and he usually had to wait until late at night to wash and dry his clothes. These clothes seemed rather clean, so back inside the main room he went, skipping on one foot at a time as he slid his socks on. :: “Ah, that’s more like it.” :: Bending down to get his shoes, Lung grunts a bit in discomfort while holding his lower back with one hand. Slipping them on one at a time and lacing them up, then tying them, he dizzily gets back upright and looks at his coffee table for some direction. :: “Now where did I put my wallet, keys, and phone…” :: Looking around the table without any coordination, he discovers all of his belongings resting on the far corner near the edge of it. Reaching for them carelessly and almost knocking over a nostalgic bottle of Coke, he puts the wallet into his right pocket and his apartment keys and phone into his left. :: “I’m too damn sleepy to drive. I think I’ll walk this route.” :: Now don’t get me wrong, it was a dumb idea to drive, especially in the condition that Lung was in. But his neighborhood wasn’t the best, to put it lightly, and a midnight walk seemed inappropriate to us anyway. But our hero decided to wobble his way to the door, saluting his room before locking the door and pulling it closed behind him. :: “The road beckons, and I’ve got the key!” :: Another spin-off of a Beck’s commercial, he was truly losing his mind. Was he that delusional from the sleep aid, or was it just late night antics playing a part in his demeanor? Was it the fact that he still felt some pressure going into a match that was now less than twenty-four hours away? Whatever the case, he wasn’t worried about a damn thing except for getting some good nights rest. So off went Lung, down the elevator shaft to the basement floor, and soon enough he was drunkard-like in his walking down the street where his apartment building was located. Every time a car went by with its lights on, it seemed to blind Lung for a moment and make him squint, this was surely a side effect of not seeing bright lights for quite some time. :: “Jeez, I didn’t know they could allow such high beams here! This isn’t the mountains ya know!” :: Resorting to talking to himself once more, it is clear to see that Lung isn’t exactly aware of his surroundings as he keeps walking down the street, the streetlights keeping him company for the time being. Keeping a mumbling hum on his way, he keeps straight while looking about at the closed shops. :: “This town is a ghostly one after dark.” :: Perhaps our hero was onto something. It seemed that after eleven at night, the streets went empty and the population went to nil. It also seemed that the farther he walked away from his complex, the more at home he felt. Maybe it was the chill in the air, or maybe it was just that he felt that some sort of pressure was being lifted off his shoulder. Never the less, a match was on the way, and soon enough some strange things would go on this moonlight walk by the complex. :: “Hey man, can you spare some change?” Scene Two: Drunken Drifters, the Infamous Barroom Brawl :: Hearing the call from what seemed to be nowhere, Lung turns to his right to see an elderly fellow against a brink wall, a tin cup within the grasp of his two palms. Shaking the cup about, our subject raises an eyebrow to the man, and his facial expression shows a bit of sympathy. :: “Hey, come on man. Spare some change so I can eat tonight?” :: The man smelled profusely of alcohol, but IL wasn’t one to mind that sort of thing, as he searches in his right pocket for some loose change. Taking out a couple of quarters, our subject drops the coins into the tin can. :: “Thank you, sir.” “Yah, you take care of yourself out there.” :: Walking away from the elderly man, Lung keeps his wobbly pace along the street, still in search for a good time. The end of the street wasn’t even a close distance away in our eyes, and the little bit of fog in the air didn’t help any. Finally, our stumbling hero arrives at a cheery oak door. It is a local bar, and from the sounds we hear from inside, it seems to be the type of place that would have a twenty-four hour happy hour. :: “Ah, now here’s my kinda place!” :: Iron Lung sluggishly makes his way towards the door, having a little of a hard time tugging it open. But finally, the pumping sound of the speakers come into our ear drums, and our hero’s eyes seem to light up and open wide as he sees girls, girls, and more girls dancing on the bar top. :: “Oh yah, this is definitely my kinda place!” :: Slowly making his way into the room, Lung keeps his head down until he bumps into a rather larger man. Looking up to him with his eyes a bit glazed, we see a muscular hand extended in a ‘stop’ sign language. :: “All right buddy, let’s see some ID.” “Um, no?” “What do you mean no?” “I mean hell no man. Don’t you know who I am?” “No, and I personally don’t give a damn.” :: A shocked look comes over Lung’s face, as the pumping music seems to come to a complete halt. Looking over the large man, Lung chuckles a bit, clinching a fist behind his back. :: “What’s so funny?” “Oh nothing, it’s just your face!” “What about it?” :: Lung hauls back and groggily slugs the bouncer right in the chin, knocking him backwards for the time being. Keeping on the attack, our hero keeps hitting the bouncer around the front of the door. :: “Come on Sail, you son of a bitch!” :: IL keeps on the assault of the bouncer, swearing up and down as he keeps calling Sails name. One more haul back and Lung has knocked the bouncer down, sticking a sly grin upon his face and dusting his shirt off. :: “Damn bouncers, they never learn.” :: Noticing that everyone is now looking at him, Lung tries to keep it cool and shouts, “Hey everybody, I thought this was a party!” Soon enough, everyone goes back to dancing and partying, ignoring and even stomping on the bouncer as the scene fades away to nothing, with Lung in the center of the action, dancing groggily all the while. ::
