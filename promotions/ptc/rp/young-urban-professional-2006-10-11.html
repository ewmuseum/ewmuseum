Young Urban Professional

11 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-11 *** Dear Mr. Abraham: Thank you for your reservation. We, the staff, hope to make your evening a memorable one. Please visit our website to submit your dinner reservation’s online. 23Club.com New York, New York Your Host - Larry Hastings Chefs - Mark Spears Daniel Bergan John Nash Eric Angelo *** As I listened to Beethoven’s Moonlight Sonata, I felt the smooth grain on the leather binder which firmly concealed the bill. I hadn’t planned on opening it since John and his second wife, Susie, promised to pick up the tab. The night was fine with the exception of the intermittent buzzing of cellular telephones and the obnoxious volume of some’s voices. I had warned the staff about lowering the prices of the appetizer’s because that would no doubt invite trouble with the locals. Alas, we remained distant on the subject. After I consumed my meal, I had to admire just how much better things had been before. When just being there meant more than having to actually tell someone you ate there because they knew. Now it was about being seen in the restaurant John Travolta played Twister with Christian Slater in. The restaurant had become noticeably watered-down since the First Lady showed up in September during the commemoration of 9/11. Now everyone who had a few bucks and a camera phone had to be there. I was hoping it was just a phase. I knew as soon as I spotted Nancy Grace however I couldn’t be seen there again. However I soon changed my mind after I graduated in the entertainment business. There always seemed to be something terribly impersonal about dining out. The intensity of the unmindful lecture consisted little more than gossip about celebrities and the front page headline on Rupert Murdock’s media darling, the New York Post. Of course I would concede my own tongue to the occasion but only so that my voice had been heard on the subject. John had to come out to defend Mark Foley for his pubescence exploits with young boys which almost gave me a hernia because I had already rehearsed my opinion from a New York Times editorial. It was like the man read the same article. By the way, John Abraham was my supposed friend from Lambert Business College. A school I never graduated from which was in Watertown. I secretly hated the man ever since he chose some loser from high school to be his best man at his wedding. To think he’d do that to his dorm mate. It was embarrassing. I couldn’t return his calls for a month. It was sometime after the subject of Ronald Regan which touched the very bowel of my soul when I felt I had to quickly change the topic in a desperate attempt at salvaging what little sanity I had at that moment in time. John had spun a tasteless web about how the man was the anti-Christ and his death marked the beginning of some biblical babble about seven years of tribulation. I thought he was an idiot before but I stood corrected. He was a rube. My mother told me never to converse with him after his sister joined the ACLU. If I had listened to her, I would be golden. The plan was basically to celebrate my entrance into Fuse with a subdued dinner at the 23 Club. Of course it was anything but subtle though I digress. It was the only place I could imagine myself before the big events that would follow. Despite the repugnance I had developed of the people. The restaurant was the only one with my tangerine marmalade and I couldn’t get back to the office the next day without it to prepare me for the horror I was sure to witness. Armand had a trick up his sleeve and there wasn’t a living thing in that office that didn’t know it. I knew my next sales opportunity would be about as retrievable as Trashcan Man’s penis. "I’m going to give that one an ‘F’." Ron said as he pointed out the waiter. His back was to them as he walked away formally with the MasterCard tucked firmly inside a leather red binder. Mexico blinked feverishly when he examined the service grade card that came with his table before removing a fountain pen from his interior pocket. He un-clipped it then locked the cap on the opposite end. "Why? These guys don’t make shit. You’ll just get him fired. How else is he going to finance that grand education from Columbia?" John mocked rubbing his hands together. Ron had a sip of Vodka before setting the glass down on the braided, white table cloth. "Now I know you must be joking." "Lighten up, Ron." John said with a white smile. "I don’t like sarcasm with my organic chicken. It makes me nauseous." Ron said as he placed a hand on his thigh. "Mexico you cad " He giggled. Ron felt the cool air conditioning enter the room just above his head as he placed his left arm on the back of his chair. He crossed his right leg over his left before taking a curious peak around the packed restaurant. "I’m a consumer. I need to feel wanted." Prior to the group joining each, other Mexico found himself in the backseat of a taxi cab. Not his favorite of places to spend quality minutes of his life yet walking through the hordes of cattle gave him no choice but to settle for the less inconsequential of public transportation. John’s bride felt she needed to get involved with her fifteen seconds. She only appeared moved by Mexico’s vanity than his wits which made him feel somewhat neutral to her. "Ron is that a Fioravanti suit?" She inquired stroking the coat’s collar. He shook his head. "No I’m on a budget now. Ralph Lauren." She gasped. "Oh baby. I’m sorry." "It’s fine. I’ll just take out my frustration in the ring." Mexico finally said. There was a caldron boiling in his stomach and it wasn’t an antacid. Learning his chances in the event made him want to experience acid reflux. Even if Ron felt dishonored he still invited the opportunity to step in the ring and remove the former member of the Prime roster from his fifteen minutes. At least it was enough to get the bookies on his side. "So you’re in the GTIT6 thing." John asked as he smiled at Susie. "Yes." He responded ignoring John’s ill-contrived quip. Despite his immediate inclination to pat himself on the back for somehow managing to sneak into not only Fuse but GTT6 he held back. The self-aggrandizement would cease at least until he was finished accomplishing what he had set out to do as soon as he wrapped the black velcro at the shin on his first pair of professional wrestling boots. There was a sense of entitlement. There always had been with him and he didn’t hold back his belief that he alone was eligible for whatever interested him. And though he usually got his way he noticed that professional wrestling was different. Sure, when he was in high school his wrestling coach was forced to put him on the team due to his blood. The basketball coach likewise had to abide by his will even though Ron refused to guard the perimeter. Though, he learned quickly in the independent’s life was not only abrasive but his royal pension had dissolved. No one cared that his family owned a multimillion dollar mansion out in Martha’s Vineyard. No one cared that he would summer frequently in the Hamptons. For the first time he had to earn their respect. John choked on his undesired humor. "And your opponent is a Trashcan?" "I’ll bitch-slap the poor bastard with a Vienna sausage." Mexico said while he removed his handkerchief from his lap. "Personally, I’m not amused. Trashcan will return to the obscurity he spawned. A minor diversion." "Prime wasn’t it? From what I understand he’s got the nuts to back it up." "Oh yeah? Well Hickory Farms said theirs were bigger." Mexico dropped the handkerchief on the table as he pushed his chair back. "Ron you’re not yourself tonight. What’s wrong?" Susie again interrupted feeling his forehead. He only grabbed her small hand and placed it back on her lap. "Work at the office? Armand still killing you poor bastards?" John smiled again. "I think it was the bumpy ride. Potholes make me irritable." As the nightfall emanated on the outside he could see Central Park. He noticed the familiar facade of red cedar trees melting in the backdrop of cedar hill like a remorseful effigy. The hill happened to be where many of the locals sunbathed and picnicked was becoming nothing more than a curved mass of shadow that grazed just at the underbelly of the horizon. Suddenly the street lamps hummed to a spectral glow and the park was visible once again. "I can’t sell what he’s giving us. Tomorrow he’s going to ask us to sell menthol cigarettes to George Burns’ corpse. I feel it." "Well it is cold-selling." He nudged Mexico with a snicker. "It’s beneath me but I’m supposed to keep a job or I don’t get in the will." He said leaning face first against his fist. John worked up enough emotion to actually feel legit. "You could get a job any - never mind. I mean if the wrestling turns into something you could drop it." Mexico wanted to hand him his Academy Award. "The blood doesn’t respect wrestling." He entertained. "I truly feel terrible for you." He mentioned when he turned to his wife. "Babe, how is the vodka?" Susie appeared glum. "Fine" Susie was more of a child than a wife. The least significant of things could get her to feeling terrible. When it rained on her birthday, she threw a fit that caused everyone including Mexico to leave. Her contention was that every year it rained on her birthday and that God must have hated her for some reason unbeknown to her. The last time the three had breakfast she claimed her egg souffle gave her herpes. After she entered the emergency at St. Vincent’s hospital, she was diagnosed fifteen minutes later with a zit. "Didn’t a football player call himself Ron Mexico as a cover to pick up women?" She said turning to John as if Mexico wasn’t sitting next to her. He sighed. "Maybe. But that’s not the point." "Eh?" John had to instigate. "I happen to be a fan of both Mexico and the name Ron." Ron contributed feeling challenged. "What was your name originally?" Susie poked once more. "That is classified information." Ron could have told Susie to ask her husband but that would be easy for her. If he did that he wouldn’t be able to forgive himself. "Classified? John, honey?" They only met up two to three times a week depending on the progress made into a scheduled subcontract. These were only products that were taken on from wholesalers who needed someone to go out and sell their merchandise. And most of that was junk that dollar stores had in back stock but that was why they needed salesmen to get out there and con the every man, woman, child and animal that they couldn’t live without a bottle-less cork. They assembled in an office on floor nine. The building was fifteen-storys in Manhattan just off Grand. The exterior was drab in comparison to the others that surrounded it but nonetheless it served its purpose. Ron always thought whenever he’d glance up at the edifice’s apex that King Kong would most likely own his building in a remarkable amount of time. Considering it took Kong only fifteen minutes to make the Empire State building his bitch it somewhat was worrying that he might be putting himself at risk should the unfortunate occasion arrive. The office itself was a dusty old relic from the twentieth century. So much so it made Mexico wonder if the interior decorator was Burt Reynolds who in a bold attempt tried to convince the company that he was straight. There was woodgrain on everything from the walls to the stick-on tiles on the floor. The best touch was when they’d break if they happened to be out of place once someone stepped on them. Ron broke a few himself but kicked them under Penis’ desk as if he had been the culprit. Speaking of which, Bob Penis, Mexico’s best sales friend, somehow sold his package of silverware. This was quite the feat given that no one else including Ron could sell theirs. Mexico awoke early so that he would get to the office first so that he could be ready to ask the Holy Grail of questions that pulsated at the surface of his mind. Whenever Bob made a sale, they called it a ‘Pistol Whip’. God knew when the man sold anything that was something to behold. If you set your watch to it you’d have to change your batteries twice before it happened again. The man was sitting in a seat hotter than Dennis Green’s and somehow he pulled one out of the hat. Mexico knew he had to give him his DAP. But at the same time find out what technique he used. Being upstaged by Penis was like losing a Miss Universe contest against Rosie O’Donnell. Ron folded his arms as he sat at the edge of his desk. "So how did you do it, Bob?" Mexico asked with a shrewd smile. Bob only giggled playfully. "Do what?" He knew damn well what Ron was asking but he had to rub in the epic victory for the Penis household so the rest of the wee-men in the office could feel worse about themselves. At that moment the man was a God among a field of cow-patties. Mexico wanted his technique so he could steal and patent it. "If you don’t tell me, at lunch, I will have no choice but to sneak a tainted leaf of spinach in your Caesar salad." He didn’t feel like he was kidding as he amiably lifted a hand. His pet smile felt fake. Enter Allen Greer. In the office some of the guys would debate the meaning to a song as their way to pass the time. This only happened one of the few times they assembled for the results meeting. This week Allen Greer, a thirty-nine-year-old nicotine enthusiast, decided to flip on the sublime; eighties pop radio station. For whatever reason after inhaling his smoke and removing it, he decided to flip on this one with his thumb and index finger around the black knob. His sleeves were rolled up halfway up to his forearm as his veins purged to the surface. He held it like he was holding a pencil by its eraser until he came upon the channel he was searching for. As he turned up the radio the office fell into a dull silence as they all tried to hear the lyrics. The song this week turned out to be "Tonight, Tonight, Tonight" by Genesis. Following the first line Ron found himself trying to record it word for word on a notepad. Everyone else was just as occupied as if they were trying to break a World War Two era Japanese code. "Did he just say he was ‘coming down like a monkey’?" Mexico disrupted as he was subdued by the shushing of his peers. After Ron had announced he joined FUSE he instantly became the most popular guy in the office. Before he’d be lucky to get a nod but now everyone had been catering to him as if he were Kate Moss. Now he was getting high fives and everything someone could receive. So one could understand how disappointed one might be when after building all the credibility he had recently was shot down by those who were now his supposed friends. "Turn that shit off." A raspy cry crowed from the doorway. Ron didn’t have to look up to find the source. Armand Killings was the sick love child of both Adolf Hitler and Cher. You didn’t know why the bastard had to exist but he never failed to surprise you with the endless malignancy best identified as his time as supervisor. Of course he’d always find a way to make their next job feel even less salvageable as the one prior. The man was a master of dollar store gems that would never even see the discount baskets of Wal-mart. Hell K-Mart used to hold a red light sale to stop people from buying them. No one made a killing like Armand. Or as he’d have everyone believe. After Mexico joined the club about a year or so ago after his father forced him to get a job he decided to go full-time with the firm. Not that he had a choice. Armand, though, wasn’t out there like the rest of them. He had the benefits of his job to enjoy, such as his work at the corporate office of AIM. It was in a loft in a high-rise in Manhattan. AIM happened to be the company they all worked for but they never knew if it was an anachronism or the actual name. It didn’t weight heavily one way or the other but oftentimes it came up over brunch. Armand knew though no one wanted to ask him directly. Killings presented a black suitcase as the room fell into a hush. They all gawked as he felt the black vinyl on his fingertips while he placed it on the table top. The table squealed as the weight of his hands found their way on the exterior. It was like judgement day once every week. The office of five was sitting on pins and needles hoping they’re sale would be worth slaving over. Even if they made base salary the commission was almost half the sales price. This was why the products were so expensive. This was also why they couldn’t sell them. Killings frowned. "All right ladies, here’s what you’re selling to the beautiful customers this week." Only the sound of chairs squeaking and scratching on the tile below them could be heard as they hunched over hoping to be the first to get a view of their next caper. As he unlocked the suitcase the awe soon turned to rabid disappointment as they noticed the metallic steel they loathed so much. It mocked them with its brilliant sheen as it reflected the office lights above. "Not the knives!" The concord was almost therapeutic for some knowing that they weren’t alone in their dissatisfaction. "Yes." "God Killings." Mexico said readjusting himself in his chair with arms crossed. "Are you trying to kill us? No one buys the knives. Besides you know people are expecting those. We couldn’t at least surprise them with the metabolic Vaseline?" The room agreed with him in grunts but Killings wasn’t having any of it. Mexico of course was appalled with how discouraging Armand could be. The man outdid himself every week. "Where’s a pen? I’m going to write my will." "A good salesman can always sell a set of knives. These have the compass that comes out of the handle. A real novel approach." He grumbled, but knowing he was asking for a miracle. "The last time I sold a set of knives was to a blind blue hair who bought them just so I’d leave. And after I went to go cash her check it bounced." Ron dismissed abruptly. Armand cleared his throat. "All the important industry data say knives are what sells so that’s what we’re doing next." "I’d sell a bowling ball to a paraplegic before I sell a case of those things." Ron hissed looking away. Armand cleared his through before turning to Bob’s always anxious exterior. "Hey Penis why don’t you change Mexico’s diaper? Ron are you part of the team or part of the problem? I think I’m going to have to mark your name on the chalkboard." ‘Oh god, anything but the chalkboard’. He thought feeling an ambition to be quiet. "All right, I’ll catch my tongue." "The name of the last failure is still up here. There’s enough room for another." The last time someone was placed on the chalkboard they not only were treated like a Time’s Square hobo but weren’t even asked to sell anything. A man had to quit so he could feed his family again. Mexico didn’t have a family to feed but salt water fish weren’t cheap to care for. "Anyone else disagree?" Armand grinned. "Good." He backed away from the table with an extended palm. "They’re all yours. You can pick up your briefcases before you leave. Don’t bother coming back until you’ve sold it." Mexico bent over to Penis’ desk when he turned to find Ron with a hand over his mouth. "Last time I was this disappointed was when I watched ‘Leprechaun in the Hood’." He said under his breath before sounding more coherent. "That movie had potential. But the acting just made it hard to believe for me. I needed to believe those were really rappers." "Never saw it." Penis said brushing him off. "All right Bob, between you and me, I don’t think one of us is going to return with a job." "I don’t think it’s so bad." Bob said. "You would think that." Penis appeared visibly concerned. "Why you say that?" Ron had already placed his Bluetooth headset on his left ear. "Why? Remember when Armand had us selling those fruit baskets door to door in June?" "So?" Bob continued. "How many did you sell?" Ron finally turned to him. "I sold a great deal -That doesn’t mean anything." "I had to buy all mine just to keep this job." "Well you have the money. If it wasn’t for my sister’s wedding, my mother would have never bought mine." Penis stood up and opened his old suitcase. He began putting a few files and notes he had taken as he filed them away. Afterwards he rolled up his sleeves as everyone prepared to decide what method of suicide would be the most subtle. However Bob appeared indifferent and that didn’t settle well with Ron. Just because he sold a set of silverware that didn’t mean he was Jesus Christ yet. He was merely Moses working himself up the food chain. Though if he did sell the knives that certainly would prove he could at least part the sea. "At least you have a mother that cares. Mine won’t allow me a month allowance without me proving I haven’t squandered my green leaves on chai tea lattes." Ron jawed less enamored by Bob’s arrogance. "Personally the Joe Lieberman campaign dolls were a joke." He insisted. Ron had to remove the Bluetooth in disgust. "Are you psycho? What’s more huggable than a Joe Lieberman doll?" "Yeah but we knew he wasn’t winning. I felt like such a tool." "He’s up twenty points now. Besides that was for a good cause. Joseph Lieberman did a lot more for you and me than we’ll ever know." He nodded feeling a sense of accomplishment. Bob finally locked his suitcase and turned to Ron. " - Like what?" "-Than we’ll EVER know." "I see." He glowered. Mexico shrugged. "My parents told me to vote for him." After Killings had fled his latest crime scene, Bob finally paced up to the front to grab hold of his briefcase of knives. Mexico watched his frail frame struggle to lift both of his bags but didn’t move to help him. This was of course due to him checking out the knives cases forcing him to anticipate his wardrobe for the following week. Ron admired the display with the fierce tapping of a thoughtful index finger on the desk. He thought the vinyl would look remarkable against his Newman suit. He thought he also might be able to reap a few perks had his outfit plus bag made him look Secret Service crazy. As if he had a bag full of guns or the like. He didn’t want to scare anybody. He just wanted to be regarded more than he thought people gave him credit for. "Thought so." Bob remarked snapping Ron out of his deliberation. "So what now?" "I think this is the part where I train."
