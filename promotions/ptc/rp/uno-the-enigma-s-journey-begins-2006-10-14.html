Uno - The Enigma's Journey Begins

14 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-14 South of the border, down Mexico way, That's where I fell in love, where stars above came out to play, And now as I wonder, my thoughts ever stray, South of the border, down Mexico way. The masked man sitting in the passenger seat of the decrepit, beaten-up VW camper van had never heard Chris Isaak’s classic song, but if he had, the lyrics would have doubtlessly been ringing through his head as the dry, arid terrain of northern Mexico flashed by. He had fallen in love in this country, not to some goddess, nor to a solid and dependable mamacita. No, his love was reserved for something else, for a sport that was as integral a part of Mexican culture as Cortes and his band of merry men. He was part of a line that stretched back almost a hundred years, a protector of the old ways and an innovator of new customs. The mask he wears is not so much a piece of clothing, rather a piece of his soul. He is a luchador. As the rickety old vehicle huffs and puffs its way along the narrow, unpaved road, his mind is working overtime, questions about everything and nothing whizzing around his brain like electrons round a nucleus. Is it a wise move to leave the adoring fans in his home country? Does it seem plausible that he will get a job in one of the multitude of companies that create the entity known as PTC? What exactly does GTT stand for? Why is his manager wearing an ‘I HEART NYC’ t-shirt? All these and more are considered, albeit with varying degrees of interest, in the few minutes it takes the van to make it up the shallowly inclined hill. The man sitting in the driving seat is in dreamland. When he visited the arenas as a young boy to watch the lucha spectaculars, he knew that wrestling would be a major part of his life. From the age of eight onwards his every waking thought was occupied by daydreams of being a star, of illuminating arenas around Mexico and indeed the world like the light of a million candles. He pored over magazines, learning about the men behind the masks, he studied tapes incessantly, using a battered old Betamax recorder salvaged from someone else’s trash. It was, it seemed then, inevitable that he would fulfil his dream. Disaster struck when he was twenty-two. His fledgling career had been nothing short of spectacular, his name, well, his ring name, becoming synonymous with excitement and ground-breaking moves and techniques. It was one of these pioneering moves that was to cost him his career, a twisting somersault tope suicida that led to a blown out knee, seven operations and an eventual diagnosis of ’never wrestle again’. What to do? For his life had been built around the business, his hopes and ambitions linked inextricably to the canvas for the majority of his life, what else was there to do? Well, manage, he supposed. So that’s what he did. If he couldn’t be in that ring himself, he would play an integral part in the career of another. Namely in the career of a man he had watched since his debut. That man was now known as M3, the Mysterious Masked Mexican, but to Jorge Ramos he would always be @&*$?! *£?|@!, the young man who he took under his wing and promised to make the greatest luchador of all time. That promise had yet to reach fruition but as the quickly failing vehicle made its way up the hill towards a new life, Jorge felt a certain sense of pride and achievement. For while his protégé, the man he had mentored for the last ten years, was far from perfect, he was now taking a step that only a handful before him had - cracking the land north of the border. The tournament known as GTT provided an irresistible opportunity for M3, a chance to show just what he could do and to gain a contract with one of the big companies - PRIME, FUSE, AWC - any one of these would make M3, and Jorge, rich, once they saw what the masked luchador could do in the squared circle. Finally, after what seemed an interminable wait but in actuality was only a few minutes, the border came into view. Considering the magnitude of the progression it represented, it was rather unimpressive, a cluster of nondescript buildings and a few flimsy barriers to stop illegal entry into the land of Uncle Sam. Despite this though, to Jorge and M3, it looked like Heaven on Earth. At this time of the morning, the queues at the checkpoints were short, a smattering of vehicles, usually well past their best, waiting patiently to cross the invisible line between their homeland and the good old US Of A, many hoping that the border guards, with their shiny badges and all-too-visible firearms, didn’t look too closely at the contents of their trunks. As Jorge brought the camper van to a stop in position three in a line, he turned to his passenger, who had been oddly quiet throughout the trip to Tijuana. "Well, how are joo feeling, eh?" The mask he was wearing made his facial expressions almost impossible to read, yet his eyes told the whole story - he was nervous as hell. So nervous, that he forgot every word of English he had been taught over the last few months, answering his manager with a shrug of the shoulders. "I thought so, joo are very tense looking, I think joo need to relax a bit. How about a little music, no?" A shake of the head indicated that music wasn’t going to solve anything right now, so Jorge withdrew his hand from the pile of cassettes in the backseat, concern showing on his face. While he’d expected a little trepidation from his protégé, a man who had never been out of Mexico in his life, never had Jorge anticipated this level of uncertainty and fear at what was to come. "Es nothing to worry about, joo hear me, es jus’ a different country, when they see what joo can do, they will love you, joo got to believe me." As he spoke, Jorge moved the van up into position two in the line - it was almost time. The car in front was one of the few modern automobiles in view and had American plates on it, California to be exact, the bear that was the state’s icon displayed proudly on a sticker in the back window as well as on the licence plate. It was waved through quickly and then it was Jorge’s turn. As he pulled up to the booth, he turned back to his charge. "Here we go, es all or nothing now, eh?" Rolling down the driver’s side window, Jorge was confronted by the border guard. A border guard exactly like you’d expect. The buttons of his shirt were strained almost to breaking point by his ample gut, which protruded so far that his belt buckle could not be seen. The bright red hair looked like it had been styled with a tub of lard and the redness of his neck added even more to the picture. All you needed was some chewing tobacco and an incestuous relationship and you’d have had yourself a good old southern farmer. As it was, he was merely a southern boy who’d ’done well fer hisself, gittin’ a job in the government’. When he spoke, Jorge listened intently, mainly because the drawl of the guard was almost indecipherable. "Mornin’ suh, and where maight y’all be goin’ today?" He let out a hearty laugh - comedy at it’s finest, obviously. Jorge was confused, but laughed along anyway. "What’s the reason for y’all’s vi- Hold on a minute, why is your friend wearin’ a mask?! Suh, could you please remove the mask!" Jorge’s eyes widened, this wasn’t in the plan. For his passenger’s mask was a sacred item, only to be removed well away from the eyes of the public, less the illusion and mystery be destroyed. "Suh, ah said could you please remove the mask!" With this he opened up his holster, making sure that both Jorge and M3 could see the gun inside. His other hand went to his radio, evidently calling for backup. "We have a code ninety-six at booth five, repeat code ninety-six, request immediate assistance." "Hold on, hold on, there is no need for joo to worry, my friend is a luchador, he always wears this mask. Joo jus’ need to calm dow-" "Please do not tell me what to do suh! Now I will ask you one more time - remove the mask!" Jorge had a decision to make, one he made quickly. He spoke rapidly in Spanish to M3, who replied just as quickly and with a not-too-happy tone in his voice. As for our border guard, well, he didn’t know what was going on and just kept repeating one phrase. "In English please!" Eventually the argument stopped after Jorge said something loud and decisive involving the word tu mama. At this point, M3 began to undo the lace of his mask as Jorge turned back to the guard, who had since been joined by two of his colleagues, a wide and affable grin on his face. "Sorry about that, jus’ a leetle disagreement." The two newcomers looked at Jorge suspiciously as the original guard finally took his hand off his gun. "Alright then, if I could see both of y’all’s passports." Jorge dug around in the backseat for a second, trying to find their passports in the mound of clothes, blankets and other junk that had taken over the rear of the van. Finally he extracted two passports, one old and well used, the other bright and evidently new. Handing them over to the guard, his heart was racing - this was it. The guard pored over the passports intently, looking hard for any discrepancy that he could exploit to stop these two from setting foot in his country. Unfortunately, everything was in order, the dejection clear to see on his face. "Alright boys, I got this situation under control now, thanks for all y’all’s help." The other guards dispersed as the original turned his attention back to Jorge handing over the two passports. "Y’all enjoy your trip." That was it. They were through. As they pulled away from the border, the wheels of the van moving as bumpily over US soil as it had in Mexico, the enormity of it all hit Jorge…followed quickly by a sense of shock that almost sent them off the road, as a tiny figure that looked like a monkey launched itself up from under the pile of clothes in the backseat. "Holy shit! What the hell?!" The tiny figure wasn’t actually a monkey, in reality it was a midget in a monkey suit, a mini wrestler who was held in as much esteem down in Mexico as the regular guys. He was high-fiving the now re-masked M3 as Jorge brought the van to an abrupt halt. "What have joo done?! Thees es not legal, he es illegal! We are going to prison for thees, mark my words!" Jorge’s face was aghast but the eyes of M3 had never looked happier or more comfortable. It hadn’t been leaving Mexico that he’d been worried about, it had been the fear of getting caught bringing an illegal, however tiny he was, immigrant, into the United States. Now that they were through, the English came flooding back to him, not the stuff he’d learned at night school but the stuff he’d learned from movies. "Chill out dude, it is all good in the ‘hood." "Oh dios mio." And so, the journey of the masked luchador began…
