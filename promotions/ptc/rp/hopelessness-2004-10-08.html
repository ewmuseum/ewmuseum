Hopelessness

8 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-08 In a few days comes the greatest test in my entire career. The toughest opponent I’ll ever face for a multitude of different reasons. This guy should have won the GTT already, this tournament. If I am to be great enough to win this tournament I will have to beat people like my opponent in a few days. Another reason this is going to be my toughest match is the same reason why I don’t want to utter his name. The sheer thought that I’ll have to face this person in my upcoming match sends shivers down my spine. Don’t think about it… It’s so difficult though… trying to get these things out of my head when I’m doing the one thing that incites these kinds of thoughts. I’m waiting: For the train, with my large Adidas kitbag weighing down my aching shoulder, it was a hard day’s training. The strap dug its way into my shoulder blade like a cat scratching against furniture. I couldn’t take it. I dropped the bag to my side. It hit the concrete with a thud. I really pushed myself today. This means more to me than a lot of people realise. A change of clothes seems to have no effect as my clean shirt is now soaked through with sweat. I’m still out of breath and panting softly, trying to regain a normal breathing pattern. But it was for good reason. This week I have my third round match in the GTT, and it’s not any ordinary competitor either. Don’t think about it… A beam of light struck my vision like a battering ram, almost making me stumble backward in the sudden contrast to the dark surroundings my eyes had adjusted to. The train is coming into the station, I’m almost dreading it. I have to lift up the bag and make a move again, onto the train and back to the apartment. All of that effort, moving my aching body all that way. And that’s not the only depressing thing about it all. I’ve been doing this almost my entire life. Go and train, get on the train, get back to apartment, go to arena, wrestle, go back to apartment, go train, get on the train… a perpetual cycle of wrestling; consuming every last joule of energy in my body, consuming every last moment of my entire life. The train pulled up at the station with a roaring screech. It’s nice to know the brakes work on these things. I line myself up with one of the doors, leaving room for the people to get off the train and brush past me as they hurry to their homes, back from their hard day’s work. I step onto the train dragging the heavy kitbag behind me and slouching into the first free seat that I come across, sitting the kitbag in the seat next to me and let out a yawn. Then I realised I’d forgotten something. You know that happens sometimes, you are so preoccupied in your own feelings that you completely forget a really important thing. I’ve left my duck in my kitbag. Grasping the zip and yanking it open in one swift motion I let the waft of stench bellow out. I put my hand in and carefully pull out the small plastic yellow duck, placing him on the cup holder so he’s nice and comfy. “Hello there,” I say regretfully to Ducky, who looks back at me a little miffed at being left in the smelly bag for so long, “sorry little mate. I’m not thinking straight today.” Talk about an understatement. I don’t think I’ve ever been in this frame of mind before. This just isn’t me. I don’t usually think about the world, my place in life, the next match. In fact I don’t think I usually think at all! But I shouldn’t think about this… thinking about this will put me in completely the wrong frame of mind. Thinking about this will reveal to me the severity and importance of the tasks I have for me this week. It’s like walking across a canyon on a tightrope and looking down to check your distance from the ground. You just don’t want to do it if you want to still be tip-toeing across the thing. So I do the usual thing for these kind of situations to keep my mind off of these things, I talk to Ducky. “Anything decent on the telly recently, little mate?” I ask the duck. There isn’t very much an inanimate object can do, but staring at the television screen is one of the things this little duck can do very well. No answer. I guess he doesn’t feel like talking to me. It figures, I think he’s still a bit annoyed at me locking him away in my stinky kitbag for the last few minutes. What do you have to do to get some decent conversation around here? That couple a bit further up the train seem to be enjoying themselves, having a good old laugh and giggle at something, I’m not sure what. “Going to talk to me yet?” I say back to Ducky, trying to get something out of him. “…………” Nothing The silence is broken from a whale of a laugh from the woman further up the train, clutching her cotton jumper at the hip and trying to hold back the tears. I don’t know what the bloke has said to her, but it must have been pretty damn funny. The man gives a slight chuckle at his joke before waving his palm out and saying something else to her, causing another huge bellow from the lady. Hang on a second. Why are they both looking down the train at me now, as if they’re waiting for me to do something… waiting for me to say something…? I think I realise what’s going on now. I think I realise why these people are so amused by what this man has been saying. He’s not been telling jokes; he wasn’t trying to impress the lady with his sharp wit. He’s been commentating… highlighting… describing what I’ve been doing. As I stare blankly up the train at the faces of these people waiting for me to entertain them some more one thing passes through my thoughts that I can’t seem to let go. As much as I try to shake it off as nothing, it’s something I can’t get out of my head: Do these people think I’m a joke? It seems that that was just the beginning of it all. Those two people were not the only two people in this carriage. And it seems that the various other people catching the train by themselves also seemed to find my conversation with a bath toy amusing. Other people pretending to watch out of the window whenever I looked their way, but as soon as I started to change where I looked I could see peering my way, out of the corner of my eye they were watching, waiting, for the next time I spoke to Ducky. Why do they find this so amusing? At least there’s one person on the train that doesn’t find this so funny, a small child trying to settle into his seat but fidgeting frantically. He looks not with a beaming goofy grin, but with a face of utter confusion. But he stares straight at me. Is there something about me that this child doesn’t quite grasp? I know I’m a professional wrestler, but I’m not massive, I’m just a cruiserweight really. Next to the kid was an elderly lady, probably the child’s grandmother taking his grandson out on a trip into town or something. The child now obviously wanting to get an answer to his bewilderment reaches over to her woollen sweater and clasps it with his little fingers, giving the arm of the jumper a few tugs to gain the attention of the lady. The lady didn’t want to attend to the child’s query. She continued to look out of the window, pretending to ignore the poor boy. Why doesn’t she want to answer? It’s almost as if she seems embarrassed about it. She doesn’t want to talk to the child in case what he says is going to make a mockery of the two of them, or maybe something else… “Nanny…” the child uttered, not wanting to give up on getting the answer he needs. “Nanny…?” he asks once more, giving a few more tugs. “Nanny…” he says again, almost monotonously. The elderly lady gives a long sigh and then replies to the child, almost dejected at being forced into talking to the kid, “Yes Joshua, what is it my dear?” she says looking down into the kid’s puzzled face. “Why does that silly man talk to the toy duck, doesn’t he know it’s not real?” “Shush dear. I’ll tell you about it when we get home.” “Okay, nanny.” Ouch. It hit me like a sledge hammer. My chest feels like it’s caving in from the devastation of what was just said. Not only does the couple at the end of the train find great amusement from my actions, not only do the other people in the train fail to resist the temptation to stop and stare at me at any opportunity, but this small child, only about six years old, knowing very little about the world around him, thinks that I’m a silly man… …a silly man Okay, I’ll be the first to admit that I do act quite eccentrically to people, particularly when I’m in the wrestling arena in front of all the cameras, and I do admit that my actions are quite silly, but I never quite realised that it is the perception of all the people around me that I am a silly man. A man that acts silly is one thing, but someone almost categorising me as a silly man. And not just a regular person, not just someone who might have their own issues in their lives dictating their perception of others, a small child. That was the first thing that hit me, but the second thing I’ve realised is equally as painful. ‘Why does [he] talk to the toy duck, doesn’t he know it’s real?’ I know most people, okay all people apart from me, think that this duck is not real, but I happen to think that this is a very special duck. Since the first day we met, with the duck blowing across the snowy street in Chicago and stopping right in front of me, only to later show up on the seat I was about to sit on, on the bus; since that day I’ve been sure that this duck is real. But it really hits it home to you when a six year old has a better grasp of reality than I do. As the other people on the train continue to wait for another classic comedy moment provided by the one-man laughing stock also known as Exceptional Dave Hurst I breathe out a large breath of desperation before my eyes squint and I lean forward, placing my head in my hands, my entire body and mind breaking down into my hands. I run my fingers through my scraggly hair to get a better grip and try and gain some control over my thoughts and feelings, but it’s no use. A small tear begins to slowly make its way down my cheek. I can’t see it, but can feel its cold wet trail tracing down my skin. I sniffle, a stream of liquid shooting down my nasal passage. So this is what people think of Exceptional Dave Hurst… the laughing stock of wrestling, the running joke of the industry. Why didn’t I ever realise this before? -----=====|=====----- The train pulls into the station. It’s my stop. I take Ducky from the cup holder and place him carefully back into the kitbag in a nice comfy spot and pull the zip back over, sealing the bag. The pain from earlier is only a passing thought now. I couldn’t care less about my physical aches and pains. It has absolutely no effect on me compared to the emotional stress I’m feeling. I pull the bag over my shoulder and step off of the train, onto the platform and make my way out of the train station. The people gather in a large grouping as they make their way through that one small gate to get out of the station. People funnel through the gate to get out of the station and as I near the gate myself I get a sharp nudge from a person making his own way out. A rather large kid, probably in his mid-to-late teens with an abundance of acne on his face grunts in disapproval and tilts his head to give a stern look to me, as if it was my fault he bumped into me. And then he sees my face and his expression completely changes. Now he’s no longer irritated by my presence, he’s shocked to see me. “Hey… you’re Dave Hurst right?” the kid says, obviously he’s a bit of a wrestling fan. “Er… yeah…” I say in reply, not really sure what to say and taken a bit aback after my thoughts from earlier. “You better not win.” He says threateningly, with a heavy breath of intimidation and anger present on his face. What a ridiculous thing to say? Why can’t I beat… my opponent this week? “Are you a Phoenix mark then mate?” I say to him as we make our way down the street, it’s the only explanation I can come up with for him saying such a thing. “No!” the guy says sternly in reply, “I’m an Angelo Deville mark, but if you win this match it’ll be a fucking travesty.” “Why…?” I ask, causing him to look away from me before chuckling “Because you’re a fucking joke, Hurst!” the kid says, almost shouting at me. “You’re a laughing stock! You shouldn’t even be competing in this tournament. You’re just a pathetic gimmick promotions hire because they want more little kids to watch the shows who know nothing about real wrestling.” “Yeah… well whatever mate.” I say moving away from his spluttering face, avoiding any more saliva attacks and taking a turn down a different street, letting the angry fan walk away. My house isn’t even this way, but I want to get away from that conversation. It’s an issue I’ve wanted to avoid my entire career. Every time anyone has ever questioned my own wrestling ability based on the way I act in front of the cameras I always skirt around the issue. It’s a strange thing, as my own name is Exceptional Dave Hurst, but I don’t even know why I keep that name anymore. I’m not exceptional, I never have been. I’m merely… mediocre. After the sidetracking and the scenic route I approach my apartment. I reach into my pocket to fetch my keys, but as I pull them out and look up at the door there seems to be someone there ringing the bell. “Hello…?” I say, causing the person in front of the door to gasp and spin around, spooked by my intrusion. “Oh! Dave… Hi!” It’s Wendy Sanchez. I haven’t seen her in quite a while. She used to be a news correspondent for Primetime Central television that often came to me for interviews, but recently she’s been working as a journalist for a newspaper with her own wrestling column for the recent surge of popularity in wrestling. “I was just trying to call you, I didn’t realise you were out.” “’alo Wendy,” I say trying to sound as enthusiastic as someone feeling the way I am can be. I stick the key in the lock, give it a twitch and push the door open. “Come in…” -----=====|=====----- “Here you go,” I say as I carefully move the coffee mug hovering onto the round marbleised-glass coffee table, one of the amenities from Primetime Central for doing so well in their tournament, the provided apartment with flashy art-deco furniture. I place my own down on the opposite side of the table and relax into my comfy padded leather chair soothing my aching shoulder muscles and making me feel more at ease and less stressed out after the chain of events from earlier. “Okay then Wendy,” I say with a sincere smile, “how can I help you?” “Well… how about…” Wendy says returning the smile, delaying her request to emphasise the surprise, “an interview. Just like old times.” “Ha, ha,” “There aren’t any cameras here, but if I could have something to put in my column that would be great.” “Sorry Wendy,” I say shaking my head, she stops suddenly and looks back at me bewildered as to why not, “I’m really not feeling myself right this moment, it’s probably not a good time.” “… Dave, are you okay…?” Wendy asks leaning towards me and trying to get some eye contact, I look straight down at the floor. She’s obviously sensing that there’s something seriously wrong here, but I’m not even sure even I know the severity of what I’m going through at the moment. “I’m fine Wendy, I just need some alone time.” I say softly, before looking up at her and into her eyes. “Okay… but if you need someone to talk t-“ “No!” I snap, cutting Wendy off, “I just need to be alone!” Wendy shifts right back into her seat, completely startled from my actions. She knows, as I do, that this isn’t me. But she doesn’t want to make the situation any worse. She makes it to her feet and walks over to the door to make her way out. How could I do that to Wendy? She’s just trying to help. I didn’t have to snap at her like that. “Look… sorry Wendy,” I say forlornly stopping her in her tracks, “but as I said, this really isn’t a good time right now.” “Don’t be…” she says, now disgruntled at my actions, “I’m sorry I bothered you.” The door slams shut leaving me in the one thing I thought I wanted for so long, but now realise that this is probably the worst situation I could have put myself in. Complete and utter silence… Complete loneliness… -----=====|=====----- I’m not much of a drinker. I’ve never smoked in my life. But I don’t care anymore. I clasp the whiskey bottle clumsily, the bottom slips on the marbleised glass and nearly goes straight off the table, but I clutch it just in time to bring it up in the air. I tilt it to the glass and pour another full glass of the strong stuff, place the bottle back on the table and then knock back the drink, swallowing the lot in two quick gulps. I shake my head once more, the strength obviously too much for me. As I said before, I’m not much of a drinker. But I don’t care anymore. As I start to pour another glass, the whiskey bottle getting close to emptiness, I see in a blur a packet of cigarettes over the other side of the table. Wendy must have left them there after leaving in a hurry. I put the glass and bottle down, reach over, grab the cigarette packet and open it up, pulling a cigarette out and plonking it in my mouth. I take the lighter, fire it up and light the tip. As I said, I’ve never smoked in my life either, but I just don’t care anymore. I’m not a pathetic kid in a man’s body. I can be a man if I want to be a man. If I want a cigarette I can have a cigarette, nobody’s going to stop me. I take a puff of the cigarette, allowing the smoke to enter my body. It fills my lungs, but is rejected instantly causing me to cough the smoke out. I continue to cough relentlessly, spluttering and choking. What am I kidding? I’m a pathetic kid in a man’s body. I mean, I can’t even smoke a bloody cigarette without coughing up like a 12 year old who managed to steal a ciggie from his dad and was trying it out. What hope do I have of taking on Jay Phoenix? Yeah, that’s right. I said it. Jay Phoenix. Not just the man who used to be my boss in PWW, not even just my friend. My idol… A man I’ve looked up to ever since seeing him tear apart all-comers in this very tournament for GTT2. Somebody I always aspire to be like, I aspire to have the credibility, skill and professionalism that this man brings with him everywhere he goes. And I’m supposed to beat this guy now. I’m not a challenge for this man. I’m not even a passing thought. Everybody knows that I’m just a fool. A person brought in for a bit of comic relief, somebody to laugh at. Somebody to make fun of as you watch him prance around like an idiot. Maybe I should do myself a favour and just not show up. I think I’d rather not show up than go there to look a fool and be humiliated by my friend in front of a huge crowd of people and millions of television viewers. I’ll just stay here. Stay here and wallow in my self pity. The spluttering is too much for my body, my stomach can’t take it any longer. That and the fact that I’ve drunk a whole bottle of whiskey are causing me to hyperventilate and my chest to convulse. Leaning forward in one quick moment with my mouth agape, I let a spray of thick liquid shoot out all over the table. I lean back in my chair, wipe the sick from my mouth and break down into streams of tears. Where did it all go wrong? I was a promising superstar. They said I was destined for great things. They said I had a great career ahead of me. What did I have? Nothing… I look around the room, looking for some kind of answer. Now I understand. In front of me, past the sick dripping from the marbleised-glass coffee table, leaning against the wall is my kitbag. The kitbag isn’t the answer to my problems. It’s what’s in my kitbag. Who, is in my kitbag. The thing that I thought of as a gift, I thought as something from heaven, here to aid me. But I was so wrong. That thing is no gift. It’s a curse. A curse set to ridicule me, a slow spiral set to ridicule me more and more. But I won’t be trapped in this spiral anymore! I lean forward and struggle to my feet, as I do so, with my balance in tatters, I catch my leg on the glass table. “ARGH!” I cry in complete and utter fury. In my blind rage I grab the table from underneath and, with all my force, flip it over and into the air, letting it crash into the floor cracking and then shattering into thousands of pieces. But I just don’t care. I walk over to the kitbag, grab the zip and yank it so hard the zip rips open. I reach inside and clutch the object of all my problems. I walk across the room, holding it up to my face, looking right into its eyes. The glass crushes under my shoes as I stare straight into the plastic duck’s face. “DUCKY!” I scream at the top of my lungs in a slurred drunken fury. “You’ve haunted me for what seems an eternity…” I walk towards the window. “For an age I’ve kept you by my side, as a companion, as a friend…” I place the duck on the window ledge, and place my hands on the handles for the window, turning them to unlock. “But what have you done, eh? What have you ever done for me…?” In one almighty tug the window slides upwards and slams into the ceiling, cracking the top of the window from the impact. “I was a promising wrestler, set for big things, and then you came along and that all went down the toilet, didn’t it…?” I grab the duck with my right hand and squeeze it with everything that I have. “Well I’ve had enough of you; I’ve had enough of your curse; I’ve had enough of you TAKING MY LIFE…!” I bring my arm back and then swing it forward throwing the duck as far as my arm could. The duck flew through the air, circling slowly. It hit the road and bounced once under a parked car. Out of sight… Out of mind… Ignoring the open window I staggered back across the glass before falling back into the leather chair and breaking into tears once more. TO BE CONTINUED…
