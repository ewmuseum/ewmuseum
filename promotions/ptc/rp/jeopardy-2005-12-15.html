Jeopardy

15 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-15 The studio was packed, with people of all shapes and sizes crammed into the padded blue seats. The official capacity of this place was 300, though at a glance (and with the help of some very good camera work), you could be fooled into thinking that the number was closer to 1000. As the host and the participants made their final preparations before the cameras rolled, Kyle, the head stage hand (most important gofer), was busily sorting cue cards. As was his habit, he had a quick glance at the questions, tested himself to determine the scope of his general knowledge. After a few shaky rounds, he picks his game up. Time for Final Jeopardy time…nice topic today. What the hell? That answer can’t be correct. Best not to ask though, the researchers get angry if you raise doubts over the validity of their questions. Better stop now, get these to the man before he gets nervous about their whereabouts and sweats that make-up off. ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* "We ready to go? Okay, rolling in five, four, three…" The last two numbers are mouthed silently, as is the custom. The familiar theme music plays over the studio speakers as the ‘Applause’ sign directs the audience. Our host enters, stage right, looking as perfect and made-up as ever. Centre stage now, where he should be. "Thank you folks, I’m your host Alex Trebek and we’re here for another game of…Jeopardy!" Cue more applause, this time not even prompted. These people are genuinely excited now that the man is standing in front of them, almost close enough to touch. "We’ve got three great contestants today so enough of me talking, let’s go meet them." Yet more applause. Seems like a fair bet that some people will have some very sore hands after this taping. As the man makes his way over to the chosen ones, out come the cue cards from the inner pocket of his perfectly fitted navy suit. Very suave. "Contestant number one, what’s your name and where are you from?" Contestant one, the soccer mom. Mid-thirties, slightly overweight and heading in the wrong direction. Pretty enough face, probably her husband found her attractive in that wholesome way. Dressed up for the occasion, a light brown pantsuit that is just a tad too small. Still, nice to see she made the effort. "My name is Joanna and I’m from Columbus, Ohio!" A small section of the crowd cheers loudly at this, obviously her ‘moral support’ crew. The rest clap politely, anxious to get on to their friend, the one who will surely win. "Columbus, lovely city, really lovely city." Whether he has ever been there is doubtful, though he says it with such honesty that you can’t help but be fooled. "Why don’t you tell us a little about yourself Joanna?" "I’m a housewife who has been married for 12 years to my husband John. I have three kids, Toby, Jessica and little Simone who are at home watching with their grandma. I’m a huge Jeopardy fan and I just decided to apply to be on the show and here I am." "It says here you’re ultimate dream is to go scuba-diving in the Great Barrier Reef…" "Yeah I think that would be fantastic because ever since I was a little girl I’ve always loved the sea and sea life so if I win today the money will be going towards a trip Down Under." "That sounds like a good plan Joanna, good luck!" As the man moves onto the next participant, his cue cards are shuffled and scanned quickly, giving him the basic facts he needs to appear like he actually cares about the histories and lives of these people. This guy is a pro. "Contestant number two, how about you, what’s your name?" The second participant is the stereotypical nerd, for want of a better description. His side-parted hair looks like it was styled by his mother…about 23 years ago, his sweater/vest/cardigan belongs on someone twice his age and his small frame isn’t helped by his slouched, hunched posture, one that speaks of nervousness and timidity. He is probably in his late twenties but has one of those faces that makes it hard to determine for sure. "I’m Kevin and I’m from San Francisco." "I used to live in San Francisco, down near Fisherman’s Wharf. You ever go to Pompeii’s Grotto? Great seafood. Anyway Kevin, what would you spend your winnings on if you manage to manage to emerge victorious today?" "Well Alex, when the original Star Wars movies came out I was too young to get the action figures so I’d spend my money on trying to complete my set of unopened Star Wars originals." "How many do you have in your collection at the minute?" "I have over 100 but I’m missing a few rare and expensive ones so that’s definitely how I’d spend my winnings." "Good luck Kevin and may the force be with you." This elicits a smattering of laughter in the crowd as the host moves onto the third and final contestant. That line was delivered so smoothly you wouldn’t even think it was rehearsed. The last in line is the old person. You know the type, sits in all day watching television, then complains that her family never come to visit her and take her out. A wealth of knowledge…when she can remember it. "And our final competitor on today’s show, tell us a bit about yourself." "My name’s Martha and I’m from Houston. And can I say Alex how handsome you look." Just what we need…a horny granny. For his part, the man pretends to be embarrassed, nice work. "Thank you Martha, I’m not sure my wife would always agree with you though. Now tell me if I’m wrong but I hear you’ve been watching Jeopardy since the very first episode, is that right?" "That’s right, I watch every day and I videotape it now too." "That’s dedication folks, let’s give Martha a round of applause." The audience agrees and claps spontaneously, no cue needed. "Now we’ve met the contestants, let’s play Jeopardy!" ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* "Here are today’s categories…" The wall of television screens changes from the Jeopardy logo to that of the question board, a flashy graphic accompanying the arrival of each topic on the board. Today’s selection is interesting and very varied. We have ‘Monty Python’, ‘The Larch’, ‘Bicycle Repairman’, ‘Michael Keaton Roles, ‘A Spanish Inquisition’ and ‘Run "It" Up’. Joanna has control of the board. "I’ll take ‘Monty Python’ for $200." "The first Monty Python movie was 1971’s ‘And Now For Something Completely…’" "What is different." "Good answer and you’re off the mark Joanna." As the show progresses we find out some interesting facts about random topics. For example, did you know that the brakes using backward pressure on tires are called coaster brakes? No, where have you been living recently?! Or that the Caribbean island Hispaniola literally means ‘Spanish Island’. Seriously, this stuff is great. As we get closer and closer to the ‘Final Jeopardy’ round, it’s down to a shootout between Kevin and Joanna. He is $800 behind but has control of the board with only one question to go. Things are a little bit tense… "Okay Kevin, this is it, you get this right and you’re through to the last round." "I’ll have ‘Run "It" Up’ for $1000 please Alex." "Alright, for a chance to take part in Final Jeopardy, here’s your question. After visiting Russia in 1919, Lincoln Steffens said of Communism ‘I’ve seen the future and it…’" Now this is a tough one. His instincts tell him to go one way but he is cautious, thinks it over for a while, too long almost. "I’ll have to hurry you Kevin." "What is works, Alex." "Did he say ‘I’ve seen the future and it works’ ? Yes! I’m sorry Joanna but congratulations Kevin, you’re going through to play Final Jeopardy! Let’s have a big hand for all today’s contestants." It’s exciting now. ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* "Here we go Kevin, are you ready?" "I think so Alex." "Well then let’s play…Final Jeopardy!" The screens change again, the lighting responding to change the mood to one of tension. Just as well they broke to fix make-up before this otherwise Kevin’s face would look like it was melting. "And today’s topic is…’Professional Wrestling’, how do you feel about that Kevin?" A slight smile has broken across his face at this. Makes you wonder if they fix these things, send the audience home happy. "It could be a lot worse Alex, I’ve been a wrestling fan for years, though only really got into it the past year or so." "So you think you’ve got a good chance?" "I guess there’s only one way to find out." "You read my mind, but first I have to ask, how much are you putting on the line here?" The $64000 question, or in this case the $8600 question. Will he go all out, looking for the big win, or will he play it safe? After all, you can get a heck of a lot of Star Wars figures for almost nine grand. "I’m going to put it all on the line Alex, let it ride as they say in Vegas." So greed got the best of him. And he doesn’t look like the Vegas type either. The audience love it though, it’s not their money so why shouldn’t they encourage him to risk it? "He’s going for it folks, he could lose it all but he’s going for it! Now we’ve got that out of the way, can we please have the question, for today’s…Final Jeopardy." Here we go, moment of truth. "What FUSE star became his company’s sole representative in this year’s GTT by defeating Buddy Kingfisher in round one?" A look of consternation is etched on Kevin’s face now. He should know this, he’s been watching GTT these last few weeks and FUSE is one of, if not his favourite promotion. Let’s think, was it Mikael Lundgard? No, he was against Hoyt Williams. Devil D Graves? Not him either, he was in a different bracket than the Kingfish. Now he knows who it is, by process of elimination it must be this man. "Who is Harlem." The crowd agrees but the screen doesn’t, the display changing to a dark red and the ‘loser’s music’ playing. "I’m sorry Kevin, the answer’s actually…Hold on, are we sure this is right? I’ve never even heard of this guy." "Cut! Jesus, Alex, we’re going to have to do that whole piece again now." "I understand that but this answer is wrong, I mean who the hell is Evan Jackson? Who checked this answer?" "I think Mike did, it must be right." "Well it sure doesn’t sound like it’s right, can we get a different question for this next take?" "No problem Alex." This isn’t surprising really, not many people outside of FUSE know of this Evan Jackson, and even those that do are unaware of his accomplishments and abilities. His victory over Kingfisher shocked many, yet it still didn’t encourage the ignorant to enlighten themselves. Instead, they sat back comfortable in the knowledge that Dallas Reeves would eliminate him and progress to the final 32. At least, that was the hope… ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*
