Welcome to Camp Cocytus: The Price of Pride

14 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-03-14 An old Cherokee was teaching his grandchildren about life. He said to them, "A battle is raging inside me … it is a terrible fight between two wolves. One wolf represents fear, anger, envy, sorrow, regret, greed, arrogance, self-pity, guilt, resentment, inferiority, lies, false pride, superiority and ego. The other stands for joy, peace, love, hope, sharing, serenity, humility, kindness, benevolence, friendship, empathy, generosity, truth, compassion and faith." The old man fixed the children with a firm stare. "This same fight is going on inside you, and inside every other person, too." They thought about it for a minute until one child asked his grandfather, "Which wolf will win?" The old Cherokee replied: "The one you feed." -An Indian moral ~<>~<>~<>~ Before… The snap-click of the small recorder’s spinning tape wheels made Rollins nervous as the PTC attorney pressed the button and leaned back in his leather chair, pulling a stack of paperwork out of a briefcase so disorganized that it was a wonder he could locate his own pencil, let alone the proper legal documents. Absently pushing a pair of sleek eyeglasses up the bridge of his nose, the shrewd little shyster slid the papers across the smooth conference table with a pen. He looked at Rich Rollins expectantly. The Renegade merely frowned, shooting the hawk-faced blood sucker a dark scowl. Rollins reached up to adjust his bandanna out of habit, but his hair spilled naked and unchecked across his shoulders. He wasn’t used to wearing Brioni business suits and button-up silk shirts, but since he had unwittingly become the baby-killing monster of the new millennium, his fashion sense wasn’t exactly the worst casualty he had sustained. Those papers were, though; they wanted to take his pride. "I trust you’ll be reasonable with us, Mr. Rollins." The attorney cracked a fake smile, the kind that oozed a sort of self-confidence that tried to hide his true disposition which was anything but. "My clients only want what is best for the business. It’s nothing personal." "Nothing personal my ass," Rollins mumbled as he leafed through the thick document, trying to read as much of the fine print as he could. The words on the first page loomed beneath him like a bottomless gorge carved from the language of legal obscurity, its inky message so innocent and straight forward, yet it cut Rich Rollins straight to his heart all the same: I, Richard Andrew Rollins, do hereby relinquish the Primetime Central Global Championship of my own arbitration and free will. The trademark(s) inherent to the title (i.e. licensing rights, images, video footage et al) are henceforth the sole property of Primetime Central and its stockholders. I agree to forego any royalties, including but not limited to title defense bonuses, marketing revenue, severance pay and/or any other sort of monetary gain associated with the Global Championship and its continued use throughout Primetime Central. It is my desire to abandon the title willingly so that another champion may be crowned in my place. I also understand that any and all occurrences of my name and/or ring moniker (read: "The Renegade") will be expunged from the PTC title histories, including but not limited to the Global title history, tournament records and Infinite Gauntlet records. It is my desire to insure that Primetime Central not be held accountable for my actions and their image not be tarnished by promoting my likeness, in whole or in part, in any way, shape or form. The sanctity of PTC and its affiliates will therefore be upheld by respecting my wishes through removing… It just goes on and on and on. Signed, _______________ Richard Andrew Rollins The long of the short of it was painfully obvious; they wanted to make him disappear, pretend that his existence was a fluke and shove it under the communal rug just so they could save face with an angry public. A multitude of evil thoughts swam through Rollins’ skull as he flipped page after damning page, absorbing the legal jargon that spelled out the death of his career as clearly as the trial signaled the end of his freedom. He had no defense attorney to act on his behalf. A dozen phone calls to as many respected lawyers had yielded nothing more than a slew of half-hearted excuses and apologies, but the Renegade knew not a one of them were truly sorry. Why should they be? He was about to become a convicted cop killer and worse. No respectable lawyer would touch him with a cattle prod the length of a football field for fear of being seen as a sympathizer to a man who incinerated a pregnant woman. He had nobody to rely on but himself, and here PTC came knocking in his darkest hour, offering him a deal. If you considered signing away your career just so your benefactors didn’t take a bad beat a "deal." "What if I’m proven innocent?" Rollins looked at the attorney, determined not to lose his temper. He needed to keep his head, especially now. "What happens if all of this blows over and my name gets cleared?" The attorney bit his lip. "Rich, we’ve seen the case file. Johnny Cochran himself couldn’t get you so much as a reduced sentence, let alone an exoneration. It’s hard to accept, but you have to face the facts; PTC will not stand to allow your fall to bring their business down with them. You have to see that, Mr. Rollins. You understand my client’s position, yes?" Rollins brushed the waiting pen with his fingers, but he didn’t pick it up. "I know what you do," he said bitterly, staring down at the documents without really seeing them. "It’s your job to sit there and poke holes in my testimony, to make me look like a complete waste of human air and smoke a cigar in celebration after your system eats me alive. You can poke at me all you want, but you know what surrounds those holes, counselor?" He looked up at the attorney then, his eyes quivering with conviction. "The truth." Rich Rollins was a powerful man. Over the course of his career he had vanquished virtually every foe who had crossed his path, crippling competitors for as trivial a gesture as looking at him the wrong way, as if he weren’t worthy of their respect. It was the same look that the pompous courtroom politician gave him now, and it made his blood curdle. He had beaten the best this business had to offer, but all it took was a supercilious legist with a pen as his sword and a conference table as his arena to catch the mighty Renegade’s balls in a vice in the one battle he could not win. And the lawyer knew it. "Your signature on the dotted line is all we want, Mr. Rollins. Please, don’t do this the hard way." Rollins snorted. The hard way. But he grabbed the pen and pressed it to the paper anyway. ~<>~<>~<>~ After… Seven hours wasn’t an incredibly long stretch of time by Rich Rollins’ standards. He had crisscrossed the globe on countless trans-continental flights, putting on shows for half a dozen promotions on seven different continents. He had endured a fourteen-hour baby-bawling marathon from Los Angeles to Tokyo and numerous train treks across the European countryside. He’d even made a cross-country race against the clock from Miami to Seattle in a rented vehicle just so he could show up to a GCW house show he wasn’t even booked for. He had seen and been exposed to more frustrations and developed more nerve-grating pet peeves than any postal worker on the planet. Now they were nothing more than trivial footnotes of the story of the life he used to lead. When you were chained to the seat directly ahead of you, looking out the window of a prison bus full of more detox-fueled degenerates than an episode of The Osbornes, seven hours was an exercise in cruel and unusual punishment. When the heroine junkie behind him threw up all over the back of Rollins’ seat, he nearly lost his mind as the smell of backwashed milk and stomach acid engulfed the entire bus. He had nowhere to go. Nowhere except Camp Cocytus. ~<>~<>~<>~ Before… "All rise for the honorable Judge Hartford." The traditional drapes of the Law graced the man who held The Renegade’s future in the palm of his hand. The courtroom rose in unison as the Judge entered, turnip-faced and brooding, hardly acknowledging the television cameras and the millions of viewers who hid inside of them. The media circus had all three rings running full-tilt, leaving Rollins stark and alone at his table trying to ignore the haughty glares from the twelve jurors who already saw the word "murderer" tattooed on his forehead. Judge Hartford politely asked them to sit; so began the case of the State of Illinois versus Richard Rollins. The prosecution was a hungry team of legal sharks just waiting to tear Rollins to shreds. Three attorneys sat ready to prove beyond any reasonable doubt that Rich Rollins killed three people and an unborn child, which was three attorneys more than Rollins had to his own credit. It was almost laughable as he sat alone, facing the impossibly informed trio all by himself. He looked on in listless denial as the lead prosecutor gave his fire-and-brimstone opening statements, condemning him with words that bit like verbal stones trying to shame him into admittance. It was time for Rollins to defend himself and cut the promo of a lifetime. It was the promo that needed to save his life. "Sorry I’m late your honor," Steven Caldera cried as he burst into the courtroom with briefcase in tow. "I had to crawl over four lanes of traffic just to find a taxi." Rollins watched Caldera push his way through the observation barrier, his mouth agape and eyes wide with shock. Caldera simply straightened his tie as he took the empty seat next to him, radiating the type of confidence that could only come from one who knew what he was doing and why he was doing it. "What the hell are you doing here?" Rollins mumbled. "You don’t need to drag yourself down with me, Steven. Go home, I can take care of myself." Caldera’s face warmed with mock indignation as he pulled a few files from his briefcase, refusing to meet Rollins’ steady stare. "And watch my most bankable byproduct bumble his way onto death row? I didn’t go to Harvard law school to let my company take a hit without trying to deflect it, Rich. You should know better." And with that he stood up, addressing the jury after a curt nod from Judge Hartford. "Ladies and gentlemen," he began in a smooth baritone that demanded nothing but their complete attention, "the man sitting before you today is an international icon, a man who didn’t attain his celebrity status by sucking up to people like you or me, and believe me when I say that I know this fact first hand. What people fail to realize - especially in a business such as ours - is that the man you see on-camera isn’t the same man you see sitting next to me. Within the confines of his profession you all know Richard Rollins as The Renegade, as a heartless and angry man who is capable of deceiving millions of people around the world with his rugged and dangerous attitude. He deceives us into liking him, no matter how debauched and depraved his methods may be. "But that man is merely The Renegade, ladies and gentlemen." Now directly in front of the jury Caldera hit a dramatic flourish, grabbing the jury box and forcing his audience to look at Rollins with a broad wave of his hand. "This is a real man sitting in front of you today. The callous renegade who you see on television is nothing more than a character I have employed and used as a cash cow for a company that I own, nothing more. I ask that you divorce the dueling personas and disregard any prejudice that you many have against my client and listen to the truth; The Renegade is capable of just about anything, but murder is just not in his character." The lines were drawn. Rollins would never have admitted it out loud, but he knew that without the help Caldera had just given him this trial would have never been a trial at all. It would have been a public execution. ~<>~<>~<>~ After… Things were more complicated in a courtroom. When you were looking at the faces of twelve people who had hate in their eyes every time they dared to catch that wayward surreptitious glance in your direction, that glare that told you they were already watching you ride the lightning a hundred times over in the privacy of their own mind, it was hard to think about anything else. Rollins had always been used to performing for people who hated him; as one of the business’ biggest bad boys he had learned to harness the negative attention and use it as an asset. Performing in front of a grand jury, however, had proved to be an entirely different ordeal. Things were not nearly as complicated when you stood naked in a barracks shower, clutching the wall for survival as a high-pressure hose rinsed your body without restraint or mercy. Rollins tried to zone out the smart ass comments from the guards as they sprayed him into a corner, cleansing his bare flesh until it burned from the relentless water pressure. "Sorry shooter, we’re fresh out of aspirin," they jeered. "But it must be your lucky day, ‘cause we‘ve got steroids out the ass!" "Who’s king shit champion now, Renegade?" "Christ, you’re more shriveled up than my grandmother. I bet she’d love to lay you down for the three-count anyway." Those weren’t the comments that got to him. It was one guard in particular - the husky fuck who held the hose - who kept going for Rollins’ jugular: "Tell me Rollins, did you torch that bitch because she wouldn’t abort your bastard kid or did you just not feel like writing that child support check each month?" His name was Barks, Officer Luke Barks. It was the kind of name that conjured up images of slobbering pit bulls and rabid boars with one broken tusk. The mental association wasn’t far off from the man’s actual demeanor, which would have made Rollins laugh had it not been for the five hundred pounds of pressurized prison water that was being blown up his ass crack. The more Rollins squirmed, the more ruthless Barks became. "Welcome to Camp Cocytus, you miserable fucking baby butcher." Oh how he hated the man. ~<>~<>~<>~ Before… Writing with the blind fervor of a man trying to chew his own arm out of a bear trap, Rich Rollins nearly tore the legal document in half with his pen as he scribbled two of his favorite words on the PTC attorney’s release form: Signed, __FUCK YOU__ Richard Andrew Rollins The attorney’s cocksure grin turned sour as soon as The Renegade slid the paper back at him. "You’re making a huge mistake, Richard. PTC has more friends in powerful places than even you, and they will do whatever is necessary in order to change your mind." Spreading his hands across the table, Rich Rollins leaned over his side of the ridiculously long conference desk, his lips curled into a feral expression straight out of a Discovery Channel exclusive on the Bengal tiger. "The day I drop my Global title is the day they drop my smoldering carcass in a hole," he promised bitterly, "and you can tell your clients that I don’t give two shits about their PR problems; I’ve got enough of my own." The attorney’s face was still frozen in wordless shock as Rich Rollins slammed the conference room door. ~<>~<>~<>~ The Verdict… "Will the defendant please rise." Thirty minutes. "Has the jury reached its verdict?" It only took them thirty minutes to come to a unanimous decision. "Yes we have, your honor." In three days the prosecution had exhausted its reserves having called only one witness: the caretaker to Resthaven Cemetery, a man who hadn’t even been on duty at the time of the incident. His bank account had gotten considerably healthier at the behest of PTC stockholders, of that Rollins had no doubt. A plainclothes juror with blonde hair and thin lips read their verdict, her hand shaking ever so slightly as she scrolled down the paper. "In the case of the State of Illinois versus Richard Rollins, we the jury find the defendant, Richard Rollins, guilty of the first-degree murder of Frank Jacobs…" A considerable - but not considerably surprised - gasp filled the courtroom. "…guilty of the first-degree murder of Officer Noel Newton…" Steven Caldera’s face stretched so tight Rollins thought he was about to bust a seam along the bridge of his nose. "…guilty of the first-degree murder of Cynthia O’Doyle…" The room began to swim. "…and guilty of the first-degree murder of Mrs. O’Doyle’s unborn child." Judge Hartford thanked the juror for her statement as he held the observation chatter down with a smack of his gavel. The prosecution were busy congratulating one another, tossing out self-congratulatory verbal blowjobs like they had just won the Nobel Peace Prize as Judge Hartford turned his attention to Rollins and Caldera. "Richard Rollins, you have been found guilty by a jury of your peers on four accounts of first-degree murder." The judge’s tone was steel and stone. "Your sentencing will be scheduled for the fifth of April, at which time I will decide the terms of your incarceration." Caldera spoke up, trying to keep a professional strength in his voice. "Your honor, if it pleases the court my client has requested that if found guilty his sentencing would be immediate. He wishes to waive his right to an appeal for a lighter sentence." The room grew quiet as the prosecutors turned to regard Steven Caldera as Rollins himself tried to keep from falling back into the observation stands. Judge Hartford considered the request for less than ten seconds before coming back with his answer. "Mister Caldera, your client has been found guilty of crimes so horrendous that it’s a mercy he isn’t hung from the rafters in this very room. While it is within his right to waive his right to an appeal, I will not grant any such favors upon such atrocious and Godless behavior. If he wishes to be sentenced now, let him say so." The Renegade’s tongue had turned to sand, but he didn’t want to drag this out any longer than it had to. "Yes your honor, I would." "So be it." Judge Hartford took a deep breath as he addressed his court. "I hereby sentence Richard Rollins to the confines of state penitentiary Camp Cocytus, under maximum security detail, until his arranged execution by means of electrocution in accordance with Illinois state law." Caldera placed a tender hand on Rollins’ shoulder as the judge looked straight into his eyes, speaking words that Rollins never thought he’d have to hear: "May God have mercy on your soul." The gavel slammed once. Its hollow echo was matched only by the sudden void that filled Rich Rollins’ heart. To be continued…
