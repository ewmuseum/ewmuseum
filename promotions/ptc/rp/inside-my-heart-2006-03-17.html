Inside My Heart

17 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-03-17 Shanty Jones sat in the plush surroundings of his hotel bedroom, feeling alone despite the presence of two other men. Chris and Paul Goodman, better known as Doctor Curiosity and Eegor respectively, were silent in their contemplation of the GTT5 competitor, and his dilemmas. The Ripper had left the room silently after tossing the vial lightly to Shan. He’d left the finalist with all of the information that he needed, and only time would solidify the choices into a decision. Shanty sat on the floor, his aching muscles complaining manfully to his brain that they had been mistreated, and could they please be given a break sometime soon. Shanty’s mind was as ever absent from his body, ignoring the please of the nerve endings and the soreness that would pervade his being once he realised that he was actually hurting. "So, vhat vill," started the doctor, before Paul put an arm across him to indicate for him to stop. "He’ll need to be alone," came the whisper. Curiosity nodded, and together the two brothers edges their way to the door, opening it and leaving the room in silence. "And then there was one," said Shanty, under his breath, proving that he had at least some sort of grasp on what had been transpiring in the room. He looked up and around, but everything had started to seem so oppressive. His life had been turned so many ways over the past few weeks that he could no longer clearly distinguish reality from fantasy, fact from fiction. Idly his hand reached out towards his head, searching for the point at which the needle had buried itself twice into his brain. If Jack had been telling the truth – and he had been, thought Shanty, some instinctive feeling or knowledge about what had made the other man do what he did – then there was a miniscule computer lodged there somewhere, filtering electrons and readying itself to attack others without even telling them where the source of their oppression had come from. It was seamless; there was nothing there to indicate anything had ever happened to him. Short of the scars that decorated, or perhaps ruined his face, he looked as normal as the next man. If what The Ripper had said was true – and again, he knew deep down that it had to be – then all of his health was a lie, a sham of the highest order. He’d been built from organs grown in the factories, their dreadful, terrible purposes becoming clearer by the minute. Soldiers on the battlefield could be saved and patched up, allowed to live further by the replacement organs grown for them, but that would never have been the main idea of the project. No, it would be something far grander, something far more meaningful in some way. It was linked to something that filled The Ripper, a normally fearless man, with apprehension and hatred. Replacement organs for celebrities, or limbs in case of accidents? Skin samples kept breathing to make them beautiful again without the need for grafts or regular face lifts? They didn’t seem like the kinds of project that jack would imbue with so much of his own energy, with so much of his own vitality. Shanty sighed. He couldn’t tell, but he had a feeling that at some point he would find out, and that point was fast approaching. The Ripper was not done with him yet. *** Frank looked around and realised that he was in a room, pouring himself a glass of water from an Evian bottle. He had no idea how he had come to be in the room, nor whose bottle, or indeed glass it was. With a measure pace, he laid the glass down on the sideboard and began to look around. Memories assailed him from all sides, but the implants were beginning to work on a smoother basis, and the miniature flashes of the past were getting more and more clouded by the minute. I feel at home here, thought Frank. He didn’t recognise the place immediately, but something told him that this was his home, the place where he should live. Opening a white panel door and walking through, Frank came upon a living room, complete with slightly worn sofa and television. Feeling tired, he decided to put his feet up and lie back for a few moments as he got comfortable, but soon the glow of the TV set cast a warm spell on him, and his eyes slowly lolled shut. Within seconds he was sleeping like a baby. *** Paul and Chris Goodman walked down the corridor within Shanty’s hotel, leading themselves towards the elevator. Upon reaching the doors, Paul hit the button that would allow them to descend, and they waited patiently. "So, do you tzink ve should see him again before ze match?" "There won’t be a match," insisted Paul. "You saw what Rollins got nailed for. There’s no way he can get out of it." "All of ze things you have seen in the past few years, and you don’t even believe that fate could lend a hand to proceedings? Paul, you have grown cynical." "Okay, so what if it happens?" asked the doctor’s brother. "Do you think that Shan will go through with The Ripper’s suggestion?" "Do most people?" "Every single one of them." The brothers lapsed into silence for a few seconds, interrupted only by the high pitched chime of the elevator reaching their floor. Shuffling inside, Curiosity hit the button that would take them down the reception and lobby, and then took a step back. "Then Shanty vill do ze same." "He’s different though," said Paul, his voice betraying an uncertainty as to what the future would hold. "Zen you vill just have to wait," replied the doctor. "Do you tzink you vill be able to make it to view ze match?" "It’s too dangerous for me," was the reply. "Though, I might just give myself up." "Vhat?" "I can’t deal with this, Chris. It’s all too much for me to see everyone that I care about get influenced negatively by me being around them. Don’t say that it’s not true because it is. I’ve seen too many people hurt by the actions that I’ve taken, not least of all your wife, Collette, and I think I’d just be better off repaying my debt as opposed to adding to it." "I vill miss you, again," said the doctor. "I know, my friend. I know." A few more moments of silence filled the otherwise empty elevator, before Paul broke it. "You know what? It seems odd to me that every time there’s a conspiracy or a murder, there always seems to be one of us involved in it." "One of us?" "No, not the pair of us, but our industry. We’re bad for everyone, you know? The two finalists of the biggest tournament in the wrestling calendar, and both of the competitors have willingly taken lives at one point or another." "Jah," said the doctor, his voice soft under the glare of reminiscence. "But our kind have done some good too." "Yeah, of course they have," agreed Paul. "I just think it’s funny that every time the world is in danger, it’s professional wrestlers that have to sort everything out." "Jah! Of course… vhat, did you tzink zat ve should leave zis kind of responsibility to amateur wrestlers? Eegor, vhat have you been tzinking?" DING! The door of the lift toned to let everyone know that the elevator was no longer moving, and had reached their destination. The doors slowly opened, and standing in front of them were a series of uniformed police officers, several holding weapons that pointed at the siblings. "Get down on the floor, and put your hands behind your head!" shouted the lead officer. "I vas just trying to get in contact vith you to say zat my brother vas…" "Get down on the floor, right now!" yelled the cop, gesturing with his gun. Doctor Curiosity sighed with resignation. It looked as if his brother might not be the only family member to watch the biggest wrestling match of the year from behind bars. *** Time passed, as it often does in these situations. Shanty Jones looked at the vial every day; spent most of his waking hours staring at the precious liquid inside. This mix of blood and other tissue samples had been treated specially, and would allow him to perform tasks beyond the boundaries of a normal man, but was that honest? Was that a true way to perform? Shan shook his head. This was not a dilemma about fair play. This stretched further and dug deeper in towards what made Shanty the man he was, and the man he had used to be. It was all about judgement. After spending a lifetime of abdicating responsibility, of fence-sitting and being tolerant, his will had snapped in Iraq, and the aftermath had made him the vessel to carry out the will of a high power, one who could choose between Heaven and Hell for the poor souls that approached him. The final one had been his closest friend in the army, Steven Atkins, and it had been with the heaviest of hearts that Shanty had let his soul chose its own path, and go down the slippery slope into Hades. Now it had come full circle, and he was left with an option that he had eschewed so many times. He had only judges people when he had deemed it completely necessary, and then not really of his own volition. Could it be that he was committing a sin to become addicted to such power? The ability to choose whether a man succeeds or fails; lives or dies has been a long and storied one through the pantheons of human history. Would he be making the decision for the good of mankind, or for his own personal gain? Shanty Jones turned the miniature glass cylinder over in his hands for a third straight night, and continued to worry about his own future, and how much of a choice he would have in shaping how it turned out. He could smash it. He could grind his heel into the receptacle and destroy it on the pale marble of his balcony, but Shanty wasn’t sure that he wanted to do so… at least not quite yet. He considered one more night of sleep, but with the tournament final growing ever closer, it was proving more and more difficult to catch the hours necessary for peak performance. At least, thought Shanty, looking at the television across the room. At least the match is back on. He lay back and closed his eyes, willing unconsciousness to take him, but he was interrupted by a whisper that was carried to him on a non-existent breeze. As if from nowhere, he saw The Ripper sat at the end of his bed, on a medium sized wooden stool. "You would like to know what has scared The Ripper, would you not?" "It had crossed my mind," replied Shanty. "The answer is very simple. It is death that scares The Ripper." "You seemed pretty effective at dishing it out, the last time I looked." "Well, that has very little to do with fear," said Jack. "It has everything to do with control… but little to do with fear." "So, why?" "A good question, and not one that many people would understand. It may take a while for you to get your head around it… but you are one of only two people who would really understand what The Ripper means, and the other is really in no state to give an answer." Shanty sat up, still fully dressed, but inclined his head for Jack to continue as he slung his legs over the side of the bed. "You are a religious man are you not, Shanty?" A brief laugh escaped Shan’s lips as he nodded assent. To not believe in God after what he had been through, after the actions he had taken in His name… that would have beena little bit too much. "If you know of Heaven, and are a messenger from there, then you will know of the other side. You would know of Hell." Shanty nodded again, keeping his counsel to himself, but secretly thinking of everything he had ever heard about Hell. He was surprised by how detailed the knowledge got, but then realised that the majority of it was from Sunday School, or from the illustrations designed many years ago to keep the populace under control. "Several years ago, The Ripper was involved in an… in an incident," said Jack, referring to the incident where he had torn down an entire stage and lighting rig on top of himself, in a quest to end the life of a fellow wrestler who had become his nemesis. "His corporeal form was ruined, was mangled beyond belief, and the journey to the hereafter was less than pleasing. When arriving, everything changed. Where The Ripper had held power there was none. Where he had been able to intimidate people, all was gone." Jack got to his feet and walked away, seeking solace in the corner of the room. "It was cold. They always portray Hell as a fiery place full of brimstone and sulphur, but for The Ripper it was quite the opposite. Hell was seeing everything that had been constructed, the friendships that had formed over the course of several years just go up in smoke. It was seeing everything die, and the name washed from history, eroded away in memories that would fade and in time would die. That was just a part for what Hell was like. In truth it was unending agony, and somewhere The Ripper would rather not have seen." Shanty rubbed the patchwork stubble that rested on the clear parts of his chin. It was odd, very strange to see a man such as The Ripper act in a way other than that of domineering command. It felt almost like a confessional. "Yet these works that The Ripper could see, they were just out of reach, tantalisingly so, until one day a vision came, one that tested patience to the limit. The Ripper’s one true nemesis was so close to achieving all of his goals, to becoming completely happy. So it had to be that The Ripper attempted to leave Hell, and was fooled into believing it was possible on his own terms… but it was not. Lucifer can always see you there, and knows everything that you could ever dream of planning." "Yet here you are," said Shanty. "Indeed. He was intrigued by the story, though he must have known it before. In the end The Ripper was allowed to cross back over into the realms of the living, on the strict understanding of a couple of very binding contracts." Shan nodded once more, hoping to build some rapport with the puppet master, better to wrest control back from him. Jack looked across, and his face was wrought with candour. "The Ripper was… The Ripper…" He blinked his eyes, and for a moment he only seemed human. "You must think me a monster, Shanty." "Given your actions, I never mistook you for a saint." "This is true, and I have been responsible for many things of which I am not proud. So many people have suffered so that a few, a select few may prosper and live on. I have done my best for them, but the contract, it stops me from doing all that I could." Jack took a deep breath before continuing. "Never make a deal with the Devil, Shanty, because it’s hard to get out of it in the end. I’d love to be able to turn around and repent, but it’s been stolen from me. I act the way I do towards living beings because I am bound by laws greater than those of nature. I cannot help the living in any way. But I can help you." "I died…" said Shan, recalling the results of the bomb blast. "Exactly. Those who have died are exempt under the terms of the deal. It makes such a horrific subset to call upon, the dead. I’ve done… I’ve done things that seemed so wrong at the time, but I was working within my limits. I killed a man, killed his soul and his body, just so I could try to help him." "And did you?" "I did. I helped him in the end," said Jack, and Shanty believed him. *** Frank, the wrestler formerly known as Cereal Killer, lay in his bed, eyes drifting downwards on occasion, only to flick back open for a scant second and then continue their cyclical motion. He was trying to stay awake to keep the dreams at bay; the nightmares where he was strapped to a table in a laboratory, where men pumped him full of drugs and slowly but surely took away his life. If he listened hard he could swear that he could hear the faint sound of a cardiac monitor flatlining. Yet all was not bad, and as the transmitters in his brain responded to the electrical impulses, the memories faded to be replaced with new ones. He had a job to start the next day, just working in a factory, but it was good honest work and a place to meet new people. With the dreams banished, this time for the rest of his life, Frank smiled and closed his eyelids shut, not screwed up as usual, but laying on top of his eyes in a beatific pose. This life, this brave new world of opportunities. It made him happy. It made him happier than he had ever been. He’d found his own rainbow, his own happy ending. *** "So you want to avoid going to Hell again?" asked Shanty. "No," relied The Ripper. "It’s impossible to avoid it. Upon my death, no matter how many little acts of mercy I have performed, they are drowned in the seas of bloodshed that I have caused. No, Shanty, I am doomed for eternity… unless…" "Unless?" echoed Shan. "The true purpose of the factories." Shanty was silent, in anticipation of the truth. "They were created for one thing only, Shanty." The Ripper rose to his feet, drawing himself up to his full height. "Immortality." Shanty opened his mouth as if to talk, but instead just stood there, breathing deeply. "Life expectancy has grown by seven years in the last ten alone, almost keeping pace with itself… but that could never be enough. Human beings will never naturally become eternal beings, who can live on in the flesh or a facsimile thereof forever. What I can do, though, is get extremely close to it. I have all of the organs I could ever need, and the tissue cultures ready to grow more of them and make them more efficient. I have every bit of the body that could be put together and more. I can make a new human being, but what inhabits the brain, well… there’s the tricky part. It can’t be replaced, and will always degrade, unless…" This time Shanty picked up on it. "Unless you had a way of controlling the very atoms, or a way on converting old atoms to new." The Ripper smiled, and his honesty and candour slipped away like a thief in the night. "We’re so nearly there that it’s almost painful." "Then do your thing. What do you need me for?" asked Shanty. "You’re the only one left." "The what?" "The only one left, Shanty. The factories had to be closed down, it was getting too dangerous to keep them open any longer. Of all the people who received treatment in them, you are the furthest along the line. The mental control, the use of entanglement, we’ve never been able to see if it still works in those with cloned bodies. Can the mind interact with the body it was not born with, even when under great stress and pressure?" "So I’m your guinea pig?" "If you do this then The Ripper will leave you alone for the rest of your life. The factories will stay closed and the world will be a quieter, safer place." "And if I don’t?" "Then the human cost be damned; the factories will open again. It is not an ideal solution, but it is one that could be deemed acceptable. Either way, The Ripper will not return to Hell. It is something you can never truly feel in your position, Shanty, but believe The Ripper when he says that he will stop at nothing, that he would torment you for the rest of your days if you do not agree to this deal." Shanty smiled as he exhaled. Despite all his precautions and beliefs, he’d been played expertly. He had no real choice. To refuse The Ripper would be to invite more misery on the planet, but to accept the offer would save the suffering of others. As the closed caption commentary on the television screen rolled past, he even thought of an added bonus. He would get to sit in judgement again, to pass a sentence of misery on a man who appeared to have a knack for getting away with murder. Shanty looked around to see The Ripper standing with a mechanical contraption that looked like something from a horror movie. It was like the hollow shell of a box, made from darkened iron, but in the middle of it, pointing downwards, was a massive needle. With nimble fingers, Shanty manoeuvred the vial into place, slipping it effortlessly under the device that would deliver its contents. He looked from the needle to the television, and saw the improbable press conference; his opponent, a murderer, relatively unscathed and walking free from jail with promises of delivering tournaments and lawsuits. "You’d better hope you win that case against the prison," muttered Shanty. "Because it’s the only chance you’ve got for a victory any time soon." The slight hiss signalled the readiness of the apparatus, and for the final time, at the hands of The Ripper, the needle slid deep into Shanty Jones’s brain, bringing with it the vial of DNA and the doom of ‘The Renegade’ Rich Rollins. THE END __________________________________________________ Author's note - the titles of the three roleplays in this round form the second half a couplet begun in the semi-finals, namely "I am your shadow in the dark / I have your blood inside my heart" from the song "Me and the Bean" by Spoon
