Delta Upsilon Iota/The Metallo Zords/Lag Sverige

25 June 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The camera cuts to a wide view shot of a picturesque two-story house, nestled beneath a beautiful arbor canopy of oak and pine that serves to shade it from the harsh midday sun. On the driveway that wraps around the left side of the house sit two utility vehicles—a black Jeep Wrangler and a garnet Ford Expedition. The automobiles are being fervently washed by a group of young men, buzzing here and there with great attention to every detail. The house itself is brick, and the white trim work stands in stark contrast to the deep red of the structure itself. The lawn is very well kept, with nary a single blade of grass overlapping into the driveway or street. Moving in closer to the house, we see two figures sitting on what appear to be black leather recliners on the right side of the front porch. With a white railing bordering its perimeter and partially obstructed our view, the porch spans the entire width of the house, breaking only to allow access to the two front brick steps. On the aforementioned black leather recliners sit two sizeable individuals, though the man on the left is significantly larger than the other. Between the two rests a keg, with the Greek letters Delta Upsilon Iota emblazoned on the front in black lettering and a polished chrome tap with the image of a skull and crossbones emblazoned on both sides. The much bigger man on the left has shaggy dark brown hair that falls down across his forehead almost to his eyebrows and is currently refilling his black 64 oz. black Carolina Panthers cup with fresh beer from the keg. Although his exact measurements are difficult to approximate with his being seated, he appears to be somewhat constrained by the oversized recliner. The man has the Greek letters Delta Upsilon Iota tattooed on his huge right biceps and the Stars and Bars of the Confederate flag on his right. His white wifebeater is taut across his massive frame, while his khaki cuffed shorts reveal massive calves just above his brown boat shoes. As he attempts to top off his beer, the last few drops suddenly trickle out of the tap. “Well god dang it. Ya got to be kidding me. I told those boys to fill this thing up right.” Taking another swig from his cup, the man suddenly bolts upright out of his recliner. Standing approximately six feet, nine inches tall and weighing in at well over three-hundred pounds, the man’s movement creates enough noise to garner several glances from the pledges even before his voice booms down to the wide-eyed boys in the driveway. “Lewis, I thought I told you boys to make sure the keg was filled up this mornin’.” Dropping the hose, the boy sprints over to the porch. “Yes sir, Brother Cobb. I went with Novice Clark at ten o’clock and we were back by quarter after, sir.” After a slight hesitation, Cobb continues. “So you’re telling me it’s gone already? Well damn, nice work Korver.” The boy scampers back over to his counterparts as the car washing nervously begins again and Cobb slowly settles back into his chair. The other man, while not as massive as the first, remains a very intimidating individual in his own right. Revealing his exceptionally defined arms, he sports a sleeveless plain black T-shirt the reads “The Man” with an arrow pointing up and “The Legend” with an arrow pointing down, along with baggy white Nike athletic shorts and black sandals on his feet. With spiked light brown hair, Korver has a silver ringlet earring in each ear and a white shell choker around his neck. Throwing his now empty 64-oz. Garnet FSU cup to the ground, Korver slumps back in his chair and angrily gestures at Cobb with his right hand. “Nice work? Nice work? We don’t have anymore beer. There is nothing nice about no beer. Nothing.” Korver closes his eyes and throws his head back in mock despair. “Why? Oh, why must the beer gods punish us for our sins? Have we not pleased them? Do we not offer up virgin sacrifices for their glory? Do our pledges not sweat and bleed for their entertainment? What more can we do, for we are only two in number?” “So you’re really liking that acting class this semester, huh?” Korver opens his eyes and slowly pulls his head back down. “Oh yeah, it’s great. Half the girls swim team just happened to sign up as well, and I don’t mind if I do.” “Korver, you do realize that you only have another semester before…” “Fuck off Cobb, of course I realize that. Why do you think I went to the library last Thursday with Summer?” “You got caught boning in the film room.” “But we were studying up until then. Cobb, man, you gotta do what you gotta do.” “Whatever man, I’m just trying to look out for ya.” “Yeah I know, it’s cool. Just don’t let it happen again. So you heard from your lawyer yet about…” “Hey. You ain’t gonna talk about your problems, we sure as hell ain’t gonna discuss mine. Specially not with them boys down there in the driveway.” “Fine, let’s just deal with the problem at hand.” “And what problem is that exactly?” Korver now jumps out of his seat, screaming down at the terrified pledges. “Beer run you little schmucks. If there isn’t a fresh keg right here in twenty minutes, I’ll have all your asses.”
