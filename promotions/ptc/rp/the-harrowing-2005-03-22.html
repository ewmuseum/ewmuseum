The Harrowing

22 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-03-22 Blinking is all he can muster now. He sits, eyes transfixed on his reflection sitting before him, smirking. The pure whiteness of the room glows around him, hurting his eyes, yet he cannot close them or look away from the daemon sitting in front of him. Dhar, on the edge of its seat, leans forward to glare at Deszaras more intently. The leering, warped Kabuki mask mocking his very existence, Dez feels sick to his stomach at the sight of such a creature. The daemon’s laugh trickles in like sweat off a brow, invidious and sickening. Yet he still cannot close his eyes, nor can he look away. The laughter dries up, halting like a cough’s end, and Dhar speaks. ‘So, Deszaras… what have we learned on our little journey? What have we learned of your desire? What have we learned about consequence and finality?’ the voice scratches against reality’s fabric. Then, as if Deszaras were flicking through channels on some demented television, Dhar’s body quivers and deforms as if made of static and his appearance changes. On and off his mask changes to one of sadness, rather than pretentiousness, the mask frowning and sullen, crying. Blood seeping through every aperture in the mask, hands appear and disappear holding its head, rubbing the tears away or clasping its ears, eyes or mouth. But as the two images flicker in and out of existence, he realises that the second image is not Dhar. No, it’s not the same daemon at all… ~One Year Ago~ The sun had set on whichever city Deszaras found himself in that week before he found what he was looking for. Walking up the alley he knew that he had come to the right place as longhaired drunken men and women in leathers, band t-shirts, and various other vomit-ridden clothes staggered past him in the other direction. As he came close to the seedy-looking establishment he could see a sign proclaiming this to be the Manor Tavern, and he had indeed found his poison distributor for the night. He walked past the bouncers without even giving their wide-eyed gazes so much as a glance of recognition, sauntering straight into the bar where generally people were too drunk or stoned to give a shit who he was. He enjoyed that sense of namelessness and selflessness that places like these give him, but what he didn’t enjoy was the sense of claustrophobic suffocation that crowds of people gave off as he stood in the middle of them. Perhaps the term was “sociopathic”… but he preferred to think of it as “selective breathing space”. Slicing through the crowd like an intent knife through butter, he searched for the most important thing in the room – the jukebox. Zigzagging his way through the maze of people and the haze of smoke, he approached the sacred machine and threw in whatever money he had liberated from his trench coat pocket with the single grab. Skipping through the selection he hit buttons nonchalantly as he found bands that he liked, not exactly sure of how many choices he had, but he didn’t care. Black Label Society, Slayer, Annihilator, Stormtroopers Of Death, Megadeth… and then the button clicked with no response, after which he turned right and sat on a barstool. Soon the bartender came over and questioningly raised his eyebrows into his forehead, stretching his face into the kind of thing that makes you want some target practise with a shovel. Deszaras simply ordered his beer, though, ignoring the urge to rip out his eyes and piss on his brain. Such things aren’t considered friendly in the inner city, he reminded himself. Then again, maybe in Texas… Beside him was a man just a shave taller than he was, fighting with a packet of peanuts as though it was for his very life. Then, suddenly, the packet exploded to the man’s left hand side, spewing the nuts all over our Resident Evil. Yet, worse than this, they went in his beer… and the culprit began to laugh. Deszaras’ head slowly turned to burn a look of hatred toward the man, who continued laughing. ‘What?’ he said to Dez, jesting loudly. ‘You don’t think that was funny?’ ‘No, I don’t.’ replied Deszaras as softly and calmly as he found possible. ‘But you…’ he lifted his defiled beverage, then threw it in the deviant’s face. ‘…You’re a joke.’ The man’s haughty laugh abruptly cut to silence before he summoned a growl, his wet face contorting into a scowl of hellish proportions. Then the two barstools hit the floor and both men stood nose to nose. ‘You realise what’s goin’ to happen here, boy?’ Deszaras’ head cocked a bit to the side in a sarcastic show of thought. ‘Let me guess… my foot… up your arse?’ ‘You think you’re a fuckin’ smart-ass, do you? Well I don’t take insults from weirdoes that think they should be in Slipknot!’ ‘And I don’t take them from a person that smells like a fart in a bag of fish.’ ‘You wanna die?’ ‘You wanna try?’ ‘What if I do?’ replied the man, two of his friends standing behind him now. Deszaras surveyed the scene, ‘Well…’ then he began calculated the odds, ‘looks like I did come to the right place after all…’ and then punched the guy in the face, feeling his nose crack. Swiftly the two other gentlemen jumped forward to try to grab him, but the first of the two found only a kick to the groin. The second managed to get past his fallen comrade and grab Deszaras by the throat, pushing him against a wall. Having been in the position many times before, Deszaras used his experience to use the wall to thrust himself forward, head-butting the miscreant and following up with a spear. As Deszaras happily laid into the guy with punch after punch, four bouncers approached, tearing him off the fallen tubby bitch. ‘Who started this shit?’ one of them asked. ‘I fucking started it!’ shouted Dez in his delighted fury before he shook off the bouncers and walked out, but not before grabbing his beer. ~Present Day~ ‘Life is no fairytale,’ says Deszaras softly. ‘It is not a movie or a game or a story. There are no heroic horseback rides off into the sunset, love does not conquer all and there are certainly no happy endings. There is only what we make as we subsist and breathe. The things that I take to my grave will not be pretty, nor will they be awful. They will be as I have made them in my own mind, and nothing more. What have we learned of desire, you ask? Have we not learned what a blessing it is?’ Dhar spits black gore, stark upon the white ceramic floor. ‘Blessing? How can something so filthy be blessed thing? Does it not push you into uncontrolled acts, unplanned and unspoken? Does it not distort logic and reason? Does it not take away our very right to think unimpeded?’ ‘Austerity narrows perspective.’ Deszaras states in reply, so calm that he almost whispers. ‘If we eat only simple food every day of our lives, our bodies will become used to only that. How, then, do we make choices to sate our palate if there is no alternative that our ignorant mind actually knows about? How do we exercise the rights you speak of, to choose and to think, when we do not know the options open to us?’ ‘Options are all well and good, Deszaras. But when the choice is to live or to die, to kill or be killed, to breathe or to suffocate – tell me what other choices are necessary in life? What other choices are necessary in any other section of our being, be it work, play, creation or destruction? We must prosper. We must. And to hinder our affluence is sin of such high order that it bares no thinking about. Why give ourselves choices that do not matter? Why wade through the flotsam and jetsam of decision, when only one true option is logical?’ Deszaras shakes his head confusedly. ‘So we have to sacrifice our freedom to flourish? We must deny ourselves pleasure and succour and whatever else desire can bring, simply so that it wont cloud our judgement? Perhaps judgement is not meant to be simple! Perhaps our minds are supposed to have myriad paths that our thinking can stride through! Why must life be black and white, when we can have all colours to see? Why must we renounce diversity? To think so is to want to be blinded to the world. How can a blind man prosper? How can a blind man be free?’ Dhar stays as still as rock in his seat, the mask scowling prominently before he scoffs. ‘What does freedom signify? What value is there in freedom to destroy one’s own life by debasing his beliefs, by choosing emotions that are damaging to mental and physical health – by choosing madness?!’ ‘By the same token, Dhar, what value is there even in self? What value can be derived from being a self in this world of untold millions of selves? If anything defines my “self” then it is the ability to desire. To desire change. The desire to better myself and the world in which I live. The desire to obtain that which I want and do not have. The desire to lose that which holds me down, or that which is old.’ ‘And therefore the ability to desire self-destruction when none of that goes your way!’ Dhar cuts in sharply. ‘Do you not realise that desire is futile? Only logic can make these choices for you. Why change if it is not necessary to survival? Why better yourself if you are amazing already? Why change the world when it does not want to be changed? Why obtain what you want when you need only strength? Why lose anything when you can have it all by using reason to deliver it to you? If it isn’t broke, Dez, don’t fix it! Must you constantly be reminded of that which you desired most, and lost?!’ And with the mention of that, Deszaras’ heart sinks… ~The End Of 2003~ The window opened, and for a millisecond Deszaras didn’t exist anymore; a condition that he could be happy with forevermore. The breeze from outside blew in, making the candlelight dance, thrashing as though it were about to die. Light seemed to be losing its perpetual doomed war against darkness. But light must surely fail, he thought, for darkness is a fundamental condition. The candle’s death throes made its light strobe and reflect off the room, making him think that the very walls were on fire. Just one more circumstance of the Hell that he must now experience until the end of his days, seemingly so because even the shortest of time periods seems prolonged to perpetuity while enduring a broken heart. He found himself so sunken, so far inside himself that he couldn’t move no matter how hard he tried. He simply sat there, staring at the candle, watching it struggle for its very existence. Still clutched in his hand was the towel that had dropped from her perfect body twenty-five hours previously. In the time that had elapsed since that beautiful moment, he had experienced the very quintessence of ecstasy and happiness, and now he found himself so despondent within his despair that the situation could be humorous. But it is not. It is far, far from it. For the past ten minutes he had been hearing things emerge from deep within his mind. Animalistic growls, pitiful and pain-filled screams, ominous whispers and, sometimes, the sound of his own heartbeat becoming so loud that it was like bass drums beside his ears. But everything else had become background noise to the whisper, taunting and devastating. ‘You thought I was gone, hmm?’ it goaded. ‘You thought that I could be vanquished with one small act of passion? A laughable notion! Your passion is all the more reason for my being here. I have to keep you from making the same mistakes over and over again.’ ‘Why?’ Deszaras managed to squeeze out of him, more a sighing sound than a spoken word. ‘Why? Do you want to feel this way forever? Do you want to keep on making yourself feel this bad again and again? Do you want to lose yourself to these worthless, useless emotions again and again? Do you?!’ ‘No.’ ‘No. I didn’t think so. That’s why I’m here. That’s why you created me in the first place. Or, rather, she did. You had very little to do with it.’ ‘She…?’ ‘That’s right. The first time she disappeared I bubbled from your mind like boiling water escaping from its pot. How else could you have managed? Then she returned and I was forced away. But now? Now I think I’ll stick around forever.’ demonic laughter echoed through his mind. Abruptly that laughter became silent as death as the door handle clicked and creaked, and more light was shed upon the room. Kiri always had the talent of brightening up a room no matter how dark it was, but still he could not move even to turn to look at her. She opened her mouth to talk to him, but upon seeing his mournful, unmasked face, she dared not. All she could do was hold him. Oblivious to her, laughter leaked back into his brain, and seemed to ring around the room like a funeral bell. He closed his eyes as the tolling bled into him. ~Present Day~ ‘Time never turns back,’ jeers Dhar, ‘What we failed to say remains unsaid. What we failed to do remains undone – so your desire to have what you once had and to be what you once were is futile and childish. Sheer desire changes nothing, especially the past.’ ‘No, it doesn’t.’ replies Dez. ‘Desire is a thing meant for the future.’ Dhar laughs. ‘Who was that supposed to convince, Dez? Me or you?’ ‘Regret and desire are far from the same thing, Dhar. Rooted in the same place, perhaps, but not the same. To desire is to have meaning,’ ‘And all with meaning,’ Dhar cuts in, ‘becomes shattered and lacerated. It always ends screaming.’ ‘Maybe so. But maybe that’s the point.’ ‘So then, if desire is pain, why defend it so fervently?’ ‘Because to feel pain is to feel human. And sometimes it’s good to be human.’ ‘Sometimes? Phff. What good is it being a human with no humanity in you? What good will “being human” do you when no one else even considers that as a course of action? Why are you so intent on being a fool?’ ‘What I am intent on is owning my own head again, Dhar. I desire it greatly.’ ‘And that’s why, ultimately, you’ll lose it.’ Deszaras rises from his chair and removes his mask. He gives a funereal smile, like the last smile of a dying man. ‘We’ll see.’ he says.
