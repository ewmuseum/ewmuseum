Dr Faustiosity - Chapter 1: The Calling

5 December 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-12-05 Alone, he kneeled on the stone floor of the Curious Laboratory. His stained laboratory coat covered his legs as well as his body, turning his body into a bulky off-white blot on the landscape of the lab. In his right hand rested a stick of white chalk, which he slowly, deliberately touched to the ground. His arm traced out a straight line, as he thought of the events of the previous few weeks. A grimace passed across his face fleetingly, before he set his teeth again, gritted them tightly together, and continued to draw. The line came to an end, and he shifted his position, his legs and arms aching from the punishment that had been dished out to him by the Latch Key Kid. Alone, without Eegor or even Collette to help him in the ring, Doctor Curiosity had needed to pull out every dirty trick in his repertoire. He’d been forced into trying to wrestle as best he could on the same terms as his opponent. The doctor had proved that he was not a one trick pony, a comic fool reliant on his assistant for salvation. He had taken the fight to the Latch Key Kid, and in the end he had excelled, getting the count of three and the passage to the final. Curiosity moved around on the floor, ignoring the protests of his limbs, and began to draw another straight line, leading almost back on itself from the previous one. Alone, he had celebrated the victory, his hand being held up in the air by the referee, the audience cheering despite them often yelling abuse at the methods he had used inside the ring. He had gone back to his locker room to find a bottle of champagne, and in the solitary conditions he had opened it to find that it tasted empty, and somehow hollow. He shook his head, attempting to put the thoughts of the past out of his head as he shuffled sideways and finished the second line. Alone, the flight, and then the car journey home had seemed different, and not just because Doctor Curiosity had previously delegated to Eegor such menial tasks as driving. The wheel in his hands felt heavy and sluggish, the car unresponsive to what he wanted, as if it too was suffering from a strange malaise. The Curious Laboratory had finally loomed into view, a stark, solitary figure, out of place in the middle of the desert. Once there the doctor had thought long and hard about his options, and they had led him to where he was, shifting the weight from one leg to the other, and completing a third chalk line on the floor, crossing the original one. Alone, he had sat up for nights on end, reading from books that he never knew he even had. His love of studying had been rekindled, evoking memories of those long nights where, unaccompanied, he had studied astronomy and astrophysics; making sense of the stars and their lives. The untouchable balls of gas, heated to unimaginable temperatures by forces beyond compare, were still far more tangible than his new studies. Time after time the doctor threw down a book, lashing out at the nearest object and cursing loudly in German. His scientific mind was appalled by some of what he read, but underneath it all he could sense a nugget of truth, something that could be brought together and used for his own gain. He searched and probed for the something that he needed desperately. The doctor drew a fourth line, intersecting with both the first and second lines that he had drawn. Alone, the doctor had come to several conclusions. He had always been a man to plan things out before acting. He was a man who liked to go into battle knowing every last detail about an opponent, and as such he had studied Dave Hurst and Craig Goat as people that he might possibly meet in the final. Neither were men he had genuinely expected to meet, but by knocking out the likes of Seymour Almasy, Jay Phoenix and Angelo Deville, they had shown that underestimating them would be a first class ticket to defeat. Doctor Curiosity was intent on avoiding such an outcome, whatever the cost. Alone, he had looked at Craig Goat and ‘Exceptional’ Dave Hurst. The former was a man just starting to come into his own. He had faced different challenges, from porn stars to the current Global Champion. He was definitely a man to be reckoned with. Dave Hurst though, was coming from the opposite direction. He had been a well-known name for a while, but had never been able to take the final step to greatness. He had more in common with the doctor himself than with Craig Goat, in having fallen at the last hurdle in the past. That looked to have all changed in Hurst now though, with his stellar wins over Phoenix and Deville, a man some had claimed was unbeatable. Alone, the doctor had been forced to reflect on his chances. Without Eegor to help him around the ring, or to assist in the building of new curious devices, he would have to wrestle at a level he had never before attained if he wanted to see off Craig Goat. To see off Dave Hurst at the same time… that would take a miracle. Alone, he drew the fifth and final line, and stepped back to admire his handiwork. It lacked a lot of the mystical symbols that some of the more romanticised books had spoken of, and it wasn’t surrounded by either flickering candles or hooded men chanting solemnly. It was purely functional. Alone, Doctor Curiosity looked down at the pentagram in the centre of the laboratory floor, and he smiled. He wasn’t going to be alone for long. ----------------------------------------- Some miles from the laboratory, four burly men sat around a table topped with a green felt cloth. Smoke drifted up from a discarded, but not extinguished, cigarette that lolled on the edge of a small glass ashtray. Cards and betting chips were strewn around the table, their importance lost after the end of the game. Between two of the men was an empty chair, recently warm with the body of a gambler hoping to get lucky, but now cooling down towards the temperature of the man’s body, lying lifeless in the trunk of the car parked outside. The brief silence was broken by a man with an ugly scar running down the side of his cheek, turning the left hand corner of his mouth into an ugly rictus. “Can you believe that guy’s nerve? If he was going to try to cheat us, he could at least have had the decency to cheat well,” he said, his rough English accent cutting through the smoke like a saw. “Exactly! He didn’t cheat even nearly as well as you, Frank,” replied the man to his left, idly turning a recently cleaned knife in his fingers. Frank, the man with the scar, laughed, a harsh, course sound brought about by a lifetime spent in smoky backrooms such as the one in which they all sat. From the way he commanded the room, it seemed as if he was the leader of this gang, if indeed a gang was what they were. “Enough about him though. I don’t need to spend any more time worrying about an idiot like that. We’ve got some bigger fish to fry, gentlemen.” He produced a crumpled piece of paper from the inside pocket of his black suit jacket that was barely large enough to cover his frame. He laid it on the table. “This guy owes us a lot of money, and it’s time he paid up.” The three other men peered at the paper, and the photograph printed out on it. It depicted a couple that seemed to be enjoying a meal, or if not enjoying it then at least looking no less bored than the other customers. One half of the couple had a thick red line through it. The other half, the half that was still alive, was Doctor Curiosity. ----------------------------------------------------------- The doctor paced across the floor to the workbench that usually served as the centrepiece to the main laboratory. It had been cleared of bubbling green liquids and steaming beakers full of some unusual curious concoction. It had, after a great deal of effort, been moved from the usual position it held, and moved back up against the wall. The doctor was not sure how much space he would need for his ritual, but he thought it prudent to not be banging in to a huge slab of oak whilst indulging in otherworldly practices. Clearing his throat, the doctor picked up the incense and brought it to the centre of the five-pronged star that he hoped would serve as a conduit. He stuck the stick of incense in to a crack in the flagstones, and lit it. A quick blow to the tip and the flame died down, thin embers glowing red and giving off smoke, which curled up towards the cavernous ceiling. He retreated back a few paces, and consulted the notes that he had scribbled on the back of an envelope. The words began to be mumbled, the slivers of truth that he had discovered behind the layers of lies and obfuscation. “Ego obtestor diabolus.” The doctor, in an alien element to his usual scientific endeavours, carried on, his voice still mumbling as he slowly lifted up his left hand in to the air. “Orsus accedo ex abyssus.” He spoke haltingly, the cobbled together Latin a grammatical nightmare, but Doctor Curiosity had learned that the power lay in the meaning of the words and their intent, not on the niceties of syntax and pronunciation. ‘I call the devil as a witness, a beginning comes from Hell’, the doctor knew that was what he had been trying to say, and the intent was of greatest importance. It’s the thought that counts. “Impedo fumidus seditio.” ‘Prevent the smoke from rising’. As he spoke the words, and formed them as something solid inside his mind, Doctor Curiosity stopped moving his left hand, which was by now level with his shoulder. The smoke stopped rising, as if it had come to a glass ceiling. It swirled to the left and right, but rose no higher. “Formo.” The doctor commanded the smoke to take shape, to form into something else. It ceased moving sideways and condensed, forming a thick cylindrical barrier in the middle of the pentagram. “Exorior,” said Curiosity, louder than before. There was a slight tremor in the air as he spoke the word meaning ‘come forth’, as if something just out of the normal hearing range had been said, or perhaps screamed. The windows rattled for the briefest of moments, and the doctor suddenly felt as if something had passed through him. The jolt took him down to his knees, and as he looked up towards the pentagram, he saw that the chalk lines had turned blood red. The keening in the air increased in pitch and volume, yet still somehow remained a noise that Curiosity sensed rather than heard. It felt like the voice of something strange, something so utterly foreign to the world that it had to fight for any kind of communication. The smoke grew darker and darker, and Doctor Curiosity’s resolve stiffened. He needed his miracle. “Exorior.” The smoke seemed to turn in on itself time after time, growing more with the repetition of the word. Doctor Curiosity got back to his feet. “Exorior!” Everything seemed to start changing at once. The red chalk lines seemed to glow, and become a viscous liquid. A fire began to burn in the middle of the smoke, sending sparks that flew out, only to be pulled back in when approaching the edges of the pentagram. “Exorior!” Doctor Curiosity was screaming the word. He was caught up in the moment, and there was no turning back any more. He felt almost as if he was no longer in control, that a force more powerful than he could understand was using him like a marionette, pulling his strings and leading him on a merry dance. The pentagram of blood started to shine brightly, dazzling his eyes, but his hand remained rooted in the air, not able to come back and protect him from the glare. “EXORIOR!” He had given himself over completely. The smoke was billowing now, filling every inch of the portal that the doctor had created. He looked down to avoid the strange light being emitted by the pentagram, and saw that more light was dripping from his body. A strange crimson glow pervaded the surroundings, coming not from the pentagram, but instead seeping from the doctor’s skin. Even as he screamed out the invocation again, Doctor Curiosity came to a realisation, watching the puissant light fall from his body and take shape within the chalk markings. “EXORIOR!” The pentagram was not a portal; it was a cage. He was the portal. “EXORIOR!” He felt like every single one of his pores was a mile wide. He was sweating power, the light falling from him and being drawn within the lines sketched out by his simple piece of chalk, a pen mightier than any sword he could possibly wield. His voice was gone, and screaming became his release, no longer any words coming out, but a primal bellow of rage and release. Sparks flew from his head, coruscating and landing on the cold flagstones where they hissed and spat like oil. Within the pentagram the smoke took shape quickly, forming and reforming as if trying to decide which body it would choose to inhabit. A melee of horns, hooves and tails flickered in and out of existence in the time it took Doctor Curiosity’s overworked heart to beat. His eyes were dry, but would not shut themselves, as he fell silent, his voice hoarse and sore. The power still flowed through him, coming from some source he could not see or feel, and it poured itself entirely in to the smoke, the creature within growing by the second. Soon it was as tall as the doctor, and then it surpassed him, extending towards the ceiling. The noise in the air became both sound and feeling simultaneously. Right on the edges of hearing, the doctor’s eardrums were tortured by a high pitched whine that shattered the remaining glassware within the laboratory. At the same time, a vibration shook the air, and everything started to waver. Lightning crackled and reality shook inside the pentagram, the fire starting to consume the figure that lurked within it, occluded still by the thick fog. Had the doctor still been screaming, he would not have been heard. A wind tore through the laboratory, though the doctor was rooted to the spot. Papers flew around him as the smoke reached the ceiling, and energy was in the air, it was all around, it was everywhere both within and without the doctor’s body. The room seemed to fill with light and fire, with power and energy, with noise and violence and anguish and longing and hatred. Then it exploded. Fire shot out from within the cloud and hit the ceiling. It lasted for the merest fraction of a second before the force tore it apart and proceeded to do the same to the roof. Huge stone blocks, tiles from the roof and wooden support beams alike were rent asunder and thrown away from the building. Doctor Curiosity himself was finally affected, thrown by the blast to land in a heap underneath his workbench. The fires danced within the smoke and shot off from one tip of the pentagram, smashing the wall in that direction and bring down the rest of the roof in a hail of deadly rubble. Some of it was caught by the next blast, an iridescent shot of what seemed like electricity that arced through the ionised air, tearing masonry and charring wood on the spot. One final blast left Curiosity dazed, a wave of extremely hot air that reeked of sulphur and brimstone that washed across the room, settling it after the raw, uninhibited violence of the calling. The doctor placed his glasses back on his face, and looked up through eyes seared with pain. Within the pentagram a horrific figure stood, snarling at the doctor. It looked a little like a man, but more the approximation of man; what you might get if you gave a child a lump of clay and told them to make a man… and then told the child to set fire to it. Burnt flesh hung off his face, and his eyes burned with nothing other than hate and resentment. He was naked, but the flames had long before seen to eradicate him of anything resembling genitalia. His legs were ugly and misshapen, burnt beyond belief and reshaped to as to afford him something to stand on. This was a man who had been killed, but who had not been allowed to truly die. His head held no hair, but the reddened scalp was distinguished by two lumps above his ears, that could have been mistaken for the beginnings of horns. The man’s face was a mess, his mouth hanging open, lipless and gaping, a large, red, forked tongue snaking around behind teeth that had been sharpened to points. He was fixing Doctor Curiosity with his gaze, and when he spoke it was as if a thousand insects were buzzing at once, flying to Curiosity’s body and biting him, stinging him and burrowing their way into his flesh. “Whhhaaaaatt hhaaaaavvvveee yyyooouuuu dddooonnneeeee?” The voice went through Curiosity and made his flesh crawl. The doctor’s stomach rebelled at the sound, lurching sufficiently to bring up a mixture of bile and half-digested food into his mouth. He spat it out, looking up from the floor at what he had called, what he had summoned. The creature lacked for a nose, with two crude puncture wounds at roughly the right anatomical position sufficing for it. Nevertheless, it sniffed at the air, and what could have passed for a smile graced its face. It spoke in a whisper, the insects replaced with the slithering of snakes, forked tongues hissing and coiled bodies caressing one another, ready to strike in an instant. “Ssssssooo? What do you requessssst of thisssss?” The sibilant sounds caused the doctor to flinch, but he knew what he had done, and he knew why he had done it. Everything he had done to get to the grand final of GTT4 was as a team. He couldn’t do it alone. He needed his miracle. Getting up to unsteady legs, Doctor Curiosity spoke to the demon. “I called you forth.” He cleared his throat, and his strained voice tried to speak with more authority. “I called you here to make a deal.”
