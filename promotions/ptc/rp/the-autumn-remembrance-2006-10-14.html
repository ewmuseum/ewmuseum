The Autumn Remembrance

Date: 2006-10-14 "Promises are the uniquely human way of ordering the future, making it predictable and reliable to the extent that this is humanly possible." - Hannah Arendt (1906-75), German-born American political philosopher. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Introduction: The Promise. How do I…how can I tell him? The thought had been swimming around in her head ever since the doctor had given her the bad news. Lung cancer. A ten percent survival rate. The words rolled off the doctors lips coldly and just the very words made Mary shiver. You‘re dying, Mrs McKail. I’m sorry. The tears welled up in her eyes. No, she thought. Be strong. Be strong for him. Mary McKail sat in her apartment in total darkness. She’d been sat there for hours, wrestling with the doctor’s words and hadn‘t bothered flicking the light switch once the night had come. It probably wasn’t a good idea to dwell on it, but how could she not? Mary shook the thoughts from her mind. She refused to sit there and feel sorry for herself. This was her own damned fault. She’d smoked practically all of her life and she’d brought it on herself. She’d smoked those damned cancer sticks, oblivious to the health warning they put on each and every pack in thick black lettering. No, she thought, I’ll feel sorry for myself later. Now I have to think about Jacob… And so she came to the same question again. How do I…how can I tell him? She’d been going around in circles like this ever since she came back from the doctors and she was just about sick of it. Decisions had to be made and plans had to be concocted for the future -- for his future. Like any wife, she knew her husband well. At least, well enough to know that he wouldn’t take the news well, but then, what loving husband would? However, unlike most loving husbands, Mary knew that Jacob had always been unstable. She met him that way and despite all that they’d been through, she still didn’t know why. He’d always refused to talk about that part of his life, not because he didn’t want her to know it, more because he just couldn’t bare thinking about it, never mind moulding those memories into words. Regardless of why, the fact remained that he was hanging from the thinnest of threads and always had been, no matter how much she’d tried to help him. It was for that reason alone she was so reluctant to tell him. What will he do when I’m gone? When she got right down to it, it was what worried her the most. She already had a solution to the problem, she suddenly realised, but up until now, she’d been reluctant to even entertain the thought. It didn’t seem right somehow. It would be as though she’d be manipulating him from beyond the grave. Despite her misgivings, she came to understand that it was the only way and, even then, it was a long shot at best. She would make him promise. Promise to live his life. Promise to move on when she was gone. Promise to fulfil his dreams and be happy. She realised the latter part of that might be difficult for some time, but she knew it was attainable. Jacob was a honourable man. Always had been. It was one of his traits that’d made her fall in love with him in the first place. If she made him promise all of those things, he would be honour-bound to achieve them the best he could. Unfortunately for them both, it didn’t quite work out as Mary had intended it to. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Return. As soon as McKail took a step out of his car, the sharp Orchard Beach wind cut right into his flesh like a machete. It was a blunt, yet effective, reminder that the Summer was well and truly over with and Autumn was here. However, today, the whether could not hope to deter him. Nothing could. Much to his chagrin, the dozens or so other citizens populating the boardwalk of the man-made beach, shared his steely resolve. But that was The Bronx for you. He hadn’t been back to the place for a while and with good reason. However, upon crossing the state lines, he was immediately reminded of the magical qualities the state possessed, as the electric atmosphere only the state of New York could possibly generate. It engulfed him like a much needed security blanket. He took a lungful of the bittersweet Autumn air and silently surmised, it’s good to be home. McKail had to admit the nostalgic atmosphere he found himself surrounded with, brought back some of the better memories of his past. Conversely, it had also prised free some of his more painful one’s from the docile state they’d long-since been imprisoned. They’d already begun to crawl out of the deep pit at the core of his being and along the way they’d drawn lots for the opportunity to be the first to torture him within his nightmares during the weeks to come. I’m not ready, he told himself, breathing heavily through the strain of it all, not yet. He’d experienced unparalleled horrors in this city and he’d long since buried them deep inside. He was a very angry man back in those and acted almost like a wild animal the majority of the time. It was only when he met Mary, he realised he had to change. More than that, it was the first time he realised he wanted to change. It was a tough transformation to undergo, but Mary stood by his side throughout it all like she‘d always done since then. After some time, he’d managed to forget the intricate details of each atrocity in turn, but over the years he felt the darkness, which came hand in hand with those memories, lurk at the corner of his soul. It was still there and what’s more it liked to let him know it. Now, he wanted his animalistic side back and fully intended to harness the darkness it contained in order to help accomplish what he needed to accomplished. He didn’t much want to, but it was the only way. His mind would soon be filled with those horror’s he thought he’d never have to experience again, but it was the price he was willing to pay. His angry, animalistic side wouldn’t serve well for him to integrate into society again, but with Mary gone, he had no real need of human interaction anymore. All that he gave a damn about now was fulfilling the promise he made to his wife and he was prepared to go to anything lengths to do so. Coming to this city and feeling it’s unarguable atmosphere already begin to pry free those memories, he knew he was already on the right path and what’s more, he was scared. The decision to unleash this upon himself, wasn’t easy to make, but he could feel deep down that it was the right thing to do. The past few months within the wrestling industry had been tough for McKail. Yes, he’d had some success. He’d won the GCW Television title in only his second match and he’d won the PTC Infinite Gauntlet championship on his first attempt. However, he also lost the GCW Television title on only his second title defence. He lost the Infinite Gauntlet Championship on his first and had done nothing of substantial value since. With the GTT6 just around the corner, McKail realised that he needed to up his game if he wanted to accomplish anything of substance. And he needed to do it quick. The first round opponent he’d been pitted against was a potential minefield that could blow his ass to pieces if he took a wrong step. On the one hand, he was a formidable opponent in his own right and had achieved more at this point in his life than McKail. On the other hand, Andy M. was wrestling against doctor orders. McKail had heard that his back was seriously injured last year sometime and he was just one devastating backbreaker away from permanent injury. Before, McKail may have overlooked this weakness and tried to win cleanly, but now, such sportsmanship was out of the window. He will be going for his back and he will be looking to break it. After such a though entered his head, he truly realised that it was only a matter of time now before the darkness emerged in a more tangible way. However, he fully understand that he had to be careful. He had too much past troubles to simply have all of the pain and anguish crammed into his head in one go. It had to be done incrementally over a period of time; this wasn’t something that could be rushed. Stood with his legs apart, by the side of his car, he dipped down to touch his toes and brought himself upright again. He repeated the movement a number of times. McKail wasn’t much for correct exercise methodology, but he knew enough to know that stretching before a run was always a good idea. McKail wasn’t comfortable being surrounded by so many people at the best of times and it especially made him self conscious when working out. In his mind, they were glaring at him bizarrely and criticizing his actions and his fitness levels. Regardless of how he thought the people regarded him, the truth of it was, the people were an integral part of any city you chose to frequent. It would be next to impossible to experience one without the other and so it was a compromise he had to make. Despite his discomfort, overall he felt good. The alcohol he’d been so dependent upon just a few days ago had been effectively purged from his system and his body was well on the road to recovery. But his mood changed soon enough. "What yer doin’, kid?" Oh fuck… McKail knew who it was, even before he raised his head toward the sound of the voice. It was his damn hallucination again, taking the form of Mickey from those damned Rocky movies. He stood there with a toothy grin plastered across his wrinkled old face as though it‘s sudden appearance was the most natural thing in the world. McKail wasn’t the least bit shocked. He’d seen this before. "Gettin’ ready ta dance or somethin’?" A week ago, McKail was injured in the ring by a pissed-off rookie who wanted to inflict some serious damage upon his person. Well, that damned rookie got his wish and pretty much caved McKail’s head in. Later that night, enter the hallucination and if he hadn’t been so soused with Bourbon at the time, it would’ve scared the hell out of him. Still, finding himself face to face with a character from a particularly successful boxing movie franchise concocted by his twisted mind, was still something of a surprise. Much to McKail’s annoyance and everlasting embarrassment, the damned hallucination had been the one to force him to realise him that he needed to change if he ever wanted to fulfil the promise he made to Mary. After that realisation took root in McKail’s mind, it had promptly disappeared and McKail hadn’t seen it since. McKail had no idea why it’d decided to show itself now. After all, McKail’s head injury had pretty much healed itself now. "What?" It continued. "Cat got yer tongue?" McKail remained ignored it and remained deadly silent. Nobody else could see or hear this thing apart from him and so talking to what would appear to be thin air by the casual spectator, wasn’t the best of ideas. He didn’t much care what people thought of him, but there were limits to his shameless ignorance. Instead, McKail finished his stretches, put his sweatshirt hood up over his out of control, long hair and headed off along the boardwalk at medium pace. He hoped the thing would take the hint and leave him alone, but he realised that was a long shot at best. "What tha hell d’ya call this, kid?" It barked at him. "Runnin’? Ya gotta be kiddin’ me?" If the thing didn’t mean it to be a rhetorical question, McKail’s silence sure as hell made it one. "I’ve seen cripples run better than this!" That was a bit harsh and it didn’t make one bit of sense. Is this fucking thing actually trying to motivate me? It wasn’t the craziest notion to pass through McKail’s mind, but it sure as hell came close. "This is pathetic," it continued. "Yer pathetic! Just what in the hell’re ya doin’ in this business anyhow?" It was a good question. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The School. The school looked even more crummy on the inside, than it did on the outside, and that was saying something. However, it was located within a particularly run down section of the Bronx and McKail and his foster brother, Johnny, knew what they were getting themselves into. "This guy better be genuine," McKail warned. At seventeen, he didn’t much care about the state of the building, he was glad of the freedom and the very notion of independence. The year before, a couple of their friends at school got jobs working for Frank Sun, a local wrestling promoter. Basically, they helped to set up the ring before shows and, not only got paid for it, but also got to take a few bumps for their troubles. Last week, Frank let them know he needed a few more guys to help out because he’d secured a couple of extra shows that month and McKail and Johnny jumped at the chance. "Don‘t worry," Johnny told him, in that serious tone of his. He adopted it whenever he talked about the wrestling industry. "He is." Johnny was just about the biggest wrestling nut McKail had ever known. Whilst McKail enjoyed watching the sport himself, his knowledge of the business paled in comparison to that of his foster brother. Johnny was a small, wiry kid and so it was no real surprise that he particularly enjoyed watching the cruiserweight’s the best. He often spoke in great lengths about the subtle poetry of their fluidic movements, but often it went way over McKail’s head. The two of them were polar opposites. Johnny wanted to live his dream and become a professional wrestler more than anything in the world. He knew intricate details about the business, as well as how to work in the ring. McKail, on the other hand, just liked the idea of freedom that was so often associated with the business. He wanted to be away from his foster parents and live in what he perceived to be the real world. Sure he liked wrestling, but he didn’t want to be involved in the business nearly as much as Johnny did. "Here he is," Johnny announced, as the brown office door creaked open at the other side of the building. "Don’t fuck this up for me." McKail sighed. Johnny was excited about this opportunity, perhaps even too excited. McKail thought he’d shit his pants when the well-built guy, clearly in his mid-forties, emerged from the office and casually strolled over toward them. It had to be Frank Sun. The first thing that struck McKail was the promoter’s pearly white teeth, shining brightly like a divine beacon of light on a grim Winters night. This clashed gracefully with his brown opaque sunglasses, his open top shirt showing a hairy, and ridiculously tanned, chest and a pair of pants that McKail knew for a fact went out of style in the seventies. He didn’t look like he belonged in the wrestling industry at all, he looked more like an extra on Dallas or something. McKail glared at Johnny and rolled his eyes, far from impressed. "Boys," he greeted curtly, glancing at his watch. He had somewhere to be and he let them both know it. "Here’s the deal. You help put up the ring and I’ll give you twenty bucks a night and show you a few bumps. Deal?" Twenty bucks? McKail considered. Is that all? "Deal," Johnny quickly replied. McKail glared at his friend sternly. He didn’t much like it when other people answered for him and he certainly didn’t like it when they made decisions for him. "Great." Frank Sun grinned. It reminded McKail of a shark he’d seen on a television documentary and it gave him a queasy feeling at the pit of his stomach. "Be here tomorrow at noon." Without another word, Frank brushed past the both of them and headed towards the exit, leaving the two of them to glare at the full-sized training ring in the middle of the expanse, in wonder. It wasn’t long before McKail broke the silence. "Well that’s just great," he declared. "Twenty bucks a goddamned night. The cheapest motel in the area, which doesn’t come with compulsory vermin, costs eighteen." Johnny glared at McKail and chuckled, much to his friend’s bewilderment. "Jake, this is what’s called ‘paying our dues’," he replied, as though he‘d been in the business twenty years. "We’ve got it easy." "Bullshit!" McKail exclaimed. "So we won’t be able to sleep in the motel every night," Johnny responded. "It’s no big deal, we’ll just sleep in the car." McKail’s eyes widened. "Sleep in the car?" Was he being serious? "Are you serious?" "Hey," Johnny said, with a sharp grin. He wore an expression that suggested McKail should be thanking him. "At least we don’t have to travel three hundred miles everyday just to get here." McKail shook his head, turned and headed toward the exit. "Whatever, man." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Insanity. Fifteen minutes into the run, McKail was already beginning to lose it with his imaginary ‘friend’. Thus far, he’d just about been able to keep his cool with the thing, but it was only ever going to a matter of time before he caved in and reacted. They both knew it. So, out of desperation, he decided to take his mind of it and mercilessly scrutinised the new throng of people he encountered with each passing second. The further along the boardwalk he ran, the more the crowd thinned out and the more colourful they became. At this side of the beachfront there were still a good deal of people having great difficulty letting the Summer go. Most wore unnecessary bright Hawaiian shirts, shorts or three quarter length trousers and brown leather sandals that Jesus himself would’ve been proud of. Once McKail factored the golden brown autumn leaves and the cutting easterly wind into the equation, there laid out before him, was one of the most absurdly surreal images he’d ever seen in his life. This city was already having it’s effect on him and for the first time in a long time, he cracked a smile. However, his uncharacteristic joviality didn’t last. The hallucination saw to that. "Gettin’ tired already, kid?" The hallucination taunted. "That’s just pathetic. Yer’ve only bin runnin’ for fifteen minutes!" Fuck off, Mickey. Just fuck off. McKail silently cursed panting heavily, immediately chastising himself for attributing it a name. It wasn’t a person, it was just a figment of his imagination. Nothing more, nothing less. "Yer pathetic, kid," It said, "ya know that?" McKail just couldn’t get his head around it. Before, back when it first appeared to him and helped him come to the difficult decision of leaving the booze behind him, McKail could at least understand why it was saying the things it was saying. Now, was a different story entirely. He figured it was just it’s way of motivating him during his workout, but McKail knew there was something else. He knew there was an underlying issue that had yet to be revealed. "Still ignorin’ me, eh?" It said and with a vicious grin added, "This ain’t no joke yer know, kid." McKail glared at it with fire in his eyes, stopped in his tracks and finally broke his silence. "Fuck you!" The surrounding people had stopped whatever they were doing and glared at him in wonder, but he didn’t care. He pointed toward the hallucination and repeated, "Fuck you!" ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Disappointment. They’d been working for Frank Sun for the better part of six months when it happened. McKail was having the time of his life. At the beginning, he was just glad be away from his foster parents, but as soon as he got in the ring with Frank to take a few bumps his interest peaked to levels comparable with his foster brother. McKail had always been the athletic type in school and so he picked up the moves and holds with moderate ease and it’d gotten to the point where the bumps didn’t bother him much anymore. Earlier on in his training, however, he’d hit the ropes and took the force of the cable liver-first, instead of relying on what Frank, at the time, told him was the ‘Latissumus’ muscle. That night he pissed blood. McKail was having so much of a good time, he almost didn’t notice how badly Johnny was coping. Although Johnny knew the business, he’d never been the athletic type. McKail had gathered that he’d had a hard time in his early years of high school, back when he was still living with his biological parents, and never got the chance to join any of the sport teams. The irony was, it was the wrestling that’d maintained his sanity throughout it all. Still, Johnny wasn’t picking up the moves nearly as quickly as McKail or any of the other guys and it was beginning to get him down. Frank himself had told Johnny not to worry too much about it and that more often than not, it took some guys longer to pick up the moves. Johnny got careless one day, during the summer of nineteen-ninety, after they’d spent a gruelling couple of hours setting up the ring, right before one of Frank’s big shows. That night, he damn near broke Sammy‘s neck. Later in our time with Frank, we saw that he often took kids off the street who’d been having a rough time of it. He was good like that. Sammy had come into the fold under those circumstances, just a couple of months after McKail and Johnny, and they’d quickly become friends. Johnny and Sammy were around about the same weight at that point and so worked together quite a lot, whereas McKail had beefed up some and often spent most of his free time within the grimy confines of Frank’s in-built gym. Frank split them both up when he realised the pairing was a little uneven. Johnny and Sammy were practicing a hip toss when Johnny got careless. A combination of sweat and carelessness prompted a loss of footing as Johnny took Sammy over, forcing the younger boy to hit the floor head first with a thud. It also twisted his arm in such an angle, it sickened everyone in the room and a few with weaker constitutions, were physically sick just at the sight of it. Johnny’s eyes widened as soon as the realisation of it fully took a hold of him. McKail could see his bottom lip quiver, as Frank checked up on the unconscious Sammy. "Oh fuck," Johnny muttered to himself. The shock of it still clung onto him with all of its worth and it didn’t look like letting up any time soon. "Hey, Johnny," McKail shouted, getting his foster brother’s attention. There was nothing he could do for Sammy that the others weren’t already doing and so he decided to deal with the other casualty in all this. "It’s okay, buddy. It was just a mistake. It could’ve happened to anyone…" Johnny’s eyes were still transfixed on Sammy’s unmoving body and he could only nod in response. At least he can hear me, McKail thought, sliding under the ring ropes. McKail put his arm around his foster brother soothingly. Three years ago they were strangers, thrown together by the fostering agency, to two parents who actually requested to care for two teenage adolescents. Boy, were they ever unprepared for what they took on. It was a turbulent time between them at first, but when they finally got to know each other, they discovered their pasts with their biological parents were pretty much the same and because of it, a bond between them grew. They’d been best friends ever since and they were inseparable. "It’s going to be okay, Johnny…" McKail reassured, as Frank turned around to glare at the two, with rage in his eyes. "It’s not going to be okay," He told them, his eyes burning a hole in Johnny’s face. "You could have killed him today, just because you’re a clumsy moron." Johnny lowered his head and remained silent. McKail had no such composure and pointed at Sun accusingly. "Fuck you!" The others could barely believe their ears. McKail knew he was showing his mentor disrespect, but at that point, he was beyond caring. "Fuck me?" Frank Sun questioned. "I’m the one that gave this clown a chance." He gestured toward Johnny with both hands, angrily. "I should’ve told him weeks ago he didn’t stand a chance in this business and everyone with eyeballs fixed into their skulls can see it." "Yeah?" McKail said, echoing his mentor’s anger. "Then why didn’t you?" "Because of you, kid," Frank Sun replied. "If I’d told him to leave, you would’ve gone with him." McKail’s eyes widened as the realisation struck him like a well placed sledgehammer shot to the testicles. "Fuck…" Johnny glared at McKail and pushed him away, his eyes filled with tears. "Johnny…" McKail off, watching his distraught brother shakily climb out of the ring. "Don’t, Jake," he said waving him away. "Just don’t." Johnny made it to the exit and slipped outside into the uncomfortably warm night air, leaving McKail standing in the ring feeling guilty for something he had no control over. This had been Johnny’s life and he’d just been told that it didn‘t want him. It had been taken away from him like so many other things in his life. What’s more, his half brother who didn’t really know a lot about the industry turned out to be better than him and more at ease within the industry. McKail couldn’t blame him for being angry. "Let him go, kid." Frank put a reassuring hand upon his shoulder. He was already looking after his investment, so soon after the accident, like the shark he always suspected he was. McKail tore his shoulder away from Frank’s grasp and silently climbed out of the ring. "Don’t let him take you down with him, kid," he shouted, as McKail strode out into the same uncomfortably warm night air and set off to find his brother before he did something stupid. "Don’t let him take you down with him." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Rage. "Takin’ this a little personally, ain’t ya, kid?" Mickey told him, with a grin. McKail chose to take it as smugness. "Who the fuck do you think you are to tell me what to do?" McKail screamed. He didn’t care what the other people thought of him anymore and he certainly didn’t care that he, himself, thought this was beyond insane. The hallucination pointed at McKail. "I’m a part of ya, kid. That gives me some authority, don’t it?" McKail was too angry to concede to a little thing such as logic. "No it fucking doesn’t. You’re worse than nothing and certainly don‘t exist." "That so?" It asked. McKail nodded, idly noting the people in the immediate area begin to scatter. A few of the more bemused, continued to watch on, albeit at a safe distance. "Ever enter yer thick skull I could just be yer conscience, kid?" Yes, I have, McKail thought to himself, but decided not to let that end his tirade. "Fuck you!" McKail pointed at his own head. "My conscience is in here, cockend." It’s smile persisted and that made McKail angrier. "Not in some Burgess Meredith wannabe! Fuck you and fuck off!" McKail felt a reassuring hand place itself upon his shoulder and turned toward the perpetrator. It turned out to be a pretty woman in her mid-forties. What the fuck does she want? The fire in his eyes made her recoil her hand immediately, yet she persisted. "Are you alright?" She asked. "Only you’re talking to yourself and…" Where the hell did she think she was going with that statement? McKail considered, his stern gaze unrelenting. Still she’d at least realised that her sentence was a moronic one, as she cut herself short and fidgeted with her hands nervously. However, she didn’t quit and tried an altogether different approach. "If you like, we could talk about it?" She asked, her eyes gentle and oddly calming. "Human interaction, kid," the hallucination chirped up again, glaring at the woman‘s breasts. "Ya could sure use this." McKail grunted something towards the figment of his imagination and, desperately trying to filter out the unbridled anger within his voice, answered her, "Hell no." The woman nodded. She wanted to help him and truly thought that she could, but if he didn’t want to be helped, what could she do? Nothing, it turned out, as she reluctantly turned and headed off, on her way. "Oh nice, kid." The hallucination, shook it’s head. "Mary wanted this for yer and yer failed her. Just like yer failed Johnny." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Tragedy. McKail searched for Johnny everywhere that night. He found his car right where he’d left it and no Johnny. He searched the cheap and nasty motel they sometimes treated themselves to and no Johnny. He searched the bars and clubs their fake ID’s had got them into and no Johnny. Hell, he’d even gone back to his foster parents house and definitely no Johnny. In the end, McKail gave up and came to the conclusion that if Johnny didn’t want to be found, he was never going to be. Days passed and even though he was worried, his mind was forced to refocus itself upon what he was going to do next. There was no way he was going back to work and train with Frank Sun anymore, that was certain, but where else would he go? He sure as hell didn’t intend to go back to live with his Foster parents again. There was no denying it, wrestling was in his blood now and he already knew it was what he was going to do for the rest of his life. Nobody could tell him any different. No matter how much Johnny’s failure and obvious resentment grated against him, it was he was destined to do. McKail stood outside of Frank Sun’s place and enjoyed the early morning mildness that menacingly threatened to break out into another sweltering day in the Bronx. It took all of his nerve to come back here and he swore to himself that he’d keep calm throughout whatever ordeal Frank chose to throw at him. He didn’t much care what his former mentor had to say. He just wanted to get his paycheck and then get gone. Frank finally turned up around about eight-thirty and approached McKail hesitantly. He had no idea whether Frank had expected him to lash out in anger or anything, but McKail had to admit the thought had crossed his mind. "Hey, kid," he greeted, already extracting his keys from his beige jacket pocket. "Frank," McKail returned, keeping a stern expression fixed upon his face. "Coming back to us?" Frank asked hopefully. McKail didn’t think it’d be that easy to come back, even if he didn‘t want to. Frank must’ve really thought something of him. "No," McKail told him. "I’m just here for my last pay check." Frank nodded, obviously expecting as much. "How’s Sammy doing?" McKail asked. He was getting uncomfortable and wanted to change the subject quick. In hindsight, there were better subjects to broach. Frank nodded, taking note of the mood of the conversation and already knowing how this one was going to play out. "Fine. Concussion and a dislocated shoulder." He paused, lowering his head. He pretended to search for the correct key, but McKail just figured he was genuinely ashamed. "How’s Johnny?" McKail shook his head. "Don’t know. I can’t find him." Frank’s face dropped. McKail could tell he felt guilty for what he said, but also knew that during that heated exchange, he spoke the truth. Frank would never take back what he’d said to Johnny and even if he did, McKail would lose what little respect he had for him entirely. Frank found the right key and went to place it in the lock, then noticed something. The door was ajar and it looked like it had been forced open. Frank turned to McKail, eyebrows raised. If he wanted answers from McKail he wasn’t going to get any. He’d been there twenty minutes and hadn’t noticed anything. Realising as much, Frank tentatively opened the door and took a step inside. The lights were on and looked like they had been all night. "Hello?" Frank shouted. There was no response and there was no sound of movement or anything. They both stepped into the building, each of them keeping their wits about them, just in case some lunatic jumped out on them with a crowbar or something. After a few tense seconds, they both came to the conclusion that there was nobody in there. They were both wrong. Frank saw it first, as he headed toward his office to retrieve McKail’s pay check. He stopped mid-stride and glared at the body lying in the middle of the wrestling ring. McKail witnessed his actions and developed a confused expression, until, of course, the body entered his peripheral vision. McKail couldn’t see it properly from his current position and so tentatively approached, but he already knew precisely who it was. Frank shot him a concerned expression, trying with all his might to keep it together. "You don’t need to see this, kid." He was wrong. McKail did need to see. The closer he came, the more the intricacies of Johnny’s lifeless frame jumped out him. The blood stained canvas immediately surrounding his body. The used gun resting right next to him. The gaping wound dug deep into his skull. Standing there, allowing the horrific scene to reach out and grab him the thought of precisely how his foster brother had gotten his hands on a gun, didn’t even enter into his head. "His body is cold," Frank told him, but McKail wasn’t listening anymore. "He’s been like this for hours." McKail fought the urge to hurl with everything he had, but couldn’t fight the emotions that promptly followed. He was forced to floor, on his knees, and hunched over, with his head resting on the cold wooden floor. He didn’t even try to hold back the tears. Frank came over and rested a tender hand on his back, as McKail’s shoulders heaved up and down. "Fuck, kid. I don’t know what to say." McKail shrugged his former mentor’s hand off his back and fought to compose himself. It wasn’t even a battle worth fighting. His eyes became bone dry and, instead, filled with anger. Frank hung his head in shame again. "This isn’t you’re fault, kid," he managed. "Remember that." "No, it’s not my fault, it’s yours," McKail screamed, an accusatory figured jammed at him. "You’re the one responsible for my brother’s death." "That’s not fair," was the only resistance Frank could muster. He couldn’t even convince himself of it, never mind McKail. "Yes it is," McKail responded. "I’m going to call emergency services and our foster parents." Frank put a gentle hand upon his shoulder just as his star pupil walked away. "Kid…" McKail whirled around, immediately knocking Frank’s hand away and catching his former mentor unawares with a wild punch. It didn’t have much power behind it, but it still drew blood from the side of Frank’s lip. Frank dabbed at it with his thumb, not entirely shocked by McKail’s reaction. "Don’t, Frank," McKail warned. "Just don’t." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Conclusion. "Don’t you dare bring Johnny up," McKail warned. "You don’t have the right!" The hallucination nodded, understanding. "Don‘t take it out on me, kid, yer the one who wanted ta remember." The hallucination looked all around him, pointedly. "That’s why ya came back here, ain‘t it?" McKail frowned, his anger subsiding. "That’s what all this is about? You’re trying to help me remember?" The hallucination smiled. McKail had spent so much time getting angry at the figment of his imagination, he didn’t even notice what was blatantly staring him in the face the entire time. "Fuck!" McKail exclaimed. He removed his sweatshirt hood and ran a hand through his wild hair, with no real purpose. "I got yer off tha booze, kid," It told him. "Made yer realise yer were bein’ a damned fool. I‘m here ta make sure yer follow through." McKail shook his head. "But that doesn’t make sense," He told it. "You know what I’m opening up here by remembering all of this shit. You know what it’s going to turn me into. I can‘t believe that fits into whatever the hell you, or Mary, wanted me to be." It nodded, it’s smile gone now. "Yer right, kid, I don’t like it, but it seems ta me yer only option ta do what ya gotta do. I’ll be here ta make sure ya don‘t go too far over tha edge." "You‘re going to make sure I don‘t go over the edge?" McKail questioned it. "You’re going to see me follow all of this through? How?" McKail grinned, almost manically. "When my head has fully healed you, as a hallucination brought on by the head trauma, will be gone." "Hallucination?" It grinned and, rather bizarrely, slowly began to fade away. "Whoever said I was a hallucination?" McKail’s eyes widened. "I’d just assumed…" He trailed off, finding the statement irrelevant now. Despite his bewilderment, his mind quickly refocused, "What the hell are you then?" It continued to grin, but remained silent as it slowly faded away into nothingness. "Fuck!" McKail exclaimed, kicking an empty plastic bottle angrily. If not a hallucination, then what in the hell was it? McKail considered, allowing the curiosity to get the better of him. Is this another one of it’s tricks? Or does my hallucination just have delusions of grandeur? The latter option seemed the most likely to McKail. Or is it just the option I’m most comfortable with? "Please calm down, sir," the man’s voice ordered. It was a calm voice, but had an underlying sense of sternness that threatened to become apparent, if McKail didn’t do precisely what he told him to. McKail turned slowly to see a police officer stood, at a safe distance, with an open hand outstretched in front of him. What the fuck…? The guy looked like he was all of ten years old and he was as skinny as hell. Kinda looked like the guy from Road Trip -- yeah, you know the one. "It’s okay." This time it was a woman’s voice. She walked into his line of vision, before McKail could turn and glare at the perpetrator. It was the woman from earlier on, the fucking do-gooder. She’d called the police. Bitch! "This nice man’s here to help you." She spoke to McKail as though he was hard of hearing or a child and it thoroughly pissed him off. He turned to walk away and said, "Fuck you!" "Sir," the police officer began, his voice sterner than before. "Please stay where you are. I have to take you down to the precinct, to get you some help." McKail sighed and stopped in his tracks. Help? It only now dawned on him just what in the hell was going on. The woman had seen him talking with thin air and then called the police. They think I’m a lunatic! "Listen, man," McKail told the police officer. "There’s been some kind of mistake. I don’t need help." "It’s nothing to be ashamed about," the woman chimed in. "You can’t help it." "Shut the fuck up," McKail told her. He had very little patience to begin with and now he’d been tipped over the edge. Mary and the hallucination (or whatever the fuck it was) wanted him to have human interaction, well I wonder if going over to the woman and breaking her nose would be much of a start! "I wasn’t talking to you." The woman’s face filled with hurt. She’d tried to do a good thing and she was getting abuse for it. Fucking do-gooders… "It’s okay m’am." The police took a few steps closer toward McKail and became extremely animated. "There’s no call for that, sir!" "I think there is," McKail told him. "I was having an argument on my Bluetooth headset and this stupid bitch calls the cops." It was a lie, but McKail figured it was worth a shot. "I didn’t see a headset," the woman told the police officer, "and he was pointing as though someone was stood right in front of him." The police officer nodded and added, "I don’t see a headset, sir." Fuck! The police officer began to edge closer toward him, his hand already around his handcuffs. "Please turn around and place your hands behind your back." McKail considered making a run for it, for all of three seconds. Sure, he didn’t much like the idea of spending a night in the police cells or time with some fucking condescending counsellor, but what other choice did he have? If he made a run for it, the cops would catch him within the hour at the most and then he’d miss his first round match up with Andy M. for sure. However, if he cooperated, he could still talk his way into getting himself released for the event. Fuck… McKail turned and placed his hands around his back. The police officer extracted the handcuffs and cautiously edged closer. "Is there any need for that officer?" The woman questioned, a concerned expression etched into her face. "It’s for his own good," the police officer assured her. Yeah, McKail thought, sure it is. This fucker just wants to get this over with. He’s got school tomorrow. As the cold metal shackles were pinched together against his skin, he twitched and the police officer nearly jumped out of his skin. McKail grinned at this and the cop attempted to save face by dragging McKail forcibly toward his squad car. However, McKail, outweighing the moron by at least a hundred pounds, remained still and the cop nearly tore his arm out of the damned socket. The police officer gave McKail a dirty look and fingered his nightstick, presumably attempting to intimidate him, but it really didn’t work. "Rodney King!" A passer by yelled, producing a chuckle from his friend. "Rodney King!" McKail couldn’t help contain his grin. "Come along, sir," the police officer said, giving up at feebly attempting to be intimidating. McKail sighed and headed toward the squad card. He glared at the woman as he walked by and shook his head. He realised she wasn’t to blame, not really, but just revelled in making her feel guilty. He knew who was really responsible for this. "That fucking hallucination," McKail mumbled. "What was that?" The police man asked. "Nothing," McKail told him. "Nothing at all."
