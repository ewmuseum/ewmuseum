Vengeance Saga (Session 1)

30 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-04-30 "I…" The pale-faced angel who fought like a demon, Shinsuke Sakura would be beautiful if she wasn't so quiet and had a better disposition. She, along with her sister Miyoko (who was in fact the primary wrestler out of the two of them) were collectively known within DRWF as the "Lebsian Jap-Cunts". It all started with Miyoko's singles opponents alleging it about her, but at the time it was no secret that she was in a relationship with one Ashley Riot. With Riot's absence, though, Miyoko brought in Sakura to tag with her, and the rumors started that they were having an affair with each other. This going on, and nobody actually doubted that they were siblings. "I don't… think we should date….." Only one person outside of Yamada Kenji's American branch of the Yakuza had seen Sakura's unpainted face since she came to America. Silvio Fiore. People considered it very odd that she would find any interest in Fiore at all when they were first noticed together as a couple in Death Row Wrestling Federation after Death & Resurrection, DRWF's pay-per-view on March 13th, 2005. In the weeks before, it was at least clear that she was taking some sort of odd fascination with the kid, but when they walked into the show separately nobody had any reason to think anything of it. However, that night, Miyoko caught Sakura and Silvio talking. Miyoko immediately ordered Sakura to attack the teen rather than to socialize. Miyoko hates Americans. Doesn't mind America, and loves U.S. money, but Americans are generally reviled by the younger Shinsuke sister. It is understandable, if one considers that Miyoko's only real conversational exposure to Americans has been through the promos put out against her by prospective opponents. They have all been crude and boorish in addressing her, displaying neither imagination nor intelligence. The wrestlers were representative of the population, and so she treated them all the same. Which was a shame because Silvio would have been a breath of fresh air if she'd tried to get to know him. He felt the same way -- that the wrestlers in DRWF were too much of the same mentality. They all treated wrestling like a high school lunch break spent passing insults back and forth with kids that one didn't like. The level of maturity, for him, left something to be desired. Fiore considered it an even greater shame that Miyoko, because he came up in rural Kansas. Home-schooled until tenth grade, he had books in the attic to read, and he read voraciously. Eventually there was nothing else in his house to read, and so he would take monthly trips to the library just to read whatever they had. It was in this way that a quiet country boy came to be more educated for his age than even many college undergrad seniors. Sakura had taken to this quality in the sixteen-year-old Silvio (to whom she had eight years in advance). His maturity, unattempted charm, and his desire to get to know people regardless of the defense mechanisms that they put up made her take to him. It then became that the heartbroken Japanese woman who joined Miyoko in the "enforcer" trade because her husband left her, the woman who was so dejected from the world and from happiness that she never spoke more than four or five words at a time, began to liven up. She chose Silvio, got happier, let more of her old personality shine through. Miyoko still didn't trust Silvio, but for the kind of emotions that he brought out of Sakura, she didn't mind him being around her in the short term. She'd eventually get past him, move on to a man her own age. "Anymore…." For about a month, it was all good. Sakura was happy around Silvio, Fiore enjoyed the company, and Miyoko knew that it would end eventually. And she was right. For while Sakura liked Silvio, on the inside, she had been changed by her life experience before meeting him. So she had become a bit of a dark personality herself. She wanted Silvio to join her there, rather than to revert to her old self. It was first evidenced, quite clearly, by their first date (which Silvio only realized was a date when Sakura said that it was so halfway through the night). She took him to an underground fighting club, where he watched her kill a man in a gladiatorial contest, enjoying every moment of it. Freaky for him, cathartic for her. She tried to make a more subtle influence on him after that, but it wasn't working. Fiore's character was clearly defined. She couldn't alter him. Silvio himself was surprised when he heard these words from Sakura, telling him while at the end of a picnic at the University of Mississippi grove that she didn't want to be involved with him. But what was interesting, and what he couldn't know, was that her excuse for breaking up with him was the opposite of what she really wanted out of the situation. If she actually did want to bring him to the dark side, what she said was closer to this. "I think…. I think that I'll corrupt you…. I don't want that….. You're perfect the way you are……" So on that cool Spring day, a new chapter would start in what would be a continuous on-off relationship between the two. They promised to be friends after that, which was easy for Silvio because he was quite the Taoist and because Sakura was shortly thereafter taken off of the Death Row Wrestling Federation roster because they were killing the tag team division (which was the only way she was going to wrestle). They could remain friendly over the phone, or exchanging e-mails. It was easy. But what they didn't realize was that it was their promise of friendship that would end up being the worst thing that they could have done. Sometimes it is better to go on one's separate ways, but they wanted to be together without being together. People can hurt themselves so much more by trying to avoid it. This is one of those times. This is one of those stories. Session One -- 2005 I'd noticed that Sakura was reverting back to her old ways. Sure, I rather liked the more expressive version of my sister, but if it meant that she kicked that Silvio brat to the curb, I could accept that she became less talkative. Besides, better that she cut him loose than for him to do just the same as Sakura's husband and up and leave her. She'd suffered one heartbreak in the past, which was one more than anyone should ever go through. I know myself what it's like to be deserted by the one you love. My romantic relationship with Ashley Riot turned violent when she vanished, leaving me so distraught that I briefly sought solace in the arms of my boss, Yamada Kenji. Not that I knew what I was doing, I wasn't thinking straight at the time. But when Ashley came back from the abyss, she had heard of my ephemeral fling with Kenji (we were a part of the same basic underworld ring) and wanted to get her revenge on me. Rather than letting me try to make amends, she came after me with a vengeance. I got so crazy that I actually shot at her with a handgun, something that I'd never consider using if for no other reason than that I'm proficient in other deadly weapons and prefer the hands-on touch. So, knowing what kind of madness heartache can put you through, I didn't want Sakura to go through it again. The first time it happened, the cheerful and loving sister of mine who loved to spend her time in the Shinsuke family gardens and painting nature and landscapes became a merciless killer. She became me, and I wouldn't ever wish that upon her. A second heartache… who knew what she'd do. Still, even if they split, they kept in contact. Sakura's a grown woman, my older sister at that, so I couldn't command her not to correspond with Silvio. I still wished that she didn't, because she was setting herself up to get hurt in the future. And if there was ever a time that I wanted to be wrong, that I hated being dead-on in my prediction, it was then. But let me explain a little clearer. I'll start at the beginning, at least the beginning of their "friendship" after dating. Sakura wasn't a part of DRWF anymore after Death & Resurrection. That pay-per-view saw Ashley Riot return from her disappearing act, pairing with emo-goth Julian Jenkins to win the Death Row tag titles from me and my sister. Jenkins was also the FTW champion (secondary singles belt), and did nothing with that belt save for one title defense. But he disappeared, ended up being fired and the title vacated. And Ashley also disappeared shortly after that. So gone were the tag champs. But rather than just putting Sakura and myself back in line to take the titles again, the genius-leader of the federation, Chase Madrid, decided to suspend the tag division altogether. Well, Sakura's contract stated that she'd only wrestle for DRWF as a tag team with me, so that meant that she was ineligible to wrestle there anymore. What it meant that there was a lot of time that Sakura had to just sit around at wherever we were staying. A lot of free time and nothing to do with it. No need to train for matches that wouldn't come, but I still had to travel for the shows. So what did the girl who had only one friend other than me in the U.S. do? She called him. She continued to spend time with him. I figured that Silvio was trying to work his way back into her mind (he never did get around to a sexual relationship with her, but I'm sure that he wanted that as well), so I wasn't surprise when he obliged her with his company. He had free time as well. Since capturing the Cutting Edge title at D&R, he'd gotten some championship perks. Namely, instead of having to drive a beat-up rusted 1979 Plymouth Reliant to all the shows, he got a private jet on the company expense account. So there was a little while where she just went on a vacation with him, flying around the country. She could do that partly because I was busy with my own problems, which in hindsight I kick myself for because it meant that I wasn't there to help her with her issues. But even more than that Sakura and I had recently gotten ourselves… I'll use the word "emancipated" from Kenji's service. We were upgraded to freelancers, free to take whatever jobs we wanted. Fiore used the word "peculium" or something to refer to what we'd done (apparently back in ancient Greece, it was a fund that slaves could set aside from their service that would serve for self-manumission). I always hated that about the kid, using obscure words and generally going out of his way to sound smarter than everyone else when nobody was impressed. As for Silvio as a wrestler, I have to admit,. He was improving by leaps and bounds every week. I remember that his first match was a tag match that he only won because of his partner getting the win. His very next match? A singles outing in which he was knocked out with a superkick and pinned 18 seconds into the match. He got off to a real slow start in wrestling. I couldn't believe it when I had to wrestle him, he didn't put up any kind of fight. But what we didn't realize was that he was actually taking a very measured effort to restrict what he did. We were all lulled into a false sense of security by branding him as the resident "enhancement talent" (the talent part used very generously). He was on the active roster even though he hadn't completed his training, started in January of 2005 after only a month of training at some place called Spartan Regimen Facility in Kansas City, MO. Silvio was slick. He figured that he could spare some early losses, because eventually a title shot would come his way despite a losing record. And it did. He came into a #1 contender's match to the Cutting Edge title (tertiary title belt) against a failed-thug named Roman Downfall. Silvio won that match, and was guaranteed a pay-per-view match against Jason Dunn, then the CE champion and quite the opponent in his own right (to this day, I haven't been able to beat Dunn, though eventually he fell off the radar so I haven't had the chance to get back at him). Downfall was a terrible wrestler, so nobody cheered Silvio for what he did in that match. And there was still a month's woth of bookings that he clumsily ambled through, losing matches left and right save for one desperation roll-up to pin running joke Shawn "No Talk, No Action" Jaxson. But when it came time for that Cutting Edge title shot, Silvio exploded with a workrate, energy, and tenacity that no one had seen before. He fought hard against a presumed unbeatable Cutting Edge champion, displaying a flair for reversing and escaping moves in ways that hadn't been done before. Not to mention that he invented multiple moves in that match alone. That set a precedent that would become Fiore's trademark early in his career. He never used the same move twice, and whatever he did use was something that nobody had ever seen before. In everything that he did, he was an innovator. He was an innovator in the initiative that he took to line up challengers for the CE title and defend against them. He was an innovator in the way that he viewed wrestling, and himself within the sport. He was an innovator in how he could never be predictable in the ring, but at the same time he was very easy to read because he was so open and accessible and had a strong moral code of honor and principle. Or so everyone thought. And I'll admit, as time passed, even I thought that Silvio was the real deal: athleticism, a brain, and a humble sense of himself to boot. But it would all turn out to be an act. He was slowly changing, and I was the only one to realize it. It started first with Demonology, Death Row's April pay-per-view (they ran one every six weeks). That was Silvio's first PPV defense of his championship, which he did successfully. After that, the quiet, reserved kid from Kansas began to get cocky. Started to be more liberal about telling everyone how great he was. Before that, he was always the one to say, "Yeah, I could lose this match." Then it became, "No way I'm losing this match." And after a while any derivative of the word "loss" was purged from his unnecessarily large vocabulary. At the same time, he was starting to get advertisers looking at him for sponsorship and endorsements. And, of course, there was the PTC Cruiserweight Tournament. Excuse me, Second Annual. I didn't even know of it, but Silvio just up and told us one day, "Oh yeah, I'm in a tournament." We were like, "What tournament? Projekt Xero's [our interfederation alliance with seven other wrestling markets] tournament at Rejekted is already set up in the brackets and you're not on the list." He said, "No, no, I'm signed up to a different one. It's run by Primetime Central." "Who?" we all quizzically exclaimed because, contrary to whatever that PTC place thought, there was a good chunk of the wrestling market that had no idea that they existed. I could agree with Silvio on that much, every federation in America tried to put itself over as having tens of millions of viewers when it was impossible that everybody could be a national place with exactly the same audience. Logistically impossible. Fiore explained to us, "It's an interfed like Projekt Xero, just not PX. They're outside of our circle of acknowledgement, lets' say. Anyway, they held a Cruiserweight Tournament last year that was pretty well received by independent observers." The reaction across the board was the same. "So?" Of course, in trying to make us care that he wanted to go off and wrestle on his own time, something that we didn't care one bit about, he said, "Well, they're holding another tournament. I've gone ahead and applied to be a part of it, and they accepted me. So I'm going to represent DRWF for this." There was British technical wrestling guru Pete Ebdon, fellow Brit Lucas Knight, Montreal native and 2pac clone Aaron Kalis, and DRWF interviewer and poster-child for the legalization of marijuana Johnny Ganja Seed. They, along with Silvio and I, were sitting at the catering table that day, having dinner before the start of the televised Black Sunday show on April 10th, 2005. The reaction from everyone was the same. "Whatever." Still, that was part of Silvio's change. And if would just get worse. I could have struck him down early and saved my sister the worst hurt of her life. But I couldn't see Silvio as anything more than an annoyance when he would turn out to be the worst person she'd ever met. And it would be another two years before I'd completely get the clue how despicable and evil that he really was. Of course, by then, it was too late. For Sakura.
