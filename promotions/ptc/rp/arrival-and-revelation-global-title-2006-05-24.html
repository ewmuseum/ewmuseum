Arrival And Revelation (Global Title)

24 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Spoiler tags is used for the purpose of helping you not read ahead until you hit the audio link, as the audio serves as the bridge between the story elements. The Vice President of Those Tournament Things crashed through the door to the men’s room., leaving a hole in the door shaped almost exactly like his silhouette. He very mechanically cracked his neck from side to side, smooth out the bits of door that clung to his suit, and stepped up to the mirror in the executive washroom. He took a in a deep breath, held it, and let it out. Then he punched the mirror, creating a splendid shattering of glass with lots of fun tinkling noises as bits of mirror bounced off of the tiles. He wasn’t happy. As a Wrestling Tournament Elemental, he didn’t imagine he could continue existing if PTC didn’t hold a tournament. He’d start to fade, lose his physical reality. Or maybe worse, on the night when GTT should be, he’d perhaps “pop” and discorporate. A desperate thought occurred to him. When was the summer Olympics? Would that be enough to hold him over? No, probably not, unless he managed to get them to include the Greco Roman style in addition to Olympic Freestyle, but could that be arranged in time? “Oh holy shitfuck,” said the Vice President of Those Tournament Things, “I'm doomed.” He sat on the floor, “That god damn moron of an Uber Judge. He’s positively destroyed me and has no idea. He just thinks that this is a nasty little act of vengeance in our rivalry. If he knew …” The Wrestling Tournament Elemental leapt to his feet, “That’s it! The Uber Judge isn’t a hater, and he wouldn’t deliberately destroy me, would he? I’ll just need to explain!” The Vice President of Those Tournament Things crashed through the door a second time, plowed through the crowd of useless executives and back into the conference room, again crashing through the door. “Where’s the – uh,” said the Vice President of Those Tournament Things, the wind leaving his sails. The conference room window was broken. One executive helpfully explained, “That damn Uber Judge mumbled something about the Hudsucker Proxy and crashed through the window!” “Not a bad plan,” said the VP of TTT, who leapt onto the table and dove out the window himself. He was in no mood to wait for an elevator. On the way down, he had some time to think, as when you’re falling from a great height, there isn’t much to do but wait. The Uber Judge probably couldn’t survive the fall, but he, as an Elemental, would probably only damage his suit. It occurred to him that if the Uber Judge didn’t survive the fall, he wouldn’t have a problem anymore, as there would be no one to spearhead the movement not to have GTT6 next year. So murder was an option too if it came to that. He looked around for the Uber Judge and saw that his rival was still falling. The VP of TTT tucked his arms in for lesser air resistance to increase his velocity, hoping to catch up to the UJ. But the UJ, who was falling slower with his arms outstretched and his billowing robes flapping violently, had fallen much further and was only seconds from the ground. The Wrestling Tournament Elemental watched in awe as the Uber Judge pulled what looked like a ripcord, and the flowing robes suddenly filled with air, causing the Uber Judge to hit the ground with a noisy bounce. The Elemental simply crashed into a bus with a load crushing noise a few seconds later. By the time the Uber Judge had deflated his robes, the VP of TTT had pried himself out of the bus and straightened his suit. “So, Uber Judge,” said the Elemental, “you intend to destroy me, do you well… wait. Wait one minute.” “What?” asked the Uber Judge. “You made the bouncy ball thing with your suit, cool, but you didn’t reduce your velocity at all while making contact with the ground, which means that your internal organs just got torq’d enough to be crushed. You’re dead. That fall just killed you.” “No, it didn’t,” said the Uber Judge. “It did too. You’re dead. There’s no internal stabilizing mechanisms there. Further it’s CLOTH ROBES you inflated, which I doubt are airtight, flexible enough to expant to the big round shape, and strong enough not to tear on impact. That’s just silly.” “Look buster,” said the Uber Judge, “My last storyline was about me taking over the world and it would’ve worked too if I’d beaten Paddy O’Shea in GTT5-” “Oh, of course it would’ve worked, if it wasn’t for those damn kids,” interrupted the VP of TTT who couldn’t resist. “Hey, the point is, that in the interests of entertaining folks, we embrace implausible things occasionally because they are fun if completely unrealistic. Like your precise nature as a character in this narrative? How real is a freaking Elemental?” “Whoa, buddy,” said the Elemental, “Did you look at advanced copies of the script? Because part of our whole damned storyline is that you don’t know that I'm an Elemental, and in your stupid happy go lucky way, you’re going to destroy me, the urgency of which you fail to grasp because you’re so damn dense.” “I am not dense,” said the UJ, “I'm the most perceptive man alive! I just let certain facts go for the purposes of making my character interesting. If I just declared I knew everything all along how fun would that be for the audience, huh? Boring! So I make mistakes, it creates a dynamic of realism and inspires the imagination at the same time.” “Oh, so you’re so smart, huh? How would this story have gone then if you weren’t dumbing yourself down?” demanded the VP of TTT. “Oh like Primetime Central is ever not going to do GTT. It’s tradition now, they’ll always do it. People don’t want innovation, they want comfort and routine. It’ll get scaled back and bit, and it’ll be boring sometimes, but it’s what the audience wants and there’s no way they’d accept a replacement.” Both the Uber Judge and the Vice President of Those Tournament Thingies stop for a moment to stare directly at you. “Even if it was something better?” asked the Wrestling Tournament Elemental without taking his eyes off you. “Even. Then,” said the Uber Judge in his most stern voice, giving you a snarl before turning back to his Elemental Rival. “Allright,” said the VP of TTT, “We got carried away there. Where do we pick this back up?” “Doesn’t matter,” said the UJ, “Even with this disaster of non-character driven discussion in the middle of our storyline, things still make more sense than any storylines they can expect to see on most televised wrestling shows. Skip right to the end.” “The end?” asked the WTE, “The payoff?” “No, no no nono,” corrected the Uber Judge, “Not the PAYOFF, just the end of this part.” “Oh, okay. Wait a moment, let me center here. Breath. Ready. Go.” The Vice President of Those Tournament Things cut a dramatic pose and shouted, “There’s only one thing to do! If the Uber Judge won’t listen to reason, then I’ll be forced to make absolutely certain he defeats Rich Rollins and becomes the NEW GLOBAL CHAMPION, which will prevent him from becoming a full time PTC Administrator because of the conflict of interest which has shown through precedent to be a poor business decision taken up by our competitors!”
