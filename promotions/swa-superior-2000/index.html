Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Superior Wrestling Alliance opened in May 2000 with a World Title Tournament and a homestead address, taking real and created wrestlers alike. By the following spring it was posting as Superior Championship Wrestling from the same address — a promotion that had, in its own words, been around for over a year in two other forms — and its announcers still spoke of the SWA as the place where its oldest stars began and its Television title was first held.

Its weekly show was Fusion, a Friday-night card called at ringside by John Melcher and his heel partner Spike McNeil, with SCW Magazine setting the next week's line-up and, from August 2001, a studio roleplay show called Unplugged. Results were decided by the executive committee's vote on the wrestlers' roleplays. “If you don't roleplay, you will lose!” its own rules warned.

The fall of Maverick runs through its 2001 broadcasts. The longest-reigning World Champion in the promotion's history lost the title in June to Viper, the partner who had turned on him with a lead pipe, and was led from the arena by men in white coats. He resurfaced in August on the big screen, discharged from a mental hospital, and sent the Five Star Psycho Dan Debacle after the new champion. By November Mike Powers held the title, and Maverick had thrown in with his old enemy, the Cash Money Brothers' “Heavy Metal” Van Hammer.

That autumn Fusion toured college arenas from Seattle to Chapel Hill, postponed a week after the attacks of 11 September 2001, and opened a women's division of four wrestlers ahead of Holiday Havok in Albany at the end of November. On 22 December 2001 the promotion was still recruiting writers and asking for new logos for Fusion, Eclipse, Unplugged and Magazine.
