Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Ohio State Wrestling opened its doors in August 2000 as a brand-new regional promotion based in Steubenville, Ohio, announcing itself with a founding document that promised original wrestlers, a closed booking system, and a single weekly broadcast to start: Sunday Buckeye. The promotion styled itself as a small, hometown operation with big ambitions, hoping in time to grow from a single-city outfit into something touring far beyond the Ohio Valley.

Its debut broadcast opened with a three-match card headlined by the federation's first-ever intergender bout, a showcase billed as proof that men and women could share a ring on equal terms. The night's main event introduction was delivered in person by the promotion's own president, who welcomed the crowd to her hometown and to what she called her life-long dream, before an unexpected new arrival -- a towering, gorilla-costumed recruit -- crashed the broadcast and stayed on as a regular presence in the weeks that followed.

A second card was announced for the following week, moving the action to a nearby football stadium and carrying several of the debut show's storylines forward, including a rematch angle and a newcomer stepping in against an established face of the roster. Newsgroup readers wrote in with a handful of practical questions in the promotion's opening weeks -- about a possible website, its size, and whether it welcomed only original characters -- each one answered personally and promptly.

No further record of Ohio State Wrestling survives beyond its second week. Whether the promotion ran additional cards, changed hands, or simply stopped posting is not recorded here.
