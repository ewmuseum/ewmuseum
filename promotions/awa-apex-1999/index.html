A weekly Usenet e-federation that opened with a mid-broadcast sale of the promotion and closed out its earliest served weeks with three of its five championships changing hands. Twenty-eight wrestlers are recorded across three served broadcasts. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Apex Wrestling Association's earliest surviving broadcast, New Year's Jam (17 January 1999), staged an outdoor arena built over a pool at Caesar's Palace in Las Vegas, the ring reachable only by a guarded footbridge: “if you end up in the water, you automatically lose, unless you were thrown in, and then you win.”

Mid-broadcast, its outgoing president, Dan Rose, announced the company sold out from under its own fans: “This company has bled me dry and I am getting the hell out of it... you can kiss my ass!” Its new owner, Rich Armani, revealed himself at the close of the same show and made his first moves on air — firing sitting commissioner Luc Stevens (offering him a wrestling contract instead) and renaming the Iron Man Championship the Hard Knocks Championship.

The same broadcast's main event crowned a new World Heavyweight Champion, Talon James Trent, when Phoenix X turned on his own ally to cost Tiny Tayshaun Walker the match.

Armani's first show under his own ownership, Armani Funktacular (18 January), installed a new broadcast team — commissioner Trent Richardson, announcer Teddy Rimino, and a third commentator, Benz Higdon — in one stroke. By its next surviving card, Wednesday Night Warfare (10 February), a further title had changed hands, crowning Dark Hawk Television Champion, and the promotion's own namesake Apex Championship had already changed hands twice since New Year's Jam.

Its own record attests continuous weekly activity into late March 1999; nothing dated later survives.
