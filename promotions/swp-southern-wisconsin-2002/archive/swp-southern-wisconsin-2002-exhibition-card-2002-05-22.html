[A crack of light breaks through blackness and a door opens from across a rugged gym floor. The pale fluorescent streetlights from outside the old hall shed an illuminated aisle to a dusty, busted-up wrestling ring lying dormant amidst piles of folding chairs and old mats and scraps of paper and popcorn boxes. From the doorway, a thin, well-dressed, broad-shouldered man with salt-and-pepper hair walks to the ring and lovingly runs a hand across the bottom rope. He pushes a few old popcorn boxes from the metal ring steps and slowly climbs into the ring. Once there, he holds his arms out and slowly spins around, taking in the mezzanine that circles all the way around the huge square building, the rising bleachers underneath the mezzanines, and the folding chairs scattered about behind the partition at ringside. He holds his arms out to his side and takes a deep breath of the dank air.
From the doorway, a clatter is heard. A short, fat, balding man in a dirty shirt and filthy khakis, laden with briefcases and papers stumbles in, dropping items left and right. He is sweating profusely and breathing heavily. At the ring edge, he addresses the man in the ring.]
KEELER: "Mr. Sandstrom, are you sure this wrestling business is something you want to do? I mean, you already have more than enough money. This seems like it's just a waste of time."
[Sandstrom stops his slow spin and looks down at Keeler. He shakes his head and then, with the grace of a cat, he takes two steps and leaps over the top rope, landing lightly next to his assistant.]
SANDSTROM: "Do you smell it, Stanley? Do you smell it?" KEELER: "Smell what? Mold? Yes, yes I do." SANDSTROM: "No, my fine fellow. The history. Do you smell the history?" KEELER: "No. I smell dust. I smell mold. And, I think I smell something dead. I know raccoons could get in here through the ventilation shafts."
SANDSTROM: "I used to come here, to the Madison Masonic Hall when I was boy so many decades ago. My friends and I used to sneak in to watch the weekly wrestling shows. Guys like 'Gorgeous Mike Garvey' and Stan 'The Slam'
Mercusiak would hammer on each other like gods at war until one walked away victorious. Then, we would hang out at the stage doors for hours in a freezing Wisconsin winter storm just to get an autograph and get that close to greatness. It was an amazing feeling.
"I want to keep that spirit alive, Stanley. I want to bring this hall back to greatness. I want to make new men as great as Garvey and Stan 'The Slam.
' I want to build up this dank, dustbox and repair it back to it's former greatness. I want to hear these bleachers rock with the shouts of fans again. I want to see people pack this place again and dream of being great.

SANDSTROM: "That's what I like about you Stanley. You're a real class bootlicker. Did you bring the sheets?"
KEELER [handing Sandstrom a stack of dossiers]: "Right here, sir. They're some of the best I could find on short notice. I am still hoping to get call backs from Mr. Goldberg or Mr. Severn, or Mr. Shamrock--"
SANDSTROM [cutting him off]: "Never mind them, Stanley. They're not what I want. I want truly great men. Men who love this game. Men who will appreciate the history in this building. Men who know what this is all about. Those men are whores for cameras. Get me people I don't know."
[Glancing through the dossiers] "Men like these guys. These men, I can make great." [The dossiers show 3"x5" photographs of some of the signed talent along with their names. The names are too blurry to read, but the pictures show a variety of faces: a scrub-faced, messy-haired Madison area resident; a brash-looking young blond with a spiked mane; a tough looking black man from the Chicago area with a knit cap pulled low over his eyes and a gold tooth glinting from his sneer; a broad-faced cowboy with a good-natured grin; a thin, black man in a cape and a pointed wizard-style hat. A wild-looking man with brilliant green hair and a face painted like a kabuki theater actor. Sandstrom shuffles through the dossiers lovingly, a broad smile grows on his face.]
SANDSTROM: "These men. They will carry the name of Southern Wisconsin Pro, they will enter this ring, and they will be my new gladiators. Do you hear the cheers? Do you hear the promise?"
KEELER: "I hear my sinuses clogging. This dust is murder on my allergies." SANDSTROM: "Tough it up, Keeler. It'll be cleaned up. I want this whole arena cleaned from top to bottom. Spare no expense. I want the ring enlarged. I want new ropes, a new apron, and new mats. Get the best you can get. Money's no option. I spent thirty damn years making fortune, I'll be damned if I die before I can spend the damn money. Get new bleachers, new seats in the balcony there, and get new platforms for the cameras."
KEELER: "Cameras?" SANDSTROM: "I bought time on some cable network. Actually, I bought the damn network. We'll broadcast this thing. I want the whole world to see how great we can be. This is what I'm thinking: Weekly shows will be here, at the Masonic Lodge. The big shows, the pay-per-views will be broadcast all over the Midwest. Start booking me some events. This thing is going to be huge, Stanley."
KEELER: "Yes, Mr. Sandstrom. Right away, sir!" [Keeler scuttles off like a crab, trailing papers behind him. Sandstrom looks back at the ring with reverence, then slowly backs out of the arena, his smile and the faraway, dreamy look in his eye brightening with every step. The door closes and plunges the arena into blackness.]
