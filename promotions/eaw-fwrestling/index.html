A small Australian fantasy-wrestling promotion that ran six numbered Outback Thunder cards and a title tournament across five months in 2008, closing on an unresolved cage-match draw and a still-open shooting angle. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Eastern Australian Wrestling opened at eaw.fwrestling.com in February 2008, signing its first wrestler — Orlando, Florida high flyer Aaron Abercrombie — on the 21st, under an owner figure, Scott Carmichael, who fronted its own newsdesk throughout the fed's life. Its application form drew a clean, fantasy-only line at the door: “We are an e-fed that does not accept applications with real wrestlers. We will only accept applications with original wrestling characters.” A roster built through February and March 2008 — John “Mad Dog” Murphy, Santino Romano, Jack Star, Bryan Willett, Dollar, and others — toward an eight-man tournament for a championship of its own.

Its own numbered Outback Thunder series ran from 20 March 2008, with a title tournament at EAW Mayhem on 1 May crowning Murphy the promotion's first Australian National Champion in a Four Corners 30-Minute Iron Man Match. Six Outback Thunder cards toured Australian state capitals — Sydney, Hobart, Newcastle, Brisbane — before a seventh, announced for 17 July, whose own results were never captured. The fullest surviving card, the sixth Outback Thunder on 3 July, closed on a Lights Out cage match between Murphy and Bryan Willett that went to a double ten-count with neither man moving, and a Detention Centre Match won by the returning strongman Dollar.

Alongside its cards, the fed staged a season-long feud between Dollar and an absent Santino Romano over a reported shooting of the Romano brothers — disputed in its own newsdesk down to a planted police witness corroborating it — and named a sister promotion, Ricochet Pro Wrestling, sharing at least one crossover wrestler and a shared regional alliance. It belonged briefly to an alliance it called the WeWA, withdrawing from it of its own accord in the spring of 2008 and moving its own message boards in the same act.

How Eastern Australian Wrestling itself ended isn't in the surviving record. Its last dated content is the 17 July 2008 Outback Thunder announcement; the site persisted unchanged online through further captures into 2010, with nothing posted after it.
