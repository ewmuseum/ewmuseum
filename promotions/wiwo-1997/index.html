Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The World Internet Wrestling Organization's earliest surviving document, an 11 September 1997 recruitment post from its founding president, called it a young league with “some of the best roleplaying I've ever seen” from a president who said he'd been in plenty of leagues to compare it to.

By 18 November 1997 a different president had taken the promotion over “since no one else wanted to,” keeping every sitting champion in place: Adam Kuhl held the WIWO World Title, Dennis Windham the North American Title, Wardancer the Television Title, and the Blood Brothers (Slayton and Chavez) the World Tag Titles. The same announcement dropped two further title lines, the NAWF and TNT/OCC championships, as no longer the WIWO's own “for the time being,” and its new president called the promotion, without qualification, “by far, the greatest Efed in the world.”

That claim didn't hold. By outside account the WIWO had folded roughly three weeks before 18 December 1997, though no closing document of its own survives.

The same president who had taken it over in 1997 revived it in February 1999, stating plainly that its founder “became somewhat bored with the WIWO and shut it down” and that he was “bringing it back,” with limited roster space for the relaunch and an open invitation to wrestlers from a folding rival fed, the INWI, he had competed in himself.

A 9 March 1999 bulletin found it running again in earnest: Scott McGregor held the WIWO Championship, “Kamakazi” Jack Atari the United States Championship, the New Scottish Brawlers the Tag Team Championship, and the Television Championship sat vacant after a match its own bulletin called an instant classic — Lance Nelson and Patient X ran out of broadcast time just as Patient X was pinning Nelson, sending both men into separate no-time-limit rematches the following two weeks, with the winners set to meet at the WIWO's first pay-per-view, Scotland Yard Massacre. No document survives after that bulletin.
