An e-mail federation that crowned its first champions in a June 1992 tournament, ran a full summer of title defenses and SuperCards, and then put all three belts against the FWF's in a Clash of the Masters, taking the tag titles and the world title split between them. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Pro Wrestling Alliance crowned all three of its championships at once. In early June 1992 it ran tournaments for the World Heavyweight, United States and Tag Team belts, and the singles final was built so that the loser did not go home empty-handed: the winner took the World title and the loser took the United States title. That match was the Volcano, billed at over six hundred pounds, against the Unlimited Warlord, who whipped him into the ropes on raw strength and had the commentary wondering aloud how anyone could get an arm around his neck. The Volcano won.

It ran through the summer while most of the newsgroup was away. The e-wrestling newsdesk came back in September to say so — “while you, and some of us, were gone, e-wrestling kept right on through the summer” — and rolled the tape on that final for anyone who had missed it, card by card, the rest of the way.

The World title changed hands three more times before the summer was out. “Studly” Scott Brikett took it off the Volcano in a match the record never shows, then lost it clean to Zangief the Terrible with a small package. Zangief held it through a SuperCard and a run-in with the Sneaky One before Gentleman Jim Gillette beat him with a figure-four leg lock, his own Wild Stallions squared off against Zangief's Towers of Power at ringside. The Volcano himself wrestled once more, at the August SuperCard, beating the tag team Pretty in Pink badly enough to end both their careers before retiring for good.

The tag and United States belts moved just as often. Da Boyz, the tournament's own inaugural Tag Team Champions, held the straps through a string of contested finishes before the Dogs of War took them in an upset. The United States title passed from the Unlimited Warlord to Kendo “the Katana” Masamune to the Sneaky One, who was still champion when the record runs out. Jack Casanova spent the summer in a running feud with Brikett and Abd Al-Hazrd, the Arabian Knight — a lead pipe hidden in a bouquet of roses, and a lost Loser Wears a Dress match — while Venus, unable to get the Alliance to recognize a women's title of its own, beat Molly MaGuire and simply declared herself champion.

Its people were ordinary handlers keeping ordinary records. One of them, arguing about a signature file in August 1992, broke off to note how badly he was doing: “7-14-1 I believe with Volcano”. That line is the only document of the federation's own that survives.

Its last recorded act was a summit. The Alliance put its World, United States and Tag Team championships against the FWF's at a Clash of the Masters, and the results split three ways: Jack Casanova beat the other federation's United States Champion by countout, Crime & Punishment took the tag titles on a fall the referee called with a foot on the bottom rope, and Jim Gillette lost the world title to a backroll press and a handful of tights. The two world championships were carried together after it, and the man who beat him was billed “PWA/FWF World Heavyweight Champion”.
