Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Maple Leaf Wrestling relaunched in mid-May 1997 on the record of a first run its own operator, “The Titan” Peter Bell, said had folded the previous December after wrestlers began no-showing scheduled cards. Its own rules documents pitched a regional, Canadian, booked federation built on old NWA lines — little or no gimmick wrestling, results decided by roleplay quality rather than volume — under a new motto answering the old one directly: “MLW. We're back and this time we're here to stay!”

It structured itself around a weekly card, Tuesday Night Impact!, paired with a news-and-rumour show, MLW Wrestle Zone, hosted by Shawn Douglas; four championships (the Northern Heavyweight, the Provincial, the Northern Tag Team, and an Iron Man title that changed hands only once a year, at its own Yukon Eric Memorial Cup tournament); and six named pay-per-views strung through the calendar, from that February cup through the Great Canadian Bash on Canada's own birthday to a December “Northern Exposure” card pitched as the promotion's one showcase for the rest of the world.

Its own preview broadcasts introduced the roster meant to fill that calendar: “No Worries” Rob Magnum, “Yukon” Jack Roberts, Rick Wright, “The Real Deal” Steve Studd, Dan Watterson, the tag team Overkill and Demolisher billed as former NICW tag champions, and a debuting rookie pair, Mark Knight and Sean Judge, wrestling together as Judgment Night. Before any of them had wrestled a single card under the relaunch, a public campaign accused Bell of playing favorites with his own friends; his own newsgroup community answered on the fed's behalf, one member calling the attacks “UNFOUNDED” and pointing out that not one card had even run yet to judge.

No card of its own ever posts a completed result in this record. Tuesday Night Impact! is previewed match by match, twice, but no script of the broadcast itself survives, and MLW Wrestle Zone's second episode survives only as a fragment of newsgroup crossfire rather than the broadcast itself.

Seven months later, in a third-party interview published that December, Bell spoke of the promotion in the past tense: “I ran a short lived fed about a year ago MLW but I made mistakes there.” He was explaining, in the same breath, why he was reviving NICW, an older, already-closed federation a friend of his had once run — not a continuation of Maple Leaf Wrestling but a different promotion entirely. No closure notice of Maple Leaf Wrestling's own survives; its own last document is that Wrestle Zone fragment, five months before its own operator had already moved on.
