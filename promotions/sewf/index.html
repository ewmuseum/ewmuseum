Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The ShawWorld Enterprises Wrestling Federation's own surviving capture is its second life: its own Rules page states its operator "began a little over 4 years ago with the original SEWF," which "prospered magnificently," before "he is back to recreate that magic." This relaunch's own Grand Opening Gala — a cold open panning across dozens of returning names before Dave Shaw declares the promotion "open for business" from a venue billed the Shawplex — is the fed's own most complete surviving document, a full broadcast script rather than a results summary, running an entire tournament bracket toward a first World Heavyweight Champion.

Outcomes were decided by a stated combination of three weighted inputs — on-card interviews, RP packets, and a separately submitted written Strategy, each on its own deadline — a hybrid of judged roleplay and strat play the fed's own Rules and Deadlines pages both describe in detail. Its own roster, rankings, and title ledger survive from early in the relaunch's own run: twenty-six contracted wrestlers named, and its three championships (World Heavyweight, World United States, and World Tag Team) each still billed VACANT, awaiting the tournament the Gala had only just begun.

The tournament's own Round 2 plays out in full on the fed's own next surviving broadcast, SEWF Sunday Night Mothership, 20 August 2000: Silky Slim submits Wyatt Wallace, Burt Clifton outlasts a chair shot from Omega's own manager — a steel chair he calls "Barb" — Simon Southward blinds Higor Rosa with green mist, and Jay Lange, aided by his own manager and sister Midori, beats Jack Paine with Paine's own finisher. The same broadcast settles a grudge Blackheart names against Dave Shaw over his own tournament draw — a Pipe On A Pole match Ryan Stevens wins with a hidden assist from Southward, pulled up from beneath the ring canvas — and closes with Ray Hagan beating "Risque" Rick Wallace only to be sucker-punched by Lange. A Round 3 semifinal (Slim vs. Clifton, Lange vs. Southward) and two US Title qualifiers are announced for 27 August on a card whose own results page does not survive; Hagan's own claim, voiced at the Gala, to an unbroken World Heavyweight reign carried over from the original c. 1996 SEWF is reported here as the fed's own in-fiction claim, not receipted by any document this wing holds.

Commentary on the 16 April 2002 Surrender names Adam Mitchell, Simon Solkoff, Tommy Kain, Smoove and Tom Collins as having “kept the SEWF thriving” before this fed's own run began; Ryan Sanders and Thomas Casey's SEWF histories are independently confirmed in the same text.
