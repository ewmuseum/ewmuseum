Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The European E-Wrestling Federation opened recruitment on 19 April 1997, run out of Europe by an operator with prior handling experience across several other federations. He wanted a fully “booked” fed, voted on by a board rather than decided by dice or point totals, one wrestler or tag team per handler, and deliberately few pay-per-views rather than the genre's usual glut: “I think it's better to have few but sensational payperviews. Were longtime feuds finally get's settled.”

Its own FAQ, that May, laid out the richest kayfabe economy of its kind on record: handlers earned Deutschmarks per contract and per win, and spent them on training camps, a rumored bribable referee, charity donations to build fan support, extra flashes, or fines for breaking the rules. Original wrestlers only, no gimmicks built on real performers, and no wrestler allowed to compete for a European fed and a US one “at the same time,” by the promotion's own in-world logic. It stood as a founding member of an alliance called the GIWA, a membership staged as an on-camera storyline inside another federation's own broadcast.

Its August 1997 supercard, The Grand European, crowned three of its four championships in tournament play: Josef “The Cavalier” Tadeuscz won the European Heavyweight title in a 15-man field, The Monarchy beat the Italian Stallions in the same tournament's tag final, and “Classic” Andrew Archer held the German title.

That autumn the promotion advertised its most ambitious show yet, a four-part, week-long Münich Cup for November — a 32-man tournament, a 30-man battle royal, a 16-team tag tournament, and a 500,000-Deutschmark purse. No results document for it survives anywhere in the record.

The last surviving card, Survival of the Fittest on 16 December 1997, crowned a new European Heavyweight Champion, “Normal” Klaus Lehman, in a twelve-man battle royal that came down to a photo finish against Dean Schlotzhauer. Lehman offered Schlotzhauer a handshake once the decision was made; Schlotzhauer took it, then hit him from behind with a German suplex, pinned him with the belt itself, and locked on a Clover Leaf before vowing on camera: “THIS IS MY BELT!!!! IT WILL BE AROUND MY WAIST WITH IN A MONTH!!!!!!”

No document later than that broadcast survives. Whether Schlotzhauer made good on the threat isn't in the surviving record.
