A Usenet e-fed hosted at a geocities/Colosseum/Field address that collapsed under "Presidential Problems" and relaunched itself 9 July 1997 as "ICW: The Rebirth," one of at least twelve distinct 1997 federations sharing the bare acronym ICW. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

By the time the earliest surviving document mentions this fed, on 1 July 1997, it was already an established promotion with its own history: a tag team title held by European United, a twelve-man Survivor Series-style match won by The Falcon and Eagle, an anything-goes cage match between Steve Winters and Wolf, a brawl that spilled outside the ring between Krypt and Big Red, and a four-way that crowned Spazzz its World Champion.

By mid-1997 the fed was publicly failing. One member active in both it and a rival league forecast in the newsgroup that it “would end up a copy” of that rival and blamed “Presidential Problems” for what he called its coming collapse; another member confirmed days later that it was “starting over from scratch” under a new president.

The relaunch broadcast, on 29 July 1997 and styled “ICW: The Rebirth,” opened with a highlight reel of that same prior run before laying out a new three-title structure: an Ultra Title (its own stated equivalent of other feds' “World” title) to be decided by a sixteen-man tournament, an Elite Title (an “Intercontinental” equivalent) for the tournament's three losing semifinalists in a triangle match, and a Super Title (a “TV” equivalent) to be settled later in its own special battle royal. Two on-screen co-hosts, billed Buddy Wilson and Shane Dewar, bickered and talked over each other and their own president through the broadcast while the sixteen tournament entrants were drawn live on air, among them Knightrous, The Falcon, The Crow, Fire Mexica, Opera Man, and Dave “Rulebreaker” Hortz.

The same broadcast announced a new weekly Tuesday show and promised the first issue of its own in-house magazine the following day — which survives, carrying a masthead, four named columns, and an editorial. No card result, roster page, or rules document survives after that 29 July broadcast; the tournament's own outcome, and how this run of the fed ended, isn't in the surviving record.
