Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Near North Extreme Wrestling survives in one document, a Frequently Asked Questions post to rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy dated 30 October 2000. It describes itself as an e-mail-booked federation out of Toronto, Ontario, started along the lines of ECW in the northeast before growing, in its own words, into something “more like WWF, holding events all over Canada and the US,” with a world tour due to complete the expansion “in the New Year.” It never forgot its roots at the small NNEW Arena in Parry Sound, Ontario, which its own FAQ calls the federation's birthplace.

Its flagship, Monday Night STORM, aired every other week on ESPN in the United States and TSN in Canada with five or six full matches; Slammin' Saturday filled the off weeks with a shorter, summarized card. A once fully-written roleplay recap, NNEW Rumblings, had by this FAQ shrunk to a plain results packet, and a post-STORM reaction show, NNEWs Flash, was on hiatus waiting for a new writer. The World Heavyweight Title stood as its top belt, formed by unifying two earlier championships, the Northern Title and the InterState Title, at a Fight of the Champions pay-per-view; a Hardcore Championship and a Mini's Championship, the latter decided in one night at Extreme Measures, rounded out its active belts.

The FAQ names its own front office in full: owner and president Anthony Shedden, a former wrestler in his early thirties; vice president Todd Boswell, who left the ring to join him; head official Glenn Thomas, a former tag-team champion brought in before the Return to Hell pay-per-view; referees Joe Banks, Ray Stanz, ‘Black’ Jack Smitty and ‘Shooter’ James Sherman; a Minis-division referee, ‘Wee’ Willy Lowman; and a broadcast team of Rob Wise and Harry Bargholz, with Michael Hawkins and John Newman hosting the pay-per-view pre-shows, all four having come over from the defunct EEWF.

No card, result, or later bulletin survives past this FAQ. Whether NNEW completed the world tour it announced, or how it ended, isn't in the surviving record.
