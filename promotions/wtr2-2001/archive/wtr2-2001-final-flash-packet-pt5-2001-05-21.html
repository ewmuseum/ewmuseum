Weekend to Remember 2 -- Final Flash Packet (part 5)

May 21, 2001, 12:00:00 AM

TO BE ADDED TO THE MAILING LIST E-MAIL [detail withheld] ********* * Creed * ********* "Swan Song" [The smooth surface of water is broken by several small objects, causing rippling effects, small concentric circles spreading outward from the disturbances. However, the aquatic calm is destroyed, as several colorful ducks and white swans dart into the picture, all eager to retrieve what they can, likely in the belief it may be edible.]
[Panning up, a man is seen holding a handful of bread crumbs over the round, decorative but protective barrier. The distant sounds of children laughing echoes off the water, though it blends together with the quacks of hungry birds, and the light splashing of the water.]
[Moving further, the camera reveals more about this individual's appearance; his clothing is a variety of black, from the simple T-shirt reading "You ARE The Weakest Link -- Good Bye," to the leather jacket bearing the familiar Ace of Spades motif, to the faded jeans, to the leather boots and belt that are both necessary and appropriately stylish. Without looking away from the pond, Creed speaks in a steady tone, his deep gravelly voice firm and somewhat authoritative.]
A fat lady singing. A final performance. A swan song. [With a casual arc, his arm sends a barrage of crumpled bread through the air and onto the water. Several of the waterfowl converge, the animalistic greed very apparent, as they nip and shriek while trying to procure the biggest portions for themselves.]
Toss out any cliche you'd like, it's all the same. After this event with Jay Ralston's outfit, the Extreme Iron Man Match, I will be done with the professional wrestling industry.
[The biker leans forward, as if in contemplation, his forearms resting on the wrought iron railing.]
There's still a very healthy amount of currency in my reserves, so it's not like I need the money. And frankly, the bittersweet joys of fame and recognition aren't that big of a deal to me... [grins smugly] ...despite what some may say.
[Offhand, he looks toward the camera, his eyes displaying a brutal honesty, and his right hand makes an open-palm gesture of a man who has nothing to hide.]
I won't lie, it's not entirely due to a lack of interest on my part. [He shrugs casually, perhaps of disappointment?] I asked around, but the few people I talked to who run the shows just aren't interested in hiring an ex-con who just got out of prison.
[Flinging the remainder of the baked-grain projectiles towards the birds, he turns around, resting his back against the metal railing.]
...and really, I can't say I blame them. During my last three months in the
UEW, I blantantly assaulted nearly half of the roster without provocation, I staged the sinking of a Cruise ship that terrified several hundred people, I had a wrestler held down and injected with an unhealthy dosage of glucose...
[He rolls his eyes at this, obviously knowing now this was neither prudent nor acceptable behavior, but unable to retract his former actions.]
So I didn't really put up too much of a fight when they finally found something to get me in court, something my lawyers didn't find a quick and easy loophole to manipulate. I only did a year's time, but it certainly wasn't undeserved.
[Looking off at something unknown, his eyes seem to focus off-camera, though whether this is out of recognition or recollection can't be determined.]
However, any wrestling organization worth its salt wouldn't hire a guy like me, and the ones that would.. [chuckles] ...let's just say most of them aren't worth working for anyway.
[Bringing his attention back to the camera, he smiles with the expression not of happiness, but of a practiced acceptance of his lot in life.]
So this brings me back to Jay Ralston, the most untrustworthy wrestling promoter known to man. I've had a few snakes ask me not to refer to Mr.
Ralston as one, they say it gives reptiles a bad reputation... [He smirks mischievously, a gleam in his eye.] I knew I could count on him to book the one match I'd always wanted to have, against the one guy crazy enough to take it on.
You see, I don't want a match where one lucky move could end it. I don't want a match where it could accidentally end prematurely, like a Table Match. And I wouldn't want something where a person could get a fluke win, like a
Ladder Match. [With hands out and palms open, the biker displays body language indicating a belief that most everyone would agree to his next statement.]
Naturally, I wouldn't want any obnoxious gloryhounds sticking their nose in my business, either.
So what I've come up with, is the Extreme Iron Man Match. [With his right index finger, he taps a black watch on the opposite wrist.] For 30 minutes straight, Alex Extreme and I will mix it up. We'll climb ladders, we'll put each other through tables, we'll pin each other - frankly, it's license for me to behave like a vicious animal for half an hour, abusing one of the few guys I know who can not only take it, but can dish it out - in spades!
[His eyes dart over his shoulder, mostly likely a glance toward the design on the back of his leather jacket, and a very minor grin plays across his lips.]
There will be police, along with arena security and many private officers I will hire myself, on the scene to insure there is absolutely no outside interference. People can find their own time to make their names, but not on *my* time.
You see, I've watched these birds most of the afternoon, and I've come to understand something.
[Turning back to the waterfowl, he gestures toward them, almost as if teaching a child some little-known trivia on the subject.]
The 'song' of a swan is neither quiet nor pretty - it makes loud, raucous noise. And I don't see that my 'swan song' should be any different. I want crashing, I want breaking, I want noises that will linger in my ears for the rest of my life, reminding me of stories to tell children and grandchildren.
[Deep-seated satisfaction is clear from his face, as the former prisoner looks out onto the water.]
I remember this quote I heard a long time ago: "This is the way the world ends - not with a bang, but with a whimper." [His eyes shift, staring straight into the camera, as if somehow looking through it to all the potential viewers of the footage. In his stare is the unwavering conviction of a man who has decided nothing will deter him from his choices, that there is no chance in hell of changing his mind, or stopping him from his intended destination.]
Not in *my* world. [Shifting his weight, Creed comes to his feet, as though preparing to leave.] So Alex, make sure you're ready for this match - because as they say, I got nothin' to lose. I'm retiring, and unless you want to join me, you better make sure you survive the match.
[Walking forward, the camera does not move to follow the biker as he leaves.] See you in Florida, 'Lex. You bring the Extreme... [Just as his clothing begins to block out the view of the park, one last line is heard.] ...I'll bring the Bang. [Fade to Black.] =========================================================== *************** * Nick Demola * *************** [The camera fades into a woodsy scene, where Nick Demola sits on the hood of his old V.W. Jetta and looks up at the sky, protecting his left side the whole time.]
ND: Star light, star bright... [Demola keeps his attention up at the heavens as he chuckles a bit.] ND: I always like how people feel they are experts on subjects way beyond their realm... just because they have delusions of knowing what they're talking about.
[Demola looks down and straight at the camera in front of him.] ND: Like the preachers who claim to know God's word, just because they've read a book and know how to shout really loud. You ever notice how they have all the answers? Know exactly what God's feeling?
I buy their jibe about as much as I accept the crap Ken Curtis has been shoveling. [Demola looks up and points.] ND: You see, Curtis, you know as much about true greatness as those idiots know about God's will. You can guess at it, you can make assumptions. You can study me all you want, in the same way that mere mortals study God.
But in the end, friend, you don't know. Because, like them... you are not God. [Demola hops off the hood of the car, albeit slowly. He turns and faces the camera.] ND: Curtis, this isn't the first time we've been in the same place at the same time. You and I in the IIWA. Battling for weeks and weeks, with you escaping each time with the gold through some nefarious means or another.
When I finally got my fair shot, the shop shut down and we moved on. Night of Glory, Curtis. We were both there as well. But you took on a different challenge, wouldn't answer the call from Nick Demola. And you went on to win the event, more than likely as a direct result of not having to meet up with me.
The LVPWA. LOCO and I were tearing up arenas all across the country, with matches that were nothing short of classic. And again I called you out.
Again... you didn't respond. [Demola moves to the driver's side of the car and puts the key in the lock.] ND: Since the last time you had the guts to get in the ring with me face to face, a lot has changed. You've been through feud after feud. You've made stars out of younger wrestlers and become quite the legend.
But while you've been pussyfooting with the children, Ken, I've been out there making a living. I've been beating each and every person who would actually accept a match with me. I took down your old pal Brody Thunder and left him laying. I took down LOCO and beat him into forfeiting his shallow claim to being the "GOD" of this sport. I beat Andrew Tucker unconscious in
England, and took out all comers in Las Vegas. In your former stomping grounds.
While you were playing with kids and making yourself a legend, Curtis, I was doing what I do best. Winning. And establishing myself as a God.
[Demola opens the door and leans on it as he glares into the camera.] ND: This is what it comes to. Three years later, Nick Demola against Ken Curtis in a main event on one of the biggest cards of all time. And if I have one piece of advice for you, Curtis, it's this...
After the match, go find a preacher. Go pray with him and talk to him about
God. Get some tips from him and find out how he gets people to buy into his line of B.S. Because what you're talking about when it comes to wrestling ain't gonna make a believer of anyone.
Then go get yourself ordained. There isn't any room for a washed-up cripple in My game. [Camera fades to Demola getting in the car.] =========================================================== ***************************** * "Lone Wolf" Brody Thunder * ***************************** [SCENE: The set of "This Week In Pro Wrestling",the weekly television show which interviews,reviews and previews the happenings in pro wrestling. This week it's on the road , being shot live from the WLMT Channel 30 television studios in Memphis, Tennessee. The show's host, Roger Allen, is ready to interview the next guest on the set as the show's producer gives him a silent countdown... 5... 4... 3.. 2.. 1...]
ALLEN: "Welcome back,fans. I want to remind you again, that later on in this hour we're going to show that taped interview I did yesterday with "Moondog"

Durango cowboy hat covering his salt-n-pepper crewcut. The two men shake hands and Thunder takes a seat to the left of Allen on a swivel stool.]
ALLEN: "Hello again , sir. Good to see you again." THUNDER: "Well Rog it's good ta be seen again, m'man." [The two share a laugh.] ALLEN: "So you're set to make your return, of sorts, to the ring at WTR2, is that right?" THUNDER: "Well I _am_ returnin' ta the ring..jus' not the way everyone pictured it, I guess."
ALLEN: "That's right. Instead of your wrestling gear, you're going to be wearing the pinstripes of a referee, correct?"
THUNDER: "That's what the contract says. We got Eddie Jacobs gonna hook'em up with Terry Sloane in a grudge match an' yours truly is gonna be in that ring to make sure there's a winner."
ALLEN: "That's a bit of a different role for you, isn't it? I mean playing the role of mediator. These are two men who you would probably rather be in the ring _against_, are they not?"
THUNDER: "I ain't never stood across the ring from Eddie but I respect him. He's from the ol' school like me. Don't mistake respect fer fear though. If there ever was a Thunder vs Jacobs, it'd be a flamin' donnybrook, lemme tell ya. As fer that polecat Sloane? He an' his pack o'lapdogs jumped me, took my gold an' left me laid up in a hospital bed fer three months. I owe that lil sonuva... gun ..a lil payback, an' Brody Thunder always pays up in full, amigo.
But the fact is that I ain't signed to face either o'them. I'm signed to wear the stripes an' raise the hand o' the winner, an' that's jus' what I aim ta do."
ALLEN: "You know both of these men pretty well. How do you see the match going?" THUNDER: "Both these guys got egos the size o'Texas so you know they're gonna get after it pretty good. It ain't gonna be pretty. Jacobs is a pretty cagey veteran, y'know, an' he's faced some o'the best around an' walked back down the aisle with the winner's share o'the purse. He might wear his soles thin on
Sloane if he gets the chance. Sloane? Now Sloane is a lil different kinda animal. Sloane plays the mindgames. He's crafty. He's strong as a flamin' bull but his ego will let him make mistakes. An' I guarantee ya, he makes a mistake in there against Jacobs an' ol' Eddie will put the lights out quicker'n lightnin', brother.
ALLEN: "Care to pick a winner?" THUNDER: "Heh-heh... you always like puttin' me on the spot, doncha Rog? Pick a winner? Hell, the winner's gonna be the man who outsmarts, outwrestles an'
outlasts his opponent in that ring. The winner's gonna be the man who comes in with a gameplan an' executes it. The winner's gonna be the man who wants it more'n the other guy. The winner... will be the better man, _that_ I can promise you, Roger.
ALLEN: "Okay, fair enough. Now then, your being signed as special referee has not come without some controversy, am I correct?
[Thunder furrows his brow in obvious confusion.] ALLEN: "I'm talking about the fact that there's been speculation that because of your recent history with Terry Sloane and the Kingpins, that you might somehow allow a certain..mmmm _leeway_.. with Eddie Jacobs."
THUNDER: "How long we known each other, Roger Allen? How long?" ALLEN: "I'd say about eight or nine years..." THUNDER: "That's right. An' in aaaaaaaall that time, have you ever known me to lie?" ALLEN: "Well..." THUNDER: "Well what?" ALLEN: "..no. I can't say that you've ever 'lied'.." THUNDER: "_Thank_ you. An' I ain't lyin' to ya now when I tell ya neither one o'these guys are on my Christmas card list,hoss. Eddie Jacobs knows he's got a fight on his hands with Sloane. The only _leeway_ I'm gonna allow Eddie Jacobs is the leeway to prove if he's the better man in that ring on that night. Is that givin' him an' advantage.. or so-called 'leeway'? No flamin' way. Cuz when the bell rings?
I'm jus' there to raise the winner's hand, plain an' simple. ALLEN: "Are you prepared for any possible _problems_ which might come from either of them?"
THUNDER: "'Problems'? Like what? One o'them gettin' froggy an' jumpin' me?" ALLEN: "You have to admit, it's a possibility." [Thunder shakes his head no.] THUNDER: "Sloane ain't _that_ gutsy an' Jacobs ain't _that_ stupid." ALLEN: "So..that's not a concern then?" THUNDER: "Look, I've got a job to do in that ring. If anybody tries any monkey business...well...let's jus' say I got a gameplan too."
ALLEN: "Any truth to the rumor that you're in full retirement now? Does this match signify the end of Brody Thunder's wrestling career?"
[Thunder draws in a deep breath.] THUNDER: "I've heard all the rumors an' all the crap on the innernet an' dirt sheets an' I wanna say this right here, right now. All this talk about retirement or semi-retirement? It's all jus' wishful thinkin' by people who are scared. Scared that Brody Thunder might jus' come to their wrestlin' promotion.
Scared that Brody Thunder might jus' take back his place in this game by force. Y'know Roger, I never _said_ I retired or was semi-retired. The flamin' media all started that bullpucky. Will I ever lace'em up an' get in the ring again?
Hell yes. But it'll be on _my_ terms this time. Will it be soon? Who knows? I've been in preliminary talks with a few promotions but nothin's been inked. The business has changed so much nowadays... I guess folks figger it's past Brody Thunder by. I'm tellin' ya right now...
..don't count Brody Thunder's shoulders to the mat jus' yet, amigo. I still got me a few good fights left an' when the time an' the situation's right.. Brody Thunder will kick the world in the ass an' serve notice on what passes fer wrestlin' talent these days. Bank on that, my friend.
ALLEN: "One final question. Which pays better referee or wrestler?" [Both men chuckle.] THUNDER: "It ain't the stripes that's fer sure!" ALLEN: "Well I want to thank you for taking the time to stop by and join us here on TWIPW and I look forward to having you back again."
[They shake hands once again.] THUNDER: "S'my pleasure,hoss. Don't fergit to tune in to WTR2. I guarantee you it will be something you'll _never_ fergit."
ALLEN: "Again fans, that's A Weekend to Remember II, live on pay-per-view in
June. Check your local listings for channels and times. Thanks again."

ALLEN: "Well fans, coming up next we've got some Florida Championship Wrestling action. It features a bloody bullrope match between "Mean" Mike McCord and
Blackjack Lanza. So stay tuned, we'll be right back after these messages."
[Fade to black...] =========================================================== ************************************** * "Magnificent Bastard" Terry Sloane * ************************************** [Fade in on a large white banner stretched across the main entrance to Pro
Player Stadium, home of the Florida Marlins baseball team, located in Miami,
FLA. . On the banner are the words "THE MARLINS WELCOME TERRY SLOANE". It flaps gently in the warm noonday breeze. The calming scene is broken by the unmistakable voice of the man known around the world as "The Magnificent
Bastard", Terry Sloane.] SLOANE - "S'great for a game,daddy." [The camera pans downward to reveal Terry Sloane standing in front of the main gate. he's decked out on an orange and yellow tie-dyed muscle shirt and matching tie-dyed jean shorts which hug his muscular thighs. On his deeply tanned feet he sports a pair of orange Chandu sandals. He stares out at the camera from behind a pair of Gruffer 750 sunglasses, his platinum blonde hair and porkchop sideburns providing a sharp contrast to his bronze complexion. An arrogant smile dominates his face as he looks at the scene around him.]
SLOANE - "A competition between modernday warriors for supremacy. A test of skill to prove who is the better of the day. A fierce struggle to prove one's mettle on the field of battle. Ah yes...
[He draws in a deep breath] ...the stench of war, daddy... it fills the air right here in bea-U-tiful Miami F-L-A, baby!" [He waves a telling finger to the camera.] SLOANE - "No-no-no...I'm not talking about these losers the Marlins and the guest asskicking team they'll be playing here tonight. I'm talking about the war of words... the verbal battle... the battle of wits, if you weeeel, daddy, between that freakin' fossil Eddie Jacobs and yours truly."
[The smile widens just abit.] SLOANE - "Please...no 'unarmed man' punchlines. It's old, tired and just as much of a cliche as the daily drivel that spills outta Eddie Jacobs' mouth, baby.
[Sloane pops the top off a bottle of Poland Springs water and takes a hit.] SLOANE - "When that team of overpriced losers, the Florida Marlins, found out I was going to be down here, they asked me to come here to throw out the first pitch tonight. At first I said 'Hey Terry Sloane can't be seen hanging out with a group of morons like you guys, daddy. It's bad for the image'. But then they said that the sexy Hannah Halloway, star of the greatest movie ever made..."The
Hell of Paradise",.. when they said _she_ was singing the national anthem...well she's been dying to meet me, phonecalls, letters, the whole nine yards.. so I felt I couldn't disappoint her any further. So Hannah, babydoll,
'Mr Terrific' will be there tonight so wear something...nice."
[He winks slyly.] SLOANE - "But that's pleasure, let's get back to business. Eddie. Eddie Jacobs. The man the idiots in the seats all call 'the Iron Man'. The man who still thinks that Terry Sloane is some new kid on the block who'll be awestruck by his name dropping and revisionist history tales. He loves to bore anyone who'll listen with his stories of great past battles. Well I heard ya, Edster.
And what I heard was the chest-beating of an old man who is slowly realizing... his days in this great sport are almost over. You think I care that some guy with VD... Vitrone or whatever the hell his name is.. you think it matters to me that you beat him in some ginsoaked bingo hall back in them hills of Tennessee? You think I care that you wrestled in the INWI or the IIWF or the SCW, daddy? The only reason _I_ wasn't wrestling there was because I was blackballed, baby! Just like Eddy Mansfield. Just like Dutch
Savage. Just like Pampiro Firpo, Bulldog Brower and Ali Bey the Turk, daddy.
How's that for name-dropping "ace"? All of those elitist promotions were too scared to have a guys like the ones I just mentioned and myself because we don't play by the rules and we don't play nice with others, babydoll. You should thank your lucky stars that they _didn't_ let me in, Eddie ol'man. Cuz if they HAD...
..I'd have retired _your_ ass long ago. Ten years in this business,daddy. Ten years of being kept down. Ten years of being forced to wrestle in high schools, Elks halls and regional territories which couldn't draw flies with a ring fulla s[BLEEP]t. I had to go to Japan to get the respect I deserved, so don't wear the fact that you wrestled in those feds like some friggin' badge of honor. They were a safe place to trump up your phony career. The good ol'boy network in it's glory. Me? I don't owe many people anything, but I DO owe Jay Ralston, daddy. He's the only one with a sack big enough to put my name on a contract over here in the states. And by signing that contract I've now got the right to legally put your AARP card-carrying ass on a gurney to the nearest emergency room, baby."

A pair of losers. So no amount of trips through history is going to change that. Thunder just better remember exactly why he's there in the first place. All that pantload's gotta do is slap the mat three times after I school you on pain and humiliation...new school style, daddy."
[He takes one more sip and then hands the bottle to his limo driver standing just off to the left of camerashot.]
