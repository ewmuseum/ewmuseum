Weekend to Remember 2 -- Final Flash Packet (part 6)

May 21, 2001, 12:00:00 AM
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
TO BE ADDED TO THE MAILING LIST E-MAIL [detail withheld] ******************************************************* * "Iron Man" Eddie Jacobs & "Lone Wolf" Brody Thunder * ******************************************************* [The scene is a small but crowded parking lot in the rural section of Western Tennessee. A few trucks seem to have windows rolled down, but all radios are turned, inevitably, to Bob Seeger. There are two men worth mentioning to a wrestling fan in this whole lot.
One is "Iron Man" Eddie Jacobs. The other? "Lone Wolf" Brody Thunder. Thunder has a bit of a confident saunter about him as he brushes past the legions of semi-drunk yahoos that crowd the door to the local dive. Standing under a flashing pink neon sign that reads "Rendezvous Bar" is, as you may guess, Eddie Jacobs. He waits stoically until Brody gets within a few feet, and then hands his cigar to the door man. Jacobs greets Thunder well enough, and the two make their entrance into the bar.
When the camera tries to come with, we're blocked.. Fade out.] [Fade back in...a caption reads "Three Hours Later." A few feet away, Jacobs seems to be indulging his more relaxed side by relieving himself on the stucco wall of said bar. He chuckles as he finishes, and waves the camera inside...thankfully admittance is gained this time. The Rendezvous Bar isn't as glamorous as the patrons would make it out to be. In fact, it's doubtful anyone in the city of Lexington even knows what language the name comes from.
Regardless, Eddie waves on, and the cameraman soon takes a seat opposite both men. While Eddie still seems...elegantly wasted... Thunder appears as sober as a judge. Must be his mustache.]
Ej: Go on...tell 'im the one about Hardin an' the hotel manager. [Thunder sort of looks a little blankly at Jacobs for a second, figuring something out.] BT: As I recall... that was _you_, ya damned drunk'n idjit. [And the dim lights go off in his eyes.] Ej: Oh...oh yeah. Ah s'pose it was. Hell, Ah guess Ah should tell it, then... [Hrmmm.] Ej: If Ah can remember it... BT: Aw christ, Eddie... what the hell's wrong with you? Ya got a big match at WTR2 an' yer out here pushin' yer liver ta the limit instead o'gettin' ready fer a fight. I know Sloane ain't no Ken Curtis or Nick Demola but damn, son... he ain't exactly no Randy Mulkey either, y'know? That's the flamin'
trouble with the business these days. The young bucks think it should be handed to'em an' the old guys feel it's owed them. Nobody's willin' ta pay the price ta hook'em up an' go the distance anymore. I've seen it in the eyes
o'many o'wrestler an' right now I see it in yours. You're jus' takin' fer granted that yer rep an' name an' mebbe our history is gonna get the job done. Well I got news fer ya, amigo.
All it's gonna take is three seconds ta change yer attitude. Don't take anything...an' I mean _anything_ fer granted,ace...or you'll become jus'
another curtainjerker. That's jus' the way the game's played these days, my friend. It ain't like the good ol' days...
[Eddie nods. Oh, sure sure.] Ej: Yeah, yeah. Wrestling. It's headed... Down? [Brody nods.] Ej: Yeah, down. An' Ah'm for one gettin' pretty sick of seein' these young kids with their Zimas an' their Dan Fogelberg, an' their hula hoops...
[And suddenly, our Southern fried friend turns a lovely shade of green and excuses himself.]
Ej: Oh God...Ah'll be back. [Jacobs makes his way to the restroom as Thunder pulls out a cigar and sticks it between his teeth. It remains unlit as Thunder shakes his head in obvious disdain.] BT: Eddie's always been one ta... push his limits... with everything. Liquor, ladies or lacin'em up. It's a damned wonder he ain't wound up face down somewhere already. I remember one night... damn.. it was back when we were both tourin' the old SCW promotion. We jus' happen ta stumble into the same barhall. I'd been there awhile when he comes waltzin' in with some barfly beauty on his arm. I see'em take a dark booth in the back an' get... cozy. I take a few shots an look over an'the lady's still there but Eddie? He's disappeared faster'n Houdini.
About twenty minutes later he's back playin' tonsil hockey with the skirt again. He kept leavin' fer periods o' time then show back up..all night this went on. Come ta find out, the crazy sonuvabitch.. he was jugglin' these
_three_ girls around at the same time but in three different bars on the same flamin' street! It was like a damned sitcom episode, watchin' him runnin' back-n-forth, tryin' ta keep each o'them from findin' out 'bout the others. Heh,heh...he ran his ass ragged 'til it fell apart on the bastard.. heh,heh. Guess they all met in the street an' _it_ had hit the fan, BIG time. Them girls all wanted to string his four-flushin' hide from the nearest streetlight. Next time we ran that town, Eddie took ta wearin' a disguise, jus' in case, heh,heh,heh.
[Thunder waves down a waitress.] BT: Gimme a shot o' Owen's Goldenrod...with a bottle chaser. [Thunder turns back towards the camera. The cameraman's voice can faintly be heard as he asks some questions of the big Arizonan.]
BT: But ya know the sonuvabitch still didn't go home alone. One o' the barmaids, a damned good looker too, well she _'escorted'_ him back to the hotel we all stayed at. He's been a flamin' lucky bastard,that's fer sure.
CM: Sounds like you and Eddie have some history...? BT: Yeah..well..'history' is one way to put it. We've been in the same promotions over the years but never really had any deals in the ring. Back then a few o' us ol'timers might head to the local tavern an' tip some longnecks an' gripe about the week's paycheck. The joke in the lockerroom was...there were three guys you didn't wanna go drinkin' with. Ken
Curtis...Jay Dubya Hardin..an' Eddie flamin' Jacobs...cuz they'd drink ya under the table an' ya never knew if you'd wake up in the whorehouse or the hoosegow.
Some nights ya'd wake up in _both_! [Thunder chuckles as he slowly shakes his head. The waitress returns with a shotglass and a bottle of Owens. Thunder tips his black Durango cowboy hat and winks in appreciation.]
BT: Yeah.. things were much different in them days. Nowadays it's all about the glitz... the glamour.. who does your intro music... how many fireworks you got in your entrance...all that bullpucky. If ya ain't in one o' the big name promotions then yer considered either irrelevant, retired or dead.
Seems like nowadays wrestlin' takes a backseat ta the circus stuff. It can dull the instincts an' erode the skills if ya don't smarten up. I mean..take Eddie. He used ta be all business when approachin' a fight. Here he is tonight, invitin' me ta take some kinda trip down memory flamin' lane instead watchin'
tapes an' preparin' fer that idjit Sloane. The old Eddie wouldn't do that. CM: So why _did_ you come here then? BT: Guess I felt I owed him somethin' cuz o' the old days. I was gonna be in town to shoot a piece with that "This Week In Pro Wrestlin'" show anyways.
'Sides..there ain't many like us left in this game. Us ol'timers..we gotta... watch.. each others backs, y'know. CM: You think he can beat Sloane? BT: Hell yes... if he wants to. Trouble with Eddie is he tends to get a bit cocky sometimes. S'like that ol' sayin'..'bout writin' checks yer ass can't cash. His mouth tends to run abit, 'specially after a good night o' bendin'
elbows, an' that can catch up ta ya down the line. Sloane is one of those cookiecutter, prettyboy, flash-in-the-pans that seem to overrun the game these days. Sure he's got the million dollar body but 'tween the ears...
it's a ten cent brain. Eddie could chew this guy up an' spit him out twice if he takes him seriously. Trouble is...I don't think he _is_ takin' him seriously.
All it'll take is one mistake an' Sloane could walk outta there with his hand raised. If I were in Sloane's boots right now, I _damn_ sure know what I'd do come WTR2. CM: What's that? [Thunder pauses. preferring to down a shot of Owens amber liquor before answering.] BT: I'd wait until the moment was right an' then I'd... [Thunder stops in midsentence as Eddie returns with a platter on which rests a pitcher of beer and four glasses. The Southerner looks around the table thinking to himself and counting, and mumbles and obscenity whilest sitting down.]
Ej: Brought us back some beer... [Thunder gives a complementary nod...not that Eddie really noticed. He sets up a glass by Brody, who waves it away. He looks towards the camera but not at it, thinking to himself whether he should get the camera man drunk. No, probably not a good idea. Jacobs pours a beer for himself and studies Brody idlely as he takes a few gulps. And then a topic comes to mind.]
Ej: Hey. Brodes. Why don'cha tell 'im the one about Hardin an' the hotel manager? [Thunder just sighs and shakes his head in disbelief that Jacobs is getting this trashed a week or two before a big match.]
Ej: Well...okay then. [Eddie takes the beer back to his lips and just about finishes the sucker off, keeping his uneasy eyes on Brody the entire time as he continually tilts
his head back farther and farther. He pulls the cup away with a sigh and looks around.] Ej: So anyway, Ah've been doin' some readin' up on this Sloane cat. Seems he ain't exactly as much of a slouch as Ah'm makin' him out to be.
[This seems to pique the big man's interest. He looks at Eddie, eyebrows arched, waiting for the reason.]
Ej: Yeah. Well, Ah mean, the kid obviously calls everyone daddy because he's not sure who his father is, but he's got more of a past that in this business. Ah heard from someone he's been wrestling almost as long as Ah have...only he's been doin' it on sawdust for dinner an' a place to sleep.
BT: He pisses an' moans about no respect. That's his gig. Says he's been 'blackballed' from the big guys here in the States. He thinks he deserves the top spot wherever he hangs his flamin' hat. Ej: An', see, Ah can relate to that. That'll make anyone bitter, and if he's anything, he's bitter. But it'll also make a man tough. Ah mean, the kid's beaten YOU fer Chrissakes...
[Brody shifts a little uncomfortably in his booth as Jacobs mentions this.] Ej: ...An' if he can do that, he might be able to beat me, too. [Oooh...the implications aren't very nice there. Jacobs pours himself another beer and seems not to notice the hard stare Thunder is shooting his way that dissipates as he looks back up.]
Ej: Anyway, so Ah ordered ten hours 'a tape of this kid. [Pause as Thunder weighs this announcement coming from Jacobs.] BT: Finally takin' him seriously? Ej: Heh...NO. Who d'you think Ah am, Randy Mulkey? This match ain't gonna make or break anything but that kid's neck. Ah'll let him do all the homework
he pleases, Brodes. Me? Well, Ah'll do a few pushups, do a few situps, an' do a fe- Wait, can't say that on TV. [Eddie cracks a Devil-may-care grin as beads of beer begin to collect on his rough beard. He leans over the side of the table and burps for a good ten seconds before leaning over and grabbing something from the floor.]
Ej: Found a quarter. [Thunder purses his lips. He stares hard at Jacobs. You can see his anger building. His eyes narrow before he speaks again.]
BT: Gawdammit, Jacobs! What the hell's happened to you? [Thunder shakes his head once more in contempt, his eyes never leaving Jacobs'.] BT: Yer right. Yer gawdamnmed right. Sloane _did_ pin my ass. But it took him an' three o'his goon squad friends ta do it. The truth is, he's pretty damned tough fer a primadonna. The polecat split me open an' then took my strap. I still owe the lil runt fer that. But here's some advice, Ed. You take 'um lightly, amigo..
..an' he'll take yer ass _out_. That's always been yer flamin' problem, Eddie. Ya never take things seriously. You've always had the talent. A gawdamned tough scrapper, hoss.
But I'll be damned if ya haven't had yer share o'luck helpin' ya out lately. Like some damned cat with nine lives or somethin'. Ya need ta remember what got ya to the Big Dance, amigo. Guys like Sloane are a dime-a-dozen..cocky, young, arrogant pissants fulla piss-n-vinegar... feelin' immortal an'
invincible. Guys like us? Hell we never went in fer all the flash. We never needed fancy outfits an' we never went to the ring with a buncha flamin'
animals like some damned dr Dolittle. We went to the ring fer one reason. To win. Anythin' else jus' wasn't an option. You need to smarten up an' get yer head outta yer... look.. [Thunder draws in a deep breath and exhales. He sets his jaw and raises another shot of whiskey.]
BT: I jus' wanna tell ya straight up, hoss.. when I put them stripes on an' that bell rings? There ain't gonna be no cavalry out there to save yer ass... catch my drift? As far as I'm concerned you two can beat the hell outta each other an' whoever's left lookin' at the lights?
He'll have _earned_ it. Would I like ta see ya put the numb on that jackass Sloane? Sure. I ain't too sure that's gonna happen. But if it does, yer gonna have ta do it on yer own. In my opinion, the best thing that could happen ta jerk Sloane would be an industrial accident... but... that's jus' my opinion.
[Eddie runs his chin for a moment thoughtfully.] Ej: Hell, Brodes, no one said life was fair or safe. Accidents happen, and despite who won our Presidential election, bad things still happen to dumb people. Sloane, since he's about as sharp as a pound 'a wet leather, is long
overdue for something bad ta happen ta him. BT: Hmmm? Ej: Oh...heh. Don't worry about it, Ace. Just the beer talking. [Eddie looks warily over his brew towards the big Arizonan, tipping the glass in recognition and grinning a moment before tilting it back. Thunder never fully seems at ease with what he may or may not have heard, but the topic changes soon enough.]
Ej: Anyway, Mom, like you said, Ah tend to let things roll off my back. So what? Life's more or less a joke from beginning to end, nothin' wrong with finding the humor in it, if you ask me. And as far as Sloane being able to pull out a quick win on me?
[Heh.] Ej: Let's just say that Ah think having my original respiratory system in tact gives me a bit of an age over this kid that present company may not have
had. An' as far as victory being the only reason guys like us went to the ring? You're right, Brody. That has changed a little.
[Cheers.] Ej: Seems some of us older fellas don't come down the aisle to win anymore. Some of us come down the aisle to get the funds to take our families ta Mouseworld. But Ah suppose that's only SOME of us, Brodes. [Jacobs leans over the table a little, playfully locking eyes with Thunder.] Ej: But as for the rest of us...and by that Ah mean me...Ah still come down the aisle to get kick some ass an' earn some cash. Plain and simple.
Sloane may have ulterior motives to brushing his teeth in the morning, man, but not me. You better believe Ah'm as goal-oriented as ever. Ah'm just a realist.
An' Ah REEEEEEALLY don't think Ah've got much to worry about. [Heh.] Ej: Now, if you'll excuse me... [Jacobs stands, tossing a ten and a fistful of change (keeping the quarter he found, of course) onto the tray to cover his beers.] Ej: Ah'm gonna head on back to casa d'Jacobs. You're more'n welcome to stop by if you want, Ace, just make sure it isn't in the next forty minutes. [The Southerner glances at his watch, and then towards the bar.] Ej: Better make that an hour. [And with that, Jacobs strolls off to some unnamed conquest, leaving a brooding Brody Thunder staring angrily at both the platter and the remaining
Owen's Goldenrod. He pours one final shotglass and raises into the air in mock toast.]
BT: Don't have much to worry 'bout? Heh... [He downs the liquor. That evil smirk returns to his face.] BT: Not much ta worry 'bout at all... ace. [Thunder tosses a few dead presidents on the table. He gathers up his keys and makes his way to the exit thinking that there's alot more to worry about than anyone knows. Even Eddie"Iron Man" Jacobs.]
[Fade to black...] =========================================================== ********************** * Demon Boy Ishrinku * ********************** [Camera opens on near darkness. The only illumination is coming from a single blood red candle sitting on the floor. As the camera approaches it, a figure appears, sitting in front of the candle. The figure dips their head to reveal black and red facepaint. The trademark "Demon Mask" of "Demon Boy" Ishrinku. He speaks, a low hissing whisper and English subtitles appear at the bottom of the screen.]
Ishrinku: "Warrrrrrlock...." [Ishrinku passes a scared hand over the flame causing it to flicker and distort his features. The flame dances in his eyes. Eyes filled with a strange calm.]
Ishrinku: "We are the same...Yes? Yes. Misunderstood creatures. That is what we are. The blood, the pain...Scars deep...Lasting forever. We hide in the darkness. We do this yes?"
[Ishrinku's eyes sudden flash as if the reflected flame has ingnited something deep inside. His voice rises to a horrible screech.]
Ishrinku: "NO!!!" [Ishrinku reaches across his body and places an object in the candle flame. The object ignites to reveal a torch. Ishrinku stands holding the torch aloft.]
Ishrinku: "The 'Demon' will not hide anymore. Warlock...You will know what they do not understand. You will be the first...but not the last!!"
[Ishrinku's torch lights up the small room to reveal writing on the walls. In red paint(?) Ishrinku has scrawled words in both kanji and English. Pain, Hate,
Death, Dismemberment, Horror, and many more discriptions of violent and vile acts. Spread amongst those words is one name. Warlock. Ishrinku surveys his handiwork and a sick smile spreads across his painted face. He then digs into his pocket and pulls out a small vial. He raises it to his lips and then pauses for a moment before spitting into the torch causing flames to shoot out into the air. The camera crew decides it is time to retreat and rapidly leave the room. The camera is jostled and pointed first at the wall and then at the ground. The last thing that is heard the horrible screams of Ishrinku.
Fade to black.] =========================================================== ******** * Apex * ******** [The scene is black, not pitch black, but black like the camera is far too close to a black object. Music, specifically, "El Distorto de Melodica" by Everclear, can be heard in the background. A voice is heard over the music.]
Voice: What is an Apex? [The scene begins to move, apparently as the camera moves. The blackness seen before is revealed to be carpet. Another black object is seen as the camera pans upward. This object is a couch.]
Voice: Technically, I think it's something like a peak or a summit. [The scene finally attains a full view of the couch and the area around the couch. Apex is seen sitting on that couch. He's wearing black wrestling boots, loose fitting blue jeans, and a black WWO T-shirt. He has his legs propped on a coffee table, and a Pepsi in his hand.]
Apex: Everyone who knows me, knows that I haven't claimed to be the apex of the sport for quiet some time. I haven't said I am the top of the line for quiet awhile. So why choose this name?
[Apex shrugs.] Apex: When I started my career in a little known and now defunct company known as the XWA, the name
Apex fit the personality I had. The people I associated with had strange names like that... after all, we were just breaking into the business. I was only twenty.
[Apex pauses, mostly for effect, and continues his rant.] Apex: That was four years ago. I've grown up. I could, I suppose, use my real name. Luis Quesada... but, the people that have been with me through thick and then, know me as Apex. But enough speaking about names. After all, this could be my last chance to directly address my fellow R-Cup wrestlers.
[Apex takes a quick drink of his Pespi, and continues to speak.] Apex: My opponent in the first round has a strange grasp of reality. He claims I've made up Zombie
Corners... that the man is a figment of my imagination. Well... does this look like a figment of my imagination? [Apex motions to his left, towards another cushion on the couch. Of course, nothing is there.]
Apex: That's seven feet of rotten flesh. Axis says he can't see him. Maybe he's afraid to see him. Afraid to accept that I will have a monster of a man in my corner during out match. A man who thinks faster than the fastest computers. A man who is stronger than the strongest forklift. A man who has forgotten more about wrestling than Axis will ever know. Isn't that right,
Zombie Corners?
[Apex looks to his left and appears to be listening. Nothing is there, but Apex's expression leads one to believe that he is quiet serious about having this monster of a manager. After several moments, apparently Zombie Corners had a lot to say, Apex again speaks.]
Apex: That's right. All this talk is just the calm before the storm. Axis is right... I have taken some nasty shots. I've lost some matches due to these nasty shots. But I get up... and I'll never quit getting up.
We don't promise victory, but we promise that you'll know that you were in a war and that we revolve around nothing, well, except that big ball of hydrogen and helium, but nothing else.
[Apex takes another drink and quickly continues to speak.] Apex: As for everyone else in the R-Cup, well, there isn't much to say. It's hard to say if I'll even make it to the second round, and even harder to guess who I'd be facing. I guess it doesn't matter, what happens happens. If I lose to Johnny Axis,
I'll go him with my head up, because I'll know that I've tried my best. If I win, I continue on and give another wrestler everything I have. [Apex stands up. He motions towards his "manager", and the exit the angle of the camera. From off screen, Apex can be heard.]
Apex: I guess we'll find out soon enough. [Fade to black.] =========================================================== *************** * The Warlock * *************** Subject: Patient The idea, you see, was pretty simple. First, I suppose, you have to know a little something about me. The way I count it, a normal life is a boring life. Now, I don't know where I came up with that, but the adrenaline talked to me one day and that is what it told me. There isn't anything worthwhile about a mundane existence.
So, like I said, the idea was pretty simple. I was bored. I was thirsty. I had been on the road for three straight months and it was starting to catch up to me. I needed a drink, but I had no money. I don't know what I did with the money... maybe I gave it to that boy around the corner over there - he said he was homeless, but you gotta wonder how a homeless kid can afford those
Ray-Bans. Fake, just like the rest of them, but I gave him the money. Or did
I? I just said I didn't know... do I know now? I think I confused myself again. Who are you?
... The plan. Yes, the plan... I was going to go to a bar and "accidentally" provoke the tough-guy crowd. They were gonna start to mess with me but I'd refuse to fall for it. They'd sit back down and I'd do something to really piss them off again a few minutes later. Viola... instant barfight. I get a good fight to clear my head, a good drink to clear the sinuses (really, I take it just for the sinuses), and then I can leave in the brawl without paying.
Sounded awesome. Until I wound up getting hurt and in this damned hospital. I can't even sit up properly - the first time I tried, I passed out. I saw all sorts of pretty little lights floating about, liquid chrome passing through strobe lights and I thought I was in heaven until I realized it was just my brain.
Well, to tell you the truth, it hurt like a sumbitch, but the experience was so great that I tried it again a couple more times.
The doctor comes up to me and tells me, "Be patient". But I'm already a patient, so why is he telling me to become one? Is being a patient a mental state of being? He's crazy. They're all crazy.
I hate being in here. It's so constricted here... so confiscated, stifling... so... mechanical. There's rules for everything. There's rules for what to do in case of a fire, there's rules for incoming patients, for outgoing patients, for patients who were in here before, for patients who are dead, for patients who are crazy... when you fill your life full of rules, you become a machine.
And it's really sad that I see these people being the same way as those on the outside. They all live by the rules. You see, even if they say they are breaking the rules and doing what THEY want, they are only living up to society's expectations of them? They're only TRYING to be different. That isn't it at all. You don't TRY to be your own self... you either ARE or you
AREN'T. There is no amount of effort involved.
And all the men and women I see on the outside are so fake, so predictable... they're automatons covered in a shell of rules that govern them.
There's no unpredictability. It's just like wrestling, really. You can read
'em all one-by-one, like a book. It's the same damn book, over and over again.
Oh, there's a few changes here and there. But it's all the same. They rolled a man by here the other day... he was on a stretcher, surviving off life-support only. It reminded me of these guys on the outside, on the inside... hell, everywhere. When you resort to repetition, you're on life-support. When life becomes a routine, how is it worth living? I don't know. Maybe I think too much.
I'm just a patient. I'm just not patient. =========================================================== TO BE ADDED TO THE MAILING LIST E-MAIL [detail withheld]
