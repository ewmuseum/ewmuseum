A large-roster federation the community itself debated in a six-post thread, named as the Electronic Wrestling Federation and run at a site called ragedwar.com. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Electronic Wrestling Federation ran from a site called Ragedwar, and by the time its own community began debating it, in a six-post thread across 23–25 May 2002, it already carried more than sixty wrestlers on its roster — a scale one rival promoter panned outright: “Any fed involving more than SIXTY (yes, SIXTY) wrestlers is garbage.”

The same critic pointed to a storyline as evidence of the fed’s own daring: a wrestler billed as Andrew Attitude was, in the critic’s words, writing “a four-part roleplay about questioning his sexuality” — the kind of angle the thread’s participants argued over rather than agreed on.

A fuller review named EFWO’s own championship and roster culture directly: the reigning women’s champion emulated the real WWF star Trish Stratus, right down to using her photo, while two wrestlers, Jade and Jessica Hagan, were both former world champions still waiting, by the review’s account, on a push to match; a stable the review called the Bartuzzunnini group — “an all female mafia” — was singled out as one of the more original ideas on the roster, and one wrestler, Salamah Ali, was said to have quit the promotion outright over how it was run.

One wrestler on the roster answered the criticism directly, pointing to her own record in the wider community’s rankings — placings of 44th and 136th in the RSPWF Top 200 polls of 1999 and 1998, third among Woman Wrestlers in 1999’s mid-year awards, 92nd in the 1999 E-W Zone mid-year Top 100, and a tie for first among Woman Wrestlers in the 1998 year-end awards. A later reply defended EFWO’s own writing on its merits and answered the thread with a roleplay of its own.

No founding document or closing notice survives; how EFWO began, how the disputes recorded in this thread resolved, or how the promotion ended, is not in the surviving record.
