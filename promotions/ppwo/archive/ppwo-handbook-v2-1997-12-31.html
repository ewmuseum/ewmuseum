Introduction... PPWO(PHOENIX Pro) is an E-Fed that made it's appearence first in June of 1997, then known simply as PPW. After a few wrong turns, it was given a full face lift, becoming PHOENIX Pro. At that point, PPWO got it's first measure of sucess, luring in several big names, and holding top notch matches for slightly over 2 monthes before closing down due to personal conflicts among staff members. Now, in early October '97, just as the mythical Firebird where PPWO draws it's name from, we are ready to be reborn and to rise to new hights from the ashes of the past... =========<> *PPWO Info... *Sucess in the PPWO(How to get it)... I'm sure you're wondering by this point, "How do I suceed in PHOENIX Pro?", well it's simple. There are several ways to reach the top of the PPWO, and here they are... *FLASH or ROLEPLAY-- A flash or a roleplay, is a way your wrestler may speak his mind. If your wrestler prides himself on his work on the mic, then this is your ticket to sucess. If you aren't sure what a RP(roleplay)is, well I'll fill you in now. RPing is easy. The main goals of it are to stay true to charecter, be descriptive, and to get your point across with out babbling. In PPWO, Your Flashes are judged by quality, not quantity. I know, alot of feds say this, but PHOENIX Pro means it. You want to try and RP so that you keep your audience interested. No one wants to read endless flashes that go on and on. *HELP WITH ROLEPLAYING! 1.) Before entering a league, it's a good idea to ask for a few cards to see what else is out there. It gives you a feel of what type of people are out there and will prevent two people from running characters with the exact same names or gimmicks. Do we really need 5 Undertaker clones running around? 2.) Before you pick a gimmick, here's a few things to consider : * Can I effectively run this character? Is this the kind of person I would be interested in writing for. For example, I once came up with a Russian Olympian type character. I had moves and an outfit and everything... then I had to write interviews and came to the realization... I couldn't come up with a believable stuff for this guy to say because I didn't know Russian and couldn't fake an accent. * Is this character believable? I personally like characters that are realistic. A guy who magically disappears or other such crap can really ruin the game. A human element helps certain characters. People have a tendency to go overboard when pushing a character (usually applies to heels) and then you get McMahon syndrome. Like a guy who was supposedly a drug dealer like character or a nuclear mutant suddenly becomes a face. Yeah, he was eating children yesterday, but now he's a fine upstanding individual! * Be careful with comedy gimmicks. They can be good and bad. Some guys can write comedy, others can't. Also, what you might think is funny, others might consider really lame. And in case you're wondering, I hated Doink (as a heel and face.) As a wise man once said "Dying is easy, comedy is hard." * Keep in mind, not all faces are friends and the same holds true for heels. Some of the biggest (and hottest) feuds in e-wrestling have been between guys of the same alignment. INTERVIEWS : * Try to keep them coherent. Spell check them if possible. * Say what you want to say, but you don't have to write an epic. A lot of the best interviews I've seen have been relatively short (one to two screens long). Who wants to read through 80 pages of ranting? * Try to stick to your alliance. Faces in general shouldn't be egomaniacs and should generally try to be nice guys or at least likable. One of the many reasons I hate Hogan. * Try to avoid "cheap heat". Cheap heat can be something that's in bad taste. Try to avoid things that are racist or in bad taste (such as beating up women). There are more effective ways to get characters over. I won Heel of the Year for a character who wrestled cleanly, but he had an incredibly huge ego and really was good at getting under other wrestlers' skins. I can't really give tips on being a face as I've found it very difficult. Most of my characters are heels, I've had only a handful of faces and there's only one of them which I thought was interesting as a face. This is something which is partially interviews, partially GM responsibility. One of the things I tend to hate is when players give their characters mega pushes as a face or a heel. If everyone in the game wasn't so lazy :-), I would like to see a somewhat frequent poll of the players to see who they think is good at getting their character over or not. There are several levels of characters : Over Heel : Ric Flair, most ECW guys. The marks hate them, but they've got a style to them that they get some cheers. Not Over Heel : The Honky Tonk Man. This character sucks. Everyone hates them. Open chants of "YOU SUCK" occur when character is around. Over Face : The guy is over. Popular as can be. Guys like Sting and Bret Hart. (I would include Hogan, but he is the anti-Christ.) Not Over Face : pre-turn Bob Backlund, Lex Luger. Yeah, yeah, yeah. Shut up. You've got the charisma of a rock. From there, the GM can gauge how over the character is. That way you don't have characters whose interviews really suck or doesn't act like a face getting huge crowd pops. WHAT TO SEND? v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v - TO GET MATCHES: Challenge, Challenge, Challenge! - TO WIN: Roleplay, Roleplay, and (did I mention?) Roleplay! - ALSO IT HELPS TO: In Ring (Sudden Death) interviews! Just leave space for the announcers to ask questions (or even write them yourself). You fill in the answers or whatever and we'll do the rest! - AND IT NEVER HURTS TO: Send in a STRAT! That's short for STRATEGY. A strategy would include how you want your guy to go about wrestling the match, or any sneak attacks/double crosses that you want to see happen. This is not required every time ... just when you want something special to happen. It REALLY helps improve the angles and plotlines. We can't always follow your suggestion to a "T," but we will try. Please LABEL it as a "Roleplay," a "Strat," or whatever it is in the subject line, followed by your character's name. Keeps us organized! :-) Also a "[PPWO]" never hurts. PPWO Wrestlers: This short information guide should help you immensly on your league participation. Things such as Strats, In Ring Interviews and Roleplays will impact your success rate in PPWO by 100%! So you'd better stay on task and start that roleplaying, folks! Here it is... v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^ v^v^v^v^ A. STRATS! v^v^v^v^v^ v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^ What is a STRAT? Well, guys, if you don't know already... STRAT is short for Strategy. For any match that you compete in here in the PPWO YOU are entitled to 1 match strategy. This STRAT will tell us how to play your character in the matchup, and it will help your chances of winning. For instance... [Bob is set to wrestle Joe on the 23rd of January. He knows that Joe has out roleplayed him and he has very little chance of winning the matchup. In order to help him in his matchup, Bob decides to write a strat. His strat is very simple and it consists of attempting to go at Joe's knees. Here is his STRAT for you to use as an example.] Dear President O'Neill I know Joe is going to kick my ass on the 23rd, but I will come at his knees with a variety of manovers consisting of Figure Four Leglocks, Grapevines, Clips, and more. At one point in the matchup, my guy Bob will attempt to take him outside of the ring for some action and attack him with a chair. That's my STRAT, sir. Thank you! From, Bob Members: Sending in STRATS is a great and easy way to add fun to the matchup and you can get a chance to see how your strategy matches up against your opponents. You are required one strat per match. So for_YOUR_upcoming matchup... get it to: [detail withheld] , Thanks, guys. In Ring Interviews! v^v^v^v^v^ v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^ What Is An In-Ring or On-Card Interview? Well, guys, have you ever seen a WWF or WCW contest where at a certain point the broadcaster says, "Let's go to pre-recorded comments from ____." ? If you haven't, you mustn't be a very avid wrestling fan! That is what an On-Card interview is. Sometimes your wrestler will want to make last minute comments about a matchup and I'll publish the on the card. Or, sometimes your guy will want to grab the mike and rant about his problems in the ring. Will It Help My Roleplaying Standing? Of course it will! In Ring or On Card Interviews are treated at the same level as roleplays, and sometimes higher level. Each card we put out should have at least _TWO_In ring or On Card interviews. If you'd like to write one up, send it to your buddy at: [detail withheld] , Thanks, and get writing! ^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^ ^v^v^v^v^ C. Roleplaying v^v^v^v^v^v^ ^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^ Welcome to the PPWO Roleplaying Tips sheet. If you are a newbie to the e-wrestling world, this should give you a jump start on how to roleplay and should help you along your journey to being a PPWO Champion! Read this closely and carefully and make sure to drop [detail withheld] telling him how you liked the sheet. Well, anyways, thanks and lets get right into the tips, shall we? ---InDeX--- + Content + Length + Detail +-+-+-+-+-+ Content +-+-+-+-+-+-+ + What Is Content? + Content is not the length the roleplay but the message that the roleplay gets across. Content is one of the three most important parts of a roleplay. A roleplay without content is just jabber and jargon thrown into a file. You must always have some sort of content in your roleplays. + Set A Goal + When you begin a roleplay, set a goal for the content you want to include in your roleplay. Tell yourself what, in a brief rundown, that you want to tell everybody in this roleplay. If you want to tell the whole FJPW that you are here to stay, then remember that fact while you are writing your roleplay. Never write a roleplay with out any topics to write about. + Does My Roleplay Have Good Content? + If you want to know if your roleplay has any content, then read it over and ask yourself what the message you got acrossed was. If you can't answer that question then the chances are, you have no body and content to your roleplay and you need to start over and try again. + More Content The Better + The more topics you have to discuss, the better. You can never have enough content. If you have enough topics to write 5 pages or so, then so be it and go though with it. Content is good people! + Elaborate on Your Topic + If your topic is "I want to challenge Squirt to a match" then ask yourself all the possible questions to stimulate ideas! Ask yourself why you want to wrestle Squirt. Ask yourself how good of a chance you have at defeating Squirt. Ask yourself what type of match you want to challenge Squirt to. Ask youself when you want to wrestle Squirt! The possibilities are endless, people! Cover every area you can! +-+-+-+-+-+-+- Length -+-+-+-+-+-+-+ + Length Is Important + One of the most important parts of your roleplay is its length. You're roleplay MUST be at least be 30 lines (3k) to get anything acrossed in our opinion's. Although it is hard to find the happy-medium between too long and too short, you'll get there if you follow these steps! + Not Too Long + Don't wear your topic down so much that nobody is going to be interested in reading it anymore. Cover what you need but don't exasperate the subject. How do you feel when you watch a 20 minute Hacksaw Duggan matchup featuring a chinlock and a clothesline? That's how people feel reading a 20 k roleplay. Once you stop having fun writing your roleplay it's probably finished. + Not Too Short + You must'nt send in a roleplay that is 3 lines long. In FJPW, everytime you send in a roleplay that is 10 or less lines, that's an automatic loss. Make your roleplay to the point but long enough so it'll take a few minutes to read and leave the reader with a good feeling. Touch base with the subject quite a bit. +-+-+-+-+-+-+- Detail -+-+-+-+-+-+-+ + Add A Beginning and Conclusion + At the beginning of your roleplay, you must _ALWAYS_ add a beginning. A beginning tells who, what, where, when and why of the roleplay. A beginning is also put inside action bars which are () or [] or :: :: or {} In a beginning, you should describe what the character is wearing, what his mood is like, where his is, what he's doing there, ect. ex. [The screen fades in to Squirt. He's sitting in a dark living room rocking back and forth on an easychair. He's wearing his street clothes which consist of a pair of faded denimn jeans, a black tank top, and a nike headband. His black hair is slicked back and his eyes are squinted and feirce. The only illumination in the room is cast by a fireplace with the embers slowly dying out. The cameraman gives him a close up as signals to come over to him.] + Add a Conclusion + The conclusion is more of the same except it would say things like, Squirt is fading out, ect. Be carefull not to make these too drab. It can get tricky to think up the setting some times. <Hint: The setting could have something to do with the content.> + Watch The Spelling + Go through the document numerous times to make sure you did'nt make any foolish spelling mistakes, make sure you add two spaces after a period, you inserted commas where needed, you did'nt have any fragments or run-ons. Don't make yourself look foolish by saying stuff like this: ex. (Wrtler): "im cmin to getg you cuz of what you ded to me that tyme when u did dat thing.." Well, I hope this helped you out with your roleplaying. Now_ROLEPLAY_ROLEPLAY_ROLEPLAY!!! And remember when you _ROLEPLAY_ROLEPLAY_ROLEPLAY_!!! you send them to [detail withheld] . :) *WINNING ON WRESTLING ABILITY-- In real life, there are wrestlers who don't give extensive interviews, yet still suceed. Take Dean Malenko for instance. Malenko did not gain his reputation by working the mic. He did so with his moves in the ring. So, in PHOENIX Pro, we offer you the option of doing this. How so? Well it's simple. If you aren't the best RPer in the land, you can do things such as writing "Showcase" matches for yourself(See Section On Showcase Matches), sending in Match Strats(See Section On Match Strats), and plenty of Challenges(See Section On Challenges). *SHOWCASE MATCHES-- A showcase match is a match against a low level wrestler or as much as I hate to say it, a "jobber". The PPWO has several "Showcase" wrestler, you can find a list of them at the PPWO Home Page. Matches against Showcase wrestlers don't count as much in the rankings as matches against Superstars, but they are an undeniable part of the game. PPWO encourages, and in some cases requires handlers to write one Showcase match a month. There are several benefits to writing Showcase matches for your wrestler. It shows us how your wrestler acts in a match, it gives you points in the rankings, and gives your wrestler more exposure. It's not hard to do, just write what happens, and we'll edit it, putting it on the card. *STRATS-- Strat is short for strategy, which is basically just what you want your wrestler to do in a match. It's an extra simple way to participate. All you have to do is to send a mail to myself saying something such as-- "Mr.President, I know my opponent is stronger, so I want to use a "hit & run" strategy in our match on tuesday. If at all possible, I'd like to take him outside the ring and use a chair to take him out for the 10 count. Thank you.". It could be even more simple, or more complex, it's up to you! You can even send in a strat saying you want your friend to interfere for you. Your options are limitless. *CHALLENGES-- If you would like to challenge a wrestler, you could do it in a few ways. Either do it in an interview(Flash or InRing), or simply send in a 'contract' to the president. All this is, is a message saying "My wrestler(whatever his name is), would like a match(specify on what kind of match)with this wrestler(the guy you're challengeing) on Tuesday." I will then forward the 'contract' to the man your challening, and if he agrees, the match is signed. *TITLES(What they are & order of importance)... Defitnently a great stepping stone to world title shots. PPWO Grand Canyon Title(Lightweight) - For Cruisers. PPWO Tucson Heavyweight Tag Team Titles- This is self explanatory, and more or less goes by the same guidelines as the heavyweight singles title. *NOTE* More titles may be added once we pick up momentum. *CARDS- As of this moment, the PPWO will only have one televised card a week(until we pick up momentum & some writers that is). We will also put out house shows 1-3 times a week. On the big show we'll have 8-10 matches, 5-6 live and 2-3 summarized. Of course, this all depends on the reaction of this federation, and the number of writers we have. *PPWO NEWSLETTER- Every once in a while (At LEAST once a month.) PPWO will put out a special newsletter keeping newcomers updated on the happenings in the PPWO. It will also feature rumors, and anything else that would enlighten you in the knowledge of e-wrestling. (Damn, sometimes I sound gay.) *PAY PER VIEW- Grand Canyon COLLISION!
