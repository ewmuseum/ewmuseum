A GeoCities-hosted e-fed founded as plain "PPW" in June 1997, folded after two months, and reborn in October as Phoenix Pro/PPWO under the same president, running weekly Friday Night Carnage and Sunday Sudden Death cards coordinated over IRC. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Phoenix Professional Wrestling Organization tells its own origin as a resurrection. Its Handbook opens: “PPWO (PHOENIX Pro) is an E-Fed that made it's appearence first in June of 1997, then known simply as PPW… holding top notch matches for slightly over 2 monthes before closing down due to personal conflicts among staff members. Now, in early October '97, just as the mythical Firebird where PPWO draws it's name from, we are ready to be reborn.” It relaunched on GeoCities, coordinated through an IRC channel on EFNet, and billed itself, tongue in cheek, as “PPWO…Please Sir…May I Have Another?!?”

Its format was built entirely on flashes and roleplays, judged on content over length, submitted by e-mail to its own staff and scored under a booking system weighting roleplays and in-ring promos three-quarters of a match's outcome. Two weekly cards ran, Friday Night Carnage and Sunday Sudden Death, under a four-title ladder — a Heavyweight Championship at the top, a weekly-defended Arizona State Championship as its stepping stone, a Grand Canyon Championship for cruiserweights, and a Tucson Heavyweight Tag Team Championship — with an eight-man Heavyweight tournament called Grand Canyon Collision planned as its first pay-per-view.

Its first card, Sudden Death, ran 16 November 1997 out of what it called the PPWO Convention Center in Phoenix, Arizona, headlined by an opening tag team title tournament pitting the Showstoppers against Enforcement and Elimination among four scheduled bouts. By its third Matchwatch newsletter, dated 11 December 1997, two of its four titles had been claimed and neither had lost: J'ster held the Arizona State Championship unbeaten at 2-0-0 since 23 November, and the tag team Money and Power held the Tucson titles unbeaten at 3-0-0 since 8 November, while the Heavyweight and Grand Canyon Championships stood vacant.

Its own record runs to 31 December 1997, a second Handbook edition with both reigns still standing and Grand Canyon Collision still unrun. No closing statement of its own survives past that date.
