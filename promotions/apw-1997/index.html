A flash/roleplay-decided independent whose 1997 record runs from a Newcastle rename card through a self-documenting war with an interfed sanctioning body to an in-kayfabe sale of the promotion itself. Eleven title lineages, ten shows and 45 wrestlers survive from that year. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

All-Pro Wrestling began life as All-Pro Championship Wrestling, launched 1 July 1996, and shortened its own name inside a two-day window in March 1997: the same pay-per-view, Anarchy in the UK II, was advertised under the old name on 7 March and the new one on 9 March.

Its own house style ran on baroque hardcore stipulations — a Four-Way-Two-Ring Barbed-Wire-Cage-Glass-Chain Death Match, a Barbed-Wire-Scaffold-Fire-Thumbtack Death Match — decided by roleplay rather than simulation, on the explicit policy that “if you don't flash you get jobbed,” with new handlers serving a 21-day trial before their wrestlers counted for anything.

In October 1997 a rival interfederation tournament's own newly-announced governing council publicly barred the promotion's wrestlers from entering at all. The promotion's own owner answered in kind, in a public reply naming his own champions — Creed, world champion Chris Douglas, Mondo Somatic, the Syndicate, Pan & Puck, Brandon Charm — and inviting every other wrestler in the banned tournament to defect, calling the ban “a very low-class move... expected from a 12-year-old.” Days later the tournament's own organizer backed down entirely, admitting there had never been an official council at all — “it was the first thing that came to mind” — and apologized to the promotion by name.

That same month its own founding operator announced he had “sold that fed for a tonne of money to MegaCorp,” and the promotion continued under new ownership into a self-declared “Era 2,” running a Christmas Carnage card that December and congratulating its own wrestlers on their newsgroup Top 200 rankings on 18 December 1997.

Its own founding operator returned to the newsgroup one last time on 19 December 1997 to say goodbye to e-wrestling entirely, losing his internet service and moving on for good, thanking the owners of the wrestlers who had “made the fed turn” and closing simply: “Thanks everyone for the good times and the bad times. Bye.” Nothing further under either name is in the surviving record.
