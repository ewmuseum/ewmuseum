Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The XWF X-Treme Wrestling Federation announced itself on Usenet on 14 January 2000, in a recruitment post describing itself in the plainest terms available: “I have made my 1st web page ... it is an internet role-play wrestling E-Federation.” The post promised three weekly cards, one monthly pay-per-view, a message board, a chat room, and an annual award show, and asked only for original, “home made” wrestlers, up to two per writer.

Its own name was contested almost as soon as it opened. By March, at least three further, unrelated Usenet postings were claiming the same four letters for federations of their own, one of them insisting outright that it, and not the others, was “THE REAL XWF.” None of the rival claims left a surviving card or roster; the one that did, reachable at its own recurring web address and its own dated broadcasts, is the one this record follows.

By April the federation was running on a three-night weekly clock: Friday Night Fury, Wednesday Night Live, and Monday Night Massacre, each posted in numbered installments across several Usenet messages the same day or the next. Five championships ran through the roster at once — the World, Submission, Extreme, and Villains titles, contested in ordinary matches, and a Hotline title that changed hands inside a single battle royal whenever the reigning champion was pinned, sometimes six times over in one night.

The federation staged its own bi-annual awards ceremony on 30 April 2000, broadcast across four Usenet posts as a costumed dinner-and-podium show rather than a plain results list. Twenty-six categories were read out, decided partly by fan ballot and, the show's own hosts said, partly by the federation's own staff. The night closed on an in-story attack on the promotion's own on-screen boss during the ceremony itself, and an outdoor, storm-lashed, buried-alive match for the Extreme Championship that the show's own voters separately named the greatest match in the federation's history.

No closing statement survives. The federation's own last attested activity in the surviving record is a June 2000 recruitment post seeking a replacement for a departing champion. By autumn, several further, differently run web pages were using the same four letters, none of them naming or otherwise connecting to this one.
