We understand that this will be a long and hard task. However we are here for the long haul. We hand pick our members. Many people have applied to enter the NAWC. Roughly 60% of them are accepted and welcomed into our ranks. Yes, indeed, this is a high percentage. Consider the fact that the standard we wish to maintain is relitively easy to reach for most wrestlers. The other 40%, can join another federation where their skills would be better utilized. They would not last long among the talented ranks here in the NAWC.
NAWC History - My name is [name withheld]. I am the sole owner of the NAWC. I have been saving up funds for quite a long time, preparing for the arrival of the NAWC. Then, on October 20, 1999, construction of our Headquarters in Chicago, Illinois was completed and our doors opened. Only a day or two later we received our first two applicants. They were quickly accepted, keeping in mind the level of quality we wanted to maintain. Throughout the early weeks of the NAWC's existance, we continued to grow. Many more talented superstars flocked to our revolving door. But we lost a few in those early days as well. We continued to grow, then, on Sunday, November 28, 1999, we held the NAWC's inaugural Pay-Per-View, Injustice For All. The show was highly praised by critics. It debuted many new stars. And, for the first time in NAWC history, a World Heavyweight Champion was crowned. Two months have passed, still more people have came and went. Several titles have exchanged hands multiple times. Now, we are only a week away from our second Pay-Per-View extravaganza, Nuclear Winter.
OUR STAFF Real-life Staff - [name withheld] - Owner - Palmer doesn't normally get involved in any of the shows. He's the guy that signs the checks and hires or fires wrestlers. You don't want to be in this guy's bad books. He can make your career miserable.
Al Capulco - President - The President is the man in charge of shows. The manager of Rio D. Giniro, don't cross him or he'll set Rio loose on you. He can also make matches, or take matches off a card at will. [Capulco isn't the "real" President of the company. It is just for an angle.]
Fictional Staff - Derek Schilling - Vice President - Schilling is a quiet man. He has the ability to add or change matches at will.
Booker Extreme - Head Booker - The Head Booker has the ability to add or remove matches from any card.
Brent Jacobson - Ring Announcer - Brent is strictly business. He is a very cowardly announcer as he'll run off if a wrestler even looks at him.
"Smokey" Joe Reynolds - Commentator - Smokey is a true face commentator. He always roots for the good guys. But will call a match down the middle all the same. He is very opinionated and heel wrestlers often threaten him.But Smokey just keeps going. He's not afraid to speak his mind.
Jenny Winston - Commentator - Jenny is a beautiful woman of around 30 years. She is a true feminist and is also a fan of the good guys.
Gerry McKinley - Backup Commentator - Gerry is a solid heel commentator. He always roots for the heels. He has a big mouth and often gets in trouble for what he says. He is very cocky. He is the Jerry Lawlor of the NAWC. Gerry is mostly used for PPVs as he does a lot of color commentary.
Doc Draven - Head Referee - Doc is a longtime veteran of wrestling. He knows the rules inside out. He gives people a hard time for cheating and interference. However, he'll let matches go on. He will count a pin at an average pace. And if knocked out, he will not disqualify anyone when he recovers. While counting the pin, he knows the exact spot to see all cheating attempts.
Kevin Bryant - Backup Referee - Bryant is mostly used for the Hardcore matches. He is a young referee that loves the newer styles of wrestling. Occasionally, when properly motivated, he'll side with a certain person or group and make them win at all costs.
Sharon Roberts - Head Reporter - Sharon is another feminist like Jenny Winston. She gets along better with the faces and fears the heels. She is often known to flirt with the faces. They like her and are usually willing to give an interview. Sharon is a very attractive woman and is extremely popular with the men. Valets often get jealous and fear that she will take their men away. This can often cause friction between her and the women of the NAWC.
Orlando Palla - Reporter - Orlando likes to interview the heel wrestlers more than anyone. He is very perverted and will often try to sneak into the women's locker rooms to get a peek at them.Valets beware.
Gregory Hollinsworth - Reporter - Hollinsworth is a wrestling buff. He wrestled professionally in the late 70s. A fan of traditional style wrestling, Gregory favors interviews with face wrestlers who are against the hardcore matches. If he is assigned an interview with a heel, he is often known to run off in terror.
RULES Rules when Joining the NAWC - You must submit a sample roleplay before you are allowed to join. This is very important as we want handlers with a good future in e-wrestling.
Each handler is allowed two wrestlers. No real wrestlers and no ripoffs are allowed. Do's and Don'ts of Roleplaying - DOs Roleplay at least once a week. Write more than ten lines. Send your roleplays to me (email address given when you join). DON'Ts Swear more than what is said in the WWF. Use sexual content in your roleplays. Mention "real wrestling federations" such as the WWF, WCW, or ECW in your roleplays Mension other e-federations in your roleplays. Mention past titles you've won in other federations. We don't care here. You start out new and you have to work your way up.
Use e-wrestling or insider terms in your roleplays such as, "jobber, e-fed, roleplay, angle, feud," etc.
Don't use common catch phrases found in the WWF, WCW, or ECW. Such catchphrases include The Rock's "Do you smell what the Rock is cookin?" The Rock's "Know your role, and shut your mouth, " "jaboni" and the like.
Tips - Be creative. An uncreative roleplay would be something like, "Jobber's music plays. Jobber comes down to the ring with a mic. Jobber says, "Hey, one of you wrestlers in the back! Come out here and i'll kick your ass!!!" Jobber leaves the ring and goes to the back." These are the typical style of 12 year old handlers. Don't fall to that level. Also, set the scene in your roleplay. Tell us where your wrestler is. What he is doing. What's going on around him. Things such as these will make your roleplay more realistic and believable.
Miscellaneous - Roleplays are sent out in packets three times a week; on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays. Occasionally we'll make an exception to this. You may find announcements at the beginning or end of a packet sometimes as well.
NAWC EVENTS House Shows - We only hold House Shows right before a Pay-Per-View. However, we are in talks with arenas for doing a bi-weekly house show as well. This is only in the planning stages at the moment. But we do have House Shows some time the week before a PPV. That is definite.
Weekly Cards - We have two weekly cards. However they are not held on the same week. They alternate once every other week. The main show is called Fight Night. It is held on Fridays and is written in a full play-by-play format. The other, Saturday Napalm, combines play-by-play with matches written in a summary form.
Pay-Per-Views - We hold a Pay-Per-View once every two months. This is to help us work on storylines, feuds, and angles better. It also helps us build up to a climate for the PPV show. PPVs are held on the last Sunday of their specific month. The name of the PPV is announced right after the Pay-Per-View before it.
Miscellaneous - All our events are sent out in segments. These segments usually contain one or more matches.
NAWC TITLES Titles Available - There are currently seven titles in the NAWC. Here is a list of them. They are in order of how valuable they are to be considered.
NAWC World Heavyweight Championship NAWC Tag Team titles NAWC Intercontinental title NAWC Hardcore title NAWC Women's title NAWC Television title NAWC Lightweight title Defending Titles - This section details how often titles should be defended. NAWC World Heavyweight Championship title - Must be defended at least once a month plus at the PPV.
NAWC Tag Team titles - Must be defended at least once every month plus on the PPVs. NAWC Intercontinental title - Must be defended at least once every three weeks plus at the PPVs.
NAWC Hardcore title - Must be defended at least once every two weeks plus at every PPV. NAWC Women's title - Must be defended every two weeks at least plus on the PPVs. NAWC Television title - Must be defended every week plus at the PPVs. NAWC Lightweight title - Must be defended at every single card plus on PPVs. If any of these titles are not defended within the specified time given, then it will be vacated.
ANGLES / FEUDS We value angles and feuds highly in the NAWC. If you have a suggestion for a feud or angle, make sure to email me and the other handler in question about it. We'll go over it and if it sounds good, I'll incorporate it into the cards. Be original with your feuds and angles. Don't rip off other feds ideas. And, if you're in a feud or angle with somebody, and they win. But you roleplayed better, don't complain about it. Your loss furthered the angle and you'll usually get payback somewhere in the future against that same person.
