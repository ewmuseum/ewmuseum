Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The North American Wrestling Council opened its own doors on 20 October 1999 — “Today, Wednesday, October 20, 1999, marks the day that the North American Wrestling Council's doors have opened” — built by its own founder and owner, Adrian Palmer, who ran the promotion's business from behind the scenes while occasionally stepping in front of the camera himself to hire, fire, or make an announcement. Its own Fictional Staff, laid out in a later FAQ, staged an entire front office as invented characters around him: President Al Capulco, manager of wrestler Rio D. Giniro; face and heel commentators Jenny Winston and “Smokey” Joe Reynolds; and a color man, Gerry McKinley, the FAQ calling him “the Jerry Lawlor of the NAWC.”

Its own second Fight Night Friday, on 5 November, ran a Royal Rumble to crown a first Television Champion, won by Rio D. Giniro over a field that included Killer Buddha and the debuting Cyric. Three weeks later, on 28 November, the promotion's inaugural pay-per-view, Injustice For All, crowned its own first World Heavyweight Champion in a lumberjack match: Dr. Hate, backed by his own faction of Cyric and Raul De'Ganeir, beat “Cavalier” Tom Knight while every other wrestler on the tournament card stood ringside as enforcers. The same card saw Rick Stevens dethrone Rio D. Giniro for the Television title.

By the promotion's Fight Night Friday of 10 December, Dr. Hate was still champion, cutting a promo goading challenger Thane Sloan over his own education (“the great, magnificent Einstein of our time, you with your three-year degree”) and turning away a fourth man, Kasady, from joining his faction outright: “We'd planned, right from the start, to be a threesome and no more.”

The promotion's own last surviving card, a Fight Night Friday taped Christmas Eve, crowned three champions inside one broadcast: Kasady won the vacant Lightweight title, Killer Buddha took the Intercontinental title by disqualification after a Cayn Bomb spot gone wrong, and Rio D. Giniro, with Al Capulco now installed as his manager and the promotion's own president both, beat Thane Sloan for the World Heavyweight Championship — Dr. Hate having lost the belt to Sloan somewhere in the two weeks the surviving record does not cover.

Forty wrestlers and one tag team, the Down Under Dogz, are named across the seven shows served here, with four titles and seven traced reigns among them. How the promotion's own second pay-per-view, Nuclear Winter, played out isn't in the surviving record; the Christmas Eve broadcast ends with a freshly crowned World Champion and no sign of closing.
