An October 1997 Indianapolis-based fed introducing its own roster and titles in a two-part broadcast fronted by an on-screen president persona. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Two undated recruitment posts to rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy introduced the Ultimate Internet Wrestling Organization from a GeoCities home page, calling for “1 single wrestler and 1 tag-team or 2 single wrestlers,” open to invented names though it allowed it “may except a few real wrestlers” too. It ran three weekly cards — Monday Night Mania, Thursday Night Clash and UIWO Saturday Night — and a pay-per-view every other month, sanctioning four titles: the World Heavyweight, United States Heavyweight, World Television and World Heavyweight Tag-Team championships.

It staged its own introduction on 21 October 1997, opening (in its own words) with fireworks and a 3D logo live from Indianapolis, hosted on-screen by its president, Derek Parks. Parks set the promotion's first card, Monday Night Mania, for 3 November at Market Square Arena, and its first pay-per-view, Winterbash 97, for 6 December at the RCA Dome — a sixteen-man tournament for the United States Heavyweight title, three-way matches for the Television and Tag-Team titles, and a steel-cage, no-time-limit, no-disqualification main event for the World Heavyweight Championship.

Parks introduced his broadcaster, Floyde Bennett Jr., a self-described former professional wrestler — ring name Peacemaker, a two-time state amateur champion before turning pro — who retired after a 1990 knee injury and moved into wrestling journalism; Bennett Jr. then interviewed three of the promotion's own wrestlers on camera. Whirlwind, billed as the first man signed, teased “secret talks” with an unnamed opponent and appeared with his manager, his own uncle Zeus. General Lee, introduced as the son of a wrestler named on air as General Red Lee Johnston, dismissed the roster as full of men who “want to be just like me” and challenged everyone in the promotion to a match. Libber Lipped Lenny closed the broadcast promising to leave Winterbash 97 as the first UIWO World Heavyweight Champion.

No further document of the promotion survives — not the broadcast's own promised second part, not a record of its 3 November debut card, and not a result of its December pay-per-view.
