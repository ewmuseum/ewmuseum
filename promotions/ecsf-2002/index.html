Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

East Coast Street-Fighting introduced itself in two writer-recruitment posts, 11 and 18 January 2002, as “a fighting fed with quite an interesting concept” — an angle-based promotion whose wrestlers acted out a criminal’s daily life, with in-character roleplays styled as “Lock-Ups” and a weekly show, On The Run, built to advance those storylines rather than just stage a match.

Its own writer categories describe the show in more detail than most first-wave recruitment posts manage: “Normal Fights Writers” for its standard, roughly four-kilobyte bouts; “Guard Fights Writers” for the zanier undercard matches between its guard characters; and “Segment Writers,” who read a wrestler’s own Lock-Up to write the follow-up developing that storyline on the show itself.

No roster, no card, and no closing notice survive; whether East Coast Street-Fighting ever ran On The Run past its own announcement is not something its own surviving record answers.
