For General Arain and Colonel Mustafa, their tenure in the NPWA wasn't always as rewarding. In the beginning there was anguish and pain for the then up & coming superstars. No one knew their name. No one knew their legacy. No one knew their triumph that would one day amount to the Tag Team championship. The Massacre were accepted into the NPWA in April of 1996. They were secretly two of Mr. Nice Guy's lumberjacks at Inhuman 1996. This match would lead to a long-lasting bond between the team and the friendly one that still abounds today despite Mr. Nice Guy's unfortunate departure. Their first big break came at Capitol Punishment 1996. They were the opening match against the original Cadaver Connection, an established and feared tag-team within the NPWA. Although they were defeated, they made their name known among the NPWA wrestlers and fans. The champs at the time saw that. The obvious fear of losing their Tag Team gold was there. The original Asian Strikers and the Massacre had many matches, but in the end the Strikers had the last laugh. The Massacre had experienced their first feud with a reputable tag-team. With some gained experience on their back, they were ready for a new challenge... but this "new challenge" overwhelmed them. Resident Evil. They almost got rid of the Massacre for good in the NPWA in late 1996 by seriously injuring the General. But the ultimate price would be paid. The two teams met in a epic "Aluminum Baseball Bat Match" at Valentine's Day Massacre 1997. Both teams gave it their all, but the Massacre had more in them. They WANTED to win, and they did. Now with victories against the NPWA's most respectable teams, they geared up for "April Assault: Vendetta in Vegas, Warfare at Wembley." They were up for their 3rd title shot since coming to the NPWA. The first 2 didn't go as expected, but the third time was the charm. The Muslim Massacre defeated the New Cadaver Connection for the NPWA Tag Team titles. Now, they have reached the top of the mountain after over a year of intense competition. One of the oldest teams in the NPWA has finally risen above the pack. But it doesn't stop there. They plan to defend the titles in true NPWA honor and spirit. Who knows how long they will keep their well-deserved titles. With many new talented tag-teams coming into the NPWA, their tenure looks questionable... "Hey, there's two NPWA shows a week. What gives? [Masters pauses, licks his lips, and smiles.] BM: You know... they say God works in mysterious ways, Skip. But the truth of the matter is... God don't have [CENSORED] on Brian Masters! [Masters pauses, as the crowd lets out a loud, long cheer.] BM: You wanna know what you saw last Thursday? It's real simple. What you saw was a twofold act. Firstly, it was two real men saving this sport... yet again. And secondly... it was the final chapter in a book which I call "The Education of Steve Sampson." SW: I'm not sure I follow you. BM: I'm sure you don't. I'm sure the little minds out there are all sitting around trying to figure it out. Y'see, Skip... a lot of people in this game are like McGreggor... like Jarrett... they can't see the big picture. They don't understand. Well, that's okay. If they wanna live in the dark, that's fine. But I ain't gonna do it. When I see that for over a year, this league has been led by champions who've been embarassments... it makes me sick. Just makes me absolutely sick. So here's what I do about it. I change it. I even up the odds a little bit, and make it so that a man I can respect... and I *DO* respect... is the champion. SW: Are you afraid of retaliation from the New Wave of Wrestling? BM: Let me tell you something... I ain't afraid of nothin' from the New Wave. Whatta they got? They've got a Freakshow owner... and ain't that appropriate. They've got the biggest damn no-talent juice monkey in the sport, and they've got a guy who's got some weird fetish for his statue. Yeah, there's a group you can be proud of as a wrestler. There's a group you can point to and say "I'm glad THEY'RE the elite in my great sport." Allow me to explain something to you, Wailen... SW: By all means. BM: Do you know why some of these people cheer for me? They cheer for me because I'm like they are. I'm a regular, no-nonsense guy, who's really good at what he does. You can bet that if I come out here and say I'm gonna beat the hell outa you, I'm gonna stand face to face with you, and do it to you, one on one. Maybe I won't win, but you will hurt. And that's the kind of men Steve Sampson is, and the New Wave ain't. And THAT'S why I did what I did. 'Cuz I had to facilitate the change, and I had to help Sampson to save our sport. I guess *THAT* proves what I said all along about it not being anything but business. SW: Are Sampson and you allies now? Even friends? BM: Wrestling, like politics, makes strange bedfellows. But let me just say this. I don't need anyone's hand up my ass to make me feel complete, not like Jarrett and his sick little friends. Ask John Hunter what that's all about... I know he's got my back, and I've got his. But it ain't about this whole fallin' over each other thing. If Sampson needs help, I'll help if he asks. If Sampson needs a partner... yeah, I'll be there. Because I learned something about Sampson in our three matches... he's as tough as I am... maybe tougher. And when it comes to smarts... he's gettin' there fast. SW: Alright... in closing... what about Mick McGreggor? BM: I'll get back to HIM on another day. Right now, all I've got to say is... Steve Sampson, you wanted closure... you GOT IT! Happy Cinqo de Maio, Sampson. ["Army of Me" kicks in and Masters storms off back to the dressing room.] EW: We're completely out of time fans, so be sure to tune in on Thursday night for TNT...for Johnny B.
