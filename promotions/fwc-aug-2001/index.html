Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

FWC was already a month into its run when its own recruiting call went out on 23 August 2001, describing a fed with one weekly card, a weekly hype update, two championship titles and a supercard at the end of every cycle, and claiming momentum with every show since its founding the previous month. Its co-president signed the call himself, asking for tag teams and roleplayers who could turn in “a coherant role-play and the occasional strategy.”

Two Vocal Blend flash shows survive from that August, both built around the Armstrong Championship. On 9 August, titleholder Jack Armstrong billed his own upcoming defense against Mr. Clean as a lopsided mismatch, reaching for a boxing comparison — “Chavez's reasons for the fight: ‘to go out with pride.’ Instead, he went out black and blue” — while his own challenger, Trevor Smith, promised to be the fast one who ate the slow. By the second Vocal Blend on 23 August, Smith had beaten Armstrong for the title, taunted him over the loss, and ended the segment by flinging the belt into a river and declaring it “officially retired.”

Nothing survives past that same-day recruiting call and the two flash shows: no full card, no roster, and no record of whether Trevor Smith's retired title was ever recognized again or how FWC's own run ended.
