GPW — News

Press Release

30 April 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
SAN FRANCISCO - (AP) After a tumultuous year of combined success and hardship, the fledgling Championship Caliber Wrestling has closed its doors.
Fans and employees alike were stunned by the joint decision of CCW founder and President Preston Everett Pressley, III, and Jimmy Jackson James, a
late-term investor in the start-up. Pressley spoke for the duo at a press conference on the subject, while James has remained uncharacteristically silent. "Given our tumultuous track record together--and recent administrative difficulties," Pressley told reporters,
"Jimmy and I decided to put our personal interests and conflicts aside long enough to make a decision in the best interests of our fans, our families, and our employees. As a result, Championship Caliber Wrestling is closing its doors effective immediately." Ironically, the federation's April 28,
2000 close would have marked its 1-year Anniversary.
On the heels of technical difficulties which prevented what was to become
CCW's last HELL TO PAY broadcast from airing in its entirety, many feel the decision is the only one feasible. The April 13, 2000, edition of H2P was preempted before fans could witness a tremendous bout in which CCW's final
World champion, The Punisher, teamed with "Superstar" Kevin Williams to defeat Tommy Gunn and Brett Hunter for the federation's World Tag Team
Titles.
The delinquint and broken broadcast was apparently the final nail in a rocky relationship between the federation and the cable-provider. "ESPN2 has been unhappy about the federation's lagging ratings," one insider confided. In turn, the failing deal with ESPN2 was a sign of bigger things to come.
Other sources confirm rumors that flagship member Tommy Gunn was making the last of his contractually-obligated appearances with the CCW, which could have effected the decision to place the tag titles around the waists of
Williams and The Punisher. "Tommy went to Preston about his decision in advance," a source revealed. "While he's not the most likeable of characters--on OR off-camera--PEP and Gunn have a long-standing, if currently strained, relationship. PEP is a business man, and he realized Gunn's future could lay elsewhere."
As it is, Tommy's future lies with Global Pro-Wrestling. Shortly after his debut with the company in January, Gunn signed a new big-money deal that will likely keep him in the GPW for a long time to come. In addition to Gunn, a number of other high-profile superstars were up for contract renewal--and
Pressley admits that the federation was in no financial situation to negotiate. During the April 13 show, many characters were being written out of future stories pending their contract renewal.
"When you have stars like Tyler Boyd, Brendan Blade and Axl Blaze looking to simultaneously negotiate their contracts," Pressley stated during a frank conversation about his decision, "You need to have all of your ducks in a
row--and some serious cash to back you up." Unfortunately turnout at house shows during the Dazz World Championship reign were lower than expected. This only served to compound financial strain accrued by the federation's NOVEMBER REIGN pay-per-view. "We tried to expand too quickly. We started with the intention of being a small, regional promotion," Pressley stated. "Then, when the ESPN2 offer was presented to us, our focus shifted dramatically, and, in the long run, it destroyed us."
While Pressley refused to say that he would never attempt such a venture again, he did say that for the present he is looking to refocus his attention elsewhere. "For years I was a manager--the top of my game," he stated.
"While I evaluate what has happened over the last year, I plan to re-enter that arena. Whether with names you already know--like 'Mr. Irresistible'
Johnny Libido--or names you don't, I could wind up back on television in some capacity in the very near future."
And what of the other talent that has called CCW "home" for the last 12 months? Luckily, Pressley--a strong supporter of the "indie
circuits"--never forced his employees to sign no-compete clauses. Most competitors are already otherwise employed--and has been all along. For the higher-profile competitors--the Gunns, the Blades, the Blazes and Boyds--their tenure in
CCW
has only upped their price tags.
"I don't think anyone regrets the time they spent in CCW," Gunn stated. "During its prime, there wasn't another federation around that could compete." In support of his manager-turned-rival, Pressley, Gunn made perhaps the most touching assessment. "Whatever Preston has ever attempted, he's been a success," Gunn stated. "If he ever decides to begin a new promotion, there will be a line of competitors waiting to apply."
When asked if fellow stars Blade, Boyd, and Blaze could follow him to Global
Pro-Wrestling, Gunn was non-committal. "While I don't doubt their abilities,
I'm in no position to help these guys out. In fact, it's questionable that the GPW would even be interested in them. These are three men who just held another promotion up in arms, just because they wanted to renegotiate their contracts," Gunn reminded everyone. "And if they were interested in some of them, that doesn't mean that they'd be interested in all of them - then where would those guys go? I think they may have just made the biggest mistake of their careers."
