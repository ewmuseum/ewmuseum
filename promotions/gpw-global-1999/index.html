A Usenet e-fed launched with a Madison Square Garden supercard that crowned every one of its six titles via parallel tournaments, then tracked champions and rankings through a weekly newsletter. Five wrestlers are recorded across three served documents. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Global Professional Wrestling billed itself, in its own rulebook (dated 10 October, posted 18 October 1999), as having “grown out of our regional routes to develop a prominent worldwide promotion,” staged as run by an on-screen commissioner, Casey Blain, whose own billed history had him installed after a court order broke up a fictional promotion he once announced for.

Its first pay-per-view, Genesis (24 October 1999, Madison Square Garden), decided all six of its championships in one night: a best-two-of-three-falls match for the Regional Title, a no- disqualification falls-count-anywhere match for the Brass Knux Title, a cage match for the World Television Title, a ladder match for the American Heavyweight Title, and tournament finals — an elimination triangle match and a steel cage match — crowning its first World Tag Team and World Heavyweight Champions.

Its own newsletter four days later named the champions Genesis produced: Eugene Robinson as World Heavyweight Champion, James Wilson as American Heavyweight Champion, The Phoenix as World Television Champion, Bart Simmons as Brass Knux Champion, Flic Rogers as Regional Champion, and Law and Disorder as World Tag Team Champions, alongside a full ranking of its roster and a “rumor mill” column hinting that one of its own factions was “experiencing drastic problems” behind the scenes.

It ran on a weekly television show, Meltdown, backed by a taped lead-in called Global Invasion, under a stated rule that a title could never sit still for more than four weeks without a defense.

No further document survives past that 28 October 1999 newsletter.
