Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Insane Championship Wrestling opened on rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy on 13 December 1997 with an eight-part debut show, Saturday Slamfest, narrated by an announce team fronted by a character calling himself Mr. Insanity.

Inside the same eleven days it ran a second multi-part show, Frightening Friday, completed a World Title tournament and a Cruiserweight tournament, and topped a Saturday house show with a fully timed twenty-two-man Royal Rumble. It also ran a weekly kayfabe dirt-sheet column, Rumor Mill, bylined to an in-fiction reporter, The Undergrounder.

Its own basic format changed five times in that same span — real wrestlers written in, then out, then back in; a simmed format, then a booked one, then simmed again under a second operator — and the newsgroup's own reaction to the simmed version was openly hostile.

It announced a restart on 24 December 1997 and again on 8 January 1998, its fourth restart under the same name; new Saturday Slamfest and Knightmare-on-Wednesday cards ran from 15 January through 23 March 1998, narrated by the same Mr. Insanity introduced at its December debut. The February restart's own World Title Tournament first round billed two of its entrants explicitly as “a visitor from the PFW.”

A further restart's press conference, held on-camera on 26 September 1998, introduced its own President and CEO, Greg Allocca, announcing new signings that included the masked wrestler Squala, a wrestler billed Tecate, and Allocca's own brother, billed “The Almighty Prey” Freddy Allocca. That October's Test of the Best crowned Edward Mason the number-one seed for a promised tournament in a triple-threat main event settled under a no-disqualification rule. A documentary episode that same October staged an invented 1992 founding myth for its own characters — a Queens gym, a cancer diagnosis, an October 1992 opening night — directly at odds with the fed's own actual first dated document, from December 1997.

A further restart's own handbook survives from September 1999, and its operator's own account in it is plain: he had tried to reopen the promotion “on multiple other occassions,” naming the 1998 run as the most successful, at around three months, and this restart as one more try. Recruiting continued toward a planned tournament the surviving record never shows taking place, and no document survives past 20 September 1999.
