National Title 5.3. World Tag Team Titles 5.4. Additional Titles 5.5. Histories 6. Application ----------------- 1. What is ICW? ----------------- 1.1. What does ICW Stand For? The acronym ICW stands for Insane Championship Wrestling. However, what
ICW means is a federation for the next generation. We have a unique style of wrestling, and a unique way of running things. The federation isn't usually one to play by the normal rules. We let our wrestlers have as much freddom as we can while still making sure that everyone has fun. That is what ICW stands for.
1.2. What is ICW?[OOC Explanation] Insane Championship Wrestling is the brainchild of Myself, [name withheld]. It started in 1992, and began as just a way for my friends and I to goof around. We just wrestled the matches on our own. Improvising with what we didn't actually have. Then, as my friends got tired of wrestling. We moved it to just a game to play around when we were bored. With cards, and pay-per-views. We used a flip of a coin to determine who won, and that was it. In early '97 I moved to the internet. I'd invite people who I met to join, but in late '97 I sort of got bored with the whole thing. I closed it down.
I have tried to re open ICW on multiple other occassions. All have failed, including the most successful which ran for around 3 months.
Now, I've decided to give this fed one more try. And to steal a line from
Taz...Join if you can, Survive if we let you!
1.3. What is ICW?[IC Explanation] A man named Mr. Insanity spent his entire adult-life wrestling. After being diagnosed with cancer he decided to open up his own federation. He hired a handful of wrestlers to work for him as they ran out of a small gym in
Queens, New York. It began on October 11th, 1992 as a combined number of 72 people were in attendance to witness the crowning of the first Insane
Championship Wrestling Champion, the Ice Cold Ninja. The emphasis was purely on wrestling at this point in time. With no microphone work at all.
That all changed when Freddy Allocca, and Tommy Richards[who were than The
Hot Property, and Lone Wolf Richards] entered the league. They brought a more Lucha style to the federation, as well as some mic skills. This led to the other guys beginning to speak, and the crowds began to double, and eventually triple in size.
The federation continued this way for 4 years with only minor changes such as the introduction of a tag team division. Then, Mr. Insanity who found himself recovering gave the federation to its most dedicated member Greg
Allocca. Greg Allocca was young, and had saved enough money to put some promotion up around the city. Before you knew it people from all over New
York City were coming to see ICW.
More people, meant more money. The more money ICW made, the wider it spread its promotion. Finally, in December 1996 the ICW cut its first television commercial. Which aired on Fox during a commercial break on 90210. This brought a humongous crowd, and gave Greg the extra amount of money he needed to begin construction on an ICW Battledome Arena. It would seat 22,000 people. With this ICW's financial status continued to grow. until late
December 1997. When a screw up in the books caused Greg to lose more than half the money he made. He immediately closed the federation down so that he could still walk away with plenty of money.
Greg has attempted to resurrect the federation numerous times, but has been unable to capture the magic that Mr. Insanity created when he opened it.
Less than 2 weeks ago, Greg Allocca sold the rights of the federation back to Mr. Insanity who has finally made a full retirement from the ring. He looks to bring ICW back to what it was after making some minor facelifts to change what Greg had turned into Hardcore Stadium back into a bigger, and more miraculous ICW Battledome!
----------------- 2. Rules Of ICW ----------------- 2.1. Roleplaying There are 2 main factors that go into deciding the outcome of a match. The first is roleplaying. Now, to avoid complaints...I have a system where after a match I will be able to show you how things went. I grade each rp on a scale of 1 to 10. Every rp you do gets graded this same way. I then find your average grade, and compare it to your opponent's average grade.
Whoever has the highest wins the match, unless of course an angle, or something else comes into play, like say if you actually want to lose.
(What's that look for? I lose on purpose sometimes, I make deals with people to put them over and if I want to tweak my character.)
Now, there are different kinds of roleplays. Flash - A short (no more than 10 lines of your wrestler actually talking) rp which you can send to me to put on cards or PPVs to add heat to a match, or as a prelude to an attack you want to happen. These don't count as grades because they are really to short to judge, but like magazine interviews are keys to furthering angles.
Pre-Taped - This is an rp you send out to the fed to do a bit of angle furthering, and to increase your chances of winning your next match.
On-Card - This is when you send a roleplay to me only, and I put it on the card. You either come out to the ring, or you have someone interview you in the back. These are worth 2 more points than a pre-taped interview.
Magazine Interview - When I interview you...either through e-mail, AOL IM, or in a Yahoo! chat room. These aren't worth points because I choose who gets them, but they are great for furthering angles. They do take a bit of time to do also.
Now, since I don't have very many rps saved...I can't give you an example of each grade, I'll just describe what is involved in getting that grade.
1 - It's pretty hard to get one of these. They are given when an rp is pointless, or doesn't make sense. It is too short or too long. Too short meaning, it should be a flash. Too long only means that you just drag something on, and on, and on, and on. Or you are repetitive.
2 - RP is either too short, or too long...see above for definitions of these. Hard to understand.
3 - An rp that isn't too short or too long, but seems to be missing something in order to fully understand it.
4 - An rp that has good substance, but no actions. Usually has a poor setting. 5 - Good content. Not descriptive enough, or overly descriptive about the surroundings. 6 - Again, good content with some action. A little too much or too little as far as detail goes.
7 - A good rp, good content, good detail. 8 - A very good rp. Exceptional content, and good detail. 9 - An EXCELLENT rp. It has everything that makes an rp good involved. 10 - This is a rarity. They have to have everything in a nine rp, and they have to have something extra in it. Something original that sets it apart from most other roleplays. Usually contains a balance of humorous, as well as serious dialogue.
There ya go, that's how I grade rps. 2.2. Angles Now, in ICW this is the most important part. You have to come up with angles. Angles are what make the game fun, and it also gets people to actually read the cards instead of skipping down to their match, and just waiting for a title shot.
I can guarantee you that if you are the greatest rper in the world, but you never have an angle...you will never receive a title shot. You might go undefeated, or have an awesome win loss record, but that won't mean the gold is going to end up around your waist.
Now, there are one-shot angles, and there are long term angles. One-Shot Angles - This is something that only takes place on one card. It can be a face to face interview, or it might be an attack on another wrestler. Maybe an interference in a match. Anything like that is a one-shot angle. Keep in mind, attacks, and interferences can backfire.
Long-Term Angles - A long-term angle is something that you and someone else plan out...maybe its a feud, or the formation of a stable. You, and whomever else discuss it, and decide what happens. When you've decided everything you can start sending things in as one-shot angles for the cards.
I don't want you to send in long-term angles because of the high probability that your going to change your minds on certain parts more than one time.
Example of a long-term angle... Let's say Bubba Joe Bob wants to feud with Lt. Arkaym Shan Routen. Bubba and Arkaym decide that they'll come up with little things for the first month of their feud...lots of interviews, and confrontations, but no actual match...then...they'll have a match on the main weekly card. No winner will be determined because they will both get counted out. Then, they'll make their way through some more one-shots on the shows until finally they make it to the pay-per-view...In Your Mouth where they will finally get it on in a weapons loaded cage match.
Now, that's just the basic angle. Sounds good, right? But it's the One-Shots that will make or break the feud because those determine how the angle goes along.
2.3. In-Ring Rules Alright, now here's stuff to know about how the actual match goes about. In ICW we use a play-by-play format for our main card. Our secondary card[if we ever get one] will be summary style. PPV's get play by play, but the staff will be asked to put forth more effort into the match to make it more exciting.
You can send in strategies to tell us how you want your wrestler to go about the match, they aren't required, and they won't necessarily be used, but they can help me out if I'm in a rush to get the match done.
The referee's have total discretion involved as far as Disqualifications go. If you get a tough ref, he might DQ you for hitting someone with a chair...if you get an easy ref...he might let everybody and their mother get involved.
Count-outs, there their...you got till 10 to get in and out of the ring. ICW uses Lucha Libre tag team rules...meaning, if your partner goes out of the ring you can enter.
You have a 5 count to get in and out of the ring when making saves, and double teaming. That's roughly it. 2.4. The 10 Commandments Of ICW 1. Thou Shalt Have Fun 2. Thou Shalt Not Annoy EDDIE! 2.5 Final Comments As far as racial/sexual slurs...don't make 'em, you get one shot with this...than I oust you.
In the event that you want to make some sort of racist angle...I will look at it, if I don't find it offensive myself I will let the federation know, and they will vote. If I get a hunanimous vote that no one is offended, I will allow it. BUT...keep any slurs your character would use to a minimum.

The Asylum The Asylum is ICW's weekly show. It usually features between 4 and 8 matches, maybe more...never less. This is where the weekly matches go down, and where the angles get furthered.
4.2. Pay-Per-Views In ICW Pay-Per-Views will be once every 2 months until we decide to add a secondary card. Which will happen at some point, but 1 a week is the only way this fed will be able to get off the ground. The first ICW
Pay-Per-View is scheduled for December. 4.3. Other Events If we get enough wrestlers...ICW will hold a second weekly card on Fridays which will have a name to be determined.
ICW holds a special card every Friday The 13th called Escape From The Asylum it usually contains pay-per-view caliber matches, plus endless surprises.
----------- 5. Titles ----------- 5.1. World Title The World Title is what every wrestler in Insane Championship Wrestling is there for. The holder of the gold belt its recognized as the best in the federation, until he is dethroned.
5.2. National Title That ever annoying title of second best...the National Title holder is immediately recognized as # 1 contender when he loses the belt as long as he has sucessfully defended it at least once. This may be altered during circumstances like if a wrestler wins a # 1 contenders match.
5.3. World Tag Team Titles The world title for Tag Teams. In ICW like many places, tag team wrestling is scarce, so the competition isn't as strong as the singles world title.
5.4. Additional Titles If the ICW receives enough wrestlers under 225 lbs...the Featherweight title will be introduced. If we get more than 30 wrestlers, The Triple Threat title will be introduced. And finally if we get more than 10 tag teams...
the National Tag Team Titles will be introduced. ---------------- 6.
