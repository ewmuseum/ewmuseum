A short-lived, cross-federation tournament brand naming an in-fiction memorial event for a wrestling character 'Jay Ralston', anchored by its own three-issue vignette anthology and a headline three-night supercard drawing on roughly eighty guest wrestlers from across the newsgroup's own shared universe. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Jay Ralston Memorial Card's own surviving record opens with a vignette anthology (16 September 1998) of in-character promos and parody commercials hyping a coming crossover supercard named for a wrestling figure, Jay Ralston. “Simply the Best” James Pillow used his own segment to talk up the card's 30-man Invitational Battle Royal from the open door of a skydiving plane, declaring he'd walk away the winner even though, by his own account, he hadn't actually been booked in the tournament proper.

Its headline event, “A Weekend To Remember” (16–18 October 1998, Hoosier Dome, Indianapolis, billed past 80,000 fans), advertised more than eighty guest wrestlers drawn from across the newsgroup's own shared universe for three nights of specialty matches: a Spin The Wheel Make The Deal series, a 30-man Invitational Royal Rumble, a Round Robin tournament, an eight-man elimination tag match, a ten-team Unholy War, a Die in Darkness match, and its own sixteen-man Ralston Cup tournament.

The card's own advance hype leaned hardest on a grudge billed as the Rabid Goat Death Match, Mad Goat against “Iron Curtain” Ivan Ramius, with Ramius declaring on camera that “the Ivan Ramius you all know is gone. Only the Iron Curtain remains.”

Days before the show, a report styled as a wire dispatch said Mad Goat had pulled out of that match entirely, throwing the card's second night into disarray, after an on-site argument with Jay Ralston himself — the same figure the whole event was staged to memorialize — who was reportedly hospitalized afterward with a heart attack.

Whether the Rabid Goat Death Match, the rest of the supercard, or the Ralston Cup itself went ahead as billed isn't in the surviving record; that pre-show report is the last surviving document.
