Sunday February 2 Card (3/3)

2 February 1997
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(The commercial break comes and goes, as they are wont to do.) EL: Here's the bell, and Joshua Barnes hands his eyeglasses to our ringside attendant. Both men in this one looked very, very impressive in their debuts last week- here;s a collar and elbow tie up, into fireman's carry by Robertson. Barnes, the former bouncer out of Indiana, manhandled Healer's Havoc, whom we saw earlier this evening, while Robertson- a headlock on the mat by the Professional- really showed us a lot on that tight battle with "The Dreamer" Randy Sheradin- Barnes rolls Robertson back on to his shoulders... 1... Robertson rolls back and still has the headlock on. Barnes powers his way to his feet, but Robertson still has the headlock on him. Barnes pushes him toward the rope- shoulderblock by Barnes. He whips Robertson into the ropes, sets for a back body drop- leapfrog by Robertson- crescent kick by the Professional as Barnes turns around. Robertson out to the ring apron now- where is he going? He flips himself in- slingshot somersault splash! The cover 1.. hooks the leg... 2... kickout! Robertson backs him up into the corner- spin kick! Another one! He sends Barnes for the ride into the opposite corner, follows him in- handspring elbow smash, and Barnes slumps to the mat. Double leg cradle by the Professional... 1... 2... kickout again. There's some kind of commotion in the aisleway as Robertson sets Barnes in a camel clutch. It's The Dreamer, Randy Sheradin! What's he doing down here? Robertson has that Camel Clutch locked on, and Sheradin is banging his fists on the ring apron, cheering Barnes on. (The crowd picks up a chant of "Go, Joshua, Go!" Admit it- the CWAA is the only place you'll ever hear "Go, Joshua, Go!") EL: Well, it looks like the presence of the man he faced last week, as well as the crowd reaction, perhaps, is starting to get to the Professional. He's barking something at the Dreamer, who turns and starts clapping his hands in time with the crowd's chant. Uh-oh- a situation developing here, as Robertson releases the hold and heads over to the ring apron, he's threatening Sheradin, it looks like, and- Barnes rolls him up from behind.... 1... 2.... kickout! (The crowd groans in disappointment) EL: Barnes lets Robertson back up, and smacks a big q fist at his head. Robertson staggers, a left by Barnes, and the Professional slumps into the corner. Irish Whip across to the other corner by Joshua Barns... Robertson bounces out of the corner, and Barnes flattens him with a football tackle, out of a three-point stance. Robertson rolls out of the ring, and we have to take another break here, everyone. We'll be right back. (Commercials advertising "Happy Days" reruns weeknights at 11:30 and "CHiPS" at midnight on the local cable station air, followed by an ad pushing the new promotion at a Dallas carwash that gets you a free air freshener and vacuuming with presentation of your CWAA ticket stub.) EL: Well, while we were away, the Professional took a breather out on the floor, and Sheradin and he exchanged words, but the Dreamer declined to get involved physically. Robertson is back in the ring now with Joshua Barnes. They lock up, and its Barnes with a kneelift. He drags Robertson into the corner, and smashes his face into the top turnbuckle. Barnes with an elbowdrop, and the cover... 1... kickout. Joshua Barnes picks his man back up- Irish Whip. Barnes ducks and catches the Professional- looks like a Samoan Drop coming up- no, it's an airplane spin- he starts spinning, and Robertson is wriggling free, sliding down his back- Robertson hooks both arms- a double chickenwing! He leans back and lifts- he's got Barnes trapped in that double chickenwing, and Barnes' feet are a good twelve inches off the mat- amazing precision by Robertson to get the hold on and amazing back strength to get the man, who outweighs him by nearly a hundred pounds, off the mat like that. There's nowhere for Barnes to go, and, well, there's the bell. A submission victory for Max Robertson, the San Francisco treat, as he pulled that hold out of nowhere and gets on the right side of the win-loss ledger for the first time. The bell's rung but he hasn't let go, yet, and Barnes still can't get free. The referee could overturn the decision and award the match by disqualification to Barnes. And here's the Dreamer in the ring! Robertson sees him and drops Barnes, and stares at Sheradin. Well, the Professional's decided that one match is enough tonight, and he's going to take his win and leave. (The Imperial March starts up again.) EL: Sheradin drops to one knee to check on the Indiana native, and it looks like Barnes has found a friend. Hopefully someone like Randy Sheradin can help the youngster out with his interviewing technique, maybe show him some- wait a second, Robertson is back again, and he just kicked Sheradin right in the small of the back! Now he's stomping on his head- now he's got Sheradin in that double chickenwing! I don;t know what his problem is- he's won his match, he should take a shower and head home now. For someone who calls himself the Professional, this is most unprofessional behavior. He's got that hold on, and Sheradin is kicking away, but there's no way out. Here comes some help! I see The Preacher, Kore Wrench, and BD "Big Dog" Brown. Kore Wrench isn't even scheduled to be here today. Well, Robertson bails out, and he looks awfully proud of himself. Wrench and Brown are helping Sheradin and Barnes up, and when we come back from this break, we'll have our special feature match, as Jak Hammer and "Hardcore" Joey Haven go at it once more. See you in a bit. (Time Life Home Video presents... Vol. I: Hunting and Escaping. Find out why they call them "animals".) (We return in time to hear the opening chords of "Bad to the Bone", which is a neat coincidence as I have a tape of a Gino Hernandez match on my TV as I write this. The 6'10"-310 pound Jak Hammer emerges. his huge muscles flexing, clad in his all-leather outfit. Jak Hammer appears to be about the 12th most popular wrestler on the card tonight, judging by the crowd's reaction. One would think he were General Santa Anna.) EL: There's Jak Hammer, a man who thought nothing of taking a rookie last week and DDTing him on the floor here in the Sportatorium, and I hope that he and Joey Haven will show a little more self-control this week. I doubt it, though. ("Highway to Hell" plays, and Joey Haven (5'10", 245, black tights, "Hardcore" t-shirt) heads down the aisle to a nice pop. He stops halfway and raises his hands in salute to the reaction, and crumples to the floor when Hammer smacks him in the back with a metal chair.) EL: Last week they didn't care that the match had stopped, now they don't care that it hasn't started! Jak Hammer with a tremendous cheap shot, and now he's kneeling on the floor punching away at Haven's head. Has the bell even rung for this one? Hammer's got that chair again, and he lays it on the floor. What is he doing? He drapes Haven over his shoulders- oh, sweet Jesus- he Samoan Dropped him onto the chair, and you could hear that one all the way back in Haven's hometown of Philadelphia! Now he drapes Haven over his shoulder again- is he going to drop on that chair once more? No- horizontal backbreaker! He's got him in a horizontal backbreaker after that back has had two collisions with a chair already. will someone please do something about this? The bell is ringing like crazy, there's the cue for help to come out here, and here comes Travis Conley and Paul Langford, and Hammer drops Haven to the floor. Jak Hammer and Travis Conley are glaring at each other while Langford covers Haven. Now this would be a real collision between bulls, Travis Conley and Jak Hammer. But Hammer's done what he came to do, I suppose, and off he goes. It looks like this match isn't actually going to take place tonight, and, well, in about two weeks we'll be starting our inaugural CWAA Heavyweight Title tournament. I just want to show everyone, could we get a camera shot of this please, I just want to show you all the latest AEWA report, and Dreamer Randy Sheradin and the CWAA made the headline, beating out the events leading up to Kickoff '97. Congratulations to you, Randy Sheradin. Anyway, as we get set for the title tournament, I hope we'll see an end to the nonsense we've seen from Max Robertson and Jak Hammer tonight. You know, in this sport, violence is a necessary evil. It happens all of the time, but for most people, it is the means to an end. Double Jeopardy will break the rules and use foreign objects, or a Joshua Barnes will resort to fisticuffs, all with the intention of preparing the opposition for their endgame. But the actions of Jak Hammer do not fit this category. for him, it appears, violence is an end in itself. He is not interested in using violence to achieve victory; he uses his matches as a forum for violence for violence's sake, and the next time he and Joey Haven meet, I don;t even want to speculate on the outcome. Next week we'll have more information on the upcoming title tournament, and, oh, no. What do you want? Major Victor Augustus Mayhem: At ease, Lane. I just want to take this opportunity to say that this program was an utter travesty. A disgrace. I am thoroughly disgusted. EL: I never thought I'd say this, but I have to agree with you, Major. The actions of Max Robertson and Jak Hammer were thoroughly unprofessional, and- MM: No, no, no. I mean, the greatest history in the HISTORY of the CWAA, Lt. Daniel "Lethal" Lawson, was not here tonight. EL: I see. Well, if I hear that this station has been flooded by calls complaining about his absence, we'll be sure to let you know. Is that all you wanted, to complain that Lawson didn't get airtime. MM: I'm on a special top-secret mission, son. If I were to divulge more than that, I would have to kill you. EL: Whatever. And as far as that "greatest wrestler" comment, I would remind you that the CWAA only has a history of two weeks so far, and your wrestler is 0-1 in his CWAA career, after that disqualification loss to Kore Wrench. MM: Always give the chaplain a break, Lane. So we let him have the win. EL: Had you waited a few seconds it looked as though he would have had it without any assistance. MM: Just make sure Lt. Lawson has a prominent role in the next program. It's a matter national security. EL: I'm sure it is. Like I was saying, next week, we'll have more information about the title tournament,- MM: Featuring Lt. Lawson. EL:- as long as he's submitted the proper paperwork, I'm sure he'll be there. MM: Oh- uh, I shall return. (Exit MM hurriedly) EL: Plus, we're still trying to get some information on that individual who was found tearing like a banshee through the storage room. See you next week, folks! -kl
