RP'S AND GUIDELINES VIII. THE APPLICATION ------------------- I. INTRO ------------------- We're back. It's kind of hard to believe, I know, but the AEWA once more is preparing to make its return to the spotlight. It's been a long road coming, but after a discussion with [name withheld] about wrestling, e-feds, and legacies, I was persuaded not only to open a federation, but also given the chance to re-open one of the top feds from a few scant years ago, the AEWA. ------------------- II. WHAT IS THE AEWA? ------------------- --A. Before We Begin In essence, the return of the AEWA will doubtless inspire comments of "This isn't the same AEWA I knew!" Truth is, you're right. The AEWA has changed, and some things will not be the same. I hope, with the able assistance of [name withheld], to make the AEWA a fun and entertaining read for the casual fan, and a highly competetive and entertaining forum for handlers. Let the games begin! --B. The AEWA's Function The function of the AEWA is e-wrestling. The crafting of original and entertaining scenarios involving fictional athletes in wrestling situations. Make a character, write his flashes, and send in strategies for matches. The better you write and more often you produce quality roleplays, the more matches you will win. Furthermore, the better and more frequently you write strats and roleplay, the greater the chance of earning a title. Have fun! --C. My Beginnings in E-Wrestling. What? You don't care? Fine. We'll just say five years ago, I found an e-fed online, created some characters, and, through luck and good fortune, was able to associate over the following five years not only in some of the best feds online, but also with some of the best writers. And now, in 2001, the AEWA has been offered to me to allow its legacy to perpetuate. --D. New Beginnings for the AEWA. Up and down since it's most recent close, the AEWA had been merged with its sister fed, the MBC. The hope that its legacy, its champions, would not be lost as a footnote in another fed, prompted [name withheld] and I, in a discussion, to begin plans not only to create a brother fed for MBC and [name withheld]'s UWF, but allow the still proud AEWA champions to not only continue their title reigns, but also to usher in the new era of AEWA wrestling. --E. The Basics of How the AEWA Works The AEWA is a booked fed and is booked on three things. One being roleplays. The second being strategies and the third being angles and storylines. Any complaints can be taken up with me. Matches and Shows are explained in sections V and VI. --F. Who are you, anyway? Me? I'm [name withheld]. President and Owner of the new AEWA. Along with my associate, who will introduce himself below, I will schedule cards, decide winners and loser in matches, and write the weekly card. If you have any questions about the fed, don't hesitate to contact me at [detail withheld] --G. Who are you, anyway? The associate guy? I'm [name withheld], Vice President and Tech-At-Large of the AEWA. I'm here to assist President Dec in running the day-to-day operations of the federation, including help with talent scouting, creative matters, updates to the public and staff relations. Like President Dec, I am available for any questions you may have at [detail withheld] . Back to Top ------------------- III. THE PERSONALITIES ------------------- --A. Before We Begin... This section will discuss most of the major personalities of the AEWA. Mostly its the commentators and administrators that it'll cover. Obviously it will change as the crew changes. Pictures are availale of each of them on the website. --B. Broadcast Team 1.) Mark Wilson, 52, is a sportscaster by trade whose twenty five year career has seen him work, apart from his prior stints in the AEWA, in St. Louis, Chicago, Vancouver, and San Diego as a play by play man in three separate sports (baseball, football, soccer). His professionalism is unparalleled in the sports business and the AEWA is pleased to have a veteran of his calibre under long term contract. 2.) Marcus Kreiger, 28, is a former hardcore wrestler whose career ended last year due to an injury to three vertebrae in his neck. "Handsome Marcus" was a four year veteran whose mike skills easily matched the high flying style he utilized, and, following a successful meeting with President Dec, Krieger was signed to a contract as a broadcaster. Kreiger is conceited, a ladies' man if ever there was one, but he is quite competent as a broadcaster. He tends to side with the heels a little more, knowing all too well you do anything you can to get a win. 3. ) Tina Bourke, 26, is a former valet whose mike skills made her more valuable than the man she spoke for. She's not a bubblehead by any means, and is quite vocal in her support of the "good guys", since "a good guy is hard to find these days". She tends to be quite antagonistic towards Handsome Marcus, as they disagree more often than not. Tends to dress in as sexy an outfit as she's able to find on a week to week basis, knowing full well that she's got a great body. Also a "colour" person. --C. Interviewers 1.) Ralph Osdam, 36, is a longtime wrestling fan who auditioned for the chief broadcasting position. While Mark Wilson had him beaten out in terms of experience, the enthusiasm that Osdam had for professional wrestling was enough to warrant giving him a chance, reasoned President Dec, and he was hired as an interviewer. While Osdam's nervousness still does show on occasion, he is quite competent, and is not the obsessive fan many would be given this opportunity. His wrestling knowledge is comprehensive, and he is a very capable interviewer. 2. ) Michelle Pereira, 23, is a beautiful woman with a sexy voice hired because she is a beautiful woman with a sexy voice. She conducts interviews for the AEWA, and is learning rapidly as she goes. However, it's obvious that her role is more as eye candy and less for intelligent discourse. Still, when you look this good, who needs discussion? --D. The Bosses (or, "Who signs my paycheck?") 1.) President Andre Dec, 26, began his wrestling career as a scout for the UWF and MBC three years ago. He has ascended to a position of prominenceearly in his wrestling career and, due to sound investing, purchased ownership rights to the AEWA from MBC President [name withheld]. With a number of provisional advertising contracts and stadium deals lined up, this Pittsburgh, PA born businessman will attempt to restore the AEWA's past glory in this new millenium. His management style is strictly Laissez-Faire. 2.) Vice President James Robertson, 26, began his wrestling career by accident. A chance encounter with a pair of wrestlers lead him to a job as a business manager for the wrestlers, which put him in contact with then Scout Dec. Continued contact between the Talent Scout and the Manager lead to a working relationship and and friendship. When President Dec purchased the AEWA, he offered a position to Robertson, who jumped at the chance tobe more involved. Like President Dec, Robertson is a firm believer in letting the wrestlers do their own thing, while he concentrates on making sure they have that opportunity. 3.) Commissioner Lorencio Garton, 32, is a ten year veteran of the squared circle, beginning his career in the AEWF in 1990. Entering semi-retirement last year, the Portuguese born Garton was offered the role of Commissioner by President Dec based on his storied career, his business savvy, and his ability to stand by his decisions forcefully, if need be. Lorencio Garton is the picture of dignity and focus in his new trappings, and is as dangerous within a business setting as he was in the ring. 4.) Women's Commissioner Elizabeth Wallace, 29, is not a wrestler. She is a businesswoman educated at Tufts University (Class of '95) who has previously served as a programming consultant at WGBH, WNYU, and WSBK television stations. In an effort to bring about a women's division unlike any other, President Andre Dec offered her the position over a number of ex-wrestling applicants based on her experience within the entertanment medium and the assumption that women's professional wrestling is as valid an athletic form as men's professional wrestling, and that previous interpretations of "slap fights" and "sissy throws" will not be continued within the AEWA. 5.) Carl Collins, 34, is a former professional wrestler who acts as a bodyguard for Commissioner Wallace. While rehabilitating an injury, he has been hired to act as bodyguard and Chief of Security for the AEWA. His loyalties are 100% AEWA. 6.) Director of Public Relations Hank Thompson, 37, is a former interviewer for the AEWA and has been promoted to this position as a reward for services well done in the past. Any public events involving AEWA talent beyond the wrestling venue will be scheduled and directed by him. ------------------- IV. THE TITLES ------------------- --A. Before We Begin... Again, here is the intro to this section. This section ought to cover everything you need to know about the AEWA's titles and if it doesn't then email the fedhead... Wait... Thats me. --B. The American E-Wrestling Association World Heavyweight Title The one and only... the big one. This is the top piece of gold to go for. It is an officially sanctioned world title, as the American E-Wrestling Association maintains its name for heritage purposes while being a part of a global wrestling fanbase. This is defended AT LEAST once every three or four weeks on the televised show. --C. North American Heavyweight Title Number two on the title list, much like the WWF's Intercontinental belt, with one distinction: The champion is considered to be the #1 contender to the World Heavyweight Belt. Defended AT LEAST biweekly, the North American Champion's schedule is a little more arduous, but it is also the second highest honor that can be achieved in the AEWA. --D. AEWA Bay Area Heavyweight Title Number three on the title list, this is most closely paralleled by the WWF European Championship. The Bay Area of California, the old hub for the AEWA, is represented by this belt, whose champion is considered to be the #1 contender to the North American Heavyweight Title and #6 for the World Title. It is defended on AT LEAST a biweekly basis. (FYI-The new base of operations for the AEWA is Boston, MA) --D. AEWA Light Heavyweight Title For wrestlers under 225 lbs., this title represents the pinnacle of Cruiserweight excellence, in matches that only a pair of light heavyweights can provide. Defended AT LEAST biweekly, the AEWA Light Heavyweight Champion was also the #1 Contender for the AEWA New England Heavyweight Title, and #6 contender for the North American Heavyweight title. --E. AEWA World Tag Team Titles The top of the AEWA Tag Team Division, titles are to be defended AT LEAST once every three weeks. A rich heritage and a division that will be very competetive. Only the best will achieve these titles. --F. AEWA World Women's Championship Unlike the AEWA of old, a women's division has been instituted with the intention of showing just how skilled and talented "the fairer sex" can be in the ring. The title will be defended AT LEAST once every three weeks, and competition can be expected to remain fierce in this division. --H. Defunct Titles/Tier Wrestling 1.) AEWA Television Title The most frequently defended belt in the AEWA, the Television Champion is recognized both for workrate and ability. Depending on roster size, this belt may be reintroduced in the near future. This belt does not fit into the tier system, but is defended regularly and arduously. It is an achievement in and of itself to win this title, and often represents the best of the workhorses of the AEWA. 2.) Tier Wrestling The Tiered Wrestling System is similar to the idea that the NWA and WWF used until the late 1980's. It looks like this: -World Champion- #1 Overall -North American Champion- #2 Overall, #1 contender to the World Championship -Bay Area Champion- #7 Overall, #1 Contender to North American Championship, #6 contender for the World Championship. -Light Heavyweight Champion- #12 Overall, #1 Contender to the Bay Area Championship, #6 Contender to North American Championship, #11 Contender to World Championship. Because each championship carries with it its own duties and responsibilities, being the number one contender for a title does not automatically give you the title shot. To request the right as number one contender, the title belt must be yielded, with the number two and three contenders competing to determine the new champion. Should the #1 contender win the match, they become the Champion of the next tier. Should they be defeated, they drop to #5 within their own tier. Wrestling Rankings will change on a monthly basis, resulting from activity, performance, and overall development. The more active you are, the better chance you'll climb in the rankings. On rare occasions, a person will drop in the rankings by winning a title. This does not affect their overall performance, nor should winning a "lower tiered title" be regarded as "a step backwards". Following a successful championship reign, a wrestler could have sufficiently showcased his talents enough to compensate for the lost title. The TV Title and American Tag Titles may be reinstated, provided the roster is large enough to support that high a number of champions. Back to Top ------------------- V. THE WEEKLY SHOWS ------------------- --A. Before We Begin The AEWA has a somewhat unique approach to weekly cards. In addition to a regular show, "AEWA Prime", there will be a listing of house shows on a weekly basis. Formats are explained below. --B. AEWA Prime A summary format show in the vein of MBC's "House of Bastards" or UWF's "Meltdown", this two hour show will, on average, feature 8-10 matches. This will be the flagship of the AEWA and will be released on a weekly basis, Friday nights. Strats and flashes are to be sent to [detail withheld] and [detail withheld] by the due dates as given. --C. House Shows Given that not all talent can appear on every card, the AEWA will also utilize house shows (unscheduled matches and results based on the roleplays that are sent in by folks who are not wrestling on any given week). Matches will be predetermined, and the results will have a bearing on future pushes. The reasoning is twofold: 1.) If people put in the extra effort and send in roleplays when they do not have matches scheduled on AEWA Prime, they're taking that extra step with their characters to keep them in the spotlight. Win or lose in house shows, the effort put into their character is not only recognized, but also may be enough to push someone up in the rankings, even if he or she loses to someone in a house show. 2.) Unless a feud becomes evident, wrestlers who face each other in a house show may use the results for the start of a new story arc. While the fed is booked primarily, scripted secondarily, the value of a well planned plot arc will not be overlooked. ------------------- VI. THE PAY PER VIEWS ------------------- --A. Before We Begin It's worth mentioning that only two of the Pay Per View's, Kickoff and Boot Camp, will keep the same name as before. This does not belittle the value of the other PPV's, but with a new start to the AEWA, a few new names only seem fitting. --B. Kickoff (January 1) Start the year off right with the AEWA. "Kickoff" was the year-starting Pay Per View for the AEWA, and without a doubt, the AEWA's signature series. Kickoff will be held on New Year's Day, the first entertainment extravaganza of the new year, and, as all Pay Per Views will, includes a defense of every title active. This is the big one, folks. Forget the bowl games, and let the AEWA start the year off right. --C. European Disunion (early May) While the name indicates "American" proudly, the AEWA will follow the Kickoff cycle with a world tour, the final pay per view to be held at a location to be determined one yearly basis within the European Union. Other tour stops for the AEWA will include Japan, Australia, Britain, and Scandinavia. The AEWA truly goes global. --D. Boot Camp (early August) Distinguished with a grueling tournament as well as other AEWA Pay Per View quality excellence, Boot Camp holds the distinction of being held at a different military base every year. A salute to the United States military, for all they do. --E. Card of Oblivion (Mid October) An obscure reference to a playstation game, and a random location each year, this is the side of the AEWA that appeals to the unpredictable. A battle royale is held yearly, with the winner earning a title shot at Kickoff. Back to Top ------------------- VII. RP'S AND GUIDELINES ------------------- --A. Before We Begin Yep. There are rules. Go figure. This section's by JJ, so I'll let him explain it in his own words. --B. Take it away, JJ. The Rules! Q1. There are rules? A. Of course there are rules. Without rules, we'd all be savages. [Muffled whispering] Ah, we ARE all savages. Never mind. There are still rules, though. Rules that better be followed or else we'll send Big Pete over to your house to do the Chicken Feed Dance on your naughty bits. 'K? Q2. Ok, so what are the rules? A. We have a few, listed below: 1. Have fun! -This is the important one. The one that we want you to follow all the time. HOWEVER, given that Big Pete's idea of having fun is to do the Chicken Feed Dance on other people's naughty bits, we realized we needed to be more specific, so: 2. Language. We're realistic, wrestlers aren't nice guys all the time and they keep people's interest by finding new and unusual ways to abuse someone else. However, understand there are some limits of taste. If you don't see it getting across on Raw or Smackdown, then it won't get across with us. IF you aren't sure if something is too far out of line, ask us. We have opinions we love to share. Swearing is tolerated in moderate amounts, much like on Raw, but if you have to go into major swear-fest, try to insert your own censor [BEEP]. 3. Violence. Ok, so go out and beat up other people. No sweat. But, you can't do anything to cause lasting, permanent harm to your opponent without their direct consent. Like above, if they didn't do it in WCW, we probably won't let you do it. If you want to do something big and lasting with another player, that is acceptable if they are willing, but understand this is meant to be a rare thing. 4. Be polite to the other handlers. It's one thing to cut a flash where you run down a wrestler. It's quite another to cut a flash where you run down that wrestlers handler, or send e-mail to that handler to insult them. This is a big, bad no-no that involves our friend Big Pete(and I think we've established that Big Pete is only good for one thing by now, right?). Seriously, have some respect for the other handlers. If you let this all get personal enough that it breaks down to that point, rule number 1 isn't being followed and we need to see what we can do to fix this. Talk to us and we will work with you. 5. Use of characters. In your flashes, you are allowed to use the interviewers and a few other select staff. Using other handlers wrestlers is allowable with that handlers permission. Some other staff, like the commissioner are usable on a limited basis. President Dec/VP Robertson are largely off-limits, nor are they to be used in the 'Corrupt Official' mode. they're nice guys and when they need to be involved, they'll make their own presence known, but until then prefer to leave things in the hands of their more than capable staff. 6. Again, have fun. We're all here to have fun. If you aren't having fun, talk to us and we will try to work with you. If you want to try something, talk to us and we will see what we can do. If you have questions about any of this, we're pretty open on a lot of things, so long as it follows the rules above. ---------------------- VIII.
