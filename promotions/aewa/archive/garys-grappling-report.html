Gary's Grappling Report

2 October 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Gary's Grappling Report by [name withheld] The AEWA invaded the USAir Arena last night, to a full place. Despite their year-long injury problems (Mike Stewart, Justin Trouble, the ICU), and some awful technical problems, they put on a good show. First Match: December Grey vs. Jungle Jim Jungle Jim is some local talent who goes to Georgetown. We were all marking out for Jimmy, but Grey won in a squash in about five minutes. Grey's got some good power moves, but seems a little suplex-happy for my tastes. The technical problems began with this match. The announcer's microphone kept cutting off as he was announcing the wrestlers. He had to repeat himself three times to get Grey's name right. After the match, a second microphone was found. Second match: Paul Wong vs. Captain Crusher The original match was the Battalion vs. the Machines, but the announcer informed all of us that Simon O'Neal was unable to attend due to his recent problems. I still don't know if Simon O'Neal is really gone or if this is a work. Nice power match between the two, including a two-minute game of "mercy" between them. The match was short (just under eleven minutes)-- I guess tag team wrestlers aren't used to the pace of single's wrestling. Captain Crusher won when he reversed a backdrop into a piledriver. Third match: Fuse vs. Lt. Lightning, Light Heavyweight Title Match Here's a free piece of advice to Freeman: Get some more Light Heavyweights! Fuse looks great, but he's 250 pounds. If they increase the weight again, everyone except for Twisted Steel is going to be eligible! I was holding my “BRING BACK SUDDEN DEATH" sign for the match. Lightning put up a great match, although like his partner, he's not used to singles matches, and it showed. Still, Lightning put on some excellent high-flying moves, including a screwdriver plancha (take THAT, Hector Garza!). Fuse let Lightning dominate the matchup, and seemed to be favoring his right ankle (was there a slight sprain?). The match ended with Lightning getting caught on the top turnbuckle and Fuse planting him with the "Electric Shock" (the best version of the Superplex I've seen). Fuse kept the title, but the fans were wowed by Lightning's performance. Fourth match: "Shotgun" Stan Crawford vs. Ryan "The Lion" Peterson Bootknife Kelly took time out from his booking duties to lead Peterson to the ring. Peterson has a ton of talent, but he's very raw. "Shotgun" is, of course, Shotgun; the guy who takes the craziest bumps in the AEWA. His matches with Primal Fear were classics. Crawford again took some vicious blows, including a top-rope bulldog down to the floor. Here's where the serious technical problems began. About halfway through the match, the ring lights started flickering. At first, I thought it was part of a storyline, but the lights were very distracting to everyone in the ring (Peterson missed a dropkick that he should have connected on). They quickly rushed to an ending where Kelly threw powder in Crawford's eyes, but the referee caught him and disqualified Peterson, giving the match to Crawford. Almost immediately after the match, the ring lights cut out (Just like on that pay-per-view from over a year ago). A couple AEWA officials came out to apologize and assure us that the situation was under control. The TV monitors were still working, so they started showing several AEWA matches from Kickoff '97 and their other PPVs. After about twenty minutes, the lights were restored. Fifth Match: Union Jack vs. Randall Osbourne The last time they came to Washington DC, Sir Gallade was defending his TV title against Randall Osbourne. Now the cheers and boos have been switched around. Osbourne's one of the best brawlers in the sport, and proved it again tonight. This was a standard brawler vs. wrestler matchup; Randall kept blasting away with punches and kicks, while Union Jack kept going back to armlocks and leglocks. These two have fought enough times so they work very well together. Union Jack took a nasty spill when Osbourne gorilla slammed him onto the floor from the ring. Both men were counted out after a half hour. Sixth match: TV Title Defense: "Bullywug" Glenn Hudson vs. "Cardiac Kid" Joey Valachi Two arrogant trash-talking high-flyers; my favorite kind :-). Valachi wore a "Pride" shirt, which endeared him to the AEWA fans. He and Bullywug got into each other's face right from the start, and both hammed it up for the crowd. The match lasted for about fifteen minutes until Valachi hit Hudson with a pair of brass knuckles. He got the pin, but Fuse came into the ring, yanked out the knuckles that were hidden in Valachi's trunks, and showed them to the ref, who disqualified Valachi. Valachi started attacking Fuse, but Hudson came to the rescue, and they double-teamed Valachi until he ducked out. This was the best match of the night. Main Event: DC Street Fight: "Crimson" Joe Reed and Twisted Steel vs. Wild Thing and Tucker Thomas A DC Street Fight match? You'd think Washington DC had a crime problem or something :-). This was a wild match. Chairs, tables, chains and a pipe were just some of the objects used by both teams (my favorite: Steel put on a Redskins Football Helmet, then did a diving headbutt onto Wild Thing). Thomas and Wild Thing took an early advantage with some powder to blind Steel (must have borrowed it from Kelly), then double-teamed Reed with a chair to attack his leg. For about ten minutes, Reed was assaulted by these two. Tucker kept slipping on submission leglocks, which seemed out of place in a Street Fight match. Steel eventually makes the save and the faces get some revenge; Reed plants Wild Thing with a backsmasher onto a chair. Twisted Steel had the move of the night; Tucker Thomas was lying outside on the floor, and Steel launches his 400+ body over the ropes to hit a suicidal plancha onto Thomas. Give credit to both Thomas for taking that move and Steel, who looks like he is back at 100%. The match ended with CJR in a figure-four leglock from Thomas and Wild Thing landing a party time splash on him. Twisted Steel came into the ring, but was stalled by Wild Thing long enough for the three count. All four men continued brawling after the match, and it was a good ten minutes after the bell rang before the participants left the arena. Despite all the technical problems, the AEWA put on a good show. Hopefully they'll be back before a while.
