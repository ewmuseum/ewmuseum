"Lethal" Lawson and Max "The Professional" Robertson 419 lb. (3) 5. Little Yvette and Jimmy Dangerous 460 lb. (5) Recaps: The month of July opened and closed with the CWAA Heavyweight Title vacant. It was decided that the former champion, the champion who never was, the June #1 contender and the July #1 contender (ie, Max Robertson, the Masked Man, Marc Thomas and Randy Sheradin) would meet in a single elimination tournament- and when the month came to a close, we were headed back to a showdown between the Professional, Max Robertson, and the mysterious Masked Man- and this time, the AEWA will sanction the match. The relationship between Jak Hammer and Marc Thomas has sufered a meltdown in the face of the Masked Man's assaults. For months, many of the top stars in the CWAA had found partners (in crime) to watch their backs: Sheradin and Barnes, Lawson and Sheradin, Thomas and Hammer. Marc Thomas and Jak Hammer wielded a dangerous blend of ring savvy, mat grappling, a ferocious brawling and presided over a reign of terror that saw each named #1 contender to the CWAA's solo titles-0n and they were undefeated in tag team action. Then came the Masked Man. He attacked the Preacher. He attcked the Dreamer. He attacked the Professional. All in the ring. And he allegedly assaulted Marc Thomas in the locker area. That the Thomas attack took place off screen, and that the Masked Man has a similar physique as Thomas had led to speculation that they were one and the same. That Lt. Lawson alone of the top contenders had been untouched by the Masked Man's assaults led others to speculate the Masked Man was in his employ. Then the Masked Man was given a crack at the Heavyweight Title, and Jak Hammer conspired to give the Masked Man the title. He told his partner he did so because he knew he could then turn around and defeat the Masked Man himself- but Marc Thomas was the man who was supposed to be in line for the next title shot. Thomas and Hammer argued over Hammer's actions, and agreed to reconcile, with Thomas offering his slot in the Heavyweight Title tournament to Hammer if Hammer won his match with TV Champ Lt. Lawson. when Hammer did win, Thomas renged, and attacked his partner. On July 20th, Thomas and Hammer squared off for the first time, and Hammer walked away as TV Champ AND as a participant in the CWAA Heavyweight Title tournament, and Marc Thomas walked away with a big headache, courtesy of... the Masked Man. The Hammer-Thomas alliance has ended, Hammer wears the TV title, and Thomas' career is in a tailspin, as his promised title shot never materialized and won't be coming anytime soon. At AEWA Battle Klash II, then-TV champion Lt. Lawson used a foregin object and outside interference to knock off previously-undefeated (in the AEWA) Fuse. Fuse took this badly, and showed up in the Sportatorium to get his revenge- little knowing that Lawson had just enlisted Mark Sherrick as a new officer in Major Mayhem's Militia. Sherrick saved his superior officer and Fuse was on the wrong end of a beating. A week later, he made his CWAA debut against "Sgt." Sherrick- and once again it devolved into a 2-on-1 attack. Fuse has vowed not to give up until he has made an example of Lawson and his cronies. The AEWA-UWF rhubarb is threatening to engulf the CWAA and ACWF. Relations between the smaller organizations have been good, but certain ACWF commentators and ACWF fans have taken a dim view of the CWAA's relationship with the AEWA, and popular CWAA star Randy Sheradin has received a cool reception from CWF fans and has been subjected to a highly controversial fine, which some see as a slap in the AEWA's and CWAA's collective face. Sending Lt. Lawson to an ACWF event to face Jak Hammer proved to be an unwise move in these difficult times, as Lawson apparently believes the best way to extinguish a fire is to pour gasoline on it, and he had some very unfriendly comments for ACWF fans, and he and Sherrick used their flagpole to fell popular ACWF Tag champs the New Sensations as they came to the rescue of Hammer and Sheridan during a post-match fracas. Roster Moves: We are not alone. "The Freak", Dave Johnson arrived to beat people up and to spread the word about alien landings in Roswell, New Mexico. Brad Gorman and Curtis Hansen have arrived and may soon be thinking twice about the wisdom of their decisions. Little Yvette went on a tour of Japan and has been unavailable for action in the CWAA (of course, the CWAA came to him in Japan...) Fuse of AEWA fame has stopped in to visit Lt. Lawson, and will be around for a few more weeks. The Trailer Park Assassins have signed to appear at the August 23rd-24th supershow in Reunion Arena. Injuries: For once, everyone appears to be fine. Fuse has a few welts on his back, but it has not slowed him down. Who's Hot, Who's Not: Hot: Jak Hammer- TV champ, Heavyweight title contender, a win over Thomas, and rumored shot at AEWA World Champion Crimson Joe Reed. Not a bad month. Not: Lt. Lawson: Recent title defneses have shown Lawson to be outgunned in the ring, but he managed to hang on to his title via illegal tactics and sheer cowardice. Jak Hammer caught up to him in a steel cage, and a mysterious woman caught up to the Major, and the rest was history. Hot: Randy Sheradin. The first CWAA Heavyweight Champion fought his way back to the top, but fell to the Masekd Man after a series of impressive showings that proved to everyone that Sheradin is still at the top of his game. Not: Byron McNultey and Michael Anderson. They, too, have held on to their titles, but have done so via count-out losses and six-man tag team losses.
