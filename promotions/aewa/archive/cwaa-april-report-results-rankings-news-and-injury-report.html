(4) Recap of Major Events: Title Changes: All three titles changed hands this month- which shouldn't be surprising, since two titles were vacant when the month began. Lt. Lawson put on an impressive display in outlasting 11 other CWAA stars in a Battle Royale, and eliminating the last three opponents, pausing along the way to hop from the ring and argue with officials over the status of his manager. And interference from "The Professional" Max Robertson, with whom the Lt. appears to have an on-again off-again professional relationship, helped put away "The Preacher" Kore Wrench. Lawson has since undergone a rigorous schedule of a defense-a-week- only challenger Max Robertson, after winning the heavyweight strap, delcined his opporunity. Nonetheless, the Lt. was the big mover in the rankings, leaping from 10 to 5 this month. That same night that Lawson earned his gold, Twister earned a measure of revenge against "Marvelous" Michael Anderson and "Pretty Boy" Byron McNultey, adding insult to injury by taking the belts in less than 10 seconds. Since then, Anderson and McNultey have added injury to insult, and Paul Langford sports a broken nose as a result, as a cowardly attack interrupted a title defense against Joshua Barnes and Randy Sheradin. Late in the month, a new chapter was written in the Sheradin-Robertson-Barnes feud, when Max Robertson, whose strategy of targeting the upper body of champion Randy Sheradin as a prelude to applying the double chickenwing had been countered in previous meetings, switched his tactics and honed in on the champ's legs. A misfired monkey flip by Sheradin smacked his leg into the steel post once too often, and a figure four leglock in the middle of the Las Vegas ring ended the reign of the CWAA's first-ever heavyweight champion. Working Relationships Preliminary negotiations with the FWA for CWAA wreslters to contend for FWA titles on CWAA programming have begun, and the ACWF was pleased enough with the performance of Joshua Barnes and Max Robertson at Rule Britannia! to inquire about CWAA participation in their Spring Fling pay per view extravaganza in late May. And the AEWA answered the CWAA's request for a World Title opportunity for the CWA Heavyweight Champion, and an agreement in principle was reached for Crimson Joe Reed to square off against "Dreamer" Randy Sheradin. But less than a two weeks before the scheduled event, Reed dropped his title at AEWA Extreme: The Pain Game, to 460-lb. Heavy Metal, and Randy Sheradin succumbed to Max Robertson, setting up the 460-200 lb. weight difference. As the crowd roared, Heavy Metal descended upon the Sportatorium and dished out the type of punishment many felt the Professional, and his sometime-ally, Lt. Lawson, sorely needed. Rumors of an AEWA World Tag Team Title defense were not realized, but the possibility remains open for the future. Strength in Numbers When Joey Haven signed to meet Jak Hammer in a steel cage one week and Marc Thomas the next, it was not that big a surprise when Hammer and Thomas co-operated in a doouble-team assault on Haven- after the match, as Joey Haven left the ring, Marc Thomas slammed the cage door in his face, and a 2-on-1 ensued. At the time it looked coincidental; Marc Thomas needed to get by Haven to earn a chance at Joey Valachi's AEWA World Lightheavyeweight Title, and the Haven-Hammer cage match was the perfect opportunity for him to get a head start. But was it coincidental? A month later, Jak Hammer and Marc Thomas were seen working again, as they disrupted the Kore Wrench-Lt. Lawson TV Title match, and have taken to accompanying one another to the ring. Right now, both men reside near the top of the rankings, and each is considered a dangerous foe- one enemy described Marc Thomas, off-the-record, going onto the TV Title battle royale, as the "most dangerous" entrant of all! The threat they pose increases exponentially when they appear together, however, and both men have CWAA Championships in their sights, and anyone defending a championship against one will also have to reckon with the other. Roster Moves: Original members Stan "the Powerhouse" Stevens, Healer's Havoc, and Big Dog Brown, have all parted ways with the CWAA. Stevens showed promise, but a nagging leg injury suffered at the hands of Lt. Lawson hampered his progress. Perhaps a respite will provide him the time he needs to heal. Havoc and Brown at one point looked like serious threats to win the tag team titles, but the time they split between solo wrestling and tag team action may have prevented them from putting together the kind of winning streak that would have taken them to the top. It is unlikely any who faced Midnight Rampage will forget the awesome power of the Big Dog nor the ferocious street-fighting skills of the Real Deal. The CWAA has signed a top young prospect in Little Yvette in recent weeks. The son of Canadien wrestling legend Ron Yvette, Little Yvette has already made a name for himself by taking the measure of Jak Hammer in a tough loss before pulling off the impressive upset win over Mark Sherrick a week later. Who's Hot, Who's Not: Hot: Max Robertson- the Heavy Metal match aside, Max shrugged off the loss to Joshua Barnes and preservered against "Dreamer" Randy Sheradin and grab the CWAA Heavyweight Title. Not: "Hardcore" Joey Haven- Haven dished out some serious punishment to Jak Hammer in the cage and at AEWA Extreme: The Pain Game, but has been little seen since then, while his former foe has pulled himself into title contention. Hot: Lt. Lawson. Winning the TV Title has helped catapult Lawson into the upper echelon of CWAA Wrestlers. Injury Report: Paul "Tornado" Langford- suffered a broken nose at the hands of Michael Anderson and Byron McNultey, and may miss 3-4 more weeks. The CWAA has refused to let Langford wrestle until the CWAA's physicians delcare him fit or he obtain's a doctor's release. Mandatory Title Defenses: On Sunday, May 25th, number one contender Randy Sheradin receives his mandated CWAA Heavyweight Title match against Max Robertson On Sunday, May 18th, Twister are scheduled to defend their titles against Anderson and McNultey. If Langford is still unfit to compete, they will be forced to forfeit the championship. -kl
