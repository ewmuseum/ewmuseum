A national booked promotion that ran continuously from January 1996 to at least the end of 1998, sustaining two weekly TV shows across 179 held broadcasts, seven title lineages, a wholly-owned regional promotion, and a year-long war with a rival federation the newsgroup's own community nominated four separate ways. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The American E-Wrestling Association traces its own founding to a prior fed, the PEWA, “one of countless feds which lasted less than 2 weeks” — Tommy Smith and Kevin Larkin met there, and when Larkin offered to help write matches just before it folded, Smith told him about an idea for a promotion of their own. It opened in January 1996 with a brief warm-up card, then World and Tag Team Title tournaments that crowned Mister Crowley and the NATO Powers as its first champions.

It ran on the TNM simulator before converting to a booked format on 22 November 1996, its own president, Bob Freeman, explaining the switch to the roster in a members' letter: winners would be decided first and then “booked...on TNM,” rewarding roleplaying enthusiasm over a pure writing contest. The simulator's own author later offered an unprompted verdict on how the promotion actually used his program: they “used TNM to give them results, but their writers retained their own creativity nonetheless.”

Its World Heavyweight Championship spent 1996 changing hands violently and often: four times in three weeks that May alone, and again that autumn when Heavy Metal took the belt from Mister Crowley before losing it to “Superman” Mike Stewart. On 2 November the two met again fifteen feet above the ring, on a railing-less scaffold, and both men were knocked off the platform in the same instant, hitting the ground together. Rather than rule a winner, President Freeman walked out and declared the title vacant on the spot; a twenty-six-man battle royal weeks later crowned Shane Matthews.

In January 1997 the promotion launched its own wholly-owned regional feeder, the CWAA, open at first only to its own handlers; that feeder's own championship was sanctioned, and twice vacated, by rulings the AEWA read out on its television broadcast, though the CWAA itself never sanctioned a title of its own. That April its two tag divisions merged when the Machines beat the Assassins in a dedicated unification match to create the Unified World Tag Team Championship. That October its reigning World Champion used a promo to publicly dispute a rival promotion, ACEW, fielding an impostor wrestling under his own name.

Its year-long rivalry with a fellow federation drew enough attention that the newsgroup's own year-end awards nominated it across four separate categories.

No closure notice for the promotion survives anywhere. A handler was still signing on as its own Television Champion as late as December 1998, and the newsgroup's own annual rankings kept citing wrestlers by their AEWA-era gimmicks for years afterward. The record simply trails off rather than ending on any dated card.

Affiliations

LaValle Cup

Member of AEWA (1996) (1996)
AEWA's own West Coast Connection (its three-time AEWA World Tag Team Champions) and Graveyard Shift units entered the LaValle Cup II bracket, with the West Coast Connection winning Pool C and reaching the tournament's own round-robin final; AEWA's own announcing duo, Matt Wilson and "Big Daddy" Michael Hurt, called the tournament's Pool B and Pool C rounds. AEWA's own announcing talent is separately credited on the tournament's first (April 1996) edition.
