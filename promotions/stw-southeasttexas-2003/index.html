Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Southeast Texas Wrestling opened in 2003 in Beaumont, Texas, staged out of the Southeast Texas Entertainment Complex — a 9,500-seat venue its own material calls the STW Arena. Its founding FAQ (17 July 2003) pitches the promotion as a hybrid open to “all talent, established or not,” hardcore wrestlers and cruiserweights alike, running on an alternating Flash Show/Big Show schedule with quarterly “Mega cards” standing in for pay-per-views.

On screen, the promotion was fronted by owner Lexington Reed, described in its own material as “neither a fan favorite or a hated man,” and called by an announce team of twin brothers Rick and Alex Marshall, self-billed as the first twin announce team in the sport. Its two singles championships were the STW World Heavyweight Championship and a hybrid-styled STW Lonestar State Championship, built to be contested across every discipline the roster carried.

A second surviving document, an in-fiction commercial staged around exploding stone lettering, has Lexington Reed step forward to recruit “the best wrestling talent there is today in the world, not just Texas,” promising a place to “any superstar... hardcore wrestler, or... high flying wrestler” willing to join.

No card, roster, or match result for Southeast Texas Wrestling survives; a ring announcer, John Morten, is named only once, inside an application template. How, or whether, it ran beyond its own recruitment drive isn't in its surviving record.
