Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Empire Wrestling opened its new year with two weekly broadcasts, Backtalk and Showdown, taped in a different city almost every week — Washington, a Maine capital, Toronto, Montreal, a North Carolina college town, Atlanta and Tallahassee all hosted its cameras inside two months. Marc Osborne and Rick Sleaze called both shows from the same announce position, trading a running bit between them of Osborne playing straight man to Sleaze's needling color commentary.

Its own front office staged an on-screen CEO, Edward Brock, and one of its own wrestlers turned that framing into a running gag, narrating a mock wire-service profile of himself mid-broadcast as though a reporter had filed it from Winnipeg.

The promotion's spine for its first two months was a stable war: a trio calling itself the Lethal Enforcers against a rival trio operating as the Dojo. Backtalk opened more than one week on the same teaser reel, cutting between the two stables and a run of the wider roster's own signature moves — the Deviator, Greater Ambitions, the Midnight Requiem, the Nightmare Bomb and a submission called Campbell's Crucifix among them.

That rivalry paid off at its first pay-per-view, Self Destruct, in a match billed as a promotion first: a wrestler's own invention, three steel cages stacked fifty feet into the air and ringed by five layers of tables, contested by two rival trios racing to reach the roof and claim national flags planted at the summit.

Its last surviving word is a house-show report filed from Tallahassee in the middle of February, one more stop on a schedule that shows no sign, in what survives of it, of slowing down.
