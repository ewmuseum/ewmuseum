The Bottom Line Back to TRIBUNE From the desk of CSWA Co-Commissioner Chad Merritt.. "Yoo hoo, I'll make ya famous...." That's right, the Kid is back, and the Enforcer is still enforcing. For those of you who thought Thomas could rid of me once and for all....what were you smoking? Before Austin 3:16, before Sting had "Showtime!", even before Hornet was "Takin' Care of Business", one man was telling it the way it is... ..and that's the bottom line. PREVIOUS COLUMNS October 24, 2000 July 12, 2000 April 13, 2000 February 1, 2000 August 21, 1999 December 16, 1998 August 30, 1998 March 29, 1998 April 1996 March 24, 1996 September 29, 1995 September 14, 1995 January 1995 *includes some of the previous columns from "Enforcer Speaks" and "The Bottom Line" January 18, 2001 I wish I could say it's a new thing to have certain factions taking potshots at me... but I can't. Where to start, where to start? I guess I'll start with the most recent: the allegations from Poison Ivy. Ms. McGinnis certainly takes a page out of Hornet's old book, asking why no one cried for her when she took a tableshot in 1997 from Troy Windham. What she's really asking is: Merritt, how can you fire Eli for putting Sweet Melissa through a table? She goes on to call for 'sanctions' of a sort against Troy Windham for his actions at PRIMETIME in Little Rock. And she even threatens a lawsuit from her mother if I don't do what she wants. I'm going to assume that Ivy is playing for the cameras... that she realizes I dealt with her fairly in our discussions about Flair's recent firing and rehiring. I'm also going to assume that Ivy knows the difference between the role she's made for herself versus that by other managers and valets. Ivy has stepped in the ring as a competitor on multiple occasions, against both men and women. She's stepped into matches and altercations as much as anyone else around here, and she's taken her lumps for her actions as well. The woman carries a cane to ring, for goodness' sakes... and now she wants to play innocent? I don't think so, Ivy. But the fact is, this wasn't the first incident with Eli. He's repeatedly been warned about his interaction with the fans at ringside and elsewhere, and he's made the decision to ignore those warnings. Then he made the decision to slam Melissa through a table. That's not an 'occupational hazard', Ivy. It was an occupational hazard when you hit Troy Windham in the back of the head with a chair. It was an occupational hazard when Melissa decided to take that chair away from you. But here's the difference, Ivy. If an intruder enters your house and attempts to, let's say, do harm to your child or yourself, you have every right to use whatever force necessary to protect yourself and your family, even lethal force. If that intruder runs out of your house and into the street... you do not then have the right to shoot him in the back because he was in your house. It's a question of degree, Ivy. And for Eli to injure Melissa's knee, pull her down by her hair, and then powerbomb her FROM THE RING to the floor and through a table -- that's the definition of excessive force. So don't come out here with idiotic rhetoric about 'occupational hazards', because I know better. And that's why if Eli so much as touches another member of my staff without being 'provoked' -- that is the term you wanted added to the contract isn't it? -- then he's fired. And I am the final arbiter as to what counts as 'provoking' and what counts as excessive force. So that doesn't mean if Roseanne Fairhurst walks up to Eli and calls him a name, that he's then been 'provoked' enough to put her through a table. Now let's move on to your question about Troy Windham. And let's look at the differences. Troy Windham didn't touch your mother or your daughter. Much like the elaborate set-up made by your 'ClaimStakers', it appears that Troy was using a ploy of his own. But then again, Ivy, if you're concerned about your safety at ringside, or if you consider being attacked an 'occupational hazard', then why would you allow your family to sit at ringside in the line of fire. I'm not going to go as far as to suggest that your mother and daughter were conveniently at ringside with Mike Randalls sitting behind them was a part of that elaborate "ClaimStakers" scheme, Ivy, because I know you better. But I question whether you even knew Randalls was there. I don't think your little faction is on the same page, Ivy... and I think you're the odd woman out. But back to Troy: he didn't put your mother through a table, he didn't touch your daughter... he didn't even say a cross word to them from what I saw. So there's your answer. I'm not going to fire, fine or suspend Troy, because he did nothing wrong, as far as I can tell. He certainly didn't take a non-sanctioned brawl into the crowd, or hit a fan, now did he? I'm going to be very honest with you, Ivy. I find it incredibly hypocritical for a 'member' of the "ClaimStakers" like yourself to have any credibility when it comes to talking about concern for her friends or family. Not after ANNIVERSARY...not after Hornet stated, and I quote: "...If you have no regard for your health, for the health of your families and loved ones, then step right up to us. If you find your lives so unfulfilling that you want to throw them away in attempt to 'stand up to the old guys', then step up to the plate. That tells me one of two things, Ivy. Either you're the mistress of machinations... or you aren't on the same page as your cohorts. I think the odds are even on either choice right now. It's up to you to make clear which one is the right bet. That's all for now, folks. Happy New Year...and we'll see ya at ELVIS LIVES.
