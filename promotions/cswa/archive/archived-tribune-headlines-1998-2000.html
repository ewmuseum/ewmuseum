Even before PRIMETIME in Pensacola, the first event in the "Swimming Upstream 2000 Tour" (sponsored by U-62 and "The Incredible Edible Egg"), CSWA officials have announced the Main Event for the following card: PRIMETIME in Mobile. The match includes three former World Champions. Eli Flair and Hornet, from the "Triumverate" or "ClaimStakers" alliance set up at A2K, are scheduled to take on Eddy Love and a partner yet to be named. Speculation has already begun to swirl as to whether Love's antics at A2K will lead to a reunion of the Powers of Love. But it also appears as if the former hate-hate relationship between Love and Steve Radder has given way to a mutual understanding. Radder and Love are currently scheduled in a three-way match with Mike Randalls at the upcoming PRIMETIME in Pensacola. Flair is set to tangle with Tom Adler, while Hornet meets former Greensboro Champion Wicked Sight. Deacon Sidelined? The TRIBUNE has learned that CSWA World Champion Deacon has not yet been cleared to wrestle by his doctor. The numbness in Deacon's right arm continues to be a question mark in the mind of CSWA trainers and medical staff. If Deacon is unable to defend his title in Pensacola, does this mean that Kevin Powers becomes the World Champion by default? We'll keep you up to date on this breaking news as we learn more. History's Over The aftermath of CSWA ANNIVERSARY 2000 was unprecedented. The history between Eli Flair and Mike Randalls said it shouldn't be probable. The history between Mike Randalls and Hornet said it shouldn't be possible. But it was. In the same night he took sole control of the CSWA and re-signed a superstar to a two-year contract, CSWA Commissioner Chad Merritt apparently lost control of the ship's tiller. An apparent triumverate of Randalls, Hornet and Flair in place along with Poison Ivy would be news anywhere. Add to it all the fact that their apparent target is Merritt, and you have something even bigger. The story of ANNIVERSARY may have been the emergence of the 'trio', but there were other emergents as well: Merritt was able to exercise some control during the evening, ousting Famine aka Cardigo Mysterian from the building in the middle of his match. Famine's wrestling history is well known on the independent scene, even encompassing a short run in the CSWA in the early 90s. Merritt's plan to keep The Dark Carnival out of the CSWA ended win Eddy Love gave assistance to his former tag team partner Kevin Powers, giving him the next shot at the CSWA World Championship. Aaron Douglas has already benefited by his association with the trio, winning the CSWA Presidential Title from Cameron Cruise when Hornet himself made the three-count. Wicked Sight may have inadvertently got the rub from the 'trio' as well. Instead of a match with newcomer Johnny Fizzbin, Sight once again held his own against a former World Champion, Eli Flair. Sight's stock continues to rise coming off the loss of the Greensboro Championship. Simply Stunning finally take the titles, making them the undisputed tag champs. Havoc turns a six-man loss at SHOWTIME into the beginning of a US Championship run at A2K. Nemesis returns to the ring to win his shot at the Greensboro Championship, while Sky Suicide makes a name for himself by breaking up the Cruise-Devon wedding. The CSWA enters a new 'era' following A2K. While the names of storied veterans like Love, Powers, Radder, Randalls, Hornet and Eli remain near the forefront of the talk, a whole new 'crop' of young superstars is determined to make sure their names are in lights. See the ANNIVERSARY 2000 replay available here. CSWA's Drug Problem? The internet has talked for months about the apparent prevalence of drug use by some members of the CSWA locker room. Followed by the infamous "Sack on a Pole" match recently, major media outlets have begun delving deeper into the issue. Most recently, CSWA wrestler Nemesis was brought up on drug-related charges. A warrant for his arrest was issued when he failed to appear in court recently. Rumors have also run rampant regarding the strange actions of Unified Tag Team Champs Simply Stunning on their way to the titles at A2K. In a statement issued by CSWA Commissioner Chad Merritt earlier today, he states that all wrestlers will be subject to random drug-testing in the future, per their contracts. The results of these tests will be made available publicly. Merritt also advises that the CSWA and CS Enterprises' drug policy states that employees may be terminated immediately upon the finding of two positive drug tests. A positive result during any company-sponsored drug test is immediate followed by a second test. CSWA precedent shows that after one such incident, wrestlers are usually fined and suspended until a drug treatment program can be completed. If a second incident occurs, there is usually immediate termination. The only CSWA wrestler ever publicly fired for violating the drug policy was Alexis Vann in 1994. The current firestorm of controversy is the first to hit the CSWA in its twelve-year history. August 2000 Previous TRIBUNE Stories from August Hornet 0ut? Thomas To Build New League In what could be an odd twist of fate, it appears that CSWA co-owner and Commissioner, Chad Merritt, may have followed through on his threat to ban Hornet from future CSWA events. Hornet is not signed to appear at PRIMETIME in Phoenix. Since his return, Hornet has been working without a contract on a show-by-show basis. Even more bizarre, it appears that CSWA co-owner Stephen Thomas may launch his 'counteroffensive' shortly...in the form of a new wrestling company. It does appear that both sides are determined to avoid a work stoppage as was ordered in June and early July by the court. TRIBUNE reporters have found out that despite his 'coup' in signing former champion Tom Adler to a CSWA contract, Thomas appears ready to launch a league, apparently with Hornet as the cornerstone. Court Battle Continues You may have noticed that the TRIBUNE has been lacking updates recently. We can tell you, it wasn't our idea. In May, CS Enterprises co-founder and co-owner Stephen Thomas filed a cease-and-desist injunction against partner Chad Merritt and CS Enterprises, Inc. Apparently the real-life feud between the two partners left the world of rings and arenas for the world of lawyers and courtrooms. The case was fast-tracked, the injunction was granted, and all CSWA business was stopped in its tracks. While contractual obligations to performers and other staff have been met, TV shows were sent into reruns, house shows were cancelled, and the court battle ensued. Following preliminary evidence in the case, Judge Hugo Black has ruled that the CSWA may continue operations under the purview of Chad Merritt, at least until further evidence is presented and a judgment is rendered. Thomas's main points of contention appear to surround the CSWA's near-bankruptcy in 1997-98 as a result of Merritt's incarceration and trial for the murder of Lyle Tallman aka The Red Midget. Thomas maintains that Merritt overexercised his power in the company, eventually costing CS Enterprises millions. Thomas has also charged Merritt with misconduct, including abusive behavior. Merritt's response charges Thomas with incompetence, arguing that Thomas's grasp of the lines between reality and fantasy have become blurred. Merritt argues that much of their feud has been 'on-air', that is, created for the camera as part of the CSWA product. The trial resumes at the end of the month. On another legal note: we reported to you in May that CSWA Commissioner Chad Merritt was attempting to sign former four-time CSWA World Champion Hornet to an ongoing deal. Hornet's previous contract with the CSWA was dissolved last year when he used an option clause to end his six-year, $150 million contract a year early. With the recent legal battle between Merritt and Thomas, Merritt was legally bound from continuing contract negotiations with Hornet. TRIBUNE insiders have learned that one of the first bullet points on Merritt's agenda is to seal the deal with Hornet. Previously, Merritt offered a deal similar to Hornet's previous contract, but for a shorter period of time. Hornet turned down the offer; in fact, to this point he has refused to discuss a contract at all. Hornet is currently involved with the CSWA on a per-appearance basis. If the two sides are unable to reach an agreement, Hornet could be banned from all CSWA events. We'll keep you updated. October 1999 Previous TRIBUNE Stories from 10/3/99 Hornet Retires! During SHOWTIME at Negril, former four-time CSWA World Champion Hornet walked out of his Unified Tag Team Championship Match, leaving Aaron Douglas to deal with the Powers of Love alone. Footage released shortly after SHOWTIME showed Hornet leaving the building. However, a CSWA camera crew got him to talk, in which the following was included "Don't get me wrong...I don't begrudge anyone anything. But all of you, as well as the CSWA, I've carried on my previously-aching back for over eleven years. From the first day I walked in to the God-forsaken Merritt Auditorium until tonight. Lord...even when I was gone for a year I still carried the place. And I'm tired of it. I'm tired of what it's made me become and because of what I've done. It's not worth it. None of it has been worth losing my best friend, or backstabbing a dear friend, or even the nine-figure deal. I'm tired.....I quit." The CSWA has not released an official response. When we here at the TRIBUNE contacted Hornet's agent, we were told that Hornet's option had been exercised, and he was no longer under contract.
