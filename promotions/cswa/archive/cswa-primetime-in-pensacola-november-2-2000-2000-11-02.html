DreamMaker vs. Carl Brigsby BB: On his way to the ring is a man making his CSWA debut, the DreamMaker. SB: That's a scary looking man. He does realize he's in public, doesn't he? The match was a squash. DreamMaker set out to show Carl Brigsby, along with the world, the wrestling skills that brought him to the CSWA. Brigsby tried a new tactic, charging DreamMaker early, only to be met with the big boot sole of DreamMaker's greater-than-size-15 shoe. Maker followed up by literally tossing Brigsby into the corner and following with a clothesline. DreamMaker stared down a few members in the audience as he waited for Brigsby to come out of the corner. Brigsby finally did, only to be on the receiving end of a sidewalk slam. Maker continued to allow Brigsby plenty of time to recover. As Brigsby got to his feet again, DreamMaker's mood seemed to change. Maker suddenly went on the offensive, chokeslamming the smaller man, then scooping him up for an airplane spin which ended in a Death Valley Driver. Rather than go for the pin, DreamMaker opted to display his finisher, a standing vertical suplex into a powerslam, which he calls "In Your Dreams," which is right where he put Brigsby for much longer than the needed three-count. Johnny Lang vs. Jean Rabesque This was a great match that really went to the wire, both men started off on fire with an all-out brawl in the middle of the ring. Lang took control after he ducked a clothesline from Rabesque and caught his opponent with a flying body press that took both men outside. On the outside, Johnny Lang went to town on Jean Rabesque, dropping him on the guard rail and driving his back into the ring post several times before rolling him back into the ring and getting a near fall. Lang continued his attack on Rabesque’s back, nailing a pendulum backbreaker and sidewalk slam in the middle of the ring before locking on a camel clutch that had Rabesque in agony. The referee asked Rabesque several times if he wanted to give in, but he refused, even though at once he appeared close to passing out from pain. As Lang rocked back to add to the leverage of the hold, Rabesque managed to summon up enough effort to get to a vertical base and dump Lang, knocking the back of Lang’s head off the canvas in the process. As both men lay on the floor trying to muster themselves the referee started the standing ten count. At nine, Jean Rabesque got up and started to unload on Lang. He got several near falls thanks to a backdrop, powerslam and belly to belly suplex combination. A pumphandle slam sent Lang to the floor where he was further punished by Jean's elbow drop off the top rope. Rabesque thought he had got a three count, but referee Worthington advised Lang’s foot was on the rope. Lang came back and stuck his thumb in Jean Rabesque’s eye before whipping him into the ropes, Lang went for a back drop but Rabesque read it and countered with a sunset flip which earned him a three count and with it the win. "XXX" Sean Stevens vs. Cameron Cruise This match really had the packed CSWA crowd going as Sean Stevens continued to try to make a name for himself in the CSWA, hoping for a win over the veteran Cameron Cruise. Stevens looked good early on, with a series of attacks that had Cruise worried. After a dropkick and clothesline combination, Stevens nailed a high cross body from the top rope that earned him a two-count and forced Cruise to go to the outside to rethink his strategy. Once back in the ring, Cruise used his experience to get an advantage, catching "Triple X" with a forearm as the referee tried to break the pair up. From then on Cruise took it to Stevens, leveling him with a spinebuster before putting the boots to him and covering him for a two-count. Cruise dominated his opponent, using several wear down submission holds to try and make him give up, but the plucky Stevens refused every time. Even when Cruise applied a modified kneeling sleeper Stevens still refused to give in. In frustration, Cameron Cruise turned it into a modified suplex, but still only got a two-count. As time passed, and the resilience of Stevens became more obvious, Cruise started to lose his cool. With it came a mistake, as he set his opponent up on the top rope for a superplex Stevens saw his opportunity and connected with several kidney punches, followed by a bulldog from the top rope. As both men got to their feet, Sean Stevens tried to mount some offense with a dragon suplex, a side Russian leg sweep and a superkick which got him a two-count. Stevens signaled he was going for his finishing move, but Cruise read this and connected with a low blow behind the referee’s back. Cruise dished out more punishment to the exhausted Sean Stevens, but once again Stevens refused to give in. Cruise set Stevens up and connected with a high cross body off the ropes; the referee counted to two before Stevens managed to reverse it and hold it for the three count for an impressive win over Cameron Cruise. Cruise was livid after the match and claimed that Stevens had held onto his tights during the count, but referee Ben Worthington said he saw nothing and the decision stood.
