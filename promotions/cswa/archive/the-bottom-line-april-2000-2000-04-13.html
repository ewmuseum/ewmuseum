The Bottom Line, April 2000

The Bottom Line Back to TRIBUNE From the desk of CSWA Co-Commissioner Chad Merritt.. "Yoo hoo, I'll make ya famous...." That's right, the Kid is back, and the Enforcer is still enforcing. For those of you who thought Thomas could rid of me once and for all....what were you smoking? Before Austin 3:16, before Sting had "Showtime!", even before Hornet was "Takin' Care of Business", one man was telling it the way it is... ..and that's the bottom line. Or at least a semi-shoot. It's time to talk about angles and pushes. I've tried to make this as clear as possible on the Rules, as well as in discussions I have on the subject in RP Central and elsewhere. The first, and probably most important point is this...and I'm sorry that it's going to come off sounding like a "Commissioner Merritt" imitation (but hey, the guy *is* based loosely on somebody, isn't he?), but here it is: I am responsible for how all angles are used. I am responsible for all match decisions. Got that one? You sure? You might want to read it again. Whether it's written by a staff cardwriter, or even decided by another member, the buck stops here. Now here's the second point...and it's almost as important. Your idea for a storyline may not be the same as mine. Over the years, I've have various discussions (and even out-and-out fights) over wins and losses, but overall, I've had more over how angles were used to progress a storyline. And I know you all know this intellectually, but I want you to etch it into your heart. Your angle is not the only one. Your character is not the only one. There are other characters and angles that have to be juggled in this act we call 'booking.' And that's where I come in. In 95 percent of cases, I use a conglomerate of all angles received to try and make sure that at least a portion of every individual's angle is used. It may not be exactly as you want, and it may not have the desired consequences or effect, but I try to use it. In that other five percent, I either have to use other angles totally, or I have a specific vision for a match and may ignore angles entirely. That's all part of the game. You are responsible for your characters. You are primarily responsible for the direction they go in, and the feuds they have. However, I'm responsible for all characters. And when your vision collides with others...that's where I make the decision. Everyone wants to be a World Champion. Everyone wants to be in the 'top tier' of characters. Not everyone is going to reach that level. To do so requires constant growth in your character and in the quality of your roleplay. To take an example, look at Eli Flair. [name withheld]'s character was developed even back in 1995, but it took time and persistence to grow the character into a CSWA World Champion in 1999. Flair's World Title shots were few and far between, partly because [name withheld] got involved in other feuds (which continued to build the character), and partly because I didn't push him in that direction. And while Pete may disagree with the way we handled Flair in specific instances, I have no doubt that he'll agree that we've been eminently fair. And that's my goal. To be fair, and to have fun. You may have a specific place you want to go with your wrestler. You may have a specific plan laid out. Let me know where you want to go. But be aware that in most cases I'm not going to come out and tell you, "No, you're not going to go that way right now." Good roleplayers have to be able to pick up on nuance. If I book a match between you and a character you weren't expecting, chances are, I've got a reason. If things aren't progressing the way you would like, there's probably got a reason. Am I always going to tell you where we're headed? Absolutely not. Again, in 95 percent of cases, you're not going to know a long-term plan...you may know a couple of twists or turns on the road there, but you're not going to know it all. I've got to keep giving you reasons to roleplay, and opportunities in which to build your character and others. In many cases with characters nearing that 'upper tier', I'm going to throw screwballs at you to see how you deal with them. Whether it's a 'losing streak', or facing opponents you weren't expecting, or a total change in storyline, I've got to see what you're made of. World Champion-caliber characters have to be detailed, consistent and have some logic behind what they do. Your character has to react within the storyline and within the characteristics you've established....not necessarily how you as a handler react. And my plans change. Based on the outflow and inflow of members, on your roleplay, on your ideas for angles...my plans change almost daily. But if you try and out-and-out flout what I'm trying to do, or 'decline' a match I've booked you, be aware right now...you're gonna job. Does roleplay determine matches? Absolutely. Does the roleplay before a specific match determine that match? Almost always. Is there a difference in deciding Main Events versus other matches? Absolutely. Long-term angles play a much more major role in the World Title picture and in Main Events than they do anywhere else. In all matches, but especially in feuds and World Title matches, I look at the history of the roleplay, the progression of the feuds, and the long-term angles that are behind the scenes. Take a look at the last year in the CSWA. Various 'top tier' wrestlers have been moved around to make way for other wrestlers and other angles. Eddy Love and Mark Vizzachero were shifted to other feuds before and after their World Title wins. Eli Flair and Deacon were moved into top slots after major storylines. Kevin Powers, while never a World Champion, has consistently moved in and out of Main Events and top storylines when necessary. Top characters learn to see part of the 'larger picture'...they know how and when to move in and out of specific feuds and storylines, and to accept the nudges that I give in specific directions. The bottom line is this... you're not always going to agree with the decisions I make. You're not always going to happy with the direction I push your wrestler in. But the key to success is learning to take lemons and make lemonade...and to do it within the storyline. You're not interested in a specific feud or in wrestling 'that guy' again? Too bad...if I sign it...you've got to learn to live with it. My job is, with your help, to make the best stories that we can together. Some will last a month, some a year, and as you've seen, some will continue to progress for as long as the CSWA continues operating.
