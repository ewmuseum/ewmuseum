The REAL Story, part three — 1992-93: We're Gonna Make It After All

1993
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
CSWA History: The REAL Story You know the history.... now get the REAL STORY. 1992-93: We’re Gonna Make It After All Reaching back into the past is a tricky thing. Memories fade…but there are always those things that you remember perfectly, or those memories that cause a flood of emotion. We’ll get to those shortly. In the last chapter, I told you a little bit about how the CSWA closed out 1992. We had created the first “superleague,” the EN (Enterprise), by bringing six leagues under the banner. Hornet had become the EN Superleague World Champion by defeating the other five league champions in two-out-of-three falls matches based on the CSWA dice-and-points system. I try and give you plenty of footnotes as I go through this saga, and here’s another about “the system.� We were both praised and maligned for the “system” we used. It was never a great secret…in fact, we posted the entire system for members to see. In a nutshell, each wrestler provided twelve moves. Each move was worth a certain number of ‘damage’ points. The dice randomly gave ‘control’ to a wrestler, determined what move or pinfall he used, and whether a move was broken, a pinfall successful, etc., etc. Many people argued that Hornet was successful because we ‘knew the system.� Actually, we were never able to figure out how to beat the system, no matter how we tried… it was just too random. Every time we tried to create the perfect wrestler (see Horse, Nemesis, etc.), it backfired. Generally, we figured that the more total points a wrestler had in his twelve moves, the more successful he should be…because he should be doing more �damage’ each time he did a move. Interestingly enough, Hornet’s points total was under 700…well under the 820 points we used as an upper limit. Go figure. The rise of the superleague, accompanied by the rise of Hornet as the EN Champ, didn’t go over too well with a few. The president of the GGWF, [name withheld], opted to leave the EN to start his own superleague, along with [name withheld], another league president. Miller and [name withheld] persuaded various leagues to join. At the same time, a couple of EN leagues were closing, as part of the regular cycle of fantasy wrestling. With the CSWA the final ‘major’ league left in the EN, we made a couple of tough decisions. The first was to have Hornet drop the title. For the first time, we ignored the system and simply booked a finish. Lex Vicious, a member of Cornette’s Corporation, won the EN Superleague Title with the help of a well-placed chair. Dream Warrior would go on to defeat Vicious shortly thereafter…and he would be the final EN Champ, for a while. The other tough decision was to bite the bullet and move the two or three remaining EN leagues under the UWL banner, the superleague [name withheld] had started. To be honest, I can’t remember the exact chain of events that led to this odd combination. At some point, [name withheld] was a key part of a superleague called the “IN”, an attempt to co-op the EN’s name. I don’t remember whether this was the original superleague that [name withheld] was a part of, or if the IN came later. What I do know is that the UWL went into politics as never before seen (up to that time anyway) in fantasy wrestling. Somehow, all the member leagues agreed to ‘elect’ a president for the UWL. [name withheld] put himself forward, then Miller put himself forward. Steve and I both knew that in the current political realm, putting either of ourselves forward would have split the vote and made things a bit tenuous. Instead, we put our support behind SUWF President (and CSWA VP, although I believe that was *after* the UWL), [name withheld]. Scott was well liked by just about everyone, and he carried the election almost unanimously. [name withheld] congratulated Scott, continued to participate for a while, and then left due to personal reasons. Miller, on the other hand, in the tradition of small dictators immemorial, decided that if he couldn’t win a fair election, he’d simply create his own superleague. Miller created the “People’s Wrestling Alliance” (PWA) with two other leagues, the presidents of which were Miller’s staunchest supporters. The �backroom’ competition between the UWL and PWA was fierce, as the war of words between Miller’s group and the rest of us continued. Scarily enough, the only things that the PWA was truly notable for were the BWFA, a league run by [name withheld], and its “Cheers� subject, which was supposed to a ‘bar setting’ for out-of-roleplay discussion. The inane babble collected there is almost legendary. On another note, [name withheld] eventually turned from the ‘dark side’, acknowledging Miller’s dictatorial tendencies. Paris later became a CSWA VP, and eventually his final league was a part of our final superleague. Although the online arguments between myself and [name withheld] are probably more famous, and certainly had more impact on fantasy wrestling, the ‘battle’ between CS and [name withheld] was certainly unique. Miller’s usual tactics were to try and backstab us when we weren’t looking. He was successful in doing so only once, pulling leagues out of the original EN and moving them to the UWL. Unfortunately for him, his ‘power’ was short-lived, especially after the UWL �election.� Some of the most telling things about Miller were his inability to accept the results of a fair election…especially in light of calling his new superleague the “People’s Wrestling Alliance.� In the same vein, while Miller professed complete aloofness from the other leagues in the PWA, behind the scenes he made various demands of the league presidents, with various threats if they didn’t comply. The PWA became a victim of poor management as well as the PRODIGY price hikes, which wiped out almost half the fantasy wrestling area there. Fantasy Wrestling on PRODIGY would never quite be the same. [name withheld] ran various unsuccessful leagues after the PWA. His second attempt at a superleague (if I remember correctly), the “IN” (Independent) league lasted a much shorter period than the PWA. Another league which literally had matches written ‘minute-by-minute’ ("59 minutes to go….here’s what happened…58 minutes to go…") faded within a few weeks. Unfortunately, Paul became severely ill a couple of times during his FW career on PRODIGY. My understanding is that he is currently well, and has his own 'recap' site which features wrestling recaps and "Battlebots" recaps. Two major events happened in mid-1993. First, the PRODIGY price hikes were announced. Most services like PRODIGY and AOL went to a ‘pay-by-the-hour’ scheme in 1993, with no option for ‘unlimited usage’ like today. Averaging $2-$3 per hour… the budding internet became pretty expensive! Fortunately, we knew that our plan wouldn’t switch until October…others weren’t so lucky. The summer saw the beginnings of a mass exodus as the largest online service at the time, with the largest fantasy wrestling base, began to dissolve. Secondly, I took a series of trips, first to Houston for two weeks, and then to Romania for another seven. We made the decision to close the league…and to do it in style. We announced a card called LAST CALL for late May, and we headlined it with the match that everyone told us they wanted to see…Hornet vs. Mark Windham. Yet again, we opted to ‘book’ the match rather than use our system, as much to keep our friendship than anything else. I don’t remember if it was on that occasion or later, but we did attempt to roll a Hornet/Windham match one time. We stopped less than halfway through because it was obvious that if we continued, there was gonna be a fight. The LAST CALL Main Event ended in the infamous ‘double-pin’ with referee Carl Young having a heart attack as he is asked to render a decision. The following year, Junior Hornet and Muppet Kid (Timmy Windham) would tease finding the tape with the evidence of who pinned who during the match. And so, the CSWA closed, and in early June, I left the country for Romania. Unbeknowst to me, Steve started the league back up in late June…and when I returned in late August, things were going strong. Maybe another time I’ll tell you the other funny story of the time, it involves the aforementioned [name withheld], but requires too much backstory to go into now. As October approached, along with our inevitable change to the hourly rate on PRODIGY, we scheduled LAST CALL II. Everyone expected another closing… instead, we swerved and finished the card by announcing that the CSWA was going ‘home,’ leaving the UWL superleague and reforming the EN with three other leagues, [name withheld]’s league included among them. Both the EN and UWL Superleague titles still exist in some part today as part of the retired Unified World Championship. Hornet consolidated the titles by defeating Dream Warriors and Polaris in matches while he held the Unified Title. Speaking of the Unified Title...there's always been a slight bit of confusion surrounding the "CSWA Unified Title" and the "Unified World Championship." Here's the explanation. The CSWA Unified Championship was created over time by Hornet in 1992. As the EN began to form, including the CSWA, the JWA, and the WDWF, Hornet challenged their World Champions. The CSWA Unified Title remained separate from the EN and UWL Superleague Titles, until they were merged into it in 1993. LAST CALL II featured the first ever IRONMAN of CHAMPIONS. The intent of the presidents involved, like AAWC Prez [name withheld], etc. was to create a Unified World Championship. The challengers were AAWC World Champion Hot Shot, AAWC and UWA star Tom Adler, CSWA Unified Champion Hornet, IWCCW World Champion Flic Rair, CSWA star Steven Flair, and Joey Melton, who competed in the UWA, AAWC, SUWF, CSWA and other federations. The IOC was a round-robin tournament done by a slightly-modified CSWA system (total number of points was upped as if each match was a championship match, etc.) All the matches were rolled out, and then we began writing up the event, culminating in the final Joey Melton/Hornet final match. (In fact, all the results from the rolled matches are still in one of two big boxes in my closet somewhere.) LCII occured in 'fantasy time' on October 24, 1993. Unfortunately, our PRODIGY plan had recently changed over, and the subsequent three-figure bill stopped us from signing back on until December. The results were posted, 1993 ended, and we began planning the next ULTRATITLE, and major changes in the CSWA.
