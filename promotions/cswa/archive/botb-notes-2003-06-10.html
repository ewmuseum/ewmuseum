GreggG — 06-10-03, 11:55 AM I did a big run-through today... awesome, awesome, awesome.



Three guys were handled superbly.



1) Shane Southern. Three awesome matches. Great booking. I've always been a big believer in that you can get over without having to win all your matches. Shane lost two and came out looking like the biggest star in the promotion. Great characters can be used in ways like that. Jason-- I've said Shane's been one of my favorites for years now and I didn't think you got your due. The past year or so (in NFW and CSWA) you've really stepped Shane up to the point where he can be used in any situation. As someone who writes matches in and kinda-sorta books some things in both leagues, I can't tell you how much I mark out for that. Shane's become the glue in two of the biggest leagues in FW.



2) Tom Adler. Tom trading one title for another is consumate Heel 101. Adler got over as a HHH-type-- always thinking, playing human chess and ended up not looking bad. It just makes everyone want to destroy him more. Expertly played. IMO, you should do one or two interviews with Adler not addressing his opponents but addressing his motives. Why he does what he does. What he thinks of the fans at home. That would perfectly tie the ribbon around the bow and have it all come together perfectly.



3) Dan Ryan. Mark-out central. I've dug Ryan's stuff since he came into the CSWA. His "Oompa Loompa" note against Guns a few months back is one of the all-time greats. The writing is there. The character is there. Now, it's breakout time. I see Ryan being in the same position that Steve Radder, Lawrence Stanley and Evan Aho have been in the past few years. On the cusp, waiting to take his spot. All three of those guys became main eventers. All three are also great characters in how multi-faceted they were-- they could be used in hundreds of ways to get themselves over and other characters over. Ryan's at that stage now and that's the highest compliment I can give to a fellow RPer.

J Chad — 06-10-03, 12:58 PM I'm glad you picked up on those three above all. Honestly, how to handle everyone was a big concern, but especially those three based on ongoing angles, etc.



To be perfectly honest, I know there was significant concern going on about the Windham/Hornet main event when it was announced long, long ago. Let me just tell you that it was *always* planned for Hornet to no-show, and for either Sight or Plett to replace him. After thinking about it, I opted for both.



We've obviously got several 'established' names in the CSWA. We've got others that are established, but not as firmly in the CSWA echelon. Part of the booking, especially for guys like Southern and Ryan, was to push them to that next level where they belong, and then see what comes of it.



With Shane, as probably one of only two or three true 'babyfaces' in the company (most of the others are just 'faces' or are turning), the challenge was to set it up so that he took the beatdown, but still came out the other side. I hope that I did that, and that Jason feels that his writing was rewarded, because that was the intent.



With Adler, Tom's had some great angle ideas, so it was easy enough simply to edit and use most of them in a way that pushed Adler as one of those 'established' guys that's still really breaking out in his own way.



The same was true with Hornet's roleplay. While he didn't have a true 'presence' on the card, my challenge was to try to 'rehash' ten-fifteen years of history and repackage it so that it was meaningful, so it showed what effect it's on Hornet and how he's dealing with it. I hope that worked as well.



I was honestly dreading this PPV, which was probably one of the reasons it took so long to get to it. But as the angles came in, it make life much easier for me, and took a PPV that could have completely bombed, and turned it into a great jumping point towards CSWA15. I think Jon brings up a pretty important point here that needs to be considered when those who are ultimately in charge decide on the direction they want to take the CSWA and how it's operated.



(and I realize that this should probably be posted down in the general discussion forum, but since it's a follow up of Jons comments I'll leave it here).



Jon and I are both angle heavy RPers. There are others here I'm sure. There's a lot of times that I send in my stuff to Chad and, right before I click the send button I look at it and wonder if he's gonna fall asleep before he gets half way through it.



But, I know in my case, much of what I submit has to do with a CYA type approach to my characters... Adler in particular.



Gregg's assessment of Adler's current position on screen (which I appreciate a lot) tells me that the direction that I've at least TRIED to push Adler in seems to be taking hold. And while I, personally don't see Adler as a heel (more a true independent contractor who truly believes what he says), the "Cerebral Assassin" image is one I've certainly tried to push (just with hopefully a better work rate and less politics that hurts HHH).



But, to pull that off, I kind of feel obligated to work Adler from MY end in such a way that it fits that image. To me, that means submitting angles that attempt to cover all the bases.



A lot like Miles... Adler tends to make a lot of enemies. When I'm sending him into any match (particularly one that involves Jon or JN in any way), I just have to ASSUME that there's another angle coming from the other end, and try to offer alternate angles to counter those.



Perhaps I take it a little too seriously, who knows. But, when it comes to submitting angles, I try to look at it the way wrestling is ultimately booked... human chess.



Chad's never complained (to me at least) about the length of my e-mails, so I don't stress over it too much.



However, it brings to mind an admission on my part. I honestly have no clue how to continue to do that for a show like On Time (at least as I understand it).



While I always try to present a decent amount of detail when I send in angles (just because I understand what I mean when I send them doesn't mean Chad or whomever he forwards them to for writing will intuitively understand it), doing them all in long form (plug and play so to speak) is going to be difficult.



If the role of On Time (from a production standpoint) is to allow things to move more quickly by allowing members to assume a certain amount of the production duties, it could push the more angle heavy characters away from that card toward PT where a more "traditional" approach can be taken.



-TW Chad — 06-12-03, 09:59 AM TW, good points. I'll try to address them briefly.



First, on the angles. Yes, you and [name withheld] (and to an extent a couple of others) send in really involved angles with counters and counters to counters, etc. To be honest (and I think this is no surprise), there are times when I ignore some of the counters... because if I didn't, the angle would crash-and-burn, or would be so convoluted that it wouldn't move anyone forward.



That being said, there are also times that I use parts of those angles directly, or when they've sparked some idea for me when I was having trouble. So there are two sides to it.



As far as ON TIME goes, yes, there's the possibility that 'angle-heavy' guys could shy away. However, if you really look at it, in some ways, OT is the most angle-driven show we've got. We're asking you guys to write the segments, plot out the matches, etc., to help drive your character in the way that you want.



What it means, though, is you have to pare down some of these massive ideas that come up. You have to realize that they may have to be broken down over several cards, which, as we all know, hasn't really been an option in the past due to the schedule.



So yes, it's something of a paradigm shift. And it's going to take time to get used to, which is why Pete is working so closely with it and with those involved. Just be aware that if Pete feels like he has to edit something down, then I've asked him to do it to keep the show where it needs to be. As I've mentioned before, the last time we tried ON TIME, it got to the point where it was so big that it lost its original purpose, which is to move the action along in a more timely manner.



Plus, I'm going to be booking a lot of the matches on both cards, rather than depending on challenges. And that really means that you won't have much a choice. ;) Because if you bomb out on your prep for ON TIME, I'm sure not going to push you on PT.



Just keep in mind the current basic structure as far as the number of matches and segments... that means that you can't do five segments worth of stuff on one card. It means that really, you can't do *two* segments on one OT, unless one is really brief.
