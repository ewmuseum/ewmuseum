CSWA Spotlight: Anniversaries Past ANNIVERSARIES PAST The lines of history are like highways: they can connect two distant points as one. They are like currents in the air or water: they can lead to places never before dreamed of. Like isobars and isotherms, they measure the pressure and temperature of time's events; like latitude and longitude, they can give precise location, but only at a given time. History's lines are both narrow and wide: take the wrong path and you end up in a place you never wanted to be. This is the history of ANNIVERSARY, the CSWA's annual pay-per-view celebration of its birthday. In some ways, it is the entire history of the CSWA rolled into bite-size portions. April 1989: Year One The two matches that everyone talks about from the first ANNIVERSARY were both fought by the same men. There are only three or four thousand people who actually saw it live in the Merritt Auditorium, then still a somewhat-modified dingy warehouse in Greensboro, NC. Others saw it on television, and tapes still circulate to this day, but the matches were so long that they were severely edited for television. It had been ten months since Horne defeated Degadeth for the title, and he faced his biggest challenge to date. Ray S. Cornette had added three huge bruisers to his "Family." Known as the Boulevard Warriors, one stood out...and his name was WALL. Within the span of 24 hours, two men put the CSWA on the map. Greensboro's hottest secret quickly became the Eastern seaboard's hottest commodity. (CUTTO: CSWA ANNIVERSARY 1989. The first ANNIVERSARY, one year, three champions. He picks up the downed Hornet and nails him with the Slam Heard Round The World, and Cornette straps the CSWA World Title around Wall's waist.) March 25 - April 15, 1990: SKYDOME As the CSWA continued to gain prominence and expand its territory, the event continued to grow. ANNIVERSARY became a month-long tour from Greensboro to Nashville, ranging north and south as far as they could go to draw crowds. OPENING DAY was celebrated in Greensboro, while ANNIVERSARY WEEKEND, the final weekend of the tour, was hosted by Nashville, TN. During ANNIVERSARY 1990, the CSWA featured some event favorites from the past year, as well as some brand new ones. The Presidential Tag Titles were created using the "Top Dog" Tournament, a precursor to the John W. Boozer Invitational Tag Team Touranment. The CS Express won the tournament, the titles, and the million dollar purse, a first in the CSWA. It wasn't a first, but the ULTRATITLE Tournament was certainly a favorite. Beginning in 1989, the first tournament was won by former World Champion Joey Melton. In 1990, the final match would come down to two CSWA fan favorites. With Mark Windham as the special referee and Hornet en route from Chicago, "Hollywood" Kerry Allen hit a discus punch to knock Bill Parsons out and win the ULTRATITLE Tournament. After the match, Cornette's CORPORATION jumped Windham. Hornet appeared to help save the day, at least briefly. Windham tossed Terminator onto the commentator's table, inadvertently knocking Sammy Benson out. As it looked like America's Team might be outnumbered and in danger of being attacked by a fireball from Safari Sakan... the lights flickered, and The Hooded Falcon appeared on the apron, knocking Sakan away and into his own fireball. As the lights came back on, Mark Windham dove off the top, clobbering Ray S. Cornette. The new facility being used in Nashville was nicknamed "SKYDOME." The feud between Bill Parsons and Mickey Benedict would eventually become known by the same name. Parsons and Benedict were actually distantly-related by marriage, and bad blood began to come to a head after Parsons defeated Mickey in the semifinals of the ULTRATITLE. While on tour, Mickey Benedict avenged his loss against Parsons, setting up a third and final rubber match. With the Presidential Championship on the line, Bill Parsons reversed Mickey's patented slingshot suplex to win the match as well as the title. March 16 - April 16, 1991: SKYDOME Returns In 1991, the CSWA held its most aggressive ANNIVERSARY Tour yet, with two series of house shows going on at the same time. ANNIVERSARY became known for multiple title changes on the road. Early in ANNIVERSARY, Bill Parsons won the newly-created Pacific Championship in a tournament, defeating Johnny Dunn. Parsons was challenged immediately in the ring by Mickey Benedict. In their fifth match of all time, Benedict defeated Parsons with the retooled slingshot, then known as the "Missile," to become the new Pacific Champ. Michael Benedict defeated his father, Mickey, just a day later, to capture his first title, the Pacific Championship. Bill Parsons went on to face Johnny Dunn again. Dunn, who had just won the Greensboro Championship in an eight man tournament, lost his first title. However, Bill lost his second championship of the tour, defeated by Dustin James for the Greensboro Title. Joey Melton's record Intracontinental Title reign was ended after eight months and two weeks by rival Sammy Flair, who was defeated the next day by former tag team specialist Beauford Parsons. Parsons managed to hang on to the title for three days before losing to the Dungeon Master. Two days later, Joey Melton started his second Intracontinental reign on March 28th, just six days after losing it. Mark Windham returned after weeks off due to a car accident. He claimed his car was sabotaged, just as we found out that Hornet, nursing a leg injury following the ULTRATITLE weeks earlier, had been kidnapped. "The Chase" was on, with Hornet eventually being rescued from his captors, Cornette's henchmen. But it wasn't before he had been rolled down an entire set of hospital stairs while in a wheelchair. March 14 - April 20, 1992: The Final Days of Reckoning: Road to SKYDOME Hornet's bid for back-to-back ULTRATITLE Tournament wins ended in the round of sixteen, when Cornette's favorite goon, Terminator, reversed Hornet's risky top rope sunset flip to win the match. Terminator would lose to Dusty James in the round of eight; but would avenge his loss later in the tour, defeating James for the CSWA Title (not World). Joey Melton moved onto the Final Four by defeating Bill Parsons. He lost to the masked "Anniversary Atom" in the finals. The Atom turned out to be Mickey Benedict. As the "Final Days" came to a close on ANNIVERSARY WEEKEND, the CSWA broadcasted its first national pay-per-view. Following the series of matches the previous year, as well as growing bad blood, Benedict and Parsons held a two-out-of-three falls SKYDOME series. The loser agreed to step out of the life of Melba Benedict, Mickey's wife. In the first fall, Bill Parsons won, reversing the Missile attempt by Bill Parsons. The second fall lasted over thirty minutes, with Mickey Benedict eventually capitalizing on two direct hits with his Missile maneuver and capturing a cradle three-count. And in the final fall, Mickey took only one maneuver...once again the Missile...to put Bill to the mat for the final time during ANNIVERSARY. Mickey and Melba were reconciled and reunited. Later in the evening, Hornet defended his World Championship. The title match that made news, however, was Mark Windham defeating Beauford Parsons for the Presidential title, having stepped back into the ring weeks earlier following a knee injury. In the CSWA's bid to put something 'different' on PPV for the fans, the "FIREHOUSE" match was born. The match would eventually become called the "Deadly FIREHOUSE" match, after the carnage witnessed that night. With hopes of gold, six men stepped into a cage structure that was set ablaze. The Dragon and Dusty James were eliminated early, as Sandman and the All-American continued on to meet the first 'Firemen', Avenger and CORPORATION member Road Runner. Eventually, All-American met up with the "Master Fireman" at the top of the cage, Intracontinental Champion Joey Melton. Melton defended his title successfully and escaped the cage, suffering little. The injury tally released later would show Dusty James in a short coma, Sandman and All-American with second-degree burns, with the Dragon permanently scarred due to third-degree burns. March 6 - March 20, 1993: A Nashville Explosion After defeating Lex Vicious in a brutal five-fall match, Hornet entered ANNIVERSARY 1993 and declareed the "Champion's Challenge" open to all comers. Ten matches and two weeks later, Hornet remained the CSWA Unified World Champion. The ULTRATITLE returned to ANNIVERSARY, this time with a 128-man field. After 112 matches, the field was narrowed to the Sweet Sixteen, including Wildstar, 1990 Tournament winner Kerry Allen, Lex Vicious, Joey Melton, Dusty James and 1992 Tournament winner Mickey Benedict. Mickey defeated Wildstar to move on to the Final Four, where he defeated Dusty James and Kerry Allen to become the only man to ever win the Tournament twice, as well as back-to-back. September 29 - October 1, 1994: The Wrestling King As the CSWA began to brand its' pay-per-views, the "tour" concept of extended schedule of ANNIVERSARY changed. In 1994, the celebration was delayed until September, when it was combined with ELVIS LIVES VIII, headlined by the third meeting ever of Hornet and Mark Windham. In this match, however, the Unified World Title was on the line. Almost a year earlier, Hornet succeeded in creating the full-fledged Unified World Title, merging the CSWA Unified along with various federation and superleague World Championships. In June 1994, disturbed by a dispute between the CSWA and a coalition of small federations under the Unified banner, Hornet relinquished the title. A thirty-two man tournament was held over a series of events, with Mark Windham winning the title, disguised as "Mr. Magnificent" due to a contract dispute. The two-out-of-three falls match at ELVIS LIVES saw Hornet take the first fall, with Windham taking the second. Tempers began to flare as the two tired competitors tried to win the deciding fall, neither giving ground as the action went outside of the ring. Windham, upset over being knocked into the steel post, attacked Hornet with a chair from the commentators' table. As Windham began to take over the match, he was distracted by Mickey Benedict's comments from the commentators' area. The long-standing Windham/Benedict feud escalated once again, after Benedict helped cost Windham the Unified Title. July 4, 1995: Mine Eyes Have Seen The Glory As the CSWA's schedule continued, the seventh birthday celebration took place at the same time as the nation's birthday, at FISH FUND X. Some of the events and feuds that were (or would become) staples in CSWA history took place at this mega-event. Mark Windham's feud with Mickey Benedict seemed to come to a head. But rarely has one match ever had consequences so long-standing...or so bizarre. Following Windham's brutal attacks and the electrified steel cage match earlier in the year, Benedict was confined to a wheelchair. In order to head off a lawsuit, the CSWA agreed to allow Benedict to create his own rules in a match with Windham. Mark was ordered to face a competitor of Mickey's choice. If he refused, he would be suspended. If he won the match, he would recover his nickname, "The Living Legend," that he had lost to Benedict. Mickey showed his diabolical streak as he named Mark's own brother, Timmy Windham, as the opponent. Timmy had suffered a neck injury weeks earlier, but showed up to compete. Mark seemed to snap, taking his aggression against Mickey out on Timmy. He would win the match and recover his nickname, but his relationship with Timmy would never be the same. In the wake following the match, El Nino tried to attack Timmy, his former tag team partner, but was held off by the CSWA's resident midgets. El Nino gained his own nickname that evening, "The Midget Killer", as the Red Midget was seriously injured. It was the beginning of Red's true fame, as he left the ring to enter the commentators' booth. The second IRONMAN of CHAMPIONS took place in the same evening. In the final match of the evening, Hornet and GUNS met for the first time in the CSWA. Tied for second place, a pinfall win by either man would win the IRONMAN and the Unified World Title. Late in the match, referee Ben Worthington was caught in the middle as Hornet sent GUNS into the corner, setting him up for the Hornet splash and Scorpion Deathlock. After a lengthy period in the Scorpion, Hornet released his signature hold and attempted to revive the referee. During the following series, GUNS countered by illegally sending Hornet over the top rope to the apron, injuring Hornet's back. With GUNS' knee and Hornet's back both reinjured, it seemed as if there might be a countout. GUNS succeeded in getting Hornet into the ring and taking over the tempo of the match as the referee recovered. Hornet continued to avoid pinfall after pinfall, but was too injured to mount any offense. GUNS began to taunt Hornet as he continued his attempts to pin the former Unified Champion. As the clock counted down, GUNS set Hornet up for a superplex. If GUNS hit the maneuver, there would be no chance of Hornet recovering. Instead, Hornet countered the top rope move, sending GUNS over the top and twelve feet below to the floor. Hornet was disqualified for deliberately suplexing GUNS over the top turnbuckle. GUNS had won, but he did not receive enough points with the disqualification win to take over the top position in the IRONMAN. Alexandr Karelin became the IRONMAN winner and new Unified World Champion as the paramedics attended to GUNS. March 31, 1996: Hot Southern Days and Rock 'n Roll Nights ANNIVERSARY returned under its own pay-per-view banner in 1996, as the CSWA presented CSWA ANNIVERSARY 1996: Hot Southern Days and Rock 'n Roll Nights. The first event after the infamous WINTER'S WARRIORS VII PPV, ANN96 featured Poison Ivy vs. Teri Melton in a Tuxedo Match, a slew of championship matches, a Loser Leaves Town match, and even the one-of-a-kind "Basebrawl" match between "Eliminator" Eli Flair and ATTAXX for the Intracontinental Title. Day One's Main Event featured the newly-heel America's Team, Hornet and Mark Windham, against the unlikely tag team of GUNS and Tom Adler. Adler would get the pinfall win on Windham, following the miscue by new America's Team member, Paul Pierce, with a loaded coal miner's glove. The Day Two Main Event showcased the first match between Mike Randalls and Hornet after Hornet discovered he had been drugged at the hands of the USN Champ, and made to believe he was responsible for the death of Eli Flair's 'wife,' Alicia. America's Team struck in full force during the match, as the apparent rift between Windham and Pierce the night before was shown to be a sham. Hornet, Windham and Pierce laid into Randalls as no one had before, taking everyone, even Randalls by surprise. April 28, 1997: Nine Years In The Making ANNIVERSARY 1997: Nine Years In The Making began a new era in the CSWA. Hornet was gone following the FISH FUND XI travesty; Randalls has stepped into a feud separate and apart from the World Title hunt. It seemed that the era of GUNS had begun. The Final Four in the CSWA World Championship Tournament featured GUNS, Troy Windham, Joe Massacre, and a masked man, later revealed to be Julius Godreign. GUNS would defeat Godreign to win the CSWA World Championship, his last major title reign in the sport. But before the festivities could begin, the Red Midget was attacked and hung. The card continued (it *was* just a midget after all), but Sammy Benson was arrested later in the evening as the lead suspect. The 'death' of Lyle Tallman would play a major role for three years in the events of the CSWA for the next three years, even causing a CSWA shutdown when Commissioner Merritt admitted to being responsible. Of course, as we all know, Lyle AKA Red didn't really die. April 15, 1998: Sink or Swim Fifty weeks following the 'murder' of the Red Midget, the CSWA would hold CSWA ANNIVERSARY 1998: Titanic: Sink Or Swim. This PPV event marked the return of the CSWA following Merritt's incarceration in a mental facility for the Red Midget ' incident.' It also marked the possible end of the CSWA if they couldn't turn a profit on their first major PPV in months. What it eventually marked, was the return of the CSWA to worldwide prominence, as CS Enterprises banded together to put on a hallmark show that was the beginning of the modern era. Featuring some of the top stars of the CSWA's heyday, ANN98 set the stage for the CSWA to begin importing new talent from across the world for its second run at the top of the wrestling ladder. Mark Windham and Troy Windham were matches against each other in a "Family Feud," while Mike Randalls and JT Tyler fought in a battle of former Unified Champions. Killer Instinct and SWAT Team faced off with the Unified Tag Team Titles on the line. And in a first, the vacant CSWA World Championship was to be decided in a simple match between two perennial enemies, Hornet and GUNS. In the end, the CSWA served up the action it has always been known for, as well as leaving the great cliffhanger...who would eventually become the CSWA World Champion? With the addition of top talent never before seen in the CSWA, the question would rage over the following months. July 25, 1999: Revelation CSWA ANNIVERSARY 1999: Revelation was all about answering the questions. The Red Midget returned from the 'grave' and into the spotlight at Commissioner Thomas's request. Mark Windham's "Awakening" proved to the world that he may have been crazy, but he was also right. Mark faced his brother Timmy Windham, once again, at ANNIVERSARY. And once again, Mickey Benedict was there. Mark revealed Mickey's disability was no more than a stunt. And as he defeated both Timmy and Mickey, it all became clear. The reason why Mark didn't race into FISH FUND Park to save Timmy months earlier... the reason why he couldn't let his hatred toward Mickey Benedict go... the reason he had to become a Lost Soul. The man who sacrificed his friends, his family and his career did so for the truth...the revelation that it was Troy, not Timmy, who was his long-lost brother. But just as that understanding had come as such a cost, the price became greater as Troy, in his anger, revealed that Windham's long-time best friend, Hornet, had been sleeping with Mark's wife. The fourth IRONMAN of CHAMPIONS featured some stars new to the top tier of the sport. Deacon, Eddy Love, Mark Vizzack and Kevin Powers all went on to hold World Titles in the CSWA. In the end, Hornet and Eli Flair met in the ring, with the winner becoming the CSWA World Champion. It was only fitting that Hornet and Flair should meet in the end. Hornet had betrayed Flair's friendship, even stealing away the love and trust of Flair's sister-in-law and manager, Poison Ivy. Ivy's trust buckled under the pressure of finding ou that Hornet had not been faithful, and in her guilt, she did the only thing she could do...she hit him in the head with the singapore cane. Eli Flair rolled up Hornet, becoming the CSWA World Champion four years after signing his first CSWA contract. Hornet would repeat a phrase used by Mark Windham after his first match with Timmy in 1995, "What have I done?" It's amazing, isn't it? ANNIVERSARY always comes full circle. Long-time feuds like Parsons/Benedict and Benedict/Windham have been replaced by feuds like the confusing Powers/Love/Radder triangle and Deacon/Randalls. At first glance, on the surface, ANNIVERSARY 2000 may not appear to have much in common with Anniversaries of the past. But as any CSWA fan knows, those who don't learn from history are doomed to repeat it. In a different light, in the simple changes of color and shadow, the second glance can be vastly different from the first. Stand in front of an impressionistic work of art long enough, and you'll find meaning within textures and patterns that you never believed possible. The simple gives way to complex; as the eye finally focuses on the slight variations it missed the first time. They were there all along... you just had to look long enough to find them. For some people it takes twelve minutes...for others, it could take twelve years. The CS Tribune SPOTLIGHT shines the light on CSWA wrestlers, their careers, and their feuds, giving you an in-depth look at what makes and breaks the best in the sport.
