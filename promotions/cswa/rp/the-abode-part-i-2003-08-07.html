The Abode Part I

7 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Steve — 08-07-03, 01:51 AM The Windham abode has been without its master for the last month. The absent touch is a requirement to being owned by Mark. Home mirrors where the heart is, and Windham’s ticker rarely lives inside itself anymore.



Like the five bedroom two hundred thousand dollar estate, Mark’s heart was left to face the last thirty days alone. Free of the usual tenants who paid for occupancy: Sunshine Del Payne and reason.



Sunlight cutting through a bedroom window as regular as good clockwork was all to mark the passing of days. The house left unchecked with no defenses against the ghosts of a once proud man and the ripples of the relationship between his greatest love Sunshine Del Payne.



It’s a curse that ghosts are never allowed to create new memories. They live though, omnipresent, always right, always a bearer of truth for how healthy the man once known as Mark Windham was.



To his ghosts Windham has often turned for evaluation. Is she the right one? Can he finally put the questions to bed?



It’s a curse that Mark Windham lives so much of his life to be forgotten, and only is awakened by the haunting throbbing of past sins.



Ghosts never vanish. For as long as they’re allowed in the world, it’s their right to live too. It’s Windham who lets them breath. It’s Windham who nurses their faint pulse when he makes headway. It’s those damn demons that have driven all hope away.



How can he quiet their passions like so many others have? How can he find peace in the place where God directed him?



Mark Windham has never lived for himself. An aging boy now, terrified of being alone. It’s when he’s left in solitude, when family and friends desert him, that Windham opens the line of communication to his past. The one constant in the course of his days have been the ghosts. Mark’s depended on their company. Without it, he’d be forced to eye the dark for answers. He’d be asked to stretch an empty hole into a willingness to love.



Windham listened to his old friends, and then hatched a plan to pull Sunshine into the past to join them.



He hears her now too.



Sees her trying in vain.



Touches her body over and over again, but the sensation dissipates with each caress. He’s ready to love her now, like he loves all things relegated to the past. As Mark Windham’s right hand reaches for the front door for the first time in sixty days he quietly hates himself for not being strong enough to nourish their love when it was still real.



Mark flares his nostrils slightly in-taking the calm air. When he and Sunshine were together, their activity set the house’s energy, circulated the controlled air. Apart, and absent the oxygen mixes with dust and settles randomly through out the estate, waiting to be awoken by the lost sleeper or a conversation between the man’s ghosts themselves.



Windham drops his bag at the foot of the black leather couch and strongly considers blending into the furniture, out of sight and mind for as long as it takes to recover.



Rest and relaxation: novel concepts for healing.



Mark’s knees bend, tendons stubbornly resisting being pain free, and he mentally draws an “X” to collapse onto.



“You’re a day early.”



Knees buckle on cue at the sound of a loaded cardboard box hitting the carpeted floor.



Wearily Windham turns his head towards the left and focuses on the woman he recently hurt and pushed too far, the mother of his miscarried child.



It’s never been said directly, but the Lost Soul is more than responsible for that tragedy as well.



“I can leave if you like. I expected the house to be alone.”
