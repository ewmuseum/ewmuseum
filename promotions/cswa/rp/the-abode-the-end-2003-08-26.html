The Abode - The End

26 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Steve — 08-26-03, 03:03 AM “You’re a day early.”



Mark Windham’s lungs were taking in air. The man was alive. A breathing contradiction to the signals the rest of his body was receiving.



He dozed off after climbing back into his car at the Sweetwater Airport. He’d take a second to relax before starting home. Windham’s heavy eyes, appreciative of the rare consideration, shut and locked, and for the slightest hint of time Mark Windham was the closest to legally dead he’d ever been before.



His right hand dripped off the steering wheel and fell harmlessly aside. A flash of light shook Windham’s conscience and his body levitated off his seat to accommodate an approaching dark brown tunnel, cave-like in appearance.



Another flash of light, the strength of this one putting the last to shame, then the Lost Soul was left jetting through a cave in darkness.



Mark’s body was cold. A misty wind pinned his arms back, an argument against reaching for physical proof that he wasn’t hostage to a dream. Was he ascending?



Descending?



God, Windham couldn’t tell.



If he could just reach for a wall and slow the fall, grab on right himself and barter for life. If there’s any man who knows what a Soul is worth, it’s the Living X-File.



What does it want?



Falling.



Slipping quietly into the next world.



Cleansing mist like glue over Windham’s eyes, the water scanning for remorse, signs that the life being taken was well-lived.



Water.



Were those tears? Windham’s own fears holding him back. He could stop the descent. He could crawl back into the misery embodied by his flesh and bones.



<i>Tick.

Tick.

Tick.</i>



When in control Windham’s heart is destined to go off.



<i>Tick.

Tick.

Tick.</i>



Accept that’s bull**** or the fall will never stop.



<i>Honk.</i>



A car accident narrowly avoided behind Windham’s parking space wakes him.



Whoever said death was peaceful was obviously still alive.



Mark’s aching right hand adjusts the rear-view mirror until his bruised face warms to the spotlight.



Hello hell, nice to see you.



Better men have died younger.



Either Mark’s lucky, or God simply doesn’t care. For the past week Windham’s slept to be taken while dreaming, the ideal way to go.



Fade. Fade away.



Peaceful.



Yeah. No horrific last second to deal with the time I’ve wasted.



Peaceful.



Yeah. Dull my senses and freeze my soul. It’s yours now God. Do with it as you wish, just take control. Cause when I do, the innocent are roped to the tracks.



I’m a train Lord. Why have you never stopped me from picking up speed?



Heavy and burning, Windham’s eyes open, he can see the world, despite the man’s wishes.



He was scheduled to arrive tomorrow. In place of bleeding into a leather car seat afraid of facing his daughter alone, Windham was supposed to be at CS Towers in Greensboro, hooked to electronics, letting his movements and likeness be digitally implanted into expensive computer systems.



CSWA PRIMETIME 2004: a perk of carrying Merritt’s financial aspirations on your back.



If it didn’t hurt to laugh, Windham would have chuckled at the thought of thousands of gamers controlling Mark’s digital clone. Maybe they can do better. Pin Hornet, make Ryan tap, raise a daughter well, keep the girl, and continue to call Ivy a best friend.



<i><b>If you can keep your head when all about you

Are losing theirs and blaming it on you,

If you can trust yourself when all men doubt you,

But make allowance for their doubting too;

If you can wait and not be tired by waiting,

Or being lied about, don't deal in lies,

Or being hated don't give way to hating,

And yet don't look too good, nor talk too wise:



If you can dream—and not make dreams your master;

If you can think—and not make thoughts your aim;

If you can meet with Triumph and Disaster

And treat those two impostors just the same;

If you can bear to hear the truth you've spoken

Twisted by knaves to make a trap for fools,

Or watch the things you gave your life to, broken,

And stoop and build 'em up with worn-out tools:



If you can make one heap of all your winnings

And risk it on one turn of pitch-and-toss,

And lose, and start again at your beginnings

And never breathe a word about your loss;

If you can force your heart and nerve and sinew

To serve your turn long after they are gone,

And so hold on when there is nothing in you

Except the Will which says to them: "Hold on!"



If you can talk with crowds and keep your virtue,

Or walk with Kings—nor lose the common touch,

If neither foes nor loving friends can hurt you,

If all men count with you, but none too much;

If you can fill the unforgiving minute

With sixty seconds' worth of distance run,

Yours is the Earth and everything that's in it,

And—which is more—you'll be a Man, my son!</i></b>



Windham must make himself a man, or choose to keep falling.



Two months prior he made the choice to turn his back on the Windham Abode and the price was deep and unforgiving. A day earlier, and too late, Windham chose home over fame.



“You’re a day early.”



Knees buckle on cue at the sound of a loaded cardboard box hitting the carpeted floor.



Wearily Windham turns his head towards the left and focuses on the woman he recently hurt and pushed too far, the mother of his miscarried child.



“I can leave if you like,” Windham’s question thrown on the floor in an effort to prove chivalry wasn’t dead, “I expected the house to be alone.”



“No, stay,” Sunshine offered, her voice coarse and distant, “It’s your place.”



“It was...our place.”



“Was it?”



For two years Mark Windham and Sunshine Del Payne shared the same address, just Windham, his lover, and the aforementioned crippling ghosts.



“Sunshine...I...tell me what to say here,” Windham’s words probed the ice between them, seeking a crack, a trail that led back inside her world, “Please.”



“Goodbye.”



“No, I know you don’t want this,” Mark forecast inching his beaten body closer fully aware of Sunshine’s weakness. He needed saving more than ever.



“You’ve never known what I’ve wanted. You don’t have the right to pretend now.”



“Dammit Sunshine I <b>need</b> you right now.”



“It’s too late for that,” Sunshine shot back, her tone filling any invisible cracks. “Look at yourself Mark. You’re a wreck. Why do you do this? Why do you put yourself in helpless situations?”



“Its all I know.”



“Whatever.”



“What do you want to hear?” Windham cried, “There’s no last layer of skin to peel back on me. I am who I am Sunshine. I tried to change for you, but couldn’t.”



Sunshine bent and took the cardboard box back in her hands, the impression she’s meant to leave on Mark fully lost. You have to save yourself. Salvation means being willing to change.



“You never tried to change Mark,” she spoke to him as if he were a child. “Don’t kid yourself. You just ran. There was a time in my life when I loved chasing. I thought I meant something to you,” Sunshine’s voice shook, “but I see now...it could have been anybody.”



“That’s not true.”



“It’s not?”



“I played to your eccentricities when I should’ve forced you to grow up.”



“So that’s what you’re leaving with. The idea that you wasted your time,” Mark replied, retreating to the couch.



“What have you done lately to show me that I haven’t?” A fair question, Mark knew it. What had he done? Never said what he thought. Loved another woman behind her back. He’d done nothing. “No, I don’t regret being with you but I can’t lock myself in this relationship anymore.”



Sunshine paused, her eyes swelling.



“I can’t be the only one growing.”



“Well I’m sorry you’re spinning your wheels here,” the Lost Soul muttered in defiance of Sunshine’s cut.



“Don’t get sarcastic. We need change.”



“I ****ing killed our child!” Windham exclaimed, jumping to his feet to play the martyr.



Sunshine stood in silence for a beat then slung the box aside.



“Oh please,” Sunshine responded. Was he serious?



“What?”



“Take the weight of the world off your shoulders you might like it,” her words darting through the air pricking at Windham’s bubble. “You didn’t cause this miscarriage.”



“You said yourself...”



“It’s happened before,” Sunshine’s retort was soft. A truth she hadn’t planned on releasing.



“What?” Mark stumbled back, descension in five minutes. “When?”



“Four years ago.”



“You didn’t tell me...” Windham’s voice gained strength as he spoke.



“I didn’t know how. I could barely take care of myself, and you weren’t much better,” Sunshine pleaded. Four years, a lifetime ago. “I never made it to the fourth month.”



“**** Sunshine.” Mark rests his hands on the back of his neck at a loss. “You’re hating me for keeping secrets.”



A good point, but Sunshine was left little choice. In four years she’s failed to impress on Mark that everything isn’t his fault. He’s right though. She should have shared the first pregnancy. The two look off each other, who’s move is it?



The mistakes of both cornering <b>any</b> hope for the relationship. Sunshine last box back in hand, heads for the door.



“My flight leaves in two hours,” she states coolly.



“Where you goin?”



“I’ve got a friend in California. I need to get away from everything.



Mark hides pain behind a laugh. “From me?”



Sunshine cocks her head in amazement, “If you had any idea how much I loved you, you’d understand what you did to me.”



Windham did know. If she suffered a tenth of what he’s encountering presently, there’s no excuse.



“Stay...please. You accuse me of running.”



“That’s not fair,” Sunshine shot back in her defense.



“No?”



Sunshine’s chest expanded, reaching for a deep breath. “I need time alone Mark,” a million times before her words have soothed Windham’s edges, saved him if only temporarily. “We’ll talk again.”



“This is for good isn’t it?” Windham asked as Sunshine pulled open the front door, an illusionist groping for the escape.



“I don’t know.”



She never could lie.



Mark steps toward Sunshine and bravely smiles. She changed his life dramatically, an odd statement when neither can see the effects, but he’s changed. In her care, the Lost Soul healed. It’s up to Windham to allow salvation to work in his life. If there’s one final lesson Sunshine could teach Mark, it’d be the one that allowed her to throw her arms around the world.



“Be good,” Windham offers as his right hand runs through her blonde hair. He leans in and kisses her. As their lips pull apart, Mark savors the taste. If he honestly thought there would have ever been a day when he couldn’t kiss her, Windham would have tasted his lover’s lips a million more times than he did. He’s not ready for life without her.



“You too,” Sunshine wishes as she backs out of the doorway.



“Sunshine,” Windham calls out. “I’m sorry.”



“Yeah, I am too.”



And so she turns and heads off, like the two have unfortunately grown accustom to, in an opposite direction. Mark Windham drops to his knees, the hurt now incomparable to the last two-months.



As she escapes, the Lost Soul knows he’ll never see her again.



When left to his own devices, will Sunshine’s legacy be that he’s able to find control.



<i>Tick.

Tick.

Tick.</i>



Alone, Windham starts over, fearful of life.



When he fears, the train can’t be stopped.
