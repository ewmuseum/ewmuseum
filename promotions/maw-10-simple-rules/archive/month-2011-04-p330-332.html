“What?”

“I’m sorry. I’m 33 – this is my last chance at the big leagues. And you’ve always said I was good enough to make it at the top.”

“But... Eisen?” Rip growled, his expression still one of total shock.

“I’m sorry,” Jean said, hanging his head in shame.

“Don’t be,” I said, shooting a glance at Rip, whose face was becoming a worrying shade of purple. “Rip?”

He let out a long breath, and his colour began to return to normal.

“Yeah... ---- Jean, you know I’m pleased for you. If I coulda chose, I’d have thought you’d get a deal in Japan or even with Cornell. But Eisen? ----.“

“You made the same move, once upon a time,” Jean said, looking up.

“Yeah, I know. ---- kid, I thought you were a lifer.”

“So did I. You know I would have ended my career here, Rip. But the money I’ve been offered...”

“Ah, I’m not cross with you, kid. You’ve gotta do what’s right by yourself,” Rip said, with a sigh. He looked across at me. “Anyway, we’ll have to find a way to take the belt off you. We’ll let you know what we come up with.”

Jean acknowledged the tacit dismissal with a nod, and quickly made his way out of the room.

“----,” I said, and meant it. “Of all the people to go, I never thought-“

“Did you have something to do with this?” Rip snarled, coming around in front of my desk and leaning on it, glaring down at me, sheer fury burning in his eyes. His face was past purple and was now a distressing shade of violet.

“What? What? No!” I barked, rocking back in my chair as Rip leaned forward still further.

“Jean’s been MAW since day one. The only way he’d leave is if he thought he wasn’t going to be used properly.”

“Or if he got a better offer,” I said, trying to be reasonable. It was difficult. I’d faced Rip down before now, but somehow this time was different – Rip looked dangerous. “Look, Rip, I understand you’re upset-”

“Upset?” Rip yelled, and with a sweep of his arms my desk was cleared, my lamp, computer, papers and pen pot slamming against the plasterboard wall, and crashing to the floor. “Upset?” he roared, and with an almighty heave my IKEA desk went flying into the opposite wall, leaving a sizable dent and hanging for a second, before landing and shattering on the wooden floorboards.

I was frozen on my chair as Rip advanced towards me. He seized the front of my shirt and hauled me upright with barely a grunt of effort.

“Tell me now why I shouldn’t just throw your --- out of here,” he snarled. I grabbed at his hands and tried to break his grip, but it was like trying to break steel clamps.

“Because I’m your best hope for overhauling Eisen one day, you stupid ----,” I yelled.

Rip’s expression changed from unguarded fury to enraged calculation in a split-second – a detail I only registered in hindsight as, a split-second after that, he drove me hard into the wall behind me, my head slamming into the barely-covered brick so hard that my vision went black.

I whuffed out all the breath in my body, and gasped and wheezed as I tried to take a breath while the back of my head complained about the rough treatment it had received.

My vision cleared slowly as people began cautiously to enter the room. Jean was there, and Heidi, and even Jay.

But Rip was gone.
