MAW: 10 Simple Rules Month

August 2011, after Summernova

in-game August 2011: the cap of dog dirt, the narrator's three allies in the locker room, Rip Chord's threat in the dark, Randy Bumfhole's move to TCW and the card for Iron; the saved copy carries no posting dates
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

*

With nothing actually bad on the show, and two or three good matches, I was disappointed that the whole thing came off feeling somewhat flat. Accordingly, as I limped backstage and found myself in a locker room where an old Stevie Grayson baseball cap sat in the centre of the room, filled with steaming dog turds, I wasn’t in the best of moods.

“Who the ---- did this?” I snarled. Jay sauntered forward, looking smug. Around the room, others were gathering their things, looking green. Brandon Smith was conspicuous by his absence.

“Jay,” I said. “You’re owning up?”

“Hell yeah,” Jay said. “Crap in a cap. It’s genius! Do you like it?”

My cane clattered to the floor as I slowly rolled up the sleeves of my shirt. Limping forward, my hands shot out, and I seized the lapels of Jay’s coat, yanking him towards me.

“Listen to me, you stupid little ----er,” I said. “This is your last chance. No more pranks, no more rumours, no more fights, or-“

“Or what?” Jay spat, with enough feeling behind it that little flecks of spittle hit my face. “What are you going to do? Fire me? My father owns this company. Christ, we actually thought you’d be useful to us, once upon a time.”

“I can’t fire you, Jay,” I said. “But there’s no law saying that I have to book you. You can sit on your arse in Hollywood for all I care. Your matches suck, you don’t bring people in to the shows, and you’re a generally unpleasant toerag who I loathe spending time around.”

Jay’s face darkened with each word, and eventually he wrenched free of my grip. Bad news for me – I’d been leaning into him and his move sent me sprawling. Jay laughed as I crashed to the ground, the wind knocked out of me as I watched his shoes walk away.

“You can try and discipline me, James,” he said, on his way out the door. “But I think you’ll find that you don’t have... a leg to stand on.”

There were one or two chuckles in the locker room as the door banged behind Jay. Strong hands pulled me upright as Steve Flash and Chance Fortune helped me stand, while Randy Bumfhole brought me my cane.

“Are you hurt?” he asked, handing it over.

“Just my pride,” I said. “Can I ask a favour of you three?”

They glanced around the rapidly thinning locker room. Kashmir Singh was fastidiously cleaning Jay’s latest prank into a bin, while everyone else was getting ready to leave.

“What do you need?” Steve asked. As always, his New York-cum-Canadian accent threw me a little.

“I need ears and eyes in the locker room,” I said. “I’m not asking you to spy on anyone – except Rip and Jay. Something’s coming down the line, and I’m certain it’s coming soon. I don’t want to be the last one to know about it,” I said.

The three veterans, all of whom had a love for the business that had transcended their better judgments to bring them to the Mid Atlantic, communicated with glances.

“We’ve got your back,” Chance said.

“I’m not fighting a battle,” I said, smiling to reinforce my words. “And I certainly don’t want to do anything that might harm the company – even if I’m gone. But I don’t want to be left...”

“...without a leg-“

“-hanging,” I said, with a mock scowl at Randy, who laughed.

“You’ve got it, James,” Steve said. “We’ll make sure you’re kept in the loop.”

I nodded my thanks, and found myself a spot on a bench far from the bin with the evidence of Jay’s genius in it. Despite what I’d said to the others, the fall had hurt – clearly my days of taking bumps were behind me.

*

It was some time later before I left the locker room. The lights were out in the arena, and I hoped that the security guards had been told I was still around. I’d had one or two embarrassing incidents in the past, working late after a show. The others had long since gone, but my aging frame had taken a lot of convincing to get up and move. I was looking forward to a hot bath and a massage – if Katie wasn’t already in bed.

With those pleasing thoughts in my mind, I wasn’t ready to be taken off my feet and slammed against the wall.

“You don’t threaten my son, do you hear me?” Rip growled in the darkness.

“What the ----?” I managed.

“Do you hear me?” Rip snarled, shaking me hard even as I realised that he was holding me several inches off the floor.

“Rip! You’re crazy!”

One hand let go of my shirt, and grabbed the back of my head, lifting my eyes up to meet Rip’s cold, dead stare.

“You don’t threaten my son, do you understand me?” he demanded, his voice now flat, leaden, cold.

“Yes!” I half-yelled.

Rip released me, and once more I dropped to the floor. It took me several seconds to gather myself.

By that time, Rip was gone.
