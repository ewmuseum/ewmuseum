MAW: 10 Simple Rules Month

September 2011, after Back To School

in-game September 2011: the fight in the locker room and Jay Chord's firing, Tommy Cornell's offer of a job, Rip Chord and Jay in the narrator's office, Heidi Brooks's contract, Erik Strong's warning and the card for Fall Of The King (the show itself is in the next page range); the saved copy carries no posting dates

I stared Jay Chord down. We were the same height, of comparable build and honestly I figured if it came down to it then I could just smack him around the head with my cane.

“You attacked Hugh,” I said. We were alone in my office, and Jay was cradling his bruised knuckles.

“Our match sucked,” he said, defiantly.

“And you’re putting the blame on him?”

“I’m putting the blame where it belongs.”

I’d entered the locker room just behind Rip after the show to find Flash and Antonio trying to haul Jay off Hugh. I’d ordered Jay into my office – amazingly, he’d complied – and Chance had filled me in.

A discussion between an overheated Jay and a flippant Hugh about their match had descended into the two men shouting in one another’s faces. Hugh had come to his senses quickly and tried to walk away, but Jay kept needling him – and when Hugh didn’t respond, Jay sucked punched him, before jumping on top and raining blows down on him as Hugh tried to cover up.

“The fault is mine. I should have remembered that you two don’t work that well together in the ring,” I said. There was a knock on the door, and Heidi came in with a bowl of ice. “Thank you Heidi,” I said, as she set it down on my desk. I gestured for Jay to sit down and ice his knuckles. “Can you ask Rip to come in here, please?” I added, as Heidi left. She turned and nodded, before heading for Rip’s door.

“So the fault for the match is mine. I apologise for that. I’ve had a lot on my mind recently, but I should have remembered.”

Jay relaxed slightly, settling into the creaking chair and giving a sigh of relief as his knuckles were numbed by the ice.

“The fight is no-one’s fault but yours,” I went on, looking through the papers on my desk for Jay’s contract. I heard Jay’s hissed intake of breath as the door opened, and Rip came in. He had a face like thunder, and for once the recipient of his fearsome gaze was Jay.

“You’ve been warned before, Jay,” I said. “And this isn’t the last straw. This is an entire sack of rocks.

“You’re fired.”

I waited a beat, before looking up. Rip was staring at the scene before him grimly. Jay’s jaw had dropped.

“What? But... you can’t... That’s the stupidest thing I ever... Dad?”

We stared at Rip together, waiting for the decision on whether I could ever expect his support again – or whether he was going to prove to be a blind, over-indulgent parent.

“Well, Rip?” I asked. “What’s it going to be?”

The phone rang, and I answered it reluctantly. I was in my office, so it wasn’t Rip – for once.

“Hello?”

“Hello, James,” came the rough, Liverpudlian voice of Tommy Cornell. I blinked in surprise.

“Tommy, hi,” I said. “What can I do for you?”

“I heard about your spat with Jay and Rip,” Tommy said. I rolled my eyes. The ‘spat’ had made its way onto every wrestling site and dirtsheet in the country. I hadn’t told them, and Rip had an inbuilt distrust of wrestling journalists dating back to the kayfabe era, so there was only one person who knew the story who could have spilled the beans.

“And Jay’s out west with you right now, isn’t he?” I asked, neutrally.

“He is. The little gobs---- has been running his mouth off since he got here.”

I felt a surge of affection for Tommy Cornell.

“What can I do for you, Tommy?” I asked. “I don’t expect that you called to pass on the backstage gossip.”

“No, I didn’t. Tell me, James, what do you think of Jay? Your professional opinion, I mean?”

“He’s a great talent, and he’ll go a long way in this business,” I said, without hesitation.

“And his attitude?”

“He’s still only a kid,” I said, without hesitation again. “If he’s giving you trouble, he just needs handling properly.”

Tommy chuckled. “That’s what I’ve always liked about you, James. You’re able to separate personal and professional – it’s very dignified.”

“I’m glad you think so,” I said. ”Was that all you wanted? Because, to be honest, I’m not exactly an expert on how to handle Jay Chord.”

“Neither’s his dad,” Tommy replied. “No, there was something else. Come and work for me.”

I froze.

“What?” I croaked.

“I’m offering you a job. You’d be head road agent at first. Then, when you’ve had a chance to recover from dealing with Rip and Jay, you can get involved more in running the show.”

My mind raced at the thought of going Hollywood. There was so much talent available. I’d have the chance to work with Aaron on the big stage. The chance to work with Sam Keith, and Brent Hill, and Wolf Hawkins... And Tommy himself!

“Why me?” I asked, at last.

“Why not you?” Tommy asked. “We’re a few months away from going global. I want someone with your credentials backstage to look after things. Besides, any excuse to ---- off Rip. You know that.”

I frowned.

“I’m not going to be a pawn, Tommy,” I said. “You’re the head of a multi-national company. MAW is struggling to fill high school halls outside of the Mid Atlantic. Don’t you think you could leave off Rip?”

Tommy laughed again. “Calm down, James. If I wanted to put Rip out of business, I would,” he said. “He gave me a rough time coming up the ranks – the same as he did a lot of people. That’s Rip. But you reap what you sow, and it’s harvest time for Rip Chord, James. He’s made a critical error in the way he’s treated you. You have to realise that.”

This time I did hesitate before speaking.

“Tommy, I won’t pretend that I’m as happy here as I have been in the past. But I have a contract, and the big show is only a few weeks away.”

“I understand,” Tommy replied. “Look, the post is there. We won’t be looking to fill it for a while yet, but you’re our number one choice. We’ll pay your relocation costs, and if you want to bring Katie out with you then I know we can find a spot for her as well. If you want to commentate, you can. If she wants to, I’m sure we can find her a spot.”

“You’re serious about this,” I said.

“You’re our number one choice. You’re my number one choice. James, I look at what you’ve done in the last few years and I’m very impressed. You’ve made mistakes, I know, but you’re sharp, and you’ve got a great mind for the business. Think about what I’m offering.”

“I will,” I said. “Tommy, thank you.”

“I’ll be thanking you if you come out here,” Tommy said. “Let me know what you decide.”

“Okay, Tommy. I will.”

“Good. Speak to you soon.”

Jay sat across from me, lounging insouciantly in the chair. Rip stood behind him, hands upon his son’s shoulders.

“In the last few days, Jay has turned down offers from WLW, INSPIRE and SAISHO,” Rip said, beaming at me. “I expect his loyalty to be rewarded.”

“Fine,” I said, shortly. “You can have a run with the Trios belts. Antonio and... Oh, Casey, why not, can be your partners.”

“I meant a real reward,” Rip said, his smile vanishing as he looked at me.

“You want me to find him a hooker for the night?” I asked. Rip flushed purple.

I settled back in my chair, my posture a deliberate mirror of Jay’s.

“Now, let’s get serious. Jay wants some wins. Understandable, and I’m prepared to write that into the shows. I’ll give him a win on every show, if you want. But I’m not doing that at the expense of the company as a whole. We’ve already got the problem of keeping Jay and Hugh apart.”

“Fine. Sack Hugh,” Jay said, carelessly.

“He’d take us to court and own the company before your father had time to down a pint,” I shot back. It was a cheap shot – in five years working with the Chords, I’d never dropped so low as to refer to Rip’s battle with the bottle. In fairness to him, he’d not touched a drop since I’d joined the company.

I raised my gaze to meet Rip’s. His face was unreadable. Inwardly, I shrugged. What was done was done, and apologising would make no difference.

“You both know the plans for the next few weeks as well as I do. They’ve been set in stone for months. They will not change – not now, not tomorrow, not ever.”

“And that’s your last word?” Rip asked. The sentence hung in the air.

“Yes.”

“I see. Come on, Jay. We need to make some phone calls.”

Rip stalked out of the office, Jay trailing in his wake. At the door, Jay turned to say something, but I had already turned my attention back to the papers on my desk. I did have a message for him, though – my left hand rested on the top of the desk, middle finger pointing at the sky.

The door slammed as Jay left the office, and I sank back into my chair. Katie had left a powder compact on my desk some time before, and I used it quickly to inspect my hair. No question – there was more grey there than the last time I looked.

“James?”

“Hi, Heidi,” I said, glancing up from the call sheets for the next Tour show. “What’s up?”

It was a moment before Heidi replied, and when I looked up I was shocked to see tears in her eyes.

"Heidi? What's the matter?"

"I just spoke to Rip... He said that the company's not going to renew my contract," she said, making an effort not to break down.

My stomach sank. Heidi took a lot of the day-to-day business off my hands, letting me concentrate on the shows and workers while she dealt with the sponsors, merchandising and so on.

"Did he say why?" I asked.

"He said that we're running at a loss, and he's making cuts."

I scowled.

"That idiot. We're running at a loss because he wanted two shows a week before we were ready. Heidi, I'm sorry. I wish there was something I could do, but I can't extend your contract when Rip's blocked it."

"I know," she said. "Thanks for bringing me in anyway – it’s been a fun couple of years."

I nodded, and put the show aside to help her clear her things. I walked her to the door, past Rip's office. The door was open, and Rip was at his desk, but when I glanced inside, he was staring fiercely at his computer screen. There could have been any number of reasons for that - but a part of me knew that he was working out how to spend Heidi's $200 a show.

"Goodbye, James. Keep in touch," Heidi said, once her car was loaded.

"I will. And perhaps in a few months-"

She shook her head.

"I don't think so. Not if Rip's still like this. And be honest, James, do you think you'll still be here in a few months?"

"Tommy offered me a job," I said, suddenly. I felt like I owed her something - I hadn't even had a chance to discuss it with Katie yet.

"Take it!" she said. "James... I think Rip's going to run this company into the ground trying to catch up with the Eisens. Get out, before he takes you with him."

I didn't know what to say to that, so I watched in silence as one of closest allies got into her car and drove away.

"Boss?"

I raised an eyebrow as Erik Strong slunk into the room, looking as though he'd found a dollar – but had paid 99 cents for it.

"What's up, Erik?"

"I've had an offer from up north. They want me back."

I nodded.

"That wasn't unexpected. Have you told Rip?"

He nodded.

"He said I should take the chance with both hands – he seemed really calm about it."

I was slightly surprised, but kept that to myself.

"Boss?"

"Not any more I'm not – but go on, anyway."

"Listen, before I go, I just wanted to say thanks for keeping faith in. For bringing me back after I left the first time. You've done a lot for me."

I shrugged. Really, I'd always looked at Erik as a body to fill the roster with. He had talent, but he didn't seem able to keep up at the top of the card.

"You're a good worker, Erik. And MAW has always been about good workers, first and foremost."

"I know. But... has anyone told Mr. Chord?"

"What do you mean?"

"Well... He's different to how he used to be. Maybe because I've been away I can see it – he just seems angry all the time, like something's chewing at his insides. And... Look, I'm going to be gone, so it doesn't affect me. He's called a meeting of the whole roster on Saturday morning – and told everyone not to tell you. I thought you should know."

I nodded, as though I had already known. In fact, it wasn't much of a surprise – if anything, I'd expected it to happen after the big show, but Rip had always been impatient.

And that was the problem. I'd given Rip a taste of success with MAW, and his long-term plan of catching and beating Richard Eisen – for there was and nearly always had been bad blood between them – had been brought so much nearer by SWF's precipitous fall from grace. Rip wasn't given much to introspection, and I knew that having SWF so near, and yet so far (for, realistically, they were still one of the top three wrestling companies in the world, if somewhat down on their luck right now) was eating away at him.

Seeming to be so near, and not being able to make that final step... Well, Rip would find someplace to lay the blame. And that place, it was clear, was going to be on me.

It was now only a matter of time.

I shook Erik's hand, and walked back inside to call Tommy and find out more about TCW.
