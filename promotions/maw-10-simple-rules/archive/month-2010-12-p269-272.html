MAW: 10 Simple Rules Month

December 2010, after Season's Beatings

in-game December 2010: the fight in the locker room and the phone call with Rip Chord, Jim Force's return to USPW and the card for End Of Days; the saved copy carries no posting dates
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

*

It was Antonio who fetched me. It was the first time I’d had to be hauled out of my office in I don’t know how long – dealing with Bret on tour always seemed to involve Rip dressing him down, for some reason.

Anyway, I followed Antonio down the hall at a fair clip, and we passed through the door of the locker room to find chaos within.

Jay and Oscar Golden were on the floor, Oscar mounted on the boss’ son, raining punches down on him as Jay tried to cover up with his forearms, to some success. Even as some shots got through, Golden was mainly missing with his punches.

I swung my cane around and into the wall with a crack, and shards of wood splintered off and pinged around the room. The noise, thankfully, was enough to make the fight stop, as Golden looked up and Chord lowered his guard for a moment.

“Steve, what happened?” I asked, looking past the fighters to Flash, who had been, with Riley McManus and Joe Benning, trying to haul Golden off Jay.

“Jay was being a -----,” he said, concisely. “Oscar took offence, and told him to shut up. Jay threw the first punch – Oscar threw most of the rest.”

“Phoenix?” I asked, looking over to where Firebird was standing. He nodded, confirming Flash’s story.

“Get up,” I said to Oscar. He scowled, but stood up, glaring down at the prone Jay as through trying to decide whether to jump back on and resume punching.

“Jay, you’ve been warned time and again, and it just doesn’t seem to have an effect. So here’s the deal – you’ve just given up 25% of this month’s salary. I won’t tolerate fighting in my locker room – at all.”

Jay stood up, looking contrite – and bruised.

“I understand,” he said, quietly. I raised an eyebrow, and noticed that Golden looked as shocked as me at how quietly Jay was taking his medicine.

“Get going, Jay. I don’t think you being here is a good idea right now.”

He nodded, and grabbed his stuff, making a quick – and quiet – exit.

*

The phone rang at 3:42 am. I was ready for it, having been drinking Coke, fixing my cane and watching late-night TV since I got home.

“Hello, Rip,” I said, as I answered.

“Kid,” he growled.

“I assume you’re ringing about Jay.”

“We had an agreement.”

“We do. But you know what locker rooms are like, Rip,” I said. “That had to be dealt with right there, right then. There was no chance to get you in to deal with it – not if anyone was going to have any respect for me afterwards.”

“You think the kid deserved a fine?”

“Yes, Rip, I do,” I said, trying to remain calm. Rip’s voice was level, hinting at anger but, so far, controlled and calm. I was buzzing with caffeine, trying to prevent myself saying something I might regret. “Anyone else would have been placed on immediate suspension or given a last warning. Hell, if it had been Bret I’d have fired him. Jay got off lightly.”

“You know his contract is due.”

“Yes. You’re going to deal with that, right?”

There was a long pause.

“I don’t know if I should. The kid’s so hard to deal with.”

“Well, I know that he won’t even talk to me. So it’s up to you, Rip. He’s an asset to the company, but if you think we can do without him...”

He snorted.

“I hired you to take care of stuff like this, kid,” he said.

“Fine. Let Jay leave. I’ll be honest, Rip, I think we’ve got the maximum bang out of him. Anything else from now on... well, he’s just another wrestler.”

There was a long pause, and I wondered if I’d been too blunt. But I’d spent over two years massaging Jay’s ego – I was ready to move on if he was.

“Okay, James,” he said. “I’ll speak to him. If I can get him to sign on again, I will. I gotta tell you, I’m feeling pretty tired.”

“It’s four in the morning, Rip. I’m not surprised.”

“Yeah, well, what are you doing up?”

“I was waiting for you to call.”

There was a short, humourless laugh on the other end of the line.

“Smartass.”

“Good night, Rip.”

He hung up, and I did the same. I tried to get some sleep, but two things kept me awake – the first being the caffeine buzzing around my system.

The second was trying to remember the last time that Rip had actually used my name when talking to me.
