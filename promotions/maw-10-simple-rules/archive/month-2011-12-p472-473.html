MAW: 10 Simple Rules Month

November 2011 into December 2011, after Remember November

in-game late November and early December 2011: Steve Flash's farewell, Rip Chord and the narrator on the touring shows, the 3rd and 10th December tour shows and the card for Season's Beatings; the saved copy carries no posting dates

“I have to go, James,” he said.

“I know – I wouldn’t try and stop you,” I replied. We shook hands. “But we’re short on the face side. I can’t even remember how the Invitational was laid out originally, but with you, Chance, Ernest and Hugh all leaving it’s fair to say that it looked pretty different.”

He shrugged.

“I’m not blaming you. What the SWF have offered you is amazing – especially at your time of life.”

He smiled slightly. “Especially at my time of life, yeah. I’m forty-five, James. I gave up dreaming of the big leagues more than ten years ago. I don’t know what Richard and Peter have planned for me, but for what they’re paying... I’m a grandfather, James. A year of SWF money will keep the kids in diapers and Christmas presents for a long time to come.”

We sized each other up.

“Steve Flash, SWF champion...”

“Don’t,” he laughed. “It’ll never happen.”

“Imagine the storyline, though. It’s the ultimate underdog story. The ultimate. I mean, who’s the champion there nowadays? Steve Frehley? He's nearly twenty years younger than you!”

“Bigger, stronger, more vicious... I don’t think I could cope with a match with him – let alone a feud. It’d be like trying to fight Primus or Knuckles every night.”

“Well, good luck Steve. Whatever happens, it goes without saying that you’d be welcome back here.”

“Thanks,” he said. We shook again, and once more I watched as another of our stars drove northwards. The dust had barely settled before Rip appeared at my side.

“We’re in trouble, kid,” he said, softly. “Any ideas?”

“Well, I’m thinking of calling Tommy and seeing if that job offer is still open,” I said. “But apart from that... We need to bring someone in for the Invitational pretty soon. Primus needs a challenger.”

“Have you written the shows up to then?”

“I have – but there’s room to bring someone in. But with Richard hoovering up everyone and anyone with any chance of getting ratings, well...” My shoulders sagged. “We’re scraping the bottom of the barrel. Someone fantastic might debut in the next few days – but equally, they might not. I think I’ll call Tommy and see if he can lend us anyone.”

“We don’t have many people to send back.”

“Kirk or Wanda...” I stopped as a lightbulb went off in my head. “Wait one second.”

“You’ve thought of something?”

I looked at the idea from every angle I could think of.

“Maybe. I need to make some calls. Tommy, Sam... Give me a couple of days, Rip. Monday morning, hopefully, I might have something.”

“Sure thing, kid. We can look at the finances then, too.”

I grimaced. At least the departures meant good news on that score – the guys leaving mean we would be saving around five grand a show. On the whole, it wasn’t a trade-off I was comfortable with, but it was a small silver lining.
