South East Madness: Tuesday, 9th June 2012

Tuesday, 9th June 2012 (South East, in-game); the saved copy carries no posting dates
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

Mid Atlantic Wrestling presents: South East Madness

Tuesday, 9th June 2012
Thea Davis vs. Raphael

This was a simple opener, designed to limit costs and give these two some further experience. Raphael picked up the win with an Italian DDT.
Winner: Raphael (D-)

*

Always Breakin’ Hollywood arrived backstage. Haley beamed at the camera:

“My boys are going to break these Team Honor idiots,” she cooed. “Who cares about honor? This is wrestling. All that matters is success and gold.”

“The woman speaks the truth,” Lenny Brown said. “After tonight, it’s the next challenge. And the next. And the next. And then we take the belts off those musical freaks – or whoever’s unfortunate enough to hold the gold when we put our boots to their faces.”

Primus Allen contented himself with a rough chuckle at this image.

*

Team Honor vs. Always Breakin’ Hollywood

Sweet merciful Lord, this was awful. Despite the best efforts of all involved, this just completely failed to capture the fans’ imagination. The newly formed pairing of Lassana Makutsi and Jared Johnson – who have great chemistry teaming together – looked slick against the ultra-modern offence of the Hollywood boys, but for some reason the fans just dumped on it from a great height. Primus Allen brought the mess to an end with an almighty Sit-Out Spinebuster on Jared Johnson for the win.
Winner: Always Breakin’ Hollywood (E)

*

Aaron Andrews was backstage, shining up his Heavyweight title. Suddenly, the lights in his dressing room went off.

There was a moment’s pause, and then Andrews said:

“Alright, you have my attention.”

The lights went back on, and Andrews started at the sight of Fumihiro Ota perched birdlike on the arms of his chair, peering intently down at him.

“What the hell do you want?”

Ota reached out and tapped the faceplate of the title belt.

“Oh yeah? And what makes you think you deserve a shot at my title?”

With a gesture of Ota’s hand, the lights went out again.

There was a further moment’s pause, and then Andrews said:

“I’ll give you points for originality, at least. You’ve got your match.”

The lights came back on. Ota was gone.

“Damn ninjas.”

*

KC Glenn and Bradford Peverell vs. Ford & Speed

This match was about planting seeds – but I hope they grow quick, as the action was pretty weak. Happily, it was also pretty short, as the full-time team made relatively short work of their opponents, who were unable to keep up with the heel duo’s well-practised teamwork. Glenn ate a Fallen Chances to give Ford the win for his team.
Winner: Ford & Speed (D-)

*

Firebird was warming up backstage, pausing occasionally to let Sienna pat the sweat from him with a towel. A backstage worker knocked on the door to let them know that they were up next. Firebird immediately left the dressing room, Sienna in his wake, still carrying her towel.

*

Donnie J (c) vs. Firebird for the MAW All Action title in a submission match This was more like what I expect from our workers – although I suppose the first three matches were about as good as I can expect, in terms of quality from our undercard. The two main eventers put together a solid contest that brought our fans back into the show at a decent rate. Neither man is a natural technician, but I don’t want to overdo prop-based matches like ladder contests and so on. Maybe a scaffold match could work?

Forget I mentioned that. I’d rather book a coal miner’s glove match.

Anyway, the action tarried on the mat for a time, but soon both men were airborne and on commentary we noted that the referee would count an unconscious foe as having submitted if he were in a submission hold – which both men seemed intent on achieving as they unleashed the big guns in their arsenals to try and land devastating blows on each other. The two men were equally matched, however, and the strategy seemed unlikely to be successful. Eventually, J caught Firebird with a series of back suplexes and rolled through into an abdominal stretch. After a few seconds – and before the move was even fully cinched in – the referee called for the bell, and J turned to see the towel that Sienna had been carrying lying on the mat. J looked on in bewilderment as Firebird walked past him, barely sparing him a glance as he left the ring and walked up the aisle with Sienna. J continued to look bewildered as we noted that he had twice faced members of the Fallen, and twice had effectively been allowed to retain his title. Rip wondered whether they had been assessing him as a potential recruit, and neither Rock nor I could think of any other explanation.
Winner: Donnie J (C-)

*

Fumihiro Ota vs. Aaron Andrews (c) for the MAW Heavyweight title This was just a great finish to the show. For thirty minutes the two men tore into one another with Ota trying every trick in his book to unseat the champion in his thirty-fourth defence. However, it was clear that after facing Ota on a number of occasions that Andrews had learned how to cope with the masked man, and he was able to counter a lot of Ota’s offence. The masked man seemed to be getting frustrated, and his usual implacable assaults seemed sloppier and somewhat off target, allowing Andrews to further his advantage before scoring with a Twisting Face Crusher for the win.

After the match, Ota was left in the ring, kneeling and staring down at the mat. Slowly, he raised his hands to his head and removed his hood, leaving his facemask behind but revealing the rest of his head. He stared at it for several long moments, before dropping it to the mat and leaving the ring, looking lost in thought.
Winner: Aaron Andrews (B-)

Overall: C
