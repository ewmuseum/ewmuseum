MAW: 10 Simple Rules Month

September 2010

in-game September 2010: the party at which MAW reaches Regional, the narrator and Rip Chord on touring and TV, Katie Cameron's boyfriend's move to Japan, a profit note, Jay Chord's rejected offers and the card for Back To School; the saved copy carries no posting dates

The party was in full swing. Jay was dancing indecently with Sienna, while Hugh looked on and laughed. Occasionally it’s hard separating fantasy from reality, and I have to admit to feeling a slight disconnect when I saw the two on the dance floor – but Sienna’s a sweet girl, not the husky-voiced, borderline trampish character she plays for us.

Rip was laughing with Jean Cattley and Casey Valentine, while Antonio was taking money off Kashmir Singh and Amazing Fire Fly in an impromptu poker match.

Sienna apart, the MAW ladies were gathered in one corner of the room, talking quietly amongst themselves. I wanted to walk that way, but before I could do more than look, Rip buttonholed me.

“Kid! We’re up to Regional!” he cried, throwing a meaty arm around my shoulders. Discreetly, I glanced at his glass, but it seemed like there was nothing but lemonade in there. Rip was drunk - but only on success. Looking across at Heidi, I caught a shake of her head, and I relaxed. She’d been keeping an eye on Rip for me.

“What now?” he asked, suddenly serious.

“There’s one guy I’ve been trying to bring in since day one. And it might be nice to put out the feelers to a few others, too. But we’ve got a solid roster, here.”

“Big enough for two shows a month?”

“At least. Give me another month or two, Rip. I think after October, it might be time to go on tour again.”

He frowned.

“We lost a lot of money last time, kid,” he said.

I nodded.

“That’s why I want a couple of months to build up a war chest. It might be nice to have a big name appear on each show, to put on a great match in the main event, but really I’m looking at four or five matches featuring the youngsters and cheaper guys. No-one on more than five or six hundred a night, for instance. It’s about half the roster right now.”

“Think you can keep it up?”

“For long enough to get us up to the next step, yeah. After that, it’ll be TV.”

“Ah. TV.”

“And pay-per-view.”

“Ah. Pay-per-view.”

“But that’s a long way in the future. Even if our shows are awesome, we’re probably talking two or three years away. I’d rather build more slowly across three regions than try and flood two and suffer from overexposure.”

“I agree, kid. It’s a good strategy. We might even want to start going nationwide once we hit a certain popularity in the Great Lakes and South-East. But just remember what happened to the guy who flew too close to the sun – he got his fingers burnt.”

I nodded thoughtfully. Over the years, I’d learned to just accept Rip’s mixed metaphors, and move on.

“Yes. I don’t want to break the 100k barrier again – not if at all possible. But I think it’s worth taking a small loss, if we can keep it small.”

Rip nodded, all trace of good cheer gone.

“I know we’ve gotta move with the times, kid. But I just wanted to give back when I founded this company. Sometimes I get a bit carried away, thinking we’re the next big thing.”

“The next medium-sized thing would do me,” I said, with a grin. “Rip, let’s be honest, the key to improving the youngsters is to get them working matches, as often as possible, as much as possible. And weekly shows is the way to do that.”

“Yeah, I know... Kid?”

“Yes, Rip?”

“I’m glad you’re here. I don’t think I could have dealt with all this by myself.”

“It’s a wise man who knows his limitations, Rip,” I said. “I can’t be the figurehead for the next generation of wrestlers. I can’t tell stories of wild battles with the young Tommy Cornell. I can’t inspire people with memories of World titles won before tens of thousands of people.

“You can’t handle all the rubbish that comes with the day-to-day running of a successful, growing wrestling company.”

Rip half smirked, half scowled.

“You’d do better without me than I would without you, huh?”

“It’s a partnership, Rip,” I said, a little meekly. The thought had crossed my mind. “And it’s one that’s going to see this company growing like wildfire over the next few years.”

“Yeah... it’s just a long way from Stanley Hall, me, Jean, and ten green kids with a lot of promise.”

“We’re still all about the future, Rip. The average age here is about 26 – prime age for improving. And when we get the finished article, like X or Erik, off they go.”

“To the Eisens.”

“Yeah, well, let’s not get into you and Richard again, huh?”

Rip grinned tightly, and then laughed.

“Ah, you’re right, kid. It’s a long time ago, and who am I to complain if my dream’s succeeded in ways I couldn’t have imagined five years ago? Hey, go talk to Katie, will you?”

I blinked at the non sequitur.

“Katie? Why?”

Rip shrugged. “Seemed a bit miserable backstage earlier. You didn’t notice?” He cracked a grin. “I may have to find another road agent if you’re not going to pay attention.”

I managed a weak smile before walking over to the girls. In truth, I’d been trying to put Katie out of my mind of late. Sending her off to patch things up with Jett had been difficult, and I’d regretted it immediately. But honestly, I was kidding myself if I thought that there was any chance of a future between this broken-down middle aged Brit, and the vivacious Canadian bombshell.

“Katie?” The group of girls turned as one. Jenny and Dawn were flanking their colleague, while Kristen was rubbing her shoulders consolingly.

“Hello, James,” she said, offering a watery smile.

“What’s wrong?”

“Jacob’s gone to Japan.”

“On a tour?”

“No...”

Her lip quivered, and I looked to Kristen for help.

“Jacob’s signed with World Level Wrestling,” she supplied.

I struggled for a moment before placing WLW. We didn’t have a lot in common with the company, so I’d never had much to do with them.

“What’s he doing if he’s not on tour?” I asked.

“He’s signed a written deal out there. He’s dropped all his other commitments,” Jenny said, softly.

“All of them?” I asked, incredulously, then winced. Katie was one of those commitments, after all.

“He’s moved out there,” she said, her voice sounding forced and unnatural.

“Oh.”

Kristen caught my eye, and nodded towards an empty spot in all the noise and bustle of the party. I followed her over there.

“Jacob’s moved to Japan for two years. He didn’t ask Katie to go with him,” she said, and stared at me, hard.

“I don’t have any contacts in Japan,” I said, defensively. “There’s nothing I can do to get her out there.”

Kristen smiled. “She said you were a good guy... That’s not what I meant, James.”

Realisation dawned, and my jaw dropped.

“You want me to... to...”

“To make Katie happy? Yes – is that such a terrible thing?”

My face settled into a frown.

“I can’t. She and Jacob were happy – he might be planning to take her off to Japan any minute.”

“He hasn’t mentioned anything-“

“But he might. Look, I understand that Katie’s upset, and I wish-“

I stopped myself short, and took a moment to regain a professional state of mind.

“Of course, if there was anything I could do as Katie’s boss to help her, then I would. But as far as I can see there isn’t.”

“And as her friend? As the man she’s attracted to?”

For a moment, I thought about firing Kristen there and then. I quelled the impulse – I was supposed to the moderate one backstage. Rip’s the hothead.

“While there’s still a chance that Katie and Jacob can make something of their relationship, I have no business getting involved. Until such time as they split up, I’m just a face in the background – do you understand? If anything’s to happen between us, I have to know that I didn’t do anything to cause the break-up.”

She looked at me in frustration. “Normally girls like to see an honourable man, but-“

“In the unlikely event that Katie breaks up with Jacob and decides that the ideal replacement is a Brit, nearly twice her age, who uses a cane to walk more than a step at a time, then at least she’ll know I can be trusted.”

With that, I turned and limped away, deftly avoiding the lambada-ing Sienna and Jay on my way. I wasn’t in much of a mood for partying anymore.
