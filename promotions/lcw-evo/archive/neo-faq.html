A: It means you can't be The Rock, Big Poppa Pump, Jerry Lynn, or any other real wrestler, active or retired. You have to create your own wrestler and your own gimmick. Also, don't use a real wrestler's theme song. Q: Will I be fired if I complain? A: In a word: no. However, if you make a habit of constantly complaining, especially why you lost this match or that match, then yes, you will be punished in some way, possibly even termination. Rather than simply complaining about why you lost, put that effort into further constructing your angle, and your effort will be rewarded. You can come to us with problems, and we will do our best to resolve them as soon as possible. Q: How do I get to be a contender for a title? A: Well, being angle-based, a lot of it depends on how well you are doing in the angle that you're currently placed in. If we see that you're putting the effort into on-show segments and roleplays, and you're honestly doing your best to move the angle along, then you will be pushed, most likely into a title fued. Q: I think that I'm a good roleplayer, but not quite good enough to be accepted into your fed. How do I go about improving? A: Your first step is to apply to the fed, as most people are their own harshest critic. If you have been rejecting in the past, then you could always study our roleplay guide and work from there. Now, with the addition of LML (Lethal Minor League), we have the ability to help promising applicants make it into one of the main LCW divisions. Shows Q: How many shows does LCW run per week? A: LCW neo runs one show per week, called Impulse. LCW Mercury, our roleplaying fed, also runs one show per week called Fusion. LCW Central has either one or two shows per week, and they are: LCW Weekly - a recap style show - and Late Night Lethal - an LCW-themed talk show. Q: When will the results be up? A: Shows will be up by midnight on the day that the event is scheduled. On rare occasions, we may have to push shows back, but they will be up within a day of the original date (this is a rarity, so don't let the likelihood of this event happening concern you). Roleplays Q: What are you looking for in a roleplay? A: In LCW neo, we are looking for two key points: character development and storyline advancement. For this reason, vignettes are going to be the preferred form of roleplaying, as, in our opinion, they greatly facilitate the character development process. Conversely, if you'd like to build up your angle with another performer, we aren't going to discourage the occasional use of a straight-forward promo to add some heat to a match-up. Q: What is the difference between a vignette and a promo? A: A vignette is a storyline roleplay, in which the primary focus is on your character and how he/she acts in everyday life, as opposed to a promo, in which the main goal is to downplay and/or verbally assault your opponent and their ring prowess. Promos tend to follow a "movie script" style, which is to say that they begin with a setting and most of the dialogue is written like so - Lex Laww: Blah Blah Blah. While vignettes can also be written this way, there are a multitude of acceptable ways to write them, including the movie script style, "chapter" style, in which multi-part vignettes are written like chapters of a book, and "story" style, in which each individual vignette is written to stand alone. Both chapter and story style are generally written in the same format as short stories or novels. Q: When is the deadline for roleplays? A: Because we are an angle-based federation, we don't have a set deadline for roleplay submission; however, if you are tapped to write an on-air segment for your character for Impulse, we'd like to have it 24 hours before the show is supposed to be up (we'll contact you and give you the specifics each time you're chosen).
