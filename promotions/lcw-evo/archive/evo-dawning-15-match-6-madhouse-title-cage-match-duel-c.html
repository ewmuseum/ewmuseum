Dawning: Match 6, Madhouse Title Cage Match: Duel (c) v Frank Heimdal

2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
LCW Evo Impulse Match 6, Madhouse Title Cage Match: Duel (c) v Frank Heimdal System of a Down’s “ATWA” filled the first floor speakers as a shot of the Madhouse title appeared on the monitor wall. As Serg Tankanian sang, the Madhouse title was replaced by a close-up of Frank Heimdal’s face on the left, Duel’s face on the right, and in the background, lighter than the two men who would be competing in the match; there was a shot of Olivia Heimdal, the look on her face a mixture of fear and anger. The video package began with Impulse 1.1, where Frank Heimdal had inadvertently dumped Duel to the outside with a back body drop. It then cut to a shot of the closing moments of the Rook Black/Duel Evo Title match from Impulse 1.3, with Duel choking out the Texan to win the championship. This was followed by clips of the various confrontations the two had endured since then, including: their encounter as part of a six-man match on Impulse 1.4, their confrontation and Duel’s challenge on Impulse 1.6, Frank dumping Duel over the front of the announce position on Impulse 1.7, Duel’s attempt to get out of the Dawning match on 1.8, which was followed by a split screen of Frank’s hitting Cranial Smoke on Lost and Duel tapping out in pain to Tommy G, and then, finally, the confrontation on Impulse 1.9, which had begun with Duel attempting to get out of this match and had ended with Duel attacking Frank with a crutch. As the vocals died down, the music continued to play and the steel cage was lowered into position. It wasn’t one of the newer wire mesh versions but rather one of the old-school blue-steel cages that looked more like a set of prison bars descending than anything two men should’ve been wrestling in. While the video package was playing, the ring crew had quickly assembled the steel cage that would hold the Madhouse Title match. Rather than the more current chain-link and aluminum version of the wrestling cage, this structure was a throwback to the older days of wrestling, its thick blue steel bars intersecting to create one foot by one foot squares. The thick walls of the cage began at the floor and reached nearly to the lighting rig that hung suspended above the ring, putting its height at around eighteen feet, with close to fifteen of that above the canvas. The crowd roared in anticipation of the match, but as the lights in the club died down and a familiar bass line began, their reaction quickly changed. LJB: Well, I certainly hope Duel derives some satisfaction from this nonsense. I sure as hell don't. This has gone on long enough, and I think this should blowout right here. You can play around with this crap for only so long before it comes down to circumstances that cannot be interpreted or confused. As the crowd erupted in boos, the first floor lights died down and a single blue spotlight flashed on, casting its light on the monitor bank. One by one, the monitors came to life in a clockwise motion to create a single white spiral. M.O.P.’s “Cold as Ice” filled the Club, and two other spotlights came on, one silver and one white. The three lights then focused on a spot on the floor in front of the first floor's vertical transit area as the phrases "Duel" and "Unity" flashed alternately on the monitors in front of the swirling spiral. The beams of the spotlights began to twist around one another and several colored strobe lights throughout the first floor turned on. Duel made his way from somewhere around the vertical transit area, rubbing and stretching his arms and shoulders, his Madhouse Title belt strapped around his waist. As he made his way to the ring, seemingly unfazed by the jarring display of lights and colors, his crutches from the two weeks prior were conspicuous in their absence. Once in the ring, Duel looked around the first floor of the club, a grossly distorted smile on his face. LH: Frank is a lot of things. He's my brother, Nikki's boyfriend, a son, an awesome cook, a good friend to a lot of people, and a helluva guy. But right now, he's just going to be one thing. A bad mother[censored]. The lights started pulsating in a strobe effect, and "MENOFEARTHEREAPER" by Pop Will Eat Itself began. On the big screen, a back view of Frank Heimdal could be seen. The camera began to close in on him very quickly, and he turned around, a look of shock on his face. He got pushed out the window, breaking glass following him down. He managed to twist himself around in mid-air, before beginning to turn into a black cartoon silhouette, and all of his surroundings turned into cartoons as well. Frank landed on the ground in a crouch, glass falling around him, and the only parts of his body that were in color were his eyes, which have gone Manga. Frank's black silhouette went into the door at the ground floor as seconds later, his assailant, another black silhouette, fell out the window and hit the ground, shattering into a million pieces. The camera followed the building up to where Frank's silhouette was standing, leaning on the sill. The camera went into an extreme close-up of his face, into the blue left eye, into the pupil, and the screen went black. The Frank Heimdal logo appeared as the man himself made his way from the vertical transit area to the ring. He slapped hands with a few fans along the path to the ring, but his eyes never left his opponent. This was arguably the biggest match of his career to date, but he wasn’t nervous. Nervous was actually the last word to describe Frank Heimdal. The most likely candidate for summing Frank’s current mindset up in one word would’ve been determined, and everything about his walk to the ring, from the look in his eyes, to the staunchness of his posture, to the grim set of his mouth - it all served to indicate that he was prepared for this match, both mentally and physically. LJB: Me, no fear, no fear, no, me, no fear no, fear no fear no, don't fear da reeeeeeeepah. LH: That was pretty. LJB: I hate that song. Frank slid into the ring under the bottom rope, and the previously open cage door was shut and barred behind him. As the door slammed, any lingering second thoughts left Frank’s mind, and he stared intently at the man opposite him in the ring. Duel simply smiled at Frank and beckoned the younger man to attack him. Frank approached slowly, showing no fear, until he and Duel were separated by mere inches. Though he had to look up to look into the bigger man’s eyes, Frank showed no fear and his gaze didn’t waver, at least until Duel moved suddenly. Frank backed away a step, but Duel had merely extended his hand for a handshake. Frank looked at it blankly, and then responded with a move equally as simply and perhaps doubly effective: showing his amazing quickness, Frank fired a forearm blow into the side of Duel’s head that staggered the big man and sent him backing into the ropes. LJB: One of the most iritating things about Duel in all his incarnations, is that disturbing tendancy to have all these "gee do I respect you a lot matches" filled with handshakes and hugs. I always thought it was a crock. If you respected someone, you went out of your way to kick their ass as hard as you could because you respected them. LH: Joe, that's really screwed up. LJB: I think the handshake crap is MORE screwed up. Save it for afterwards. Frank wasted no time in pulling Duel into an Irish whip, and as the champion turned and hit the far ropes, Frank caught him in the chest with a running knee lift that sent Duel slamming backwards into the cage. Again, Frank gave Duel no quarter when he grabbed the big man’s head and attempted to slam it into the cage wall, but Duel blocked and countered by elbowing Frank in the stomach and then slamming the former chef’s head into the cage first. As Frank staggered back from the cage wall, Duel caught him in the temple with a forearm shot of his own, only to receive a chop to the chest from Frank. Duel fired back with another forearm shot, this one connecting with Frank’s chest/neck area. Frank again returned fire with a knife-edged chop, only to receive another forearm from Duel. The two men exchanged blows like this for several seconds, until Duel finally blocked one of Frank’s chops and pulled the smaller man into an Irish whip. LH: So. LJB: Yeah. As Frank returned from the ropes, Duel looked for a clothesline, but Frank rolled underneath it, coming up on his feet and pivoting to quickly fire a hard kick into Duel’s thigh. Frank smiled grimly at the yelp of pain that escaped Duel as his thigh muscles rapidly cramped, but he continued to kick away, sending the top of his boot into the same spot on Duel’s thigh several more times. Duel finally threw a meaty arm in the way of one of Frank’s kicks, so rather than lose momentum, Heimdal cinched Duel in for a side Russian leg sweep, turning his body somewhat so that the back of Duel’s head hit hard off of the cage. Without fully releasing his hold, Frank turned his body sideways and applied a cross kneebar, wrenching away at Duel’s knee. With the focus of his offense on Duel’s leg, it was clear that Heimdal was hoping to slow the Madhouse champion down, and perhaps even prevent the big man from climbing out of the cage. LH: I have an idea, let's think of positive things about Duel. LJB: Why would we do that? LH: So we're not always saying the same damn [censor] about him. After nearly a minute of agonizing pain, Duel used his free foot to kick Frank hard in the face when the challenger sat up to gain more leverage on the hold. With Frank stunned momentarily, Duel shook his leg free and both men got back to their feet. Duel threw a hard lariat, throwing his whole weight into the move; however, Frank proved again to be the quicker of the two men, and he ducked the move and charged to the ropes. As he came back, he attempted to execute a hurricanrana on Duel, but the champion’s larger mass allowed him to plant himself and prevent the move. He fell backwards, dropping a surprised Frank Heimdal face-first into the cage wall with a variation on the traditional rope drop hot shot. As Frank hit the cage, Duel quickly moved out from beneath him, allowing the challenger to slide to the mat. Without wasting a second, Duel grabbed Frank in a rear waistlock and executed a release German suplex, which left Frank lying in the middle of the mat on his back. It was then that it became apparent that the hot shot had left its mark on Frank in the form of a cut over his left eye, which was just starting to ooze blood down around his ocular cavity. The fans, known for their “bloodlust” which had carried over to Evo from the other extreme promotion that had formerly called Philadelphia its home, roared their approval, even if it was the fan-favorite who was bleeding. LJB: Duel doesn't tend to be late. LH: Is pretty fashionable, hardly cutting edge, but well groomed. With Heimdal bleeding on the mat, the champion saw his opening, and he began to climb the cage wall, clearly favoring his left leg, which had been brutalized by the kicks and the submission hold. Frank sat up, shaking his head to clear the cobwebs, and he saw that the champion was making his exit, so he quickly got to his feet and ran to the cage wall, where he grabbed Duel by the ankles and pulled hard, yanking the surprised champion from the cage wall and causing him to drop throat-first across the rope. Because of how far he had ascended when Frank pulled him down, Duel remained sprawled across the top rope, doing his best to regain his wind. Frank saw his opportunity and quickly climbed the ropes before leaping off and executing a leg drop on Duel’s head, which drove his throat down once more onto the top rope. LJB: He's an okay wrestler too. LH: And can breakdance. However, it wasn’t all paradise for the challenger. After hitting the move, he found himself with nowhere to go and this forced him to fall to the mat behind Duel. Fortunately, he managed to turn his body as he fell and he landed on his side, thereby preventing head trauma or spinal damage that would’ve resulted had he landed on his head or tailbone, respectively. LJB: But other than that... LH: Well ... Both men were slow to get their bearings back, and they circled one another warily before Duel faked a tie-up. He backed away, and then immediately threw himself forward, catching an off-guard Frank even more off-guard with a spear that sent the challenger’s ribs into the ropes and the cage wall directly behind them. With Heimdal vulnerable, Duel reacted quickly, ramming his shoulder repeatedly into Frank’s midsection, trapping the challenger between a hard place and an even harder place. After several shots, Duel pulled Frank into an Irish whip, but much to his surprise, the challenger actually reversed the move. Duel wasn’t surprised enough to allow himself to be Irish whipped, though, as he planted his feet and reversed the move again, this time sending Frank back into the cage wall. Duel again attempted a clothesline, but for the third time in the match, Frank avoided the move, ducking and then quickly rising to throw a back elbow into Duel’s head. In one fluid motion, Heimdal turned and delivered a palm thrust to the base of Duel’s neck, snapping Duel’s head back and then driving it forward into the cage. LJB: Let's just say that other than those nice things, his distorted little worldview and twisted antics are destroying everything good that could potentially be there. And the consequences, well, they'll be long lasting. As Duel staggered backwards, his hands immediately went up to his nose, and from the red liquid leaking from between his fingers, it was apparent that Frank wasn’t the only bloody competitor in the match. Without hesitation, Frank pushed a stunned Duel into the ropes again and then kicked him hard in the back of the knee, forcing Duel to slump with his upper body over the top rope. Frank backed up halfway across the ring and then ran forward, dropkicking Duel’s face into the cage and sending a spray of blood from the champion’s now cascading nose into the front row of fans. LH: Oh if I had a cordless headset and a sponge. LJB: Want blood? LH: And then I'd take the sponge, and BURN IT. Frank backed away momentarily, but only long enough for Duel to get back to his feet. Before the champion could even turn, Frank charged forward, using a spear of his own to slam Duel into the cage once more. Frank quickly stood back up and raked Duel’s face across the cage bars, opening a cut above the champion’s eye. Grabbing Duel by the head, Frank delivered a reverse DDT and then quickly began to climb up the cage wall. With Duel sufficiently stunned, Frank easily ascended to the top of the cage. Breathing heavily from the exertion of the match and the strain of the climb, Frank still managed to throw a foot over the top of the cage, which drew a huge roar from the crowd. However, the champion had managed to get to his feet, and he shook the wall of the cage, stalling Heimdal long enough as he got his balance to allow Duel to climb to the top rope and grab Frank’s ankle. Keeping his left hand on Frank, Duel managed to climb to the top of the cage as well, and at the top, he paused, his head just barely over the top of the cage. Frank still had a good 18 inches of leverage on his side, and he used one hand to slam Duel’s face off of the top of the cage, taking care to keep the other firmly gripped around the uppermost bar. LJB: Well, what can we say about Frank? LH: Well, Frank right now is being bad. But he's good at it, and there's a time and place for being bad. I honestly don't think he could do anything to Alex that I wouldn't forgive him for if not violently approve of. Duel’s nose impacted the thicker top of the cage, causing another shower of blood to spray the nearby fans, and he lost and balance and fell backwards. The only thing that prevented him from tumbling to the canvas so far below was Frank’s leg, which he had managed to catch with both hands as he fell backwards. Frank shook his leg mightily, but to no avail - Duel’s grip was too strong. Unlike three weeks prior, when Frank had cast Duel over the front of the announce position, the big man threw himself backwards from the wall, almost as if he were repelling, and this caused Frank to finally lose his precarious balance on the top of the cage. As he began his descent, Frank looked ahead of him, and there, directly in front of him, on the edge of her seat in spite of his best hopes, was his sister, Olivia. Frank smiled at her and threw his leg back over the cage wall. Livvie gasped, but Frank knew what he was doing, and he used his complete freedom from the wall of the cage to maneuver his body so that he landed squarely upon Duel when the two men hit the canvas. However, the impact of the fall was enough that neither man moved on impact, and due to the stipulations of the match, Jason Sanders, who was outside of the ring, couldn’t even count the pinfall that Frank was left in perfect position for. LJB: And now, a nap. After nearly a minute of hushed silence from the crowd in which neither man moved, Frank began to stir. While this brought a cheer from the crowd, it quickly died down as they realized that Frank was only moving because Duel was crawling out from beneath him. Apparently, the bigger man’s musculature had allowed him a better cushion upon impact, and he was the first to stir. He crawled out from beneath Frank, after the challenger had had him legitimately pinned for over a dozen three-counts, and he slowly got to his feet before staggering to the ropes in an attempt to regain his balance and catch his breath, much of which had been knocked from him when they hit the mat. Duel silently cursed the stiffer surface of the Evo mat, which had once been a huge proponent for, back in the early days of the Club. That seemed like an eternity ago, and he had been a different person then, their body under control of its rightful owner. LH: Duel's nap is over. His next nap shall be a dirt nap. He pulled Heimdal to his feet and slapped the challenger roughly across the face a few times. It was much more empowering to subdue an opponent who could actually put up a struggle than the wrestling equivalent of walking zombie, or so he thought. He then whipped Heimdal into the corner before charging in, but Frank sprung forward and threw his legs into the air, arching them over Duel’s head. Or so he thought. Duel actually saw the move coming and put on the brakes as he charged in, turning to face the center of the ring as Frank’s ankles came down onto his shoulders. Duel wasted no time, walking away from the corner a step before torquing down on Frank’s legs and slamming him hard to the mat. Without pause, Duel quickly pulled Frank’s feet into his armpits and then slingshotted the challenger, sending him flying directly into the cage wall over the turnbuckle he had just been in. Duel grabbed Frank by the ankles again, pulling him to the mat and then pulling the challenger to the center of the ring. As he drug Frank, a red smear followed the challenger’s head on the canvas, almost like a slug’s trail of slime. However, this wasn’t a simple excretion meant to aid in travel; it was a trail of blood, as Frank’s forehead had apparently been busted open. LJB: Ew. I think I bled like that once. With Heimdal lying face-down in the center of the ring, Duel made his way to the cage wall that faced the announce position and began climbing, using the ropes for support. As the stood atop the top rope, he realized how much this match had taken out of him, and he paused momentarily to catch his breath, unaware that Frank was stirring on the mat. The challenger got to his hands and knees and crawled to the ropes that Duel was currently balancing upon, and he slowly pulled himself up, first to the middle rope, then to the top rope. With Duel’s feet mere inches away from his face, Frank threw himself backwards without releasing his hold on the ropes, which proved to be enough to cause the champion to lose his footing. Duel slammed into the cage, causing his drying and crusted nose to start bleeding once more, and he found himself hanging onto the cage with only his arms to support him. However, the redoubled weight of his body soon seemed to return to normal, even without his feet being on the top rope. Duel only had a moment to speculate about the sensation however, as he quickly found himself turning involuntarily towards the center of the ring. He looked down, surprised to find his weight balanced across Frank’s shoulders, with Heimdal’s bristly hair, which was already beginning to turn from brownish-blonde to an unnatural shade of red, rubbing against his bare stomach. LH: Right about now, I bet Duel wished he'd practised that huricanrana more. Before Duel could even react, Frank stepped forward, taking the two men away from the cage. Duel punched him hard in the small cut that was near Frank’s widow’s peak, opening it up more, but he found that one punch was all he could muster without losing his balance and falling from his perch. Before Duel could focus on not falling, Frank himself fell backwards, slamming the back of Duel’s head into the cage wall and leaving the big man half-sitting on the top rope and half-slumped between the rope and the cage wall. Frank slowly got back to his feet and saw another opportunity that he couldn’t pass up, so he ran to the corner and leapt to the middle rope… LJB: Booyah! …before springboarding into a dropkick that send Duel’s head slamming hard into the cage again. This was enough to send Duel sliding down the cage wall, the top rope bowing outwards to allow him to continue his slide down the wall. As he fell, Duel’s left leg became entangled in the top and middle ropes, as the two had crossed as the top rope returned its original position after Duel had slid past it and the middle rope had bowed outwards, just as the uppermost rope had moments before. Realizing that he was trapped, Duel reached out and grabbed Frank’s ankle before the challenger could move to another part of the cage and climb to victory. Frank twisted and tried to crawl free, but this left him in position for an ankle lock from the upside-down champion. LJB: This is weird. LH: Yeah, the upsidedown ankle lock of rediculousness. LJB: I don't think that it hurts any less just because it's upsidedown. LH: I don't see it becoming a signature move though. LJB: Not in here anyway. After keeping the hold locked in for half a minute or more, Duel, satisfied that Frank wouldn’t be able to climb out of the cage in the time it took him to escape the ropes, released his grip on Frank’s leg. Using his tremendous abdominal development, Duel leaned upwards, nearly folding himself in half, but it paid off as he pried the ropes apart and fell the rest of the way to the mat. Both men got slowly, unsteadily to their feet, and they exchanged a brief stare before locking up in a collar-and-elbow. After a hard forearm shot to the head, Duel whipped Frank into the corner once more, and again, he charged. Diving forward, Duel attempted to spear Frank into the corner, but Heimdal simply fell sideways, dropping to the mat and avoiding all contact. However, Duel wasn’t so lucky. LH: BONK! He ended up spearing the ringpost, and his head slammed hard off of the cage, which further aggravated the small cut over his eye that had been bleeding for the better part of the last eight minutes or so. When Frank saw that Duel was unmoving, still sprawled in the corner and resting on the middle turnbuckle, he seized the opportunity and pulled Duel free of the corner. He threw the big man over his shoulder and then sat out, delivering a fire thunder driver. Cranial Smoke. LJB: He done got smoked. Awww. With a great deal of strain, Frank held Duel on his shoulders as he rocked forward and got his feet beneath him again. With a great deal of exertion, his muscles literally shaking from the strain, Frank stood back up, only to sit out once more and drop Duel to the mat with another fire thunder driver, the second Cranial Smoke in less than a minute. This time, however, he let Duel slide from his shoulder and to the mat and then got back to his feet. LH: He got two smokes. He got cancer. He died. The End. Running a hand through his crimson hair, and then shaking the spikey mass on top of his head, Frank smiled confidently in spite of the pattern of blood that appeared on the mat. He made his way to the cage wall and began his climb. Slowly, torturously, he inched his way up the wall, bar by bar, foot by foot. As he reached the top, Frank paused long enough to catch his breath before throwing his right foot over the top of the cage. This time, though, there was no Duel to catch his ankle and bring him back to the mat, and Frank threw his other leg over the top of the cage as well. LJB: Duel, lies bleeding expertly in the corner. However, the plan to slick the cage bars with all the blood in his body seems to be progressing slowly. I recommend he start bleeding FASTER. Now, there were only fifteen feet of space separating him from the first singles championship in his LCW career. Frank began to crawl down, but after climbing halfway, he just backed off of the cage and dropped the final eight feet to the floor. Upon impact, he bent his knees and leaned backwards, landing hard (but not too hard) on his posterior. Exhausted but smiling, Frank accepted the Madhouse Title from Jason Sanders as the bell rang. LJB: Livvie, you got an awful pretty smile. Y'know that? Winner and NEW Madhouse Champion: Frank Heimdal (Match Time: 32:14) LH: Thank you, Joe. Frank looked up to Livvie, who was leaning out over the front of the commentary booth and staring happily at her brother. Upon seeing the first real smile on her face in a long time, Frank realized that all the punishment, all the excursion, had been worth it. He looked down at his new title belt, and catching a distorted reflection of his blurry visage in its gold plates, he smiled. He really looked like shit, but it didn’t matter. He hadn’t thought he could win, but he had proven himself wrong, and here he was, only the second Madhouse Champion in Evo history. His smile growing bigger by the moment, Frank half-walked, half-staggered towards the first floor elevators, the effects of the blood loss finally overwhelming the effects of his adrenaline. Meanwhile, back in the ring, Duel somehow managed to stagger to his feet and out through the cage door, and he caught up to a winded and wounded Frank before the new Madhouse Champion was able to get to the elevators. Feeling the familiar hand on his shoulder, Frank whirled around, his hands balled into fists and ready to strike. Instead, he found a beaten man facing him, a man with his hand extended and a weary smile on his face. It was what Frank didn't see in the smile that made him shake Duel's hand - the maliciousness, the killer intent - it was all gone. Then, in a move even more shocking to Frank, Duel mouthed two words that were only heard by Heimdal himself and the fans very nearest the two battered combatants: 'Thank you.” Then, in a move not so surprising, Frank collapsed, falling into Duel’s arms. Duel threw one of Frank’s arms over his shoulders and helped the new Madhouse Champion into the elevator that would take them both to their respective locker rooms. LJB: Frank countered the idiot handshake of doom by pretending to fall asleep, I see. Very smart guy there. LH: You just called someone smart?!?
