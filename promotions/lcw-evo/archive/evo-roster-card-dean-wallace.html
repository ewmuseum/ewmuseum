LCW Evo - The Evolution Continues Real Name: Dean Wallace Birthdate: Janurary 26, 1975 Hometown: Tempe, AZ Height: 6'3' Weight: 230 lbs. Nicknames: 'The Prime Example', 'The Closer', 'The Terminator' Status: Heel Manager(s): None Current Teams: N/A Entrance Music: 'Prime Example', Talib Kweli Wrestling Style: Technician Signature Moves: ShutOut (Sitout Tiger Driver '91), spinebuster, german suplex, pump kick, crucifix powerbomb, release dragon suplex, Tiger Driver '91 Set Up Move: A Cut Above (Falling Ace Crusher ) Finisher: The Prime Example (Sitout Reverse Fire Thunder Driver) LCW Title History: LCW Tag Team Champions w/ Frank Heimdal(1) LCW Award History: 2001 Tag Team of the Year w/ Frank Heimdal Other Federations: None Other Federation Awards: None Bio From the NCAA Division I Baseball Record Register: ALL-TIME LEADERS: Most Saves, Career: Wallace, Dean (86), Arizona St., 94-97. Most Saves, Season: Wallace, Dean (23), Arizona St., 1997. CWS CHAMPIONS: 1996 Arizona St. def. Stanford, 3-2. MVP: Dean Wallace, Arizona St. Quite a resume for a kid who grew up in Tempe, AZ, with only his father to support him. When Dean was 8, his mother was killed in a parking lot accident. Dean's father was left to manage the family, which included Dean and his brother Ryan, 5 years Dean's junior. Dean had big dreams. He grew up playing baseball in the summers, football in the fall, and basketball in the winter. He continued this all the way up to Corona del Sol High School, in Tempe. Dean lettered all four years: 3-point specialist for the basketball team, quarterback for the football team, and ace pitcher for the baseball team. Baseball, however, was Dean's true calling. After high school, he committed to his hometown college, Arizona State, to play baseball. And play he did, lettering all four years. Dean was put in the bullpen, earning the title of The Closer, or relief specialist. Whenever Sun Devil games were close, Dean hit the mound, and it was academic from there. His first two years, the Sun Devils were in the process of a rebuilding period. It culminated in 1996 with a College World Series title victory over Stanford. Dean, who got the save in the title game, was named the tournament MVP. The next year, it looked to be more of the same. Scouts drooled over Dean's golden left arm. He went the entire regular season without blowing a save. The Sun Devils made it back to the CWS title, facing LSU. With the Sun Devils up 6-5, Dean walked the leadoff batter, got the next two, then allowed a devastating game-winning home run. Little did Dean know as he walked off the field, head down, that it would be for the last time. Wallace went in the 2nd round of the pro draft: number 40, to the Oakland A's. But his agent was unable to swing a deal. Dean became a free agent, but he had already gained the unfair reputation of being hard to negotiate with, and went the whole year unsigned. Dean's career was over. The next few years are a blank. Shortly after the 1997 World Series, Dean and his father got into a heated arguement that saw Dean leave home for the final time. He drifted around the state of Arizona, working in baseball training facilities; secretly hoping for the big chance that would never come. On a whim, he enrolled himself at Jackson Hartnett's Dojo in Tempe, expecting to keep in shape by doing some lifting and treadmill work. What he found was a wrestling ring, and a new dream to pursue. In early 2001, LCW came calling after seeing Dean perform in indy shows in Phoenix. After a brief stint in LCU, Dean was promoted to the big time. He made his debut after the Genesis PPV, defeating Angel Teves. Soon after, he was paired with Frank Heimdal, and, as the 'Rat Pack', the team captured the Tag Titles at the 8/28 Tempest, and retaining them in a spectacular table match at Torn. Dean's future certainly looks bright. After all, he's been to the top before.
