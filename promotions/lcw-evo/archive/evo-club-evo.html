LCW Evo Card Archive

Club Evo

February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
LCW Evo - The Evolution of Lethal! Club neo Tour Click on the area of the club you'd like to receive more information about. About the Club: Club Evo was formerly a vacant lot in downtown Philadelphia. After extensive demographic and regional studies, Edward Olmstead decided to base neo's shows in Philadelphia, one of the five largest cities in the United States, and probably best known for its rabidly devoted wrestling fans. Using Alex Lawson, better known as LCW Evo's Duel and a Philadelphian all his life, to scout out a location, Edward decided to build Club neo - the ultimate night club/sports bar/sports entertainment complex. Completed in October of 2001, Club Evo looks to revolutionarize the way you watch professional wrestling! Club Evo has a crack staff of security to ensure that no one under 21 makes it into the club, and in compliance with Philadelphia's zoning laws, plain clothes officers from the Philadelphia Police Department regularly patrol the club to ensure that nothing goes awry. Ground Floor: The ground floor is the hub for the wrestling aspect of the club. On nights that shows are being run, the first floor is strictly non-smoking, and violators are removed from the premises. Should future disturbances occur, blacklisting is also a possibility. To ensure that the LCW Evo talent have only the cleanest air, 13 air purifiers/circulators are installed throughout the walls with direct ventilation to the exterior of the club. Entrance/Security Checkpoint: This is the home base of the Club Evo security operation. Due to the strict rules governing entrance into the club, at least two bouncers are on-hand at all times, and at peak hours, as many as four may be working the door, with half checking ID's and the guest sign-in book, and the other half patting down for weapons and escorting violators out of the club. Although four sets of glass double doors open into the club, the security area funnels the traffic down so that only one couple can make it into the club at a time. Coat Check: The coat check area has two functions: Guests with jewelry or accessories deemed unacceptable by the security staff (ie, spiked collars/bracelets, etc) can deposit the offending object here for pick-up on their departure. Also, the head of club security has his office in the back of this area, which is outfitted with a dozen monitors that correspond with the closed circuit television cameras installed througout the club. Vertical Transit, Floor 1: On the first floor, there are five elevators, three of which are for the general public and go only to the second floor. A fourth elevator is for the lighting technicians and goes to an elevated catwalk near the first floor ceiling that leads to the lighting rigs and other scaffolding necessary for the Impulse show. The final elevator goes to the upper level, and is for talent only. There are also two sets of stairs for use in the event of a power outage or fire, and they are exit only on third floor, so as to prevent people from sneaking onto the upper level. Monitor Wall: On the first floor, there's a wall of 15 monitors that can be used to broadcast a single huge image or 15 separate images. During Impulse, the monitors serve two purposes: they can display a wrestler's entrance video package, and then, during the match itself, multiple replays can be shown around the live action, all without taking away from the picture. Bar: While both bars on both floors are fully stocked, the first floor bar serves as a sports bar six days of any given week, so the primary focus is on beers and lagers. Among the drafts on tap: Budweiser, LaBatts, MGD, Becks, Yuengling, and Heineken. Above the bar are several monitors which are usually set to ESPN or FoxSports, to cater to the "real sports" crowd. Ringside Area: The ringside area is surrounded by a black retainer wall, nearly a foot thick, that is reinforced with steel and then padded with high-tensile rubber for cushioning without much give. Hanging above the ringside area are the various lighting rigs used for the show, both for even lighting during the matches as well as the individual lighting required for the wrestlers' introductions. Ring: The ring is a regulation LCW ring, purchased from David Beaumont by Edward Olmstead and then modified in several areas: the ringposts are now stronger and permanently attached to the ring because it is never taken down, the ropes have been tightened for more tension, and the canvas itself has less spring for greater impact, forcing the LCW Evo competitors to be better bumpers. Announce Position: Here is the area where Joe Black and Livvie Heimdal are really given a chance to shine. Not only is there an announce table complete with monitors, headsets, and all the trimmings, but Joe Black also has a private line to the bar for immediate beverage delivery should he feel the need for refreshments during the show. The announce position is elevated five feet off of the ground as well, putting it about six inches above being level with the actual canvas of the ring. This was also at Joe's request, so that he could have a better view of the action. Technical Audio/Video Area: Directly behind the announce position is the area where Impulse shows are really made. The production chief and his assistant have constant feeds from both the actual LCW cameras and also the digital camcorders, and their job is to decide which feed to run to which monitor for maximum visual impact. Also working with them in this area is the sound chief, who's responsible not only for ensuring that Joe and Livvie's commentary is clearly recorded for splicing into the finished show, but also for ensuring that the correct microphones are on when they need to be. This area is also elevated, and it's protected by 3 inch thick plexiglass on all sides (save for the front, where the announce position is located) to protect the equipment from liquid damage and/or theft. Second Floor: The second floor serves as the primary "club" area, featuring a more advanced lighting system as well as a huge dance floor. Smoking is permitted on the second floor at all times, and it is also equipped with thirteen air purifiers, not unlike the first floor. Vertical Transit, Floor 2: The second floor bank of elevators numbers at four, with three for the general public and one for the talent to go the upper level. Also, like the first floor, the second floor has a set of steps for emergency use. Bar: The second floor bar is also completely stocked, but its primary focus is on hard liquor, so as to tailor more to the club scene. Rather than televisions, the second floor bar is equipped with a smoke machine (located at the end of the bar so that it can put smoke out into the club) and several sets of mutli-colored computerized lights to liven up the dance floor. Dance Floor: The main feature of Club Evo's dance floor that sets it apart from the other clubs in the area is that it is made of 12 inch thick glass panes, all securely fastened in place both on top and from beneath by a steel grid. This allows patrons on the second floor a unique view of the action during Impulse, and the cameraman aren't above going up there for the killer shots too. Also, the dance floor is more or less surrounded by couches and other seating apparatus for those who choose not to dance or who need a break from dancing. Monitor Wall: This monitor wall is smaller than the one on the ground floor, consisting of nine monitors as opposed to fifteen. On Impulse nights, the monitors show a feed of the show, but on the other nights of the week, they are generally used to show giant-sized plasma computer-generated plasma displays that pulse and throb in time with the music being played on the second floor. Upper Level (Talent Only): The upper level is the last bastion of sanity for the talent, but due to a new law passed by the Philadelphia City Council specifically for Club Evo, no members of the roster are allowed on the upper level during the show, only before and after. How each roster member deals with this will be up to them, but they will be forced to spend at least two full hours within the Club proper each week. For safety's sake (and also as part of the wrestling theme, the entire roof of the club is surrounded by ten foot high chain link fencing. Vertical Transit, Upper Level: The upper level transit bay has a single elevator and a set of steps that can only be opened from the outside. To reach the upper level, all talent must have their badges or be accompanied by the Security Chief. Locker Room: The locker room area is pretty standard. There are several single rooms that can easily accomodate one or two wrestlers and/or entourage members, as well as several suites with private bathrooms that are primarily for stables and/or family members. The locker room area also contains a men's and women's restroom area, both of which are complete with showers and hot tubs. In order for the bathrooms to be entered, talent must swipe their badges, and their guests must swipe visitor passes through a maglock on the door (this prevents men from entering the women's room and vice versa).
