Dawning: Champions

2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
LCW Evo Impulse Champions The Madhouse Champion sat in his dressing room in a plain wooden folding chair, which, from the looks of things, wasn’t originally a part of the plush surroundings in which he currently found himself. His black boots were on, as were the surgical scrub pants that comprised his ring attire. Around his waist was the Madhouse Title, its gold plates giving off a dull glow in the fluorescent lights of the room. He appeared to be in the process of taping his forearms and hands, as the plain white athletic tape wound its way from the middle of his forearm to his right wrist. He pulled another long strip of tape from the roll and ripped it into smaller pieces, taking care to tape his knuckles so as to avoid damage to them in the match that would occur in a few minutes’ time. With that out of the way, he began to slowly and methodically wind the roll around his left wrist, taking care to keep the tape taut as he wrapped to give support to the part of his body that was most vital to his offensive arsenal. It was tricky business, this - if the tape was too tight, he’d lose flexibility in his wrists, which would make it next to impossible to apply Unity or to climb out of the cage. On the other hand, if he let the tape become loose, he ran the risk of injuring himself should something go wrong during the match. Of course, he wasn’t expecting anything to go wrong. Even if Heimdal somehow had miraculously improved since Alex had faced him in December, it still wouldn’t have mattered. He wasn’t Alex, and he didn’t make mistakes. Not this time. That had been his undoing before, and that had forced him to become a spectator once more, watching as Alex had taken their body and joined that troupe of morons. But what Alex didn’t know was that he had been planning, and when Alex had gone to sleep, he had taken over. He and Gabrielle had crafted Lost into the vicious and sadistic machine that he had become, but that had apparently not been enough, as Aaron had failed in his match against Gryfon earlier in the night. Perhaps more “conditioning” was in order, but, at any rate, what was done was done, and Lost’s ramblings of “he’s back” meant no more to Duel than Gabrielle’s smug smile after he had dispatched Dante Andersen just short minutes ago. All that mattered now was all that ever mattered - himself. He was Duel, and… He was interrupted in mid-thought by a knock at the door. “What is it?” he demanded impatiently, finishing up the wrap on his left wrist. The door opened, and a blue-haired women entered, the smell of her unwashed dreadlocks offended Duel’s nose almost as much as her pale white skin and pronounced make-up did. While women in general were repulsive to him, this one was particularly foul, and he didn’t want her in his sanctum sanctorum just minutes before he was to perform. “What is it?” he repeated again, knowing that the tone in his voice would convey the message to the offensive visitor. Instead, he was surprised when she hefted a camera to her shoulder and muttered a single word. “Speak.” And with that, the red light on the digital camcorder turned on. “Ah, I should address this to Frank, I think. Heimdal, you should consider yourself lucky. A little less than a year to the day after my initial awakening, you did the same thing that Mike Static did.” “I heard your comments earlier, Frank, and you’re absolutely right - this is all your fault. You were the one who set me loose again. You were and are the one who is responsible for everything I’ve done since January. You are the one who allowed what happened to Rook Black to happen to him. And most of all, you are the one who helped me to reshape Evo into something more fitting, more suitable, for what we’re about to achieve.” “Are you ready for that, Frank?” “Are you ready for the real true evolution of Evo? I think, deep down, you are ready.” “You and I, we’re not so much different. You talk about honor and duty, and how you’re tired of it. What you don’t realize is that no matter what you say, you’re tied to those concepts irrevocably - they make you who you are just as much as that mutated chromosome made your eyes.” “And it’s those concepts that keep you from becoming one of us. Michael and Aaron and I? We’re not bound to anything but ourselves, and that’s what makes us what we are.” “Champions.” “You may have done the same thing that Static did in setting me free, but you’ll never be the same caliber of a competitor as Static was, Frank. Static was a champion . But then he left, and when he came back, he lost his edge.” “He decided to enslave himself to the same outdated standards that you currently subject yourself to, and look at what happened to him - Aaron drove him away. You say you’re tired of those concepts, Frank, and I say that you’re a liar.” “You’ve got one chance to prove it to me, and if you don’t, make no mistake - I’ll tear your head from your shoulders where you stand just as soon as I’ll look at you. You’re right about one thing tonight, and that’s the fight. It will be glorious, and it will be my glory when everything is said and done.” “Prove me wrong, Frank; I’m begging you.” “Now, you,” Duel said, gesturing to the cameraperson, “Get out of my quarters, now.” Though he said it without raising his voice, Duel’s steely tone spoke volumes about the seriousness of his command. “You’d better watch yourself, Duel,” Eilene hissed, “We may not like Steve, but we like you even less.” Duel smiled at the goth, but it held no warmth. “He deserved everything that he got, and I wouldn’t hesitate to do that to any one of you.” Duel paused. “Even you, sweetheart.
