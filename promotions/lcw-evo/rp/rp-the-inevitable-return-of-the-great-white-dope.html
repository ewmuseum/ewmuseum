The Inevitable Return of the Great White Dope

9 January 2002

LCW Evo: The Evolution of Lethal! Legend Board | Back to Legends Main Rook Black Entitled: The Inevitable Return of the Great White Dope Written: January 9th, 2002 Location: A parking lot in downtown Philadelphia It's dark out. We're in a parking lot in downtown Philly. We're looking at a man leaning against the back of an Electric Green 2002 Mustang GT. The personalized plate reads 'I AM'. The man we're looking at has a days worth of unshaved beard. He's wearing a black leather jacket, black jeans tucked into black cowboy boots. We zoom in on the man's face, seeing distinctive vertical scars just under his eyes. Rook: First of all, I'm in. Rook: After all, I must obey the inscrutable exhortations of my soul, godless heathen or not. Rook: As for Tim ... Rook shakes his head sharply from side to side, making an audible cracking noise. Rook: Congratulations on your new job. I can always count on you to roll down the path of least resistence whenever the opportunity is presented to you. Enjoy your nice transition from one of 'us' into one of 'them'. Rook makes an ugly smirk. Heh. Rook takes a deep breath then rolls his eyes. Rook: So now we're going to have a nice killer tournament to determine who might be the second best wrestler in elseedubeeeveeoh. Rook: Don't get me wrong. I like tournaments. It's a privilege to watch your opponents fight each other. Take measure of them and all that. Rook: But I don't like not being IN them. Rook steps off the car deeper into camera shot. Rook: Not getting the option to throw down. Being seperated, severed from the blood and the desire. Pushed back to being the result of the tournament. Perhaps being even the furthest from everyone's mind as they measure their strategy and strength against the other eight men. Rook: I don't care for it. Rook: But then, it's not exactly my decision to make. Rook: But I'll be watching. And I already know. The show will be stolen by whoever wants it the most. Resting on laurels will get you a nice big L in the very first bracket. It's a night where desire and sacrifice and explosive lateral strategy will crown a tournament winner. A celebration of victory so joyful and profound that will last for thrity seconds. Thirty seconds of absolute bliss, that will end when I arrive in the ring to shake the winners hand. Rook: And he'll know, when I look him in the eye, he'll know, that the fight had only just begun.
