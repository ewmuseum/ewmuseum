Legend Board | Back to Legends Main Duel Entitled: Solitude Written: January 30, 2002 Location: Duel's Philadelphia Home Alexander Lawson, the man better known to the world as Duel, lay sprawled across the expansive black Corinthian leather couch within the living room of his apartment, one arm thrown haphazardly over the backrest of the sofa and one leg hanging off of the front of the cushions. He looked as though he hadn’t slept in days, and in a way, that was truer than he could realize. The man controlling his body wasn’t Lex Lawson, not really; he was simply a malignant part of Lawson’s personality, not unlike a tumor or other harmful growth, that had developed from years of Lawson mishandling his emotion. Rather than releasing his negative emotion, Lawson had chosen to spend the vast majority of the last twenty years of his life as a stoic, bottling up all the fear, aggression, and hurt that he had suffered since his father’s death in 1981. Lawson had always believed that he was channeling these feelings, first into his boxing matches, and then later into his wrestling career. However, he learned otherwise in early 2001, when those repressed feelings came to the surface in the form of a fragmented identity. Calling himself “Felony”, this dark alter-ego wreaked havoc on LCW for several months, and in the process, he cast a shadow forever over the life of Alex Lawson. After severe head trauma in April, Lawson returned in June of 2001, calling himself Duel, in light of the war that was going on within his brain. With three individual personalities all vying for dominance, Lawson often found himself losing control of his own body at inopportune times, and this cost him severely on more than one occasion, most notably when he was counted out during a World Title match that he had already more or less won. By the end of July, Lawson had more or less gained control of his divergent selves, and everything seemed to be fine. Until December 13, 2001. After taking a back drop to the outside of the ring wrong, Lawson was transported to Philadelphia General hospital, where test results came back showing that he was fine. Due to his history (three severe concussions in less than a year), he was to be kept overnight for observations, but when Mike Static, Lawson’s longtime rival, was assigned to the bed next to him, Lawson’s psyche snapped again. In light of Felony’s return to the public eye on Impulse 1.3, which ended with him crushing then-Evo Champion Rook Black’s hand with a beer keg, Lawson had remained in his home. A self-made recluse, he had been avoiding all human contact for seven days, and now, with Impulse a few mere hours away, the repercussions of his actions were starting to sink in. Not that it bothered him. On the contrary, he was delighted, as he had always been, to test the boundaries within which he existed. That had led to his undoing the past spring, but after months of planning and covert actions, he believed that he had finally gained the upper hand, and he wasn’t about to allow his confidence to get in the way of his judgment this time around. With a sigh, Duel made an effort to compose himself, slowly maneuvering into an upright position, the supple leather of the couch making faint noises as his bare upper torso slid across it. Rubbing a hand across the week-long stubble that had accumulated, Duel reached a hand out somewhat tentatively to his answering machine, which featured a flashing seven in the message window. The first voice was one that Lawson hadn’t heard in over a month: his mother’s. “Alex, this is your mother. I’m just calling because I talked to Olivia, and we’re worried about you…” Without hesitation, Lawson depressed the delete button before hearing the end of the message. As the second message began, Duel immediately depressed the delete button again, banishing the telemarketer to a digital graveyard where a multitude of his fellows awaited him. The third message was from Lawson’s bank, announcing that they had sold their residential sector to Citizens’ Bank, and that he could expect a new booklet featuring the details of his new plan in three to five business days. This too was deleted, as was another message from his mother that followed it. She had apparently seen what he had done on Impulse and was even more worried about her only son. “Four down, three to go,” Duel mused as he paced across the room and twisted the blinds to allow some of the late afternoon Philadelphia sunlight in. As the next message began to play, a smile played across Duel’s face, which was lit in uneven bars as he stood looking out the window. “Lex, this is Dave. I think we need to talk, Lex. You, Aaron, and Mike have all been acting completely irrationally, and frankly, I’m worried. If you’d like, we can continue our sessions, and hopefully we’ll be able to continue with the progress we made last fall…” Duel languidly crossed the living room, reaching out and pressing the delete button on the machine once more. As the next message started, Duel shook his head in disgust before deleting it once more. “If you were really psychic, Cleo, you would’ve known that I wasn’t going to pick up the phone, wouldn’t you?” Duel smirked, but the smile quickly faded as the last message began. “Alex…” It was Olivia Heimdal, sounding hurt and hesitant. “Alex, I’m not sure what’s happened between us in the last month, but I don’t like how we are. You’ve been so…so distant…you never return my calls…we’ve barely spoken since early December, and after you did…after you did…what you did…to Rook…you walked away from me.” “That hurt, Alex. It hurt a lot worse than the lonely nights waiting for you to call, than the hours I spent at the club before the shows hoping to spend a minute or two with you.” At that point, Livvie’s voice lost its composure and she started to stammer. “Alex…I’m not sure what we can do…where we can go, but I…don’t want to…be here…not with you. When we…when we started this relationship, it wasn’t meant to be like….like….like this…” And with that, the message cut off, though not by any action of Duel’s. The look on his face was one of confusion, but that quickly faded as he reached for the phone. After lifting the handset from the cradle, he punched in several numbers and then lifted the cordless receiver to his ear. “Mike? Yeah, it’s Duel. Just wanted to go over some things for the show…”
