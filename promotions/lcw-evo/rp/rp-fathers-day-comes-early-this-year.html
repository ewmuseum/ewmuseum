Father's Day Comes Early This Year

15 January 2002

Legend Board | Back to Legends Main Lost Entitled: Father's Day Comes Early This Year Written: 1-15-2002 Location: east Jersey State Prison, Rahway, NJ The past is a hard topic for Lost. Hard in the sense that most everything that happened before his personal evolution from Apostle to Lost is a haze, and hard because no matter how hard and fast you run from your past, it is still a part of you. In reality, the beginnings of Aaron Antioch's insanity was much farther back than his name change. During Lost's youth, back when his names was Matthew Green, his father, James Green, was an alcoholic and spouse abuser. On one particular night, James hit a shot on Lost's mother, Eileen, so well that it did not leave a cut, bruise, or black eye. It did, however, leave her in a coma from which she did not awake. Shortly thereafter, James Green was found guilty of murder in the second degree and sentenced to 15 years to life. It so happens that said 15 years has almost come to an end, and James Green is up for parole soon. Walking with a purpose, Lost enters the meeting room of the cold and altogether frightening prison. Lunacy follows close behind. When being searched for weapons, Lunacy thought to himself 'I already have a weapon at my disposal, I call him Lost.' Lost sits down at the Plexiglass partition and takes the telephone off the dial. After waiting patiently for a few minutes, James Green enters the room in an orange jumpsuit with the escort of a guard from the State Correctional Police. Mr. Green sits across from his son and removes the telephone from the wall. 'Hey Matt.' 'My name is Aaron.' 'I didn't name you that.' An uneasy silence. 'After everything that happened, could you blame me for changing my name? Could you hold it against me for not wanting to be associated with a murderer like you?' James Green looks defeated after that comment. From a few feet behind Lost, Lunacy looks on and hears half of the conversation and lipreads the other. He understands that this imprisoned man is in fact his uncle, but feels little remorse. If there was no murder, then there would be no Lost. Probably a horrible way of looking at things, but, such is life. 'Ya know,' Mr. Green continues, running his hand through his crew-cut, graying hair, 'when I got your letter that you were coming, I had to fight some guys off because you signed it.' 'It's nice to know I'm over with the con community.' Lunacy notices the role reversal here. James Green, convicted murderer, is showing a great deal of compassion and warmth, reaching out to his estranged son. Lost, the secondary victim of the crime, shows none. 'Ma...Aaron, I don't know what good it will do, but I am sorry, I am truly sorry. I know that won't make things better.' 'You're damn right it won't, Dad.' Even Lunacy, a pokerface if there ever was one, is taken aback by the sarcasm, anger and ill-will in Lost saying 'Dad'. Lunacy begins to debate stepping in, but he is certain that this is cleansing for Aaron. 'You can't make things better. What's done is done, sad to say. There's no bringing Mom back, and even if there WERE a way, I'm not sure I'd want to, not with you up for parole in a couple months.' 'Are you going to be there at my parole hearing?' 'You're Goddamned right I'm going to be there, and I'm going to make sure that you stay right where you are, on the other side of this glass.' James Green sits back in his chair, stunned. He realized that his son not ahowing up at the hearing would probably be good, but him showing would be a 50/50 deal. Not anymore. Now he was faced with the realization that, with the light at the end of the tunnel so close, he'd probably have to spend another 10 years in here. But in 10 years... As if reading his father's mind, Lost continues. 'And in ten years, when the next one comes up, I'll be there again. and in twenty years, and in thirty years, and so on, until you've wasted away to nothing.' James Green, stunned, says the first thing to come to mind: 'No son would do that.' 'I stopped being your son the second that a woman who did nothing more than cook, clean and love her family, lost her life because of you.' Lost moves to put the phone back on the hook, but his father gets in one last comment. 'Hey, I love you boy, you're my pride and joy. Maybe if I get out, we can work something out.' Lost hangs up the phone and stands up, hands on hips, almost ready to burst from over-stimulated emotions. To vent them, he reaches back and slams his fist full blast into the partition seperating him from his father. The Plexiglass shakes and gets a guards attention, but it stays intact. Lost exits the room with his father's head hung in shame, telephone up to his ear. He looks up when he hears a click and sees his nephew Michael holding the phone. 'Uncle Jimmy,' Lunacy says in a mocking child-like voice, 'I think you're safest here in jail then out where the bad man can get you.' Lunacy laughs a maniacal sadistic laugh as James Green is taken back to his cell.
