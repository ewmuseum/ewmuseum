Step Nine: Reason

23 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
LCW Evo - The Evolution Continues Legend Board | Back to Legends Main Entitled: Step Nine: Reason Written: 5/23/02 Location: Tempe, AZ Dean Wallace is seen grabbing a well earned rest on a bench at Tempe High School’s baseball field. As he raises a bottle of Pepsi to his mouth, a familiar (at least to anyone who’s ever attended a baseball game) tune starts to play. Out on the field, all 13 of Dean’s kids run around (Well, beat the drum and hold the phone - the sun came out today!) Matt and Ricky (the autistic kids) eat grass, and Scott (the ADD kid) picks it and throws it into the air, and Dean is desperately trying to get them to stop. (We're born again, there's new grass on the field.) David Watkins rounds the bases like a bat out of hell, chugging around first. However, evidently, he’s forgotten about second base and is making a beeline for third. (A-roundin' third, and headed for home, it's a brown-eyed handsome man; ) Dean grabs the bat out of Scott Page’s hands, attempting to stop the young man from further denting the fence (Anyone can understand the way I feel…) (Oh, put me in, Coach - I'm ready to play today; ) Ground ball to short, Henry leans over to pick it up, he’s got it… he’s holding it… now he’s rolling it into the outfield… oy vey… (Put me in, Coach - I'm ready to play today; ) Fly ball to right… easy play for Cody as Devedrick rolls him to the ball… Alastair, get out of the way… Al, out of the way… Al!… base hit… (Look at me, I can be Centerfield.) Cody rips a screaming liner to right!… first baseman’s asleep… literally… wake up, Randy!… aw, crap, that’s gonna hit him… Dean Wallace lays out, and makes a diving catch just in front of a sleeping Randy Keeton. The kids (the ones paying attention) applaud. Randy awakes as he hears the ‘thump’ of Dean hitting the dirt. RK: Hi, Coach Dean! What’d I miss? 5:01 DW: Alright, that’ll do for today. Bring it in! The kids all flock to Dean’s side. DW: Good work today, gentlemen. I think it’s safe to say that all of you have a pretty good grasp on the fundamentals, and I think we’re ready for the first game next week! The kids cheer. DW: Alright. Before you all check out today, though, I’m going to ask your parents to come over here… Dean flags down the team’s parents, and they come join the circle. DW: Now, parents… your kids are doing a great job. So great, that I thought they deserved a little… LAYNE STALEY: I’m…. the man…. In the box… DW: Ryan… (shakes head) you missed your cue, dude… Ryan Wallace stands in the parking lot, opening the back of Dean’s Escalade. RW: I didn’t know the radio would turn on, dude! LS: Bur-ied in… my… DW: AHEM! Dean has become sensitive to profanity. RW: Can I roll it out now? DW: Whatever. Ryan removes a barbacue from the back of the SUV. He follows that with a cooler. Then another. Then another. DW: Folks, we’re having a barbeque. Another cheer from the kids. 6:30 Dean Wallace attends to the grill, cooking the last of the hot dogs. His car continues to kick out the jams, as it were. The kids have long since finished eating and are chasing each other around the bases. RW: ‘Nother weiner, por favor. Dean serves his brother another dog. He grabs the last two and, for the first time all night, goes to sit down on the grass. No sooner has he taken a bite of hot dog when somebody sits down next to him. Michelle Rodriguez. DW: How’s it going? MR: Fantastic. Thanks for doing this, Dean. Saved me a lot of time tonight. Plus, the kids’ll be so wired that they’ll go right to bed. DW: Anything else I can do to help? MR: Short of dusting, not really. Dean has a laugh at this. DW: So, whaddya need? MR: Well, I wanted to thank you again for watching the kids two weeks ago. That’s 12 times. Stop, or I’ll start thinking I’m a public servant of some kind. DW: Again, I say it’s never a problem. MR: You don’t understand. I think I do. And, frankly, it frightens me. MR: Those kids adore you. You’re the closest thing to a father figure they’ve really ever had. Phew. Not what I thought it was. DW: What do you mean by that? MR: Well… Michelle hems and haws for a bit. MR: At one time, Cody and Devedrick were… relatively… normal. I don’t think I want to hear this. MR: Devedrick never really had parents. It’s a miracle he was born without any significant damage, because his parents were both heavily into drugs. The Detroit drug scene is pretty frightening, and those two got caught in it. Dean gnaws on his hot dog. MR: His father’s still in prison. Will be ‘till the day he dies. The way I understand it, he killed a man during a botched drug deal. Christ. DW: I’m not too sure I want to hear the rest of this… MR: Just listen. Michelle finishes her dog. MR: His mother… she’s just a mess. When Devedrick was two, Mrs. Goodwell walked up to her neighbors and left the kid on the doorstep. DW: Cody’s parents. MR: Right. Cody’s father is… was… my brother. Was? Shit. I don’t want to hear this. MR: They were meant for each other… Corey and Rhonda, I mean. They had a beautiful son. He was very precocious… learned to read when he was two. The family was very close-knit. And then, one day, out of nowhere, it’s a family of four. Dean’s lost his appetite. MR: They took great care of the kids. And, as you can imagine, the two got to be great friends. They did everything together. MR: One day, Corey and Rhonda snagged Tigers tickets. The boys were so excited!… On the way there… DW: Stop… MR: Some jackass hit the median coming the other way and careened into the other side. They hit their car head on. My brother died instantly. MR: My sister-in-law died when the car slammed into the guardrail and flipped 4 times. Jesus… MR: Long story short… Cody ended up with some broken vertebrae, and Devedrick was in a coma for a week. Oh my God… stop now, before I start crying or something. MR: He came out with some pretty severe brain damage, and Cody came out in the chair. No orphanage would take them, and I was the first person they contacted to take care of the kids. I had just graduated from Grand Canyon University and was trying to get a job. I couldn’t have taken them… but they left me with no choice. I wanted the best for my nephew and his friend, so I took them in. They were 6. I was 22. Unbelieveable. DW: I don’t… really have anything to say… MR: I just thought you should know that. And know that they really do love you. In the whole ‘favorite uncle’ way. Dean is speechless. People are starting to leave the park. Michelle stands up and corrals the Odd Couple 2.0 and heads to their car. Dean and Ryan are the last two there. As they round up the barbeque, Ryan asks a question. RW: What’d Michelle want? DW: Nothing much. Just to thank me. RW: You alright? DW: Yeah. Comparatively, I’m terrific. END Site designed by W.E.B.
