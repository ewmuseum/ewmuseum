LCW Evo: The Evolution of Lethal! Legend Board | Back to Legends Main Frank Heimdal Entitled: Default Written: 02/03/02 Location: The house Sunday afternoon. 3:03 in the pm. Frank stood at Livvie's door. It's been a nice Ohio winter. Frank could wear lighter shoes without incurring frost bite, and he didn't have to bother with a coat. Just a sweater and jeans. He wasn't sure what he wanted to do, or what he wanted to say. He could hear Marv, Livvie's German shepard, at the door making quiet, half barking, half snuffling noises. Frank knew it was Marv and not Goldie, the female, because Goldie never made noise. That's how they were trained. They don't bark. They just bite. The door was unlocked. Frank let himself in and immediately crouched down, taking Marv's big head in his hands and roughly scratching behind the ears and digging his fingers into the dog's muscular neck. Marv rewarded Frank with a happy rumble. Livvie walked out of the kitchen. She knew it was Frank before he even walked through the door. She looked beautiful for nobody in particular, her long dark hair spilling over her shoulders, wearing a green scoop-neck shirt and boot-cut jeans, bare foot. Frank stood. Brother and sister regarded each other. Frank wanted to gently thump Livvie on the back, to see if she sounded as hollow as she looked. He could see it around her eye sockets, the way her shoulder bones poked out lately. She hasn't been eating again...why not? Frank had a feeling... Livvie wanted to grab Frank's face and bring it an inch away from hers. She wanted to check the winshield washer fluid/ anti-freeze eyes for signs of something. Something besides what she'd seen lately. She wanted her brother back. Not this automaton. 'Can we talk?' Frank asked simply. Livvie was taken aback by the question. He never had to ask her for that. 'Of course we can, Frankie. Do you want some coffee--' 'I mean talk , Livvie.' Frank cut her off. 'Oh.' Livvie knew what he meant. 'We haven't done this in a while. I wonder if I remember how...' 'There's no right or wrong to this. We do, or we don't. Can we try?' Wordlessly, Livvie walked to the living room and sat down on the couch. Frank laid down on the floor parallel to the sofa. Livvie sat cross-legged and looked down at him. 'So, what?' 'I don't know. Talk to me. Tell me how you've been. We've barely seen each other for weeks.' Frank laced his fingers over his stomach and stared at the ceiling. Livvie decided lying down was a good idea. She stretched out on the couch. From the side, she was elevated over Frank. From a top view, they looked to be lying side by side. Picture it in your mind's eye. Livvie thought. Then she began. 'Do you ever feel sugar-coated?' Frank thought about that. Applying a feminine term to himself felt kind of odd. Odd, but it fit this time. 'Yes.' 'I've been feeling that way lately...but I'm not sure when it happened. I can't very well blame it on Alex, or whatever's left of him right now. And I can't blame it on my job. Or my friends. It's all my fault. I'm having problems defining when I...changed. ' 'Relationships can sometimes cause a loss of identity. I have a little experience with that...' Frank smiled sardonically, '...but you're right; you can't blame it on the other person, be it boyfriend, co-worker, friend. It's all you. Go on.' 'I remember a time...not too long ago. Remember how we were, Frankie? When we were younger?' 'Yeah. Were we on crack?' Livvie giggled at this. Frank's smile had become less sardonic, more pleasant. 'Maybe,' Livvie continued, 'but I remember a time when we were indestructible, in out own way. No, not indestructible...more...what am I trying for here?' Frank's eyebrows knit together. 'We compromised when we had to. Never before. What a lot of people attributed to typical stubborness was, in actuality, just us trying to be us and trying not to be beaten up for it. And you and I were beaten up in all sorts of ways...' '...but NEVER beaten down. I remember, Frankie. That was how we got through those times...' 'Being a teen is painful.' 'But we have more opportunity to cause our own pain when we're adults. It's less external, more internal. We find more and more reasons to punish ourselves.' 'This hurts.' A non-sequiter, but it applied. Frank rubbed his eyes, digging the heels of his hands into his sockets. That hurt too. Good. 'Livvie, I feel like what's happened between you and Lex may be my fault.' Frank removed his hands from his eyes. 'I can make a direct correlation between when I dropped Lex on his head and when all of your problems with him began. And there was another time, way before I was in LCW, when Lex sustained head trauma, and...' Frank trailed off, not even wanting to say what that results of that incident were. 'There's the guilt factor, but it's more than that. I'm supposed to take care of you, and instead I'm the one that may have caused your pain. And if I didn't cause your pain, I still feel like I haven't been around enough for you. I feel like I've failed you.' 'In your defense, I haven't been very receptive.' Livvie shifted to get more comfortable. 'I knew you'd feel guilty, Frank. And don't think the concept of blaming you didn't cross my mind. But, the more I contemplate, the more I realise that anyone could have jump-started it. Bumps to the head are an occupational hazard in this line of work. You and I both aware of this. And maybe it wasn't you. There's always that...' 'Thanks for trying to make me feel better...' 'I'm not done.' Olivia cut her big brother off. 'Maybe this was a good thing, regardless of whose fault it was. Better now than twenty years from now.' Livvie's face darkened. She had hoped to get as many years out of Alex as possible. But that didn't seem to be the case with him now. She continued. 'And if it was you who did it, that's even better.' 'Why me?' 'Because you're aware. You know what to do with me. You may have gotten me into this, but you know how to pull me out too. It'll take time...but I'll heal. And so will you. Your pain is as important as my pain. And don't try to diminish that because you feel like you deserve to hurt too.' 'Then why you? Of all the people anywhere, why you?' Livvie paused and thought for a moment. 'Why not me? I can handle this. Even though I want to die and I've been leaving sad, maudlin, pathetic messages on his answering machine, and I want my man back in the worst way, I still know that this is temporary. This will pass. I may not be the same afterwards, but this situation is as permanent as I want it to be.' 'We can face things now....' Frank started. Livvie finished 'But that's the mindset we're in at this moment. I'll revert back to the 'queen of pain' and you'll go back to you...'Livvie started. 'But we need to take advantage of the frame of mind we're in before we lose it.' Frank sat up suddenly. Livvie followed suit. Frank turned so that he was facing her, looking up. She turned so that she was facing him, looking down. 'I need to talk. Just talk.' 'You never talk. Frank, how do you feel?' 'I feel like I'm displacing air for no good reason. I feel shapeless. It's all ambiguous, really...I can't pin-point this. It's not even as if I've been feeling lately. I can't even hate myself. I can't see the way I used to.' Frank's eyes stared through Livvie. 'I thought I was being liquid, I thought I was being adaptable...' Frank closed his eyes and looked for the words on the insides of his eyelids. 'But maybe I was just being a wimp. Neutrality is a nice concept, but sometimes I wonder if it isn't just a way to not have to choose sides, to not have to face anything, to not have to...fight.' A tear made it's way out. 'Can I fight and still be considered a good person?' 'Frankie, you're nothing but good. That's why you're so hard on yourself.' Livvie was thrilled. Frank was having a break through. She grabbed his hands and squeezed them as hard as she could. Frank's eyes flew open at the pressure. The tangent began. 'I'm tired of feeling broken...' Livvie started. 'I'm tired of baring my throat...' Frank said. Livvie-'Are we stupid?' Frank-'Are we deluded?' Livvie-'Maybe both...?' Frank-'We never used to take the kind of shit we do now. You were the voice...' 'And you were the action...' Livvie said, 'I want to feel good about something, anything, again...' 'This may not be the healthiest suggestion, but it's all I can think of right now. We need to remember the last time we were okay, the last time we were difficult to hurt...' Frank waited, to see if Livvie knew what he was getting at. 'And we need to act like that. We can pretend...just for a little while. But this time, we'll be careful. We used to hurt people and push them away. We can make a point of not hurting the people who have been taking care of us right along...' 'We'll keep an eye on the innocents. Nikki...she's perfect. She never gets mad, she never says anything bad, she's been so patient with me.' 'And Dean. Dean just is , and that's beautiful. He loves and cares in his own way. We'll keep an eye on him too. But the rest...' 'Well,' Frank's mind was beginning to shift back, 'I think we can afford to be a little more selective, don't you? I'm all about keeping an open mind, but keeping an open mind was what got us into trouble in the first place.' 'That's a bad attitude. But you do have a point.' It's done. Frank's eyes hurt, and Livvie felt exhausted. All of the emotional bloodletting had taken a toll on them. Livvie and Frank stared at each other blankly, the conversation already making it's way into their subconscious, like a fading dream. They stood up. 'What do you want to do right now?' Livvie asked as she walked to her bedroom. She shut the door and started to change. Frank raised his voice so he could be heard. 'I want to see Nikki.' It was comforting to watch Nikki work. It was nice to see the structure that he had in his life at the moment function and act, proof that it was real and concrete and not only that, his. And it was a person. Most of all, Frank wanted to tell her he was sorry. 'Where is she?' Livvie called through her bedroom door. She pulled off the awful tight shirt she was wearing and peeled herself out of her jeans. She stood in front of her closet in her underwear, waiting for his response. 'At work.' The thought of a boilermaker and a hug from a body that yielded when she squeezed it sounded really nice to Livvie. Nikki was still a minor mystery to Olivia. But a very comforting one nonetheless. Livvie went to the bottom of the closet and pulled out a huge black hooded sweatshirt and a pair of baggy jeans. As she stepped into her pants she asked 'Can I come too?' 'Of course. You need to get the hell out of here.' Livvie finished dressing and walked out of her bedroom. She walked to the refridgerator and opened it. She reached way back, and came up with a pack of filterless Camels. She took them out of the baggie and opened them up. Livvie answered Frank's questioning gaze. 'It's a trick I learned last time I quit. If I always have cigarettes, I know I can have one if I need one. And right now, I need one.' Livvie found a book of matches and lit up. She inhaled delicately, picking tobacco off her lower lip. 'Filterless?' Frank asked. 'I wanted to make sure it would be as painful as possible when I did decide that I needed a smoke.' 'That's my old brand.' Livvie passed him her lit cigarette. Frank took it, and took a healthy draw. Then he choked. He gave it back to her. But she had moved over to the phone stand. She held the cordless phone in her hand, contemplating it. 'Do you want to...call him?' Frank sounded worried. 'More than anything.' Olivia hurled the phone as hard as she could against the far wall. It smashed apart and landed on the floor. Livvie giggled. 'What did that poor phone ever do to you?' Frank asked jokingly. 'It never rang.' Livvie stared at it as if she were about to cry. Then she snapped out of it. Frank gave her a knowing look. Then he clapped her on the back like one would hit a football player, causing her to stumble forward and stare at him in amused reproach. 'Let's rock, Livvie-Loo.' finis ============= No cameras, boys. You know the drill...
