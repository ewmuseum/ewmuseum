PCWF — Roleplay

The French Fury Jean De Mort, shoots kayfabe

1 December 1997
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
<Scene opens with Tim Cross preparing for a sit-down interview in the opulent home of the French Fury Jean De Mort> Cross: Ladies and gentlemen, it is my distinct pleasure to be conducting this interview, and if internet rumours have been any indication this interview will be watched by fans all over the world. The French Fury Jean De Mort has allowed me and a small crew into his home at his beautiful estate to speak. In this interview you will hear not from Jean De Mort the public figure, but Jean De Mort the human being. A man, whether you like him or not who has committed his life to professional wrestling with a dedication that is truly admirable.Jean has stated that I am to pull no punches and that he will shoot from the hip on such topics as the state of professional wrestling today, the current crop of talent in the PCWF, his future in the PCWF, and what I am truly looking forward to hearing about, his recollections both good and bad abou this past in the former ICW. Jean, I appreciate the opportunity you have afforded me. Now to get this interview going, I have a totally surface observation, you seem to have improved your english skills since we last saw you. Jean: I am happy to have this opportunity to speak. What I have to say might anger alot of people, even some of my friends in this business, but it needs to be said. As for my english, haha, it hasn't improved, I have always been fluent in English. The fact that when I'm in America I use french words when I speak is merely an indication of the pride I feel for my French culture, not a sign of ignorance. Cross: Noted. Now, on to more important topics. You have requested that I pull no punches and I will not. You've accomplished alot, just from reading your bio- it shows that you have won the WAR International and Liberty title, you were the 2 time Heavyweight Champion in the VWF promotion, the head promoter at LAW as well as having a successful stint in the ICW. What possessed you to get back into wrestling after a nearly two year long abscence? Jean: My love of this sport does not end with my own ego as it does for most. I've elevated the sport of wrestling to a point where it was an art-form. Wrestlers currently have denegrated my achievements to making the sport nothing more then choreographed garbage. It angered me as a professional, and I did what any man would do, I took action. Cross: Now Jean, there are those who say that you're days are done. You know as well as I do that this is a sport with a very short memory. Are you at all concerned with the fact that you might not be as respected among rookie athletes as you once were? Jean: I would be lying if I said no to that question. Certainly when one has accomplished what I have recognition is a consideration. However I did more in the short time that I competed internationally then most would have accomplished in a career. The fact that I stopped early on for a period, should only bring up one question among the rookies, that is what would have happened if I had never left the sport at all. Cross: Jean, concerning your former ICW stint. How do you feel generally about that experience, in terms of how you were marketed to the public and the opportunites given to you. Jean: As you know Tim, Mark Bagwell and I are very good friends. At the time of the ICW he was an active competitor and had a little less creative pull .I'll come right out and say that I was underutilzed in the ICW at the time. Noone respected my talent. I considered it an insult that I was booked into a feud with The French Falcon Jean Paul Richard. If anyone remembers this cretin they will remember that I continually defeated him, yet I was propelled into match after match. My room for advancement was capped in the ICW by promoters who thought the idea of two French men feuding was more appealing then granting me title shots. I do consider my time in the ICW a valuable learning experience. Simply put now I would not consider going back to that role. In WAR and other federations my potential was met with opportunity, here that was not the case. The politics has changed somewhat. Those in power then are shifted around and Mark Bagwell has put a whole new innovative pull into the promoting, so I expect better treatment. Put simply I am the only man to be around from those early ICW days. No one else can claim that. Richard went into obscurity, and I stand here now. Cross: You speak of those ICW days as a learning experience. How do you feel you have grown professionally since then? Jean; I've grown in all ways possible. I've honed my technique in the ring down to a science. I'm a better wrestler and I've mastered the politics that you need outside of the ring to guarentee you get the opportunites you deserve. The French Fury the PCWF is getting is a much smarter and more talented athlete then the one that the ICW had. Cross: Jean you have spoken on numerous occasions of what you feel is wrong with the world of pro-wrestling. Any elaborations on this? Jean: Yes. I can give you one name. Now you might want to turn the censors on for this, but it is the process in wrestling that I refer to as the "Shawn Michealsization" of the sport. Where dancing and choreography becomes the key components to success. Three names in the PCWF, from the top of my head that come to mind are Le Rebel, Playboy Ronnie D, and Shane Axis. They all try to conform to this ideal. I consider myself honestly the best technical wrestler in the sport today. None of these guys could lace my boots in the ring, since none of them know wrestling. They pose and dance but none are students of the sport. I'm coming back to capture the PCWF title and to send a clear warning to all of these 'high flyers' that this sport belongs to wrestlers, not carnival acts. Whether or not they respect me personally, they will learn to respect me professionally. No high flyer could ever match me in the ring. Dance as you like, when it comes to wrestling, I will come out on top. Cross: Jean, as provocative as those statements are, don't you feel that you are overstating your case? I mean the three wrestlers you named are among the biggest draws of the PCWF? Jean: That happened due to poor promoting and the lack of an alternative. Noone with my talent coupled with my charisma has been around since I left and Mark retired. There are others that don't compete in the PCWF, but the PCWF itself has degenerated into a carnival act. People whether they hate me in the United States, or love me in France will respect my ability. Not my looks, dancing or pyrotechnics. I ran LAW I understand the importance of marketing in developing a league. I also come from a wrestling family and I understand tradition and respect. I come to legitamize the PCWF World Heavyweight Title. The Week of December 1st, I come to do just that. Cross: Jean this interview has run far overtime. But I thank you for the chance to speak. It was certainly insightful, and I doubt if we'll ever have this private an opportunity again. I'm sure you've angered alot of people by these comments, and it will be interesting to see how things develop. Jean: Thank you for the opportunity to speak. I can credit Mark Bagwell with having the guts to let me speak my mind. I meant every word of what I've said. I'm not resting on my laurels. Shane Axis, come to the ring and find out if I can back up what I say. You won't be happy with the result. Cross: Wow, well that's all the time we have folks!
