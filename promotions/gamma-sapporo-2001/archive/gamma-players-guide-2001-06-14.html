GAMMA (Sapporo, 2001) — Rules

GAMMA Players’ Guide, Version 1

14 June 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Goukai Alpha Mixed Martial Arts: Players Guide v 1 What We Are: Goukai Alpha Mixed Martial Arts is a booked Japanese puroresu fed. GAMMA is relatively small, somewhere between BattlARTs and FMW in real life, or in the e-wrestling world a little smaller than the Tokyo Organization of Puroresu. The style of the fed is that of BattlARTs, with pins nonexistent, being replaced by 10 count knock outs. Roleplaying: To be successful in GAMMA you must RP. The two types of RPing are flashing and strating. Strats are the most important aspect of RPing in GAMMA. If you dont strat there's a pretty good chance you'll lose. Flashing is also important, but secondary to strating. Strats should give how your wrestler is planning on winning the match plus any spots and how you wrestler will react to how the match finishes. The first no show in RP'ing will result in a warning. The second no show you'll be jobbed. The third you're out of GAMMA. If necessary, your character will remain in fed until the end of the tour, acting as a glorified jobber. Shows: GAMMA will run three to four week tours with usually two shows per week. Most shows are house shows that will receive a summary type report. The final show of the tour will be at a bigger venue (Around 4000 seats. House shows are usually 1000-2500) and will be a TV taping for a Samurai! TV Battle Station, and thus be full write up with match commentary. I understand the feeling of doing a lot of work on RP'ing and the match you were working on only gets a couple paragraph report, that's why for house shows you will only be asked to write one flash per touring week and a two to three paragraph strat for each match your character. Not too strenuous. However, since I will put in a lot more work on the tour ender, I expect you to also. Flashes should be a little longer and the strats more in depth. Remember, the more you put in the more you get out. Titles: For the first few tours, GAMMA will not have any titles, waiting until it has established itself before it awards its championships. In the mean time, the planned titles GAMMA will recognize are: 1. GAMMA Openweight Title - GAMMA's one and only singles title which is open to all competitors. 2. Combative Tag-Team Titles - Bought by GAMMA's owner/president, Ryota Kazama, from the defunct G-Pro. The top team in GAMMA will hold these titles and defend them against all comers, including teams from other companies. Both titles will be required to be defended at least once a tour, usually at the tour ender. The exception is during the Gemini Battle Festival tour in which no defenses are required. Tournaments: Each year GAMMA will run two major tournaments. 1. Sapparo Open - A two day, single-elimination tournament held in the city of GAMMA's headquarters, Sapparo, with top singles talnet from GAMMA and other promotions participating. 2. Gemini Battle Festival - A round-robin style tag team tournament. The top two point winners face off at the conclusion of the first stage for the GBF title. Characters: The stereotypical GAMMA wrestler will be the following: Japanese, a junior heavyweight, 18-26 years of age, and have a shoot-style based offense. Of course there will be places of two to four heavyweights and one or two gaijin, but I'd like the majority of the GAMMA characters to fit this discription. GAMMA won't be solely shoot-style, as it serves more as a building point. I have no problem with wrestlers having elements from lucha, US Pro-Style, or anything else. Also, GAMMA is unafraid to bring the weird hair-dos and somewhat gimmicky wrestlers. Staff: -Ryota Kazama - Owner and president of GAMMA. As a youth he was trained in jujitsu and spent several years in vale tudo competitions in Brazil before going into business. Kazama is a fan of all battle arts and any Brazilian fighter he watches. He's a strong supporter of "borderless pro-wrestling," claiming that the fans deserve the best match ups possible. Kazama also serves as color man for the Battle Stations. -Homare Ota - Former junior reporter for the World Thai Kickboxing Federation, he took the job in GAMMA as the lead reporter. Ota also does play-by-play for the Battle Station broadcasts. -Uchu Suda - GAMMA's referee. He's competed in the featherweight class of the WTKF in the past along with several other fighting arts promotions. Suda looks enough like the popular jr. heavyweight Shoji Suzuki to wrestle as Shojicito Suzuki on some lucha libre undercard. -Tsutomu Yasumi - GAMMA's ring announcer Lastly: The final thing I will say is that remember that GAMMA is a game. Have fun with it. Put on an effort, but don't take winning and losing too seriously. If you feel that the outcome of a match should have been different, discuss it with me; I'm open to criticism, on anything. Just don't bitch. Occasionally, you'll be asked to job to elevate a main event feud. I won't make you job, but remember that if you do, there's a good chance the guy you jobbed to will return the favor not long down the line. Feel free to suggest anything you feel that could improve the fed. Also, I will be looking for guys to help out with some of the match writing for the Battle Stations, so any help with be appreciated and rewarded with a higher push. I'm not doing this whole thing for glory, but to have a good time. I hope you do the same.
