GAMMA (Sapporo, 2001) — Card

Tokyo Sports Wire: Arena Show, Differ Ariake

21 July 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
TOKYO SPORTS WIRE =-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= PROMOTION: Goukai Alpha Mixed Martial Arts EVENT: Arena Show VENUE: Differ Ariake – Tokyo (1,800) ATTENDENCE: 1689 DATE: July 21, 2001 REPORTING: Homare Ota GAMMA’s second rally did much better drawing wise, even though it was running against the CLLL Mexican Tag-Team Title Tournament across town, but that can be attributed to the heavy use of the hometown company, TOP, talent. On the card were three of the four TORA Wanizame Memorial Tournament semi-finalists, including the winner and TOP Jr. Heavyweight Champion himself, Shoji Suzuki, who fought in the main event. The other two were Takao Kimura and Mike Johnson, each in singles action. After the fiasco of GAMMA President and Owner Ryota Kazama being denied use of the GAMMA exhibition match between Kenji Kobe and Eiji Yatsu at the T-3 finale card by TOP President Fujiwara, and the subsequent slap in the face when Fujiwara said that TOP didn’t have the time to air the match at all, it came as a surprise to some when Kazama said that he hoped the working relationship to continue. In reality, Kazama has no real choice but to use TOP talent as the GAMMA roster is extremely small and the opening of CLLL Japan took away many of the freelancers that GAMMA had planned on using. However, as a return blow to the Tokyo Organization of Puroresu, President Kazama told TOP officials that no taping of the show would be done by either side, so TOP would have no way of airing the matches. This was proven false, however, when a small camera crew came out during the main event. After the rally Kazama claimed that the footage was asked for by Eiji Yatsu and Super Kitty! to be used as training material. Even though it had been announced days before, President Kazama came out before the show and announced that Daijiro Kuroyama and Takumi Mako had been allowed to drop the show so they could participate in the CLLL Mexican Tag-Team Title Tournament. The opening bout had been changed to a straight IKAJIN v. Kuwabara singles contest, while recently signed Kitty! Visual fighter Akitoshi Kubo had been picked up as a replacement partner for Eiji Yatsu. To make up for the unexpected changes CLLL Japan officials promised that a Mexican Tag-Team title defense would occur at next GAMMA rally, which would be a Fighting TV Samurai Battle Station taping. These announcements got a nice pop from the crowd, as they were looking forward to seeing the first title match in GAMMA, even though the actual taping would most likely occur in Sapporo or another city in Japan. Opening Bout: IKAJIN [CLLL Japan] (5’8, 225 lbs.) v. Noboru Kuwabara (5’10. 237 lbs.) Both fighters are coming off loses, a pair of them in IKAJIN’s case, and were fired up to prove themselves. While the Sex Dogs member, IKAJIN, is probably more known, most of the fans were behind the 43-year-old Kuwabara. While IKAJIN may have had a string of bad luck, Kuwabara’s age prevents him from bouncing back. Many of the fans in the very intimate Differ Ariake could see Kuwabara’s pure desire to capture a victory. Like his match with Ikeda at the debut rally, IKAJIN was part of another mat wrestling focused match with ground technician Kuwabara. However, unlike that contest, the fans were much more interested in the contest. There was a small section in the crowd that came up from Kuwabara’s nearby hometown to cheer on the “Yokohama Cobra” as he was known in J-Fight. The opening sequence saw IKAJIN and Kuwabara not messing around in a stand up section, instead taking it straight to mat, showing off probably every way to escape or reverse out of a wakigatame. The most interesting of which was IKAJIN reaching back with his free arm while in the move and pulling Kuwabara over into a backslide type move. IKAJIN then flipped Kuwabara over and slapped on the wakigatame once again. Kuwabara then somersaulted through the move and locked on a front facelock. He quickly got up and hit a DDT, and followed it up by locking on a rear naked choke while IKAJIN was in a sitting position. Before Kuwabara could fall back, IKAJIN grabbed Kuwabara’s ankle and started twisting. Kuwabara let go of the hold and IKAJIN did a reversal into an Inidian deathlock. IKAJIN then slapped on a ground octopus hold, while switching between using a clawhold while making sucking noises and grinding his elbow into Kuwabara’s side. Kuwabara tried reaching the ropes, but IKAJIN had the move on too tight. Kuwabara nailed several elbows to IKAJIN, causing him to break the hold slightly, giving Noboru an instant to escape and slap on a hizajujigatame. IKAJIN tried kicking Kuwabara off, but he was forced to try for the ropes. Right when he was about to grab them, Kuwabara got up and pulled IKJAIN back to the center of the ring and put on an anklelock. In a less dangerous move, IKAJIN was able to climb to his feet with Kuwabara still having the hold on. Kuwabara was able to take IKAJIN back down once, but the second time IKAJIN hit a koppou kick that crushed Kuwabara. IKAJIN then slapped on a jujigatame, but Kuwabara was able to get to a kneeling position, in a flash IKAJIN released the hold and got behind Kuwabara and pulled him up to hit an Iconoclasm. IKAJIN went for the Okankogatame, but before he could sit down on Kuwabara’s back, Kuwabara pulled IKAJIN forward and did a somersault, hooking IKJAIN’s legs so Kuwabara ended up going between them and ended up with IKAJIN on his stomach before him. It took about half a second for Kuwabara to get into the back mount, but it was almost half a minute before he was able to lock on the shinni no maki (cobra clutch). The fans popped big for this, and “ahh”ed when IKAJIN might be able to pull his way to the ropes. However, Kuwabara locked on a body scissors, keeping IKAJIN within his clutches, forcing the squidman to submit. Noboru Kuwabara (Cobra Clutch -> Submission, 8:13) IKAJIN The exhausted Kuwabara celebrated after the match and told the crowd this was his first step in a long road of his comeback. He said he knew the Sapporo Open would likely be almost half a year away, but promised he’d make a big showing in the tournament. Second Bout: Takao Kimura [TOP] (5’10, 194 lbs) v. Hisashi Kuroyama (6’1, 240 lbs.) Even though Takao Kimura had a big height and weight disadvantage to Hisashi Kuroyama, the former Club ACES member is in a severe slump and the TWMC Tournament Finalist was able to hold his own. The match opened up with Kimura trying to go head-to-head against Kuroyama in stand-up, but the former professional shoot-boxer was able to use his reach advantage to deliver hard punches to the “Hatchet Man’s” face and follow it up with vicious kicks to the body. Kimura tried to counter with some puroresu moves, going off the ropes, but he was met with an extremely stiff lariat that echoed through the arena. This scored the “Black Scourge” a knock down, but Kimura was able to make it back to his feet at the four count. Knowing that he was being overpowered, Kimura decided to take it to the mat, faking a kick and taking Kuroyama down with a double-leg, but Takao ended up in Kuroyama’s guard. Kimura started by trying to deliver punches, but Hisashi was able to hold Kimura in check. Kimura then took hold of Kuroyama’s arm and somersaulted through the guard, causing Kuroyama’s arm to be twisted. Kimura followed this escape by locking on a version of the short arm scissors and delivering elbows to Kuroyama’s head. Kuroyama tried to use his free arm to punch Kimura off, but Kimura grabbed hold of that arm and put on a keylock. With both of his arms in submission maneuvers, Kuroyama had no choice but to scoot his way to the ropes for a break. On the restart, Kuroyama tried to rush in and deliver a shotay, but Kimura countered by hitting a hot shot. With Kuroyama’s throat hurt, Kimura was quick to put on a standing rear naked choke. Kuroyama was quick to fall to a knee, soon after Kimura started delivering headbutts to the back of Kuroyama’s head. This did hurt Kuroyama some, but it weakened the rear naked choke, letting Kuroyama escape. Kimura tried rushing Kuroyama, but Kuroyama was able to whip Kimura into a corner where he laid in a rentless series of knee strikes followed by a belly-to-belly suplex. Suda started counting Kimura down, but Kuroyama went over to his opponent and brought him up. Kuroyama looked as if he was going for a Northern Lights Suplex, but Kimura countered with a DDT. Kimura then floated over into the back mount where he had the STF applied in a flash. Kuroyama tried pulling himself to the ropes, making it almost three-fourths of the way there, but he was forced to tap out. Takao Kimura (STF -> Submission, 8:03) Hisashi Kuroyama Semi-Main Event: Kentaro Satake (6’1, 246 lbs.) v. Mike Johnson [TOP] (6’, 223 lbs.) After the card, many people said they though that this match and the opening bout should have switched places, as many people grumbled about this match. In the opening minutes, this looked like it may be a good contest, but the final outcome brought boo’s from the crowd. The first five minutes were a blur was Satake came straight at Johnson, trying to nail him with a barrage of punches and kicks to the head, but Johnson was able to counter with speed to dodge about half of the strikes and use arm drags to take Satake down. Satake, however, was able to counter this with quick suplexes and grounding Johnson where he laid in knees from the side mount, and then punches from the mount. “The UK Destroyer” was able to escape this dangerous position by hooking Satake’s arms with his legs and taking down in a sunset flip type move into a hizajujigatame. Satake swam to the bottom rope to force the break. After returning to the vertical base, Satake nailed a strong lariat and followed it up with an inverted death valley driver. Johnson was able to make it back to his feet, but he was in sorry condition. Satake, likewise, was already tired from his light-speed attack. What followed was two minutes the combatants circling each other, neither man trying to do anything offensive. Suda warned each fighter that if they didn’t do anything soon, he’d throw the match out. When they didn’t, Suda called for the bell and declared the match a no contest. Kentaro Satake (No Contest, 6:47) Mike Johnson Main Event: Shoji Suzuki [TOP] (5’6, 205 lbs.) & Hayato Ikeda (6’0, 225 lbs.) v. Eiji Yatsu (5’9, 207 lbs.) & Akitoshi Kubo (5’7, 177 lbs.) Hayato Ikeda was introduced first, soon followed by Shoji Suzuki, who came to the ring wearing his TOP Jr. Heavyweight Championship belt. Suzuki received the most applauds of all the wrestlers and politely waved to the crowd and slapped hands with several fans at ringside. He didn’t look aggressive (That look is very rare for Suzuki), but he was very focused. Yatsu and Kubo came to the ring together. They were kind to the fans, but they made several gestures to Suzuki, motioning that Yatsu was the man to who deserves the first title shot. Yatsu told Kubo to step out of the ring, as he wanted to be the man to start for their team. After a short discussion, Ikeda went to the outside, letting Suzuki begin the match. Yatsu acted as though he’d changed his mind and was going to let Kubo begin the match, but after a pop from the crowd he stepped back in. When Suda called for the bell, both Suzuki and Yatsu came out of their corners and met in the center of the ring. After a short stare down, Suzuki exploded with a shotay palm strike that landed square on Yatsu’s chin. A second shotay hit, but Suzuki’s jumping spin kick didn’t, and Yatsu was able to hit several front crossing kicks to Suzuki’s midsection. Yatsu tried closing the distance and laying in some knees, but Suzuki countered with a couple elbows. Shoji attempted another shotay, but Yatsu jumped up and hit a la Majistral into a jujigatame. Before Eiji could start wrenching on it, Suzuki got to his knees and started hitting Yatsu with punches with his free arm. Yatsu gave up on locking on the hold and tried to roll away, but Suzuki took advantage of the shown back and put on a wakigatame. After a couple seconds in the hold, Yatsu did a forward roll out and onto his feet, but Suzuki kept hold of Yatsu’s arm and hit a la Majistral into a jujigatame of his own. Yatsu continued to move around, and he was able to trap Suzuki’s foot with one arm. Yatsu quickly started twisting, causing Suzuki to break the hold. Eiji got to his feet and in a flash put on the Muscular Clutch (Hands free standing Indian Deathlock). The fans applauded not only for Yatsu’s muscular pose, but also for the great grappling sequence. After being trapped in the hold for around ten seconds, Ikeda came in and drilled Yatsu with a kick to the face. Yatsu broke the hold, and both Yatsu and Suzuki were able to tag out. Ikeda and Kubo each sprinted from their corners and met in the center of the ring, each man attempting to hit the other with punches. For about a minute the crowd saw a mini-boxing exhibition, as both men tried to use classic boxing technique to bust up the other man’s face. Also like classic boxing, none of the punches landed, which received a few grumbles from the crowd. Trying to surprise Ikeda, Kubo attempted his jumping spinning back elbow, but Ikeda scored a takedown into the back mount. Ikeda tried to put on a rear naked choke, but when he reached his arm across, Kubo grabbed hold of it and used old wrestling technique to reverse into a hammerlock. In a flash Akitoshi had a short arm scissors applied, and quicker than that added an armbar to complete the Rings of Saturn. With the match in jeopardy, Suzuki entered the ring and blasted Kubo with a kick to the flash, sending the tiny Jr. Heavyweight halfway across the ring. Ikeda capitalized, hitting a sumiotoshi (head and arm throw) and uranage (back drop driver) in quick succession. Ikeda then tagged it Suzuki and put Kubo in position to take a Suzuki moonsault, which the TOP Jr. Heavyweight Champion hit gracefully. Suzuki delivered a couple short shotays to Kubo’s head then tagged Ikeda back in. Suzuki held Kubo up to take a dropkick, but Kubo was able to move out of the way, and Ikeda flew by. As Suda got Suzuki to leave the ring, Kubo hit an Inferno Kick to Ikeda. Kubo then applied an inverted facelock, which he wrenched for several seconds before nailing an inverted facelock brainbuster to a big pop from the crowd. Ikeda was able to reach his feet at eight, but he had to deal with Yatsu, who just tagged in. Yatsu came in and hit several knee strikes before using a Gannosuke Clutch to take Ikeda down, which Yatsu quickly converted into a hizajujigatame. Ikeda was able to make it to the ropes, and when Yatsu attempted a Tartarkin Roll, but Ikeda countered by slamming his knee into Yatsu’s face. Ikeda hit several more knee drops before brining Yatsu up. Ikeda then jumped up, wrapped his legs around Eiji’s body, and took him down into the guard where he applied a Kimura shoulder lock. Yatsu was able to lay in punches to Hayato’s body as he fought his way into the half guard and finally the full mount. Yatsu laid in several punches before grabbing Ikeda’s arm and swinging over into a jujigatame. Ikeda looked like he may tap, but Suzuki came in and dropped a big knee across Yatsu’s face, causing him to the break the hold. Ikeda was up first and hit a uraken, which he followed up with an exploder. Ikeda tagged out to Suzuki, who went right back to work on Yatsu. Suzuki hit him with several shotay palm strikes before whipping Yatsu into the corner, hitting a leg lariat, and flipping out of the ring. As Yatsu stumbled out of the corner, Suzuki flew to the top turnbuckle and hit a dropkick to the back of the head. Yatsu went down hard, and Suda started the count, but Yatsu was able to reach his feet at six. After taking a few token punches from Yatsu, Suzuki hit two consecutive German suplexes. On the third, Yatsu was able to counter with a forward roll into a hizajujigatame, but Suzuki kicked at him several times and locked on a hizajujigatame of his own. Kubo was forced to come in and break it up by stomping on Suzuki several times. Suzuki attempted to hit an exploder, but Yatsu blocked and hit an uranage. Yatsu then quickly hit his Flaming Splash (Running Shooting Star Press). With his ribs hurt, Suzuki went into a fetal-type position, and Suda began the count. Suzuki noticed this and jumped to his feet at five, only to be met with several knee strikes to the temple. Yatsu then pushed Suzuki towards Yatsu’s corner and tagged in Kubo. While Yatsu held Suzuki in a standing double armbar, Kubo laid in a series of punches to Suzuki’s ribs. When Suda ordered Yatsu out, Kubo hit a reverse Russian legsweep and locked on a Stretch Plum, further working on the original Jet Jaguar’s ribs. Ikeda came in a second time to make the save, laying in a pair of headbutts to the young Steel Wind Dojo trainee. Kubo broke the hold and tagged out to Yatsu. Similar to before, Akitoshi held Suzuki while Yatsu hit several kicks. After Kubo left the ring, Yatsu yelled to the crowd before jumping up for the Yatsu Special (Frankensteiner into a Triangle Choke), but Suzuki was able to counter with a variation of the Spiral Bomb (Spinning Lygerbomb). Suda begun counting down Yatsu and Suzuki rolled to his corner and tagged out to Ikeda. When Yatsu didn’t move at five, the crowd started yelling for him to get up, which he was able to do at eight. Ikeda was on full offense, laying in vicious muay thai style kicks in between hitting the ipponzei (shoulder throw), osotogeri (major outer reaping throw), and kuruma gaeshi (wheel throw). Ikeda tagged Suzuki back in to apparently finish Yatsu off. That appeared to happen when Suzuki hit a brutal running shotay, but Yatsu was able to climb the ropes and make it to his feet at eight. Suzuki attempted a second running shotay, but Yatsu avoided it and drilled the TOP Jr. Heavyweight Champion with a Sky Striker (Knee led leg lariat). Suzuki looked down and out, but most of the fans didn’t believe he wouldn’t be able to reach his feet until he didn’t move around the six count. Seeing this, Ikeda jumped off the ring apron and sprinted over to where Suzuki was to yell for him to get up. At eight, Suzuki made it to a knee, and at nine he was able to get to his feet to a big pop. Yatsu attempted to lock on a standing katahajime, but Suzuki hit several back elbows to Eiji’s head. Suzuki then reversed quickly and put on a hammerlock and half nelson. As Shoji started to lift Yatsu up for the Suzuki Speical, Yatsu tried a forward roll, but Suzuki used the strength within him to haul Yatsu up and hit the move. Eiji landed directly on the back of his head to a big “oh” from the crowd followed by a pop for Suzuki. Suda started the count, but it appeared Yatsu would easily be able to make it back to his feet. At five he pulled himself to the ring ropes. At six Yatsu grabbed hold of the ropes and pulled himself to a knee. At seven he pulled himself onto both feet, but he was still holding on tight to the ropes. At eight Eiji let go of the ropes, attempting to stand without assistance and he crumbled to the ground. Suda finished he his count to a huge pop from the crowd. Shoji Suzuki & Hayato Ikeda (Suzuki over Yatsu, Suzuki Special -> KO, 15:12) Eiji Yatsu & Akitoshi Kubo After the match, Suzuki helped Yatsu up, and shook hands with both of his opponents. Yatsu was fairly humble to Suzuki, while Kubo continued to gesture that Yatsu would be the man able to take Suzuki down. After Suzuki took back his belt, he started the crowd in a “GAMMA” chant and was a recipient of a large round of applauds. Another solid rally by GAMMA. With all the squabbling between Fujiwara and Kazama it’ll be interesting to see if Suzuki-Yatsu II ever occurs, but it can be easily determined that if a Suzuki-Yatsu match occurred, it would be able to draw a significant crowd. Again, the thought of having a solid roster haunts GAMMA. Very few combatants have contracts with the company and most of work for at least one other promotion. Before tours can even be thought of, Kazama must sign a number of GAMMA-only combatants, or his dream of running a puroresu company may die.
