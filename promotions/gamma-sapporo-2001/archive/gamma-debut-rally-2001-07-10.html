GAMMA (Sapporo, 2001) — Card

Tokyo Sports Wire: Inaugural Show, Teisen Hall

10 July 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
TOKYO SPORTS WIRE =-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= PROMOTION: Goukai Alpha Mixed Martial Arts EVENT: Inaugural Show VENUE: Teisen Hall – Sapporo (1,200 seats) ATTENDENCE: 1078 DATE: July 10, 2001 REPORTING: Homare Ota Even with a main event including two of the top junior heavyweight stars in Nippon, not surprisingly GAMMA wasn’t able to draw a sell-out for its debut rally. While Yatsu and Kobe do have some drawing power, most fans are hesitant to buy into the new promotion that says it’ll offer a unique, and probably non-main stream, style. President Kazama is using this show and the second GAMMA show, that’ll include TOP talent, to educate the fans on what GAMMA will offer. Ryota Kazama opened the card thanking all the fans that choose to attend and promised them that GAMMA would stay very loyal to the city. He then gave the basic rules of the match. Matches will have a 20-minute time limit and can be won only by knockout, technical knockout, disqualification, or submission. All strikes, besides those to the eyes and groin, throws, body attacks, and submission holds on anything but less than three fingers are legal. If a combatant commits one of the illegal attacks, they’ll receive a warning. After that is a DQ. President Kazama expressed his hope the combatants wouldn’t attempt these attacks, as he hopes all matches will have clean, decisive finishes. Before leaving the ring, President Kazama once again thanked the fans and hoped they’d enjoy the show and come to see GAMMA on a future date. Opening Bout: Ashwin Seko (6’5, 250 lbs.) w/Makoto Iwase v. Hisashi Kuroyama (6’1, 250 lbs.) The first ever contest in GAMMA pitted too combatants with plenty of accolades to their names. Seko is a two time former OKN (Kickboxing Organization of the Netherlands) Heavyweight champion while Kuroyama won the 1998 and 1999 Mega World Battle Leagues in J-Fight. Since entering puroresu, Kuroyama has won the now defunct Oita Pro Wrestling Tag-Team titles as one half of Club ACES. Seko is a pure kickboxer, wearing the specified attire for that sport (gloves and shorts.). Kuroyama himself has competed in shootboxing, but has since become more of a complete fighter. The contest itself was fairly sloppy, but was exciting for what it was. The match opened up with both combatants taking a kickboxing stance and circling each other. Iwase started yelling at Seko, something he won’t stop doing the entire match. Kuroyama was the first to attempt anything by throwing a low kick to the thigh of the large Dutchman. Seko retaliated with a middle kick to Kuroyama’s side, and neither man looked hurt. Kuroyama then exploded with two quick kicks to the thigh and then a third to the head, forcing Seko back. A few elbowsmashes had Ashwin into the corner where Hisashi started to lay in a relentless series of knees to the midsection and legs of Seko. With a big jab to Kuroyama’s head, Seko turned the tables and laid in several body kicks to Kuroyama followed by a jab, uppercut, left hook combination. Kuroyama slumped in the corner, and referee Uchu Suda pushed Seko back and started the count on Kuroyama. Hisashi was up at 7 and went towards Ashwin in the center of the ring, this time taking a grapplers’ stance. Nothing happened for nearly half a minute, only circling, until Seko threw a kick, which Kuroyama caught. Hisashi quickly delivered a dragon screw and locked on a hizajujigatame to a big crowd pop. Seko swam to the ropes to cause the break. When both combatants reached their feet, Kuroyama faked a kick, then a shoot, and went behind Seko to hit a huge release German suplex. Seko looked hurt, but was able to make it to a vertical base at the 8 count to a big pop. Kuroyama moved in to try a belly-to-belly suplex, but Seko pushed him off, delivered a jumping knee, a jab, an uppercut, a second jab, and then a straight punch caused Kuroyama’s knees to buckle. As soon as Hisashi hit the mat, Seko was celebrating, as he knew Kuroyama wouldn’t be able to answer the count. Ashwin Seko (Punch -> KO, 5:43) Hisashi Kuroyama Second Bout: Kentaro Satake (6’1, 246 lbs.) v. Noboru Kuwabara (5’10, 237 lbs.) This match pitted the veteran, 43-year-old Kuwabara, the winner of the J-Fight Mega World Battle League, against Satake, a young man who was trained by some of the best in MMA, the Gracies, but his past is quite checkered. Satake was introduced first and from the moment he entered the ring he was pacing liked a caged animal. As soon as Suda called for the bell, Satake rushed Kuwabara with a spear. Kentaro attempted to get into the mount, but Kuwabara kept his cool and put Satake into the guard. Satake refused to back down and tried to rain in the punches, but Kuwabara was able to keep Satake away with his legs and block most of the punches. For nearly half a minute Satake attempted to just go straight at Kuwabara with punches, but was unable to do any punches. On one, Kuwabara let Satake throw his entire body weight forward. Noboru rolled with Satake’s forward motion, coming up on top in the mount position. Satake had a look of anger and surprise on his face. He tried to buck Kuwabara off, but Kuwabara held position and grabbed Satake’s arm, looking for a jujigatame. Kentaro knew enough to grip his hands together, but Kuwabara was able to lock on a keylock. Satake immediately released his hands, and it was a half second before Kuwabara swung himself over into a jujigatame. Satake at first tried to get himself up and deliver punches, but Kuwabara just extended Kentaro’s arm further, forcing Satake to go for the break. All this just seemed to anger Satake more. Kentaro quickly moved towards Kuwabara, who faked a double leg takedown and went behind and locked on a waistlock. Satake quickly hit several back elbows to Kuwabara’s head, who released the hold. Satake then hit a pair of swift kicks to the head before hitting a Satake Slam (Angle Slam). Satake continued his attack by hitting a T-Bone suplex. Satake tried to continue the assault, but Suda pulled him off and sent Kentaro to his corner before starting a ten-count on Noboru. Kuwabara was able to answer at 8, which may have been a mistake as Satake came full force at him, nailing a series of strikes that backed Kuwabara into the corner. A big right hook looked to knockout Kuwabara, but Kentaro nailed a Tazzmission Suplex. Suda immediately ended the fight, saying that Kuwabara wouldn’t be able to continue. Kentaro Satake (Tazzmission Suplex -> TKO, 6:13) Noboru Kuwabara Most fans didn’t know how to react to Satake. While he did receive some applauds, many where dumbfounded at how he relentlessly attacked Kuwabara in the final moments of the match. Semi-Main Event: Hayato Ikeda (6’0, 225 lbs.) v. IKAJIN (5’8, 225 lbs.) Ikeda was coming off a semi-finals loss to Shoji Suzuki in the TORA Wanizame Memorial Cup on Sunday, while IKAJIN was the complete opposite, coming off a win in CLLL Japan’s debut card, forcing Daijiro Kuroyama to submit in a six-man match. Ikeda was one of the first wrestlers to sign on with GAMMA, and President Kazama has high hopes for him, but he wrestled somewhat of an uninspired match tonight. This match was very mat-based, only picking up in pace during the last few minutes. The fans reaction was mixed. Some enjoyed the mat wrestling, while others were bored with it. The match opened up with Ikeda and IKAJIN meeting in the center of the ring. Ikeda started to circle left and IKAJIN followed suit. Quickly, Ikeda switched and moved right, grabbing IKAJIN’s leg and pulling him down. However, as Hayota went for position to lock on a hold, IKAJIN spun himself around on the mat and kicked Ikeda off. IKAJIN quickly got back to his feet and sidestepped a Ikeda attempt at a double leg. IKAJIN then threw himself onto Ikeda’s back, and “road” Ikeda for about half a minute before grabbing Ikeda’s arm and locking on a wakigatame. Though the masked IKAJIN is a talented grappler, he was very sloppy here, not putting his full weight into it. Ikeda was able to slide his body around and jump into the full mount position. IKAJIN covered up form the shotay palm strikes. Ikeda then quickly grabbed IKAJIN’s arm and swung around into a jujigatame. Before Ikeda could fully grab hold and extend the arm, IKAJIN was able to get to a vertical base. Ikeda still tried to lock the hold on, but IKAJIN flipped Ikeda onto his back, forcing Ikeda to break the hold. IKAJIN spitefully kicked Ikeda in the kidneys a few times before locking on a half-crab. After about ten seconds of attempting to counter the hold, Ikeda decided to cause the rope break. Ikeda came up holding his back and noticed just in time the IKAJIN kick that was coming right for his head. Ikeda blocked it and caught the second attempt. Hayato delivered a super quick dragon screw and locked on an akiresukengatame (Achilles Tendon Hold). Ikeda then transitioned it into a hizajujigatame, which got the big pop from the crowd being it’s one of his finishing moves. All IKAJIN was able to do was grab Ikeda’s foot and start twisting, which seemed to do the job. Ikeda released the hizajujigatame and IKAJIN was able to get up and jump into a side mount. There he locked on clawhold and made loud sucking noises (As he thinks he’s squid-like.) to a somewhat comedy pop. In a flash, IKAJIN moved into a octopus hold and started to grind his elbow into Ikeda’s side, who had no choice but to pull himself to the ropes. In a huge mistake, the Sex Dogs member started whooing and gyrating his hips for the crowd, leaving him wide open for a kick to the head and an uranage. IKAJIN was down until he reached his feet at 5, at which point Ikeda nailed several heavy punches and followed it up with a fisherman’s neckbreaker that crushed IKAJIN. It wasn’t until the 8 count that he was able to reach his feet. Ikeda jumped at the apparently punch drunk IKAJIN for his forward rolling hizajujigatame, but IKAJIN countered with an inverted lygerbomb. Quickly IKAJIN hit his Miracle Ecstasy (Nowada Bomb), but Ikeda was able to make it to his feet at 8. IKAJIN felt as if the win was in his grasp as he whooed to the crowd and hit a cross-arms Iconoclasm. As IKAJIN was about to lock on the Omankogatame (A straight-jacket submission hold), Ikeda spun himself around onto his back and kicked IKAJIN back with both feet. Ikeda jumped to his feet and delivered a huge running jumping knee strike to a loud “oh” from the crowd that put IKAJIN down for the count. Hayato Ikeda (running jumping knee strike -> KO, 10:16) IKAJIN Main Event: Keiji Kobe (5’10, 217 lbs.) & Daijiro Kuroyama (5’7, 183 lbs.) v. Eiji Yatsu (5’9, 207 lbs.) & Takumi Mako (5’9, 164 lbs.) While some fans grumbled that this wasn’t strictly a Kobe-Yatsu one-on-one contest, President Kazama knew that now wouldn’t be a good time to give away probably the biggest drawing match GAMMA can currently put together. Two days ago at the TOP Tribute To TORA series ender, Kobe and Yatsu went to a 10-minute draw that neither man was happy with. The exhibition match was intended to bring attention to the young company, but it didn’t have much effect on drawing a crowd since the fans in Sapporo may have to wait several weeks to see the match on TVT, if ever. As noted before, Kuroyama was coming off of a loss to IKAJIN on Sunday, so his confidence level was extremely low. The match started off with Kuroyama and Mako matching up in a nearly minute long sequence with Mako throwing an absurd amount of punches, landing maybe half, and Kuroyama retaliating with his signature blistering kicks that rocked the scrawny Mako back and forth. Daijiro was able to back his opponent into the corner where hit a quick koppou kick followed by a handspring elbow. Kuroyama quickly tagged in Kobe, leaving Mako in one of the neutral corners. Kobe attempted to come in with a springboard dropkick, but Mako slipped out of the way and tagged in Yatsu. The fans gave a good pop for the match-up. Eiji was in no hurry and didn’t rush Kobe, even though the opportunity to was there. He motioned for Keiji to come towards the center of the ring. Like the Mako-Kuroyama match-up before, Yatsu and Kobe started with striking, each trying to kick the other’s head off. After a particularly nasty kick, Kobe hit a suisha otoshi (watermill suplex) to take Yatsu down where he locked on a manjigatame (or octopus hold). Yatsu was able to reach the ropes, but a dropkick to the face later Kobe locked on a half crab in the middle of ring. Yatsu first attempted to pull himself to the ropes, but Kobe repeatedly pulled him away, so he counter the hold by putting on an anklelock, which forced Keiji to break the half crab. Yatsu quickly got to his feet, tripped Kobe down, and locked on the Muscular Clutch (Somewhat of a hands free standing Indian deathlock.). While Yatsu was expecting cheers when he flexed his muscles for the crowd mostly he got a negative reaction from the pro-Kobe crowed (Keiji Kobe is from just outside Sapporo.). Starting from here, Yatsu and Mako controlled Kobe decently well for several minutes, using plenty of tags and double team and assisted moves, starting with a diving double footstomp by Mako on Kobe while Yatsu still had the Muscular Clutch on. Others included a double high kick to the head, a Mako Michinoku Diver II followed by a Yatsu Flaming Splash (Running Shooting Star Press), and a dropkick assisted Tiger Suplex by Mako that which really got the fans going to get Kobe up at the 8 count. Kobe was able to escape a dangerous position however, when Yatsu tried to bring him up into a Gory Special powerbomb-style, and Kobe turned it into a hurricanrana. Keiji quickly made the tag and Kuroyama came in using his super stiff kicks. Daijiro then hit a German suplex and a Northern Lights suplex in quick succession to set up his Kuroyama’s Special Lock. Yatsu looked nearly out, and Mako was forced to come in and break the hold with a series of punches. After Suda escorted Mako out, Kuroyama and Kobe stayed on offense, each hitting a move or two and tagging out to the guy on the outside who’d do the same. Kuroyama hit a belly-to-belly suplex, a backdrop suplex, and locked on a jujigatame for a short time before Yatsu was able to cause the break. Keiji was more focused on arm, just pounding and wrenching on it. After several minutes, Kobe got Eiji up into the Back-to-Back Elevated Hammerlock. The fans popped loud for the Kobe finishing move, about a third of whom heard that Kobe might have won their contest two days ago with the hold if the time limit wasn’t winding down. Takumi again had to make the save, this time using a Yakazu kick to Keiji’s head. Kobe let go of the move and fell to the ground and quickly made the tag. While Suda ordered Mako to stay out of the ring, Kuroyama came in, hit a snap suplex, brought Yatsu up, and started yelling for his rotating-style sheer-drop brainbuster. Seeing this, Mako pushed the referee out of the way and hit a devastating running uppercut on Daijiro. As Mako did his trademark “I’m a little teapot” dance, Kobe came in with a springboard dorpkick, hitting it unlike before, which knocked Mako to the outside. Kobe then hit a springboard plancha, which took a lot out of both men, who started brawling on the floor. In ring, Kuroyama was the first to make it to his feet, but the battered Yatsu was also able to make it to the vertical base. Kuroyama hit some more of his cringe bringing kicks before nailing a backdrop suplex. Again, Daijiro brought Yatsu up for his rotating-style sheer-drop brainbuster, but Eiji was able to block the hold twice and then grabbed Kuroyama’s head and delivered a series of knee strikes to the temple. Yatsu went off the ropes to gain speed, ducked a Kuroyama leg lariat, and on the rebound hit a Sky Striker (Leg lariat with the knee making impact) to a big crowd pop. Kuroyama made it to his feet at 8 while Kobe screamed for him on the outside. It looked like Yatsu was going to be able to take full control, but Kuroyama nailed two big kicks. Yatsu blocked the third however and in a flash performed the Yatsu Special (Frankensteiner into a triangle choke.) Kobe was about to come in from the outside to make the save, but Mako held him back long enough so Kuroyama was forced to tap out. Eiji Yatsu & Takumi Mako (Yatsu over Kuroyama, Yatsu Special -> Submission, 16:51) Keiji Kobe & Daijiro Kuroyama After the match Yatsu and Kobe stared each other down before Kobe and Kuroyama left the ringside area. Down the entranceway, Kuroyama could be seen apologizing to Kobe for letting him down. Yatsu and Mako celebrated in ring, Yatsu climbing the turnbuckles and giving his old powerfist to the crowd and Mako doing his “I’m a little teapot” dance uninterrupted. Overall, the show was solid. Nothing overly spectacular happened, but a good rally put on by the new promotion. After the card, President Kazama mention how he is planning a second show for later in the month and confirmed reports that TOP talnet would make an appearance. He’d officially announce the card in a few days, but noted how the original card may not be what occurs at GAMMA’s second show. --- Peter E-mail: [detail withheld] AIM: [detail withheld] GAMMA Group: http://groups.yahoo.com/group/gammapuroresu
