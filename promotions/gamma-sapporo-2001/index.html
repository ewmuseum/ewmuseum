Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

GAMMA opened in Sapporo in the summer of 2001, the creation of Ryota Kazama, a businessman and former vale tudo competitor who billed his new promotion as a shoot-style-leaning puroresu league somewhere between BattlARTs and FMW. Matches were decided by knockout, technical knockout, submission or disqualification rather than by pinfall, and its owner opened his company's trophy case with a set of tag-team titles bought from a closed rival, the Gunryo Pro, ahead of ever awarding a championship of its own devising.

Its Players' Guide asked handlers for two kinds of writing, a shorter flash and a fuller match strategy, planned an annual singles tournament in its home city and a round-robin tag tournament for the summer, and staffed its broadcasts with a reporter, a referee and a ring announcer of its own invention. Beneath the promotion's ambitions sat a persistent problem: its signed roster never grew past a handful of names.

Its inaugural rally at Teisen Hall on 10 July 2001 leaned on that thin roster's best angle, a tag match reuniting two rivals fresh off a run in another promotion, and closed with a submission win in front of just over a thousand fans. Its second show, an arena date in Tokyo eleven days later, leaned even harder on borrowed talent, headlined by a visiting champion from a partner Tokyo promotion and closing on a knockout in the promotion's biggest match to date.

A title match between that visiting champion and the promotion's own top native draw was announced for a third show at the start of August 2001, tied to a tag-team title defense on loan from yet another partner company. The held record ends there, mid-announcement, with no result, no closing statement and no further word of the promotion in any form.
