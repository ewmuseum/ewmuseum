Application (finally!) <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> 1. What is ACW? (fictionally) - Of course, ACW is the acronym for Atlantic Coast Wrestling. ACW is a very small, independent promotion that is based on the East Coast, usually states nearby the Atlantic Coast (duh). ACW goes by one rule when it comes to most things: there are no rules. Atlantic Coast Wrestling combines the beautiful footwork of aerial/technical/lucha styles with the gritty, no-nonsense, violent approach of garbage (hardcore) wrestling. Expect to see a good wrestling match... as well as some blood. 2. What is ACW? (realistically) - Well, ACW is an e-fed. Simple as that. Um...I don't think there's anything more to say about that. :) <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> 2. Who runs ACW? 1.1. Fictionally James Cross runs ACW. He's pretty well-to-do and finances all operations with the help of his staff. They not only help him make booking decisions, but keep him in line. The guy's got a bad temper... and he doesn't take very well to criticism at times either. Real nice guy, though. 1.2. Realistically I, Jimmy T., run ACW. I've been involved in this "great sport" of e-w for around 4-5 years. I've tried my hand at 2 e-feds, both having moderate success. Now I've started this one, obviously. By the time you're reading this, ACW shouldn't be considered a "fledgling e-fed" anymore. :) Oh, yeah: I'm a nice guy. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> 3. Some important stuff 1.1. Roleplaying To say the least, this is an absolute _MUST_ in ACW. If you're booked for a match -- jobber or not -- your job is to ROLEPLAY. It makes a good impression of yourself on me and the other members of the fed. So, unless you have a valid excuse...roleplay at least once or twice a week. As far as the length of roleplays go, I don't want friggin' _War & Peace_ or _Moby Dick_, simple as that. Short, sweet, and straight-to-the-point gets you good points...however, in most cases, it is quality tends to beat the living shit out of quantity. Uh...yeah. :) Anyway, roleplays should be logical. By that, I mean if the event is in New York City, don't write your roleplay from, say, Manchester, England. That's just ridiculous, unless it's a debut roleplay. 1.2. Formatting Yes, Atlantic Coast Wrestling DOES have a specific format in writing roleplays, in-rings, etc.. Here's what the structure of your roleplays should look like...well, at least while you're writing promos for the card itself. Use squared brackets ( [ ] ) and use initials for your character's names. If your character has one name, use one letter. Example: [Tommy Grayson runs on-screen.] TG: Holy crap! [Grayson runs off.] Also, when you do a pause, use just three periods. "..." not "........" I know there are some of you who think that adds extra emphasis or some shit like that, but...no. That doesn't fly with me. :) 1.3. Strats Strats -- although optional -- are VERY appreciated and can help the matchwriter (that would be me...or someone else in some cases) make his job THAT MUCH MORE EASIER. Once again, I say that you don't NEED to write a strat...but it's APPRECIATED. :) As far as they're concerned, strats don't need to be long, winded epics as well. I mean, just a summary of several things: a. Mindset b. Gameplan (this should be the biggest part of the strat) c. Spots (specific things you'd like your character to do) d. Ending (win, loss, etc.; how you'd like it to happen) e. Entrance 3.4. Angles That's the thing about ACW- angles. Angles and feuds are probably one of the biggest parts of the structure of ACW. Angles are the best thing, yo. You should work with someone else in the fed with an angle...or if you don't know what to do, just throw what you've got in mind at me and I'll help you out. I'm very approachable. Just IM me, e-mail me, catch me on mIRC, whatever. Easy to reach. Also, keep in mind that I can't have two of the same type angles going on at the same time. 3.5. Other Yeap...the "Other" section. Um, basically, I guess I should say volunteers for matchwriting would be GREATLY appreciated. The singles roster of ACW, at a maximum, will be 20-25, minimum will be 15-20. The tag team roster, maximum, will be 8-10. Minimum will be 5. I'd also like a good heel:face ratio, which will be a factor in acceptance. This'll be explained later. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> 4. ACW Shows 1.1. ACW TV Currently ACW's flagship show. No fancy-shmancy play-on-words title, no big-ass screen or tron, no huge stage aesthetics, no big arenas. Not only that, ACW TV isn't live. Each ACW TV show will be held from a small venue. Things along the lines of small sports arenas, to high school gymnasiums, to nightclubs (large enough ones), to beaches, and yes, even to the ACW Arena- the revamped, rebuilt, redecorated former strip club. ACW TV currently airs on FOX Sports Network affiliates as well as America One affiliates. Usually, 3-5 matches will be held on an ACW TV program. 1.2. Roleplay Packet Self-explanatory. 1.3. Wired! Hosted by Wicked Voodoo commentator Johnny Marks, who's finally found a job. Basically, it's a magazine-type program discussing the latest happenings in ACW. 1.4. Supercards <Ahem> It's a SUPERCARD not a Goddamn PAY-PER-VIEW, okay? :) After all, we ARE independent and stuff...and on that note, ACW supercards are distributed through videotape or DVD at your local video store. ACW will have a supercard every one to two months, depending on how the schedule is set. There is no set number as to how many matches will be held on a supercard. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> 5. Personalities 1.1. President/CEO James Cross Cross is one of the more mysterious individuals in Atlantic Coast Wrestling, as he rarely appears on-screen. However, he is known to make statements on behalf of ACW, which sometimes ends up in disaster. Case in point, the war of words between him and UWF President Sarah Reed. Cross is a big mark for the old school as well as the new school and basically meshes both styles in ACW. No telling if he's a good guy or a bad guy. 1.2. Commissioner/Creative Director Stephen Lowery Lowery is the rotund on-screen boss of ACW. Lowery, truthfully, is intimidated by Mr. Cross...however, that doesn't really come across on- screen, where Lowery can pose all he wants. He's looking for trouble, basically. Lowery is also responsible for bringing in alot of the ACW talent. 1.3. Commentators & Interviewers a. Chris Lansberry Lansberry is the face commentator. He's a huge mark for the sport for years and years since he was a child. Chris was hired by Cross for his enthusiasm. Lansberry is your typical yuppie-looking guy, sporting some boss-looking bidness suits and, er, a not exactly full head of hair a'la George Costanza from Seinfeld. b. Josh Davis Ah. The Johnny Rotten of heel commentators. Davis was a former interviewer for the now-defunct LVPWA until he became disgrunted because he was never used. Nowadays, the blood red haired (dyed obviously), band t-shirt wearing Davis sits alongside Chris Lansberry and the beautiful Kennedy Michelle Chambers at the announcing booth. c. Kennedy Michelle Chambers The beautiful former blond bombshell commentator of International Championship Wrestling. Absolutely stunning from head-to-toe. Sometimes smart, sometimes ditzy...but you'll still get caught up in her charm. d. Seth Hawkins The younger brother of head official Rodney Hawkins. He's the Hawaiian shirt-wearing guru of backstage interviewing...or something. He'll interview your ass, dammit. He'll do it! 1.4. Referees a. Head Referee: Rodney "Squirrel" Hawkins The by-the-books tough guy. Can't get a damn thing by the bastard. He got the nickname "Squirrel" because he runs around like one and is ALWAYS there to make the count unless he's distracted. b. "Bulldog" Mark Morgan A former wrestler. He usually does the tag team matches, hardcore matches, and <insert number here>-way dances. He takes no guff from anyone. c. Jake Madison The most lenient of the referees. He'll let almost anything slip by, from low blows to eye rakes, etc.. However, he'll use his common sense when he thinks things go too far. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> 6. Titles 1.1. Atlantic Coast Championship The big kahuna of ACW titles. If you win this one, you're on top. You're the top kick. You're the big dog. Simple as that. Expect the challengers to be flying at you. 1.2. Television Championship You're second best if you're holding this title...and I'm not just saying that. Holding on to the TV Title is a tall order; it's required to be defended at least every one to two weeks. Holding it for any prolonged length of time is a testament to your effort. 1.3. Tag Team Championship If you're a tag team and you get this title, you're the king of tag teams. At least in ACW. :) <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> 7. Joining - Atlantic Coast Wrestling is NOT the place for egos and the such. If you're just looking for wins, this isn't the place for you. - Laziness won't be tolerated. If I don't see anything from you for 3 to 4 weeks, you're off the roster. There'll be no e-mail informing you either, because I'm not going to waste my time trying to think of a cordial way to say, "you suck. I'm kicking your ass out. Goodbye." - No shoots, unless you ask permission. A thinly-veiled shoot is fine in my book, but just try to inform me about it. - If you don't get along with any other members of the fed...I'm simply not taking action unless you feel extremely alienated or vice versa. - Applications will be looked over with extreme prejudice; in other words, I'll consider the things such as grammar, logic (weight, moves, etc.), gimmick, type of wrestler, and alignment. And YES, there WILL be times where I will consider even WHO THE HANDLER IS! It's EXTREMELY, EXTREMELY, EXTREMELY, EXTREMELY, EXTREMELY, and VERY HIGHLY UNLIKELY that I will REJECT an application based on who the handler is. Sort of a one in a million thing, if you will. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> 7.
