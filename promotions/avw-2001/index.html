Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Appalachian Valley Wrestling opened in January 2001 as a self-described regional fed, its weekly Appalachian Extreme broadcast staged in the AVW Arena in Bowling Green, Kentucky, before crowds of a few thousand. It rose from a failed merger: its two co-presidents had folded their earlier fed, XWW, into a larger one that never delivered a card, and they started over with a set roster, an apology, and an offer to carry the lost roleplays forward.

Its first weeks were built around an empty top title. A Bluegrass Rumble seeded the Bluegrass Title tournament, and the scheming chief executive "Cool Hand" Luke Sampson spent the rounds punishing Max Pain for talking back, costing him the top seed and stacking a handicap match against him. The tournament final of 26 February 2001 was ruled a no-contest from the ring and sent to a steel cage at Bluegrass Bash, the first pay-per-view, held in Lexington that March.

The undercard mixed comedy and brawling. The chalkboard-lecturing Board of Education, first holders of the tag team titles, feuded with Southern Tradition, the crowd's bar-room favourites; the hapless Laramee was dragged from match to match by the Master of the Catch-22; and a rhyming pimp gimmick in fuchsia and gold became one of the roster's calling cards.

Most of the later cards went out as text files on the fed's website rather than to the newsgroup: Mega-Extreme, Kingdome Come, Going the Distance, a tribute card after 11 September, and the MTM pay-per-view in December. Along the way it opened a cruiserweight division and a developmental fed called TCW, held voice conferences for its roster, and answered a summer rumour of its closing by pointing to the card it had just put out.

It was still recruiting at the close of 2001, its fall cycle ended and a new one about to begin.
