A fantasy federation that opened with the pay-per-view See No Evil on 14 September 2008, ran a weekly Parental Advisory and the pay-per-views The Bleeding and Thankful Sins from a Tokyo-styled front office, and ended in December 2008 when its owner vanished from his office and, by a later news entry, the company merged with another federation. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Wrestling Unplugged opened on 14 September 2008 with the pay-per-view See No Evil, which crowned Requiem its first World champion, David Gunn the first Short Circuit champion and Chad Vargas the first Contender champion. Its owner, Jayson Burrows, and co-owner, Eric Evans, had come from Barely Legal Wrestling Alliance, and its staff page says so; Johnny Hall, who had owned that fed, came back in October as Director of Operations. Its second show calls Hallow Wicked 'the former BLWA Champion'.

Eleven of its shows survive in full and a twelfth survives as an announced card. Nine weekly Parental Advisory broadcasts run from 22 September to 22 December 2008, the fifth is missing, and the pay-per-views The Bleeding on 19 October and Thankful Sins on 23 November were staged, in the fiction, in arenas in Lowell, New York and Tokyo. Its Prime Time belt began as a title Porter MaCleod brought from another federation and merged with the Short Circuit title at The Bleeding; its One Shot title passed through five reigns between September and December 2008, and its weekly Feed carried the backstage news.

In December 2008 its owner vanished from his office during a Parental Advisory, a Japanese board of directors met over the gap, and a new boss redecorated the office on 22 December. The live copy of its news page says that Wrestling Unplugged had merged with Anarchy Wrestling Federation by 30 December, and nothing later survives under its own name. A company that took the name Wrestling Unplugged again in June 2009, carrying another company's titles, is held under its own wing.

Predecessor to WU (Wrestling Unplugged, 2008)
