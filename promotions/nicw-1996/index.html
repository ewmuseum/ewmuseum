Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

New Internet Championship Wrestling billed its own Wrestlefest supercard, announced 13 May 1996, as “the NICW's version of Wrestlemania”: fourteen matches headlined by three-time World Champion Falcon defending against “Masta Ace” Mike D'Angelo in a thirty-minute Ironman match, alongside the Zulu Nation against the Dukes of Hazzard in a double ladder match and Dark Master defending the NICW Transcontinental Championship against the Last Prophet.

A backstage bulletin the following week filled in the promotion's own churn: Junaid “Jackknife” Mannan re-signed, several teams left the roster temporarily, Dark Master was rebuilding his gimmick to be “a more violent” and “less invincible” character, and Falcon himself was tipped to leave the NICW once Wrestlefest was over. The full card, posted 19 May, added Shadow Warriors against the Returners for the NICW Tag Team Championship, Fury against Bird's Eye in the final of a North America West Title tournament, and a three-way street fight among King Curtis, Juggernaut, and Tom Ace.

Only the main event survives in full, in a special cut posted 23 May 1996 for the newsgroup: Falcon, “the Bird of Prey,” defending against D'Angelo, the UWC Champion and a first-time challenger, called by Arnold Fenton and George Anderson from a raucous Madison Square Garden crowd. D'Angelo took the deciding third fall with a Claw Hold submission to win the title two falls to one, ending Falcon's third reign.

No further NICW document from 1996 survives after that broadcast.

On 1 January 1999 two returning operators revived the promotion, describing NICW as having stood for “tradition, not gratuitous violence or bloated cards” and promising a return to “down to earth traditional wrestling on a weekly basis” after what they called a year's hiatus since the fall of 1996. By its own official rankings bulletin of 21 April 1999, the revived NICW crowned “The Unforgiven” David Pietka World Heavyweight Champion at 6-0-0, Dr. Hate Transcontinental Champion at 6-1-0, and the Hall Brothers undefeated World Tag Team Champions at 4-0-0.

No NICW document after that rankings bulletin survives.
