A 1999 Usenet e-fed launched with a test broadcast in July before crowning its first World Heavyweight Champion at its debut pay-per-view in August, run by a small handwriting team rather than open roleplay submission. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

No Nonsense Wrestling opened with a test broadcast, The Pilot, on 3 July 1999, and followed it on 18 July with the full launch its own announcer had teased on air as “the dawn of a new era.” Its own creator described the promotion, in a note posted that September, as “completely handwritten” and “co-run by myself and two other bookers,” reporting “around 15 active members” and a rumour board that drew “around 7 messages a day.”

The Pilot closed on an over-the-top-rope Battle Royale for the whole roster, which came down to Mark Daniels and Stephanie Davis after an already-eliminated Ed Case climbed back into the ring to help finish off Jack Slade. Davis kissed Daniels, who then climbed out and dropped to the floor himself, leaving her the last one standing — a finish its own broadcast called “a tainted win but none the less Stephanie Davis is the queen of NNW tonight!”

Its first pay-per-view, SuperBash, aired 28 August 1999 from what its own broadcast staged as a sold-out Madison Square Garden, and closed with a bloody, weapons-heavy main event between Ed Case and Dario Giovanni that went through two broken tables before its finish turned on interference for Case's benefit twice over: Danny De Mon dragging the referee out of the ring to erase a two-count against him, then Jack Slade running in with a baseball bat to put Giovanni down for the pin Case needed. Hugh Strickson announced Case the first NNW World Heavyweight Champion.

How it ended isn't in its surviving record. Its last surviving document, dated 2 September 1999, describes the promotion as still active, with about fifteen members.
