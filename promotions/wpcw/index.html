Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World Professional Championship Wrestling opened on 20 November 1997 with an ambition stated plainly in its own founding ad: “trying to unite all the great wrestlers from top feds in ONE organization,” judged on participation rather than a simulator. It named five champions before its first card ever ran — Michael Robinson (World), “Franchise” Richards (United States), Metal Melatia (Unified), Vicious (Television), and the Awesome Starr Brothers (Tag Team) — and planned two weekly shows, Monday Night Mayhem and Saturday Night Slam, plus house shows twice a week.

Nearly a year later, on 30 October 1998, it resurfaced with the debut episode of a new weekly program, Night of All-Stars, taped before more than 20,000 fans at the Rosemount Horizon in Chicago. Its own president used the broadcast to build toward its first announced pay-per-view, FightFest 1: The Revolution Begins, set for 15 November — confirming a Triangle Match among “Lightbearer” Doomsday, Shawn Jamison, and “GlacierIce” Drake for the number-one contendership to the vacant United States Championship, and a Parking Lot Brawl between “Hotshot” Vic Perry and “Iron Man” Steel for the World Championship. The main event, a two-out-of-three-falls match between promotion veteran Mr. Excellent and newcomer Jack Vars, ended in a disqualification after a debuting heel, “The Perfect Masterpiece” Richard Destiny, ran in with a chair and attacked both men, fleeing only when cruiserweight champion “Homicidal” Jarret Scott came to his friend Mr. Excellent's aid.

The same week its own newsletter, Outside The Ropes, ran roleplay vignettes for its roster alongside its show news — among them a Hollywood-set segment for its own tag team, the Best of the Best (Shaun and Jonathan Riche), who by then had also placed thirtieth in the RSPWF's own Top 200 Tag Teams ranking for 1998.

After what its own broadcast called “weeks of technical difficulties,” Night of All-Stars returned 17 January 1999 as an expanded three-hour show on its own new e-TV channel, live from the Key Arena in Seattle before an expected 22,500 fans. The card opened with a newcomer, Justin Christopher, wrestling for an actual contract against Chaos Kid, and carried a non-title Cruiserweight bout pitting champion “Homicidal” Jarret Scott against another new signing, Stanley Magnificent.

No document survives after that broadcast.
