Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Wrestling International Alliances ran a Thursday show called Insanity, a Brinkster free-hosting account and a copyright line that read WIA Entertainment Inc. Its Terms of Agreement are four sentences long and one of them is “We follows the rules under Primetime Central, and we will stay by them.”

What it left behind is mostly judgement. Its weekly rankings give every wrestler in the top ten a paragraph of staff commentary — who impressed with their roleplays, who stepped it up after a bad night, who was the most active wrestler it had. Its F.A.Q. answers the question about not hearing back on an application with “Simple. You didn’t get in”, and the question about posers with a request never to ask about posers again.

On 26 January 2003 the federation changed hands. Its founder handed the whole thing to one of his own superstars, and the announcement is written by the new owner: things would continue as normal, the pay-per-view pushed back a week, roleplaying open until 2 February. Three editions of Insanity are named in the fed’s own archive and none of them survives. How it ended isn’t in the surviving record; the last capture of the site is 26 March 2003 and every one after that is an empty page.
