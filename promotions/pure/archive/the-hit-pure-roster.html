Bio

Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid's Signature Moves

The Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid takes great pride in having what many call the most extensive move list in professional wrestling. While some may scoff, The HIT strongly feels that it is necessary to have many moves, for every opponent is different. Here is his dizzying array of signature tricks, divided into sections.


Strikes:

For a long time, the Trapezoid has felt this was the weakest part of his skills. Being a smaller man, he is simply incapable of generating the power of a 300 lb. man with his punches. To compensate, the Trapezoid has developed a powerful list of strikes that, although unusual, are certainly cruelly effective.


The Minus Sign- Trapezoid simply bounces off the ropes and extends his body, headbutting his opponent in the nuts. (Spear to Groin)


Order of Operations- HIT stings his opponent with a right jab, a left cross, a left jab, and then spins, catching his opponent with a rotating backfist. (Punch Combination)


Chicago Math- Measuring his opponent carefully, the Trapezoid fires a kick to the back of his foe’s right knee. As he buckles, HIT steps back and creams him with a roundhouse kick to the temple.


Numberline- The HIT bounces off the ropes, rotates 360 degrees, and clocks his opponent with a vicious forearm strike. (Roaring Axe Bomber)


Table of Values- Trapezoid knees his opponent in the midsection, bounces off the ropes, and clocks the foe in the face with a running kneelift. (Knee Combination)


118 Degree Superkick- HIT simply steps back and superkicks his opponent. (High Angled Superkick)


Rotation Complex- The Sultan of Sides simply somersaults into an axe kick to his foe's neck. (Flipping Axe Kick)


Least Common Denominator- Simple kick in the nuts. What did you expect?! (Nutshot)

High Impact Manuevers:

The bread and butter of The HIT's moveset. A combination of ingenuity, study, and hours of practice in a wrestling ring have produced these moves, among the most deadly anywhere.


Irrational Number- The HIT lifts his opponent vertical as if for a suplex. He rotates 450 degrees, and then plants his opponent with a Falcon Arrow, spiking him on his head.


(Rotating Sheer Drop Falcon Arrow)

X Cubed- The Sultan of Sides picks his opponent up for a belly to back suplex, and hits it. He rolls to his feet, and hits a second. Once more he rolls through, this time spiking his opponent on his neck with a backdrop driver. (Rolling Belly To Back Suplexes to Backdrop Driver)


Geometry 101- Lifting his opponent into a fireman’s carry, The HIT plays to the crowd, then jumps, rotating 180 degrees before delivering a Death Valley Driver. (Leaping Half-Spin DVD)


Additive Inverse- The HIT lifts his opponent up in a crucifix powerbomb, making him look like an addition sign. He then drops his opponent on his head, hopefully making him flop over to his stomach. (High Angle Crucifix Powerbomb)


Times Tables- Using his deceptive quickness, the HIT hops up, and sits aside his opponent’s shoulders. Pushing off, he elevates himself into the air, and bulldogs his nemesis on the way down. Can also be used to counter the “electric chair” situation, where HIT is forced to be atop a foe’s shoulders. (Elevation Out of Electric Chair to Bulldog)


Perpindicular Bisector- Lifting his foe into an inverted crucifix, HIT flips his foe over, catching him for a sitout Tombstone on the way down. (Inverted Crucifix to Sitout Tombstone)


Negative Integer- HIT locks his foe into an inverted facelock. Grabbing a leg, the Trapezoid hits an odd inverted fisherman’s buster, landing the enemy on his face. (Inverted Fisherman’s Buster)


Real Zeroes- Trapezoid locks his opponent in a front facelock, then drops straight down.

(Front Falling DDT)

Logarithmic Function- The HIT bends his opponent over for a powerbomb, and crosses his arms. Lifting the man in the air, the HIT drops him over his shoulder with a powerbomb, usually held for a pin attempt. (Straitjacket Thunder Fire Powerbomb)


Quadratic Equation- Trapezoid double-underhooks his foe. Lifting him vertical, HIT spikes the victim on his head with a brainbuster. (Butterfly Sheer Drop Brainbuster)


The Square Root of Negative Three- The HIT pulls an arm across his opponent' face, choking him with it. From here, HIT takes his opponent over with a reverse fireman's carry, or an Angle Slam, as it is popularly known in the U.S.A. (Armtrap Angle Slam)


Algebra I- Bouncing off the ropes, the Trapezoid somersaults forward, and leaps into a senton, spinning 180 degrees, so he lands chest first on his opponent. (Tumbling 180 Senton Splash)


Algebra II- Often used to follow up Algebra I, HIT runs into the ropes, cartwheels so his lands with his back to his foe, and hits a standing moonsault. (Cartwheel to Moonsault).


SAS Postulate- Locking his foe in a full nelson, the HIT drives forward, ramming the opponent face first into the turnbuckle (Full Nelson Facesweep to Turnbuckle)


CPCTC- Standing for a geometric theorem (congruent parts of congruent triangles are congruent), in the HIT's arsenal, it takes on a much more deadly tint. The HIT clotheslines his opponent (C), then picks him up and piledrives him (P). Scraping his foe from the canvas, HIT chokeslams him (C) back down. Lifting him back up, Trapezoid administers a Tombstone (T), then goes up top, and hits a corkscrew senton (C) for the pin. (Combination of Moves)


30-60-90 Triangle- The HIT administers a series of Michinoku Drivers, each one becoming more of a headdrop until the final vertical one. (Triple Michinoku Driver)


OverRotation- The HIT shoves his opponent into the ropes face first, and, as he rebounds, catches him with a tilt-a-whirl chestbreaker. (Tilt-a-Whirl Chestbreaker)


Euclidean Geometry- HIT lifts his opponent up in a fireman's carry. Tossing the legs in the air, the HIT shifts his foe into a sitting reverse DDT.(Fireman's Carry to Reverse DDT)


Complex Fraction- Hooking his opponent for a belly to back suplex, the HIT instead hits an inverse backdrop. On the way down, the HIT executes an Ace Crusher on the falling victim. (Inverse Backdrop to Ace Crusher)


Difference Quotient- Crossing his opponent's arms, the HIT drops him with a DDT. (Straitjacket DDT)


Vertical Angles- The HIT lifts his opponent up in a suplex. As he holds him there, he hooks one leg, twisting the opponent into a Fisherman's Michinoku Driver. (Vertical Suplex to Fisherman's Michinoku Driver)


Two Column Proof- Lifting his opponent onto his shoulder with two arms hooked, HIT runs, and plants his foe with a sitout Dominator. (Double Underhook Running Dominator)


Parabolic Path- The HIT launches his opponent high over head in a back body drop, forcing the opponent over the top to the floor. (Super High Hangtime Back Body Drop to the Floor)


Perfect Square Trinomial- Trapezoid doubles his opponent over center ring. Hoisting him up into powerbomb position, HIT runs, and powerbombs him into one corner. Lifting him back up, he charges cross-ring, and plants his foe with a powerbomb to the opposite corner. Resetting his foe, he charges midring, and leaps, planting his foe with a sitout powerbomb. (Double Turnbuckle Powerbomb to Sitout Powerbomb Pin)


Trapplexes: Trapezoid, like many others, names his suplexes after himself. However, the HIT's suplexes are not common in the least. They are among the most odd, unusual, and of course deadly, suplexes in professional wrestling.


Triangle Trapplex- The HIT locks his opponent in an inverted full nelson. Summoning his strength, he takes his opponent up and over in a suplex. Can be released, or bridged for a pin. (Inverted Full Nelson Suplex)


Two Dimensional Trapplex- Locking his opponent in a one handed chokehold, and hammerlocking one arm, HIT tosses his opponent high overhead into a flip, forcing him to land on his back. This Trapplex is always released. (Choking Hammerlock Overhead Release Suplex)


Algebraic Trapplex- Hooking his opponent for a T-Bone Suplex, the Trapezoid takes him over and twists around 360 degrees, but holds the bridge. (360 Bridging T-Bone Suplex)


Geometric Trapplex- HIT holds his opponent by both legs, as if for a wheelbarrow suplex. As he pulls the opponent closer, he locks on a full nelson, and takes him over with the added momentum. Usually bridged. (Wheelbarrow Dragon Suplex)


Trigonometric Trapplex- The Trapezoid applies his Extraneous Solution half nelson-chicken wing. From here, it’s a simple lift into the Trigonometric Trapplex. (Half Nelson Chicken Wing Suplex)


Sine, Cosine, and Tangent- While not Trapplexes, this suplex combination is listed here due to its sheer insanity. The HIT starts off with a German suplex. Rolling through the manuever, HIT changes his grip to a Tiger Suplex, and nails this. A final rollthrough follows, as HIT applies a katahajime, and suplexes his opponent one more time with what is called a Tiger Suplex '85, and holds this for a pin. (German-Tiger-Tiger '85 Suplex Combination)


Secant, Cosecant, and Cotangent- Psychotic suplex combination #2 for The HIT. Secant, the first of the three suplexes, is a straight Dragon Suplex. Rolling through, the Trapezoid applies Cosecant, a crossface chickenwing (Millennium) suplex, and nails it Rolling through one last time allows HIT to lock his opponent in a double hammerlock, and from here, it is a simple lift to a bridged double hammerlock suplex for a pin. (Dragon-Millennium-Double Hammerlock Suplex Combination)


Submission Holds: While not many in number, the HIT's submission moves are among the most innovative around, combining odd lucha stretching techniques with traditional manuevers.


The Word Problem- The Trapezoid grapevines his opponent’s legs. Leaning forward, he crosses his opponent’s arms across his face, and pulls back. (Indian Deathlock Combined with Straitjacket Submission)


Paragraph Proof- HIT takes his opponent down, and locks on a Texas Cloverleaf. However, as he applies the move, he intensifies the pressure by dropping to one knee.


(Liontamer-Style Texas Cloverleaf)

Extraneous Solution- Probably the most simplistic Trapezoid submission, The HIT applies a half nelson chicken wing combination, joining his hands behind the foe's back. (Half Nelson Chickenwing)


Top Rope Manuevers: The most spectacular of HIT's arsenal, Trapezoid's moves involving climbing or jumping off the top rope. A master of these tactics, HIT's list of these will get full usage in PURE's Junior Heavyweight Division.


Long Division- Sitting his opponent on the top rope facing the crowd, the HIT climbs up, delivering a devastating Reverse Brainbuster. (Top Rope Reverse Brainbuster)


The Slope Formula- One of HIT's showstoppers. Set up is the same as for Rent-A-Hero's Gravity Defier, except ending in a sitout reverse X-Factor.


(Turnbuckle Walk to Sitdown Reverse X-Factor).

Power to a Power- Standing on the top rope with a groggy opponent, the HIT double underhooks his foe, before lifting him vertical and leaping, driving his opponent into the mat at a headfirst 45 degree angle.


(Top Rope Double Arm Facebuster/TD 91)

Circumference of the Circle- Trapezoid lifts his opponent in a fireman’s carry, somersaulting with him off the top rope. (Top Rope Rolling Fireman’s Carry)


Fermat’s Last Theorem- The HIT stands his opponent on the top rope, facing the crowd. Leaping high, the HIT is able to take him over to the ground with a reverse hurricanrana.


(Top Rope Standing Poisoned Hurricanrana)

Planck's Constant- Standing on the top rope, HIT lift his opponent up for a powerbomb, but falls back, driving his opponent's face into the turnbuckle.


(Top Rope Fallback Turnbuckle Powerbomb)

Archmedes’ Screw- The HIT poises himself up top, leaping off with a 360 degree corkscrew clothesline.


(Corkscrew Top Rope Clothesline)

Area of a Trapezoid- The HIT leaps off the top rope with a simple top rope elbowdrop.

(Top Rope Elbowdrop).

Trapezoidal Translation- Perched on the top rope, the Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid leaps off facing the crowd, in essence doing an inverted shooting star press. (Inverted Frontflip Shooting Star Press)


Super Finishers: In Japanese wrestling, athletes often have manuevers they only haul out against especially difficult opponents. It is only in the most difficult of matches that Jushin Lyger executes a Shooting Star Press or Top Rope Brainbuster, as it takes an extremely persistant foe to force Kenta Kobashi to use the Burning Hammer. The Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid has several of his own. These moves have never before been used, and, as such, their effects are unknown, and are considered among the most devastating moves in existance.


Parallel Bases II- An advanced form of the Trapezoid's signature Parallel Bases, The HIT lifts his pumphandled opponent up over his shoulder. Sitting down, he spikes the opponent headfirst unprotected to the canvas. (Pumphandle Emerald Fusion)


Ten Times The Terrifyingly Titled Traumatizingly Terrific Temerous Trapezoidal Trouble (AKA T To The Tenth)- Probably the most insane move in the history of wrestling, it is a combination of ten moves starting with the letter T. Trapezoid Tiger Drives his opponent, then goes up for a Top Rope Fistdrop. He then applies a Triangle Choke to weaken the opponent. Lifting the opponent to his feet, he hits a Tangent, a Triangle Trapplex, and then a Trigonometric Trapplex. Climbing up to the top again, HIT performs the Trapezoidal Translation. He then climbs the opposite corner and hits a Top Rope Splash. Trapezoid delivers a Tiger Driver '91, then ends the combination with a Top Rope Tombstone. (Combination of Moves)


Horrifyingly Insane Trapezoid's Signature Moves

The Horrifyingly Insane Trapezoid is The HIT's inner being..his deepest, darkest desires for violence. When in this mental state, he can execute tactics he deemed 'too dangerous' for use. Further, his move names are more demented..moving away from mathematics to the torment within his soul.


Cry of the Fallen- Lifting his opponent up into piledriver position, The HIT drops to his knees, executing a modified Tombstone piledriver on his foe. (Reverse Tombstone Piledriver)


Blind Hatred- The Horrifying Insane Trapezoid sits his opponent on the top rope, facing the crowd. He climbs to the apron, and locks a three quarter nelson on, using a three quarter neckbreaker to the ring apron. (Top Rope Ace Crusher to Apron)


Funeral Pyre- Flying off the top rope, HIT grabs his opponent's head, driving it backwards into the mat. (Top Rope Headsmash Headsmash to Mat)


Schitzophrenia- Here, the Horrifyingly Insane Trapezoid displays his power, with a pair of powerbombs. After administering both, he holds the opponent high in the air, and releases him with a flapjack. (Double Powerbomb to Flapjack)


Dementia- A modification of Schitzophrenia, with the double powerbomb leading to a Dominator. (Double Powerbomb to Dominator)


Neurotica- A further refinement of Dementia, with the double powerbomb ending in an Abandon Fate, the old finisher of Neurosis partner Kent Anthason. (Double Powerbomb to Blue Thunder Facebuster)


Societal Ills- Trapezoid takes his opponent's left leg, and drapes it over the middle rope, executing a plancha over the top onto the limb. (Plancha to Left Leg)


Biography

The Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid has had a very interesting life, to say the least. He is a man of many talents, and almost as many quirks. When one lives in Mormon Salt Lake City, and is an atheist, one can expect many oddities.


He was born to loving parents Jeffrey and Maria in a nice middle class neighborhood in Salt Lake City. Soon after his birth, the loving couple had family over at their house, to celebrate the HIT’s birth. To this day, his first and last name are unknown. Since he has legally changed his name to The HIT, (Mr. Trapezoid for teaching) and his parents will not speak to the media, it is likely this information will remain forever unknown.


While passing young HIT to an uncle, Jeffrey unfortunately bobbled the child, dropping the infant on the side of his head. Maria immediately called 911, and the paramedics arrived, rushing the baby to the hospital. At first, it appeared no real damage was done. However, in his emergency room, young Trapezoid woke up next to a second grade student, who was desperately studying his shapes. One had been carelessly left on his bed. Trapezoid saw it…and the image on it, an isosceles trapezoid, would change the HIT’s life forever.


In his early years, he believed that he WAS an isosceles trapezoid. He enjoyed counting games from the time he was six months old. He was well ahead of everyone else in his classes mathematically, studying such subjects as geometry and algebra when he was in 6th grade. He finished the most advanced calculus course his high school had to offer by 9th grade, leaving the school no choice but to bus him to Brigham Young University to take more advanced mathematics.


Sadly, this would be the best college education he would get. His parents were too poor to send him to college, so he attended a local two-year community college, and graduated with honors, in math of course.


His degree was in business, a subject he despised. However, he knew mathematics was his passion. So, he decided to use his degree to get something he always wanted….


A substitute teaching license.

Going back to Brigham Young High School, The HIT stepped in as a substitute geometry teacher. His odd teaching techniques and bizarre behavior quickly made him a favorite substitute among students. While nearly being barred for life due to his substitute health teaching foray, after persuading the principal with what would come to be known as the SAS Postulate, The HIT was granted a permanent position as a 10th grade Geometry teacher, known to all as Mr. Trapezoid.


Many of his students were wrestling fans, as was The HIT. For years, the HIT had been practicing on the side, joining various schools. He signed a contract with the IWO’s IWO Minor League, or the IML. He then worked out a deal with the school, in which he would only teach one period a day to allow for his schedule. This makes each of his students extra special.


He debuted in the IML to rave reviews, capturing the IML United States Title in a three way match against Lance McNally and the champion Phyre with a 450 splash. Soon after though, he got the call up to the IWO. Dropping the belt to The Executioner, he was ready for the next stage of his career.


In the IWO, he came out fast. Many expected him to be an utter joke, yet he rose quickly through the ranks. Facing Jax Stone for the IWO North American title, he dropped one fall to Jax’s Drop Like a Stone, yet took two straight falls for the title. He held this for about fifteen days, before dropping it to “The Real Heel” Evan Levine.


From here, he was booked to take on Samuel Potright in a Trick or Treat match, the second of its kind. Trick or Treat II started out as being over the Extreme title, but quickly turned into a rivalry. The HIT emerged victorious in one of the bloodiest matches of all time, but his injury toll was staggering. Over 200 stitches, a broken leg, second degree burns over around 15 percent of his body..all of this forced him to vacate the Extreme Title a day later.


While on the shelf for six months, the HIT’s warped mind went even further off the deep end, as he formulated a plan to go after Potright. Competing under a mask as Why, he assaulted Potright, and kidnapped his lovely wife Beth. After using a knife to draw her blood, and then drink it from a glass, he convinced the then World Champion to accept Trick or Treat III for the World Title.


After the most emotional, violent, and sadistic match in the history of professional wrestling, Sam Potright drove Trapezoid headfirst into the mat in overtime with War Within a Breath to defeat him. However, an executive quirk left The HIT World Champion, and Samuel unable to see Beth for the rest of his life. At this point, The HIT was heartbroken. He had the World Title…but he couldn’t live with causing the emotional demise of his nemesis. He excersized a loophole in the match contract…sending him into retirement and surrendering the title, yet earning him Potright’s undying respect…for then.


As he recovered from this, even more insane match, he received a call from an unlikely man: Kent Anathason. He needed someone to join himself and Nuke in Primetime Championship Wrestling, to form a group called Neurosis.


Neurosis formed in the summer of 2001, fighting groups such as the Dark Ages. Quickly, things degenerated, as Neurosis was thrown headlong into the path of The Red Army. Anthason and Nuke quickly left, despite HIT and Nuke teaming to nearly overthrow Da Westsidaz reign as tag team champions of PCW. This left the HIT alone to battle The Red Army. Despite much opposition from groups like The European Union, The HIT received no help in his battles.


He eliminated Alexander Popov and Nikita Morozov from PCW, while capturing the PCW Cruiserweight Title from White Mexican, and the Television Title from Kid Wonder. Finally though, his run came to an end. In a retirement match against Rent-A-Hero, Ivan Stanislav, leader of The Red Army, planted HIT with The Red Scare, allowing RAH to pick up a win, and evict HIT from PCW. He immediately swore vengeance on both men.


It didn’t take him long to find a new federation, signing on with Epic Wrestling. In a Poisoned Pyramid Deathmatch, he won the promotions North American Title, holding it for two months before dropping it to nemesis Samuel Potright, and leaving.


At this point, The HIT went looking back to PTC for work. He found PURE, a new Japanese promotion starting up. Seeing this as a perfect opportunity to test his skills, the HIT began a makeover.


Masking himself, he is now ready to face the best in PURE. Can the HIT succeed here? We shall see…
