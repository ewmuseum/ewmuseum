Bio

Appearance:

Joey is 6'4' tall, and weighs in at 227lbs. He has shoulder length blonde hair (natural...not bleached) that rests at the bottom of his neck. He is a well built character, with an immense lower body strength, which he uses to his advantage to break submission holds.


Attire:

Joey normally wrestles in a pair of baggy black pants (similar to the ones Tajiri wears) with the name 'FLASH' in gold writing down the left leg in English, and down the right leg in Japanese. He also wears a tight black t-shirt with high-cut sleeves (midway up his biceps) His attire is complete by black tape around his wrists.


Outside the ring, he wears casual clothes, or, on live shows, his ring attire and a black leather overcoat.


History:

Born in 1973 in Bradford, England, Joseph Mark Wilson was raised by his mother and father in a posh neighbourhood of the city. Mark Wilson (Joseph's father) worked as an Assistant Manager for an electronics firm, and just three months after Joseph's third birthday, was offered a new job for a fast rising company in Japan. He took the offer.


Joseph spent his childhood years growing up in Urawa, just outside Tokyo. Joseph spent his school years learning the Martial Arts, but never really excelled in any of them. Just after his 12th birthday, on the 9th of March 1985, Joseph had an encounter with a sport he could not forget. The Road Warriors had their first Japanese match on the first puroresu card ever held at Ryogoku Kokugikan (Sumo Hall) promoted by All Japan, and Joseph was in attendance.


To say that one night changed his life may be a little over-estimating, but it went a long way in helping achieve his future.


By the age of 19, Joseph, now known to his Japanese friends as Joey, had decided, after seven years of watching major companies across Japan and on nationalised television networks, that he wanted to join the art of puroresu.


After training in his school years in Martial Arts, and in an American wrestling class, Joey knew that he could at least perform the basics that he had seen week in, week out. Applying for a dojo in Urawa, Joey spent a lengthy 9 months learning the ropes, and gaining confidence, experience and knowledge of the art.


On the 17th of November, 1992, Joey Wilson debuted on an IOD show in Morioka, wrestling against a much more experienced Mexican wrestler, El Soldado Enmascarado, and lost via a Dragon Suplex in just under 10 minutes.


Undeterred, Joey plugged away, assuming a Japanese name (Shi'gurya Keji) and wrestling under a mask for BWA, IOD and various independent groups in Japan.


6 years later, and with a lot of experience behind him, Joey travelled to Mexico, and began to work independents to gain experience in the Lucha Libre style. Wearing his mask as Shi'gurya Keji, he debuted in LLI against a touring Super Crazy, beating him with a Gord Buster in 8:07.


In the meantime, Joey had attended various tours and independent shows in England, wrestling under his real name. His last appearance was two weeks after his LLI debut, losing to Alex Shane via Show Stopper in 16:35.


The LLI organisers decided to unmask Joey, but still didn't like the name Joey Wilson. Joseph quickly compromised, and the name 'Lightning Kid' was assumed for his two year stint in Mexico. LLI and FMV were the two main benefactors of his talent, however appearing on various tours and independents, Joey gained a lot of experience from fusing the Lucha Libre and puroresu styles together, along with a natural ability in submission based wrestling.


In the year 2000, an offer came through from a new promoter in America, the XCWF, for Joey to wrestle under the name Joey Flash. Joey accepted, and flew out to Oregon for the first card, using the Lightning Strikes variation of the Gord Buster to pick up numerous wins. Remaining undefeated, and picking up an award for Match Of The Year from the XCWF fans for a ladder match against Thomas Raine (Joey winning with a variation on Akinori Tsukioka's Moonlight Bomb in 30:23) Joey left the company in a mass exodus when new owners took over and refused to cover wages.


2001, Primetime Championship Wrestling opened its doors to an academy. PWA not only taught people the wrestling basics, but also the 'American style' of 'microphone skills' and 'promo techniques.'


Joey never graduated PWA, dropping out for the lack of true sportsmanship he had experience in Japan and Mexico. Apart from a brief appearance in NTICW, Joey worked the British scenes under both aliases (Joey Flash and Shi'gurya Keji), before word was passed about a new era opening in Japan.


Joey watched as PURE established itself in Japan, before shutting its doors early in its run. Re-opening under new management opened applications to PURE, and Joey was granted a roster space, again under a mask as Siena.


Recent happenings have caused Joey to revert back to the name Joey Flash in PURE, but only time will tell if it's good enough for him to make a name for himself again in Japan.
