Crossroads

21 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Crossroads The air of secrecy and trepidation was lifted. This thing called wrestling, that which had influenced so much of his life, was no longer just the subject that was tied to all the stories that were told. The first encounter had come and gone, and it was a relief. No more questions about how the sound of the crowd and surge of adrenaline would feel. The threshold was breached and the time has come for a man to set about becoming his own. The hot pavement seemed to exhale beneath the feet of the few foolish enough not to seek out the comfort of shade or the luxury of air conditioning, breathing out its collected warmth from the sun like a panting dog. The effects were not imaginary, but readily perceptible as the radiated energy gave a rippled effect to everything in the distance. It made the road ahead blurry and uncertain. :: How appropriate :: The future which lay ahead for Kevin Sandusky was just that, blurry and uncertain. Things were progressing, but not as expected. The debut had been made and even though he hadnt lost, nothing had been won either. Still, opportunity abounds. The random draw of the tag tourney had placed him in direct contact with his boss, as a teammate. A fortunate turn no doubt, but high risk as well. Failure to perform at or above an expected level would mean certain damage to his career. A successful showing would be the break he needed to really emerge from anonymity that he was currently suffering from. Still, the more thought put into it, the more this pairing with Miyagawa was predestined. In due time similarities and connections unfold themselves like a map before the mind. The familial traumas, wearing thin the traditional meanings of family. The derisive rift between Alex and Boda with Alexs child in the midst was all too familiar. Kevin had been that child, caught in a trap between pride and obstinacy. He prayed the unborn child wouldnt have to go though the same ordeal. But beyond the ties of tenuous family dynamics, there was the overall disdain for the most selfish of selfish, Kensuke Suzahara. Kevins eyes narrow and his nostrils flared at the mere thought of the man. Adrenaline and endorphins began to flow through his veins as his subconscious mind echoed in agreement with his rational conscious. Kensuke Suzahara the disenfranchised, the ingrate, the self-made martyr. Kensuke was younger than Kevin himself, but had climbed to heights unheard of. CCW Grand Slam Champion PCW Tag Champion one of only three men to have ever appeared in CCW, PCW, and OSW. Kensuke had the kind of life that would satisfy anyone. Anyone but Kensuke. Hed thrown it all away. All the fans, all the respect. In a selfish rage hed become that which hed most hated. Dont believe it? Ask Akiko. Ask Christine Tanner. More so, ask Kyle Lamen. Kyle, a good friend and former CCW Tag Champion whom had teamed with Uncle Joey. Former PCW Cruiserweight Champion, and all around prodigy. Until the Stranger. Until the neck brace. Until the surgery. He was a bright, shooting star of a wrestler and a real class act, until Kensuke Suzahara. One of these days, Kensuke would get his, and whether it was Uncle Joey, Miyagawa, or Kevin himself; he wouldnt rest until the world knew what Kensuke Suzahara was. Kevin: Your name should have been Sandusky, Kensu. You have more in common with the man than I do. Kevin spat on the broiling sidewalk, just to get the disturbing thought out of his head. Hed come to Tallahassee for a reason, and regardless of whatever internal turmoil he was going through, hed keep a positive outlook and a smile on his face. With a quickening step, Kevin made the last few blocks in rapid succession. The sliding door of the warehouse was open, and from inside all manner of din was emanating. In fact, rarely an eye was batted as Kevin strode in the front door and dropped his bag like he owned the place. Only three people even acknowledged his presence. Two women in the ring, and a stocky kid who was slowly rising from the incline bench. The blonde and redhead in the ring stopped exchanging the finer points of hair beales in order to give Kevin the critical once over. The kid from the weight bench sauntered over a noticeable case of ILS (imaginary lat. syndrome) distorting his frame. Kid: Were not accepting students right now. The tang of arrogance rang out in the kids voice, as he forcibly inhaled to deepen his chest. Kevin: Well, thats good. Id hate to have to show you up. Kid: I oughta- Get back to your reps and leave our guest alone! A voice boomed out from the small office along the south wall. The voice, while not gruff or angry was stern enough to snap the kid back to a normal posture and send him packing back to the incline bench. Kevin: Exodus? That you back there? Fricken-A~!  Slowly emerging from the office is a figure familiar to the average wrestling fan, and at the same time heartbreaking to the kind of fan that follows PURE. Kyle Velocity Lamen, his hair all spiked out and dyed partially blond. On his face was that trademark grin of his, but just as conspicuous was the brace around his neck. Kyle: Kev, long time, no see. What brings you down to CWS? Kevin: I need to learn a thing or two about tag wrestling. Know anybody who could help? ***to be continued***
