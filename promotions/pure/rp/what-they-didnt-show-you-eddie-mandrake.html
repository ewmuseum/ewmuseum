What they didn't show you: Eddie Mandrake

March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What they didn't show you: Eddie Mandrake Written: Joint RP Eddie Mandrake is out in the crew lounge area, dressed in his ring attire: purple tights with a red dragon print on the left leg. His boots are the same shade of purple, with white laces. His wrists are taped white, his hair is cut slightly shorter than usual. His body is wet from a shower earlier, a respectful tradition to wash off before a match in a puroresu ring. Mandrake stretches, hopping around and throwing some punches and kicks to limber up for his match. A straight punch, body hook. A back roundhouse kick is blocked.
Mandrake: Who're you? The small, yet thick ninja pushes Mandrake's foot aside. #16: You may just call me #16. I am a member of the SNC. Mandrake shakes his head, then goes back to his pre-match exercise. Mandrake: Oh, yeah. That 40-count faction that's just one roster spot. A lot of trouble to go through over nothing. But whatever floats your boat. I don't have time for autographs or advice sessions, or anything like that. If you didn't notice, I'm in a mini-tournament for the PURE World Heavyweight Title.
#16: Oh yes. I know. In fact, I'm counting on you to lose. Mandrake throws a unintentionally slippery kick. Regaining his balance, he looks back at the ninja.
Mandrake: What? #16: It would not be right for a gaijin to win PURE's highest championship. Especially one as repulsive as you.
Mandrake: Repulsive? #16: You are in a No Disqualification match. That is not what puroresu is about. Puroresu is about honorable competition between two athletes in a test of strength and skill. There is nothing honorable about a melee where the object of the match is to pummel your opponent with every weapon imaginable. Simply put, you are not compatible with the principles of puroresu, you do not deserve to be in the federation, and it is inexcusable that a gaijin like you would hold the native Japanese down from a title that is meant for them.
Shaking his head, Mandrake takes a fighting stance and goes back to his exercises. Mandrake: You need to relax. #16 steps in front of Mandrake, who pulls his kick. Mandrake: Look, I'm trying to be nice here. #16: Go home. Go back to PCW, if they'd even accept you. Go back to your sports entertainment federations where it is acceptable to keep a skilled Japanese competitor from getting the spotlight that he deserves to put on a wrestling showcase for the world to see. Go back to your 'clusterf*cks' and your horse-rapings, your Golden Hummers... puroresu doesn't need you. PURE doesn't want you.
Eddie Mandrake laughs a little, unable to take the ninja seriously. Mandrake: Ever hear of diversity? #16: Diversity? Funny, you refuse to whole-heartedly practice it in your own country, but expect it here....
Mandrake: Look man, I'm here to do a job-- #16: Then job. Ignoring #16, Mandrake continues his train of thought. Mandrake: I don't need, nor do I want your respect. All I care about are the fans, and that they leave the arena satisfied in watching great matches.
#16: They are satisfied with your matches. It's just the ones where you are passed out by the end of it all. And if you weren't already booked, and being released by the end of the pay-per-view... I'd gladly show you how right I am.
The ninja is starting to get to Mandrake, who clenches his fist. A hand taps him on the shoulder. He turns around.
Mandrake: What? The small producer steps back. Producer: Mandrake-san, your match is next. You should start on your way to the ring. Mandrake turns back around, but the ninja isn't there. #16 is nowhere to be seen. Mandrake: Where'd he go? Producer: Who? It is not important. Please, come with me. Mandrake shakes his head and scratches it. Spinning on his heels, he follows the producer to the pre-entrance area.
