Doghouse

26 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Doghouse Hed spent the last week and a half pouting. But the worst parts were that he both knew it, and had justified it to himself. Im on my own, paying my own way. Hell, Im only 19. Im allowed a bit of teen angst every now and then. But in the recesses of his mind he didnt want to listen to, he knew that had his stepfather been around hed have been doing manual labor around the school in the triple digit Tucson sun. But when he woke in the morning, something in the back of his head made itself known and forced its way to the forefront. It was time to get back to work. Otherwise, how would he be able to continue to live in the kind of opulence he was used to? And as that thought rattled around his brain, he looked out across the tiny studio apartment. Clothes strewn across the floor, dirty dishes on the counter, a pile of DVDs and PS2 games on the floor in front of the makeshift entertainment center. Start easy, clean the house up. Then, clean yourself up. He thought to himself as he started snatching up laundry from the apartment floor. Of course, housecleaning is truly mindless work. As the messes slowly became less and less, his mind drifted from the work at hand, to the works that lay ahead, and behind. One second. One lousy second was the difference. His hindsight screamed to him that maybe if hed gone off the ropes instead of through them. Maybe hed have been there in time. But whats worse was the silence afterward. Sure, the crowd roared for those two jackass false faces. Seriously, try to think of another focused and righteous warrior that talks as much yet does as little as Inphino. Try to think of one thing that fruit Mango ever did to honestly be able to call himself a hero. All that nice guy crap they pull is out and out bullshit. The Dragons were pounding on Tiger and Cege four on one. Where they hell were the heroes then? But even the triumph of the frauds wasnt what had gotten to him. It was the silence of his partner and employer Miyagawa. Not even a word after the match. Just a glaring look through his Kool-Aid encrusted eyes that said Where the hell were you on that one, kid? Just a glare, and then he walked off. It was a strange sensation. There was an explosion of sorts inside his head at that very moment. A vision passed through his sight, startling and gruesome, but disappearing just as quickly as it came. Still, where the hell did Miya get off doing that? The entire tag tourney hed been so wrapped up with Cianan that hed left the tag load entirely on Kevins shoulders. Why the hell is it the new kids fault when the main eventer drops that ball? And now, instead of going back after the Tag titles, or after the Dragons, or even the Junior Heavyweight title; he was supposed to spar with Bushido. A man he held no ill will toward what so ever. Hed been straightforward and reliable in the match against the Dragons. Although it would be a great exhibition, what purpose does it serve? The answer is simple. Dont let the boss get pinned, or get used to the doghouse.
