Life & Times of Isao Asai - Part 1: 'A Professional Courtesy'

20 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Life & Times of Isao Asai - Part 1: 'A Professional Courtesy' Written: 1 / 20 / 2003 'Bear witness, as I fulfill this destiny..' The picture on the television screen shows clips of a young man doing numerous high-flying wrestling moves before suddenly fading out to show only the following phrase in white block lettering over a black background; Isao Asai, a true professional. That done, the picture on the television fades out, while at the same time our picture pans out, and we realize we are in a small office with two Japanese men huddled around the television, both wearing neatly pressed business suits. The first, whom we recognize from the small vignette on the screen as Isao Asai, is smiling broadly and nodding as he turns toward his companion. The other man, a stern faced older looking man seems genuinely overcome with shock and surprise. Gauging his reaction, Isao’s expression sours.
“Yoshida-san, you are not pleased?” said Asai, the tone of his voice betraying his disappointment.
“Isao, you have been with this company for the last ten years,” said the older Yoshida-san, leaning back in his chair and eyeing Isao carefully, “It has been a fear of mine for some time that I would lose your skill to a rival company, but I certainly didn’t expect to receive your two week notice on the basis that you decided to pursue a wrestling career.”
Bowing his head, Isao moved away from the television and stood in front of what was obviously Yoshida-san’s desk in the cramped office.
“Believe me, Yoshida-san, it was never my intention to dishonor you or Dynamo-TEC of Japan by this. However, the Puroresu tradition fills me with a sense of belonging and awe that no achievement in the business-world could compare to. I feel that by staying here, I would bring harm to the company.”
Yoshida-san’s eyebrow shot upward hearing this. “How so?” “Yoshida-san, I can honestly say that I will never be able to focus on anything except Puroresu for the rest of my life. Staying here, I would be a burden, and bring shame to everything people like you and the rest of Dynamo-TEC have worked so hard for.”
Wheeling his chair around to face the television, Yoshida-san rewinds the footage to review Isao performing all those wonderful wrestling maneuvers, secretly delighting his young employee’s grace and dedication, but knowing that professionally he still has to remain steadfast. He freezes the tapes with Isao mid-air, about to perform a moonsault, and wheels back around to face the young wrestler.
“Isao, look at yourself. You’re Dynamo-TEC of Japan’s leading sales manager, a very honorable title for one of your age. You have a future here, one that entails wealth, great influence, and unquestionable power. All this is well within your grasp, but you would give all of it up to become a clown, flying through the air and jumping on top of people?”
The venomous tone in which Yoshida-san spat out the last statement bit deep into young Isao, but did nothing to sway his decision.
“Yes, Yoshida-san. I would.” A frustrated Yoshida-san huffed loudly at Isao, who defiantly raised his head, bearing a large resplendent smile. The gesture seemed to infuriate Yoshida-san, whose mouth did the complete polar opposite of Isaos’ and his eyes narrowed, brimming with anger.
“Isao Asai, you are officially terminated from Dynamo-TEC of Japan. Your petition and other privileges are hereby revoked, your paycheck for this week will be mailed to you, and you have twenty-four hours to empty your office before security personally escorts your belongings off Dynamo-TEC property…Now go.”
Yoshida-san quickly motioned toward the door; dagger-like eyes aimed toward Isao’s throat as he bowed politely and exited the office in silence. Our view switches to the hallway outside the office as a still smiling Isao straightens his tie and begins walking down the hall. He doesn’t get far before another younger man comes up behind him, a half-smile painted across his face.
“Isao! I saw you on TV last night!” the young man said, “You were incredible! Hey, how’d it go with old man Yoshida? Must not have been that bad, you’re still smiling.”
“Kenji,” Isao replied, “I’m glad you enjoyed the match. Next time, come to the show, I’d love to see you there.”
That said, Isao just continues walking down the hall, leaving an astonished Kenji to stand a moment in stunned pause before catching back up with him.
“Come on, Isao! Don’t hold out on your old friend Kenji! What did Yoshida-san say? Did he cut your pay? Take away your vacation time? What!?”
“Actually, he fired me…” “WHAT!?!” shouted the once more astonished Kenji, “That old buzzard watches as much Puroresu as the rest of us, how can he be so insensitive!? Gah, he doesn’t know what he’s losing! I’m sure you’ll get signed on with another company, easily!”
“It’s for the best, I suppose, I was planning on quitting anyway.” “Really!? You mean you’re actually going to wrestle full-time?! Live the hard life on the road, touring for peanuts? Wow, Isao. I’m really proud of you.”
“Thanks Kenji, I appreciate your support. Now, help me clean out my office?” “Sure thing!” The scene cuts, obviously to later, and shows both men in another small office, filling empty boxes with numerous items from drawers and off the desk.
“So, how exactly do you do all those moves? I remember seeing you at the company softball game; you could barely hit hard enough to make a single.”
“It’s not about how hard you hit, Kenji, it’s about the show you put on. It’s about the way two men wage a war of both the physical and mental, imposing the will of their minds in each move they make. It’s like a physical game of chess.”
“Huh, I never really thought of it that way.” “That’s what I love most about the sport, the detail and imagination that go through the minds of the competitors as they step in-between those ropes. No two matches will ever be exactly the same.”
Kenji nods, the understanding dawning on him like sunrise over Tokyo Bay. Both men are then taken by surprise as an attractive young business-woman barged into the office, and placing her hands on Isao’s shoulders.
“Isao! We all heard about what happened with you and Yoshida-san! I’m so sorry! I’m going to miss you so much! I know I’ll be able to see you on PURE TV, but still…It’s not going to be the same around here without you!”
Since the woman had said all this in the span of about two seconds, it took both men’s brains a good five seconds to recover and comprehend, accented by the trademark “Uh.”
“Uh…Thanks Kiyoshi, I’m going to miss you too,” said Isao, turning his head to the side and smiling widely at the girl.
“Heh, don’t worry Kiyoshi, I’ll still be around!” Kenji chimed in. “Yeah…I guess so.” Kiyoshi replied less than enthusiastically. “Anyway! That’s not what I came by to tell you! The rest of the sales staff want to all say goodbye together! We’ve gathered in the conference room and they sent me here to fetch you, come on!”
Isao’s expression dimmed somewhat as it seems like he was about to protest, but before he can even get a word in edgewise Kiyoshi grabs him by the arm and begins dragging him out of the office. Seeing no alternative, Isao reluctantly succumbs and follows her, Kenji following behind with a chuckle.
Meanwhile, none of the young professionals notice a small figure entering in the office behind them. The sound of miniature gears and servos operating is our only hint of this foreign presence as it approaches closer. Finally, when the sound has reached its climax right underneath our field of vision in the office, we are suddenly met with the image of a small silvery metallic paw cresting the edge of one of Isao’s boxes and then cut to black.
