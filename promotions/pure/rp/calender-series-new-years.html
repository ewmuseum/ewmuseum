Calender Series: New Year's Written: The Past Long Island, it’s an interesting place. It’s the suburbs of the busiest place in the entire world. I think that is the best way to access it. It gives you a good idea of how everything in Long Island New York works. With all the ‘Oh my Gawd’s and such it’s just a very predictable spot. The girls are all amazingly superficial and will marry the guys, the doctors, lawyers, doctors, stock brokers, dentists, doctors, and brain surgeons. They’ll all so predictable. To become something different than that is an amazing achievement. It’s like getting away with murder; it’s just not going to happen.
But this is where we take place. This is where the chapter begins. This chapter entitled January 2001. January second actually in particular. My parents had invited me and about twenty-five of our relatives, just a small quantity of them, up for New Year’s Eve. Well actually it was my mom who invited everybody. This morning there were only a few of them left though.
I love my relatives, but you have to be clever about this matter. Getting away from them is a trick I learned when I was about fourteen. You’ve got to talk with them for long periods of time and get them to drain their entire bit of conversational skills. They’re all very intelligent people, but conversationalist they are not. Most anyway, the charismatic ones won’t talk to anyone for more than thirty-seven seconds without getting extremely bored, grabbing some gin and moving on. But anyway the trick to getting out of the bad conversations is to act like you’re better than they are. Annunciate. Speak with very pronounce vocabulary. Know what you are talking about and gesture in ways that make it seem as if you are giving a speech. Be simply improved upon what their expectations were of you. That’s all, raise their standards a bit higher. This does something in addition to getting out of long, drawn out conversations actually. It also gets you out of the common saying, “He’s so cute,” and into, “He’s such a bright young man,” with each word being more amplified than the last.
Yes, this is my family, and yes, this is how they work. Yours don’t? But this does not matter. What matters is January second, 2002. In the dawning of a New Year I had decided to try and become a wrestler. Out of the five of our original group, Oliver, Roland, Jimmy, Patrick, and myself, only Ol had left under any circumstances. To tell you the truth I didn’t expect Roland or Pat to last more than two weeks, but they’ve lasted close to two months. Roland has been talking about quitting though, and I think the New Year has pushed him to hug that decision. Pat seems fine. Roland really just couldn’t keep up with the workout regimen. I’m actually surprised I’ve been able to. But I’m sidetracking here. The main point about the New Year is that I had decided to try my hardest to become a wrestler and would probably end up graduating wrestling school and college around the same time in May. I could either become an investment banker, fun fun, or try and get onto “the scene” of wrestling. Tough choice since Crypt, the head trainer, had told us that even if we did graduate we probably wouldn’t make more than $1,000 a month for awhile, possibly ever. Worse came to worse in the end I could easily still become a banker, but I really wanted to try for the wrestling business.
So here I was in my parents house. I’m about to tell them everything when all of a sudden…
“Hey mom! Hey dad!” The prodigal son was home. Jason was a few years older than me and now working as a lawyer in Las Angeles, California. He is absolutely the last thing I need. He’s the kind of guy that will scream that he got another A+ right before you tell your parents you got a D. Selfish jackhole.
My mom screamed: “Jason! Oh, what a surprise!” “Jason!” my dad yelled with a huge smiled on his face. “You nearly scared your mother half to death.” He got up to shake the success’ hand.
Mom hugged him, tightly. The three of them, with large smiles on their faces, looked like a big ol’ matzah ball. They were all wearing some kind of tan or beige and really just looked like they hadn’t spent most of their lives next to the trendiest city on the planet.
“Hey Jace,” I calmly stated while waving my hand unenthusiastically. Everyone seemed to stare at me as if I had just yelled, ‘Fuck you, you fucking fucker.’ “What’s up?” Jason looked at my mother and then looked back at me with a phony smile on his face. My face was dulled. “Hey brother.” He walked over jockilly. “How ya doing?” and he punched me in the arm. He was about two inches taller than me, even though that is totally strange since I’m already taller than dad. The thing about this brother of mine in a two piece suit is that he always tried and did show me up. He’s so selfish it astounds me.
I rubbed my arm, because otherwise he’d keep punching me. “All right, you?” He held out his arms and turned as if he were about to announce that he were God himself. “I, Jason Levin, got promoted!”
My mother gasped and ran to grab at his arm. He looked down at her as if she were a peon and she didn’t act differently from one.
“Congrats bro.” “I always knew my son had success written all over him.” “Hey, I’ve gotta go upstairs,” YES! “But I’ll be right back down.” Dammit! And so the little punk ran upstairs and this was basically my only chance so I just came right out and said it. “Mom? Dad?” They both looked up at me. “I have something to tell you about my career.”
“You have a job? Already?” my mom pleaded. I sighed. “Uh, no. Actually, I’ve been professionally wrestling for the past few months and when I graduate college I’m going to pursue a wrestling career.” My speech was the speed of a freight train. I let out a quick gushing breath and waited.
And waited. And then I waited some more. No response from the peanut gallery. Footfalls were my only sound effect as Jason came barreling down the stairs. He looked at everybody and his smile diminished. “Woah, whose funeral?”
A truer statement never said.
