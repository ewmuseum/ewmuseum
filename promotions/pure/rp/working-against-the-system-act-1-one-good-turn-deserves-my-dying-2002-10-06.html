Working Against the System: Act 1- One good Turn deserves my Dying

Working Against the System: Act 1- One good Turn deserves my Dying Written: October 6, 2002 So you have come to the conclusion that I am not the normal person or the average man that the world wants to represent them. But really where is the fun or the thrill of being average or even representing anyone but myself. I am here for the competition of actual competitors and not some normal average man. Well, actually I would take on any man or women just as long as they put up some sort of challenge.
My question has always been why do people believe so little in them self? What is there to be afraid of anyway? I don’t believe anyone is afraid of their own shadow following them and maybe creeping up behind them and stabbing them in the back when their not looking. So what else does that leave us with?
Could it maybe be that your ability isn’t up to the amount of punishment you give your self? Or maybe that the system will not allow you to do any better then your own body and mind can allow you? Whatever reason it is, you have to learn to over come it, and push on farther then you can or you will continue slip farther unless you don’t let go.
One good Turn deserves my dying Vilas walks allow down an abandoned street covered by the mist of a cool morning breeze that brushes the leaves across the concrete floor of what is a city street, and continues to move it’s way up the bare street into the alleys which collect the foliage in all it’s knocks and cranny’s. One by one each piece places them self in the correct corner and awaits an even bigger breeze to push it more.
Vilas minds his own path and clenches his fist in his pocket and walks on. He takes a sudden turn and finds himself face to face with death. The cold feeling races up his spin and fills his body to the brim with a chill that is much colder then the gentle breeze.
Is this my end or what would be my final turn until I die? Face to face with death flashes over his face as he stands still. Oddly enough no fear is showing but man can’t see inside the human body. The barrel starred at me like a third eye to the man holding it. He held a bag in his left hand but what was in it wasn’t what concerned Vilas. Neither moved, and both looked for a grasp on the situation.
I would think by now he would have done something instead of just standing there. He slowly made a move in the wrong direction. Still grasping the gun in a hand that was beat red with pain and chill from the breeze. He stepped just to far and collapsed to the ground. For no reason at all he just fell. Vilas looked down at the man and nudged him a few times. He was out cold in the breeze. His bag was there and Vilas reached down keeping his eyes on the barrel. He opened the bag and found nothing.
Who robs a store and takes nothing? Someone who wasn’t looking for values but looking for answers.
He was taken down by the system. But it was that very same system that made him come for nothing. Vilas looked down at the barrel and slowly reached forward for it. He snapped back when a sudden movement struck him and sent chills up his spin again. He snatched the gun and took a few steps back. He opened the barrel to find nothing. No bullets and no threat of life of any kind was found.
The answers he is looking for must mean life or death. Or else he wouldn’t have carried a gun with any murder weapons.
Vilas looked back at the direction he turned. One wrong turn and he would have been safe, but he took the right turn. The right turn? He almost was killed and yet it was the right way to go. He chose the path he followed and not some over powering spirit. That same spirit could have very well killed that other troubled man, and yet he still fights with him self.
I wish I had a reason not to believe but it’s just my choice. I chose the path and almost got my self killed.
He turns back and finds the troubled man coming back to reality. Vilas walks away and ducks behind a small post to stay out of sight. The man has enough trouble and didn’t need to see him. He got up and looked down at the gun that was lying on the ground. He picks it up and looks at it in his hand. Tears start to flow from his eyes and rest on his cotton shirt. He takes the gun and places it in a trash can. He then slowly sits down on the curb and lets the tears flow. Vilas slowly backs away from the place of his feet and walks away.
I gave up trying to help, because I have to help my self. But that could have been one good turn that deserved dying for. He is troubled but so am I according to others. Yet I choose this life and he must chose as well. Just hope he doesn’t die for it. Last thing I need is the guilty of one less dying.
