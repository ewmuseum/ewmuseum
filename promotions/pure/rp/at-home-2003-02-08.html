At Home

8 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
At Home Written: 2/8/03 I was just sitting around in my Tokyo apartment one day last week. Anyway, I was watching a little bit of television. I was doing nothing of importance really. I did not have anything else to do at the time so I decided to sit on my couch and view some quality Japanese programming. That’s if you can call watching animated people who all do the same stupid things as well as sharks swimming around with laser beams attached to their heads quality programming in any way, shape, or form.
Hoshi: Geez. Japanese television kind of sucks. It’s really quite boring. Too bad I really do not have anything else to do today. All of a sudden, my little boy Renzo quickly comes walking up to me. It really would not have been much of a shock to me if he had not just been laying on the floor playing around with a few of his toys just a few seconds before.
Renzo: Hey baby, can I have some cacalate milk? Why did he refer to me as baby? Beats me. Sometimes I think he’s crazy. Maybe he got it from me. I say, “Hey Baby” to him all of the time. Hoshi: Huh? Renzo: Can I have some cacalate milk? To be honest, I did not think that he had said chocolate. It sounded like something else to me for some odd reason. Ca ca or something. You know, the word that most kids use for crap or shit. I guess I did not understand him is because sometimes what it sounds like he said is different from what he really said.
Hoshi: What? The child begins to get a little impatient with me, which really is not all that unusual. Renzo gets impatient all the time. All kids get impatient very often. Hopefully,
Renzo will grow out of it and become more patient as he gets older.
Renzo: Can I have some cacalate milk, mom? Peas! Peas! Peas! The talk of ca ca and peas was very strange to me. Fortunately by this time I was able to understand him.
Hoshi: Oh...Do you want some chocolate milk, honey? Renzo: Yeah! Yeah! Cacalate milk! Cacalate milk! Hoshi: Calm down. I’ll get you some chocolate milk. Just wait a few minutes. It’s then that I decided to get up to make him what he wants. I walked to the kitchen, which really was not much of a walk because we live in a somewhat small apartment, and opened up one of the cabinets. I quickly got out a small blue cup, because Renzo is too small for a bigger cup of course, and shut the cabinet. Then I opened up the refrigerator and take out the milk.
There wasn’t much milk in the jug but there was enough to pour Renzo a cup of it, which I did. After pouring the cup of milk I walked over to one of the cabinets and opened it. No chocolate syrup. I tried each and every one of the other cabinets with the same result. No chocolate syrup.
Hoshi: This is not good. Renzo: What is not good, mommy? He heard me. Great. I knew I should not have said anything like that out loud. Hoshi: Nothing, honey. I walk back over to where I had set the milk down and hand it Renzo. Hoshi: Here you go. Renzo: But mommy, there’s no cacalate in my milk. Hoshi: I know, honey. Just drink it, okay? Renzo: I want cacalate in my milk, mommy. Hoshi: We don’t have any chocolate, baby. I had to tell him. I thought it would get him off of my back. Unfortunately, that was not the case.
Renzo: I WANT CACALATE IN MY MILK!!!!!! Hoshi: Honey... Renzo: I WANT CACALATE IN MY MILK!!!!!! Hoshi: Baby... Renzo: I WANT CACALATE IN MY MILK!!!!!! Hoshi: WE DON’T HAVE ANY CHOCOLATE SO JUST DRINK YOUR MILK AND BE QUIET!!!!!!!!!! I did not want to yell at him. I almost never do. However, he was being so annoying that I just could not help myself. It got him to be quiet at least.
Everything was quiet for the rest of the day, actually. He decided to drink his milk without having chocolate in it and I went back to watching
Japanese television until I finally fell asleep. And so ended my sitting around in my Tokyo apartment last week.
