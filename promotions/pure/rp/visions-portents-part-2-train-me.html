Visions & Portents (Part 2): Train Me..

10 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Visions & Portents (Part 2): Train Me.. ﻿Does everyone really have a price? Perhaps, the better question is where does one draw the line between the shining pillars of morality, dignity, and tradition and the comfort of wealth, power, and prestige? At what point does selflessness get tossed to the wayside in favor of personal survival? Whatever the answers may be, it is a clear distinction that these questions are only ones asked by those who now live in a decision theyve already made. Whether they suffer in a pit of despair comforted only by the maintained stature of their pride in a refusal or lavish guiltily in the sin of acceptance through their coalescence, they are burdened by their choice. This is where we find Trang Van Chien. Though his body is pampered and refined through an expensive training program and relaxation regimen by his new manager and patron, Sir Gregory Rhine, his mind is a battlefield of inner conflict and hesitation.
He knows he cannot change the path he is on now that he has promised himself to the cause of the World Socialist Party without accepting major repercussions, yet he finds himself distracted physically by the callous and whimsical way in which he was goaded into making his decision. He questions the motives and the principals of his new confidant, and while he has never been one to be unduly paranoid, he cannot help but wonder what schemes brew in the mind of the polished westerner who oversees almost every aspect of his life now. What future awaits the two of them in the sorted world of international politics and Puroresu Wrestling? Only as the mists of the future disperse will we know for certain.. The relatively cramped apartment Mr. Rhine had provided for Chien was a palace in the eyes of a man whod spent his life in an enclosed cinder block hut back in the land of Vietnam, and then in slummy backwater bunkhouses when he joined the Peoples Army, then finally in infrequent homes of other junkyard federation wrestlers when he first journeyed to Japan. To think this place was his alone, and he was in control of it was a heady euphoric sensation of pride and achievement. He kept the place impeccably clean despite doing all his eating, bathing, and most of his training within its boxed in walls. He only left on rare instances to get some fresh air, meet with Mr. Rhine, or pick up his groceries. The other tenants living around him were nice enough people, although they picked up quick that Chiens Japanese was about as good as their Vietnamese. Whenever they saw one another theyd exchange friendly nods and smiles, but nothing more. This all culminated into a sort of isolated feeling for Chien. Now that hed left the indies behind and was competing in the cream of the puroresu wrestling crop he rarely saw any of his old acquaintances from his amateur days, and had yet to meet any of PUREs roster formally. He was all alone. Just him and the verbal babbling of Japanese television to keep him company. Hed take the opportunity to watch some as he trained, trying to piece together his grasp of the language and keep his body in tune at the same time. Today, he was doing just that. He sat in front of the TV on one of those rowing machines going nowhere with the extreme tenacity of an athlete as he watched a local form of entertainment called anime. It was unlike anything hed viewed on television before; cartoons with serious stories and a few times, sex and violence. It immediately raked him in as an addicted viewer. In fact, so addicted was he to the program that he barely heard the sound of the door to the apartment being opened behind him. He was only snapped out of his anime reverie by the hauntingly familiar sound of Mr. Rhines voice. Chien, Im glad to see youre keeping fit, he said in an almost sarcastic tone, I was afraid that with you spending so much time cooped up in this rather modest accommodation that youd begin to sag. Chien continued rowing as he looked over his shoulder at Rhine. The westerner was decked out in his usual apparel of a modest blue suit, black shoes, and the blue star lapel on the collar of his white undershirt. He always felt the twinge of self-consciousness whenever they met, more so now that he was only dressed in his sweat ridden workout clothes. Something inside him though scoffed at this idea and focused more on the subtle fact that Mr. Rhines body was nearly devoid of any muscle definition. This nagging sensation that had been taking a sort of mental precedence ever since he accepted the deal made it clear to Chien that of the two he was clearly physically superior. Without the money and the party to back him up, Rhine was just another hulking barbarian. These thoughts surprised Chien when they popped into his mind, and he immediately averted his gaze back to the television and converted all the negativity into intensity toward rowing the thick metal half-oars back and forth. When Chien did speak, it was edgewise and obviously distracted. I keep in shape in my own way, Mr. Rhine, he said, I hope you appreciate that and will not attempt to interfere. Oh no, I wouldnt dream of it Chien, Rhine said slyly with the smile once again decorating his mouth, I merely wished to come by, check up on you, and discuss your upcoming four-way brawl for the PURE Asian Title. I have it well in hand, Mr. Rhine, Chien replied solemnly, I will bring all the attention to your cause that youll need with my victory. Thats the kind of self-assured answer I like to hear, Chien. Rhine retorted as he stepped beside the rowing Chien with his arms folded, However, for my sake can we simply discuss how you feel things will transpire the night of the event? Simply, I will enter the ring along with the other three men, Chien said without a hint of doubt, and after what will hopefully be a spirited competition I will leave it as the winner. I see. So you fear nothing from the racial abusive American Ali Khadaffi? Or the technically skillful Cege Hayabusa? Rhine inquired with a critically upraised eyebrow. Hearing this, Chien immediately stopped rowing and looked straight up into the eyes of Mr. Rhine. They stared in silence for a long moment, the harsh face that permeated Chiens features almost striking Rhine fearfully. Finally, he answered in a quietly intense tone. I am beyond fear, Mr. Rhine. For these men I will bring a pain they have yet not been able to convince of. I will adapt to every maneuver they attack me with, and when the match has ended and the smoke has cleared. I will be the undisputed victor of my first PURE competition. As Chien finished his statement of action he slowly turned back to the television and returned to rowing. Ah, I take it is because you are better than they are? Rhine said in an instigative tone, the smile on his face broadening only slightly. No, Chien replied flatly, It is because I am a true warrior and they are merely sportsmen. When they have spilt blood, then I will see them as my competitive equals. Until then, they are all blind men trying to define the color of a cats fur simply by the way it feels. Now, if that was all you came for, Mr. Rhine, I would very much appreciate you allowing me to get back to my training. Of course, Chien, of course. was Rhines timid reply as he slowly exited in much the same stealthy fashion as he had arrived. As Chien continued rowing, his nerves burning with the pain of fatigue as the hours of training began to wear on him, stared almost through the television to the wall beyond. He fought the images of his bloody past, the conflicts of real battle he had forsaken in place of this more dignified substitute. He knew there would always be something he held over every competitor in PURE. He was not someone looking to build a future, he was a man looking to demolish a past.
