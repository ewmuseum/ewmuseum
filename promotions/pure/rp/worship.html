Worship...

12 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Worship... Honor. No, not a definition of the word but the feeling that one receives from it. Honor is what drives Bushido. Honor is a feeling, or a way to live life. Honor is what makes people unable to say his full name until his training is over. Honor has taken his life, and somewhat turned it in reverse. But if honor were a man, he would look like the one that they call Little Soldier Our scene opens on a shot of a lone candle. Flickering A candles life can be snuffed out so easily, gone in an instant, but never re-lit. Is a humans life the same? Are we all just here for our one moment of glory, never to be lit again and never to have the flame inside us burn? If one embraces Buddhathen the flames grows long and prosperous. The camera pans out to show a very dim scene, something looking like an abbey from medieval times, but up front is a statue of a large Buddha, the gold fading from the years of corrosion and exposure. In front of it kneels a man known to fans of PURE as Bushido, a man who was once called Sukoshi Gunsotsu. We also see that the candle from before lies lit on top of a statue of a small dragon. Bushido lifts his head up, seeing Sensei Ko standing off to the side, leaning on the wall. Sensei, do you not worship the all powerful one? His sensei chuckles and calls back the student, You still have much to learn, young one. I will keep my worship in my way, and you in yours. Bushido looks back at him, his eyes wondering and searching deep into the mans soul. How, though? I see Buddhas soul in front of me, and I see you with no homage towards him. Is that not blasphemy master? His master raises his eyebrows, his interested piqued by the young students questions. Do you trust me, Little Soldier? Bushido nods as Sensei Ko leans back against the wall, smoking his pipe while watching Bushido rise. The young man bows as low as possible in front of the giant golden statue and then rises. As he rises, he pulls out his sword from its sheath and steps up to right in front of the enormous statue. Wielding the sword skillfully, Bushido runs the hoary blade over his palm, going over what seems to be a line etched into the memory of his skin. Blood drips PitPat The blood from his hand drips onto the lap of the Buddha as he reaches down to kiss the statue, never flinching from the cut. He lets the blood flow until no more can fall, at which time he kisses the statue and stares down at the dragon, the flame burning more limply now. Dragons Sensei Ko laughs out loud as he hears his students voice talking about a mythical creature. What does that mean, apprentice? Bushido looks down at the cut on his hand as he explains, Dragons are the most honorable of creatures. They do not cheat, and they never act dishonorably. Ko nods, but still not understanding what this has to do with anything. Thenthose Chinese peasants know what they do as they disgrace Japan and Buddha. Ko just looks on now, understanding what Bushido means. This week, they learn respectthey learn honor. Just as Bushido finishes, a wind suddenly blows the candle out Whoosh!
