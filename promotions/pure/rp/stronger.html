-Stronger- ~*~ Fear is another word for ignorance. People are scared of what they dont know. One thing Im aware theyre scared of is me. Why the hell would they be scared of this Hell Katwho stands at only 511 and 165 with a toned body, whos got a face thats real easy on the eyes, you ask? Im not the biggest or strongest person aroundthere are plenty of men still twice my size who could probably break me in half. There are plenty of guys who could crush me in a ring with merely their hands. Hell, some seven-footer could crush me with his weight. Fuck it, my own DWO presidentCallisto Banewhos also a wrestler in OSW, could probably crush my skull in with her muscular thighs. She stands 6 inches taller than me and a hundred pounds heavier. Im not easy to defeat in matches, but at the same time, Im not indomitable. But then what the fuck do people find so intimidating, so different about me? Lets just leave it at my looks, for one. When I first came out in wrestling, people would look at me like, theres a hot chick. I used to have my blue locks light honey brown, and Id dress pretty cute. Id come down to the ring in a little dark blue velvet dress that barely covered my tight ass, and some knee-high boots. Id have a nice, ethnic tan, but would generally look like your average pretty little all-American girl. The scariest thing about me, to them, was probably my love for dark lipstickoccasionally, even blackand my dog collar fetish. But no one really minds that...maybe I was just some Prep playing Goth for the week. It could just be a gimmick. Of course, later, my clothes got less cutesy and girlyand simply more punk, and scarier...but thats another story. Sure, I was a bit taller than most females, but sos Stacy Keiblerand who finds her intimidating? Like Hell, people could tell this much I was no Chyna. So, I wasnt this butch little thing that no one wanted to look at, never mind touch. I had some pretty good muscles there. I started out just managing guts back in my first days of pro wrestling. So people had no idea what was in store. When they signed me as a female to the company, people had their usual reactions. Oh, another T&A manager...I wonder who theyre gonna hook her up with? Then when Im announced as a female wrestler, its likeOh, heres this little piece of eye candy. Theyll probably book her in some mud wrestling matches and Bikini Contests with other managers, and then theyre through with her. Then she can go back to managing and being a valet- cause female wrestling dont bring in the ratings. I mean, she has this whole banging body and blow-dried hair thing going. No one wants to see her getting all sweaty or whatnot fighting! Only catfights get ratings!! Then came the real ball breaker. My first few federations had no female wrestlers. The only females in the company were prissy little interviewers and valets and managers and the occasional girlfriend. As in, none of them were active in the ring, unless they were cat fighting with each other in matches. So my president would come out with this cute little ideathat this sweet little thing named Ms. Gina Muniz was trained to fight... Men. Now that was too much for them. The idea of a woman with a full time career of fighting men? No, no, nothatll kill the ratings!! No one wants to see women active in the ringunless theyre pulling off each others clothes in cat fights!! Oh, please. So, when they were done laughing, theyd book me against jobbers. Yea, thats right. Low carders... New comers... Mere, pathetic no ones. Did I ever get a main eventer? Hell no...Not even a mid-carder! Okay, for a while, you could argue it was because I was just a new comer. But after months of paying my duesas any wrestler has toI was still down their, doing those little dark matches or those low little matches that merely start off shows. The kinds of matches no one really gives a flying fuck about. And, after a month or so of paying dues, a wrestler usually gets a shot at a title. Any title, even if its a jobber title...a cruiserweight...whatever the case is. Id have to say, for any wrestlermale or femaleeven if I was inexperienced at the time, I wasnt all that bad. I had spent months kicking ass and nuts by then. I suppose Id have opened a few eyes. Yes, I did get a few people to sit up straight. Hell, I bet I got a few retired; Old Schoolers to spit out their drinks watching me fight. I even went over, though a heel, with a handful of fans. The idea that a female, who looked harmless at a single moments look, could make you do a double take in the ring...and kick your teeth in as well as the big boys... ...If not better. At the same time, I was booked in jobber angles. I was booked with jobbers in the most insulting, pathetic angles. Hell, even Stephanie McMahon could have written better storylines then I was in. I remember clearly facing this dude who held the Womens Title in one of my earliest federationsin an Evening Gown match. Not to mention that there were no other femalesLord knows whyso yours truly was stuck facing him in a joke of a feud. Not even to bother pointing out how many times I had to job to him in several pointlessly annoying matches. I remember even having to lose to him in a Womens Title matcha Bikini Contest, mind you. So, was this what I had worked so hard for? Was this the dream I had worked for since watching Shawn Michaels in 92, as a preteen? Was this what I had believed I could make work, as soon as I stepped into a wrestling school in New York City 3 years ago? What I had been thinking to make of my self by trying out for the all-boys wrestling team in my high school? Or, far before itwhen I had backyard wrestled with friends at a younger age and broke my pelvis bone while being power-bombedhad I imagined this was what would come from it? Was this the dream I had so persistently, unwaveringly carved out for myself by training at places dominated by malessuch as my high school wrestling teameven as I was laughed at? Mocked and ridiculed for? Told to my face Id never made it, and talked of behind my back for? Was this what I had been hoping and striving for when I brushed myself off and tried again? When I kept on going, no matter what people said or thought about me? Was this the reward for my sacrifice? What I had deserved for the same determination, blood, sweat and tears I had given, that any man has put into the sport got?
Was this honestly what I had busted my ass for? It was hard to find work as a serious female wrestler, so in several companies, I stayed merely for the reason that I may not get a job like this again. Of coursed, I continued to work and train and muscle up, paying more of the dues than I honestly needed to. I stayed, in a hope that something would come of my sacrificein hopes that someone would give me some respect, or at least some recognition. The thought that eventually Id get a shot at more than the Womens title...that is, when I did come across federations with women. It never happened. So I just picked up and left when I got the feeling Id never get the pushes I believe Id ever deserve. Some people just seem to be living in the past...A time when women have a place in wrestling, besides standing behind their man and looking pretty. Thats not my job, and Id never be happy doing just that. I usually got booked with other women, if given any good matches at all. For some reason, I didnt feel satisfied in those matches. It was as if I wasnt good enough to rough it out with the guys. Like they were above my level...I just couldnt fuck with the big boys anymore. It said to me, go play with the girls. Youre not good enough to wrestle men, or go for any of their titles. Sadly enough to say, in most federations, I never got farther pushes than shots at the Womens, the Hardcore and Lightweight/Cruiserweight belts. Usually, I did shock everyone and bring those low/ mid-carder titles home...but it wasnt enough satisfaction. I dont need an ego to know Im above wrestling jobbers and women only. It takes merely a good look around to see that female wrestlers these days have come up recently from their manager-eye candy-and-cat fighting-only days. They dont belong being limited to facing each other anymore. Recently Ive seen a lot of improvement while searching for a non-chauvinistic federation. Its an improvement...a big step forward. It almost brings a smile to my facing...seeing that some federations have seen it to be not a problem to have female Cruiserweight, Hardcore, and even Intercontinental champions. But the big, taboo idea is having a female World Champion. As in, to say, out of all these talented, tough monsters of men...
We let a woman have the World Belt? This is the one person we have representing our company...and for it to be a woman? After all, the World title is the crown jewel of a company. It holds the most weight, in all senses of the word. The most respect, the most heat, the most reverence, the most envy...all of that, putting aside the idea that its the biggest title literallyin size and weight.
Then to have a woman representing everything the company strives for, respects, and wants to become...Hell no. What was the point in suffering through all these horrible bookings and storylines? They were just waiting for me to get scared and quit...to give up. I think I know why Ive had to put up with a lot of this...As if they fed me all this crap until they believed it would become too much for me...like I had no endurance for pain and disrespect, with me being a woman.
Maybe their true intention with booking me like that wasnt underestimation... I think it was to degrade me. To break me. Eventually, I stumbled across a federation this April, where women werent look down on as much. They were treated more equally, even. The Owner and President were both female, if that put more weight into the idea. That very company...that started out as a little nothing...which is now known as Dominant Wrestling. A respectable company, that can get anyone to do a double take watching...if not be instantly hooked by. Sure, my first few matches were against other women...but eventually I got a shot at the Tag Team titles, with my male partner; against another tag team. There it wasa new concept for me...to be taking a step back from working alone, and go with a partner for a tag team title. Hey, it definitely was more respectable than a Womens title in most peoples eyes. After a while, Callisto Bane recognized I had some talent, and some time in May, I was booked in a World Title Rumble with four others for the vacant crown jewel [as the former one walked out]. One other female was even in that rumble. I didnt walk out victorious, but that was no downfall to me. It only gave me more of a hope...as if all I had endured was just a step ladder up to that point. It made it all far more worth it. My first World Title shot...even if I lost it, and it was in a rumble. It, in my opinion, was long overdue...but it happened. I would go on to have one Intercontinental title reign...and keep that Tag Team belt with two different partners in a monopoly from April til August...and have one more World Title shot... Win or loss, it was finally satisfaction and recognition. The namewhether the Hell Kat or Gina Munizfinally had significance. I finally had the credibility as a wrestler I believed I deserved. So, a month or so, I went and enrolled myself in a tryout for the PURE tournaments. Why? To get myself some more widespread recognition...Im not strapped down to competing in DWO at all. Ive had enough of a feel of their talent...and now I look for a different taste. That I got when I faced Tim Walden in round one. They put me in a match with a dude they referred to...I hope, jokingly, as a perennial jobber. I laugh at that title...Underestimated, yes. Not taken seriously enough, true. Was he not the man who defeated PCWs Jeremy Howard at one point? And is not FSW considered a tougher federation than DWO to get into and do well in? And when I researched and wrestled this Walden, I wasnt surprised to discover he wasnt all that of a pussy hes made out to be by FSW. Hes definitely no talent less jobber. And as I do not consider myself a jobber...if I find him competition, he is definitely talented in my eyes. So, he ranted about being unrecognized and being considered a jobber in his promo? Believe me, Walden; Ive been in your shoes. Ive been considered a joke, a jobber...Ive been in just as pathetic storylines. So Ive decided to give you a nice, long walk in my shoes. I could go on and bore you more, but this is just to see that the road to becoming the feared, acknowledged Hell Kat has been a long one. It just so happens that I was the one managed to advance to become one of the Elite Eight in a matchup of one of those remaining in the PURE Ultimate Jr. Tournament. By chance, luck, or skill...so be it. Here I am, goddamned proud, whether I go on to advance in this round...or not. With that... I welcome to my own little Hell, my career of hard knocks. Just wait til you hear me out about my personal life. They shouldve known better than to treat me like that... Cause look at me now. Like they say... What doesnt kill you only makes you stronger. ~*~
