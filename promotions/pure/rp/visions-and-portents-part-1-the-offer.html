Visions & Portents (Part 1): The Offer..

7 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Visions & Portents (Part 1): The Offer.. How would you spend the last few months of your life? Certainly in the greater scope of things it is but a mere granule of sand in eternitys hourglass. Still, to be able to know with clarity of vision that beyond the near future your fate has been sealed contains within it a sense of contentment and certainty. Do not be confused, for a man to live every day of his life in such a mindset would make him ultimately reckless an unproductive. However, to tell this same man that his end is coming, and he has time to prepare for it, and you single-handed sharpen his focus to a daggers precision. Also, do not think that death is ones only key to jumpstarting enlightenment. Those who possess and maintain this certain strength of will can apply a razor like focus and drive to any cause or profession to which they adhere. It is a gift rarely seen and oftentimes overlooked. Trang Van Chien came to Japan with a dream to wrestle puroresu that was so powerful that nothing within the bounds of creation could stop it from being fulfilled. He had suffered through poverty, humiliation, pain, suffering, and abuse. Yet somehow the dream to compete, to honor the sport was so mighty that it carried him when he thought he would fall, it feed him when he starved, and dulled his senses when pain had beset him on all sides. Do not think of Chien as a character worthy of sympathy, he knew with full cognition the prices he would pay and the sacrifices life would demand of him in exchange for a chance to be immortally remembered as both warrior and showman, as both vicious fighter and resplendent artist. What fate serves up before him is both his reward and his punishment, and like a man he accepts both with equal amounts of joy and humility. This is what it takes to start out as wrought iron and be forged under the burning ambers of flame. To have all of ones flaws beaten of them until they become entirely..PURE. The International Hotel was never a place Trang Van Chien, a burgeoning local wrestler, would ever expect to find himself invited, but there he was amongst the flowering crop of the West as they surveyed with hungry eyes and clattered in their babbling tongues to one another of the East and its many mysteries yet understood by their kind. Though hed always shunned public opinion as a blueprint of existence he couldnt help but feel a little self-conscious among these suit-clad gigantic barbarians in his simple black shirt and gray cargo pants. Stress mounted his back like a curious ape and sat there with its weight unevenly distributed a bit and causing him to sweat profusely. The tension was finally enough for him to risk giving his invite another once over before some bouncer subjected him to the shame of being ejected. As casually as possible he bypassed the main foyer hed entered from and preceded down a solitary utilitarian hallway to take up a stationary position beside the door to the men lavatories and withdrew the small piece of stylized paper from his pocket. It read as follows: Esteemed Mr. Trang Van Chien, Your presence is requested for a meeting of the utmost importance in the near future. While I cannot afford to risk revealing too much over such a primitive form of correspondence, I will divulge that I can be found residing at the International Hotel downtown from where you are now. There will be a party in progress, look for a Caucasian man with black hair, brown eyes, and a blue star lapel. That is all I can say, for now. From the tone and dialect of the later he could tell it was indeed the handiwork of a western gentleman but it also seemed extremely cryptic in nature. He prided himself on possessing very adjusted reflexes, but somehow an agent of this mysterious memo writer had managed to stuff an invitation into his front jacket pocket while he went along ignorant of his new cargo until he later removed it at his simple apartment home by chance. Upon reading it he had immediately dropped everything and made his way here, to the high rise spires of the International Hotel. Now, as he stood outside the mens bathroom re-reading the tiny paper shred he began to convince himself the whole thing had been a hoax. It wasnt the first time hed been the butt of cruel jokes at the hands of people who didnt understand or respect the culture and ways of others. Still, why go through the trouble of learning his name and sneaking something into his coat just to send him on a wild-goose chase through the Wests elitist class? It didnt matter; some people would go to any absurd end to make a fool of someone whom they or their people wronged by. As this mindset overcame Chien he immediately began to stomp his way back out through the same busy foyer on his way single-mindedly for the doorway. There, out of a swelled group of Caucasian businessmen a single one emerged and placed himself directly in Chiens path in a fluid motion. Chien would have simply ducked past him as just another similar face among this crowd, had it not been from a moments hesitation causing light to glint off a shining object on the mans collar, a blue star lapel. It caught Chien in an almost dreamlike wave of surprise. As he stood speechless, the taller Caucasian smiled warmly and gave a very slight nod and bow to Chien. I have been expecting you for some time, he said in almost perfect Vietnamese, Trang Van Chien, its certainly an honor to meet you. Chien returned the bow with a more dignified one of his own before straightening himself and addressing the unnamed westerner in a very calm collected voice. I thank you for both your reception and courteous invitation, Chien replies politely, However, Im afraid while you seem well-informed on myself, I cannot even return your introductions. Of course, Im sure you have many questions for me, says the man with a sudden cheerful smile, However these matters are best discussed away from the keen ears of such gossiping folk as these. Come, well take an early luncheon on the terrace. Chien nods and follows him as he carves a path for them through all the crowded masses until they arrive upon a spacious covered balcony with a scenic view of the Tokyo Bay. The man with the blue lapel takes a seat and motions for Chien to take the other, which he does slowly. Moments later an English waiter arrives and takes both orders through the blue lapelled businessmen. As the waiter scurries off to his task, this man straightens his suit and sets his elbows on the table, his hands being steeped in front of him as he begins his pitch. Chien, I know more about than you could possibly imagine, he says in an almost cocky tone, I know about your life in Vietnam, what you went through to get out of there, what youve been doing since you got here, and why youve decided to stay indefinitely. I understand and do not doubt your information, Chien replies now in a somewhat agitated manner, but all I care to know now is who are you and why have you called me here? A direct man of action, the man remarks, Thats what I like about you Chien. However, before I can reveal my identity there are other things I wish to discuss. As I was saying, I know all about you, and most importantly I know about your passion to compete in this lands amazing spectacle they call; Puroresu. As he continues into his speech their food arrives, and while Chien slides his dish to the side and focuses all his attention on what the man has to say, the businessman takes infrequent bites and refuses to speak until hes swallowed each, following ages old western mores of tact and manners. Yes, I know how youve been punishing your body night after night in these urbane hardcore menageries where the bloodthirsty among these people exalt in their sickened delusion. Yet, even after allowing this to scar your body beyond all salvageable recognition, you still proceed to compete. Frankly, its an amazing tale of inspiration in these dark times. Now then, let me introduce myself, I am Gregory Rhine. Chien nods to him in turn with the one received. I am glad youve finally decided to divulge your name to my, Mr. Rhine. Now, Id much appreciate the same explanation of what this lecture has to do with our meeting. Yes, I was getting to that. Now, let me first explain myself in the context of this situation. Im English by birth, but I must admit my family has always been entrenched in Asian society. Ever since Japan open their borders at American gunpoint in the 19th century, my family immigrated. Before then, we could only watch from afar, from our high castles in the Old World and wait for mysterious Asia to finally let her doors open to the other half of the planet. Anyway, like the rest of my line, Japan and Asia in general has seated itself in my blood. I could never leave this country even if I wanted to. I feel like Im almost a part of it myself, and in that sense I also feel obligated to this nations well being. Thus, I have dedicated myself to preserving the rights of the Japanese people, and in fact expanding them. Chien, I represent an organization named The World Socialist Party. We are all dedicated to saving the common people of Asia and the world from being held beneath the boot heel of the rich and oppressive. Of course, Im sure you can understand that our message is not often times very well received by the bourgeois industrialists and festering deposed monarchs of both this nation and others. We need solid spokesmen for our cause, role models for our members, etc. And here is where you come in. Chien could easily see through this indoctrination, and looked ready to just up and leave at any moment. Yet, something kept him settled firmly in his seat, probably the prospect of a sponsor. We know how popular pro-wrestling is back in Europe and the States, continues Mr. Rhine, but here in Japan they take it to another level. They dont just cheer or boo, they become almost full immersed within the stories told by these athletes. If one of them were to espouse the same values that the WSP did, they would be a great boon to our cause. You dont need me to get your message out, Chien replies with an amused smile upon his lips, Im sure you could find an ad agency to plaster the town with flyers, get TV spots, set up rallies. Im just another grappler, sir. Im not a revolutionary. On the contrary, Chien. Mr. Rhine shoots back with a finger pointed accusingly across the table, You are indeed a revolutionary, of the highest caliber. Your nation beat back the American juggernaut while others could only follow in lock step compliance with it. Youre also a former member of the Vietnamese Peoples Army. All these are what drew us toward picking you as our spokesman here in Japan. Im honored that youd offer me such an honor, but Im jus- Hes quickly cut-off by Rhine, mid-sentence. Is money the issue? Certainly, it always is, he says with that same smile playing across his features, Surely you understand that taking this deal would mean generous grants made by our organization to you. Im sure your current salary is enough to live off of, but certainly if you were to join with us you could live comfortably here while sending something back to your family in Vietnam. Now, how does that suit you, my friend? Chien could only sigh at this mans overbearing attempts to gain his new spokesman. Though he knew the money would be just as helpful as Rhine had said, he decided it was best he explain the situation before they make any sort of commitment. Look, Mr. Rhine, as I said Im very honored youd consider me for this position, Chien tried to make things as clear as possible as he spoke, However, if you know so much about me you know where Im at now no one pays much attention to my politics as much as they do how much danger I can be put into in the ring. Upon hearing this Mr. Rhines smile spread from ear to ear as he began to fish something out of his inner coat pocket. Once more, Chien, I hate to correct you but once the contrary is true. I had one of my agents intercept your mail, and I found this. Im sure youll be pleased with it. He withdraws a letter and holds it toward Chien, but this only hardens the Vietnameses expression to a dour frown. Rhine picks up immediately and pulls it back toward him, opening it carefully. Ah, I suppose you cant read Japanese yet, quite alright, Rhine says politely as he pulls the letter from the envelope and begins to read it with that same smile upon his face, Its from the offices of the PURE federation, they want to offer you a contract. It was beyond anything Chien could have ever imagined, he wanted to grab the contract out of Rhines hand and clarify it himself, but he certainly could not read in the vernacular of the local populace. He could only stew joyously in his seat and allow the smile to force its way onto his face. He was in an almost euphoric state as Rhine went on talking across from him. Im sure your speech in Japanese is also somewhat limited, therefore allow me to offer my services as a translator, and manager. Youll need someone to look out for your interests, and certainly I am that man. Do we have a deal? In the confusion, joy, and sheer spark of the moment only one thing came to Chiens mind in response. Yes, we have a deal. Then, as Rhines grin grew and he began to buzz past Chien all the details off his intricate plans and schemes, we slowly fade off to black.
