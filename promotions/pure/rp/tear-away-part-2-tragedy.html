Tear Away // Part.2 // Tragedy

Tear Away // Part.2 // Tragedy There I was... Stood at the forefront of a new dilemma. Jap-Tsai, my long time friend, had just been hospitalized, and as I received the shallow news, I made my way down to the hospital. The journey to the hospital was a time for me to regret some of the things I may have said or done in the past. I tried to remember the good times Jap-Tsai and myself have had, and as they all came flooding back, I found myself in overdrive. Each and every moment brought a shed of tear. I never fought he meant this much to me. Perhaps, in life, the ones who mean the most are the ones who bring a tear to your eye, a smile to your face, a gleam to your eye. Regardless of how I was feeling, I put Jap-Tsai to the front of my mind. I figured that I owe him something after thirty years of his own time wasted on me excelling in the wrestling industry. As time passed by, I approached the hospital. Standing outside with my legs trembling and my heart beating in a quick manner, I took the time to consume what I was facing. The hospital, from the outside, looked like a place where the souls of the dead are kept. It didnt look nice; it was more like hell redefined. Of course, this is just the way I see it. Having stepped inside the hospital, I was greeted by my wife, Catrina, who was already there, waiting patiently for me to arrive. Thank god youre here, Shirata She greeted me. I took her into my arms gently and held my hand firmly around her head as I gave her a gentle kiss on the cheek. She seemed upset, and emotional, like me, probably because having introduced her to Jap-Tsai a few years ago, she had a soft spot for his humor. I could never realize why she had such a soft spot for it. I usually put on a fake chuckle to show good manners, but in all honesty, Jap-Tsai can be a funnyman at times. As time stood still, and the anticipation of the news came from the doctor, we were beginning to grow tired. Sitting on the bench in so much distress was highly irritating. I couldnt help but think what they were doing with him, and what is actually wrong with him. It seemed as though my luck had finally arrived when the doctors promptly communicated with us and let us know the condition of Jap-Tsai. Hes in a stable condition, Mr. Rinjiro. Theres no immediate cause for panic; well be keeping him detained within the hospital for further assessments. In the mean time, you can go visit him. Said the Doctor. I suppressed a smile of relief and joy. Catrina and me walked the corridors of the hospital. The foul smell of death ran through the corridors, you could almost tell by looking into the rooms who was going to die. They had pale faces, glazing eyes looking up at the ceiling, and of course, they looked the pictures of bad health. Finally, we arrived outside Jap-Tsais assigned hospital room...
