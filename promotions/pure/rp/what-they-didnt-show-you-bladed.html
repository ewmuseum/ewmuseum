What they didn't show you: BladeD

March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What they didn't show you: BladeD Written: Joint RP #33 is walking down the hallway in the direction of Hoshi Akagawa's locker room. Back in the opposite direction, Dave and Steve are cheering for themselves over getting a match. #33 casually walks down the way. As she turns a corner, she sees PCW-touring wrestler BladeD, doing push-ups in the middle of the hallway. She looks at him and shakes her head, laughing as she passes by. BladeD takes offense.
BladeD: You have a problem? The ninja stops, turning to him. #33: It's just that I find it funny how someone as utterly useless as you manages to keep a position within PURE.
BladeD ceases his pushups and stands to his full height of 6'4', using the towel over his neck to dry off the perspiration from his face and chest. His grey-fatigued shirt hangs halfway from his right back-pocket.
BladeD: I am sick of everyone walking past me, joking at me for losing to that Ho chick. She steps up to him, pointing a finger in his face. #33: Her name is Hoshi Akagawa. And I expected her to win. BladeD slaps away her hand and wipes off his arms. BladeD: I let her win. I always allow the women to beat me. I'm not the type of guy that wants to hurt women.
The ninja laughs once more. #33: You can't use that excuse. Women in the wrestling industry acknowledge and except any risk of personal injury that may befall them. You men, always trying to cover up for your own shortcomings. You lost because despite what you learned in America, you can't win a test of skill and discipline by building larger than necessary muscles and just throwing your weight around hoping for a victory.
BladeD: Hey, I take pride in my physical conditioning. #33: Good for you... but how much success has it gained you in a 'big-time' federation? You may have been able to victimize smaller American men who prescribe to the same methods as yourself in federations where they hire off the street. But here in Japan, it is very different. You must train, you must dedicate yourself to it; not take up wrestling because your previous jobs did not work out for you. This isn't a consolation. This isn't easy.
BladeD: I know that. She shakes her head. #33: Do you? You've been using the same methods, and it's not working for you. You make excuses, blame others... but you are the problem. You're just another big man who thinks that the world owes you something that you have never earned.
BladeD: I served my country.. #33: Interesting, then, that you're not there, but here? Perhaps your 'accompishments' are not as significant as you think they are?
BladeD cracks his knuckles. #33: But enough of dealing with you. I only attend to one set of riff-raff a night, and my time has already been taken. I would suggest you watch my performance. Hoshi and I shall show you, as well as the other untalented gaijin, how you should conduct yourselves in a PURE ring.
She turns away and walks off towards Hoshi's locker room.
