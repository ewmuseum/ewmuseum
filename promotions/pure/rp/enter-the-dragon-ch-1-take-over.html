Enter the Dragon: Ch. 1: Take Over

October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Enter the Dragon: Ch. 1: Take Over Written: Past Three Days ‘Hey guys get a look at this,’ Chi’ing says getting the attention of his two partners and their manager. ‘Here I’ll read it out loud for you…’
The four men are relaxing in a rented motel room in a small town in Japan. Chen is on one of the two beds watching ‘La Blue Girl’ grinning ear to ear, as each scene gets better and better. Chi’ing is sitting on the floor at the base of the bed that Chen is sitting on with a newspaper in his lap. Ming Kochi the Black Dragons Manager slash Advisor is sitting in the corner of the room on the phone wheeling and dealing with someone unimportant to name. The last member of the Black Dragons is Chiang Ho Nam and he is sitting in a chair on the balcony over looking a gorgeous purple sky as the sunsets. All three men have an ear turned to Chi’ing as he begins to read an article from the newspaper.
‘Japanese gangsters are being driven out of their own turf by shrewder and more ruthless Chinese gangs.
What is happening in Kabukicho, a red-light district, is a reflection of Japan's troubles in competing with China and other Asian countries in the last half-dozen years. For the same reasons that Nissan and Hitachi are in trouble -- high labour costs and inflexible management -- the bars and brothels of Kabukicho are passing into Chinese hands.
The Chinese is displacing even the yakuza, the Japanese gangsters who see themselves as the toughest businessmen.
“Our biggest problem is the rise of the Chinese mafia.” “The Chinese gangs are taking business from us in every area -- in prostitution, in gambling, in fencing stolen goods,” said one yakuza.
“The difference between us is that Japanese yakuza think of long-term business relationships, but the Chinese mafia thinks just of the short-term.”
Still bickering and distrustful because of the legacy of World War II, Japan and China compete for markets and influence around Asia.
Many Chinese and Japanese alike believe that Japan's dykes are crumbling and that in another decade, there will be several times as many Chinese in Japan as today -- for this is where the money is to be made.
“None of us like Japanese men,” said a 25-year-old Chinese woman from Shanghai who acknowledged working as a prostitute in Kabukicho for the last year.
“But this is business.” She takes classes in Japanese and English in the day, then goes to work in the bar from 9 pm to 5 am.
“It's tough, but I'm making a lot more money than I did in China. With the money, I hope I can go to the United States,” she said.
Despite strict immigration rules for the Chinese, their numbers in Japan are surging. The official figure has risen to 252,000, from 95,000 a decade ago.
Many of the Chinese are professionals -- engineers, software designers and bankers -- and attract little attention.
It is the thousands of gangsters and prostitutes clustered in Kabukicho who have become notorious, inflaming anxieties and hostilities towards the Chinese. One in particular is a force known as the Black Dragons. There are an estimated 400 members and more in Japan today that call them the Black Dragons.
The Japanese Yakuza complain that Chinese gangsters are multiplying wildly and are so brazen that they have taken to robbing Japanese mobsters at knifepoint or gunpoint.
The collapse of the “bubble economy” hurt Japan's huge sex industry. But Chinese bars and brothels pay employees less and are able to thrive despite the recession.
Chinese gangs also prospered partly because they have been quicker to adopt high technology, like equipment to forge passports and make fake magnetic-strip cards that fool pachinko-game arcades and allow the user to play and win prizes. They are now trying their hand in the wrestling industry as four high standing Black Dragon members are now competing under written contracts in Japanese based PURE.
Said the Yakuza: “Whether in fake magnetic cards or fencing stolen goods or even competing in a sport like wrestling, they go about things with a real system.”
“The Chinese mafia is very, very good at business.” One high standing leader of the Japanese Yakuza had this to say, “They have been taking Japan over piece by piece and what better way to spread their power than by touring Japan as wrestlers! They need to be stopped and they need to be stopped by someone that is Japanese!”
The four men have focused all their attention to Chi’ing as he finishes reading the article they all have smiles that matches the horn dog Chen with a smile ear to ear. Chi puts the paper down and stretches out his legs as he leans against the bed with his arms crossed behind his head.
‘Now isn’t that the truth?’ Ming remarks. ‘What?’ Chi’ing looks up to Ming and asks. ‘The Chinese are very, very good at business!’ Ming recites with a smirk on his face. ‘I don’t know about you guys but I am ready to kick some Nu Era ass!’ Chen remarks. ‘If they think Byron got a beating they better wait until we get out hands on them, right Chi?’
Chi’ing begins to stretch by spreading his legs in a sitting splits and reaching down grabbing the soles of his feet. He rolls up to his feet and gets in a fighting stance with a determined look on his face. ‘Oh man those two will wish they had never met! And when it is all over those two won’t even recognize each other!’
Chen and Chi begin to laugh as Ming just shakes his head and continues talking on the phone and Chiang Ho Nam just turns his attention back on the sun set and the purple sky with one thing on his mind…
Meanwhile Chen shifts on the bed to a cross-legged position with his hands in his lap and a smile on his face. ‘Hey you guys remember when we took the 36-Oaths?’
All of them get a glazed look in their eyes as each of them stare off into the past remembering the fateful day they went through the trials and underwent the 36 Oaths…
