Homeward bound no more...

8 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Homeward bound no more... The desert winds howl, hot and fast. But not as fast as the speck of black speeding across the desert floor, snaking its way down the ribbon of asphalt that connects this desolate wasteland with the rest of humanity. Slowly at first, but ever growing, a new sound rises above the constant pressure that comes from the wind. Shrill, yet rumbling. It rises to a fevered pitch before the speck of black close the indeterminable gap between here and there; and races by with all the fervor of a demon chasing after a wayward soul. Its amazing the ways you can describe tripping the hammer on a 2002 Aprilia Mille whilst speeding though the Infinity Valley in New Mexico. But any way you cut it, you want to do it fast. Before the droll of equidistant ridgelines to the left and right, and the road leading on well over the horizon clench you in its grasp and force you to submit to its arcane powers of mental fatigue. Time marches on, minute by minute, hour by hour. But to the minds eye, time stands still as you race headlong to no where. If there ever were such a place as hell on earth, this would be it. A land wider than the mind, sparely populated by travelers who succumbed to the valleys tedium, and forfeited the remainder of lifes journey. These people let slip of hope, and deigned themselves to eke out a life in the barren wasteland. The fortunate ones were unsuccessful. But into this nightmarish clime speed Kevin Sandusky, aka Ebola III. On a quest to fulfill a fathers request to make a final farewell to another. ::God Im glad Uncle Joey lent me the bike, this wouldve taken forever in the Olds. Hell, I could swim to Japan in less time than itll take me to finish this trip. How much longer?:: Kev took a peek at the trip meter, rolling though 875 miles round trip thus far. ::Only 125 more to go. Fine, I can make that in under 40 minutes. Lets get this over with.:: Kevin grits his teeth and twists the throttle again. Charging headlong into his greatest shame, at his stepfathers request. Hopefully for the last time. [flashback] It had been a great day. Everyone had come out to Joses little training facility in South Tucson for the farewell get together. All the guys from school, all the guys from the SWA circuit stopped in, even Uncle Joey had gotten some time off from the CCW schedule to come home for a few days. It was the standard tell an embarrassing story about Kev, then say how much youll miss him sort of deal. Mom cried, Jose beamed, Joey caught up with all the people hed missed in the last couple of years. A real Norman Rockwell kind of scene, through the skewed shroud of the wrestling business. After a hearty toast, a dirty limerick that Blackjack Slater had written especially for the occasion, and the round of handshakes and Youll do us proud, most people went on about their lives. The relatives all went home to watch the game, the friends from school all went out drinking, and mom took little Erik home to get to bed early. The sun drooped behind the western mountains as the lights went on over the old ring in the facility. Uncle Joey and Jose put Kevin through one last training session. Each man poured out every bit of knowledge they had learned over the years. Tricks learned over the years on the road as Ebola and Spanish Fly Jose Gonzales respectively. Sweat and pride mingled with the lingering anxiety that an ocean of separation brings to a family, in an euphoric moment of togetherness that Kevin could never forget. And as the midnight hour came to pass, and the work of the evening was done, it was Jose who brought up the subject. You have to tell him he said, one last time. Say your farewells, make your peace and let it slip from your mind. Uncle Joeys opinion was somewhat different of course. We could just bury him in the desert somewhere, its not like anyone would care. But in the end, Joses patience won out. One last request by the father who didnt have to be, but was anyway. Of course, Joey refused to be held completely out of equation, and demanded that Kevin take his bike. At least this way you can get it over quick. Just leave it at Ace Hawks place in LA before you fly out. Hell watch over it and till I can get out there to pick it up. [/flashback] The miles rolled by in flash, as the bike crept ever closer to the destination. Wagon Mound, NM. It wouldve been a one horse town, but the horse died of boredom and dehydration years ago. Kevin often wondered how hed find the man amidst the rusted hulks of abandoned trailers and barns. Uncle Joey suggested he follow the smell of cheap liqour and stale tobacco. He was right. Kevin rolled up slowly to the dilapidated, 5th wheel style trailer slowly. A rusted solid, mid 70's Dodge pickup stood watch in the dustbin that could be mistaken as a front yard. A tarantula scurried across the dash and the shattered glass remains of the truck's windshield, eyeballing Kevin as a possible threat to his microcosmic kingdom. A wafting of hazy blue-grey smoke lulled out the torn screen of the front door of the trailer, as a terribly scratched Santana album rang out in the background. The whole place reeked of cheap whiskey, with the stench distributed equally between the trailer and the pile of broken glass bottle near the tailgate of the Dodge. Kevin delibrately stalled out the engine of the bike, illiciting a reaction from inside the trailer. A perceptable groan arose, both within Kevin and within the trailer. Through the sun-faded curtains, an outline of a man could be seen stumbling across the floor of the trailer. The screen door flies open with a well more force than required, the door itself squealing in terror on it's rusted hinges from the shock. 'WHAT THE HELL DO YOU WANT!?!' There in the doorway he stood. Long, thin, and oily grey hair partially concealed his face. His t-shirt, sweat stained and straining from pressure of holding in the massive gut that lie underneath. He wore cut-off fatigue shorts that looked like that hadn't seen soap. water, or a day's disuse in 6 months. In his hand was a hlaf empty bottle of whiskey that stank like bile in the heat of the desert. The stench was only made worse by the stale smoke that poured forth from the man's lungs as he spoke. Yet another traveler who had given into the desperate hell of Infinity Valley. But this one deserved it. Kevin's eyes narrowed, his body grew rigid, and his nostrils flared even though he tried with all his might to seal them closed with sheer will against the scent of failure that surrounded this place and this man he had come to see.
