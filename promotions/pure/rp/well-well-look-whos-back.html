Well, well... Look who's back....

4 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Well, well... Look who's back.... After a length of time when PURE had been closed... it has opened up again under the guidance of one Sean Williams. Quite interesting that he didn't care enough about PURE to try to prevent it's temporary closure (let's just say its 'hiatus'). But at least he jumped in when he did, and now PURE will come back up to its feet. The phoenix project is what this next incarnation of PURE will be. Indeed, we shall rise up from the ashes of the former PURE, where but 30% of its roster actually gave a damn about the fed. I shall be returning, along with Mang-A-Hero, Inphino Blitz, and others. Miyagawa has a child on the way, and he'll be concentrating on raising his family. Noblest of reasons, I don't condemn him for not returning. This fed is about puroresu wrestling, but apparently the 20% owners want to try for some sports entertainment influence. That's bad business. That may go in America, but this will be Japan, and you can't do the same things there that you would in the US. Puroresu is untainted wrestling at its finest. Young men and women giving their hearts into wrestling... the spirit of competition, the thrill of battle, and putting on a wholesome show; that's what puroresu wrestling is about. That's why it's so unique in the world, and THAT'S why wrestlers like myself wish to be in this federation. I got my start in the business because of puroresu. Not by competing in it, but by watching a Michinoku Pro event on a trip to Japan. That was back when I still had sight. That was the only wrestling I had ever seen, but it was so exciting and engaging... it was so inspiring that I knew that I wanted to wrestle for a living. I knew that I, formerly having no direction in life whatsoever, had found my calling. And I finally get to live my dream and fulfill my wishes, while giving back to the industry and the style that made me who I am today. This could be a stretch, but I believe that I am what puroresu represents. I hold my honor codes in high priority. I don't work for fame or riches, I work because this is what I want to do. I've dedicated myself fully to this business. I've given my eyes to this business, and I go on; everyday in the morning I wake up, and no matter what happens during the day to put me in a good or bad mood, I'm still thankful that I have this opportunity and this talent to wrestle anywhere I want, but especially in PURE. The American wrestling fans may not appreciating, but the Japanese fans do. And look what I've accomplished in the past incarnation of PURE. First champion in PURE history. First Junior Heavyweight Champion. Only two-time Junior Heavyweight Champion. And look what I did with my time in PURE. Excellent work, the adoration of the fans... I beat some of the top names in wrestling, Asian or otherwise. Mang-A-Hero & Inphino Blitz, Jordan X Shabazz... PURE Tag Team Champions, PURE Asian Title Champion, respectively... And I, the Junior Heavyweight Champion at PURE's closure, was the only one who upheld any legacy of PURE by defending my title during the 'hiatus.' The others were content to rest on their laurels and wait for PURE to reopen, if it did. I, on the other hand, defended my title when I didn't even have to. Charlie Loc won the title in a 3-way match, pinning Alexander Isawa Miyagawa. So until PURE restarts and he'll be made to defend or vacate his title, he's the Junior Heavyweight champion. But fact remains he's only the champion because I agreed to defend a title I could've went ahead and mounted on my wall at home. So in the end, who's the more virtuous? All in all, there's nothing really to say, despite all the air I just wasted. I, arguably the best wrestler in PURE before its close, have returned. That's it. End of story.
