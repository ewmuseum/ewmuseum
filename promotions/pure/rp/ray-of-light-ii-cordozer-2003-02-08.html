Ray of Light - II; Cordozer

Ray of Light - II; Cordozer Written: 02.08.03 So all credit the honorable... The question in all of our outcomes depending on this battle royal for the Heavyweight title slot is simple, you win you go onto greater pastures, but what if you lose? What if in all of the possible outcomes of such an event, one loses and then burns himself into oblivion, never heard of or seen again from the public because he's been used, broken down and placed into the same direct fate as Jordan X Shabazz or Miyagawa during there tenors here.
But, as you all know - I wouldn't settle for being second best, because the same trait has been passed down upon me not once, not twice, but three times in a row now. It burns me inside, only to fuel the fire that is and forever will be - a champion.
I can run my fingers through my hair, feel that burning taste of undesired breath smelling the way it is in the morning, but you cannot take back what he has given you. Sure, I'm here, sparky red hair, played and loved by the fans for every single glimpse they catch of me, and for what?
An unactive board who only cares about pissing their way into stardom? My eyes burn their sharp distrust for the sights around me, not knowing what is the truth and what is only something I could believe if it were. The thing is, the use of drugs makes your head swell, your mind think of different things in life, unlike every otehr preacher that has used the term 'drugs are bad'. For who, those who misuse them and then space out?
A week ago, after the match with Senji, I question my body's endurance. I had tapped out, rather dropped down from a damn clothsline, something Wilson would have wanted to keep credibility of his title - All Asia. He buckled down when I approached him about the King of Deathmatch torunament, the same one I had thrusted my life upon and thought I was the sure victor. Until he pulled the plug and PURE went belly up for how many months it did.
'Cordozer Stevenson used to be one of the more gifted high flyers in the world, now he's bred himself on garbage wrestling.'
Back in the worlds of shoot fighting, the MMA, FMV, they prided themselves upon working to see who was the better man. Nothing was staged, nobody told you to 'go home in the ninth.' You busted your ass and then went out to see if the pastures were clear enough for you to gain title shots.
Working in the first Wilson installment of PURE, it was a nut house. I was promised this, promised that, just about everything you can imagine for the business to make certain a thrive was coming. I was pressed in the magazine, dubbed the 'Christ of Hardcore' or some shitty moniker Wilson dropped on me and then I was off, putting myself through more physical harm than mental.
Dollars and cents, all Wilson cared about then, and under the moniker of him trying to make it better, dollars and cents are still his ways of business.
For the short twenty years of my life, I have been business oriented, broken down by the mind and put up by the men and women of the industry as a waning star. Looking at a plastered image of the PURE World Heavyweight title I question myself.
Is it worth it? Picking up that belt, rasing it in front of 70,000 people at the Tokyo Dome, chanting my name.
For a future, yeah its worth it.
