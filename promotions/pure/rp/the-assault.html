The Assault

2 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Assault 'The unicorns were a race of beautiful and majestic animals that were fewer in number than their horse cousins. However, when man discovered their existence, they went into frenzy, and slaughtered them all. That is man's modus operandi it seems, that no matter how beautiful or majestic something is, in man's eyes, it is evil if it is not the same as what they have set the standard to be.' -Jonathan Drachen, book review back in his college days. Drachen did not know then, how prophetic his words were. This is an average time, an average day. You already know that disaster is brewing. A day where something good will happen to you, and you just know it. Maybe youll find a 20 dollar bill, meet your future spouse, or win a raffle. But most likely, you will be suffering later on. Youll be walking along, minding your own business, enjoying the ecstasy of the average day you are having and wham, theres a nice, green, crisp Jackson. Or a filthy grayish-red mess that used to be your brain and face. On this day, Drachen is walking down the street, on the sidewalk, minding his own business. He is smiling, he can finally control the gift of prescience that was given him by his creator. He can enjoy all the glorious fruits of knowing and being able to foretell all of lifes great mysteries. He can tell the whole world, from a mountain top, what travesties will befall them, and how to deal with them. The first thing he sees, on this stroll of this average day, is a child. He peers into this childs eyes, and sees the darkest thing his gift of prophesy has ever shown him. What Drachen saw, we will not say. It is far too horrible. Needless to say, Drachen was taken aback by the evil and darkness that he saw within this childs future. Drachens mouth hung open. He could not believe it. He turned around, spun around, as the child walked past him. He saw the fine brown hair of the rear of the young mans head, and saw again the dark future that the young man would attain, and could not imagine how horribly devastating it would be to not just the young man, but all of his fellow kind. This youngster was going to be quite a despot if someone did not do something about it fast. Could Drachen have foretold this, only knowing the look on the young mans face? Just seeing the slight fire in his brandy-colored eyes? The downcast look of his features? Do these features portend to someone being the most evil megalomaniacal power ever to be known on planet Earth? Then again, didnt a certain persons failing out of art school necessarily mean the same thing? Jonathan decides to further look into the situation. He begins to follow the young man and finally catches up to him. He knows the problem, and how to fix it. The gift of prophesy. He taps the young man on the shoulder. Drachen is only a foot taller than the young man, so if we were to go on a height prediction of age, the young man would be about 12-13. 'Hello sir, how ya doing today?' Drachen asks with a smile. 'Fine! Why do you wanna know?' The kid replies with a sneer. 'Well you look pretty down, looks as if you might need some cheering up,' Drachen replies with an even larger smile. 'You some kind of faggot or something, with the white clothes and shaved head, you look pretty scary,' the young man replies with a grimace. 'Ahh, it doesn't mean anything. And no, I am not a faggot. I just was concerned, I usually don't see people so depressed. I was wondering if you might need some cheering up? And no, that doesn't involve taking you in the back of some van parked down the street and molesting you. That's what you were gonna ask next and that's why you were looking down the street before I said it, isn't it?' 'Wha- How did you know that?' the kid asks with shock on his face. 'It's a question prophets get a lot, usually the first or second one actually. It doesn't make it in to the record books though. Some form of censorship or something like that.' 'What did you mean by cheering up?' The kid asks, and in Drachen's view, the blackness already starting to fade in his spirit. 'Maybe some hot chocolate at that place down on the corner, in full view of the public. Why are you still worried about that?' Jonathan replies with a bit of frustration towards the end. The look on the kid's face brightens even more, 'Wow, hot chocolate, my favorite, how did you know?' 'Intuition. So to speak.' About 5 minutes later, Drachen and the kid are sitting in a booth at the cafe around the corner. Drachen has finished his mug, and the kid is still drinking his, with a big smile on his face. Drachen can see that the darkness is completely gone now. What mere trifle certain things seem, is often just a guise. It's funny how a person can save the world by being nice to somebody. It's also scary though, how certain things could have been prevented, if not for just slight actions by certain people. Drachen has one leg up on the booth cushion and is looking outside now. 'What a nice day this seems to be.' 'Yeah, it's been a pretty bad one up until now-' the kid replies. 'Shh son, I know what happened, you don't have to bring it up, I know it hurts to. I know you probably never want to think about it again. You don't have to, just remember that good things happen to good people as well as bad things. I have to go now, you take care of yourself.' Drachen leaves a 20 on the table for the kid and walks out. The kid watches Drachen walk away with admiration, and says one last thing while smiling ear to ear. 'Good luck with your match on Ikusa, Mr. Drachen!' Drachen turns around, smiling back. 'Good luck in life Jeff.' Drachen then leaves the cafe, and walks out onto the sidewalk. How did he know my name? The kid wonders and then thinks with a smile, I am glad that there are people like him. Drachen walks down the street, it is an average day, and it seems to be turning out well. WRONG. The pipe came, high, and hard and to Drachen's forehead. Drachen had no chance to defend himself. He didn't even see his attacker until it was too late. And certainly afterward he did not remember it. But funny, he couldn't see it beforehand. And afterwards, he would never see anything beforehand again.
