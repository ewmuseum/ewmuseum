Standing Alone Part 2

22 January 2003

Standing Alone Part 2 Written: 22/01/03 'Taxi!' A young woman beside me hailed a navy blue cab. I too was attempting to get one of the many cabbies’ attention, but had been unsuccessful. She and I had been speaking to each other over the past few minutes about the virtues of clear sight. By the way, this particular brunette was inebriated.
'Hey, why don't we share a cab?' 'All right', I nodded once, 'where are you headed?' She told me, and then yelled directions at the driver as I helped adjust her slender figure inside the taxi. Almost immediately, she slumped over to the other side, nearly cracking her skull on the window.
'Ok buddy,' the taxi driver began exhaustedly 'I've had a long day. If you don't mind, keep her, ' he jabbed his thumb at the woman 'off of my back. I don't have the patience for drunk chicks this late at night when I'm sober.'
I laughed lightly, nodding my head. He raised a questioning eyebrow as he looked back at the woman.
'Hey buddy... you didn't give her anything... did you?' No matter how many times I hear something like that, it makes me sick. To think that people still stoop so low in the world today, even with our advanced society and laws and morals. It's all bullshit when a question such as that has become commonplace. Ah well, not my fucking problem. Stupid American!
'No, of course not.' And at a leisurely pace we were off, driving in the direction of the woman's home through a more run-down area of the city. As I gazed out of the window, I saw just how bad it was. Graffiti on every possible place, men and women wearing gang colours and insignias, a schoolyard looking like a garbage dump; in the backs of alleys, on the steps of offices and restaurants, on the street-corners, were homeless men and women, huddling around whatever they could find. It was like a movie, but it was really just a bad dream come to life.
A teenage couple walked through it all, stumbling over everything in their path. The boy hung his arm around the girls' shoulder, and she swung her hair in his face playfully, laughing in a drunken stupor.
'It's a shame to see that...' The driver waved his hand in the direction of the teens and sighed. 'Kids like them don't stand a chance in a neighborhood like this so late.'
I shrugged. 'Maybe they live around here.' 'Yeah, maybe... but probably not. I've only known a few kids who could do that,' the cabbie said with a sad expression, 'and those two aren't the type. Then again if you can survive here, more power to you.'
I smiled, but not soon after did it drain off of my face. In a few minutes, we were out of that area, on our way through some commercial buildings. The woman shivered, hugging herself tightly. I leaned over and checked if she was all right, and finding that she was, I draped my jacket over her. My hand brushed her cheek accidentally, rousing her from her sleep. She reached up and grabbed my hand through the jacket, nuzzling her fingers into my palm. As she spoke, I could tell that she had sobered-up somewhat.
'Where am I?' 'Don't worry,' I reassured her, 'you're in a taxi, on your way home. It's all right.' 'Oh.' She blinked, and then cringed, bringing her fists up to her temples, feeling a wave of nausea pass over her momentarily. 'What the hell was I doing?'
'Exactly what you had planned, I suppose.' I spoke as nonchalantly as possible. 'You were drinking, having some fun with your friends.'
'Were you one of them?' 'No.' Of course I wasn't. I just met the poor girl outside, waiting for a ride home. She looked confused and hurt when I told her that... almost as if she was expecting a different answer.
'But I recognize you from somewhere... I thought that we were...' she made a wide gesture with her arm. Her voice wavered, quivering along with her hands as she spoke. '... before I drank... and I don't... know...' Her eyes welled up with tears. 'I don't know!'
'Poor girl', I thought. Not a hell of a lot I could do to help her, though; just try and calm her down.
'Don't worry, I'm sure you didn't do anything you'll regret. Just sit tight, you'll be home soon, and you can sleep in your own bed. When you wake up tomorrow, you'll be fi-'
She leaned over, wrapping her arms around my neck. I stopped talking, letting the woman find whatever comfort she could. She stopped crying. her face was inches from mine; she was so close that I could smell what she had been drinking. However, when she spoke, there wasn't a trace of slurred vocabulary at all.
'Who are you?' Damn. I hate it when people ask me that question, because I never seem to think up of a name beforehand. That'll teach me to procrastinate!
'Is that really important?' 'Yes,' she readjusted her dress, “yes it is. I would like know someone if I'm drunk and lying on top of them.'
'Fair enough...' I hesitated. I needed a name, fast! The first one I could think of, I blurted out.
