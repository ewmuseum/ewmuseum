Go hard, or go home..

19 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Go hard, or go home.. The flat was absolutely tiny by American standards. A tiny kitchenette, a pair of stools next to the small counter. An olive drab loveseat stood in the middle of the open area of the flat, just in front of the makeshift entertainment center and behind the weight bench he cobbled together. Beyond it all was a futon in the corner and the door to the tiny bathroom. Simple, spartan, downright bare. And it was all his. Kevin scurried about the apartment, grabbing odds and ends that he might need while at the show. The basics. Soap, towel, shampoo, his mask. How'd he get so pressed for time he wondered. Everything had seemed compressed recently, ever since he'd finally made the move to Tokyo. The pace of the city was downright dizzying compared to the relaxed pace of southwestern desert life in Tucson. The hustle and bustle of the new environment has taken him by surprise, so much so that he hadn't realized the inordinate amount of time it had taken him to make the improvements to the bathroom. Namely raising the showerhead by a foot and a half so he wouldn't keep bashing the back of his neck into it everytime he got shampoo in his eyes. For a brief moment he considered chopping off the long locks that he'd twisted into a topknot for the time being. The thought was readily dismissed. It was almost his only way of meeting new people. Blonde hair was good luck in these parts, and half the time people would almost flock to him just to rub his head for luck. People would ask who he was and why he was here. After the first few encounters he'd learned to just answer with his first name and say he was a wrestler. The exact answers of 'Sandusky' and 'Ebola III' often brought about a negative reaction. Not the kind of thing he needed in a new place, so he'd taken to just introducing himself as 'Kevin' and 'a wrestler'. Most of the time it'd evoke a smile and some kind words of encouragement from locals who were glad he wasn't another one of those damn American Marines that had made such a bad impression on the country. F'n Jarheads. Of course, he could understand their predicament. The Japanese women were hard to read as far as age went. Anyone from the ranges of 15 to 25 all looked about the same, and it was just about neccessary to sneak a look at their ID before even thinking about bringing one home. So to that end, he hadn't even tried. Not worth the risk yet. The clock on the wall chimed almost angrily. 5 PM. He was late and getting later with every second that passed. Not a good way to impress the boss, whom he was teaming with again. He quickly tossed his mask into his tank bag with the rest of his ring gear and tossed the odds and ends into a backpack. Kevin snatched the keys off the counter, grabbed his helmet and headed out the door. 5 stories down to the street, in hurried circles down the fire escape. He'd barely remembered to be careful of the one bad step that was rusted through. The last thing he needed was a turned ankle the day of the show. Jumping down the last few steps Kevin skipped over to his 'horse', so to speak. The black and red Aprilia RS 250 was chained to the 5 inch water pipe that ran down the side of the building. The grin on his face was broad as he pressed the start switch and the little engine revved to idle. It was the ideal transport. Light on the gas, quick, nimble enough for a few thrills without being overly dangerous on the already treacherous Japanese roads. It was even a good conversation piece. Italian iron in Japan was pretty rare. Kevin slapped the tank bag into place and slid a small map into the clear plastic sleeve that was taped to the top. After a week and a half, he was still unsure of the way out of the neighborhood, let alone across town. He fiddled momentarily with the handheld GPS that had been a gift from his uncle and programmed in his route. God bless the technology. The LED blinked a few times before finally displaying his course. Satisfied, he slid the GPS into the plastic sleeve. 36-24-36. The combo to the Master-lock flew by, and didn't fail to bring an adolesent grin to Kevin's face. The simple things.. With the bike free of it's tether, Kevin swung his leg over the machine, and bumped the throttle to kick the bike down to low idle. After securing the back pack in place, Kevin pulled on his helmet, leaving the dark visor up for better visability. He walked the bike out to the edge of the alley and peered out into traffic. Across the avenue, a group of those 'vague aged' women giggled and waved. Hey, the chicks dig the leathers, no matter what country you're in. Kevin gave them a quick wink before slapping closed his visor and speeding out into traffic. No time for playing grab-ass with what was most likely a roving pack of jailbait in pushup bras, he was on the clock now. He'd finally gotten his own place and his own job and was responsible for his own actions now. He'd gotten a huge break by being teamed with the boss, but that meant big expectations. 'Go hard or go home' as Uncle Joey had put it. The thought put a smile on Kevin's face underneath the helmet. He'd been doing things the hard way his entire life. Why change now?
