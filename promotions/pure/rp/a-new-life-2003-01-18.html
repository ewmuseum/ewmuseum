A New Life

18 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A New Life Written: 01/18/03 The rattle of an empty can blown by the wind across a tarred footpath is all that can be heard. Blown westwards by the string easterly winds. Suddenly, the can comes to a halt, impeded by a size 11 black Nike shoe, its tattered and old looking, with part of it ripped. The face of the man in which the shoe belonged to, looked down at the empty, crushed can, which was branded ‘Coca Cola’. Same old, same old. When you look down to see what brand a can is the first thought that will pop into your head is and probably will always be Coca Cola. Shows the power up advertising and promotion.
The same foot the stopped the can then kicks is lightly, which carries it over to the other side of the street and onto the nicely mown, green grass. The foot that kicked it then goes right back into the position it was, lazily holding up the rest of the man that leaned against the wall behind him.
His hair is brown with bleached blonde tips. It had a wet affect to it. It is spiked up untidily, mainly because he could not afford gel so he just using water.
His face was tanned and hansom. He had big blue eyes and surprisingly no scars to be seen. His face was not long but not exactly round either.
He wore an orange and black top saying 'Bronx' in pre 1800 style writing. It had no sleeves. He also wore baggy black jeans and white sneakers.
He stared lazily at the river that stood between him and proper civilization. You see, this young man is known as Peter Perez. He is from the Bronx, he is poor, and he will always be poor. That is the long and short of him. For that fact is, no matter how much money he makes is this lifetime, it will not change anything, and he will still be living in the Bronx, without even a house or a bed to sleep in.
It’s a rather perplexing situation that you would not usually come across. A high paid, popular athlete through a nation still living in poverty? Not an every day occurrence. Though no matter how realistic it may be, for Peter Perez, it is a reality.
No doubt… the situation is perplexing you. Why is it this way? Why is he living in poverty despite the money and exposure he receives? Well, that is a long story. Peter Perez is a fighter, a Mixed Martial Artist if you will.
He grew up fighting, fighting for whatever bit of finance he could get his hands on. He would fight ten men for five dollars if he had to. Someone call this bravery, other’s scrubbiness. Though he saw it as desperation. Desperation to keep his family alive.
He started his life off living is a small apartment with his family. They did not have much but they had a good life. Though that all soon changed. His brother’s sisters, cousins and all the Perez family began wanting more. They tried dealing with big gangs, hoping to somehow make it as a prestigious crime family, hating living with what they had. They wanted more! The Perez family was tough, all fighters, though all intellectual as well. They felt they had what it took to be a large crime syndicate family, or ‘gang’ if you please.
This unfortunately went pear shaped. They negotiated with the wrong gangs, started fights with the wrong gangs and messed with the wrong gangs. It’s a lesson in life… be happy with what you have because you do not appreciate it more than when it is gone.
When Peter was eleven they were forced out of their home, all the Perez’ families. All the gangs who they owed big that if they did not pay the money off told them, all of them would die. And the Perez’ knew this would be the case. So they scoured the Bronx, all of them, around fifty or so. They found a rather large alleyway with a door that lead into a small warehouse room. It seemed to be deserted altogether, so they made this there home… all of them.
Over the years that alleyway has become quite cosy, believe it or not. There is not much room considering there are over 60 of them these days, but still. It was between two, now abandon, brown brick industrials buildings, each identical, about fifty feet high. They used tin to make a roof over the alleyway, and two of Peter’s cousins, who were quite the ‘handy men’, constructed a door, also out of tin. They also had steps which led onto the roof of one of the buildings were the children played soccer and basketball a lot. Yeah, it sounds dangerous, but hey, it’s the Bronx.
A lot of the family had small jobs, in shops and dry cleaners around the area, most were owned by the local gangs. Though a lot of the male Perez’ got their money by fighting. Peter one of those. He loved the adrenaline rush, but if he did not have to he probably would not do it, because no matter how exciting it was, there was more bad than good.
He competed in street fights and underground fights for money more a long time. Then, when he realized he was probably the best fighter around he started training properly. At the age of sixteen he looked for a fighting promotion of some sort to join.
A week ago, he was accepted into a Puro Organization called PURE. It was mostly Japanese styles but there was a MMA Division (Mixed Martial Arts). It was the happiest moment of his life; finally he could help pay off his families debts more significantly. Though he would never be rich, he was still be making more than he was off the streets.
There was one problem however. This ‘federation’ if that is what you would call it, was in Japan. Though Peter did not mind, as he could come home to the Bronx quite often, due a travelling expense clause in his contract.
It had all worked out for the best, and finally he would get to see the world and get away from this wretched place he called home. Though there was one thing for sure, he would miss it.
The clouds in the sky began to merge with the strong wind, they were grey. Looked like bad weather. Peter sniffed his nose. The cold was starting the get to him. He waited anxiously constantly looking at the two-dollar watch on his left wrist.
He seems tense, and rather apprehensive. He turns his head from right to left and little too often to be considered comfortable. On the pavement beside him is and old, shabby, blue rucksack with brown leather straps. It was filled with something quite obviously.
The night was not getting any younger the blackness was gradually jacketing the surrounding area. Peter looked up into the sky and saw the dark, heavy clouds.
“Looks like rain.” He said into himself, quite sarcastically. Suddenly Peter’s attention turns to a more significant matter. As two oriental men dressed in shabby suits, approach him from the west. They travel along the footpath, each with a briefcase in his right hand. They try to look ‘executive’ per say, but it is obvious they do not pull it off satisfactorily, where Peter is concerned anyway. Their suits are ripped in places and looked dusty, as if they have not been worn or for that matter cleaned in years. One man’s blazer is much to small for him while the other’s trousers trail along the dry, street pavement.
Peter straightens up and faces the men as they come closer. He reaches his hand down and picks up the rucksack that sat on the pavement beside him. He stands, trying to put on a brave face as the men come right up to his face.
“You have the money?” The man on the right said in an Asian, or more specifically Vietnamese accent.
“Yeah, I have it.” Peter replied promptly. The man on the left then looks down at the rucksack. He reaches for it quickly. Peter anxiously steps back but is too late as the man grabs it.
“Just remember, you’re behind on your payments! And the Waka Gashira will not be happy! Remember… do not mess with the Yakuza!!!” The man on the right said again.
Abruptly the man on the left swings at Peter and hits him on the kidney. It is a vicious shot that sends Peter to the ground. He lies on his knees holding his stomach in pain coughing loudly.
“I don’t care what other gangs want their money! You pay the Yakuza!” The man on the right states, with rather bad grammar.
The two men then walk off as Peter lifts his head, looking after him. He is thankful that next week he will not be there to pay the Yakuza and some one else in the family will have to make the delivery. Though, if that’s what all Asians were like, he was not so sure about PURE after all.
He was glad however, that he would get time off this life. He loved it, though he did not like to admit that fact. Though still, he had never had a holiday before, and this was the chance of a lifetime for him, a dream!
He knew however that he had something to prove against one Vito Wynn. He is an experienced competitor in PURE, and Peter will certainly have something to prove. Vito had an epic war with one S2, or Suicidal Stevenson, who is now one of the main competitors in PURE. Though Peter did have one advantage in the match…it was MMA, and he knew he would feel right at home.
Life was about to change for Peter Perez, anxiety and desperation filled his soul in a conflicting battle. He would go to Japan to live out his dream, he may not have the money that everyone else had, but still, it was good enough for him.
