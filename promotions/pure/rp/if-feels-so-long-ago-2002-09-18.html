If Feels So Long Ago

18 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
If Feels So Long Ago Written: Sept 18, 2002 It Feels So Long Ago... I stepped off the ramp way in the airport, stretched my shoulders a bit, as I looked in the airport,  'Full Bloods,' walking around silently, their heads down, silent.  Everyone was so silent.  Everyone was so calm.
It was the same six years ago.  The first time I came to this place, my escape.  This is where I wanted to be, to learn my craft, the one I do now.
I shifted my bag over my shoulder, and shoved my hands in my coat pocket.  Akiko was waiting, I didn't want to disappoint her.  With slow, even paced steps, I went on, back to my past, and on to my future.
Upon exiting the airport, I immediately hailed a cab, and sat in.  I grunted my directions as simply as I could, until he said he knew where to go, before closing my eyes, and feeling the vibrations of the road, the car, and the world. 
It caused me to remember.  I hated remembering. What seemed like ages ago, around, the same date, actually.  I was fresh out of high school.  I left, after a summer of making enough money to get here, and no real plan monetarily for after.  I was poor, in a new place, and excited.  A very dangerous combination, I quickly signed up in a PURO, fed, and taught English part time, at the airforce bases, and too those who would pay, all the while.  The only thing that my mother helped me with was her native tongue. 
The days of training were rough, especially because they saw me as an outsider, a 'gaijin.'  Which was a harsh way of saying a foreigner.  I must say, I took many, many shots with a shinai to the back, for fouling up.  I learned martial arts on the side as well, practicing in the temples, and wherever else, I could.  I was having success, building skill, in both. 
Unfortunately, I was still far below the rest of the class, and there was much hazing.  I tried to take it straight faced, but after a while, it dug under my skin, and down on to my heart.  Every 'accidental,' stiff move, every 'accidental' blow spot, engraved itself into my brain.  Every broken bone, bruised kidney, and black eyes, that could have been avoided, was there in my mind.  In secret, I hated them.  I hated my instructor, I hated my peers, quite frankly, I hated everyone, and everything.
Finally, I graduated, a year behind my classmates, I wasn't a prodigy that's for damn sure.  I started working matches, dark matches mostly through out the smaller federations in Japan.  Because I was still rather green, I was mostly being picked up for the new coming 'extreme,' federations.  'Garbage federations,' Jamar probably creams his jeans thinking of them..
I laughed a bit to myself, and at the 20% owner of PURE.  It was funny, seeing as I was probably re-hired, with Jamar thinking I'd join him in Tokyo Pop.  Everyone knows that I hate Puro, and what it stands for.  I hate Miyagawa, I hate the tradition, and the cruelty and coldness of the people. 
I tapped on the glass to signal the driver to stop.  He blinked at me, over his shoulder, then nodded.  Pulling over to the left side of the crowded street, the electronic door slowly opened, and I stepped out, stretching to my full height.  I felt like walking, I knew that I would soon be cramped into a small hotel room, waiting for the show.  Waiting for my opponent.  Enmity.  I needed to get stretched out, and prepare, I pulled a few thousand yen from my pocket, and placed it in the tray for the driver, then watched him speed off to his next task.
Always coming, or going, never giving themselves time to reflect.  Like ants, or beasts of burden.  No self image, only a hive consciousness.  I am sure that's what Sean, of the PURO/DX side wishes to create.  Happy army ants, that will never complain, or rise against him. Those, that always know their place, and are easy to manipulate.  People who believe tradition is all.
I shook my head in disgust, as I continued to remember.  What happened, in years past.  My first trip to The Land of the Rising Sun. 
I was facing one of my mentors.  A veteran in the little known Indy feds in Japan, twenty some odd years in the business with no big breaks, and he was perfectly happy just working.  Doing what he could, taking exactly what was given to him, and not even wanting to ask for more.  The match was my first televised match, ever.  It was odd, that I was facing my former mentor, we knew each other quiet well, and despite my outward pleasantries, I hated him for how he treated me.  The tradition, that those not from Japan, were lesser, that came out in everything he and the other students said, boiled my blood, and I could feel my face turning red, as I stared at the balding, yet, brawny man in the ring.
We started off with a sign of respect, a bow.  Before we walked around, once more trying to feel each other out.  As we made the first lock up, he roughly slapped the back of my head.  The hazing apparently continued.  We continued on, and he refused to let himself be affected by my moves, he was tough to work with, and I found myself struggling often. 
I got frustrated. Well, maybe that's too light of a word. I pulled by bag higher over my shoulder, it was growing really uncomfortable after all this walking, and maneuvering through the masses.  Luckily, I had nearly 100 to 70lbs on almost everyone in the country, and was bulling my way through, easily, making my way towards the hotel, stomping on toes, and using my shoulders to maneuver my way around.
The final straw was, when he had me in the corner, we had everything planned out, he hit me with a hard chop to the chest, it stung so bad it made my eyes water.  He gave a small frown, he brought his hand back up, and backhanded me across the face.
I snapped, though, not for the last time. My hands stretched out, and wrapped themselves around his throat, and my forehead found the bridge of his nose.  Blood sprayed out, and he clutched his face, his nose was at an awkward angle, obviously broken.  That wasn't the end of the torrent, as my shin drove itself into his ribs, sending him to the side, he stumbled, and fell onto the ropes, with his legs out, and the only thing holding him up was his arms.  I had walked over to his straightened leg, and raised my foot, and brought it down on his knee.
The crack was sickening.  A mix of security, and wrestlers immediately swarmed me, pulling me out of the building.  I was barred from competition in that federation, and was nearly blacklisted for my offence from every other federation in Japan.  I went back to America, feeling the same, and still angry.  That was that.  My first time in Japan, one of the reasons I hate this place so much, after all my abuse, when I dolled some out, I was the one punished.  I was the one treated like shit, once more.  I was the one stepped on.  I hated this place.  I hated the tradition.  If I had any power, I would end, this shitty tradition, which caused me so much pain. 
I would end the evil Pop Culture fanaticism of TokyoPop, which endangers this sport's greatness, with high-risk matches.  There will be a balance between the two.  There will be a new revolution, a new way to live and wrestle in Japan.  Fuck Tradition.  Fuck Sports Entertainment. 
I wrestle. I fight. I growled, as I finally passed through the waves of people, grabbing onto the handle to the entrance of the hotel.  I stalked through the doorway, more intent on my ways, steaming angry from the emotions that were brought up on my walk.  The elevator opened, and the smiling face of my Akiko Hiroshima, glanced at me, and frowned, her brown eyes looked at me, asking what was wrong, with out so much as a word.  I just shook my head.  I didn't feel like saying anything, my actions would speak louder then anything I could ever have to say.
This was the beginning. This was the new revolution of Japanese wrestling. Down with tradition. Down with sports entertainment.
