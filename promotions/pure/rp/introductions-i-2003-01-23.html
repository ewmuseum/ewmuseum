Introductions I

23 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Introductions I Written: 1-23-03 The scene slowly fades into a small business type area of Mito, Japan. We find Jesse Ramey seated on the outside of one of the building in a small wooden bench. Jesse is clothed in a silk looking suit of types, very beautiful. He folds open a Japanese paper with his picture in the middle of the “sports” section. It has an article featuring the debut show of PURE, when a man seats down next to Jesse, startling him a bit.
Man: Hello, sir. Jesse nods as he continues reading the PURE portion of the paper. Man: I was at that show. Jesse continues to read the paper, showing no recognition to the man seated next to him. Man: The ending of the show was very poor in my opinion. Not in the fact that the wrestling was superb, but the fact that the Steven Lee guy won the match so dishonorable.
Still not paying any attention to the man sitting next to him, Jesse just continues reading the paper, although you can tell he is beginning to get a little fidgety.
Man: I will tell you one thing though, that Jesse Ramey sure did know how to put on a performance for the crowd. He was truly an honorable man and should have won that match, although his actions prior to the match were somewhat uncalled for.
Jesse: (Looking over at the man from the corner of his eye) And you say this because? Man: Glad to see you’re finally putting yourself into the conversation. (The man places on leg across his knee and leans back into the bench) I say what I said because, everyone in that arena thought Jesse had more honor for himself than he did.
Jesse: (Laying his paper down and getting a bit frustrated) Would you have had enough honor to keep yourself from doing what he did when what happened, happened?
Man: Most definitely, young one. Master Ramey is skilled in the arts of fighting, but he is not skilled in his mind.
Jesse: And how would you go about making him skilled in his mind as well? Man: Through training. Jesse: (Rolling his eyes) Jesse has been through every training known to man and I don’t believe going through any more is going to make him more skilled.
Man: That’s what you think, young one. You are about as skilled as Master Ramey, seeing as you believe that through training someone cannot get better, but the truth is that through training one can only get better.
Jesse: Old man, you have no idea of what you are speaking. (Looking at his watch) I have an appointment to get to, have a nice day.
Jesse stands and begins to make his way from the bench as the old man looks down at the paper, with Jesse’s photo in it.
Man: And you sir, have a good day as well, Master Ramey. The scene begins to fade to black as the camera begins to focus in on the photo of Jesse in the paper.
