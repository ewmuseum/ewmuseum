No Fear...

13 October 2002

No Fear... Written: 10/13/02 Fear. ‘Fear’ was a word that meant nothing to me. I never felt fear much. Anyone would be scared of some things, if they weren’t, they were just plain crazy. I’m not crazy enough to do some things, but I will take a lot of risks. It’s who I am. I don’t care what you think about me, it’s just the way I am. Even if I am afraid, I show no fear…
If it’s something that needs to be done, a risk worth taking to save a life or any other important cause, I’ll be there. I know I’m not some super hero or any kind of super human, but I carry courage. The courage to do things that no one else would ever dream of doing, such as sky diving, car racing, dirt biking, and well, saving people in a burning house...
“Help!” the woman cried as she was hanging out of the window of the burning home. “Oh god! Please help!”
I was in my neighborhood in Los Angeles, California. It was late at night and quite dark, with only one thing flashing, someone’s home was on fire. I had gone out for a walk. Why did I go out for a walk? The reason for that would be because I had nothing else to do. I hate doing nothing. I have to be doing something and taking some sort of risk. But walking wasn’t much of a risk, was it? It wasn’t. But it was something to do, at least.
And now, I have stumbled upon risk. I have just stumbled upon the rush of life. A burning house.
It shouldn’t be amusing at all. Some family is on their way to losing their home. Their lives are also at risk. But deep inside of me, I was amused. I was about to put my own life in danger, I loved it. I wanted to danger, and now I have found it.
There were no fire fighters around, and I could tell they may not be here for a while. I hadn’t heard any sirens sounding. I was going to take it into my own hands.
I rushed over to the house, running through the debris that was being thrown from the burning home. I busted down the door, and screams rang in my ears, women and children.
Dark, smoggy black smoke filled the inside of house. I covered my nose and mouth with my hand, closed my eyes and rushed up what I could make out to be stairs out of the blinding darkness of the smoke. I reached the top of the stairs and began to hear a lot of coughing to my right, I rush toward my right and felt around for a door knob.
Found it. I busted into the room, I got down on my knees to see a little bit better so that I would be below the terrible amount of black smoke.
Two children. A boy and a girl, who must have been around the ages of 5-8. I was going to have to pull this off quickly. I stood up and ran over to them.
Then I began to basically hack my lungs out. I took a little bit of the black smoke in and fell to the ground. I began coughing continuously.
Don’t give up now! I thoughtfully demanded to myself. I raised myself back to my feet, also trying to calm the burning pain within my throat from the smoke. I grabbed both children, holding them each in a single arm. I ran out of the room, and then I heard banging. Someone was banging on the door across the hallway.
WHAM! I busted down the door, a woman, the same woman that was crying for help out of the window. I carried the two children down the stairs with the woman right behind me. We ran out the front door as I gently let the kids down and collapsed on the ground.
Coughing, I wiped the sweat and black smut off of my face, and suddenly, I heard sirens. I wasn’t the kind of guy to sit around and be the hero, I didn’t want to stick around and have all of these cops and fire fighters tell me that they admire my bravery.
I stood back on my feet. I could finally breathe a little better then. “Oh my god, thank you!” the woman said as she ran up and gave me a big bear hug. After a few seconds she let go. “What is your name, sir?”
“The name is Chase. Chase Herrera,” I answered as I listened to the sirens closing in on the burning home. “But now I am afraid that I have to leave.”
I ran off. As I said before, I don’t want to be admired and all that other crap. I did something good, and I would now live with that in my own mind. It’s a lot better than getting a lot of attention. I didn’t need attention, I needed action.
RING! RING! My cell phone rang as I ran. I stopped, I was much further away from the house now. RING! RI— I picked it up. “Hello?” I said. “Hey, Chase,” it was my manager. “Remember that wrestling organization in Japan? PURE?” “Yeah,” I answered. “Well, get ready and pack your bags, we’re going to Japan,”
