The Lesson

30 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Lesson In a Japanese casino (for Western tourists, mainly), The End is going around with Jeremy Johnson. JJ: Hey, so what are we going to do first? End: 'First, you need to learn the rules.' JJ: I know the rules for the game. End: 'No, you know what they've told you. But that's only half of it.' They walk past a blackjack table. End: 'Take this game, for instance. The odds are always in favor of the house. That's regardless of the rules. But there's certain things you can do to bring things back in your favor.' JJ: 'Like what?' End: 'First of all, sit down at the table.' JJ sits in a center seat. End: 'No. Sit at either end of the table. You'll have slightly better odds that way.' JJ moves. End: 'Now, cash in.' JJ gets some chips for his money. He nearly sets them down on the table to begin with. The End yanks him out of his seat. End: 'You see. You don't know anything. Sure, there's rules to a game. There's also the rules to life. Life's a game, as well, but much different.' The End turns JJ back to the table. End: 'Rule #1 -- Just because you can get into the game right away, that may not be the best time.' JJ turns back to the table. The dealer draws 21 right away. JJ: Hey, you kept me from losing my money. How did you know that? End: 'It's always best to wait to make sure that everything's run its cycle before you try to join. Get in in the middle of something, and you could get in over your head.' JJ: Can I get in now? End: 'Fine.' JJ sits down to the table. After a few minutes of play, he wins $200. End: 'You started with $500 and won $200. Now, what do you do?' JJ: I'm on a roll. Bet it all? End: 'Of course not! You won enough, leave the table.' JJ takes his money and gets up. End: 'You have to know when to stay in the game and win to get out.' JJ: But I was doing well. End: 'Yes, but all the good cards had cycled to the bottom of the deck by that point, and in a situation like that, the dealer's usually the first person to get to them.' JJ: So I'd have a better chance of losing than winning? End: 'Exactly. Gamble when you think you can win, but know when to get out of the game on top.' JJ: But what if I start losing first? I stay in the game and try to make it back? End: 'No. You cut your losses and move on. So, let's go take out a loan.' JJ: What? The End meets with the casino's proprietor. Owner: Ah, End San, would you like another loan? End: 'Yes, the usual amount and payback deadline is fine.' Owner: 'Very well, $300,000 due back in one week.' The proprietor cuts The End a check. Owner: It is nice doing business with you again. End: 'Likewise.' On the way out, JJ looks dumbfounded. JJ: You can just walk in and ask for that much money? End: 'Of course.' JJ: So what're we going to do with it? End: 'Sit on it.' JJ: What? End: 'You think something this big I'd take a chance losing on some stupid gamble the house has the odds in?' The End takes JJ aside. End: 'I don't get paid much from wrestling. Never have. I don't even ask for much money. Know why?' JJ: Why? End: 'Because of this. Get a 1K, 2K, or 3K loan from a casino, right? Do that at about 20 big casinos, and put it all into a number of different accounts, so I may have maybe $2 million of money that's not even mine in the bank.' JJ: Yeah? End: 'Take advantage of daily compoun interest, and by the end of the week, I pay back all of those loans...' JJ: 'And you keep the money you make on the interest?' End: 'Exactly. It's still a gamble, everything is. But it's the safest possible gamble I can make. I change the rules. I change the game. I do things my way and come out winning in the end. And besides, it helps to build my reputation.' JJ: Which is? End: 'That I ALWAYS pay back what I owe others. (And just guess who that's targeted to.' JJ: I think I've got it. Lemme go and check in at work, I'm supposed to be interviewing Jordan X Shabazz soon. End: 'Go ahead. And don't forget what you've learned here.'
