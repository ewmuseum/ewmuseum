Homesick

15 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Homesick Written: 1/15/03 I miss it already. The past couple months of my life have been a nightmare. Never before have I felt so alone, despite being with my father the whole while. My first experience in a big BIG city, New Jersey, was humiliating, shameful, and unforgettable, unfortunately.
The urban world seemed unaccustomed to me. So I thought. From what I hear, it may just be New Jersey.
Since arriving in Japan, I’ve been received quite nicely, much to my surprise. The worst is over, and I’m ready to move on, ready to make a name for myself… just all around READY!
Then again, who isn’t ready, apparently? Despite all this… the warm welcome, the good treatment, I still miss home. No, it’s not the hellhole that is New Jersey, but rather my reserve. It sounds strange, I’ll admit. If anyone wanted to get into an argument based on who had a harder life, I would win HANDS DOWN based on my living conditions alone, with a select few who could challenge me. Still, sometimes I feel like I abandoned them. I didn’t have close friends, or a high school sweetheart that I felt connected to back home, but seeing myself succeed while I knew full well what was going on back home… it was certainly a let down.
And even my urban experience seems worth it, now that I am where I strived to be. And everyone back home would go through the same Hell I went through, and then some, just to be called a success. Looking back, I feel pretty selfish. I feel selfish that I made such a big deal over being what I saw as being outcast, when I should have viewed as a test.
I guess I’m having a revelation of sorts. Why though? It’s not like I this just hit me for no apparent reason. Well, I recently got to see a copy of Axcess. And one person that caught my eye, in particular, was Senji Yulav.
I watched this man, and I was astonished at some of our similarities, mainly in how we came off to the public. Both of us felt like exiles.
This guy WAS Kohana… multiplied by ten. Watching this guy made me ask myself, “Is this how I sound? Is this how I come off? As a bitter, mean spirited man?” The answer is no, which my father claimed. However, I still felt ashamed that I, or someone else, might compare Yulav and myself in the attitude category.
While America may have left a bad taste in my mouth, it by no means turned me against it, though, it seemed like it was going to.
So when asked if I was willing to take on Yulav in tag action, with BladeD as my partner, and Tenzan as his, I jumped at it. Not because I’m a patriot, but simply because I needed to kill that bitterness in me. Something Yulav represents to me. Of course, patriotism will be the official reasoning given, but what does it really matter.
He may be able to intimidate children, but I’m not intimidated so easy. He may be a Universal Outlaw, but he singled out my country. Why my country is relevant in Japan is beyond me. Well…
We’re not in Kansas anymore Yulav.
