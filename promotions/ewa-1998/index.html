A GeoCities e-fed that scored matches on a blend of roleplay and written strategy, crowned a gorilla the deciding vote in a title match, and staged tournament rounds in the White House and the halls of Congress before relaunching under its own retired champion turned President. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Extreme Wrestling Association opened on free GeoCities space in the spring of 1998 with a card called Heaven and Hell, judged the way its own Rules and Regulations page spelled out in a scoring formula: half a wrestler's grade came from the quality of their own roleplay, another slice from how often they wrote at all, and the rest split between a submitted written strategy and the angle a win or loss would serve. Its own application turned away anyone billed under a real wrestler's name outright — "Originals only," the rules said twice.

Its early broadcasts, Wednesday Night Warfare, crowned a first Extreme World Champion the way the rest of the fed liked to work: an eight-man tournament that ran through a Washington Zoo gorilla pit (the deciding fall went to a gorilla the announcers named Mopie), the floor of the U.S. House of Representatives with Newt Gingrich as special guest referee, and the Oval Office, with a walk-on from a sitting president and two foreign heads of state played for laughs. JD Freeman won that tournament and, promoted to Vice President that summer, stopped wrestling without ever dropping the belt, and the Extreme World title — later renamed the Global Heavyweight Championship — never found a new champion in the rest of the fed's surviving record.

From September 1998 the weekly show rebranded as Extreme Measures, and the fed's four other belts — North American, Television, Global Hardcore, and Cruiserweight — changed hands repeatedly across the fall and winter: the Television strap alone passed through five wrestlers between June 1998 and January 1999. A World Tag Team title went to the stable Death Row at the January 1999 pay-per-view Yuletide Beatings, the last of four named pay-per-views the surviving record holds (BloodBath, Devil's Night, December to Dismember, and Yuletide Beatings itself), alongside two "Thursday Night Slaughter" specials and a StableWars event.

By the site's own last dated update, 3 June 1999, the fed had gone through a visible changing of the guard: its original commentary team and most of its 1998 title-holders are absent from a wholly new heavyweight and cruiserweight roster, and the promotion's own former North American and World Tag Team Champion, Wayne "The Train" Rothschild — written into a career-ending injury and a stint as Vice President under a previous President, Artful — had become President himself. The site was actively promoting a comeback card, Extreme Measures of 24 May 1999 at Madison Square Garden, when this capture was made; how that card went, and what became of the promotion afterward, is not in the surviving record.

Storylines

The fed's own weekly show — Wednesday Night Warfare, then Extreme Measures — ran May 1998 through January 1999 as one long serial, stables forming and colliding faster than any title ledger recorded them. The arcs below are recovered from a show-by-show read of the whole surviving run, not from the champion-announcement lines alone.

The DR killings Written by AI, 2026

A masked figure signing notes only "DR" shot Flying Fury dead with an arrow mid-match on the fed's second card, and weeks later chained a wrestler named Zaman into his own trailer's bathtub and detonated it on live television — both killings staged and played straight by the announce team, both entirely unsolved in the surviving record. JD Freeman himself received a note signed "DR" congratulating him on winning the Extreme World Title and promising he was next; nothing in the run's own remaining shows ever names the culprit.

Devastation Inc. and the Innovators of Violence Written by AI, 2026

JD Freeman built a heel stable on-air out of his own newest signing, SuperReactor, and a defector from the babyface ranks, Chris Douglas — "the birth of the most devastating force in the EWA," by Freeman's own words. Devastation Inc. drew an answering trio in Ray Simmons, Tommy Grayson, and Johnny Sledge, the self-styled Innovators of Violence, and the two crews spent the summer running each other's matches, right down to a mass ringside brawl that swallowed Death Row and the Riot Squad along with them.

A title that changed hands off-camera Written by AI, 2026

The North American Championship crowned Connor Chambers in a five-man elimination match in June, and by the fed's own next surviving cards had already passed to Tommy Grayson and then to Suicide King — each new holder simply defending the belt on-camera with no televised change of hands recorded anywhere in the surviving run. The Television title did the same, Ray Simmons's June win giving way to Fang holding it uncontested by August. Both lineages are recorded exactly as thin as the corpus leaves them — and the pattern breaks cleanly in October, when a newly installed VP explains the gap away by simply erasing it.

The masked men Written by AI, 2026

A masked man attacks Cyris at StableWars '98, in language a later broadcast echoes almost word for word as "the same masked man that attacked Cyris during the last PPV" — a phrase that places StableWars, not Bloodbath '98, as the thread's own starting point. The device recurs at StableWars itself a second time that same night, a different masked man running in for Death Row's own side of its stable war before Cyris turns traitor on everyone in the ring including him; at Bloodbath '98, a "Masked Superstar" attacking Cyris on his own birthday finally unmasks as Pimp Daddy J; and a separate masked ally interferes for Pimp Daddy J himself the week before that, never identified. None of these masks are treated as the same man in this record — the fed's own text never says they are, and forcing a single identity onto a recurring costume would invent a fact the run itself never commits to. A cleaner reveal closes out October: Fang, reported missing for weeks, turns out to have spent that stretch wrestling masked as Gouki, feuding with Capital Punishment before returning to his own name at the fed's own on-air relaunch. Two more masks unmask across the winter on the same terms: Ronny Wonder as the "retired" Dirk Ryan, and the Riot Squad's own Helldorado as Jerry Micheals all along.

Devastation Inc.'s coup, and Cyris's rise and fall Written by AI, 2026

JD Freeman used a video-taped ambush of two rivals to declare Devastation Inc. was "taking over" the EWA in September 1998, then forced President Aaron, live in the ring at StableWars '98, to name him Vice President under a contract clause — the same broadcast on which Cyris, believed loyal to Death Row, revealed himself as Freeman's own planted traitor and joined Devastation Inc. Cyris went on to win the Global Hardcore Championship three separate times (September, October, and December 1998), the second and third reigns following a wholesale title-stripping a new VP, Artful, ordered at the fed's own on-air relaunch that October. He announced his own retirement from the ring, on camera, in the main event of Yuletide Beatings in January 1999 rather than defend the belt one more time, disbanding Devastation Inc. by handing the promotion to Death Row before the show closed.

The hitman murders Written by AI, 2026

An in-house detective, Dee Brown, spent the fall investigating who killed Lonewolf, and named his answer live in the ring that December: JD Freeman had hired a killer to remove Dingo and Tommy Grayson's own uncle Johnathon Grayson after the two threatened to expose an insurance scheme skimming EWA wrestler-death payouts, then had Lonewolf killed for the same reason once he found out — and when Freeman himself couldn't cover what he owed the hitman, the hitman killed him too, the "accident" the fed's own broadcasts had described weeks earlier. Brown's own interview was cut short by an attack before he could finish, but the confession stands as the fullest explanation the surviving record gives for Freeman's own death. A second, unrelated on-air killing closes out the run unsolved: Crystal Laine, Wayne Rothschild's own fiancee, is found with her throat cut in the ring at Yuletide Beatings, dropped in during an entrance meant for a debuting wrestler, Hans "Fox" Taubmann — questioned on camera, never charged, and never resolved in anything this collection holds.

Death Row and the comedy undercard Written by AI, 2026

Drifter and Pimp Daddy J's own stable, Death Row, grew a roster (Cyris, Verge Darsow, O.G.) across the summer feuding Devastation Inc. to a standstill, while a very different kind of stable ran the undercard for laughs: Hanging Judge and Alistair Cloudfruit's Jobbers Inc., which absorbed Quicksilver, Hammer Canseco, and the tag team Venom before inducting Mopie, the Washington Zoo gorilla who had already decided the fed's very first title tournament, as a full member.

Death Row outlasted its own defection: with Cyris gone to Devastation Inc. and Verge Darsow written out unexplained, Violent Dark and Tyrant joined the stable that winter, and Drifter and Violent Dark won the newly created EWA World Tag Team Championship at Yuletide Beatings in January 1999 — the promotion's own last title change in the surviving record.
