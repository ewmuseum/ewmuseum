A Seattle-headquartered regional promotion that closed abruptly in 2002 and relaunched in 2004 with rescreened classic matches, a new roster, and a training program for newer roleplayers. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Western States Wrestling was born mid-run, not from nothing. Its own May 2002 broadcast opens on the final card under its previous name, World Street Wrestling, its own announce team calling it live: new ownership, a new championship for a Pacific Northwest that replaced a retired European title, and a new Interstate Championship in place of an old Intercontinental strap. A North American Championship and a unified Hardcore Championship were both handed out that same night and never defended again; the promotion's own 2004 return still called its winner, Krim'nal, its last North American Champion, and the last Hardcore Champion, Nova Johnson, simply gave his belt away backstage to a friend a month later.

Across that summer — twelve more broadcasts and a beach pay-per-view called Summer Showdown — the WSW Championship never changed hands, held throughout by “Superstar” Sean Southward against his own brother, a masked veteran turned villain, and a debuting challenger who tried to bury him under a Singapore Cane. A twenty-man Royal Rumble crowned that challenger; a returning former champion and a returning masked star both won their first matches back; a Lord of the Ring tournament was announced for that September and had begun crowning its own quarterfinalists by the promotion's last surviving broadcast, 17 August 2002 — a main event that ended on a stranger's chairshot in the dark, a note left unread on a fallen wrestler's chest, and no answer surviving.

Its own 2004 comeback broadcast looked back on the run's own high point: a four-belt unification match at Payback (14 October 2001) in which the promotion's own commissioner inserted a fifth, unbooked wrestler, The Mutt, into the match — who left with three of the four championships after three straight finishers the broadcast calls Impounders. Its relaunch FAQ, posted 25 August 2004, opened with a plain admission: “In September 2002, Western States Wrestling (WSW) closed its door for what many thought was forever. Two years later, everything has changed.” The new WSW planned a twelve-to-sixteen wrestler roster, two championships (a WSW Championship and an Interstate Championship), two shows — a weekly Saturday Night broadcast and a syndicated Power Hour — and a training program, the Steele Dojo, for newer roleplayers to develop outside their main characters.

Its actual return aired 18 September 2004, headlined by former Pacific Northwest Champion Jason Reynolds against newcomer Carl Blair, while veteran “Krim'nal,” the promotion's own last North American Champion before the shutdown, set his sights on the vacant WSW Championship two years after the title had eluded him. Its last surviving broadcast, a week later on 25 September, closed its main event on two former Interstate Champions, Eric Lee and Mr. Ed, trading finishers in front of a crowd split between them.

The surviving capture of that main event cuts off mid-fall, the pin never resolved on the page. No further broadcast survives; whether the relaunched Western States Wrestling lasted beyond that night isn't in the surviving record.
