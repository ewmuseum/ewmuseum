Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Extreme Wrestling Organization dates its own birth to 1 April 1997, marking the date itself a year later as its own birthday. By December of that first year it had already closed once to rebuild — “The eWo was closed so I could build a new page. The fed at the moment is full of jobbers. I'm about to go on a firing spree” — purging its roster twice in the same month before relaunching for a New Year's Day card.

Its own handbook describes a booked promotion rather than a simulated one, deciding matches by “heat/push/storyline” built from three kinds of writing it called flashes, in-rings and strats, across three divisions and four championships — World Heavyweight, Intercontinental, Light-Heavyweight and Tag Team. It also kept a standing stable of losers to laugh at, the J.O.B. Foundation, fronted by Rich Jericho.

Its broadcast of 23 May 1998, Slammin' Saturday Night, ran from New Orleans and opened on flashback footage of its own Hardcore Title feuds before introducing the promotion's first female tag team, Sandra “The Horrible” Maria and Carissa Marsh, in an exhibition against the veteran pairing of Sam Ramey and Eric Fox.

That July, its own on-screen founder, President Kozak, stepped down on camera and handed the office to his Vice-President, President Nikithser, framing it as a transitional period while the roster and front office were reorganized; by the promotion's own later account it closed outright the following month.

It returned 20 March 1999, and within days President Kozak announced, again on camera, that the promotion would merge with a second federation to crown every champion together at a joint pay-per-view on 4 April 1999. Its last surviving broadcast, aired 29 April 1999, shows the wrestler Ragnarok celebrating a win of the Heavyweight Championship days into the merged promotion's run, declaring himself undefeated and daring its other champions to test him. Nothing later survives.
