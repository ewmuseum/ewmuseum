Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The International Invitationals of e-Wrestling was proposed to rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy on 28 December 1997 by a single newsgroup member, not by any one federation. His own account of how it started is a small story in itself: an early call for a combined all-star fed drew almost no response, and the idea was nearly dead until a dream of impossible dream matches — Ric Flair against Shawn Michaels, Andre against Hulk Hogan — sent him back to Deja News hunting down e-wrestling's own top talent by hand. He was deliberately selective about which feds he let in, by his own account, “to justly keep the word 'Invitational' in the name.”

By 1 February 1998 twenty matches were signed, forcing a one-night plan into two; IIWF and MLWO were among the federations that sent wrestlers. The event crowned its own on-screen president, Ron Deschenes, presiding over an eight-man in-house tournament run for a trophy bearing his name, the Deschenes Cup.

Both nights, 28 February and 1 March 1998, played from the SkyDome and closed on a steel cage main event between “Lone Wolf” Brody Thunder and the hometown favorite, “Playboy” Ronnie D, who won it.

The Deschenes Cup itself went to Diamondback Chris Myers, who beat Simon Lebec, Creed, and J.W. Hardin across the tournament's rounds and, breathless backstage afterward, called it the night he went from “minor league” to “top of the wrestling world.”

Its own organizer's closing letter, posted 5 March, called the whole thing the most rewarding thing he had done in e-wrestling, and thanked the “Net.Buddies” he had made putting it together — already looking ahead, even there, to doing it again.

A second running, IIeW '99, was announced on 23 October 1998 and extensively planned — a full signed card, a rescheduled three-night format — before its own organizer confirmed on the newsgroup, on 30 January 1999, that he was calling it off before a single night aired. No broadcast, result, or roster document survives for it anywhere in the record.
