Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Allstar Wrestling of North America presented itself as a merger: three earlier regional leagues — the UCFW of St. John's, Newfoundland, the CSW of Anaheim, California, and the HFW of Hamilton, Ontario — each collapsed, after which UCFW's own owner bought out the other two and built one operation, headquartered in St. John's with further offices in Anaheim and Hamilton. Its own handbook dates the buyout's signing to “January 1st” and its own body text says the whole enterprise “began in January 1999,” with an opening card planned for that February — though the same handbook's own header, oddly, is dated a year earlier, 27 January 1998, an inconsistency the document itself never resolves.

Its kayfabe staged cards at five real arenas across Canada and the United States — Memorial Stadium in Newfoundland, the Georgia Dome, GM Place, Copps Coliseum, and the Anaheim Pond — carried on the FOX Network in the US and TSN in Canada, and styled itself as clean, mainstream “family entertainment,” not “hardcore” or “mainstream extreme.”

It planned two weekly cards, the larger Saturday Night Security Breach (hosted by Sky Williams and Tom Warner) and the smaller Wednesday War (Doc Dudley and Tom Warner), plus six themed Supercards a year standing in for pay-per-views it couldn't yet run: a fenced-parking-lot battle royal, New Year's Nightmare, in January; a challenge to its own Extremist Champion, X-Treme Dreams, in March; War Games matches at MayDay in May; a heavyweight tournament, Lord of the Ring, in June; its flagship event, Wrestle Cade, in August; and an all-stipulation card, Hardcore Halloween, in October — alongside an annual awards ceremony held every year on its own president's birthday, 30 August.

It ran four titles — World Heavyweight, World Tag Team, North American, and an Extremist title reserved for no-disqualification, no-countout matches — decided by what it called a “Human Judgement System” weighing roleplay quality, ongoing angles, submitted strategies, and effort, and it barred real wrestlers, their gimmicks, and even the words “e-wrestling” or “fantasy wrestling” from its own roleplays: “within the game, it's wrestling to the characters.”

This handbook is the only document of it that survives; no card, roster, or closing notice of it is recorded.
