A Usenet e-fed running a weekly hand-written broadcast toward a six-title championship ladder, its owner deliberately hands-off in favor of an in-fiction Commissioner. A Wednesday Night WarFair card from the Louisiana Superdome survives alongside its founding overview; eight wrestlers are recorded. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

House of Champions Wrestling introduced itself on 29 March 1999, drawing a line under how its cards were made: “Cards- ARE NOT SIMMED, they are all hand written by the card writing staff.” Its own owner, signing himself Gary, said plainly that he had no interest in playing an on-screen authority figure himself — “I do not intend to be a McMahon or Bischoff and get involved in the fed beyond controlling it” — leaving an in-fiction Commissioner to run the promotion's own business on-screen. It planned six championships: Heavyweight, North American, International, Extreme, Cruiserweight, and Tag Team, the first three to be decided at an April supercard and the Extreme title the month after.

Its weekly broadcast, Wednesday Night WarFair, aired 1 April 1999 before a sold-out Louisiana Superdome. Its opening match ended when George “The Greatone” Goldstein bribed his opponent, Jerry “The Traveler” Travis, with a briefcase holding $25,000 to walk out and take a countout — and Travis took the money. A later match saw its own color commentator, doubling that night as the masked wrestler Colossus, leap the announce table to blindside one competitor with a steel chair, clearing the way for Swamp Thing to finish him with a crossface hold he called the Crocodile Smile.

The same broadcast advertised its first supercard, Lord of the Rings (25 April 1999, Houston Astrodome), built around two rings running at once and a Royal Rumble to crown the promotion's first Heavyweight Champion, and previewed a Lights Out Double Hell Exploding Barbed Wire match still to come between Ah'riman and Cherokee.

No further document survives; whether Lord of the Rings was held, or who its six championships' first holders turned out to be, isn't in the surviving record.
