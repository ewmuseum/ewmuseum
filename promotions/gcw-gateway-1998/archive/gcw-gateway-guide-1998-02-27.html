Some of you know me as the handler of Alex Reaver, Jeremy (J.P.) Steele, or, lately, the Upper Classmen. If not, you might know me from my last fed, NOW. NOW ran for 10 months, and closed when my co-President had to lose access for 2 months due to a move. I was taking a break from things for a few weeks when this happened. He closed NOW ... only to find out days later that he would be keeping access after all. It's not his fault, but there was NO way I could bring NOW back after that. It's name just wasn't that high anymore, despite a solid roster and staff.
Well, the itch came back to me. I wanted to write and book again. I wanted to be in charge. Instead of a fed, I had an idea --
SHOWCASE. It would be a special little thing, where wrestlers could face each other on neurtral ground, 2 or 3 times a month, in a single match card for the e-wrestling world to see. Unfortunantly, the feedback for it was _very_ minimal. That left me thinking ...
GCW is what I thought up. It's not going to be big, and it might not even get to be that respected. Frankly, I don't care about that anymore. I just want a small, regional fed to call MINE, a place where I can write, see some good, fun roleplayers at work, and not get attached and obssessed and addicted and ruin whatever chance of a life I've got. :)
GCW isn't going to be the next IIWF or MLWO, unless people consider a tiny St. Louis based federation with limited exposure capable of that. Well, maybe it will end up as one of those notable feds. If it does, it'll have to spend MANY months at the bottom ... and that's where I'm willing to take it. Being the
'best' isn't my concern. Providing an on-time, consistent, ENJOYABLE fed is.
I'd like to think I did that with NOW, with the help I recieved. I hope I can do it again, except without the bumps and bruises. So, read on, e-wrestling ...
and let's see what happens. °°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°° GCW History We haven't even had a match yet, how are we supposed to have a history? :) Well, we do have an orgin of how we came to exsist. I guess that's what this section is for.
As spring lay on the horizan, 13 year old prodigy, "the Stuff" Zach Hyatt, looked for something more in his favorite past time. He already had a sucessful program on another fed's review show, but he wanted more. He wanted to call the action, to bring in the talent, and watch it grow and expand to something the world could enjoy. But, as most 13 year olds, he didn't have that much cash. So, he found Calvin
Wylde, older brother of former DPW and GFWA tag star Scotty B. Wylde. Calvin had the cash and the connections to open stock in a small federation. They didn't have much, but enough to be able to air a weekly program on Fox Sports Midwest every Friday night, enough to house a decent sized roster with average (but not more) contracts for the wrestlers, and to purchase two championship belts. They're not COMPLETLEY gold, but they're quality metal lined with gold, so they're not some cheap plastic stuff, either.
Anyway, Calvin had the cash and the connections ... but he didn't know EXACTLY what to do with it. Zach did, and thus, the 13 year old was appointed Commishioner of GCW. Zach had many friends in wrestling, and he used his ties to begin recruiting. Was he successful? I don't know, we just started. :)
So, now, GCW lives inside the Kiel Center in downtown St. Louis ever Friday night, with Fox Sports Midwest airing the program, as well as a batch of interviews twice a week.
Gateway City Wrestling has begun ... how far will the regionally based blue-chip organazation go? We'll see!
°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°° GCW Participation In GCW, roleplaying is the main key to victory. However, winning in the long run will take more than just spitting out a decent roleplay every week. No, it takes a lot of things. The wrestler's with the most 'push' do well in the long run. Push is decided by a handful of things -- roleplaying skill, consistency, ability to draw 'heat' or get 'over', ability to develop 'character', and ability to get involved in angles or storylines.
Let's be honest, folks ... if all GCW was was a handful of matches each week where all they did was wrestle, it'd be pretty boring. :) So, make friends, make enemies. Get in touch with fellow handlers about running an attack on someone, or an in ring together.
Do things to make your wrestler NOTICED. Get involved and have fun. Don't just do a roleplay or two a week and never get involved ... 'cause that will only get you so far. What can you do? Well, for starters, there's roleplays. On Mondays and Wednesdays, a packet of rp's will be sent. These count towards that Friday's matches and play a good chunk in booking. So, one way to stay on top of things is to roleplay on a regular basis, to a good degree.
Roleplays are a big chunk, but they're not ALl you can do. In ring or on card interviews are also welcome, but these
WILL count as one of your two roleplays for the week. They also count towards the match, though, so it's worth doing if you like speaking in front of the audience on the night of the matches.
Strats also play a smaller part. Whether they're to attack somebody during their match or how YOU want to wrestle your match, these can make a difference. Little things add up!
Angles are a big part of success. Don't just simply wrestle and go home ... start a feud, start a friendship, create a storyline. GCW handlers' addies are always available for you to get in order to contact others to set something up.
°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°°° GCW Shows & Titles Gateway City Wrestling produces one weekly show, Roadkill, broadcasted on Fox
Sports Midwest at 8PM, Central. Hopefully, several other U.S. affiliates of Fox Sports will also carry the show. It will feature
4-5 matches, plus whatever in rings or on card interviews sent in that week. Every two months or so, there will be a supercard. The supercard might be in a different venue in the Midwest, and feature double the matches as a normal card.
The announcers? GCW Commishioner "the Stuff" Zach Hyatt steps in as the face commentator, while Rob Scatterson does the color commentary as the heel. The ring announcer has no name, because he's not important. He just holds the cue card. The referees will fluxiate to whomever Wylde and Hyatt can afford, so they, too, remain nameless.
GCW will hold _two_ titles for it's talent to compete for. If a tag team division evolves, a third title will be set up. The titles are as follows: GCW Missouri Heavyweight Title: The big one. All the wrestlers in GCW are eligble to compete for this, regardless of weight. It is defended every three to four weeks on Roadkill, as well as on supercards.
GCW Television Title: The secondary title, which is still a great accomplishment. Defended every other week on Roadkill, as well as on supercards. Wrestlers below 290lbs are eligble for this title.
