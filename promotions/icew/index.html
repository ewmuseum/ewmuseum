Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

ICEW opened on rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy on 14 August 1997 as a rename, not a new start: its own founding post announces that “the league formerly known as ACEW is now International Championship Extreme Wrestling” under a new fed head, who met the genre stigma head-on — “I know some people see the name Extreme in a fed's name they are automatically turn their nose up at the fed” — and asked skeptics to judge it on the cards themselves, past and future.

Its first Hardcore Saturday Night, three days later, ran same-night tournaments to crown two champions. Jeff “Madfox” Matthews outlasted “The Player” Jason Polo in a brawl that spilled into the crowd and onto a guard rail before Matthews closed the tournament against Mr. Bungle with a chair-assisted finish he called the Fox's Lair, becoming the first ICEW Cruiserweight Champion of the World. Immorality won the companion Heavyweight tournament over Glenn “Plastic Surgeon” Skiba to become the first ICEW World Heavyweight Champion.

Both titles were defended at the second Hardcore Saturday Night on 31 August: Matthews retained against Suicide, and Immorality retained against Skiba only after the match itself was voided and restarted mid-broadcast — Skiba's identical twin had swapped in unnoticed by the referee, and once his manager's own protest exposed the switch, the whole match resumed from scratch with every second on both sides banned from ringside. The same card crowned ICEW's first Intercontinental Champion in a ten-man battle royal won by “Lucky Man” Dylan Dice, who shoved the fed's own on-screen president down and flipped him off when handed the belt. That battle royal's masked mystery entrant, billed only as Deathbringer, pulled off his own cowl mid-match to reveal “Iron Man” Mike Madland, who had been “pretending to be Deathbringer to fool everybody” the whole time.

Its own president kept defending the fed's reputation in the newsgroup even as the cards ran: on 1 September he wrote, “Alrighty then, I am the president of ICEW, yes, we are an extreme fed. Is that so bad?”, and passed along what he said his members and other fed heads had told him — that he wrote “some of the best cards they have ever read.”

That 1 September post is the last dated document the surviving record carries. No later card, title change, or closure notice survives under ICEW's own name; the record simply stops there, eighteen days after its own relaunch.
