A December 2001 thirty-two-man tournament that invited entrants from across the newsgroup to crown the year's best wrestler, its own founding post remembered mainly for a persistent misspelling of the word "tournament." Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

MEW's thirty-two-man tournament opened for entries on 14 December 2001, pitched as a search for “an outstanding wrestler,” one wrestler per newsgroup screen name, judged by three to five judges hand-picked in advance and expected to run three to six weeks before crowning a winner on a well-known wrestling site. Its own founding post invited anyone without a federation to also look into National Championship Wrestling, and set two ground rules up front: no arguing the outcome of a match, and no waiting until the deadline to send in roleplays.

The tournament's own founding post spelled “tournament” as “tornoment” throughout, and the misspelling itself became the newsgroup's own running story for the next two weeks. One reply told the poster to “spell ‘tournament’ right and maybe the rest of the post might be worth the read”; others defended him, noted he had only been asked to copy and paste the announcement on someone else's behalf, or argued the error said nothing about his fitness to run a tournament at all. The exchange ran to a dozen replies across ten days, trading jokes about turkey theft, brain surgery, and Christmas cheer along the way.

A second controversy followed close behind: readers accused the same poster of stuffing the newsgroup's own RSPWF Year-End Awards ballot, pointing to several near-identical ballots sent from different addresses within minutes of each other. The tournament's own promoter answered directly on 25 December, denying he had written or posted the original announcement himself, denying any involvement in the year-end ballots, and admitting bluntly of the tournament itself: “I well admit this tournament is shit... I dont have nothing to do with this tournament but advertise and write some results.”

The last surviving document, from Christmas Day 2001, is still this argument playing out. Whether the thirty-two brackets were ever filled, whether any round of the tournament was actually written, and who if anyone was ultimately named the year's best wrestler isn't in the surviving record.
