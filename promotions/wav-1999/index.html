Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Wrestling Alliance of Vegas launched live on Independence Day 1999 from a sold-out MGM Grand Arena, opening with footage of a founding press conference in which its president, Tony Lattanzio, announced his and his brother Tino's public split from a rival promotion, the Apex Wrestling Association, after being passed over for a tag title shot there. Tony declared himself the WAV's owner outright — “I own the WAV, I run the WAV, I am the WAV” — and introduced Tino as his first signing, naming nine more wrestlers already under contract.

Tony did not appear at the debut show itself; Tino spoke for him, confirming that both the Vegas Championship and a Death Valley title would be decided at the promotion's first pay-per-view, Tsunami, based on performances rather than a tournament. The debut card's main event, Tino Lattanzio against “Malibu” Mike Swanson, went to a hard-fought finish, with Tino escaping a surfboard-assisted DDT attempt to win with a top-rope tornado DDT of his own.

Three weeks later, on 25 July 1999, WAV crowned its first Vegas Champion in a fifteen-man battle royal. Tony Lattanzio appeared this time, taking a seat at the announce table with an enforcer, Rob Ackerman, and producing the title belt on air. The match ran through eliminations by Anaconda, Shinja, and others before Tino himself was eliminated in a double knockout alongside Anaconda — engineered by Ackerman pulling Anaconda's foot at Tony's own whispered instruction — and a returning rival, Mentor, cost late leader Knightfall the match with a bone thrown from ringside. Eddie Lobos capitalized on the chaos, eliminating Jack Spades last to become WAV's first Vegas Champion.

No document after that battle royal survives, and no closure notice survives either. How Eddie Lobos's title reign, or WAV itself, played out from there isn't known.
