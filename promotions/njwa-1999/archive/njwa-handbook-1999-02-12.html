NJWA Handbook v1.0

12 February 1999
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hello, My name is Zach Tool, I handle "Hurricane" Mark Winters in IMWA. Well, as you can probably tell from the subject, I've decided to give fed-heading a shot. I decided to to create the New Jersey Wrestling Association because, firstly, I have always wanted to run a fed but couldn't find the time, until now.
Secondly, I've been in my share of promotions, I've seen what makes them work,
I've seen good points and bad points. NJWA is a mixture of what I feel makes a good fed. Not everybody will enjoy my promotion, but not every fed is for everybody.
I've seen the problems that many feds have had... lateness, lack of participation, lack of quality, and most important: lack of fun. I can't promise that NJWA will be the best, all I can promise you is that I will do my best to make NJWA fun for all members. That's the most important thing to me, the fun.
On top of making NJWA fun, I want to change the lateness and quality that plagues so many promotions. To make sure that the fed is of best-quality, first
I made it a regional promotion. Second, there won't be 10-12 matches per card, more like 6-8 at the most. This way, if a match writer can't come through, I've got enough time to write the match.
With a list of things that make a fed crumble from within, I believe that I will be able to run a very successful promotion by steering clear of everything on that list. This fed may not be perfect, but it will be perfect for me. I want to build this fed the way that I see works the best.
I'm not here to get the biggest names possible, or the most popular, I'm here to get the best workers. The best handlers. I don't care if they were voted #1 three years straight, if they don't participate, they aren't very good to the promotion. So what I am looking for are people who are devoted to ewrestling, and more importantly to NJWA.
Thank you for reading this post, the handbook is below, and if you like what you see, an application is on the very bottom of the handbook. Please return it to
[name withheld]

How NJWA is run -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- This is a description of how the NJWA promotion is run and who makes the important business decisions around here.
a. What do "booked" and "simmed" mean? Booked and simmed are ways to determine the winner of a match; simmed uses a computer or dice-based system to decide who the winner is, while booked is left up to the booker (usually the President and/or a Presidents advisor(s))
to decide who wins. Most of the "big name" feds on the internet today are booked, using flashes, angles, strategies and handler involvement to determine the victor.
b. Which does NJWA use? Here at NJWA, we use the booked method to decide the winners. We feel that if left to a computer, the fed wouldn't be life-like, which is what we are shooting for.
c. Who decides this? The man behind the decision-making is the President, Zach Tool. At certain times, he will ask advisors who he believes should win.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 3. Guide for new entrants -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- The following is important for new entrants to NJWA and/or ewrestling itself. It explains what role-plays are, flashes, strategies and more. Please pay extra attention to this segment.
a. Flashes A flash is a segment that you write for your wrestler that makes him or her get "over" with the crowd. The term "over" means that the character is either very liked or disliked. You do these from various locations, whether they be a church, a store, a mountain or whathaveyou. In a flash, you will describe the scene and the wrestler and you will talk about current issues, opponents, allies or anything else. When you write a flash, there are things to remember; there are also things to not do.
Always: - Start the flash with a scene. You decipher the scene from the talking using "[]" brackets around the paragraph. - When starting to talk, use this format: 'INITIALS: Talk, talk, talk.' An example, for the loved member of NJWA's "Wrestling Impaired Factory", Milk Man, could be:
'MM: I'm gonna beat you from pillar to post!' You can also use three initials. An example, for another loved member of
NJWA's "Wrestling Impaired Factory", "Moronic" Ike Rush could be:
'MIR: I'm tired of your crap!' - Be slightly descriptive in your flashes; we don't want a novel, but we don't want lack of effort, either. You don't need to make them more than a paragraph, I don't think that that's too much too ask considering that half of the paragraph will be describing the scene.
- Be polite. You don't have to be the kid of a Priest or anything, just don't use racial slurs or anything else that would offend somebody. If you do, your flash will be cut out or edited, and after several times, your employment with NJWA will be terminated.
- [BEEP] out foul language. This is said in just about every fed, but I like it: if you can say it in WWF or WCW (On TV that is), then you can say it in NJWA. Any other vulgar language will be [BEEP]'ed. out.
- End the flash with "[Fade.]" or some other type of closing. Don't leave a flash on the edge like that. Never: - Use another wrestler -- harmful or not -- unless you have permission from the handler. This will only get you on the wrong side of the handlers and in trouble with the President.
- Beat up on NJWA personalities unless you have permission from the NJWA President, Zach Tool. This means, no beating up on Rob Abbott, Ronnie
Johnson or any other NJWA official.
- Use terms such as "e-wrestling," "role-play," "flash," or any initials such as LOL, ROFLMAO or any others. You can say "Ha, ha, ha," or "heh heh," but never LOL, LMAO or any others.
b. In-rings An in-ring is when your wrestler comes to the ring -- during a card -- and talks about what ever is on his mind. If he has something to say about another wrestler, here is a good time to say it. This is also a good time to set angles because the wrestler that you are working with could easily come to the ring to build on the angle.
This type of role-play can fall into two different categories: good or bad. Many in-rings are very well created; nice layout, interesting dialogue, you know, nice little heat machines :). On the other hand, many are out to try to get the character over while they fail miserably. Here are a few do's and don'ts for in-rings.
Always: - Follow the flash guidelines as stated previously. - Include a detailed entrance; this includes entrance music, pyrotechnics, or any other sort of entrance that you may want. - Keep a realistic view on everything. We are by no means an "extreme" promotion, please keep what you do on the NJWA air to a minimum of what you see on WWF or WCW.
Never: - Space your in-ring all over the place. Here's what I mean: 'MAN: I'm..... the..... best.... damn.... show.... going.' I hate that to no end. Keep it reasonable; if not, I have to spend my time editing your in-rings... which isn't fair for me.
c. Interviews These are probably the best way to get your wrestler started in NJWA -- unless of course an angle is being set up to debut your wrestler -- they allow for the wrestler to be able to express his beliefs; ethnic background; history; and plans for the future. This will allow the front office, and the rest of the promotion, who exactly your wrestler is, what he is like, how good of a character he is.
The guidelines for interviews are basically the same as for flashes, there are however a few things add to that list:
- Always have the interview take place from the back of the NJWA Arena. There is a white interview room with a blue NJWA banner plastered on the back wall. A podium set up on the small stage and chairs scattered about. Use this wisely.
- Feel free to use any of the NJWA's personalities; I would prefer that you use Zach Tool, "Hurricane" Mark Winters and Bob Ryan on the stage, but whatever floats your boat.
d. Angles I can't tell you how important angles are; they keep the fed going. I will create as many angles as I can, but your main job as a NJWA handler is to create angles with other handlers.
Unlike many feds, we don't base our promotion on mainly angles, though. Angles play an important part, but more like 50% of the equation. We also need handler participation, quality role-playing; and showing interest in the promotion. Without an angle, your wrestler could very well sit at the bottom of the roster for some time until I decide to cut the dead weight
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 4. Instructions for new entrants -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- The following is for what you are to do upon entry to the NJWA. Follow closely. a. Squash match Your first match will be a squash match against one of our own lovable
"Wrestling Impaired Factory" (The jobber union. ;)) members. There are six to choose from:
- "Moronic" Ike Rush - The Jabrone - Milk Man - "Paperboy" Luke Walker - "Unholy" Rick Hayes - Johnny Cool Guy Each has a biography on the NJWA webpage. Your job is to write your squash match; all you do is pick a jobber, write the match and send it to me. It's very simple. There is a sample squash match on the webpage, give it a visit. That is what every squash match should look like (except for the wrestling action itself... you need to write that ;)).
b. Introductory interview/in-ring There are two way to introduce yourself into NJWA: if you are involved in an angle, and in-ring would be suitable; if you are just entering the league blindly, an interview is your *only* option.
Please follow the role-play guidelines while completing either one of these assignments. -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 5. Shows -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- Currently, NJWA has to televised shows: one a wrestling show, the other an role-play show. If I can get enough time on my hands, or if I can get somebody to write a show for me, I would like to add a "squash match show" to the list of cards. Until then, we are stuck with one wrestling show per week. Don't like it? Volunteer to write matches or the squash match card. Inquiries here:
[name withheld] ;) a. Sunday Mayhem The main show of the week, this card will cover 6-8 live matches per week,
1-2 squash matches, and the Television title will be on the line every week!
The Atlantic County title will be on the line in the main event once a month, at least. WHERE: NJWA Arena (Atlantic City, New Jersey); or another small venue in
Southern Jersey. WHEN: Every Sunday, unless noted otherwise. HOSTED BY: Jeremy Cecil, Martin Andrews and "Crazy" Mark Wilson b. Listen up! This show is the televised role-play show of the week. The show is used mainly for interviews, although flashes are also on there. The role-plays are interrupted by the announcers who comment on them; sometimes good, sometimes bad. WHERE: A studio in the NJWA Tower. WHEN: Usually Saturday night, unless otherwise noted. HOSTED BY: Maria Pontech and Jeremy Cecil. c. Flash packet This is sent one time during the week; it is a list of all of the flashes received so far. Interview usually aren't on this packet, but sometimes get in. This is just an indexed list of flashes, this isn't a televised card.
WHEN: Usually in the middle of the week, it depends on the activity of the handlers that week.
d. Update Not a show, but a very useful piece of information - everything you need or want to know about NJWA this week is on this mail. The upcoming cards, the previous cards, handler information, news, roster, etc.
e. Supercards You want big-time action? This is where to come. Supercards are where dreams are made, careers are ended and angles build. These are pay-per-views, they are merely cards that are roughly two times the size of
Sunday Mayhem. Both titles are on the line, about 10-12 matches on the card. There are four of these cards, once a season; two in the winter.
f. Pay-per-view The only pay-per-view of the year, it is held in the NJWA Arena... in grand fashion. The summertime attracts many people to Atlantic City, so the NJWA has decided to hold Heat Wars, a different kind of wrestling pay-per-view to attract fan base. Will it work? Only time will tell.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 7. Personalities -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- The people that mold NJWA into what it is; the announcers, interviewers, referees and other NJWA personalities.
a. Front Office a.1. President Zach Tool - The Head Honcho, the man behind the screen. He is the President on and off camera. The real life Zach Tool determines winners, angles and everything else to keep the fed running well. The on-camera Zach "runs" the fed; he will make his presence known every now and then; making decisions for the promotion and other presidential duties. (BTW, the real-life President Zach is currently looking for a female intern... it gets mighty lonely in the presidential office, you know. ;))
a.2. Chief Official "Hurricane" Mark Winters - The man who steps in to stop the nonsense. The man is large, so he can stop any problems that may occur. His tell-you-how-it-is attitude goes over well with the fans, although he doesn't pay much attention to them.
b. Announcers b.1. Martin Andrews - The face announcer. Always trying to look on the positive side of things, which upsets Jeremy Cecil and Mark Wilson.
he often gets too involved in the match, causing Jeremy to usually slap him in the back of the head.
b.2. Jeremy Cecil - The heel announcer who likes to take advantage of
Martin Andrews. He's a con-man in disguise, he's a lady's man, and he's also "the rootenist tootenist announcer this side of the
Mississippi!" as he so loves to tell us. b.3. "Crazy" Mark Wilson - The man is nutcase, plain and simple. He's more of a tweener announcer than a heel because he's not "always with us." Coming out of a two-years stay in a mental hospital,
Jeremy Cecil is always trying to buy Wilson's pills off of him.
b.4. Maria Pontech - Blonde hair, blue eyes, plenty of curves. She's the blonde-bombshell of the NJWA, using her body as a means of getting attention. She's known for her short skirts, tight tops and high heels.
c. Interviewers c.1. Bob Ryan - Bob's not too confident in himself, always being "stepped on" by NJWA superstars, fans and other people. He gets his job done, but not too well. For this, Jeremy Cecil is on a campaign to get him demoted to janitor.
c.2. Nick Williams - The guy who gets the dirt. He's been nicknamed
"Dirt" because he can dig up the scoops... always seems to know more than he's supposed to. The "average" interviewer, suit, black hair, very well-spoken.
d. Referees d.1. Lawrence Clark - The head referee, been in the business for nearly twenty-years. The man has an eye for the ring, he can spot an illegal hold, move or moves in an instant, and he'll call you on each one.
d.2. Cue Ball - The strangest referee around, bald head, plump and small. He waddles around the ring, and loves to see illegal maneuvers. He'd let you get away with spitting in your opponents cuts.
d.3. Jeff Markman - Very controversial, he has a short attention span and has been known to miss plenty of illegal holds and even pinfalls.
Wrestlers get upset quickly with this guy, they have a right to. d.4. Luke Smith - The smallest, size wise, of all of the referees. Brown hair that's combed over, very polite and usually beaten up by the heel wrestlers. He'll call most illegal holds, but not all of them.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 8. Titles -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- These are the sanctioned titles in NJWA, both have different characteristics, so pay attention. (Note: As of the beginning of the fed, there will be no tag team division. As the fed grows stronger, and I get more match writers, a tag division will be added.)
a. Atlantic County title - The main titles. This is for the Heavyweights, the best of the best hold this title. It is defended on every supercard and on occasion on Sunday Mayhem. When you hold this title, everybody is gunning for you, so watch out.
b. Television title - If you hold this title, you're the workhorse of the promotion. Every week you defend the title, and your next stop is the
Atlantic County title.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 9. How-to's -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- These are simple explanations on how to accomplish certain things in the NJWA. a. How to set up angles This is a very important section because of the importance of angles. To make an angle, email a handler that you feel will work well with your character, start talking, then ask about setting up an angle.
It's that simple. The harder part is determining what kind of angle you are going to come up with. It could be as simple as attacking your enemy to a double-cross for your enemy's girl. Your only limitation is your imagination.
After you and your enemy set up the angle, email me and if I like it, boom, it'll go into the next possible card.
b. How to get on a supercard or PPV To get on either one of these cards, just participate. Come up with an angle, show me that you're involved and participating. Also show me that you have the ability to flash for a supercard. I don't want to sign somebody up for a match that takes valuable space if they aren't going to role-play or be involved. It's a waste of time and energy.
Or you could just win a title and you'll automatically be on the card. ;) c. How to do well in NJWA There are several ways to do well in NJWA: role-play consistently with quality, be active, and help out where ever you can. We can always use help, if you want to write a match or help with something else, don't hesitate to email Zach Tool - we'll find something for you to do.
We are always in need of helpers, this is the toughest part of running a promotion. We need writers, bookers and people who just want to be nice and help in anyway. This shows the President that you are willing to do help out the NJWA to further your career and to make yourself one of the better handlers. I look for things like that.
