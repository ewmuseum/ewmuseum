Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The New Jersey Wrestling Association opens its own surviving record in its founder's own voice: “My name is Zach Tool, I handle 'Hurricane' Mark Winters in IMWA… I've decided to give fed-heading a shot.” Its own Handbook v1.0, posted 12 February 1999, describes a deliberately small regional promotion out of Atlantic City — six to eight matches a card rather than ten or twelve — booked rather than simulated, with Zach Tool doubling as its on-camera President alongside his own in-ring persona, Chief Official “Hurricane” Mark Winters.

Its own roster of announcers, interviewers and referees was staged as a small soap opera in itself: face announcer Martin Andrews forever undercut by heel partner Jeremy Cecil, unstable third man “Crazy” Mark Wilson fresh out of “a two-years stay in a mental hospital,” and referee Cue Ball, described as a man who “waddles around the ring, and loves to see illegal maneuvers.” New signings began with a squash match against one of six named jobbers — “Moronic” Ike Rush, The Jabrone, Milk Man, “Paperboy” Luke Walker, “Unholy” Rick Hayes, and Johnny Cool Guy — before an interview or in-ring introduced them properly.

Its own flagship, Sunday Mayhem, ran from the NJWA Arena in Atlantic City with the Television title defended weekly and the Atlantic County title defended monthly; a role-play show, Listen Up!, and a mid-week flash packet rounded out the schedule, building toward four seasonal supercards and a single annual pay-per-view, Heat Wars, timed to Atlantic City's own summer tourist season.

No card, roster, or title result survives beyond the handbook itself; no further document under this promotion's own name has come to light.
