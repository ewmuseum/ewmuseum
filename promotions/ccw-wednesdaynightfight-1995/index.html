Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

CCW Wednesday Night Fight survives in a single document: a recap-and-preview broadcast dated 15 November 1995, posted to rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy under commentators Drake Austin and Michael Smith. By that date the promotion already carried a full championship picture — a World Heavyweight, Intercontinental, United States, Tag Team and Television title apiece — and staged itself as a going weekly program building toward a Sunday supercard, “The Survivor Series '95.”

Three matches aired that night. Shawn Michaels beat 2 Cold Scorpio in a non-title match that went back and forth through a piledriver, an elbow drop and a pair of near-falls before a second superkick put Scorpio away. Raven retained the Television Championship over Razor Ramon after Ramon's own cornerman, the 123 Kid, turned on him mid-match and helped set up the finishing DDT — the broadcast recapped the feud behind it in full, tracing it back through a legdrop through a table, a hospital stay, and a Television Championship that had already changed hands twice between Ramon, Steve Richards and Raven. Sting closed the card retaining the United States Championship over Richards under a loser-leaves-the-promotion stipulation, ending Richards' run on the spot.

Between matches, Hulk Hogan sat for an interview with Brian Wright to explain a Texas death match he had signed against Sabu, casting it as a step into “the more extreme scene” after Sabu's own interference had cost him a shot at Ric Flair's World Heavyweight Championship. The broadcast closed on a wrinkle rather than a resolution: Raven needled Ramon on camera over having no partner left for Sunday, and Ramon, reached separately, vowed to face Raven and Cactus Jack alone if he had to.

The recap ends by laying out that Sunday's full Survivor Series '95 card — Ric Flair defending the World Heavyweight Championship against Michaels, 2 Cold Scorpio defending the Intercontinental Championship against Bret “Hitman” Hart, the Hogan–Sabu death match, Sting defending the United States Championship against Hunter Hearst-Helmsley, a tag elimination match, the Public Enemy defending the Tag Team Championship against Harlem Heat, Taz against a mystery opponent, and the Steiner Brothers against Kama and Sid — none of which this document shows contested. No second broadcast survives, and how the promotion, or the Survivor Series card it previewed, played out is not in the surviving record.
