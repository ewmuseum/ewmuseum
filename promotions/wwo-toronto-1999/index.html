Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World Online Wrestling's surviving record traces its start to April 1998, staged inside one wrestler's own in-fiction biography segment: a struggling attorney named Steve is recruited by his old friend Brian Papa, who is starting a wrestling promotion, and makes his first appearance playing “O Canada” at the promotion's own first card before turning wrestler himself as The Saxist. Its own subject lines took care to disclaim any connection to the better-known federation sharing its own three-letter acronym, spelling out its full name once as distinct.

The one broadcast its surviving record holds in full is a Toronto SkyDome pay-per-view, Tournament of Gods, aired 20–21 March 1999. Co-presidents Sean Shea and Geoff Munn opened it by recapping the month before: a four-way brawl at a Code Red supercard so chaotic — The Saxist ran in uninvited after feeling he'd been denied a spot — that the World Heavyweight Championship had to be stripped and put up in a 28-man tournament instead.

Four men — Jack Ryder, The Saxist, Erebus and “Silent but Violent” Bruce Cornell — drew byes into the field as the promotion's own top contenders; a fifth, Stevie Graham, forced his own way in over the presidents' objections by challenging a rival for his spot and winning it, sporting a knee brace the promotion's own doctor had ruled him unfit to compete in. The final turned on the arena lights repeatedly cutting out around key spots: Erebus finished Jack Ryder with the Black Death sleeper to become the vacant title's first champion.

How the run ended isn't in the surviving record; its last surviving document promises a further card for 29 March 1999 that isn't itself part of what survives.
