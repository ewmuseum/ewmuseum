Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Midnight International Wrestling Association staged its first-ever telecast, Midnight Mayhem, on 17 November 2000 from the Archer Arena in Charlotte, North Carolina, introduced by an announce team, Danny McLean and Butch Bronkhurst, whose bickering over the card ahead opened the broadcast before a bell had even rung. Its first match ended without one: after the crowd took up a chant branding his opponent a copycat, the Loud Mouth Fan walked out, was counted out, and then told a backstage reporter he wouldn't face the Showstoppa at all over what he called the man's “lifestyle.”

The card's main event was a ten-man battle royal to crown the promotion's first Television Champion, run through a costumed brawl — a chainsaw-carrying entrant, a monstrous, soul-collecting Gabriel, a wrestler in a full dinosaur head, and two more wrestlers who arrived hidden inside a pantomime horse — that Axes Rainbow 209 won after interference cleared bigger men out of the ring around him.

The undercard planted two feuds the following card would carry forward: a brutal, outside-the-ring brawl between Gary and the Stinger that the broadcast turned into a cage match booked for the promotion's first pay-per-view, and Rex Denero's running war with the Trailer Park Heroes, Nasty Ned and the Loud Mouth Fan.

By its next telecast, on 24 November, the Association was carrying an unsolved mystery — Bronkhurst attacked in a men's room after the first show and refusing, on air, to name whoever did it — alongside the debut of a new tag team, Maelstrom and Epitaph, managed by the Inquisitor, whose opening interview needled the promotion's own president, Rev. Midnight, by name and closed with a throat-slash gesture aimed at him; the same broadcast announced that the tag team titles would be decided in a four-team elimination match at the coming pay-per-view.

No further telecast, result, or closing notice survives; whether that pay-per-view was ever held, or how any of these angles resolved, is not part of the record the Association left behind.
