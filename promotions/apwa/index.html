Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Action Packed Wrestling Association opened on 12 June 1997 with a recruitment ad staged as a mock “Chicago Tribune Classified.” Its operator, Brett, recruited writers before he even asked for wrestlers, reasoning that “everything in the APWA” should be “on time and well planned” first; within two days he had twelve members signed and a first card set for the 28th, one self-mocking follow-up ad titling itself “Worst Promotion for Members Ever” and earning a nod from the newsgroup's own reviewing circle for having, in their words, “a definate sense of humor.”

Within a month the promotion merged with another, Electronic Global Wrestling, to form EAPW; by August it was operating again as APOW, crowning Pipe Bomb its first National Heavyweight Champion at a debut broadcast, Wednesday Wallop.

Back under its original name that September, it staged a seven-part pay-per-view, Tourney Smash (12 September 1997): J.D. “The Bird” Raven won the National Championship by forcing Shawn Diggs to submit to a reversed Figure-Four Leglock, and the night closed on a ladder match for the vacant APWA Championship in which Taylor “The Godfather” Luchinni and Brandon “The Hurricane” Honeyman each fell from the ladder more than once before Luchinni climbed it a final time to pull down the title. The broadcast ended on a teaser for a follow-up special, a Royal Rumble-style card billed “In Your Face, Da' Man of Da' Ring.”

That promised card never surfaces in the record. Three days after Tourney Smash, the operator announced he had renamed the promotion again, to Chaotic Championship Wrestling, after a separate merger deal had fallen through and left it needing wrestlers once more.

By November its own Player's Guide introduced the renamed promotion as “one of many imaginary fantasy wrestling leagues on the internet,” recapping its operator's run across all of the fed's own names alongside his own record as a wrestler and titleholder elsewhere in the hobby.

No further document survives after that guide. Whether the promotion ran a card under its final name, and how it ended, isn't in the surviving record.
