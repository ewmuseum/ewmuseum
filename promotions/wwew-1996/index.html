A 1996 Usenet e-fed whose two named pay-per-views -- a tag-stable tournament and a UK-set international supercard -- survive complete, run under a three-federation alliance with two sister promotions. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World Wide E-Wrestling introduced itself to Usenet on 30 July 1996 with a plain announcement from its own president: the fed already had a home page up, already had a full roster and was “not accepting any more wrestlers,” and only wanted feedback on the new page.

Its first surviving pay-per-view, Stable War '96 (19 September 1996), ran a single-elimination tag tournament opening with the Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse — Blackout, Pestilence, Nightshade and Drake, the last already carrying the promotion's own Light Heavyweight Title over his shoulder — against Alex's Assassins (Alex Hart, the Archangel, Cold Blooded Nitrogen and Diamond Davey Dynamite). The Horsemen won a back-and-forth match, Blackout pinning Dynamite with a rollup; the same card built toward a ten-man battle royal to crown a new WWEW World Heavyweight Champion.

Its second surviving pay-per-view, World Combat II — Extreme in England (13 October 1996), was staged as a full interview show ahead of that night's card: manager “Sexy” Sara Smith and her charge Sir Olympia challenged World Champion Big “Daddy” Mac to a title-versus-title match against a self-created “Superstar Heavyweight Title” Olympia claimed to have won wrestling for the EWA; Tee Jay was rumored expelled from his own stable, the Pain Cage, ahead of a USA Title Tournament bout with the Archangel; and a comic Heaven-and-Hell segment — God and Beelzebub trashing an announcer's own house on their way to a No Holds Barred match against Metal Militia — closed out the interviews.

No further pay-per-view, card, or closing notice survives past that October broadcast, itself still mid-tournament when the record stops; how the battle royal, the USA Title tournament, or Olympia's title-unification challenge were resolved is not recorded.
