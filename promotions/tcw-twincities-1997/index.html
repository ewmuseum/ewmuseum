A Minneapolis fed formed in September 1997 by the departing fed heads of the NPWA and TPW, one of two simultaneous, unrelated 1997 federations sharing the bare acronym TCW, which the community itself repeatedly disambiguated by name in period before this one merged into TMW at year's end. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Twin Cities Wrestling launched its own weekly magazine, “Twin Cities Weekly,” on 13 September 1997, describing itself as the “brainchild” of the recently departed heads of the NPWA and TPW, based out of Minneapolis and staged at Williams Arena, a 13,000-seat campus venue the fed called “The Barn.” Its first show ran four days later.

It planned three weekly programs — the magazine itself, a midweek broadcast, and a weekend main card — plus a roughly monthly pay-per-view, and crowned all four of its founding titles inside a single opening month: a bracketed Heavyweight tournament, a Royal Rumble for the Intercontinental belt, a four-corner lumberjack match for the Television title, and a scaffold match for the Xtreme Championship. Its on-screen president, Mr. Scott Labat, was billed as a strict disciplinarian ready to fine, suspend, or expel any wrestler who broke the rules.

On 30 December 1997 the TCW merged with the Northern Lights Wrestling Promotion of Toronto to form the TMW (Toronto-Minneapolis Wrestling), carrying over top stars from both rosters; the newsgroup's own contemporaneous replies, preserved alongside the announcement, openly questioned why the merger had happened at all.

Its own 24 May 1998 return bulletin narrated, in the fed's own kayfabe voice, that the promotion had fled to Canada over “illegal business dealings,” sheltered briefly inside a small promotion that Canadian authorities then shut down, and had now come back to Minneapolis “to stay,” rebuilding around its original announcing duo out of a smaller venue, a converted armory.

No further show or closing notice survives past two near-duplicate recruitment ads posted in the two weeks that followed.
