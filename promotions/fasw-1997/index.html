Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Federation of All-Star Wrestlers put up a website on 27 April 1997 and reported 450 hits inside its first ten days, “not bad for a fed that hasn't started yet” ahead of its first card that coming Saturday. Its own future president had held off using the FASW initials months earlier for an unrelated venture, saying only that he “didn't feel comfortable using te FASW initials even if I was allowed.”

That first card, Saturday Main Event on 10 May 1997, opened not in the ring but at a joint press conference with Classic Championship Wrestling over “The Bullet” Ricky Revolution's departure from that promotion to sign with FASW, staged live from the St. Paul Civic Center. The wrestling itself opened with the debut of Lethal Enforcer, who submitted Chris “Action” Jackson with the Lethal Lowdown after a weapon-assisted beatdown; when J.T. Wild tried to make the save he was fought off, and the debuting Valtharius the Mad attacked both battered men once the bell had already rung. The same card opened first-round brackets for a FASW World Television Title tournament and a FASW World Tag Team Title tournament.

Its name drew imitators: a June 1997 post accused a new same-acronym “FASWA” of lifting FASW's own fed name and FAQ without credit, warning the newsgroup, “you could at least try and hold on to fed longer than 5 days!”

A year on, its FAQ of 5 May 1998 still describes a heat-based booking system capped at two roleplays a week per wrestler, a World Heavyweight and a World Television title, quarterly pay-per-views, and cards run across arenas and convention halls in both Canada and the United States. The surviving record runs to a Main Event card announced 12 November 1998, confirming the promotion continued at least eighteen months past its debut; no closing document survives.
