Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The World Internet Wrestling Federation introduced itself on 4 June 1997 in a complete founding package, opening in its own president's words: “Hi I am the WIWF President. My fed World Internet Wrestling Federation,” followed by rules, a card schedule, a title list, and plans for a homepage and an IRC chat room.

It set three cards a week — Monday Night Bash, Wednesday Night Superstars, and Saturday Nite Top Turnbuckle — plus a monthly pay-per-view, required each wrestler to roleplay at least twice weekly, capped recruits at two wrestlers apiece unless told otherwise, and barred real-wrestler portrayals outright.

It named six championships: a Universal Championship at the top, a United States Title, a Superstars Title defended only on its namesake card, a Gimmick Title contested only in gimmick matches, and World and Million Dollar tag team titles.

Within days, a reply in the same newsgroup accused its president of trading on an older, well-remembered WIWF's own reputation for the new fed's name — a dispute the founding post's own community response preserves, not a claim the WIWF made about itself.

No card, roster, or result of its own survives. Months later, another federation's roleplay referred to its own tag champions as having held their belts “before the pres disappeared” — a passing, in-fiction receipt of the WIWF's own collapse, not a closing statement in its own words.
