A promotion attested across dozens of newsgroup thread titles, its own five-part broadcast and a staff call surviving from a two-week January 2001 window before its roster was purchased into a successor federation. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Wrestling Anarchy Organization's surviving record is a single broadcast, “Reckless Abandon,” already airing on 7 January 2001, opening on a video package set to Three 6 Mafia's “Hit 'Em” that cut between wrestlers already established on its own roster before the card itself began.

The broadcast's main event crowned a champion in dramatic fashion: Kurt Madison stacked an announce table on the turnbuckle, a ladder on top of the table, climbed to the top of the ladder, and won with a corkscrew moonsault splash through his downed opponent. Elsewhere on the card, a grudge match pitted a self-proclaimed “heartthrob mastermind” against a wrestler billed as a vigilante, run to a Kids in the Hall theme song played over the entrance. The card's biggest angle belonged to a wrestler returning from retirement, who revealed on camera that he had orchestrated the man he had publicly managed into a losing spot on purpose, tore up that arrangement, and signed his own contract to chase the promotion's Icon Heavyweight Title himself. The main event, billed as one wrestler against a mystery opponent, closed the show on a reveal and a beatdown: the mystery opponent turned out to be a returning “Mr. Main Event,” who sided with a new ally against both men already in the ring and left them laid out as the broadcast ended.

A staff call the following week, 15 January 2001, shows the promotion looking to rebuild behind the scenes rather than in the ring: its own president posted to the newsgroup seeking three match writers and a webmaster, describing WAO as “going through a bit of a change.”

No founding date survives for the Wrestling Anarchy Organization, and no later broadcast under its name does either. A different federation's own handbook, dated four weeks after this staff call, describes purchasing WAO's roster into its new promotion under the same co-president — the last trace this record carries of what became of it.
