Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Rocky Mountain Wrestling Association staged itself out of an arena in Boulder, Colorado, its surviving record running from early February 1998, with a weekly broadcast called Overdrive called by Jeff Stewart and “Chillin'” Chad Killian. Its own signs in the crowd — “RMWA ROCKS MY WORLD!”, “DEUGAU 4 PREZ”, “OSIRIS IS MY DAD” — are as much a part of its own record as any match.

Its own Rocky Mountain Championship changed hands at Avalanche, a ten-part supercard that decided all four of the promotion's own titles in one night: “The American Dream” Kyle Manson beat the reigning champion Scott Winters in a four-way that also drew Kuwasi “Black Fist” Shakur and an unnamed mystery man, while Osiris defended the Heavyweight title against “Diamondback” Dan Travis across two further parts of the same card. Manson did not hold the Rocky Mountain belt long: “The Hollywood Heartthrob” Dave Diamond cost him the title soon after, setting up a rematch the promotion's own record carries as its own next main event.

Its own tag division ran through the stable KAOS, whose members Nik Nyce and Rick Erickson dismantled the Raving Psychos and then talked down every challenger in turn — Nyce, moonlighting as a community-service history teacher in one of the promotion's own vignettes, telling his students to forget the past and “LOOK TOWARDS the FUTURE.” A parallel feud ran between Anthony Chambers and “The Paralyzer” Patrick Bordeau, tracing back to a stable called the Posse that Chambers had already walked away from once.

The promotion's own last surviving broadcast, 30 March 1998, opens with Diamond and Manson set to finally settle their Rocky Mountain Championship dispute, while a second rival, “Psycho Cowboy” Chris Hurt, vows on the same card to carry Diamond's head home in a bowling ball bag. How, or whether, either grudge was ever settled is not in the surviving record.
