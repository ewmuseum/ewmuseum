Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

HOW opened in August 2000 as, in its founding F.A.Q.’s own words, a showcase for graduates of its CEO’s own wrestling school. Its on-screen head was “Wild Thing” Jonnie Adonis, billed as a former Hardcore Championship Wrestling world champion and a former talent scout and interviewer for Super Japan Pro-Wrestling, who founded the Wild Talents Wrestling School in Washington, DC after that promotion folded, then started HOW to give his students a stage. The roster mixed rookies with a smaller number of veteran or once- prominent wrestlers, kept at roughly two rookies for every veteran.

Its broadcast cast filled out the picture: Eddie Rose, a former SJPW play-by-play man, called the action; his wife Sharon Akiyama-Rose, SJPW’s former head interviewer, worked the interviews; Stan “The Man” Caravan announced; and Jesse Reynolds, a former UWA star turned referee, worked alongside two more officials, twin brothers Jim and Mick Johnson. Two championships anchored the card, a Mid-Atlantic Heritage title and Mid-Atlantic Tag Team titles, each defended every four cards.

Its weekly show, Ring Wars, ran from a self-described Heritage Wrestling Arena in Washington, DC and aired up and down the East Coast on public access, staged deliberately without the trappings of a bigger promotion — no pyrotechnics, threadbare production values, and an explicit rule against racist, sexist, or homophobic conduct even as the F.A.Q. pitched the promotion as an old-school throwback. In place of pay- per-views it planned occasional home-video-style special cards built around a long list of named specialty matches, from a Texas Bullrope Match to a rarely used Barbed Wire Cage Match.

No document survives past that founding F.A.Q. Whether Ring Wars ever aired, whether either championship changed hands, or how long HOW lasted is not recorded.
