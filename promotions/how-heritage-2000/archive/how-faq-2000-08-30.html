HOW — Rules

Heritage Organisation of Wrestling F.A.Q.

INTRO & STAFF: Welcome to Heritage Organisation of Wrestling (HOW)! HOW started operations in August of 2000. The CEO of HOW is "Wild Thing" Jonnie Adonis, a former Hardcore Championship Wrestling world champion, and former talent scout and interviewer for Super Japan
Pro-Wrestling (SJPW). HOW was created to showcase talent from Jonnie's "Wild Talents" wrestling school, located in Washington, DC. Most talent comes from said school, though there are some veteran competitors on the roster. Contact for HOW is [detail withheld] [me] My real name is [name withheld]. I book and write up most of the matches and put together the cards.
HOW is an independant wrestling promotion based in The Mid-Atlantic region, so it sometimes holds cards in Virginia, Maryland, North & South Carolina, as well DC. WRESTLERS: HOW is primarily a federation for new wrestlers, though we accept wrestlers who were either once big stars and are now considered "washed up", or wrestlers who never got past the mid-card status in larger promotions. The ratio of rookies should be 2-1, if at all possible.
PARTICIPATION: HOW is going to be run in a very loose style, as to accomodate the schedule of the fedhead. That means that cards can, and probably will, be late. I (and any staff I acquire), will do the best job possible, but I do not want anyone coming in to expect cards every week, on time. It may sometimes be impossible for me to do so, so I came up with an in game reason for late cards.
Therefore, participation every week is *NOT* required. You can
"tape" comments, or do interviews on a card when you are not competing if you want to, though no penalizations will be given.
You should do so when you are set to compete, as it counts partly towards the outcome of your match.
PERSONALITIES: These are some of the colorful personalities that exist in HOW: Jonnie Adonis: This former world champion was a face wrestler in his early years. He became more of a tweener during his hardcore wrestling days. He worked as a talent scout and interviewer for
Super Japan Pro-Wrestling, a promotion once greatly respected in some circles. With it's demise, He went on to found the Wild
Talents Wrestling School. There, he trains people in the arts of
Pro Wrestling, so as to prepare them for a career in this great sport. As owner of the Wild Talents Wrestling School, he started
HOW to give his students an opportunity to hone the skills they learned in actual competition. He's also brought in some experienced wrestlers to help draw some crowds to the shows.
Jonnie acts as CEO of the promotion, in his persona of "Wild Thing" Jonnie Adonis. Eddie Rose: Eddie was formerly a play by play man for SJPW, but when that promotion folded, he was out of a job. He has since worked with Jonnie Adonis at his wrestling school, helping wrestlers on their mike skills. With the start of HOW, Eddie is now the play by play man, a role he knows so well.
Sharon Akiyama-Rose: Eddie's wife, and former head interviewer for
SJPW, Sharon followed her husband to the US, and worked alongside him training rookies on their mike skills. When HOW opened, Sharon literally begged to be the on-air interviewer.
Stan "The Man" Caravan: Stan is HOW's resident ring announcer. It's his job to announce wrestlers, psyche the crowd, and inform fans to check out HOW's various sponsors.
Jesse Reynolds: This former UWA superstar has seen many a battle in his days. A combination of age and injuries ended his career, so he became a referee. As a former wrestler, it's hard to intimidate this 300+ lb senior referee, which is why Jonnie gave him the job.
Jim Johnson: Another referee, he's your standard Pro Wrestling ref. Mick Johnson: Jim's twin brother, and the third HOW ref. Much like his brother. RULES OF CONDUCT: Some basic rules for the promotion Violation of these rules can lead to wrestler suspensions. Repeated violations can lead to expulsion from SJPW. The Rules Of Conduct are as follows:
1) Only 1 licensed Bodyguard/Manager/Valet allowed at ringside. This includes tag team partners! (of course, run-ins by the partner does not count) 2) No attacking HOW fans (can't afford a lawsuit!) 3) All wrestlers must act in a professional manner (ie watch the language if at all possible, and keep it in character) 4) Racist, sexist & homophobic conduct will not be permitted. While the big leagues may allow this to happen, HOW tries to teach it's stars the way it was in "The Good Old Days"
WHY THE RULES OF CONDUCT EXIST: HOW is set up as a simple little fed. It's not glamourous or glitzy. It's low budget, back to basics, old school wrestling. Think NWA/WCW back in the late
80's/early 90's, but with lower production values, and a largely rookie talent base. This may turn some of you off, but I want HOW to stress the skills of the wrestlers, not how violent they can get. If you feel these rules are restricting, then HOW may not be a fed you'd want to participate in. Hopefully you won't feel that way, but if you do, I understand why you wouldn't want to join.
STRATS [Optional]: Strategies can give you an extra edge in your matches. (as long as you send them in fast enough for me to use them!) Strats are optional, but can be a deciding factor in who wins if it's a tough call. A sample strategy might be something like this:
Great Muta will not try to match power with Kensuke Sasake. He will use his speed to out maneuver the bigger man. He will hit as many high flying moves as possible. Dropkicks, Flying Elbows, Spin Wheel
Kicks, etc. He will also go for pinfalls whenever the big man is off his feet (hopefully stunning Kensuke Sasake with a Dropkick off the Top or something similar). If he gets in trouble, he will roll out of the ring, hoping for a breather. If Kensuke Sasake comes out after him, he will get back into the ring asap.
TITLES & RANKINGS: HOW has the following titles: - The Mid-Atlantic Heritage Champion - The Mid-Atlantic Tag Team Titles There are no Television or Cruiserweight/Lightheavyweight titles in
HOW. If the roster has a large amount of cruiser/lightheavyweights, then I will consider a special tournament, along the lines of the Super J Crown in Japan, just for them. HOW isn't designed to handles a large roster of many titles, so this kind of belt had to be discarded.
Titles must be defended every 4 cards (equivilent of 30 days). They must also be defended against a top contender or it doesn't count as a defense. You can, of course, still give unranked wrestlers a shot at the title(s) (sometimes angles make this a necessity). If a wrestler is 1/2 of the Mid-Atlantic Tag-Team
Champions, they may ALSO hold a singles title at the same time.
They will be busy though. :-) Rankings are computed after each wrestling card. I decide who gets ranked where. Win/Loss record isn't the end all of ranking. If someone with a bad win/loss record suddenly pins a top contendor, or a champion in a non-title match, they could be ranked higher than someone who is undefeated, yet has not yet faced the upper echelons of competition. Plus, depending on what angles between handlers are going on, I may adjust rankings accordingly. If the number one contender is in a feud with someone other than the champ, but the #2 man is feuding with the champ, he may temporarily be named number one contendor for the duration of that feud.
HOW MATCHES WORK: HOW is a BOOKED league, meaning the I decide who wins the matches, based on the current angle(s) and participation of both parties in question (flashes, strats, etc..)
CARDS: We have 1 show a week from the Heritage Wrestling Arena in
Washington, DC. It is shown up and down the east coast on public access channels, which means sometimes the show is pre-empted for other programming (this is the in-game explanation for late cards). The show is called Ring Wars, and it features between 3-5 matches, interviews and/or taped segements by wrestlers, etc. This show is very low-rent: There are 2-3 camera crews, but lighting is much like ECW in it's early days. No high-tech pyro, etc is used, as the promotion can't afford it.
I do have to state here that I reserve the right to change dates, change format, etc, for any reason (see comments about pre-empting
HOW programming)
PAY-PER-VIEWS: HOW has *NO* PPV's. This promotion isn't geared to do so. However, that doesn't mean we won't have large cards. We will, but they will be presented as if it were a home video, not as a live event. Some matches from these special cards will also show up on Ring Wars (kind of like a recap for fans, like ECW does), and may or may not be counted towards the total of matches set for that edition of Ring Wars.
These special cards will also be used for specialty matches of all kinds. I'm gonna bring back matches from the good old days, and let feuds use them to add to the intensity of things. Here is a list of the specialty matches HOW will allow:
Texas Bullrope Match (must touch all 4 corners to win, and comes with an authentic cowbell in the middle of the bullrope)
Texas Death Match (must answer a 10 count after the pinfall) Russian Chain Match Strap Match (Insert City name here) Street Fight Steel Cage Match (steel cages in HOW will not have a roof on them, will be older style, and about 10-12 feet high)
Dog Collar Match Submit or Surrender match Scaffold Match (knock opponent(s) off the scaffold to win) Falls Count Anywhwhere match No DQ match No Countout match (can be combined with NO DQ) Barbed Wire Cage Match (yes, they did have these long ago): This match is for extreme circumstances only, and should be *RARE* This match is the most hardcore match HOW will ever feature.
CONTACTS: You may contact the other wrestlers in the HOW to get to know them, set up teams, feuds, etc. Make sure that once you set something up, that both (or all) of the parties confirm it with me so I know. There will be a list of the current HOW Handlers that goes out every once in a while through the mailing list.
FINALE: I hope you all enjoy/have enjoyed HOW as much as I have bringing it to you.
