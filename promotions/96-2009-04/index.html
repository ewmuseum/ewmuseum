A fantasy federation on the fwrestling.com hub that ran from April 2009 to March 2010, as 96 Promotions with a weekly eXpose from Las Vegas, as Wrestling Unplugged from June, and as 96 Promotions again from 18 October 2009, when its pay-per-view Revolution decided every title anew and its eXposes turned on a fight over who owned it. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

96 Promotions begins its recorded run on 27 April 2009 with the page headed eXpose II, 'the second ever airing', staged at a 96 Arena in Las Vegas by two owners, Jacob Smith and Samantha Ross, one of whom had given away four hundred and fifty of five hundred tickets. A second-hand ring collapsed during the Elite Championship three-way that closed the show, which Michael Ahriman won; Salim Raj won the Commonwealth Championship in the first match.

The next two shows, on 6 and 17 May, added a co-owner who bought his place with a briefcase of cash, Chad Vargas's Lugar title win over Makayla Fox, Makayla Fox's Commonwealth win over Salim Raj, and the debuts of Brandon Maddox and Akilliz. Its rules page, which the directory stamps 29 April, says staff decide matches from roleplays and that the company has no women's division.

Its first pay-per-view, Screaming For Vengeance, was promised for the end of May and appears on 7 June as 'the return Pay-Per-View event' of Wrestling Unplugged under a new boss, Jacob Smith: Drake Daniels won the Lugar title, Requiem won the Elite title from Jinx, and Parental Advisory 11 a week later, from Tokyo, renamed the Lugar belt the One Shot. The belts carried over, so the pages are one company under both names. Nothing survives between 14 June and 18 October.

On 18 October 2009 the pay-per-view Revolution, renamed from Reckless Endangerment in a day, announced that the company was no longer Revolution Promotions but 96 Promotions again, scratched every match and had six men fight in a Chaotic Cage for all of the titles. Johnny Styles outlasted Colton Vargas, Melody, Nathan Lancaster, Carmine Vestieri and Jinx to become the first 96 Revolution World champion, and Nathan Lancaster, the last man pinned, took the Prodigy title. The belts were reset, not the company: the same name, the same eXpose and the same owner carried on.

The three eXposes that survive from that autumn follow a fight over the company itself. Jason Burrows, the former owner of Wrestling Unplugged, walked in on 26 October demanding an executive post and punched the owner, Jayson Smith; on 3 November he claimed to own 96 through a clip of rights signed over and declared Nathan Lancaster the champion after he pinned Johnny Styles; on 9 November he signed Osh Vaughan as the future of the company and Smith answered with Shane Hunt. Its roster page lists three tag teams and an injury note, and the last page in its directory, in March 2010, welcomes a new federation called xXx Wrestling.
