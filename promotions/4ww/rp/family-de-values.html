Family De-values

3 December 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Family De-values Rustin Slade 4WW Exposures #1089 Date: 12/03/04 Location: Boston, NY (The camera fades in to a small suburban town of Buffalo, NY. We are watching a car pull into a driveway on a hill, where a quaint Victorian home is lit by its Christmas lights. The home is lightly dusted with snow. The car that has just pulled in turns off its engine and the door opens, as we watch Rustin Slade get out. He’s got on a nice overcoat and is dressed up real nicely. He walks to the door with a bouquet of roses in his hands and a box of cigars in his other hands. He walks onto the welcome mat at the front door and knocks. An older lady comes to the door and opens it for Rustin. She has a great big smile on her face.) Lady: Rustin! Welcome home! (Rustin holds out the roses and hands them to the lady.) Rustin: Here you go Mom. Just a little pre-christmas gift. Rustin’s Mom: Oh they’re beautiful. (Rustin enters his house and as he circles the corner, he gets picked up in a great big bear hug from a large stocky man. The man also has a big smile on his face. Rustin begins to turn blue from the lack of oxygen he is receiving due to the hug.) Man: Welcome home son! Rustin: (out of breath) Hey pops. (Rustin hands him the box of cigars in his other hand. His father opens it up and his eyes light up in appreciation. He releases the bear hug he has on his son and goes off to sniff the cigars.) Rustin’s Dad: Thanks pal! (Coming in from another room in the house enters a smaller version of Rustin. The only difference between the two is the boy’s age [who looks to be at least 17] and his hair color of brown.) Boy: What’s happening Rustin? Rustin: Rex, what’s going on? (The two brothers slap each other a high five. Rustin grabs the boy into a side headlock and gives him a hard noogie. The boy shoves off Rustin, giving him a smile.) Rustin’s Mom: Well Rustin....where is she? Rustin: Where’s who mom? Rustin’s Mom: My daughter-in-law! You promised me you’d get one this year for me. Rustin: (Frowning) Sorry mom, my luck’s been real bad lately. I can’t get a passing glance from half of the women in New York City, let alone their phone number. Rustin’s Mom: Oh son...maybe next year. Rustin: Yeah maybe...and that’s a big maybe! (Rustin’s dad comes back into the room, now with a lit cigar in his mouth. He puffs on it a little, and then puts it in an ashtray for a minute.) Rustin’s Dad: Well, how’s the job going son? Is everyone at the law firm treating you well. (Rustin freezes in horror. This is just the question he did not want this father to ask. Rustin quit his job the law firm to wrestle for the 4WW full time.) Rustin: Actually pop, that’s what I need to talk to you about. Rustin’s Dad: Oh? Well let’s talk over dinner. Rustin: No dad, let’s talk about it now. Mom you need to hear this too. (Rustin’s Mom nods in recognition but seems to be lost in preparing the meal that is on the stove. She is currently picking up a pot of pasta to drain. She brings it over to the sink and drains it, running some water over the noodles quickly.) Rustin: This is kind of hard for me to say, but here goes. I left the law firm. Rustin’s Dad: What! Well are you at another one or something? Rustin: No Dad, I’m in a different field. I decided to do something else. (Rustin’s Mom is carrying the noodles back to the stove now.) Rustin’s Mom: And what is that son? Rustin: I’m a professional wrestler. (Rustin’s mom drops the noodles all over the floor. Rustin’s Dad, who is sitting down slams his fist down on the table. However, Rex’s eyes go wide) Rex (Rustin’s Brother): Cool! Rustin’s Mom: Rex shut up! Go to your room. Your father and I need to talk to your brother. (Rex leaves the room and heads towards his room. Rustin’s Father and Mother are now staring at Rustin with grimaces.) Rustin’s Dad: What the hell were you thinking? Rustin: Pop... Rustin’s Dad: No don’t even say it. I can’t believe it. All that money we spent on you for college. For Law school! This is how you repay us. Rustin: Pops, you don’t understand. It’s my passion. (Rustin’s mom now begins to cry. Weeping, she puts her hands over her eyes.) Rustin’s Dad: Now look what you’ve done! You’ve upset your mother. I can’t believe this shit Rustin! You come home, buttering us all up with gifts, and now you tell us this. Get out of here. You’re not welcome in this house until you get back into that law firm. Rustin: Dad! Rustin’s Dad: Leave! Rustin: Dad, I’m going far in this federation. Rustin’s Dad: Boy, if you don’t get out of here, you’re going to be wrestling with me! (Knowing his father is serious, he leaves his house. The home where he grew up in and the two people who had helped raise him. The people who raised him to be a lawyer. He still hears the angry rants of his father and the loud sobs from his mother as he starts up his car. He drives down to the Boston Post Office and slips three tickets to Stormfront for his family into an envelope, and leaves a little note with them.) (Mom and Dad, I know your really angry with me right now. I’m not the son you’ve dreamed of. I’ve abandoned your hopes and dreams to follow my own. I am making something of myself, just not in the ways you wanted me to. It’d mean a lot to me if you came and offered your support at this week’s event in Pittsburgh. I have a title match against another wrestler. If I don’t see you there, I hope you will at least understand why I entered into the world of professional wrestling. I love you guys. Please come. Your son, Rustin P. Slade) (As Rustin finishes the note he slips the envelope with the tickets into the mailbox. He frowns as it slips into the box. He gets back in his car and we see him driving along, past a sign that says “Now Leaving Boston” towards the I-90 towards Pittsburgh. His hopes and dreams would be fulfilled this week at Stormfront. It is this week that Rustin wants more than just a passing glance from the other wrestlers in 4WW. Rustin and Paul have met before, but never with this much riding on the line. Tempest gold could mean everything for Rustin, and he smiles as he enters the I-90 towards Pittsburgh, knowing his day in the sun is only a day away.)
