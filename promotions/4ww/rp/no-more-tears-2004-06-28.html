No More Tears

28 June 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is Jay Phoenix’s own first-person backstory, from his PWW years to the vow that brought him to 4 Winds Wrestling, posted on 28 June 2004 to The Quill, a general creative-writing board on rpcommunity.com, in its “The beautiful games” forum, with the two replies the topic shows.
Phoenix · 28 June 2004 What goes in a wrestler's mind when he realises that his next match may be his last, as he steps foot into the ring with an opponent who crippled his brother? …No More Tears I was seven years old. I stood there looking up at the figure of a man who had been central to my World for as long as I could remember, a man who was like a God to me, having an almost mythical status in my eyes. Seven year olds are fickle with their adoration and their hero worship, flitting from their favourite cartoon or comic book superhero one day, to a sports star the next. Heroes come and go, as changeable as the weather. Not for me though, for seven years … a lifetime at that time … and for ever after, that man had been THE MAN to me, held in higher regard than any of the other heroes around. Superman … no contest. Hulk Hogan … who? Hiawatha himself … a shadow. I know that Hiawatha may sound like a weird hero for a kid of seven to have, but for a seven year old Native American child, son of two of the greatest advocates of Native equality and legacy around, and grandson of one of the single most respected medicine men and shamans among the People, Hiawatha was the next thing to divinity. Next best thing, but not close enough. For the man that I was looking up at was my grandfather, and for all my life he had been my hero, the man who knew everything about everything, the man who kept me safe from the things in the dark … and took the time to explain just WHAT those things were with truth, rather than trying to pretend that it was just the wind or creaky beams in the house … He was the man who was my rock and anchor while my parents were travelling the Country … and the World … spreading their message and doing their work … he was the man who I knew would always be there for me, would always look after and protect me, would always make everything alright. I was seven years old, looking up at my grandfather, the man who had just told me that my parents were not coming back, that they were with the Spirits now after their truck had left the road on the interstate. I was seven years old and the most important man in the world had just told me that my parents … the people I loved so very much …were dead. I was seven years old and my world was crashing down around me, and the man that I trusted above all others couldn’t look after me or protect me from this, he couldn’t make everything alright. I was seven years old, my stomach feeling like it was being tied in knots while a hand was grasping my heart tightly, squeezing it so that it could no longer beat … my lungs aching for air as if I had not drawn breath in days, the dry racking gasps coming from my mouth sounding like that of a dying elderly man breathing his last. I was seven years old, and it was the first time that I can remember crying. … I thought that I would never stop. … I thought that I would die myself, I know that even then, as a child, I wanted to be dead too, I thought that I would not be able to carry on and I wanted so desperately, with every fibre of my being, to be with my parents one more time, to hold them and kiss them, to have them smile at me the way they always did, to beg them not to get into the truck … or to get into the truck with them, to drive down that interstate, sitting between them as they chatted and talked about their last trip, as they told me … their little Flame … all about the things that they had seen, and the people they had met. … to hold onto them oh so tightly as the end came … to die with them. I was seven years old, my parents were taken from me, my grandfather … the infallible hero to my young eyes … had been proved, simply because he was the bearer of the message, fallible, and I remember so vividly that even now, two decades later, I can feel the stab of pain and taste the hot, salty tears as they ran down my face to stain my lips with their stain. … a stain that cannot be seen, but can still be felt. My first memory of tears, and my first experience of a dream being broken, as I realised that there are no heroes. Halos can break you see, pedestals can crumble, and no matter what sort of rose coloured glasses you look through, the world is still turning and people are still only human. So you would have thought that after an experience like that I would have learned my lesson, that I would have been ready for life to kick my again, that I should have expected the unexpected. Well … I didn’t. Sure, life went on, and in many ways it did not change that much. My grandfather … the man that everyone, including me, called the Shaman … raised me, probably as well as my parents would have, and with as much love as he raised my father. I never went without anything, because thanks to the work of my parents, and my grandfather’s own investments, we were what is commonly referred to as ‘more than comfortable’ … obviously I didn’t realise it then, but the fact that my grandfather actually owned the twelve thousand acre reservation that his tribe resided on should have given me a hint that we were wealthy. More than wealthy … we were very, very rich. Apart from the fiscal stability though, I had so much more. I had the love and affection of a very wise man … one of the few real Shamans left to the People, a man that was almost one with nature itself, and a man that not only knew all the old ways and teachings but he actually loved them daily. So my life was blessed with the teaching and education that independent wealth can bring, combined with the old ways of the People … the medicine wheel, the stories and dances, the rites and lore, the Spirits. I was blessed also with the fact that … according to my Grandfather … I was born with a Destiny. Now everyone is born with a destiny, that is an inescapable fact, but me … so he said … I was born with a Destiny, a subtle, but vast, difference. I was marked from birth … with my flame red hair, a colour next to unknown among our People … a birth that happened on the concurrence of a set of stars long prophesised, under the tail of a burning comet. A Destiny that proclaimed me as the Eternal Flame … the embodiment of all that was good and best in the People. Their learning and teachings. Their humility and pride. Their compassion. … and their warrior spirit. Even though I believed and trusted in my Grandfather implicitly … in the ten years that passed I had never really thought back to the day he told me my parents were dead and blamed him as my seven year old mind … a traitor to itself … had done for that split second … but I have to admit that I couldn’t bring myself to believe this … myth … about me as he seemed to. … though I was always the fastest, the strongest, the most agile among my peers. The lessons and stories that were passed around the gatherings seemed more like a memory to me than a new thing learned. The old skills … the medicine and the spear for instance … were child’s play to me from the start, while the older boys in the tribe lagged behind me after months of training, I was competing with the men of the tribe themselves. … and winning. It didn’t matter that he thought that I was the Spirit of the People reborn, and I thought that I was just a pretty gifted kid with a knack for things, we were family, all that we had, and all that we needed. … and for years it was all good. I was twenty years old. I stood there looking down at the figure of the man who had been my entire World for as long as I could remember. I had not been fickle as a child, nor as a young man growing up. My grandfather had been my parents, my friend and my teacher, the man that I trusted to and listened to above all others. The man who always knew best. I was twenty years old and it was the second time that I can remember crying. My mind flashed back for the briefest of instances … when the first tear hit my lip, and my tongue flickered out involuntarily to taste it’s bitter salty flavour, and the seven year old child who had just lost his parents was back again. … but only for a second … a second that was more than enough to make the feelings running through me at that time explode a hundred, a million times worse, tearing me up inside and wrapping me up with fingers of insidious confusion and doubt. It was not loss or pain that had brought these tears to my eyes, it was not childhood fear. It was anger and frustration … at least to start with. I had been offered a place on the Olympic archery team, which considering my heritage was both ironic and extremely humbling … or at least it should have been. My grandfather was so very proud of me, and I knew that he would not have been … could not have been … any more proud if I had been his son. I was twenty years old though, and I had my own ideas about what I wanted. A friend of mine from the local college … a guy called Rick James who had majored in journalism while I did Native American Theology and Psychology joint honours, who was a couple of years older, but considering I had graduated two years early that wasn’t surprising … had told me that he was travelling to New York to take up a residency programme with an entertainment network called Vanilla Networks. Rick had always been into wrestling, and had brought me along with him for the ride … even though at the start I laughed at the idea of grown men in tights ‘wrestling’ … as if any of it was real to my mind … inside a ring for money. After a very short time I was hooked though, and I was in awe of these men … these modern day gladiators … who could compete and entertain with so much skill and ability. It was this love of wrestling that had promoted Rick to go into journalism, as he was only just over five and a half foot tall he knew that he would never fulfil his dream of wrestling, but writing about wrestling he would be able to do. So after three years of running a fanzine for wrestling, and a degree in journalism, Rick was going to go and work for Vanilla Networks … the company that just so happened to own his favourite wrestling federation … the PWW. … and he wanted me to come with him. ... he had talked to one of the wrestlers, and showed him a tape of me working out, and Rick wanted me to try out for the PWW. … me … wrestling for a living … living Rick’s dream for him … a dream that I secretly shared. Nothing could stop me, nothing at all! Nothing except my grandfather. I was twenty years old and I was watching my grandfather walk away from me after telling me that if I chose wrestling over the chance for the Olympics I was choosing wrestling over him. I was twenty years old, full of foolish pride, and I knew better than him … I knew that he did not mean it, that he would understand my decision if he just thought about it, that he would agree with me … that he would not turn his back on me and walk away. I was twenty years old and I was wrong … so very wrong. I was twenty years old and that is the second time that I can remember crying. I spent nearly a year training with the PWW’s best teachers in the Breakthrough Academy as they called it, a play on words as such, meaning on one hand that you would breakthrough and become a star … or they would break right through you in the attempt to get you ready. I didn’t speak to my grandfather in that year, but I worked hard, I worked harder than I ever had before, and within a year I was ready. … I was more than ready. Once again my Destiny kicked in … or my natural athleticism if you are a sceptic … because I walked into the Academy a total novice and within a year the trainers proclaimed me as being as good as a five year veteran. I suppose having the Stars themselves and the Warrior Spirit of the People in your corner gives you a head start in these things. Anyway, the PWW called me up and told me that they were giving me a chance, a chance that I took with both hands. I wrestled my first match on the one year anniversary of leaving my grandfather … and wrestled a second the very same night. The first match was meant to be a dark match to get me started, against an older and more experienced veteran of the ring, who was supposed to check my ability without hurting me too much. … only problem is that he was the one who got hurt when he tripped over some cabling on the way to the ring and fractured his arm. A quick rearrangement later, and instead of a dark match with a friendly opponent, I was pushed into the ring against the current Television Champion of the company, a man whose name was Kain Threat. I was so scared that I thought that I would pass out there and then. I had watched Kain Threat on the television many times, he had been the TV champion for months now, and Rick had even interviewed him for the PWW magazine ‘Rewind’ … and had told me that just talking to the man terrified him … where Threat had mentioned that he started wrestling only because it was a way he could hurt people and not get arrested. I don’t know how I did it but I managed to get into the ring without falling over, and twenty minutes later the bell had rung. I was still alive. Of course I didn’t win the match, but I did manage to last twenty full minutes and not get pinned, I had wrestled the great Kain Threat to a draw, and even managed to get a hint of a grudging smile of respect from him ,,, just before he clocked me over the head with the belt on the way out of the ring, busting me open and leaving me thinking that it was a nice place to have a quick nap. The PWW trainers were amazed at my performance … when I woke up they told me this … and Rick was there with a big smile on his face as he held a cold compress to my throbbing head, telling me that I was the talk of the locker room, the rookie kid who had taken a time limit draw from a champion in his first match. … and then his smile faded as he stared behind me. The Shaman, my grandfather, stood there, a mask of emotionless ness upon his face as he looked down upon me, the blood fresh upon my brow. Without moving, without asking me how I was he simply asked if I had learned my lesson, if I was ready to give up this foolishness and go home with him. I lay there in shock … the last year of my life, the last twenty minutes in the ring, the pain that I was no feeling throughout my body from the blows I had taken, especially my head where later on fourteen stitches would be required, all foolishness in his eyes? Biting back the tears in my eyes … I was twenty one and would NOT cry … I shook my head, a small gasp of pain from the movement coming out of my mouth against my wishes, and simply said no, that I would not go home with him. I had made my decision, I had made my life choice, and I was not going to back down. Reaching down to my cheek, my grandfather smiled at me, telling me with pride evident in his voice that he was proud of me … that he had had to see if I could walk my own path my own way, and that my lesson was indeed learned and learned well. I had wrestled my second match … that of my own decision and confidence in my choice … and I had won this one completely. I was twenty one years old and as my grandfather brushed my bloody hair from my eyes I cried again. Tears of joy. I am twenty seven years old, and the years have come and gone. I stayed with the PWW for over five years, gaining all the accolades there that were possible, winning all the titles that they had, and eventually even owning the company and watching as hungry young wrestlers walked the path I had taken. The Shaman … my grandfather … was often by my side, and was just as often not. Through it all though he was in my heart. Just as other men and women were. People like the half brothers and sister I discovered I unknowingly had, filling my life with family where before I thought that there was just myself and my grandfather. Celest and Chris Hatchett, my half brothers, from my fathers first marriage to a woman I never knew about. … and Sarah, my half sister, the most kind, caring and beautiful woman in the World. During the years I discovered family and friends … and during the years one friend became family. Johnny Blaze was almost the opposite of me in every way possible. Brash and confident … loud and extroverted, he was a man who enjoyed life to it’s fullest each and every day. He wrestled in the PWW the same way he lived his life, explosively and dangerously. A brawler and a powerful man, Johnny made up for the lack of finesse with determination and sheer willpower. … the same determination and willpower that won him the love of my sister. One time enemy in the ring, became my brother in law if not in blood … though we spilled enough of each other in our many matches throughout the years. Blaze became a teacher of sorts to me as well as one of the closest friends I have ever had. … and then he was taken away from me. I am twenty seven years old, and for the third time in my life I am crying. I am standing over the bed of Johnny Blaze, watching as a machine forces his chest to rise and fall with pumped oxygen, watching as machines ensure that his heart beats, his brain remains active. Watching as the only thing that keeps the body of Blaze alive is a hospital and technology. There is no spark of real life within him, there is non of the trade mark arrogance and humour to be seen. There is just the empty carcass that used to be Johnny Blaze and is not just a shell kept alive but not by himself. … like a puppet with it’s strings cut. I am twenty seven years old and the tears are running down my face as I kiss goodbye the forehead of my friend, my brother … Johnny Blaze. I am twenty seven years old and the tears roll down my face as I bite back a scream of despair that wants to burst out of my mouth to the Heavens above and demand why this has happened. I am twenty seven years old and as I cry I swear to myself … and to the man who lies on the bed before me, a man I love … that I will get revenge for this act, for I know how it was done, I know why, and I know who caused it. It was done in a wrestling ring. It was done through sheer spite and evilness. It was done by the Fallen One … Nephilim … Geoffrey Ian Childress. I am twenty seven years old, and two more things I swear to myself. The first is that I will pay Childress back for everything that he has done … that I will take everything from him, for in this one moment I do not just blame him for what has happened to Johnny Blaze but I blame him for all the pain that I have ever felt, I blame him for the loss of Blaze, for the loss of my grandfather all those years ago, for the loss of my parents, for the loss of my heroes and my innocence. I blame him for everything! … and to make him feel that loss, he will have to lose everything. I will start with his pride and his joy, I will start by taking away that which he cares about above all else. He is not a man like most though, he does not care about his wife, his friends or his family. No, he cares most about his company, feeling that Four Winds Wrestling is the epitome of his work, that it is his life. So I will take that from him … but before I do so I will twist the knife. I will make him hate the federation he so loves. I will take the 4WW and I will make it my own … I will become the very best that the 4WW has to offer and Childress will look at it and only see me. I will enter his gauntlet, I will fight his warriors, and I will triumph. I will take the North Wind from it’s master, and I will become the North Wind, and then step by step I will take everything until I become the Four Winds itself. … and that will be the start! The second thing that I swear to myself? That is simple. No more tears … never again … I am done hurting, I am done caring and I am done crying. Now there is only revenge, there is only me and Nephilim, everything else is just a step in the journey. … no … … more … … tears … _________________ Halos can break you see, pedestals can crumble, and no matter what sort of rose coloured glasses you look through, the world is still turning and people are still only human. ---- Teh Lutt · 30 June 2004 Phoenix, you're the man That was awesome. _________________ Oh, Rock of Ages, Do not crumble, love is breathing still. ---- DagothKinslayer · 30 June 2004 I love this rp.
