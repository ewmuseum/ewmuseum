Do you Believe in Magic?

7 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Do you Believe in Magic? Rustin Slade 4WW Exposures #1254 Date: 5/7/05 Location: Amphitheater in UK [The scene opens in some type of arena. Rustin Slade settles down into his seat in the crowded amphitheater. The feature attraction tonight is Ron Jackson: Master of Magic. Since Rustin’s been dealing with the strange and unexplainable more and more lately, he decided to see a magic show. Maybe this way he can understand a little better what the hell’s been going on, and what kinds of tricks he’s getting from the strange Carribean Man.] [Rustin sat unimpressed during the show as Ron made things appear seemingly out of thin air. Rustin was easily convinced that this was fancy sleight-of-hand, which he could do quite well himself. He did the usual other tricks such as the disappearing assistant. The box was rigged, obviously. However, the last trick that Ron performed left Rustin in awe. Rustin sat amazed as Ron levitated off the ground at least twenty feet into the air. The crowd clapped furiously, showing their appreciation. Rustin, however, could not explain this one. There was no wires; no platform that blended in with the lights to raise Ron up into the air. It just didn’t seem to fit with the other tricks....] Later that evening....After the Show... [Rustin waited for the last of the Ron Jackson fans to clear out of the arena, getting their autographs. Rustin then approached Ron as he was getting ready to leave.] Ron: Hey sorry man, I just finished the last autograph. Rustin: I don’t want an autograph! Look there’s something I need to know. Ron: Well, I don’t reveal secrets to my tricks, if that’s what you’re going to ask. Rustin: I don’t think there was a secret to your last trick tonight, was there Ronny boy? [Ron, getting ready to leave, suddenly stops when Rustin points this out.] Ron: Of course there was! There’s always a secret... [Rustin shakes his head and wags a finger.] Rustin: If I didn’t know any better, I’d say you levitated all by yourself tonight. Ron: But you obviously know better...right? Rustin: Do I? Ron: Mr. Slade, magic is just a stage art! [Rustin slams his open palm against the wall, making a loud noise that echoes through the arena. This causes Ron to jump back a little. He eyes Rustin a little more carefully now, perhaps out of fear, or perhaps out of pure anger. It’s hard to tell.] Rustin: Look pal, I’ve had enough of this fake stage crap. Where’d you learn that trick from? [Ron starts to try to run past Rustin, but Rustin holds strong and blocks Ron’s exit. Ron tries to break through, but Rustin’s pure muscle power holds the magician in his place. Ron now starts to get a little angry.] Ron: You don’t know who you’re messing with man! Rustin: I think I know exactly who I’m messing with. Some down and out magician, who was stuck doing parlor tricks all his life, until he learned some real magic. Ron: You’re crazy man! Rustin: Am I? Why don’t you just tell me where you learned that real magic from. Ron: There is no such thing as real magic! Rustin: Well it just so happens buddy that I’ve been being chased around by some crazy Caribbean guy who thinks there is. He did a fancy little levitation trick with a skull that looked a lot like your little performance tonight. I couldn’t explain it. [Rustin grabs Ron by his shirt.] Rustin: So where’d you learn how to do that? Something very important is at stake for me. I need to know! [Rustin releases his grip on Ron, and looks sadly down at the ground.] Ron: And what would be at stake? Rustin: Well my sanity for one, but more importantly, a girl I care for... [Rustin kicks his feet around on the ground. Ron notes Rustin’s sadness. With all the commotion that was being made, a man in a dark suit and sunglasses comes in from somewhere unseen. He’s obviously a bodyguard. Ron motions for him to go away.] Ron: It’s okay Bruno. There’s no problem. [The man gives a grunt and leaves the area.] Ron: You’re right. I was a down and out magician. I was stuck doing these little snot-nosed kid’s shows for a dog’s age. Then I stumbled across a man. A man who offered me a chance to redeem my pathetic career, and kick it up with some real magic. [Rustin’s eyes light up.] Rustin: Who was he? Ron: I didn’t know at first. But I started taking lessons from him. I thought he was a crackpot at first, but he kept telling me to believe. “BELIEVE!” He’d always shout that when I just thought about giving up. Rustin: So what happened? Ron: Well I got better. I released my fears and I began believing. Then strange things started to happen. Rustin: What kind of things? Ron: Well small things at first. I didn’t need to put much effort into my sleight of hand anymore. It seemed my card tricks would perform themselves. Then it started to get weirder. Rustin: How do you mean? Ron: I was able to submerse my hand in corrosive acid without getting any effects. I could put my hand through a solid brick wall. Then finally I was able to levitate. I went big, doing my new magic tricks. Rustin: What happened to your teacher? Ron: I don’t know. I thank him everyday of my life though. He gave me a second chance. A chance to do real magic. No more stupid parlor tricks. Rustin: Hey look, do you have a picture of this guy. [Ron reaches into his wallet.] Ron: You know what, I just might. [After fumbling around in his wallet a minute, he produces a picture of the man. Rustin needs to take only a glance at it. It’s the same Caribbean man that’s been following him.] Rustin: His name...what’s his name? Ron: I don’t know if it’s his real name but he called himself Henry Voodoo. [Rustin, hearing the name takes off quickly. Ron calls out to him, causing him to stop in his tracks.] Ron: Hey...remember...BELIEVE! [Rustin gives a head nod and heads out of the arena in a rush as the scene fades to black.]
