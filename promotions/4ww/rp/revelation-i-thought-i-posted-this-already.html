I'll bleed, (for no one), the demons, (but myself), that drag me down (for me and no one else) - “Not Falling” by Mudvayne Draven never expected a grand welcoming when he returned to the 4WW. He knew that there would be opposition. For Draven that was nothing new. People were always shooting off their mouths about him. This time is no different. Kris Kanton shot his mouth off not once, but twice about Draven and he doesn’t even know the half of what he has gotten himself into as a result. Draven was the very first West Wind title holder and he can surely be at that point again. There’s no way he is going to let the likes of Kris Kanton stop him. “Nothing New” The diner looked much the same way that Draven remembered it from the last time he was there, before he went back to the academy. It was a quiet mom & pop establishment. The wood paneling walls were covered with framed articles from the local newspaper covering the towns high school sports teams as well as a few pictures of famous people that dropped in on their way through this part of New Mexico. It was one of those places you see in the movies. There were booths along the walls, a few tables in the center, and of course there was a bar from which you could see what was happening in the kitchen. This is where Draven liked to sit when he came to Sloppy Joe’s. He would sit at the end, mostly out of sight of the rest of the patrons and sip hot tea, watching the older gentleman that always sat in the booth furthest back. They were always arguing about sports, politics, you name it. They were wise with their many years of life and Draven just enjoyed soaking it up. Draven had listened to them long enough to learn that their names were Otis and Louis. Otis was a slender black man with a smooth face and bald head. He wore pants too large and held them up with suspenders and glasses that rested on the tip of his nose so that he’d have to look with his nose in the air just to see. Louis was an old immigrant from Italy, the old country, he’d call it. He had wrinkled olive skin and a full head of gray hair. He reminded Draven of his grandfather. Usually Draven just listened and never interrupted them, he wasn’t even sure they realized that he listened in, but today when he walked in the diner they motioned him over before he could even sit down at his usual spot. Otis: “Come here, son. Have a seat.” The old man patted the red vinyl seat next to him. Louis: “Yes, Draven. Please join us.” The old man’s words startled Draven. He wasn’t aware that they knew who he was. His amazement was apparently showing on his face because both of the men gave a hearty chuckle. Louis: “You act surprised that we know who you are ? Please, we know every celebrity’s name that comes in Sloppy Joe’s.” Otis: “Yeah, that sexy wife of yours was in here last week. What a dame, she is!” Draven can’t help but crack a smile. Draven: “So I suppose you two have known all along that I sit and listen to you two argue about everything like an old married couple?” Otis: “Well we may argue about a lot of things, lad, but one thing we agree on is that young man Kris Kanton.” Wow, not only do these men know who Draven is, they are current on the events as well. Draven is impressed so he stays silent and waits on the old man to continue. Otis: “He needs to be taught a lesson, and you should teach it to him, you are the headmaster aren’t you?” Draven: “Well, yes. I am the headmaster of the 4 Winds Academy, but Kanton is no rookie.” Louis: “Nonsense. What he said on TV and then again at Stormfront proves that he has a lot to learn and shouldn’t be writing checks with his mouth that his ass can’t cash!” Other customers in the diner look up at the vulgar outburst by the old man, then quickly return to their meals. They are used to the old men in the corner getting loud. Louis: “Sorry, for my language but let me show you what I mean. Hey, Judy!” The old man motions for the waitress to come over and a middle age woman in a white apron with large pockets on the front comes over with a notepad in her hand. Judy: “Ready to order desert, Louis?” Louis: “No ma’am, not just yet but I was wondering if you’d let us show this young man something on the TV set in the back. Joe won’t mind, he won’t even know if you let us go now.” Judy points her pen at Louis and shakes it. Judy: “If I let you back there, you better not move anything around, you know how picky Joe is about his office.” Before she could finish the old men had already started to lead Draven through the swinging doors into the kitchen. “You’re a doll, Judy!”, yells Louis over his shoulder as he passes through the doors. LET’S GO TO THE TAPE It took the men only a minute to swoop past the cook busy as his grill with the mornings orders and found the closed door that lead to Joe’s office. Inside it was actually a fairly nice office. The desk in the corner was a mess, covered with papers and files but across the room was a leather sofa and a large entertainment center. It looks as though Joe might spend some late nights here. Otis: “Sit down here on the sofa, Draven. We have this tape of Kanton to show you.” Louis pops a vhs tape into the player and turns the set on. It takes a few moments of fast forwarding but soon they find the segment they are looking for. It shows Kanton talking smack about Draven and his claim of being hardcore and about their Highlight match. Louis pauses the tape and Kanton’s face is frozen on the screen. Otis: “See what we are talking about?” Draven runs his hands through his long black hair and sits back on the leather sofa, letting himself sink into it a bit. Draven: “Guys, I don’t know what you are trying to show me, but I already knew that Kris was an idiot.” He sits forward again. Draven: “Everyone saw that Kanton boasted big about winning that match and then HE, not me, was the one that got pinned. He lost that match, I just simply didn’t win. He lost, not me. Louis lets excitement escape into his voice. Louis: “Yes, that’s right, he lost it not you, but you still didn’t prove yourself to him, not that you should have to but you can’t let people go around thinking that they can get the better of the Order. You’ve got to make that contender’s match a real bloodbath, Draven. I’m talking violence beyond anything the 4 Winds has seen yet. That pushover Zane Ozz thinks he is the hardcore god and you need to put him in his place. You have to start by tearing down Kris Kanton and destroying him You are the Innovator of Violence and you need to show them why you have that moniker! You need to go in that ring and give them the Irish Bastard treatment just like Sean taught you. You owe this match to him and to Mr. Childress.” Draven scratches his head and thinks for a moment. Draven: “Gentleman, I know exactly how I am going to do it. Thank you for the little pep talk, but I think I can handle it from here and if would excuse me I need to get home to my wife.” Both of the older men pat Draven on the back and show him out of the diner. As he crosses the street to his car Otis shouts out to him. Otis: “Don’t forget to get back her to the states after your victory. I’ll have Judy whip you up something special!” Draven waves goodbye and speeds away in his car. A TOTAL SURPRISE Draven made it home fairly quickly in his brand new 300M. He loved the car, it was a sleek black with tint all around. It felt like a mobster’s ride. He had the stereo blaring Mudvayne and was just pumping himself up for the match at Stormfront. If it was blood and violence they wanted, it was just what they were going to get. It was only a 15 minute drive home from the diner and when he finely pulled into his gated driveway ( why do we Americans park on driveways and drive on parkways? Never mind, it’s not important). Draven knew that Claudia was home but wasn’t expecting her to meet in inside the garage when he stepped out of the car. Draven: “Honey, I’m flattered you’d wait for me like this but it’s really not necessary..” Draven cut short with his submissive wife joke when he realized that Claudia was not at all smiling. She was, in fact, sucking back tears. Draven: “Claudia, what’s wrong?” Claudia: “You got a letter today in the mail. It was postmarked Seattle, Washington.” Draven tried not to let it show, but his jaw dropped. It couldn’t be, but it had to be them. The only people that Draven and Claudia knew from Seattle was Draven’s birth parents. People that he had not spoken to since he was 7 years old. Draven could only imagine what they would want with him, they had never tried to contact him before though he had just once and vowed never to do so again after receiving his letter back marked “refused”. Draven: “Did you read it, Claudia?” Claudia: “No, I thought you should open it. They are your parents, Draven.” Draven gave a snort of disgust at the comment. Draven: “Not by choice, they aren’t. Annie was my mother, the only parent that I ever needed.” Draven picked the letter up off the table and knew immediately that it was from his parents. No one else in their right mind would use such expensive stationary on a simple letter. It was hand-written in a very fancy handwriting. He hated that he came from such a rich and snobbish bloodline. Draven: “Claudia, will you hand me that letter opener on the desk?” With a single flick of his wrist, Draven cut the letter open and tossed the envelope in the waste can. He unfolded it and quickly read it down to the bottom of the page. Once there he turned it over, looking like he expected more. Upon finding the back side blank he reads it again to himself leaving Claudia in the dark and getting anxious. Claudia: “Draven are you going to tell me what it says or just keep me in suspense? What do they want?” A few more moments of silence pass before Draven can speak. Draven: “It’s my father...he’s...dying.” Claudia can’t believe her ears. Claudia: “What? What does it say, Draven?” Draven drops down onto the sofa, sticking both hands into his hair. Draven: “Niles, my father, is dying of cancer and he wants me to come to Washington before he dies. Apparently he wants to patch things up after all these years.” Claudia: “That’s great news, Draven. You’ll finally have a father. Wait, what is that attached to the letter?” Draven: “It’s two first class round trip plane tickets to Seattle. He wants us to leave right from Stormfront to come see him. He doesn’t know how much time he has left.” Draven: “He says he has something to tell me that is going to change my life forever.”
