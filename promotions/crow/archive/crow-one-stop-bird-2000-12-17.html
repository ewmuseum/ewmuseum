7.2. Strats A Strategy is a guideline for how a player would like their character to go about acting during a particular match. It can either be a gameplan that will hopefully be employed while competing in a match, or it may be describing how a character will want to become involved in the action before, during or after a match (think interference or sneak attack).
A match Strategy is a description of how a player would like their character to go about their match. It should describe things such as the pace a character wants to dictate, the path a character wishes to take in breaking-down and beating an opponent, and the defense and counter-attack plans that the character would like to have in reserve.
Although a Strategy may seek a certain situation to be created during a match (a "spot"), it should not specifically state a whole series of maneuvers and holds. A decent, descriptive strat should be around
5 to 10k in size, though there are no limits in either direction. Since match Strategies hold so much impact if it is a close match in determining an outcome, it is advised that one be written for each match a character is involved in.
The other type of Strategy often involves a foreign party to a particular match becoming involved. Like a match strategy, a player should present what they would like their character to carry-out. In the case of writing sneak attacks or the like, do _not_ write it in
"Flash" format (written like a Roleplay). If you have dialogue for your character to say, write it by all means.. but don't assume that everything will always go as planned.
And most important of all... The Post Match Strategy In the CROW, what goes on during the match tells the story, 90% of the time. Often, however, the action continues long after the match is over, and entire stories are occasionally told immediately following a match. This may consist of immediate singles match challenge interviews, or a beatdown, or a refusal to release a finishing submission hold.
7.3. Angles The backbone to any fed is good angles. Without them, you've just got a bunch of imaginary guys rolling around on the mat. We want to know why your guy wants to win, why he hates his opponent, why he wears an eyepatch in the ring, why he freaks out when his opponent goes after his knee. It's stuff like that that makes people enjoy your character's work, and makes them want to see more of him/her/them.
Angles aren't "required", but without them, I believe you'll find the CROW a very hard place to get noticed in.
