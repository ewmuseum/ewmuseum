Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

CROW's own recruitment copy narrates its founding in March 1998, formed to give a home to wrestlers left without a federation after an earlier promotion closed. It ran through most of that year before a mid-1998 merger folded it into two other promotions and closed its own first run; it came back on 15 November 1998 with a pay-per-view it called Rejuvination.

Its two surviving 1998 pay-per-views, Schools Out For Summer (26 July) and Scorch Wave 98 (30 August), carried its own World Heavyweight Championship through a running feud. At the first, champion Mad Goat retained against “King” “Flamboyant” Paul Dare inside a three-way elimination Hell in a Cell, helped along by his own manager, Mr. Menace, a self-billed former UWF World Champion. At the second, the two met again in a two-out-of-three-falls, falls-count- anywhere match, and this time Dare left as the new champion.

Its Tag Team Championship changed hands the same night: the Gospel Preachers, who by their own commentary team's account had held the belts since May without a clean loss, were beaten by the Element of Pain, a team the broadcast's own commentary accused of having “stolen” an earlier shot at the titles. Its International Championship turned over at Schools Out For Summer as well, “Ego” Alex Nelson losing the belt to “Equaliser” Rob the Rock after a run-in aimed at someone else struck Nelson instead, and Rob the Rock carried it through a ladder-match rematch a month later.

CROW kept publishing after its relaunch: a newsletter it called Edge reached at least one issue by 28 October 1998, and its own broadcasts continued into the following year, with a two-part MidWeek Saga on 1 April 1999 and a countdown to a second Scorch Wave posted that September.

No closure notice of its own survives; its last surviving document, from 5 September 1999, still promotes an upcoming show.
