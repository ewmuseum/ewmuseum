A single-fedhead e-fed that ran two distinct recruitment cycles eight months apart -- a spring 1996 build-out that named its own roster, and a December 1996 closing notice reciting a full title history immediately followed by its own relaunch call -- before the corpus record ends. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The EUWF opened its doors on 3 April 1996 with a short, confident announcement — “Its Running great right NOW!!!” — already boasting a pay-per-view transcript thirty-one pages long.

By June its roster included Mr. Fabulous Frank Auston, MoonCharmer, Dukes, Spartan, and several more, though its operator was down to admitting he was “only alowing a few people left.”

Its own December closing notice recited a complete title history across three named pay-per-views. Kidman won the World Title in an Opening Day battle royal, lost it to Sniper in a steel-cage triangle electric-baseball-bat match at Go For the Gold, and won it back from Sniper at Monday Night Massacre — a reign the notice credits him with never losing outright, since he left the promotion for a stretch and the belt simply went vacant behind him. Alex Extreme won the Intercontinental (later Diamond) Title from Spartan in a ladder match at Go For the Gold, lost it to Johnny Banger, and Spartan won it back from Banger at Armageddon, where the Dukes of Hell (later renamed Just Dukes) also took the World Tag Team Championship from Americas Least Wanted, undefeated at 14–0 and called, in the notice's own words, “the Best team I have ever seen in my life.” Auston won the US (later Internet) Title in a battle royal at Monday Night Massacre falsely claiming an unbeaten record, before losing it to Mooncharmer at Armageddon. The same notice announced the promotion closing to new members and teased a first Hall of Fame.

Five days earlier, a different self-identified owner, Bret Young, had already announced a relaunch under the same name, recruiting several of the same wrestlers back — Magus, Jeff Bidwell, and Prophet among them — alongside new arrivals recruited from other feds.

No document past that relaunch call survives. Whether the reopened promotion, or the January return it promised, ever ran isn't in the surviving record.
