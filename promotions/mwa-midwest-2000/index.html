Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

A self-described territorial promotion calling itself the Midwest Wrestling Alliance began broadcasting to the newsgroup in December 2000, a wrestler's own introduction placing its true start in late August of that year and crediting an experienced owner who had run federations on and off for several years.

Sunday Night Wildfire, staged from St. Paul, Minnesota, opened with a flashback to its own championship changing hands by a chair shot from its on-screen president, then ran a tag team tournament, a returning star's tirade, and a five-man confrontation over the very title that had just changed hands, each man's claim on a rematch argued out loud, live, in the ring.

Holiday Hell followed from Chicago, Illinois, its own supercard opening on a mock drug-testing press conference and closing on a bloodied parking-lot brawl between two of its former champions. The federation's own chief executive interrupted the broadcast mid-match to strip its president of his authority over the referees, on camera, in front of the crowd.

A further supercard, New Year's Madness, was billed for January 2001 with a world title triple threat, a toughman title defense, a tag team final, and a three-way debut match; no result for it survives in this record.

The bare acronym MWA was never this promotion's alone. Six other federations recorded elsewhere in this museum carried the same three letters, and three further 2000-dated federations share it as well — among them an earlier, Minooka-billed federation whose own mid-year return announcement names a different host and different operators, with no document linking the two records.

No further activity survives past the New Year's Madness preview, and no closing notice of any kind follows it.
