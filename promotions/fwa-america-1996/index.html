Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Fantasy Wrestling America opened on 21 November 1996, billing itself as “the official e-fed of the TNM Newsletter” and posting its own rulebook the same day over the signature of its president, Matt Cole. That rulebook set four championships — the FWA World, North American, Cruiserweight (under 245 lbs), and World Tag Team titles — and its own stripping rule: a champion out for 7 or more cards, or who went 30 days without a defense, lost the belt.

It ran two weekly broadcasts out of Charlotte, North Carolina: Superstars Of Wrestling, its flagship show, called from the “sold out FWA Arena” by announcers Lance Sharpe and Vince McDuffey, and Face To Face, a call-in interview program taped from the FWA Studio.

Both shows built toward one date: 7 December 1996, its first pay-per-view, Winter War Games, live from the LVJM Coliseum in Winston-Salem. The first FWA World Champion would be crowned there in a three-way, no-rules Triangle of Terror match, its three combatants earned through qualifying bouts run on Superstars; the same card carried the first FWA World Tag Team Title match, the Watsons against the Goodfellas, and a Cruiserweight Title match pitting the Great Tsunami against a Lucha Libre daredevil billed as Skah.

Its own record introduces returning Super Japan Pro Wrestling star “Magnetic” Morgan Starkley with a full career retrospective — his high school wrestling room, his training under Guy Wallace, his run in Super Japan under promoter Koji Hasani, and his Super Japan World Television Title reign — before staging him into a running comic feud with a self-styled rapper wrestler, Grandmaster G, and into a mystic angle built around Merlin, whose manager Atlanta channeled visions of an “Oracle” foretelling his matches.

Its debut broadcast closed on a darker note: a mafia-styled stable calling itself “The Family,” led by a figure billed only as The Don and enforced by Luca, Scarface, and the Godfather, stormed the ring to beat down Mad Dog Mortimer and both members of the Watsons, The Don pulling a pistol to finish the assault while the announcers reacted live on air.

Through December its roster churned — a closed roster briefly reopened for tag teams on 11 December, then reopened again two days later after “losing a couple of handlers,” with a Royal Rumble already being advertised as the next card to debut new wrestlers on. No document survives past 18 December 1996, and whether Winter War Games, the Triangle of Terror match, or that Royal Rumble were ever staged is not in the surviving record.
