Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The No Holds Barred Wrestling Alliance described itself, in a 29 December 1995 post, as a fed “run by WLS 5.1” with an original roster that had “reached 30+ wrestlers (2 per handler) in 2 months without much advertising.” Uninterested in the newsgroup's running arguments over which fed ranked best, its own operator declared the standard simple: “The ones you're in… they are fun and you look forward to each card” — concluding, tongue in cheek, that the NHBWA was naturally the best of all.

On 23 January 1996 the NHBWA staged its first pay-per-view, “Let's Get Ready to Rumble,” billed live from the SkyDome in Toronto before a sellout crowd of 70,000 and called by Roger Axelen and Gordon Low. The card was a single match: a two-ring, 56-wrestler Battle Royal carrying the entire roster, reigning World Heavyweight Champion Conquer included.

Conquer himself fell past the 35-minute mark, dropkicked out by Argentin Rocker; the field thinned past the 70-, 80-, and 100-minute marks as tag partners and stable-mates combined to clear rivals, until three members of the stable T.O.P. — Warlock, Ken Lee, and “Wrangler” Dutch Daniels — faced Missel and Tidal Wave alone. Warlock outlasted them all, finishing Missel with a Burnt Stake Piledriver off the second rope, to win at 2:27:14 and become, in Gordon Low's own call, part of “the best match in NHBWA history.”

The surviving record breaks off two months later, on 24 March 1996, with its own operator denying that the fed was closing: “It's not closing. That's just an ugly rumour.” No further word survives either way.
