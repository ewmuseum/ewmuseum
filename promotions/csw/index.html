The independent, pre-acquisition life of CSW (Cyber Space Wrestling) — a prowrestling.com efed its own alumni call the founding institution of the whole hobby, four title lineages deep, whose entire cast walked into CWF-Pro and whose own ledgers and own wrestlers cannot agree whether it ran eight months or eight years. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

CSW — Cyber Space Wrestling — was not born a CWF-Pro brand. It was an independent efed with its own promoter, its own roster, its own boards and its own four-belt title system, and its own ledgers say so in the plainest terms available: each one carries a champion running eight to fourteen reigns deep before the same line stops it cold — “Title is stripped by Pres Burns who has Acquired the CSW Efed.” That acquisition, dated February 2004 on all four ledgers’ own headers, is the edge where this wing ends and the buyer, CWF-Pro, picks the same belts back up under its own name.

It ran on prowrestling.com. A dozen of its own alumni say so in the same breath, word for word — “the CSW from www.prowrestling.com” — and the man billed in-house as its president is given the hometown “prowrestling.com” and the founding line “Prowrestling.com started there very 1st efed called the CSW.” He started it, that page says, with a man named Zoron, who took the promotion over at the end and closed it.

Tank won the CSW Title first, eliminating Mercury in a battle royal; Mercury turned around and won the CSW National Title's own first battle royal by eliminating a young Miguel Sanchez, and the two of them feuded over it, he says, for two years. Sanchez took that belt twice and was named the Icon on the second. The Raxton Brothers dominated the tag division outright — three of nine recorded reigns, plus a fourth spell the ledger pointedly refuses to number, restored to them after the CSW itself stripped a rival team's disputed house-show win and handed the belts back. Dozens of the wrestlers CWF-Pro would go on to build its own eleven-year run around — Sanchez, Mercury, Tank, Machine of War, R@ven, and most of the rest — open the “Earlier Career” section of their own later bios the same way: rookies in the CSW, “like all the great legends did.” In its own alumni's telling the CSW is not anyone's prologue — it is the place an entire generation of the hobby says it came from, and the one they name when they are asked to explain themselves.

Nothing survives of CSW hosted under its own name — no site of its own, no roster page, no card, no news. Everything held here is read through the buyer's own retrospective ledgers and its wrestlers' own bio prose, all of it already served under that wing's own archive (accession 0090) rather than re-served here.

How long did it actually run?

The wing's own record cannot agree with itself, and the gap is not small. The four ledgers head themselves January 1996 to February 2004 — eight years. Roughly twenty of the fed's own wrestler bios say six to eight months, ending in 1997: Jim Boss, its own lead commentator, writes “The CSW closed down after only running 6/7 months”; Prowler “untill it closed after running for 6-8 months”; Denny Battista “the fall of the CSW in it's 8th month”; the Insyders “the efed closed down in early 1997.”

There is a reading that fits both, and it is recorded here as a reading rather than adopted as a fact: CWF-Pro's own post-acquisition ledgers describe the 2004 unifications as absorbing three CSW generations at once — “the CSW I, II & III National Titles,” “the CSW II & CSW III World Tag Team Titles” — and JL Black, who ran the second and third, says plainly that “He had Pres Burns help with the title history of the CSW.” The eight-year span may be a single lineage drawn backwards across all three promotions. This wing holds only what is printed above each ledger's own acquisition line, and leaves the contradiction where the corpus leaves it.

Storylines

CSW left no card behind — not one show, results page or recap survives anywhere in the record. What survives instead is four numbered title ledgers and the retrospective “Earlier Career” sections of the wrestlers who were there, written years later on somebody else's website. The arcs below are drawn from those, and every beat cites either a ledger line or a named character's own bio prose. Nothing here is reconstructed from a show, because there is no show to reconstruct it from.

The Ultimate Brother Hood, in two waves Written by AI, 2026

The stable that would run CWF-Pro for a decade was built inside CSW, and its own founder tells the story in three sentences: Miguel Sanchez “started the Brother Hood. Then the turned into the New Brother Hood. Then the Ulitmate Brother Hood.” The first line-up he names is Jenny Love, the Loose Cannon, Zamza, TGS, Lord of Illusions and Mr. Ravishing — and it did not survive its own run. Lord of Illusions was thrown out (“After a few months with him, Zamza and Sanchez fired him from the UBH. he was very bitter about it”), TGS were beaten out on-screen to make room for a new tag team, and the Insyders came in over the top of them: “Started out as a tag team for the UBH after Sanchez had the TGS beatin out of the gang.” What emerged is the line-up Legend and PJ Brown both call the second wave — Sanchez, PJ Brown, Legend, Facing Death and the Insyders — and the stable made champions of its own: the UBH “did help him win the CSW Royal title,” Facing Death's page says, “His only title he ever won.” Not everyone who claimed membership got it. Stone Cold Stinger Hitman Hart “did believe that he was in the UBH but Sanchez has gone on record that he was never apart of the group.”

Sanchez and Mercury, and the belt neither of them opened Written by AI, 2026

The CSW National Title's own first line stages the feud that would outlast the promotion: Mercury wins the inaugural battle royal “by eliminating Miguel Sanchez.” Mercury's own page picks it up from there — “His main fight was with Sanchez after he became the 1st National Champion. It was always Sanchez vs Mercury. They have been in a feud for over 2 years” — a span longer than most of the fed's own alumni say the whole promotion lasted. Sanchez took the belt twice off the back of it, and got his billing from the second: “After winning the National title for a 2nd time. Sanchez was named by the roster as the ICON in the CSW and in Ewreslting.” A second, quieter feud ran alongside it over Jenny Love, and the record only mentions it because it was restarted somewhere else years later: the Heartbreaker “would restart a feud they had in the CSW. Where they were fighting over Jenny Love.”

The CWO, and the tag titles the office took back Written by AI, 2026

The UBH was not the only faction. Cold Deem Hart led what his own page calls “the 1st CWO (canadain world order) with Heatbreaker, Mike Hart & Denny Batista” — and it is the one CSW stable that appears on a ledger under its own name, holding the tag belts as “CWO (Cold Deem Hart & Mike Hart)” on the tag title's own fourth reign. Between the two of them the factions account for most of the promotion's champions; Mike Hart carried the Royal Title into the acquisition itself.

The tag division's own last angle is the only time CSW's front office appears in the record acting as one. The ledger records the UBH pairing of Sanchez and Zamza beating the Raxton Brothers for the belts — “wins titles at a house show, however titles stripped by the CSW and award back to the Raxton Brothers” — and then does something odd: it lists the Raxtons again without incrementing their own reign count, an unnumbered “X” sitting where a tenth reign should be. Zamza's own page denies the whole thing happened. He and Sanchez, it says, “were going to win the CSW World Tag Titles at the following PPV but the csw shut down that same month.” His own card, on the same page, lists him as a CSW World Tag Team Champion. The Museum keeps the ledger's version, the numbering quirk and the denial, and settles none of them.

What does not survive

Counted rather than implied. No shows at all: not one CSW card, show or pay-per-view is named anywhere in the 381 documents, let alone recapped. The only CSW events the record stages are the four inaugural battle royals — undated, unnamed, known only from each ledger's own opening line — one unnamed house show, one unnamed debut bout, and one pay-per-view that Zamza's own bio says the promotion closed before it could hold. With no shows there are no matches and no appearances, which in this registry live on show rows. No roleplays: the CSW's own boards are attested, but nothing posted to them survives.

One award declined rather than dated by guess. A “CSW Hall of Fame” is attested twice — on Jenny Love's own card and on Miguel Sanchez's — in exactly the shape this corpus names the UCWF's, TDW's and SIW's own halls. No document lists it, dates it, or records another inductee. It is kept on those two tenures instead of invented into a ledger.

Eight championship claims that no ledger carries. Charlie, Jack Kelly, Prowler and Reefer King each claim a CSW National Championship absent from that belt's own fourteen reigns; Nitewing, Redwing and UNIQ Styles each claim a CSW World Tag Team Championship absent from that belt's own nine; and Mr. Ravishing claims a “CSW US title” that has no ledger, no second mention, and no place in the four-belt system every other document describes. All eight are recorded on their own tenures. None is structured as a reign.

One wrestler named once, and left unminted. “Raider” — Machine of War's own first CSW opponent, “He won his first bout over a guy named Raider” — appears nowhere else in the corpus. He is named here rather than turned into a record that would say more than the one clause supports.

Three CSWs, and a fourth that is a stranger

This wing is the first CSW only. JL Black's own later CSW 2 and CSW 3 are separate institutions with their own frontier rows — his bio dates them (“JL's CSW 2 would last 3 months… The CSW3 would not really even get off the ground. It only lasted 2 weeks with 5 wrestlers”), the fed's own 2001 board timeline stages CSW 2's war on CWF-Pro, and the wrestlers' own cards keep the generations apart by name throughout. Eight characters who name a CSW in their own bios carry no tenure here for exactly that reason.

A fourth CSW on the frontier is not this one at all: the frontier's own RSPWF-era row resolves its acronym to “Cat Scratch Wrestling” — a different expansion, a different hub, and seven ranked wrestlers none of whom appears anywhere in this corpus. A bare acronym never joins; it has not.
