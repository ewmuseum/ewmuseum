A Washington, D.C.-based promotion whose founding handbook laid out four titles and a house arena before any wrestler had signed. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Capital City Wrestling Alliance's surviving record is its own handbook, posted 1 May 1999 and disclaiming, in its own words, any “Enfringement” on the trademarks of “the WWF, WCW, ECW” or any wrestler's copyrighted name. It set a target roster of about two dozen wrestlers, promising to fill any early gap with its own jobbers “to give you a taste of the Cards that will be produced.”

It staged itself from its own 2,500-seat arena in Washington, DC, with a weekly show, Warped in Washington, airing on local cable at one in the morning and occasionally billing real wrestling veterans Marty Janetty and Rick Martel as guest appearances alongside its own roster. Supercards were planned every six to eight weeks, the first for early June 1999.

Four championships were named — the Washington DC Championship, defended once every three weeks; the Capital Hill Title, its own “stepping stone”; the Superfly cruiserweight title; and the Washington DC Tag Team Titles — with its own president described as “just a kineving little bastard” who set things up and blamed the fed's owner for it. No wrestler had yet signed for any of them.

No roster, card or title reign otherwise survives; the handbook itself is the last surviving document under this promotion's own name.
