A GeoCities/Yahoo-Groups dream-booking hub whose "Owner," Matthew Hopkins, ran an interfed of at least three member promotions (UWF, XWF, ICWC) casting real professional wrestlers by name across nearly a decade of captures, from a 2001 UWF newsletter through a 2009 UWOW-branded relaunch. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

United World Of Wrestling is a real-wrestler dream-booking hub, not a fed with its own invented roster: every named competitor across the served corpus — Ric Flair, Mick Foley, Triple H, Kurt Angle, Sting, Samoa Joe, Brock Lesnar, and dozens more — is a real professional wrestler, cast by the site's own hand into fictional title chases and storylines. Its earliest captured material is a 2001 UWF (United Wrestling Federation) e-mail newsletter billing an upcoming Madison Square Garden supercard; its latest is a 2009 UWOW-branded relaunch. Across that span the site's own "Owner," Matthew Hopkins, is himself a recurring on-screen character — in 2001 feuding with an in-fiction Vince and Stephanie McMahon over control of the promotion, and in a 2008 Starrcade broadcast opening the show under the threat of the real McMahon family's own storyline war on his company.

By its own later captures the site operated as a three-fed alliance under the UWOW banner — the UWF, the XWF (X-Treme Wrestling Federation), and the ICWC (International Championship Wrestling Council) — each with its own title lineage, its own news page, and championships that the record shows physically changing hands BETWEEN the three feds (a UWF Intercontinental Champion "moving to" ICWC with the belt in hand; an XWF Television Title built directly on a discontinued UWF one). This shape — a genuine interfed structure, not just a shared banner — is why UWOW is held here as one wing rather than split into its three member feds.

Storylines

The wing's own two served interfed supercards run as one continuous cliffhanger rather than two unrelated shows: the 2008 Ultimate Supercard's own unresolved final beat is Brand New Fear's own cold open, three months later on the page.

"Vince?" — Hopkins and the McMahon Family Written by AI, 2026

The 2008 Ultimate Supercard ends with Matthew Hopkins leaving the Skydome dejected after a night that cost him control of ICWC's own general manager's chair, only to be served with court papers in the loading dock by three men in suits and dark glasses. Hopkins looks up at them and mutters one word — "Vince?" — and the show cuts to black before anyone confirms it.

Brand New Fear (Jan '09) answers the question by force: Vince McMahon arrives at Madison Square Garden with Stephanie McMahon, Triple H, Shane McMahon and Shawn Michaels in tow, declares himself the inventor of sports entertainment itself, and puts Hopkins on notice over "the suffering my daughter went through" — an unresolved kidnapping angle from a Starrcade broadcast this wing never captured. Hopkins claims to already know the kidnapper's identity and needles the family throughout the night while crowning a new UWF World Heavyweight Champion and firing his own ICWC general manager, Vince Russo, on camera. The night's own main event has Hopkins accept Triple H's open challenge himself; Triple H Pedigrees him repeatedly, but the match is swallowed by the reveal it was always building to — Stephanie McMahon is grabbed again at ringside, a second masked man clubs Triple H from behind, and both kidnappers unmask as Sting and Kurt Angle, the latter holding up the TNA World Heavyweight Championship. Jeff Jarrett steps out to announce a new alliance between Hopkins and TNA and a declared war on the McMahon family; Hopkins is spirited out of Madison Square Garden by the TNA contingent (Scott Hall and Kevin Nash flanking him throughout the night) as Stephanie McMahon is left down after a Death Drop from Sting. No served document held by this wing carries the story any further.
