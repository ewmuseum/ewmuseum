A Las Vegas independent running out of Sam's Town under sibling co-owners, founded in 2001 by its own account and still running four years before the held record's first sighting of it. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

VXW dates its own founding to 2001 twice over, independently, in its own broadcasts: an anniversary special in July 2005 announces “Birthday Bash IV... the fourth birthday of this company,” and the following year's own special declares outright, “Welcome to Birthday Bash Five! That's right, the VXW is five years old!” Its own record traces that history through an earlier name, too: the company ran for several years as the Vegas Wrestling Federation before an on-air sale staged its rename to VXW in the spring of 2005.

Run out of Sam's Town in Las Vegas under a pair of sibling co-owners, its weekly Xtreme Prejudice cards built each year toward a Birthday Bash anniversary show, and its own broadcasts made a point of the stakes: after a Spin The Wheel Television Title rematch turned into a real fire spot, the promotion's own commentary noted that both wrestlers were taken to UMC Medical Center for first- and second-degree burns before being cleared to wrestle the actual rematch at Birthday Bash.

Its title picture grew inside the ring as often as out of it: a Rage In The Cage cage match at Phear Phantastic 2005 created a brand-new Spin The Wheel Championship on the spot, awarded to the first non-champion to pin another non-champion inside the cage, and Damian Payne walked out with it in only his second night in the company, pinning Justin Harper with a partner's own illegal assistance.

Recurring aggressors like Sebastian and the Dierdorf family ran a string of ambushes through the promotion's later cards, jumping returning veterans before the bell as often as beating them in the ring, while the company kept bringing its own past back into the present — the Rodeo Boys returning for one last run, a taunting video insert from a long-absent Travis LaGrange resurfacing to distract two feuding rivals mid-brawl.

Its penultimate surviving card, New Year's Eve of Destruction 2006, put both the 24 Hour and Spin The Wheel Television Championships on the line at once in a three-way match run under its own special rules for who could win which belt — and ended in a no-contest, both titles still unclaimed when the bell rang for the last time. The VXW Tag Team Championship changed hands the same night, to a new team billed afterward as The Outcasts.

Its last surviving broadcast, Sin City Fights, aired 7 February 2007 and closed on its own main event refusing to stay decided: Cobra and a young partner won by pinfall, only for the beaten challenger to be driven through the announce table again once the match was already over. No further document survives; how, or whether, VXW itself ever formally closed isn't in the surviving record.

Affiliations

Vegas Wrestling Federation (VWF, Vegas, 2001–2005)

Predecessor to VXW (2001) (April–July 2005 (VWF's own last document 11 April 2005; VXW's own earliest surviving broadcast 25 July 2005))
VWF's own last surviving broadcast, The Gauntlet 2005 (11 April 2005), ends its own on-air Gauntlet match with a returning character announcing he has bought the company; the on-screen VWF logo is destroyed and three new letters lower into place: V-X-W. VXW's own commissioner later confirms the same history directly, in the promotion's own words: Vegas Violence "kicked off the VXW, back when we were called the VWF." The identical posting-account address runs both tags without a gap, and a large set of VWF's own 2003–2005 wrestlers — including a VWF Heavyweight Title contender fighting under that same banner as early as 2003 — recur as VXW's own already-held wrestlers from VXW's own earliest surviving broadcast onward.
