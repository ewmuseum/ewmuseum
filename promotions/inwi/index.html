Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

InterNet Wrestling Incorporated introduced itself on rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy on 4 March 1997, pitched directly against the genre's own norm: “are you one of those people that don't have the time to do role-plays or read real long results? Well then this is the league for you!!” It ran an “Independent Circuit” schedule of two to five house shows a week plus one monthly major card, every wrestler required to be an original character.

Over three nights that March — in Utica, Binghamton, and Albany — it crowned all six of its inaugural championships in a single tournament week, closing in Albany when “The Icon” Pimp Daddy Brown beat “Hardcore” John Stillwater for the first INWI Heavyweight Championship. The same night's new United States Champion, Jason Starr, won only with a blatant outside assist; the promotion's own on-air Board of Directors let the title stand but fined the interferer $1,000 and barred him from ringside for the rematch, a governance beat as much a part of the record as the match itself.

Recurring stables such as the Brotherhood of Sour Grapes carried its storylines through the year, called by a broadcast team anchored for most of the run by Brian Newark, with an on-air Commissioner, Jerry DeLuise, whose live rulings routinely drove angles rather than merely narrating them. Its own United States Championship was renamed on the air, in mid-October, to the New York Championship, the same champion's reign continuing unbroken under the new name.

The promotion carried its flagship studio broadcast, Hot Spot, into 1998 and 1999 before staging a three-night farewell, 19–21 March 1999, its owner, Pete DeLuise, framing it throughout as the fed's own two-year anniversary. Every remaining championship changed hands or was confirmed across the weekend, closing on a four-way elimination match that crowned James Pillow the final INWI World Heavyweight Champion.

DeLuise's own closing speech, delivered in the ring to the assembled roster, named dozens of the wrestlers who had passed through the promotion over two years and revealed, as what he called “the two year secret,” that its longtime interviewer, Tiffany Love, was his wife.

A December 1999 newsgroup thread closed the question of any further continuation: a proposed revival was put to DeLuise directly and turned down, reported secondhand as his decision that he was “done with that pipe dream” of running a federation again.
