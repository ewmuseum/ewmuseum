Frequently Asked Questions. Kinda.

12 February 1995
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Something that should NOT be included in the FAQ, but is one anyway. 1. "What is the RSPWF?" 1a."What's the difference between the RSPWF and fantasy wrestling?" 2. "Who is in charge of the RSPWF?" 3. "How do I join the RSPWF?" ----- 1. "What is the RSPWF?" The RSPWF, to be honest, is nothing more than a joke. The RSPWF (short for Recreation Sport Professional Wrestling Federation) is an examination of several things involving the "sport" of professional wrestling.
First, a brief history. In 1990(?) [name withheld] created the RSPWF Bandwidth titles, to recognise great posts and great posters, as well as infamous people in professional wrestling. Don ran several "Supercards"
featuring a mix of personalities from professional wrestling and frequent posters on the newsgroup rec.sport.pro-wrestling. (hereafter referred to as simply rspw) While the titles themselves were referred to as the Bandwidth championships, the entire "program" (for lack of a better term) was dubbed the
RSPWF. The last SuperCard written by Don was in March 1992(?). In January 1994, during a somewhat intense debate over match ratings, [name withheld] created a post ("New And Exciting Ways To Laugh At Ourselves") in which he suggested bringing back the bandwidth titles, and proposed four matches he would have signed. The post was well-received in both the newsgroup and private e-mail, so the following Friday, Peter wrote the four matches out as he felt they would have finished, added a fifth, and posted the entire card as the first RSPWF SuperCard in nearly two years, titled "Winter Washout".
From the beginning of his stint as writer of the RSPWF, [name withheld] has professed two things: 1) that it was an examination of the current (as well as several former) angles used in professional wrestling; and 2) that it was an examination of the posting styles and the people who post them to the rspw newsgroup. There are those who don't really take either statement for it's word; [name withheld] still believes that he's held true to those two statements since day one.
Therefore, taking [name withheld] at his word, the RSPWF is: 1. an examination of current and former angles used in professional wrestling; and 2. an examination of posts and posts to the rspw newsgroup. It would be advisable for this document to take Mr. [name withheld] at his word, mainly because he's the one _typing_ this document.
1a."What's the difference between the RSPWF and fantasy wrestling?" Mainly, the difference exists in characters. Everybody, without fail, who appears in the RSPWF has posted something of note to the group, or at least has caught the writer's eye. At least half of the characters that appear in the fantasy games will NEVER post in rspw because they are fictitious. An example would be Mexican Sensation Mascara Zograda.
"But this doesn't explain RSPWF people like Chris White Wolvie or The Dark Cheetah." Actually, yes, it does. Chris White Wolvie began his stint in the RSPWF under his real name, which won't be mentioned here by his request.
And though "Dark Cheetah" is not her real name, she is, in fact, a woman (several different sources can verify this) and she HAS posted to RSPWF, thus she isn't fictitious.
The other big difference, which has also remained a constant since April, is the question of relativity... how the shows relate to the group. The RSPWF's motifs are outlined above, and since the question of a group split came up in May 1994, nobody has really bothered to question these. On the other hand, a lot of people are still wondering what exactly the RSPCW has(had) to do with r.s.p-w, except for the fact that it is a "game" based upon pro wrestling, and that some (but not all) characters are people who post to rspw.
----- 2. "Who is in charge of the RSPWF?" See above. [name withheld] is. Period. Nobody else. That doesn't mean that every bright idea you read about in the RSPWF posts originated from him, though. It isn't half the time. As stated above,
[name withheld] examines (and twists) angles used in professional wrestling, but what angles are used is entirely up to [name withheld]. Quite often, various angles are suggested, and almost as often, those angles are incorporated or used outright in the RSPWF by [name withheld]. But he has the final say. And by all accounts,
[name withheld]'s wealth of bad angles to draw from is indeed vast. He's been known to put things from recent WCW history on the same card with things that he saw once on an obscure WCCW TV show from 1982. That doesn't mean he can't use a little help here and there.
Insofar as interviews go, [name withheld] actually writes less than half of anything posted as an "interview". Most of what _is_ posted as interview is submitted by e-mail (mostly; [name withheld]'s been known to extract posts) to [name withheld], and he edits those into his big post, so long as it follows the current
(twisted) storyline. However, Mr. [name withheld] is quick to point out that he reserves the right to edit a submitted interview, or not use it at all.
----- 3. "How do I join the RSPWF?" You don't. That is, you don't "join" per se. If you post to the newsgroup, you are fair game. It's as simple as that.
One of the competing leagues, the RSPCW, professes that the RSPWF is an ego game. It isn't. If you post something that catches [name withheld]'s eye, or if your posting style does the same, or if you're known for SOMEthing on the newsgroup, chances are you WILL be used in a show sooner or later. But we urge people not to flood the group with posts, hoping that they'll get written in;
more likely than not, they'll annoy a lot of people, while not saying much of anything at all.
The catch, though, is that if you're used, in almost every case, your GIVEN NAME will be what appears in the post. Very, VERY seldom are net.handles used as characters. (see above for reasoning of Dark Cheetah and Chris White
Wolvie exceptions.) A pointer [name withheld] would hasten to add is this: "Half of what the RSPWF is about is being able to laugh at yourself. If you post here, chances are you
WILL get into a debate of some sort. If that's the case, the question is: Can you step back outside yourself, look in, and laugh for a moment? If you can't do that, then you probably will never get the point of the RSPWF. A simple majority of the posts in the rspw newsgroup are serious in nature, whether it's ratings debates, or questions about a favourite wrestler, or flames. The RSPWF is designed to lighten the group, and give everybody something to laugh at.
These people aren't laughing _at_ you. Hopefully, they're laughing _with_ you."
