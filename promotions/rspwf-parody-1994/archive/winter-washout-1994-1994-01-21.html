[RSPWF] Winter Washout 1994

21 January 1994
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
RSPWF Winter Washout '94 happened last night in front of a sellout 18,000 at Tokyo's Budokan Hall. It was certainly a night to remember, and everybody who is anybody in the wrestling business was there... notable absences were Terry
"Hulk" Hogan (had a hangnail), Vince McMahon (couldn't figure out which steroids to pack), Ted Turner (heard a rumour that the Braves were on), Andre
"The Giant" Roussimoff (lost a casket match), Dino Bravo (smoked one pack too many), and, above all others, Yokozuna Rodney Anoia (simply couldn't fit on the plane) and Virgil (afraid he'd have to job for Barry Horowitz too).
The dark match of the evening, not shown to the fabulous people watching on Pay-Per-View (incidentally, they got to see the show before it happened), pitted "Hardcore Alaskan" Sean Ryan against [name withheld] in a falls-count-only- in-the-ring match. The fans oohed and aahed their way through what turned out to be the tamest match on the card... it ended after Ryan executed his patented
"Alaskan CyclonePlex" (variation of the Ocean Cyclone Suplex by Minami Toyota) from the second rope and pinned [name withheld] at 15'02. After the match, both men shook hands much to the delight of the crowd.
Rating: ***1/2 The first match shown to the PPV audience was for the newly created RSPWF Flyweight title... [name withheld] against Tony Shubert, best of three falls. The unfortunate thing was, the idiot who signed the match put a 30 minute time limit on it. This did become crucial eventually.
- The first fall, though somewhat wild, ended in rather questionable fashion when [name withheld] scored a pin on Shubert following a DDT. The only problem was Shubert's foot was on the ropes. [name withheld] was awarded the fall, though, because the ref didn't see it. Time elapsed: 14'51.
- The second fall also ended chaotically. [name withheld] was yelling some odds out to a kid at ringside when Shubert snuck up behind him and schoolboyed him for the pin at 29'41. The only problem was that Shubert had two handfuls of trunks when he made the pin. [name withheld] complained about this to the referee, and the ref questioned Shubert... who responded no. That's when the fun began... because walking down the aisle was none other than Jimmy "The Greek"
Snyder. Snyder told the ref that Shubert had indeed been holding tights, and the ref motioned for the clock to begin again and the match to continue.
Shubert stupidly got into a shouting match with Snyder, and was then pushed against the ropes from behind. [name withheld] tried to execute a reverse cradle, and got a two, but Shubert reversed it and got a two... and [name withheld]
reversed THAT and got a three... but he pinned himself. [name withheld] went over too far in the cradle, and Shubert got a shoulder up. Shubert was officially-- and legally--awarded the second fall at the official time of 29'53, leaving both men only 7 seconds to score a pin on the other.
- And it nearly happened. At the 25th second of the 30-second interval, Shubert clocked [name withheld] from behind with a roll of quarters. Five seconds later, the bell rang... but Shubert was too busy posing. He eventually made the cover... but the ref only counted one before the time limit expired.
OFFICIAL DECISION: 1-1 TIE. RATING: ***3/4 The next match was the exploding ring "Quebec Province" rules match between "Hacksaw" [name withheld] and Chrispy Palacios for the RSPWF Southern title. [name withheld] came out first, but without longtime manager "Cactii" [name withheld]. Then
Palacios made his way toward the ring... and that's when [name withheld] decided to make his presence felt. When Chrispy was halfway to the ring, [name withheld] ran out from the backstage area and blindsided Chrispy with a chair shot to the head...
Palacios crumpled to the arena floor, but [name withheld] picked him up and carried him to the ring and shoved him in it. The bell rang, and [name withheld] covered Palacios with one finger. Three seconds later, [name withheld] was the new champion.
OFFICIAL DECISION: RICK [name withheld] WINS BY PINFALL. RATING: DUD (match? what match?) The first half of the big Double Main Event was to fill the RSPWF World Tag Titles... two of three falls, inside a steel cage, as Jeff and [name withheld] faced off against huge favourites [name withheld] and Pete Barlow, The Ohio Connection.
The [name withheld] went inside the cage first, then the Connection... and things got wild instantly. Two minutes into the match, Barlow hoisted Jeff up for a gorilla slam, but instead climbed to the second rope and threw Jeff over the top of the cage and down to the concrete floor, leaving Barry to be double-teamed. The ref was more or less powerless to stop the Connection, and after two more minutes of Barry being brutalised, the ref stopped the match in the first fall and awarded the titles to the Connection at 4'25.
OFFICIAL DECISION: [name withheld] AND BARLOW WIN BY REFEREE'S DECISION, 1ST FALL. RATING: ***** (hey, who DIDN'T want to see Jeff tossed out?) The second half of the big Double Main Event was the big 4-Corners match to determine the new R.S.P-W World Champion... the first man to come to ringside was "Diamond" Dave Scherer. When it was announced that he would start the match outside the ring, Wade Keller ("The Torch") stood up from his ringside seat and shouted obscenities at both Scherer and league commissioner Cal
Jewell, saying it was a travesty that this was allowed to happen. Announced second was Andy "The Animal" Patrizio, who came to the ring rather unnoticed by most of the fans. It was then announced that he, too, would start outside the ring. Next came the announcement of the special guest referee for the match,
Ric Flair. Of course, Flair got a standing ovation from the crowd, not only because of who he was, but because of what he had with him: the barbed wire baseball bat that would be suspended 15 feet above the ring. It was then announced that the next two men would start in the ring... and the first one announced was Evan "Tex" Schlesinger. He came to the ring and got booed very, very heavily and unmercifully by the Tokyo crowd. Then "Killer" Herb Kunze was announced, and the fans began popping like mad. However, as Kunze was making his way to the ring, he was attacked from behind by "Sir" [name withheld], who was shouting, "My Rumours are better than your TidBits, dammit!" It took
20 scrubs and 4 referees to pull [name withheld] off of Kunze, but the damage had been done. The ringside doctor ruled that Kunze was unable to compete in the match... and Scherer was appointed to enter the ring.
- "Tex" Schlesinger lost the first fall of the match at 20'06, after Patrizio nailed him with a dropkick from the second rope, and then Scherer gave him an Oklahoma Stampede which made "Dr. Death" Steve Williams burst into tears, saying, "He does it as good as I do." Scherer scored the pin. Schlesinger, though, was not eliminated, as it takes two falls to be completely eliminated.
- Patrizio lost the second fall of the match following a very interesting turn of events. As Scherer and Patrizio fought in the ring, Patrizio attempted to Irish whip Scherer across the ring, but Scherer reversed and sent Patrizio over instead. However, Schlesinger was holding the top rope down, so
Patrizio went flying outside the ring. Andy landed on his feet, though, so he pulled Evan down from the apron and began pounding on him. Patrizio was counted out at 33'10. Patrizio had one fall left.
- Patrizio and Schlesinger continued battling through the 30-second rest period... during which time Scherer got into a shouting match with Wade Keller again. The shouting match was still going on at the end of the rest period, and as the bell rang, Patrizio, still the legal man, slid back into the ring and schoolboyed Scherer for the pin at 33'16.
- The action began again in the fourth fall with all three men facing elimination. During a wild melee where Patrizio started a shouting match with referee Ric Flair, Keller from his ringside chair tossed Schlesinger a gold chain, which he then wrapped around his fist and used to knock Scherer out. Flair stopped arguing with Patrizio long enough to count the three, and
"Tex" eliminated Scherer at 49'24... leaving "Tex" Schlesinger and Andy "The Animal" Patrizio. - Needless to say, Patrizio did not wait for the 30-second rest period to end; he jumped Schlesinger even before he had gotten off of Scherer. Patrizio pounded and pounded on Schlesinger, eventually throwing him outside the ring.
Scherer, still at ringside, slid the ladder at ringside in to Patrizio and then went to the back. Patrizio climbed the ladder and got the barbed wire baseball bat. Then he slid out of the ring and began pounding on
Schlesinger's forehead and back with the barbed wire. Schlesinger did get the bat eventually, though, and used it to bust open Patrizio. After a while, both men were tired out from loss of blood... REAL blood, not this blood capsule bullshit. Anyway, soon the bat was out of play and it was just the two men inside the ring. At one point, Schlesinger whipped Patrizio across, and the two knocked heads. Schlesinger fell into Flair, knocking him half-conscious, before falling to the mat himself; Patrizio ended up being leaned against the ropes but also only half-conscious. Then out from the dressing room area came none other than...
"Cactus" [name withheld]. [name withheld] got to ringside and, much to the delight of the fans, pushed Patrizio off the ropes and onto Schlesinger. A half- conscious Flair counted three, and Patrizio walked away with the title at
68'45. OFFICIAL DECISION: PATRIZIO ELIMINATES SCHLESINGER TO WIN, 5TH FALL. RATING: ****1/2 After the match, new RSPWF World Champion Andy "The Animal" Patrizio had this to say: "You know, nobody out there in R.S.P-Wland gave me a shot to become the new Champion... well, here I am. I've got the gold, and you can bet I'm going to be champion for a long, long time. Shaka brudda."
Also interviewed was Ohio Connection's Pete Barlow, who, when asked about his team's victory, had the following words of wisdom: "It felt out there, you know? I mean... it really felt out there... It was kinda tubular, in a radical sort of way, but mostly... it was out there."
The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy begins like this: "Space," it says, "is big. Really big. You wouldn't believe how vastly hugely mind-bogglingly big it is. I mean, you may think it's a long way down the road to the chemist, but that's just peanuts to space."
"Killer" Herb Kunze reacted to "Sir" [name withheld]'s sneak attack like this: "Oliver, for a long time I trusted you, now you have to turn your back on me and the fans... and you know, that just isn't right. I'll make you a challenge: you get a partner of your choice and I'll get a partner of mine, and we'll all get together and fight at "Spring Cleaning" in April... or don't you have the guts?"
As of press time, [name withheld] had not accepted the challenge, but rumours have it that he's going to.
The broadcast ended with a still shot of Andy "The Animal" Patrizio holding up his newly won RSPWF World Title belt in celebration. Then it faded to black.
