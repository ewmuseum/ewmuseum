This is dedicated to the clueless among us that have no concept about what's being done here.

I wrote the first update segment since the retirement of [name withheld]. Hopefully, I will be able to write one of these segments every Monday, but they will be out every Tuesday at the latest. If you don't get one, e-mail me and I will send you a copy of the update/supercard.
Now, you may be asking yourself, what the heck is this guy babbling about? This is why I've put together a little treatise on what the RSPWF is all about. For lack of anything better, this will serve as a FAQ.
Frequently Asked Questions About The RSPWF by [name withheld] This document is Copyright 1995 by [name withheld]. Feel free to send any comments, suggestions, questions, improvements, or love letters to

Table of Contents 1) The Organization 2) The Purpose 3) The Format 4) The History 5) The Booker ============================================================================== 1) The Organization Q: What is the RSPWF? A: The RSPWF is an acronym that stands for the Rec.Sport.Pro-Wrestling Federation. Q: And...? A: Oh, you wanted a longer answer. Okay. [name withheld], a former booker, said that "The RSPWF, to be honest, is nothing more than a joke." That's a bit too simplistic, but fairly close to the truth. To be more specific, the RSPWF is a parody of the major wrestling organizations in the world today, i.e. the World Wrestling Federation, World Championship Wrestling,
Extreme Championship Wrestling, etc., using people who post to the group rec.sport.pro-wrestling (hence the name). It is also intended to be a parody of the goings-on on the group itself. One of the biggest problems that many people find with a newsgroup (and this one in particular) is that the people on it take their opinions far too seriously. The RSPWF was created as a way to lighten things up on the newsgroup.
Q: So, in essence, it's a fantasy league, right? A: By strict definition, yes, although in practice it has nothing to do with any true fantasy league. This is far different.
Q: But you just said it was a fantasy league! Why won't you take it over to rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy, you moron?
A: Bite me. Q: Okay, I'll be nice. But why won't you move? A: The reason is actually pretty simple...very few people want it to go over there. Q: Eh? A: Allow me to explain. Rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy is a relatively recent creation, put together in early 1995 via a 2/3 vote of the newsgroup. After the RSPWF began its second run in January 1994 (see
Section 4 for more details), fantasy groups began to pop up rapidly on rec.sport.pro-wrestling, and a lot of people complained that the newsgroup was becoming clogged with info on fantasy leagues where it should have been taken up with real information, like flamewars between
[name withheld] and [name withheld], [name withheld]'s statistical analysis of match ratings systems, and inquiries into whether or not the Ultimate Warrior was dead. So the r.s.p.w.fantasy newsgroup was established, according to
Internet guidelines, and the vast majority of the leagues went there. The RSPWF did not go over there, partially because of [name withheld]'s adamant refusal and partially because of an overwhelming sentiment on the part of posters and lurkers that the organization *was* in fact different from other fantasy leagues, and therefore was entitled to stay.
2) The Purpose Q: Okay, we've established what the organization is. But what does it do? How does it contribute to the newsgroup? A: A fair question. The reason it exists is because it has been proven, through practical experience, that what r.s.p-w needs more than anything is a dose of humor, and a sense that what we're discussing *is*, after all, pro wrestling, and not anything that will change history, regardless of those who believe otherwise.
Q: But you're using people's real names...real people. What if they get offended by some of the things you write?
A: What about it? Most people who are on the newsgroup know that the purpose of the RSPWF has nothing to do with offending people. Both previous RSPWF bookers, [name withheld] and [name withheld], have said that their purpose was to lighten people up about pro wrestling in general. Indeed,
[name withheld] made the point that it needs to be understood that more often than not, nobody is laughing at you. "Hopefully, they're laughing *with* you."
If someone is legitimately offended by what is in the RSPWF, they are encouraged to contact the author, whether it is the booker or someone else, and let them know.
Q: Okay, but how does the league contribute to the newsgroup at large? A: Aside from the humor aspect, the organization serves as something of an editorial about the people, places, and ideas in the world of professional wrestling. For whatever reason, the nature of the business seems to draw a lot of, shall we say, the seedier personalities in life.
Herb Abrams comes immediately to mind. The RSPWF, more often than not, reflects the views of the newsgroup in general. This is not always true, but it is true more often than not.
3) The Format Q: So how does the RSPWF work? A: Each week, the booker writes up a Special Update. These updates vary in length and are done in a manner somewhat similar to the average syndicated television show, with some adjustments done to accomodate the limitations of it being on a computer screen. Perhaps a better analogy might be to put it in terms of a TV script. I dunno.
Q: But how come that until lately, we haven't had very many updates? A: You'd have to ask the former booker that question. I don't follow him around, you know. :>
Q: Anyway...I assume the Updates lead to something? A: Absolutely. Q: Would you mind telling me *what* they lead to? A: I thought you'd never ask. :> Like most wrestling organizations, the RSPWF will have several Supercards throughout the year. Theoretically, the booker can come up with any idea he wants, but a general format has been established in the past couple of years: Winter Washout in the third week of January (when the students come in for winter semester); Spring
Cleaning around the first of April; June Jollies around the first of June; Summer Sizzling at the start of the fall semester; Crash of the Titans at the end of September; and Super-Starrcade on Thanksgiving. Q: So how do I join the RSPWF? A: Well...you don't. You're chosen by the booker. If you post, the chances are you will be used at some point in an angle. This is not a guarantee by any stretch of the imagination, but it's the best answer I can give. Please, do not bother the booker about joining. He will get irritated and more than likely chokeslam you many many times. If you are used, your given name will be used. Period, end of story. So, unless it is your given name, no nicknames (other than what the booker tacks on for the sake of angles, characters and such) will be used.
Q: But that doesn't explain characters like Dark Cheetah, The Masked Crunchman, etc. etc. A: Actually, yes it does. There are a few people who post under nicknames either out of necessity or because they simply choose to. For instance,
Dark Cheetah is a lady I have known for over a year now who is a fairly frequent poster to the group...for that matter, many of the people on the group have seen her at some point. The Masked Crunchman, similarly, is someone I have gotten to know in the last several months. So, there is a little breathing room on that.
Q: You've been talking about the booker for a while now...well, who is he/she? A: Currently, [name withheld] (that's me! :>) is the booker. Q: So, he has absolute control over all the angles that get put into the RSPWF? A: Basically, yes. However, that doesn't mean that he's the only one writing anything. Previous bookers have insisted (for some length of time, at any rate) that they be the only ones writing angles and such for some time. However, [name withheld] doesn't believe that. While he, and he alone, decides what is used, when it's used, and how it's used, posters on rsp-w are encouraged to send angle ideas, storyline ideas, and especially interview segments to his e-mail address. Historically, about 1/2 of the ideas in each update/Supercard come from the participants themselves.
4) The History Q: Whose idea was this, anyway? A: I don't know all the details of this, so please bear with me. In 1991 (?), a gentleman by the name of [name withheld] decided that he wanted to honor
(and, in some cases, dishonor :>) various posters for their contributions, good and bad, to rec.sport.pro-wrestling. In keeping with the spirit of professional wrestling, he did so by creating the RSPWF
Bandwidth titles. To my knowledge, no records were ever kept. Q: Then what happened? A: After two years or so, [name withheld] left the group and retired. After a few months, [name withheld], after yet *another* of the interminable flamewars that commonly take place on rsp-w, created a thread called "New and
Exciting Ways to Laugh at Ourselves." He proposed that the RSPWF titles be brought back, and that because of the flamewars, he had some excellent ideas for some matches to be put together. It was one of the most well-received threads in a long time, so [name withheld] booked the matches...and the RSPWF, in its present form, was born.
Q: What are some of the highlights of [name withheld]'s booking? A: Please, I don't have *that* much time. :> Ask anyone on the newsgroup, and more often than not they'll have several famous moments for you. As for me, it's not my place to say. To each their own.
5) The Booker Q: So how did [name withheld] end up with the job? A: After a year and a half, and after some very intense flamewars with several prominent members of the newsgroup, [name withheld] said that it had stopped being fun for him, and resigned the booking position effective
August 20, 1995. He left it to four people, [name withheld] included, to determine the fate of the RSPWF. After some discussion, the job was given to him.
Q: How often do real-life events in wrestling correspond with the RSPWF booking? A: Pretty well, actually. Angles that are ridiculously stupid will more often than not be parodied in the RSPWF. And believe me, in this sport, there is plenty of material. However, it's the philosophy that most corresponds to real life. In other words, booking in the RSPWF is done in a manner similar to real life...storylines that make sense have to be first and foremost. Often, fans will follow a storyline that is good even if the wrestlers perhaps aren't very good, but few will follow a good match if the storyline is idiotic, and rightfully so. Obviously, on a computer screen, you have to take the booker's word for the workrate, so a good storyline is even more essential in the RSPWF than in, say, ECW.
