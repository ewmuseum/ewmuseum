Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The RSPWF, the Recreation Sport Professional Wrestling Federation in its own FAQ's expansion, was a parody federation written on the rec.sport.pro-wrestling newsgroup. Nobody joined it. Its roster was the newsgroup: a poster who caught the writer's eye was booked into the next card, almost always under his or her given name, beside the real wrestlers, promoters and newsletter writers the group argued about. Its FAQ gave it two purposes, an examination of wrestling's own angles and an examination of the people posting to the group, and hoped the people it booked would find the newsgroup laughing with them, not at them.

It took its name and its first title from earlier games on the same newsgroup. In 1991 posters had called a voting league of real wrestlers the RSPWF, and in 1991 and 1992 the Dreamcards had crowned Bandwidth champions; its own FAQ tells those years as its first run. In January 1994, in the middle of a quarrel over match ratings, a new writer proposed bringing "the RSPWF Bandwidth titles" back, and Winter Washout '94, staged in Tokyo's Budokan Hall, opened the production.

It kept a shape of its own: a weekly Special Update written like a television script, interview segments the cast wrote and mailed in themselves, and a calendar of supercards, Winter Washout in January, Spring Cleaning in April, June Jollies, Summer Sizzling at the start of the fall semester, Crash of the Titans at the end of September and Super-Starrcade on Thanksgiving. Its cast answered back in character in the card threads, a rival poster fed, the RSPCW, called it an ego game, and more than once the newsgroup asked whether it belonged there at all.

The first proposal for a newsgroup of fantasy leagues, in April 1994, named the RSPWF and the RSPCW as leagues the new group would serve. When rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy was created in April 1995 the RSPWF stayed where it was: by its own account its writer refused to move, and its readers held that it was different from the fantasy leagues. That writer resigned in August 1995 and left the fed to four of its posters, who chose a second writer; he staged Crash of the Titans '95 and a three-part Super-Starrcade '95, and in December 1995 the newsgroup quarrelled over who got written in.

It was still posting in December 1997. How it ended is not known.
