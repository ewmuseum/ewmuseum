A weekly booked promotion reborn in February 2002, whose Monday Metal broadcast crowned its Women's and North American champions and whose first pay-per-view, Awakening, crowned a World Heavyweight Champion from a fourteen-man tournament. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

IEW came back in February 2002. Its Monday Metal broadcast from the Civic Center in Charleston, West Virginia, called by Willie “Mo Mo” Peterson and Axel Grimes, crowned Little Buff Women's Champion and gave the vacant North American title to “Simply Electric” Eli Johnson in a battle royal refereed by the promotion's chief executive, the Showstoppa PIC.

The same night The Lost Soul returned, and PIC booked himself against him for the promotion's first pay-per-view, while Jason Craven was twice seen backstage paying off Damon Warrior.

Awakening followed on 17 March 2002 at the Pyramid in Memphis. Adam Richardz won the Extreme title, Jason Craven won the World Tag Team titles on his own, and Tom Tombstone beat Jared Sengir in the final of a fourteen-man tournament for the vacant World Heavyweight Championship, with Bullnutz counting the fall.

Bullnutz then fired PIC as chief executive, named himself president and booked PIC against Sengir for a pay-per-view in Chicago called Slaughter. Those two broadcasts and a call for new wrestlers survive; nothing later is held.
