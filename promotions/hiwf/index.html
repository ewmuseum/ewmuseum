Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The High Impact Wrestling Federation spun off on 22 June 1997 from a recently folded federation: its own founding announcement names its president as that federation's former Vice President, joined by three more of its former staff. It ran book-and-simmed, weighting interview and flash quality into match outcomes, for wrestlers only — “please no real ones.”

Its schedule ran two cards a week, Saturday Night Slam Jam and Wednesday Night Reckoning, plus two interview shows, one of them the twice-weekly S.Y.M. (“Speaking Your Mind”), voiced throughout by a kayfabe-broken comedy desk.

Its World Heavyweight Championship was decided 15 July 1997 in a steel-cage tournament final where both finalists went off the top of the cage through three tables; it was awarded to “Mr. Universe” Tommy Drummond on the technicality that his feet touched the floor first, and it was still on his chest as of the federation's last attested card. The same night, a Tag Team Title Death Match crowned the L.A. Mafia champions over the Hate Machine on a pinfall with one second left on a sixty-minute limit; they lost the belts to the Nobles about three weeks later, a transition its own cross-promotional magazine narrated rather than a card ever showed.

Its Halloween supercard, Escape From New York, 31 October 1997, opened with a mob boss buying the use of a portrayed Mayor of New York's own city for twenty-four hours with two suitcases of cash, then staged every match of the card at a named city landmark.

After what one of its own posts calls a two-month hiatus, the same president relaunched the federation “from the ground up” in early 1998, rebuilding its roster and reviving the S.Y.M. interview slot at a new address. Its documented run reaches at least a “Friday Fights” broadcast in May 1999, which still names the same World Heavyweight title and previews a pay-per-view under it.

Its first pay-per-view's own card doesn't survive, only its build-up and two recaps; no closure statement survives at any point across this run, and its fuller roster is known only from recruitment ads rather than a roster document of its own.
