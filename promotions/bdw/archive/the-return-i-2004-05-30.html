The Return I

30 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is BDW’s own results page for its first pay-per-view, live from Madison Square Garden, as it stood in the operator’s own 8 June 2004 site backup.

Backstage: Outside Madison Square Gardens By: "The Irish Bastard" Sean McClancy

The cameras come to life with the scene of the outside of the arena for tonight’s show. Marching around is what appears to be hundreds of women, each holding a picket sign. Some of the signs depict exatly what they’re here for: To protest the ‘known rapist’ known as Emillio Dervante participating in the show, let alone it’s Main Event. The women march around with their various signs and such, as a lone new reporter is seen outside. The bleached blonde starts to talk into another camera as the BDW’s cameras pick it up. It starts as she takes her cue from the reporters back at the news station

Reporter- Good evening. Yes, I’m here tonigt at Madison Square Garden, where BreakDown Wrestling will be holding their Return Pay Per View Event. In light of this last weekends events for one wrestler, things are beginning to look interesting. As one Working Immegrant, Emilio Dervante will be participating in tonight’s main event against JJ Jarone and Sean McClancy, he’s coming in here with literally hundreds of women protesting his participation, and his freedom at this time. For not yesterday, was Mr. Derante released from a New York court after they dropped charges for rape on a local woman. In return they also awarded him damages for mental anguish. Since then the judge in question released an official statement that it was the woman’s fault for drinking too much that she was attacked by Mr. Dervante and he was released. However, in a firestorm of after effects, we have a New York State judge on adminstrative leave while an investigation is going on, and as I said before, literally hundreds of women protesting here tonight. Some of New York’s finest have had to beef up the secutiry as these women appear to be ready to tear this man apart if they get near him. As she says that a few women rush up to where she’s at, and putting their signs in the camera’s range, they start to scream obsenties into the camera for the news crew there. Just as they do, a car arrives. An expensive one. A group of arena secrity guards with a couple of New York’s finest step up. As the door opens, and Emilio Dervante steps out, the women try to rush the scene. The secutiry hold him back while the angry women scream and appear to be out for blood. Dervante rushes into the arena entrance, disappearing from their view.
