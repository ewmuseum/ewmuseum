BDW ran a hybrid roleplay-and-angle promotion out of a small owner-driven roster, crowning its first World Heavyweight Champion and Broken Champion at its own inaugural pay-per-view, The Return I, before a battle royal on its own 7th Static named a third co-owner. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

BreakDown Wrestling ran twice. Its first site was up by the autumn of 2003 — a new one launched on 14 October 2003, the old one “saved” — with a weekly show called Sanitarium and a roster its own top five of that day led with Chezina and Revelation, Revelation's tag partner Apocalypse beside them. By January 2004 it had shut: its own news of 7 January had Co-Owner Jeffery alone, promising to re-open it and to close it for good if no staff came.

It came back in April 2004 as “We're Back...”, a hybrid promotion — 75 percent roleplay and 25 percent angle by its own rules — rebuilt one contract at a time. Jeffery, its founder, wrestled alongside the roster he signed, billed by that first name alone, and brought on a second co-owner in mid-May 2004.

Sunday Night Static ran weekly behind the call of Bridget Matthews and Lane Scotts, and a World Heavyweight Championship tournament ran through it to the fed's first pay-per-view, The Return I, live from Madison Square Garden on 30 May 2004. That night Jeffery fired three wrestlers on camera, Apocalypse bid the federation farewell, Arik Caine took the Hardcore Championship, Brock RockHard the Broken Championship, and Sean McClancy — “The Irish Bastard” — the World Heavyweight Championship, over Emilio Dervante and JJ Jarone.

How it ended isn't in the surviving record — the last dated document, a Jackson Kable roleplay, runs to early January 2005.

Storylines

BDW's surviving record holds three full show scripts — the 6th Static, The Return I and the 7th Static — and eleven roleplays, one of them from the fed's 2003 first run.

Brock RockHard vs. Jeremy “The One” Page Written by AI, 2026

Brock RockHard's own debut ran straight into a feud: his own roleplays (message #10 onward) stage his late father's best friend, Bill, begging him to turn down BDW for a smaller federation, while RockHard fires back at Jeremy Page by name before they ever lock up. The fed's news has RockHard debuting “targeting” Page on the fifth Static (16 May 2004); the 6th Static's own results page stages their match a week later, won by RockHard after Co-Owner Jeffery climbed in from the crowd with the referee down and cracked a chair over Page's skull — the finish RockHard's own retrospective roleplay (#12) owns up to: “the owner seems to have some beef with Mr. Page.” They met once more at The Return I, in the five-way Broken Title match, where RockHard pushed Page off the top rope into the security barrier on his way to the title.

The World Heavyweight Championship: McClancy, Dervante, and the ladder still to come Written by AI, 2026

Sean McClancy won the World Heavyweight Championship tournament's triple-threat final at The Return I: his manager Annie framed Emilio Dervante for an assault backstage, Dervante was handcuffed and freed only on a lawyer's word, and McClancy pinned him after knocking him down with the title belt, JJ Jarone already put through the announce table — the only title change the fed's own title-history ledger ever recorded. A week later, on the 7th Static, Dervante attacked McClancy before their own rematch could start, both men chased Annie around ringside, and Co-Owner Jeffery scrapped the match outright rather than let it continue as booked. What Jeffery announced in its place — a ladder match at Anger & Rage, 27 June 2004, with Annie banned from ringside — is the last the surviving record says about it; no results page for that show, or any later one, survives to say who actually left with the belt.

The Hardcore title standoff Written by AI, 2026

Arik Caine won the Hardcore Championship at The Return I, the night he burst into Co-Owner Jeffery's office and threatened to sue him for keeping him out of the main event: in a Hardcore Elimination Match billed for nine and fought by five, he pinned Jackson Kable and The Rebel, dropped Jeffery with a 2x4 when the co-owner came in with a chair, and pinned Andrew Irving last. A week later, on the 7th Static, backed by Bruce, he used the same threatened lawsuit as his excuse to refuse a title defense against Methid, walking out of the arena with the belt still on his shoulder rather than risk it. BDW's own later champions snapshot shows the Hardcore title with no name against it at all — a vacancy or a change the surviving record never dates or explains.
