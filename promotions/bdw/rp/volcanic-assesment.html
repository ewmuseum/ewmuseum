BDW Roleplay — #12

Volcanic Assesment

27 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Volcanic Assesment Brock RockHard BDW Roleplay #12 Date: 5/27/04 Location: “The one eh?” [A laugh bellows out from deep within Brock Rockhard’s massive barrel chest. Sweat drips out of his tasseled hair and down his sharp cheekbones. His baby face is full stubble as he hasn’t had time to shave. He’s wearing a thin white T- shirt and yellow mesh shorts. He’s sitting up on a bench. The bar is racked behind him and a small white Powerade towel is draped across his shoulder.] Brock: You sure were the one weren’t you Jeremy… the one who lost to an inexperienced rookie who had never fought a professional match before. I already know you’re going to blame the loss on the co-owner, but before you blow all that hot air and expend all that energy it takes your brain to think make sure you replay the invents in your head. I think that you’ll find I had the match well under control before that incident even occurred. It might not have seemed that way to the crowd, but you know Jeremy! Seriously, save the excuses. [After Brock finishes his minor diatribe he takes the towel from his shoulder and wipes his brow before promptly returning it to it’s resting place over his shoulder. His mind wanders.] “YOU HAVE TO WORK HARDER BROCK, LONGER BROCK!” “YOU CAN’T SETTLE FOR SECOND BEST SON!” “TO GIVE ANYTHING UNLESS THAN YOUR BEST IS TO SACRIFICE YOUR GIFT SON!” [The last was a quote his father had picked up from the greatest American distance runner ever, Steve Prefontaine, but he had used it so much that it had more or less become his son’s motto. Brock envisioned his father standing over the weights pressing down on them demanding that his son push through the pain.] “FORCE YOUR MIND TO MAKE YOUR BODY TAKE YOU SOMEWHERE YOU’VE NEVER BEEN!” [Brock had never minded the yelling. His father inspired him; his father inspired everyone. Brock pictured himself running side by side his father, the two carrying tires over their heads until they could no longer hold their arms above their shoulders, until their hands were black and their hearts were stronger. Brock’s father had instilled in him a work ethic matched by very few and most importantly the heart of a lion.] SLAM! [Brock’s daydreaming was interrupted as the door to their basement training facility slammed behind a large man. Brock stared up at the man and the two lock eyes.] Brock: Here to tell me you were wrong? [The man, Bill, Brock’s father’s best friend and partner for those of you who don’t recall, was wearing jeans and a two-button collared shirt. His hair was parted on the side and his lack of style probably contributed to his goofiness, but the man was stout and would be intimidating to most.] Bill: Precisely the opposite Brock. You won that match because the owner seems to have some beef with Mr. Page. Everyone saw him out wrestle you. [Brock stands up and takes a step towards Bill.] Brock: Is that so? [Bill simply nods, his cold black eyes penetrate most, but Brock holds his glare.] Brock: You’ve been around the sport long enough to realize he was laboring and that I had the upper hand regardless of what it seemed like. [Truth be told Bill probably did, but it didn’t matter. His goal was to keep Brock out of the big time until he was ready. Bill felt his was his responsibility to keep his best friend’s son from being injured, from making the wrong decision. He sighed at the boy and took a step towards him, arm length apart.] Bill: Even if you’re right, and you’re not look at the task in front of you this week. You’ve been thrust into a match not only with Page, but with three other solid wrestlers all vastly more experienced than yourself. [Brock’s frustrations can not be masked. He wears them on his sleeve, but he is currently managing to contain his awful temper.] Brock: My father taught me the sport. Hell, you taught me the sport Bill. I’m ready! [Bill tries to place his hand on Brock’s shoulder, but Brock knocks his arm away before the hand gets to close and the intense stare down ensues.] Bill: It may seem that way Brock, but this is a dangerous match. There’s too much opportunity for injury with five guys in one ring. I really wish you would reconsider. Besides that, last week you had the whole underdog complex. Nobody knew what to expect from you, but now they know and you can bet they’ve watched video on you and will exploit your weaknesses. Even if you do win that title, as if you deserved it after one whole match, are you ready for the target that will be on your back? [Brock pauses for a moment to take in what his father’s best friend says. Brock is a smart kid and he knows that Bill just wants what is best for him. Bill’s words do register with him, but he’s so intent on living out his dream and making his deceased father proud of him that none of it matters. It’s all fuel to the fire, doubts that inspire him to push on harder than before. Brock nods as if to say I hear ya.] Brock: You’re damn right I’m ready Bill! Sure I’m thrust in a match with five others Bill, but these guys aren’t Seymour Almasy or Syko Arsonist or Hessian, they are not superstars. They’re solid wrestlers in a solid federation. Bill: Which is really all you are Brock. Brock: The sky is the limit for me Bill. I watch endless tapes of these guys compete. I study everything about my opponents, wrestling is MY LIFE, and from what I can tell I’m not certain that it is my opponents sole reason for existing and that gives me a distinct advantage. Bill: Are you stating that you don’t think your opponents want this as bad as you Brock? Brock: They may claim that they do Bill, but they don’t. And bare in mind that there is a huge difference between wanting and needing. These guys aren’t putting in the hours in the weight room I am, and they sure as hell aren’t watching reels after reel of film. Bill: You think you have it all figured out don’t you? [Brock cocks his head sideways, staring curiously at Bill. Where’s he going with this? Brock ponders for a moment and then replies.] Brock: I think I’m facing some goof balls honestly and before you say anything, hear me out. Let’s take Methid first. The guy seems to be a good ole’ guy. He’s definitely someone I’d probably hang out with backstage, but the guy seems so content running his grocery store that I doubt he’s a serious contender to this match. I know I shouldn’t doubt Bill, but in this sport to be the best it has to be your life, day in and day out. Methid also stated he sold his crack and was out of rehab in three days. There is a difference between reality and fantasy and in this case the line isn’t even blurred. Nobody with that kind of drug problem gets cleared after three days and then they don’t promptly get to own a grocery store. Guys with those kinds of problems get cleared after six months if they’re lucky and work on assembly lines the rest of their lives. When it’s all said and done I can respect Methid and his good guy approach, but honestly I’m just not seeing the fire. This guy is worried about sacking the meats with the meats and his new house as opposed to hitting the weights. I think the whole wrestling scene still might overwhelm him and the bottom line is I’m not going to be intimidated by a guy like that. [Bill nods in approval.] Bill: Underestimation will beat you every time, but I do see your point. What about the other wrestlers? Have you studied them as close? Brock: Certainly. [He pauses to run through the remaining opponents in his head.] Brock: We’ve more or less covered Mr. Page so allow me to touch upon Rob Zone. This guy just makes me laugh. On camera he’s trying to come off as the ultimate hard ass, but as far as I’m concerned talking crap and bullying a camera crew and some random guy when you’re 7’1 is pretty cheap. That’s just not my style, but to each there own I suppose. This guy is so brutal that he can’t get his words straight so what makes me think he’s going to be able to get his shit straight in the ring. For example Rob Zone says that there are great competitors but he exceeds our level by far and a little while later he states that he’s not totally sure he can take us because he hasn’t been in the ring with us? Well which is it Mr. Zone? If he exceeds our level by far than wouldn’t he be able to take us regardless of whether or not he’s been in the ring with us? He’s writing checks his body won’t be able to cash my friend. If you expect me to believe that all the lights in the street just turned off by your sheer will and then the dude’s girlfriend just left with you then you’re as out of touch with reality as Methid. One of my favorite things is how we’re all supposed to know who Rob Zone is. The wrestling world is so big today, and there are so many wrestlers and we’re supposed to know who Rob Zone is like he’s a global title champ or something. I mean give me a freaking break. After our match people are going to know Rob Zone as the guy that quit BDW because he got his ass handed to him in his first match by Brock Rockhard! [Bill sighs.] Bill: The guy was pretty successful in NGW Brock and he’s got a lot more experience than you. He’s one I would be weary of. Just because he’s brutal at cutting promos doesn’t mean he can’t bring it when he steps in the squared circle. Brock: I suppose Bill, but the fact is you have to let me learn my own lessons. You have to throw me in the river and let me stay afloat because fighting my own battles and learning from my own mistakes is the only way I will be able to reach your level…. [A slow, reflecting pause] … My father’s level. [Bill looks at the youngster for the first time without the stern glare, but with compassion. He loves Brock as he would a nephew, perhaps even his own kids.] Bill: Look kid, I still disagree with this whole notion. [An irritated Brock tries to interrupt but Bill cuts him off and contines.] Bill: But you’re a smarter kid than I give you credit for so while I’m still going to try to talk you out of this, I’ll leave it for another time. Brock: I’d appreciate it. Bill: However, I am intrigued about what you think about your final opponent Pretty Boy Evans so fill me in before I depart and leave you to your work out regimen. [Brock smiles at the older man and you can feel the tension in the room ease for the first time since Bill interrupted Brock’s workout.] Brock: I thought you’d never ask. [thin smile] He is great. [Brock sits their content, with just those three words. Bill looks suspiciously at Brock, but anticipates that there is more. He tries to get a read on Brock who just sits there looking mildly content with a goofy grin staring back at his father’s old partner.] Bill: I give, how’s he great? Brock: He’s a great comedian. He can make me laugh all day long and he can make the crowd laugh thus he’s a pretty good entertainer and has a place in this business, in BDW. [pauses] However, he has no place in the ring with me for the Broken title because I won’t have time to laugh at him. I’ll be too busy analyzing everything that occurs, waiting for that precise moment when I can capitalize on the smallest of mistakes. You see Bill, if you took hot lava and waited for it to turn to obsidian and then carved the most perfect structure you could, you’d end up with me. I’m not 7’1 like our friend Rob Zone but I’m still a big powerful man with mobility and speed. I’m like a volcano in more ways than one. I feel like I have hot lava flowing within my veins waiting anxiously to erupt and spew forth over my opponents and come Sunday that’s precisely what’s going to happen. You can count on it! [Bill nods his head, he’s chuckling quietly to himself. Brock looks up at him sort of puzzled.] Brock: What is it? Bill: Nothing. Brock: Come on, seriously, what is it? Bill: I just want to know where the teleprompter is. [Brock’s eyes brows raise. It’s increasingly obvious that he’s pretty confused.] Bill: How long have you been wanting to recite those lines? [Now Brock bellows out a massive, hearty laugh. He smiles and shrugs.] Brock: They were impromptu, I swear! [Brock continues to laugh and Bill right along with him as he we fade out.]
