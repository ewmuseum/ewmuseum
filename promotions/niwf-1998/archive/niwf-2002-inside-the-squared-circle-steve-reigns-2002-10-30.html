Inside the Squared Circle w/ Steve Reigns

30 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Note: Inside the Squared Circle is a new weekly 'show'. It's similar to the interviews Jim Ross used to do on Monday Night Raw a few years back I N S I D E T H E S Q U A R E D C I R C L E [ We are now back from commercial. In the middle of the TV screen, the words: Inside The Squared Circle appear. The set is shadowy and under soft lighting are black and white photographs of great wrestling matches. From the shadows walks a man in jeans and a sports jacket.] Steve Reigns: Fans, welcome to another edition of Inside the Squared Circle, with me, Steve Reigns. Over the past two years, Desmond Walker has seemingly come from out of nowhere and taken over the industry with his flamboyant attitude. Many insiders have dubbed him the next Shawn Micheals, or better yet, the next Jason Starr. Those comments haven't been lost on Walker. Lately, Walker has been feeling the heat from former BWF World Champ Lance Nelson. Nelson has a disliking for Walker's attitude, and believes the Old School Title is a bit more prestigious then what Walker deserves. I had a chance to sit down with Walker a few days ago on the set of his new motion picture Warhammer II. He's a very focused young man. [We then segue into a clip from Desmond Walkers debut match, in which Walker wrestled under a mask in the BWF ... The scene then morphs into Walkers victory of the NIWF Purist's Dream title] Steve Reigns : You've had a pretty successful run as of late, Desmond. In your opinion, what has been your greatest accomplishment in this business? Desmond Walker: Well, Steve, I think that in THIS business, you would obviously have to look at the fact that I won the Old School title in my first year in the NIWF. I have continued to hold that title longer than anyone in the NIWF has held any title and that's pretty impressive. Being mentioned in the RSPWF awards in my first year is quite the honor as well. But really, just surviving in the NIWF is probably a bigger accomplishment than all of that. SR: Tell us something about yourself that no one knows. DW: Something that no one knows? If I tell you, Steve, then someone will, won't they? <grins> Okay, I was on "Reach for the Top", a local high school quiz show back in my early teens. I guess people that watched it know, but I haven't heard anyone bring it up recently, so maybe no one watching this knows. SR: It's interesting you picked that particular instance. You never address the earliest days of your career, as a trainee in the BWF. Many saw you as the next Flex Kavana. Not exactly the most auspicious beginning for the "Marquee Man". We've even looked into some footage from the BWF's Texas training grounds and found you carrying a plastic title belt for DFW-Pro, an affiliate of the Lone Star Wrestling Alliance, Nelson's company. [A silent tension fills the room. Reigns sits still, cautious, realizing he may have hit a nerve] DW: That was a long time ago. Any business I did with that Texas armory crap is in the past. SR: So you give no credit to Lance Nelson for getting you where you are today? Could that be a foundational issue in this feud today? DW: I'll say it again, Steve. Very slowly and very clearly. Any business I did was in the past. SR: Fair enough, Desmond. So, after a short career for the Winnipeg Bombers, why did you become a wrestler? Were you a fan? Why are you here today? DW: Steve, I became a wrestler because people looked at me, they looked at my body and my natural charisma and realized that I was a can't miss superstar and you know what, they were right! Yeah, I was a fan growing up. I loved Shawn Michaels, Ric Flair, the Harts, all of it. I'm here today because this company is the future of sports entertainment, and I will be the flagbearer. That's a shoot, Steve. I have the potential to be the greatest professional wrestler of all time, and I know it! I've had the best teachers, but all I can do now is use my god-given talents to use what they taught me and take it all to a new level. SR: Recently, Lance Nelson and his group of friends confronted you in an extremely violent fashion. During that confrontation, Nelson and his group defined what Old School means to them. It's your turn. What is old school about to you? [ A clip from last week's mayhem runs, as Nelson brings in Dennis Windham and Tommy Graves ... individual clips of each man running down what Old School means to them air] DW: Old school is the way things used to be. Steve, when I won this title, it was known as the Purist's Dream title. Stupid name, right? Yeah, it was. I renamed it the Old School title as a rib. Everyone would come in expecting a classic scientific match, and I'd use old school Jason Starr classics like my Sleep Walker, and totally catch them off guard. What Nelson and his washed up posse are doing is classic old school and they think I can't handle it. Well, I've handled everything this federation has thrown at me so far, and I'm still standing, so I'm sure this won't be a problem. SR: You seemingly have no respect for anyone in this business? Is that true? Is there anyone that you do respect? [Old stock footage of Walker and Jason Starr working out at Starr's old wrestling school] DW: I respect Jason Starr. I respect Jimmy Cougar. They trained me, and without their help, I definitely wouldn't be where I am today so soon. Oh, I'd have eventually gotten here, but they sure helped it along. But yeah, the Glamour Boys, that's pretty much it. The rest of them only wish they could be the Marquee Man. SR: How are you getting ready for the upcoming battles, you do know that there will be more then a few nights of crimson flows, right? How do you mentally prepare, knowing what Nelson has accomplished in the past, and how he's gone about it? DW: That's just it, Steve, it's all the past. What I see now is an old man who needs to surprise me and use all his buddies to jump me. Hey, do you think Eric and I couldn't do the same thing? Don't you think I could go running to Jason Starr and get him to sick some goons on Nelson and put him out? Of course, I could, but what fun would that be? No, I'm going to do this the "old-school" way and beat him at his own game! Yeah, he'll probably mess me up a bit along the way, but I can take it, and it's nothing compared to what I'll do to him. SR: How do you see this ending? DW: Steve, simply the only way this can and will end will be with the Journeyman turning tail and running back to El Paso to do his tired old act in front of 50 flabby grannies, and his two buddies right behind him. SR: After this is all done, after all the bloodshed and turmoil, do you think it's possible to coexist in the company with Lance Nelson? DW: The question is moot, Steve. I won't have to co-exist with him. He'll be gone, and I'll move on to bigger and better things. SR: Where do you go from here? [Clips of Walker on the set of Warhammer 2 are shown.] DW: Well, I continue my stellar Hollywood career. Warhammer 2 is scheduled for a January release you know, and I've got a couple scripts I'm looking at right now. After this movie comes out though, I can pretty much write my own checks. And of course, when I have time for it, I'm sure that at some point in my second year, I'll finally win the World title I so richly deserve. SR: This is your platform, Desmond. Finish the interview on your terms. DW: You want to talk Old School? I've got a challenge for Lance Nelson. [Walker pauses a second, hesitating.] DW: Nelson, best of five series, winner takes the old school title. Yeah, I've got a broken arm, but that won't be a problem. One good arm on a suntanned superman is better than anything you've got, old man. But do me a favor and leave your little bitches at home! And when it's all said and done, the Marquee Man will hold the gold and well, you'll still be OLD, Nelson. [Camera goes back to Steve] SR: Some strong words by the defending Old School champ, Desmond Walker. This war will only build. In the end, who will actually be able to call themself the Old School champ, and will there be anything left of that man when it's all said and done? [Stops for a second, to let everyone swallow what he just said] Who knows. Stay tuned folks. This one's gonna be a great battle. Make plans to join us next week when the NIWF World Heavyweight Wrestling Champion will sit down here in the studio with us to discuss his feeling on the state of wrestling, the stranglehold WWE has on the industry, and many many more subjects on Inside the Squared Circle. I'm Steve Reigns. Good night.
