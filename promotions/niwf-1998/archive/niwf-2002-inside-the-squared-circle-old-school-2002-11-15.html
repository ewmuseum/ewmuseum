Inside the Squared Circle -- A Must Read for Old School Fans

15 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Subj: [NIWF] Inside the Squared Circle Date: 11/15/02 7:22:28 PM Central Standard Time From: [detail withheld] Sent from the Internet (Details) [The scene opens with thunder and lightning splitting the sky over a coliseum structure. From the lying flies a championship belt as brass music lays. The belt circles the empty arena and the ring at its center before plummeting into the mat with a glorious explosion. A graphic appears.] INSIDE THE SQUARED CIRCLE With Your Host This Week "Hot Water" Bobby Ladd (Bobby Ladd walks out in the trademark blue blazer of the NIWF and jeans onto the set. The mood lighting reflects off exhibit size photographs of NIWF historical moments. The studio audience offers polite applause.) Bobby: Good evening and welcome to Inside the Squared Circle. I'm your guest host this week, Bobby Ladd. Steve is on vacation and will return to the program next week. Tonight, we have a real treat. One for which I am extremely thankful. As you may or may not know, I was blessed to be the Vice President of Talent Development with the Best Wrestling Federation and before that to be a Talent Coordinator for the El Paso-based Lone Star Wrestling Alliance. As such, I had a very special opportunity to interact with the gentlemen we will be profiling tonight. He is a 3rd generation superstar. He is the heir of a wrestling heritage that spans to the late 1800's and the carnival days of wrestling. He was the longest reigning BWF Champion. And he is one of the most creative minds in the sport today. Our distinguished guest tonight, Mr. Lance Nelson. (Wanted: Dead or Alive plays as Nelson appears from backstage in black denim from head to toe and snakeskin boots. He shakes hands with Bobby and acknowledges the crowd before sitting down.) Bobby: Lance, for those in the audience who aren't aware of our relationship, just how long have you known me. Lance: I think you were there when I was conceived weren't you Bob? Bobby: (laughs) Close. I've known the Nelson family for many, many years. Lance, today, I want us to talk about the amazing history you have in this sport. And as you prepare for the first of your Best of 5 series with Desmond Walker for the Old School Title, I want us to reflect on exactly what old school means to you. Lance: Sounds good to me. Bobby: First, let's talk about how you got into this sport. Where does the story begin? Lance: The story actually begins at the turning of the 19th century. My grandfather was a barman and gambler in Louisiana. It was a boom time in the bayou and he had amassed a nice business near the Gulf. But as any cajun can tell you, beware a big blow. A hurricane all but wiped him out in the fall of 1907. He was about to lock up when in walked a drunk man in a rumpled tuxedo. He begged my grandfather for a drink. My grandfather assured him we were closed, but he wouldn't take no for an answer. To get rid of the drunk, grandpa gave in and poured him a bourbon. Turns out the man dies at the bar before finishing his drink but he's already handed my father payment for his liquor. A deed to the Great Western Carnvival. Bobby: The Great Western Carnival. The beginnings of the Nelson wrestling empire. Let's watch this video! (The scene is a broken bar. A man sits at the lone table that remains intact. He scribbles with a pencil atop a makeshift financial statement. The words ASSETS and LIABILITIES are in bold print.) Voiceover: James Nielson was in trouble. That one drink had brought him into a bizarre world he had never seen. Listing his assets to include a worn tent, a goat with a single horn, and a dwindling troop of carnies, he couldn't see past the heavyweight of a $1000 past due loan and two months back payroll. A strongman, a bearded lady that doubled as the "unicorn" handler, and three midgets could not put on much show. Fate would deal Nielson a very strange hand indeed. (The scene ends with Nielson spotting the Boston Globe newspaper on the table. The scene fades as we return to the studio) Lance: My grandfather spotted a sports column on boxing written by former lawman Bat Masterson. In it, Masterson mentions that the champion J.L. Sullivan had agreed to fight a "wrestler" by the name of George Hackensmidt. Doing a little research he discovered that many carnivals were now offering wrestling of a "carnie champ" for a price. My grandfather took every last dime he had and bought an ad in the local paper and immediately changed the unicorn logo of the Great Western Carnival into Great Western Wrestling. His strongman, a man by the name of Abraham Dickinson, became his first champion. If that name sounds familiar it should. Dickinson's grandson would later work creative for both the LSWA and the BWF. Bobby: So what became of Great Western Wrestling? Lance: Great Western was a touring company. Grandpa was using his midgets to fight opening matches and drawing big crowds for Dickinson's main events. Then the unthinkable happened. Yellow fever hit the wagons and Great Western stopped touring in Mud Creek, TX. Bobby: Great Western folded then? Lance: Not quite. It was at this time that Devereaux Dubay showed up. Dubay had a lot of money from an old oil well in Mud Creek. He put in for part ownership of the business. With a boost of capital, GWW reopened its doors in a centralized gymnasium in Mud Creek. The Mud Creek Wrestling Alliance was formed and my father was growing up to take his place in the business. Bobby: Which we will see here. (Scene changes to grainy 8 mm film showing a "Russian" fighting a masked hero) Lance: That's Dad when he was working his Ivan Nelsof gimmick. His real name Evan Nelson was easy enough to Russify. He was a big heel against Major Glory, a Captain America type. However, as their feud faded into the 60's, they found their greatest success as the Regal Observers, a masked tag team. Regal I eventually passed away and was buried with his mask on, identity still a secret. The Regal Observer II would change masks and make his own career. The man you know as my father, the original Johnny Thunderbolt. Bobby: So let's talk about how you got into the business. You graduated high school and turned down a scholarship to University of Texas-El Paso. Why? Lance: After seeing oil fields and wrestling gyms for the better part of my 18 years, I wanted as far away from Texas wrestling as I could get. I walked on at the University of Iowa and was enjoying my anonymity. Bobby: Until the pros called? Lance: That's right. I blew my knee out in my pro debut for the Falcons. Bobby: And then? Lance: And then I disappeared. I made a few calls to some guys in Japan and got a trainer. I trained there for two years. When I started making a name for myself, I disappeared again. I went to Mexico and trained in their style. I trained in shootfighting with Dan Severn. Then, I came home to debut in EPCW, El Paso Championship Wrestling, the new and improved Mud Creek. I united the EPCW and MCWA titles in a match against Tully Blanchard. Bobby: And became your Dad's greatest star. Lance: And those were good times. It was when I met Tommy and Dennis. We were great friends but the promoters, including Dad, were forcing us to fight each other to bring in the green. We decided to define our careers in those matches. Bobby: Which brings us to the BWF. Lance: That's right. We had garnered a good following in Texas when I received a call from [name withheld], Devereaux's grandson. He was doing talent work for the BWF in Winnipeg and wanted to broker a development deal with us. In return, I brought my best five boys and made TV. Bobby: And you began making history. The Masked Luchadores Union, the Star Spangled Kid, you and your brothers. Lance: That's right. I even put Mitch under Dad's mask as a tribute to him. Dad finally made it big. I only wished he could have seen it when I won the world title. Bobby: Which brings us to my next question. How do you handle filling your Dad's shoes. Lance: He was an amazing man. I try to be that kind of man when I deal with my brothers. As you know, Mitch suffered a severe back injury that keeps him from wrestling most of the time. He does some road agent work for the indys. He misses the bright lights. And Matt, he's gonna be better than any of us. He wrestled his first match at 16 and one. We don't call him the Prodigy for nothing. Bobby: And Mitch is watching out for him on the road. Lance: Right. Bobby: Tell us about "that night". Lance: I was the longest reigning champion in the BWF's history. And Slash Richter was insanely jealous because he had never carried the belt with the distinction I was able to. So he threatened to quit and embarrass the company if I didn't job the title. So in 18 seconds I lost the belt and damn nearly lost my mind. I left the BWF shortly after and it was a true penthouse to the outhouse story. Within months I was back to living in a trailer park in Mud Creek. I couldn't make rent. I was selling my gear just to eat. And to be honest if it wasn't for you I probably wouldn't be alive. Bobby: We don't have to talk about that. Lance: Yes, we do. (Pulls out the LSWA silver belt) When I was in my darkest days, I gave up the symbol of my heart and soul. I pawned this belt to buy bourbon...and .38 caliber shells. Bobby Ladd found me in the Bar With No Name in Mud Creek and gave me another chance. Bobby: And now you're back. Lance: Back with a goal. To teach the next generation the respect for the tradition of this sport. To teach them to dance with the girl that brought them to the show. Bobby: Which brings us to Desmond Walker. Lance: That's right. Bobby: What do you have to say to him. Lance: What you see on Mayhem is one thing. What you see here is something else. But there is the legitimate truth that I don't care for Walker. He has no love for this sport or dedication to it. He admits that it's a stepping stone to movies and bigger fame. I have only one thing to say to that. Whatever. We don't play that game in Mud Creek, TX. There is nothing transitional about wrestling. It's a cruel and unforgiving mistress, but she is seductive none the less. If its in your blood, you will WRESTLE until you can't do it anymore. So my goal for you Walker is to teach you about this sport. To show you that its more than a cute catchphrase and a pretty face. Its more than fifteen minutes of fame to get you a role on the Happy Days Christmas Reunion special. It's about blood, and tradition, and integrity. And after I'm through with you, you may not like me, but by God you will respect me, the generation I represent, and the sport I love. Bobby: That's about all the time we have for today. But Lance, I want to again warmly thank you for bearing your soul on national television. Lance: It was my pleasure, Bob. Bobby: Tune in next week, fans, when once again Steve Reigns brings you another behind the scenes look at a wrestling superstar's career. For Inside the Squared Circle, I'm Bobby Ladd. Goodnight everybody! (credits roll as Nelson and Ladd shake hands and walk offstage together) http://community.webtv.net/niwfwrestling/NIWFWrestling
