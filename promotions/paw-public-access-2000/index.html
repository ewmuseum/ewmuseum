Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Public Access Wrestling ran its cards out of a small television studio, opening every broadcast on the same red, white and black banner and closing every one under a shared production credit. Two announcers carried the desk from the first card to the last, and a small, tight roster meant the same handful of wrestlers carried nearly every angle across the year.

The promotion's own on-air president doubled as an active competitor, drawing boos and feuds of his own alongside the wrestlers he presided over. That double role ran through the year's first supercard, Primal Rage, and its own follow-up, Back & Bad, weekly cards building a Texas Heavyweight Championship, a tag team championship and a television championship around a cast that barely changed from card to card.

Summer brought a six-part supercard, Summer Burnout, and a widening of the roster; the fall brought a hunt for outside talent that turned into the promotion's most ambitious project yet. By December, a touring Japanese roster and referee had crossed over for a season-closing supercard, Winter's Nightmare: PAW versus Japan, run alongside the promotion's own regular titles and settled in the visiting team's favor.

A weekly recap show carried the fallout into the new year: a champion's status left open, a stalled deal with an outside promotion, and a roster restocked with wrestlers arriving as the touring Japanese team headed home. The promotion's own last surviving card ran one week before the year's end, still running, still recruiting, with no closing notice of its own on record.
