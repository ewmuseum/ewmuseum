A touring fed producing shows under its own "On The Road" banner across 1998, taped that May at a Kansas community-college gymnasium, whose own broadcast names its CEO in kayfabe and later ran a Slim Pickens pay-per-view at Dallas's Sportatorium. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Great Plains Wrestling Alliance's own surviving record opens with no founding notice at all — its earliest document, a touring broadcast called “On The Road,” taped 24 May 1998 at the Garden City Community College Gymnasium in Garden City, Kansas, already presents the promotion as an established operation running under GPWA CEO Roger Stillwell. Commentators Trent Musfurter and Timmy “The Turk” Uyuklu opened the card by plugging the road ahead to a companion show, GPWA Slim Pickens, and the card itself closed its first match with Commander Cody beating “Primetime” Cashmere Harris by pinfall.

The card was built around a chase for the promotion's own first heavyweight title: a Triple Threat between Jack Emerald, Dane Thompson and Violent Librarian was staged to decide who would face Angry Chef for the inaugural Great Plains Heavyweight Championship, while Jimmy Lightning and Billy Thunder defended the Great Plains Tag Team Championship against “The Magnet” Ron Baxter and Hiroki Date.

By the time of its next surviving document, a broadcast jointly produced with ICBINR called “Boiling Point” (7 February 1999), the Great Plains Heavyweight title had already changed hands under a cloud: Tad Detmer held it as champion while Larry Pickens insisted, on air, “Detmer stole that belt” and demanded it back, with the promotion's own front office setting a rematch for a future On The Road card. Thunder and Lightning had lost the tag titles as well, to a team billed as Black and Orange, closing out their own reign with a parting line to the new champions: “We held them titles for a good ten months.” The joint broadcast also carried ICBINR's own United States Heavyweight Champion, Reginald Parkhurst III, introducing himself in full: “I, Reginald Parkhurst III, United States Heavyweight Champion and Master of the Piledriver…”

How the promised rematch for the Great Plains title played out, and how the promotion itself ended, isn't in the surviving record — the February 1999 joint broadcast, served only as its first of four parts, is its own last surviving document.
