Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

DYNASTY announced itself on 10 January 1999 with a teaser for a 32-man Icon Heavyweight Title tournament. Its own Official Handbook, posted three weeks later, filled out the picture: three weekly broadcasts — Legends of Wrestling on Saturday nights on UPN, a Wednesday clip show called Ring Report, and a promo platform, Under the Mic, running five nights a week — and four championships, the Icon Heavyweight, North American, Cruiserweight, and Icon Tag Team titles, built around bi-monthly pay-per-views.

Its own on-screen cast fronted the promotion: owner Terrance Goldsworthy, described in the handbook as stepping in personally whenever something got “so out of line that the commissioner can't handle”; Commissioner Albert Vasquez, who ruled on matches and suspensions; commentators Trevor Hall and Jean Lamont, the latter billed as disliked by three in four fans; and correspondents Wayne Vengeance and Paul Martin.

DYNASTY debuted 27 February 1999 from the Joe Louis Arena in Detroit as, in its own words, “the beginning of a new era.” By April its roster had grown to about twenty wrestlers, prompting an eight- or sixteen-team tournament to crown the first Icon Tag Team Champions, and one of its own, “Sweet as Silver” Midas Runt, opened a feud with “the Diabolical Bastard” Rip Fury ahead of his own Legends of Wrestling debut in San Antonio. A March tournament, the Snake Eyes Gauntlet, crowned “TGDS” Tim Beck as Icon Heavyweight Champion.

DYNASTY closed abruptly on 24 June 1999. Three days later its president announced a relaunch, promising a return card, Parental Advisory I, on 18 July at the Marine Midland Arena in Buffalo: Beck defending his title against the winner of a new eight-man tournament, and a twelve-man battle royal to crown a new North American Champion.

No document survives confirming that return card was ever held.
