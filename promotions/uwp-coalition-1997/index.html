A play-by-mail and e-wrestling coalition hub recognizing five unified inter-fed championships across six or more member federations, whose own ads name a changing champion roster and an annual member-presidents' World Cup Challenge, corroborated as still operating five years after its earliest captured document. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

United Wrestling Promotions billed itself not as a wrestling promotion in its own right but as a coalition, “a group of play-by-mail and e-wrestling games who work together to enhance fantasy wrestling as a whole,” recognizing a shared slate of “Unified” championships any player could challenge for, regardless of whether their own game recognized UWP at all.

An August 1998 ad names its five Unified champions as of 19 July that year: World Champion “Screaming Drillbit” Jason Keening, out of three other e-feds not themselves affiliated with the coalition; Tag Team Champions the Rodeo Galoots (Deputy Jeff Coyote and George Lionheart, of ACE International Wrestling's USA Division); Lightweight Champion Live Wire, of Cyber Mayhem; Six Man Champions the Socialist Wrestling Organization (Thor, Spawn and the Scarlet Surfer, also Cyber Mayhem); and Women's Champion Reigna Kabuki, also of ACE International. A companion ad the same day announced UWP's annual World Cup Challenge, a presidents-only tournament open to any member willing to sanction a qualifying battle royal.

By its own account at the close of 1999, UWP had become “an umbrella organisation” of six federations — three sharing one name across a European and an American branch, plus a fourth on the e-wrestling side, and three more play-by-mail promotions — and staged a joint supercard, December Destination, at Candlestick Park in San Francisco, with outside promotions contributing untelevised dark matches for their own talent.

That card turned on two Unified title changes: Reigna Kabuki, already champion in the coalition's own August 1998 billing, lost the Ladies' title to a challenger named Eve in a bloody fifteen-minute brawl that ended on a spinning spine buster, and World Champion Omega Red — staged as an apocalyptic villain vowing to end “the Atomic Puke's meaningless career” — lost his title to the challenger he was taunting, beaten by a piledriver onto the concrete outside the ring.

No document past 29 December 1999 survives in UWP's own record; how long the coalition, or its Unified championships, continued past that night is not recorded.
