Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The SHOW, in full The Sandy Hook Outlaws of Wrestling, introduced itself through a founding FAQ posted 18 November 2003. Its own words were blunt about its size: “a tiny, tiny fed” with “No TV deal. No huge audiences. No big salaries,” run through taped shows and internet reports on a touring schedule that would flex with “interest and the financials.”

Its owner, Whit McCoy, is staged as a “semi-wealthy business man” who made his money in the coal mines of eastern Kentucky and sank it into a wrestling promotion in his own hometown of Sandy Hook, population 991 — strong locally, “very, very weak” everywhere else. The same FAQ names his staff: announcers Henry Voss and Tony Miracle, interviewers Terry “Gramps” Witzel and Steve Kendrick.

The promotion planned a weekly Monday-night internet recap show, also called The SHOW, running three to five matches told mostly as prose reports weaving promo excerpts into the results, a post-card roleplay show called SHOW Offs, and occasional fully written “supercards” every seven or eight shows. It carried one championship, the Showcase Strap.

Whether The SHOW ever staged a single card isn't in its surviving record, which holds nothing beyond this founding FAQ.
