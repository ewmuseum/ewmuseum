Closing * * * Backround (Story Line) * * * In early 2000, a group of high school students from Brooke High School in Wellsburg, West Virginia started a backyard wrestling federation called Downtown Championship Follansbee Wrestling. The two founders were Adam Johnson and Jimmy Rowing, who were best friends before the venture took place. DCFW grew within it's boundaries of Follansbee and came to be known as the place to be for teenagers on Saturday afternoons. After almost a year of profit from charging to get into the shows, Adam and Jimmy had an idea. Why not go a step farther and actually try to make a federation known through-out the Ohio Valley. Thus, the Ohio Valley Wrestling League was formed. Jimmy Rowing's father was a loan agent at a local bank and was easily able to talk him into a 3,000 dollar loan. This money would buy an old warehouse in downtown Wellsburg, West Virginia, a wrestling ring, and some old television camera and lights. All the wreslters from DCFW would come into the old warehouse every Sunday and fight in front of crowds no larger than a hundred. Eventually, near May of 2001, things begin to blossom. They were making more money than they ever expected and wrestlers from all over the Valley were coming to compete in the league. Adam and Jimmy knew it was time to take a even a step farther and go for the big show; a television contract, tours, and large fan bases. This would only come one step at a time. And, this is where we leave off; the rest is in the future, nobody knows what is going to happen. * * * Backround (Realistic) * * * The Ohio Valley Wrestling League was founded on April 15, 2001 by [name withheld]. Brad, being a member of the real life Downtown Championship Follansbee Wrestling, thought it would be interesting to run a story line off of it, like the one above. For interested people, the OVWL will be ran to the point where it has to slowly build up. Nothing major at first; just the basic needs like a ring, lights, sound systems, fans, and you, the wreslters. It is up to _YOU_ to decide what is going to happen with the OVWL. The OVWL is also be ran like a small fed with wrestlers who aren't the "best." Matches in the cards probably won't go pass 9-10K. Except for PPV's, where the wrestlers will give it their all with matches up to 12-15K. This is good since Brad is the only one running things and won't get burnt out writing extremely long matches. * * * Meet The Staff (Real Life) * * * 1. [name withheld] - Brad is the President, Co-President, Math Writer, Head Booker, and everything else that it takes to run a fed. You can contact him for anything you need. If you want to plan an angle, e-mail him or message him. If you just want to talk for the hell of it, message him or e-mail him. * * * Meet The Staff (Story Line) * * * 1. Adam Johnson - Co-President. A chubby 18 year old, with very high speaking skills and isn't afraid to tell people how it is. He can be your best-friend or your worse enemy. 2. Jimmy Rowing - Co-President. A nicely built 19 year old that is kind of slow in the head but has what it takes to win people over. He is strikingly funny with fat wit and good comebacks. He is usually good friends with everybody; it is hard to make him mad. 3. Dan Brown - The head play-by-play annoucer. A very short 17 year old who has a very big mouth that he can't back up. The fans love him, but the wreslters hate him. Dan has an impressive amount of knowledge towards wrestling. Dan is usually considered the heel annoucer. 4. Zane Harvey - Zane is pretty much Dan's second hand man. Zane is the youngest of the bunch, only 16 years old. But, he is also a 4.0 student and can quickly prove you wrong with anything you say. You don't want to mess with this kid. Zane is generally considered the face annoucer. 5. Amanda Chambers - The ring annoucer. A tall, very thin hottie. She is the oldest of the bunch and takes care of all the salaries and behind the scene business with Adam and Jimmy. She is currently enrolled to attend Yale University. 6. Walter Ramsey - The fat guy of the bunch, who serves as the backstage interviewer. He is the one who handles most of the in-rings and everything like that. * * * Broadcasts * * * a. Past Enemy Lines - Past Enemy Lines is the weekly flash/roleplay show that will be, usually, sent out on Saturday afternoons. b. Code Red Sunday - The weekly show that OVWL puts on. This is the big show that you don't want to miss. c. Pay Per Views - The OVWL won't be putting on Pay Per Views at first. To tell you the truth, our first Pay Per View will take place, when I feel, we are ready to tour the state which won't be too long of a wait if things go right. * * * Championships * * * a. Ohio Valley Champion - The big one; the one everybody aims for. If you hold this, you're considered the best wrestler in the OVWL. b. City Champion - This belt is usually held by new comers into the OVWL before they make the next step and move into the scene for the Ohio Valley Championship. c. Tag Team Champions - Pretty much self explanatory, don't 'ya think? * * * Roleplaying Tips * * * The OVWL does not impose a limit on the number of flashes from each character per week. _HOWEVER_, chances are if you are scheduled for action and don't send in a flash, you won't be getting a check mark in the win column. Anyone who fails to produce flashes regularly, or remains silent for long periods of time, will be ejected from the league. In actual flashes and role-plays, swearing should be kept to a minimum. Unless absolutely necessary (and by that, agreement on the management's part is required), nothing stronger than ass should be said. If necessary, use [Bleep] in place of most swearing. Nothing that could get a show kicked off the air by the FCC should be used (racism, constant swearing, etc.). Wrestlers are _NOT_ permitted to physically attack any and all officials, ring announcers, television announcers or interview personalities _UNLESS_ you receive permission. Stylized text is _NOT_ used in the OVWL. One exclamation point is usually enough, and adding extra ones will only hold up the shows as they're edited out anyway. * * * Strategies * * * Strategies, or strats for short, are needed in order to win matches. It's up to you which kind of strategy you send in, a long drawn out one or a simple paragraph or two. "I wanna win, 'cause it'd be cool," is _NOT_ a strategy. Focus on your opponent's weakness, and explain how your wrestler plans to wrestle against them. Failure to send in strats for long periods of time will result in a league ejection, much like non-flashing wrestlers. * * * Angles/Match Booking * * * The mark of a good league is how well it's storylines and angles are thought out. Angles are what determines in most cases how and what matches are scheduled for the week. Flashes and strategies are dependent on good angles, otherwise proposed attacks may not be run unless they're for a specific purpose.The easiest way to get matches: ask for them! Sending in challenges to other wrestlers are looked favorably on, and in most cases will be scheduled whenever possible. Again angles come into play, and those challenges that seem to "make sense" will be given preference in schedule dating. Title matches usually are given to those who prove themselves in past matches, and simply asking for a title match without reason (or storyline) won't get you one, guaranteed. * * * Singles Application (Send All Application to [detail withheld] ) * * * * Handler Information * Your Name: E-Mail Address: AIM/ICQ/IRC: Can you write matches?: [ ] Yes [ ] Rarely [ ] Never Character Fed Experience: Your Fed Experience: Do you care if I write angle furthering vignetts for your character?
