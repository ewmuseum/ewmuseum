Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Ohio Valley Wrestling League opened in June 1999 out of a converted warehouse in Wellsburg, West Virginia. Its own information packet stages the promotion's birth as the work of a wrestler burned out on the “politics” of the bigger federations he had passed through, who watched a small, unscripted-feeling show convince him wrestling “should be” fought for its own sake rather than staged for stars, then “sold all his earthly possessions to buy a warehouse, and a ring” and restored the building himself with a few friends. He wrestled there as well as running it.

That packet, dated 10 June 1999, planned the promotion's growth in stages: house shows first, passed hand to hand on fans' own camcorder tapes, then a public-access show, then syndication, then, eventually, pay-per-views “once every 3 or 4 months.” No title would be contested before any of that: “The Ohio Valley Champion will not be crowned until we hit Television.” Title matches, once they existed, would run no-disqualification by design, so a champion could never walk out on a defense and keep the belt.

Its one surviving card, Battle At The Warehouse, ran 13 June 1999: a nine-match lineup that included a casket match between Grim Reaper and Paradox and a no-disqualification, falls-count-anywhere bout, closing on a gauntlet main event pitting “Superstar” Sean Copeland alone against three opponents, Chris Douglas, “The Dominator” Dom Abbriano, and Brett Matthewz — with its own promoter promising Copeland a stipulation match against himself if the three-man team lost, and a built-in scoring handicap for writing against three opponents at once.

No result for that match, or any further card, survives in its record.
