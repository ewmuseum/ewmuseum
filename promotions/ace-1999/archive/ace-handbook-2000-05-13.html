Introduction American Championship E-wrestling was opened on July 4, 1999, by President Kyle Humber and Vice President Chris Boyett. However, after only a month, the league ran into financial trouble and it was forced to close.
In May, 2000, successful Memphis businessman Scott Hunter, 35, known as "The Kid," resurrected the ACE, which he had recently bought out. Knowing little about wrestling, he hired Brett James, a longtime wrestling booker, as his commissioner. Hunter and James re-opened the
ACE for business on May 12, 2000. The ACE is an original-wrestlers only, booked fed. This means that your success is determined by your roleplaying, character development, and angles.
We are e-mail-based, and we use a closed-flash system. Roleplays are sent to the President, and they are sent out in roleplay packets, usually two or three times a week.
2. Staff Real staff-The actual staff consists solely of one [name withheld]. Mr. [name withheld] can be reached at [detail withheld] , or on AOL Instant Messenger as [detail withheld]. Fake staff: Scott "The Kid" Hunter, President and Owner-At 35 years young, Hunter, a millionaire in the insurance business, re-opened the ACE in May 2000. He knows little about wrestling, so he leaves most of the wrestling duties to Brett James. However, he knows everything there is to know about business, so he runs much of the business on his own.
Brett James, Commissioner-James, 37, a longtime behind-the-scenes booker, was hired by Hunter as the commissioner. James appears on camera very often, and is often on the spot to sign big matches and the like. He is a persistent man, and always gets what he-and the fans-want.
Tommy Daniels, Commentator-Daniels, 42, serves as the main commentator. He calls it as he sees it, and he is the main one calling the action. However, he sometimes tends to side with the faces. Jimmy Quince, Color Commentator-Quince, 27, a former radio talk-show host, is breaking into the wrestling business. He does not know a lot of the terminology, but he provides tidbits on the wrestlers. He also tends to side with the heels, and enjoys a good street fight.
Michael Tiller, Ring Announcer-Tiller, 39, is known to ACE fans simply as "The Mike." He is known for his loud, drawn-out ring announcements.
However, he has been known to say whatever the wrestlers tell him to about them. :) Ryan O'Brien, Interviewer-O'Brien, 45, is a former reporter on TV-13 in Memphis. He knows how to get an interview and asks all the right questions. Amber Adams, Interviewer-Adams, 24, is a former college cheerleader who used a phony major in communications to get through school. She is now on the air in the ACE, though she would probably be better if she said less.
Jimbo Bradley, Head of Security-Bradley, 33, a former police officer, is called in whenever the wrestlers get out of control. He brings his security squad with him.
Kelvin Blake, Head Referee-Blake, 41, has been a referee for 15 years, and has seen every trick in the book. He is always right there for the
three-count and calls it right down the middle. He is not intimidated by the wrestlers, and gets in the face of wrestlers who mess with him.
Stan Schmidt, Referee-Schmidt, 34, an import from Berlin, lays down justice the simple way. At 6'2" and with a muscular build, Schmidt WILL kick your ass. Because of this, he rarely calls for a DQ, but he's there for the three-count, which is usually fair.
Peter Green, Referee-Green, 25, is the rookie on the job. He is easily intimidated and/or bribed, so his count is not always fair. He is also fairly quick to call for a DQ.
Charlie Pitsky, "Hardcore" Referee-Pitsky, 37, is the referee who is called in when two wrestlers who really want to settle their score go at it. He is really there just to count the pin.
3. Titles ACE World Heavyweight Title-The big one, this title is the one that every man in the ACE is shooting for. Its holder must defend it once a month on television and at every pay-per-view. The top three wrestlers in the league, as well as the other champions, may challenge for it.
ACE Television Title-The secondary title, this title is more competitive than the World Title. It must be defended every week, as well as at every pay-per-view. Any wrestler in the league may challenge for it.
ACE World Light Heavyweight Title-The title for light heavyweights, or those wrestlers 225 pounds and under. It must be defended twice a month on television, as well as at every pay-per-view. The top five light heavyweight contenders may challenge for it.
ACE World Tag Team Titles-The only titles for tag teams. These must be defended once a month on television and on every pay-per-view. Any tag team may challenge for them.
*The champion may defend his title more than the required amount if he so chooses.* If there is enough interest in the league, we may eventually add an American Title as a secondary title. 4. Rankings The ACE's system of rankings determines who can challenge for titles. There are three divisions: the World division(all wrestlers), the Light Heavyweight division(only light heavyweights), and the Tag Team division(only regular tag teams.)
First of all, recent performance is given major consideration. In a wrestler's last 7 matches, a win is given 3 points, but he will lose 3 points for a loss.
(NOTE: Matches against jobbers do not count toward this, and for the sake of simplicity, a jobber is any wrestler specifically hired by the front office.)
For all other matches, a wrestler earns 1 point for a win and loses 1 point for a loss. An example: Two wrestlers both have records of 22-7. However, wrestler #1 has a 3-4 record in his most recent matches. This is worth 9-12, or - 3 points. His prior record was 19-3, worth 16 points. So wrestler #1 has 13 points. Wrestler #2, on the other hand, has won his last seven matches, earning him 21 points. His prior record was 15-7, worth 8 points. So wrestler #2 has 29 points.
In addition, former champions have an advantage. A former World champion(within the past 6 months) gains 15 points, while a former
Television champion gains 5 points. 5. Shows The ACE has two weekly shows. American Standard-The ACE's main weekly show, which is broadcast on Monday nights. It airs from 10 PM-Midnight Central time on TNN. It generally features 5-7 matches, as well as interviews with the ACE's stars.
Friday Night Fury-The ACE's second weekly show, Fury is syndicated to stations nationally. It generally features 6-10 matches featuring the
ACE's newer wrestlers and mid-carders. It may also have a couple of interviews. This show is written in summary format.
The Deal-ACE's roleplay show, which comes out two or three times a week. It features roleplays by the wrestlers of the ACE. Pay-Per-Views: The ACE will only have one pay-per-view every three months. Its pay-per-views will take place in June, September, December, and
March. 6. Roleplaying Guidelines First of all, roleplaying is the single most important part of the league. Your success in the league will be determined by roleplaying(or flashing), strategies, character development, and angles.
Most important are character development and angles. Handlers who come up with creative angles and develop their characters well in their roleplays and interviews will have much more success than handlers who roleplay pointlessly; in other words, I am not impressed by 100 lines of garbage.
Roleplaying should be done to develop your character or further an angle. You must roleplay once a week. Inactive wrestlers will be jobbed and eventually fired.
Angles, or storylines, are the backbone of the league. In some cases, I will create angles, but you are encouraged to create your own angles as well. All matches will have something to do with an angle, so there will be no pointless matches on the cards.
Strategies are a great way to make sure that what you'd like to get done during the match gets done. They won't necessarily help your chance of winning the match, but they don't hurt, and they help me greatly in writing the match.
