Introduction Before July 1999, [name withheld] had had several unsuccessful tries at running e-feds. But this one, American Championship E-wrestling, or ACE as we like to call it, would not fail, he told himself. He hired two people to help him, and here we go with the ACE. Remember, everybody, just have some fun in the world of e-wrestling.

Cards ACE has two cards live, televised cards a week, Tuesday Night Torture and Friday Night Fury. TUESDAY NIGHT TORTURE-Our Tuesday night card. It is our secondary card, similar to Thunder or Heat. It will usually feature 5-8 matches, and is in full, live-commentary format. It will have some interviews, but there will not often be title matches on the show.
FRIDAY NIGHT FURY-Our Friday night card, as well as our main card. It features 7-10 matches on most nights, and is also in full-commentary format. It may have a few interviews, and there will be some title matches on the show. HOUSE SHOWS-ACE will occasionally have house shows. These are written in summary format. (Note: The main way to get matches is to challenge. While the upper- card matches on Fury will usually be signed by the front office, low- and mid-card matches on Fury and all matches on Torture are open for challenges.
Unfilled spots will be filled a few days before the show.) 4. Pay-Per-Views ACE has six pay-per-view events per year. They happen every other month. Wild Card(August)-This pay-per-view features an eight-man tournament. Seven men will be determined before the event. The eighth will not be known until the night of the event.
Wrestlefest(October)-The feature of this event is a sort of tournament, in which sixteen men are divided into four brackets, with the winners meeting in a four-way match.
Countdown to Armageddon(December)-The main event of this pay-per- view is an Inferno match.
Valentine's Day Death Penalty(February)-This card's main event is a Death Match. All-Out War(April)-This card features the infamous "War Games" match inside a steel cage. Survival(June)-The feature bout of this card is a Royal Rumble-style match, with the winner usually getting a World Title shot.
5. Titles ACE has three singles titles and the Tag Team Titles for the time being. ACE World Title-The highest belt in the league. The holder of this title must defend his title once a month on TV and at each pay-per-view. A wrestler must be among the top five contenders to challenge for the belt, although the champion can put the belt on the line against anyone. Just think of it this way-if the champion gives you a title match, it doesn't matter if you're one of the top contenders.
ACE North American Title-The second-highest belt in the league. The holder of this belt has all rights to a World Title shot any time he wants it. For this reason, it is often used as a stepping stone to the World
Title. It must be defended twice a month on TV and at each pay-per-view. A wrestler must be among the top ten contenders to challenge for it. ACE Television Title-A title designed for newcomers and mid-carders. Any man in the federation can challenge for it, regardless of ranking. The champion must defend it each week on Friday Night Fury, and at every pay-per-view. For this reason, the holder of the belt must be a
"fighting champion," so to speak. ACE World Tag Team Titles-The sole tag team belts in ACE at this time. Any team may challenge for these belts(this is to allow two singles wrestlers to team up and go for the belts.) They must be defended once a month on TV and at every pay-per-view.
(Notes about titles: First of all, the holder of a title is allowed to defend the title more than the required number of times if he wants to.
Second, we don't have a set rankings system. Instead, the top ten contenders are determined by how well you participate in the league, i.e. sending flashes, the quality of your flashes, etc.)
6. Roleplaying a. Flashes Flashes are the bulk of roleplaying in this league. They will generally be sent out in flash shows, along with the other flashes of the day. We have a few rules pertaining to flashes, so please read carefully.
1. We request that you flash at least twice a week. It is only required that you flash once a week to remain active, though. 2. We have a "fourteen-day rule" pertaining to firings. Once seven days have passed and you haven't flashed you will be warned. After five more days you will be given your notice that not flashing withing 48 hours will result in your termination from the league. Finally, after fourteen days you will receive your pink slip. (Oh, and by the way, in the time you're not flashing you will be jobbed to hell and back.)
3. We request that all flashes be at least 20 lines in length. This is not being strict, and it is not a fine line(we may let it slide if it's just a shade under the limit.) This is to prevent people from sending five- line flashes.
4. We're looking for quality, not quantity. We're not fooled by a 90-line flash that has no meaning. 5. You may only do one flash per day. You can do as many as you want as long as you do not exceed this limit, but just remember that sending in six flashes that are exactly the same will probably result in a loss. Think of it like this: if you send in five flashes and your opponent sends one, but his one flash is better than all five of yours, then he will win.
6. Try to keep cursing to a minimum. If you're unsure about whether or not you can say something, follow one simple rule: if you can't say it on TV, don't say it here. If Stone Cold can't say it, then you can't say it either. All words that are unfit for TV must be censored, and I mean more than "f*ck." You must do something like [beep] or [censored]. We have zero tolerance for racial comments.
7. You are not allowed to use other wrestlers in a flash without their handler's permission. This means that he must send his approval to the President and Vice President before the flash is sent out. 8. You can't beat up cameramen, interviewers, or other ACE officials in your flashes. It gets kind of tiresome, and besides I've got to pay a hazard check for them. :)

Strategies Now, before I get to this section, let me remind you that as in many feds, strategies do not affect the outcome of a match. They only help the writers, which is why we request that you do them. Here is the format for a strategy.
ENTRANCE Tell us how you want your wrestler to enter, including theme music, pyros, actions, etc. You can also possibly have him take the microphone before the match and have him say something, much like Konnan's catchphrases before a match.
MATCH This is the bulk of the strategy. It tells us what you want your wrestler to do during the match, including what kind of moves he will use, etc.
POST-MATCH Tell us what your wrestler will do after the match. Give us descriptions of his post-match behavior in the case that he wins, as well as if he loses.
ANYTHING ELSE? If you want to interfere in a match, tell us here. Also tell us any other things you'd like to have, like a special appearance or something happening backstage.
c. On-card Interviews Tuesday Night Torture and Friday Night Fury will each feature some interviews. These are the kind of interviews you usually see on TV, such as when Hogan comes out for his mic time or when Mean Gene interviews someone backstage.
On-card interviews MUST be in one of two forms: (1)an in-ring interview in which your wrestler makes his way to the ring, takes the microphone, and does an interview, or (2)a locker-room interview, which may or may not be conducted by our interviewer, Ryan Ross.
We have limited space on cards for interviews, so you must tell me ahead of time that you want an interview.

King Silver-Our resident Luchador jobber. Don't be fooled by his stocky physique. The Old Age In-Laws-The tag team jobbers of ACE. 8. Creating a Wrestler a. Rules of Creation 1. First and foremost, we only allow original wrestlers, and no ripoffs. This means no "Stone Cold" Kevin Aston's or no wrestlers named "The Rock." 2. Your wrestler may not be more than 7'3" or 500 lbs. (Seriously, how many people do you know of who are really that big?) 3. On the other end, your wrestler may not be less than 5'4" or 140 lbs. Rey Misterio Jr. is the smallest wrestler I know of who isn't considered a midget. 4. Your wrestler may not have a style that doesn't go with his weight class. No 300-pound high flyers, or 200-pound powerhouses. 5. Your wrestler must have a real hometown or "Parts Unknown." Places that don't exist on Earth can't be used. (i.e., your wrestler can't be from Hell or Uranus.) b. Application You've gotten to this part. Now fill this sheet out and send it to [name withheld] and [name withheld] .
