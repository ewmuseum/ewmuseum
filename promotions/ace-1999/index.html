A twice-weekly Usenet federation founded in the summer of 1999, running a six-pay-per-view yearly schedule and a four-championship title ladder behind a mix of real and in-fiction staff. Six wrestlers are recorded from its founding handbook; no card or result survives. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

American Championship E-wrestling's surviving record is its own Handbook Version 1.0, posted 7 July 1999. It frames itself as its own founder's latest attempt after “several unsuccessful tries at running e-feds” — this one, he told himself, would not fail — and names two further staff he hired to help him run it.

On screen, ACE staged itself with its own separate cast: a president, Kyle Humber, and vice president, Chris Boyett, called games by an announcing team of Lenny Baker (face) and Will Porter (heel), with Rebecca Freeman as ring announcer and Ryan Ross as interviewer.

It ran two weekly televised cards, Tuesday Night Torture and Friday Night Fury, and laid out six pay-per-views across the year: an eight-man Wild Card tournament in August, a sixteen-man, four-bracket Wrestlefest in October, an Inferno match headlining December's Countdown to Armageddon, a Death Match for February's Valentine's Day Death Penalty, a War Games cage main event for April's All-Out War, and a Royal Rumble-style Survival card in June feeding its winner a World Title shot.

Four championships were named — the World, North American and Television singles titles, plus the World Tag Team Titles — each with its own defense schedule and contender rules, though the handbook set no wrestler against any of them: no roster had yet signed, and no reign or match survives.

How those plans played out isn't in the surviving record; its last surviving document is a further recruitment notice, dated 5 August 1999.
