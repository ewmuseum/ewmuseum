[CEW] - The Fed Disappeared, But It Wasn't Gone...

26 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
After a nine-and-a-half year history, Cyber Extreme Wrestling disappeared without any explanation. Days ago, with just as little explanation, the federation returned, as if it had never left. What happened, and how did it survive? PITTSBURGH, PA – Since 1994, Cyber Extreme Wrestling has been around in one form or another. Chris Powers' Hardcore Championship Wrestling and Jeff Atkinson's National Wrestling Federation were two feds typical of the late '90s federation battles. HCW lost the battle, but won the war, as Powers' showed up years later as part-owner of CEW (which had evolved from the NWF), to the shock of Atkinson and the entire wrestling world. Atkinson disappeared after some time, and CEW thrived. Superstars swelled the CEW ranks. Bloodbath, Konvicted Felon, Kadeem Jones, Phobos, Scott Slade, Sledge, and Wes Slater are just some of the names to have hoisted the World Title above their heads. AC is a five time CEW World Champion. Top Gun (now known as Mark Knight) has held every singles' belt in CEW history. 'Showtime' Nick Alexander has, throughout his career, ended the career of many CEW wrestlers, including, it was thought at one time, the career of AC. Muzzolini, leader of Murder Inc. and a master at mind games, has held the Tag Titles on three occasions and is currently the CEW World Champion. For years CEW has been a federation for the 'smarts.' CEW was not the federation for the average television viewer. It didn't happen week in and week out for the world to see on network or even cable, instead being shown in uncensored glory on late-night HBO television. CEW was the type of federation that a lot of true wrestling fans knew about, and some even watched, while it passed under the radar screen of the average viewer who didn't frequent Project E-Fed, EWNews, or EWPlanet. But, even after close to 10 years of history, Cyber Extreme Wrestling was still considered a lesser federation by most American fans. The CEW still hadn't reached the levels of the fWos, IWOs, 21ws, and OSEs of the world. In late 2002 and early 2003, things were beginning to change. CEW had invaded and made noise in both Fedwars and SEI. Powers had re-created HCW, but the attempted invasion was repelled. The 100th episode of Thrill aired, with AC taking the main event victory over Donnie Decosta, the only NWF legend AC had not previously defeated. CEW was on the annual road to Retribution, the federation's second-largest PPV. Suddenly, it all stopped. The web site was replaced with a 404 error, without any explanation. No one answered or returned phone calls made to CEW headquarters. Wrestling media were without press releases and information for CEW events. News was scarce, but as far as the wrestling world was concerned, the company known as CEW simply disappeared. But the federation did not. A closed-door, wrestlers-only meeting in late February made sure of that. The wrestlers, for the first time since the battle against HCW nearly split the company, had to set aside their personal and professional differences, and unite ... as one. And unite they did. The roster banded together and decided to keep CEW alive. Tim Weich agreed, and stayed on as head booker, making the decisions and keeping the cards in order. James Everett III used his own bank account to pay expenses, paying for arenas, concessions, transportation, and television production. At first, the group was only concerned with filling out dates that had already been set. To the surprise of everyone involved, not a date was missed. No fans asked for refunds on missed house shows. No weeks of HBO programming went without Thrill in its Tuesday late night timeslot. No PPVs were cancelled or even postponed. No fans, or obligations, were left unsatisfied. While some on the roster were lost during the last few months, the core group remained. AC, SNA, Muzzolini, Mark Knight, Kris eGo, Buzzsaw, and Descendent all continued to put their bodies on the line, even when they weren't sure from where or when their paychecks would come. Cassandra Avery came out of obscurity to capture three titles at the same time, a feat never before accomplished in CEW history. As a group, they steamrolled through week after week of Thrill, reached a fever-pitch at Unleashed and peaked at Houston's Astrodome with the 2003 edition of Retribution. It's been nearly two months since Retribution, and the annual epic, Rage in the Cage, looms on the horizon for Cyber Extreme Wrestling. Things have started to go right again. Muzzoini will defend his title at High Impact against Descendent, "Showtime" Nick Alexander, and Da*Link*Went. AC gets Shayne Gowers in a return match from Retribution. And new blood is just beginning to look to CEW for employment, including a rumored potential superstar currently under developmental contract. In February the CEW roster decided that the federation that they had bled for would not die. Instead, it has grown. CEW Entertainment has been created, returning to the public forum with not only a new moniker, but new leadership, new members, a new lease on life, and a new focus. 2003 is already half over, with 2004 already rapidly approaching. The past nine and a half years have been full of ups and downs for Cyber Extreme Wrestling. What will the future hold? As always, only time will tell. Cyber Extreme Wrestling
