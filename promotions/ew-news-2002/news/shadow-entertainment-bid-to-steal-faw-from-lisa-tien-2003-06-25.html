Shadow Entertainment Bid To Steal FAW From Lisa Tien

25 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Shadow Entertainment has joined the running to buy Furiously Addictive Wrestling from its current ownership. There is a now a bidding war between Lisa Tien and Shadow Entertainment for the rights to the brand we know as Furiously Addictive Wrestling. Shadow Entertainment’s representatives arrived in New York on Tuesday for discussions about the availability of Furiously Addictive Wrestling – and the company could discover as early as next week whether or not they have landed the rights to FAW in a deal reportedly worth $18.9m dollars. FAW Administrators Aaron Lepley and Shaun Reyes want the future of FAW decided quickly so the federation can make the right steps to recovery. FAW spokesperson Adam Hutton said: “We will talk with Shadow Entertainment’s representatives on Wednesday, and negotiations will continue throughout the week and over the course of the weekend.” “We wants talks to be done in a calm and formal way out of respect for the parties involved. This is, after all, the future of Furiously Addictive Wrestling, and any decision should not be rushed under any circumstances.” The rights to the FAW brand now seems a straight fight between Lisa Tien and Shadow Entertainment, who have both worked with each other in the past, most notably within IOW. Who are Shadow Entertainment? Shadow Entertainment are a worldwide live entertainment company worth $4bn, which owns venues and media channels in 35 countries. Shadow Entertainments had a brief stint in the Professional-wrestling world with Innovation of Wrestling. Shadow Entertainment have represented some of the biggest names in Professional wrestling through the Innovation of Wrestling later renamed Icons of Wrestling, – Chris Mezz, Mike Extreme, Great Dragon and Dave Gibson. But it is much, much bigger than that. Owned by Lance J. Sillerman, a publicity-shy New Yorker, it has been described as the "cookie monster" of the entertainment industry because of its policy of buying up smaller wrestling companies. FAW: Furiously Addictive Wrestling
