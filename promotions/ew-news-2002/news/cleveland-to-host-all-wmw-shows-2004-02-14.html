Cleveland to host all WMW shows

14 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Once a regional company, Wrestling Midwest toured Canada, Mexico and Australia. Now, back in the midwest, a purchase was made to keep WMW in Cleveland, for good. Ink now resides on the once clean dotted line as Cleveland signs a contract to keep Wrestling Midwest in-house. WMW Owner Brad Johnson and the City of Cleveland signed a contract late Friday, February 13th on the purchase of the old Cleveland Convocation Center. “We’re calling it the WMW Arena,” said Johnson. “Cleveland wanted to sign us to make sure we stayed here, and brought our product to our fans here, and our fans from afar to here. Together, we purchased the arena.” Mayor Jane Campbell said, “At a time when Cleveland needs it most, it’s imperative to bring in business that attracts, not only a local audience, but also international tourism into the city.” “We’ve been negotiating for months,” said Brad Johnson, WMW owner, “but, after coming back over the Holidays we knew that this was the right decision.” WMW spent the entire holidays at local Cleveland hospitals and at Tower City, putting on free shows, signing autographs and even singing Christmas Carols. Noah Hanson even dressed up as Santa and passed out presents at Rainbows Babies and Children’s Hospital while others read stories. “It’s a good feeling when you know you can give back to the community that helped you get started,” said Johnson. “Now, we are partnering up together for this new venture.” The new venture being from now on, all Wrestling Midwest will take place at WMW Arena, in Cleveland, Ohio.
