Fans Rave Over WMW's Award Show Rumble

13 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
WMW promised an award show like no other, and boy did they deliver. The main event shocked many, but pleased all that tuned in. "We wanted an awards show that would shock everyone that tuned in, and leave a lasting impression in their mind," remarked WMW Owner, Brad Johnson. Well, if all they wanted to do was shock the public, they got their money's worth and more. After announcing the huge 35-man rumble for a main event, WMW was forced to deliver. And boy did they ever. After a really extravagant awards ceremony itself, the huge match was underway, and for over an hour it captured all the fans' hearts, the attention of critics, and the attention of many new viewers as well. "It was the first WMW show I've ever seen," said first-time fan, Ian Robertson, "but now I'm hooked." "Fans want something new, you know?" replied Johnson. "You can't invite 30,000 fans to a simple award ceremony anymore. Sure you can please them enough with a few stale jokes, and cheap wine; but why not wow them with an early match of the year candidate for next year?" Match of the Year candidate? If this match is simply only a candidate, one can only wonder what else WMW has in store for this year, a year that is already starting to look pretty impressive for the company. Let's also not forget that WMW had a huge risk involved in making this match. They advertised more than ever before, and were dealing with the returns of old superstars and the debuts of new ones. "In a situation like that, the company takes a big risk," said Johnson. "With returns, you always have the chance that they might not show up. It's a one time deal, so if they don't show, it's not like they're fired, and nobody every knows how well a rookie is going to perform." The show, which aired originally Saturday night, has already been rerun twice with similar turnouts in Pay Per View buy rates. The match results themself aren't being announced at the request of Brad Johnson. The show is being rerun once more, and plans for it to come out on VHS and DVD are already in the works. Wrestling Midwest
