Why Lee Clark Will NEVER win and NWC Title

14 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Brain-Typing allows Cade Clouds to explain why Lee Clark can't win the big one, and why Chance McMillian, not Ryan Stephens, will lead the Polar Opposites to the Atlantic Tag Titles. Why Lee Clark will NEVER win an NWC World Title? “Starman” Lee Clark is one of the greatest NWC wrestlers EVER. He was NWC’s 17th best wrestler last year. He has numerous regional level titles to boast, but with countless NWC title shots, he’s yet to win the big one and it all makes sense now thanks to brain typing. Lee Clark is an INFJ (Introversion, iNtuitive, Feeling, Judging) or an “Inventor” in regards to brain typing. He’s self-confident; independent, reserved, single-minded, conceptual; seeks knowledge; not impressed with authority; determined, analytic, stubborn, skeptical, scientific but none of those things make him a champion. The big match has become a gut wrenching bungie jump with no harness for Lee Clark and it’s mostly because he’s a feeler and not a thinker. As a feeler Clark considers the disappointment of losing and the euphoria of winning. He’s subject to the emotion of the moment and it effects him in big matches because he’s thinking about how to overcome previous mistakes, a definite distraction. When asked if Lee could learn to shake off an errant move and move on like a thinker would, The brain typing Dr. said, “Doubtful, your brain is your brain, it’s hard to change.” Why Chance McMillian will carry the Polar Opposites! Chance McMillian and Ryan Stephens are truly polar opposites, on two totally different ends of the proverbial spectrum; however, their brain types have merely one major difference. It’s that difference that leads me to believe that Chance McMillian, not the veteran Stephens, will lead the Polar Opposites to the NWC Atlantic Tag Titles. McMillian is an ESTP while Stephens is an ESFP. Those of you unfamiliar with brain typing and have no idea what I’m talking about just bare with me. E = Extraversion S= Sensing F= Thinking T= Feeling P= Perceiving Both McMillian and Stephens are energy-expending, loud, vocal guys making them extraverts. Both McMillian and Stephens are use their five senses, and rely on concrete, actual and realistic things making them sensers. Both McMillian and Stephens both are universal and adaptable making them perceivers. Chance is a thinker while Stephens is a feeler. Chance wrestles with emotion and compassion. Chance lays it all there. He grasps the things he should do in order to win, as opposed to the logical and systematic approach Stephens takes in the ring. Stephens extravert personality does make him a fiery fighter, but when it comes right down to it, when the match is on the line Stephens is aware of that fact. Stephens has thoughts in the back of his mind, “Will I fail?” while Chance doesn’t have those thoughts. Mistakes from earlier in the match don’t affect Chance, they’re out of his head by the time the next move is performed, while Stephens’ mistakes linger with him, and he asks himself “what if?” There is no doubt that the Polar Opposites are one of the top eight or ten tag teams in the National Wrestling Council, but perhaps it’s Chance that leads the team, though most consider it to be the veteran, Stephens. Their brain types categorize them as an “Opportunist” and an “Entertainer” both brain types no doubt successful for the world of wrestling, but in the clutch time it’s the “Opportunist” Chance who the Polar Opposites need in the ring! [NWC] - National Wrestling Council
