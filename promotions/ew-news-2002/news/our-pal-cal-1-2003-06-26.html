Our Pal Cal #1

26 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Too often, people resort to using PCW as the butt of poorly-executed jokes because: 1) we ignored them, and 2) we were designated for that purpose because the more closely-knit federations didn't want to step on each other's toes and damage their working relationship. Too often, people resort to using PCW as the butt of poorly-executed jokes because: 1) we ignored them, and 2) we were designated for that purpose because the more closely-knit federations didn't want to step on each other's toes and damage their working relationship. To say that this was a bit unfair is something of an understatement. We were neither informed about being designated for this purpose in the first place, and we were expected to just sit there and take it because those lobbing the insults saw PCW as lower than they were (and as such, should have been grateful for any attention it received even if it was bad). We stood back and were gentlemen and gentlewomen about it, leaving these people alone because we had more class than to get into a playground war of words with people who obviously hadn't matured past the 5th grade. I'm glad to say that for the most part, that trend has died down. People have grown up and stuck to their own affairs, existing on a live and let live mentality. You stopped giving us free advertisement, and we didn't bother you. You were happy. We were happy. We had our respective spheres of influence and stayed out of each other's business. But now, a resurgence of PCW-bashing has sprung forth in the form of a man living in the basement of his mother's house. And now, we're not talking about Aimz's stalker. No, I'm talking about one Mick P.. And this isn't your run-of-the-mill verbal attacks on PCW, this is worse. He has made it his mission to soil the reputation of PCW. This is something of an oxymoron in his case because he doesn't have a high opinion of us. Then again, if we were such an easy target, taking as many jabs at us as he does would hardly be either original, or worth any merit or attention that he's hoping to get. No, Mick P. knows that PCW is held in high regard, so he seeks validation by attacking it. Okay, I'll play. P. has taken many an occasion to talk about people who come from PCW and about how they're succeeding elsewhere while they didn't get what they deserved in PCW. He talks about the success of The Uprising and Tim Walden in fWo's Survivor III. Well, P., let me school you on these people's PCW track records before you rant anymore on PCW"s inability to handle its talent. The Uprising, made of Jordan X Shabazz and Richard Andy M. (who were actually enemies in PCW), remains in the competition. Shabazz came into PCW in the early summer of 2002 as part of a feud with the Neo-Nazi Mark Makonie that was carried over from NTICW. The Ultimate Authority took both men in as it was actually a decent feud, and they wanted PCW to make money off of it. Shabazz came in and quickly sided with The Malice Crew, joining Duke Williams because he had nothing else going for him. After the Makonie feud, which Shabazz won, he went on to feud with John Crichton for the Television Title in his first opportunity to earn a push for himself. Crichton came over with Andy M. and Benjamin Pailthorpe from their federation G.... I can't remember anything past the first initial. Wasn't that great anyway. Long story short, he lost, and after the mass roster-firing, he was rehired. Then came the stable wars which saw he and The Malice Crew against The Ultimate Authority against The Political Machine (which included Crichton and Murray). The Phantom Phive were in there for like the first week before they all faded away. Shabazz was given an opportunity to reclaim the PCW World Title against John Crichton, to avenge his loss as he had gone into something of a rut, lapsing in his in-ring workrate. But again, it was not to be and he lost. From there, he left The Malice Crew with Rush Mason, joined up with Thug roC and became The Uprising. That didn't last for too much longer because Thug roC was fired and Shabazz left PCW. Richard Andy M. came into PCW before Shabazz as a member of The Forgotten Ones, along with John Crichton and Benjamin Pailthorpe. Pailthorpe was quick to injure himself, and Crichton was quick to rise past the lower card and given the chance along with Shabazz to prove himself in Television Title competition. Murray was the odd man out. He wasn't stellar in the ring, but he didn't have an excuse for it. He lingered in the Rising Star Division for the longest time, and though he won it, he was quick to lose it against a flash-in-the-pan known as Lance Bell. Murray somehow got himself into a feud with Rush Mason, which was enough to get him up into the Extreme Division. What happened, however, after the feud (which he actually won) was that he hoped that the momentum would carry him for the rest of his career. He did absolutely nothing after that besides becoming a Wog that no one could understand and taking a fat man named Raab as his manager. His in-ring performance didn't improve; in fact, it got worse. He actually was drafted BACK into the Rising Star division for a tournament to crown a new champion. Tim Walden was in PCW for a good long while. He was in PCW2, as well. He managed to defeat people such as The HIT, Jeremy Howard, and he won the PCW Television Title on three occasions, as well as becoming the longest-reigning PCW Cruiserweight Champion in a number of fighting defenses. But, despite all of that, Walden is remembered (by himself as well) as a PCW jobber surpassed only by the likes of HANK~! and Jeremy Johnson. Why? Simple; unlike those latter two, Walden had talent. No one denies that Walden is capable in the ring. But what Walden's problem is is that he goes through phases. I some phases there's a fire under his ass, in other phases he's dragging ass. Sadly, those stages in his career in which he managed to do nothing were much more and much longer than the times that he actually got motivated to do anything. His best performances were in cluster matches where he could sneak up on people and hit them with something to eliminate them. That could actually be the case in Survivor 3; with others to distract from him, he's not that bad. It's when he's put into a smaller clutch situation that he is most likely to bomb. PCW gave Walden chance after chance to redeem himself. At his best, he was close to the Continental Championship. But as soon as we gave him the opportunity to get to the next level, he became the lazy jobber Tim Walden once more and sank back down in the rankings. And don't even get me started on the man to whom you are affectionately known as "Prison Bitch," Semore Smoke. You blame PCW for the way in which his career was handled, but Smoke did jack sh*t in PCW and you know it. His first mistake was coming in and getting involved in the feud between Jon Kano and "Controversial" Corey Williams. That was career suicide walking in the door, and what you fail to either understand or mention in your articles is that IT WAS HIS IDEA! He may have been stoned at the time, but if that's the way that he wishes to conduct business, it's not our job to babysit a full-grown adult and make sure that he doesn't make bad career decisions; he has to learn that for himself. After that uneventful feud, he was just around. Didn't do anything other than losing in a Rising Star Title Tournament. He entered into a feud with a man named Bernard Colt and dragged him into a match for the PCW Extreme Title when Colt had a bad case of stomach flu and wasn't even capable of wrestling to the best of his ability. If not for that, Smoke might not have even won. he certainly didn't keep the title for very long, losing it in his first defense to a healthy wrestler named The End. He again did nothing until PCW Alliances 2K2, where he teamed with Jon Kano and was quickly eliminated, after which point he went on the injured list for months on end until finally just getting released from PCW. Well, Mr. P., I challenge you to come upon this realization: PCW has and always will give its workers a chance to shine without a glass ceiling. It's up to them to take the ball and successfully run with it, though. We're not going to hold their hands through their endeavors, they're grown people and should be able to handle themselves, wherever they are. It's not a sign of poor management that these people didn't do well in PCW. If anything, it's a sign of good management that they weren't treated as inexpendable superstars that may suck if they wish and get a push. PCW requires just as much, if not more work to succeed as any federation that you actually like. Those people that you say that PCW didn't use correctly were people who didn't have their acts together. We fired or released them for not working to the best of their ability because giving them chance after chance just wasn't working. Being let go could actually have helped them to get their acts together. Shabazz has improved a great deal in his work ethic since PCW showed him that he can and will actually be de-pushed if he doesn't have a good workrate. Smoke said he was retiring, then showed up in a federation that was just opening up and thus couldn't be too picky about who they hired. Whoop-dee-doo. Tim Walden has a team with a librarian from his hometown because he didn't want to attempt anything major on his own; if he ever becomes lazy again, we won't be surprised. If you like another fed, that's fine, sing its praises. If you dislike PCW, that's fine. But badmouthing PCW every chance you get without it even being possibly misconstrued as constructive criticism is sophomoric and doesn't speak to any level of maturity or sophistication that you may be trying to present (if you indeed are attempting to do so). Enjoy fWo or hWo or A!W, aWc, ACW... any of those feds. That's your choice and we won't fault you for it. PCW isn't for everybody, we admit it. But PCW isn't as bad as you would like us to be. If it were, you wouldn't need to say anything. The worst part about your pointless ranting about PCW is that the federation is under entirely new management than what was around at any of the times that you feel that certain persons's careers were trampled by poor booking. There's absolutely no sense in blaming the new PCW management now for what the old PCW management has done in the past. That only serves to be an annoyance and it makes you look stupid (that and saying that Padrona worked in PCW when he was never let anywhere near the fed; he was in Puroresu Ultimate Ring Entertainment). There are two vocal vice-presidents, and a president who hasn't even made himself apparent to the general public like every other wrestling company president does, and they are always giving the newer signees a chance to go up against the established stars and to get pushes if they work hard. That's the way it's always been. IF THEY WORK HARD. If people come here and work hard, it's not PCW's fault, it's the wrestlers fault. Start acknowledging that you smarmy smarkish bastard and keep us the hell out of your self-mastubatory empowerment rants. On behalf of Primetime Championship Wrestling, I am Cal Seaver and to the readers of this response column I say good day (yes, P., even you). Primetime Championship Wrestling fWo Survivor III
