The Ivy League: Away From Me

21 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Looking for wrestling news? Looking for some dirt? Looking for lots of bile to be spewed out on the well- deserved shrikes of the professional wrestling world? Look somewhere else. I've been doing this for over eight years now, in one form or another. I entered the wrestling business at the young age of twenty, because there was someone important to me that I felt needed a close eye kept on, that I felt needed someone RIGHT THERE at all times to lean on in times of weakness. From the start, this very good friend of mine and I had little to nothing in common, in fact, the first time we met I broke his nose for daring to take my sister out on a date. But as time went on and I saw his intentions were honorable, I grew to tolerate him, and he tolerated me. It should've been the best year of my life - my hard work in college had paid off in spades and I looked to have a fairly easy final three semesters. Bare minimum of credits, top ten percent of my graduating class, making mom and dad proud, the full nine. Things... didn't work out that way. I don't blame EJ. My sister was my best friend, my sister was HIS best friend... and when she was taken from us we leaned on each other. Admittedly, he leaned more on me than I did on him because I still had a brother and two parents to help me get through it. EJ had nobody. But despite how hard that year was, it delivered to me a friend that I know I'll have for the rest of my life and beyond. But that year did something else to me: it showed me that if this was going to be my career path, I had better get used to the fact that all I had to offer was a set of curves and short skirts, because that's all women in professional wrestling are good for. Heh, they'd never seen Ivy McGinnis before, that's for damn sure. I didn't bust my ass to earn a full scholarship to college, and then bust my ass s'more to KEEP that full scholarship to let it all go to waste as a sideshow piece of meat. And maybe it was a side effect of the fact that I worked full time hours at Center Field from junior high all the way through to graduation, maybe it's the fact that I was a full time student and a full time professional wrestling personality at the same time, but my natural tendancies to be busy, *all* the time, took over as I started to gain more exposure. As things started off, I was perfectly happy being in the corner, playing my part as a loudmouth babyface with permanent PMS. My concerns were EJ's mental health outside the ring, and my grades. But as he slowly climbed out of the pit of depression and I finished off my requirements, I wanted to do more. I wanted to have the respect that seemed to have been denied me by virtue of gender. You can imagine my surprise when my request to fill in on the broadcast team from time to time, and my request to oversee some basic production of promos and video montagues was actually approved by Merritt and Thomas. I had my shot, I wasn't gonna let them down. And from all accounts, my work was flawless. 1998 came and went, the transitional year for so many things wrestling- wise, and I was back again. This time, with a concept for something called the Ivy League. Not much of a concept, really - a few paragraphs about whatever was going on in the CSWA at the moment, whatever was on my mind at the time - simple stuff. I figured, after I was out of the business people started calling me all the time asking for my opinion on things - Troy over GUNS, the deconstruction of the Unified Title, the Montreal incident... if there's a market for my opinion why not tap it? And again, it was a hit. Let's recap, shall we? At this point it's mid- 1999, I'm 24 years old, have the most- read column in the world of professional wrestling as far as people INSIDE the business goes, and have managed two separate CSWA World Champions at this point. I'm asked to oversee post- production work on PRIMETIME because Merritt doesn't trust anyone else to do it right. I've quickly become THE standard on what it means to be a strong woman in the business. Many tried to do what I do better than me, but every single one of 'em failed at it. I said don't be me, just be YOU. Fast forward to early 2002 and the single happiest moment of my life. The moment I grabbed Sean Stevens by the chin and kissed him mid- ring, because I knew he wouldn't. I kissed him because for the first time in my life, I felt like I could make someone else happy and I felt like that person could make ME happy. I was still busy as all hell, working for the CSWA and the fWo. Writing my column when I found the time. Teaching the manager wannabes in the fWo developmental community the fine art of psychology. But that moment in the ring with Sean made me a complete person. Fast forward to the middle of 2003. The past year was a rollercoaster of activity, as my job scope increased to include production in the fWo, column writing, managing Eli Flair and both members of Team EQ. Actual BUSINESS management in the form of Center Field - my brother and I share responsibilities in the family business - and in Valerian's Garden as I worked out a deal for them on CSE. But through it all, because of the people I was always around... it wasn't work. It was *fun.* Trading shots with Desire was fun. Talking strategy with Twitch was fun. Trading barbs with Spyder was fun. Once it was all behind us, working with/against Joe Campbell's Asylum was fun, and Joe is probably the one person in this business or a business like it that understands how *I* see it. Fast forward to today. July 21st, 2003. Everything is exactly the same as it was on July 14th, 2003, with one notable exception. Last Thursday, following his victory for the fWo Internet Championship, Triple X Sean Stevens decided he wasn't happy in our relationship and ended it. That's not really fair to Sean... he wasn't happy for a long time, I think, and that was simply the moment he realized he couldn't even begin to know how to fix it. Now that you're all caught up, I can tell my story. And maybe you'll understand why I just told you a thousand word story just to get to this part of it: I'm less than a month away from my twenty- ninth birthday, and I have nothing to show for it. Take my career, take all the work I've put into this business, take every word I've spoken or written... and they're all worthless. My legacy will be one of a woman who gave her life to the wrestling business only to find that the business didn't love her back. They'll say that I never half- assed a single moment of my time, that I never phoned in a performance, that I never ever did a thing to the business that didn't improve things. But they'll also say that I died alone and unloved. My personality and my 'character' puts a lot of people off. Either I intimidate a person that might be interested in seeing me socially or I present a "challenge" to an idiot guy who wants to tame the shrew. Neither of them are what I'm looking for. Amy Campbell said something to me last Wednesday night at a diner... she said that she understands why I do what I do - because I want to be remembered for being a woman of action. Because I want to be able to look back and know that I didn't take any shortcuts. Because I don't want to have led a life with no impact on my surroundings, both immediate and far- reaching. Because a life that had no meaning is just as empty as a life that's filled to the brim with work. The only problem is that some of us don't know how to find a happy medium. This is Ivy.
