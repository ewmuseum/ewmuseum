The Ringside Seat #1 - Where the fWo has Gone Wrong

30 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A harsh but constructive overview of the policies and attitudes which have ensured that the fWo’s product has become a mockery of itself. There are layers of creativity; booking is a very, very difficult job. It's an unappreciated job. It's a responsibility of many complexions. But when the writers of the show not only fail entirely to capitalize on characters whose popularity is skyrocketing, but manage to totally and unequivocally shit all over their appeal and piss off their fans at the same time, there's something desperately wrong. Let’s start from the top. Vic Creed- Creed is a lethargic, overplayed character, with above average charisma, mediocre mic skills and dull in-ring work. He should not be carrying a show bursting with fresh characters who should have been booked as players to draw money six months ago. The difference between the fWo today and the one in 1998 - when it was doing similar business - is simple. In 1998, there were a couple of great headliners like Jim Dudley and Cactus John, seconded by a couple of over-rated guys who never drew substantial money like Eron the Relentless and Kid Kool, and an entire roster of hard working but generally passé workers and characters that weren’t drawing any money. Today, there are a couple of great headliners like Eli Flair, XXXstacy and Great Dragon, seconded by a couple of over-rated guys who never drew substantial money like Erik Kelly and the Kodiak Vic Creed, and a huge depth of talent on the lower card who are begging for the opportunity to bring this company back to the prosperity it knew just a few short years ago. I have no sympathy for the fWo. I love wrestling. I love the talent in the fWo. I love the company. But I have no sympathy for the cowardly, self-gratifying booking and business practices which have driven it into the ground. There's a new locker room etiquette that nobody - bar Vic Creed - is allowed to look good for two segments in a row. It's a system of "you scratch my back, I'll scratch yours." It's a system whereby nobody's feelings get hurt. It's supposed to be good for backstage morality. It's supposed to make bookers' jobs easier, as they can site "the system" instead of telling a worker the truth. Fuck that. I don't remember reading that George Washington appointed less-talented corporals to go up front with his best commanders because "it would be more fair." It's no way to run a war, or a business. This is a capitalist world, and the fWo knows all about that; pushing the locker room like it's a communist state won't make anyone feel good when more and more wrestlers end up on the unemployment line, driving six hours to make a hundred bucks a night in a high-school in Philadelphia. The fWo isn't stupid. The bookers aren't ignorant; a couple, perhaps, are inexperienced or simply in the wrong business. However, the majority know exactly what's going on. Sooner or later, someone has to have the balls to say it like it is. Someone has to take the fall; do it for the business, do it for their own sense of integrity, but do it. Sooner or later, someone's going to have to have a chat with Kodiak Vic Creed, with Erik Kelly, and tell them what’s happening tonight. “Tonight, Vic, you’re putting over Eli.” “Tonight, Erik, you’re putting over Dragon.” But… why? "Because you're not drawing. We're pushing someone new; so go and do the job, and come back and get paid." Am I… losing my job? “Of course not. Your character has been pushed for several years straight. You do a great job. The company appreciates it. Your character is burned out; let’s bring it back to the mid-card, do something creative and new with the character, and push it back up there in six months time with new faces and new opportunities in the mix.” What if I put him over in a tag match, and then we did a screw job at the pay per view? That would develop him, while keeping the belt I have strong. “We need to give someone a full push. The intent right now is to establish new, credible characters as headliners.” I’m not doing the job to Eli. “Then pack your bags and go home. You get paid a lot of money to be a professional. A lot of talented, hard working guys put you over to make you a star. If you can’t even return the favor, you have no place in this locker room.” I know what you are thinking... what does the Internet reporter know about the business... but I’ve been in a locker room. I know. It’s intimidating. It’s crushing. It’s gut-wrenching. But the company has to come first. You know what it's called when the same wrestler looks good for two segments in a row? It's called a PUSH. Examples of guys who have been pushed: Ruben Ross, Boston Strangler, Crusader. Brand Frontier isn't going to become a Ruben Ross unless he gets the push. Eli Flair isn't going to become a Boston Strangler unless he pays off the support fans have invested in him by winning some big matches. There's nobody up-and-coming today; but six months ago there were. There were wrestlers like Spyder, wrestlers like Aimz. But now Spyder is burned out, the "buzz" around his potential killed by flat-out bad booking, and Aimz is heading the same direction. Why? Because they're "young" (i.e., they're in their mid 20s and don't have their hands in the payroll's pocket)? Oh, because they're too "inexperienced" to be pushed over "established veterans" (i.e. performers who at one point or another had drawing power, but are now over the hill and unable to realistically compete with the "young guys"). This is wrestling. These guys were trained to be wrestlers, not politicians. Wrestlers specialize in wrestling. Some can play the political game. Some can’t. One in a million can do both. Today's fWo product is one of the most pathetic excuses for television I've seen since the days of Duke The Dumpster taking on Joey Maggs on Wrestling Challenge. Creatively, this product is so putridly and profoundly nauseating, festering, at the bottom of the metaphorical wrestling garbage can, that I'd rather see a random Indy show every Monday night - brimming with guys I've never heard of and the hope that my enthusiasm will be rewarded by the promoter - than watch talent I love get mis-booked, seeing them raped of their drawing potential, every week on fWo programming. Fans don't give a shit about petty politics. They don't give a shit about people's egos, and they don't give a damn about the silly horseshit squabbles that lead to the fWo's product fluctuating between glimpses of brilliance and total horseshit on a weekly basis. Nobody gives a damn why it is that guys like Brand Frontier and Eli Flair, who are poised to break out as major stars, are being stuck in meaningless matches week after week; they just get frustrated and sooner or later turn off the television. If you're going to push someone, push a guy who minds his own business, and like him or not, has broken his neck for the fans. Give them something consistent. Give them something that speaks for itself. Give them something that will open its own door, and leave itself out at the end of the night. Fans want a good show. Give it to them. fWo Wrestling
