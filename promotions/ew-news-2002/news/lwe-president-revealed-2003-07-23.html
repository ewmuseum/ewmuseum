LWE President Revealed!

23 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Yuri Takayama, Japan's #1 Freelance Reporter, managed to catch and sit down to interview Lionheart Wrestling Exhibition's apparent owner and president. A lot was revealed about the federation and its owner. Read the translated transcript below. Yuri: Hello. My name is Yuri Takayama, and I am lucky enough to be speaking with the wrestler Yuto Sano, who has been announced as the President and Chief Executive Officer of Lionheart Wrestling Exhibition. Thank you for this interview. Sano: Thank you for taking an interest in LWE. Yuri: The first question that I should ask is... well, quite frankly, I need to ask who you are. Sano... I've not heard that name. Who are you? Sano: It's understandable. I have been working mainly in Europe. You wouldn't know about me, however, because I've been wrestling under a mask as Gran Sanosuke. Yuri: Oh, that was you. I know who you are now. You are the German National Wrestling Alliance's most decorated champion. Sano: Yes. Yuri: You were still actively competing at the age of 52 years of age, before, in your own words, "taking a short vacation to pursue other interests". Was LWE the interest that you were talking about? Sano: Indeed. The opportunity was presented to me shortly before the GNWA Grand Title match against Der Große Krieger. Yuri: That was quite a match that the two of you put forth. For the two of you being at a combined 95 years, it was an impressive display of technical wrestling and high-flying skill. Congratulations on your successful defense. Sano: Well, thank you. Yuri: So what is happening now? Sano: The GNWA is holding a 64-man tournament to decide a contender to the Grand Title. That should take 3 months, which should hopefully be enough time to establish LWE. Yuri: Are you defending the GNWA Grand Title during that time? Sano: Possibly, but this interview isn't really about me, it is about the Lionheart Wrestling Federation. Yuri: Of course. So, what is the goal of Lionheart Wrestling Exhibition? What could it hope to accomplish what has not already been done? Sano: That is perhaps the first misconception, and the biggest misconception, that we must break. LWE will not be anything new to the Japanese wrestling fans. It won't be terribly innovative, it will use the tried-and-true method of pitting two to six wrestlers against each other in a test of courage, endurace, and skill. Yuri: And how would this be different from the other wrestling federations on the market in Japan at the moment? Sano: There are a great number of new federations that are cropping up. There are the federations like Osaka-Pro or Toryumon; Keiji Muto's new project WRESTLE-1 (which I mention separately from All-Japan Pro Wrestling)... they are all run by respectable businesmen, but it is a shame what the federations have become. "Wrestling" in those formats have become a mere routine. The matches are slow, uninteresting; the highest draws are brought in and made to lose to the largest, most unfit "athlete" that is touring with the company at the time. And it's not just those shows. All-Japan Pro Wrestling is having monetary issues from a low attendance for its shows. This wouldn't be a problem if it hadn't agreed to pay 24 million yen to a rather unimpressive Bill Goldberg for only two appearances. Yuri: And LWE would not be like that? Sano: No. Lionheart Wrestling Exhibition will focus on the old-style of wrestling. No hardcore matches; no overpaid and undertalented workers; no bells and whistles, no pomp and circumstance. LWE is for wrestlers. I have a firm belief that the Japanese wrestling fans can get entertainment out of a well-wrestled match without anything else going into the production. We won't make the mistakes of the last wrestling federation to open within Japan, namely Puroresu Ultimate Ring Entertainment. I enjoyed its very first incarnation, and the first two shows that were produced before it hired Ali Khadafi and the ideal of strictly puroresu competition flew out of the window. Isawa Miyagawa had a good thing going until that point. Yuri: What do you mean, you won't make the same mistakes as PURE? Sano: You see, PURE, while a nice idea in theory, was carried out completely wrong. The president was American, the show was carried only on American television, and the workers were mainly gaijin. That was perhaps the worst thing -- for a federation based in Japan to have fewer native workers than workers from the North American continent... it didn't seem right. Yuri: They reported that the turnout of Japanese applications was low. Sano: That is not true. There are a great number of aspiring Japanese wrestlers and veteran independent talent who are all seeking a platform from which to establish themselves as the future of the business. There are wrestlers that no one has heard of that could be the aces of their companies. They simply need exposure. PURE would not give them that exposure. Japanese wrestlers, at least. White Americans, nothing against them, had no problem finding employment in PURE, even though they had no ability to properly work in a puroresu environment. Yuri: Are you going to be taking gaijin into LWE? Sano: Of course, gaijin will still have a place in LWE, as well they should be involved. My problem with PURE came from the fact that the gaijin were getting all of the spotlight, over more qualified Japanese. I don't blame Miyagawa for that, as he soon ran out of time to administrate PURE. I blame the Americans who took control of it soon afterward and turned it into, for lack of a better description, WWE Japan. It was not anything close to puroresu, it was American sports entertainment. Ridiculous gimmick matches. Wrestlers whose skills inside of the ring could not match their arrogance. Yuri: And you plan to go against that grain? Sano: Indeed, I shall. LWE will be completely wrestling-based. We shall show our shows live to the people, and we shall schedule it for the convenience of Japanese fans, not American viewers who quite honestly won't be watching our program very often. The wrestling will be confined to the ring, and a few feet beyond, and shall not in any way involve "hardcore" elements. Yuri: What are the exact steps that you plan to take to ensure that this vision of yours remains intact? Sano: To start, we have rented the Hironori Park Open Space. It is a simple open-air park. We can accomodate up to 2,500 wrestling fans and put on wrestling shows where backstage antics are all but eliminated, and weapons cannot be stowed anywhere for use. There will be no gimmicked matches; contests will be between individuals or teams that must rely on their skills to win. Yuri: What's so special about using Hironori Park Open Space? There was not another area you could have rented that suits your purposes? Sano: Yes, of course, but those were bigger than necessary. I will not kid myself as the owners of PURE did; they traveled to arenas that they could not possibly fill. LWE doesn't have the funds or the drawing power to go to the Tokyo Dome, we won't even try until the federation is an established powerhouse. To pick any place with a capacity of less than 3,000 is keeping our view hopeful, but humble. We couldn't draw more than 3,000 to start, and even that is a great stretch. Yuri: Tell me about the federation itself. What kind of titles will it feature its wrestlers? Sano: Lionheart Wrestling Exhibition will have three titles. Wrestlers at 98 kilograms (216 pounds) and over will have a Heavyweight Title to contend on a singles format. All wrestlers under 98 kg will have a Junior Heavyweight Title that they may contest on their own. Yuri: And I take it that there will be no wrestling between the weight classes? Sano: Not in singles format, no, unless a Junior Heavyweight has so far outperformed in his own weight class that he may shift to the Heavyweight class. Yuri: Is there a separate title for the joshi? Sano: No. I am a firm believer that a skilled woman can defeat a skilled male wrestler if given the opportunity. Therefore, there are no separations by gender in LWE, only weight class. And that is only for the purposes of championship contests. Most of the joshi will be in the Junior Heavyweight class, but if there is a woman over 98 kilograms, she will be placed in the Heavyweight division without a second thought. Yuri: Is that it? Sano: I don't think I could run a respectable traditional puroresu federation if I did not include tag team titles. Yuri: How many people on a team for the championship? Sano: There are only two-wrestler teams for the tag team titles, but they are open to any combination of wrestlers from any weight class. I can say that if the federation lasts long enough, it will host three-wrestler team events and other activities. Yuri: What about the wrestlers themselves? Have you procured any talents yet that you can tell us about? Ones we haven’t seen? Sano: Indeed, there are so far nine talents in particular that have signed with Lionheart Wrestling Exhibition. For the sake of surprise, though, I'll only reveal three. The first is a Mexican luchador named Cimmerian. He fits the profile of exactly the type of wrestler that I am looking to hire. He simply can't talk; can't "perform" in the sports entertainment sense. He has in-ring talent, and that's all he'll need. Yuri: Interesting. Who else in the way of puroresu talent? Sano: Yohji Yano. Yuri: Aha... Any joshi? Sano: We have a joshi named Juri Hyobanshi. Yuri: I suppose that's all that matters. I know that you've already revealed three wrestlers, but I must ask, perhaps if for anything else to draw more interest to your product... are there any big name Japanese talents that have signed with LWE? Sano: I shall name only one. Yuri: Very well. Sano: Makoto Toyota Yuri: THE Makoto Toyota? Hailed as the next Naoya Ogawa? Sano: One and the same. Yuri: Ah... he is very selective about the federations that he tours with. To acquire his services could be taken as a good omen. Sano: Is there anything else that you would like to know? Yuri: When is LWE opening? Sano: Within the next 28 days; many wrestlers that we have signed still need to clear their work schedules, or get work clearance... there's no certain date about it. Not to mention that we are getting qualified applicants all the time. Yuri: How many workers are you aiming for, and how many do you have? Sano: We have over 20 wrestlers signed. We wanted fifteen, and have that many and more. The doors, however, will never be closed to applicants if they are talented enough. Yuri: Any names among them? Sano: No big names, no. Yuri: So what is next between now and the opening? Sano: More preview shows. Yuri: What is your opening plan? Sano: I will hold three tournaments concurrently, one for each championship. Participation in each tourmament will be restricted to only the first eight wrestlers or teams who sign with Lionheart Wrestling Exhibition and agree to compete. Yuri: Must wrestlers apply to the federation as a tag team to enter the tag tournament? Sano: No, but I would urge that at least some participation be from regular teams. Yuri: Well then, I guess that's it for the question and answer period. Sano: No problem at all. I've business to attend to. Yuri: Thank you for your time. Sano: Your welcome. Oh, before you go, I must ask you a question. Yuri: Me? What about? Sano: Your employment status. You are currently the best freelance reporter in Japan. Yuri: That is what they tell me. Sano: Could I possibly convince you to work exclusively for Lionheart Wrestling Exhibition? Yuri: Possibly; we'd have to hammer out a few things. Sano: I understand. I shall provide you with the address to our offices, please stop by soon. Yuri: I will, thanks. Lionheart Wrestling Federation
