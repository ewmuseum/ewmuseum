Prominent Wrestling Owner Announces Retirement

21 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Seven years is a long time, even for a man who once claimed to be God. He has been reverred as a great owner by many who have encountered him. But no matter what anyone says to change his mind -- he says this is it. Though, there is still a final farewell to be had. It took seven years to end the career of Tom Mason, a man who built himself into the wrestling industry from nothing. Starting only in December 1996 after being given a position by a friend, Tom Mason quickly started to catch onto the industry ways and eventually was able to launch a few rather successful promotions of his own. Simply put, Tom Mason may not be a legend in terms of owning a company that completely dominated the wrestling industry. He may not even be remembered for the skills that he possessed, but he has said that he will be remembered by one date -- March 27, 2004. What is March 27, 2004, you ask? That would be the date that Tom Mason named his final production. March 27 is being labeled "Total HaVok IV: The End of The Circuit" after the event "Total HaVok" which Mason himself founded in 2001 with the start of XCWF. The event is scheduled to be a four-hour Pay Per View of great expectations personally for Tom Mason. Not many details on what will be involved have been released, but Tom Mason told reporters that he was planning something great, something to be remembered by. The event is supposed to be taking place from East Rutherford, New Jersey, in the Continential Airlines Arena. That venue being the very first that Tom Mason stepped foot in during his seven-year career in the wrestling industry. On March 27, it looks like it will be the last one he will step into, at least as far as actively being an owner or part of the industry. His retirement, of course, comes just months after the announcement of Real and Graphic Entertainment's return for 2004. It was a bit of a surprise to a number of people, including those who worked with him, but all have said to stand by the decision that Mason made. Here is just an exerpt of a speech Mason made: "This experience has been something I will not forget; not even if I wanted to. For seven years I have literally poured my blood, my sweat, and my tears into this, treating it like a real-life job that it is. And to state my resignation is something I wish I never had to do. It is the hardest thing for me to ever come out and say." Information on "Total HaVok" is said to be released in a press conference that Tom Mason will be holding the first week on March. You can be sure that the information will be passed along to EW-News.com are all other news sources interested. Real and Graphic Entertainment
