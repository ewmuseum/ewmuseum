"From Behind The Mirror" Edition Two

20 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Doubted Preston would return? Well, he has and this week he delivers some facts on the top regarded wrestling promotions out there. And Jason Hughes sits "On The Spot" with Preston for an interview. Oddly enough, following the last (and first) edition of “From Behind the Mirror” people complained to a lack of opinions and information involved with the column. In order to attempt to tailor to those suggestions and inquiries made, hopefully this edition will be a bit more to the liking of others. And if it is not, there is no telling what exactly pleases people. To any extent, this week we take a look into the success obtained by many of wrestling’s promotions currently running, even a few that are defunct. The questions asked were: How is success measured and determined? Or more importantly, of what do fans measure the promotions that they are fond? In order to look at this situation, I have taken the time to reflect on a number of threads on internet forums that talked about the promotions in wrestling and what fans generally liked about them. After you take a look at the facts and compare them to the things you see in wrestling, success is pretty easily determined. Most places that run off of a rather spread-out fan base are successful not necessary because they hyped themselves for weeks or months, but because they produced something that made the fans look at them. Mostly it will be the talent brought in by the company that gains the interest of fans around the world. It is definitely not hard to believe, because talent is what entertains people. If you have someone that is skilled on the microphone or someone who is naturally charismatic in their actions, who would not want to watch them? For one, I certainly enjoy a great wrestler who can both talk the talk and walk the walk. There is nothing worse than someone who cannot take the actions to back up their words, or vice-versa. Promotions like Fans Wrestling Organization, Primetime Championship Wrestling, and countless others are all deemed to contain the talent worthy of being called “top-notch.” The question that I would like to answer: Is it necessarily true? Not to open a can of worms with my opinion, I think there is a split down the middle. There would be no denying that certain wrestlers in those promotions are extremely skilled in their work, but at the same time a number of wrestlers can be deemed overrated or just plain terrible. It happens everywhere you go; some people stand out rightfully and others wrongfully, while some people just sit at the bottom of the ladder waiting to climb up. Personally, I have never been a big fan of PCW or fWo, but I do regard the amount of respect that people give to those places. As two superb examples of wrestling industry success, Primetime Championship Wrestling ran for three-years and encompassed one of the largest fan bases wrestling has seen. It offered a strong weekly reliability with two shows and even three at one point. No matter what anyone thoughts about its quality of work done on the shows, those who did revere it stayed interested and were loyal enough to follow the company through to the end. Well, the first end. At this current point, many are aware of the remount of Primetime Championship Wrestling, but the first wave of its return has yet to gain too much interest, possibly due to the way the last era went out. Much in the same, Fans Wrestling Organizations has been a huge fan based promotion, pulling in possibly the largest fan base in wrestling at this current point. When people hear wrestling, many will immediately think of fWo, and that certainly can be a compliment. Anyone can admit that fWo has been the noted “heavyweight champion” of wrestling in recent years in producing big name talent and storylines that many have viewed as the best out there. But perhaps it is the extremely well-constructed and extravagant website that sets fWo apart from everything else out there. Whatever the reasons people have for visiting certain places, one thing stays true: opinions are what matter the most when it comes to success. No federation will be at the top of the world just because it produces what many critics deem “the best work.” It is the word spread by the fans around the world that draw more and more people to watch the shows and bring the ratings higher, and the revenue in for those companies that end up being more popular. Is this to say that any place is at the top for the wrong reasons? No. Because the only thing that matters, as it has been proven, is that the fans watch and they feel entertained. It does not matter if a promotion has mediocre wrestlers who are the best on the microphone or vice-versa, as long as the fans watch it repetitively. Facts all over internet communities show Fans Wrestling Organization to be the leading promotion amongst wrestling fans, as a majority of discussions around the internet pertain to the promotion, encompassing approximately 1597 different mentions in topics on the EW-Planet forums. For those interested, a number of other highly regarded promotions (unofficial; as determined by volume of discussion through an online search) include : 1. Action! (approx. 870 topics) 2. the Asylum (approx. 758 topics) 3. Primetime Championship Wrestling (approx. 523 topics) 4. Old School Wrestling (approx. 514 topics – defunct) 5. jOlt Wrestling (approx. 389 topics – defunct) 6. Championship Wrestling League (approx. 354 topics) Those figures may give people a little understanding to the lasting impact of the federations on that list. It may also help to show people how dominant a few of those places are over the rest of the online discussions. A bit more trivia: the majority of the promotions researched barely turned up over 100 topic results. Only a handful more managed to get as high as 200 or more. This goes to probe the fact that online discussion needs to be furthered and many promotions have a long way to go until they see the lime-light. Of course, there is room for more discussion on the internet all the time, and EWPlanet is only one of the many online wrestling discussion communities that are available to public chats. Even when you log onto sites like EW-News.com, you will see that there is not as diverse a following as they can be. However, a number of those above-listed promotions do not post frequent news on EW-News.com, as none have been researched to have turned up any posts all this month. The only mention noted on the EW-News.com website would be the mention of a handful of those seven above-mentioned in the January 6 “Top Newsmakers.” With all this said, there is no way to separate the facts well-enough to show what promotion is best in every aspect of the wrestling industry without involving your opinion. One thing I have mentioned before, and will continue to mention, is that no matter how many facts prove to one thing being great, the ultimate decision of “great work” lies in the response it draws from the wrestling fans. Critics may be labeled as the ones with the degrees or expertise in picking apart a wrestling match, storyline, or what have you, but the fans know what they like and will always tell the truth in their response. Rising Grappler Heading into the depths of the rising wrestling world, Furiously Addictive Wrestling’s “The Lee-gend” Lee Smith has spent three years of his career chasing gold. A man who has been given very little recognition outside of the realm of FAW, the wrestling industry has yet to really come aware of his amazing abilities. His greatest achievement right now has to without a doubt be capturing the FAW Heavyweight Championship just a few weeks ago, when he defeated Orlando Rivera. Before that, Lee has carried on a struggle to the top, and at “When Worlds Collide” on January 4, 2004, Lee Smith ended Tony Vigilante’s career in the company. In that same night, he battled to the end of the main event extravaganza as he and Adam Jacobs put on a great finish, and Lee Smith narrowly felt out of the ring first. Wearing the championship belt now, Lee Smith proudly carries the weight of the FAW locker room on his shoulder, and having spent three years getting to this point, one can assume he will do it with great pride. For those interested in knowing, Lee Smith will put the heavyweight Championship on the line against Adam Jacobs on March 21, 2004, at Extreme Beating 4. On The Spot Lucky enough to have a fill-in this week, Jason “Old School” Hughes joined me for a little “Q&A” backstage at a Core Wrestling event. Scheduled to appear, Tony Vigilante has been trying to keep up with a hectic schedule and shall be rescheduled to a later date, not yet known. But Hughes and I did manage to have a great session and some very interesting things were brought up involving his career, pursuance of a taken woman, and other possible career options on his plate. In addition, Jason gives a few inside notes on his match against Skill Willie on February 22, 2004, at Gold and Glory for Core Wrestling. Preston; “So, Jason Hughes has decided to fill in for a missing Tony Vigilante? Thank you for coming to do this on short notice. What made you jump at this chance?” Jason; “I heard you served free pretzels.” Preston; “I do not know how that rumor started, but feel free to pick up one for fifty-cents on your way out. You work for Core Wrestling, how is that experience?” Jason; “Overall it's fun. They are based in Europe so I get to travel between the states and Europe often. The talent is some of the best there is; Gideon Fontaine, Neil Fracas, Ed Gesumaria. Of course, they are all on my side. The champion, Skill Willie is an amazing athlete and one of my greatest opponents to date. The owner, Matthew Lanier is, well, a bitch.” Preston; “Speaking of Skill Willie for one, you have a match against him at Gold and Glory – an I Quit match. How have preparations for that been?” Jason; “Well, I have been in one I Quit match before and it ended with me losing and then having to be stretchered out of the ring. Those types of matches; I Quit, Iron Man, Last Man Standing; those types of matches take a whole other style of discipline to not even win but simply compete. In an I Quit match you can't look for the quick pin or a submission that will knock your opponent out. You have to bring him pain, make him feel the pain and then just as he is set to pass out, cinch it in so that he screams out those two little words. I Quit. Preparation, nothing more than building my own endurance up and working to ignore pain. I have men put me in the most painful of holds and work to not think of the pain, to not give up, to resist until I see my own opening and then go in for the kill.” Preston; “You seem ready and confident, for sure. I want to ask you quickly about something I saw recently on Core. Just the other day you were involved in a rather interesting conversation and overall situation with Veronica Paige on Smash. I, along with many others, sense a certain sexual tension. You expressed some feelings, were disappointed, and it looked like you were awkward. Anything to regret maybe?” Jason; “Only fools regret things. I do them. The issue between Veronica Paige and myself, that book is far from being closed. She is something amazing, something truly amazing and I can't help but sound like a complete idiot around her. She does something to me that few people have ever done and I will not let last week be the final attempt of mine to get her.” Preston; “What is next for you with her? Plan on buying a car or house to hold that hot tub or any of the others things you may plan on giving her?” Jason; “You know, she is currently involved with Jake Finale. So, the way I see it the first thing I should do is show her exactly why, like every other thing in life, Jake Finale falls just a little bit short of the bar I set.” Preston; “I want to move on, but you have created an enjoyable real-life soap opera. Any concern that Jack Finale may spoil your fun any maybe cost you the Legacy Championship at Gold and Glory even?” Jason; “Jake Finale will not involve himself in the main event. Skill Willie's record against me in Legacy Title Matches is one win and zero losses, and that was in part due to the interference of Matt Lanier. Skill is not going to let his friend involve himself in the match...he has to much pride and cares too much for what those peasants in the stands think. To see him victorious in two Legacy Title bouts against me, by way of interference on each occasion would destroy their faith in him and he just won't have that.” Preston; “Jumping to Matthew Lanier now, what exactly sparked this bitter combat between the two of you? As far back as I remember, you two just seem to clash within Core.” Jason; “Personally, I think he is jealous that I have hair and he can't even win with Rogaine. Of course, it could be the fact that I am wealthier than he is, more talented than he is, I'm damn sure cuter than he is, I don't listen to a word he says unless it suits my own purposes. Matt Lanier does not like having an employee that he cannot push around. That is why he dislikes me and the rest of the Upper Echelon, because unlike his paper champion and every other man on the Core roster we do not bow down to his demands. We make our own rules and he knows from past experience, that to go against the U.S., not him, is how careers in Core are ended.” Preston; “Okay, cooling off for a minute, how is it working over in Europe on a weekly basis?” Jason; “Well, besides Germany, France, England, Austria, Spain, Ireland, Scotland and Belgium, it is a very nice continent.” Preston; “So, places like Italy, Greece, and Portugal are fine then?” Jason; “Oh, Portugal can be added to the list of crappy places. Greece, Italy and Switzerland are fine.” Preston; “One thing is for sure; visiting Italy, Switzerland, and even Iceland and Norway can be fun. And I think you know what I mean.” Jason; “Mmm-hmm. All very nice countries; the women there... Damn.” Preston; “More bang for the buck, indeed. Anyway, you may have a strong hold in your position with Core Wrestling. But I would bet your talent is something much sought after by scouts. Any places particular that have tried to draw your service?” Jason; “At different points in my career I have had FAW, LOW, DWO, WWA, HWF, NWC, Action, PWW and RaGE show interest in me.” Preston; “Certainly some good places in there. Without particularly naming, any of them actually made you consider leaving Core Wrestling and pursuing a position somewhere else?” Jason; “There were two but in the end I decided that I preferred Core at this point in my career. The future however, in always in question.” Preston; “How long do you see yourself working for Core Wrestling, and why?” Jason; “At the very least, I will be in Core until summer as I have a contract with them through May. After that it depends on whether or not I am still enjoying myself or not. I don't see anything happening that would cause me to leave Core, but I said that about Global Wrestling a year ago and look at how that turned out.” Preston; “What one thing do you think you most owe to your success in wrestling at this point?” Jason; “In a roundabout way, my fear of heights. So often now do you see wrestlers scale the top ropes or attempt a high-risk move and they end up paying for it; taking years of their careers. I never had that opportunity. I am fine in a plane or a tall building but when I am up high and there is nothing stabilizing me except for a turnbuckle. I just can't do it. Since I couldn't do those things I pushed myself to be the best mat wrestler out there. I have gotten to a point where I can go toe-to-toe with any wrestler in the game and beat them on the mat. That ability, that ability to slow a match down and win with little physical effort on my part has won me matches, titles, money and years to my career.” Preston; “One last question. Even if you did not give it any thought before, if you were not a professional wrestler, what job would you most like to have?” Jason; “People have actually called me Satan for the things I have done in that ring. If I wasn't wrestling I would be a lawyer as I was pre-law when I began my career. So regardless of if I was a wrestler or not people would still be calling me Satan.” Preston; “Thank you for your time, Jason. It has been fun. I will look forward to another sit down in the future. Good luck with Core Wrestling and your match on Sunday.” Jason; “Thank you and thanks for having me.
