Retirement isn't Easy

29 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Cult-hero, The Butcher, believes retirement isn't that easy. Dear Reader, If you have never heard of me, please let me introduce myself. My name is Jimmy Cortez but in the wrestling world I go by my stage name...The Butcher. I began wrestling professionally in 1980 at the age of 16. My career was lack luster and pathetic compared to today's new age superstars that grace the squared circle day in and day out. Irregardless of what other people say, I had fun and even though my losses far outnumber my wins and I only have one title to my credit, I do consider myself a successful wrestler. Finally, after 22 plus years of wear and tear in the business, I decided the retire from active competition in November of 2002. It was a hard decision to make because wrestling was all I've done most of my life but I had to do it. My body could still take the grueling schedule but it was the psychological toll that my wife Nikki and I could not take anymore. New guys in the business will learn quickly that the life of a professional wrestler is not all glamour and glitz. You move from place to place so often you forget where you are sometimes. Your body becomes a torture chamber each day you wake up because of the night before. You grow tired after many months, if not years of getting a few hours of sleep a night. But the hardest part of wrestling is not seeing those you love and care about as much as you want. Daily telephone calls simply cannot add up to seeing the person face to face. And that is why I had to give it up. The purpose of this column, if you haven't all ready guessed, is to talk about retirement so I'll get on with that. I've been retired from competition for about 10 months now so I think I have a good understanding of how it is and I'd like to share my thoughts on it for those considering retirement in the near future. It feels like being on a roller coaster that slams into a brick wall. Everything just stops and you have more time that you can possibly deal with. You go from having maybe an hour or two of personal time to having 24 hours of doing whatever you want. Believe me, being burned out from retirement comes quickly and that's why a lot of people don't last very long after they exit the ring for the last time. Wrestling is an addiction and when it's taken away, you find yourself on a de-tox program you might not be ready for. Committing suicide is a very real option and I've known a few that have done such a terrible thing. God rest their souls. To relieve their addiction to wrestling, some wrestlers turn to hard drugs like cocaine, heroin, and even binge drinking just to get another taste of the exhilaration they fail to feel anymore. I traveled down that path earlier in my career and believe me, drugs won't get you anywhere. Overdosing on pain killers is another common problem most retired wrestlers face. Your body is so used to going full bore night in and night out, that when you take that away, your body starts to fall apart on you at a much quicker rate. You'll have to deal with the fact that you won't be in touch with many of your former competitors because they'll be busy moving on without you. It does a lot of psychological damage when all of your friends and co-workers are not in your life as much as they once where. It is hard to deal with that and it leads to depression. Retirement is a state of mind. You'll know when you're ready for it but you must plan ahead before you make the transition. Save your money because in most cases, you won't receive a pension from any wrestling federation you've worked with. Involve yourself with the community. That's one of the great things about wrestling, all the people you meet. Just because you retire doesn't mean appearing in public in a no-no. Do your best to keep in touch with old fellow competitors and stop by backstage whenever your former employer is working a show in your area. It doesn't just do you wonders, it does the current roster as well. Personally, if I gave up wrestling 100% I would be dead. Wrestling is a part of me and always will be. That's why I've gone out of my way and opened a wrestling school in San Francisco called The Butcher's Clinic. I found a way to still work and give back to the sport that's given me so much. It's really something when you see one of your former students wrestling in an independent match. It feels almost as good being in the match yourself. Whatever you do beyond the ring is up to you and I won't look down on your decision. We're all different and what works for me might not work for the next guy. Just do something positive and keep your chin up. Who knows, you might find life more rewarding beyond the mat. It's a big world out there with endless possibilities. It's up to you to make the most of it. Good luck. - Jimmy 'The Butcher' Cortez
