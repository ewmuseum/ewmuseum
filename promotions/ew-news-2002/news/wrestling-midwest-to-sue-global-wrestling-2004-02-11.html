Wrestling Midwest to Sue Global Wrestling

11 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
An un-aired interfederation match and a possible cover-up on Global Wrestling's part has led to Wrestling Midwest pursuing legal action. CLEVELAND - After an on-air blackout of an interfed matchup, the only thing left when the lights turned back on was a pending lawsuit. Wrestling Midwest is leaking the possibility of it pursuing legal action against fellow PTC Company, Global Wrestling on a breach of contract, following it's decision to drop an interfed match from it's broadcast earlier this week. On February 5th, WMW's Justin Evers was on contract to face GW's Kenji Lee in an interfed matchup, to be aired. "I blew him out of the water," said Justin Evers, furious about the situation. He also said that he finally "was going to help his federation to get some recognition in the PTC Realm, but now all of his efforts appear to be for nothing." WMW Owner Brad Johnson said, "We've already started an investigation. We hope to bring an end to the situation, with the unmoving goal to get this match aired." GW was unavailable for contact, however the day the results were to take place, they claimed that Lee was in a car accident, in hopes to explain the situation. "It's a lie," said Johnson. "They have no right to do this to Evers or PTC." Wrestling Midwest
