The Ivy League: Riding in Cars with Boys

16 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The difference between today and yesterday in the wrestling business is easy to spot - it's as simple as Point A to Point B I spent six hours in a car over the weekend with Eli Flair, Amy Campbell, and Twitch, one of the product managers for the fWo. Aimz was a bit out of it, as she was... SICK... late last week, and Twitch was still a bit hung over, but it was an enjoyable ride. We talked about music, the latest run of crappy movies to hit the theaters, and how Aimz has a mental deficiency where anything that was released before 1999 is considered to be 'old.' Old Man Flair and Old Man Twitch took offense, but since I still have a year and some change before I turn thirty... I'm exempt. Really. We talked about the wrestling business, too. Things we've done, things we were planning on doing - things that were in the works right now. Eli has some thoughts for the rest of the year - not just for himself - that blew Twitch away. Aimz is still getting used to her role as Hardcore Champion and the fact that she's gotta be more aggressive when it comes to her work and demanding a fair shake when it comes to how she looks in her matches. My laptop and my 'archived body of work' - several years' worth of columns on every subject under the sun, from every promotion known to man - was a big hit. The 'Evolution of a Psycho Bitch' is apparently going to become a party favor in the locker room. Twitch schooled Eli in spades on how to cut a classic heel promo, but to be fair, Eli drove the entire three hundred miles and was concentrating on the road, too. But all Aimz had to do was inform Twitch that he's fat and he got insanely flustered. What does this have to do with ANYTHING? Nobody in this business drives anymore. Everyone likes to fly, because it's faster. Sure, it's faster. Sure, you can't very well drive from Atlanta to Denver and expect to make the show... but the death of the road trip has definitely had an impact on the way the business is run. When Eli Flair and I first entered the fWo locker room in the fall of 2001, it was easily the most professionally - run I'd ever seen. The board was up at noon, everyone hit every cue exactly at the right moment - you can guess this was after the days of the FtfWo and the multitude of ownership changes - and everyone worked with everyone backstage to make the show as good as it could be. But that was about it. Aside from a drink or two at the hotel after the show, everyone essentially left each other alone. Keep in mind, Eli Flair and I were almost exclusively in from the CSWA at that time, a place we had been in for the better part of six years. A company where three of the four men who wrestled on the first card back in 1988 are still actively involved - one of whom is the current CSWA World Champion. It's a company that has been run by one (or both) of two men for its entire span of existence. A place where, to sound like a 1980s sitcom, everyone knows your name. It harkens back to the days when the CSWA was merely an east coast 'regional' promotion, and the talent drove from shot to shot. The fWo had barely seen such an action - until the house shows kicked back in a year ago. Only a handful of us knew what to expect on a house show tour, and so those few helped organize everyone else on what was needed. For house shows, you need a lotta patience, a lotta energy, and at least three other people to share a car with you. Ever rented a car? How about nightly for weeks at a time? It gets expensive, unless you're splitting the cost with three other people. In return, you get company on the road. People to eat with. People to talk to to keep you awake and keep your energy flowing. People to bounce ideas off. People that'll shock and amaze you with the things you learn about 'em. A year ago, myself, Eli, Sean, Brand Frontier, and Lance Knight hooked up for reasons that are still largely unknown to me - but I'm glad we did. These guys are some of the best friends I've ever had in the sport, and Sean is far more than a friend. Alex Newsome was nothing more than a highly capable wrestler with very little in the way of psychology and that 'sports entertainment' stuff that you really do have to have in your style to get a reaction from the crowd these days. If you ask Alex about those road trips, I hope he'll tell you that they helped him work on his charisma. I see the masterful job he's doing these days and I recognize one or two basic things that we helped him with - only he's taken the ball and run as far as he can with it... I'm proud of him. There's a million Road stories like that... and they all revolve around someone with unlimited potential that just needs a little helping hand to get it STARTED. I can guarantee you, that the boys and girls who drive from shot to shot WITH each other go farther and do better than the boys who take their disposable woman on the road and fly from show to show, barely paying attention to anything but their own press and their balding old head. Me, I'll take six hours on the road, a burger joint on the side of the highway, and enough stories to tell my great great grandchildren any day of the week. This is Ivy
