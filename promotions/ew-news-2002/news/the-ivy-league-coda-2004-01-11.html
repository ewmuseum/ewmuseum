The Ivy League: Coda

11 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
This is not an appropriate tribute to the Countdown Six. But it's the best I can do. I've started this column more times than I've started everything else I've ever written, combined. My problem was that I didn't think I had the skill necessary to give my six fallen brothers an appropriate tribute. Then I realized the truth. It didn't matter what I said about them. They're dead. The Countdown Six don't care what I write. The problems they faced are over. It doesn't make a difference what you say about a person when they're gone - the importance is how you treat them when they're still here. Why should I suddenly change my opinion on what an asshole Spyder was? His spirit would haunt my dreams every night just to point and laugh. Spyder wanted two things from the business - to build an unforgettable legacy and to be the most hated, reviled wrestler that professional wrestling had ever seen. Now, I realize that he and Dragon came up short on the tag team belts, but think about it... in the span of one year, Spyder won every singles belt in the fWo. He and Kellen Kinkade won the 2003 Meltdown match. Whatever legacy he hoped to build, he succeeded several times over. Temerario wanted somewhere to belong - and his legacy will say hello to us in approximately nine months. Neo - whoever he truly was - has hopefully found a measure of peace. John Willis showed up for work every day and did his job. You can't ask more of a man than that. And the stories that man could tell... the things he's seen... you don't work for a company for six years and have nothing to show for it. He and I once talked about writing a book, and just spilling all the stuff that I know about the seedy underbelly of the CSWA and him, from the fWo. At least Disney has decided to pay his pension out to the Willis family... it's the little things, but it'll never truly add up. As a new face amidst the four in charge, I can't even say I miss Twitch and Smooth the way Ted does, but their legacy is currently under fire and it's our job to protect it. I didn't watch a lot of fWo programming before I signed my name to the contract in the fall of 2001. For years, I didn't watch any professional wrestling, at all... and by the time the fWo came into existence I was working for the CSWA, and I ask you others in the business - at what point do you have time to watch another promotion's shows? Between my own television, between house shows, between traveling and trying to find a few hours a day to eat, sleep, shower, and stay in touch with the people back home... usually the eat, sleep, shower section of my life gets trimmed down. And now with my responsibilities in the front office I don't have time to do anything anymore. I have a point in there, really. I can't speak for Tallison, for Smooth, or for Twitch, but I can't believe that I didn't upset a few feathers when Fox offered me this current job, quickly turning me into his 'go-to' girl for all his ideas, for all the things he couldn't work out, for everything under the sun. But to my own credit, my focus has always been on the product itself. I don't care what makes the fWo stronger; I'm going to recommend we do it. But between the four of them and the five of us, we managed to turn out a second half of 2003 with a legacy that rivals any six month period in any professional wrestling promotion anywhere, I'd bet. And yes, that legacy is under fire. Nobody was prepared for Disney to swoop in and take over like this. How can you be prepared for a gasoline fire? For a coma? For shotgun injuries? But I put it to you, what does Disney know about running a professional wrestling promotion? What do they know about this company, specifically, other than a fiscal bottom line? Exactly. Not a goddamn thing. Silver Fox is still in charge of the fWo, due to the conditions of his contract and current state of being. However, I fear for the kind of promotion he's going to Wake up to. And he will wake up. He has to. The problem is that there's currently nobody equipped to take charge of the fWo. There is no leader - none that I'd listen to, at least. This guy Murphy has no history and no track record; there is absolutely no reason in the world for any of us to care what he says. And if he goes the route of 'I pay your salary,' and expects to get a reaction from us, he's even more naive than I thought. Ted is a drunk - simple as that. He was good as a support to Fox, but as a solo leader, I don't think it's going to work. And as hard as I know he'd try to run the promotion with the best of intentions... Ted knows he's no leader. Me? HAH! That's funny. People like me, they listen to me, and they respect me. The roster trusts me. And I appreciate it to the ends of the earth. But I am the absolute last person on the face of the planet that should be given even a skosh of power. I'd use it. I'd abuse it. And I'd love every moment of it. Not in the way that Tom Murphy is most likely going to abuse whatever power Disney gave him - I'd have an advantage in the fact that I'd know how to do so with creativity. I am well versed in the art of professional wrestling politics, even though I don't dabble in them. No, the solution of what to do with this company is not very easy to figure out. Everyone has their own opinion, I'm sure - and nobody is right. This is not a wrestling promotion. This is a company with a lot of scared, nervous employees that don't know what their future holds. There's no more dangerous animal than a scared child. And none of this is a proper way to carry on after the tragedy of the Countdown Six. None of this is a proper way to say that we'll carry on what they tried to do - contribute to or run a successful wrestling promotion. Trite? Yeah. But fuck you, I've earned the right. My biggest concern is the rest of the roster, and the fact that while a leaderless ship is bad enough, a leaderless, disorganized ship is even worse. We could deal with the inevitable Disney power play if we would all stick together and make the shows as powerful as possible, regardless of what the outsiders in our midst would try to do. Will Brand and Crisp work together as the top two in this company? Will Murphy listen to myself and Ted when we tell him that something - ANYTHING - really is for the good of the company? More likely than not, this promotion and its history is less important to these people than the fact that it's just a project that makes money and looks good on a resume. More likely than not? It's an inevitability. The last time we ran a show, six men died. At least five people were taken to the hospital for observation, treatments, or reconstructive surgery - and we still have no idea what happened to Dynamo, Jack Lundin, and Fallen. The next time we run a show, everything will be different, and there's nothing we can do about it. We couldn't stop what happened on New Year's. But we can do our damnedest to make sure we take a cue from what happened - and come through the ringer even stronger than before. IF we can do that - and I know we can - we stand a chance of survival. And it would be enough of a learning experience to move on. Because with every fiber in my being, I know the price we paid on Countdown will never - ever - be worth it. This is Ivy too.
