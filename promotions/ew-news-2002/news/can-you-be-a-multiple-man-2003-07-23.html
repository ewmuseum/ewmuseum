Can You Be A Multiple Man?

23 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Some people demand to be in five feds at once. But being unemployed can have it's upside... read on to find out. Well guys, It’s been a long road going, and this is where we’ve ended up. That federation we’ve been in for years just kicked the bucket and now we’re out here in the bitterly cold winter. But is that all so bad? No. It isn’t. I’ve seen fairly well known wrestlers out there that have been in three to four federations at once. Sure that’s fine and dandy when it comes to the end of the day and you take home the little brown envelope with all the cash in it, but think of the toll on your body? Say three feds. That could be a possibility of three televised matches per week. Plus house shows. Plus Pay Per Views. Plus training. Now with all that said and done, do you honestly think that you are going to end up with a social life? Hell, do you think you have the time to spend all that hard earned cash? Personally, I’ve never been in more than one federation at a time. They have a tendency to sign me to exclusivity contracts, which I don’t mind since well… it stops me from trying to kill myself by attending seven shows in seven days (you catch my drift). But I can imagine, and I imagine it must be pretty darn grim indeed. Myself, I’m currently getting divorced. The break-up was due to wrestling. I was spending more time with my locker friends then with the person I was meant to be having a relationship with. And that was when I was in just the one fed. Then again, had I been in more than one, I might never have had the time to meet the girl, or marry her for that fact. Free Agency is good. You actually get time to do more training than usual, or at least, you get the time to do the training that you want to do. Rather than fit it in at random hotels and motels around the country. You get to keep yourself in peek fitness. Hell, I feel great, I haven’t had a cold for months and my hay fever is starting to clear up all on its lonesome. You feel good about yourself. But the downside is one simple thing. Money. You see, capitalism can have its downside. (Don’t let Ivan read this, he’ll list another fifty downsides) Of course with free agency does come the lack of money to pay the rent. But this is where all that money from the three feds comes from. That’s of course presuming that you haven’t done a Jake the Snake and blown it all on cheap hookers and drugs. Don’t get me wrong, there are times that I’ve wanted to be back in the ring. I miss the cheers and boos of the crowd. I miss hearing my name being chanted. I miss headlining Pay Per Views (here’s looking at you, Boda). But if I had to choose that life over my family life, I’d choose the family every time. I might only get to spend time with my son on weekends, but I’d rather do that, than anything else imaginable. Don’t forget though, that you really cannot burn the candles at both ends. Sleep is a very necessary thing. Lack of rest can mean death, especially in some of these more extreme federations that’ll do anything for ratings. You act a bit dozy in a match and suddenly that chair-shot you were meant to take over your forehead crashes into the side of your head and cracks your skull open. Just because we’re wrestlers doesn’t meant that we’re superheroes. We can be hurt, we can be injured, and eventually we will all die. But when I’m old and sixty four, I don’t want to be sitting there on my own with my two hip replacements, shot knees and arthritis all the way through my body. I’d rather take the option where I look healthy still have plenty of grandkids about. Just something I thought you might want to take a think about. Alex.
