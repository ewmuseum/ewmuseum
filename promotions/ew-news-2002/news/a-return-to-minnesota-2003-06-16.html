A Return to Minnesota

16 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
For those of you wishing to relive that nostalgic feeling in the pit of your stomach that is Minnesota State Wrestling check out Sam Wolack's recollection of MSW's Monster: Outside In. Will the energy that was MSW return when Central visits the Mall of America in Minnesota this week? As many of you know, I’ve recently had to end my in-ring career, but for you NWC News fans, I wasn’t gone long. This past week I turned on the TV and watched NWC Central presents Center Stage for the first time since my retirement. It was then when I saw that the new Central Man-in-Charge Adam Calloway announced that the Central was going to hold a card in the Mall of America in Bloomington, Minnesota. This drew about many memories for me. It was here that I got my start in Minnesota State Wrestling. The day was June 7th 2001. The venue, the Mall of America. The event was MSW Monster: Outside In. Many of us in the MSW locker room thought that it was going to be another big card. Little did we know what was going to happen. Little did we know that it was the last time that we would ever compete as a group in an area that was filled with wrestling tradition. I had just made my return to the NWC the card before getting involved in a singles match that my uncle was a part of. Those of us that were on the pre-show were glad that we were even involved with the card. We didn’t realize what had already happened. For me, coming to the ring to AC/DC’s “TNT” with my uncle was as close to being a dream come true as possible. I was going into the match knowing the history behind it. My uncle Jay had faced our opponents for that match twice before. The Mercs had gotten the win in the first match. Chaos got a win for the MSW Tag Team Titles. The Mercs had a hand in my uncle’s partner, Mike Durant being injured. This match was going to be amazing. My first big card, I had wrestled on a couple of cards for MSW before going to Japan, and the fans were on fire. Even more special was the fact that we were the match that directly preceded Monster. So we had to put 150% effort into the match to keep the fans on fire for the next two hours. Outside In was coming, the fans knew it, the wrestlers knew it. We were getting pumped for the card. We were watching the Dralcos lose to the team of Peter Nad and Shaun Cooper on the monitors. We knew we were coming up. While some vignettes were playing, we headed to the guerilla position to head to the match. “TNT” filled the arena, and we came out. We then waited as the Mercs took their merry time coming to the ring. When the match started, we were a ball of fire. The match didn’t last long, we got the win. The fans were going at a fever pitch. The card was starting. Monster was upon us. The night was electric. Kid Ego and the Gutterpunks won the opening match of Monster over Hammer Stenanidol and Spunky White Bread that kept the crowd on their feet. The energy was there for sure. All of us in the backstage area knew that it was a good night for wrestling. We didn’t have a clue what had already happened though. Next up, current Upstarts member JD Danzing was defending a singles title against Bill Neno. The MSW TV Title was on the line for this match. As was expected from all the competitors that night, Neno and Danzing blew the top off the Mall. Danzing won to retain the title. We were still oblivious, but rumors were starting. Perry Evans and Sean Ryan followed that match with an amazing display of athleticism. These guys were feeding off the energy from the previous 4 matches. They were also feeding the energy. Evans got a win with the Northern Lights Suplex. The rumors in the back were starting to sound alike. Robinson was selling MSW. Next up was the brutal Hardcore Match pitting Johny Hotrod and Dez Bradley. This match as I said was brutal. Neither wrestler would probably ever be the same afterwards. Hotrod would win the match. The rumors were true. Robinson was selling MSW. The next match was a battle. One of the most sadistic tag teams in NWC History Malignance was taking on Wasted Youth and Joshua Lee. This match was back and forth. Many times we thought that it was over, only to see a last second kick out. Malignance eventually got a big victory. The rumors were circulating again. MSW was already sold. One of the big aspects of MSW was it’s cruiserweight division, and that title was on the line next when Saerin Aurora took on Champion Jared Sykes. This match was also electric. The news backstage that MSW was sold didn’t diminish what we put out there. Sykes hit the Syke-Out to get the win to retain the title. Maybe the other wrestlers weren’t paying it no attention, but I sure was. The new owner had a great locker room and the best fans in the country. Lee Clark was taking on Azaul in the next match. Clark is still one of the most underrated stars in the NWC and he proved it in an amazing match against Azaul. Michael Bold was the guest referee for this match that ended after the New Dawn. The ring had broken earlier in the match, but the fans kept it alive. The new owner was going to need to invest in a new ring. Between that and the next match was a funny fight between Hiro Saotome and Homeless Havok. I’ll let Hiro tell the story of that cock fight. It’s not my place to. Next up was a Pride at Stake match between Ibrahim Seck and Michael Djin. This match was brutal. The object was to bring the opponent to a cross and light it ablaze. It didn’t quite end that way. The cross ended up after the match unburned on top of Seck. The medical bills were already growing for the new owner. Next was the MSW North Star Title match between Paul Ryder and Adam Calloway. One of the most controversial finishes in MSW’s history. The match ended by pinfall with Calloway barely getting the shoulder up. Or did he? None of the cameras picked it up. We can only take the ref’s word for it. The new owner would have to talk about the officiating as well. Hiro Saotome took on Jake Summers in a match that was the end of a very emotionally brutal rivalry. This match would only end when someone either said I Quit or was unable to continue. And that’s how the match ended. Hiro Saotome won the match with a crossface that put Summers out. The end of the evening was approaching, who was the new owner? Next up, Crash and Burn went up against the three headed monster called the Dynasty. Tony Pride and Idol Austin were defending the titles that night. Eugene Robinson was still playing owner. Well, the match was nothing short of being a classic. Crash and Burn some how came out the MSW Tag Team Champions. The new owner had to be smiling. Then came the last match of the night. BloodAngel and Eclisis. The two biggest names in the MSW, ever. They were good friends, neither one of them would let that get into the way. For Jonathon and Edward, this match was going to be a classic. Neither one could come out the same. When it was over, the BloodAngel was the victor. The fans were on their feet. The night was over, and they saw one of the most amazing cards possible. Nothing could bring them down. Except for this. After the main event, Disturbed’s “Fear” entered the Mall through the loudspeakers. At the entrance was the new owner of MSW, David Cooper. For MSW, this was not a good thing. For the greatest fans in the world, they were about to get their hearts broken. After a night of amazing wrestling, the best card that the members of Minnesota State Wrestling could provide, it was over. MSW was dead. LSW started back up. A legendary promotion in Minnesota was now gone. The roster would go on to win NWC Level titles. Wrestlers like Hiro Saotome, BloodAngel, Perry Evans and Jay Draven would go onto becoming household names. But there was nothing like Monster: Outside In. The best card ever presented by MSW was it’s last. Hopefully that energy will be back when the Central comes to the Mall of America. Hopefully the fans of MSW will come out and give the Central roster as much respect as possible. I’ll be there as a resident of St. Paul. Rush Hour will be a card that has been over 2 years coming. [NWC] - National Wrestling Council
