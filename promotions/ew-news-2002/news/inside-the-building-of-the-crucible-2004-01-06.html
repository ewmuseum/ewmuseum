Inside the Building of "The Crucible"

6 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It has never been seen before, and Real and Graphic Entertainment will debut it on January 9, as the company marks its first show of the 2004 Season and the first in nearly five months. Find out the inside story on the pain that awaits the participants at New Dawn this Friday night! Thanks to the perks of having the one site that get receives the inside scoop on anything involving Real and Graphic Entertainment, SE-Live.com was given the chance to go inside the facilities where the infamous 'Crucible' was being constructed. The engineers and architects who were involved in the construction of the Crucible were nice enough to give the employees visiting the facility a tour and indepth look at the structure itself as it was completed just last week, following a short Christmas vacation. "The Crucible is not something that took us just a few days to construct. It was a project we spent the whole month of December constructing and we had specific orders from Tom Mason himself. This may look relatively simple in construction, but making sure it was safe was the most painstaking part of it all," says senior architect, James Wilkenstein. "It took a good one hundred or so man-hours to produce and I do believe it is going to be one of the most brutal structures to ever be made out of a mesh caging." That statement by the senior architect, and chief designer of the the Crucible, seems to be a bit hard to believe. But as our employees learned, the Crucible was being made out of a harder, more durable and ungiving mesh caging. As many people are aware, a mesh (or chain-linked) fence or cage is generally easier to fall into than steel rods or piping. The difference with this caging is that the links are closer together and tied down tighter to the main framing and secured with more than loops of steel. "We worked around the clock to secure the caging to the frame, and most of the hours producing this cage were on the account that we actually wielded the caging to the frame. That means that if someone falls into it, you get as much give as smacking into a wall. It makes for a sturdier structure, but unfortunately for the men in this match, it will be more painful. Think of it as falling on thousands of little needles on your living room floor." From the sound of the information gathered when we had the chance to talk to the designer of the Crucible, it appeared as if any presumptions could be thrown out. The way it sounded after this small interview, this match could be a test of enduring more physical pain from the cage than any opponents. And to see just what James Wilkenstein was talking about, our employees were about to get into the Crucible and walk around it, as it sat around a ring replicated to the same size of a Real and Graphic Entertainment ring. Looking at this structure, its twenty feet separation from the ring mat to the top of the Crucible seemed to be higher than your usual twenty feet. It was far wider than you could imagine, about seven feet out from the ring on both sides. The platforms were made out of the same caging as the walls, and as James said, the caging both felt and looked dangerous. It did not sway when you tugged at it, and the cold metal had an eiree feeling to it on its own. Beneath the platforms outside the ring, there are four "cages" which will house the four wrestlers who will start outside and enter as the match progresses. These really are cages as the men cannot even stand up in them. It will be an interesting feat as these men will be sitting in these cages, unable to really stretch, and then be forced to enter into a highly competitive and predicted intense match up. This experience of being able to check out the Crucible and preview the structure and only imagine the pain that will be happening in it on Janauary 9 has to be a great experience. But the real experience lies on that night, January 9, as Pat Dwyer, Chris Collins, and Kaz will step into the ring against Shawn Kain, Jamal Kage, and Evan Darkness. It can be said that the match will be possibly the most brutal to date in the history of Real and Graphic Entertainment. It should also be noted in conclusion, that no one will be able to get out or into the Crucible without the series of keys which lock it. Those keys, to add, will be hanging above the ring and suspending on a cable, only to be lowed to lock the cage once all six participants are inside. They will then be raised back up until the official calls for them to be lowered to unlock the Crucible. Real and Graphic Entertainment
