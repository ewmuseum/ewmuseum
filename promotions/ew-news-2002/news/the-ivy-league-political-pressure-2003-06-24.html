The Ivy League: Political Pressure

24 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Some people never learn... "It'll never sell. It's a mockery of the sport, two guys who were past their prime years ago." -'Smart' CSWA fans, on the advertised Battle of the Belts XVII main event, Hornet vs. Mark Windham. "She's just not believable - she doesn't fit into the whole identity of what a Hardcore Champion is supposed to be." -'Smart' fWo fans, on Aimz as the Hardcore Champion. "New Frontier Wrestling has plenty of talent just waiting to bust out... they should cut their losses and give this guy a pinkslip before they can't draw flies anymore." -'Smart' NFW fan, on why Shane Southern's heavy drawl will impede his ability to draw as their champion. Can any of you tell me why any wrestling promotion in the world is still in business? No promoter listens to the fans. No promoter listens to the Internet. No promoter would know a decent wrestler if it came up and bit 'em on the ass. Promoters do everything in their power to drive their paying customers away with shoddy booking, inexplicable pushes, and political champions that nobody in their right mind would want to see headline a card. That's why Battle of the Belts XVII shattered ANNIVERSARY 2000's buyrate. That's why the fWo has been on a run of near- sellouts for every card since Cyberslam. That's why the Asylum remains on the cutting edge of what a professional fighting promotion is measured against - regardless of their level of actual professionalism, the work Joe Campbell and his roster does is commendable. So why are ANY of us still in business after we've proven that we can't be bothered to listen to our fans? Because the fans who are talking the most have the least to say. It's sheer politics that Hornet and Mark Windham, a pair of athletes who have risen to the occasion, time and time again - were signed to face each other. It was sheer politics that Eli Flair defeated Evan Aho to earn the first shot at the belt - only the SECOND he's had at the CSWA World Championship, ever. It was sheer politics that saw Dan Ryan, a man who has done precious little to endear himself to the CSWA management, win a Battle Royal to give him the NEXT shot at it. Wait… that last one doesn't fit. That last one is a classic case of "Man Earns Shot Without Being Big Name" - and that's not allowed to be spoken of for fear of the Webgeeks' point being moot. Do I get upset when Amy Campbell's work is derided endlessly just because she happens to be a woman and the Hardcore Champion at the same time? No. Admittedly, it's not a good thing to have bad ribs and be the Hardcore Champion at the same time - but anyone who doesn't see an athlete who works her ass off isn't looking in the right place. Does it bother me to hear Spyder or Shane Southern decry Eli Flair's fWo and CSWA, respectively, Number One Contendership position as politically motivated? Not in the slightest. Look closer. Look closer at the people who make the most headlines. You won't see a single 'overrated' wrestler on there. The names you see in the news are the names of people who are trying desperately to break through a psychological glass ceiling and become a big name by going to news resources and telling people that they're big names. You'll never see Kodiak Vic Creed or Eli Flair on a website shilling themselves. You'll never see Brand Frontier or Lance Knight on a website shilling themselves. You'll NEVER see Mark Windham anywhere near an interviewer or a computer. Spyder has used the News to generate a ton of heat for himself at the expense of a good number of 'overrated' names including my own… and he's having the time of his life with it. Instead of wondering why there's a 'Rat Pack' holding all the political cards in the fWo, take five minutes out of your day and understand why those wrestlers have formed such a close- knit group. Look at their track records in the fWo and ask yourself if these people need politics to succeed? Triple X and Lance Knight, along with Triality, revitalized the tag team division this past winter. Trip calls himself the single greatest wrestler in the world today - you'd be hard pressed to prove him wrong. Lance Knight, once described as poetry in the ring and valium on the mic, has become one of the most exciting parts of the upper- midcard of the fWo, with no ceiling in sight. Brand Frontier is a Hero, the likes of which I haven't seen since The Deacon, the likes of which I haven't seen since Hornet's days as the Greatest American Hero. To call him a politician, to say - to IMPLY - that he doesn't deserve every single thing he's gotten from the sport so far, with miles still to go, would be to undermine everything that this sport is built on. Eli Flair is the hardest working man in the wrestling business. Not "one of," but THE. No discussions, no debates. There is no single person in this business who puts more into making his opponent look like a million bucks, who puts more into giving the fans a memorable match, who spends hours helping the younger guys improve their speaking skills and storytelling ability. Amy Campbell, if she were a man, would be the Darling of the Internet. This smallish wrestler who can fly through the air, who can take a beating and still get up to dish out in kind… she'd have Chris Benoit syndrome and a fanatical following to boot. But as a woman making it in a man's world on her own terms, she's the Darling of the Internet… up to a point. And that, my friends, is hypocrisy. Why am I defending them? Because they're my friends? Because they're part of my clique? Because that's my role in this sad state of affairs - they manipulate the locker room and I justify it? Because there's nothing to justify? Because it's just as political to decide, based on personal preference, who should be on top of a wrestling promotion. Because all the politics in the world can't help a wrestler who doesn't have the in- ring ability and natural charisma. Because all the talent in the world coupled with none of the politics can only hold the truly talented down for so long. Because it's my job. I'm taking next week off, so all of you who count the days between Ivy League columns will have to find something better to do. Go outside. Go to the movies. Go tell someone you love them. Go do *anything* besides sit in front of your computer. After all, it's just the Internet. This is Ivy.
