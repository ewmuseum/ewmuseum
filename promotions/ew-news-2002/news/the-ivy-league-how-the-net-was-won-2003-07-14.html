The Ivy League: How the Net Was Won

14 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
And lost... and lost... and lost... and lost... and lost... Don't get me wrong, I love what I do. The very fact that a little girl from Queens can influence the way the business and sport of professional wrestling is seen by others, both peers and fans, is nothing short of amazing. And I'm well aware that the more popular someone or something is, the more open to personal attack they become. I'm a big girl, I can take it. All I ever ask is to hit me with something legitimate. Tell me why my opinions are wrong, or biased, or inaccurate. Tell me why you disagree with me and BACK IT UP. That's the nature of debate. That's the nature of discussion, both heated and civilized. I don't pretend to offer anything but my own take on what I see in and around the professional wrestling business, and my own life as well. I don't offer my opinions as more than the truth or less than the truth - as I see things, they *are* the truth. According to certain unnamed shrikes, this makes me an untalented and biased fWo cheerleader because I don't address dirt on specific people. Well, take this one down, because it's the only time you'll ever see me respond to direct criticism. See the title of this column? The Ivy League. See the byline? Poison Ivy. I was hired to write the things I do - these *specific* things. If the editors of the News wanted the same old slop about how the business is in a slump and the fWo doesn't know how to build up new main eventers and the CSWA doesn't know how to book a feud without using someone who's been in the company for at least ten years, they'd have hired someone else. Remember the golden rule - if they come to you, they need you. If you come to them, you need them. Unsolicited and unconstructive criticism is the work of a jealous mind. Sammy Benson is the best example I can think of. The man plays the fool because it sells on television, but he's one of the most intelligent minds this business knows. You want a good idea of what goes on in the CSWA behind the scenes, you read Happy Hour. Sammy will only take so much abuse before he stops cooperating with the part he's expected to play - look beyond the jokes about my 'hooters' and the drinking and you'll see a man who knows his stuff. Spyder, our esteemed fWo Internet Champion, has the entire IWC eating outta his hand. His worked shoot columns have raised the ire of many, the amusement of many, and the interest of *all*. You want to down what Spyder's got to say? Fine. But that means you care enough to read it and comment. That's the greatest irony of all. Love me, like me, or hate me, you will *never* be able to ignore me. Say what you will about the rest of the column- writing fools that the fWo seems to have all around - since November I can count off Sean, Ruben, Kelly, Eron, Aimz, Lance, and Lowell who've all written at least one column for the fWo website or for the News. And I bet each and every one of them would be the first to admit that writing a column isn't their strongest suit - if it was, they'd leave the ring and write full time - but that they had something they wanted to get across and the website was the best way to do it. But you, dear Internet Wrestling Personality, can only decry these men and women for not living up to your standards of column writing. You take a bunch of wrestlers, put 'em on the road for three hundred days a year, plus personal appearances, plus working out, plus trying to have an actual personal life, and ask 'em to give life- altering insight into the nature of what they do? I work twenty hour days minimum and don't take bumps - I'm surprised I put forth what I do. When's the last time you told one of the boys that you were grateful and appreciative of the extra effort they put forth to give a little blurb on a website? When's the last time you read a column by a hardworking wrestler and said "Y'know, we're really lucky to have someone around who cares enough about the business and the work they do in it to keep us updated on the various goings- on in the company? Or would it be too much to ask to drop the Internet Heel facade for ten lousy seconds? Bah, some people will simply never learn. This is Ivy.
