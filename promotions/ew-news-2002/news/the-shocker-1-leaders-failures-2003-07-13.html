The Shocker #1: Leaders & Failures

13 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
They've learned. They've paid their dues. I hadn't. The others that had failed with me hadn't. The wrestling world swallowed us up and spit us right back out. Does anything ever stay the same? Is there really a standard anymore, or is anything and everything destined to doom and failure? What happens when the thankful take on a thankless job? I find myself wondering about these very questions nearly daily, curious as to if any of them yield positive results. It never seems like they do and never seems like they ever will. I'm talking about being the top gun. It may be the only thing on earth where one can be at the top and bottom at the very same time. That position only belongs to one type of people: the leaders. Leaders come and go, but they're all subjected to the same cruel and unusual punishment and torture and give it back to their subjects in return. It almost seems as if they never left. It's the same sick and tiring daily routine that govern our daily lives. Good leaders are the worst. All they can possibly bring is chaos and misery, and I can say this with the most utmost respect for all great leaders alike. However, it's the great leaders that set the bar so ungodly high, that none of their mortal predecessors can come remotely close to grasp within a yard's reach of the same standard they set. They eventually leave, and in doing so, leave us all gasping for air, as we slump down into what seems like eternal perdition just waiting for another great leader to rescue us, and then bury us again. The same seems to hold true for this wrestling world we all live in. In short, we thrive for the best but eventually die like the rest. The rhyme scheme almost seems cliché by now. However, and for whatever reasons, there is so much more to this predicament than just that catchy little phrase. Why it's that way is easy: human nature. Trying to describe the intricate workings of human nature and why it is that way is a different story. One thing is for sure. It's our human nature to want the best, and once we have tasted the best, there is no need to ever regress back to an inferior state of being. What's worse, is that there truly never is anything that can be classified as the "best" because once we taste the best, we find the need to find something better, which brings us in a full circle back to my theory on leaders. Whenever a great leader comes around to set a standard for us to follow, it takes time to reach that standard or break it again. Time is something else that is still yet beyond our comprehension. Some people think they understand it, but all they have explained to us is how mathematically certain digits can take the place of time and give us numbers to live by. Time is a lot more difficult than any set formula can lead us to believe. Since time is so difficult, it's nearly impossible for us to understand why something so simplistic as the changing of the guard can be so seemingly unfeasible at times. It is because of this that any leader that comes inbetween two great leaders (and there will eventually be another one), whether they are awful or fairly decent with their leadership skills, is doomed to failure. The same holds true for the great leaders. They are eventually doomed to failure as well. The reason is simply that there will always be someome better. They may not be failures in the present, and when we look back on the past we may not consider them failures either. However, it's the future that counts. Anyone can live in the present and be happy. Happiness is a key component of life and shouldn't be wasted. Living in the past is pointless. Living in the future is impossible. The future is the one thing that will always be coming, but won't ever arrive. Sure, one can put a timer on something, and once again tie time to digits, but when it comes down to it, when the future arrives, it's no longer the future. It's the present. Once again, time has fooled us all. We can't judge the future. We can judge the past, and we can judge the present. And somewhere down the line, long after a great leader has passed, in comparison with the next best thing, the great one will be simply second best. It is the same endless cycle that will always take place, regardless of what we can try and do to stop it. All leaders are just eventually doomed to failure. Our world of wrestling defines this clearly. Being the leader of anything in our business is nothing more than ritualistic suicide disguised in thoughts of happiness and acceptance. This crude guise of greatness can fool even those who aren't easilly suckered into anything. I can always say I've only been fooled once. I was fooled into believing that leadership in the wrestling world could lead to greatness and bought into it just like it was a Trojan Horse. Once I fell on my face, I picked myself up and went back for more, just like we all do. I saw other organizations thrive, and rake in the dough. I didn't want that. I just wanted to be a part of something special. I raked my hard-earned money together, and used it to purchase some of the most low-rate equipment I could get my hands on, and then went and gave out contracts to young inspiring kids with the same goals as mine. I was doomed from the start. 'Why is this?' I wondered. I still do, as it's something that's just unexplainable. How can it be that something that everyone wanted so much was just too far away to attain? Eventually, I realized the answer to this question, and it is the same answer so many aspiring young leaders in the wrestling world have received. I am not the best. I knew this then, but am fully aware of it now. There is always someone better. They've learned. They've paid their dues. I hadn't. The others that had failed with me hadn't. The wrestling world swallowed us up and spit us right back out. Most normal people would have given up and tried something else. However, in this industry the words "give up" only exist when one is subjected to unendurable pain while in the locks of some expert ring veteran. We can't give up! Failure is inevitable, that's easy to accept. Not trying is just something so ridiculous, that mentioning the fact that there are people out there who do not try is truly discouraging to us all. Therefore, there's only one thing left to do. Try again. Many can try and try, and many will fail miserably, rather than those that just fail slightly, since at the present time their flaws are so minute that we really do not even care. Now it hits me. That is exactly what we are all striving for as leaders, and even as members of the wrestling world. We strive to exist in a state of being, where our minds have been numbed to the point that we can never believe what we are a part of is not as good as it gets. This is not a bad thing at all. If the entire world with us can live in this feeling of being so close to immortality that we can touch it, we would all be living in a perfect world. I have explained that the great leaders will eventually fail, and that everyone else will fail as well, but I have yet to answer one question. Why is this happening? Why are we failing? My friends, the answer to that is simple. We are on top. We are the leaders. Whether we are good, bad, big or small...We are the leaders. When something goes great, it is known. We can touch the feeling of greatness, and it's an awesome feeling. When we fall, we know it. There's no such thing as a small fall in this business. You are either on a pedestal that only you and those with excellent insight can see, or you are at rock bottom, and everyone can tell that one. When something goes wrong in the wrestling world, even something so tiny that only few people hear about it, it is the leaders that become the scapegoats and take the blame. Being a leader in the wrestling world, whether an office member of a low independent local organization, the owner of one of the most prestigious organizations on the planet, or even the owner of a cable network wrestling television channel like myself, is a thankless job. I shouldn't use thankless. There are those loyal enough, and proud enough of our accomplishments, that they will stick at our side without complaint as long as we are there to provide the rest of the world with our entertainment. They are one of a kind and very hard to come by. These are the fans that show their appreciation for what we provide to them and these are the fans that I respect for putting the only thanks in a thankless job. However, for the most part, we will do our work, and give others the enjoyment they seek, while we ourselves get the pure joy of knowing the truth behind the false skin. We know that as long as we are not getting pounded by the wrestling world, that we are doing a good enough job to keep people happy. It may not keep everyone happy, but that is almost impossible to do. Even when something is as good as it gets, there is still room for improvement to try and make more people happy. Not long after my major failure at trying to run an organization, I decided to start from scratch, and grow stronger. I offered my limited skills and learnings of the wrestling world to a local office of a fairly young organization. It was an alliance, which hosted only seven smaller leagues at the time. I was quickly recruited by someone just in search of some small tasks, and was on my way to the Pacific Coast. Eventually, after time took it's toll, and my dues were being paid, I was offered the head position of the Alliance's brand new organization, and I took it greatfully. This was great. I was able to move close to home again, and I finally had funding for a project that I wanted to do in the first place. I was so thankful for the second chance, and did everything in my power to find the right talent for the job. I succeeded. The league thrived, and still does to this day. I had kept every idea I had ever thought up on my own, and used many of them. Some were worthy of awards, and some failed, but my overall success was well noted. I ran the league with pride, and I am still very proud of my accomplishments, even through the criticism I took along the way. My point to all of this? I was a thankful person that took on a thankless job. I was actually looked up to in this industry after a while, and the feeling was incredible. Over time, I eventually worked up enough courage and had enough money to try, I decided to part ways with the Alliance in order to seek out one idea I had never tried, and I am proud to say that I am finally realizing this dream. However, everything changed overnight once I left. Not literally overnight, but it was only about a week that something so obvious occurred it was shocking. The league that I built from the ground up changed completely. The title belts were renamed, the office I had was all released, and my legacy was suddenly over, as if I ran headfirst into a brick wall. I knew once I left, I would eventually disappear, and would have to let go of a job that I still loved and fade into oblivion. I just was not sure it would happen that quickly. All that was not even the worst part. The worst part was the loyal fan base I had did not even seem to notice. It was if they were so mesmorized by a good thing, that even during a total change of it, they didn't even care. This is not just my experiences either. This happens to us all. If it has not happened yet, it will happen eventually. With my current source of employment, I am constantly searching through organizations, and all of their partnerships. I see everything that goes on backstage when I read info, and speak to other heads of prestigious, and not so prestigious companies. It happens. It happens even to the best. Take the late great PCW for instance. For years I watched their programming, just dreaming of the glory that they had. To me, and to many others, they were the standard of what wrestling should be like. Everything was so tight with the organization it was almost scary. When it seemed to be going its strongest, one day everything disappeared, and shocked us all. It was as if we were all hit over the head with a hammer. It takes something like this to realize that in wrestling, there is no immortality. What is here today may just be gone tomorrow. As long as this industry is around, it will always be that way. Now there is no standard to me. I'm stuck in an empty vacuum, looking for that one organization that stands out above the rest. Will I ever find it? Eventually. There is no doubt in my mind that one day someone with the skills that it takes will step up and show us once more how it is done. I have found many strong organizations lately while searching, and many have decided to air their promotions with me. Eventually everything came together to form that little piece of wrestling paradise. The key to everything, and the reasons for why we do what we do became so obvious I could not believe that I never got it before. The business is not about being the decisive best there is, but rather the best we can be. If we never achieve perpetual greatness, that is fine, as long as we were great in our minds and we enjoyed what we did for the world that watched. We can strive for greatness, but greatness can only be achieved in our own minds. We are leaders. As long as we hold the key to the present, and we are happy with what we have done, then greatness is what we have achieved. We can hide what we cannot see. We do not have to believe what in the back of our minds we already know. There are not many of us that can do it. There are not many that want to do it. We are the true pioneers of our industry for all time to come. Let them judge the past in the future. Make them judge the past. They can be happy with their present. Our present and our destiny are one in the same: We are great for what we do, and no one can take that away from us. Was this shocking to you? If not, I think you better re-read it.
