The Ivy League: Private Lives

7 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Does being in front of the camera negate the individual right to privacy? There's been a show played and replayed on VH-1 for the past few weeks, maybe longer - about the paparazzi and how they operate, how celebrities love or hate their presence, and the double standard many people operate with, saying "You can come take my picture when my new movie is opening or when my new album comes out, but that's it. Take my picture when it benefits me, if it doesn't, leave me alone." I never understood how these people could be the way they are. It seems like a simple arrangement, doesn't it? If you want the photographers and journalists around you when it benefits you both, it's part and parcel of that twenty- million- per- movie that you put up with them when you're walking down the street. Likewise, if you don't want them around at all, if you don't want to be bothered, EVER, when it could help you or not, the journalists and photographers should respect that. Professional wrestlers are strange creatures, however... with different rules and different idiosyncracies. I never really understood it until this past weekend, spent in Boston with Sean, my brother and his family. Let me preface this part by explaining that I have never shooed reporters away. Since I do this in some fashion for a living as well, I know how to handle them. It's a lot more fun to mess around with them and theirs than it is to be hostile and leave them to write whatever they want. And it's a little- known fact, if you don't want your picture taken, give the lens the finger. They can't print it but they also can't say you're being difficult. It's a lot more fun, and a lot more conductive to sanity within the wrestling business, to keep the journalists on their toes. Wrestling fans are a different story. It's a simple matter of showing basic respect. It's a matter of talking to me instead of about me. It's a matter of interrupting a quiet dinner with my boyfriend, given a napkin shoved in my face to sign, and then ignored completely because I'm just that chick, I'm certainly not the Blue Eyed Badass. It's a matter of renting a hotel room in the place we're going to be staying before a show, getting up at dawn to try and catch an interview or photo op with one or more members of the wrestling business, and surprised at the red- eyed, sleep deprived glare of death you receive. To be fair, it's only a small portion of fans and media who do such things. But the small portion is the most vocal. It's the guy who gets verbally berated after interrupting the dinner Sean and I are having who runs to the message boards to talk about what a bitch I am. The guy who respectfully waited until we were finished, the guy who approached us outside and shook Sean's hand, shook my hand, and thanked us for what we do - he never speaks. He just gets an autograph, a photo op, and a tip of where the bulk of the company is most likely to go after the show is over. But that guy never runs to the 'net. Fine by me, he's a lot nicer when he keeps to himself. And to the people who think the people involved in the wrestling business are all unfriendly, stuck up, and full of their own crap... I leave you with this. We pay our own expenses. We log over a thousand miles a week of driving. Three hours of sleep is a luxury. Most of us can't afford to get hurt and take time off television, unless you've got alternate forms of expression like I do. It's always the people who don't realize how much it takes to survive in this business who get the attitude. That's not a coincidence. What it boils down to are two cardinal rules that every professional wrestler and noncom can agree on. Two things that everyone, no matter what their situation, can embrace as canon law. Let us eat. Let us sleep. You'll be surprised how much friendlier the Psycho Bitch gets when she's treated, oh - I dunno... Like a human being? This is Ivy.
