WCWF Roundup: Judge/Duke, WrestleWar on horizon

28 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
While the action hasn’t stopped in the World Class Wrestling Federation since Asian Assault, the importance of the action hasn’t always been realized. But with WrestleWar only one week away, that’s about to change. The big news to come out of recent WCWF programming is this: The first man to challenge The Judge for his WCWF World title is none other than Nathanial Duke. The WCWF legend and force behind the Fates defeated one of his former charges, Shawn Stevens, to win the shot at Judge’s belt. The two men who have held the World Title the most times going one-on-one? Sounds like a pay-per-view main event… but it’s going to happen on July 30th at Wednesday Night Wreckage! Stevens’s reign with the Hardcore title, which he won at Asian Assault, came to a quick and rather strange end. With the belt now back on 24/7 defense, Thump attacked Stevens backstage the following Sunday on Impact and got a three-count — only to learn that Stevens had already handed off the title to his former tag team partner Larry Gowan, who’s been inactive for several months due to injuries inflicted on him mostly by Stevens. Thump went off to look for Gowan, and found him sometime during the week, as he arrived at the next Wednesday Night Wreckage as the new Hardcore Champion. During the July 20th SNI, Thump was in the middle of challenging Stan Stone to a First Blood match when “Rapid” Rick Reno ambushed him in the ring and got a three-count of his own, winning the Hardcore title! A gutsy, successful move by the newcomer. Most WCWF stars would be thrilled to hold the International Television title. Dane Rennier isn’t most WCWF stars, though. The former World Champion and Grand Slam winner hasn’t been quite the same since winning the belt from Raymond Chandler at Asian Assault. Maybe it’s because he lost the United States title in the same match, but he’s almost seemed disgusted by the prospect of being the INTV Champion. Still, he’s too good to lose it, as he’s proved over the past three shows, beating Reno, “Misfit” Jonny Morgan, “The King” James Michaels, and Jedah Arcador. Arcador has earned another shot at the title on the upcoming edition of Wednesday Night Wreckage on July 30. The good news for Rennier (if you can call it that, considering) is that he and Stan Stone will also have a chance to regain their Tag Team titles from Jester and Brad Jackson on the same show after winning a qualifying tournament. The only person who’s had a relatively easy time the last few weeks has been U.S. Champion Matt Walker. Rested, healed and riding high after getting the “can’t beat Dane Rennier” monkey off of his back at Asian Assault, Walker returned to the ring on SNI to defend his title against Dan Bonez. The two had met at the beginning of the year, very soon after Walker joined the federation. Bonez won that first meeting, but this match was a testament to how far Walker has come since then, as he defeated the Hall of Famer decisively. The July 30th WNW is already shaping up to be a pivotal show. And on top of it, the WrestleWar PPV is four days later, and rumors are already circulating that the stakes will be higher than they’ve ever been. World Class Wrestling Federation
