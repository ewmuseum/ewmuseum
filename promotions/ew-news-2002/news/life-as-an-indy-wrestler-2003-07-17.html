Life As An Indy Wrestler

17 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Take a look inside what the life of an indy wrestler is all about. If you look into the past history of every professional wrestler in the world today, you find that out that the all started at the same place. They all started (well most) at the Indy level, or in other words, the minor leagues of professional wrestling. Core Wrestling is neither a Indy wrestling promotion nor a professional wrestling promotion, but a game we who could only dream of being a professional wrestler play. We right “roleplays” about the life of our character and the struggles they go through. Most of us don’t even have a clue, or even a glimpse of what life in an Indy or professional wrestling promotion would be like. Well, one of us does. Meet [name withheld]. He’s an indy wrestler from Columbus, Ohio. Those of us in Core Wrestling are better familiar with him as Chris Gil. Donte’ is an Indy wrestler who goes by the name Damien Kass. I had the privilege to be able to ask Donte’ a few of questions about his life as in an Indy promotion and as an Indy wrestler. I always thought it would be a life of fun and excitement, but after reading Donte’s answers, being and Indy Wrestler isn’t what it’s cracked up to be. cW.net – When did you first realize you wanted to participate in an Indy wrestling promotion? DDK – I didn’t so much want to participate in an indy show. I didn’t even know what an indy promotion was when I got into the business. I just knew that my dream involved me being in a wrestling ring and somehow wrestling. I knew it was something I needed to do on some level, and on some level of involvement. Just so happens I got my break with the local guys and not with the bigger companies that were around at the time...which was very unlikely anyway. But, I knew after watching my first match on the TV as a child…that I needed to be apart of it cW.net – What prompted you to this decision? In others why did you want to do join up with and Indy promotion? DDK – It was a chance to fulfill a life long dream. And here I was 21 years old and I was going to get to finally do something I had dreamt about, and wanted to do for a long time. There wasn’t going to be anything to stop me. My even getting involved with the business was actually boring. Nothing like traveling 200 miles and sleeping in my car, or eating raw potatoes while sleeping on a piss and raggedy mattress. I was at a taping for WWF Superstars. They taped like a months worth of shows and aired them. I was at the guardrail and The Bushwhackers came out. They grabbed some random skinny guy licked his face and knocked his glasses off. That guy was a local talent. I got to talking just by chance. He said he was a wrestler—I didn’t believe cause the guy was so skinny—we exchanged numbers a week later I was training. Very boring. I am lame lol. cW.net – What Indy Promotion have you been apart of so far? DDK: This answer could literally take days. I have even forgotten some places. But, Ill see what I can do to remember. AIW, IWA, FIW, PWX, EWF, MadMar Entertainment, BWA, RCW out of Canada, PWC, RCW, XMCW, OTH Wrestling, NWA Tri-State, SWF…I think that is what I got…OWL! I know I am leaving out a few. I am a whore, I get around lol. cW.net – What accomplishments have you made in that/those promotions? DDK: No matter what I want the people to go home and say I was involved with the best segment on the show. The best mic spots, the best match etc. not always possible but I thrive for that goal every show. I have I think win a strap for just about every promotion I’ve been involved in. The best one and one that meant the most was the winning the IWA World title. I had busted my ass in the IWA for years, and I said from day 1 I would be it’s World Champion. I think I took me 5 long hard years but it finally happened. I am not going to front. I cried when I did it. It was like a huge relief. I finally accomplished this one thing that made me want to work hard and not be ignored by the promoter there. I spilt a lot of blood, and paid a lot of dues there. And when the 3 count happened, I kid you not…I was fighting back tears. It was one of the most emotional moments of my life…and hottest. It was an outside show, and I was wearing thick black vinyl pants. That did make it suck. But, winning that title put it all out of my head. cW.net – What name and gimmick to you currently compete under and what other names and gimmicks have you used? DDK: For the most part except for a handful of matches I have either been Damien Kass, or Dangerous Damien Kass. I think that’s like a record. I think I did like 2 shows in my 6 years as not being Damien Kass now that I think of it. I've known guys that changed gimmick/names every other show…literally. cW.net – What are your goals as an Indy wrestler? DDK: I swear before I retire I am going to wrestle in Japan. That has always been my goal. I am hoping that it will happen in the next few years. I plan on getting some tapes together to send out. I actually worked for a promoter recently that has connections there. So, I hope that can pan out somehow soon. I would give my arm to work in Japan. After that my last thing would be on a pay per view. I wouldn’t mind being in the WWE but I highly doubt that would happen since I am relatively small by Vince standards. I am only 6 ft. and while I am working on it I am not built like a monster so I realize my chances are slim. But, that’s not to say it wouldn’t happen. I would gladly be the announcer that gets his ass beat! I can take a hella ass beating! cW.net – What is the life of an Indy Wrestler like? Do you travel a lot? Does it pay a lot? Those sorts of things. DDK: The indy level can pay really well…if you’re name means something. While I could honestly say there is at least 1 place in the world where the name Dangerous Damien Kass means something. But, that promoter isn’t in a big rush to pay me the money that would make it worth me working for him. Hell…he isn’t paying shit. Once you get enough exposures and some name value you can start asking for more. Like if I was to get on TNA for like a month and do some decent work that would translate into me getting good money afterwards. If I got signed to do some stuff for the WWE in a developmental and get well known, more money. Do Japan come back and get some good press…more money. That’s why guess like Raven can ask for 1500 and transportation and not get laughed at. I can ask for transportation, and like some more kick back. But, that’s all. Nothing that I can make a living doing unless I had a booking every night of the week…which isn’t going to happen. As far as travel last weekend I went from Columbus, Ohio to Ashland, KY, which is like almost 3 hrs. Then from there to Nitro, WV, which is like an hour & 20 min. Then back to Columbus, which is about 4 hrs almost. That was like one weekend. I’ve gone as little as 10 min. and as far as 8 hrs or so (Going to Canada.) You get to see the sights trust me! cW.net – If you were called upon to give your words of wisdom to someone wanting to join an Indy promotion, what would you tell them? DDK: Don’t do it lol. There is a lot of disappointment on the indy level for “unknowns” like myself. Lots of shady promoters out there they will try their damnedest to not pay you if they can get out of it. The pay will suck going into it. There are a lot of negatives, and games. But, if you can stomach it, and tough it out…. In the long run it will be all worth it. I have met some amazing people. Some people I never would have never known if I didn’t get into the business. My tag partner asked me to be his best man for his wedding in a few months. I was honored. Cause there are people that he has known for a lot longer than me, and better friends with. The fact that he asked me is amazing. I’ve seen and done things I never would have been able to do if I wasn’t involved with this business. I’ve gotten to be in a ring with Hacksaw Jim Duggan and work with him in front of a packed house. I got to defend my United States title / pin / Ax from Demolition who I idolized back in the day. I’ve been to Canada. As much as this business takes from you and beats you up mentally and physically it gives that much back in return. I wouldn’t give anything for the people, the memories, and things I’ve seen and done. I’ve gotten to do what I love, and I’ve gotten paid to do it to boot. You can’t beat that.
