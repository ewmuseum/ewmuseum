NWC Legend: Hunter Sabuani

29 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
One of NWC's greatest wrestlers ever set to return!? Get to know The Peerless One....Hunter "Sublime" Sabuani NWC LEGEND: HUNTER SABUANI Hunter Sabuani was the illegitimate son of the patriarch of a prominent Las Vegas family and a spiritualistic Apache from a nearby reservation. For the early part of his life, Hunter (born "Mirror-Hunter") lived with his mother on the reservation, learning the Apache way of life and moral code. But when he was only nine, Hunter's mother died under circumstances he won't discuss, forcing him to move to the city with his father's side of the family. But he never really fit in with the night life and family business, so by the time he was sixteen, he'd left home and hitchhiked across the country, meeting people who would later become his friends and cohorts in the world of wrestling. An autograph from Ric Flair sent Hunter spiraling onto the road of professional wrestling, and it was in Boston that he had his first break, given to him by the promoters of the regional indy fed called the New England Wrestling Coalition (NEWC). Here is where Hunter first met his closest friend, "The Axeman" Vatch Indesson when both were midcarders in the NEWC. They didn't stay midcarders for long. Hunter and Vatch catapulted to the top of the NEWC card through a combination of attitude and talent, and here they remained for an impressive stint before the NWC came calling for Hunter. Devon Justice, then the commissioner of the Seattle Wrestling Federation, was forming his own federation and signed Sabuani away from the NEWC as soon as his contract expired. Hunter debuted in the GCW at ‘Let the Games Begin’ in August of 1998, winning his first match against "G.Q." Elan Desire and Shawn "Rock" Hudson. It was during this match that his valet, then known as Miss Madison, made her first appearance. By this point the NWC had taken notice of this undefeated youngster. Hunter was fast becoming a fan favorite. In the era of big men, Hunter became a trailblazer for the new generation of high flyers emerging at the time. Quickly erasing the preconceived notion that you had to be a bigger man to be a draw in wrestling. More importantly the NWC’s wrestlers took notice of Hunter Sabuani’s promos. In the age of men sitting in front of a camera hurling insults at each other, Hunter did something much different. Hunter brought drama to the NWC and invented a ‘slice of life’ style that has been emulated by nearly every NWC wrestler since whether they know it or not. The feud with Elan Desire heated up, leading to the formation of the Crew in September 1998 with Sabuani and Indesson teaming with Hunter's friends Stagminn and LSW superstar Damon Hayes. They later added Jonathon Gunz to the mix to complete the stable. So what does a man do when he has a unbelievable fan following and a stable of the hottest young wrestlers in the council? He goes in search of titles. In October of 1998 Hunter defeated Goliath at ‘For All the Glory’ to become the very first GCW Georgia State Heavyweight Champion. Later that month at GCW's first pay-per-view Battle of Atlanta, he defeated "The Moronic" Joshua Lee to capture the National Wrestling Council World Heavyweight Championship, a title he held for three weeks before losing to Steve Sire in a battle of the NWC’s two most charismatic superstars. Following this loss, Hunter's career took a nosedive, with a long winless streak going from November all the way to February of 1999, including a clean loss to "The Strangler" Seth Bishop. Finally coming off the slide on the 27th of that month, Sabuani defeated Scorn for the GCW Southern Heritage title, which he held longer than any man has to date. The fortunes of his live-in girlfriend Madison increased, however, as her relation to the missing and presumed dead Devon Justice was revealed and she became half-owner of GCW. At NWC Across Enemy Lines 2, however, things started to change. Miss Madison came to the decision that Sabuani needed to defeat Tony Pride and Steve Sire that weekend, and she would do anything in her power to assure it. She cheated several times, but it was for naught, as Sire managed to steal the win and the NWC title. Over the ensuing weeks, however, Madison rode the current into darker and darker waters revealing a manipulative, Machiavellian side to herself that culminated in her orchestration of a complete takeover of GCW, using Hunter as her weapon. A few more weeks of solitude and careful planning and Madison managed to convince Sabuani that he was carrying an undue share of the burden in GCW and that he needed to write his own check. This led to the unthinkable -- Sabuani turned on Indesson in a tag-team match and joined his now-fiancee on the wrong side of the tracks. The NWC’s most popular hero had turned his back on the fans for love. With Madison Justice as the owner of GCW and Hunter Sabuani as its most marketable star, the lovers instigated a reign of terror in the NWC’s Georgia region. Even going as far as to recruit a band of like minded villains as his personal army, the Coterie. Hunter’s Coterie and his former cohorts in the Crew waged war though out the NWC’s Georgia and Texas regions and Sabuani had appeared to reach the height of his popularity and become the biggest star the NWC had ever seen. Despite constant threats by Sabuani’s most feared soldier, the masochistic Scorn, it was actually another NWC competitor who finally got their hands on Madison Justice. Jerome Jenkins, an accomplished wrestler and rival of Sabuani savagely beat Madison Justice. Suddenly the NWC’s most manipulative woman learned what it felt like to be vulnerable. This set Hunter on a path of revenge and also caused great strain on his relationship as Madison wanted to distance herself from the wrestling business. The wrestling worlds premiere couple’s problems only intensified as the NWC was fast approaching the Road To Purgatory tournament in early 2000. [name withheld], the NWC’s owner had come out of the shadows and seemingly was drawn into a war for control of the promotion he founded with a mysterious figure who would later be revealed as Desade. Every army needs soldiers and [name withheld] began recruiting with one of the NWC’s premiere tag teams, Jon Atlas and Tony Pride signing up as well as a young Eugene Robinson turning his back on the fans and aligning with the sinister owner. The recruitment process didn’t end there as [name withheld]’s Syndicate joined forces with the Uppermost Echelon of J.W. Oswald, Ben Van Iten, Collin Montgomery, and later Billy Page. With two of the three NWC mega-stables joining forces, the ever independent Hunter Sabuani found himself the odd man out. While technically a rulebreaker, Hunter’s in-ring performances and charisma made still had him ranking as the NWC’s most popular wrestler with fans, a fact that didn’t sit well with [name withheld] and his Syndicate-UME alliance. Things became even more complicated when [name withheld] and Desade agreed to a contest to be held at the NWC’s Road to Purgatory PPV. Desade would choose 8 fan favorites for her bracket and [name withheld] would choose 8 rulebreakers for his. The champion of each of their brackets would face off in the finals to decide once and for all who would own the NWC as well as the winner receiving a world title shot. Surprisingly [name withheld] picked Hunter to compete in his bracket. Things became even more complicated on the romantic front, partially due to this tournament. [name withheld] decided to assign his chosen champion, Eugene Robinson, to seduce Hunter’s estranged fiancé, Madison. Hunter had also was in the process of being pursued by someone else. Katsidy, the mysterious protégé of Desade, had decided that not only did she want to replace Madison Justice as the NWC’s premiere diva but that she also wanted to replace her at the side of Hunter. The NWC had chosen Venice, Italy to hold the massive Purgatory compound and to stage the PPV there. Hunter had flown to Italy without Madison, and upon checking in to his hotel room found an unexpected guest. Katsidy had conveniently mis-booked her reservation and was desperate for somewhere to stay. So Hunter reluctantly agreed to allow her to share his room. Unbeknownst to Hunter, Madison had also traveled to Venice. The appropriately titled Babe, had recently returned to wrestling with a new attitude and outlook on life. Gone was the conniving bitch, and in her stead the fans found a confident and beautiful young woman. Ironically the woman who seduced Hunter to a path of evil couldn’t convince him to change. While in Venice, Madison spotted Hunter sharing a lunch at a café with the ever scantily dressed sex kitten known as Katsidy. Assuming the worst, this event caused Madison to finally accept an offer of a dinner date with Eugene Robinson. While on her date with Robinson the pair enjoyed a ride in one of Venice’s famous gondolas. As tradition dictates any couple on a date are expected to kiss as they pass under one of the cities many bridges and as the pair did so they were spotted by none other than Hunter. The original Purgatory tournament was a 2-day, 16 man, cutthroat affair. Hunter Sabuani was by far [name withheld]’s least favorite entry from his side of the tournament and as such Hunter’s first round opponent was none other than a young and dangerous NWC star known as Ben Van Iten. Already having earned his first NWC world title, Van Iten was out to prove that it was no fluke and wanted to make his name at the expense of Sabuani. In a hard fought duel, Hunter defeated the young upstart and set his sights on the remainder of [name withheld]’s field. Reaching the finals of his bracket, Hunter stared across the ring from none other than [name withheld]’s chosen champion, Eugene Robinson. Exacting a measure of revenge by defeating Robinson, Sabuani advanced to the tournament finals. Facing Hunter in the finals was a competitor he was extremely familiar with, martial artist and former friend, Hyuk Suh Kim. To further complicate matters, Kim had recently begun being managed by Hunter’s fiancé, Madison Justice. The former friends engaged in a fast paced Iron man match, which even saw Madison interfere to aide Kim against a rulebreaking Sabuani. Hunter finally defeated Kim, and not only secured the NWC for his hated rival, [name withheld], but also secured an NWC World title shot for himself. The NWC’s most popular star had a date with the World championship and had helped declare a winner in a war that is just reaching its end four years later. Back in Georgia, trouble continued between Hunter and Madison. All was brought to a head as after weeks of cat fights and insults Madison and Katsidy were scheduled to face each other in a sanctioned GCW match. The special referee? Hunter Sabuani. The match wasn’t what anyone expected as the seductive and manipulative Katsidy also revealed that she was a trained fighter and easily began dominating Madison. At a key juncture in the match, with Hunter finally seeing enough and obviously not happy with seeing the woman he loved pummeled, he stepped in to make the save. Or did he? Just when everyone expected Hunter to save Madison, he instead turned on her viciously beating his fiancé. Madison Justice was gone from his life, destroyed by the monster she had created. Any other woman would have faded into obscurity, but Madison refused to be chased away from wrestling again. She continued managing Kim, and generally began making Hunter’s life a little less comfortable. Even going as far as befriending Ben Van Iten, who had recently won the World title for the second time in his young career and was headed for a collision with Hunter at Friday Night Fury TV special. Friday Night Fury was billed as an absolute epic. Like Sire vs Sabuani before, once again Hunter found himself in a battle between the NWC’s two most charismatic figures as well as two generations. The difference was that now Hunter was the older and more experienced wrestler. After weeks and weeks of build-up and a virtual war between the factions of the Syndicate-UME and the Coterie the battle all came to a head. In the final battle of what many consider the greatest feud in NWC history, Hunter Sabuani and Ben Van Iten put on one of the greatest matches in NWC history. After nearly an hour and amidst much Syndicate interference, Hunter was defeated by Van Iten in what many consider a passing of the proverbial torch. A lesser man would have simply faded away much like Steve Sire did when he was beaten by his protégé, J.W. Oswald, but Hunter Sabuani was not normal men. Deciding that the Coterie would be hunted and systematically destroyed by those making the rules, Hunter decided that they needed equal footing. With [name withheld] all but disappearing from the NWC his power was left in the hands of his daughter, Kara Holbrook. Sabuani had Katsidy befriend the young and impressionable Holbrook and suddenly Hunter Sabuani now wielded as much political clout as the Syndicate-UME faction. At Across Enemy Lines 3, and event legendary for its unaired main event strangely enough, Hunter Sabuani set out to steal the spotlight from the Syndicate, who appeared to be fading away amidst a war with its former allies in the UME. With Tony Pride retiring after his J-crown victory, Hunter did the unthinkable and declared that his valet, Katsidy, was the new J-crown champion, tarnishing what was at that point the NWC’s most proud title. Katsidy wasn’t without surprises of her own as she introduced Hunter to his new protégé, NWC newcomer Devin Fox. Trouble instantly arose within the Coterie with Fox, the younger brother of Katsidy, added to the mix. Katsidy maintained the peace between the Coterie’s two alpha males, but it was obvious from the start that a bizarre relationship of competition existed between Sabuani and Fox. The two men even going as far as to make wagers on things such as “Who can cripple Jake Summers first without even laying a finger on him?” It became obvious that an era was coming to an end as GCW was steadily heading towards its demise. Nearly caught up in the spirit of nostalgia, Hunter Sabuani began second guessing some of the decisions he had made over the last few months. While Hunter was deep in thought various members of the Coterie, led by Katsidy and Devin Fox, were convinced that it was their time to take action. This effectively split the group between those loyal to Sabuani and those who felt they should be more aggressive. Shockingly Hunter began making overtures for reconciliation with Madison, a move that wasn’t appreciated by Madison’s 7’ foot bodyguard and best friend, Darien Thorsen. GCW, the organization built around Sabuani, had decided to finally close its doors. To cap off the event a tournament had been initiated to determine who would be the final champion of the organization. Hunter advanced to the finals in one bracket, and the other was won by Scorn, Hunter’s horrific second in command and a man who seemed to live for pain. On the first night of Burning of Atlanta, Hunter challenged Thorsen to a match to determine whether Sabuani would be allowed to speak to Madision. The two men fought viciously, one to protect Madison and the other in a bizarre attempt to win back her heart. While fighting atop a cage, suddenly disaster struck as both men fell through. Thorsen injured his knee and was permanently put out of competition. Hunter was also injured badly as his shoulder was dislocated and possibly even worse than that. Injured and at his lowest, Katsidy, Fox and their new Coterie turned their backs on Sabuani declaring that he was done. Strangely the only person who seemed concerned about Hunter, was the woman he betrayed, Madision Justice. That is until Scorn arrived. Always possessed with a grotesque hatred for Madison for her flawless beauty and the fact that she stood for everything that he never could be, Scorn decided to destroy her once and for all. That is until the injured Sabuani rose to fight off Scorn and save the woman he loved. Thus the stage was set for an epic battle. Scorn fighting an injured Sabuani, who had seemingly turned his back on all the edicts of pain and misery that he had spent months corrupting Sabuani with. Hunter on the other hand fought to protect the woman he would always love from a monster and final redemption for all that he had done wrong in his NWC career. Fighting injured, Hunter put on a display that can only be described as inspirational as nothing Scorn hit him with seemed to stop him. The pain was however to much to bear as eventually he was forced to succumb to the dangerous and vile Scorn. After the match scorn sought out Madison, in an attempt to finish what he started, but Hunter rose again. Fighting Scorn off and stealing the GCW title that he had seemingly made a highly respectable title, Hunter collapsed clutching the title, a fitting end to an NWC era. If Hunter Sabuani’s life had been a Hollywood movie, it would have ended there. A tragic hero redeemed in the end, but real life isn’t that kind. With most of the GCW roster moving to New York and LCW, Hunter Sabuani decided to retire amidst his shoulder injury. Many had hoped that he would someday return to get revenge on his traitorous valet and protégé, but it was another LCW superstar who would lure Sabuani out of retirement. Nearly from the moment Sabuani announced his retirement, reputed NWC tough guy, GD West, immediately began taunting and challenging Sabuani to return to face him. Sabuani wasn’t biting however, even when he was assaulted by West on a visit to the LCW while visiting Madison who had relocated to New York to start a new life for herself. Eventually a highflying rookie by the name of Nobody began appearing in LCW. When LCW held its first PPV, Nobody was scheduled to face GD West. Unmasking to the shock and delight of the crowd Nobody was Hunter Sabuani who managed to defeat West. This only served to infuriate West who decided that he would be the man to finally end the long and storied career of Hunter Sabuani. It was decided that the two men would face in an “I Quit” match at the NWC: Epic PPV. West viciously worked Sabuani’s injured shoulder and Madison, appearing once again in Sabuani’s corner, tossed in the towel. This loss didn’t put an end to this feud as Sabuani challenged West to face him one last time on the next LCW PPV. In a hard fought duel, Hunter out wrestled his brash challenger and finally put an end to the Sabuani-West feud. Or so we all thought. With Hunter Sabuani being named as the challenger for the NWC world title at Across Enemy Lines 4, Hunter realized that this was probably his last chance to regain the title that Steve Sire cheated him of so long ago. There was one complication however as GD West upset Collin Montgomery to become the World champion. Once again GD West and Hunter Sabuani would be forced to fight each other, and this time more than their pride was on the line. In a grueling match that tested the mettle of both men, Hunter Sabuani finally regained the title that he was denied for so many years. The West-Sabuani feud was over, with Hunter proving that he could still be a force with the younger competitors of the NWC. His mission accomplished, Hunter began to think more and more of retirement, and when Wyatt Connors upset him for the World title it became obvious to Hunter that his time was over. Hunter faded into retirement and the career of the man who changed the game and the way people view the NWC was finally over. Hunter Sabuani proved to be a true NWC Legend.
