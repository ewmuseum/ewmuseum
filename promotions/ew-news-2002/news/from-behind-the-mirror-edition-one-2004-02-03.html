"From Behind The Mirror" Edition One

3 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Finally, it is my turn to give you the truth on the things everyone wants to hear about. This week, I provide the facts about Hype and the good influences as well as the bad influences it has in wrestling. Welcome readers of the internet wrestling realm to the first edition to hopefully many editorials of “From Behind The Mirror.” As much as everyone tends to feel that things in life are always presented fairly, equally and without certain favoritism, no one can deny that people do have tendencies and that is what makes us all human. The ability to communicate through speech and sign, the ability to think, and the ability to express emotions, they all play an important part in each of our lives. It is only natural to have opinions and to want to express them. The objective presented here is to take an issue and explain it not in an opinionated manner, but in a factual manner. By taking real facts and figures, statistics and making historical references when needed, it is my objective to provide the most unbiased and straight-forward answer to the topic of discussion. At certain times, an opinion or two may slip in and my best efforts will be made to identify that it was in fact an opinion and my best effort will be made to keep from making too many opinions. In order to keep this as truthful as possible that is what is needed. With all that said, the first topic of discussion in “From Behind The Mirror” has been prior publicized as “the current situation over hype in wrestling.” There is no medium out in the world that does not provide hype in some way, shape or form. And to quickly make anyone unaware, now aware, hype is in more common words the publication of some sort of information and advertising to build up a product that is either getting set to be put on the market or is already available to the public. It is simple evidence that to be in the public eye and make a name for yourself or a company one must promote a product. Nothing that goes unknown and stays below the public eye will be amounted to success on a regional, national, or global level. Companies in the world may start out small, but as they gain credibility all are pushed to the top by one thing. That one thing is advertising, or as wrestling knows it as hype. Recently, the debate seems to be going on in the wrestling community as to whether or not hype is something that helps or hurts a promotion that is coming into the industry. Those two views can each provide their own valid reasons as to how hype can affect a product in a good or bad way. After careful investigation, it is in my effort to provide some detailed explanations as to how hype can help and hurt a promotion. In a list of five things good and five things bad about hype, as the reader you can determine your own conclusion as to the ultimate right answer in this rather undulating topic. Five Good Things About Hype 1. Exposure: A key element in beginning the road to success. It is the one thing that any product that wants to become the best, or at least contend with the best, has to have in order to receive attention. Whether the attention is deserved or not, it is exposure that will ultimately bring about that decision by the attention that is paid to the product. Exposure can also come in the way of sponsors who take a liking to the product and want to help endorse in their established atmosphere. 2. Fan-Base: Another big element in obtaining success. Coincidentally, it is brought about by exposure to the world by hype. The amount of attention and fans a promotion has will determine even more exposure for that promotion. Fans help to determine how well your product is doing and tell you exactly what is good and bad about the things coming out of your company. Next to financial bankruptcy and lack of employees, fan-base is the number one thing that will either make or break a company. 3. Veteran/Skilled Talent: With the exposure and fan base, and even money, a well-promoted company will draw more skilled talent and veterans of the business. Wrestlers are not stupid; they like to be recognized for their skill and the different abilities that they possess. Having more talent, and more importantly higher caliber talent, a promotion can head to the top. 4. Success: How can you leave out the biggest thing hype can lead to? It is without a doubt success. Yes, not in all cases. But it is the greatest benefit from hype. When you put the four other aspects together, along with dozens of other little factors, you can eventually end up with a product that the public grows to respect, endorse and solicit. While it certainly may not be guaranteed to all; if promoters can play their cards right, success is only a matter of time away. 5. Respectful Reputation: With success a company can gain a good amount of recognition. The recognition given can help to build a reputation for the Owner, executives, and wrestlers involved in the company. That makes it easier for someone to get a job somewhere else, if in the case that the company ever takes a turn for the worse. At the same time, it helps to gain respect from other companies, their workers and independent works and the fans. A reputation is a great thing to have and a terrible thing to waste. It allows a company to try new things and, if in the event something does not always work out as planned, the company will be less criticized based on a better reputation. Five Bad Things About Hype 1. Over-Exposure: Some people may question the ability to overly expose something, but it is done. Too many times, companies spend more time working on promoting something than they do actually presenting the product itself. Over-exposure can be done in a manner of advertising for months and months without having anything to provide potential fans or endorsers with. Some people become sick of the hype about something just because all that surrounds it. 2. High Expectations: The bar for a promotion that exhibits too much hype can be raised beyond reach. It can be set unconventionally high to the point where expectations are completely unreal. High expectations can be a bad thing when a company does not have the talent or the resources at the time to produce the product that people are looking for. 3. Disappointment: Sure, hype can be a great thing and lure people to a company or wrestling promotion. It is the feared reaction of disappointment that can occur and negatively affect the mounting success of something. If fans do not like the action produced by a company following the hype surrounding it, a company will be left to make explanations as to why it did not deliver. Hype has to be carefully planned and only promise and advertise what a company can provide. 4. Failure: It is the one thing no one ever wants to end up having to admit. When something fails it is never good and it gives off more disappointment to people. Failure can be brought on by disinterest from overly exposing the product being hyped. This is why it remains extremely important for people to carefully plan and work towards success, not immediately expect to achieve it with a product. 5. Tarnished Reputations: People remember the good things that people do, and at the same time they do not easily forget the bad things. People that do not have a reputation for success in the past will be tarnished with a reputation that people recognize for bringing about failure in the event that a company fails. That brunt falls mostly on the shoulders of the Owner, but wrestlers and other employees of a failed company must also be prepared for a bit of hesitation on the part of other companies to offer them jobs in some cases. When you take the two sides and you put them head-to-head there are great deal of things that appear to be just the opposite. But sometimes, it is the failure that beats out success, despite the fact they are antonyms. A lot of people take success and measure it based on the failures of others and while some people may succeed with one thing, another big product could fail and a past success will be forgotten because of that recent failure. No matter what, people must remember that it is a rough world and that the opinions of fans and critics heavily determine the success and failure of products. It is never easy to see exactly where hype will take a product and while the keys above show the potential affects of hype, they do not show exactly what can cause a good or bad reaction from critics, fans, and the general population on hype. The reaction solely lies on the information that is presented while hyping a company on the brink of emerging, and even one that is already in full-swing. From taking points on what to do and what not to do, potential Owners and wrestlers can have a good understanding and even an advantage over prior challengers. Some points that I have been able to gain, not based on opinion but based on looking at different companies that have tried to hype and gauging the reactions that have gotten, may be able to help those looking to try for themselves. Such ideas as public press conferences, weekly interviews, commercials over a small time, and just advertising exactly what will be offered have worked. These ideas have at times failed, but that happened to be because of uninteresting material provided in the different works. Instead of going on about just how great a company will be, majority of attempts have led to the belief that fans and critics want to hear what exactly the company has in the way of weekly television, talent rosters, championships, and additional benefits in the way of money and awards at times. Too many companies have, and some still do, attempt to sell themselves as being “the best” and that immediately has turned off fans. When a company turns off fans with that simple, over exaggerated line, it breeds the way for only one thing and that is failure. Simple facts show that everyone can believe they are the best but proving to be the best is a different deal and it is harder to do. Through showing dedication, reliable information, and high-quality work, a company can rise and live up to the hype it started out as. In many cases, hyped products can grow beyond their hype and end up bigger than imaged. Easy examples that will be given to those who ask the question “What companies are successful?” are places like Fans Wrestling Organization(fWo), Primetime Championship Wrestling(PCW), Action!, New True Internet Championship Wrestling(NTICW), among many more of both the past and present. The next question to ask: How is success measured and determined? Or more importantly, of what do fans measure the promotions that they are fond? Of course, to answer those questions now would be too easy and it would defeat the purpose of another column. As one thing plays into another, readers now must wait to see the answers and supported facts that which the truth behind those questions reveals. Rising Grappler For the first named Rising Grappler, you must turn to a rather under spoken promotion in wrestling. Setting himself aside from most independent workers and rookies, Jason Hughes has become a main event caliber superstar in Core Wrestling after only being present with the organization for four months. Though he has yet to capture the Core Championship, Jason has won other awards and seems to be on his way to the main event in the next few months. His devotion to Core since the day he signed his contract is really a one of a kind quality that you do not find in many young stars. If you were to ask his fellow employees and his employer, Matt Lanier, Jason Hughes has been described as being “irreplaceable” for the attitude that he brings and the quality of work in which he provides for Core. Perhaps there are other wrestlers out there worthy of being known for their achievements, but for the work that Hughes has done in only four months as a member of Core Wrestling, he takes the inaugural award of “Rising Grappler” on this February 3 edition of “From Behind The Mirror.” On The Spot With great remorse for this announcement, it is here that I make it known that Tony Vigilante, advertised as this week’s first ever guest, was unable to find the time to sit down. Many know that Vigilante has been recently laid off from Furiously Addictive Wrestling and dealing with other troubles even. He contacted myself personally just days ago to make the cancellation and the decision was made not to make a replacement call. Instead, Vigilante has given his word to be present on the next edition of “On The Spot” which will be held on the February 17 edition of “From Behind The Mirror.” The Closing Words Regardless of your personal opinions and feelings towards myself in my recent week’s comments, I leave to understand that my words are not intended to threaten, assault, or humiliate for my own benefit. Anything that I do and ever will say is done in order to be put across a point and in most cases that point involves the truth. I do leave you with my sincere hopes that you enjoyed and were intrigued by this new addition to our wrestling world. I look forward to the next edition on February 17 and continuing to receive e-mail at [detail withheld] in reply to this column.
