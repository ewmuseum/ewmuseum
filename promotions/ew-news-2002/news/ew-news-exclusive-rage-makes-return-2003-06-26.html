EW-News Exclusive: RaGE Makes Return?

26 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It is a story you will see no where else. EW-News has it and it is one that no wrestling fan can pass up. Real and Graphic Entertainment disappeared on June 4, and this article answers the question on everyone's mind... Ladies and Gentlemen, wrestling fans alike, this is an article that is brought to you exclusively for EW-News.com. No where else will you find the words, the reasons, the truth behind this said topic. The only place that gives you the truth behind this controversial and rather interesting breaking news is EW-News.com along with the help of Real and Graphic Entertainment, the topic of this Breaking Story. A quick backtrack to March 1, 2003, as Real and Graphic Entertainment entered the market of the wrestling industry and gained moderate and rather immediate attention thanks in part to the growing influence under Tom Mason's name. Tom Mason, of course, is the owner and active CEO/Spokesman for Real and Graphic Entertainment, and it is due mostly to him that this story is possible. But if anyone can remember the debut of RaGE in March, the company was steadily climbing under an umbrella of two names: Version Two Wrestling and No Boundaries Wrestling. Up until recently those two names had produced a combine product of over twenty(20) televised shows from March to the end of May. An impressive number of shows produced solely on the Fox Network with the cooperation with Rupert Murdoch and his company. Added to that number is the four Pay Per View Specials that Real and Graphic Entertainment aired from that time as well. Those numbers would soon coincide as the executive decision was to unite the company under one name and continue with a Wednesday evening programming show entitled "Fearless". That seems simple enough right? Well, it did indeed seem simple on paper, but what would happen on June 4, 2003, the day that Fearless was scheduled to debut shifted exactly what Real and Graphic Entertainment had planned. That day something happened that affected the company's future production, until now. The strangest part of all this was that no one, absolutely no one, outside of Real and Graphic Entertainment and those few other executives of companies involved, knew nothing about this. But why was this, or what was this that was happening? Simply enough, it appeared that much like in the case of Viacom's The National Network(TNN), Real and Graphic Entertainment was becoming subject to a lawsuit over the name of its product. As Spike-TV was being assaulted by a ridiculous injunction by Spike Lee, Johnnie Cochran, and the judge of that case, the same could be said for Real and Graphic Entertainment. The only difference between these two suits were the fact that Johnnie Cochran was not involved as the lawyer for these opposing companies to RaGE. Another difference in the cases was presented in the fact that RaGE was under attack from more than one company, three to be precise. Lost are you? The name of Real and Graphic Entertainment(RaGE) was being put into question by three other companies that contained a form of the name "Rage" in their corporate names. Those companies included Rage Software, which back in 1999 and 2000 was a leader in computer software production; TheRage.com, an online company that sells video games from Sony, Nintendo, and Microsoft, and Rage Communications, a company that spends its time as a Internet website promoter, developing sites for those that require services. Together, these three companies posed a lawsuit against Real and Graphic Entertainment on June 4, halting the production of any and all Real and Graphic Entertainment subsidiaries. That decision right there was the reason for the immediate stoppage and non-excused disappearance of Real and Graphic Entertainment. For weeks, both Fox and RaGE have kept fans and sponsors in the dark over this issue, simply calling it a "problem that will be resolved in a matter of days." Quite obviously, this matter was not resolved in days, but rather it has taken weeks. Over the coming weeks, a secret and non-publicized lawsuit was being conducted in the court halls of Atlantic City, New Jersey. Though it is hard to believe, somehow these four conglomerates managed to avoid the public's eye, until now. This article is as exclusive as anything can get, being the first to present to facts to the public. Through those three weeks that this trial went on, all three companies tried to present their own cases, and a collective case, against Real and Graphic Entertainment. The presiding judge of this case, who has denied the use of his name in this publication, listened without the use of a jury, which also helped to keep this trial away from spreading to the public. All three plaintiffs claim that for three months Real and Graphic Entertainment has hurt their business and lowered their public image. Finally, the judge was able to reach a verdict on June 25, in which he declared the case "an utter and ridiculous waste of both his and the US Federal Court System's time." With those comments, the gavel was used to bang the plate on his pedestal and he motioned for a dismissal of the trial, stating that Real and Graphic Entertainment would indeed be able to retain its name and resume its productions as it was doing prior to this ordeal. In addition, Real and Graphic Entertainment Owner Tom Mason reported to the judge that the company has lost nearly $10 million because of this suit. That number was derived on the fact that RaGE still had to pay Fox for it's Wednesday evening spot, sponsors conceded their contracts, wrestlers asked for releases, and tickets had to be refunded. That report was enough to draw one million dollars in backing from each of the three companies that were involved against Real and Graphic Entertainment by order of the judge. The judge also ruled that those companies would collectively provide Real and Graphic Entertainment with another 2.4 million by July 6, 2003. As far as any appeals on the court decision of the judge, there are none expected. Due to the case being thrown out by the judge after hearing the side of all three plaintiffs, there is no possible way for them to provide a retrial or appeal the decision. The judge has said that if they can produce proper proof of losses to their companies since March, there is a possibility of him hearing this case and taking it a little more seriously. The three companies have until September to produce the papers to the judge in order to make any attempt at an appeal and they must wait until at least August 1. How did all this information finally leak to the public? It was leaked after Tom Mason decided to come out of a three week "hiding" and inform Fox, his sponsors, and the media at EW-News.com about this long and tedious month. Due to the positive outcome for Real and Graphic Entertainment, Tom Mason had the following to say when he contacted EW-News and was asked about the return of his company: "There is no doubt about it; Real and Graphic Entertainment is coming back. I have made sure that we will indeed make up for our company's losses and the disappointment that we have delivered to our fans. Anything as far as hiring new workers will be handled in the next week, our website will be back up and running, and I am ready to get myself back into my leather chair and work at my desk. It is time once again for Real and Graphic Entertainment to produce the entertainment that it did from March to the beginning of June. We can only think of this as a break and just continue business with the company as it was before this whole situation came to be." The rather confident attitude that Tom Mason has on this situation does indeed bring some comfort to the many that have been interested and drawn themselves towards RaGE in the past months. Right now, EW-News.com can report that Real and Graphic Entertainment will be returning with Fearless every Wednesday night for two hours, and on Sunday nights, Uncensored will make its long awaited debut on Fox for one hour each week. This show will be the RaGE version of an independent wrestling promotion's attempt at a successful television show, which should be something interesting to look out for. As far as Fearless One, the debut that was expected on June 6, 2003, Tom Mason commented on saying that he is working a deal with Rupert Murdoch to give Real and Graphic Entertainment time to redeem itself with a special presentation of that show which is said to be a hell of a production. We have indeed been told to stay on our heels and wait for that show, which should air sometime in the next week, setting up the return of Fearless on July 5, as it will be a special edition to allow the company to settle back in. For more information on Real and Graphic Entertainment, visit Rageproject.com and the Community Forums located there. Stay tuned to EW-News for more exclusive coverage of Real and Graphic Entertainment and watch the RaGE website for a rumored new design in the coming weeks. Real and Graphic Entertainment
