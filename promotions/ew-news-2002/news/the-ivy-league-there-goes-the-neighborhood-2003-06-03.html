The Ivy League: There goes the neighborhood.

3 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hi! How are ya? Give me five seconds and I'll catch your attention. Ten seconds to make you love me. Fifteen to make you hate me. Twenty, and you'll come back to read me every single time I write. So if you're anything like me, you're sitting in the car from one shot to the next, going over a stack of paperwork from various record promotions for a highly talented band you're trying to get signed, you're looking over the schedule of interviews that one or both wrestling promotions you work for want you to produce, and you're trying to figure out what to write for the promotion- specific columns you have a deadline for. Never mind even trying to have a personal life in there, too... spend some time with my better half or my baby niece. Oh yeah, my bosses are Mickey Mouse and Chad Merritt. Pity me. But through all of that, I found myself in a quandary - eleven whole minutes a week to myself! What to do with that time? Then... Damon Trask called me and guilted me into writing for his website like I promised I would do. And if you've ever heard Damon Trask beg... it's really pathetic. So here I am, your humble Psycho Bitch, newest columnist for The News. For those of you who're familiar with my work in the past, you know what you're looking at. For those of you who might not be... who am I kidding? Everyone knows who I am. Right? In any case, don't expect to see a new one of these every week. I don't have the time or the energy, you don't have the patience. There's precious little positivity going on around the wrestling business today. I say 'around' because upwards of 95 percent of locker rooms have remained largely unchanged in matters of attitude since the dawn of time. Most of the boys are of the attitude to just go out, have a good match, and get paid. And if you happen to get over, you make more money and get motivated enough to have a better match - it's a neat little cycle that always pushes a person to perform better. Unfortunately, it's the vocal minority that brings the Wrestling Webgeeks outta the woodwork to call for the head of the Prime Offender. And it's always the same story - this guy sucks because he can't work, this guy is great because he can. And any time the person that the Wrestling Webgeeks like isn't given the 'GODPUSH', it's the worst thing ever. What the hardcore fan has forgotten is that this is supposed to be sports entertainment. When the fans are calling for Voran Xias' head, it's because he's doing his job and doing it well. When the fans are throwing garbage at Tom Adler, it's because he has a role in the CSWA and he's playing it to perfection. Voran is not the source of all evil. Tom Adler is not the source of all evil. You want to know why you're not entertained? Take a look in the mirror. Instead of complaining about how Hornet and Mark Windham are both washed up and over the hill and shouldn't be main eventing the CSWA's upcoming BATTLE of the BELTS, just take a look at what they've both been doing in the past year and realize that they deserve to be where they are. Instead of clamoring for new faces in the fWo's main event scene, give Kodiak Vic Creed a chance to be Champion. And also realize that just last night, Eli Flair won the rights to face him at Meltdown for that belt. Instead of bitching over the fact that the two men in the Battle of the Belts main event also happened to wrestle on the first card in CSWA history, there's a whole slew of hungry midcarders just waiting for the champion to be decided so they can go for it. Former Champions Evan Aho and Eli Flair are the least of their worries - what about Triple X? What about Mike Plett? What about Dan Ryan? Lest I fail to mention the man I feel will lead the CSWA into the future once the old guard does eventually retire - what about Shane Southern? Do yourself a favor - the next time you watch a professional wrestling show, presumably because you're a fan - let yourself be a fan. There's no harm in enjoying what we put on television every week, we want to entertain. We want you to feel like you've gotten your money's worth. We want you to debate the good and bad points of every single athlete and noncombatant sent out to the ring. But I draw the line at armchair bookers who think they know the business better than the people who are in the business. Everything has its positives and negatives. And if you can't see that... then do us both a favor. Change the channel. This is Ivy.
