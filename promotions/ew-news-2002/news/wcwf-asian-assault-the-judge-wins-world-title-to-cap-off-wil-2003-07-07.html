WCWF Asian Assault: The Judge wins World title to cap off wild night

7 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Shocking returns, brutal retribution, and a history-making performance highlighted the WCWF’s annual trip overseas as Asian Assault aired on pay-per-view on June 29th from Osaka, Japan. Before the opening battle royal started, Dan Bonez came to ringside and announced that he was pulling out of the match because, in his words, “These other chumps aren't even worthy to clean out the toilet in my apartment!” So he took a seat at the commentary table as “Rapid” Rick Reno, Charlie Owens, Chris Blair, “The King” James Michaels and “Misfit” Jonny Morgan battled for a shot at the International Television Title. The match cane down to Reno and Morgan, and Bonez made his move. He left the table, the lights went out, and when they came back up, Reno and Morgan were laid out in the ring — and standing above then were Bonez, JD, and Steel! The legendary Beat Down Crew had returned! From there, the open invitational for the Hardcore Title seemed to start, as champion Brad Jackson fought Reno to ringside. Well, “fought” may be a relative term as Reno, who looked none the worse after the BDC attack, actually got a two-count on the champion before Jackson turned the tables and threw him into a bank of electrical equipment below the stage. But Jackson was caught off guard again, this time by a returning Thump, who was sitting in the audience and took him down with a spear! They fought on the entrance ramp until Jackson got the upper hand by focusing on Thump’s still-injured knee. He was about to crush Thump’s knee under the ring steps when Matt Walker launched himself down the ramp, spearing Jackson against the ring apron. But rather than go for the pin, Walker helped his fallen tag partner to the back. He then came back out to team with Raymond Chandler for the two out of three falls, two-title tag match. Dane Rennier and Stan Stone attacked before the bell, with Rennier getting the early advantage on Walker. The tables turned, and Walker and Chandler kept Rennier isolated while working on his lower back before things settled down to Rennier and Walker. After a submission exchange and an extended running sequence, Rennier went for the Thunderstruck, but Walker dodged the move, grabbed Rennier, and pinned him with Beat The System to win the U.S. Title! Rennier found himself in even more trouble when Chandler re-entered the match, but he countered the PDB with a tornado DDT to buy himself some time, then took it to Chandler with more of his patented high flying before connecting with the Dane-O-Might, then the Final Eclipse, and winning Chandler’s International Television Title. He then tagged in Stone, who went after Chandler like a rabid pit bull. Chandler took advantage of Stone’s recklessness by wearing him down with chain wrestling, transitioning from move to move like few in the WCWF can. Stone freed himself and went after Chandler again, but Chandler caught him, and THIS time, the PDB found its mark and got the pinfall. Oh, and about that extra stipulation that Nathanial Duke was going to announce for that match… there wasn’t one. If Brad Jackson thought his night was over after attacks from Reno and Thump, he was wrong, as Shawn Stevens, who hadn’t been seen since Opposites Attract, found him on the upper level of the arena. After some brawling, Jackson threw Stevens off of a landing down to ringside, then followed him down with a flying elbow drop. It got a TWO count. After taking a minute or eight to rest, they began fighting at ringside. Stevens set up a chair on the Spanish announce table, then avoided a spear from Jackson, picked him up, and delivered Suicidal Tendencies (top-rope jackknife powerbomb) through the table! With that, Jackson’s four-month Hardcore title reign came to an end, and perhaps just as important, the title returned to 24/7 rules. This brought us to the main event and a match that’s been brewing for nine months — Jester vs. The Judge for the world title. In a strange occurrence, Stevens, and not the Judge’s usual LKO crew of Atom, Nuke, and Dante Hightower, accompanied Judge to ringside. What started out as a technical exhibition turned into a surprising display of power from the unpredictable, but smaller, champion Jester. He controlled the pace after a while, getting several near falls until Judge maneuvered out of a dragon sleeper. Judge went for the Hung Jury, but Jester dropped down and hooked him up for his Punchline reverse DDT, but Judge lifted out of it and hit the Hung Jury. Three seconds later, The Judge had won his sixth WCWF World championship. The question now is: with Judge as the champ, and the majority of the titles out of the Fates’ hands… what will Nathanial Duke’s next move be? WCWF Asian Assault Results: Osaka Dome, Osaka, Japan June 29, 2003 Battle Royal for International Television title shot: “Misfit” Jonny Morgan & “Rapid” Rick Reno declared co-winners after double knockout; both will face INTV Champion next week Best of Three Falls Tag Match for the U.S. and INTV Titles; Raymond Chandler (c-INTV) & Matt Walker d. Dane Rennier (c-U.S.) & Stan Stone, 2-1: Fall 1: Walker d. Rennier by pinfall; Walker wins second U.S. Championship Fall 2: Rennier d. Chandler by pinfall; Rennier wins fifth INTV/International/Television championship (combined reigns with all three titles) Fall 3: Chandler d. Stone by pinfall Hardcore Invitational: Shawn Stevens d. Brad Jackson (c) by pinfall; Stevens wins second Hardcore championship; title returns to 24/7 rules World Title Match: Judge d. Jester (c) by pinfall; Judge wins sixth World championship Event Notes: Judge’s six World Title reigns breaks a tie he held with Nathanial Duke… Judge has been world champion in each year of the WCWF’s existence… Jackson’s Hardcore title reign was the second-longest in WCWF history, behind Nathanial Duke’s 150-day reign as Redemptor in 2000.
