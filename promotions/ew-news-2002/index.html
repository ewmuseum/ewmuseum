An interfederation news desk that ran the hobby's news strictly in character, by written rule, and refused to print the word "e-wrestling" — and that also laid fifty-five federations' weekly shows out as a television schedule and rated one federation a month on a published rubric. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The News opened on 2 January 2002 with a post called "Welcome to The News," and the first thing it told its readers was how it would be allowed to read. "Everything must be in-character. If anything has the letter 'E' before 'wrestling', we won't run the story." The second rule was no shilling — nothing that sounded "even close to 'Come join my fed'." For the two years it ran, the hobby's interfederation news desk was written as if the hobby were an industry, by a rule it wrote down on its first day and printed again on its submission page in 2003.

It came in two shapes. The first was a staffed newsroom: fifteen bylines across 968 records between January 2002 and early February 2003, the editor writing 575 of them. Then came a gap of nearly four months in which nothing survives, and the second shape, a submission wire — 1,068 stories between 30 May 2003 and 25 February 2004 under 375 bylines, filed by anyone who read the posting rules and agreed to the republication clause. Its own rules refused news for any federation that had not yet posted a card.

Around the news it built the instruments of a trade paper. A TV Guide set fifty-five federations' weekly shows on a broadcast grid of forty-four stations, with Primetime Central as a pay-per-view channel. A Spotlight rated one federation a month on four published axes — roster talent, show quality, star power, longevity. A weekly Top Ten had a guest wrestler name the ten best in the business, and the page said in so many words that the picks were not the staff's. There was a Match of the Week, a Pin Up of the Week, a Hall of Fame that inducted a single character, and an awards show, "The Newsies?", that was announced with a host and never held.

Its masthead was a costume, and it said so. Five staff writers with staged careers — a ring announcer from a defunct federation, a German hardcore veteran who retired in 1993 after thirty-five years, a wrestler whose career a replaced knee ended, a fan who grew into writing, and a freelance field reporter whose name is a Frank Zappa album — stood in for a masthead, and the disclaimer spoke for all of them: "Some of it may be blatant lies. That is a fact we will not dispute."

It stopped without a notice. The last real story is dated 25 February 2004. On 17 April its reporter list was rewritten as "FWrestling.com Staff Reporters," the same four personas still titled eWNews Reporter, and the last rows its engine took include tests filed under that site's section label. Whether that was a handover or only a refit is not in what survives. Its About page said it had been established in 2001; its first surviving record is from 2002.
