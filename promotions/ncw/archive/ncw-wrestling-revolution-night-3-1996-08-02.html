[NCW] WRESTLING REVOLUTION NIGHT 3

2 August 1996
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
W R E S T L I N G R E V O L U T I O N R E C A P [Sitting in front of a desk is NCW's own Jeremy Tuttle. Behind him on a monitor is the NCW Logo. It changes to the Wrestling Revolution logo and has the word RECAP underneath it.] Fans, welcome back to NCW's recap of the events of WRESTLING REVOLUTION! Tonight we'll see the events that transpired on Night Three. There was two extra matches on the card than expected. Since the World Tag Team belts and the International Tag Team Belts had been unified by Sudden Impact, and two teams had legitimate claims to a title shot. The Ghetto Boyz had been voted to get a shot at Sudden Impact's International Tag Team Titles, and the returning Damage Incorporated had been voted to get a shot at the World Tag Team Titles. Both teams demanded a shot at Sudden Impact. To settle the matter, an extra match was added to the card. The Ghetto Boyz would face Damage Inc. The winner of that match would get the title shot later in the night against the champs. Also added to the card was a grudge match resulting from the events that took place at Night Two between "Iceman" Brian Knight and "Heartbreaker" Matt Foxx. All this, plus the title defenses and more. Let's go to the action that happened at The Pyramid in Memphis, Tennessee on July 27th. NIGHT THREE: 14 Matches 1. Cardiac vs. Bernadette Martin 2. Badd Company vs. Roundhouse 3. Lady Rose vs. Miko "Steele Rose" Mizadori 4. Airborn vs. Hellfire 5. Pestilence vs. Rebecca "Dark Star" Maddick 6. TITLE SHOT MATCH Damage Incorporated vs. Ghetto Boyz 7. GRUDGE MATCH "Iceman" Brian Knight vs. "Heartbreaker" Matt Foxx 8. JUNIOR'S TITLE DEFENSE Overgiver vs. "The Preppie" Tiffany Van Heusen 9. INTERNET TITLE DEFENSE Thomas N. Tyler vs. The A-Man 10. INTERNATIONAL TITLE DEFENSE Mandarin vs. Tank Turner 11. WOMEN'S TITLE DEFENSE Eve vs. "English Assassin" Arian Ash 12. WORLD LIGHTHEAVYWEIGHT TITLE DEFENSE The A-Man vs. Fallen Angel 13. WORLD TAG TEAM TITLE DEFENSE Damage Incorporated vs. Sudden Impact 14. WORLD HEAVYWEIGHT TITLE DEFENSE Mark Destructo vs. "Hot Stuff" Chris Taylor Again the superstars of NCW were out signing autographs and taking pictures with the fans. Several NCW fan favorites visited St. Jude's Research Hospital and spoke with the children under medical care there. The matches were preceded by a mini-concert by some of Memphis' local musicians and then the matches were on! Match 1: Cardiac vs. Bernadette Martin Cardiac had seen the tricks that Lady Rose had pulled during other matches and did not fall for any of her ploys. Keeping to a strict gameplan, Cardiac wore Martin down and secured a pinfall just after thirteen minutes in this Junior's matchup with tilt-a-whirl slam. Winner: Cardiac via pinfall (tilt-a-whirl slam) in 13:12 Match 2: Badd Company vs. Roundhouse Another fast paced tag team matchup say Roundhouse square off against Badd Company. Both teams fought hard and sometimes got away with rules infractions, but the Memphis audience seemed used to seeing that sort of thing. After twenty minutes, Badd Company seized the advantage and held onto it to score a pinfall after a double dropkick. Winner: Badd Company via pinfall (double dropkick) in 23:21 Match 3: Lady Rose vs. Miko "Steele Rose" Mizadori The Memphis fans seemed strongly behind the "Steele Rose" in this Battle of the Roses. Miko used an onslaught of martial arts techniques against her much taller opponent. Angela Thorne fought back with suplexes and clotheslines. Soon Miko had Rose on shaky ground and went for the top rope. Miko was signalling the crowd for a flying kick when Rose dove for the ropes and caused Miko to lose her balance. Miko fell off the top onto the ringsteps and seemed hurt. Lady Rose exited the ring, pushed Miko back in, and after slapping her several times in the face, locked her in the Royal Genuflection. The referee kept checking to see if Miko had submitted, but she kept refusing. Finally the referee checked her arm and as it fell the third time, the referee called for the bell. Winner: Lady Rose via submission (Royal Genuflection) in 22:36 Match 4: Airborn vs. Hellfire Airborn stormed the ring and locked up with Hellfire immediately. The two Juniors battled around the ring and wound up nearly in the crowd. Both Juniors seemed intent on being the next person in line to challenge for the Junior's belt, regardless of who was the victor later in the night. After several high impact moves, Hellfire went for a cover, but Airborn kicked out at two. Hellfire went for a high-risk move off the top rope, but Airborn literally dropkicked him in midair! After that, Airborn took over with suplexes and high-impact dropkicks and clotheslines. Hellfire fought back with a kneelift and an Irish whip, but Airborn reversed it. Airborn ducked for a back bodydrop and Hellfire went for a sunset flip, but Airborn dropped down onto Helfire's shoulders and scored the pinfall. Winner: Airborn via pinfall (reversal of a sunset flip) in 15:38 Match 5: Pestilence vs. Rebecca "Dark Star" Maddick Pestilence came out looking just as distracted and upset as her fellow members of the Pale Riders. It seemed that Adam was no longer associated with them and were feeling the effects of his abscence. The "Dark Star" capitalized early with a series of bodyslams and a piledriver on her larger opponent, but Pestilence finally responded and kicked out of an attempted Boston Crab by Maddick. Pestilence then began breaking the rules as she raked Maddick's eyes, choked her on the ropes, and used the tights to attempt a pinfall. Maddick kicked out, but Pestilence caught her with a vicious neckbreaker. Pestilence then went up top and came down on Maddick with a legdrop that she calls Congestion. Maddick attempted to kick out of the pin, but the referee didn't notice Maddick's feet on the ropes. Winner: Pestilence via pinfall (Congestion) in 12:39 Match 6: TITLE SHOT MATCH Damage Incorporated vs. Ghetto Boyz The Memphis crowd cheered the return of Damage Incorporated to NCW, just as if they were from Memphis, not New Orleans. This match was brutal. The Ghetto Boyz were jumped on their way to the ring and the bloodthirsty Memphis crowd loved it. Ramos and Porteaux smashed both Ghetto Boyz into almost everything on the way to the ring then threw them into the ring, before climbing the turnbuckles and dropping elbows on both Boyz. When order was finally restored the Ghetto Boyz were in little shape to mount much offense, but they did try. The damage however was already done, and almost fifteen minutes into the carnage, DI brought it to an end with the Dog Collar, much to the delight of the Memphis crowd. Winner: Damage Incorporated via pinfall (Dog Collar) in 14:27 Match 7: GRUDGE MATCH "Iceman" Brian Knight vs. "Heartbreaker" Matt Foxx This match started off as psychological warfare, when Knight came to the ring with valet Vogue. Knight came to the ring in tiger striped pants, and doing a "Heartbreaker" gimmick. Vogue, Knight's valet, made her way to the ring second, once again wearing a "Matt Foxx Fan Club" t-shirt. Foxx did not find humor in any of this, as he charged the ring, attacking Knight during his "pre-match" antics. After clearing the ring of his opponent and former valet, Matt Foxx wanted to keep his early dominance constant. He did just that for the first half of this match. Foxx executed each move with perfection, while Knight seemed a little flustered in this match. At the 13 minute mark, Foxx sent Knight over the top rope, sending him crashing to the floor. This was Foxx's fatal mistake. Jason Speece came charging to the ring, complaining to Ivan Nothin that Foxx's clothesline should be a disqualifying maneuver. Nothin was having nothing of it, and immediately sent Speece back to the locker room. Speece checked on Knight, and proceeded to the back. Knight at this point, rolled back into the ring, and just began to dominate Foxx. Knight went to his roots, a solid ground attack, which Foxx was not ready for. Knight slowly dismantled Foxx for the next seven minutes. Foxx was clearly on the defensive. Twenty minutes into the match, Foxx was battered, tired, and clearly not ready to continue. Vogue jokingly started a "Heartbreaker" chant from the fans, and Knight dragged Foxx by the hair over towards Vogue. Vogue then grabbed the house mic and announced that she was DUMPING Matt Foxx. She then throw her "Matt Foxx Fan Club" t-shirt in Foxx's face, and slapped the "NCW Heartbreaker" right in the face. This was Vogue's fatal mistake. Knight executed his "Colorado Avalanche", a spinning DDT, and jumped onto the ropes to soak up the jeers. Instead, he was startled to hear the cheers of the fans as Matt Foxx executed a kip-up, and delivered a dropkick to the back of Knight. Knight tumbled over the top rope, and fell to the floor. Foxx leaped onto the top rope, and leaped off with a beautiful cross body, leveling "The Iceman". For the next four minutes, the action was all outside the ring, with the referee distracted by either Vogue, Knockout, or both! Knight and Foxx by this point were both bloody, as they returned to the ring. At this point Vogue leaped onto the ring apron. Nothin, trying everything to get Vogue off the ring apron never noticed Vogue toss FOXX a steel chain. Knight dropped like a rock to the mat, grabbing his face, dispite the fact that Matt Foxx had done nothing! Nothin saw Knight on the mat, and saw Foxx's chain, and immediately disqualified Foxx for the use of the chain. Winner: "Iceman" Brian Knight via DQ (foreign object) in 26:13 After the match, Knight, pretending to be injured from the chain, challenged Foxx to face him again, this time in an Aspen Extreme Match. Foxx readily agreed. Match 8: JUNIOR'S TITLE DEFENSE The Overgiver vs. "The Preppie" Tiffany Van Heusen "The Preppie" Tiffany Van Heusen stood of the verge of a clean sweep at Wrestling Revolution and only had one more obstacle to face, the Junior known as The Overgiver. Lady Rose, flush with her previous victory opted not to accompany Tiffany to the ring. Tiffany started off very confident in herself and landed several high-impact moves on the Overgiver, including bodyslams, clotheslines, and dropkicks. Tiffany seemed to be dominating her opponent until she flattened the Overgiver with a flying clothesline then turned to the Memphis crowd to scream at them how she was winning.....and the Overgiver did a very familiar looking "zombie situp". Tiffany turned around and saw the Overgiver getting up, then went for a dropkick which the Overgiver amazingly batted aside. Tiffany looked at a loss and then tried to leave the ring, only to have the Overgiver grab her by her hair and physically pull her back into the ring. The Overgiver whipped Tiffany into the ropes, then elevated her with a high back bodydrop. Tiffany again tried to run, but the Overgiver grabbed her and leveled her with a short clothesline. The Memphis crowd, often fans of the Undertaker, enjoyed cheering the Overgiver on. Tiffany was in deep trouble when the Overgiver nailed her with a powerslam, but instead of going for the pin, the Overgiver looked at the Memphis crowd, and in response to their urging, planted Tiffany with a Tombstone piledriver. The Overgiver covered and the referee counted to three. The Overgiver's victory was short-lived however, as Lady Rose came to ringside carrying several documents, just as the referee was about to award the Junior's Title to the Overgiver. After reading the documents and consulting with a guilty-looking Overgiver, the referee announced that that match decision was reversed and the Overgiver had been disqualified due to age violation. It seemed that the Overgiver's had a birthday in June and that, being now 18, the Overgiver was no longer a Junior. The Memphis crowd booed as the Junior's Title was handed back to Tiffany, but cheered the Overgiver nonetheless. Winner: "The Preppie" Tiffany Van Heusen via DQ in 18:39, due to age violation by the Overgiver Match 9: INTERNET TITLE DEFENSE Thomas N. Tyler vs. The A-Man The Memphis crowd also cheered the ever-explosive TNT as he made his way to the ring. The cagey veteran fought hard and dirty against the A-Man, but in the end, the A-Man's youth and even dirtier tactics prevailed. At the end, TNT was pounding on A-Man in the corner, standing on the second rope, when A-Man grabbed the back of TNT's wrestling trunks, pulled them until he rolled backwards, then pinned him with both feet on the ropes. TNT was highly incensed that he not only had been beaten, but that he had been out-cheated. He seemed to enjoy the heelish cheers of the Memphis fans as he went back to the lockers. Winner: The A-Man via pinfall (rollup) at 11:46 Match 10: INTERNATIONAL TITLE DEFENSE Mandarin vs. Tank Turner The Mandarin taunted Tank to try some of his newfound martial arts prowess on him, and Turner accepted. This was a mistake however as Turner soon found out. While Turner had learned a few tricks from a master, The Mandarin was a master in his own right. Mandarin soon held the upper hand after a stunning combination of pressure point strikes, throws, and nerve locks. The ever-unpredictable crowd got solidly behind Turner, though and soon he had powered his way out of a neck pinch nerve hold. Turner fell back on what he did best and used high-powered wrestling to slow down the smaller, faster Mandarin. After the fifteen minute mark, the A-Man came back out and, without the referee noticing, pulled down the top rope so that Tank Turner fell out of the ring. Mandarin capitalized and used the opportunity to punish Turner on the outside. Putting Turner back in the ring, Mandarin went up top. When he jumped towards Turner, Turner put his foot up and Mandarin landed on it. Turner pulled himself up and staggered back into the ropes where A-Man was waiting. As the referee was telling Union Kane to stay off the other side of the apron. A-Man jumped up and grabbed Turner from behind. Mandarin leapt at Turner with a flying kick, but Turner managed to break free at the last moment. Mandarin blasted A-Man off the apron and stood there stunned long enough for Turner to roll him up from behind. The referee turned around and counted the pinfall. Winner: "American Original" Tank Turner via pinfall (rollup) in 18:31 Match 11: WOMEN'S TITLE DEFENSE Eve vs. "English Assassin" Arian Ash Eve came out on a quest to win her third NCW Women's Title, and took it straight to Ash from the onset. Eve didn't let Ash play her normal defensive gameplan and kept the pressure on her throughout the match. Once Eve got Ash worn down she went for a piledriver, but Ash reversed it into a backdrop then turned and grabbed Eve's leg. Eve reached up and caught her in a small package, but Ash kicked out at one. Ash went to work on Eve's leg and soon had softened it up for the Assassination. Eve made one last ditch effort and threw Ash off into the corner. Eve charged in and mounted the turnbuckle, surprising Ash with a victory roll. Eve nearly won it then and there, but Ash got a foot on the ropes. As Eve was setting Ash up for a superplex, the crowd reacted loudly. Jammer had come down the aisle and was brandishing a chair. Security were doing all they could to stop him but he was nearly to the ring when they noticed. Eve nailed the superplex, but then noticed Jammer. She was distracted just long enough for Ash to get to her feet, but the crowd alerted her to Ash sneaking up behind her. Eve blasted Ash in the face with the Original Sin, but there was no referee to count the cover. The referee had been distracted by Jammer as well. When he did turn around, Ash was able to kick out before the third count landed. Eve was obviously frustrated but attempted to set Ash up for another superplex. Ash responded with a rake to the eyes and pushed Eve off the turnbuckle onto her back in the ring. Ash waited on the turnbuckle and as Eve stood, she looked dazedly out of the ring at Jammer, and fell victim to a flying cross body from Ash. The referee counted and Ash was victorious. Winner: Arian Ash via pinfall (flying cross body) at 25:45 After the match, Security managed to escort Jammer out of the ring area and Eve made it safely back to the lockers. Match 12: WORLD LIGHTHEAVYWEIGHT TITLE DEFENSE The A-Man vs. Fallen Angel Definitely the award for most ring appearances at Wrestling Revolution should go to the A-Man. In his final match over the three week period, the Internet Champion stepped into the ring to challenge the World Lightheavyweight Champion in what looked to be a spectacular matchup. Despite A-Man having already wrestled that night and Fallen Angel's severe beating at the hands of the Jammer, both men looked ready and anxious for the match. It started off quickly with each man going for quick pinfalls and submission holds with each lockup. Several near falls occured in the first five minutes, including a surprise Angel Death Roll that A-Man only just kicked out of a the last instant. A-Man nearly decapitated Angel with a flying clothesline, then quickly hit a neckbreaker and covered for two. Both men see-sawed back and forth. A-Man nailed his finisher at the twenty-five minute mark and everyone though it was over. Somehow, Angel got a foot on the ropes and the match continued. By the thirty minute mark, both men seemed exhausted but the pace continued. Angel flew off the top rope and A-Man amazingly caught him with a powerslam, only to have Angel roll with it to put A-Man's shoulders on the mat. Again and again, the crowd came to it's feet, cheering both men. After the forty minute mark, both men could barely stand, but each kept trying high-risk manuvers. At the end, A-Man attempted to set Fallen Angel up for a superplex, but Angel pushed him off the top back into the ring. When A-Man stood up. Angel flew from the top and nailed A-Man with a FLYING ANGEL DEATH ROLL for the pin. The crowd cheered both of these men for the amazing match they put on. Winner: Fallen Angel via pinfall (Flying Angel Death Roll) at 42:24 Match 13: WORLD TAG TEAM TITLE DEFENSE Damage Incorporated vs. Sudden Impact The crowd was hyped for this match as Alex "The Ace" Porteaux and Eddy "Mad Dog" Ramos came to the ring. Everyone expected a brutal match and they weren't dissappointed. Steve Ramsey and Scott Taylor, having seen the ambush on the Ghetto Boyz, came to the ring carrying chairs. Damage Inc. seemed amused by the precaution and applauded Sudden Impact. Once the bell rang, these two teams exploded into action. Tagging in and out, Sudden Impact tried to put Damage Inc. away quickly, but they couldn't quite do it. After twelve minutes, DI took command and Porteaux and Ramos went at SI with wild abandon. Every double team move imaginable was executed by these two tag teams, and as the match passed twenty minutes, the referee got in the way of an Irish whip and went down for the count. At this point, the match degenerated into a brawl that spilled out of the ring. Memphians dove for cover as DI and SI entered the stands and were brawling in the crowd, much to the fans' delight. Soon all four men were busted open and there was no official to stop the fight. Scott Taylor suplexed Alex Porteaux into the concrete steps, Eddy Ramos bodyslammed Stever Ramsey through a row of seats, and when the referee finally woke up, he found himself all alone in the ring. He immediately counted to ten and called for the bell. The crowd didn't seem to mind since the fight continued for another five minutes after the bell sounded. Security finally was able to separate the two teams. Winner: NO CONTEST via DCOR at 27:29 Match 14: WORLD HEAVYWEIGHT TITLE DEFENSE Mark Destructo vs. "Hot Stuff" Chris Taylor This match had been long awaited, since these two superstars of the sport have had some memorable matches in other federations and had put on an incredible performance against each other in the Octagon at RingStorm. Unfortunately, the match never happened. When Mark Destructo was introduced, his music played, but only his manager, Thomas F. Jones III, came out. He produced a document, supposedly from a doctor, stating that Mark Destructo was unfit for action and could not wrestle. The Memphians started booing but Jones ignored them. He also showed documentation of where the Commissioner of the UWA, one D. Alexander Autrey, had verified this doctor's restraint. The official looked confused and called for President John Moore. Moore looked over the documentation and could not disprove the validity of them. Jones told the crowd not to fret, which drew tremendous boos, because by contract, if Destructo couldn't wrestle, a replacement could be named. Jones further stated that he himself could, by contract, name Destructo's replacement. He then stated that since Chris Taylor didn't see fit to keep the UCW Title he won earlier in the year, maybe he would see fit to defend the NCW Title against the man he beat for the UCW belt. Many people in the crowd started reacting immediately to this but Jones lowered the mic as he spoke to Jones and the ring announcer. President Moore was obviously incensed by Jones' choice, but could not break the binding contract Jones held. When Jones and Moore left the ring, it was announced that the replacement for Mark Destructo would be none other than THE JAMMER!!! Jammer came out to a chorus of boos but he was focussed only on the ring. He entered the ring and waited on Taylor. When Blaze and "Hot Stuff" appeared the Memphis crowd gave them a standing ovation. Chris didn't even slow down as he sprinted down the aisle, already in his wrestling gear, and dove into the ring. Jammer and Taylor immediately began trading punches. The bell rang and the war was on. Jammer kneed Taylor in the groin and began slamming double ax-handles into his back. "Hot Stuff" responded with an uppercut that lifted Jammer off his feet and dropped him on his back. Rather than going for a pin, Taylor started slamming Jammer's head into the mat. Jammer retaliated with an eye-gouge and quickly executed a neckbreaker on Taylor. Jammer went from there to driving knees into Taylor's back and whipping him hard into the turnbuckle Taylor finally reversed one whip and charged in, standing on the ropes pounding Jammer's face as the crowd counted. Taylor then monkey flipped Jammer out of the corner. Jammer rolled to his feet in time to catch a flying side kick to the head. Taylor picked him up and whipped him into the ropes. Jammer ducked a spinning leg lariat but ran into a Hot Shot on the rebound. Taylor grabbed Jammer's leg and the crowd reacted as he started working it over with kneedrops, elbowdrops, and the bottom rope. Jammer finally kicked Taylor off and resumed his attack on Taylor's back. Jammer nailed a tilt-a-whirl backbreaker, followed by a knee to the back from the top rope. Jammer started choking Taylor on the bottom rope, but the referee pulled him back. Jammer almost hit the referee but decided to go back to choking Taylor instead. The battle went back and forth for nearly half an hour, Jammer working on Taylor's back, Taylor working on Jammer's leg, until at the twenty-seven minute mark when Jammer whipped Taylor into the referee. Once the referee went down, Jammer pulled out the brass knuckles and went to work on Taylor's head again. Blaze slid into the ring and pleaded with Jammer to stop. When she did, he took a swing at her. She ducked and came back up with a FIREBALL from inside her purse. Jammer dropped the knucks as he was diving for cover. The fireball missed, but Blaze grabbed the knucks and threw them into the crowd. Fortunately, no one was injured by them. Jammer started to charge after Blaze but Taylor tripped him with a legsweep. Taylor, bloody and battered, grabbed Jammer by the hair and dragged him over to the turnbuckle. He slammed Jammer's face against the turnbuckle until Jammer looked nearly out. "Hot Stuff" then turned Jammer around and gave him a Bullet Train to Hell. Jammer looked out of it, but there was still no referee. With the crowd cheering him on, Taylor dragged Jammer over to the ropes, pointed to the timekeeper's table and told them to clear out. They did and Taylor, ironically using one of Destructo's old finishers, powerbombed Jammer over the top rope and through the timekeeper's table. Taylor pulled a bloody Jammer out of the wreckage of the table and rolled him back into the ring. Jammer was bleeding as well and Taylor covered him as Blaze tried to wake the referee up. When she finally did, the referee crawled over and slowly counted. SOMEHOW, Jammer got a foot on the ropes. Incensed, Taylor leapt up and crashed down on the leg. Jammer screamed out in pain and Taylor crashed down on it again. Dragging Jammer into the middle of the ring, Taylor locked on the Figure-Five Leglock and Jammer submitted seconds later. Winner: "Hot Stuff" Chris Taylor via submission (Figure-Five) at 37:29 That was the climactic finale to NCW WRESTLING REVOLUTION. Lots of great action and lots of surprises.
