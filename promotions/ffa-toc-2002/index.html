Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Female Fighting Alliance announced itself on 27 March 2002 with a single event in mind and no federation behind it: one night at Madison Square Garden, an eight-woman Super 8 tournament modelled on the ECWA’s, a hardcore cup, a tag challenge, and a hundred thousand dollars a side. New York the first year, Tokyo the next, and back again every year after that. Its organiser said plainly that he had the time to run a yearly supercard and not the time to run a federation.

It was built for women’s wrestling alone, at a moment when the promotions that had specialised in it were closing. Entries came from Battling Ring Angels, the UWF, BLAZE, the NIWF, Land of Enchantment Wrestling and Deep South Wrestling, and from wrestlers whose own federations — the Grand Dragon Wrestling Alliance, WHiP, MBC, WISE, EFW — were already shut. Six judges were named, one from each participating federation, none allowed to judge their own. One invitation came back refused, and it was posted in full with a laugh attached.

The card of 24 May 2002 set the Super 8 as a bracket of Grand Dragon veterans against the wider field: Marissa Monet and Micki Duran, Andrea Chandler and Officer May Order, Madison Stone and “Tornado” Tara Smith, and a Battle of Britannia between Lisa Drake and Brittany Yorke. Below it sat a seven-team tables, ladders and chairs elimination, a sixty-minute Ironwoman match billed as a tribute to the Grand Dragon Wrestling Alliance two years after it folded, a four-corner match under scientific rules, and a hardcore cup whose namesake was also its referee.

Three flash packets reached the newsgroup: twenty-five promos in May, twenty-four more in June, and a last round on 1 July, all anchored by a broadcast desk of Jerry Styles, Noel Schmertner and Heidi Leick arguing with each other between segments. Officer May Order was written back out of retirement on fifty thousand petition signatures; Daisy Butterfly answered from a pool hall; Brittany Yorke had her butler take the interview.

Nothing followed the third packet. No result, no bracket, no cup awarded, and no notice that the show would not happen. The last word the Female Fighting Alliance left was a set of promos for matches that were never wrestled.
