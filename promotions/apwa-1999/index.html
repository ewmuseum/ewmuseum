Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The American Pro Wrestling Alliance's surviving record opens with its debut broadcast, Saturday Night Slammasters, taped 13 February 1999 from the Disney-MGM Studios in Orlando, Florida. It opened not with a match but a walkout: Robert Payne, carrying a championship belt the promotion's own president publicly refused to recognize as official, delivered a farewell address to a booing crowd and left rather than defend it.

The card that followed staged Tom Strong over a rookie known only as C, The Patriot over a taunting masked villain called The Decapitator, and Robert Payne over “The Heartbreak Kid” Shawn Michaels. Between matches, the broadcast cut to a newly signed grappler, Jessica Starbird, fielding a phone call about the recent collapse of the IEWA — the fed she had been champion of — and explaining her decision to start over in a promotion just getting off the ground itself.

An eight-man battle royal closed the show for the vacant Television Championship, its field also carrying Ricky Morton and Robert Gibson. C, beaten in the night's opening match, returned within the battle royal to eliminate his own conqueror, Tom Strong, last of all, becoming the promotion's own first Television Champion.

By its own Player's Guide, posted three months later on 8 May 1999, the promotion had settled into two weekly cards — the flagship Sunday Night Slammasters and a secondary Wednesday Night Warzone — based out of Illinois though touring cards worldwide, and had named four championships in all: the World Heavyweight, World Light Heavyweight, World Tag Team and World Ladies titles.

How the promotion's own further plans played out isn't in the surviving record; that same Player's Guide is its last surviving document.
