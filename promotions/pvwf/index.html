Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Two complete three-hour broadcasts of PVWF's own weekly show, Monday Night Madness, survive from December 1997, both already treating the promotion as an established concern with a running pay-per-view calendar — X-Mas Explosion, the Royal Rumble, a Valentine's Day Massacre, War Games — and a standing on-air authority figure, Head Executive Doug Zanghi.

Its dominant stable, the Truth Commission, marched to the ring in matching khaki and red berets behind the World Champion, Aaron Bolles, alongside Slim, Elle, and the Commandant. Both surviving broadcasts end the same way, with the show fading out on an unresolved brawl: the first when a returning wrestler, Zwa, attacked “Nature Boy” Nick Langworthy and the babyface locker room answered in kind against the Truth Commission; the second when a Tag Team Championship defense between Slim and Elle and the tag team Los Boricuas broke down into a disqualification and a full brawl between the two sides.

Between the two broadcasts, its own pay-per-view, X-Mas Explosion, changed three of the promotion's five championships by its own account: Slim and Elle won the Tag Team Championship in a triangle match the broadcast itself calls “controversial”; Pearl took the Television Championship from Big Dave in a tables-and-ladders match; and Billy Dahl reclaimed the Intercontinental Championship, becoming, in the broadcast's own words, “only the second two-time PVWF Intercontinental champion.”

Aaron Bolles held the World Championship across both surviving broadcasts without defending it on either, wrestling only in non-title matches; a discontinued Women's Championship is recalled only once, in passing, when one announcer asks on air, “Whatever happened to the PVWF Woman's title, anyway?”

No founding document or closing notice survives for the run either side of these two shows.
