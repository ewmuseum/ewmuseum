Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

National Extreme Wrestling traced its own founding, on the words of the man who ran it, to October 1996, becoming a full-time operation under him a year later. He wrestled under the name GoodFella and staged the promotion out of a home arena in Clearwater Beach, Florida, in front of crowds his own broadcasts counted in the thousands.

Its surviving King of the Mat 2 pay-per-view, 6 April 1998 from the Rosemont Horizon in Chicago, ran a full night of title defenses: Assaf “the Terminator” Levavy retained the Heavyweight Championship against Patty Babes Carter, “Stylish” Steve Miller kept the North American title against “Dynasty” Drew Snyder by disqualification after a returning masked man interfered, and Eddie Jacobs beat the Intercontinental Champion Balial — billed as “the Lord's Messenger” against Jacobs's self-declared anti-Christ — in a Crucifix match that left Balial tied to the cross the loser was bound to. The underdog Crash Test Dummies won the tag team title tournament outright, and James “Simply the Best” Pillow won the ten-man King of the Mat gauntlet after his own stablemate stepped aside rather than face him, earning a Heavyweight title shot at the next pay-per-view.

Weeks later, at Friday Night Slam Dance back home in Clearwater Beach, GoodFella announced his own retirement on air, revealing his given name, Brandon Oliver, to the crowd and handing the promotion to two new co-presidents, Bob Wilhem and Mark Mitchell. He named a third successor in the same broadcast — his own son, the wrestler “Desire” Brandon Bloom, as the promotion's Executive Consultant — and the two embraced in the ring as the crowd chanted the promotion's own name.

A pay-per-view planned for that January, Hell for the Holidays 3, was cancelled before it aired. The promotion regrouped around a Memorial Cup Tournament instead, its sixteen entrants named in a March 1999 announcement and the event itself staged 1 May 1999 at the USF Sundome in Tampa.

The last surviving document in this record, dated 30 June 1999 and captioned in its own words as “made for N.E.W. before it closed down,” opens on a memorial photograph of GoodFella himself before introducing a debuting wrestler, “So Damn Cool” Jay Emes, to a roster he never got to join beyond that one roleplay. No later document, and no further word on how the promotion actually ended, survives past it.
