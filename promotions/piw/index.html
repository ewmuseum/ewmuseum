Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Pinnacle of Insane Wrestling opens its own surviving record on 8–9 March 1997 with a Saturday Night Riot broadcast staged from a club it called Club Expo, an entirely invented roster. The show opens on taped promos: Dr. Death needling an opponent named Ruckus over a signature move he calls the “Dorkus bomb,” and the five-member Royal Family — a Lawyer, an Adviser, a Ballplayer, a King and a Champion already carrying the fed's own tag titles — announcing a takeover of the promotion built around getting their Champion a world title shot.

The same broadcast turns into a staged invasion. It cuts to captured footage of a rival's own card, where the newly crowned Extreme Wrestling Association North American Champion, Magus, is still celebrating when a group of its own wrestlers — Hardcore Harris, Johnny Extreme, the Eden Brothers, the Royal Family and Jim Johnson — storm the ring and Slade beats down the rival's champion with a baseball bat. Johnson takes the rival's own microphone to explain the raid as payback for being passed over as that federation's regional partner, and at a press conference later on the same show throws the promotion's name into a cross-federation tournament called the Summit and its own Summit Cup, naming the rival along with two others as the competition to beat.

Its main event that night, a barbed wire cage match, has “Hardcore” Matt Harris retain the World Title against the Royal Champion at nineteen minutes and thirteen seconds, only for the rivalry to spill back in during the broadcast's own close: Magus interrupts the wrap-up, chair-shots Jim Johnson, and confirms a Summit Cup match against Slade set for 7 April.

Where the war itself landed isn't in the surviving record. A further dispute over the Summit Cup, dated 6–10 April 1997, is the last activity attested for the promotion; no result of the promised Slade–Magus match, and no closing notice of any kind, survives past it.
