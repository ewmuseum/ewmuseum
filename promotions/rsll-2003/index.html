A cross-federation invitational tournament drawing wrestlers from across the newsgroup into three simultaneous elimination brackets, voted on by its own entrants, launched in October 2003 and relaunched under the same name in January 2005. Its two first-round broadcast nights survive in full; no champion is crowned in the surviving record. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Rising Stars, Living Legends announced itself as a cross-federation invitational staged at Caesar's Palace, Las Vegas, its own update of 19 September 2003 opening with “the response to this tournament has seen phenomenal, so much so that it has doubled in size.” It ran no fed representation of its own; instead its entrants themselves voted on every match's own promo quality through a private mailing list. Its own bracket split into two singles tournaments, the sixteen-entrant Living Legends Cup and the seventeen-entrant Rising Stars Cup, run alongside a tag-team round robin, the Brothers In Arms Cup.

Night 1, broadcast 8 October 2003 by announcers Ed Roxley and Dan Cash, ran nine matches, among them Langston Crete's own first-round pin of Conan Kabashi and a countout finish between Brian Jordan and Zodiac, billed as stablemates at ICON.

Night 2, on 3 November 2003, ran nine more, including Nick Demola's own first-round win over Jesse Ewiak by a handful of tights.

The tournament's own closing notice, posted 5 December 2003, states plainly: “The tournament has concluded.” No further broadcast night survives here, but a January 2005 announcement for a second installment names the actual champions from the first: “Last time Langston Crete won the Rising Star tournament and Nick Demola won the Living Legend tournament.”

How the quarterfinal, semifinal, and final rounds that produced those two champions actually played out isn't in the surviving record: only the two opening broadcast nights survive as shows.
