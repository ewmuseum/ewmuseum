Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Professional Fantasy Wrestling put its own history in one line, in a January 1999 recruitment flyer: “It has been around since March of 1997… It has gone away for a few weeks on 2 occassions, but it has never died.” It billed itself as low-commitment by design, wanting roleplays of forty to a hundred lines rather than the much longer entries it said had become “ridiculousness” at “most of today's so called TOP federations” — the object, in its own words, was never originality but fun, and one ongoing angle had started as “a rip off of the current WWF corporate angle” before drifting away from it.

It was aiming for about thirty wrestlers, three per handler, mixing created characters with unclaimed real-wrestler personas already on its own roster — The Rock and Mankind sat unclaimed at the time of the flyer, while The Patriot and the tag team of J.C. Ice and Wolfie D were held as NPCs, jobbers any handler could draft and write for.

Two cards ran weekly: Sunday Slam, three or four matches, fully summarized, and Hardcore Thursday, its main card, running as many as twelve matches with the main event written out in full and the rest called play-by-play. The World Title, held by Freddy Allocca, was defended only occasionally; the US Title, held by Tommy Richards, changed hands often enough that the flyer called it busier than the Television title had ever been; the IC Title was to be crowned in a football-season-style tournament once the roster reached twenty, and a Tag Team Title was set to be decided at the first major Hardcore Thursday, 18 February 1999. Pay-per-views ran bi-monthly rather than monthly, for lack of wrestlers, staged as a longer, fully written Hardcore Thursday special with every title on the line.

A newsletter attested from 13 March 1999 finds Freddy Allocca still champion seven weeks later, defending the belt at a pay-per-view called Shadowed Light, and records a newly struck alliance with a promotion sharing the initials WCCW. How it ended isn't in the surviving record; the last fact on file has it still active that March.
