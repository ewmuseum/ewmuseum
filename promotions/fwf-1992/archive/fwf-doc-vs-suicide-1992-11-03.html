FWF: Doc vs. Suicide

3 November 1992
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

Message-ID: <1d5a6oINNmro@flop.ENGR.ORST.EDU> Date: 3 Nov 92 07:36:56 GMT Organization: Oregon State University Lines: 443 NNTP-Posting-Host: xanth.cs.orst.edu Hello, everyone. It's been a while, I know, but you'll get over the disappointment of seeing me come back. :-) This time around, FWF World Champion Doctor Death defends his title in an open contract match accepted by "Suicidal" Paul Stuart. Commentary from both individuals: Stuart: "I just can't take it any more! It's too upsetting, too painful, that I'm in the FWF and I HAVEN'T KICKED YOUR STINKIN' TEETH IN YET, DOC!!! Well, that's about to change, because you were stupid enough to sign an open contract that somebody, even a newcomer like me, could take. Well, you're about to find out that the most dangerous men aren't the smart ones, not the dumb ones, but the ones who don't care! I don't care about the World title, though it wouldn't be too bad to have. I just want to cave in your skull, and it doesn't matter if I do it to myself in the process! Get ready for KAMIKAZE!!!" Death: "Yes, indeed, I have offered an open contract, which was sanctioned by the Fictional Wrestling Federation's Championship Committee, for the very simple reason that a World Champion should indeed be able to take any and all who challenge him. Stuart, you must indeed be an unhinged individual, for to step into the ring with the caliber of Doctor Death in your debut for this federation is indeed indicative of suicidal instincts. Soon, you will learn why my name means the demise of all who dare to oppose me. I am Death, and death is the broom I take in my hands to sweep the world clean!" And thus, the match unfolds . . . ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- Golden Earring's "Radar Love" begins its noise over the UTC Arena's PA system, and the wide-eyed individual walking down the aisle, almost giggling at the noise that greets his arrival. A lot of questions about the man arise at once. Alfred Grinchley answers some of them. "THE FOLLOWING CONTEST IS FOR THE WORLD HEAVYWEIGHT CHAMPIONSHIP! IT IS SCHEDULED FOR ONE FALL. COMING DOWN THE AISLE, HAILING FROM SIESTA KEY, FLORIDA, IS THE CHALLENGER. HE WEIGHS TWO HUNDRED AND FOURTY-ONE POUNDS: 'SUICIDAL' PAAAAAUUUL STUUUUUUUART!!!!" A slight gasp emanates from the marks at the announcement of his weight, and it is wondered just how fitting the "Suicidal" moniker fits the man. Suicide heads up the steps, not looking where he's going, and climbs into the ring, looking at all the noise in the arena with a somewhat demented grin on his face, his blue eyes sparkling with glee at the situation. He likes the big matches against the big opponents. Why? Don't ask me. The macabre tone of "Toccata and Fugue in D Minor," played upon a grand concert organ, bursts out over the previous song, and the arena's crowd hushes in grim respect. The Grinch's voice is unhindered by anything but the music as he makes the second introduction. "AND HIS OPPONENT, FROM PARTS UNKNOWN. HE WEIGHS TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SEVEN POUNDS, AND HE IS THE REIGNING WORLD HEAVYWEIGHT CHAMPION: DOOOOOOOOOOOCTOOR DEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEAAAAAAATH!!!!" Suicide sees the Doctor walking quietly down the ring, his head down and enshrouded by the ebon hood over his head. The cloak covers him from head to toe, and a few new fans try to peer underneath it to see the eyes of the man striding down the aisle. He looks up, and behind the mask of black steel, the fans meet his gaze at last. But not for long, for the eyes the color of jet with the flecks the color of freshly-spilled blood are terrifying to behold. It is not merely the eerie color. It is not the hatred which radiates with those scarlet flecks, not the evil which the eyes represent. No, it is the unholy truth in the horrid eyes which gives them pause, the fact that the man is untouchable, unassailable in his domination of the FWF. Death's eyes rise to meet those of Suicide, in the ring, and the silence seems to leave the smaller man already in the ring uneasy. The eyes narrow in suspicion, as the cowled figure slowly climbs the ring steps, peering contemptuously at the referee for the event. The Grinch is nowhere within his sight any longer, and for that, the ring announcer is quite relieved. The command to the official is unspoken, but definite, as Death stands unmoving on the ring apron. The ref parts the ring ropes, and Death, his arms unmoving, ducks under the top rope and walks into the ring, his hands allowing his cloak to fall away and reveal the World Championship belt around his waist. The black leather of the strap, the gold of the belt itself, the black cloak and kilt, the gold threads hanging from the kilt, it all seems to mesh, as if the belt was made expressly for this man. It is a grim but obvious image, one that is not lost upon any who see it. The clasp of the cloak is undone, and the cloak falls to the canvas. The ring attendant folds it neatly and waits for the metal mask which comes after, leaving Death's head covered by a leather mask of the same color. The mask is expertly designed, leaving no hinderance to Death's field of vision, yet no part of his features beyond his eyes and lips is revealed either. The belt is removed, and given to the official, who holds it high over his head. This is what competition in the FWF is all about. The World Heavyweight Championship is the end goal of all who enter it. Nothing less. The belt is handed to the attendant, and the bell sounds for the match's beginning. The challenger crouches, waiting for the slightest movement by the Doctor that presents opportunity. Death, for his part, leans against the ring ropes, testing their spring, eliminating minute variables that may be critical later in the match. Turning back to Suicide, Death prepares to lock up with him. Suicide springs to his feet, launching a roundhouse kick at Death's head! Death ducks it, but the smaller man aims a reverse one, continuing to rotate his body and launching kick after kick at Death's head and torso! Death gives ground to him generously, finally catching a foot as it passes overhead. He smiles at the smaller man, who leaps up and tries to catch Death on the base of the skull with his other foot, but the champion has seen this trick too many times to fall for it. Suicide's counterattack passes cleanly over his head, and the smaller man falls awkwardly to the mat on his belly. Death turns around, crosses the smaller man's shins, hooks them with his own leg, and bends back into a bridge! Suicide cries out in pain, though he almost seems to be enjoying the noise the fans are making at the sight of the deft counter, but he has driven Death close enough to the ropes before getting caught that it is little trouble to get hold of one, and Death gives a clean break. The champion is still trying to feel out the challenger, who comes into this match with no background or known tactics that Death could have prepared for. Slow pace, watch for weaknesses, and take the match as it comes to him, Death's game plan is ready. Suicide gets back to his feet and starts a similar series of shots, this time varying the height of each kick to keep Death off guard. Death's eyes widen as one lands on his shoulder, crimping up the muscles of his right arm, and he backs up quickly again, ducking under the top rope to force the referee to send Suicide away. Suicide bounds over the top rope, dropping a leg on Death's head on his way to the floor, somehow managing to land on his feet when he gets there! Death cries out in shock and pain, as Suicide yanks him out of the ring, slamming the larger man's head against the security railing, leaping up to the ring apron afterwards. He waits for Death to turn around, sees him slumping to the fence, and does a perfect moonsault off the bottom rope, landing against Death's chest, provoking several fans to scoot their chairs back in fright to avoid being hit themselves! Suicide rolls out of the 'sault onto the floor, and Death collapses onto his back. Suicide quickly runs back into the ring, and the referee begins to count Death out of the ring. Suicide climbs to the top rope from inside the ring, however, and drops a flying elbow down onto Death's chest on the floor! Death tries to keep his thoughts cohesive as he is pounded with right hand after right hand, trying to get some bearing, finally propping a knee inside his attacker's thigh and shoving him overhead from beneath, rising to his feet as soon as he's loose. He backs away, coughing, his hands up desperately for mercy, as he backs into the ring. Not surprisingly, Suicide is greeted with a knee to the head as he tries to follow, and Death finishes the attempt to reenter with a boot to the temple, leaving Suicide out on the floor. Death staggers back after loosing the ropes, and falls on his rear in the ring, dazed at the furious attack by the smaller man. Suicide gets back to his feet, and Death resumes his stomping away, not bothering with any particular science, finally bringing the challenger up to the apron by his brown hair. A shoulder to the gut stops him from bringing him all the way in, and Death stoops over a moment. As he stands, a second shoulder doubles him over. Suicide leaps over the top ropes, trying to pull the Doctor over in a sunset flip . . . Doc goes nowhere, holding onto the top rope with his hands and stomping on Suicide's face beneath him. He steps away from the smaller man, trying to still get some bearings. Finally, he drags his challenger into the middle of the ring by his right foot, kicking inside the thigh, just behind the knee. The muscles curl up painfully under the blows, as Death steps over with his own left foot, setting his shin behind the ankle, setting his right knee above his opponent's. A quick hop and drop to the knees, and Suicide's knee bends back at an angle beyond the intent any creator could have had for it. The challenger's eyes bulge, as he gives Death a swift kick on the right shoulder in retaliation, knocking him loose. But the exchange worked out quite well in Death's favor--a shot to the shoulder in exchange for a momentary hyperextension of the knee isn't too bad a deal. Suicide slides back on his hands, finally getting to the ropes and making it to his feet. Death steps back to dodge a wild springing pair of feet off the ropes, and seeing Suicide still holding on to the top cables, moves in before the smaller man lands, dealing a right hook to the jaw. Death looks at his opponent disgustedly, clasping his left hand on the throat to both choke and push him into the corner. He doesn't like the unscientific style he's had to take in the match, it's clear. It seems too unsophisticated for his tastes. A reverse knife edge chop has every muscle of Death's body behind it, sending Suicide's legs heaving up into the air with the sheer force of the impact. A second chop nearly hauls him over the ropes to the outside again. A kick to the right knee again, and Death allows Suicide to stumble out of the corner, stooped over. He hefts him into the air in a gut wrench, setting him on his right shoulder before planting his upper back on the canvas, keeping his hands set on the smaller man's belly to keep the momentum downward strong. The smaller man is motionless on the mat at this point, as Death goes back to the ropes. A knee lands on Suicide's head, and Death rolls sideways into a somersault and back to his feet in one fluid motion, leaving his opponent in a serious world of hurt. He curls him into a simple cradle for the cover. One! Two . . . Whether by instinct, continued survival, or just by unconscious twitching, one of Suicide's feet finds its way to the bottom rope. The fans murmur in surprise, until Death brings his opponent back to his feet. He sets the left arm overhead from behind, lifts Suicide up by the thighs and waist, setting him on his right shoulder as he slowly steps out from beneath him, and his back falls in a perfect horizontal position, seven feet straight down. Nice flight, bad landing. The man's body does not bounce in the slightest, for Death permits no shunting of force into propelling him back upward again, leaving it all for the spine. To the lay fan, it's all the same, no big deal, but the hardcores see the artful grace in it. (And my readers are probably telling me to shut up and get on with it right about now. :) The hardcores start cheering for Death, and he answers with a blood-curdling war cry that raises the referee's hairs on end. The marks respond with a chorus of boos. As the fans make more noise, Suicide sits up, listening intently. A demented grin breaks over his features, and he comes up to one knee. Death turns, and Suicide shows him the stupidity of turning away from a match. A fist to the gut elicits a cry of pain and a cheer from the marks. Suicide looks out wonderingly, as he sends Death to the ropes, catching him in a side suplex off the rebound, bringing more noise. He shouts wordlessly in fury, getting more and more frenzied. It is as if the noise is what is spurring him onward. He sets Death on his feet again, holding him up by the mask, before launching up in a standing drop kick which sends him flying into the corner. Death puts his hands up, getting his feet under him, and kicks Suicide in the midsection as he nears. He sends the smaller man to the ropes again, trying for a side suplex of his own, but Suicide ducks under, getting his right arm over Death's collarbone on the left side, turning around to hook a leg in an abdominal stretch! Death screams in agony, realizing that he hasn't seen the worst of it yet, as Suicide throws his own weight backward, toppling Death over onto his shoulders! One! Two . . . Death manages to get off the mat, but not by a whole lot. He's still in the stretch, as Suicide tries to shift around beneath him to get his shoulder back onto the mat. Finally, Death looks over his head, seeing the ropes from his inverted position. He reaches out and grabs the bottom one, and Suicide has to let him go. But as Death gets back to his feet, Suicide catches him in a reverse waistlock, flopping him up and over into a German suplex! The referee again counts. One! Two . . . Again, Death manages to get his shoulders off the mat, but he still has yet to recover any momentum. Suicide comes off the ropes and clotheslines the larger man down, leaping off the mat as he hooks the arm around the neck to make certain Death goes down. Death stands wearily, and Suicide hooks him down a second time. The World Champion stands a third time, and Suicide rushes him again, missing. Death turns, seeing Suicide coming off the ropes, and misses with a roundhouse Russian sickle attempt. On the third pass . . . Death ducks a bit, grabbing Suicide near the ropes by the neck and belly, using the smaller man's momentum to propel him upward! Military press? No . . . Death's not bothering to waste the energy to hold him up. He's putting every fiber of muscle in his body into the move, and Suicide is sent hurtling head over heel through the air, and the fans watch with rapt fascination as his body describes a parabola eleven feet high, his arms flailing wildly and almost humorously, for the hardcores' tastes, landing on his back with such force that he bounces a few feet back up into the air, finally coming to a halt entangled in the ropes on the opposite side of the ring. Death, adrenaline flowing, shrieks in unutterable rage. Suicide, for his part, finds the ring lights to be fascinating subjects for study, from the way he is staring blankly upward at them. The fans stand and shout in enthusiasm, and Death moves to finish his opponent off. He sets his foe in a front facelock, looping an arm overhead, and lifts him up by the waist, waiting a moment to step back before dropping the foundation out, and Suicide lands flat on his back in the middle of the ring. Death grabs his right leg, winding a finger in the air. The fans' buzzing goes into crescendo as Death spins around the leg, bending it at the still-aching knee, leaning over to set it against the opposite leg. No! Suicide reaches up and rolls him over into an inside cradle! One! Two! Death surges out of the cradle, his eyes bulging with fury, and he lunges at Suicide, blatantly gouging his opponent's eyes. A punch to the jaw takes its toll, but not enough, as Suicide makes his way to one knee. Death punches him again. Suicide flinches back, but stares defiantly at the larger man as he gets to his feet. The noise of the fans spurs him on, as Death goes for another shot, only to see it blocked. A second shot with his left hand is also blocked. A rising knee catches the smaller man in the midsection, and the left arm goes around his head, hurling him over the hip. Suicide's leg is instantly looped around Death's head, pulling him off, but not bothering to hold him. The two rise to their feet, Death a bit quicker, and he gets a second headlock in. Suicide shoves him to the ropes, only to be blasted to the mat with a shoulder off the rebound. Death sets a moment, going for a clothesline, only to be lifted up by the waist again, as Suicide tries to hold his weight up, finally dropping his throat over the top rope, leaving him cringing on the mat in pain. Suicide hears the excitement of the fans, and leaps to his feet, shaking his fists in frenzied wrath. He delivers a thrust kick to the jaw, sending Death flying into the corner, then whips him across to the opposite corner. He hesitates a moment, then launches into a handspring series, a second spring, very tight turn on the bounding, going for maximum spring in minimum space, and the reason why is apparent soon. He launches up into the air, turning 270 lengthwise to land with his feet square on Death's sternum, shoving off to go into a forward somersault and back to his feet, all in one undetached movement. Everyone in the arena stands agape, as Doctor Death staggers out of the corner and flops on his back, twitching spasmodically like a fish out of water. Suicide shouts in crazed jubilation, as he kicks Death over onto his belly, leaving him in the middle of the ring. He steps over to the corner, mounting the turnbuckles to the top, and springing out in a 360 in midair before landing with his knee on the small of Death's back. Death's eyes squint in a grotesque grimace, and Suicide shoves him back on his front with one foot. He grabs Death's ankles, and stands over him, stepping through from the inside of the left leg to the outside, crossing the right over the left, and stepping over, turning Death onto his belly again, pressing into the knee with all his might to add pressure to the sharpshooter. Death's eyes roll back in his head, and the temptation to succumb to the blackness creeping in upon his vision is more than small. But he bits his tongue against the pain, looking around for a way out. He's in the center of the ring, with absolutely nowhere to go. He tries to crawl on his hands, and Suicide is forced to hop a bit to go along with him. Despite the desperation of his situation, he smiles. His opponent is too small to keep him in one place. If anything was taught to him in the Zen monasteries of Tibet and Japan, it was that sufficient will can overcome any pain. Death's will is inflexible, and he proves it by shutting the pain out of his mind, propping himself up with his hands, and putting his entire and quite considerable leg strength into shoving Suicide loose. Suicide tries to hang on, but Death has some of the strongest legs in the FWF, and to shove 241 pounds away is little trouble for him once he sets his mind to it against the pain. Suicide goes flying through the middle of the ropes to the outside, striking his head on the floor. Death has little interest in following him, for no ammount of concentration will allow him to stand for at least a couple of minutes. Suicide groggily gets to his feet, and slides under the bottom rope back into the ring at the count of eight. Suicide crawls to Death, trying to kick at him. It is pathetic, and it is foolish. Death grabs the right leg, looping his own left over it, bridging up on the one leg to bend his opponent's knee. Suicide tries to kick away, but he is playing into Death's hands. He catches the leg, sets the opposite foot over the left knee at the ankle, and loops his own right leg over said ankle. It is clumsily done, and some fans don't recognize it because of the lack of the characteristic spin-around usually used to apply it, but the end result is a figure-four leg lock nonetheless. Death braces with his left foot and twists with all his might remaining, and Suicide gasps in agony. He tries to slide toward the ropes, but goes nowhere fast. He wilts to the mat before he is halfway to the ropes. One, two . . . Nope. Suicide isn't out yet, and he keeps one shoulder off the mat. He slides some more, trying to get to the ropes with grim determination, finally making it there. Death rolls out of the move, finding that he is that much closer to being able to stand. Suicide, however, is able to stand almost immediately, for it was his head hurting that prevented it before. His legs will permit it, but he's careful not to put too much on either one by itself. He glances at Death rising to his feet, and knows that neither one will be able to last much longer. The next big mistake would decide the winner. He turns to Death, catching him in a belly-to-belly waistlock, lifting him up overhead and curling back into a bridge with Death beneath him, shoulders on the mat in a Northern Lights suplex position! The official dives to the mat and counts! One! Two . . . Death isn't quite finished yet, though, and he gets back off the mat. Suicide's weight again is simply not enough to hold him down. Suicide rises first, turning Death into an inside cradle! But Death gets out of this at one! A second cradle follows, and the ref can't even count before Death gets out of this one. Death runs back to the ropes, his legs getting stronger now, and Suicide aims a clothesline at his head. The larger man ducks it. Suicide ducks for a waistlock, and the fans smell spine buster a mile away . . . Unfortunately for Suicide, so does Death. A front facelock results, and Death lifts up with it enough to duck under Suicide's left arm at the same time as he hooks the smaller man's left leg. Suicide's eyes widen in horror as Death takes a step to make sure he won't land in the middle of the ring, before turning him over into a cradle suplex! The bridge is flawless (pardon the pun), and Death has his hands locked the moment Suicide lands! One! Two. . . And three! Death releases, and stands, his arms raised in jubilation. The referee, himself awestruck by the quick ending, raises his hand in victory. The Grinch makes the last tribute: "THE WINNER OF THIS BOUT, AND STILL WORLD HEAVYWEIGHT CHAMPION: DOCTOR DEATH!!" However, Death looks Suicide in the eyes with respect. Both know that it could have gone either way at this point, and Death is quite interested in seeing to it that the next open contract match is a little bit better prepared for. ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ Well, there it is, folks. I was actually quite undecided as to how I would have this one end, since I know everyone likes a surprise upset. The popularity of the first Flawless vs. Doc match would seem to indicate this. However, for reasons I will keep secret for now, Doc had to keep the title. Still to come from the FWF singles office: The Invincible Titan Morgan defends his U.S. title against one half of the World Tag Team champions, Aaron "Lightning" Murphy. The Mighty Annihilus takes on Kane the Undertaker.
