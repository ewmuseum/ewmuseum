FWF: Titan vs. Reed

13 April 1992

Message-ID: <1992Apr13.185238.7088@CS.ORST.EDU> Date: 13 Apr 92 18:52:38 GMT Sender: usenet@CS.ORST.EDU Distribution: na Organization: Oregon State University, Computer Science Dept. Lines: 294 Nntp-Posting-Host: prism.cs.orst.edu The Fictional Wrestling Federation's main office has opened up again after the weekend, with the second mandatory title defense by an FWF World Champion against the U.S. Champion. "Crimson" Joe Reed, the U.S. Champion, will step into the ring with the World Heavyweight Champion, the Invicible Titan Morgan. Comments from both: Reed . . . "After a long wait to get into the FWF, and after a long wait to get a shot at the United States Heavyweight Championship, it's finally here. I have a match with Titan Morgan for the World Title. Well, I'm not going to sit here and bore you with jabbering. Morgan, I'm going to let you go on, listen to you lay it on thick . . . and then I'll beat you in the ring." Morgan . . . "You know something, all you TitanManiacs, I didn't know how long it was gonna take before I'd get a chance to prove myself, that I didn't beat Annihilus for this title by a mere fluke, that I'd finally get a defense! No time in the history of the FWF has the World Championship been successfully defended against a U.S. Champion. And that's something I've gotta think about as I step into the ring. But I'm not scared in the least, dude, because I've got the strongest force in the universe riding my bandwagon, and the largest arms in the world ready to run wild on you! Joe Reed, yeah, you've paid your dues, and you've had a long wait to get to today. But when you step into the ring with Titan Morgan, that's when all the stops go out, all the Maniacs line up against you, and all power of the World Heavyweight Champion is brought to stand the test of time! And when I walk out of the arena, I will still have this Title around my waist, because when you mess with the best, you'll fall like the rest!" --------------------------------------------------------------------------- The tune of "Badd Company" strikes the ears of the fans in the Charleston Civic Center in South Carolina. The fans have a mixed reaction to the man in the red mask who walks down to the ring with the gold belt around his waist. Many of them follow the instinct to simply vilify anyone who opposes Titan Morgan, and boo mercilessly for him. Some figure he's a face, despite it all, so why not cheer for him. Some figure he's a good wrestler, and so is Morgan, so why not cheer for him. They're still arguing amongst themselves as Alfred Grinchley begins the introductions. "THE FOLLOWING CONTEST IS FOR THE WORLD HEAVYWEIGHT CHAMPIONSHIP! IT IS SCHEDULED FOR ONE FALL. COMING DOWN THE AISLE, IS THE CHALLENGER. FROM LEXINGTON, KENTUCKY; HE WEIGHS TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-THREE POUNDS. HERE IS THE UNITED STATES HEAVYWEIGHT CHAMPION . . . `CRIMSON' JOE REEEEEEEEEEEED!!" The man's blue eyes glance all about the spectators arrayed both for and against him in this arena. He thinks about the possible outcomes that this match could have. He ponders wryly the possiblity that his own number-one contender, Adrian "the Mauler" Romulus, may be left with a vacant title to contend for a second time. Then he can no longer hear himself pondering, for there is no hearing above the deafening roar that responds to the opening notes of "Eye of the Tiger" over the PA system in the Civic Center. The Grinch hesitates, then tries to shout over the the sound. "AND HIIIIIS OPPONENT, FROM VENICE BEACH, CALIFORNIA. HE WEIGHS THREE HUNDRED AND FIFTEEN POUNDS, AND HE IS THE WORLD HEAVYWEIGHT CHAMPION: THE INVINCIBLE TIIIIITAN MOOOOOOOOOOOORRGAAAAAAAANNNNN!!!!!" The man parts the dressing room curtains in the yellow-and-azure shirt and tights, and points his fingers at either side of the aisle, raising his arms in salute to the TitanManiacs who are going utterly berserk in their adoration of this man. Titan Morgan needs few credentials to be great, but he has them anyhow. He is the only man in the FWF who has ever pinned the Mighty Annihilus, and it is only fitting that he now has the World Heavyweight Championship as a prize of that feat. He thinks of all the heels who are gunning for him and his title, and can take some relief that he is up against a face in this one. Not that "Crimson" Joe Reed is going to make this any easier. Indeed, the man is the number-one contender for his title. As he enters the ring, he takes his golden shirt and tears it from his chest, hurling it out to the fans without even caring where it lands. There is a mad scramble in the direction of where it flew, as a dozen marks rush to catch it. The radioactive-orange chest shows no sign of blemish or flab, and his blonde hair is full and almost shoulder- length. His mustache reaches just to the lower curve of the lips, and his blue eyes meet those of his challenger. He removes the World Title belt, handing it to the referee. The ref holds it up high, allowing all in the Civic Center to see what this is all about. The bell sounds, the music fades, and Morgan and Reed square off in the ring, standing eye to eye still. Morgan smiles at his smaller opponent, but Reed makes no expression of outrage at the gesture. Reed goes for a collar-and- elbow tie with the champion, and is just as swiftly hoisted off the ground and hurled bodily into the turnbuckles unceremoniously. The fans erupt, as Morgan flexes the huge 25-inch biceps and shows off the trapezius muscles by flexing the arms in front. Reed rolls his eyes skyward, and Morgan notices the reaction. Nothing more than slight irritation here. A second collar-and-elbow tie, and Morgan immediately tosses Reed over the hip. Reed stands, running back at Morgan, who flips him back in a Japanese arm drag. Reed lunges again, trying to duck under it, but Morgan ducks with him and repeats the move. Reed stands a third time, not foolish enough to get tossed a third time. He nods his head slowly and smiles, not seeming to be particularly concerned about the advantage Morgan seems to hold. The match is still young, and he has plenty of oomph left. He's just scouting so far. Reed hestitates, but then risks a third lock-up. This time, he is quicker than Morgan, and takes the World Champion over the hip in a headlock. Morgan just as quickly loops the leg over the head and forces him off. Reed is surprised as Morgan releases him from what might have been a devastating head- scissors, only to find that his surprise has left him off-guard. Morgan backs him into the corner and deals out a hard series of rights and lefts to the abdominals, followed by and uppercut, followed by a reverse knife-edge chop. Reed half-ricochets off the turnbuckles, and gets hit with a second chop. Morgan starts up with the fisticuffs again, and Reed tries to hit back, but the man is too big and he's hitting too hard. Morgan dishes out three more hard chops before finally bowing to the irate referee's wishes and whipping him out of the corner. But Morgan is only a foot behind Reed in his uncon- trolled run, landing a swift lariat instants after Reed hits the opposite corner. Reed slumps against the turnbuckles, shaking his head. He is no longer in scouting mode, he's in hurting mode. Morgan waves his hand, cupping it to his ear, receiving a positive response from the crowd. It does give Reed a little time to think, and he needs it at this point. When Morgan goes back to the corner, Reed decides to throw the rulebook out and pokes him across the eyes. Reed turns Morgan into the corner and deals out a series of rights and lefts of his own. The more fanatic marks as well as the hardcores start to boo at this, and Reed finally brings Morgan out of the corner, and lifts him up for a bodyslam. But he surprises the champ, and drops him hard across the knee. Morgan arches his back for a few seconds, as Reed backs to the ropes. A leg is dropped across Titan's throat and sternum. The message is sent, by using one of his own finishers on him. Reed brings Morgan back up to his feet, and loops one arm over his head. He tires to lift . . . but can't quite get 315 pounds up. He tries again, but Morgan blocks it this time. The Titan, now recovering from the leg drop, puts one hand flat on Reed's belly and ducks, forcing him down. He lifts the man up into the air until he is perfectly vertical, holding him there to enjoy the upside-down view of the world around him. A crescendo of fans' support meets his ears the longer and longer he holds the U.S. Champion in the air, until finally, a full 35 seconds later, he lands the man's back firmly onto the canvas. Morgan looks out to the fans, throwing his arms up in the air. He stands up Reed, slapping his left arm, ducking around behind him as he loops it over his head in one graceful motion. He lifts the man up, dropping him hard on the knee. Reed gets no respite after the atomic drop, as Morgan lifts him back up, steadies him horizontally, steps slowly out from under his weight and lets him fall straight down to the mat. The impacts are starting to wear Reed down noticeably, for he is barely moving at this point. He knows he has to come up with some offense soon, or this one is history. Morgan covers Reed for the pin. One! Two . . . Few are surprised when Reed takes up one shoulder. Morgan gets up, and Reed rolls over to the side of the ring, finally going outside. He has to get some sort of offensive going, or he's meat for the eating. The Titan comes out after him, standing him up . . . . . . and gets his head rammed unexpectedly into the steel fence for his trouble. Reed shakes his head, having saved his strength outside the ring long enough, and sends Morgan back the barricade, smashing the small of the back. He then puts Morgan back into the ring, and goes in after him. He sets Morgan up for a vertical suplex, and this time he gets the man up. He can't hold Morgan up for 35 seconds, of course, but he doesn't really care. He's thinking of economical moves, not showboating. Morgan falls to the mat hard, and Reed is quickly on his feet again, readying his next move. This man wastes no time. Reed grabs Morgan's shins under his shoulders, and slowly twists the man over onto his belly, sitting back in a Boston crab. The pain to the lower back multiplied, Morgan grinds his teeth against the scream that instinct wants to let loose. The referee asks for a submission, and he lets it out in a loud "NO!" He tries to get his hands underneath him, but it does him little good. Morgan falls back down in a sort of mutant push-up position, with Reed still grinding away at the spine with the crab. The official again asks for a submission . . . . . . and Morgan answers by kicking Reed off of him. Reed bounces off the ropes and almost trips over Morgan on the rebound backwards, and turns to the champion in surprise. But Morgan is still hurting on the mat, trying to make his way over to the ropes. Reed stops him with a kick to the aching small of the back. Morgan, lying on his belly, stops for a rest. Reed gives him none. He lifts Morgan up by the arms, squatting over him, draping the arms over his knees and seizing him by the chin, bending back with all his weight. The camel clutch picks up where the Boston crab left off, working away at the lower back. Reed doesn't seem to expect Morgan to give this up, but a little pain in a man's life never hurt. :^) Morgan tries to get his arms off of Reed's knees, but to little avail. The man still has him thoroughly ensnared. He shifts his knees a little, slowly and painstakingly getting them underneath him. Reed sees that Morgan is likely to get out of this one, too, and lets him have a breather for a moment. But he's not thinking mercy in his mind. He brings Morgan back up to his feet, signalling for the Kentucky Back- smasher! This is what he's been aiming for all along, of course. He takes Morgan in vertical suplex position once more near the ropes. He lifts Morgan up, slingshots him off the top rope, and twists into a crunching power slam as he flips the champion over! Morgan's lungs exhale involuntarily in a heave of air, as Reed hooks the leg. One! Two . . . Morgan heaves out of the cradle, coming to his knees halfway across the ring, shaking his head in unutterable fury that this man would dare to think he could pin the Invincible Titan Morgan! Reed, wide-eyed, can do little more than punch him. But the Titan only stares at him, shaking his head. As Morgan seems to forget him in his effort to regain his feet, Reed reminds him with a second fist. The official warns him against it, but Reed's eyes are fixed on Morgan's, again shaking his ahead. Reed hits him a third time, and Morgan stands, shaking his fists and his head as he walks around the ring raging. Reed nails him with a drop kick. Morgan stops, still standing. As Reed stands, Morgan raises the finger, shaking it in the U.S. Champion's face, warning him of the punishment that is to come if he is foolish enough to continue. Reed goes for another punch, but Morgan lifts his arm, catching the blow on the tricep harmlessly, grabbing onto Reed's mask with his left arm. Morgan deals out a closed fist of his own, and Reed reels back. Morgan hits him a second time, and a third, backing him to the ropes, then whips him across the ring, stepping forward to snare him in a waistlock, lifting him up and turning him around, sending all his leverage into vertical momentum that is concurrent with gravitational force. (I.e. DOWN.) Reed bounces off the mat in the spine buster, coming to rest on his back as Morgan goes to the ropes, and comes off with the dreaded Leg Drop of Doom! Morgan covers Reed for the pinfall, one, two . . . A big fat guy in a black mask that is little more than a collection of straps with coveralls that mix red and black comes into the ring and kicks Morgan off. The official quickly calls for the bell . . . ( *** Jim Ross commentary mode on *** ) "Oh my gosh, it's BIG TRUCK VADER, coming into the ring; he just hammered the champion off of Reed. Oh, no he's got a chair, Vader with a chair into the back of the head! Morgan is hurt! He's lying on his back. Reed's getting out of the ring, but the story is Morgan lying with his back exposed. Vader, off the ropes . . . Oh, no the VADER BOMB! First the Kentucky Backsmasher, now the Vader Bomb to the back of the Invincible Titan Morgan! Vader's trying to break the back of the World Heavyweight Champion! "Vader picks Morgan up, OH MY GOSH HE'S GORILLA PRESSING HIM! THREE HUNDRED AND FIFTEEN POUNDS! He slams him down, kicking him until he's diagonal in the center of the ring! Vader's climbing the top rope!" (Vader, at this point, is screaming something on the order of "NO PPHAAANE!!") Ross-mode continues, "Vader, off the second rope, OH MY GOSH, get some help out there. He's completely demolishing the World Champion! Morgan is hurt, and I'm afraid he's hurt badly. Harley Pace is coming out, raising Vader's arm in victory, as help finally come from the locker room; but Morgan is hurt, and he's hurt badly! The jury is out; how much damage has been done do Morgan's back? He's hurt, and he's hurt badly . . ." [ad infinitum about "hurt and hurt badly"] ( *** Jim Ross commentary mode off *** ) --------------------------------------------------------------------------- Sorry. I wanted to. I couldn't resist. Coming up, of course, a matchup between the Invincible Titan Morgan and Big Truck Vader (Van Vader's big brother :-) for the World Heavyweight Championship, which will be my official commentary on the current WCW angle, of course. Assuming Morgan keeps the title after that, he will then defend against Doctor Death.
