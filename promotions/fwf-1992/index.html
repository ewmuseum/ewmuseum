A scripting league that wrote its matches out move by move on rec.sport.pro-wrestling, numbered every one of them, and ran its World Championship through four hands before Doctor Death took it back. Four title lineages, 27 shows and 57 wrestlers survive from its 1992–1995 run. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Fictional Wrestling Federation wrote its matches out. Not results, not a card and a winner — the whole match, move by move, hold by hold, posted to rec.sport.pro-wrestling with “FWF: ” at the front of the Subject line so anyone who did not want it could killfile it. A single match ran to two hundred lines and more, and the writer signed off underneath with what was coming next.

It ran three hands and kept two ledgers. One writer took the singles and the editing, a second took the tag teams, a third wrote matches and sent them in; an archivist in Alaska kept the back catalogue on floppy disks and offered to post copies to anyone who sent him return postage. The federation's own newbie post put it plainly: characters were welcome from anyone, and “anyone that has either a wrestler or tag team they wish to submit should send one of us mail.”

The ledgers are what survive best. By January 1993 the singles file ran to sixty-eight numbered matches, each one a line for the bout and a line for the finish, with a bracketed arrow where a championship changed hands; the tag file ran to twenty-two. Four championships move through them. Doctor Death took the World Championship out of the founding tournament, lost it to a man who hit him with a stick, and won it back with the Vulcan Death Grip. The United States title went through six reigns and a vacancy. The Intercontinental title was awarded to whoever won a twelve-man battle royale on somebody else's card. The tag titles were decided by an eight-match round robin whose winners left a broken back, a broken arm and a concussion behind them.

Its roster was mostly its own inventions — Titan Morgan, the Mighty Annihilus, a Dark Star and a Greased Lightning and an Iron Warrior — with television wrestlers spoofed in beside them for a laugh or borrowed straight and given better matches than they were getting. Its own booker was open about why: he had watched enough flying, and “in the FWF, the guys who mimic Jushin Liger are few and far between.”

It reached outside itself twice. In April 1992 its World Champion faced another federation's on a shared card, title against title, billed as settling who the real world champion in fantasy wrestling was. In the winter it ran a Clash of the Masters against the PWA, three championships at once; the world title came back unified and the tag titles did not.

How it ended isn't in the surviving record. Its last held match report is of 7 January 1993 and its last held post of the 25th, its archivist writing that the matches “haven't come at a fast enough pace to put enough of a drain on my time” and that anyone who wanted the old ones should write to him at home. Four days later the discs stop.

Storylines

The arcs below are recovered from a show-by-show read of all twenty-four held cards, plus the fed's own singles and tag ledgers.

The Blade Runners' breakup Written by AI, 2026

"Rock" Steve Rockwell and "Sting" Stan Golding enter the fed's own founding tag tournament as the Blade Runners, trained partners who cannot get past the round robin together. Golding cannot accept the loss the way Rockwell does; in their own tag ledger's Match 13, against Devastation, Golding gets himself counted out and then beats Rockwell with the timekeeper's bell, deserting him mid-match. "Sting" is billed "Scorpion" from that match on, allied with "Greased Lightning" Damien Flayer; Rockwell reforms the Blade Runners with "Flash" Johnathon Flann. A Scorpion-and-Flayer tag match against the New Blade Runners ends in a double countout brawl that spills into the aisle, both former partners still fighting as officials drag them apart (fwf-blade-runners-vs-scorpion-flayer-1992-10-06).

Storm's reign of terror Written by AI, 2026

Aaron "Lightning" Murphy and Jerry "Thunder" McGaghney, together as Storm, win the fed's own founding tag tournament by breaking Demolisher Venom's back and hospitalizing one of the Blues Brothers with a bell and a chair along the way. They hold the Tag Team Championship from that tournament until Devastation takes it in November 1992 (fwf-devastation-vs-storm-1992-11-03), a match Storm loses only after breaking "Hurricane" Andrew Simms' arm the week before and finding him at ringside for payback. Stripped of the belts, Storm signs an open contract and squashes Sultan Sitan El Sagga and Nicholas Scratch in the surviving record's last tag match, breaking Scratch's back with the same Boston crab that ended the Demolishers ten months earlier (fwf-sagga-scratch-vs-storm-1993-01-07).

The Dark Star and the Professor Written by AI, 2026

"Dark Star" Dominic Walters spurns a handshake from debuting veteran "Professor" John Goulding, beats him, and piledrives him unconscious outside the ring before the Latin Wildfire runs in to make the save (fwf-dominic-vs-professor-1992-04-10). Goulding becomes the Wildfire's own second, and Walters allies with Jerry "the Stratofortress" Henderson — who beats the Wildfire once as a last-minute substitute for the Skull Cruncher, with an assist from Walters at ringside. When the Wildfire finally gets his shot at Walters directly, a broken hand keeps him from wrestling it himself; he sends "Vision" Stu Watson in his place, and Watson beats Walters by disqualification when Henderson interferes with a chair (fwf-dominic-vs-wildfire-ii-1992-11-13).

The Clash of the Masters Written by AI, 2026

Twice the surviving record has the fed reach outside itself. In April 1992, Doctor Death's FWF World Championship is put up against the EMWA's own John Girder at Dreamcard V, a match Death wins by disqualification with a set of planted brass knuckles, previewed and recapped across fwf-titan-vs-doc-ii-1992-04-15 and fwf-lightning-vs-doc-1992-11-18. That November the fed's own ledger records a full Clash of the Masters against the PWA — World, United States and Tag Team champions on both sides. Death beats the PWA's own World Champion and carries both belts afterward, billed "PWA/FWF World Heavyweight Champion"; Titan Morgan's United States Championship and Devastation's Tag Team Championship both survive losses at the same event with no change of hands recorded anywhere held (see the title ledgers for the full, sourced disagreement).
