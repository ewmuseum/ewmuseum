Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

FCW called itself “the WCW of E-Wrestling” in its own words, running one card a week and a monthly pay-per-view on a mixed roster of real and original wrestlers from its earliest surviving post, 25 November 1997. Its house rules ran just like the promotions it borrowed its style from: angles and run-ins, a match ending in disqualification if a wrestler refused to job, and stables allowed only at the president's own discretion, “so One Faction isn't the Whole Fed.”

It fielded four championships — the FCW Heavyweight, the Intercontinental, the Tag Team, and the Cruiserweight titles — each protected by a signed contract that its own president admitted he would “mostly” let a wrestler out of, and planned a Women's Division if enough female wrestlers signed.

It closed and reopened within five weeks: “The FCW just recently closed down to fix up the Federation a little bit,” read a 14 December notice, and by 25 December it announced its return with a changed roster policy, dropping real-wrestler portrayals for originals only ahead of a planned first card on New Year's Day 1998.

Its last surviving post, 31 December 1997, was still taking anyone: “Bad Talent, Good Talent, Will take anyone right now.” No card, match or title change of any kind is in the surviving record, and nothing later survives.
