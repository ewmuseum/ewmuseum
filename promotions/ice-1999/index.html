A federation whose own recruitment posts and self-published history trace a founding, a financial collapse, a resurfacing, and a company merger across three years, still recruiting handlers into 1999. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Independent Championship E-Wrestling was formed in May 1996 under its own President and quickly signed circuit names Robski and Sabin Rene Figaro to a roster that ran twice-weekly booked cards plus a wrap-up show, ICE Cold. Its first pay-per-view, ICE Storm, opened with a Scaffold Match between Robski and Super Furry Animal and put “Deadly” Dylan Vaughn's World Title on the line against Holey Guacamole, announcers Larry Jenkins and Jerry Connors calling the card live.

By its own account, ICE folded that August over financial irregularities, just ahead of a planned Tri Stable War Games spectacular. Its President resurfaced that October alongside a new business partner, bringing back old names and adding new ones, among them the tag team Better off Dead.

In January 1997 ICE's own leadership bought the parent company of a rival promotion, Electronic Roadkill Wrestling Inc., from its own head and merged it in, seating him on its board. Previewing the coming year that December, he wrote that ICE would contend for the annual awards the way IIWF (a league he and ICE's own President were both part of) had the year before, and signed off, “ICE will meltdown the rest!” The partnership soured; he quit the board claiming ICE had muscled him out of his own company, and on 7 February 1997, at a Madison Square Garden pay-per-view called Frozen Warriors, ICE's own account records what it calls Black Friday — the collapse that brought the promotion, and its President, down.

ICE was still recruiting past that point: its last surviving document, 26 May 1999, asks for heels and tag teams and reproduces the promotion's own Players Guide history in full. No further closure notice survives.
