ICE Looking for Handlers (26 May 1999)

26 May 1999

Hi all ICE is still looking for handlers, but for some strange reason, we are more in need of Heels than Faces right now... strange thing I know.
We are still in need of Tag Teams, so send those in too :) Mike -- ><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><>< Independent Championship E-Wrestling Players Intro and Guide V 1.6 ><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><>< ><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><>< Independent Championship E-Wrestling History ><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><>< ICE was formed in May of 1996 by current President Mike Elliott. ICE was to immediately attract some top singles and tag team wrestlers to bolster the creation of [name withheld]. Such well known e-wrestling stars like Robski and Sabin Rene Figaro were soon to sign contracts with ICE.
With the arrival of Bone Lord and The Mighty Jack Ov, ICE was to see an ongoing feud between Robski and the so called Kaos, Karnage and
Killing; the KKK. This feud culminated in libel suits being fired at Robski by Bone Lord's manager Missy Voodoo concerning an alleged pregnancy. When a local organisation, the MWL folded, ICE's ranks were to be bolstered further by the arrival of the Fraternity of the Stone, and
From Canada with Cod. The Cod's joined the Southern Man and Robski in the Commonwealth Companions, and ICE was to see it's biggest feud as the Companions, Fraternity, and KKK went at each others throats.
Robski's wrestling in ICE was to be limited during the latter part of July and August, following a bitter feud between himself, a dedicated Birmingham City supporter, and White Yorke, a fan of soccer rivals Aston Villa. Robski was eventually cleared of all charges related to an arson attack at Villa's home, Villa Park.
August was to herald the apparent end of ICE. Just prior to Below Freezing, ICE's biggest PPV spectacular, and the venue of a mammoth Tri Stable War Games match involving the Fraternity of the Stone, the KKK, and the Commonwealth Companions, ICE was to go under due to financial irregularities. President Elliott denied any involvement, but ICE was closed down.
October 1996. Mike Elliott resurfaced along with a new business partner, a lady known only as Shaz. ICE was to come with them. This was a new age in ICE history. Many old stars were to return, but many new ones were to arrive. The Armed Forces, former Tag Team World
Champions elsewhere, and a new tag team sensation in Better off Dead. The singles roster blossomed once more. Things look good for ICE. January 1997, and ICE entered it's newest era. With profits sky rocketing, ICE President Mike Elliott, contacted head of Roadkill
Productions, Zach Hyatt, with an offer to buy the parent company of
Electronic Roadkill Wrestling Inc. The deal went through, and ERWI was merged into ICE. Zach Hyatt took a place on the Board of Directors, and a new age blossomed.
What looked so promising, is normally not. And thus it was here. ICE blossomed with this new partnership, but it didn't last. Politics began to play their part in ICE, and when Hyatt quit the board in a rage, claiming that ICE had muscled him out of his company, he left.
ICE was rocked, after all, Hyatt had taken a very prominent role within the ICE board. Elliott looked at the situation, perhaps through tinted glasses, but kept plodding on. Mistake? Who knows, but what came after really seemed to indicate it.
February 7th, 1997: The day the walls came crashing down. A very fitting description for what happened, what many since have called
Black Friday. ICE had set up shop at Madison Square Garden for Frozen Warriors, the first Pay Per View following the merger, and what would turn into a disaster that would bring ICE crashing down, but along with it, Elliott would fall, and fall hard.
