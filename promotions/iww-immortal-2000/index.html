Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Immortal World Wrestling introduced itself on 6 May 2000 promising staying power other e-feds lacked, under a president who wrote that “the staff won’t let this close,” and laid out a detailed rulebook: a hundred-line cap on roleplays, an all-original roster with no copies of real-world wrestlers, and stables capped at six members.

Its planned weekly show, Sunday Showdown, was to run from arenas across the United States, with every title potentially defended in a given week. Four championships anchored the promotion — a World Championship, an Immortal Championship as its top contender's belt, a Hardkore Championship defended in extreme-rules matches, and a Cruiserweight Championship for wrestlers under 145 pounds.

The founding post laid out an entire year of pay-per-views by name and date, from a January “Stable Aggression” event at Madison Square Garden through a Christmas Day “X Mas In Hell,” staged at a mix of real arenas and invented ones the promotion admitted did not exist, “but it’ll be good matches” regardless.

No further document survives. Whether IWW staged Sunday Showdown even once, or any of the pay-per-views it announced a year in advance, is not recorded.
