Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Master of the Squared Circle announced itself on 18 April 2000 as a sixty-four-man, four-region tournament run “as if it were a NET federation,” its own FAQ billing it as the tournament that would finally settle who really was e-wrestling's “top dog.”

Its bracket ran five rounds toward a single final in Honolulu: a one-flash house-show round of thirty-two matches split between San Diego and Portland, a two-flash round in Buffalo and Denver, a three-flash round allowing strategies in Boston and Phoenix, a four-team round in Orlando and Salt Lake City, and semifinals in Nashville, seeding decided entirely by the tournament's own organizer rather than by any wrestler's record.

Applications arrived from across the hobby — entrants representing the UWF and a Canadian federation among them — and by 27 April its organizer reported all sixty-four slots filled, naming Rick Styles and Mad Goat among the bigger draws who had signed on.

Roughly four weeks into the tournament, after forty-eight of the sixty-four had roleplayed their way through the early rounds, the organizer stopped running it without any announcement of his own; participants who wrote to ask were, by their own account, ignored, blocked, and cursed at.

The newsgroup argued for days afterward over whether the bracket could be salvaged with its remaining sixteen entrants, and one contestant's own handler offered to take it over; no later document survives to say whether a restart ever happened, or whether Master of the Squared Circle ran again under any name.
