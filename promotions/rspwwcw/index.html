Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Rec.sport.pro-wrestling's World Championship Wrestling ran its own weekly Rawnitro and Thunder broadcasts as a WWF/WCW parody whose roster was the newsgroup itself — its own posters wrestling as themselves and as invented personas alike, called by an announce team voiced as Vince McMahon, Jim Ross, Jerry Lawler and Bobby Heenan. The RSPWWCW brand is first attested in its own record on 6 January 1997, already mid-tournament for a Cruiserweight Championship with Taskmistress Liz Michael holding the World Heavyweight belt.

Seven championships ran through its own record, none of them settled: the World Heavyweight title alone changed hands more than two dozen times across the surviving record, from an inaugural tournament won by Zoogz Rift through reigns held by Waru, Jack Epstein (three times), Dollywood, Powrhug Kare, Stone Cold Paul Smith, and Taskmistress Liz Michael herself on four separate occasions before the belt reached FunkyM. Its Tag Team Championship belonged for most of its life to one act, the Perfect Canadians (the Maritimer and Deadshot), who won the inaugural tag tournament and reclaimed the belts three further times across the following two years against a rotating cast of challengers.

Its own community wrote itself into the show as much as any storyline did: a live hotline segment teased a press conference over whether two of its own stars were adding a member, breaking up, or forming a stable; out-of-character newsgroup arguments over the fed's own booking politics ran openly across its own pages, personas and posters trading barbs in the same breath; and its own broadcasts leaned into the joke, panning across crowd signs mocking its own performers and noting on air when its own “sign Nazis” moved in to confiscate the ones that went too far.

How it ended isn't in the surviving record. Its last surviving document, from the Alamodome in San Antonio on 21 August 1999, carries a stable calling itself the DamNation hunting for Taskmistress Liz Michael, by then billed as the promotion's own on-screen owner, and promotes a further card the surviving record does not itself hold. A rival newsgroup promotion was still describing RSPWWCW as a live counterpart as late as December 1998.
