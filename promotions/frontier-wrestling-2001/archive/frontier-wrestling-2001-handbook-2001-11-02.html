---------- Frontier Wrestling [Official Handbook, v 1.0] ---------------- Table of Contents: ---------------- - 1. Introduction - 2. Rules and Regulations - 3. Roleplaying System - 4. Titles - 5. Frontier Cards - 6. Character Application ---------------- - 1. Introduction: ---------------- Kayfabe: Frontier Wrestling was founded in 2001 by former professional wrestler turned businessman/aspiring wrestling promoter Jason "Hybrid" Scott, with one main objective in mind: produce a succcessful, competitive, and exciting alternative to the frenzy of "sports entertainment" promotions out there. How do we plan on doing this? Frontier wishes to combine the creative and compelling storylines of mid-90s Extreme Championship Wrestling with the sheer workrate and athleticism seen in Japanese wrestling as well as the late 1980s NWA/WCW. Frontier intends on bringing old-school workrate and in-ring storytelling to the forefront once agian. Real Life: What's shakin' everybody? My name is [name withheld]. I am an 18 year old college freshman, and a native of Tulsa, Oklahoma (Tulsa Community College Football, baby! Still undefeated! :-)). I have been into this little e-wrestling thing for close to six years now, working in too many obscure federations to remember, however most of my best work was during my NAIWF stay as Hybrid. I'm not claiming this federation will be the next EMWC, but dammit, I plan on having some fun making it as good as I can. Hopefully enough of you guys are down for that, and we can make Frontier into a kick-ass little promotion. ---------------- - 2. Rules and Regulations ---------------- This section deals with the rules that are to be followed while participating in Frontier Wrestling. I do not want to limit your freedom in how you handle your character, but there are things that need you should know in advance. These rules will be enforced, with different consequences for different rules. However, all of these rules can potentially lead to termination. - 1. No physical attack of anyone else's wrestler or manager in your flashes or in-rings unless I receive a small note from them saying it is okay. As far as during matches, you can request an attack, but I will only allow it to happen if it makes sense [read: furthers an angle]. There will aslso be no attacking of any Frontier Wrestling employee unless I give you special permission. This includes announcers, staff, referees, camera crew, backstage workers, etc. If you break this rule you will get you a warning, and a second occurance will lead to you looking for a new fed. - 2. Profanity should be left out of your flashes and in-rings. I will allow words such as "ass", "damn", "hell", and "bitch." Anything more than these words should not be used. - 3. No one will leave Frontier Wrestling holding a title. If you decide to leave the federation while holding a belt you will be jobbed out on the next card, period. I hate it when someone joins a fed and quits as soon as they win a championship, as to protect their win/loss record. That kind of stuff isn't going to happen here. - 4. If you lose a match and are curious as to how or why it happened, don't complain. I don't make booking decisions to be spiteful, nor am I biased towards unworthy wrestlers, so don't get an attitude if you lose. Ask me nicely and I will explain to you what led to the decision making towards the match, and perhaps suggest some things for you to improve upon in the future. There aren't going to be any Goldbergs here. - 5. All angles need to be approved by me as well as all attacks and other things. I have final say on what airs on our programs and that is the way it is. However, I am very leniant in what I allow and do prefer that you come up with as many storylines for your wrestlers as you can. Just remember that any angle you have must be played out before moving on to a new storyline. That is the way it should be, to keep continuity going. - 6. Stipulation matches will happen rarely in Frontier, solely for the purpose of keeping them meaningful when they do occur. Once upon a time in wrestling, a no-disqualification match was a big deal. Ladder matches? Never even seen in main-stream wrestling until the early '90s. These kinds of bouts will be saved for FW: Special Edition. -------------- - 3. Roleplaying System -------------- The roleplaying system is an open-flash system. I prefer this system over the closed-flash mostly due to the fact that it gives the fed a "twenty-four hour" feel to it. Something (hopefully) is always happening, instead of just whenver I send out a flash packet. As for roleplaying rules; please don't overdue the swearing. If Steve Austin doesn't say it, you probably shouldn't say it either. This is supposed to be television, folks. Oh yeh, and no racism. I don't care if you're a Klansman in real life, but leave it out of my federation. I guess I should let you know how roleplaying will be judged. I like to be entertained. I totally dig a good wrestling promo. Mix the sports entertainment correctly and you will win. But before you submit a flash, do everyone a favor and run your roleplay through spellcheck. Spelling errors are distracting and I don't like distractions. Number of lines means nothing to me, as long as it is long enough to get your point across but not so long that you are just rambling. I don't know how else to describe what I am looking for, just do what you can. ---------------- - 4. Titles ---------------- World Heavyweight Title. The big one, folks. The World Title is what every wrestler has his eye on when they enter Frontier Wrestling. This baby is held by the best, and tends to be defended once a month, unless I feel like booking title defenses more often. :-) Light-Heavyweight Title. Designed specifically for wrestlers 235lbs. and under, the Light-Heavyweight Title is considered a major accomplishment for our cruiserweights. Once again, title defenses are atleast once a month, or whenever I feel like it. World Tag Team Titles. Self-explanatory, I should hope. I am quite a big fan of tag team wrestling, and would really like to see it flourish in Frontier Wrestling. Like the heavyweight title, only for tag teams. Defended --- get this --- atleast once a month, or whenever I feel like booking a title match. ---------------- - 5. Frontier Cards ---------------- FW: Revolution. The weekly program for Frontier Wrestling, airing every Monday night at 10:00 p.m. EST on Fox Sportsnet. Usually includes four or five matches, and a title match every once in awhile. For the most part, however, those are left for the big shows. Which takes me to... FW: Special Edition. Some call 'em pay-per-views, we call them Special Editions. Current plans have a Special Edition penciled in for every two months, however this could change depending on how things go. Normally will include seven to eight matches, with all three titles being defended. Should make for some quite interesting and memorable television if things go right. ---------------- - 6.
