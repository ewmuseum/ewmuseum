A Rhode Island-based Usenet fed opened in June 1998 under its own on-screen wrestler-president, closed in March 1999, and reopened in 2002 with weekly Friday Night Fallout broadcasts and a one-night International Championship tournament. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

International Championship Wrestling opened on 4 June 1998 as the project of its own wrestler-president, James “Simply The Best” Pillow, whose founding handbook promised a weekly Friday night show, a midweek card, and six titles topped by an International Championship. Friday Night Fallout went out from Madison Square Garden a week later.

Its 1998 pay-per-views were its high-water mark. At Summer Sizzler Shane Diamond kept the International Championship after a brawl through a restroom, a concession stand and the parking lot; by Shockfest the title belonged to “The Italian Stallion” Tony Paseno, and Pillow himself lost a sixty-minute Iron Man match to Diamond when the referee turned the brass knuckles he had confiscated on him.

Eight months after founding it, Pillow closed the promotion because, in the words of a community petition of 6 March 1999, it “wasn't fun anymore.” Former members looking back in 2002 remembered a second president running it after he stepped away, and a fed that had been closed for about three years.

In February 2002 he reopened it. A new FAQ placed the promotion in Rhode Island, kept him off camera as owner, named Shaun Riche its president and brought John Williams and Shane Propone back to the commentary table. Friday Night Fallout returned from Boston on 15 March 2002 with old names — Alex Cote, Tommy Lionheart, the Masked Superstar Jobber, Tha Dragon, who peeled off his wig and became “Top Notch” Nelson Alexander — beside new ones.

Its return to pay-per-view, International Incident at Madison Square Garden, crowned a new International Champion in a one-night tournament: Lionheart, on an injured knee, beat Hawkins in a steel cage after the favourite, Setzer Van Strife, went out in a double disqualification. Van Strife took the Television Championship two weeks later and Pietka the North American Championship a week after that.

The last surviving Fallout, from Toronto on 3 June 2002, ended with Lionheart and the True Living Legend agreeing to a title match at Canadian Conquest, a pay-per-view announced for 30 June. No later card survives.
