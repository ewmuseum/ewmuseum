An Atlantic City-based federation naming its own kayfabe corporate ownership, running two weekly shows and a monthly pay-per-view with a Heavyweight and Television title tournament. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Atlantic Coast Championship Wrestling reported itself, on 25 January 1999, “open for the last 6 months” with “21 wrestlers under contract” and room for more, planning to add a tag team and a lightweight division and promising to “AUTOMATICALY hire” anyone whose application it liked: “the key word for our league is FUN.” In its own kayfabe, it was headquartered at Dunn Towers in Atlantic City, New Jersey, owned by Dunn Enterprises and its founder, Calvin Dunn.

Its February 1999 handbook laid out the rest of its in-house cast: Calvin Dunn as a rarely-seen owner who mostly appeared at pay-per-views, Bryan Sharp as its hands-on president signing matches and handing out suspensions, Drake Savage as head of security, Max Money and Jason Keller announcing every broadcast, Damon Cutter ring-announcing, and Marty Dell and Whisper Hayes on backstage interviews.

It ran two weekly shows — Pure Excitement on Saturdays, from the Dunn Sports Complex on the Atlantic City boardwalk, and a Thursday preview show, ACCW Update — plus four pay-per-views a year: Crowning Glory in March (a sixteen-man tournament for the Glory Cup), Coastal Clash in June, Summer Madness in August, and Hell on Earth in November, headlined by “the dangerous Hell on Earth match.” A Heavyweight Championship, defended at every supercard and roughly every three to four weeks besides, sat above a Television Championship defended every other Pure Excitement.

It booked rather than simulated its matches, weighing roleplay quality alongside submitted in-ring strategies and ongoing storylines — its own handbook called angles “possibly the most important aspect of ACCW” — and it expected new signees to lose early on purpose, on the stated theory that “everybody loses a match or two” to prove they were “in it for the long haul.”

It was still running a multi-part pay-per-view broadcast that December; no closing notice of it survives.
