A newsgroup-posted fed that ran weekly broadcasts and pay-per-views across 1997–1998 and a shorter 2003–2004 run, its founding tag team titles decided in a January double pin and its Anarchy Championship changing hands the same night. Five title lineages, seven shows and 42 wrestlers survive. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The earliest trace of this Australian-operated newsgroup promotion is a passing third-party mention in March 1997: “one is the AEWF, which is full.” Its own later material dates its start to that May, under its own president, running two weekly cards and three house shows for more than twenty wrestlers by January 1998, each one built on a 200-point creation sheet splitting work, strength, experience, technical skill, and “blading/madness.”

Its 27 November 1997 broadcast, Vicious Circle Tonight, ran on its own comedy as much as its wrestling: Joe Mama, already billed as “the first AEWF superstar to have held all the singles belts, except the King of the Air,” gave away his King of Extreme crown to a drinking buddy, Samael, faking a leg cramp mid-match, then dropped a second title the same night in a match against another friend that looked just as fixed — prompting the promotion's own president, on camera, to accuse him of desecrating the belt.

On 1 January 1998, Thursday Night Whiplash rewrote the championship ladder in one night: Moo Kame took the Anarchy Championship from Dreamwalker; the King of the Air match between champion Sean Waltman and Wardancer ended in a double count-out after a returning wrestler, his arm in a cast, blindsided Wardancer with the ladder meant for their match, prompting an announced three-way ladder rematch for the belt; and tag champions the Snakes, Viper and Python, kept their titles against the Dark Side on a double pin.

A Zielinski Cup, announced that same period and named for its own president, opened four brackets — singles and tag, roster and outside newsgroup entrants alike — the first round posted 30 December 1997.

The promotion went quiet after 1998 and returned in late 2003 under the fuller name All European Wrestling Federation, publishing on its own site an elaborate invented history reaching back to a fictional 1932 English boxing promoter, Reginald Humphries Sr. — kayfabe lore written for the relaunch, not a real predecessor to its actual 1997 debut. The restart staged its first show in five years, the Touring Main Event, from the Royal Albert Hall in London on 31 October 2004, and by 7 November had moved its tour to the Palau Blaugrana in Barcelona.

That Barcelona card announcement, carrying no results, is the last surviving document. How the touring era played out, and how the promotion ultimately ended, isn't in the surviving record.

Affiliations

European E-Wrestling Federation (EEF)

Predecessor to AEWF (1997)
The EEF's own founder is named by the AEWF's December 2003 information document as the AEWF's original founder, and the AEWF's own history narrates the EEF's 1998 collapse and its rebirth under the AEWF name; the EEF's dates end where the AEWF's begin.

IeWF (1998)

Crossover booking with AEWF (1997)
This one-off card's own 3 March 1998 update names an All European Wrestling Federation wrestler, Rune, joining its main event to make it a four-way match, and its own 11 March update was issued under the byline of that federation's own Co-President.
