Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Global E-Wrestling Alliance started up as a one-man operation on 28 June 1996, opening to the newsgroup four days later and running its first card, Sunday Struggle, on 7 July. Each handler controlled exactly one wrestler — a deliberate choice, in its own words, meant to drive “much more interaction between handlers in order to co-ordinate tag teams, alliances, stables” — with matches simmed on TNM, two commentated cards a week, and a monthly pay-per-view.

It grew steadily against a field of short-lived rivals, and by January 1997 was pitching that longevity directly: “Not many leagues last this long... You want to join a simmed e-fed with top-notch writing... you don't want to join some new e-fed that's going to last two weeks before the fed head gets bored.” By March 1997 it had grown to about 35 handlers.

On its own first anniversary, 2 July 1997, it began converting from a simulated to a fully booked promotion: “we are gradually moving away from writing matches off of the TNM logfile,” its own recruitment post explained, seeking writers on quality rather than an established name. Its own web presence grew alongside it, moving from a Brock University tilde page through two GeoCities addresses, the second of which had grown into an eleven-federation community news hub by December 1997, alongside its own channel on DALnet.

Its only surviving complete 1997 title defense ran inside a shared inter-promotional broadcast rather than its own card. On 4 December 1997, Global Heavyweight Champion Dr. Hate — two months into the title after beating J-Man at Business of Punishment II — defended against founding-era star Red Raven in a bloody 14:15 match: Hate smuggled in a scalpel to bludgeon Raven's forehead open, flattened the referee with an errant moonsault, and finally finished Raven with his Assisted Suicide lariat after Raven's own figure-four couldn't put him away.

The same broadcast found its tag division troubled: commentary called the champions, a villain outfit billed as the Fourth Reich, evidence of “the sad state of GEWA,” feuding that week with the team of Hardcore and Ed Salazar in a non-title brawl.

No document survives past its own December 1997 news-hub update. Whether its conversion to a fully booked promotion was completed, or how it ended, is not in the surviving record.
