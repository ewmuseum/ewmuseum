Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

FWG, the Fantasy Wrestling Group, opened in the first half of 2001 with five titles, a Tuesday flagship called Rampage and a Friday undercard, High Impact Friday. Its own FAQ set the fiction in the hands of a Chairman and a Commissioner, running a small roster toward an August calendar of pay-per-views.

That calendar ended in a single stipulation. At its License to Kill PPV on 5 August 2001, a ten-on-ten tag team match staked the whole promotion: the losing side's federation would close. Two of FWG's own men turned on their team in the closing minutes, and a rival promotion walked away with the win, the World Heavyweight Championship unified overnight into a World Triple Crown, and, in the fiction, ownership of FWG itself.

The promotion carried on under its new masters. A revised FAQ two weeks later named four titles headed by that Triple Crown, and the weekly cards kept running: a Cruiserweight change of hands on the next Rampage, and a Deathmatch title that moved through two more sets of hands inside a month.

Its WrestleSeries PPV, on 2 September 2001, was the biggest single card FWG ever served: a tag title three-way fought with tables, ladders and live explosives, a ladder match for the Television title, a four-way Deathmatch brawl that left the arena for a parking lot and a fast-food counter, and a main event in which the Triple Crown changed hands again, back to the man it had been taken from at License to Kill.

By the end of the year FWG was running its own cross-promotion tournament, open to handlers from several other federations, and had agreed to a merger with another promotion, announced to a newsgroup audience already arguing over whether it would survive the new year.
