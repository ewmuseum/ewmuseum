Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Internet Relay Chat Championship Wrestling dates its own founding to February 1996 by its own on-air account: color commentator Rob Spare, delivering a shoot-style rant more than a year later, recalled being hired as the promotion's “very first color commentator,” then moved into managing “two of the top wrestlers ever to step foot in a ring,” Ravishing Rick Rude and Vader, under a promised push he said never fully arrived.

It introduced itself to the newsgroup on 20 May 1997 as “a e-fed that uses real wrestlers and is booked... running for about 15 months,” the same night it ran its second annual pay-per-view, IRCCWMania II, from Philadelphia's CoreStates Center: twelve matches and five titles, headlined by Stone Cold Steve Austin defending the World Heavyweight Championship against “Hollywood” Hulk Hogan. The opener pitted Shawn Michaels against Hunter Hearst Helmsley, billed as the fed's first-ever World Heavyweight Champion, who had won that title in a battle royal at Fight For The Titles '96.

The same card crowned a new North American Champion and an inaugural Continental Tag Team Championship, while its pre-show Countdown special ran a video-package recap of Raven's serial destruction of Tommy Dreamer's allies — Rey Misterio Jr., Roddy Piper, the Sandman — building to a six-man tag pitting Dreamer, Misterio and Sandman against Raven, Psycho Sid and Dean Malenko.

Its surviving record ends a week later on Monday Night Cooked, 26 May 1997, run from Three Rivers Stadium as part of a stated tour of American ballparks: the newly crowned Continental Tag Team Champions, the Eliminators, defended against the Gangstas, Shane Douglas returned to his hometown chasing Rick Rude, and Rob Spare announced he was stepping down as a manager on air, promising a return to the broadcast booth within a week. No closing notice survives.
