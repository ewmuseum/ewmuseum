Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The WCIWF opened on 24 November 1995 with a single marathon broadcast, called to order by a three-man announce team — Jim Moss, Vince McMoney and Todd PettingZoo — with a roving reporter (“Bored Alfred Hayes”) and a ring announcer (“Coward Finkel”) whose names spoof the real broadcasters of the era. That one card crowned champions across four of its five titles: a twenty-man battle royal decided the World Championship, an eight-man tournament settled the Television title, and a seven-team tag gauntlet plus a second tag tournament filled out the World and US Tag Team titles.

Jumping Joey Smith won the World title out of the battle royal; the Great Oni took the Television Championship; Jumping Jack Jones and the Brown Bomber won the World Tag Team title, and Xtra Tuff and Everlasting Payne the US Tag Team title. The opening match of the night — the Jet beating the Brown Bomber in 3:12 to open the Television tournament — drew its own broadcast team's praise for brevity against a following match its own commentary joked ran long enough for a bathroom break. The US Championship tournament final ended in a double count-out and the belt went unclaimed.

A card-two notice dated 1 December 1995 tells its own handlers that only three sets of challenges had reached the fed's offices so far and asks for more. No second card survives, and nothing else about the promotion — not even its own full name, which neither surviving document ever spells out — is in the record.
