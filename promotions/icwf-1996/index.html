A thin July-August 1996 ICWF cluster whose own recruitment ad spells out "Intense Championship Wrestling Federation," staged in an interfederation crossover PPV against the held OWA wing, roughly eleven months ahead of an unrelated same-acronym 1997 AOL federation. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Intense Championship Wrestling Federation traced its own founding to 23 July 1996, when a post declaring “This IS the Intense Wrestling Federation!!” pointed newcomers at a Geocities sign-up address. It was still recruiting there in October the same year, and by August 1999 its own pitch to free agents was built entirely around its own staying power: “The ICWF has been around for 3 1/2 years NONSTOP. Meaning, we never closed down...ever,” naming a robust singles and tag roster as proof. By July 2002 the same claim had grown with it — “a federation that's last for nearly 6 years, which is almost unheard of in the e-fed world” — run, its recruitment posts said, on commitment, participation in the promotion's “flash-floods” (its own term for character role-plays), and angles built with other members rather than a fixed script.

Its own sixth-anniversary broadcast narrated that run year by year. The first year crowned the Sandman as its first World Champion, ran through stables like the Circus and Old Skull, and closed with Johnny “Tap” Ving winning its Intercontinental belt to become its first Grand Slam winner before retiring. The second, a year the federation says it nearly closed down in, brought a change of president, the rise of the Empire and the Poop Troop, a scandal at a card called VD14, and its first Mother of All Tournaments. In the third, a three-way match for the World Heavyweight title between Petty Officer Pain, c4 and Tom B. Stone left Pain champion and c4 in a coma; by the fourth, Anthony “Rolls” Royce held the title, Tom B. Stone retired, and a feud sent one president to the hospital and returned Johnny “Tap” Ving to the office. The fifth saw the Inner Circle become its own dominant stable before Samson betrayed his own ally Patrick Shade to break it up, and the sixth — called, in the broadcast's own words, “the darkest year in the ICWF” — brought a takeover by Lone Wolf's own regime, Samson's second World Title win at a card called Holiday Horrors, and the rise of a new stable, the Illuminati.

That sixth year closed with its own Birthday Bash VI, built around a four-man ladder match for the vacant World Title between Steve Barnhardt, Blankman, Suicide (fresh off winning that year's Mother of All Tournaments) and Sin City Nick, with the former champion Samson angling, on crutches, for a role in it too. The card itself turned one scramble match into an impromptu three-way for the vacant Cruiserweight title, which Syd Barrett won over Kathryn Velvet Thomas and David DuPree; crowned the Mob Squad new Tag Team Champions out of a multi-team Borneo cage match; and saw Damian Payne's Intercontinental title defense against the Mathematician end in a no-contest when a running war between the Illuminati and the Children of Sin swallowed the match, complete with a table set alight at ringside.

The surviving broadcast cuts away from there to a brutal backstage assault, Thomas Swinger putting Kathryn Velvet Thomas through a table, and promises to return for the main event — presumably the World Title ladder match itself. Nothing in the surviving record shows what happened next: one further part of the broadcast is not held, and who actually won the ladder match, or the World Title, that night is not known. The document's own repost note separately dates the original broadcast to September 2000, at odds with the sixth-year framing the same broadcast otherwise carries; the record does not resolve the discrepancy.

The federation was still recruiting as late as October 2002, calling itself one of the longest-running e-mail and web federations still active. How or when the Intense Championship Wrestling Federation finally closed is not in the surviving record.
