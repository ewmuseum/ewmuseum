Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Universal Electronic Wrestling Federation opened on 18 November 1991 with a rulebook and eight championships. Wrestlers were built on paper — a name, a style, eight moves picked out of a list of seventy-two, a manager if he wanted one — and matches were settled on point totals, with a weekly cycle of title defences, a ranking formula that paid out more for beating better opponents, and a bi-monthly supercard called GrappleMania where the cage matches were allowed. There were four men’s singles belts, two men’s tag belts, and a women’s division with two of its own. The rules end on a signature from its president that is not a person’s name at all.

It went dark inside three weeks of its own GrappleMania of 1 March 1992 — another federation’s newsdesk called it extinct on the 20th, and by mid-April was reporting it out of action and about to change hands. When it came back that May it had a new president, who reinstated every standing champion and dropped the women’s division outright: The Ultimate Lanny still the World Champion, Admiral Ax the Intercontinental, Pain the Atlantic, Lord Plunder the Pacific, the Dangerous Emotions and Carnage holding the tag belts. He wanted the cards running again by 25 May. Four days later he wrote that all the champions were coming back “with the exception of 2”, and the record does not say which two.

Other people’s newsdesks carried it all along, and closely: through the winter of 1992 a rival federation’s weekly programme printed this one’s championship table, champion by champion, with every wrestler’s win-loss record beside his name, and ran its title results as news — four changes of hands across five belts in three weeks, and one World Championship match called move for move. Then “with both the PWA and the UeWF active all summer long” in September, second of the eight federations one poster set beside the FWF that November, and a reader in the same argument who found somebody else’s cards more entertaining than the ranting he already got at home: “Hell, I get that in the UeWF!” The last thing it left is its administrator writing to the open newsgroup in December 1993 because his replies to a reader kept bouncing. Its name was still one of the three worked examples of the subject-tag convention when the first Call For Votes for a fantasy wrestling group of its own went out that spring, and a reply on the games newsgroup in March 1994 still listed it among the leagues then running. By that September the wrestling newsgroup was explaining it in the past tense.

No card of its own weekly cycle survives here. What survives of its wrestling is the tournament that crowned its first champions, staged across four nights of a December 1991 summit that four federations shared, and that record is now held and served. It settles three of the eight belts and settles nothing about the other five. The World Championship final it built to — and the World Tag Team final beside it, and a tournament for the women’s tag belts — were all advertised for a fifth night that survives nowhere at all. The weekly cards were real and running by then: on the fourth night of that summit its own number one and number three tag teams were billed as such “after three weeks of action,” and one of them was in the ring to avenge a loss from the week before. Not one of those cards survives. What survives of them is somebody else’s summary of them, a week at a time.

The Summit of E-Wrestling

Member of UeWF (1991) (27 December 1991 – 30 December 1991, as the served corpus runs)
A member of the second Summit, named in its own opening line, and the federation whose own inaugural championship tournaments the Summit staged inside itself across four nights — World, Atlantic, Pacific, Ladies and World Tag Team. Three of those first champions were crowned there.
