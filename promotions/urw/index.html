A Seattle-based promotion whose September 2003 opening broadcast, outside its own not-yet-finished arena, ran a full card toward a title tournament and closed with the roster brawling in the street. Forty wrestlers are recorded from its founding FAQ and staff list. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Ultimate Revolutionary Wrestling introduced itself through a founding FAQ posted 23 September 2003, promising a promotion of “50+ hard-working dedicated members” built on a contract system, an in-house money economy, a rankings ladder, and a weekly newsletter, AFTERSHOCK. Its own words set the tone: “URW will change what you think of e-wrestling.”

It debuted 29 September 2003 with Monday Night Revolution: The Inaugural Brawl, staged in the street outside its own arena in downtown Seattle after, in the broadcast's own words, “the damn Fire Marshall” barred its 800-strong crowd from going inside. Announcers Felix Rose and Stevie T. Dee called a card headlined by a Seattle Street Fight between “The Franchiser” Shawn Johnson and “Anybody Killa” James Lowery; Johnson stole the pin with a crucifix after referee Kevin Justice had been knocked cold in the chaos outside the ring, and the broadcast closed with the entire roster brawling in the streets of Seattle around him before DeeZ NuTZ clotheslined him off the turnbuckle mid-celebration.

Its follow-up newsletter, AFTERSHOCK Issue 1 (1 October 2003), opened a Television Title tournament and set up a 25-man battle royal for the vacant URW Universal Championship the following week — the top of a title ladder its own founding FAQ built around six belts: the Universal, International, United States, Television, Suicide, and World Tag-Team Championships.

Whether that battle royal, previewed for 6 October 2003, was ever fought isn't in its surviving record, which ends with that preview.
