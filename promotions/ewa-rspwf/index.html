Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Extreme Wrestling Association began circulating on rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy in the fall of 1995, built from scratch by an operator known as PrezRick, and ran a five-title picture — World, North American, Television, World Tag Team, and a since-added Extremist Championship — across two weekly shows, Sunday Night RARE and a mailing-list-only Wednesday WAR.

Its first named pay-per-view, “Offensive Dreams” on 2 July 1996, opened with Sir Joe and “Maniac” Mike Gorman fighting on top of an opening SkyDome for the win, and closed on a near hour-long World Heavyweight Championship classic in front of a reported 68,000 fans: “Dreamlover” Trey Porter retained over “Offensive” Alex Adams at 56 minutes, 42 seconds, a match Adams himself applauded from the canvas afterward. In between, Hollywood dethroned Magus for the Television Championship, the Maniacal Mailman won his third North American title reign after “Sadistic” Steve Stone held the vacated belt for all of fifteen seconds, and Gunnar Gaines won the Extremist Championship from Dark Destroyer by putting him through a table.

Its second, “Extreme Realization” on 30 July 1996, put the World Heavyweight Championship on the line in a ladder match between Porter and Dark Destroyer that ran the belt up a scoreboard fifteen feet above the ring: Destroyer broke the only ladder in the building fighting for it, and Porter, hanging by his arms off the scoreboard itself, had to jump for the belt to keep it. Every other title on the card held: Hollywood, the Maniacal Mailman, and the Good Knights all retained, and the show closed with Dark Destroyer aligning with Hellshock's Cartel faction in the ring, only to walk out with Super Scott and the exhausted champion instead.

That same July, someone hijacked PrezRick's own e-mail account and used it to pick fights with rival federations the DPW and the XWC in his name; Rick posted publicly to disown the forged messages and asked whoever was responsible to stop. Not long after, he stepped away from the promotion entirely for roughly three months, handing it to a co-operator known as SKLOKAZOID, before returning in January 1997 to find the promotion had swept the newsgroup's own 1996 community awards — Match of the Year, Best Show for Sunday Night RARE, and third overall among the year's federations — and wrote publicly to thank the readers who had voted it there.

The promotion kept running into 1997, still staging Hellshock against contenders like Phoenix for the World Heavyweight Championship on its Tuesday Extreme broadcast, and the newsgroup's own record shows it still active as late as January 1998.

Much of the promotion's own run beyond these two pay-per-views — its weekly RARE and WAR broadcasts, a third named supercard, and most of 1997 and 1998 — is not accounted for here. No closure statement of its own survives, and how or when it ended is not in the surviving record.
