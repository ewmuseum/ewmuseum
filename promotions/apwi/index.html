Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

American Productions Wrestling Incorporated's surviving record opens with the first segment of its debut television broadcast, taped 6 July 1999 from the American Productions Arena in Orlando, Florida, weeks after its own founding handbook had promised a launch “within a week or two.”

The broadcast opened as a video package introducing its roster over hip-hop, showing wrestlers signing contracts, training, and — several of them — burning their old MVW shirts on the way in. On camera, its CEO, Carissa Kareem, introduced newcomer “Hawaiian Hotstuff” Johnny Adonis, a self-declared former Mohawk Valley Wrestling Oneida County Champion, who turned his own introduction into a taunt at the crowd and at Kareem herself.

Kareem's own boyfriend, the rapper Andre Washington, billed as “Hundred Proof,” stormed the ring to defend her, only to be put down with a dragonscrew legwhip and elbowdrop before security separated the two; the segment ended with Washington publicly putting a bounty on Adonis's own head and demanding to be shown to another wrestler's locker room.

Ahead of the matches themselves, the segment named a battle royal to seed a round-robin tournament for the still-vacant World Heavyweight Championship, with its bottom two finishers set to meet for the Television Championship instead, and a main event pairing Mitch “Sure Fire” Gatlin against “Showtime” Shawn Sylkk.

How any of it played out isn't in the surviving record; the broadcast's remaining parts, and the founding handbook itself, are not themselves part of what survives.
