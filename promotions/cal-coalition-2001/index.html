Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Coalition of Affiliated Leagues was founded on 15 October 2001 out of a split inside the National Wrestling Council (NWC). Its founder, a career NWC wrestler, had taken over the NWC's own affiliate-training program, the NWCal, in August 2001, when it stood at one active federation and, in his own words, “days away from death,” and built it back up to six active federations within a month. At that point the NWCal and the NWC split into two separate groups, and CAL was created, in its founder's own words, “to serve as competition for the NWC.”

CAL took the same shape as the NWC it split from: a coalition of regional, roleplay-board federations that shared one set of World Champions across all their territories, “think old-school NWA.” It kept the name CAL as a nod to its NWCal roots, where it had always gone by “the Cal for short”; the alternative COAL was considered and rejected as too easily mistaken for a coal-mining outfit.

Its founder framed the split explicitly against what the NWC had become — “a void of ego and politicking” — and pointed to the NWC's own out-of-character message board as part of the reason for leaving.

A written constitution, adopted 31 October 2001, put an Executive Board of up to nine votes in charge of CAL-wide decisions — a Chief Executive Officer, a Director of Publicity, a Webmaster, and one vote per affiliated territory. The CEO organized events between territories but could not decide CAL-wide matters alone; the Director of Publicity ran a “Loose Affiliate” program recruiting new member leagues; and each territory's own president was free to run their federation as they saw fit, so long as it didn't damage the coalition's image. The website itself was required to carry a fixed set of features: the Constitution, an application, title histories, an event calendar, and an out-of-character board called The Collective.

Ten days later, on 25 October 2001, one of its member territories, CEW, folded amid what CAL's own news post called “fed-head juggling, power problems, and overall hectic managerial troubles,” closing with its own line from Metallica: “Do you bury me when I'm gone? Do you teach me while I'm here?” CAL welcomed CEW's wrestlers to apply elsewhere in the coalition and posted an opening for a replacement territory, stating it would only take on a federation “fully running on its own feet” rather than, as it put it, “a sick puppy” it would have to nurse back to health.

The surviving record ends there, within the same month CAL was founded. How long the coalition or any of its remaining territories lasted, or how it ended, isn't in the surviving record.
