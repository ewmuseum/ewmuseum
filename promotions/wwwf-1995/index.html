A short-lived May-June 1995 fed built on a strict 'one wrestler, one real person' house rule against multi-character stables, with its own dedicated web site and a round-robin building toward its first Broadcast and World Tag Team title matches. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World Web Wrestling Federation announced itself on 23 May 1995 by its own three rules: “The WWWF...World Web Wrestling Federation lives by Three Commandments” — One to One, meaning every wrestler and manager was one real person, with teams and stables built only by combining separate people rather than one person running several characters; Always On Time, a promise of a regular card schedule; and Posting Only, with cards and interviews posted to the newsgroup or its own web site and e-mail kept to business only.

A recruitment call a week later restated the same three rules as a pitch to prospective bookers and writers, still short of hands. By 3 June a hype post named its next card — a round robin to decide a challenger for the Broadcast Title against Reservoir Dog, and a seeding match for a World Tag Team Title tournament — and staked its whole appeal on the one-real-person-per-wrestler rule, boasting by name that it wrestled unlike War, AWO, FWA or ICW.

Six days after that post, the operator of a fellow newsgroup promotion, NRWF, announced he was closing his own federation and folding its roster into this one, in his own words: “I am helping to run the WWWF, so I will move you all to that league.”

Nothing further under its own name survives. Its own last document is the 3 June hype post; what became of the round robin, the Broadcast Title, or the absorbed roster isn't in the record.
