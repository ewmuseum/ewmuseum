Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Midget Pro Wrestling posted its own founding rulebook on 11 April 1997, billing itself “THE BIGGEST 'LITTLE' FED ON THE NET” and insisting, twice over, that its all-midget roster was no joke: “THIS IS NOT A PARODY FED!!!! THIS IS AS SERIOUS AS THE FIRST E-FED WAS.” Every wrestler had to be an original male midget character, capped at four feet and 200 pounds, decided by weekly flashes and strats; a handler did not have to be a midget to apply.

Its own weekly schedule ran three programs — MPW Prime Time on Saturdays, the flash-driven MPW All-Stars on Wednesdays, and a monthly house-show report, MPW In the House — alongside a full calendar of planned pay-per-views: a January SlamFest styled after Japanese supercards and featuring guest matches from other federations, a spring-break Fun in the Sun, a July Fourth Stars and Stripes card, a Halloween Fright Night with stipulations drawn from a pot match by match, and a December Winter War fought through randomly paired “WILD” card teams.

Its front office was laid out in full before a single wrestler had signed: commentators Lil' Lucky, a former Mexican Midget Heavyweight Champion, Joe Williams, and “Sexy” Sally McClane, drawn from her own record as a wrestler in the GDWA and a manager in CCW; hosts Sam Malone and Randall “Big Dawg” Hawkins; and security chiefs Mohammed Stone and Trey Hawkes, both previously McClane's own bodyguards in the GDWA and CCW.

Three titles were laid out the same way, unwon: the MPW World Heavyweight and All-American Championships in singles competition, and the MPW World Tag Team Championship, each carrying its own monthly defense requirement. The rulebook closes by thanking the CCW, the GDWA and the NIFW for their influence and hoping to make history as, in its own words, “the FIRST Midget E-fed.”

The rulebook was reposted verbatim two days later, and that repost is the last attested activity in the surviving record. No signed roster, card, or closing notice of any kind survives.
