A previously-closed federation whose 2003 comeback special restages its own final champion's title win in flashback and stages its own original operator crashing the broadcast to reclaim the company on camera. A 1999 debut roleplay survives from the earlier run; thirty-one wrestlers are recorded. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

IWE's own surviving record opens with a debut. A newcomer calling himself “So Damn Cool” Jay Emes crashed a television reporter's vox-pop outside a Chicago ticket line on 10 June 1999, grabbing the microphone to announce his own arrival ahead of a card billed for the IWE Coliseum: “I'm here in the IWE for one reason and one reason only, warm-ups... the ink is already dry on my N.E.W. contract.” He used the moment to run down his advertised opponent, General America, outright — no result from the card he was promoted for survives. By the promotion's own later account, its IWE World Heavyweight Championship “was last held in 1999” before its own broadcast declined to name the champion.

The promotion returned four years later with a special it called Back From the Dead, posted 2 July 2003 and billed for the previous Saturday, opening on a mock-newsreel cold open tying itself to the Great Chicago Fire before promising “Chicago will be ablaze once more.” Four championships were contested on the night. The AeWF Television title changed hands on a career-versus-title stipulation: Rune survived two kickouts of the champion King Snake's Snake Bite, then knocked him cold with the belt itself and pinned him, ending Snake's in-ring career on the spot. The Intercontinental Championship, billed as vacant for four years, was decided in a two-out-of-three-falls match between the Outlaw and “The Reincarnation” Christopher Maxwell — the first fall a cactus-and-scorpion deathmatch the Outlaw won by driving Maxwell through the scorpion pit's glass, the match itself going to the Outlaw for his first title in over four years. Elsewhere on the card, Lawerence Williams and Eddy Robinson fought a scaffold match for a flag mounted three stories up; Robinson won it after surviving a twenty-foot fall through a stack of tables.

The newly reinstated IWE World Tag Team Championship went to Enemies of the State in a four-way elimination match, the deciding fall coming off an assisted Shooting Star Press after an unseen chairshot. Enemies of the State had been paid to run interference in the main event that followed — a three-way for the vacant World Heavyweight Championship — but turned on the man who paid them, powerbombing him over the top rope to the concrete instead of eliminating his rival, and let Johnny Malibu pin the last man standing for the title. Malibu closed the show by turning on his own celebrating ally, Knee High, dropping him with the same finisher that had just won him the belt.

The broadcast's own final angle undid its own authority structure on camera: the man booked all night as IWE Owner was revealed as a front, cut off mid-sentence by the promotion's own returning founder, who declared himself “the _true_ IWE owner” and had the newly crowned champion and tag champions beat the deposed owner down in the ring before the broadcast ended.

What the revival became after that night is not in what survives here to tell.
