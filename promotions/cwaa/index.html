Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Championship Wrestling Association of America opened on 26 January 1997 as a regional division of the AEWA, launched roughly a year after its own mother federation and pitched as “an ideal setting... to experiment with new character ideas” for handlers already working there. It ran one card a week from the Sportatorium in Dallas — billed in its own copy as “former home of World Class Championship Wrestling” — kept its roster deliberately small, and by its own account posted no advertisement of its own to the newsgroup until it had proven it could deliver a program on schedule. Its own written constitution, circulated that April, laid out a weekly rhythm down to the hour: lineups announced by Wednesday night, the show itself in mailboxes by 10 PM Eastern on Sunday, matches decided on roleplay quality, storyline sense, and creation-sheet comparison in whatever mix fit a given booking.

The debut card crowned no champion but set the promotion's own tone: a shy rookie bouncer beat a fang-baring brawler in the first match the fed ever aired, a masked tag team stole a decision through a fake ankle injury, and the night closed backstage on a masked man vowing revenge for his father's death in that same building fifteen years earlier. A sixteen-man tournament that spring gave the promotion its Heavyweight title, won in the final by “The Dreamer” Randy Sheridan; an eight-team tournament crowned the Tag Team champions, and a twelve-man battle royal crowned the Television champion, defended, by house rule, on every single broadcast after that.

The Heavyweight belt moved twice more across the surviving record — to “The Professional” Max Robertson by decision that April, then vacated by its sanctioning federation's own ruling that June and won back in a tournament by a masked wrestler billed only as the Masked Man. By 7 September that same champion was revealed on-air as Marc Thomas, the same reign continuing under his own name. The relationship with the AEWA ran both ways on record: its own headliners appeared on that federation's pay-per-views, and its World Heavyweight Champion, Crimson Joe Reed, twice defended his belt on CWAA programming, beating both Thomas and Robertson across more than an hour each time. A separate crossover put two CWAA regulars, Lt. Lawson and Sgt. Sherrick, over as ACWF World Tag Team Champions.

A two-day Supershow that August, staged at Reunion Arena, was the promotion's biggest card: a new Six Man Tag Team title, decided by tournament, went to Joshua Barnes, Randy Sheridan, and “The Preacher” Kore Wrench, and Twister reclaimed the Tag Team belts to become the promotion's first two-time champions. A hard-drive failure at the fed's own headquarters kept three weeks of cards off the newsgroup afterward; a single recap post on 28 August closed the gap in its own words rather than leaving it silent, reporting every result and title change from the missing fortnight in one long dispatch.

Its own last surviving card, 7 September 1997, ended with the visiting world champion successfully defending his title against Robertson, then Randy Sheridan asking him, on camera, for his own shot at the belt; the champion countered by offering a non-title match the following week to earn it, and the broadcast closed previewing that show. No document survives past this one — whether Sheridan got his ten minutes isn't in the surviving record.
