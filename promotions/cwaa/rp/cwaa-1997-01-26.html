CWAA — Roleplay

CWAA, 26 January 1997 (Part 1 of 3; Part 2 not held)

26 January 1997
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Eric Lane: Welcome everyone to this, out first edition of Championship Wrestling Association of America, coming to you from the world-famous Sportatoriumin Dallas, TX. I understand there are some talent scouts from the AEWA here to see the CWAA's youngsters in action, and here's what they'll watch this evening. Former bouncer Josh Barnes squares off with Healer's Havoc. "The Preacher" Kore Wrench is in action tonight, facing Lt. Daniel "Lethal" Lawson. Joey Haven tells us he'll be bringing the so-called "Hardcore" style from up in Philadelphia to Jak Hammer. The team of Twister will be here, facing a mystery team. And in our feature bout, Max "The Professional" Robertson, takes on my guest at this time, "The Dreamer", Randy Sheridan. (Enter Sheridan) (Crowd pops as "Dream On" by Aerosmith and the Philadelphia Symphony plays. The 170 pound Randy Sheridan makes his way to ringside, exchanging high five's and handshakes with fans. At ringside, he shakes hands with Lane.) EL: Welcome to the CWAA, Randy. How does it feel to be wrestling your first ever feature match? Randy Sheridan: It's great, Eric. I'm not really much of a veteran, since I only made my pro debut over the summer, but I've already felt a title around my waist, and I'm looking forward to trying to become the CWAA champion. EL: How does the CWAA compare to some of the bigger organizations you've been in? RS: I love it. My first ever match was in a big organization against a big star named Robski. (Crowd boos - they've heard of him.) I got my butt kicked. Here in the CWAA, we all start off even, and we all start off hungry. We're young, and here you'll see the talent get a chance to shine, rather then being thrown to the wolves. EL: Your nickname is "The Dreamer." Could you tell us where that name came from? RS: Nobody thought I could do it. My friends, my family, the other guys in wrestling school, they thought I was too small, not talented enough, whatever. They told me I was dreaming if I thought I could make it as a professional wrestler. Well, I know that dreams come true, because mine did. So I'm proud to be called the Dreamer, because it shows what I had to overcome to get to the ring. That's why my big move is called the Dream Come True, and I hope to use it to put away Max Robertson. EL: Which brings me to my final question. What do you know about "The Professional" Max Robertson? RS: Not much, Eric. But I'll find out tonight, just like he'll find out about me. And if he's anything less than a total professional tonight, he'll get beat. I've got a real good record against people who take me lightly. EL: (Shaking Sheridan's hand) Good luck tonight, Randy. He's looking to be his mark in the CWAA, and we'll see what he can do later tonight. (Exit Sheridan) EL: But before then, we've got four exciting matches, and when we come back after this break, Joshua Barnes and Healer's Havoc will launch the CWAA's inaugural event. Back in a few... (The historic first commercial break offers the audience the opportunity to squander their hard-earned cash on psychic hotlines, used cars (local cable stations for the indy fed), quality legal representation, and the quicker-picker-upper.) (The 6'5" 292 pound former Bloomsburg, IN bouncer, Joshua Barnes comes in, self-consciously adjusting his glasses, which slide down his noise, lubricated by the nervous perspiration, no doubt. He wears long black jeans and an emerald-green ring jacket. As he removes his jacket, an "IU" (Indiana University) tattoo is revealed.) EL: While we wait for Healer's Havoc to arrive, I'll get a few words from Mr. Joshua Barnes. Joshua...? (Barnes looks around nervously, whispering to the man behind the camera, "now?". Apparently getting an affirmative answer, the man removes his wire-rimmed glasses, wipes the sweat from his short black hair.) EL: Joshua, your professional debut and the CWAA's debut, as well, will begin in a scant few moments. Any thoughts? Joshua Barnes: Ummm....Hi there. I'm Josh Barnes, and I've just joined up...signed up with the CWAA. I'm never wrestled before- at least, not in the ring. But I was a bouncer at the Country & Western Bar over in Fort Wayne and this wrestler came in, and told me that I could get a better job as a wrestler. And then the bar was shut down, and I was out of work... So I came down here, and now I'm in. Anyway, I'm really looking forward to getting into the wrestling ring, and competing against the other wrestlers soon. So...(pauses, uncertain of what to say) Anyway, thanks for listening, and I hope to see everyone soon. Bye. EL: He seems a little nervous, folks, and in his situation, who wouldn't be. Well, I see Healer's Havoc coming to the ring- eww! He...doesn't seem to have..any teeth. A big gum-filled feral grin covers his face, and...is that a dog collar he's wearing? coming behind him is, I assume, Mr. Tip Smith, Havoc's manager. (From the aisle, Healer's Havoc could be heard to holler:) HH: I'm very pleased to be in the CWAA. I have one thing to say Joshua Barnes I'm going to beat you within an inch of your life! (HH then picks up a chair and bangs it on the floor a few times.) EL: Well, that was...interesting. Havoc is no small man, but Barnes looks to have at least 30 pounds on him, despite their similar heights. There's the bell, and the CWAA has officially started its operations. (Which makes this a good time for another commercial break.) EL: Barnes is handing his glasses to our lovely ring attendant, and Healer's Havoc comes running at him with a double sledge in the back. Another, and another, and he has Barnes up against the ropes and punches away. Barnes into the ropes, and Havoc catches him in a sleeper, but it's early, Barnes escapes and slips behind him with a hammerlock, but Havoc runs to the ropes, and Barnes has to release the hold. The ref calls for a break, and Barnes backs off carefully. Tip Smith appears upset about something, but I can't quite make it out. so far, this has been an even matchup between two unpolished powerhouses, and we'll be seeing a variety of different styles tonight- Barnes has Havoc in an armbar now, and really is wrenching it on- Jak Hammer is another big powerhouse we'll be seeing, while I understand Lawson is more of a mat technician, and our feature match will showcase a pair of young high flyers. Barnes whips Havoc into the ropes, clothesline! Havoc on his back, and Barnes heads to the corner and gets down in a three-point stance, I think I know what's coming here...Havoc staggers back to his feet, and Barnes knocks him right back down with a tackle that could have gotten him into the IU football program had he not opted for collegiate wrestling. Barnes pulls him back up by those long black locks, sends him back into the ropes, and spinebuster slam! 1..2..hooks the leg...3! That's it! Joshua Barnes over Healer's Havoc in our first match ever, and the crowd sounds quite pleased. They've really taken to the shy rookie, who accepts his little wire-rimmed glasses from the ring attendant, and, in all honesty, seems a little embarrassed by the attention he's getting as he leaves the ring. Meanwhile, Healer's Havoc does not look at all pleased with the results, but he'll get another chance in due time here in the CWAA. [continued] (The theme to the film "Twister" plays) EL: Here come Paul "Tornado" Langford and Travis Conley known collectively as "Twister", and they'll be a part of the first-ever tag team matchup here in the CWAA. (Langford is 6'3"-244, with short brown hair, green eyes, and an impressive physique with the proverbial washboard stomach. His dark brown trunk have a tornado on the backside. The similarly-dressed Conley is 6'8"-315, with a youthful blue-eyed, blond-haired face. The two are led by Mother Nature. Conley seems more than a little excited to be here.) EL: As of this morning, we still didn't know who the opposition would be, but during our last break I was informed that they go by the name of Double Jeopardy. (Lynyrd Skynyrd's "Sweet Home Alabama" plays, and a large, plain-clothes-clad individual follows two men to the ring. The first looks to be about 6'6" and over 250 lb., with muscular arms bulging out from beneath a CWAA t-shirt. His face is hidden behind a blue leather mask, and he wears yellow trunks. His partner is 5'11" and about 230 lb. He wears a green trenchcoat and "flashes" the crowd, revealing a pair of pink tights. He also wears an ankle brace.) EL: Right now, I don't know who either of these individuals are, nor who it is whose with them, but that little stunt with the trenchcoat tells me that these are not men I really want to get to know any better. For now, I'm just going to refer to them as DJ1 and DJ2, DJ being an abbreviation for "Double Jeopardy". Hopefully by next week I'll know a little more about these two. Well, there's the bell, and DJ1, the one with the mask, is going to start of against the Tornado. Already DJ2 is grimacing and pointing to that ankle; if it hurts so much, perhaps he shouldn't be getting in the ring today. Before locking up, DJ1 is flexing, showing off his physique. It is impressive, but the shows little, if any, appreciation. Langford looks to the crowd, and he repeats the gesture, and the crowd reacts much more favorably, and already DJ1 is upset. Well, pouting and stomping around the ring like that, kicking the ropes, isn't going to make them like him any better. Now DJ1 runs at Langford, who catches him in an armdrag takedown. DJ1 back up, charges again, and again Langford with an armdrag, and DJ1 hits the mat hard. DJ2 runs in, and Langford ducks down- back body drop, and both halves of double jeopardy head for the floor to regroup. The crowd is really getting on their cases. Langford out onto the ring apron. Awww- isn't that nice, as DJ2 gives his partner a quick hug to help lift his spirits, and the DJs turn around- dropkick from the apron by Paul Langford- one boot on each man, and the DJs are down on the floor. I don't know why the ref hasn't been counting, but Langford hops back into the ring and raises his hand, and the crowd applauds. In the meantime, the DJs are slowly getting up, and DJ2 is admonishing the referee over something, it sounds like he wants Langford disqualified for hitting him when he wasn't in the ring yet. OK, DJ1 up on the apron, and DJ2 climbs into the ring. The referee is trying to get DJ1 back in, but DJ2 claims there was a tag while they were out on the floor. I can't believe the referee is going to allow him to continue. Speaking of continuing, we'll continue with this matchup when we get back. (Yet another commercial. Time for a new leading brand.) EL: Well, nothing much has happened during the break. The Double Jeopardy keeps stalling, and its taking its effect on Travis Conley, who is just about bursting with repressed energy on the apron. But its Langford and the non-masked DJ in the ring, and they lock up. Langford drives DJ2 back into a neutral corner, and the ref calls for a break. DJ2 is complaining about his ankle again and points to it. Well, it might have more credibility if he actually pointed to the one with the brace on it. He's affecting a limp as he moves back to mid-ring. Another lockup, and DJ2 drops to the mat clutching his ankle. The referee is checking to see if he can continue, and Langford turns around in disbelief, saying something to his partner and manager, and- well, DJ2 baited him with that one. I think anyone who's been around very long at all could tell there was nothing wrong with that ankle, and DJ2 has just taken the Tornado down with a single-leg takedown, and now he's leaning back in a half-Boston crab. The referee is asking him again about that ankle which looked just fine when he sprang to his feet, and he affects a quick grimace, but it's Langford who's in real pain now, as DJ2 is really applying pressure. DJ1 is calling for a tag, so DJ2 releases the hold and, pausing to give the Tornado a stomp on the head, and tags out. Our blue-masked friend is back and drops a ferocious series of double sledges onto Langford's back. Now he's standing on his back and flexing for the crowd again. Now he picks Langford up- powerslam! The cover..1.. kickout! They need to wear the Tornado down a little more before being able to put him away with such a basic maneuver. DJ1 pulls him up by the hair- Irish whip, and DJ1 ducks down- leapfrog! Langford lands on his feet, hooks on a full nelson- suplex! Dragon Suplex, and he bridges..1...2...DJ2 breaks it with a kick to the head. The referee admonishes him over him, and DJ2 points to his ankle and starts complaining about it again, while Travis Conley tags in! Running clothesline flattens DJ1! And again! He covers...but the referee won't count! He's summoning Langford back in, arguing that there was no tag! Langford and Conley argue with him over it, and DJ1 has a chance to get a breather. Langford has to come back in the ring, and locks up with DJ1. DJ1 hip tosses him to the Double Jeopardy corner, and DJ2 holds the Tornado back by his arms while DJ1 pummels his midsection with punches and kicks. DJ2 tags in and sets Langford in a side headlock. No, it's a running bulldog! He covers..1..2...kickout! DJ2 stomps away and tags back out. DJ1 in again, and he doubles Langford over with a kick in the midsection, he's setting him up for a piledriver, and down he goes. Another lateral press...1...2...kickout again! The crowd is stamping its feet and clapping in time, trying to lend some support to the Tornado, and Conley is stretching as far into the ring as he can, waiting for the chance to tag in. DJ1 tags out, and its DJ2 back in again, and he's got a camel clutch locked on. we have to break again, if there is a finish during the break, we'll replay it for you when we return. Stay tuned.. (Commercial break. Would you like to make more money? Sure, we all would.) EL: During the break, DJ2 has had that Camel Clutch locked on the entire time, but Langford won't quit. DJ2 releases the hold, and gives another stomp on the back. He picks the Tornado up, whips him into the ropes, catches Langford- no! Langford turns DJ2 around and swings him down onto the mat! Belly to belly suplex! But no cover, as Langford just rolls over onto his own back, and DJ2 is in obvious pain. The referee starts the double count, and both men are looking to get into their corners and make the tag. Conley is standing on the bottom rope reaching in, and DJ1 is doing the same. Langford is closer to his corner- he stretches out...the tag! Conley is in, and he races over to the opposite corner and drops and elbow onto DJ2 before he can tag out! Know he pulls DJ2 up...Irish whip- he scoops him up... bodyslam. DJ1 charges, and Conley scoops him and slams him, too! Conley turns around, and a running clothesline flattens DJ2, who rolls under the bottom rope to the floor. A running clothesline takes down DJ1, as well! Travis Conley crouches in mid ring, fists cocked, and DJ1 is on his knees, begging for mercy. Conley throws a right hand to the face and a kick in the head. Correct me if I'm wrong but the legal man right now is on the floor complaining to anyone who'll listen about his ankle. Conley is going for the mask now! He's tugging on that mask, and he's got it turned- DJ1 can't see- the eyeholes are back around his ear! DJ2 is back in the ring, and Langford in to head off any 2-on-1. He punches DJ2, who falls into the corner. Langford over to help his partner de-mask DJ1, and DJ2 is clutching his ankle again- no, wait- he's removing the brace. He staggers up to his feet- oh, for God's sake, he's favoring the wrong leg. He takes the brace, and slams Langford in the back of the head with it and tosses it aside. The third man who came out with Double Jeopardy is reminding the referee that Conley has the wrong man- DJ1 is not the legal man right now. DJ2 grabs Conley from behind in a waistlock- and brings him back over his head...German suplex...1....2...3! He got it! Double Jeopardy with the pinfall victory over Twister. ("Sexual Healing" starts to play as DJ2 gyrates in the ring and Twister and Mother Nature petition the referee.) EL: DJ1 has picked up the brace and is now showing it to the ref, claiming that Langford or Conley had taken it off of his partner. He's got that mask on right again, now- and Langford lunges toward him! But Double Jeopardy bail out, and start heading back up the aisle. That big guy who came down with them is blocking their escape, and- oh, aren't they big men, taunting Twister from the other side of their bodyguard or whoever he is. Well, it's in the books: Double Jeopardy gets the win, and DJ's ankle looked just fine as he legged it from the ring. Well, we're almost ready for our big feature match. (Darth Vader's old "Imperial March" plays) EL: Here comes Max Robertson, who calls himself "The Professional". (Robertson is 5'11", 200, with red & black tights. Has a cleanly-shaved head and an unfriendly mien.) EL: Mr. Robertson, a word if I may. What are your thoughts on your upcoming debut match against a former tag-team champion, "The Dreamer" Randy Sheridan? Max Robertson: Well Eric, obviously Randy Sheridan is the man to beat here in the Championship Wrestling Association of America. I've studied his dossier, and I know that he's the only former title holder in the league; I know he has more experience than any other wrestler in the league. But really, that doesn't worry me. EL: Why is that? MR: Well, you see for Mr. Sheridan, this is just another match. Sure, he'd probably like to win, but you see, Wrestling is what I do. I've been training for my professional debut for over five years. This match means everything to me; I'm at my physical, emotional, and psychological peak. There is nothing to distract me from this match, and I promise you, I'll do what it takes to win. EL: But don't you suppose all rookie wrestlers think that? What makes you special? MR: That's a good question, Eric. You see, Wrestling is my life. Some look at wrestling almost like a hobby. I know that Kore Wrench is an ordained Minister. Daniel Lawson, is still an active member of the Armed Forces, I believe. What percentage of the time do you think these people spend thinking about wrestling? 30? 50? 60 at the most. But Eric, I think about wrestling all the time. 100 percent of the time. Another problem wrestlers have is that they can't separate life in the ring form life out of the ring. Wrestling isn't a popularity contest, and wrestling isn't a boxing match. Fan support doesn't win championships. Feuds with second rate wrestlers don't win championships. Smart wrestling and quick pins win championships. I understand that, and that's why I'm going to beat Randy Sheridan, and that's why I'm going to become the first CWAA Heavyweight Champion. EL: Thank you for your time, Mr. Robertson. MR: Thank you, Eric. EL: Max Robertson, "The Professional", waits in the ring. ("Dream On" by Aerosmith and the Philadelphia Philharmonic strikes up, and the crowd reacts very positively. As "The Dreamer" Randy Sheridan heads out.) EL: There's the bell, and our feature match has begun. These two have very similar builds, with a slight weight advantage in Robertson's favor. They lock up, and it;s Robertson with a knee lift. He wrings Sheridan's arm behind him in a Hammerlock, and Sheridan drops to his knees and takes him over in a fireman's carry, but Robertson holds on to the Hammerlock, and Sheridan still can't break free. Sheridan on his knees on the mat, and Robertson got that hammerlock on him still. Sheridan powers his way back to his feet and pulls his and the Professional's weight over to the ropes, and Robertson has to break the hold. Robertson releases it, carefully backs up a step- savate kick! Robertson just backed up far enough to nail a savate kick on Sheridan, who tumbles to the floor. Robertson on the apron- he leaps down, and catches the Dreamer in a rana on the floor! He just jumped down from the apron around Sheridan's shoulders and flipped him over onto the floor, all in one fluid motion! Robertson back up, he waits for Sheridan- spinning kick knocks Sheridan into the ring steps. Robertson rolls back in under the bottom rope, then back out again; he must have just wanted to break the count. Robertson grabs Sheridan by the hair and smashes his head onto the ring apron. Now he rolls the Dreamer back in and follows him. Irish whip, and the Professional hooks him around the waist and pulls back- Northern Lights Suplex! The cover..1..kickout. Robertson gets back up and waits on Sheridan, and catches him in an abdominal stretch, grapevining the leg, and he has it applied just about perfectly, with both feet flat on the mat. Sheridan is straining to counter in some way- he's too far from the ropes to break it that way. I can see him straining for a hip toss, but the hold is just applied to tightly for him to break it. He's still straining, and he pulls hard enough to get Robertson's left boot off the mat for a second, but he doesn't have the strength to pull all the way into a hip toss, and Robertson gets set once again, and has it applied just as well as a minute ago. We've got to take a break, but we'll be back for the conclusion of this, our feature match. (Psychic hotlines and the like are shilled.) EL: We're back, and Sheridan has finally hip-tossed his way out of the abdominal stretch, but Robertson has maintained the advantage with a Boston crab, a move designed to no doubt to neutralize Sheridan's aerial skills later in the match. Well, the Dreamer reaches the bottom rope, and the hold is broken. Robertson pulls him up the hair, and sends him in for an Irish Whip- Sheridan is up- is he going for a neck scissor- no! Victory Roll...1..2...Robertson rolls him over...1..2...Sheridan rolls over again...1...2...Robertson grabs the rope, and the cover is broken by the referee. Both men back up now. They lock up again- Sheridan with a snap mare takedown into a chinlock, and he slaps an elbow smash onto Robertson's skull. Sheridan pulls him up now, Irish Whip, dropkick by Sheridan. Robertson staggers back up, another dropkick! Robertson falls back against the ropes- Sheridan charges at him- cross body press- but Robertson dumps him over the top- Sheridan lands on the apron on his feet...spins Robertson around....sunset flip! 1..2...Robertson rolls and grabs hold of the tights...1...2...Sheridan rolls back again, and they;re in the ropes again. Both men back up again. They lock up...hiptoss by Sheridan. He charges- monkey flip, and Robertson is sent across the ring. Robertson back on his fee, but the Dreamer is waiting- savate kick rocks Robertson back into the corner. He staggers back to mid ring- European uppercut by Sheridan- snap suplex! Sheridan with the cover 1..2..kickout. Sheridan decides he needs to wear his man down a little more first, I guess, and he locks on a side headlock. Robertson struggles to break free- his face is beet red from all of the exertion. He grabs Sheridan's tights, lifts him up- back suplex! Robertson back up, and a series of stomps. He grabs Sheridan by the hair, sends him for a ride across the ring into a neutral corner. Robertson sets on the other side, comes across the ring, up he goes- handspring elbow smash in the corner! Sheridan goes down, Robertson to the outside... he;s on the apron, has a hold of the tope rope, leans back and launches himself back into the ring- a slingshot somersault splash! Hooks the leg..1...2..kickout! He picks Sheridan back up, sets him for a backslide...he has him over...1..2...Sheridan pulls out of it, he;s trying to get Robertson over, he's got him halfway there. the Professional is fighting it with every fibre of strength...he' sliding...sliding...Sheridan has him down....1...2...Robertson pulls back again...(the crowd groans in disappointment)....he pulls Sheridan over..1...Robertson has his foot on the middle rope for leverage...2...the ref sees his foot on the rope and calls for the break. Both men back up, and Robertson jaws with the official for a second. Meanwhile, Sheridan bounces off the ropes...cartwheel kick! Robertson goes down, and Sheridan plays it up for the crowd. He goes up top- flying dropkick! Down from the top rope, and he drove his boots right into Robertson's head! He goes up top on the other side, the crowd is on its feet...Shooting Star Press! The Dream Comes True, he calls it. The cover...1...2...3! He got him! Your winner in out feature match is Dreamer Randy Sheridan, and the crowd just loves this young man, the pride of Des Moines, IA. ("Dream On" plays yet again.) EL: Well, there you have it. Our first episode of Championship Wrestling Association of America. We'll be back next time, and I understand the promoter is trying to negotiate a rematch between Hardc- wait a second. I'm being informed there is a disturbance in the back somewhere. We're going to send a camera crew back there now... (The scene switches to a large man wearing a black wrestling outfit with silver streaks on it. He is standing inside the basement area of the Sportatorium, and he seems to be looking for something. Extra turnbuckle pads, ringposts, ropes, and padding a strewn about the room. He picks up a badly dented, discolored metal folding chair, and seems to have found what he is looking for. He turns to leave, and sees the cameraman.) Unidentified Man: Hey, what the hell are you doing here?? Can't a guy look for something without a camera in his face?? I guess not. Well, as long as you are here, I've got a few words for the CWAA. You see, wrestlers, this arena holds bad memories for me, because my father, the idiot good guy wrestler he was, was attacked from behind, and killed in the ring, when he was smacked in the face with this chair. (Holds up the chair) The doctor said that part of his skull pierced his brain, and he had an aneurysm, and died. Now, I am here to make sure that everybody remembers my father, and nobody forgets me. I assure all you wrestlers out there that once you feel the wrath that I have built up in the last 15 years, you will wish that my father never was a wrestler. (looks up at the ceiling) Father, I promise you that I will gain the title of this federation, and make sure that you're death is avenged, and the fans of the scum who did this to you are now wrestlers here and will be destroyed (Starts shouting) I PROMISE YOU THAT I WILL DESTROY ALL THAT GET IN THE WAY OF MY VENGEANCE AGAINST THIS TRAVESTY OF JUSTICE!!!! (The man shuts up, and smiles. He threatens to waffle the camera with the chair he had in his hand as we fade to black.) -kl
