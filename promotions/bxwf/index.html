Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

BXWF launched on 15 January 1997, its founder introducing it on the newsgroup as a promotion built on “Tnm 6.2 plus some enhancements from myself.” Two weeks later, at a scripted press conference staged for the cameras in London, its president, Joey “the Jet” Koteas, promised wrestlers “the same oportunity as the next man,” zero tolerance for abuse toward “fans, wrestlers or there handlers,” and a broadcast deal in the works with a fictional cable consortium that would carry BXWF's cards free to viewers in exchange for a flat monthly fee — enough, he said, to guarantee every wrestler purse money “win, lose or draw.” Asked whether the roster would include stars like Hulk Hogan, Psycho Sid or Sean Michaels, Koteas refused outright: “we do not want Famous Names here in the BXWF.” The launch, originally billed for 1 February, was pushed to the 15th over what he called “unforseen dificulties.”

A staff bulletin dated 5 February named the promotion's commentary team, Tony and Phil Mason, its interviewers, and its president, with all three titles still vacant. It carried one piece of business: an interview segment with newcomer the Hideous Zuki that turned violent on camera, his manager Mr. Deltoid making the introductions before Zuki broke interviewer Mike Chipman's nose and beat the accompanying cameraman off the air.

BXWF's one surviving full show, Thursday Terror, aired 15 February 1997 — not from the London Arena the press conference had promised, but from Wembley, packed, by the commentary team's own account, with fans “waving their arms, screaming and wearing BXWF t-shirts.” The Mechanic beat Hamish Mcdoogle after using a weapon called the Wrench to force a fast three-count Mcdoogle protested at length. The Hideous Zuki beat Beefstew Lou Marconi, Mr. Deltoid tripping Marconi at a key moment before Zuki finished him with the Nippon Devil DDT. And 'Playboy' Brett Michaels beat Fatty Turnbuckle by submission after a woman at ringside, Tammy Rich, twice shoved Turnbuckle off the top turnbuckle to break up his advantage.

No further card is served in this record. Whether the broadcast deal with the cable consortium was ever completed, whether any of the three vacant titles was ever contested, or how long BXWF ran past that one night at Wembley is not something the surviving record answers.
