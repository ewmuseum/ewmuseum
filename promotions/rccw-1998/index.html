A renamed promotion that staged one surviving beach pay-per-view in Fort Lauderdale around its first anniversary in late 1998, watching two titles walk out the door with departing champions before its own recruiting posts trail off that November. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Rose City Championship Wrestling ran under an earlier name, PMCW, before its surviving record picks it up in its second year, in September 1998, recruiting after “a little drop off in membership.” It described itself as a hybrid of simulated and booked wrestling — roleplay quality earning a wrestler enough push to win the sim — with plans, stated the same month, to move to fully booked results. Seven championships were on its books that autumn: World, Cyber, Hardcore, Tag Team, European, Television and Lightweight, though only the World title still had a holder after a run of champions had quit.

Its one surviving card, the Last Chance PPV, ran 12 September 1998 on a beach in Fort Lauderdale, Florida, opened by President Dorchak announcing the promotion's next event, Devil's Night, for 30 October at the Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum — every match gimmicked, and, as its own broadcast notes, the one-year anniversary of the RCCW under either of its names.

The card turned on titles changing hands by attrition as much as by pinfall. Octane walked to the ring unscheduled, announced his retirement, and left with the European Championship still around his waist before a bell ever rang. Freefall beat Heroine by double countout to become, by the broadcast's own words, “the final RCCW Womens World Champion” as the title was retired outright. A Television Championship match between L-Mo and Carnage ended in no contest after Crackshot ambushed L-Mo with a chair, and Piston handed his own Lightweight belt to Black Jack Dealer in a show of sportsmanship only to blindside him, reclaim it, and leave the promotion for good. Two returning former tag champions, Demon Within and Grendle, interrupted the Hardcore title match to attack Quezzy moments after Jason Creed had already beaten him for the belt. The main event, a Taipei Death Match between Clutch and Maverick fought inside a barbed-wire cage, ended with both men bloodied and walking out of the cage together to trade their World and Hardcore championships as a gesture of respect.

By the same night's close, President Dorchak had confirmed the Cyber Championship gone as well, leaving the promotion running just five active titles. Its recruiting continued into the autumn, still listing its championships as of 22 November 1998, its own last dated trace. No closing statement of its own survives past it.
