History

Dominic, born to Janice and Terrell Crazy on June 15, 1980 was a normal child. He played, cried, and ran just as normal children do.
But at the age of 10 months Dominic’s parents could tell that his life would be one of trials and tribulations. You see Dominic’s first word was not the same as most children’s would be. Some children’s first word would have been Daddy, Momma, maybe even no, but for Dominic that was all different. Dominic’s first word was hell. At first his parents thought it was just a one time thing, he may have heard them say around the house. But as time progressed they began to get worried. Whenever the baby would drop a toy, or fall he would say that word. One day Dominic was given a bowl of soup and surprisingly uttered the words, “To Hell with it”.
After that once in a lifetime childhood incident, Dominic went back to acting and playing as a normal child. But again things changed.
On the night of June 15, Dominic’s birthday to be exact, Dominic and his parents decided to take a little ride to the nearby Chucky cheese for a little birthday celebration. The day went by normal, Dominic opened his presents, sang the world famous song “Its my birthday and I’ll…”, and even went shopping. But as the day ended and his parents were about to open the door to leave Dominic stopped them. He then turned to his mother and said allowed, “Today is the day I die”. His dad playing slapped him across the head and began to laugh and told everyone to file into the car.
That night the unthinkable happened…. The Crazy’s family Honda flipped and fell off the bridge into the dirty Cape Fear River. Dominic’s limp and wet body washed up to the bank and was found the next day by a local fisherman. Dominic’s Mother and Father where never found.
After the accident Dominic began to talk about what had happened to him. He began to tell people about his near brush with death.
After the news of his experience got out around the community people began to wonder, “Is this boy all there?”.
After a month of investigation, North Carolina’s highest ranking Penitentiary, Dairy King’s filed a complaint and took him in as one of there patients.
After 8 years of tests, questions and exams Dairy King’s gave up hope and decided to release him.
After being released from Dairy King Penitentiary Dominic was offered a contract with then small Indy Corporation XWA.
After wrestling for 3 years in XWA, Dominic, now 24, is looking for bigger an better things.
