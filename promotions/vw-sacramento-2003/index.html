Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Vintage Wrestling — VW — was a website federation whose weekly show was Sunday Night Strife. It was running by the spring of 2003, when a roleplay of Shawn Fox’s billed him as its World Heavyweight Champion, with a Vintage Wrestling World Heavyweight Tournament under way to find his challenger, and when its officials objected in public to Global Wrestling trading on the name of Christopher Camero, then “currently an undefeated member of Vintage Wrestling.”

That first run was over by November 2003, when Emilio Dervante was signed elsewhere as a “former Vintage Wrestling Superstar.”

It came back in April 2004 as Vintage Wrestling V2.0. Its front office opened the doors on 12 April — “after a year and a half of pain and suffering, after four months of planning and working, Vintage Wrestling is finally open” — with Rey Campbell, Rimian and Officer Stone among the wrestlers coming back, and its FAQ called the new VW “not really” a whole new one: a few new characters, a few old ones gone, a second show, a new site and a bigger staff.

The format was “All Angle”: each wrestler’s job, its rulebook said, was “to find a partner and work out a complete angle.” The first returning Sunday Night Strife was carded for 18 April 2004 with Jesse Ramey against Statuz Quo in the main event, and the first Thursday Temptation for 22 April with Officer Stone, The British Bomber and Damon O’Halloran in a three-way for the #1 contendership to the World title; a pay-per-view, Culmination, was billed as coming soon.

Its site stops in May 2004 with both results pages still reading “Stay tuned!” and its five championships — World Heavyweight, International, Xtreme, Cruiserweight and Tag Team — all vacant. How the return went, and how the story ended, isn’t in the surviving record.
