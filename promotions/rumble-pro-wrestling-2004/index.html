A closed-flash, application-based indy promotion styled after ROH and MLW, opened in January 2004 as the successor to its operator's own previous, closed federation. Forty-four matches survive across six broadcasts, crowning first champions for both its World Heavyweight and Tag Team titles. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Rumble Pro Wrestling opened on 17 January 2004, pitched by its own founder as “a true ROH/MLW/Indy style federation” that would move away from the standard format of one long television feud settled at a single supercard, running instead toward several feuds at once with titles able to change hands on any card. Staged in-fiction, the promotion was the creation of Chris Chronic, who had closed his previous fed, Five Star Wrestling, for financial reasons before assembling this one.

Its first broadcast, Back For More, aired 8 February 2004 from the Romano Gymnasium in Duluth, Minnesota, with Curt Russell alongside owner, president and color commentator Chris Chronic on commentary. Six men qualified across the card's first two shows for a four-way match to crown the promotion's first World Heavyweight Champion — Justin Kell, Trevor Rodgers, Travis Cade and “Genocide” Jack Ripper among them — settled through singles qualifiers, a tag match, a scramble main event and a Rumble Pro Rumble across its first three cards.

Justin Kell emerged as the first Rumble Pro World Heavyweight Champion; no document in the surviving record shows the title match itself. He kept the belt through the whole run the surviving broadcasts cover, successfully defending it at Absolute Power on 15 May 2004 against Lark Fenriz, who had become the new number-one contender only after Jack Ripper and a stable calling itself The Hatebreed sabotaged Travis Cade's own match and Ripper battered the bloodied champion with his own title belt. On the same card's second broadcast, The Hatebreed's leader, Cardinal Synn, sent his enforcer Anger after Kell in a non-title match Kell wasn't obligated to take — and won anyway.

A Rumble Pro World Tag Team Championship was contested the same spring, decided in a nine-team, one-night tournament: Real Xtreme beat Afternoon Delight, then Van Wild and Scott Starring, then Travis LaGrange and Werewolf Gregorson in the final at Initiation Rites on 10 April 2004 to become the first champions, and turned back a challenge from Lance Williams and James Robinson at Absolute Power the following month.

The promotion's last surviving broadcast, Apocalypse Now on 29 May 2004, closed on a four-corners match to determine a challenger heading into an announced Rumble Pro Invitational tournament. No closure notice survives, and whether that tournament or any further title defense ever happened isn't in the surviving record.
