Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Its president introduced the XVWO on 31 December 1997 as “a new e-wrestling fed. We are currently looking for made-up wrestlers and tag teams. We have one card per week, Friday Night Fury, and you will be required to do one roleplay per week. We are booked, we don't use one of those cheap-@$$ sims,” hosted at http://members.aol.com/MengDa/xvwo.html. A separate fed directory had already listed “XVWO — X-tremely Violent Wrestling Organization” three months earlier, in September 1997, at a different web address — an oddity its own surviving record never resolves.

By its own later account it “officially closed on February 1, 1998” and “re-opened on April 4, 1999, after over a year on the shelves.”

Its 1999 handbook lays out what it had become: despite its name, “we are actually more of a traditional federation... for the most part wrestlers will fight pure scientific matches,” booked rather than simulated, with each match decided by scoring the week's submitted roleplays on a scale of one to ten. It ran a five-day weekly schedule — Monday Night Massacre, a flash show hosted three times a week by an announcer billed as Black Sand, the flagship Friday Night Fury, and a Saturday recap — barred real wrestlers or public figures from its roster outright (“you can't be Lex Luger or Richard Nixon”), and ran four titles: a World Heavyweight Championship, a Television Championship, a World Lightweight Championship for wrestlers under 235 pounds, and a World Tag Team Championship.

A recruiting post that May set its first card under the relaunch for Friday, 28 May 1999, at a new web address.

Nothing dated later survives; whether that card, or the promotion itself, ran past that date isn't known.
