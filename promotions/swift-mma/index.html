A forum-hosted mixed-martial-arts promotion on the FW Central hub whose top wrestler-turned-fighter ran the table to win its only crowned championship at a bankruptcy-clouded first pay-per-view, after which the company was sold to new ownership and never produced another documented show. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

SWIFT: MMA's own founding article, posted 29 November 2009, announces the promotion as Shiner LLC owner Colin Corvitz's bid to build a mixed-martial-arts company with an “open doors” policy toward professional wrestlers looking to prove themselves “legit fighters,” broadcast exclusively on the company's own channel, MMA-HD 238. Its own FAQ sells the pitch directly: “no gimmicks…no holds barred…NO WEIGHT CLASSES,” with fights decided by an edited simulator and fighters submitting pre-fight promos ahead of each card, styled after a UFC countdown package rather than a wrestling angle.

Its debut show, Retaliation, ran 9 December 2009 before a reportedly packed Sears Center Arena in Chicago and carried a single bout: long-tenured professional wrestler “Gentleman” Jonathan Marx against amateur cage fighter David Abbey, which Marx won by TKO one minute and twenty seconds into the second round. Empire Pro Wrestling's own owner, Dan Ryan, watched from ringside and drew a commentary acknowledgment as a fellow wrestler taking in Marx's own entry into the sport.

Four further Retaliation cards followed into early 2010 as the roster grew and periodic rankings tracked its fighters; Marx's own winning streak reached four wins and no losses, while “The Prairie Samurai” Landon Price, billed as the promotion's own home-grown talent, built a 2-0 record of his own.

Those two met for the vacant SWIFT Championship in the main event of the promotion's first pay-per-view, One Shot at Glory, 6 February 2010 at the HP Pavilion in San Jose: Marx won in the third of five scheduled rounds to become the first SWIFT Champion. The company had already filed for bankruptcy before the card aired, dissolving its own pay-per-view television deal, but Corvitz kept his word and let paying fans see the fights anyway rather than cancel the show.

Every remaining asset was sold to Shiner Incorporated afterward. Corvitz and his own booker, Josh Fieldings, were let go outright; the commentary team, Troy Young and Andrew Parker, stayed on as magazine reporters. A new CEO, Toby Withers, described in the promotion's own account as known for “ruthless antics,” eliminated weight classes entirely over the objections of the gaming commissions the company answered to.

The promotion's own last surviving document, a 27 July 2011 retrospective, records the original staff's rehiring under a new time-slot deal with ESEN, with “no set contract date for airing,” and one signing announced: Marx, returning to defend the title he had never lost. No later show, result, or notice survives to say whether that broadcast ever happened.
