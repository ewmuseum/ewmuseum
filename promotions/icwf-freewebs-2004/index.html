A Freewebs-hosted federation whose 2006 restart frames a fictional injured wrestler's turn to promoting as its own kayfabe origin story. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The International Championship Wrestling Federation's earliest surviving trace is a one-line launch pointer, posted 21 December 2004, announcing itself as “A Fun Real Wrestler Fed” with its first pay-per-view due that Friday. By its own later account, that run lasted about eight months.

Its January 2006 return staged two different origin stories a day apart. The first, “Prologue,” restages the promotion as the creation of a fictionalized Bret Hart, driven out of a rival company, XCW, by its owner after Hart's own nephew, Teddy Hart, cost him a world title match against Sting; Hart, fired and furious, returns home to Calgary vowing “a federation FOR THE FANS.”

A second document, its Revised Information Packet (25 January 2006), replaces that story entirely with a different in-fiction founder, Lance Avery, staging a career-ending neck injury suffered in a rival promotion, CIW, a long depression, and a year spent saving money before a fictional financier, the World Sports Network, funded an arena in his hometown of Seattle.

That relaunch built its ladder around four championships — a World, Television, Hardcore, and World Tag Team title — the Hardcore belt decided through a “Spin The Wheel” deathmatch tournament, and its flagship, Sunday Night Survival, hosted by Stevie T. Dee and Marcus Tate, running five to ten matches a card.

Whether the 2006 restart, under either of its own two founding stories, ever aired a card beyond this packet isn't in its surviving record.
