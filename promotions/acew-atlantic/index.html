Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Atlantic Coast E-Wrestling launched in April 1996 billing itself from the start as “The Official Developmental League to the AEWA,” and, in a later recruitment post, as one leg of a three-way internet partnership with two sister federations.

Its weekly broadcast, ACEW Wrestling Champions, aired from a venue it staged as the Paramount Theater at Madison Square Garden, fed by a Philadelphia training school — the ACEW School of Wrestling on South Street — that sent homegrown graduates onto the same cards as wrestlers billed in from Hong Kong and Japan. Off the newsgroup, it also staged at least one live battle royal over IRC.

Its own record turned violent in early 1997: the Metropolitan Championship went vacant after a tournament entrant, Ricky White, put a rival, Lazor Lemon, in the hospital in what its own broadcast called “the very stupid thing,” and White himself sat in jail awaiting trial while the promotion opened a tournament to fill the belt he had emptied.

Its own developmental tie produced defectors as often as talent: two of its champions later left for the AEWA, one carrying his ACEW title with him under a new name.

The promotion did not close so much as rename itself: on 17 May 1997 its own operator announced a new federation, in his own words, “formerly known as ACEW (Atlantic Coast E-Wrestling),” carrying the same roster forward under a new banner. Nothing further survives under the ACEW name.
