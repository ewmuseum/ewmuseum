A Canadian-run fed continuing an NCW predecessor lineage, its weekly 'Scraps' broadcast and two pay-per-views (Onset of Fury, Fatal Fall) surviving across the second half of 1997. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Simply Canadian Rasslin Alliance ran on realism and originality by its own stated rule — no wrestlers built to comic-book scale, and a roster judged on background and personality rather than raw numbers — and gave its own membership a backronym: a SCRAPER, “Simply Canadian Rasslin Alliance Personel for an E-fed Rassler.” Matches ran on a strict weekly rhythm: one flash due every Sunday decided whether a wrestler had a match at all that week, with suspension after a second missed flash and expulsion after a third.

Five named programmes filled its week: Monday Night Scraps, its eight-match flagship, hosted by Jon Grey and Jack Hazard; Tuesday's recap show, Probing SCRA, hosted by Tyranny Dios and Warren Whirry; a Thursday fan-appreciation hour hosted by two employees of the Canadian chain Harvey's; Friday's Double W Report, a full house-show recap from Warren Whirry alone; and Saturday's magazine, Spinebuster Scoops. Four championships ran through them: a World title, a North American title defended roughly twice a month, a Television title capped under 280 pounds and defended weekly, and Tag Team titles.

Its own showcase card has Jack Hazard heckling the broadcast almost as much as he calls it, dismissing a newcomer named Zamza right up until Zamza wins with the Zamza Clutch, and Justice Inc. holding onto the Tag Team titles against the Pros in a match Hazard spends insisting is boring while the crowd, by Grey's own account, stays glued to it.

Its first pay-per-view, Onset of Fury, closed 15 September 1997 with the World Champion, Lifetaker, outlasting Lucifer's power advantage with a handstand headscissors for the pin, Hazard too drunk by the final bell to call it. Its second, Fatal Fall, closed 13 October with a chaotic brawl decided by outside interference — a masked figure and his valet blinding one half of a two-man advantage with a spray to the eyes — leaving Centris standing alone over the House of Pancakes.

Its weekly Scraps broadcast is attested continuing at least into mid-December 1997, and separate community record finds the same show still running into early 1999 with a new World Champion crowned that February. No closing statement of its own survives; its 1997 Usenet record simply runs out.
