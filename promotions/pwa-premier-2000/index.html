A May 2000 booked e-fed run by a single handler under the in-fiction banner of Premier Wrestling Association, its own noteworthy-figures list naming an invented owner, commentary team and officiating staff. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Premier Wrestling Association introduced itself on 26 May 2000 with its own history already seven years deep: founded as Championship Pro Wrestling in the summer of 1996 by James Oslo and a co-owner out of Middle Georgia, renamed and relocated to Orlando, Florida after that co-owner sold his half to pursue a career in comics, and expanding its tours into Alabama and Georgia before landing, in its own telling, a recent syndicated television deal.

Its own roster of “noteworthy figures” named James Oslo as owner and president, Tommy Flynn and Jack Hightower as its two-man commentary team, Ron Nord, Kazuhito Tokeshi, and Brian Hicks as referees, Richard Anderson as ring announcer, and a security chief known only as Walter.

It described itself as a booked federation allowing each handler up to two wrestlers and two non-wrestling characters, with three championships — the PWA World Heavyweight, Television, and Tag Team titles — assigned at random to start “in order to keep the storyline going that this fed has been running for seven years,” airing on a biweekly television taping schedule split across two shows apiece.

No roster, card, or later post survives; whether this account of the Premier Wrestling Association's history and staff ever produced an actual show is not part of the record it left behind.
