Premier Wrestling Association: An E-Fed

26 May 2000
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
____________________________ Premier Wrestling Association An E-Fed By Mark (v3.0) "What the hell?" You would think that I would have learned by now. It would appear that I haven't, because I'm starting a new e-fed or at least attempting to. People have told me that I'm good at it, and people have been asking me to start a new one as of late.
Believe it or not, it's actually pretty fun to do.. most of the time. I've done some thinking, and came up with a few solutions to some of the problems I've had in the past.
One thing you should know is that I'm not trying to create "the best fed on the net" or any other cliche. I'm just starting a fed because it's something fun to do, and my interest in the wrestling on TV is currently fading.
PWA HISTORY The PWA started seven years ago as the CPW -- Championship Pro Wrestling. Two men had the goals of launching yet another independent promotion that they hoped would one day make them millions. Michael Wilkins and James Oslo got things rolling in the Summer of '96 and slowly but surely began to grow. The organization quickly gained popularity in its base of
Middle Georgia. They began to hold shows weekly in places like Macon, Warner Robins, Perry, and so on. However, in the summer of 1996, Michael began to lose interest and finally sold his half of the company to James in order to pursue interests in the comic book world (he's a great artist).
In the interest of himself and other parties, James relocated the promotion's headquarters to the Orlando, Florida area. The promotion had been renamed the Premier Wrestling
Association and they began to tour a more expanded area which included Alabama and Georgia. After four years of continually picking up steam, and various states on its tours, PWA has worked itself into a respectable position including a recent syndicated television program which has everyone involved with the PWA excited.
Where will it go from here? PWA GENERAL INFORMATION The organization will function in a way comparable to ECW, and for all comparison purposes, should be the one to use. The major difference will be the Extreme Wrestling factor. We will still have it, but there won't be nearly as much emphasis as ECW has. Our emphasis will be on (cliche mode) Premier Wrestling.
The fed is run by me, [name withheld], and that's it. I make the calls. I may or may not ask for people's assistance in certain things. Whether they choose to or not is my problem, and it'll stay that way. The fed will be run under booked rules, meaning I make the call on who wins with my own judgement. I really liked the concept of dice wrestling, but this is one of those factors that takes too much of my own time -- and booking it is a fast and effective way to run a fed. Of the three I've run (simmed, dice, and booked), the booked fed went the longest.
You will have the ability to control up to two characters. You are not required to use them both. You will also be allowed to have up to two valets/managers/non-wrestling characters. Whether both are with one character, one split between each, or none at all is up to you. Tag team wrestling will be handled as a "side-project" like manner. There will not be restrictions on wrestlers who wrestle in tag teams. For example, a wrestler wrestling in a tag team will still compete in singles competition and be eligible for singles gold. Tag teams will be formed from two characters, and both characters should notify me to make it official. Tag teams may be created from both of your characters, should you choose to do so. However, it is not required of your characters to be involved with one another. I would prefer that tag teams be created by partnering with another handler, but am not restricting it to this. I just feel this gives more suspense to whether a tag team will actually break up or not and makes for a true team.
There's no reason to dedicate a section to the titles, as they should be pretty self explanatory. The titles that are up for grabs are the PWA World Heavyweight Championship, the PWA Television Championship, and the PWA Tag Team Championship. I am currently considering how to decide who will start with the belts, and may just assign them randomly
(!) in order to keep the storyline going that this fed has been running for seven years. Then, we can just go from there with the angles. (I may also pick out who the champion's current rival is at random, as I'm sure plenty of people will have claims to why they should be in the title angle -- even though nobody will have a good one. We'll discuss it when we've actually got a fed going though. I'm just a little tired of the typical battle royales and tournaments to decide the champs.. and it wouldn't make much sense with the storyline I'm presenting.)
PWA NOTEWORTHY FIGURES These characters are worth pointing out, as they are generally staff members, officials, etc.:
James Oslo - Owner & President of PWA Oslo is the heart of PWA, as he pretty much runs everything behind the scenes. You will very rarely see him on the camera, and will probably hear his name a whole lot more. Oslo handles daily operations of the PWA which include signing the wrestler's checks, overseeing the product, controlling the product, and being the overall visionary behind the product. He is 45 years old and was raised in Dallas, Texas.
Tommy Flynn - Commentator Flynn is the main commentator and leader of the two-man team. I hate to say it, but he's your average play-by-play guy. He's excited about everything, he hates it when the heels cheat, and he gets blown away when the good guys win. Flynn has been involved with the promotion since it moved to Florida , and is very involved with the product along with his close friend, James Oslo. Flynn is 26 years old and has been involved for about four years. Now that they have a syndicated broadcast, Flynn will be able to show his abilities to a national audience instead of the local crowd on local television.
Jack Hightower - Commentator Hightower is the color commentary man. He isn't a former wrestler or anything like that, but he looks like he could have been one. Hightower approached PWA about trying to get a position, as he already knew he wouldn't be able to wrestle because of injuries. Oslo decided to make him the color commentary, and was happy with how Hightower and Flynn interacted with one another. Hightower has done the commentary for about a year and really seems to enjoy it. He is 30 years old, and plays somewhat of the smartass/tough-guy role on the microphone.
Ron Nord - Head Referee The man that has earned the reputation of being "Built Nord Tough," Ron Nord is a bald-headed bad man in stripes. He doesn't take anyone's crap, as the Nordinator is there to enforce the rules and make sure that his officials can do the same thing without the wrestlers taking advantage of their position. Nord is an experienced wrestler and referee.
Kazuhito Tokeshi - Referee "You think Tokeshi punka bitch? Tokeshi no punka bitch!" Tokeshi is the international connection on the referee squad, and tends to be the one that is sent to task when the match has some sort of special or dangerous stipulation. He is a very deep individual, and takes offense to being called a "punka bitch."
Brian Hicks - Referee There's another referee, whose name will not be given out at this time, that always seems to call in sick at the worst times for Brian. For some reason, Brian always seems to come in on his day off when this happens. This generally ends up in him missing pre-planned engagements, something bad happening, and him saying that he's not even supposed to be there. Brian is the least experienced of the referees, but seems comfortable with his situation.
Richard Anderson - Ring Announcer "What's he doing with that mic?" Richard Anderson is a very experienced ring announcer, as he served many a year in a major promotion. I don't know what else to tell you about him.
:) Walter S. - Security Heading this security team, the man known only as Walter is quite the formidable security guard. If you cross his barricade, you're entering a world of pain. Walter takes his job very seriously, as he has seen many young men sacrifice himself in the War just so this promotion could even exist. There are rules at a PWA event -- and he intends to uphold them. As long as the show isn't on Saturday.
PWA SCHEDULE The PWA holds TV tapings just about every two weeks. The flagship show, which is just called PWA TV, shows these tapings on a one-hour weekly basis. So how does that work in e-wrestling terms? Well, matches and happenings from the same show are just aired over a two week basis. Here's a basic layout of what the schedule will look like.
1st Sunday - Second week of TV Taping #1 is aired. An update of some sort is sent out. 1st Thursday - Deadlines for the next set of TV tapings. 2nd Sunday - First week of TV Taping #2 is aired. 2nd Thursday - Second-chance Deadline for any type of RP or strat for the second week of TV Taping. 3rd Sunday - Second week of TV Taping #2 is aired. An update of some sort is sent out. NOTE: Same as the top of the list, which means this is a loop. :) With a weekly hour show, things will be more spread out, and things may flow a little better as people may have ideas of things they want to do on the second week of a taping after seeing what happened in the first week of that taping. However, all matches will not be written out. On average, probably two or three per week will be written out. At the end of the second week of the TV taping, a report of the entire show will follow - explaining everything that happened, whether it was on TV or not. The matches that will air on TV will be the ones that carry the most heat, and have the most going for them. In other words, if you want to be on TV, this is an incentive of sorts to get involved. There will also be other things such as the occasional in-ring, backstage happenings, roleplay segments, and more.
Pay-Per-Views will be held on a sketchy basis, and may increase as things go along. The PWA has only recently begun doing PPVs, and has only held no more than three. PWA GUIDELINES So, how does the fed work? Well, like I've said, it's a booked fed. Roleplaying is important, but it doesn't necessarily have to be in the standard "flash" format. Anything from in-rings to backstage attacks are roleplaying, and as long as you're developing the character or the angle, then you should have nothing to worry about. There will not be an
"RP Show" or a "Flash Show." All roleplaying will be done on the actual shows with the wrestling themselves. I plan to do some sort of "ECW Pulp Fiction" like segment, which was alot of the guys talking in various locations backstage after an event over the theme from
Pulp Fiction. I don't care about how much you curse.. that's up to you. But I'm probably going to edit it with [BLEEPS] so.. if you want to sound like Mike Awesome, that's fine with me. Also, please write like a somewhat coherent person. I know the human race is generally stupid but if you send an RP that looks like a four year old typed it, I'm going to get rid of you for the sake of everyone. "ok u undersatnd kewl!"
As far as strategies go, please don't go overboard. You can customize what your wrestler does on the card/in the match to any degree you want -- but I don't want 20k strats for standard cards. That's ridiculous. If you feel you have to send those come PPV time, then
I'll understand that. Strategies will have weight come time to deciding who wins. I just don't need 300k to sort through before making decisions.
Oh yeah, this goes without saying -- don't sit on your ass. Participate. Otherwise, you're just wasting the time of me and all the other players.
If you don't like things that are going on in the PWA, come to me and tell me about it. If you don't like whatever it is that I have to say in return, quit. This is probably the
20th fed starting today. There's lots of other ones out there. APPLICATION If you're still interested after reading the above, then just fill out everything past the dotted line, cut and paste it into an e-mail (I prefer that over attachments that I have to manage), and send it to
[detail withheld] .. If you're not interested, then just toss it. I hope that you'll all join, and I can once again run an active fed that is alot of fun.
PS For those that know me, this app is not difficult. That's one of the things I decided to do away with. ;)
PSS If you know anybody that you think would be interested in joining, please give this to them. I have sent this to only a handful of people and any help recruiting would be appreciated.

WRESTLER DETAILS In the following sections, you should describe these to the best of your ability. I mean, if you want to do a bad job, that's up to you. It doesn't take me as long to read it as it takes you to write it. Appearance should describe the wrestler's appearance which includes his physical appearance and his common entrance/ring attire. Personality should describe the wrestler's personality, and if he doesn't have one, then you should explain what he does have.
