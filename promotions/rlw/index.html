A Chicago-based, DePaul University-affiliated indie promotion that crowned its own inaugural Red Crown Champion through a twelve-entrant tournament before going on hiatus in November 2015 with a final pay-per-view left unfinished. 42 wrestlers, 12 shows and three roleplays survive. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Red Line Wrestling opened in August 2014 as a Chicago indie promotion working in partnership with DePaul University, staging its shows in university facilities and streaming them online with a paid Subscriber Exclusives tier for extra character content. Its own founding FAQ pitches a house style somewhere between NXT, Pro Wrestling Guerilla and Chikara, with low word-count expectations and no wrestler required to appear on every card.

An inaugural Red Crown tournament ran across its first shows into that winter, crowned at its own first special event, Rush Hour, 11 January 2015: Ivan Dalkichev beat Russ Spackler in the final and was anointed champion on the spot by his own mentor, Nathan Fear, who set the crown on his head himself. The broadcast closed on a staredown between the new champion and his own waiting number-one contender, The Second Coming.

The title held through the spring. By Slamtrack 10, 31 May 2015, a fifteen-man battle royal to determine Dalkichev's next challenger went to Hittora, only for Dalkichev himself to storm the ring unannounced afterward and attack both Hittora and The Second Coming with his own finisher, the Titan Bomb, twice over.

The angle turned again when Fear tried to call off the promotion's own security by threatening to pull Dalkichev's contract; Dalkichev answered by tearing the contract up in front of him. The broadcast closed with its own announcer promising the main event of a next event, Mass Transit, was set.

That main event never aired. The promotion's own operator posted a hiatus announcement that November, admitting the show had stalled under a one-person staff and that Mass Transit's own script was never finished, adding only that the promotion “may reopen one day.” No later document survives.
