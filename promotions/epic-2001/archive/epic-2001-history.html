History of Epic Wrestling

What this is The federation's own history page, the story it gave itself, as the Wayback Machine captured it on 6 February 2002.
Epic Wrestling | Main | History History of Epic Wrestling The story of Epic Wrestling began with a young man named Matthew McGrath. Matthew McGrath was born into a wealthy family. His father, Kent was rolling in millions... literally. Kent McGrath was the owner of a highly successful cola/snack food company at the time. At a young age, Matthew was drawn into the business known as Professional Wrestling. He absolutely loved watching it, but his outlook on the industry itself changed drastically as he grew older. Kent's cut throat business style had found it's way on his son. In 1981, Matthew approached his father with a business proposal for a Professional Wrestling organization. He had titled it Project: Epic. Matthew knew his paycheck as the Head of Public Relations for his father's company just wouldn't cut it. He needed his Kent's financial backing to support it. Kent had really never thought much of his son's ability to lead a company because he was often to busy with his own dealings. Matthew's proposal really caught him off guard and he was very impressed with his son's research and ideas. However, Kent still rejected his son's proposal. Mellisa, disappointed with her husband's dealing with something her son worked hard on, kicked him out of the bedroom. For the next two weeks, Kent had to sleep on the couch. He tried sneaking in the room late at night, but his wife, Melissa wasn't having it. Wanting his bedroom back, Kent agreed to support his son's idea. Matthew's idea in approaching making his organization a reality was quite solid, but he left out one thing... just which wrestlers were available? Matthew thought long and hard about how he could obtain a solid wrestling roster. After four months, he realized he wasn't going anywhere with this and did the next best thing... purchase other wrestling organizations! Matthew used his money to buy two semi-major leagues, AW (All-American Wrestling) and cWa (canadian Wrestling association). Due to their success, the owners were hesitant, but Matthew had the money and as we all know, money talks best. Matthew flew to Japan, but he knew even if he could break down the honor of a Japanese wrestling owner he most likely wouldn't be able to get all of that organization's roster. Akira Akiyama, one of Japan's most talented wrestlers. He was one of the few individuals who could do it all. High flying, Power, Brawling, Technical, Shoot Fighting, whatever... he excelled in it. Akiyama was the current World Champion in his organization for 23 months strong. Matthew had went to a house show they were holding which would solidify his reign as World Champion for two full years. After the event was over, McGrath approached his prey. He was actually going to attempt to sign the star. Matthew's first offer was shot down. As would be the second offer. McGrath's Third offer... 250,000 dollars a year along with a house near their headquarters in North Carolina had been enough to lure the talent superstar to North American territory. Akiyama's promoter was livid to say the least. In total, 15 of his workers were needed to restrain him from getting his hands on McGrath and Akiyama, but Akira's treachery didn't come without a price. The promoter had sat down with other independent and major leagues in Japan. They agreed upon his punishment.. His name would forever be erased from all records giving him any type of acknowledgement. His name wasn't even allowed to be uttered on the shows and any signs bearing his name or reference were torn to shreds. McGrath didn't care. Neither did Akiyama as the legacy he would build in being apart of McGrath's federation that would topple that of his in Japan. After removing the weaker members of both AW and cWa Talent Rosters, McGrath had assembled a staff of wrestling purists to assist him in succeeding with this project to the best of man's known abilities. The purists were quite skeptical of Akiyama's loyalty after seeing how easily his allegiances could be manipulated, but McGrath calmed them down, assuring them that the contract was air tight. Using the name of McGrath's project, the name of the company was titled Epic Wrestling. A roster was formed, ideas were made, and on March 14, 1983 they were all finally going to be put to use. Matthew intelligently used his father's influence to get televised. He managed to squezze 6:30 to 8:00 O'clock time slot on Monday's from Fox. They titled the show, Ground Zero as it would be the base of all things concerning Epic Wrestling. They show started with Akiyama appearing to large pop outside in the parking lot and explaining his situation in Japan. Akira then proceeded with the burning of his old promotion's World Championship, in a trashcan. In the main event, the promotion's first title would be decided between AW's United States Champion, Wayne Carter and cWa's Canadian Champion, Nicholas Nova. Carter picked up his first win using his modified Elevated Boston Crab (Courage Required) finisher and became Epic Wrestling's North American Champion. The next show had cWa's World Champion, "Suicidal" Cameron Wilson and AW's World Champion, Trent Morgan going at with Akira Akiyama in a Triple Threat Elimination Match for the World Championship. After a 45-minute match in which the crowd witnessed an array of moves never before seen on American Television, Akiyama got the pin on Cameron with his Shooting Star Frog Splash (Crystal Method) Finisher and with that, he walked away with the win and to right to once again call himself "champion". During December of 1990, Matthew realized he had a money maker on his hands. Epic Wrestling was doing well, very well. The wrestlers were coming up with moves and taking risks that not even Japan had seen. The American crowd loved it. The World Wrestling Federation and Championship Wrestling League were both gaining success with their string of highly successful Pay Per Views. Until December, Epic Wrestling programming as sign of loyalty had been signed to be exclusive to Fox. Fox decided to ammend the contract to release them from the clause, giving them Pay Per View options. The Epic Executive Board named the first Pay Per View, Epic Warfare. Epic Warfare went on to become their flagship Pay Per View event. Epic would continue to only prosper. Wrestlers became involved in the Make A Wish Foundation. "The Legend" Donaven Daniels, Wayne Carter, Akira Akiyama, and "Suicidal" Cameron Wilson were among the most requested. Unfortunately on Tuesday August 23, 1995, during a creative meeting for Unleashed, Professional Wrestling had lost it's most creative and innovative mind. Matthew had been arguing with one of his fellow board members when a sharp pain shot up his arm and chest. Epic Wrestling Chairman, Matthew McGrath had died in his chair as a result of an ill-fated heart attack. He was 43 years old. In his honor, Unleashed was renamed The End of an Era. It was to be Epic Wrestling last event.. ever. It achieved one of the highest Pay Per View buyouts any Wrestling, Boxing, Porn, or Movie Event has seen to this day. The show was incredible. Both North American and World Championship titles were on the line. Matthew's youger brother, Marcus McGrath was going up against North American Champion, Scott Knight in a Table Match for the championship. Marcus had vowed to win the match to finally make his brother proud of him. After 39-minute contest, McGrath sent him to the floor through a table using his Top Rope Powerbomb (Final Fate) finisher. The Main Event would go down as the best match in Epic Wrestling's History. World Champion, Wayne Carter had created the most insane match to seen... ever. He called it a Final Destination Match. It was similar to what is known as an Iron Man Match, but it would last 90 minutes. ANYTHING and EVERYTHING was legal. However, the trick was to score a pinfall, you had to pin the champion. The other participates of the match included Akira Akiyama, "Suicidal" Cameron Wilson, Tigerhawk, and Trent Morgan. Near the final 25 minutes, some of the combatants spilled backstage and boy did it get ugly. Using a vending machine to leap off, Wilson had pinned Morgan with his Shooting Star DDT (Suicidal Tendencies) finisher leaving him tied with Wayne and Akiyama. Akira and Carter were still fighting on the stage by the entrance. Akiyama gave Carter a Body Press Drop off the stage through a table. Akira's position was a bit off and Carter hit the edge of the table, knee first. Altough, that very injury would result in this being his final match, Carter wasn't done yet. Akiyama made his way into the back to find the champion. After wondering into a bathroom, he was met by a Spear by Tigerhawk with such force he had been driven through the sink! Tigerhawk went back outside only to be Tombstone Piledrived onto a vending Machine by Wilson. When the camera had turned back to Carter's position to show the crowd he was receiving proper care, Wayne's body was gone. Limping backstage, Carter got word Wilson had scored the last pinfall. Trent had recovered finally and was back on the assault with Wilson. Morgan seemed to get ready to add himself to list of men with 2 wins when he slammed Wilson down with his Destiny Hammer (Death Valley Driver) Finisher, but Wayne had finally reached the scene and snapped Trent into the wall using a Cobra Clutch Russian Legsweep. After releasing Morgan from his grip, Carter crawled over to Wilson and made the cover. Akiyama had finally emerged from the bathroom, but he was too late. Wayne had gotten the win and time was up. With a match like this, Epic Wrestling's Executive Board considered not closing, but things just didn't seem the same without Matthew around. Rather than make Epic Wrestling a shell of it's former self, they agreed to stick with their original decision to close it down. Everyone one except Morgan and Carter went pretty much went their separate ways. There were few exceptions though. Wayne opened a Training Academy which he named The Carter Academy. Morgan on the other hand founded an Entertainment Company which he named Epic Entertainment. They sold Televisions, VCR's, and Computer Software. Trent's older brother, John had found himself in gambling debt. He had spent all of the money for his son's senior year tuition. John was always making stupid decisions like that. Sure, he was the older brother, but throughout most of their childhood, Trent always had to get his older brother out of trouble. This situation was no different. Because of John's immaturity, Trent was often the one who took care of his son, William. Before William had started High School, Trent and John both agreed that John was not a suitable father for the young teen and they didn't want to see him in a foster home either. Therefore, Trent was made his legal guardian. John always did feel bad about having to put his son in that situation which was why he offered to pay for his college tuition. When William turned 24, Trent had contracted AIDS. The years went by and Trent's condition began to worsen. One day in his office, Trent had fainted. After being rushed to the hospital, the diagnosis was Trent had came down with the flu. There was a desperate battle going on in his system and without any antibodies, Trent was on the loosing side. William quickly came to the hospital to be by his uncle's side. However, Trent was more to him than an uncle. He was always that "cool" uncle. After all he was a former Wrestling star and owned one of the biggest Entertainment Companies, but he was also the father figure in his life, something his real father could never provide him. As Trent laid in his bed, dying from the sickness that was overcoming him, he made the dying man's request. From when William was just 8 years old, Trent would allow him to come to the backstage area. William would often grab a chair and sit near the curtain to get a good view of the show. So when Trent asked him to restore and run Epic Wrestling, he understood. Sure his uncle owned a big company, but it didn't come close to the trill Wrestling gave him. He performed live on television, entertaining people. That thrill, was one he was and always had been addicted to. He couldn't help himself. That day.. March 26, 2001, Trent Morgan was pronounced dead. Before Trent was taken away from us, William agreed to carry out his uncle's wishes. When William left the hospital that day, he felt sad for Trent's death, but confused. William had the heart and love for the business. He also had a degree in Business, but Matthew had something he didn't, money. McGrath was Loaded. William only made 40,000 a year. That sure wasn't gonna cover any contracts. After the funeral, William sat home in his Manhattan apartment considering his predicament. Someone had knocked on door, but Morgan wasn't expecting anyone. After looking through the peephole and seeing what appeared to be a lawyer, William opened the door. What he would be told surprised even him. Trent had left Epic Entertainment to him along with 6 billion dollars, almost half of his savings. William owned Epic Entertainment, but Trent's CEO was placed in charge of the company. He knew William would have his hands full. William knew what he had to do. Trent never asked anything of him until know. After approaching the living members of Epic Wrestling's old Executive Board and telling them his plans, they gave him the organization, free of charge. He used the Internet to get back in touch with business side of things in wrestling. William was determined to make it work. Only one problem, all of the big stars that could give Epic that needed momentum for a restarting organization, were signed. Things were looking rather bleak until he came upon a headline titled "CWF Bankrupt!" Where a federation is bankrupt, wrestlers are available. After clicking the link to see what names were among this roster, he couldn't have been more delighted. Names such as then IWO and Current IOW World Champion - Kent Anthason, DWF Hall of Famer - Doozer, Akira Akiyama's Protege - "The Forgotten Angel" Seth Anson, IWO Grand Slam Winner - High Flyer, "The Icon" Ben Archer, and IWO Extreme Legend - Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid let Morgan know he found what he needed. Realizing knew he'd need a capable staff, Morgan opted to buy the entire fed all together. William contacted Fox to repair there past relationship with Epic. The meeting went well. Fox was still recovering from being somewhat of a joke with it's "When [Fill in the blank] Attacks" television series. Realizing they were in need for better television programming, Fox took the wrestling show and would also improve their original timeslot, giving them 9/8pm Central compared to it's old 6:30/5:30 Central. Epic Wrestling Chairman, William Morgan is now ready to unleash Epic Wrestling on the World and make his mark.
