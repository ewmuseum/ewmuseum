He knows hes big, powerful, talented, etc. and makes sure everyone knows it. His one trait that stands out the most is his temper. Kenny has learned to take no crap from ANYONE. No matter what the situation is, hell always defend himself with words or his fists. Along with his temper, he has a very bad mouth. Hes doesnt try to intimidate anyone with it, its just second nature. He loves annoying and harassing fellow wrestlers just as much as the staff members. Being late for shows and breaking the rules shows Kenny doesnt care about anyone else BUT him. Usually, when not feeling so well, he pays other men to do his "dirty work". For example, if he is having a problem with a wrestler and isnt in the mood to settle it himself, hed pay another wrestler or even the cameraman to take care of it. Usually when the person refuses to do the job, or doesnt get it done, there is hell to pay. Now Kenny isnt miserable, although it may seem that way. Hes very confident in himself and has nothing to be ashamed about. In fact he smiles and laughs much more than most of the other wrestlers. But thats when he kicks someones ass so bad its funny. Inside the ring theres no doubt hes very talented. Kenny has a variety of powerful moves that send chills down the spines of the fans. He was once named Thunder, because the sound of his opponents body hitting the mat resembles the sound of thunder. The fans love to cheer Kenny as well as boo him. He doesnt pay much attention to the fans, and certainly doesnt care about them. But the fans dont appreciate Kennys late match habits. Once a match has become very close, and Kenny is becoming worried, he has no problem with bashing a steel chair over his opponents head and getting DQed. Then if hes still pissed, hell continue and bash it over the refs head. Thats the kind of person he is. Lots of low blows and cheap shots are used too. Kennys bad habits, which include heavy drinking and drug abuse, have become one of his biggest weaknesses. Kenny is a big man who doesnt have as much energy as the others are. He also has no patience, and likes to beat his opponent badly and quickly. Overall hes a big New Yorker who doesnt take crap from anyone. Championships arent important to him. The only thing thats important is kicking ass, even if it means cheating. Historical Information Epic Titles Won: None Epic Awards Won: None Past Promotions: Prof. Championship Wrestling, uKw, Dream Wrestling, oWa, Fighting Zone, Five Star Wrestling Past Titles: PCW - [International, Intercontinental, Hardcore] Fighting Zone - [World] uKw - [European] DWF - [Stables] Past Awards/Accomplishments: none |Biography| Born in New York City on November 17th, 1972. He was raised by his father and lived in an old, cheap apartment in a dangerous location. Pat entered school with lots of high hopes. But as one of the biggest bullies around, not too many people liked him. People pretended to like him only because they didnt want to be on his bad side. Pat was very big and always got what he wanted. The friends Pat did have, which were quite a few, had the same attitude as him. That included horsing around, being disrespectful, and laziness. Most of those traits are still with him today. Ever since he signed up for Little League Baseball, at 6 years old, he was recognized as one of the best athletes in the area. He always played physical and never took losing as an option. His athletic ability declined in his teen years. Pats father had no idea what his son would do on weekends. Whenever his father had to spend the night at work, or was away, Pat would meet up with his friends and party for as long as they could. He was rolling joints and emptying beer cans at 14 years old. All the partying started to become a problem however. As a C student up to that point, he was now failing most subjects and not showing up for class. Pat was forced off the school football and baseball teams due to this problem. His father became worried and soon found out what his son had been doing. That was after finding a stash of marijuana hidden in his desk. Pat wasnt severely punished, but it marked the end of his partying. He was 17 at the time. There wasnt any possibility of graduating, since he had failed the previous two years. So he started skipping school and got a job in a fast-food restaurant. He was disappointed in himself. Disappointed that he had spent most of his years dealing and abusing drugs. And since he had no High School diploma, there were limited job opportunities. This was about the time he looked into wrestling. Any type of job involving physical activity would suit him just fine. But being a pro athlete was out of the question. Instead he checked out fighting. There was many underground clubs in the city, but it was too risky. One night he asked his father what he thought about becoming a wrestler. Once he realized Pat was serious, he called up a friend of his. Pats dad had a friend who was in the wrestling industry. He worked for a small independent wrestling league in Florida. His name was Jim Armstrong. Jim had a talk with Walsh and decided to do him a favor, and give him a shot at the sport. Of course he started out very slow, but everyone saw some hidden talent in him. Pat was enjoying the sport, which helped motivate him a little. But unlike the others he didnt have a background. Living in Florida was very fun as well. A little too fun. His old habits caught up to him again, and at the wrong time. Soon he was missing shows, not showing up for training and overall being a lazy bum. Jim warned Walsh this was his last opportunity for success, and that it would never come his way again. Walsh knew Jim was right, so he agreed to be supervised by a few of Jims friends. His drinking and smoking slowed down a little, and his wrestling skills were improving. He took the name Y2W in early 1992. He had just turned 20 years old, and had 3 years of wrestling under his belt. That was the time to move up and make a living out of the sport. Scouts from the popular BWO organization were impressed by Walsh natural ability and intimidating appearance. After viewing a well-done promo at what would be his final show for Jim and the crew, BWO took a chance and signed him. Its 2002, 10 years later. Over those 10 years he had his ups and downs. But for the most part he remains one of the most recognized wrestlers in the industry, and truly found the job for him. But all his cockiness, drinking, smoking, cursing, and disrespect has kept him from becoming a major superstar. He hopes to step away from the hardcore wrestling style hes used to, and settle down in a major organization. Hes already 30 years old a veteran. His body has suffered quite a bit of bumps and bruises over the years, but no serious injuries. His main concern was finding a steady organization he could rely on. Now that he's gotten the call from epic, could this be the break he's been waiting for?
