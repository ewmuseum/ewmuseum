Epic Wrestling | Resources | Hall of Fame Epic Wrestling's Hall of Fame Matthew McGrath August 28, 1995 In the highest of an exceptional situation does a the Owner of a Professional Wrestling Organization get listed in the Hall of Fame of his own company. However, Matthew was a one in million. He was very well respected by his roster for the we handled business and every Monday Night, over 3 Million Fans tuned in to view the end result of a man who had poured his heart out to provide the best quality professional wrestling on the Earth. Rest In Peace, Matthew We thank you, as you ran Epic Wrestling with passion unrivaled. Wayne Carter August 28, 1995 Upon his arrival to Epic Wrestling first ever Ground Zero as All-American Wrestling's United States Champion when left the event as it's North American Champion, nobody could've guess that the young star they were watching would evolve into the most acclaimed wrestler to step through our metallic blue ropes. In his twelve year career in Epic, Wayne would lay claim to the World Heavyweight Championship for an unparalleled five times. To this date, Wayne still holds the record for longest title reign along with a twenty five month reign as World Champion (Fifth Time). In addition, Carter was the first person to win the North American and World Heavyweight Championships. Wayne was the last Epic Wrestling World Heavyweight Champion before it's closing in 1995. Akira Akiyama August 28, 1995 If you've ever wanted to ask Michael Jordan why he plays Basketball, or Tiger Woods why he plays Golf, or Joe Montana why he played Football, they'd give you the same answer - that they were given a great talent and were born to use it. Akiyama's extrodinary talent happened to be... hurting people. Once he had lost the title for the second time to Wayne Carter, the Japanese Legend began to show his darker side to the fans. After a brief hiatus, the focused former champion, went on a destructive warpath by injuring referees, officials, delivery people, Jason Slade, and retiring Sebastion North with his Backdrop Driver. No fan could ever forget the sickening feud between him and Cameron Wilson after Akira had given his girlfriend, April a Drop Toe Hold onto the steel entrance ramp and abducted Wilson, then jammed a stick between the seat and accelerator pedal causing Wilson's new Camero to drive him off a bridge. Luckily for Wilson, the bridge was no more than 20 feet. Akiyama went on gain defeat Wilson for the title, beating Cameron, one day before Wilson's reign could reach the One Year mark. Akira got another title reigns as World Heavyweight Champion, rounding it off to his Fourth Reign as World Champion. Akiyama currently holds one of the best win loss records with 363 victories and is the only person to have never submitted to Carter's Courage Required Finisher. Trent Morgan August 28, 1995 In his earlier years in Epic Wrestling, Trent was very close with Akiyama. Morgan and Akira often took their frustrations out on the unfortunate soul who just happened to be nearby at the time. The two of them would often share a laugh when Trent injuried someone with his Destiny Hammer (Death Valley Driver) or Akiyama with his Backdrop Driver. Following Morgan's title loss to SCW or "Suicidal" Cameron Wilson, the injuries became more frequent. It was during this time peroid, Trent began to distance himself from Akira. He was just getting to out of control. Morgan eventually split ways with Akiyama after Akira's Backdrop had ended the career of Sebastion North. North had been a close friend during his tenure in All-American Wrestling (AW). When Wayne had defeated Akira during Carter's four title reign and the madness caused by his former ally finally began to subside. Following a victory over the man who ended his third title reign, Adam Renzor and his capturing of the World Championship once more, it's was happening all over again. Morgan, finally decided to put an end to the mayhem he had partially created. Live on the Ashes of the Fall Pay Per View, it was Akira Akiyama vs Trent Morgan in a Steel Cage. Most people will tell you: Until the Final Destination, this was undoubtedly the best match throughout his career. Everything Morgan tried just wouldn't keep the beast down and vice versa. Trent was still testing out a maneuver he thought up a few weeks in prep to the match. Seeing no other resort, Morgan catapulted himself from the apron out the third rope before pulling off a beautful Dragon Attack which he later named the Astroid's Descend. Three seconds later, Trent Morgan won the World Heavyweight Championship for a Third Time. "Suicidal" Cameron Wilson August 28, 1995 Arguably the greatest daredevil in the history of our great business. On the April 17th edition of Ground Zero back in 1989, Cameron would add the crowning and most daring achievement to his legacy when he lived up to his "Suicidal" moniker. Wilson had delivered a devastating 450 Splash from the Top Rope, six rows into the crowd onto Trent Morgan to become the World Heavyweight Champion of Epic Wrestling for a third time. However, due to the height of jump combined with the 10 chairs he fell into, Cameron was prenounced dead in his latest installment of unmeasurable risks. April 17th, 1989 was not to be the end of Suicidal Saga when to the astoundishment of every man, women, and child who had their eyes on the Three Time World Champion, Wilson sat up and unselfishly waved to his beloved fans that he'd willingly risk his life for to entertain every Monday Night.
