A play-by-email women's wrestling fed founded in May-June 1994, the earliest women's electronic wrestling federation on record, whose own rulebook and continuing 1995 recruitment notices attest a full second year of operation with a hosted website and its own wrestler-creation ruleset. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Electronic Women's Wrestling Association opened on 28 May 1994 with its own Rulebook V1.0, a play-by-e-mail federation in which only women stepped into the ring, though men were welcome as managers. Its own sign-up rules barred real names outright while permitting an obvious play on one: “no Hillary Clinton, but Hillbilly Clinton is ok (only one of her, though).”

Its house ruleset had each wrestler choosing nine moves from a named list of sixty-six, from the ordinary (bodyslam, DDT, sleeperhold) to the house-specific (Ghetto Blaster, Ghostbuster, Veg-o-matic), with a handful marked as illegal and good for a cheating roll if used anyway; a tag team needed two wrestlers whose moral alignments differed by no more than one step, plus a shared finisher and its own short list of double-team moves.

It ran a full second year on the strength of that rulebook, its own July 1995 recruitment notice pointing new signees toward a Bikini Bash pay-per-view before their debut, and by April 1996 it had become a charter member of the Summit Wrestling Alliance, switching its house ruleset over to TNM. It announced itself “back for real this time” again that July, with a new web-hosted sign-up form.

Its own operator disbanded the EWWA outright on 20 October 1996, in a single sentence: “I am disbanding the EWWA due to a complete lack of time.”
