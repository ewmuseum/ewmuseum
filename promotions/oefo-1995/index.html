A ten-arena hybrid arcade-fighting/pro-wrestling/Ultimate-Fighting game, modeled on a published tabletop rules system, that filled its own roster within three days of its founding and was grounded three weeks later without a single sanctioned match airing. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Outlaw Extreme Fighting Organization introduced itself 5 October 1995 as, in its own words, “an exciting mixture of arcade fighting games, pro-wrestling, and Ultimate Fighting,” its rules built on White Wolf Game Studio's tabletop system “Street Fighter: The Storytelling Game” and its premise pitching fighters “from the alleys of New York to the hills of Malibu” against one another for “revenge, greed, glory, honor, or desperation.” Its home was a fictional pyramid-shaped arena on the Mississippi River in Memphis, Tennessee.

That arena carried ten sanctioned fight settings by its own count — a square ring, an octagonal ring, caged versions of both, an unringed “Ultra-Cage,” an alley/street setting, scaffolding, a setting called “The Web,” and two others called “Ground Zero” and “The Explosion” — plus one sanctioned match staged outside the arena entirely, a bout it called the “Ultimate Survivor” match. Under its own rules, fighters controlled what their characters said and whom, where, and when they fought, while the outcome of every match itself rested with its own gamemaster, whose decisions the organization declared final.

The promotion's own operator grounded it three weeks later, on 27 October 1995, with no sanctioned match ever having aired. A single unconfirmed line seven months after that, on 1 May 1996, claims a revival; no further activity survives.
