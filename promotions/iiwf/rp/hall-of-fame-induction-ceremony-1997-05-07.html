IIWF — Hall of Fame

IIWF Hall of Fame Induction Ceremony, 7 May 1997

7 May 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Laser beams cut the sky over the Downtown Portland Ritz-Carlton Hotel. Long black limosines pull up to the door. Throngs of crowds push against the velvet barrier ropes in hope of seeing their favorite superstar. The camera cuts to the IIWF regulars.] TD: Another gala event for the IIWF leading up to Birthday Bash this Saturday. I'm Tim Dross and with me is the capable Larry Morton and the radient Becky LaRue. LM: Thanks Tim. Becky, that is certainly an interesting gown you're almost wearing tonight. Did you buy it by the inch then run out of money half way? BL: Larry, you know how the engineers hate cleaning drool out of the microphones. TD: Here come the superstars... ["Nifty" Ned Norton is the first to arrive. A bald, goateed fan waves franticaly, displaying his "I'm nuts for Ned" shirt to the camera.] BL: I thought you said "Superstars", Tim. [More wrestlers arrive: Ike Sampson, Shinja Chow, Serge Annis. Marty Warnett steps out to the screams of prepubescent girls. He points out that he is wearing a Chris Quigley t-shirt under his tux jacket. Stunt Team USA step out of a limo with Kangaroo Carlyle and the Guiding Lights. Sabin Figaro arrives, but no one notices. Subway Psycho drives up with Sasha and Mench, quickly ushering them inside, his hand covering the camera lens. Aces of the Deep step from a car to an appreciable cheer.] CROWD: Flood! Flood! Flood! TD: It's good to see some of the old faces turn out for this event. BL: Does this mean we'll see Moondust tonight? TD: I hope not. You're the only one who seemed to like him. BL: He's the only one in the IIWF who dressed better than I do. He also gave me some dynamite makeup tips. LM: It's my understanding that Simon Lebec is still in Brazil, and the Man of Steel isn't ready to exit rehab. BL: And "The Machine" Hunter Robinson is still dead. [There is a hushed moment of silence as a wheel chair bearing Joe Latta is lowered to the sidewalk. He waves to the crowd and they cheer. They cheer again when Dan "Flash" Kauffman, Brandon Bennett and Carla Daughtery step from the van and push their friend inside.] TD: I understand that Joe has been recovering slowly from his attack by Casey James. BL: And I see than Carla is recovering slowly from her latest nose job. TD: On the subject of Casey James, Speak of the Devil. LM: Who? Who? Deathbringer? Prince of Darknes? Requiem? Archangel? Altair? TD: No. The Syndicate. [The Syndicate's two luxury limos empty their contents onto the curb. Champion Casey James points at the departing Joe Latta and laughs himself sick. Lau arrives, Tiger Claw and Brody Thunder. Kenny Tanaka can be seem waving from the front seat now wearing a chauffer's cap. Last from the Limo steps IIWF legend "The Masked Outlaw" J.W. Hardin. Becky goes wild.] TD: I see the Syndicate didn't bring their chef with them this time. LM: Which reminds me. Thanks for the Bar-be-que last Sunday. I still remember the taste of your wife's potato salad. Mmmm-mmm. TD: Thanks Larry, I'll tell her. It's the marjorum y'know. So, I guess Lau trusts the food this time. LM: The IIWF chefs are the best...[Becky chokes]...but don't you think that $100 a plate is a bit steep? Oh, I paid it, but even still... TD: Larry, we work for the IIWF. We don't have to pay. LM: But... But... Becky told me... I gave her the money to buy me a ticket... BL: What? What? Why is everyone looking at me like that? [More stars arrive. Billy Shakespeare, Onslaught, Tony Starks, Cold Spell, Otto Verhoeven with Heidi.] BL: Pity Steve Roberts isn't here. For some reason this Teutonic Tramp appeals to him. TD: We won't dwell on the unfortunate circumstances of why Mr. Roberts will not be in attendance. BL: Steve Roberts plays Michael Jordan: he shoots... He scores! Or maybe more like Dennis Rodman: he shoots... he gets suspended! [Last to arrive is the official IIWF limosine from which emerges President Spreadbury, Vice President [name withheld] and two junior suits who are mostly annonymous save for the one wearing high tops with his tux.] BL: I see the President is wearing his penguin suit tonight. TD: Why don't we go inside.? Waiters bustle in between, serving drinks. Steve Kowalski pitches his dinner roll at Joe Petrow and only a quick thinking Armed Forces keeps things from getting out of control. The Punster entertains the crowd.] P: Monty Hall of Fame... Hall of Fame and Oates... Deck the Halls of Fame with Holly... [Sparkplug Lee steps up as Punster sits down. Lee adjusts his powder blue tuxedo and takes the mic.] RA: Ladies... Gentlemen... wrestlers of all shapes, sizes, and ages. Welcome to the First Annual IIWF Hall of Fame Banquet! [Fireworks explode on the giant video screen erected behind the podium. Tim, Larry and Becky take their seats at the lead table.] TD: Today we gather to recognize two wrestlers, both retired, who helped to make the IIWF the top federation in wrestling today. Now many names have graced our roster: From Crippler to Phantom, from Crimson Storm to Magus. We've had two "bad boys" and one "Painbringer." We've got a Sandman, a Hangman, a Highwayman and a Man of Steel. From Fisto Flash to Flare to "Nuclear" John Bomber, we've had everything from A to Z. Or from Abie to Zed if you prefer. But these two men have made their mark on this league like many of those others have not, or could not. They are: "Angel of the Sun" Hakiro Matsuoko... [The martial artist enters from the back. The video screen lights up footage of him performing a flying spinning leg lariat. He nods respectfully to the audience who applauds. He shakes hands with the presenters and the company brass. He bows to Sparkplug Lee who bows in return and the two collide heads. Hakiro takes his seat.] TD: ...and our next honoree, the man called "The Outlaw" J.W. Hardin! [Hardin strides out, cowboy hat, boots and a tuxedo. He sports a sheriff's star for humor. The video displays assorted scenes of Hardin executing the Cattle Buster DDT, most notably on Scott "The Whine" Bloom, Dan Kauffman and European Champion Gunther Der Pferd. Hardin shakes hands with Tim, ignores Larry, and plants a kiss on Becky which buckles her knees. He exchanges handshakes with Spreadbury, and is about to shake hands with VP [name withheld], but thinks better of it. He walks past his chair at the head table and instead joins the Syndicate where they sit. He props his feet up on the table and catches a bottle of Kessler's thrown to him from across the room by Pale Rider of the High Plains Drifters.] TD: The life of a professional wrestler isn't an easy one. Here to put it all in perspective is the world's oldest living wrestler: Roughrider Roosevelt. LM: I didn't know he was still alive. [Roosevelt shuffles to the podium, his mask hanging limply over his eyes. There is polite applause. He winks laciviously at Becky.] BL: Keep dreaming old man. You haven't had a Hardin in years. RR: In my day it wasn't this easy. There were no airplanes to jet you from Portland to Toronto to London. Oh no. We took the train from Paduka to Minot to Clovis to Greeley. And there were none of these plush locker rooms with saunas and massages and doors on the toilet stalls. No sir. We would carry our gear in a duffel bag. Sometimes, if you were lucky and had a spare nickel, you could wash your jock more than once a month. And none of this television Pay Per View. We had an old drunk beating out the match on a teletype and yelling through a megphone. Fireworks? We didn't get fireworks. We got a sagging stripper holding a sparkler between her... [President Spreadbury snaps his fingers and security drags the octagenarian away.] TD: Let's go on with the ceremony. [Larry Morton takes the stand.] LM: "Angel of the Sun", always the epitome of class and honor, barring that unfortunate time with the Syndicate. [Groan from the Syndicate table] BL: Careful, Larry. Have you learned nothing from Steve Roberts' Big Adventure? LM: There are three jewels which I see in the crowning achievements of Hakiro Matsuoko. The first is he had the honor of being the first EVER IIWF Intercontinental Champion. [Video: Hakiro receiving the customary congradulatory handshake from the President.] LM: The second is he is one of only two, er, three men to ever wear a second belt when he won the Cruiserweight title. [Video: A sweaty Matsuoko holding high the Cruiserweight belt, flames and fireworks explode behind him.] LM: And lastly, after a brief hiatus in Japan, he returned with a new attitude and a new finisher. The spectacular Butsumetsu Drop. [Video: A montage of Hakiro performing the drop. He spins in air for what seems an eternity as separate matches are layered on one another until he crashes back to earth on an immobile Jumpin' Jack.] LM: Let's take a moment to celebrate his illustrious career. [Video: Hakiro performing a frankensteiner on Deathbringer. The gargantuan dead man tumbles over the top rope.] LM: That moment came during the inaugural intercontinental tourney. [Video: Matsuoko and Tiger Claw exchanging martial arts blows inside a cage. The video dissolves to Hakiro missing a towering moonsault off the cage. Tiger Claw covers for the pin.] LM: He lost that title to Tiger Claw in what had to be one of the most amazing bouts of martial artistry in IIWF history. [Video: Sparring with Tiger Claw in a private gym. Casey James interupts and questions the two about a particular strike they had just performed.] LM: Later he was to join with Tiger Claw as a friend in the Syndicate. He had influence on a young Casey James... influence that may have been enough to win James the IIWF World title. [Video: Landing the Butsumetsu drop on "The Enigma" Tazeko Musashi.] LM: When Musashi entered the league, Matsuoko immediatly recognized his childhood sparring partner, and the two renewed a blood feud as to who deserved the right to use the devastating Starsault Press. But for all in ring acheivements, there may be one thing more memorable... his amazing entrance. Let's watch it one last time. [Video: Hakiro enters from under the backstage arch, a cacaphony of oriental drums begins. He wrists and ankles taped, "Angel of the Sun" wears a frighteningly-painted red, black and green Kabuki devil mask. Gold horns and white fans protrude from it. He climbs up the ring apron, then onto the ropes, tightwalking them to their center where he balances perfectly. The ring posts explode in pillars of fire, the lights strobe the frenetic crowd.] LM: Thank you, Hakiro. [Tim Dross steps to the microphone.] TD: Let's take a short break for this message from the IIWF. [Grainy video of a portly baseball player at the plate.] VO: They have camps for people who want to live out their baseball fantasies... [Video of an undersized skater shooting a puck past an aged hockey goalie.] VO: They have camps for sports fans who want to live out their hocky fantasies... ...But what if you don't play that game?... ...What if you don't play that game!... ...What if you have a _real man's_ fantasy? [Caption: "THE IIWF FANTASY WRESTLING CAMP" Video: An executive type performs a top rope press slam on Scott Bloom] VO: You've always wanted to be a wrestler. NOW LIVE IT! [Video: A plumber drops a bulldog on Maurice McArthur.] VO: Learn from the best! [El Super Gecko shows an admiring audience how to climb the turnbuckle] VO: Face legends of the sport! [Heat and Hirise, otherwise known as Towering Inferno, fight a tag match against a pair dressed in McDonald's uniforms.] VO: IIWF FANTASY CAMP: Where The Big Boys Play With You! [Back in the Ritz-Carlton, Sp, [name withheld] and Dross all look wordlessly aghast at each other. Becky breaks the silence.] BL: Well, there is another award winning slogan from marketing. TD: Yes, um, rather. I don't know how to begin to announce our next inductee. BL: I do. Get out of my way, toupee top. [Dross sits begrudgingly.] BL: I remember the first time... the first time... [Becky's mind wanders off] Oh. Um. The first time the Outlaw appeared on Midweek Mayhem. No, he wasn't wrestling. He and Brad Kinder... aaah, Brad... He and Brad Kinder were bashing the Subway Stinko's head in. [The video plays in the background. Kinder and Outlaw double team Subway Psycho outside. Together, they throw him into the ring steps. The tunnel dweller slams into the steps and somersaults over them.] BL: Yeah, that's the one. This was all a precursor to what the Outlaw had in store for all of us. By the time he was finished, he'd joined the IIWF's Horsemen, got Steve Roberts drunk, made Steve Roberts camp out in the desert, joined Casey "Whitebread" James and Man of Steel, and double crossed them, then triple crossed the Horsemen and joined the Posse. Then came the tragic months. Those months that he went to Europe and showed them how a real cowboy wrestles. But he came back, if only for a little while. And he picked up where he left off. An alliance with Brody Thunder and more chaos for Dan Kauffman. At one time he had the whole IIWF choosing up sides for Ring Wars II. It was a sad day for us all when he decided he was done. A sad, sad day... [Becky brushes a tear from her eye and quickly takes her seat, pretending to busy herself with the shrimp cocktail.] TD: All this is true, but so few know about his career previous to signing with the IIWF. He spent time in a federation lost to memory somewhere in upstate New York. There he was every bit the contender that he was here. Even though his every move was dogged by a manager known only as The Prime Minister. But all that is past. Let's look at some video of his stunning time in the IIWF. [VIDEO: Hardin pounds Scott "The Whine" Bloom into the concrete outside the ring. The video fades to Bloom being gingerly stretchered out of the auditorium.] TD: Coronation Clash. Before the bell had even rung, The Outlaw had sent his opponent to the emergency room. It set the tone for things to come. [Video: Outlaw using the Cattle Buster DDT on Dan "Flash" Kauffman. After the first, he picks him up and does it again. Then executes it a third time.] TD: Later that night at the Clash, he faced Dan Kauffman for what was to be the first of many times. He made certain that "Flash" was not going to progress further in the tournament. [Video: Tony Starks screaming and writhing in pain as Hardin winches on a figure four leg lock.] TD: Administering complete and utter defeat for his opponents became an art form for this man. He moved from brawler to technician for this match. Despite the official's demands, he didn't let loose the hold until Mr. Starks had already suffered a debilitating knee injury. [Video: Subway Psycho stands in the ring with Tim Dross. Subway holds high his "People's Champion" award. Outlaw storms the ring with Tiger Claw and others, putting the boot to the People's Champion. Dissolve to a shot of Outlaw outside the ring downing Subway Psycho's lawyer sidekick, Mench, with a Cattle Buster DDT.] TD: I'm starting to see a theme here. [Video: Accompanied by the Ghoulish Cadaver, Hardin tosses the limp form of Dan Kauffman into a coffin. They lock it with triumphant grins.] TD: He and Cadaver. Not quite the Doublemint twins. [Video: Outlaw attacking Deathbringer. The then IIWF champion is distracted, and Otto Verhoevan gets the pin to win the belt.] TD: He had friends... and he had enemies. [Video: He and the Syndicate backstage at "IIWF Saturday Night" brawling with the Players Club.] TD: We didn't know it then, but this was the beginning of the Outlaw passing the torch to the next generation. [Video: Hardin and Brody Thunder execute three cattle buster DDTs on Dynamite and Reyna of the Player's Club.] TD: He came into the fed with three DDTs on Dan Kauffman, he left with three more on Kauffman's friends. [Video: Outlaw standing mid ring, a trickle of blood from the corner of his mouth, holding high the IIWF World Heavyweight Championship Belt. The video fades to black.] TD: At this time we welcome the wrestlers in the audience to share a few words about tonight's honored guests. Since one man worked with both, we'd appreciate it if Brian Lau would honor us, and them, with his thoughts. [Lau makes his way to the front. The is a commotion at one table where the waiter is explaining to Dirt Dog Unique Allah that by law he isn't allowed to serve him any more alchohol.] BL: Ladies and gentlemen, I want to express how proud I am to have been involved with the two men that are being inducted to our Hall of Fame tonight. As far as I'm concerned, J.W. Hardin and Hakiro Matsuoko helped to make the IIWF what it is today, and that's a class act in professional wrestling. If you are to ask anyone who matters in this sport, that is to say anyone in the Syndicate, who the most important participants to ever be in the IIWF were, Matsuoko and Hardin would be the first two names on the list. I've had the pleasure to work with both men, but I want to take the time to talk about Hakiro. As you know, Hakiro held both the Intercontinental title and the Cruiserweight title in the IIWF. Once a bitter rival of Tiger Claw, Hakiro came to me because he was fed up with the lifestyle of pleasing the fans. His style, being a mixture of technical prowess and aerial assault, was as effective as it was impressive to watch. The period of time that Hakiro spent with the Syndicate is one that I look upon with pride, because it was a time when the Syndicate was in a state of order, and that was partially thanks to Hakiro himself. We had a bit of trouble near the end there, but that's okay. Hakiro, I know I don't usually do this, but all that is forgiven. Clean slate. I wish you prosperity in your career in Japan, and if you ever need the help of a manager, you know who to call. I now give the floor to Casey James. [A spotlight swings over to illuminate the champ. Steve Summers rushes over to give him a hand-held mic.] CJ: If there is anyone who I believe is an inspiration to my career, it's Mr. J.W. Hardin. The Outlaw. The Man. This man has guided me through some serious times in the year that I've known him, and every match he had, I watched. When I carry the IIWF World title, and I guarantee that I'll still be holding it after Saturday, I like to think that I'm holding it with as much pride and honour as the Outlaw himself. He was the first IIWF champ, so he set the standard. At that time, I was the puke-inspiring American Hero, but Hardin helped to change all that. It all started with a little six-man tag match where Hardin teamed up with me and Man of Steel against the other three Horsemen. Hardin turned on us, but he turned on the Horsemen as well. I saw that, and at the beginning, I was angry for being such a gullible fool. Then as I watched the tape over and over, I began to feel something strange. I felt the desire to be like the Outlaw. I wanted to hurt people like the Outlaw did. I wanted to become the master tactician that the Outlaw was. I wanted to be a badass... Just like the Outlaw. Well, I am what I am now, and everything I do is in the honour of the Outlaw. So tell me, Hardin... Am I doing a good job? JWH: You're the kind of guy that every boy should want to grow up to be. CJ: Yeah... a real [BLEEP]ing American hero. DS: [aside] And you laughed at me for putting this show on ten second delay. Ha! [Hangman staggers over and grabs the microphone from Casey's hand.] HM: Well, it's been a long year here in the IIWF. We have all gathered here for an event that honors great competitors who have been on the top of our sport. I want to congratulate the "Outlaw" J. W. Hardin and Hakiro Matsuoko on an honor that will be bestowed on only the greatest. Both have had some very intense matches and for the most part inflicted pain on whoever they came into contact with. The fans have been treated to only the finest and most skilled matches whenever these men stepped into the ring. I raise my glass, and hope you will all join me in saluting these great competitors. [There is a hearty cheer from the crowd and much clinking of glasses.] TD: Anyone else? BS:Yeah, over here. [The spotlight swings over, momentarily blinding Billy Shakespeare.] BS: [shielding his eyes] Strangely appropriate, don't you think? And worry not -- especially you, Ronnie Paris -- this chair will stay on the floor like it's intended. VOICE: [from crowd] Shut up and get on with it. Ya want another bash on the head? At least that guy knew when to shut up. BS: I only met Hardin once in combat... [Video of the Battle Royal. Shakespeare performs the Curtain Call backflip, Hardin catches him in midair and spins to body slam him into the mat. He hooks a leg for the pin.] BS: Aye, that was the deed done. While this was the only time we met, the man followed me across two federations, ever present... [Silent video of a younger Tim Dross in front of the IHIW logo doing the Report. The Outlaw breaks in on the broadcast, ranting.] BS: Yea, he was responsible for the firing of Rockin' Ross from station KRGO. A lot of you have said how much you miss him. Others will say what a great competitor he was. I say this, from Coriolanus: "He's a very dog to the community"! But no other could create chaos like he could. My only regret is that I never got a chance to even the score between us. [He sits as Manager Josey Wales signals for the microphone. The light swings over.] WALES: Now y'all just shut up and listen to me. I'm an old time cowboy. Not the type you see in any Hollywood picture, but a real crap eatin', whiskey drinkin', son-of-a-bitch cowboy. There are very few of us left. There are even fewer who I consider myself fortunate to have known. One such man is the "other" Outlaw, J.W. Hardin. Hardin... I ran with your pappy. I know you two had your differences but you were also more alike than you reckon. He was one of the meanest hombres I ever knew... but then again YOU are the meanest man to set foot on this Earth. The times we spent working together and all the times on the ranch you spent with me, Pale, Easy were times I won't soon forget. The door to my ranch is always open and there's a horse always saddled for ya. 'Til then, ride on, cowboy... ride on. TD: Right now, in what I am told will be a touching tribute, current IIWF superstar "Quickstrike" Chris Quigley has a few words to say. [Quigley, who is sticking out like a sore thumb in his jeans a black Reebok t-shirt, amid all the other wrestlers in tuxedos, walks up onto the stage and taps the microphone before speaking.] CQ: The IIWF Hall of Fame. I guess for you two, this is the pinnacle of achievement, although you've both achieved quite a lot. "Angel of the Sun" Hakiro Matsuoko, I don't know a great deal about you, other than the obvious, you were a stand out wrestler. Probably the greatest Oriental wrestler to compete in the IIWF in it's history. It's a shame we never faced in the ring, I would've liked to test my skills against you, and I think I probably would have won. [Mummering is heard from the crowd as Quigley looks around, somewhat satisfied.] CQ: Then of course, "Outlaw" J.W. Hardin. This is your big night, pal! This is the time when you've made it official. "I'm out of wrestling, and Chris Quigley can now become the undisputed greatest of all time." I've got no personal problem with you, Hardin. But Roberts apparently feels that we aren't in the same league. I agree with him, actually. You aren't in my league. I'm a much better wrestler than you'll ever be, and when the inevitable day comes that I make the IIWF Hall of Fame, I hope I have some young star like myself there to speak such kind words about me. Thank you very much! [Quigley steps down from the stage and instead of getting back to his seat, walks clear up the aisleway and out the door, as the people in attendance are shocked. As he walks past Hardin, the big cowboy reaches out and empties his glass of Kesslers on Quigley's boots. "Quickstrike" stands dead still for a moment, then continues his exit. Hardin grabs his crotch and mutters something under his breath which makes the nearby Brenda Hawkings blush.] TD: Thanks, I guess, Chris. BL: He comes and goes just as fast. I understand that is why the ladies call him "Quickstrike". A real man would have stayed and took it like a man. TD: Let's move on to happier topics. Here, to present the Hall-of-fame plaques, the IIWF President: [name withheld]. [There is a murmur as the IIWF President stands up and reaches into his inside pocket, retrieving a sheaf of papers, which he unfolds. Reaching into another pocket, he pulls out his reading spectacles, and dons them. He looks around the room -- but before he is able to open his mouth, there is a commotion from the back of the room, followed by a crash. The camera cuts to show Steve Roberts shoving a waiter to the floor as he barges his way into the room. A number of wrestlers whoop with delight as Roberts, slapping hands with a few of his sympathisers, storms up to the table on the podium.] DS: Steve Roberts, you're suspended. You were told to stay away from this event. SR: [his voice picked up by microphones on the table] Yeah? Yeah? You think you can stop the Soundbite coming out here and paying his respects to the "Outlaw"? Matsuoko, hey, I couldn't care less about him, but the "Outlaw" -- he's the real man of the IIWF. [in a mocking tone] Please, Mr. President, won't you sit down? [The IIWF President arches an eyebrow and stands face to face with Roberts. A silence descends over the gathered wrestlers. Roberts refuses to back down -- and then shoves the President back into his seat. Again, a cheer rises from certain members of the crowd. The President looks shocked, and shakes his head as Roberts grabs a microphone, moving in front of the table and walking around as he speaks. Morton looks mortified, while Dross looks up, alarmed, as if considering whether to stand up and defuse the situation. Becky is grinning like a Cheshire cat. The IIWF President summons over an usher and whispers something in his ear.] SR: Well, well, well. Look at all the people here tonight. So many bitches. So little time. [Bellow of laughter from many of the gathered wrestlers.] A helluva lot of self-congratulations going on here tonight. A lot of guys who want to take credit for making the IIWF the best goddamn wrestling league in the world. Hey, Spreadbury, you just keep patting yourself on the back, buddy, 'cause no one else is gonna do it for you. Let me tell all of you something, let me tell you why the IIWF is great. It ain't about face paint or cartoon gimmicks. It ain't about some 600 pound guy doing a moonsault, or a bunch of whiny prima donnas complaining about booking approval. It ain't about dead guys fighting over spare change or women brawling in the aisle, and it sure ain't about some Canadian knock-off who comes in here with his prissy sunglasses and his little leather jacket saying he's the best thing the Federation has ever seen. [Security is now closing in on Roberts.] The IIWF is about kicking ass! And there ain't no man alive ever kicked ass like J.W. Hardin kicked ass! [Big roar from the audience.] So you can form your little "New Generation" alliance and talk about how you're gonna run the IIWF... but there ain't never gonna be another Outlaw. Never. Hell, half you people here aren't fit to be in the same room with J.W. Hardin, and Quigley... is Chrissie still here? Quigley, not only will you never be man enough to wear J.W. Hardin's belt -- you aren't even fit to lick his boots. Although, from what I hear you'd like to try. [Security has now reached the podium, and is leading Roberts away as a "Shoot, Soundbite! Shoot" cheer starts in the crowd. Roberts yells out over the noise:] Kick his jobber ass, Blackheart! Ain't life grand! [Roberts is hauled out of the hall. He breaks free of the grasp of the security guards as he nears the door, and bows to the applauding wrestlers. He then allows himself to be bundled from the room. The assembled guests settle once more as the IIWF President stands again.] DS: The first letter in the IIWF stands for "International", so it is only appropriate that one of our first inductees is a wrestler from Japan. The "Angel of the Sun" proved conclusively that you don't need to be a big man of body, only big of heart to become a star in this business. Hakiro Matsuoko, welcome to eternity. [He holds up the plaque and Hakiro walks to the podium. He accepts the award and a handshake. Dressed in a suit, hair cut short, he looks rather proud.] HM: When I came to the IIWF last year, I came for one purpose, to fight the best wrestlers in the world. And I am very happy to say that did happen. I was the first man to wear the Intercontintental Title and I lost that belt to, in my opinion, the greatest IC champ of all time, Tiger Claw. I won the Cruiserweight title, defeating Randy Acorn. When I lost that belt, I am happy to say that I was in the ring to two men whom I consider my equals in the wrestling ring, Takezo, my former training partner and my friend Tiger Claw. My fondest memories are quite strange actually, they are when I lost the IC Title to Claw. I always said that I wanted to give the fans what they paid for when I wrestled, and in that match, I gave everything I had and the moonsault off the cage is something I would do over again if I could. My other fond memory of my time here is when I was in The Syndicate. Claw and myself, along with Brian Lau helped to mould Casey James into what he is now, the IIWF World's Heavyweight Champion. I am glad to see that Casey took so much of what I had to teach him to heart, it has served him well, as I think it will serve him well in his match with Chris Quigley this Saturday. I might not have always done what the fans wanted but, I did what I thought was right, and I did it my way. However, my greatest memory of the IIWF is one that is happening right now. I was picked to be one of only two men to be the inaugural inductees in the Hall of Fame. And I am very honored to be in with such a competitor as J.W. Hardin, I think the best wrestler ever in the IIWF. When President Spreadbury contacted me in Tokyo a week or so ago and told me that I was going to be in the IIWF Hall of Fame, I was moved. I am honored to be thought of in such a high regard by the promoters and my contemporaries in wrestling. One goal I have always is to bring honor to my family, and I think, now, with all my titles and this honor... it has happened. So, I thank all of you, especially Claw, Casey, Brian, Takezo and all the bookers, promoters and the executives in the IIWF. I can say nothing else but that I hope to be able to live up to the honor that has been bestowed upon me. Thank you all... [He sits] TD: Thank you, Hakiro. DS: And our second inductee is a man for whom I have a great deal of respect. I freely admit that there have been occasions on which the administration of the IIWF and the "Outlaw" have locked horns -- and it was usually the "Outlaw" who came out on top -- but J.W. Hardin is a true professional. A man of principles -- somewhat questionable principles, but principles all the same -- a man of action, a man of honour... It is no overstatement to say that the "Outlaw" J.W. Hardin set the standard for the IIWF. Whether anybody will live up to the standard that he set is something that only time will tell, but the day Hardin walked out of the Coliseum for the last time was a day when, well, my life got a whole lot quieter, for one. But here is a man who has the almost unanimous respect of his peers, the admiration of a whole generation of fans; a man who can truly claim to be a legend. Ladies and gentlemen, I give you the "Outlaw" J.W. Hardin! [Brian Lau and those seated at the Syndicate's table lead the applause for Hardin, who makes his way to the podium. Dressed in a black tuxedo with a bolo tie and a black formal cowboy hat, the "Outlaw" takes a moment to look out at the dignitaries and guests seated in the ballroom. He steps back from the mic and spits behind the stage, then a half-grin appears on his face.] JWH: Ain't... life... grand! [Brody Thunder's voice booms across the ballroom: "Tell 'em, Hoss!"] When I received some message about bein' in a hall of fame, my first thought was, "What the hell do I need another award fer?" But L'il Becks told me this was the _IIWF_ Hall of Fame and I began to contemplate ever'thin' that I been through in this here fed. Them were some mighty fine times and some mighty fine scuffles. I know I ain't never seen eye-to-eye with a lot of the hombres in this room... mostly 'cause you little [BLEEP]s ain't tall enough to _look_ me in the eye. [grumbling comes from the back of the room] Aw, shut the hell up, Don Antonio! Like I was sayin', we may not have agreed on how to conduct business, but we all had one thing in common: We was wrestlin' in the best damned fed around. [Big pop from every table.] Now I hear momma's boys like Chris Quigley flappin' their gums about ever'thin' they've accomplished in other feds. Well boys... you ain't accomplished ONE DAMNED THING until you've done it in the IIWF! I look out here tonight and see Otto Verhoeven, a IIWF World Heavyweight Champion. I look out and see Deathbringer, a IIWF World Heavyweight Champion. I look out and see the Subway Psycho and even Danny Boy Kauffman, both IIWF World Heavyweight Champions. And I look out and see my amigo Casey James, _THE_ IIWF World Heavyweight Champion. [Casey can be heard yelling, "Damn straight!" from the audience] It's time y'all realized that yer in the company of wrestlin's elite here. This is the best competition you'll ever face in yer careers, and if ya don't believe it... just look at the handful of men who have held the world strap. I've been in other feds wrestlin' tubs o' goo like Spam Marvelous Spam. And I been to Europe slappin' around queer boys who mighta been Poutine's relatives. Yeah, I've busted heads around the world, but they don't put up a fight like they do here in the IIWF. So Quigley, you go on blabberin' about what you did in them other feds, 'cause there ain't nobody listenin' any more except yerself. Ya got yer butt kicked by the Players' Club and ya ran away like a spoiled kid. Me and Thunder had to clean up that mess for ya. [Danny Dynamite must be restrained in the back of the room] Ya got yer butt kicked by the Syndicate and ya tried to change yer look. Well Chrissie, it ain't about where ya run or what ya look like... or even what ya say. It about what... you... do... in... the... ring! Show me the money, Chrissie Boy! I don't think you've got the guts to make it here! And you want a piece of the "Outlaw"? There are plenty of parkin' lots and saloons around. I'm easy to find. To quote a certain kick- ass rookie: Anyone. Anytime. Anywhere. Now I'm tired o' talkin' about you, Quigley. Gawd knows you do enough of that yerself. People are sayin' that this is _my_ night. Well the hell with that! I've had plenty of nights in my life, and _my_ night was 'bout a year ago when I held the IIWF World Heavyweight Championship belt over my head. Tonight is 'bout a career... and I reckon it's a night fer the people who helped me get this far. It ain't easy goin' from campsite to campsite on yer own, but when ya ain't got no family... it's the only life a man knows. Gawd knows I've had my share o' arguments with him, but IIWF President Danny Spreadbury gambled on me and signed me to a contract early on. But it made _him_ a hell of a lot more money than it made me, so I don't know why I'm even thankin' him. [laughter fills the room] Early on in the IIWF, I met up with a coupla boys who could hold their own inside _or_ outside the ring. Yeah, I'm talkin' 'bout Flare and Kinder -- the original "Horsemen". [a smattering of cheers fill the ballroom as the camera zooms in on Flare, Victoria Secret and Kinder seated at a table to the side] Boys, it just wasn't meant to be between us, but we had some HELL-acious times before that Jolly Green Giant feller screwed things up. [cut to a shot of Jack Haley with a sour look on his face] Horseman for Life? Hell, why not? I also found a family in the Posse. Josey Wales was like a father to me... 'cept I never put a bullet in _his_ head. [nervous laughter fills the room, except for Wales, who roars with laughter] Pale and Easy, them boys are hard-ridin', hard-livin' and hard-wrestlin' champions. You'll wear them belts again, amigos. I reckon I need to say a few words 'bout Brian Lau and the Syndicate. [grumbling again fills the room, but Lau simply nods his head toward Hardin] There are only a few men in wrestlin' who deserve to be called geniuses... and there ain't many of _us_ left. Hehe. But Brian is a mastermind and it's been a pleasure to work with him on a few different... shall we say "projects". Casey, Claw, my amigo Brody Thunder, Dark Disciples, y'all are aces in my book. And now Pain, Inc? Whatever. It ain't my business any longer. And I don't know where the hell he is tonight, but I'd like to raise a glass of Kessler's to a feller I've known in this sport longern' any other. Y'all know him as Cadaver, but I just like to call him "Spook Boy". I've fought against him and I've fought with him, and I'll tell you right now that there ain't a tougher SOB this side o' hell. There are a couple o' folks outside the ring I want to thank. First, the best damn male commentator in all of wrestlin', "Soundbite" Steve Roberts. He's the type o' broadcaster that I was a wrestler: whether you like him or not don't matter 'cause he does the job! Truth is too rare from people who talk fer a livin', so thank Gawd fer the "Soundbite". And then there's the best damn female commentator an' ever'thin' else... Miss Becky LaRue. I like to call her the master of the legscissors, if ya know what I mean. [as laughter fills the room, Becky just winks at Hardin] She's a sweetheart, especially for havin' to work with that Barry Morton ever' week. The IIWF Hall of Fame. What does that mean to me? I reckon there ain't been a bigger honor to come this cowboy's way. I walked into this fed with the likes of athletes like Tiger Claw, Casey James and the Subway Psycho aimin' to just bust a few heads. The victims were losers like El Lobo Negro and Mr. Blu Tone. [El Lobo Negro yells something in Spanish from the back of the room] Yeah? Su mama es un perro, Paco! Damn wetback. Who the hell let him in tonight? Where was I? Oh yeah, this fed has seen some of the best wrestlers the world has had to offer, and I'm proud to share the spotlight tonight with Hakiro Matsuoko, mostly because he never beat me. In fact, only _two_ wrestlers ever did beat me in the IIWF. And I'm undefeated against Dan Kauffman -- that's _two_ victories, Quigley. So I reckon Matsuoko and me are goin' out on top. We've been through the best wrestlers the best fed could throw as us. We've set the watermark, boys. Now I'm challengin' y'all to reach it and join us in the IIWF Hall of Fame. Anything less... is second best. Gracias... and adios! [He sits to a standing ovation, taking a moment to tip his hat to the crowd.] TD: Ladies and Gentlemen, the Outlaw. As much as I hate to say goodbye, this concludes tonight's festivities... BL: Not for me! I've got Kinder _and_ the Outlaw together again! TD: So for myself, President Spreadbury, the IIWF staff, Larry and Becky.. Goodnight and may all your dreams of champions come true. BL: In two hours they will! [The credits begin to roll. President Spreadbury jumps to the microphone.] DS: Don't forget the IIWF Hall of Fame is open every day of the year! Only $6.00 admission, $5.00 for the children. See memorabilia! You might even meet a wrestler! Visit the gift shop stocked with plenty of your favorite merchandise. Just call... [His words are drowned out by the IIWF theme music. Their tension to a breaking point, Brody Thunder and Mad Dog Watkins argue over who gets the last eclair. The terrified waiter runs away, dropping the disputed dessert to the floor.
