A seven-nights-a-week Usenet booking promotion that ran its own Hall of Fame and Golden Grapple Awards, crowned five championships in fifty-two recorded reigns across one hundred and eight held shows, and spent one February defending its own name against a second fed that tried to claim the same acronym. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The International Internet Wrestling Federation traces its first broadcast to 7 May 1996, a date it later fixed as its own birthday; the operator who actually built it had posted the first recruitment call two days earlier, out of what its own newsdesk called IIWF Towers in Portland, Oregon. It was booked, not simulated, deciding matches on roleplay quality under a philosophy its own recruitment posts repeated outright: “good writing = good push.” Every wrestler entered through its own written application form — name, gimmick, moveset, finisher — original characters from the ground up.

By early 1997 it was running seven nights a week, its own weekly Hotline column carrying backstage rumor and storyline news read by on-air hosts Tim Dross and “Soundbite” Steve Roberts, and planning its first Hall of Fame around that same May anniversary alongside a year-end honors broadcast, the Golden Grapple Awards.

That February it fought off a challenge to its own name: a newcomer announced a second league under the identical acronym, and the promotion's own wrestlers and fans answered in public, citing its own newsgroup Fed of the Year award and established roster as reason enough to ask the newcomer to pick a different name. The challenger withdrew within a week: “I've decide to scrap the league.”

Five championships changed hands across dozens of recorded reigns and pay-per-views — the Ring Wars series, the Coronation Clash tournament, Snow Brawl among them — and a talent-exchange treaty with a fellow federation made its own Intercontinental Champion the first wrestler in its history to hold a title from each side of the arrangement at once.

Its own record ends without a formal closing notice. On 1 August 1998 it billed a thirty-man Eternal Rumble, “IIWF Forever,” to decide its World Heavyweight Championship “FOREVER”; a fan wrote afterward, “IIWF is gonna be missed. Thanks for the memories.” A separate document outside its own record still bills a wrestler as reigning champion months later, so exactly when it stopped running is a question its surviving record leaves open rather than answers.
