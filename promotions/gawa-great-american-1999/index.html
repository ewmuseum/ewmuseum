Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Great American Wrestling Association opened, by its own owner's account, “May 1st, for the 3rd time,” and its founding handbook (1 June 1999) frames that owner's own career as the federation's origin story: six years wrestling, a start and quick retirement in a fed called EWO, then a run in the Awesome Internet Wrestling Federation collecting its Canadian, Intercontinental, United States, Television, Tag Team and Hardcore titles before a car crash and, later, a beating that left him unable to wrestle at all — after which, in his own words, “he decided that he can't WRESTLE and stay in great shape, so he decided to hit the ring as a President & Owner.” It billed itself modestly against that backdrop: “we aren't THE greatest federation of all-time… but to my members, I provide and offer everything I can.”

It ran on four championships — the All American Title, the Extremely Hardcore Title, the European Title, and the United States Title — across three cards a week (Sunday Nite Fight as its main broadcast, plus the Wednesday Super-Stars and Thursday House Show cards) and monthly pay-per-views it called WrestleKrazy and StarrCadia.

Its first pay-per-view, WrestleKrazy II (30 May 1999, from Madison Square Garden), crowned Slasher a new United States Champion in a ten-man battle royal and closed with a no-disqualification title-vs- title main event between All American Champion Saber and Extremely Hardcore Champion Striker Phrost that ended in a double pin, switching both belts at once: “your NEW Extremely Hardcore Champion… SABER! And your NEW GAWA All American Champion, STRIKER PHROST!” The same card's other title match, a three-way for the European Championship, ended the opposite way, with a no-contest that left Enigma the new champion regardless. A mid-card match between President Xavier and Vice President Roberts turned on an in-ring reversal: Xavier pinned Roberts clean, only for Roberts to produce a document at ringside that flipped the decision in his own favor.

Days later, Sunday Nite Fight (6 June 1999) opened with a much-hyped “double debut” between former All American Champion The Dream and Chris Knight that ran to a time-limit draw, and closed with Enigma retaining the European Championship by double disqualification and Slasher fighting off a chair-shot interference attempt to retain the United States Title.

No further card survives past that 6 June broadcast.
