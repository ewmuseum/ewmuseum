A farm league that published its own supercard calendar a year ahead, retired both its belts in May 2004, brought them back sixteen months later in a thirty-one man Rebirth match, and left behind a championship lineage complete to the day. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Foundation of Brutality crowned its first two champions on the same night, 6 December 2003, and wrote both of them down: Jamie Noble beat Kraven in a tournament final for the Heavyweight Championship, and Ozena beat Tony “The Marvel” Mason for the Wrath Title. It kept writing them down for eighteen months after that, to the day, and worked out afterwards how long each man had held on.

It was a farm league, and it booked like one. Two of its four 2004 Heavyweight reigns ended in a vacancy rather than a loss, both because the champion had been called up to Revenge; a third belt changed hands the same way. Then everything stopped on 25 May 2004, and for sixteen months there was nothing.

What came back in the autumn of 2005 called itself Rebirth. Thirty-one men went into a battle royal for a Heavyweight Championship the promotion had reinstated that night and Christopher Daniels came out of it; a four-team tournament crowned new tag champions in the same building. The Wrath Title, which its own ledger by then listed as a retired belt whose banner nobody could find, came back on 8 December with a division you had to beat a member of to enter, and rules read out on camera off a yellow sheet of paper.

It ran Sacrifice every week out of The Igloo in Pittsburgh and The Pyramid in Memphis, published a supercard calendar a year ahead — Genesis, Full Force, Farmageddon, Exile, Rebirth, Decimation — and told its wrestlers there would be no general manager looking over them: come out, wrestle, and win. It ran an Eddy Guerrero Memorial Cup in the weeks after the real wrestler died.

How it ended isn't in the surviving record. The last card held is the Sacrifice of 19 November 2005; a Decimation thread was still running in December and a Genesis card was open for January. What survives whole is the lineage: three championships, fourteen reigns, every one of them with the number of days beside it.

Storylines

Seven of the Foundation of Brutality's own nights survive — five show scripts, one narrative recap and one hour of a five-hour supercard — alongside the booking sheet its roleplay forum and results forum were showing on 15 December 2005. The arcs below are recapped from those, and stop where they stop.

The white paper Written by AI, 2026

Chris Jericho and Carlito spent the autumn of 2005 guarding a folded sheet of paper and refusing to say what was on it. On 19 November they announced they would read it out once both of them had won — “this small slip of paper will change the entire way this Foundation of Brutality is run” — and then Carlito had a dizzy spell, dropped a set of steel steps on himself, was counted out along with Ric Flair and left the building in an ambulance. Jericho won his match alone, got as far as “we were holding, none other than…the rights—”, and the arena went black. When the lights came back Blood were driving away in their Cadillac with the paper, and Jericho and Todd were covered in a red substance Todd identified, after tasting it, as marinara sauce. The fed's own booking sheet carries the rematch — Chris Jericho, Todd and Carlito against Blood, stipulation “Right to the White Paper” — on the Decimation card of 8 December. No results page for it was ever captured, and the contents of the paper are not in the surviving record.

A Cannon's past, exposed Written by AI, 2026

The 19 November show is built around five video packages narrated by a voice the audience cannot see, each one replaying a different piece of Matt Stanton's career and reading it back to him as a record of quitting: his TPG debut in April 2004, carried into the ring by his manager for a count-out win; his loss to John Bradshaw Layfield on Revenge that July; his Pure Title loss to Tex Graham on Underground that September; his Pure Title win over Lord Greatest the following January, which the voice calls “incredibly short and unimpressive” because he won it from a placeholder and quit the company inside a week; and the Loose Cannons, the tag team the voice blames for holding Alex Hoffman back. Stanton lost the main event to Christopher Daniels in eighteen and a half minutes, and Hoffman — the voice — came out of the crowd and beat him bloody. He had Stanton up for the F-5 when AJ Styles arrived: “So I called up an old friend. I was ready, Alex.” The fed's own booking sheet carries Hoffman against Stanton in a grudge match whose thread was still live on 14 December, and a four-way for the Heavyweight Title with all three of them in it.

Rules on a yellow sheet of paper Written by AI, 2026

The Foundation of Brutality's second belt had been dead since May 2004 and its own lineage listed it as “[Retired Belt, Banner Not Available.]” On 13 October Carlito wandered into the departing Headmaster's office, found a yellow sheet of paper on the desk and read it to camera: a memo dated 10 October over the signature Allan Snow, reinstating the Wrath Championship as a no-rules belt, capping the Wrath Division at ten wrestlers, and making entry to it a match you had to win against somebody already in it. The division filled up over the next six weeks — the fed marks its members with a “(w)” after their names on its own booking sheet — and by 19 November the road to the belt ran through three matches in a row: Gangsta Thunda beat Randy Orton for the right to face Baxter Baxter, whose winner would pick his own Decimation opponent. Dr. Greenwood ended his own segment that night by sending everyone who had interrupted it to the Wrath Division. The lineage gives the ending: Baxter Baxter beat Gene Snitsky for the reinstated title on 8 December 2005, nineteen months after the belt had last changed hands.

Who actually runs this place Written by AI, 2026

In August the Acolytes cornered Headmaster Al twice in one corridor demanding to know “who's REALLY in control of your domain” after he gave the Sonic Death Monkeys the night off; they lost their qualifying match anyway, with the Monkeys not even in the building. In October he interrogated the whole Marcello family one at a time over a kidnapping and got his answer from the one who thought he had said nothing. A week later he left, in verse, and Sean O'Haire came out to name his successor: “And that man is…NOBODY!” From that night the promotion had no authority figure at all, which Christopher Daniels noticed immediately — “there is no longer a general manager around here. The inmates run the asylum now. And I'm at the top of the food chain” — and used to book his own title defences.

The kidnapping of Desmond Butler Written by AI, 2026

Two brothers who dusted the barricades on their way to the ring won a four-team tournament at Rebirth and became FoB Tag Team Champions. Between shows one of them was kidnapped and the other beaten, and on 6 October the Headmaster spent all six of the night's segments questioning the Marcellos about it — the uncle, the cousin, the father, and finally the son, who told him “Nothing” and then told his family every single thing he had not said, including that it was Trinity who put the hit out. They opened the door to leave and the Headmaster was standing in it. A week later The Butlers lost the belts to the Detroit Street Thugs without Jeffery ever getting tagged in, and neither brother appears in the fed's record again.

An Eddy Guerrero Memorial Cup Written by AI, 2026

Eddy Guerrero died on 13 November 2005. Six days later the Foundation of Brutality opened its show with eleven bell tolls and a video package cut to Creed, running the real wrestler's real career and then his career in this fed — entering PWE alongside Matt Hardy, winning the GWF Heavyweight Championship back off him, a cage match with Ryan Styles, a frog splash on Nicholas Pryde the previous week — and then went straight into a radio segment in which Chris Jericho said the condolences out loud and Carlito read the card. In the weeks that followed the fed ran an Eddy Guerrero Memorial Cup on its own roleplay forum, in numbered rounds, alongside the ordinary weekly matches: Phil Lao against Shocker, Vaughn Archer against Billy Bingham, Teddy DiBiase against Xavier Rose, Goldust against Rick Robinson, Bruno Santorini against Amar Ojes, a three-way for one man's Round 2 place, Big Show against Devin Muse, and a Round 2 tie between Bruno Santorini and Teddy DiBiase whose thread, last posted on 14 December 2005, is the last thing the Foundation of Brutality's own roleplay forum ever carried. No round of it has a surviving result.
