Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

A booked-by-email attitude-era promotion run out of its founder's spare time. Its flagship was Saturday Night Blood Bath, recapped every Sunday, backed by two more weekly shows. Its PPV order is confirmed — King of the Ring, In Your Computer, Halloween Hell (1 November 1998), and a Christmas Clash deliberately counter-programmed against WCW's real-world Starrcade '98. It ran a documented kayfabe ring death — "the late Carnage," the fed's first fatality angle — roughly a year before the GIWF would try the same trick. And it originated the "In Your Computer" pay-per-view brand outright: the next fed inherited it whole rather than invented it. Its own house style ran juvenile shock-comedy bits alongside real-geography rivalry angles — a "King of Minnesota" feud between Curt Raines and Jazon Parzon chief among them.

How the IWF itself ended isn't in the surviving record — no closure post, no final card, no last word from its own founder. The trail just runs out sometime in 1999, and the same hands were already regrouping a few miles down the road under a new banner. What survives is what a later "Museum" folder on the promoter's own drive chose to carry forward, plus the 27 handler-written bios and the application form that started the line's own character-creation tradition.

The site even preserved its own character-creation application — a 250-point split across Worker, Push, Stamina, and Charisma.

The IWF is where several of the line's longest-running names first appear. The handler behind Coma (kayfabe real name Joseph Rosso — a character he carried into the GIWF as Commissioner) went on to found the GIWF himself. The IWF also introduced Bob "Monkey" Charlie of the Bob World Order and Lew Spectre, both names that recur for years across the line's later chapters.

Storylines

The Carnage angle Written by AI, 2026

The IWF staged its own first kayfabe ring death: Carnage, killed in storyline at Halloween Hell 1998. The moment itself isn't in the surviving record — the recovered Halloween Hell recap never mentions him by name — only Christmas Clash's own retrospective line survives: "Following the war at Halloween Hell which saw the IWF's first ring fatality," Carnage's own mentor, Crow, went one-on-one with self-proclaimed "Hardcore Champion" Hades for a belt announced to become a legitimate IWF championship as of that night on. The two "obliterated" a whole stock of chairs getting there. The ledger and the show's own blow-by-blow disagree on who actually won — see the Hardcore Championship's own page for both readings, recorded side by side rather than resolved. This is the lineage's first death-and-rebirth beat, roughly a year ahead of the GIWF's Gabriel and the UWF's Ben Jericho.

Affiliations

XEWF

Invaded IWF (1998–99) (as of 1998)
The IWF’s own roster lists “the invading XEWF faction” among its named stables, sourced from the promoter’s own surviving files. Separately, Coma’s own 1998 IWF bio, written by the handler who played him, says he came to the IWF “by way of a folded promotion called XEWF” — and a Slammy acceptance speech places XEWF chronologically after “the IWF (different one)” in the same handler’s own fed history. These are two related but not fully reconciled threads — an in-fiction invading stable named XEWF, and a real prior fed some handlers came from — presented here as found, without forcing them into one story.
