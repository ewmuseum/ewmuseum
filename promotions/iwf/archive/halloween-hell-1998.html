Halloween Hell

IWF PPV Recap: Halloween Hell Sunday 1st November 1998 - pg1 Welcome to new look IWF recap, the first recap of an IWF PPV event. It has to be said that it was quite an event too. We opened the show with Travis Atkinson and David Jones reminding us of what was to come. An ominous remote trip to the scene of the buried Alive match-up and straight into the action. First match-up was the four corners tag decider featuring Team X-treme, The Crash Team, The Government and the Mexican Maurauders. The Crash Teams Ashley Ward kicked off proceedings against The Governments FBI. After a breif exchange it was quick tags all around with neither team making that much of an impact. As we passed the seven minute mark the Big 400 pounder FBI hit the 747 on Rico Rodriguez and the first elimination was secured. No sooner had 'Red Hot' Neil Scott stepped up to the plate then he stepped back down and took an early shower. Suffering a frog splash at the hands of Macho 'Red Hot' had to be a little embarrased after promising so much in the pre-match build up. Left left with Team X-treme facing up to The Government who we're told have some history between them. TX were dwarfed by their much larger opponents and for quite a while took a pastinbg for their troubles. Macho in particular sufferred and in the end it was all too much and The Government took the match to earn themselves a shot at the Tag-Straps. The evenings next offering saw the eagerly anticipated Lightweight title decider between current champion Todd 'Ratboy' Prentiss and ETW member Tommy Gunn. The pre-match hype promised a lot and the match had a lot to live up to. It started up at a furious pace with a couple of near falls for both guys. As the match drew on the pace slowed which was a good thing for the eventual winner who would have to defend the belt agianst former World Champion MaK later that night. Gunn showed a lot but in the end the class of Prentiss showed through after hitting the Rat Trap for the 1-2-3. Prentiss has held the belt for a long time now and with the lightweight roster looking a little thin its difficult to see who will take the strap from him. Following the championship match fans were treated to yet another title fight in the form of the Carribean Strap match between Intercontinental champion Cerberus and challenger Bob "Monkey" Charlie. The bout was quite a lengthy affair and as with all strap matches quite brutal aswell. In what was possibly an upset we saw Youngbloods leader Bob Charlie take the title from the recently crowned Cerberus. There was a mixed reaction from the crowd and I have to believe that unless Charlie pops up with one or two more interviews then this reign is going to be a short one. No gimmicks in the next match and quite how it made its way onto a PPV card is beyond me. In order to decide the king of the jobbers we saw Superstar who has lost his last four matches facing Archangel who has lost his last eight!!! It was won by Superstar owing to outside interference from the Joker. The crowd cheered although probably didn't give a damn. In a re-match from last weeks Saturday night Bloodbath we saw IWF North American Tag Champs Team X-treme facing off against Armageddon in a first blood No Holds barred match-up. As expected proceedings began at a lightening pace with quick tags all round. In fact the only suprising thing about this match was that it took over three minutes before the first foreign object was introduced. We witnessed Pheonix introducing Seek to yet another of those IWF Fire Extinguishers. The twist here ... a freshly painted fire extinguisher, red of course. With a crimson stain of red paint across his head it was only a matter of time before Tim White called a hault, which is exactly what happened. TX still the champs, Armageddon robbed of the straps. If Tim White is really so dumb that he can't tell paint from blood I hope this guy is nowhere near my house next time I decorate!!! As the gimmick loaded PPV wore on Wan Chun Hai battled with Sik in the winner gets a hoe match death match. Oh you can bet Kama Mustapha's attourneys are all over this already. Not a bad match but one which will be remembered for this quote from Travis Atkinson: He has the Flaming Metal Barbed Wire Baseball Bat with small 10,000 Volt Lightbulbs wrapped around it and a mix of thumbtacks and nails stuck to it!!!! David: What A Weapon!!!!!!!!!! OOH!!!!!!! Chung has been nailed with it!!!!!! Oh My God!!!! Hmmm, who thinks these things up? The evenings brutality continued and the eventual winner was Wan Chun Hai who left the arena with a little extra baggage if you know what I mean. Next offering saw Executioner & Tenzan of the Warrior Clan squaring up against Clearance. There wasn't a whole lot of interest in this one although there was a shock result with Clearance taking the pin. We're told by Travis Atkinson how big an upset this was with Blarney pinning the #1 contender for the Title Tenzan (?) when did that happen? The IWF front office is really doing a lousy job of informing the press of their rulings as of late. Anyway the night continues .... [PART TWO] IWF PPV Recap: Halloween Hell Sunday 1st November 1998 - pg2 At the midway point of the Card we saw the fifth metting between fierce rivals Coma and John Constantine. Both guys had been particularly verbal before the match and with the scores level at two wins each it was guaranteed that both combatants were gonna go hell for leather for the win in this casket match. In a series of high impact moves from both wrestlers early on their intentions were made clear. Constantine was looking for the London Bridge and similarly Coma was looking for the Flatliner. The first to hit the finisher was Constantine but still feeling the effects from an earlier Coma onslaught was unable to make it to the casket. Unbelievably the crowd were not into this match at all, chants of boring could be heard and many fans saw this as the time to get themselves a hot dog or two!! All can say is that this match must have come over better on TV than live as I thoroughly enjoyed it from start to finish. After suffering no less than three London Bridges Coma was able to hook on the Flatliner dead center of the ring, as the clocked ticked by Constantines grip on conciousness began to falter and after two minutes the North American Champ decided the time was right to try and end the match. Still fighting, Constantine was still able to get to his feet only to be knocked down and placed in the casket. End result: Coma retains the title and as an added twist John Constantine is sent back to England in the casket. In between time the fans had some sort of concert going on, it ain't wretling so I'm not writing about it. As the music died down we moved straight into the fourth championship match of the evening. The undefeated Champions The Warrior Clan fought it out with FX's Hellraizers. With the fan interest a little higher Akira and Dark Beast opened things up. Many people including me expected this one to be a bit of a squash match, the Warrior Clan have been on a roll since beating the Crash Team for the titles and have had about seven sucessful title defences since. Hellraisers, whilst not exactly being jobbers haven't anything like the sucess of the Clan. Well, at an IWF PPV the odd does happen and there could have few people who expected Hellraizers to pull off the victory, but thats exactly what they did when Mad Bull hit Akira with a flying axehandle. The next offering was the eagerly awaited 'King of Minnesota' match between former Intercontinental Champion Curt Raines and former World Champion Jazon Parzon. The build up for this one has been BIG. Both guys have made no bones about their feelings for the other and this could quite easily have shared main event stauts with Reaper -v- Williams. In the IWF additions poll both guys took 50% of the vote and the crowd were equally divided. Jesse Ventura took time out of his busy schedule to be guest ring announcer and introduced Jazon and manager Angie Winchester followed by The Raineman: Curt Raines. With feelings running as high as they have been neither guy hung back with Raines in particular being more agressive, an early powerbomb gave a sign of things to come. Parzon hasn't been the most popular of IWF superstars but deserves respect for his resilience, not only did he take another powerbomb but kicked out of the Rainedrop DDT aswell. After a back and forth period of the match things appeared to be going Parzons way when Earl Hebner was distracted and Angie Winchester hit the ring brandishing a high heeled shoe. Reaching back Winchester paused allowing Raines to move out of the way and brought the shoe down onto Parzons temple, with the ref back in the game it was an easy 1-2-3 for the new King of Minnesota: Curt Raines. For those of you that think the Winchester thing was a mistake, it wasn't, she left the ring on Raines' arm leaving Parzon defeated and furious. The main event .... World Heavyweight Champion The Grim Reaper taking on the IWF's franchise player, two time Champion Rob 'The Legend' Williams in a buried alive match. There was a video montage depicting their rivalry but that'd take a page in itself to recap so thats not gonna happen. The Legend got a huge pop from the crowd as he arrived by helicopter in stark contrast to the chorus of boos aimed at the champ. The match was very solid and you'd think more than enough to entertain even the most cynical of fans ... not the IWF fans apparently that yet again chanted 'boring'. As the punishment flowed from both sides and after the Reaper made good use of a baseball bat on Williams' head you'd think the end was near. Not with the Legend who fought on and took yet more punishment having his face busted wide open on the concrete floor. Former champ Edge marked his return to the IWF with a run in on Williams and things started to get out of hand. Williams' manager Ripp hit the Reaper with a chair shot and still the two battled on. Remaining at ringside Edge again made his presence felt by hitting Williams from behind again. In the end the deck was stacked against the Legend by too much and the Reaper took the win dropping Williams into the burial plot and sealing the casket lid shut. With the rest of the Warrior Clan and Edge helping to fill the plot things looked bad for the Legend who has made no secret of the fact that he has few friends in the locker room. As the night drew to a close the final suprise of the evening came in the form of Williams saviours. Former World & Intercontinental Champion El Dorado made his return accompanied by a mystery man. As they fought it out with the Clan the screen faded to black leaving us all waiting for Tuesday night Riot Squad to find out what happened. Whether the Golden One is planning on a full return to IWF action is anyones guess but one thing is certain and thats that Williams needs some help in his feud with the Warrior Clan and Edge. Overall it was a good PPV with its fair share of title changes and new feuds. It does also leave a few questions to be answered: 1) With MaK announcing his retirement from the Lightweight Division - where does it go? There doesn't look to be anyone able to take out Prentiss at the moment and perhaps a Junior Heavyweight blet would produce a litle more interst. 2) How many more times will Coma and Constantine fight? 3) Will the Golden One return to full time action in the IWF? I guess we'll have to wait and see. Till next time. These pages are not affiliated with, sponsored or supported by any pro-wrestling organization.
