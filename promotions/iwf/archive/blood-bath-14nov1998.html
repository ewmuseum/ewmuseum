Saturday Night Blood Bath — 14 November 1998

14 Nov 1998
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
IWF Bloodbath Recap Sunday 15th November 1998 - pg1 Welcome to another edition of Bloodbath recap. Having had a week off for reasons unknown we're all back and ready to go. Bloodbaths Card was a little shorter than usual this week so I shouldn't be boring you all for too long. This saturday night marked the departure of Jazon Parzon, Macho and Jorgy from the IWF along with Vice President Travis Atkinson. Anyone who wants to can send him their good wishes for the future can do so here. Naturally with Travis' departure President Rob Latulippe will be looking for some help with the cards and if your interested you can contact him here. Well enough with the plugs and on with the card... First up to plate was the Latest addition to the Youngbloods stable Lew 'Strictly Business' Spectre facing one time TX member Phoenix. It should have been a sign of things to come when Pheonix immediately left the ring and returned with a chair which was put to good use as a springboard for an early lariat. As the match progressed Lew Spectre succeeded in raising the tempo of the match hitting some spectacular high impcat moves. To his credit, cheered on by the partizan crowd Phoenix took everything and kept on coming. The contest concluded somewhat prematurely just as it was heating up, Pheonix was on something of a roll having just hit Spectre with a three move combination of Lariat, Jumping DDT and Splash. Seeing that his guy was in trouble Richard Vaughn reached through the ropes and tripped Pheonix. Unfortunately for Spectre it happened in full view of James Beard who had no hesitation in calling for the DQ. The next offering of the evening saw the Lightweight Franchise Todd 'Ratboy' Prentiss squaring up against the Floridian Sensation and IWF newcomer Blaster. Prentiss seems to have undergone a personality change since the return of Psycho to the IWF's lightweight ranks and at the commencement of the match you would be forgiven for thinking that Prentiss' mind was elsewhere. Putting it bluntly it was all Blaster!! The new guy really took it straight to the champ and for the best part of two minutes Prentiss could nothing other than block and try and live out the storm. Showing the wrestling mind that has kept him as champ for so long Prentiss was able to turn the tide with a jumping DDT and slow things down and gain a little equlibrium by going straight for the abdominal stretch, the full nelson and the Cobra Clutch. After the calm the storm whipped up again and a handspring moonsault almost got Blaster the pin. Things went Blasters way for the couple of exchanges before they were evened up again. I have to believe that this was the point where things went wrong for Blaster. I have to believe that the match was there for the taking but wanting to inflict maximum damage he brought a chair and baseball bat into the ring. After Prentiss was nailed with the bat it looked like the end of the Longest Title Reign in IWF history but the silent Turner Brown showed his worth not once but twice by placing Prentiss' foot on the ropes to make the save. Prentiss was up after his rest and like a man on fire ... he really took the match to Blaster and after a near fall the Interference of Turner Brown was enough to set-up blaster for the Rat Trap and the 1-2-3 for Prentiss, oddly enough the crowd cheered. At the conclusion of the match it appeared that Prentiss' fears about Psycho were well founded. Running to ringside Psycho made good use of the Baseball bat and broke it in half over the Ratboys head. The crowd were stunned to see Prentiss, dripping in blood get back to his feet and go after Psycho. As soon as Psycho chalks up a few wins in the IWF I can't wait to see these two get together. The first of the evenings tag matches saw the IWF's two largest teams in Towers of Power and The Government. In all honesty I couldn't get into this match and neither could the crowd who chanted for the referee most of the match. The Government turned out the eventual winners though how they have such a good record is beyond me considering I haven't heard one interview from them since they arrived in the IWF. The second tag match of the night saw the Newly Formed Hounds of Hell consisting of World Champion, The Grim Reaper and his silent friend Executioner agianst two time former champs The Assasin: Ashley Ward and 'Red Hot' Neil Scott. Both teams set off at a blistering pace with Ward and The Executioner all going for the early pins. The crowd again weren't into it again which is a shame as there was a lot of good action. The Hounds of Hell had the better of the majority of the exchanges and it wasn't a hughe suprise when they eventually took the win courtesy of the beheading on Ward by the Executioner. It seems that ever since they dropped the titles to TX that the Crash Team is lacking in motivation, Ashley Ward in particular looked as though he just couldn't get going in this match and from what I've heard there were one or two expletives aimed at The Assasin in the lockeroom after this match. By far more interesting was the conclusion of the match which saw IGF member MaK climbing into the ring and attacking the Grim Reaper. Given their history the last person you'd expect to save the Reaper would be Rob Williams but thats exactly who did. Willams' hero roll was only short lived as IGF founder Edge made his way ringside and hit Williams with a vicious chair shot. It looks like IGF are planning to make their mark on the IWF in a big way ... These pages are not affiliated with, sponsored or supported by any pro-wrestling organization. [PART TWO] IWF Bloodbath Recap Sunday 15th November 1998 - pg2 The card drew on with the nights kick it or quit match with Yoshirio Tenzan facing up against MaK, the stipulation being that unless MaK pulls out the win he is out of the IWF. It started off all MaK, hitting Tenzan with everything he had. As usual the crowd really gave Tenazn a hard time and despite a mini revival on his part MaK was like a man possessed and really took things to Tenzan on the outside. Tenzan tried to slow things down with a few submission moves. Things spun around in his favour for a while with some good moves but MaK dug deep and somewhat suprisingly one of his greatest performances was met with disdain with the crowd who told him to "go home". Oddly enough the match was won by a simple elbow drop and MaK retains his employment and earns himself a title shot. A good solid match-up was the next. Three of the IWF's main eventers in the same ring. Five time and current North American Champion Coma met up with Former World Champ Rob Williams and former Intercontinental Champ Bob Charlie. The match started up with what has been an eagerly anticipated pairing for a long time. Williams v. Coma. It was back and forth to begin with, Williams took the upper the upperhand only for Coma to drag it back. After a short lariat the N.A Champ went for the pin but couldn't really have expected the Legend to stay down for the three on the back of that. Williams took a quick tag out to Charlie and Coma was sent straight into the guard rail. Charlie mounted some serious offense and then mysteriously tagged out to Williams leaving Coma to take another beating. The quick tag between Charlie and Williams continued and it wasn't until the eight minute mark that the N A Champ was able to earn himself some respite. The exchanges between Charlie and Williams were roughly 50/50 and there can be no doubting that about the onlt face to exist in the IWF at the moment is Rob Williams who really got the crowd going. It was a long match with two much to go into here, needless to say that after spending so long in the ring Coma was the first to go at the seventeen minute mark. With Coma out of there Williams stepped up a gear and it was only a further 3 minutes before Charlie succumbed to the Inverted Piledriver and Williams took a huge pop from the crowd before being beaten down by IGF to add a little fuel to their fuedal fires. In the main event of the evening Jazon Parzon defeated Curt Raines in his farewell match ... who didn't see that coming!!! Not that the recaps are usually anything to write home about but I know this one was poor, I'm ill and not really up for it. Final thought: Wrestlers who try to bribe the prez with 'quit' threats (you know who you are) ought to be fired, not given a shot. Till next week, see you then.
