IWF PPV Recap: Christmas Clash Sunday 27th December 1998 - pg1 Welcome to another of the IWF's PPV recaps. Christmas Clash was thrown right in against WCW's Starrcade '98 and was probably the most hyped up IWF event of all time. Did it live up to that hype? ... You decide. Kick off with President Latulippe informing us that the IWF has just matched the record attendance for the Pontiac Silverdome set by the WWF with Wrestlemania II. Ever the businessman his cohort Larry gets straight on with it and calls the first match-up, 'Real Deal' Ozzy Douglas facing Youngbloods newcomer Lew Spectre in a falls count anywhere match. Fans of the IWF will know that there has been a reasonable amount of heat between these two with Spectre in particular resorting to sneak attacks to add feul to the fire. Intentions were made clear from the outset when Douglas brought in a keyboard and laid Spectre up the side of the head with it. Douglas went for the high impact power moves whilst Spectre stuck to his more technical gameplan. Certainly as far as Spectre was concerned this paid off. Off the canvas having been on the wrong end of a Piledriver he applied the figure four sleeper which, oddly enough saw the match end. Douglas caught in the middle of the ring with nowhere to go taps to give the big V to Spectre, the crowd were not happy. Following the war at Halloween Hell which saw the IWF's first ring fatality, self proclaimed 'Hardcore Champion' Hades went one on one with the late Carnages mentor, Crow. Latulippe announces that the Hardcore title becomes a legit title in the IWF after Christmas Clash leaving everything to play for in this one. It was a typically aggressive match from both guys. The IWF better have had a good buy rate for this one because they'll need a new stock of chairs after the amount that were obliterated in this match. With Hades very quiet in the build up to this one Crow always had to be the favourite and in the 20th minute and at the second time of asking Crow hit the crows nest sending Hades through a table and to the mat ... new Hardcore Champion. The first Tag-match of the night saw Clearance face off against relative newcomers the Pyschotic Clownz. Jack Blarny showed us all that the spirit of Christmas ain't dead by taking a mic and wishing all the homeless children of the world a merry christamas and dedicating the match to them. A nice gesture, but a little hollow when your about to beat someones brains out dont you think? Fairly even match at the opening with both teams using their heads and getting in and out quickly. Apparently the Clownz have had quite a distinguished past, that being the case you can bet that their current standing in the IWF tag ranks dosn't sit too well. Both teams were aggessive in offence but less than intelligent in defense which led to an all round entertaining match-up, not that the fans agreed, but what the hell do they know anyway, stupid marks. Anyway, Doc Revenge of the Clownz looked pretty impressive but it was nonetheless Da Loon that picked up the win for them. Using a foreign object he hit O'Shady and used the opportunity to apply the 'Ultimate Revenge' and gain the 1-2-3. You can bet that that ain't gonna sit too well with Clearance. Homeless Children around the world sobbed. Post match, the current Tag Champs, The Hellraisers flew down to ringside and began putting Clearance into a world of hurt. After a minute or two the entire Youngbloods Stable hit the ring to haul off the Hellraisers, why the Youngbloods would save Clearance is anyones guess. In a loser leaves town Match we saw The Canadian Rage Cage beat one time partner Blade. With Cage now entering the Singles world the big question is who's going next? Cory Osmond, Cages manager now has three singles wrestlers and no tag-team. My personal tip would be for Edge to return to team up with Douglas, yeah we all know that Edge lost to Williams in a loser leaves town match but provided he stays out of singles competition I doubt if the Front Office would have a problem with that. In all honesty I'd like to see it happen, not that I have a great affinity for Edge, just that the tag-ranks of the IWF are thinning out and could do with some more meat on the bones. With Latulippe & Larry contemplating who was the best team ever in the IWF we moved on into the second half of the show. For those of you that care Larry opted for the Crash Team, Latulippe plumbed for Omega. Speaking of the Crash Team, North American Tag-Title defence against the newcomers, the Dream Warriors consisting of John Constantine & Sandman. The pre-match hype saw the aptly named and hot headed 'Red Hot' Neil Scott throw down a loser leaves town gaunlet, which was reject by the Warriors on the basis that they'd feel guilty kicking the Crash Team out. Scott started off in his own uniquely risky and frankly stupid way. The Warriors used quick tags and double teams and it wasn't too long before Scott was in trouble. On the wrong side of a double brainbuster Scott relied on the calmer head of Ash Ward to make the save. Shaking the cobwebs he mounted an onslaught against Constantine and put him in the hammer lock submission. After a long time Constantine looked to simply black out in the ring. Danny Davies lifted the arm three times and called for the bell. The Crash Team take a quick win that can only have left the fans a little disappointed, it promised to be a great match but Constantine looked to be in serious trouble, perhaps theres more than meets the eye to this win. [PART TWO] IWF PPV Recap: Christmas Clash Sunday 27th December 1998 - pg2 From here on in it was title defence after title defence. The first saw the culmination of the longest running feud and certainly one of the most entertaining in IWF History. Lightweight Champion Todd 'Ratboy' Prentiss, who has held the belt since the inception of the Lightweight Division squared off against his only real threat and long, long time nemises Psycho. The history between these two guys is legendary. In the lightweight tournament Psycho was due to face Prentiss in a 'Ratboys Domain' match for the belt. Circumstances intervened and the match never happened. Its true that Psycho holds a victory over Prentiss albiet by DQ but with Prentiss' mind seemingly elsewhere with talk of 'The Traveller' rife among the lockeroom Psycho had to be favourite to take the belt. The match itself was worthy of the high billing it received. Both guys are somewhat of an enigma in the IWF and the crowd who have always given both a lukewarm reception didn't really know how to react. Psycho utilised the daring and highflying style that has marked his ascendancy to the top of the Lightweight Divison, whereas Prentiss did what he does best and just scrapped his way through the match. After a breif but even exchange Prentiss took charge hitting an unanswered six moves before Psycho deftly reversed the situation and went for a quick pin. Didn't work, but definately worth a go. Prentiss had his wits about him to get out of the way when Psycho went for the Springboard missile dropkick. Psycho had the upper hand for a while and had no fewer than four near falls before Prentiss was able to gain some composure and swing things his way. As the match entered the fourteen minute mark Prentiss was able to hit the 'Rat Trap' which was enough to keep the exhausted Psycho down for the 1-2-3. An excellent match and it looks like Prentiss will carry the belt into the new year. The title theme continued through the night as the silent 'Big Six' Jack Henderson defended the Intercontinental Strap against former holder Bob Charlie. The controversy surrounding this one has been fuelled further by the remergence onto the scene of former holder Curt Raines. The talking stopped, although it never really started from Hendersons point of view, as both guys made their way to the ring. The match was unusually brutal with chairs and foreign objects a plenty. It was quite evenly contested until it made its way out of the ring where it has to be said that Charlie had the better of the exchanges. No sooner had the combatants re-entered the ring when Big Six hit 'The Bash' on Charlie for the 1-2-3 and a sucessful defence. Not going to spend much time on the next one. World tag champs Hellraisers squared off against former Champs the Warrior Clan. In all honesty the outcome was in little doubt, Akira of the Warriors had two days previously announced his retirement only to agree to remain active for this match-up. That having been said the match was fiercely contested but Madbull & Darkbeast took the 1-2-3. The penultimate match of the evening was the unofficial main event dubbed the battle of the two biggest egos in the IWF. North American Champion Coma squared off against two time World Champ Rob 'The Legend' Williams. The build up was heated with the insults flying thick and fast. Coma had promised the death of 'The Legend' and the birth of 'The Myth' whilst alleging that Williams was past his best. Wiliams countered with Peodophile allegations and a number of celebrity interviews all backing the Legend. There was no doubt about who the crowd were routing for, within seconds Williams received a standing ovation whilst Coma, hated for his arrogance, was met with a hostile reception. The early stages were probably edged by Coma who immediately went to work on the joints of the Legend. Things turned when Williams hit the spinning DDT and mounted some serious offence agianst the champ. In serious need of recouperation time Coma was able to hold the Legend in a front facelock for a long time and move on to mount his own push forward, a push which was cut short by a piledriver on a ringside table. Coma again went for the long time submission tactics holding Williams in a Cobra Clutch for a further 40 seconds when things took a turn towards the strange. Former World Champ MaK appeared down at ringside to get a good view of proceedings. Perhaps having one time rival MaK at ringside distracted The Legend a little, after a short offencesive burst Coma reversed a Frankensteiner attempt into a Powerbomb and took the pin. Oddly enough a small faction of the crowd cheered the champ. Its no secret that Coma is hated by the fans, but it appears that his lockeroom following is every bit as hostile. No sooner had the bell rung that one time challenger Cerberus ran down to ringside and began to do a number on the champ. Strangely the Youngbloods again acted as peacekeepers dragging Cerberus to the back. No sooner had the champ got back to his feet when Freak appeared at ringside to begin beating down on him again. The Youngbloods appeared for a second time but were this time back jumped by Misled Youth and the Death Cult. The card was put on hold for the next 10 minutes as security tried to separate the waring factions. The final match of the evening saw World Champion, The Grim Reaper defending against Alien in a buried alive match. Both guys have had a se-saw record against each other. The match was shockingly short (5mins) and saw the Reaper drop a title that he has held for quite some time after having been hit by a clothesline of all things !!! The night ended as The Reaper vowed revenge and Alien celebrated amist a plethora of pyro. A good PPV with a number of suprises, the main question has to be who will add to the depleted ranks of the Tag and lightweight divisions and why did the Youngbloods make the saves for Clearance and for Coma, twice.
