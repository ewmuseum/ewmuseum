Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

ZWF -- "Hardcore Wrestling" by its own served title -- ran under an account name, "zwftake2," that implies a second era of a fed by this name; nothing in the held corpus dates or documents a first one, and the 1999 RSPWF Top 200's own unresolved "ZWF" tag on a wrestler named Golem cannot be confirmed or ruled out as that earlier era. What survives is a 2002 World Championship chain staged almost entirely by real WWF-era wrestlers under their own names, called by Jim Ross and Jerry Lawler under his own broadcast nickname, "The King."

The World Championship changed hands five times across six weeks: Brock Lesnar filled a vacancy by beating Jerry Lynn by default on 1 July 2002, The Undertaker beat Lesnar eighteen days later, Sting beat Undertaker, the belt sat vacant again until Lynn beat Lesnar to reclaim it, and Undertaker beat Lynn for a second reign -- the fed's own current champion as of its latest content, and separately credited by its own home page with TV and Hardcore reigns the corpus never explains. A served Summerslam broadcast stages four more title lines: Lita takes the Light Heavyweight belt from Chris Jericho; Jericho keeps his Eurocontinental title over Booker T; Jeff Hardy takes the TV title from X-Pac in a Triple Threat with Zero; and The Rock keeps the Hardcore title over Supernova in a weapons brawl. The same card closes on Undertaker beating Triple H clean in a steel-cage World Title defense, then Sting and Triple H beating him down after the bell -- Sting draping the title over him in an echo of the ledger's own separate record of a Sting reign the two documents never explicitly join. Zack Harris, the fed's own on-screen authority figure, and Bret Hart fight to a double pin the same night, and the original character Moonwolfe upends Chris Benoit.
