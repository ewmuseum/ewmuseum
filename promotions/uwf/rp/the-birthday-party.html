The Birthday Party

5 May 2006

Tessa Windsor — Chatham, MA

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Already a month had passed since I’d last stepped into a ring of any sort. A very long month at that. Ever since UWF decided to go on a small Spring Break of sorts, plenty of things had surfaced in my mind, to occupy that time. There were the kids, there was money that wasn’t coming in, and of course, plenty of relationship crap to kick around. Its funny how the one time I have to sit back and relax, is the one time I decide to go and add even more chaos to my life. Instead of using my time off for a well deserved vacation, I go and sign a contract with GCW, all for the sake of adding a few extra dollars to my kid’s college funds. In reality, I needed the steady paycheck that GCW would offer, but I couldn’t help but feel that ounce of guilt every time I went to sleep alone, knowing that at any second something could happen to either one of my kids, and I’d be too far away to do anything for them. Perhaps that’s why I felt the need to finally give back to my kids, after all the precious time I’d taken away from them. And given that Evan’s birthday was all but a week away, now was the perfect time to do so. After five years of performing in front of thousands, you would have thought that a crowd of maybe a hundred, if even that, would have been a breeze. But this was a crowd on a whole new level. With fifty parents, and twenty five of Evan’s classmates and closest friends on top of the collection of preteens Christa had invited along for her own entertainments sake, it wasn’t up to me to go out there and wrestle a good match. Now, I had to tackle the had to focus on my other career as a professional mother, and show that I had the capability of being just as good a mother, as I was a wrestler. Then again, that didn’t promise much on some days…. “I still can’t believe he gets to have a party. How come I never got to have one?” It should’ve been highly expected coming from a girl on the verge of her teenage years, still I would have figured Christa would have been glad just to have us all together. “Listen, Christa, I’ve already told you, I’m sorry. What else do you want from me?” Huffing and puffing back at my niece, turned daughter, I’d tossed the last of the ribbon her way, before climbing back down to her level to sort this scuffle out. “A party.” At twelve years of age, it was well expected that she’d still revert back to her old ways of thinking, to get what she wanted. After all, she was still just a kid. A kid that had lost her father at just eight years of age, and been abandoned by her real mother. And now, there was me in both their places, and well, I wasn’t exactly doing that great of a job. “Fine… you’ve got it….” I answered in between struggling to fold the ladder shut that I’d use to give me the extra boost to reach the top of the doorway. Kinda pathetic that here I was a professional wrestler, not even tall enough to reach the top of the doorframe, or strong enough to close a ladder, when it decides it’s going to stick, “when you turn thirteen.” “That’s just a few months away!” She was sure to remind me, as she flipped the calendar to September, as if I’d forget the day. “Good, then get planning.” I half teased as she stuffed her hands in the back pocket of her jeans, and rolled her eyes as she returned to her own little posse, who seemed just as determined to land a party just as big. “Mom, guess what!” Ah, the duties of a mother never ended. I could only imagine all the trouble the two put the poor nanny through during the day. Hopefully, the pretty penny I offered her to stick around made up for that. “What?” I questioned, finally giving up on the ladder as I nonchalantly shoved it into the corner of the utility closet, in hopes that no one would see the mess of cleaning supplies and other such necessities that had accumulated inside of it. “Chris is here!” “WHAT?!” Don’t get me wrong, I was happy for Evan. At least for him, seeing Chris was a delight. I, on the other hand, wanted to crawl into the unoccupied space in that closet, inhale a little bit of every bottle, and die, after hearing this. It’s not that I hated Chris. I just hated the fact that with Chris around, plenty of questions were bound to bloom, such as the status of our relationship, and the turmoil surrounding it. And being that this was my one chance that go out and live life like a normal person, I didn’t want that one chance blown. “Don’t worry, he’s being good.” Coming from a child barely nine years of age, it sadly didn’t mean much. Good for a nine year old, consisted of listening to his parents, doing his homework, and keeping out of fights. Sadly, even out of those, the only one Chris at twenty eight years old, was capable of keeping on top of was his homework. In fact, his so called homework, was practically his life. “Where is he?” In a panic, my head flew from side to side as if it were attached to a spring on one of those bobble head dolls. “Over there.” Had I not wanted to make a scene, I would have reminded Evan how impolite, especially at a moment at this, where the last thing I wanted for anyone to notice, was Chris’ presence, or for him to notice mine for that matter Surely enough, within earshot distance, stood Chris, fraternizing with another parents. At least to some relief, it was one of the father’s. As is, the last thing I needed, was for him to shamelessly flirt with someone’s wife, and have it all fall on my shoulders, just because he can’t decide when he’s related to us, and when he isn’t. “Hey!” Just as I had feared, he’d spotted me. Unfortunately, the only way I could get across to him that now was not the time, was to roll my eyes. And well, after making the six hour trip from New York, just to be here for Evan, he wasn’t going to buy into the ‘just leave me alone’ bit. Quickly, I dashed for the nearest corner, knowing full well he’d follow me. “Look, behave yourself.” I preached, as if he were Evan, as I watched the small group congregating inside the living room, out of the corner of my eyes. “There are several other parents here from Evan‘s school, with their children. And I don’t want to be embarrassed.” “Look, I’m not here to start anything. I’m just here to do the right thing.” Coming from a man who diverted any sort of responsibility in life, it wasn’t exactly easy to just take his word for it, but I didn’t exactly want to be the one to mess up things this time around. “Fine….” Even after four years of putting up with his crap, I found it hard to turn him away once more. So, I pinched my lip in between my teeth, and prayed for the best, but not without expecting at least a little something in return. “Just do me one favor, please?” “What?” Cocking his eyebrow up as is he were some kind stud, he anxiously awaited his chance to play Romeo, as if he already knew what I was going to ask. “Keep away from the whole wrestling bit, please?” Much to his dismay, not only were his advances denied, but his passion extinguished. “Fine….but I don’t know how you expect them not to find out.” Pulling his own beautiful blue eyes towards his heavily bandaged nose, he made it clear that I owed him big time for this one. “Well Tessa, and who might this be?” Before I could finish with Chris, along a woman I’d hope to avoid. The so called rumor mill of the community, Claire. If there was anyone that’d shit on my reputation faster than the press, it was her. Unfortunately, being that her daughter Jasmine was good friends with Evan, it was all too often for my liking that I had to deal with her. “Oh Claire, this is Chris…” Out of clear view, I jerked my elbow into his ribs, so that he’d give her the proper kind of greeting. “Storm” He added in, much to my distaste, as if he were something special. After shaking hands with Chris, her attention returned to me, with a questioned that couldn’t be avoided. “So what’s your relationship with Chris, Tessa?” “HA!” After months of trying to win me back, I could almost hear chorus of laughter erupting inside of Chris’ head now that’d been put in an uncomfortable spot. “She’s my…” Chris started up, knowing very well that there was no modest way to put what we were, other than exes. And considering that neither kid biologically belonged to us, it would have only made it all the worse to add that part in. So if there was anyone to break the bad news, it might as well be him. “Fiancé.” I cut off, much to his surprise as I swung up my left hand and held my ring finger up proud. Adorned in fourteen karats of gold, the sapphire in the middle stood out like a sore thumb. The whole reason it didn’t look like a traditional engagement ring, is because it wasn’t meant to be. But desperate times, call for desperate measures. With no surprised, Chris decided he’d finally put on the father cap, and silently slithered his way out in the direction of Evan. I couldn’t blame him, I would have loved to ease away just as easily, but instead Claire pulled her wine glass away from her lips, and offered more of a discussion. “So when are you guys getting married?” “Good question.” After dropping the hint a handful of times, and only being shunned, I’d always wondered just that, until I decided to move on. Of course then he decides he wants to move on with our relationship. And they say women are complicated…. “Next summer.” I contemplated aloud, while figuring, “It leaves plenty of time for people to forget about it.” “That’s wonderful. Hopefully we’ll see more of him around here than we do now.” “HAHA, wishful thinking there.” I’d hoped for the same thing for years now, and well obviously, by the ways things were now, that never happened. Still, I offered the same thought once more, “Hopefully. His work schedules kinda hectic….” “Oh really, what’s he do for a living?” “He’s an actor.” It’s not exactly like I was lying there, I mean the average person did consider us wrestlers, nothing more than actors, after all that talk that professional wrestling was nothing more than scripts and stunt workers. “An actor?” Her heavily painted eyebrow arched up in interest; as she diverted her view back to Chris in hopes that she could match him up with something big, like a multimillion dollar movie, or hit T.V. series. “What has he worked on?” “Oh he’s just an amateur…” Brushing it off like it was nothing, I hoped to at least discourage her interests in the matter, as there were only such much stretching of the truth I could muster up in one sitting, without it all coming back to bite me in the ass. “For now, he’s just breaking into the business.” “Is that why we never see him around? From what I hear the acting business is a pretty crazy one.” Obviously she had some kind of clue of the turmoil between us two, yet she knew how to dig up the dirt on us, without having to tread directly into it. “Yeah….but that’ll change soon, I hope.” As optimistic as I tried to be around others, I knew that nothing was going to change. I’d been telling myself that same thing for almost four years now and still, nothing had ever changed. It’s a good thing they already knew about my career, because judging by the tension in the air, calling myself an actress, wouldn’t have been a swift options. Obviously, that last bit too, seemed a little be too unbelievable. “CAKE AND ICE CREAM CAKE AND ICE CREAM CAKE AND ICE CREAM” At least I had a group of twenty or so rowdy children to save me from my latest blunder. “I suppose I ought to get to the present bit, before we have ourselves a riot on hand.” I causally offered, to ease away from the conversation. With how little time it took me to get everything ready, I would have never imagined how quickly gossip could spread around. Then again, I’d never met a woman like Claire. By the time I came strolling into the living room, I was already met with a bunch of curious stares, not so much as to what I did, but how I dealt with what Chris apparently did. Judging by the scowl on Chris’ face, things weren’t going too well for him either. Still, I dealt with them the same way I dealt with fans. Dawning a gratifying smile for those who’d now been looped into the Windsor family circus, I’d turned the attention towards Evan, who I hoped would steal the show. Hopefully, that’d at least get Chris to put away his sour attitude for more than thirty seconds. Of course, if there was a span thirty seconds where Chris wasn’t busy wallowing in his own self pity, it was spent in the spotlight. As much as he pissed and moaned that he hated people, he sure did love to win over their attention. So naturally right before Evan could soak in an ounce of attention, in came Chris. Propping Evan up on his shoulder as if he’d done this father stuff daily, he’d managed to lap up every bit of attention that should have been directed Evan’s way. “So do you have any projects lined up?” “Would we have seen you in anything yet?” “Who takes care of the kids when Tessa is gone?” “Are you in Hollywood trying to get work when you’re not here?” Admittedly, some of blame fell on me. I could have been up front about his career, and his involvement in the family, especially considering it was already known that I was a wrestler myself. But who really wants to admit that their love interest was a man with an explosive temper, who picked his career over his own family ninety percent of the time? But if he wanted the attention, I was more than willing to give it to him. So when he turned his attention towards me, I gave him the go ahead nod to use the little acting ability he did posses, in hopes that he’d improvise. If there’s anything I could depend on Chris for, it would be his ability to carry the majority of the attention. Even in our PCW stint, he always made it clear that in his eyes, and probably ninety percent of the wrestling fan base, that he was responsible for the success we had. Like a fool, Chris burst into a tune of Happy Birthday. Naturally, like the self absorbed asshole he was, he took the lead role of an involved parent and looked to me to support him. I should have been happy about it, really. After all the chasing I’d done to finally get him into this spot. Still, every time I caught a glimpse of a grin on his face, I couldn’t help but trace it back to the times where he nearly had me killed, all because I got in the way of his career. Maybe I should have been honest, and finally had him exposed for the true asshole he was. But I’d already dug myself a hole, well above my head, so I stood there with my arms folded across my chest as others joined in his song, and gave him a dose of his own medicine. Shrugging it off without surrender, he lead the march towards the cake. Obviously, my little guilt trip wasn’t working, so once again it was I that waved the flag. This time, I strolled up along his side, and joined in the effort to calm the masses in the distribution of cake and ice cream. Perhaps it wasn’t all that bad. Come present time, he’d finally been what I’d wanted him to be all along, a supportive father, and a loving man. My nerves had been soothed, until I uncovered his gift to Evan, one that I’d admittedly forgotten all about. Why Chris had let it slip was beyond me, especially after his attempts to make up for the past. Still I wasn’t going to let it slide. If he was going to learn how to be a decent parent, I was going to teach him straight up. “Uh, I think we missed one.” Some turned back to the living room, while others decided to skip this last one out in the kitchen. “Who is it from?” Inquired one of the other mothers, giving me a reminder of my own, on how to be a good hostess. “Chris,” was my nerve filled response, as I looked back at him with high expectations. Handing the colorfully wrapped gift (which was surprising considering Chris’ dull personality) I clenched my tongue between my teeth as I saw what emerged from it. Just great, Chris’ newest shirt. So how do you explain that one? You don’t. “I didn’t know he did that kind of acting.” One parent sneered, as I slipped behind Chris’ bulky mass, as if he were the one supposed to take the bullets for my lie. The others pumped questions towards Chris, obviously making him a bit uneasy since it was my fabricating of the truth, which got him into this mess. “Yeah, I’m a wrestler. That’s why you never see me. I live in Chicago where the company is located. When I can, I try to fly out here to spend time with Evan.” He casually answered in the same way he dealt with wrestling savvy fans. Evan, careless of the crap unfolding before him, gathered his gifts, with his friends in tow. He was all too used to the drama between Chris and I, and knew by now to escape before it reached it peak. There I stood, made into a speechless fool by the questioning stares of a dozen parents. By now, I was used to the negative attention. It was the same kind of stare the thousands of fans that attended UWF and GCW’s shows offered, whenever I made a decision they didn’t support. Chris, however, found it much more uncomfortable when it was at a personal level. So he offered to do the thing he was best at, packing his bags and leaving. “I’m out of here; tell Evan I’ll try to see him before my flight for Chicago.” It wasn’t the first time, and probably wouldn’t be the last time I heard such a thing come from his mouth. Still, each time it managed to drive nails into my heart, “Fine, run away again.” Here I was, acting like he rained all over my parade. It wasn’t me he was walking out though, it was his kid, the only person who had any sort of remorse left for him. You’d think he’d feel fortunate to even have his affection after how many times he’d abandoned Evan before, but no, Chris was still just a boy himself. Now I was left, facing the harsh reality of it all. It didn’t matter how hard I tried, things would never normal in the Windsor household. And worse of all, I’d chased Chris away again. Surrendering to my lie, I lowered my head and chalked up another loss on my behalf. “Good job Tess, you messed things up again.” Not only was I a loser in the ring, but in life as well. Shamefully, I’d never own up to it.
