UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #753

Kids are Scary!

22 Oct 2004

Joshua Justice — I dunno. Some grade school

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[A small boy, about the age of 10 walks from the bathroom stall and looks into the small mirror in front of the sink that he’s washing his hands in. His name is Peter.] Peter: Have you ever seen somebody and known right away that you were in the presence of greatness…? [Though he is young, he fixes hair in the mirror.] Peter: Someone who was truly larger than life…? [He turns the water off and takes some paper towels to dry his hands.] Peter: …a Living Legend? [He walks out of the bathroom and looks at a UWF promotional poster. On the poster is the professional wrestler fighting to obtain the Television Title on the next card for the UWF. On the poster, striking a very patriotic pose, is Joshua Justice.] Peter: That’s how Joshua Justice makes you feel… [It was the second to last period for the day. About five more minutes until the big pep rally where Joshua Justice himself will give, more than likely, an impressive speech. Suddenly, a shadow looms over Peter as he stares up at the poster. Peter turns around expecting the school bully or the pinhead principal to be standing there, but it’s neither of them. Peter looks back to the poster and then quickly turns a full one-eighty to see that Joshua Justice is the man that is behind him! Peter stands there with his jaw dropped and no sound coming out.] Justice: Hello, Peter. [Peter manages to make a sound.] Peter: You… you know me?!? [Justice points to the nametag stuck onto Peter’s t-shirt. Peter looks down and then blushes with embarrassment.] Justice: Don’t worry son, I won’t tell anybody. [Justice turns and walks towards the auditorium, leaving Peter almost pissing his pants. Justice gets to the auditorium and walks down to the stage. He steps behind the pedestal as a bell rings. Out of nowhere, tons of kids run through the back doors of the auditorium and fill the seats up. The auditorium is completely filled up. It’s so filled up that there are kids sitting in the isles as well. Justice knocks on the mic to make sure it’s working and the crowd of kids become silent.] Justice: Hi kids! I came here to talk about a few things. Let me talk about war first. No war is ever good, kids. I’ve been in a war and I saw a lot of men lose their lives in that war—most of them not much older than you out there. Looking back, things do seem like they were much clearer then. It was easy to tell right from wrong. We were the good guys and we were fighting against a horrible evil! We believed in what we were fighting for. [All of the kids are silent. There’s not a smile in the crowd anymore.] Justice: I’M FROM THE UWF! [The kids all jump to their feet and applaud and cheer for Justice.] Justice: Yeah—these days it’s like everywhere you look, people are arguing. Everybody’s got an opinion and they think that if they yell it louder than everyone else, that means it’s right! Sometimes I think they get so busy yelling that they don’t even realize that they agree with each other! It gets confusing… and frustrating… [Again, the kids are silent. They are still up on their feet, but the cheers and applaud have stopped.] Justice: WHO’S GONNA BEAT DREAMMAKER AND BECOME THE UWF TV CHAMP!? [The kids all shout out in unison.] Kids: JUSTICE! [Justice smiles.] Justice: I can see where it might be hard to figure out who and what to stand up for… [Some of the kids start to sit down as they all silence up again.] Justice: …but don’t you see—all those things that I just mentioned that make me so frustrated… [The rest take their seats as well.] Justice: …are many of the same things that make me proud to be an American. [The kids all jump back to their feet in cheers because for some strange reason they are all really patriotic. Some sappy patriotic music starts to play softly in the background.] Justice: Because in America we have the freedom to speak out about what we believe in, to offer up new ideas about how to do things. The freedom to make changes and to make a difference. To make ourselves better… [Some of the teachers start to cry because his speech is so touching.] Justice: …and hopefully make things better for everyone else in the process. That’s what it’s all about to me. [Most of the kids in the crowd are just in total awe.] Justice: I’m going to make this short and sweet because I made a deal with the principal. All of you are going to be let out early today! [The kids all jump to their feet, for those of them left sitting, with cheers of joy.] Justice: I will take down Dreammaker. [Cheers from the kids.] Justice: I will become the UWF Television Champion. [More cheers.] Justice: And there’s nothing ANYONE can do about it! I will set the UWF straight! I took care of Dicky Flint! I took care of Seraphiend! And now I will take care of Dreammaker! Next on my list after that will be El Biano and The Communist! THEY WILL GO DOWN! [The crowd of kids go completely insane and start to dance around like monkeys on heroin. Justice look out at them and you can see fear in his eyes.] Justice: Just say no, kids! [Justice runs away as the kids start a riot. He runs out the side door of the auditorium and holds it closed as the kids try to rush out after him.] Justice: These kids are insane! I gotta get out of here so I can win that TV Title! [The door starts to bulge open and Justice jumps away from the door.] Justice: AHH! [Justice take off running as hundreds of small children come bursting through the door. Justice jumps in his limo and it pulls off with screeching wheels as the kids go chasing after it.]
