The Birthday Party (Chris' POV)

4 May 2006

Chris Storm — Chatham, MA

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Please pardon me if I ramble. My minds not quite right you see. Maybe it’s from all the drugs I indulged in during my youth, or maybe it’s because I haven’t slept for three days. Three days is a long time without sleep. Eventually the mind stops counting the hours and the days mesh into each other. The only way you realize time is by the light outside. It’s all about endurance really. Not only does the body need to be fit, but the mind as well. Why have I put my body through that kind of torture? Personally I would like to tell you it was because I was training for the new UWF show. The Meltdown…urgh Melting Point…urgh Mojo…urgh Homo…ah fuck, I don’t know what the hell it’s call. Not that it matters, the damn name will change three more times before it airs. The truth is I just couldn’t sleep. I had a lot of shit on my mind. Like an upcoming birthday… Despite the growing sleep headache, I continued to try and straighten my tie. I knew I needed to try and at least look like I hadn’t just stayed awake for three days. Looking at my watch I cursed to myself for only having a few hours to finish getting ready and make it to Evan’s birthday party. This would be the first year that Tessa arranges the entire party by herself. So I have no idea what to expect. I don’t even know if she’s expecting me to show up. She never sent me an invitation. Maybe she didn’t think I needed one. Or maybe she just doesn’t want me to show up. Either way, it doesn’t matter. I’m still planning to try and arrive around noon. I can remember pulling up to the house and seeing all the cars outside. For some reason it bothered me. Out of all the times I tried to remember, I couldn’t think of any time that so many cars were parked at the house. That is of course unless you count the time all the cops showed up to arrest me for alleged domestic abuse. Ha! And they thought they would be able to take me down with just three cops. Anyways, that’s beside the point. That’s in the past and we shouldn’t worry about it. Right now I just have to worry about the nine years old celebrating his birthday. At least it was comforting to see the house in chaos when I walked through the front door. Tessa was in the mist of trying to jungle arguing with Christa; all the while she was trying to corral the kids into the living room. “DAD!” With only a few seconds of advance notice I was suddenly tackled and nearly knocked me off my feet. “So what did you get me?” Evan asked as he eyed the gift wrapped box in my arms. I had to hold the gift over his head and angle myself around him. “You can open it later when you open the rest of them.” I handed the boy his gift and smiled as he jumped up and down with the gift. “Now go put it with the rest.” Evan nodded and hurried away into the crowd with the gift. With the birthday boy done, I went about looking around for nothing in particular. It felt strange seeing all the adults and children running around the house. We never really did gatherings when I lived with Tessa. All the children played in the living room while the adults stood around the kitchen and chatted about meaningless things. Gah, they were so boring. Why the hell did the middle class have to be so boring? “So did you see that last episode of House? That show is great. House is such a rude person.” I rolled my eyes. Talking about late night television? Can I just shoot myself now? One of the dads noticed me and starts staring. A lack of sleep made me want to punch him, but I doubt Tessa would appreciate it much. Instead all I could mutter was “yeah?” He started pointing at his nose. I guess he was talking about the bandages I had on my nose from when it was broken. In an attempt to get him to stop acting like a jackass I clued him in to my broken nose. “Now how did you manage that one?” he asked, as if he was actually interested in the story. I just shrugged. “A guy threw a chair at my face. Broke in two places on contact. Cost me about five grand to get it reconstructed back to the way I like it.” Now for some reason after I answered his question his jaw dropped. I hate when people react to my stories, it always annoys the crap out of me. “That, uh, happen a lot?” he stammered, clearly fazed by the answer. He tried to correct himself from looking like a retard, “I mean, you got into a fight or something?” “Let’s just call it an occupational hazard” I told him before looking away. Out of the corner of my eye I caught a glance of Tessa. I waved to her so she would see. Not surprisingly she rolled her eyes and walked over. She was probably making sure I wasn’t flirting with another woman or some shit. “Hey” was all I could manage to greet her. Tessa nodded her head to signal for me to move over to the corner. “Look, behave yourself” she told me, “There are several other parents here from Evan‘s school, with their children. And I don’t want to be embarrassed.” I hated being lectured like this but I stood there and took it. I knew how much this meant to her and didn’t feel like being the source of another family screw up. Should I mention I’m the reason we never had many parties. One of the other parents had spotted us and came over. If there was any words to describe her it would be “Mrs. Robinson”. “Well Tessa, and who might this be?” she asked looking my way. By the way Tessa grinded her teeth I could tell either one of two things; either she didn’t like this woman or this woman was making a pass for me. I hope it’s the second. Damnit it’s never the second! “Oh Claire, this is Chris…” “Storm” I ask, cutting her off. Tessa shot me a dirty look but Claire didn’t seem to notice. Exchanging formal greetings, Claire finally asked the one question Tessa had been hoping to avoid, “so what is your relationship with Chris, Tessa?” “She’s my…” I started to speak but before I could get much more than a word in edgewise, Tessa cut me off. “Fiancé,” and with that she proudly showed off her large sapphire ring I had gotten her for Christmas. To say I could have been knocked over with a feather would have been an understatement. If you consider the fact that I go toe-to-toe with two hundred plus pound men on weekly bases; that says something. I was half tempted to tell Claire the truth but my own stupidity stopped me from doing the honest thing. And truth be told, it felt good to hear those words coming from Tessa’s mouth. So instead I just excused myself and headed off to see Evan. As I walked out of the room I made sure I slapped myself on the forehead extra hard for being such a dumbass. There was no way a lie like that could NOT lead to trouble. Looking around the living room, I spotted Evan playing with a little girl. I managed my way past the little tykes that ran around on a sugar rush and walked over to Evan. “Hey you’re that guy on the tv!” one of the other kids yelled. Like an alarm bell, all the children stopped what they were doing and turned their heads toward me. Before I knew it I was surrounded by midgets. “Really? You’re on tv?” “Cool! Do you know any celebrates?” “Oh course he knows celebrates, if he’s on tv!” “Do you date Paris Hilton?” “Gah, who hasn’t dated Paris?” Ok, so that last one was me. What? The chick is a slut. She makes Kitsune Oni look like a nun. Trying to wrangle the kids into a ground, I started to entertain their questions; “Yes, I am on television and I do know a few celebrates. But they only star in those movies you guys can’t watch until you’re older.” Their little faces lit up when I admitted to knowing a few big names, though I’m not sure they quite understood what I meant. They probably thought I meant I hung out with the likes of Tom Cruise on the weekends or shit. Frankly I wouldn’t hang out with that placenta-eating weirdo if he invited me over for chips and dip. “So I take it you kids watch wrestling then?” I figured I would entertain them with stories about how much of a loser Seymour really is and how Clyde actually does smell like a burnt ass crack. But they all started shaking their heads. “Uh…so you don’t?” “No, Evan told us you were on tv. He said his dad’s on tv every week with big celebrities,” one of them said, speaking up for the group. I looked around for Evan to see him standing off and away from the ground. He never made eye contact so I could tell he was embarrassed. I could only imagine the stories he had most likely told them about my television career. And I highly doubt it involved getting fined for fist fighting on the last Supremacy. From the other room I could hear Claire’s high pitch laughter and I could tell Tessa was stuck having to be probed by questions. I had little desire to go back and try and be entertaining for that woman, even though I did want to know what Tessa was telling her. I could only imagine what stories Tessa was creating to try and explain things. She was never very good at lying but I had the feeling Claire was dumb enough to believe just about anything as long as it was gossip. One of the kids grabbed my shirt and tugged on it to get my attention, “so what celebrities do you know?” “I’ll tell you when I’m older” I said, patting the kid on the head, “but for now I need to go much sure everything ready for later.” It seemed the children knew what that ‘later’ was and started chanting “CAKE AND ICE CREAM!” at the top of their lungs. Even Evan joined in on the chanting and soon all the kids were rushing past me to the kitchen where they knew the goods stayed. I just shook my head. One minute they were clamoring with questions and the next they were bouncing off the walls for cake. Following the last child out, I made my way to the kitchen to see the parents had now gathered around as well. Something seemed wrong. All the parents were huddled around each other talking. For some reason it reminded me of high school when all the popular kids would gather around to talk about the ‘uncool’ kids. I should know; I was one of the populars. I must have had a sour look on my face because I saw Tessa from across the kitchen shooting me a dirty look. I tried my best to smile but instead it just came off as a scowl. As I approached the table one of the mothers, who I didn’t know (not that I knew anyone there) looked across at me. “So I hear you’re an actor, Chris.” At first I cocked my eyebrow and looked over at Evan, thinking he had told the parents how I was on television regularly. Before I could reply, one of the kids cut me off. “Yeah and he knows plenty of famous people too! Only he can’t tell us who until we’re older.” At this point I was glad looks couldn’t hit. I would be six feet under. Actually make that twelve. Tessa looks REALLY pissed. In an attempt to try and shift the attention off myself, I picked up Evan and lifted him onto my shoulder, and carried him over toward the center of the group where the kitchen table was. Stacked on the table were an assortment of wrapped gifts, various cards, and one large chocolate cake that had all the children drooling. Sadly all my efforts did was trap me inside the circle of parents with no decent escape route. That’s when the questions started to fly. “So do you have any projects lined up?” “Would we have seen you in anything yet?” “Who takes care of the kids when Tessa is gone?” “Are you in Hollywood trying to get work when you’re not here?” I looked to Tessa for help since she was the one who had started this whole mess after all. If she wanted me to behave myself, she needed to help bail me out of this mess. With each question I felt like exploding on the parents and exposing the truth. Who cared if I wrestled for a living? Was it my fault white collar jobs choked me? That I was never born to work a blue collar lifestyle? I’ve never even worn a collar before! Ok that’s a life. There was this one time…wait…never mind, you don’t need to know that story. Tessa just nodded to me that told me that she either had no idea how to help or was enjoying watching me on the hot seat. Given her attitude toward me in recent months, I would wager on the second. I looked to Evan, who stared hungrily at his cake. Thinking as fast as I could, I reached for the lighter and started lighting the candles on the cake. Soon all nine were in flames. “Happy birthday to you!” I cried, looking to Tessa for her to join in. “Happy birthday to you!” There was a moment of awkward silence. The parents and children alike seemed confused from the random jump of discussing my career to singing ‘Happy Birthday’. “Happy birthday to you!” a third time I cried. Finally other parents started to pick up the hint and sang along. Not before long the children joined in. One person I noticed who wasn’t singing was Tessa. When it seemed I would get no help from Tessa, I decided to cut the cake myself rather than let her do it. Evan blew out his candles, much to everyone’s cheers, and I cut the first slice of cake. It seemed as if Tessa had enough, or finally caved it, because she moved in and started to help divide out the cake for everyone. When the last piece of cake was distributed, it was time for the main attraction and the one thing that would expose Tessa’s lie… Presents. Evan reached for the first gift and ripped it to shreds. A new basketball. He moved onto another gift in all it’s horribly wrapped glory. I’m going to guess that one came from the single dad. Evan opened the gift and held up his new baseball glove. Another gift and now a new football. Was Evan just some amazing athlete and I just missed it? What the hell’s up with all the sport gifts? Soon all the presents were unwrapped and Tessa was left with a huge mess to clean up. She handed me a black trash bag and together we tried to clean up the wrapping paper. The parents were moving back to the living room area when Tessa stopped him. “Uh? I think we missed one.” There, buried in multicolored wrapping paper was one lone gift that had been overlooked. Tessa flipped it over to look at the tag. Her face instantly went sour. I cursed myself for having forgotten about my present. Not that I had wanted Evan to open it sooner, but I actually meant to hide it after the lie had spread throughout the house. “Who’s it from?” one of the random parents asked. Handing the gift over to Evan, she hissed out the name I already knew, “Chris.” Tessa handed the present over to Evan, who happily ripped the wrapping paper off. Buried under all the badly wrapped paper was a black “Punisher-equse” shirt with “Rock Superstar” wrote across the back. The shirt itself was my new merchandise the UWF had decided to pump out. Under the shirt was also my new action figure the UWF put out. It came complete with a mini replica North American championship belt to put around the figure’s waist. The parents stopped and looked at each other. Though Evan, who had made a collection out of my merchandise, loved the gifts, Tessa’s lie had been exposed. I was no more of an actor than she was; I was nothing more than a wrestler. There was a weird silence that hung in the air. Evan was the one who finally broke the silence, seemingly unaware of the mess around him. “Thanks dad!” he said as he hurried to put his new things with the rest of his collection. “So…” one of the fathers said, not sure how to word it. “You’re a wrestler as well?” I looked over to Tessa to see if there was some answer she would give me. She was too busy biting her bottom lip, though. “Yeah, I’m a wrestler. That’s why you never see me. I live in Chicago where the company is located. When I can I try to fly out here to spend time with Evan.” As Evan scampered away with his gifts, there was an odd silence hanging from my last words. No one really knew how to deal with the news. Not that I saw it as a big deal. So what if our jobs were different? With every passing second I could feel the eyes of the strangers boring into me. It was strange really. Here I am, a man that performs live in front of millions week after week, yet I am getting pissed off over about twelve or so parents looking at me. Finally, after what felt like ten minutes, I turned away. Darting my head to the side I looked over at Tessa, “I’m out of here; tell Evan I’ll try to see him before my flight for Chicago.” Tessa just turned her head away, clearly pissed off. “Fine, run away again.” Some of the parents started to shift around uncomfortably when Tessa and I started to argue, though I think I saw Claire taking down note. I don’t remember everything that was said though. My mind wasn’t really there you see. I’m sure it was the same shit as always, how I’m never there. How I always abandon them. Etc. At the time I really just wanted to get out of there. Grabbing my jacket (which I later found out wasn’t my jacket and that I had never brought a jacket), I walked off, slamming the door behind me and in Tessa’s face. My face felt red hot. My blood was boiling. Like a ticking bomb, I wanted to explode. Likely I can. I can take out all my pent up anger on the roster of the UWF when the new owners decide to throw us at each other like a pack of hungry wolves. Good. That’s just what I need right now, for someone to throw me a freakin’ bone.
