UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #775

Reunions

7 Nov 2004

Mike Montgomery — Halifax, Nova Scotia

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What's that old saying? 'Once more unto the breach dear friends, once more...' or something, isn't it? Well either way that's where we find ourselves at the moment. The last time we saw the World Heavyweight Champ, he was being eliminated from a tag team title match on SAS. A lucky pin courtesy of the hype machine made wrestler that is Angelo Deville. He should be upset, Deville hasn't done a single thing to merit the push that a pinfall over the world champion brings. AFter the match he was then assaulted by the remnants of the Chaos Society...the UWF's forgotten stable...and his own tag team partner, the man he won the World Tag Team Titles with in January, Ezra Kazee. Now remember, we've established that he SHOULD be upset by these events, but he's not. If you think about it, is there really any reason for him to be? He was beaten on a nearly ten day late show that showed the poorest production on the UWF's part since it's inception and will most likely never be referenced again. A show nobody will remember. No, the Champ is perfectly content because of what the people WILL remember. The people will remember Montgomery walking into Live and Loud, the BIGGEST Universal Wrestling Federation Pay Per View of the year, stepping through the ropes for the match that he made famous, the main event, and beating the only UWF superstar that he does not have a previous pinfall victory against in order to walk out of the biggest match of his career still holding the World Heavyweight Title. How can you not be happy after something like that? The scene opens to the night time beauty of the Halifax Waterfront. Street lamps line the docks, bathing their wooden planks in light but leaving the harbor itself bathed in darkness. The unobstructed view of Dartmouth, Halifax's sister city, allows the camera to pan across the beautiful view of it's lights at night, belying the trashier nature it shows during the day. When the camera finishes panning around, it settles on a wave shaped sculpture bathed in the bright glow of a floodlight. Residing near the Maritime Museum of the Atlantic, it is the brightest area of the Waterfront at night, and isn't supposed to be climbed on. Several signs circling the piece of artwork read 'Do Not Climb' in big bold letters. Tonight, as with every other moment of time since the sculpture was placed on the Waterfront, those signs have been completely ignored. Mike Montgomery sits on top of the sculpture, knees curled up to his chest, eyes staring out across the harbor. Expression reflective, almost lost in thought, it is something of a surprise to see him smile as he senses the approach of the camera. 'It's kinda funny, you know? They all said I couldn't do it. My World Title reign that some of them said was a fluke anyway was about to end. Everyone figured Tsunami Oni would walk out of Live and Loud the World Heavyweight champion. That was the way the experts said the show had to end. The thing about experts is, they don't like to be proven wrong. That's why they can't stand me. I'm not the one that's supposed to win. I'm not supposed to be at the top. Thing is...I am. Eventually, people have to accept that the main event is where I shine. The bigger the match, the more thrills I can and will provide. Live and Loud Four will go down as the crowning achievement of my career. 'But maybe they're finally starting to figure it out. Maybe that's why they're putting me back in the ring with Tsunami Oni. Maybe that's why my World title is on the line again. Hell, maybe they're just throwing darts at the wall and seeing what sticks, who knows? Either way, it's money. Either way, I have to walk down to that ring, once again walking into my part of the show, my kind of match, and once again having to pull a rabbit out of the hat to keep my world title. But I can deal with that. I've done it once to silence the critics for a little while. I've bought myself a little peace. 'Of course, on the other hand maybe the match is the result of a need for a different kind of peace. In order to beat Tsunami Oni, I had to do...things. Things I can take no pride whatsoever in. I didn't like what I did, but it had to be done for the sake of the win. Now though, the rules of the game have changed. Before now, I could afford to go it alone, I didn't need to have people in the dressing room that would back me up, no questions asked. Of course, that was then, this is now. Now, it's a whole new ball game.' Mike jumps the meter and a bit from the top of the sculpture down to the ground, the gravel crunching beneath his feet. He slowly strolls toward the end of a peer, hands tucked into his coat pockets. 'Truth be told though, I suppose it really doesn't matter. I owe Tsunami, one way or the other. Way I figure it, this is my chance to pay up. Besides...I just gotta know.' Montgomery sits on a bench near the end of the peer, legs splayed out as he stares across the water. 'And that's a wrap!' A crew person calls from just beside the camera. Montgomery smiles, popping to his feet and walking back down the peer. He shakes hands with several of the crew members, then stops in front of the segment director. 'Nice script man, I was really feeling that one.' The director shrugs, 'Well what can I say, in all honesty the script is only as good as the person reading it.' 'Maybe, but either way, it was well written.' 'Please, a thousand monkies with a thousand type writers, as they say...' Mike turns at the sound of this new voice. For a moment he is utterly speechless. Then a large smile spreads across his face. 'Well I'll be damned.' 'Kinda, yeah.' The small spider-monkey, world famous both for actually being able to talk, as well as successfully managing a multi-national corporation and a professional wrestler's career, makes his way over to the world champ. The two old friends quickly shake hands and embrace. 'Man it's good to see you, how you been buddy?' Spiff quickly clambers up onto Mike's shoulder, 'Not too bad, not too bad. Just had to get a few things sorted out with the R&D people, you?' 'Two world titles, a tag title, and another Stablewars championship, what can I say, no real reason to complain.' 'You sure about that?' Spiff asks, causing Mike to stop dead in his tracks. 'What're you talking about man?' The pair reaches Mike's Hummer-limo, parked on Lower Water Street. Montgomery's bodyguard, Gavin Frost, steps out of the back, holding the door open for his boss. He hesitates at the sight of Spiff, but Montgomery holds a hand up, signalling that the primate is okay. 'Don't worry Mr. Frost, he's an old friend.' 'Not to mention your boss.' Spiff haughtily adds. 'You must be Spiff then,' Frost says simply. Frost heads for the front seat as the two old friends pile into the spacious rear. Spiff hops off Montgomery's shoulder and ambles over to the limo's mini-bar fridge, where he quickly mixes together a bananna daiquri. 'Nice to see you kept the mini-bar stocked.' 'What kind of host would I be if I didn't,' comes the quick response, 'now what did you mean earlier when you said there was reason to complain.' Spiff climbs up into a seat, sitting directly opposite Mike, 'I've been keeping an eye on you since I left, watching all the shows I could. Joshua Dunn's in a coma...Kevin Starr's career may be over...you screwed Ezra over completely...you brainwashed Tsunami Oni's girlfriend AND threw his dad in jail, and there's nothing to complain about? What in the hell were you thinking??' 'What was I thinking?' Mike answers, starting to get angry, 'I was thinking that I had finally gotten to the top of the mountain and that I would do whatever it took to stay there.' 'No matter whose life you ruined to get there?' The comment causes Mike to hesitate for an instant before he answers, 'I was willing to do whatever I had to. Am I taking any pride in what I've done? Hell no. But it had to be done, and I'd do it over again in a heartbeat if I had to.' Spiff closes his eyes and massages his temple for a moment, which on a spider monkey looks kinda cute, 'What happened to you man? You are definately not the same fun loving guy you were when I left. You used to be about the fans...about putting on a show and having a good time doing it. Now look at you. Now you're about title belts, power and manipulation...I honestly don't know who you are anymore man.' 'You think I don't know all that already?!' Mike quickly retorts, 'You honestly think that I don't look back on this year and no part of me mourns what I've done to my friends and the people that trust me?! Believe it or not, I hate what I did but it was what I believed had to be done! And hell, if you were SO concerned about what I was doing, then why the hell didn't you come back before now?!' 'I was too busy!! If I could've found the time. If I could've made it happen, believe me I would've. I couldn't do it, and believe it or not it was killing me!' Realizing how far out of hand the two of them are getting, he stops and takes a quick breath, 'Look, I'm here now and that's what matters.' 'Yeah, I guess you've got a point,' Mike answers, 'you got any plans for tonight?' 'Just a quick errand and then Supremacy.' Mike smiles, 'Well alright.' *** The next day, Mike sits in his office at Asylum Industries. He picks up his phone, planning to call in Deacon Blaise and Logan Cale for another pre-match conference. He hesitates, staring at the receiver for an instant before noticing a small package sitting on his desk. Laying down the receiver, he picks up the package and removes the wrapping. Pulling out the contents, he tosses the packaging into the trash and in his hands is a black Team Canada hockey jersey and a small note attached to the crest. It reads simply 'Remember who you really are.' Mike smiles and places the phone back in it's cradle. Perhaps this time, just for old time's sake, a different approach was in order.
