UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #233

A Flurry of Activity

20 Feb 2004

Mike Montgomery — The Halifax Offices of the Universal Wrestling Federation

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
We open in the offices of UWF Canada, specifically an office on loan to the Executive Vice President of Talent Relations...and part owner of the UWF, Mike Montgomery. The scene opens with Mike, standing with his back to the camera, looking out one of those really cool floor to ceiling windows that big offices have. Hands clasped behind his back, Mike looks out over a city buried in snow. His city. He almost seems to smile as he watches the people trying to dig out. 'Lot of hard work going on out there. Of course for Halifax, that really shouldn't be much of a surprise. Mother Nature seems to enjoy throwing disasters our way. Just last September, Hurricane Juan struck. Damage was in the millions, but we endured. Windows were repaired, people sent back to their homes, life went on. 'Now, we have what they're already calling the Blizzard of '04. A bit early for that. After all, it's only mid-February. Then of course we come to the UWF which has survived it's own share of near disasters. Tumultous ownership, people coming and going as they see fit, but still we endure. 'Speaking of imminent departures, I would be remiss to not address the apparent departure of one of our most popular superstars, namely, Moraless. You see Moraless, I think we need to clear up a bit of a misunderstanding. I like you. Whether I did or not, it would be foolish of me to get rid of you. You're popular. Win or lose, people want to see you in that ring. As a UWF administrator, I would be negligent in my duties to not give the people what they want. Therefore, I am duty-bound to do what I must to keep you in the UWF.' Mike reaches behind him, grabbing a coke bottle off the desk. He opens it and takes a large gulp, all without turning toward the camera. He puts the bottle back on the desk before continuing with his monologue. 'As I said before Moraless, you intrigue me. You haven't done much, and yet you're one of the more popular guys on the roster. That's quite the accomplishment, and a very unique talent. Yours is a charisma that is unparalleled. 'And from your latest promo, I see you're also a realist. Very smart of you. Sunday will indeed be the fight of your life, but mine as well. You see, the people like the Player's Club, but when it's down to you vs. me, the people will side with you every time. Like I said, the people love you. 'But of course, charisma doesn't win matches. The people won't win you matches. Having them on your side is a tremendous advantage, let's not forget that, but they're not the ones who have to score the 1...2...3. They can't do that for you. That's all on you. Think you can do that? Can you lock in the Moral Judgement and score that all important three count? 'That's the difference between us right there. See, when I'm looking across that ring at you, I'm thinking to myself 'I'm going to beat him.' Not because of my position in the Administration, not because I'm in the Player's Club. Once that bell rings, all those things are forgotten. I'm thinking I'm going to beat you simply because I know in my heart that I can do it. 'Your little prison routine won't scare me. It's a rather fascinating glimpse into your past, but that's all. I've done the prison thing, the goth thing, the mental ward thing, it's pretty much you name it and I've done it. But a victory against someone with drawing power? Someone like you? That's a rare pleasure. That's something I don't often get to say I've done. At the beginning of Supremacy, I'll be able to see I have the honor of stepping into the ring with you. By the end, I'll be saying I had the ability and the pleasure of beating you and causing your Fall From Grace.' Mike finally turns from his window to the camera. A small grin moves across his face as he leans in close. 'And tell your director friend that he's free to sue. 'You're going to have the best representation money can buy, courtesy of the Player's Club.'
