Dreamning: Part 1

14 Jul 2004

Fosta — Unknown

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I find myself in a room. The room is unfamiliar, but the ambiance I find myself surrounded by is not. Isolation. Loneliness. Remorse. Where am I? This room. It’s old, and dark. The walls should be painted white, but paint has peeled from them, reluctant to be included in the evil I feel coming at me in all directions. One single painting hangs from a wall. Enclosed in an intricately designed frame is a shining, black shoe. It looks as if it shines so strongly that light comes from it. It is the single light that shines in the room. But unlike most lights, I see no hope in this one. The shoe is menacing, powerful. It controls me. This shoe, with a small amount of the material belonging to the wearer’s pants, is all that is captured in the painting. The floor in the painting is identical to the floor I stand on. Wood: Decomposed and fragile. Am I supposed to stay in this room? Or am I supposed to make my way through the halls of this house? Is this room part of a house? Turning, I see one door on the wall farthest from me. Something is drawn on a piece of paper held to the door by a thumbtack. Taking a step towards it, I look down to the floor to ensure I don’t step on one of the many broken boards and see that I’m not wearing any shoes. I look down along my chest and stomach, right down to my toes without seeing one item of clothing. I’m naked. I feel my face start to burn red as I reach to my head. It is bald. I have no hair on my head, my beard is gone, and I have no clothes on. I feel vulnerable. Is there anything in this room to wear? No. I’m exposed, and I have no way of covering myself from anyone. But there is no one here. The only person I am naked in front of is myself. I am by myself. I am by myself and I am the only person here to judge me. Moving closer towards the door now, I can see what is sketched on the torn piece of paper. Nine warriors. Large, stone statues. One, two… nine of them. Each is identical. Each holds a sword with both hands. The swords run along their armour, with the point several inches from their face. Masks cover their faces. The masks resemble a demon, or a serpent. They look like Japanese Samurais. Giant stone statues of Japanese Samurais. Grabbing the cold metal door handle, I reluctantly begin turning it. Pushing the door open lets cold air flow from the next room. It sends chills down my naked back. I push the door open and see that in the next room, there are two doors on the opposite wall. Right and Wrong? Good and Evil? I take a step in and instantly, without warning, walls shoot from lines in the ground and all the way up to the room. I stand at the front of a corridor wide enough only for one person to move through. To my right is another corridor for me to turn down. In front of me are two more directions. At the end of the passageway is a t-intersection with a left and right. I can’t go straight ahead. I take my first step toward solving this maze...
