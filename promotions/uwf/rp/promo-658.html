UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #658

Off The Record

31 Aug 2004

Kyle Cross — Toronto, Ontario

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Camera comes up on Michel Lansberg ans the set of TSN's 'Off The Record' sports interview show. Lansberg is dressed casually, and stands before two empty chairs, facing each other. Michael: We've been doing these wrestling 1-on-1's as long as we've had a show here at 'OTR.' You ask for them, we've brought them to you. The Rock, Stone Cold, Foley, The Hardys, Heyman, Booker T, even Vince. But the truly special nights are when we have fellow Canadians on the show. We've had them all... or at least, all but one. For years, fans have been trying to get Kyle Cross on the show. Now, many of you who only follow wrestling here in North America may be saying 'Kyle who?', but for the last ten years this Canadian wrestler has been wowing fans in Japan, and earning a reputation as both a hardcore legend and as one of the most charismatic wrestler in the business. Now, he's signed a long-term contract with the Universal Wrestling Federation where he'll be locking horns the Wednesday against Malcolm Kennedy for the UWF Television Championship. Fade to OTR opening seqence. Afterwards the camera comes up on Lansberg & Cross sitting in the aformentioned chairs. Cross wears jeans and a 'BJPW' t-shirt. Michael: Welcome to the show, Kyle Kyle: Nice to be here. Michael: Are you still a Canadian? Will you be indroduced as being from Toronto when you come out to the ring on Wednesday? Kyle: I still use my Canadian passport. I'll always be a Canadian. It's is true though that coming here to Toronto to dilm this interview is the first time I've been back here in over a decade. The last time I was in Canada, Bruce Hart was giving me a lift to the airport in Calgary to fly to Japan for the first time. It was supposed to be a six-month talent exchange. It lasted ten years. Michael: But, despite the way it's being billed, this is not your first UWF run, is it?. Kyle: No, no it's not. A few months ago, I was approched by the UWF, and signed a contract. However, right at the last minute, some contractual problems arose in Japan. I was allowed to fly over here and do a couple of appearances, but neither the UWF or I put much behind it. It was all done real hush hush. They didn't want to get involved with me knowing I wouldn't be able to stay, and I thought I'd missed my big chance to break into the western market. Michael: So what's different this time? Kyle: Well... I went back to Japan and worked out the end of my obligation. Then I started doing a lot of freelance bookings over there, a lot of cross-promotional stuff... Then I heard about the Regal Rumble being in Japan, and I called the guys at UWF. I figured that they'd hang up on me... but instead they offered me a long term contract, and in my first match since the Rumble, I'm getting a shot at the UWF Television Championship. Michael: Will North American fans now get a chance to see the type of extreme action that made you a legend in Japan? Kyle: I would assume so. If only because that's a part of who I am. I don't expect to be booked in nothing but barbed-wire matches here in UWF, and anyone who thinks that's all I'm capable of is in for a shock. That being said, I've always gone to the limit for the fans. When they leave the arena on any given night, if it's not my match they're talking about, I regard it as a personal failure on my part. That applys every time, no matter if I'm the show opener or the main event. Michael: You're also know for frequently joining the fans in the stands, and sitting with them both before and after you matches. Will we be seeing any of that here in the UWF? Kyle: Count on it. They're the reason I put my body on the line. They're the ones who pay to go to the shows. They want us to be accessible to them. I didn't even realize the fan following I had in North America until I posted my e-mail address on a website of a Japanese promotion I was working for at the time... about four years ago. That was the first time I realized how huge the tape trading market had gotten over here. I was getting mail from all these fans, beggin me to sign with the WWE or UWF. So, now that I'm actually here, I owe it all to them. I'm sure that the UWF wouldn't have shown so much interest in me if not for those fans. Any career I have, I owe to them. Michael: So, do you think you can win on Wednesday night? Kyle: Look, Malcolm Kennedy is a very talented wrestler. He just won the title as a result of a stipulation in the Regal Rumble as I understand it. I'm sure he's going to be tough. I'm actually going right from the studio to my flight, but I have a little personal DVD player, and some DVD's of his matches that I'm gonna watch on the flight. Michael: Do you think he's ready for you? Kyle: He might be... but he might be too tied up in the hype. See, if he doesn't know anything about me, he just look at the way I was billed at the Rumble... a hardcore legend. If that's the case, he may figure he can just bide his time in the match and wait for me to get myself eliminated. Or maybe he'll figure that I'm useless unless I'm swinging a chair, so he'll figure he can outwrestle me. On the other hand, maybe he's off somewhere watching DVD's of me right now. Who knows? I will say this, if he doesn't bring his absolute best, he'll be leaving that ring 15 pounds lighter, because the UWF Television Championship will be over my shoulder. Michael: Now that's unexpected. Your whole career, you've made a point of not being a belt chaser, prefering instead to just be the most entertaining wrestler in the whichever promotion you were in... so now, here in the UWF, has that changed? Is it all about the belts now? Kyle: Not at all. Quite the opposite, in fact. But, this is a big fed. It has the biggest roster of any promotion I've ever worked for. But I've waited for a decade for my opportunity to come right into the living rooms of North American wrestling fans, and with a roster the size of UFW's, TV spots are difficult to come by. It won't be easy to get on every show... unless... Michael: Unless you win the one UWF title that has to be defended on every UWF broadcast. Kyle: Exactly. That's why the Television title means so much to me. Because I live for the time I spend in that ring, in front of my fans. That title is the key to me getting there. Michael: And then what? Kyle: If I win? I wrestle, every night, to keep it until somebody comes along who wants it more. After that, or if I lose... I don't know. The THC Title would seem like an obvious fit. And remember, I may be experienced, but I started young. I'm 28 years old and I've never been a World Champion... so there's always that. Michael: Well Kyle, we here at OTR wish you luck. You'll come back and do it again? Kyle: Sure will, Mike. Thanks for having me.
