Regal Rumble: Ghosts of the Past, Fears of the Future

13 Aug 2004

Tsunami Oni — Japan

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The suite is exquisite, obviously in a five-star hotel. And the view of Tokyo in the morning from the balcony is nothing short of breathtaking. There Tsunami Oni stands, stripped of his make-up, devil horns, and colored contacts. He wears a linen suit with a white T-shirt, one hand in his pocket and the other holding a drink as he stares at the city before. The door to the balcony slides open and out comes Shiatsu, dressed in a short, silk robe and looking like she just woke up. 'Sweetie,' Shiatsu says. 'How can you not be tired after that flight? It totally kicked my ass.' 'Hmm?' he answers. 'And drinking already?' 'I'm still on San Francisco time,' he answers. 'Uh huh,' Shiatsu replies. 'Whatever you gotta tell yourself. So what's up, babe?' 'Just thinking.' 'About what? The Rumble?' 'Among other things,' Oni answers. 'C'mon, if anyone deserves a main event slot at Live and Loud, it's you.' 'Sometimes I wonder about that,' Oni replies before taking a sip from his drink. Shiatsu looks stunned. 'Are you serious? Former World champ, IC champ, champ of champs? Ring a bell? Hall of Fame, maybe? Or how about Greatest Cruiserweight Ever?' 'That was then,' he answers. 'This now. I barely squeaked by those second-rate luchadores to get my contract. I've been out of active competition for a long time. And face it, smaller men like myself are at a disadvantage in these sorts of things. If I climb to the top rope, some could just knock me off and I'm done then and there.' 'Please,' Shiatsu says. 'You've beaten giants, sweetie! Remember a little someone named Danny Jaye? Or G. Streinbeck? Most the guys you've been in that ring with got you beat on size and you still come back on top.' 'It's not just that,' Oni replies. 'I'm thinking of something from my days of running around in that mask. When opponents would claim I had to cheat to win. That I need you to defeat them. And I would brush them off. Tell that I cheated for the pleasure of it, turned it into an art form. Now... now I wonder if they're right. Am I really the force I boast to be? Do I really live up to the Greatest Cruiserweight claim? Or am I just mists and frying pans, a one-joke show with no punchline anymore?' Oni takes another swig from his glass. Shiatsu walks up behind him and wraps her arms around his chest , resting her chin on her shoulder. 'Sweetie,' she says. 'You're really selling yourself short. Sure, you've had your share of tainted victories, but be realistic. You've done nothing that none of those other guys wouldn't do. The difference is you've been a lot better at it than most of them. You're the man than gave Unforgiven a fishermanbuster off a ladder and through a table. It was you that countered Lacuna Debris' attempt to slam you onto a steel chair and win the Contest of Champions. And what about last year's Live and Loud? You won that tourney with skill, pure and simple. You've earned everything.' 'But can I do it again?' Oni says. 'Do I still have the heart? Is the drive still there? Does the ruthless heart it takes to win still beat within my chest? I wonder, did my star rise so quickly just to burn out with the same velocity? There will be so many eyes upon here in my own homeland. Can I live up to my own hype?' 'Yeah, all those eyes on right here in Japan,' says Shiatsu. 'You're thinking about him, aren't you?' 'Yes,' Oni answers. Shiatsu turns Oni around and looks him right in the eyes. 'Listen, sweetie,' she says. 'You have to see him. This... nothing between you two, it's not right. You need to make things better. When you said who he was at the funeral, I just couldn't believe it. You were ghosts to each other. Things need to change.' 'It's been so long....' Ono begins. Shiatsu puts a finger to his lips. 'That shouldn't be an excuse to avoid him. That's a reason to go see him. As time passes, the bitterness just grows. You keep this up and the next time you see him will be at his funeral, if you would even care enough to attend. So take this opportunity t heal some really old wounds. Tsunami Oni lets out a sigh. Shiatsu gets him a playful punch in the arm. 'Look at you!' she laughs. 'The bad-ass Devil from the Deep Blue Sea is afraid of a little old man!' 'He's not quite that helpless,' Oni replies with a raised eyebrow. 'So you say. Damn, you're willing to step into the ring with ex-cons and headcases, but this is too much for you!' 'Fine, fine,' Oni says. 'I'll see him. I'll see him.' 'Great,' Shiatsu says. 'Now, this little lady is going back to bed. I don't wanna still be jet-lagged when I watch you win the Regal Rumble.' She gives him a peck on the lips and walks to the door. Tsunami Oni calls after her. 'Hey.' Shiatsu looks at him. 'I love you, you know.' Shiatsu smiles at her man, eyes twinkling. 'Sweetie,' she replies. 'Just go before I hurt you.' Japan is called the Land of the Rising Sun. But the city of Masuda is on the west coast and the view from the beach is the setting sun vanishing into the Sea of Japan. A well-dressed man in his late middle age stands there, staring at the view. He has the look of a hard man used to a hard life. If the beautiful scene before him has any impact on him, his eyes do not betray it. Nor do they reveal any reaction to the sounds of footsteps in the sand behind him. 'Back again?' says the man with turning. 'It was six years before your previous visit. And then it took the death of your mother to get you to return home. You do still count this as your home, don't you.' Behind the man stands Tsunami Oni, dressed as before, looking very subdued. 'I will always honor Masuda as my birthplace and Japan as my homeland,' replies Oni. The older man allows himself a humorless chuckle. 'Really? After so many years of hailing from Hiroshima.' 'That was part of my image in the past,' Oni answers. 'And for your image you lied. Just as you lie about your past. As you lied about your mother. The lies come so easily to you.' 'It was never out of shame.' This causes the older man to spin around a glare angrily at Tsunami Oni. 'Shame? What do you know about shame? Year after year of hearing nothing from my only child. Hearing stories of you painting your mother and I like a couple of fools for your own amusement. Watching you carry on with that whore. That is shame! AS you you've left off all that foolishness you usually parade around in, like some circus clown!' A cold wind blows, as if the elements have chose to reflect the moods of these to men They look at each other for long moments.' 'You never did forgive for becoming a wrestler,' Oni states. 'This is not about the wrestling,' his father replies. 'Wrestling helped fill the spirits of our people during the American occupation, just as the Yakuza filled the bellies.' 'You've always been so proud of your cronies,' Oni says with a touch of bitterness. 'Cronies?' growls the older man. 'That is your own blood you speak of! My blood, and that of uncles, grandfathers, ancestors going back many generations. But none of that mattered to you. You turned your back on all that and disappeared to God knows where.' 'Mexico,' Oni answers. 'I went to Mexico to train.' 'And you never said good-bye to me. And not one word at your mother's funeral.' Oni's blood heats up as he steps closer to his father. 'Do you think I enjoy this?' Oni exclaims. 'Do you think that family means nothing to me? It means more to me that anything!' 'Is that why you call yourself by that ridiculous name?' his father yells back. 'Will you give that up?' Oni shouts. 'What I call myself in the ring is not a slap to your face! It's a gimmick! Something to get attention! I'm not trying to hurt you!' 'Then it amazes me how much you've accomplished with no effort!' the older man turns his back on his son face the sea. Behind him, Tsunami Oni has his hands on hips as he inhales deeply, trying to reign in his emotions. 'Listen,' Oni says. 'just put the martyr act on the shelf fora second.' His father turns to glare at him. 'Don't act like this is all on me,' Oni continues. 'All the time I was a child, there was nothing from you. You weren't there.' 'I was working to provide for you!' 'I'm not talking about when you were out of the house. I'm talking about when you were there. All the times I came to you hoping for an ounce of recognition. To see that you were proud of me. That you supported my dreams. I would tell you about my hopes, my fears and and you couldn't tear yourself away from the damn paper! Tell me, do you remember me showing you this?' Reaching into his jacket pockets, Oni pulls out battered flier. He hands it to his father. The older man stares at it. It's for a wrestling show in Masuda, seven years past. And one of the competitors is named Yoshihiro Yamada. 'That was my debut match,' Oni explains. 'I trained at a dojo here in town. I left a flier just like that for you, remember?' The old man stares at the paper in his hand. 'I remember,' he says quietly. 'I thought it was junk mail...' 'Not really the best answer, father,' says Oni bitterly. 'you weren't the. And you weren't at any of the other matches I had for months after that. Each time you had business. Entertaining 'associates' by taking them to some sex club. Intimidating 'clients' that feared for their lives. I didn't disappear to Mexico. You just didn't notice I had left!' The older man still stares at the paper. 'I... I would like to keep this, if I may...' he says. Oni seems taken back for a moment. 'Sure,' he answers. 'Of course. In fact, I got something else for you.' Oni reaches into his jacket again, pulling out an envelope. 'They're tickets to the Regal Rumble in Tokyo. One's for you and there's some extra.' The hard look in the eyes of the father softens as he accepts the envelope. 'You really want me there?' 'Listen,' says Oni. 'We've both lived lives as heartless bastards. Criminals. Villains. And we can rationalize very action we've taken. Family, honor, destiny... whatever. But when my mother died, I did a lot of thinking. And one decision I made was that I wanted us to stop being heartless bastards to each other.' A smile creeps onto the older man's face. 'I would like that as well.' Oni returns the smile. 'Great.' 'You know, son,' says his father. 'You take after your mother a lot. She was the emotional one. The creative one. Myself, I was raised to be hard, just like my own father.' 'Yeah, I remember not liking my grandfather very much.' 'I felt like I never really connected to you. That we were never alike. Now I think about those qualities you share with your mother and how foolish I was not to try. Because that is how she lives on, through you. 'Thank you,' says Oni. 'You have no idea how much that means to me. So I'll see you in Tokyo?' 'Of course. And... I apologize for calling Shiatsu a whore.' Oni laughs. 'Forget about it,' he says. 'if someone doesn't call her that at least once a day, she thinks she's doing something wrong.' 'I'll be glad to meet her.' 'Me too.' And as the sun's last rays fall into the sea, a father and son embrace for the first time in far too long... It's late in the the evening when Tsunami Oni returns to his Tokyo hotel room. He opens the door to his room. Shiatsu is sitting on floor in simple jeans and a t-shirt, looking at a magazine. She looks up at her man. 'So...' she asks hopefully. Tsunami Oni kneels beside her with the merest glimmer of water in his eyes. He takes her hand in his. 'Thank you,' he says with a voice quivering with emotion. Shiatsu wraps her arms around him and holds him for a long time. 'You did good,' she whispers. 'You did good.'
