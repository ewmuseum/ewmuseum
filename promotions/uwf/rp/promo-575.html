UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #575

The easiest person in this world to disappoint is yourself...

25 Jul 2004

Fosta — SAS arena

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Fosta and Andy sit in front of a television broadcasting the live stream of Sunday Afternoon Supremacy. Fosta, not having been booked for the show, is dressed casually. He wears a black t-shirt and a pair of jeans. Written across his chest in white is ‘YES, THIS IS MY SAYING.’ Andy is in a black suit with a black pinstriped shirt, but his professional attire is contradicted by his posture. He slouches deep into his seat and his shirt is dotted with popcorn bits. Fosta is doing the same thing, although he has dropped less popcorn on himself than his manager. The bra and panties match is coming to an end, and our team appears very entertained with what they’re watching. The backstage area bustles with life. Producers moving back and forth, wrestlers talking to one-another, stretching. All this surrounds Andy and his wrestler who are oblivious, all their attention focused on the show. Tonight they are spectators. ‘Fosta!’ An unknown voice calls, ‘Focker needs you!’ Fosta glances backward as he pushes another handful of popcorn into his mouth. Looking at his watch for a minute before standing up, he speaks with Andy. ‘You coming with me?’ Andy shakes his head, his mouth too full for him to speak without spilling his half-chewed snack on himself. Brushing the crumbs off of his t-shirt, Fosta moves towards the interviewer. A blue-gray backdrop is hanging in front of a camera, and to the left of the camera is a bright light. For a set that is only ever erected for several hours at a time it is satisfactory. Focker is flipping eagerly through a folder of his, while the cameraman shouts at a crewmember to move the light to another position. A microphone hangs above Focker, who now stands in front of the camera. The cameraman now shouts at him, and Focker complies by shuffling a little to one side. The interviewer makes eye contact with Fosta, and Fosta comes to stand next to him. ‘Hello Fosta.’ Focker keeps flipping through his folder while he speaks. ‘Hey John, how are you?’ Focker looks as if he didn’t hear him. ‘Steve, can you move the light a little higher up please?’ A crewmember adjusts it. Focker closes the folder and places it at his feet. ‘We just have some questions about your television title shot.’ ‘Okay.’ He turns to the cameraman now. ‘We’re ready to role Bob.’ The cameraman lifts his hand and counts down with it. Three… two… one. ‘Welcome back fans, this is Sunday Afternoon Supremacy.’ John’s voice is laced with colorful tones now, and his tired, busy expression is disguised with an anticipating one. ‘Here with me now is one of the UWF’s newest signings, Fosta.’ He turns to Fosta. ‘Fosta, in three nights you get your first shot at a UWF title. You will face the winner of tonight’s television title match for your shot at the championship. How are you preparing for a match without knowing who your opponent will be?’ Fosta gathers his thoughts. Just play along. It won’t last forever. ‘John, it doesn’t matter who my opponent will be on Wednesday. What matters is that I know I’m better than both of these men. I’ve wrestled with Rich Rollins before and I’ve wrestled with Dave Storm, and you know what I learnt? Do you know what I found out when I was hit by those men?’ Fosta turns from John and towards the camera. Now he talks to the audience. ‘When Rich would wind back his fist and strike me in retaliation, something was missing. When Dave Storm threw a boot deep into my stomach, I could tell that there was something missing. It’s what differentiates the better wrestlers from the best wrestlers. It’s what wins matches that can go either way. And when your body aches and your head feels like it’s going to explode, it’s what makes you get back up and continue.’ A grin escapes out the side of Fosta’s mouth, like a crack in a brick wall. ‘Rich and Dave don’t have it, John,’ He looks up towards the ceiling. John’s eyes follow Fosta’s but he is lost as to what he is staring at. The smirk disappears from Fosta’s face as quickly as it emerged, and he looks back to the camera. He looks through the lens into the minds’ of his potential opponents. He wants to pick them apart further. Understand every motivation and purpose for all of their actions. ‘But Ido. I have a reason to wrestle, which will take me further than anger ever could. Further than the quest for perfection will ever take a man. The easiest person in this world to disappoint is yourself, John, and that’s why I am going to win my match on Wednesday Night Underground; because I’m not only wrestling for myself on Wednesday night. I’m wrestling for two people.’ Fosta stops talking. He glances up toward the ceiling again, and runs his hand along his beard. A small amount of hesitation delays his next sentence, and at a whisper, he speaks. ‘I’m wrestling for my brother.’ John gives him a moment, then probes further into this revealing statement that Fosta just made. ‘Fosta… who is your brother?’ Andy has moved toward the camera, and now he stands behind the cameraman. Fosta glances toward him, and they make eye contact. Andy nods towards Fosta, and through eyes full of empathy, smiles in support. ‘After the battle royal I told my manager Andy Hawkins that I should have won the match; that I was the best man in the ring. And I believe it. He asked me why I lost the match if I was the best man in it..’ He takes a deep breath. ‘Eighteen months ago, my brother was killed in a bank robbery, and I was responsible for his death… Shots were fired, and I was scared. I grabbed my brother in fear of his life, and pulled him in front of the bullet that killed him. Had I not been there, my brother would be with me today.’ He brushes his hair back, a sad laugh is forced out his nose. ‘He was one of the best wrestlers I had seen. I would stand in awe on the apron when I watched him wrestle. He was on an inevitable path to become one of the best entertainers this business would ever see, and when I pulled my little brother into me to try and protect him I killed him. When he died I lost half of myself. I feel like a child lost in a shopping center. I lost the battle royal because I couldn’t admit this to myself. I didn’t know what to do about the situation I was in. I didn’t know how to forgive myself and move on, but now I do. He was better than me. He was my better half. After the battle royal I made a promise to my brother Joshua Baldwin. I promised him that from that day onward, I would be as good as he was. As focused as he was. That the determination and heart he would bring to everything he done would be in me. And that’s what I have that my opponent won’t on Wednesday night. I will have the focus, the determination and the heart of Joshua Baldwin; the best man I will ever know…
