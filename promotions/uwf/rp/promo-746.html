UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #746

A Day at the Gym

19 Oct 2004

Joshua Justice — Justice Jym!

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[The scene opens up in front of a gym. Oh, this isn’t just an everyday gym! No, it’s not that simple. This is the Justice Jym, spelled that way for some ungodly reason. The public relations person probably thought that using the letter J would be cute. Far see it that way. So, other than the corny-ass name, the gym is fantastic. The gym has state of the art equipment along with the best trainers in the world all in one place. Who could ask for more? Oh yes, we could! To top it all off, the owner of the gym is a celebrity! Well, it’s not any huge celebrity. Photographers aren’t outside of the gym trying to catch him doing something for the papers. No, he’s not Britney Spear’s new boyfriend! God, I can’t believe you would think that? UWFs very own Joshua Justice is the proprietor of this gym. Yeah, I know that makes you excited. You know what else should make you excited? Today he’s doing some personal training for FREE! For setting up the scene, this is way too much to put in it… dammit. Walking in the front door, the UWF cameraman is greeted by a ‘greeter’ kind of like in Wal-Mart. Well, in Wal-Mart there is an old person usually… Here at the Justice Jym, there is a nice fit woman in a bikini. The greeter says a seductive ‘hello’ to the cameraman and the cameraman rushes away before he has an accident in his pants! Before the cameraman can go any farther, he runs into Joshua Justice himself.] Justice: Hello kind sir! [The cameraman stops in his tracks and focuses his camera on Justice.] Justice: Good morning! Today, I’m going to show you the proper work out for your arms in order to get fantastic arms like these— [Justice poses for the camera with a huge smile on his face and then continues to speak.] Justice: These are the same arms that are going to escalate my career in the UWF. These are the same arms that are going to pound away on this Dreammaker fellow. Now, follow me and we shall begin. [Justice walks over to where the weights are located in the gym. He takes a seat on a bench and raises a dumbbell with about forty pounds on in to behind his head.] Justice: First, we will do seated one-arm dumbbell over-head extensions. [Justice positions it where his upper arm is vertical and his elbow is extended.] Justice: You want to lower the dumbbell as far as you comfortably can behind your head, which usually results in the weight touching your upper back lightly in the bottom most position. [He pauses for a second; feeling an intense stretch along the entire length of his triceps, before raising the weight back to the starting position.] Justice: You want to make sure to keep the upper half of your working arm stationary. [He does a quick few more and then he switches arms.] Justice: However many reps you complete, make sure you match that with the other arm to prevent the creation of a strength imbalance or exacerbating any existing imbalance. [After doing the same amount of reps with the opposite arm, Justice puts down the weight. He stands up and stretches his arm before walking over to a pull up bar.] Justice: Having balance in your arms strength is good so that you can overpower opponents like Dreammaker where they will never know what hit them. The arm strengthening exercise we will do next is called a close-grip chin. [Justice jumps up and grabs a hold of the bar above him. Using a narrow underhand grip, Justice hangs from the bar.] Justice: You want to pull, rather than jerk… [Pulling, Justice lifts his body till his chin approaches the bar. Lowering himself slowly, he returns to the starting and repeats.] Justice: If you have trouble lifting your bodyweight, I recommend that you use an assisted version of this such as using a Gravitron machine. [Justice does a few more chin ups to round his reps off before dropping back down to the ground. He stretches yet again while moving on to where some weights are set up for some curls. He positions the seat pad so that when he sits down his elbows rest nearer to the bottom than the top of the vertical side of the arm pad.] Justice: Now, hold the weighted bar with a shoulder-width grip and bend your elbow slightly before the rep even begins. We will do a preacher-bench EZ-bar curl. [He flexes his elbows in earnest to begin curling the weight at a moderate pace, under control. He stops once the bar nears his chin, squeezes his biceps hard for a one-count, then slowly lowers the bar back down.] Justice: You want to slowly lower the bar rather than simply letting it drop. You wan to work the muscles going both ways. [He sets down the bar and stands. He walks over to where the parallel bar is. After selecting parallel dip bars that are slightly wider than his shoulders, he lifts himself using a neutral grip.] Justice: You want to do the parallel-bar dip with your palms facing your body. [He lifts himself until his arms are fully extended and his torso is nearly upright.] Justice: The closer your arms are to the sides of your body, the greater the triceps stimulations is. In this starting position, your knees should be bent at a ninety-degree angle with your ankles crossed. Without letting your elbows flare out and keeping your torso as vertical as possible, you should bend your elbows to lower your body as far as you comfortably can in a controlled descent. At this point, you should feel a stretch in your shoulder joints. [He straightens his elbows to lift his body back up, pushing until his arms are fully extended. He lets go of the bar and stands up. He looks into the camera.] Justice: If you’re a beginner like Dreammaker, and find the dip exercise too difficult, try going through a shorter range of motion and then gradually increasing the depth. Also useful are machines that allow you to do the exercise with less than bodyweight. Now, let’s go to our last exercise today. We’re going to do something so simple that the Dreammaker could understand it. We’re going to do standing alternate dumbbell curls. [Justice walks over to where the free weights are and picks up a pair of dumbbells and holds them at arms’ length so that his palms are facing each other.] Justice: You want to stands with your feet approximately shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, torso erect, low back slightly arched and abs tight. [He begins curling one dumbbell towards his shoulder, turning his wrist as he goes so that by the time the dumbbell approaches his shoulder his palm is facing up.] Justice: At full contraction, you want to squeeze your bicep hard and then lower the weight back down to the side. [He repeats using the other am, continuing in alternating fashion until he complete his reps. He sets down the dumbbells and looks towards the camera, while flexing.] Justice: Now, just look at the results! You should do three to four sets of eight to ten reps each. You do this every other day and you too can look spectacular and not like Dreammaker! [Justice smirks and steps away from the weight area. He walks back towards the front door of the gym.] Justice: It was nice to have you here today. My opponent that I am challenging for the Television Title is over-confident after his ‘big’ win. His fight was nothing compared to what I went through. I beat what was it… SIX other men in order to get the chance to take on the Television Champ. You beat one guy in a cage. Big freakin’ deal! Some last parting words before I kick this out-of-shape poor excuse for a cameraman out of my gym… Eat right, drink plenty of orange juice, and remember… JUSTICE ALWAYS PREVAILS! [He lets out a half-funny half-strange battle cry and boots the cameraman out of the door. He shuts the door behind him and walks away as the scene fades to black.]
