Day 11: Downtown Dregs

30 Jul 2004

'The Renegade' Rich Rollins — Chicago's Downtown Loop

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Day 11 “You want to know how Chicago started? A bunch of guys in New York got together and said, ‘Hey, I like the crime and poverty all right, but it just isn’t cold enough. Let’s move west!’” -Annonymous [Dusk.] [As the sun slowly fades over the jagged cityscape of the Windy City, an odd orange-red glow encases the sprawling metropolis as it does every night, a synthetic sunlight generated by the looming buildings and productivity below. The steady, snaking streams of taxies and buses slither by as if on fast-forward, the lives of the people within focused on their own business. Some said that at this exact time of the evening, just a few minutes after the sun had said its farewell on the day, the city of Chicago became the gate to Hell itself. The glow was maddening to those who weren’t used to it, but on this particular evening, it doesn’t bother one man.] [The lapel of his black leather jacket pulled close to his face, the wind whipping his hair in spirals across the nape of his neck, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins walks through the bustling commerce of downtown Chicago, his self-concerned look and steady walk speaking volumes of his desire not to be bothered. Up around the Downtown Loop, one block over from the Miracle Mile that is the heart and soul of Chicago, people act much differently than in The Renegade’s neighborhood. He noticed it every time he made one of these trips; people didn’t act like strangers, even if they were. Even the beggers dressed like they might actually have a job somewhere, and did their begging with a full set of teeth in their head as opposed to spitting nonsense around Rollins’ block.] [Quite a different beast, the Loop. The term “ghetto“ no longer applied.] [Couples sat and ignored the roar of the elevated train as it wooshed overhead, sending half a dozen fat pigeons in as many directions, all the while sharing an ice cream within the confines of the nearest Baskin Robbins. A Krispy Kreme on every street corner. DePaul University, across Wabash Avenue from Roosevelt University of the Arts.] [Despite himself, Rich Rollins shivers at all the productivity. This kind of wall-to-wall window shopping was most definitely not his kind of neighborhood.] [Shoving his hands in the pockets of his jacket, Rollins saunters on past another dounut shop, ignoring the glint in a man’s eye he catches as he walks on. Just another begger, looking for his next hand out. The man shrinks back as Rollins pushes past him, the icy cold chill of his eyes radiating intensly enough for the poor begger to keep his distance all together. Sighing and shaking his head, The Renegade presses on. Toward salvation. Toward redemption.] [Or, more accurately, toward his fucking paycheck.] [Finally stopping in front of a small shop entrance, Rollins checks the sign. The simple neon green words “Lacy’s Pawn” shines back at him, and he grins. Pushing the door open, he steps into a wall of scented car freshener air as the little bell rings, signaling his arrival.] Female Voice: Shop’s closed, whoever you are. [The voice practically drips with impatience, which widens The Renegade’s grin even further. Strolling past a few ailes of random junk, he hops on top of an empty cashier counter and knocks his palm flat on the polished surface.] Renegade: Surprise. Woman: Ack! [Startled, an incredibly attractive woman in her late twenties lurches up from underneath the counter, almost smashing her head in the process. A shiny mane of golden hair falls down her shoulders, accenting the simple black belly shirt and athletic pants barely covering her lithe figure.] Renegade: Hello, Lacy. Did you miss me? Lacy: Jesus Christ, you almost gave me a heart attack! How did you get in? I locked the door an hour ago. Renegade: It opened just fine for me. Lacy: Well, fuck. Renegade: What’s the matter? Lacy: That must mean… (she sighs)… look, I’ve been dealing with these rag-tag north-siders who’ve skimped on their bets for three straight weeks now. I thought they broke into the place the other night, and might have stolen a key. Renegade: Want me to kill ‘em? [Lacy laughs. The Renegade doesn’t.] [She stops laughing as his look implies he wasn’t joking. Trying to break the awkward silence, she starts shuffling a few papers underneath the cash register.] Lacy: Um… no. I think I can handle myself, thank you. Renegade: Whatever. Anyway, I came by for my payment, if you don’t mind. Lacy: (dripping with sarcasm) Well shucks… and here I thought you just wanted take me to dinner. What were you betting on? Renegade: Myself. [Lacy stops shuffling papers and looks up. Rollins meets her inquisitive gaze unflinchingly, and she returns it in kind. Truth be told, this woman was one of the only people Rollins had ever known not to be intimidated by his stare.] [Not that it bothered him.] Lacy: So, you laced the boots back up, huh? Renegade: Yup. Lacy: I thought you gave it up. Renegade: I did. [Silence.] [Placing both hands on the countertop, Lacy drills holes with her eyes into Rollins’ face, her body language screaming of impatience.] Lacy: So where do I fit into all of this? You know I stopped watching wrestling since… well, since the incident. [“The incident.” A much easier way to say “kidnapping.”] [The Renegade merely nods somberly, averting his gaze to her polished fingernails tapping on the countertop. Two years ago, Lacy had been abducted by a homicidal serial killer in the greater Chicago area, and was thought to be dead by police officials and the FBI. What the police and Federal agents hadn’t counted on was Rollins’ tenacity to find her.] [Not to mention his feelings about the blonde bombshell. But he would never even admit that much to himself.] [They never found the killer.] Renegade: I know, Lacy. I know. But I’ve been waisting the prime of my life in a shithole dump in this city for far too long. I would have gone insane had I not signed with the UWF… Lacy: The Universal Wrestling Federation? Damn boy, you really picked a nice new watering hole for yourself. Renegade: Anyway, I’m their World Television Champion and successfully defended my title for the second time against a guy named Fosta. I was betting on myself. What were the odds? Lacy: Who was your bookie? Renegade: A guy who works for you around my neighborhood. Calls himself Becker. Lacy: Becker?! [Her sudden alarm makes Rollins stop in his tracks. Arching his eyebrows, he senses her immediate discomfort.] Renegade: What’s wrong? Lacy: That’s one of those hood rats I was telling you about. Renegade: I thought you said they were on the north side. Lacy: I thought so, I didn’t know so. Renegade: So… I take it my bet is null and void? Lacy: Well, I sure as hell didn’t see any of the money. How much are you out? Renegade: About five grand. [Lacy pushes back from the countertop, absently running one well-manicured hand through her mane of blonde locks. Taking a minute to think, she looks The Renegade hard in the eyes with an idea.] Lacy: Who’s your next defence against? Renegade: Jonny Roxx. Talented little bastard who thinks he’s a rock star. Lacy: (laughing) I’m sure he does. Renegade: So what does he have to do with my getting back my money? Lacy: Well, since we go way back and all, I’ll take your five grand on the house. I’ll have to check the statistics, but I’m sure you could at least double it, supposing you win. Renegade: “Supposing”? Lacy: Okay, when you win. There’s just one catch. Renegade: And that catch is? [Lacy leans back into the countertop, almost touching Rollins’ nose with her own.] Lacy: You find Becker, and you knock his dick in the dirt. [She smiles, and licks the tip of his nose.] Lacy: Are we clear? [The Renegade leans back, the hint of a wry grin tickling the corners of his mouth.] Renegade: Crystal. [Without another word, The Renegade turns on his heels and leaves the pawn shop, depositing himself back into the thick traffic of sidewalk jockeys and taxi cabs. Yup, there was no doubt about it.] [Oh, how he hated Downtown Chicago.] [Fade to black.] ~Fin~
