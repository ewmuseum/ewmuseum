All Good Things Must Come To An End (Part 1)

Saturday, Aug 28, (year unrecorded)

Roy Lewis — Downtown Los Angeles, California

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The sky turned black as the sun set and a red and bloody moon rose over the City of Angels. The sky was clear of clouds and for the first time that anyone could remember, the people of Los Angeles could see a starry sky. It was truly a night when something amazing was going to happen. Amazing and possibly destructive and evil. The City was beaming, with the sounds of cars honking and sirens blaring, the city was its normal old self. No-one looked up to the starry sky to see what they may never see though the pollution again. No-one except one man, perched atop the side of one of the city’s many skyscrapers; watching as the insignificant “ants” went though their normal, boring lives. His life was anything but boring; it was one of death and destruction, where lovers are torn apart and the innocent fall victim to the guilty. He was a demon walking among modern society. He recalled a time when he was just like those people, leading long and boring lives, how foolish he had been. Memories flashed though his mind of a young man jumping off the top rope, diving of ladders, holding up his first Championship Belt, standing beside his best friend as she walked down the aisle toward him… “That life is over for me.” His voice was deep and disturbing but at the same time it was also soft and almost forgiving. His face was unseen by the long black hair he had covering it, but as he shot a glance at the sky his hair fell back revealing a pale and emotionless man. He may have been handsome once but now there were only a couple of scars and a dirty and tangled beard. He shot a glance down the side of the building, almost a hundred stories. His intentions this night were for the best, He would soon be with his love. As I walk though the valley of the Shadow of death, I fear no evil. The overused saying was used once more, for this time death would come swiftly. The dark night would hide his descent, no one would see him until he hit the ground and even if they did, anyone dumb to try and catch him would they themselves me crushed under the weight of his falling body. He could have a chosen another way to meet his end, one not so ‘messy’. He kept a gun in his apartment, just in case. He could have done it teen style and slit his wrists or he could have hung himself, but he thought this was the most dramatic way to the end of the Roy Lewis Saga. A sudden flutter of wings caught his eye and he turned to left, the direction of the sound. Perched on the side of the building about a meter beside him was a Raven. An omen of Death, great. The bird was unnaturally large and it looked out into the skyline, like its eyes were transfixed on something floating in the sky. Roy glanced in the direction the bird’s eyes stared. There was nothing there just the City skyline, maybe it was staring at the Hollywood, maybe it was a tourist bird. Roy mentally slapped himself for that one, the thought was idiotic, not the thought of a man about to kill himself. Whatever the bird was staring at wouldn’t matter in about thirty seconds. Roy stood up, staring at the concrete far below he raised his arms in a crucifix pose and shifted his weight forward till he was leaning far over the edge. He turned one last time to the Raven, and the Raven stopping staring into the void and turned its dark head to Roy. Roy stared into its eyes and the Raven stared back. There was something wrong with its eyes they weren’t birds eyes but rather green like a person’s. Suddenly a voice in his head was shouting at him, telling him what he was about to do was wrong, a mistake. “No.” He said out loud to the voice in his head. “I must do this” “No, you don’t…” The voice was no longer shouting, it was now soothing, and made him calm. “I don’t care” This last statement shocked Roy to the point where he almost fell over the side of the building. He caught himself though and instead fell back on his ass. He sat on the moist concrete, staring up at the Raven. Its eyes were back to the way they should be. It glanced at him one last time the bird that had such an affect on him flew off in the direction of which it had stared. Roy felt wetness on his cheek that moved down over his lips. Tears. He hadn’t cried in almost a year. A simple bird had saved his life, and brought back to him want little sanity was still his. ___________________________________________________________________ He once again walked with the living, though he still didn’t want to. The voice in his head, the voice of the Raven, the voice of Jessica, it had allowed him to live another day. He just wanted to make his way to his apartment and drink himself stupid, till he could not remember why he was so miserable. The streets were packed with people enjoying the nightlife; he wished he could be as cheerful. He looked down an ally as he walked past. About halfway down was homeless person. He sat there on an old ragged blanket. I guess it could be worse he thought. He continued on down the street. Everywhere he looked couples were having fun together, spending time together, it was dizzying. His world was spinning, he couldn’t think, it was all a blur. Images where flashing, memories, ones he didn’t want to remember, painful ones, violent ones. A jolt snapped him back to reality. A person in a black Disturbed hoodie had bumped into him. He tried to get a look at they’re face but it was covered by the hood. They didn’t stop or look back. “Sorry” Roy mumbled. He turned and began to walk away. “Apology accepted” Roy spun around, the voice was cold and haunting, it felt like his insides had been turned to ice. He knew that voice, bells were ringing in his head. The hooded person was gone, and the voice was now replaced with a shill scream. Everything happened in slow motion from that point on. As he glanced to his left he saw a women scream and began to reach out from the other side of the street. A few feet beside Roy, standing in the street near him, was a young boy, no older then 5. The woman was screaming because a car had just taken the near corner at full speed and was heading straight for the kid. It would never be able to stop in time; it was way over the speed limit. Roy’s reaction was purely out of impulse. If he stopped to think the kid would be nothing more then a blood stain on the car’s grill. “Shit kid! LOOK OUT!” He ran as fast as he could and tackled the kid out of the way. They hit the ground as the car rushed pass, just then hitting his brakes. When the car came to a stop the driver got out and ran to them. He was ranting about “this dumbass kid”. The concrete hurt like a bitch and a rock had jabbed in to his rib, and the kid was crying. He may have gotten a bruise, but it was better then dead. The women ran into the middle of the street, almost getting hit herself, and knelt beside the boy. She picked him up and hugged him tight. She was crying too. “Thank you” She managed between sobs. “No Problem” He responded painfully and he picked himself up off the ground “We should get out of the road” Traffic was backed up in both directions. Roy helped the women and her son to the sidewalk, while the man got back in his car and drove off. Roy made sure the kid was fine and turned to the crowd. Quite a number of people had collected to see what the commotion was about and in the middle of them was the hooded person. Whoever it was had his back turned to him so he could not see his face, if it was indeed a he. ‘Hoodie’ started to walk away and as they did, they gave him a thumbs up. The crowd closed in around him to congratulate him, and cut off his path to the hooded person. He would find who it was, just not tonight. The people around him made him think though. If he killed himself, the boy would be dead too. He suddenly felt a little better. For once he actually wasn’t mad at himself for not finishing the job. He looked up and the starry sky. “Don’t get you’re hopes up” He said to no one in particular as he finished shaking hands and walked toward his apartment. “All good things must come to an end”
