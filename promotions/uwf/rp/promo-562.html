Fosta — The haunted house, the arena

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A maze. Every wall is exactly the same. How am I supposed to make a right decision? Nothing to judge on but instinct, and trusting my instinct has only every lead to trouble in the past… I begin to walk, and move past the first turn. Two more steps and I am facing a wall. Just left and right. Will turning right lead me to the door on the right? Does the door I enter matter? I move to the left. It just feels right… Left turns, right turns, dead ends. It goes on for quite a long time with no indication that I am moving in the right direction. But eventually I make a turn, and stare at a door. Relieved, I move my hands down the wood towards the handle. Another moment of anticipation. I have no idea what is behind this door. It could be another maze. It could be a dead end. It could offer an answer as to where I am and where I’m going, or it could bring up more questions. My hand finds the doorknob, and I begin to turn it. Pushing it open I see that the next room is empty, except for a mirror that sits in the middle of the room. Odd. Each step I take into the room echoes. They bounce off the walls and back to my ears. My own reflection reinforces my nakedness to myself. This house seems to make me look at myself. Why am I naked in here? Why am I staring at a mirror? ‘You’re never leaving this house, you know?’ My reflection? It just spoke to me… ‘You don’t have the brains.’ I turn away from it for a minute. This is too weird. Everything about this dream is too weird. I want out. Looking back, I stare into my own eyes. ‘What are you talking about?’ I stuttering, I ask another question. ‘Why do I want to get out of here?’ ‘You put yourself in here.’ He smiles menacingly toward me. ‘Doubt. Regret. This has trapped you an a room that you need to escape.’ I try to talk, but he cuts me off. ‘You need to escape from the haunted house that is your mind. But you won’t.’ Chuckling, he looks to the ground. ‘You’re trapped in your own mind forever.’ ‘Is this about my brother?’ ‘Everything is about your brother!’ He steps out of the mirror as if it is an open window. ‘Is this what he wants to see?!’ He steps towards me and I take two steps back. ‘A drunken, hopeless nobody?!’ I feel the cold wall against my back now, and he moves closer. ‘Your brother is dead, and he has taken every shred of desire from you to his grave!’ ‘No!’ ‘Yes, Fosta!’ ‘NO!’ Anger, frustration and fear. I throw my fist towards him in denial, and like glass he smashes in front of me. * * * * * Fosta sits on one of a flight of steps, his face hidden behind a wall of matted brown hair. He wears his wrestling pants with an old tshirt. His boots have been pulled off, and rest on their side a few feet away from his, admitting defeat. To his right is Andy. Clad in a white dress shirt and black pants, he paces back and forth behind Fosta. His hands rest on his hips in annoyance. ‘Fosta. There were ten men in that match. It doesn’t matter how good you may be as a wrestler, you had no chance of winning that match.’ ‘I should have won Andy.’ His voices comes seething from behind his hair. ‘I’m better than the men in that match.’ ‘Then stop telling yourself that, and ask yourself why you lost.’ Andy has stopped walking, and looks down at the back of Fosta’s head. ‘I have no doubt in my mind that you are the best man that entered the battle royal that you just lost. I’ve seen you wrestle.’ Fosta looks up towards Andy now. He brushes the hair from his face. ‘Tell me why you lost that match, Fosta, and we can make sure it never happens again.’
