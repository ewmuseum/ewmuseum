UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #532

New Beginning

13 July (year unrecorded)

Fosta — A rest area in the desert

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Five o’clock on a Sunday morning. From a small, dusty rest area beside the forgotten road that was once a freeway, immeasurably large desert nothingness leads to a mountainous horizon. The sun rests on the jagged line illuminating cloud in a bright orange and pink. An eagle hunched as if holding something precious in his grasp, leaps into flight and is instantly one hundred feet above the ground, leaving the hillside rest area to hunt mice. The tired groan of a bus echoes over the valley as the breaks slow it for another sharp turn. Unwillingly the bus moves into the opposite lane trying to stay on the narrow road and moves toward the rest area, which contains a bin, to bench seats and two toilets. Rocks crunch against one another and dust is lifted as the wheels come to a halt and the doors fold on themselves and open with a hiss. Most of the passengers exit the vehicle with intent to take photographs of the beautiful scene, visit the bathrooms, or smoke a cigarette. One is a weary but eager man with an untidy auburn beard and darker scruffy hair. Several beads and braids in his hair show an odd self-consciousness that isn’t suggested in any of the man’s other traits. He wears a white tank top and gray tracksuit pants, and moves toward the edge of the rest area to look over the valley. Pulling a packet of cigarettes from his pocket, he removes one and holds it with his lips, lighting it with his other two hands. The smoke fills his lungs and chemicals cloud his blood, soon moving to his brain. He exhales the smoke, and with it his anxiety. The singlet exposes two large, rock hard arms that come from broad shoulders. They relax as he rests against a rail. He enjoys the picturesque landscape while his cigarette burns between his lips, and takes another drag before staring at it with dislike. I really need to give these things up. One more reluctant drag, and the cigarette is stomped out. While considering having another the man moves towards the bus, and a phone rings in his pocket. Who could that be? He answers. ‘Hello?’ ‘fosta. It’s Andy.’ The voice crackles over the mobile phone. ‘Where are you?’ He glances at his phone for a second. ‘I’m about two hours out. Are you still picking me up?’ Andy hesitates for a moment. ‘I can’t in two hours, I have to be somewhere.’ ‘You have to be somewhere?’ ‘You’ll have to catch a taxi…’ fosta sighs. ‘Okay. No problem. I’ll see you later Andy.’ He flips the phone shut and puts it in his pocket while getting back onto the bus. Two hours and it all starts again. I just can’t wait. Settling back into his seat, he prepares for the final two hours of the daylong trip. Passengers climb back on board while those sleeping are frustrated by the interruption. The driver announces the next stop to fosta’s satisfaction, and the bus leaves the rest area. He soon returns to the slumber the others are so fond of. Five Years Earlier Fosta looks rather different. The beard and long hair have since replaced a clean-shaven face, and short brown hair. The anxiety is absent from his mind and standing next to him is his younger brother. Slightly less muscular, but equally as toned as his older brother, Josh stands side by side with his brother in a queue at a bank. The familiar smell that every bank has fills the air. Walls of course are white. Adorning them, of course, are advertisements for various loan offers and the like. Rather than the boredom that usually accompanies one in a bank queue, both brothers are very eager. Fosta looks to his brother with a smile familiar to him. ‘First stop at the Royal, right bro?’ His brother looks back mischievously. ‘Yeah, of course. I have a score to level with you. I’ve been practicing my pool.’ A large enthusiastic smile greets his brother’s challenge, and Fosta reaches around his brother, resting his arm on his shoulder. ‘It’ll take you six consecutive wins to bring the score even Josh. Last time we got drunk at mine we played a few games.’ Josh’s silence, which resulted from shock, is soon broken. Three black figures rush into the bank, accompanied by screams from the customers and the spray of bullets. Fosta and Josh hit the ground, and Fosta covers his ears. Both are huddled very closely together as the rounds buzz over their heads, bringing death to people unfortunate enough to be in their path. Fosta yells to his brother, who looks up to him. Sorrow and regret pour from his eyes as they well. ‘I’m going to die, bro.’ A tear rolls down his cheek. ‘You’re not. You’re not going to die.’ He pulls him close… A small shout comes from Fosta, and he sits up straight in his chair on the bus. A tear has rolled down his cheek and disappeared into his beard.
