UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #559

Crossroad to Nowhere

17 Jul 2004

'The Renegade' Rich Rollins — Slums of South Chicago

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[The sun has just barely begun to peek over the horrizon of Lake Michigan, bringing with it another day of commerce in the Windy City. Early morning dew still soaks the grass. A morning not unlike any other Saturday morning… children stay cooped up in their homes as the sun continues to rise, either sleeping in or watching cartoons. Going to the comic book store. Buying an ice cream or ticket to Wrigley Field. Hot dogs. Cotton candy. Bus tickets and subway passes. SubWay sandwiches. ] [Mindless consumers.] [Across the city from all of this productivity, away from the glowing center of society, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins sits ridgid in a burnt-out sofa, cigarette burns and broken springs popping out the side every time he shifts his weight. The windows of the seedly little bar he’s been calling home for the past couple of years are covered with moth-eaten blankets pulled from trash bins around the corner, allowing faint rays of silver sunlight to play across the wood floor, exposing thousands of dust motes in the few beams of light filtering into the otherwise dark interior. The only sound in the entire building is that of the small color television sitting on a cardboard box against the wall, in front of Rollins. Holding his rapt attention.] [Stop.] [Rewind.] [On the screen, the final moments of the Television Championship match from Stable Wars plays itself out, the sounds faintly distorted with the poor quality of the cheap television. Ruff Ryder begins chopping Jay Vyper in the middle of the ring, but Vyper turns things around and lands a superkick… followed by another superkick to Loverboy… the Snake Byte lands…] Tommy Waters: (on-screen voice) …winner, and NEW Television Champion… [Stop.] [Rewind.] [ … Vyper superkicks Ruff Ryder, superkicks Loverboy … the Snake Byte … ] Tommy Waters: (on-screen) … NEW Television Champion… [Stop.] [Rewind.] Tommy Waters: … NEW Television … [Rollins sighs. He’d been sitting in this uncomfortable sofa all night, reviewing tapes and interviews. Supremacy matches. Underground segments. An entire crash-course on the history of the Television title over the last two months lay on the floor around the old television and its cardboard pedistal, littered among the remains of too many late nights with Jack Daniels to keep The Renegade company. When a bar was where you hung your hat at the end of the day, sometimes it was the people that weren’t really “there” that kept you going through the night. Jack Daniels. Sam Adams. Good ol’ Bud and the Green Fairy.] [Rollins had gotten a kick over that. A fan from Germany had sent him a bottle of Absence, a drink banned in the United States due to its hallucinogenic qualities. For a week straight, The Renegade went a couple rounds with the Green Fairy every night. His way to escape. And he loved every minute of it.] [Running one dirty hand through his mop of dirty shoulder-length brown hair, Rollins yawns and rubs the sleep out of his eyes. Or rather, the sleep he never took. Every time he closed his eyes, another image would open them. Images of his past. Old opponents ready to settle old grudges, or old promoters screwing him out of another paycheck. Images of his future. Sitting alone and desperate in this shithole roach motel for the rest of his life, becoming one of those old hermits people told outrageous stories about.] [But sometimes the truth is stranger than fiction.] …OCK-KNOCK-KNOCK-KNOCK-KNOCK-KNOCK-KN… [Jerking himself upright, The Renegade gives a little start at the abrupt interruption. Blinking repeatedly, he glances over at the inset clock in the VCR above his television, and realizes he had fallen asleep. Pulling on a white tank top that had been tossed over the top of his sofa, The Renegade stumbles through a door into the main bar area. Without bothering to move one of his “window curtains” to see who was pounding on his door, Rollins pulls back the latch and swings it open.] [At first, all The Renegade can see is a big white light as the sun nearly blinds him. Two seconds later, the white light becomes Jeremy Gonzolas.] [Jeremy Gonzolas, UWF coffee boy-wonder. Rich Rollins’ own personal interview man.] [Christ, how pathetic.] Renegade: Get in. [Grabbing Gonzolas by the lapel of his white UWF tee-shirt, Rollins forcibly hauls him inside and closes the door behind him. The room immediately plunges back into its pseudo-darkness, with the filtered silver sunlight and dust mote armada marching through the air. Jeremy Gonzolas takes a second for his eyes to get used to the light. The Renegade doesn’t give him that second.] Renegade: Did the hot water break down and you decided to come and slum it with a pro, coffee boy? [The words were meant to be light-hearted, but sounded more harsh than Rollins intended. Jeremy just flinches.] Jeremy: Here, the boss wanted me to give you something. Call it a bonus for your work last Wednesday. [Pulling a bundled package from under his arm, Jeremy tosses a folded-up piece of cloth at The Renegade. Rollins catches it, turning it over in his hands before unwrapping the thin plastic wire holding it together. The cloth unwraps, and the simple black UWF tee-shirt reveals itself, the same style as what Jeremy was wearing. A laugh escapes from Rollins’ throat, but not one of amusement.] Renegade: Well damn, Gonzolas. I don’t know what to say. What an original, thoughtful gesture. [Taking five steps backward, The Renegade comes up to a small coat closet near the entrance and starts to jimmy the handle. It pops open with the sound of metal scraping metal, but swings open just the same. Stepping back to allow Jeremy a view inside the tiny hanging space, The Renegade tosses the UWF shirt into the void within.] [Along with a sea of other wrestling-related promotional shirts.] [Global Championship Wrestling. Primetime Championship Wrestling. The Cyber Wrestling Union. Dozens of different shirts, all with the same logo design with different letters emblazoned across the front. A mountain of merchandise made for two dollars and sold for twenty, all targeted at the same underpaid, oversexed demographic of men who keep paying the price, no matter how outrageous.] [Consumerism at its finest.] [But Rollins got them all for free, if “free” was busting his ass in the ring twice a week, win lose or draw. Just add another shirt to the pile, another three seemingly arbitrary letters symbolizing another wrestling organization on another plain tee-shirt, and nobody would know the difference. Nobody except Jeremy Gonzolas, who now stood looking into Rollins’ closet like an Amish child would look at a Penthouse.] [Confused.] [Slamming the door shut, The Renegade breaks his stare.] Renegade: (dripping with sarcasm) Thanks. I needed another rag to wash the car, anyway. Now all I need is the car. [Brushing past Gonzolas, Rollins slides behind the bar and starts running his fingers along the bottles lined up on shelves along the back wall, some half empty and others completely dry. Without shooting a look at his visitor, Rollins pulls a bottle of Scotch from a shelf and unscrews the top, upturning the entire bottle into his open mouth. One gulp, two gulps, and the bitter liquid starts running down the sides of his cheeks in streams. Slamming the bottle on the bartop, he smacks his lips and tries to hold back his puckered face, only half successful.] Renegade: Want a drink? Jeremy: No. Renegade: Well, you must’ve come here for somethin’, kid. And I doubt it was to give me that tee-shirt.You gotta train to catch? Jeremy: No. Renegade: Then sit down and have a drink. I don’t like visitors who won’t let their hair down a little… gives me the impression that they don’t appreciate hospitality. You appreciate hospitality, coffee boy? Jeremy: Yes. [Pulling a relatively clean glass from under the bar, Rollins pours a good amount of the remaining Scotch into it, followed by a warm can of Pepsi he finds sitting on the back shelf. Sliding the glass across the bar, he motions at the empty barstool.] Renegade: Sit. [Jeremy Gonzolas complies, dusting the seat off and plopping himself down, taking a moment to smell the contents of the warm glass in front of him. Trying to change the subject, he ignores the drink and presses on.] Jeremy: I don’t get you, Rollins. I just don’t… I don’t understand how you do this. Renegade: Do what? Jeremy: Do this. (he motions around the room) Live like you do. Act like you do. Drink like you obviously do. And yet you pull these miraculous wins out of your ass whenever you step into the ring. I ran a background check on you last night, Rich. I know your pedigree in the wrestling world, your World titles and accomplishments all over the globe. Before you retired, you were like the First Son of Chicago, the people around here loved you that much. I watch you train, running around the Navy Pier at night when nobody can bother you… I see the passion, Rollins. Why the hell can’t you? [Silence. The Renegade‘s stoney face doesn‘t flinch, but doesn‘t move to stop Jeremy, either.] Jeremy: The fact that you still manage to win in competition means one thing, man. You got talent. You’ve got the chance to pick yourself back up out of this… this half-assed excuse of a living in this low-rent dump you call a home. If you could just get your act together, spend some time training to wrestle and not trainging to drink, you’d be damn near unstoppable. Absolutely unstoppable. [The Renegade, now leaning onto the bartop with his hands folded, looks down at his interlocked fingers. Refusing to meet Jeremy’s uncomfortably penetrating gaze, Rollins allows a handful of heartbeats to pass before he takes a breath, and responds.] Renegade: Why the hell do you care so much about a bottomed-out wrestling schmuck like me, kid? [Leaning closer over the bartop, Jeremy almost whispers.] Jeremy: I was there, Renegade. Four years ago, November 13th. Right here in Chicago, on any other Monday night, just like every other Monday night. Except on that particular evening, you won your first World Championship in the Cyber Wrestling Union. Do you remember what you did after that match, Renegade? Renegade: (mumbling to his hands) Got trashed with the boys afterward. Jeremy: Maybe. But that isn’t what I’m talking about. No, Rollins… you did this. [Reaching into his back pocket, Jeremy Gonzolas produces an old, wrinkled red cloth. Pulling the wrinkles apart, he holds it up so The Renegade can see. Now with Rollins’ full attention, Jeremy addresses the cloth.] Jeremy: I was a fifteen-year-old kid then, Rollins. And as I sat ringside, you came down after winning that title, took this bandanna off of your head, and placed it on mine. I never forgot that. And I never will. [For once in his life, Rich Rollins is too stunned for words. For a man who had made a living off of verbally tearing people down, this unexpected revelation was more than enough to keep The Renegade quiet. ] [Jeremy tenderly places the dirty old bandanna on the bartop next to the stale Pepsi and Scotch, the room so deadly silent that a hammer couldn’t break the tension.] Jeremy: I came here to give you this, Rollins. Where you go from here is up to you. Hell, what do I know? I’m just the coffee boy, after all. [And with that, Jeremy Gonzolas rises from his seat and walks across the dimly-lit space between the stool and the door, not even bothering to give The Renegade a last glance. Unlatching the door, he admits real sunlight into Rollins’ dark little world for only a moment, closing it behind him and returning everything to the status quo: dark and depressing. Always, so depressing.] [Alone, not for the first time in his life, Rich Rollins stares at the piece of red cloth on his bartop, right next to the glass of Scotch and Pepsi.] [The dirty old bandanna.] [The Scotch and Pepsi.] [The crossroads of his life, bundled right in front of him.] [When he would look back on the moment, Rich Rollins never knew how long he sat looking at those two objects, the drink and the bandanna. It could have been minutes, it could have been hours. But when all was said and done, the bandanna was back on his head where it belonged, and he was back where he belonged.] [In the ring.] [Fade to black.] ~Fin~
