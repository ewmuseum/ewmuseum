Fixing things, and not fixing things

14 Jul 2004

Joshua Dunn — Family Run Jewellery Store

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Perhaps, it was too much to hope for, but Joshua did get his revenge on the Corporal, even though the sweet taste was tainted by the interference of others, and a few more shots to the ribs with the cattle prod for his trouble. In any Case, it was obvious that someone wanted to use Dunn as a stepping stone once more. Tsunami Oni attempted to get a shot for the world title by enticing Dunn with the false hope of greatness in facing him. In reality the filthy scrounger wanted to use Dunn’s achievement in the Stable Wars PPV to get a free shot at the world title. Similar to how other people have used Dunn to get what they wanted, but we won’t name names now, that’s just not playing cricket] People were staring, it was understandable. When you see someone wondering down the street with a large dented trophy and a head covered in bandages, it’s hard not to stare. Granted Joshua had tried to hide his bandages under a hat, but since the bandages made his head two sizes bigger than what it was, it just made him look worse, so he decided against it. A few people got out of his way as he walked past, he wasn’t looking angry but then again, people see what they want to when it suits them. Right now they saw someone that was probably going to hurt them if they didn’t get out of their way. When your 284 lbs to start off with, it doesn’t take too much imagination to figure out what might happen if you don’t get out of the way. Joshua was doing one thing only, he wasn’t out to hurt people at all, he was going to get the Stable Wars Trophy mended. After having it battered off of the corporal’s head and his own, it was a little worse for wares. He had finally washed that foul blue mist off his face just this morning, it really was hard to get rid of. He had his own quarrels with Tsunami Oni, but that was for another time, he had more important matters on his mind. One of those was to get this trophy which he had fought so hard for repaired so he could really appreciate it. He’d found the store he was gong to in a local directory catalogue. It wasn’t world famous, or highly acclaimed, it hadn’t even won any awards for outstanding service. But it was low key, and to Joshua, that is what mattered. Not being well known meant that they would have to do a good job, just in the hope that word of mouth would do them justice. That’s why he found himself walking down this road, and being stared at by all these people, he was kind of glad that none of them really realised who he was, because all the enquiring eyes were making him a little red in the face. *** Horace was in the store again; his uncle left him in charge while he had gone out back to do some stock taking. He wasn’t the happiest of people, firstly his parents named him Horace, and he knew it sounded more like a horse than a person to anyone reading or hearing that name. Secondly, he was under appreciated. His uncle never let him do anything important, he was only allowed to mind the store when no one was in it, do the chores of sweeping and taking out the trash. His uncle wouldn’t even let him do the stock taking, as he “would only mess it up”. He had once tried to tell his uncle that he could never get good at anything until he was actually allowed to do it. All that achieved was getting him a slap in his face and a speech about why not to talk back to your elders. That was the last time he ever thought it would be a good idea to disagree with his uncle, so now he sat bored in the stuffy old jewellers shop. He didn’t really understand stock taking, especially in his uncle’s shop, seeing as he never sold anything and never bought in anything, and no one really knew where it was, how could the stock level ever change? It didn’t seem to bother his uncle that much, and that outburst had earned him another slap. He gazed out the window in a delightful daydream watching the world pass him by, his head propped up by his hand holding his chin. A sigh escaped him, he was bored, it really is pitiful when a dream world can be more appealing to a person than real life itself. Being an average teenager, he was confused about a lot of things, and wanted to go out with his friends now and again, but he was hardly let out of the shop, his uncle viewed it as deserting him, because there was always some menial task he had lined up for him to do. Still there were always his thoughts to keep him company in the empty store. He wasn’t really prepared for what was about to happen, he didn’t really think it COULD happen. But even though, while staring out the window, he saw a man sail past the window, he didn’t catch a glimpse of who he was, as he only focused on the trophy and the head bandages, the odd sight, was enough to bring him back to reality briefly, only to dismiss it as a figment of his imagination. He couldn’t have seen such an odd image, well it was just crazy enough to have actually happen, but nothing even remotely exciting happened to him. Even when he was asked out by this girl he liked, he had to decline because he knew what his uncle would do if he thought he wasn’t going to be around when he needed him. He really hated his uncle, but was too frightened to do anything. The shop door chimes jingled, it was a set of wind chimes hung above the front door, just another skin flint investment by his uncle. You couldn’t even hear the things from the next room, let alone the store room right at the back. The unexpected intrusion was a welcome relief from the mind numbing boredom, but horror soon set in relief’s place as he realised it was the nutter with the dented trophy and the head bandages that had entered the shop. He started to wish that he had done something useful with his still short life. Joshua Looked around the store, it was just like he imagined it would look like, a nice family run business with the atmosphere he was used to back in good old Scotland. Going by that, it was probably going to do a good job of the trophy too. He did like the wind chimes, a little corny, but much more pleasant than some of the brash buzzers you had in other stores he had been in. He took a look around the store, Watches, clocks, other brass and silver ware… yup, the clutter was very like that of home, the only difference was the slack jawed spotty adolescent behind the counter, he wondered if he was a sports fan, he had a look about him that said this shop was his life, whether he liked it or not. Not meaning to be rude, He had a short browse around the isles of trinkets and other such jewellery, for him and for her. He was taken with a pewter Scotsman figurine, it was that quirky in itself, and on the base he was sure that was supposed to be a wild haggis by the feet, well it definitely was not made in Scotland that was for sure, he picked it up to see if it had a made in mark. It was horrible, Horace just couldn’t help staring, the man was just looking at the items in the store, probably wondering what their black market value was, he wondered it he was hiding a weapon in that trophy and intended to hold up the store with it. Thinking about it for a bit, he decided that the trophy WAS probably the weapon, and it looked as if it had been used before too. He was sure the bandages on his head hid some hideous scars incurred from his latest bar fight. He had picked up the Scotsman figurine, he probably only thought of it as a man in a dress by the way he chuckled as he saw it. As he picked it up and looked at the underside, he was wondering if he thought it would make a good bludgeoning instrument, or if it was a good weight to throw around the store. Dunn placed the figurine back down and wondered over to the counter, much to the horror of the boy at the other side. Just like the people outside, he say the battered trophy, he saw the bandaged head, and saw exactly what he thought he saw. Well maybe it was about time to set some assumptions to one side. He spotted the till on the counter and gave the boy a sideways glance to see how he was reacting, he thought he was about to be robbed. He couldn’t believe it, his uncles shop was actually going to be robbed, the man stood there in broad day light a couple of feet in front of him and was eyeing up the till, this was crazy, he was never so scared in his life, the gorilla of a man had to be in excess of 300 lbs, he was huge. But he couldn’t understand why he thought he had seen him somewhere before. He was grinning, he knew exactly how scared he was, and was certain the man knew too. Any minute now he’ll get a whack over the head with the large metal trophy. JD: Believe me, if I wanted to, it’d be gone It took a minute for the words to sink into the boys head, he was still running away with himself convinced that Dunn had to be some escaped criminal, technically he would be right, except that was way in the past. He watched the boy blink a few times and finally wake up to look into Joshua’s face for the first time, seeing the person and not the assumption. Horace looked, at the man after he spoke, he couldn’t quite explain it but the man had completely transformed, he looked at the man’s face and saw a friendly booming face grinning back, the tension in the air just melted away and he took a sigh of relief and sank back into the chair he was sitting on. He was kind of sad; he was starting to enjoy the excitement, sometimes real life could be more interesting that fantasy. JD: I’d like to get this thing right here fixed up like new, you think you could do that for me sonny? The friendly manner was nice to hear, it was much more pleasant than his uncle’s tones he took with him, he viewed him more like a tool than a blood relation. Realising he wasn’t actually answering the man, he stammered, catching himself off guard Horace: eh, well yes, yes I think I can. JD: That’s excellent then The boy seemed friendly enough, but possibly didn’t have much practice at socialising. He wondered why that was. But there was more shock to come the boy’s way, he had already spied the UWF posters hanging up in the back, he was a sports fan, and eventually he’d realise who he was. Then the whole shock and awe process would happen all over again. It happened most times he went out in public, but since he had the bandages applied to his skull, that’s all people around him saw, it was a useful mask of sorts. Horace fumbled with his uncle’s book of orders, the last one was over a year ago, no one used this store at all. He found a pen, scribbled on a scrap receipt to get the ink flowing and put pen to paper; just realising he needed to ask a few important questions. Horace: What’s the name? And this is how it would start then JD: Mr. Dunn Horace: First name? JD: Joshua Horace scribbled down the name, it wasn’t too hard to guess where to write everything down, his uncle’s book was neatly titled and columned, some times being a neat freak was a good thing too, and it also helped him guess at which questions to ask. He knew he’d have to call on his uncle for advice at some point, but hopefully not just now. He looked down at the name he had just written, something was triggering the back of his mind, but he just didn’t know what yet. He took a look at the battered trophy to make Mr. Dunn think he knew what he was doing, and he saw the inscription on the surface, it Read “Stable Wars”. His jaw dropped again, this time with gleeful recollection, and he took another look at the face which was still smiling down at him. It was, he couldn’t believe it. JD: That’s right Joshua cut off the boy’s stammering, he almost laughed, he took a bit longer to realise than most people do. He just watched as the boy stared back, but he needed to get this trophy mended, and having someone idol worship him in the store wasn’t going to get it done. JD: So how much is it going to be to fix? And he knew he’d have to call on his uncle now, he couldn’t price these things, and his heart sinking, he called on him, and the abuse would start as soon as Mr Dunn left the shop, he could feel another slap coming on for not calling on him earlier. Horace: Uncle Tim! Emerging through from the back room, from the store room behind that came aging uncle Tim, his balding head on full display, he was happy about his age, and wanted other people to know that he could have retired, but he hasn’t. He was already cursing his nephew what did that idiot boy want now, he swore he could never get anything done around here for that boy, why did he even let his sister talk him into keeping him around the store. It wasn’t that he despised children, he just didn’t have the time for their immaturity, and that’s all Horace was really, a big kid, no matter how much hair he had where Tim: What is it now Horace? Horace: Customer, he wants you to fix his trophy Tim fixed an eye on the customer, the huge hulk of a man wasn’t his usual breed of customer, in fact lately his breed of customer was extinct. But he instantly disliked him, he was everything he wishes he could have been, well built and confident, able to do anything. To be honest, all he wanted was to be respected himself, but no one paid any attention to an old coot who owns a jewellery store. Dunn instantly dislike the man that stepped through the door, he was everything he was glad he wasn’t, a shrivelled up old coot with a napoleon complex, but he came here to get the trophy fixed, and he like the look of this place, despite it’s owner Time took a gander at the trophy, it was damaged to, putting two and two together and getting three, he presumed it was beaten off the man’s head and that’s why he had the bandages, he wasn’t completely wrong, but then again he wasn’t completely right either. The damage wasn’t too severe, he shouldn’t even need to re-engrave it, should be a nice straight forward job, about $500 should do it, after all it is a big trophy Tim: About $500 should do it, the damage isn’t to severe there JD: That should be fine, if you just charge it to this account… He fished into his pocket, setting the trophy on the counter, he produced a slip of paper with an account number on it, it was the UWF account, every wrestler was issued it, seeing as all of them were the owners of the company, they could charge things to the account as “necessary expenses”, after having his shares sold without his notice, he was certain this would be ample payment for it, it wasn’t that he couldn’t afford the sum himself, it was just the principal of the thing. He tossed the bit of paper on the counter as the owner picked it up and spied it through his glasses. Tim: Well, that’s that taken care of then, it should be ready in a week, is that all right with you? JD: perfectly fine, I shall be back then to pick it up This is it, thought Horace, maybe now there will be some life changing moment, Joshua will say or do something and his uncle will lay off him for a while at least JD: goodbye Horace didn’t quite catch it as he was to wrapped up in his own little fantasy world, but he just watched as his salvation walked out the door, leaving him without any life changing speech or a spiritually uplifting one, and just he and his uncle were left in the store alone, his heart sank back to where it was before, he already knew what was coming Tim: Why didn’t you call me earlier? Joshua walked away from the place unhappy, he didn’t want to leave the kid in there, he could tell he was probably going to get a beating from the way the owner kept looking at him, but what was he supposed to do? Act with divine intervention? Get sent to prison fo assaulting an old man? Tell him how to run his affairs? Real life just doesn’t work like that
