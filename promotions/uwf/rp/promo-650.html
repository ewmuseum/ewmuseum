UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #650

For the sake of the story

17 Aug 2004

Mike Montgomery — Somewhere over the mid-Pacific

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
This time our scene opens in the cabin of a corporate lear jet. The logo of Asylum Industries is clearly visible on the wall at the back of the cabin. The camera pans around, showing the plane's two passengers, UWF Executive Vice President of Talent Relations and Asylum Industries CEO Mike Montgomery, and his bodyguard, Gavin Frost. A stewardess enters from off camera, pushing a beverage tray. She places a coke in front of Montgomery, and a beer can with a plastic cup in front of Gavin Frost. 'There you are gentlemen. The pilot has asked me to inform you that it's safe to turn on electronic equipment now.' 'Thanks Stacy,' Mike nods, 'thank the pilot for me and tell him I'll be leaving tickets for him and the flight crew at the Tokyodome.' The stewardess smiles, 'I'm sure he'll appreciate that sir.' Montgomery retuns the smile as the stewardess heads up to the cockpit. He stands, reaches into the overhead compartment, and pulls out a laptop computer case. He opens the case up, pulls out a laptop and places it on the tray in front of him. He reaches into the case again, this time pulling out a wireless mouse and wireless network card. He plugs the card in, then turns the laptop on. 'Let's see what's happening in the big bad world,' Montgomery says while waiting for the system to boot up. Frost, meanwhile, simply takes a sip of his beer, eyes scanning the cabin, always watching for anything out of the ordinary. After a moment the laptop beeps, signalling that the system is online. Montgomery quickly opens the browser, scanning through his email. 'Well well, UWF stock's still going up...despite Ms. Preeest's assertions that I don't bring in the bucks. I knew there was a reason I kept my stock. Now then...' For the next few minutes, Montgomery scrolled through the UWF's business website. Next, he downloaded video of recent UWF promos. 'Ezra...Ezra...Ezra...I am so very, very disappointed in you.' 'Problem?' Frost asked. 'Not exactly. Just disappointed with a rather naive 'common man.' You remember when I accused Ezra of hypocrisy because he only complained when the Club's rulebreaking didn't benefit him?' 'Yes.' 'Well, our friend Ezra, it seems, essentially thinks that I can do whatever the hell I want simply because, at the time, I had the power of UWF Commissioner. The thing is, quite simply, it doesn't quite work that way. The Commissioner has limited power when it comes to changing matches. We make them, however once they're made, it falls under the perview of the booking committee.' 'What about his 'card is subject to change without notice' defence?' 'Well, that's true, sometimes the card is indeed subject to last minute change. However, the card is not subject to change because I'm not willing to accept losing my Intercontinental Title shot because Ruff Ryder decided to stick his nose where it didn't belong. That would be what we call abuse of power. Know the right people, the right play, and in this business you can get anything you want. Commissioner or not.' 'So half the stuff you did as Commissioner wasn't even legal?' 'Half?' Mike asks with a derisive snort, 'Hell, let's be honest, if the Player's Club was involved, we either bent or broke the rules with everything we did. IC Tournament? Dunn set it up, Ezra and I were in it to keep the belt in the club, and I made it double elimination for myself to increase our chances. Sound fair to you?' 'Nope.' 'Sound sporting?' 'Naw.' 'Sound like something the Common Man would go along with?' 'I would think not.' 'Most people would. Thing is, our good samaritan went along with it. Hell, I think he liked it. I think the Common Man has his first taste of true power, and I think he liked it.' Frost ponders this for a moment, 'Well, you know what they say. Absolute power....' 'Corrupts absolutely. My dear Mr. Frost, I do believe you're learning. I think Mr. Kazee is having a bit of an identity crisis. The good sport...tasting true success he doesn't have to work for...and all of a sudden you don't know if you're going to go the honorable route or if you're willing to do whatever it takes.' 'And before you know it...' 'You have a world title opponent in the middle of a moral crisis,' Montgomery finishes, 'I love it. Now then let's see what else...ah...now this is interesting...' Montgomery turns the computer toward Frost so the two of them can watch the Champ's latest promo. 'Alexia Young...this I can use.' Montgomery opens another window, connecting him to the Corporate Security branch of Asylum Industries. He also pulls a webcam from the case, attaching it to the laptop. 'AI Corporate Security Division, how can I...oh Mr. Montgomery, what can I do for you?' 'Alexia Young. Corporate Stockbroker. She's got access to UWF Personnel files and I'd like to know why. I'd also like to know why in the hell her company was handling Dunn's stocks. All Player's Club finances were managed by Asylum Industries.' 'I'll see what I can dig up.' And with that, the screen would go dead. Just after closing the window, Montgomery returns to watching Dunn's promo. 'Now that I can definately put to use. So Ms. Young plans to be in Tokyo. Mr. Frost, get on the phone to the Tokyodome's boxoffice. I want to make absolutely certain that Ms. Young is able to get backstage for the show. Frost nods, removing the air-fone receiver from the seat in front of him. Meanwhile, Montgomery's laptop begins to insistently beep, indicating an incoming call. Montgomery re-opens the browser, surprised to see an image of the Asylum Industries security man he'd just been talking to. 'Well, that was quick.' 'As it turns out sir, we actually have a fairly thick file on Ms. Young. Stocks in anything amd everything she can get her hands on. She's done pretty good playing the markets, almost too good really.' 'Interesting,' Montgomery mutters, 'anything we can work with there?' 'Not as of yet no. According to the man we have in the US Federal Prosecutor's Office, Ms. Young has been indicted for insider trading on three separate occasions, her company on five more occasions.' 'And nothing stuck?' 'Absoluty nothing,' the employee answers, 'they call her 'Teflon Young' for a reason. Everything the Treasury Department finds is circumstantial at best. Worst case scenario she ended up paying a fine of about twenty grand. I'm emailing you what we have on her financial status.' A small window pops up to indicate that the message has been received. Montgomery clicks on it, opening the email. 'Well,' he says to himself, saving the file to review later, 'you were right. Twenty grand is barely a slap on the wrist for her. Can we get anyone close to her?' 'I think we can manage something.' 'Good, I've got a few ideas. Get somebody in place. Dunn's promo mentions a fellow by the name of Garry Henderson, friend of Young's. See what you can find on him, I think we'll be able to use him.' 'Alright, I'll see what I can dig up. Depending on how much of Ms. Young's practices he's aware of, we should be able to find a paper trail on him. I'll email you when I have more.' 'Fair enough,' Montgomery responds, 'I'll be looking forward to it.' Montgomery clicks off, then pauses, a thought suddenly coming to him. He pulls the air-fone out from the slot in front of his own seat and dials. Waiting a moment, he says into the phone 'Yeah it's me. I need you to meet me at the airport with a package. No...no...yeah, that one. Perfect, I'll need three, is that doable? Excellent...I'll see you when we land.' Montgomery sat back and smiled. If all went as planned, the world title would soon be his. Afterall, it was looking more and more like he was the only one with the will to win, no matter what it took.
