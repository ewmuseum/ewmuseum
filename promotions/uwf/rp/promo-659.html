UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #659

Do the Right Thing!

31 Aug 2004

Joshua Justice — America's Best Gym

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Opening up in a gym seems like a lame thing to do. It’s done way too much. In fact, it wouldn’t hurt to say that it’s OVER-done. But that’s okay; steaks are liked when they are rare and well done. So, BLAH! The gym is small and only two people are in it. There is a man doing bench presses and another one doing squats. Both of them have their back to the camera. The cameraman walks over to the man doing the bench presses. The weight on the bar seems too much for anyone to lift. The man’s muscles are bulging out of everywhere on his body. Veins galore expand and contract with each press up. He puts it on the bar and sits up. The veins in his temple is pulsating like crazy. He stands up and lets out a yell of passion and anger. He picks up a towel and wipes some of the sweat off of his body and then throws the towel on the floor. He starts to make his way towards the locker room area. All of a sudden, the man doing squats stops and glances over to where the dirty sweaty towel lies on the floor. He lifts the weights, too a large amount, and puts them back up. He turns towards the man (and the camera) with a serious look on his face. He has blonde hair, blue eyes, and has a good build. He looks like an All-American guy. This is Joshua Justice. This is the man that kicked Dicky Flint out of the Regal Rumble just a few nights ago. He looks towards the muscle-full freak.] Justice: Excuse me, sir? [The muscle-full freak, we’ll call him Bob, turns to face Justice and smiles.] Bob: What do you want punk? [Justice walks up to Bob and tries to get in his face. Bob is a good seven feet tall.] Justice: You see that towel over there? [Bob looks over.] Bob: Yep. Justice: It doesn’t belong there. [Bob looks back down to Justice and chuckles.] Bob: I’ll put my dirty towels wherever I want to put my dirty towels. And who are you to tell me what I can do? [Justice leans in a tad bit closer and whispers.] Justice: Don’t you know who the owner is? [Bob smirks.] Bob: Yeah, I heard about that guy. ‘S supposed to be some tough bastard or sumtin’, everybody be afraid of him. Justice: That’s right. Bob: Yeah, this place is exclusive for ‘wrestlers only’ and stuff. Justice: That’s right. Bob: You wanna know a secret? Justice: Sure. Bob: I’m not a wrestler; I’m just a body builder. Justice: Isn’t that interesting. Bob: Yeah, he must be some schmuck. Hell, I got in here. [Bob begins to chuckle very loud and then he just stops.] Bob: Wait, what does this have to do with my towel? [Justice smiles and puts his arm on Bob’s shoulder.] Justice: I’d like you to pick it up. Bob: Excuse me? Justice: I’m asking you nicely if you would pick up your dirty towel and place it where it belongs. Bob: Who are you again? Justice: I am Joshua Justice. This is my gym. That is my bench press you were using. That is my towel you threw on my floor. Now pick it up. Bob: Kiss my— [Before Bob can finish the sentence, Justice takes his arm and bends it behind him in an awkward position.] Justice: Now, I know that you were not going to say something nice and polite there. This is my gym and I would hope you would respect that. I am Joshua Justice and I believe that you must always do what is right. You did a few things wrong. First, you applied to this gym that I specifically placed in this position for pro-wrestlers only. You are not a wrestler. Then, I saw you benching a whole lot of weight and you didn’t have a spotter. You didn’t even have a bottle of water to make sure you were plenty refreshed. After your ‘workout’ you proceeded to throw your sweaty towel onto the ground and leave it there. Those are a lot of mistakes and I want you to apologize. Bob: …I-I’m… …s-s-s-sorry? [Justice lets go of him and pushes him forward to the towel. He grumbles as he picks up the towel and starts to walk back to the locker room. He tauntingly throws the dirty towel into the proper receptacle and then lunges towards Justice. Justice moves out of the way and trips Bob. Bob slams down to the floor. Justice grabs Bob’s arm and twists it behind him. He pulls Bob’s arm back and he places his foot square into the center of his back.] Justice: Now, I asked you politely to do what you should have done in the first place and you didn’t. That means that you are a very bad person. Now, when I let you up, I expect you to walk out of here and never come back. Do you understand? [Bob nods his head yes very quickly. Justice lets him up and he runs to the door. He runs towards his pick-up truck, starts the engine and screeches away as Justice walks to the window. He stares out at all of the people walking by.] Justice: Only twenty-five percent of Americans are good Americans. This man known as Bob was not a good American. Dicky Flint… he is not a good American. He makes me physically ill. I get sick to my stomach every single time I see him walking by. Flint, you are not American. You are trash. And on Wednesday, I will take out the trash. I showed you at the Regal Rumble how easily I could take you out. This time, before you throw any excuses my way, I’ll let you prove yourself to me one-on-one. Good luck and God Bless. [The scene closes on the stern look Justice is giving towards every single person walking by the door to his gym.]
