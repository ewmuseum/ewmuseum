UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #529

No Time for Innocence

12 Jul 2004

'The Renegade' Rich Rollins — Slums of Souch Chicago

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[No matter how many times he swears to change his ways, a bum is still a bum.] [A whore, still a whore.] [Children keep their innocence until they mature, but children are still spoiled. Still pampered and kept blissfully ignorant until they get the chance to grow up and expose themselves to the real world and make the same mistakes of their mothers and fathers. To become that bum, pissing himself to keep warm. That whore, stripped of any concept of love or compassion.] [Or that wrestler, the man who never could let it go no matter how many times it burned him.] [Strolling aimlessly through the slums of South Chicago, these thoughts fly through the mind of Rich 'The Renegade' Rollins, bouncing a small black rubber ball against the cracked concrete. Dressed down in an old pair of tattered blue jeans and a white tank top, the absence of any wind leaves his shoulder-length brown hair to hang lifeless across his shoulders, a dirty old red bandanna tied around his scalp. Despite the dangers of the dark South Chicago usually offers, Rollins walks on down another nameless street, the old sign knocked down long ago by some bottomed-out junkie looking for his next fix.] [The streets don't bother him.] [This is home, and always has been. Since he was the innocent child, Rich Rollins grew up around this same neighborhood, grew up with the stranger your mother told you never to talk to living next door. That child molester who put razor blades in Halloween candy across the street. That old man who snatched children from street corners with promises of candy two blocks over. Urban legends of the underbelly of society, that's what Rollins called them. They exist, these people of ill repute, these monsters of childhood myth, and Rich Rollins used to call them neighbor.] [For two years, Rich Rollins has been merely that. Rich Rollins. A former professional wrestler who hit his peak early and spent the rest of his life since trying to play catch-up. 'The Renegade' went into seclusion, leaving regular old Richard Andrew Rollins to deal with life the old-fashioned way: as a regular Joe. Another stranger on the street, working nine-to-five to support himself and the family he never had.] [But the call of the ring never lets its true warriors rest.] [Without realizing where he was headed, Rollins looks up to find a seedy old bar sitting on the street corner, heavy metal music wafting across the empty evening air from the swinging double- doors that serves as the entrance. A few hoodless, tireless frames of old cars litter what used to pass for a parking lot, a sprinkling of old motorcycles spread between them. Shrugging, Rollins begins to walk toward the music.] Renegade: Why not. [After all, he owned the damn place.] [Stuffing his black rubber ball in a jean pocket, Rollins jogs across the empty street, ignoring the half-coherent plea for change from another aimless bum, bundled up in newspaper against the side of the husk of an old '59 Chevy pickup, reeking of urine.] Bum: S'more coinz, bub? Ge'mere, I gotta login snuff... [The hobo continues to mumble unintelligibly to himself as The Renegade walks by. The music begins to die down as he approaches the double doors, and the lights suddenly blink out of the beer-stained windows. Rollins slows down as he approaches, the hairs on the back of his neck standing on end. The last whisper of music is silenced, leaving nothing but the sound of the mindless hobo to drift through the night.] [Cautiously, The Renegade eases open one of the double doors, peering into the now pitch black chasm of his seedly little bar. Edging another few feet into the void, he reaches along the wall for the lightswitch, the irises of his cold blue eyes sweeping the darkness, desperately trying to adjust. Another inch along the wall, his fingers feel the chipped plaster, groping for the light switch...] Everyone: SURPRISE! [Without warning, the lights blink on and nearly blind The Renegade, flushing his vision with a bright flourescent flash. His heart skipping three beats, Rollins backs against the wall, blinking repeatedly until the smiling faces of his local patronage of bottomed-out beer swillers greets him. A large 'UWF' banner hangs over the bar top, and half of the denizens starting to crowd around him sport simple black 'UWF' logo tee-shirts. The sound of everyone talking at once drowns all thought.] Crowd: Welcome back to the big-time! Crowd: Who woulda thought... Crowd: ... back from retirement, ready to kick ... Crowd: How do you feel? Crowd: How does it feel, Renegade? Crowd: How do you ... Crowd: Time for a celebration! Crowd: Make Chicago proud, Rich! Crowd: How do you feel? Crowd: Are you ready to get back in the ring? Crowd: How does it feel ... Renegade: ENOUGH! [Silence.] [Pushing a strand of hair out of his eyes, Rollins surveys the room. An array of liquor bottles - his own liquor, no less - lays scattered across both pool tables, half a dozen empty overturned plastic cups soiling the green felt around the edges. Another dozen cups lay crushed on the floor, proof positive that this 'surprise party' had been going on long before he got here. And would have continued, he thought, had he never arrived at all.] Renegade: Last call has come and gone folks, and his name is Rich Rollins. Get the hell out of my bar. [The shocked gathering are too stunned to move; instead, they stare at their hometown hero with their mouths open, either from legitimate surprise or a drunken stupor, Rollins can't decide. He stands with his arms at his sides, meeting every gaze with his own in turn, causing everyone to stare downcast at their feet. Slowly, ever so slowly, the assembled well-wishers shuffle toward the swinging double-doors, some still holding bottles and glasses of beer or any number of other potent concoctions.] Renegade: Leave my liquor where it is. Unless you want a first- class trip downtown to remove the broken glass from your skull those bottles will leave, that is. Either way, you ain't stayin' here, so start steppin'. [Less than a minute later, the bar becomes what it has been for the last two years: a hollow cave of Rich Rollin's own self pity, empty except for the echo of doubt in The Renegade's own head.] [And the lone figure of a UWF office lackey, that is.] [Standing with an air of uncertainty, stuffing a few notes into the pocket of his 'incognito' Old Navy cargo pants, Jeremy Gonzolas watches Rich Rollin's backside. Now sitting alone at the deserted bar top, grabbing a half-empty glass of stale beer poured for somebody else, Rollins kicks back and takes a hearty swig... and stops. Without turning around, he addresses the straggler standing in his doorway.] Renegade: I told you all to leave. I'm not lookin' for well-wishers, so take the hint and hit the bricks before the bricks hit you, pal. Jeremy: I'm not a fan, Rollins. I work for the UWF. [Taken by surprise, Rollins finally spins his barstool around and sizes the man up, nodding his head slightly in recognition.] Renegade: Yeah, I remember you. Gonzolas, right? You were Richard Hayes' coffee bitch when I was in the office yesterday signing off on the contract. What are you doin' here? Jeremy: Hayes wouldn't send one of the top-tier interviewers to this ... well ... this area of the city, so they ... Renegade: ... sent the coffee boy? (he chuckles sarcastically) Way to make a guy feel important. Jeremy: Who, you or me? Renegade: Pick one. [The Renegade spins his barstool back around and swirls a finger around the rim of his dirty mug, already lost in his own musings amid the stench of stale beer. Edging closer, Jeremy takes a deep breath and approaches the bar.] Jeremy: Mind if I talk to you for a few minutes? Renegade: Why? Jeremy: Well... because that's why they sent me, I suppose. You're the new guy, and people want to get to know you. Renegade: And you're just the coffee boy, so take a ticket and get in line. Jeremy: It's still my job. (he pauses) So, what made you come back to wrestling? [Such a simple question. Such a complicated answer.] [The Renegade sighs. He stops fussing with the beer mug and finally turns to look at Jeremy Gonzolas for the first time, really look at him. With a face as stoic and emotionless as Rollin's, Jeremy doesn't know what to expect.] [Truth be known, neither does Rollins.] Renegade: Why did I come back to wrestling? That's the big one. The ten-million-dollar question. Maybe I didn't want to give it up. Maybe it's the only way to release aggression in a constructive way, I dunno. Jeremy: So why did you hang up the boots in the first place? Renegade: Because I wasn't angry anymore. [The speed in which The Renegade answered the question takes Jeremy aback for a second, but he regains his composure. Immediately, he fires back another one.] Jeremy: So... you came back into wrestling now because... because you are angry again? Is that it? Renegade: (silence) Jeremy: I don't mean to pry, Renegade. You don't have to... Renegade: You sneak into my bar with half the inbred Adam's Family rejects of the South Side, drink my beer without my permission, then assault me with questions about my intentions? MY intentions? But of course, you don't mean to pry. Of course not. That wouldn't be prudent, would it? Jeremy: Do you always answer questions with more questions? Renegade: Well now, look at you. Maybe they'll promote you from coffee boy to paper boy, what with a journalistic instinct like that. You want answers? Fine. I quit wrestling because I didn't have anything left to prove, and my ambition went down the tubes, along with my will to win. So for two years I've sat around in this piss-ant bar, with these piss-ant people, wondering where the hell my life went. And yes, it angers me. I'm angry at magazines that tell people how they should feel about themselves and what they need out of life, how much money they need to make to feel special and important. I'm pissed off at the guy sitting outside right now, too far into his own self-loathing to pick himself up out of the puddle of his own urine and make something of his life. And most of all, I'm pissed at myself for doing the same thing, all the while judging everybody else without doing a damn thing to better Rich Rollins. The Renegade. You want a reason for my return, some all- heralding catchphrase that will make everything become crystal clear? Here you go: f*ck society. I'm here for one purpose and one purpose only, and that's to bring closure to the only passion in my life... wrestling. [Jeremy stands still for a handful of heartbeats, absorbing The Renegade's words. The silence builds, and Rollins stares down the simple interviewer with eyes of blue fire, refusing to let the moment break.] [But Jeremy finally does it.] Jeremy: Well, you'll get the chance this Wednesday in a ten-man battle royal for a shot at the Television Championship. Do you have any comment on that? Renegade: A battle royal, huh? So that's my debut in the Universal Wrestling Federation... a clusterf*ck of curtain-jerkers vying for a shot at the Television title. Jeremy: Is there a problem with that? Renegade: Problem? Have you listened to a word I've said? The UWF higher-ups just did me a favor. Right out of the gate, and I get nine other would-be superstars to release myself upon like a nuclear warhead, and believe me... Hell ain't got room for the remains I'm about to send its way, I promise you that. I damn sure promise you that. Jeremy: So... Renegade: I have things to do. It's time you headed back to the coffee machine, Gonzolas. [And with that, The Renegade spins back around to lose himself in the swirls of his stale beer, as if Jeremy Gonzolas had never existed. After another minute of silence, Jeremy takes his leave and doesn't look back, trying to ignore the piss-stained bum by the old '59 Chevy pickup.] [Alone again, The Renegade waits.] [Wednesday can't come soon enough.] [Fade to black.] ~Fin~
