UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #636

Ghost Man on Third

13 Aug 2004

Misfit — Seattle, Washington

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Sometimes at night, I sit in my room and think; my own form of self-torture. Like smoking or drinking, it’s my own way to slowly kill myself. Thoughts cut inward through the heart like a knife tearing through flesh; like a tear through the papers that hold my cluttered thoughts. It’s been so long since I came back to you, I don’t know what to do. I can only hope that you accept my return, as you always have. That you’ll take me in your warm, loving arms and welcome me home again. But, somehow, I know that’s not the way it will happen. Somehow I know that when I run to you, things never get better. Somehow, I know... The cold consumes me, assaulting the warmth you last brought until there’s none left. Nothing but callous chilling… it’s cold to the fucking bone. You have no idea what they’ve done. They have no idea what they’ve done. Have they destroyed me? Maybe so. Maybe I’ve finally cracked, like they always said I would. Maybe not. Maybe I am just a broken psycho. Or maybe not. Is there a method to all of this? Maybe not. Or maybe, just maybe, I’ve known all along. Maybe I could see that gleam in the eye this whole time. Maybe I knew where Eden lie dormant all along. Perhaps it was just a façade. Perhaps. Maybe I knew the positions all along. But soon, I can see them behind me. Walking. Running. Trying to catch up. They tried, oh they tried, but no. They couldn’t. They couldn’t keep up. They couldn’t keep me down, so they tried to keep up, but no. It didn’t happen. It couldn’t. They’ve tried everything, and nothing worked. There was no cure. No remedy. No vaccine or inoculation that could help. Nothing that would work. So it came to this. Another fierce but ultimately laughable try. Don’t they get it? Will they ever? No. As long as life continues, they’ll never understand. There is no end to the beginning. There is no beginning to the end. It’s a campaign of distraction and revisionist history. It’s a shame, I doubt they’d even notice. It’s a shame, I doubt they’d even care. No one is to know about this. But whatever I have been getting myself into lately has been slicing inches from my wrist. It’s my fist versus the bottle and that’s how bad this could hurt. Against this, I won’t feel a thing. No one is to know about this. We were so much better than this. This is what living like this does. If you think this is as low as it goes, thank God you weren’t there to watch me hit bottom. Fighting like the devil, hell bent on a so-called revenge. But that isn’t what this about. There’s no factor in this formula. It’s all being done by ear. It’ll be a two-for-one. A hope for revenge to kill a hope for truth. A blindfold held ready for opening eyes. A lesson for one and all to learn. A chance to view the carnivals firsthand. A front row seat to the laurels of war with two battles to be fought. The first to take place on a small scale with three competitors. Three malefactors, two foreign elements, and the lone force amongst the swarm. The one rebel causing malformation amongst the masses; the martyr of a nation that shuns him. He stands alone as the others sit and watch. Like-minded individuals who would rather sit idly by as one wages war so that they can claim victory, too, when the war is over, no matter the winner. This is what living like this does. The malefactors stand firmly against the martyr, knowing there isn’t much chance of victory. Maybe a firm step against him, but, in the end, destruction awaits them. Foreign elements are just that; foreign. Like a virus, they too will be purged. First will be claimed a victory in battle; then will move on to a war. A separate war, but a war nonetheless. This is what living like this does. In this war, many stand on separate sides; a free-for-all of the elements, the occupants of the nation. Amongst them stands the martyr, like a beacon in the swarm as all of the imaginable elements clash together. As with any combination of volatile elements, a chain reaction occurs causing a great explosion in the nation. This is what living like this does. Victims lie scattered across the field, tattered and torn; broken and bruised. Malefactors defeated; blasphemers destroyed. Standing victorious over them, the martyr rises like a phoenix. One may ask how the victor could be a martyr, but it’s true. A victor, a martyr, a scapegoat. This is what living like this does. He stands alone against a worthy cause, one which many are behind, yet he still stands alone. He fights a combined force, the sum of which is greater than himself. Yet he emerges the victor, regardless of technical victories & defeats. He is victorious when people finally see. He is victorious when they understand. He is victorious when people think. This is what living like this does. Only a few fleeting moments before Armageddon. For a few minutes, the insides will be Armageddon. A huge eruption of hatred and anger; an explosion of passion and conviction. This is what living like this does. [We settle in on a smog filled sky, a noisy hustle and bustle going on below. We see smoke stacks and other tall building in various states of disarray as we slowly pan down towards the streets. Cars move along at a slow pace in the almost traffic jam conditions as a small number of people walk down the sidewalks. We're taken on a small tour of the crowded streets before we come upon Misfit, walking the streets towards the camera, dressed in black work boots, baggy and torn jeans, and a black AFI 'Death of Summer' t-shirt.] Misfit: 'No matter where you go, you have to have a home. This place...' [Misfit looks up and all around in different directions and squints as he does so.] Misfit: 'This place is like home... but home is nowhere. A home is an abstract idea. Home is a place where one feels comfortable; a place where one goes to relax. For me, home is nowhere. There is no place to feel comfortable… no place to feel safe.” [A car speeds by, going almost too fast for what we saw earlier. There’s a loud honk in the background and the sound of breaks squealing.] Misfit: “You see, this place used to be what I would call home. But then I come back, and it’s been ruined. All of it, ruined by industry, just like the wrestling world. I used to think that maybe, if nothing else, I could call the ring home… but no. No one understands; no one ‘gets it’. It’s all about marketing; it’s not about the fans and what people really want to see. It’s all about what the office heads think will sell.” [Audio obviously laid over the real track fades up. Crowds cheer and chant as commentators rave about Misfit’s various hardcore acts before the audio fades again.] Misfit: “Nevermind the cheers. Forget about the reactions. I’m not safe; I’m not sane. I’m not marketable.” [Misfit twitches slightly and explodes with anger.] Misfit: “Listen to them!” [The audio of cheers fades up again.] Misfit: “Listen to the cheers! This is what they want! This is what they need…” [The audio fades away as Misfit recomposes himself.] Misfit: “But it’s not what they’re going to get. At least not from me. I was told that my style wasn’t safe; ‘garbage’ wrestling isn’t safe. So I adjusted. Like the cancer I’ve always been accused of being, I mutated and adapted. I traveled the wrestling world and learned it all. I’ve been in shoot fights, I fought some of the hardest technical battles in Japan, I’ve soared above the rings with some of the most respected luchadors in the business.” [Misfit smirks.] Misfit: “I can do it all. But you, Kenny… you don’t seem to be convinced. You still just see a hardcore maniac… a ‘garbage’ wrestler… a menace. That, Kenny, is what outcasts me. I’ve adapted, and the system still rejects me. I still grow inside like a cancer, like a foreign cell… changing, mutating to survive against the torrent of medicine trying to kill me.” [Misfit turns down a dark alley, revealing a small colony of homeless bums sitting on crates. Misfit takes a seat amongst them.] Misfit: “These people have nothing. It rains so much here, they don’t even dry sticks to rub together for fire. No food, only rain water to drink…” [In the background, a particularly dirty homeless man picks up a rusted bucket and takes a huge gulp from it.] Misfit: “… and yet they accept me.” [The man brings the bucket over to Misfit and offers a drink, which he politely refuses.] Misfit: “They don’t ‘brown bag it,’ like most people think they do. They can’t afford it. They don’t fit into all the stereotypes that people think they do. Neither do I. That’s why they accept me. They feel my pain… but soon, Kenny. Soon, you will, too. You’ll see firsthand that damage that can be done when the system kicks…” [Misfit begins losing his composure again.] Misfit: “When it pushes…” [Misfit explodes into a flurry of hatred and anger.] Misfit: “When the system rejects you after all of your sacrifices! You will know Kenny what it feels like to be outcast! What it feels like to be rejected! With all of the odds stacked against me, you will know when I beat you, Kenny, how it feels! You will feel MY PAIN!” [A few of the homeless men pat Misfit on the shoulder as he tries to recompose himself and the scene fades away.]
