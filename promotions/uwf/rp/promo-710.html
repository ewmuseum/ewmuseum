Chronicles of Persia, part 2: Dilemma

date unknown

Nick Persia

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Don't look after your shoulders! What happened, happened. You don't need to look back. You shouldn't waiste your whole life looking at the past. That's what my old man used to day. I am very fond of him. He supported me in everything I did. He thought it was a good thing to fight where you believe in. But how far would you go? Where is your limit? When does your conscience start to toy with your mind? When are you manipulated by your feeling for good? Often I wonder if some people have even moral. Look at these terrorist organisations. They raise the bar at a point where you can literally do anything. Just say you do it in name of the Jihad. The sacred war and your actions will be tolerated. But I am not too suprised with that. Why would someone commit suicide? For a greater good perhaps? Then you should tell me what a greater good is. Is it something you try to achieve? Or is it something you simply can't comprehend? So much in this world I realdy know yet so much in this world is new for me. I like to look at it as if I am on a journey, discovering new things. Explore new worlds. And in some way, that's what i literally do. I am on a joruney. Each day I am on the road. Each day I meet new people and see new things. New things, well, I surely need to get used to this image. My dad laying in a hospital bed in a very critical condition. His face looks old. You know what they say, age wears the flesh. It never attracted my attention, but now it did. He had wrinkles all over his face. Underneath the white blanket he looked so peaceful. Not that I believe in karma but I believe he has finally found his peace. He's in a coma after a serious ailment. The Doctors are giving him a day, maybe two. They told me he's braindead. If he survived the night he would never wake up again. He would sleep for as long as his life would last. My dad was someone you looked up to. He had everything a son could wish for. He loved us, but was also strict when he needed to. He did what he had to. I remember this one time there was a fire in our nieghbour's house. My dad saved their dog personally by stepping trough a wall of fire twice. That's the most bravest thing I have ever seen. And that was like twenty years ago. I was just a child then. But I will never forget it. Maybe I am telling this out of melancholy. Going back to the great dad I had then. But now there's not much left of him. He's laying there. Helpless. I pity him. I hear the sirenes again. An emergency. Apparently a kid has been hit by a car frontally. His situation does not appear to be good and his fate stands upon the edge of a knife. The hardest fight of his life. Either live or die. There's no other way. There is this one story I once heard. They say that a man in a coma always dies in peace. That his dreams are so real that he actually thinks he's still living. But if he wakes up after that long period he remembers nothing. Quite sad actually. I will never get to know him as he once was. The paramedics rush the kid into the operation room, no time for any scan at all. The doctors are coming from all side. The blood is everywhere on the face of the young boy. His clothes are ripped apart and thrown onto the ground while they are moving him. They are running while they already examine him, they take a turn and disappear out of sight. I see my dad again. He made a noise I can not place. It's not mumbling, but it's not coughing either. I can't place the sound. But that's not what worries me. I am more concerned about other things. How am I going to tell mum? It all happened so fast. Even now after a long period of time I still don't know what to say. I think I will just tell what happened. There is a chance she won't understand though. She has memory loss. It's getting worse. Everything is getting worse, or so it seems. My mother can't live alone. She can't travel so she can't come with me. A troubling thought, but for now I have no solution. Time is running out. Decisions have to be made. Quickly. What to do, what to do? It's ironic how I always listened to my dad and he always said that you should always do everything you can to save the innocent. But now I stand before that chance and I hesitate. Not knowing if I make the right choice. Everything is a test. If you give the wrong answer you fail. 'Sir, can I speak with you now?' the nurse asked I nodded back as a reply. 'We have a bit of a situation. A little boy has been hit by a car and needs a lung transplant immediately. Your father has the same blood type. Now I am asking you, will you consider this?'
