PhD — UWF Headquarters

As the scene slowly begins to fade into focus we can see a picture of the Universal Wrestling Federation headquarters building. Outside the building it looked quite at harmony: cars were slowly driving by, many a people were walking and quietly chatting amongst one another down the sidewalk, the birds quietly chirping atop the ledges of the building. However, inside the Universal Wrestling Federation headquarters were quite the opposite. Phones were ringing off the hook, a rather large verbal argument began to ensue between two people at the front desk, and doors were slamming left and right in the variety of hallways inside of the building itself. Nevertheless, among all of this, there was a man in the corner sitting very properly talking on a black Sprint PCS telephone with one finger in his ear so that he can hear the conversation a bit better. Though the man was talking on the telephone, his total concentration was not on his telephone call – but his surroundings. As you could see his eyes moving impatiently around, almost appearing to be nausea and very nervous watching all the people running around just to yell at one another. As he wiped: a few beads of sweat from the top of his forehead. “Paulie, Paulie are you listening to me?” Paul Henry Dawson: “What – oh yes mother. You have my complete attention” Paul lied as he continued to nervously look around. Mrs. Dawson: “Are you a little nervous Paulie?” Paul Henry Dawson: “Too be quite honest with you Mother, I am a bit more nervous than you would think”. Mrs. Dawson: “Well honey that is normal. You are going into a new environment. A new career, new bosses, and new people.” Paul Henry Dawson: “But this reminds me so much about school. Big buildings, bad food for lunch, gorgeous women completely ignoring me, and to top it all off – I am surrounded by huge guys wanting to kick my ass.” Paul Dawson’s mother just let out a light giggle, which made Paul Dawson pull back the cellular phone and look into it deeply with a bewildered look drawn upon his face. Paul Henry Dawson: “Mother why are you laughing?” Mrs. Dawson: “Paul dear, you’re not that skinny little boy back in high school. You have grown – for the better. Paul Henry Dawson: “Yes, I know mother.” Mrs. Dawson: “Yes, I know. But no matter how big you do get, you are always going to be my little baby boy.” Paul Henry Dawson: “I know Mommy” Paul said cracking a very weak smile, before taking a quick look around to make sure that nobody was looking. Paul pushed himself up out of the painful chair that he was sitting in, and decided to stretch his legs. Upon pulling both of his legs back to loosen his muscles out, Paul Dawson began to pace back and forth past the row of chairs continuing his conversation with his mother. Mrs. Dawson: “You know Paulie, I am still not too crazy about you wanting to fulfill some crazy dream about becoming a professional wrestler. You had a great career; it is why you worked so hard in school. You were a professor of one of the greatest colleges in this country. But it’s fine, it is just some kind of weird mid-life crisis you are going through, you will be back to teaching in no time. Despite his mother’s beliefs, which still had valid points to them – Mrs. Dawson wasn’t too far off. Paul did work as hard as he could in school to somebody become a professor at a highly esteemed school – it is what he always wanted to do. Spread the knowledge that was once given to him. He loved to teach, though Paul was far from a mid-life crisis. The truth was he did not quit his job at Boston University he was fired. Why? In Paul’s eyes: for giving the justice that the school bad boy dissevered – and finally gotten. Paul Henry Dawson: “But school was just so darn hard, and this is just the same thing. We both know just how much trouble I had in school.” Mrs. Dawson: “Trouble in school?” Paul’s mother surprisingly asked. “You had straight A’s throughout your entire educational years. Do you know how many copies I have of your honor roll certificates from kindergarten to your senior year of high school? Or to mention that you were on the Dean’s list at Boston University.” The small simile that Paul Henry Dawson had on his face quickly disappeared, and hastily turned to an embarrassed look: a set up watery eyes, rosy red cheeks, and a slight frown. Paul’s mother was not too far off, her baby boy: Paul Henry Dawson was in fact a very brilliant child growing up, receiving straight A’s throughout his entire educational background. The learning part was never very hard for Paul; in fact he loved his studies, knowing the world that he does not know was his passion. He felt there was only one way a man could be measured – by this intelligence. However, his real problems in school were not his academic but his social problems. Never really very popular in school, Paul didn’t grow up with many friends in fact a good portion why he was the top of his class in school was – because learning was his true friend. His books, pencils – they never made fun of him or tried their best to embarrass him. Because of this, he really didn’t have a lot of luck with girls either – which drew plenty of pressure on Paul through his entire adolescent life. Mrs. Dawson: “Paulie – Paul dear are you listening to me?” His mother asked, but it was obvious that Paul was not paying attention to her. In fact he was completely blocking her out – as he stared off into space. Did Paul see something that caught his eye? No, from time to time Paul tended to slip out of reality and begin to daydream. What that went through his mind, nobody would know – as Paul was the type that kept to him-self. Slowly Paul would slip in and out of memories – never any of them good. 'Today is the first day of the rest of your life.' An astounding round of applause was heard all around. 'Today, everyone here are full grown adults. Prepared to make that first step of a long journey.' We then were able to see the shot come into view, inside a small assembly room. With a graduation ceremony was taking place, with a large group of young adults standing at the sound of those very few words. 'Now come and take that very first step as I call your name and receive your High School diploma.' Another round of clapping came from the soon to be graduates of the class of 1990. 'Bane, Roberts' was heard over the system inside the assembly room, with the face belonging to that name walked onto the stage; to take his first step of his life. While Robert Bane was accepting his diploma, you couldn't help but notice a certain ease with a few students still waiting for their diplomas. 'Paulie want a cracker?' said one of the boys in the second row flicker the ears of a young boy standing in front of him. Nevertheless, the lad seemed to pay no attention to him. The boy repeated, 'Paulie want a cracker”. However, once again the boy did not so much as turn to look at him. A boy that stood next to Paul leaned over asking 'what are you doing here Paul. You don't belong here. You should just go home now.' You could see the boy called Paul simply wipe a tear from his eye, still not saying anything to either one of the boys. 'Why don't you two just leave him alone,' demanded a boy who stood on the other side of Paul. 'You know Paul, you don't have to take this. Say something to them; better yet - sock him one.' The boys just laughed together, each giving a resounding 'yeah right”. Finally, the dean called upon the name 'Paul Henry Dawson, the Vale Victorian”. Paul's eyes lit up, with a face of ease donned upon him walking toward the stage and up to the podium. He walked over to the Dean, who shook his hand and gave him the diploma finally standing back to let Paul Henry Dawson say a few words to the class of 1990. Paul cleared his throat, looking very uneasy into the stands where his fellow alumni and family stood, anxiously awaiting his speech. However, Paul seemed to hesitate, he couldn't remember the words; the speech he memorized for hours - gone. Then it came to him, Paul then leaned over the podium and threw up all over the stage, with the families in shock - others in chunks. The Dean then rushed over to grab Paul and take him from the stage, but Paul Dawson refused. He approached the podium one last time - a mess, but nonetheless he returned. Paul opened his mouth to speak, but as he did a lime green substance fell from the ceiling, covering Paul Dawson in slime. Paul slowly wiped the junk out of his eyes to look up, where he saw a now empty bucket with a rope attached to it. As Paul looked over to the rest of the alumni, he saw his entire senior class laughing at him, with two of them waving and smiling. Seeing this, Paul did nothing but turn and walk off of the stage, leaving the assembly room. Mrs. Dawson: “Paulie – Are you listening to me?” Finally Paul snapped out of it. Paul Henry Dawson: “ Yes – Yes mother, of course I am listening”. Just then the ringing of a telephone could be heard coming from the front desk; the woman still arguing with the man pointed him to sit down where she received a dirty look before as he stepped to the side of the counter. The receptionist then picked up the telephone with a happy “hello, front desk personnel”. After listening to the person on the telephone for a moment she hung the telephone up and called a “Mr. Dawson”. Paul had a look of both shock and excitement on his face, as he slowly made his way over toward the receptionist and the front desk. Walking over with a big smile he gave a friendly “yes” to the woman with a sour face back on her face now that she had hung up the telephone. Headquarters Receptionist: “Mr. Paul Henry Dawson?” she fully asked. Paul Henry Dawson: “Yes, that is me”. Headquarters Receptionist: “Mr. Montgomery will see you now.” Paul Henry Dawson: “Thank you” Paul replied with a smile, still getting a scorn look by the receptionist. “Well mother, it’s time for my interview. I’ll call you later and let you know how things go.” Mrs. Dawson: “All right Paulie, time for good luck.” Paul Henry Dawson: “What do you mean?” Mrs. Dawson: “Good luck my little buddy.” Paul Henry Dawson: “Um, thanks mom.” Mrs. Dawson: “Oh no, you know what I mean.” Mrs. Dawson was always about her child; little Paul was all she had once Paul’s father passed away ten years ago. However, she always gave Paul extra attention – embarrassing attention, yet attention. Making him trick or treat until he was twenty years old, doing silly acts for her friends like singing kid songs and stupid sayings: whether or there were kids his age around. As much as he loved her, she drove him crazy at times. Paul Henry Dawson: “Oh no! No way mother, I am not doing that here. There are people watching.” Mrs. Dawson: “Paulie please, for mommy.” Paul Henry Dawson: “Mom –“ Mrs. Dawson: “Oh dear, my only child – can’t do the simplest of things to make this old widow get her one brief enjoyment for a day.” Paul Henry Dawson: “Mommy, please don’t cry.” Mrs. Dawson: “No, go ahead and get this job. Forget about me – forget about your mother and her health.” She said as you could hear a hint of crying in her voice. Paul Henry Dawson: “Mom –“ Mrs. Dawson: “Yes?” Paul Henry Dawson let out a small sigh looking at all of those surrounding him. Paul Henry Dawson: “Thanks” Paul said, and then swallowing both his spit and pride. “Buuudy!” Mrs. Dawson: “That’s my little Paulie Shore, now go get them baby – I love you.” Paul Dawson once again looked around to those smiling and snickering around him, as he mumbled, “I love you too”. Mrs. Dawson: “What was that dear?” Once again Paul seemed a big embarrassed, as he lowered his head to talk into the telephone. Paul Henry Dawson: “I love you too!” he practically screamed getting attention of most in the lobby room. Looking around, Paul was now a very pale color as he hit end on his cellular phone, closing it up and putting it back in his front right pocket of his black jeans. He then looked back up at the secretary, with a smirk on his face – yet still not very believable. Paul Henry Dawson: “Damn girlfriend, can’t get enough of hearing it”, he stuttered out. As the receptionist just looked up at him with an evil grin bestowed on her face. Headquarters Receptionist: “Sure, down the hall and to the left please”. Looking quite embarrassed: with a severe excretion of sweat beads dripping from his forehead. Paul Dawson nodded his head in acknowledgement, as he made his way down the hallway in which he was directed – as he left view the group standing around the front desk began to snicker with one another – even a few leaving out loud hard cackles making comments about the man that just left the front lobby.
