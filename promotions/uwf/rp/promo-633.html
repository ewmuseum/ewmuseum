Regal Rumble: Four Rules

14 Aug 2004

Mike Montgomery

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
So I changed huh? You got a phone, pick it up, call me The Player's Club is dead. This absolute truth must be understood and accepted before any others. Only when this is accepted can recent events truly be understood. You see, it was in an effort to make the wrestling world understand this truth that Mike Montgomery re-assumed leadership of the Player's Club. It was in an effort to make the wrestling world understand this truth that Mike Montgomery made his declaration. It was in an effort to make the wrestling world understand this truth that he signed one of the biggest four way matches in history, allowing the club to go out in the blaze of glory it truly deserves. How come we dont even talk no more And you dont even call no more We dont barely keep in touch at all And I dont even feel the same love when we hug no more And I heard it through the grape vine we even beefin now After all the years we been down Aint no way no how, this bullshit can be true We family and aint a damn thing changed, unless it's you Once this is understood and accepted, we move on to the second truth. This truth is recognition of the betrayal committed by Ezra Kazee. At this point, I'm sure you're wondering what I'm talking about. Ask yourself this, what is the greatest sin a man can commit? Rape? Murder? No, it is the sin of betrayal. In this case, the betrayal takes the form of hypocrisy, possibly the worst form imaginable. For you see, Ezra joined the Player's Club for the same reason as the rest of it's members; career advancement. Ezra knew how the club worked, knew what it's members did. He knew what he was getting into when he accepted our world champion's invitation and he accepted this without hesitation. He cannot claim ignorance. He cannot say that he did not know what was going on or what it's members did. And yet, despite all this, when the Player's Club machinations were publicly revealed, the 'Common Man' cried foul. He objected to the actions of Joshua Dunn and Kevin Starr. Why? Because he did not benefit from it. Where were his morals when then Commissioner Montgomery changed the tag title match at the Clash to allow him and Ezra to be on the same team? Where was his sense of fair play? And the Intercontinental Title Tournament which he won? A tournament that was organized by the Player's Club and not by the UWF. Why did a lack of respect for the rules not matter then? Why? Because these infringements benefitted Ezra. But the changes made to the rulebook by Dunn and Starr? These he did not take advantage of and as such he did not benefit. He did not benefit, and as such he cried foul. Ezra was more than happy to turn a blind eye to the practices of the Player's Club until the day finally came when those practices did not work to his advantage. This truth centers around the idea that it is necessary to accept that the business of Sports Entertainment does not center around sportsmanship. It centers around the idea that it is necessary to do whatever it takes to win. It is a cutthroat business in which one cannot have permanent friends, in which one must be willing to make sacrifices. To hurt people. To betray people. One must bend or break the rules at every opportunity, seize every ounce of power possible because this is what it takes to get ahead. It is not a physical contest between two stellar atheletes. This has never been the case. It is not about who is the better physical athelete, but about who is the best mental athelete. It is about who can spot the biggest advantages and exploit them to the most benefit. The formation of the Player's Club and the fame it brought to it's members was one such opportunity that was expertly exploited. That is the idea that we will see Kevin Starr in the Regal Rumble. We will see Ezra Kazee in the Regal Rumble. We will see Nick Persia in the Regal Rumble. We will see Joshua Dunn in the Regal Rumble. What we will not see, is Mike Montgomery in the Regal Rumble. This is because Mike Montgomery, a gifted cruiserweight...brilliant tactician...opportunist...will arrive in Tokyo as a contender but will leave as a champion. This fourth truth is why Mike Montgomery will not be in the Regal Rumble. He cannot win it because he will not be in it. He does not need to win it. He will go on to Live and Loud, but not as a challenger. He will go to Live and Loud having regained the title he lost through no fault of his own. He will go back there a champion. This final truth is the idea that one must accept the third truth. One must be willing to win at any cost, willing to place victory above all other concerns. The fourth and final truth is the idea that the win is all that matters.
