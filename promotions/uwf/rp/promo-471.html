UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #471

I wish I had gone home

27 Jun 2004

Joshua Dunn — Outside the UWF Complex

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Chalk one more up on the defeat board for Joshua Dunn, The Corp got lucky with a desperation move at the last minute, but then again… who’s keeping score? Stable Wars is just visible on the horizon, the first round card shown on the official UWF site, although speculations have been going on for some time, we finally have the actual card, and what a card it is. First up, a nice warm up to the actual Stable Wars, there is the TV title fatal four way. Then the carnage begins, with the first round of stable wars, three matches where the winners of each one goes on to a rumba in a three way main even, with a total of twelve men all in the ring at the same time, it’s sure to be a great card, and with the likes of Joshua Dunn, Digital Mortality, Seymour Almasy, Kevin Starr, Corporal Punishment, Moraless and Kenny mercury all trying to get to that coveted trophy, it’s sure to be one hell of a PPV One thing on the minds of millions, on the build up to the second most famous UWF card after Live and Loud, is how is the Players Club going to prevail? Witnessing setback after setback, their golden age of superiority must be nearing its end mustn’t it? With their founder out of action with a broken leg, and suffering loss after loss to the various other members of the roster, their morale must be hitting the bottom of the metaphorical barrel. Or so you would think Fuelled by ambition, and by the desire to keep their reign as top stable firmly routed into the heads of all that oppose them, they seek the Stable Wars Trophy. And with a three time Stable Wars finalist wrestling with the reigns of leadership to get the trophy which has eluded him for so many years. We are of course talking about Joshua Dunn, the Communist Scotsman, who has somehow won the hearts of millions. [The scene opens out in the front of the UWF Complex. The perfectly maintained paving slabs shine with a hint of yellow under the artificial street lighting provided by the ornamental street lamps leading up to the big glass doors, no one is around, but the weather is appalling, even for this time of night, the rain is lashing down, making it difficult to see all that’s happening around us, granted at the moment it’s not much. There is however one solitary figure visible, just off centre of the camera view, the collar of a jacket turned up in a vein attempt to keep the rain off himself] [This man is possibly average height, with nothing to compare him against it is difficult to tell. His brown hair lies plastered to his head with water, he looks like he’s been here for some time. The man is in fact newly recruited UWF reporter, Jack Kipling. Looking for his big break to get him into the main office of UWF HQ, he’s been designated with the graveyard shift to keep an eye out for any wrestlers in the early hours of the morning. Of course, there are some quiet spells in this athlete watch of his, and the rain only makes the wait worse. [Then again, there’s always that feint hope of there being one man using late night gymnasium facilities after being thrown out from another all night establishment.] [Taking in a deep breath of the sharply moist air and exhaling an impressive cloud of condensing breath, Jack bounces up and down on the spot, trying to keep his body temperature above absolute zero, a couple of drips falling from him, lost in the rain around him. He has another sigh and looks around at the landscaped area in front of the UWF complex, the budget spent on a fancy home stadium as it were. He shakes his head in defeat] Jack: You really expect a first class wrestler to appear right now and give you an exclusive interview? You are an idiot for taking this joke of an assignment… Fool [He’s about to walk away and get dry when he hears a noise, a noise that sounded like a swing door opening and shutting again. He spins on his heels, his knee length coat spinning around like something out of the matrix. Through the thick soup of rain in the air he can barely see a figure approaching the road, but he does see now the car that he missed before, the black ford, recognisable in its position, only a selected few vehicles are permitted to park in the front of the UWF building, that of owners and of athletes, and in the UWF, they are one in the same. The identifying pass and number plate on the car blurred out for legal reasons on your TV screen. But to Jack, they are as clear as ever, and his bleak face lights up with the intensity of the sun that won’t be seen for another 6 hours at least. He runs over to the figure whose fast approaching the car, and we can now see has a holdall slung over his shoulder. While trying to intercept the figure, Jack calls out, desperately trying to get him to notice him] Jack: Lucky Break here I come. MR. DUNN! MR. DUNN! Please, can I have a few minutes of your time please? [Frantically Jack runs towards, who we must presume is Joshua Dunn. His arms waving enthusiastically, his drenched self slapping through every puddle between hi and the Scotsman. To his relief Dunn turns to see what the commotion is. He drops the hold all to his side and watches with patient curiosity as the maddened Jack runs towards him limbs flailing. He still just stands there watching him when Jack finally catches him and starts panting unstoppably, while doubling over attempting to catch his breath, puffing up a quickly dissipating cloud of condensing air] Jack: A… Moment of… your… time please? [He starts panting again but seems to be able to catch his breath, possibly to the disappointment of Joshua. Finally able to stand up straight again, Jack faces the man he pursued for a brief spell of time. Well I say face, but now we can see that Jack must be below average height, as he comes up to Joshua’s Chest. One corner of Dunn’s mouth turns up at the end as he looks the wannabe reporter in the eye] JD: Make it quick, I’m getting wet. [The rain trickles down Dunn’s exposed upper arms and starts to soak through his muscle shirt. The cold soothing his aching red hot muscles] Jack: Sorry, I… well… ahh… [The corner of the mouth turns down again and an awful frown replaces it. Jack just looks blank, seeing as he wasn’t exactly expecting a UWF Superstar to appear on his graveyard shift, let alone let him ask questions, he was caught a bit off guard. Too bad for him] JD: Time’s up, now if you excuse me [Dunn slings the holdall over his shoulder again and unlocks his car with the key, dumping the back in the back and climbing into the driver’s seat. Jack’s jaw almost hit the floor, his big break and he blew it, an immense gut feeling of anger mixed with disappointment flooded his every being, and with a sudden prang of journalistic come paparazzi instinct, he ran round to the other side of the car and hopped in along side of the Communist Scotsman, much to the distaste of Dunn] JD: Get out of my car Jack: But it’s wet out, and your going my way anyway [Dunn fixes another deadly scowl onto Jack, whose cheeky smile just grins back] JD: You don’t even know where I’m going yet Jack: But you’re going my way, and I can ask you a few questions on the way [Another smile is fired at Dunn, and this one dissolves the frown fixed on his face, and Dunn turns in the seat and starts up the petrol engine of the ford. He laughs to himself and shakes his head] JD: I hope not all of the new UWF reporters are like you Jack: No, I’m one of a kind [The car pulls out of the parking space at the side of the road and down the road a bit before some little niggling detail surfaces to the top of Jack’s mind] Jack: How did you know I was new? [Dunn laughs to himself again] JD: You think all reporters are told to wait around in the rain in the middle of the night for UWF superstars to appear? [Jack turns and faces out through the windscreen, his face white with embarrassed shock, this expression prompts a bellowing laugh from Dunn, as he signals left to enter the speedway on the way across town. Strangely enough, the road is mostly desolate, apart from the odd car passing them on the other side of the crash barrier. Artificially planted trees line the speed way forming a wooded area at either side, where one mayor or other though it would be a good investment, a couple of them damaged where a careless driver has strayed too far off the road and smashed into them. The wipers of the car whip back and forth at the high speed setting, but fail to keep the rain off the windshield for any reasonable length of time. Sensibly Dunn keeps the speed to a safe minimum, treading carefully over the wet road] Jack: You feeling a bit down after your loss to Corporal Punishment? JD: Not really [He keeps his eyes fixed on the road and peers over the steering wheel trying to get a better view point of the road, the screen starts fogging up, and this promptly leads to Joshua turning on the demister fan sending a large gust of warm air through the car] JD: He’s a slippery customer, I’m just glad I found that out before stable wars; otherwise he would have caught me off guard in the tournament Jack: You’re not bothered about the loss? JD: No, I do have one of the best losses to wins ration in the whole UWF, not many people can say that. Jack: I suppose [His reply seems a bit deflated, not getting much out of the superstar as he concentrates on driving. He thinks to himself for a minute, causing an uneasy silence in the car, the demister fan creating an edgy ambiance. Then another string of questions leap to the forefront of Jack’s head] Jack: How do you think the Players Club are going to fair in the Stable wars tournament? JD: What do you mean? Jack: Well you seem to have been on a bit of a slump recently, your impressive array of titles have steadily flaked away, you’re only left with the world title and that’s only because Mike’s unable to step into the wrestling ring JD: Yes, there have been a bit of finalisation needed I guess, but you have to realise that we have to adapt to a different method of running the stable efficiently, it’s not that easy Jack: Is that because of the friction between you and Kevin Starr JD: Starr’s an intelligent boy, he’ll realise that there’s no way he’s going to win stable wars without the aid of Me and the rest of the Players Club, he better get his team face on in time. Jack: So you’re not intimidated by your first round drawing against the Chaos society? JD: Chaos Society? You mean the grouping of people with the worst record ever? Jack: They hold the IC title, and had the TV title JD: That proves one of them has a glimmer of hope. Other than that, there’s nothing that the superior athleticism and team work of the Players Club Jack: Are there any Stables that you’re particularly concerned about? JD: Well, as no one knows about the mystery stable, you have to be a bit nervous, because you have no idea who they are, for all we know they could be a matching of hall of famer’s coming together for the sole purpose of wining stable wars, or they could be a random conglomeration of jobbers thrown together by senior management to make up numbers. The Resurrection are intimidating, only because of their dirty tricks, for example trying to take out their main rival stable by taking out the founder of the stable. That plan wasn’t as well thought out as most things that Digital usually devises, but it did suffer us a crippling blow, pardon the pun. Jack: And of Chrono Cross? You’re not worried about them? JD: What is to say about them, Corporal Punishment is still and aging crock, he has to fight with his own incontinence along with the other wrestlers, Kenny Mercury is a joke, Seymour Almasy has fallen from his untouchable prowess and Lew Spectres manager… well that was just hilarious. In fact I don’t expect to see a promotion from most of them. Jack: And what of the Iron Curtain [Dunn’s head jerks at the mention of the Curtain, cringing at those horrid memories, of the “medication” and the “cleansing”. It isn’t very pleasant to have your memories wiped from you with no way to get them back. No wonder the next response from Dunn was what it was] JD: Please, do not mention them in front of me; it reminds me of a time I would rather forget Jack: But you still hang onto the Communist beliefs long after that time, why is that? JD: I said leave it Jack: Is there something that no one knows about that makes you hang on to that belief? [Dunn uncharacteristically loses his cool and takes his eyes off the road for a moment and looks directly at Jack with red hot flames burning in his eyes] JD: I SAID LEAVE IT [A silence follows as Jack looks intimidated by the harshness of the tone in Dunn’s voice, and holds himself as far away from him as possible because of the relatively compact space of the car. Dunn keeps and eye on him for a moment longer and looks back to the road, perhaps that look was a fraction of a second too long…] [In the road a stray dog, mangy and soaked to the bone by the rain, has wondered onto the lane Dunn’s car is driving in, caught in the headlights, the retched creatures eyes are picked out by the dipped headlights of Dunn’s car. Mesmerised with fear it just stands looking at the impending doom coming towards it, stopping it from running away to the relative safety of the central crash barrier. Too late does it decide to make for that haven] [Inside the car, a panicked look comes over the face of Joshua and Jack as they lay eyes on the creature in their lane. Jack holds desperately onto the seat, his knuckles turning white, Dunn has forced himself back into his seat firmly, his elbows locked straight and he swings the steering wheel to the left in an attempt to avoid the animal. The dog runs to the central reservation, but the damage has been done. Try as he might, Dunn loses control of the car. The back wheels swing right round as Dunn tries to correct the angle of the car, the wet road lubricating the surface of the tarmac, allowing for the wheels to spin right round and to the other side] JD: HOLY JESUS! Jack: AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAaah [Dunn tries to straighten out the car again, but he steers too much, just causing the swerve to be amplified forcing the car off of the road and onto the side embankment through the trees. With Jack screaming all the way, the car tires suddenly find grip in the sludgy mud of the grass around the ornamental trees, catapulting the car further into the trees. Joshua swings the wheel first to the left, and then to the right, and to the left again, somehow getting past several of the trees] [In the centre of the field of vision of the camera, eerily centred on the windscreen, directly in front of the car and fast approaching is one of the first planted of the trees, and there was no avoiding it, but nevertheless Joshua tried] [Swinging the wheel to the right one last time, he catches the tree with the left headlight, spinning the car around 180 degrees and slamming the rear right hand side door into another tree finally bringing it to a halt. The engine block smokes dangerously as the engine overheats, radiator fluid leaking out of the front of the car, the rain crashing around the car, rattling off the roof] [Inside the car, the camera has been flung into the front of the car and sits at a slant, a large crack down in the left corner of the lens. We can see Dunn slumped over the steering wheel, blood trickling down the side of his head] [The Scene Cuts out to Static]
