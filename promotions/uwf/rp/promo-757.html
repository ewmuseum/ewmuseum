UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #757

History repeats itself

date unknown

Nick Persia — A dark park

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
L’Histoire se repeter Do you speak French, Adien Pryde? I think that you know what it means, but let me clear it up for you. It means that history will repeat itself. You know what I am pointing at. I am pointing to all my victories I’ve had at your expense. I’ve never lost to you. That is a fact. Another fact is that will not change, Mr. Pryde. I can tell you a dozen stories about how I am going to beat you, and I could tell you a thousand stories why I am the better man. However, I think no one would care. There are more important things in the world than you, Pryde. I could do better things with my time then wasting it at your unworthy being. Your appearance might be that of a human, but your behaviour is that of a dog. You play along very nice with some aggression, but always by the rules. Not once you have done something against the rules. You claim to be an anarchist, but I don’t see why they call you an anarchist. I think it’s more ironic than serious. Just like you are. Nothing more than a comedy act. Nice for once, but boring thereafter. You’re minor, Pryde. You can just as well acknowledge it, for your own good. You can claim to be my nemesis and all of that, but in the end what does it matter? You are just a tiny fraction in this world. You mean nothing when you take a look at the other guys in this federation. Accomplishment is something you definitely lack. Where you failed, I succeeded. What has changed since our last confrontation? Is there something that happened that makes you more of a threat to me? The simple and plain answer is: no. I’ve only gotten better while he remained himself. He’s no more than an obstacle you just can’t get rid of. He’s like a weed in a garden. Each time you destroy it, but every time it weasels its way back to you. It never ends. So it is with you Pryde. You never end. Your suffering will never end. Not as long as you are here. Not as long as I am here. Nick Persia is sitting. As he looks around him he noticed that he is alone. After him there are trees, many trees. They appear to be very old as their seize is huge. Before him there is a huge lawn, as far as the eye can reach. The grass is short, like it received a cut not so long ago. The light of the sun is reflected by the moon on the earth. In the glasses of Persia we see the light once more reflected, as if it was a mirror. Nick Persia: “Do you know why I am here? Do you have a clue why I am here? Do you have the faintest idea, Mister Adien Pryde? I think not. I am sitting here, enjoying the moonlight, in the middle of the night because I want to. I don’t care what other people think. I don’t care what they might say about me. I simply do not care about poor souls anymore. You see, I think trashing other people is only to make yourself feel better.” Nick Persia takes a good breath. It’s not cold on this night, but the wind is still blowing very hard, especially in this open area. Nick Persia: “I will tell you the truth now. Not my opinion, not your opinion or the opinion of Richard Hayes, but my own. I am telling you the truth. I have defeated you each and every time. You name it, I have done it. I never failed at winning a title. You on the other hand, failed to beat me. You failed to take a title. You failed to do what you are supposed to do. You failed to do what you want to do. Admit it, you failed you. You failed yourself because you did not live up to your own expectations. You are an embarrassment to your own family as they felt you could have done better. They’d rather have a son who isn’t famous but is someone who people look up to than a famous joke.” Nick Persia gets a smile on his face, but it’s cold. No warm glance in his eyes or humour in his eyes. It’s the mere future what gives him this devilish grin. The sights of seeing someone suffer, although he was not a sadistic person. He just wanted to find a way to get rid of the anger. Nick Persia: “As you might have realised, I am not making you a star. I make many things in this world, like money. But above all else, I am making the people turn the television to watch the Universal Wrestling Federation. They know my name. They know my face. They know my deeds. I am known for my deeds. I am known for my victories. I am known for my accomplishments. You might have questioned yourself what you are known for. I tell you. When people hear your name they think the following: “Isn’t that the guy Nick Persia beat?”. I agree it’s cruel, Lord knows I do. As much as I don’t like the facts as they are I can’t change them. Do you know why? It’s the blatant truth.” The words echo back, slightly more monotone than they were when Nick spoke the words. Nick Persia: “There is no need to feel ashamed. You aren’t a failure. You are a great athletic person with a great future in front of you. The future smiles back at you. It really does and as good as you are it is simply not enough. Do not despair as many have taken the same road you are about to take. The hard way. Your ego didn’t want to believe it. You always said it. You were the best, weren’t you? You were the best. Until you met me, quite some time ago now. But there’s something that has changed you as much as it had changed me at the time.” Nick Persia smiles again, but a warm smile this time. Nick Persia: “You would have never guessed it, but you also beat The Plague. It meant a glorious career for me. I won a title and I broke a record. Would this be a strange twist of fate? Once again, the answer is no. You see, I am your God. You can’t defeat a God. You can only match him…….” Nick Persia whispered these last words as he walks away from the park with the wind in his back, heading to the west.
