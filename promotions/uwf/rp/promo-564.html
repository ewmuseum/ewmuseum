Dropping in on the enemy part 3

for SAS (year unrecorded)

Jay Vyper — East Berlin

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
…Their weapons were aimed directly at Team X. The foremost silhouette began to shout at them in German, telling them to throw down their weapons, to put their hands over the heads, and to surrender. They wanted the Zhevakovs, of course. Only a heartbeat passed before the defectors came out of the house into the street, with their hands held above their heads. Smart, actually. They didn't really have any way to get out of that house, and if they stayed inside, they were almost certain to be killed by the East German soldiers. Team X was silent, awaiting Vyper's instructions. They didn't wait long. 'Stratus, take out the lights,' Vyper growled, almost inaudibly. 'Beast, you and me'll hit the fence. Shouldn't be too much trouble. Jade, cover Stratus and follow our lead.' Silence followed. They were waiting for him to count. 'One.' The East German officer shouted at them again. 'Two,' Vyper whispered. The Zhevakovs were cursing them from behind. But getting captured was not part of the game plan. Not at all. 'Go !' Vyper roared. Stratus spun, pulling a modified Uzi from inside his baggy shirt. He fired at the precise moment that the Germans did. Their huge spotlights exploded with his gunfire, and David Stratus went down, hit by a two bullets, at the very least. In the new darkness, Vyper and Beast ran at the soldiers in front of the fenced-in end of the alleyway. Vyper only got a few shots off before he took a bullet in the shoulder and dropped his weapon. Vlad took one bullet as well, but he and Vyper both kept moving. Both men were Very thankful for Kevlar as Beast shot the last of the guards. Jade blinked as her eyes adjusted, but she picked up Stratus's Uzi without even glancing down at it. Her own semiauto pistol wasn't much good in this kind of firefight. And Stratus didn't need the Uzi anymore. He had other weapons. Stratus rolled across the filthy cobblestones of the alley and rose quickly, Shooting as fast and as accurate as anyone ever had. He and Jade turned to keep pace with Vyper and Vlad, but Stratus glimpsed something from the corner of his eye that brought him up short. In a widening pool of blood and gore lay Grigorii and Katrina Zhevakov. It seemed they'd caught a bullet or two. Stratus was saddened, but he'd seen senseless death hundreds of times before. The defectors knew the chances they were taking. Traitors often ended up taking a bullet. 'Stratus, move!' Jade shouted. Some of the soldiers behind them had recovered, and bullets were flying again. Jade started after Vlad and Vyper, toward a massive hole in the fence that clearly marked the trail of Vlad. But even as he moved away, something else registered in Stratus's mind. Something not quite right about the bodies of Grigorii and Katrina Zhevakov. Their throats were bloody. Even torn? There were bullet wounds as well, but bullets hadn't done that. Stratus was furious, but there was nothing to be done for it now. That was war. He wished he could believe that he wasn't getting used to it, to the death and deceit. But he knew he'd be lying if he did. Where once Stratus's stock-in-trade had been freedom, it had now become death. They ran past dead East German soldiers, and Stratus pushed it all from his mind. If he had to be numb to do the job, then numb was how he'd live. He and Jade ran through the hole in the fence, weapons at the ready. Vyper was waiting on the other side. 'Where's Vlad?' Jade hissed, as they fell into step with Vyper. 'He took point,' Vyper replied. 'Jade, you'd better give me the disk.' Stratus frowned. They kept moving, but Jade reached inside her blouse to retrieve the silver disk. She hesitated a moment, and Stratus didn't blame her. Now wasn't the time or the place for… A whistle. Team X whistle, coming from straight ahead. From the open street where two men, one huge and one short and broad, stood silhouetted in the light from the street. Vyper and Beast. 'Jade, don't!' Stratus snapped. But Jade had figured it out as well, and Raven knew she'd blown her shot. She laughed, even as Even as she lifted her mask and her faces morphed back to her normal beautiful face. 'Can't blame a girl for trying,' she said. Vyper and Vlad were twenty yards ahead when the voice came down from above. 'No,' it said. 'You can't.' She'd leapt from a second story window, and the tiny, flexible young woman's kick dropped Raven to the cobblestones. Stratus knew fast: He was fast. But this newcomer. ...dressed in a black jumpsuit, her red hair cropped in a pageboy cut, she looked like a high-school kid, even as she blocked Jade's punch, and then took Jade down with a kick to the gut. Somehow, the girl had the disk in her hand, and it glinted in the light from the street. Vyper was shouting, running toward them. Any second, the East German soldiers would be coming through the hole in the wall. Sirens wailed not far off. Stratus had his H&K semiauto in his hand, aimed at the girl's heart. There wasn't anything she could do to him. She hit him; he would have pulled the trigger. He hesitated. Electricity arced from her wrist, and Stratus screamed as thirty thousand volts surged through his body. It only lasted a second, but that was enough to drop him where he stood. At least he was still conscious. Any normal man would have been out cold. When he managed to look up, Raven and Jade were already on their feet, drawing an aim at something moving high up on the wall to the left. It was the girl, of course. But how? Of course, how didn't matter. They had the girl in their sights. “Move it!' Vyper shouted behind them. Gunfire erupted from the shattered fence they'd left behind, and Vlad and Vyper returned fire, trying to keep the Volksarmee from coming through the fence. But the distraction had been enough. Both Raven and Jade had been thrown off. The girl was gone. So was the disk. Team X was up and moving. 'Meet back at the pickup,' Vyper snarled. 'I don't care how you get there, just get there!' 'What about me?' Raven asked. Vyper glared at her. 'What about you? Go!' he shouted. All hell broke loose, but they didn't need to fight anymore. Running was easy. Jade would have it the hardest, Stratus knew. But she'd find a way. She always did. As she fled across the rooftops, the black thief slowed for only a moment: just long enough to grab her long coat and the peasant scarf she used to cover her hair. Only half a block from where soldiers were still trying to make sense of what had happened-not to mention trying desperately to find any of the spies they thought for certain they had cornered-Natasha crawled carefully down the side of a building to street. She hadn't believed her KGB controller when he'd first explained how the gloves and boots would work. That the synthetic stretch-fabric would possibly stick to any thing- especially since the science used to create the micro suction technology was top secret-seemed too much like science fiction for Natasha. But it worked after all. It had saved her life, in fact. Natasha moved along the fronts of buildings, trying to keep out of sight as much as possible. Dawn was only a few hours away, and any lone woman walking the streets at this time of night was likely to be stopped and questioned-particularly if she didn't look like a prostitute. Soon enough, however, she was moving into the newer section of East Berlin, where shining metal and concrete towers had sprung up in what was once the center of Berlin. Natasha considered the newer architecture, side by side with venerable old structures, a scar on the city's face. But that was one of the many costs of progress. It disturbed her deeply. She glanced up at the TV tower, nearly twelve hundred feet up, and saw that even with the moon only a sliver, the tower's stainless steel sphere reflected its light. There was something about it that gave her hope, like a beacon, of sorts. Natasha turned, left and was comforted to know that her warm bed in the Serolina Hotel was only a few blocks away. In the distance, sirens still wailed. But as she walked, one siren singled itself out. It grew louder, as if it were heading this way. She thought to hide out in a recessed doorway, try to get off the street. Then she realized it was not just a siren. It was like a voice. Screaming. Wailing. Not him, she thought. Not now! The Black Thief ran. Not because she feared her pursuer, But because she was angry. She'd been so close. But then, she realized, Cassidy could not have found her by chance. He had to have been following her. She tapped unconsciously at the belt pocket on her jumpsuit, inside which she had hidden the disk. He would not have it. The last time they had met, he had forced her hand. Natasha was a spy, not an assassin. She would not kill unless she were in danger, or specifically ordered to do so. But Cassidy had forced her to hurt him. She'd do it again if she must. Kill him, if it came to that. But she wouldn't let him have the disk. Plate-glass shop windows reflected only the night off to her left. The wailing grew louder and lower. He was almost upon her. Natasha ran to the shop's door, used all her strength to dock at the wood next to the lock. The frame shattered, and then he was inside. Even as she ducked her head into the shop, the plate glass shattered from the power of Cassidy's Fist. Natasha shielded her face from the glass. She crouched in the dark and listened. If she could get the jump on Cassidy, get him with her 'widow's bite,' the electrical charge she could fire from her gloves, she would get away clean. But if he saw her first, that gun of his would take her down instantly. Then all would be lost. Footsteps crunched in glass. In the distance, there was shouting and the sound of approaching vehicles. Polizei, or Volksarmee. Either way, Cassidy wouldn't want them finding him. Whereas if they found Natasha, it might be uncomfortable at first, but she would get to go home alive. If she had some way to signal them…if she was conscious long enough to signal them. One grating foostep. Then another. Then…nothing. 'Well, well,' a gravelly voice said from outside the shop. 'If it ain't Tom Cassidy. Fancy meetin' you here, Inspector.' Natasha recognized that voice. It belonged to the agent named Vyper. But how? “Aye,' Cassidy said “An' I'd say the same to you, Vyper, if I didn't have a little rabbit down a hole, and not a great deal of time to complete the hunt.' Shouting, even closer now. A gunshot or two. 'Yeah, the thief,' Vyper agreed. 'I followed her here.' Natasha's mind reeled. Had he actually somehow followed her trail? What kind of man could do such a thing? 'Ye're after her too, then?' Cassidy asked, his voice tightening. 'She's got somethin' I want,' Vyper explained. 'What happens to her after that, I don't much care.' 'Good,' Cassidy said. ' 'Cause I mean to kill her.' In the darkness, the Black Thief’s eyes grew wide. For the first time in a very long time, she felt like the teenager she was. Espionage was a dangerous game, but it was a cold, unfeeling one as well. Yet there was something in Agent Cassidy's voice that told her this was very, very personal. And that he meant what he said.
