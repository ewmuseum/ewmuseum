Bad Memories

16 Jul 2004

Kevin Starr — Around the docks in Boston

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Kevin was 7 years old… he had just gotten a ball and glove for his birthday and he was eager to try it out. He excitedly walked up to his Dad who, as usual, was sitting on the couch in front of the television with a cold one in his right hand. Kevin quickly thought up the right words to ask the question, it was a very rare occurrence for his Dad to ever do something with him, but Kevin was confident that this time it would be different. Kevin popped the question quickly, waiting for his Dad to guzzle down the beer left over in his throat and respond. He nodded towards Kevin, giving him the fake smile that we’ve all used before. He didn’t want to play catch, waste of time he thought, but his wife had been asking him to spend more time with Kevin for years now. Kevin jumped up and down excitedly towards the backyard, it was the first time he got to spend some one on one time with his Father for as long as he can remember. Kevin positioned himself at one end of the backyard, while his Dad slowly stepped over to the other. They began to toss the ball around, back and forth. Kevin smiled and laughed as he watched his father curse under his breath as he rustled around in some bushes, searching for the poorly thrown ball by Kevin. Finally his Dad spotted the ball, deep down within the bush. He reached his arm way down in when an idea came across his mind. Who was he fooling? He didn’t want to play catch. He’ll just pretend that he can’t find the ball and go back inside. Kevin’s Dad looked at him and shrugged, telling Kevin the unfortunate news. A disappointed Kevin Starr watched as his Dad dropped his glove down and waltzed back inside. Kevin knew he was lying, he could see the ball from here, even at a young age he could see right through his Dad, he didn’t want to play with him. Tears began to build up in Kevin’s eyes as he walked over to the ball, but he was able to fight them back, according to his Dad, only little girls cried. He reached in and grabbed the ball which was in clear sight and looked back up towards the house. There, he could see in plain sight, his Dad sitting on the couch drinking beer. Realizing that his Dad chose beer over himself, he was unable to hold the tears back any longer. [We return to present day.] [There he stood… the man that most children look up to and idolize, the man that most children go to when they have a problem, but not in Kevin’s case. He hated everything about his Dad.] [Kevin stared out through the heavy fog and rain, staring in to the eyes of a man he had not seen for 15 years. Kevin was at a loss for words, something that doesn’t happen very often to him. He didn’t know what to do. He just stood there silent, watching his Father standing there in front of him with open arms. Kevin didn’t want to hug him, not after what he put him and his Mother threw. The tension between the two builds as the wind begins to pick up around them. His Dad finally breaks the silence, realizing that this is going nowhere.] Dad: “Hey-uh. Why don’t we get ourselves some shelter, it’s starting to come down hard. Come on, I’ve rented out a place just down the street here.” [Kevin didn’t reply, but he was right though. The rain started to whip around against his face, feeling as if someone was poking him with needles repeatedly. His Father led the way back over to his apartment down the street, Kevin decided it was best to follow, after all, he wanted some answers.]
