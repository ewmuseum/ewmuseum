UWF (2001–07) Roleplay

Clark RP 1 (Gary Clark's return)

14 Dec 2005
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is A roleplay written by the handler of Gary Clark for his return to the UWF in December 2005, e-mailed to the UWF's booker on 14 December 2005; this is the e-mail, from the Janitor's own mail archive, donated on 9 October 2026. The sender’s address is marked.

Part of the same return as the angle he sent for SAS two days later. Served whole, as written.

---------- Forwarded message --------- From: Gary Clark [detail withheld] Date: Wed, Dec 14, 2005 at 7:12 AM Subject: Fwd: Clark RP 1 Nearly four years have passed. Things to remember; absolutely nothing to forget. The dark of the UWF was rising back in early 2002. His life in the UWF had been turned upside down. He left into the wilderness, not seen by anyone in the wrestling world since. An exit from the world that created him; the world that formed him. And where did he go? 4 years and he just vanished?! People wanted by the Mafia disappear. Special Agents who "die" disappear. Popular, successful wrestlers don't just disappear. They fade out, like the old school promo. This guy, he just disappeared. Became transparent. For all intents and purposes, this guy died. But people just assumed that he had fought his demons and paid his dues. They assumed that when no-one mentioned his name any more, he was as good as dead. His life was forfeit, according to the fame hound's rule of "bad publicity was better than no publicity". He vanished, and without as much as an email, a telegram, a fax, a text message, a phone call or a scribbled note on a pizza box. His life was packed up into a holdall. Everything that this guy had worked for over the years was packed into crates and put into storage. He left with a green fabric bag slung over his shoulder, an MA1 jacket and a pair of roughed up old jeans. He left his life behind, and he was done. November 2005 - a small town outside of London, England . The town was small. It was tiny, in fact. The town was probably best described as a social holocaust. People went there to live their mundane lives. As towns go, it was a tragedy. 5, 10, 15 years ago, this town had a thriving market as a port on the River Thames. People went there to socialize, to meet others, to drink, to shop... to live. After the mid 1990's, the town went into a slump that it never recovered from. The invention of out-of-town shopping centres put paid to the fiscal micro-economy that this town had to offer. Now, the main attraction of the town was its "King Rooster" fried chicken house, and the vast amount of bookmakers. Nothing could help this town recover, and people rarely ventured far from their doors for fear of being infected with this disease. They didn't want to be on the edge of virtual combustion. The town destroyed everything it ever had. The church bell tolled for the 9th time as it showed 9:00am . A wintry breeze blew across the town square. The empty town square. The pigeons were the only lives that were not afraid of the history of the town's demise. Not quite the only life, actually. A long haired man, wearing a black and white fleece, walks through the middle of the pigeons, causing them to fly in every conceivable direction. As he walks through the crowd, a mobile phone rings. The trill of the standard tone scares the few remaining pigeons away, as the man takes the call. "Hello?" "Your life was never quite the same, was it?" "Who is this? How did you get this number?" "Neither matter. But answer me this, my friend... you've been living unfulfilled for the last few years. You've never really regained what life you had. You packed your bags, and then you walked down that train line to the nearest station. I know. I watched you. I watched you throw everything away in one moment of madness. Do you regret it?" "Who exactly do you think this is?" "I know full well who this is, Mr Carter. You never really expected just to disappear, did you? Not for good." "Who are you?" "I'm a concerned associate. You know me. I know you do. More importantly, I know you." "Wha... who..." "I'll be speaking to you soon, Mr Jack Carter. If that is what you want to call yourself, I'll be speaking to you soon." The call terminates, and the long haired man looks around the town square. There is not a person in sight; the area is completely deserted. The man flicks his hair back, and strokes his long, untidy beard. Sitting down on the nearest empty bench, not hard in an empty town, the man holds his head in his hands. He felt unfulfilled. He knew it, and the guy on the other end of the phone knew it. And if they both knew it, then others knew it. Had four years gone that quickly? Had people forgotten? He guessed so. Did people still wonder what had happened? Obviously. He took out his wallet, and leafed through the notes to find a piece of paper. A dog-eared, creased, screwed up piece of paper. All it contained was a telephone number. It was all that was needed. He needed to hear a friendly, reassuring voice. And for once, the friendly reassuring voice wasn't just the speaking clock. In four years, he hadn't even looked at the piece of paper. Yet, when his lowest ebb was there, he knew that if things were to get dragged up, all he had to do was call the number. One telephone call would end it. Yet, every time things got bad, and his memories kept rearing their ugly heads, the number was thrust back into the wallet. There never was a time when things got so bad as to call the number that would bring that time back. Closing the wallet, he looked at his hands. They were shaking. His emotion had the better of him, but he wouldn't let the past win over the future. The number was screwed up into a ball, and pushed back into his pocket. He knew that later on, he would rescue the piece of paper and put it back into his wallet. Of course he knew that. But at this moment in time, he was like a junkie who was coming clean. His need for a fix had passed, and for at least the next fifteen minutes, he would feel like he had conquered Mount Everest again. But he knew. He knew that these things don't just go away. They just get worse. And nightmares… well, nightmares don't always re-occur. Just the worst ones.
