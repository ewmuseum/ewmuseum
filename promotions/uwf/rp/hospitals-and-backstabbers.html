Hospitals and Backstabbers (SAS)

5 Dec 2005

Chris Storm — Hospital

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(Note: At WNU, Tessa Windsor and Seymour Almasy attack Chris Storm with a steel chair and Chris is sent to the hospital.) He hated hospitals. Which, considering his current condition, was a bad thing. Pain rippled across the back of his skull as Chris moved around in the small white bed, trying desperately to get comfortable. “Stop fidgeting so much, Mr. Storm. You don’t want to reopen your stitches.” Chris looked over at the nurse and growled. He hated hospitals and he hated being here. Reaching to the back of his head, Chris felt where his skull had been busted open by that violent chair shot at the hands of Seymour Almasy. As much as he hated to admit it, Almasy had been the first man to send Chris to the hospital in a long time. He had fought many battles, losing many of them. Despite that he had always been tough enough to handle whatever his opponents could dish out. Not this time though. On Wednesday Night Underground Tessa had lured Chris into a trap and allowed Seymour to catch Chris from behind with his guard down. After the chair shot everything went black for Storm. The next thing he was able to remember was waking up in the hospital, alone. The nurse gave Chris a smile as she looked up from his chart. “You have a visit, Mr. Drake. Do you want to see them now?” Chris’ heart nearly leaped out of his chest at that moment. He knew she wouldn’t leave him here to riot alone in this hospital. Not with how she knew how much he despised the places. Tessa felt bad about the set up and came to apology. Or maybe, just maybe she wasn’t the one behind it. That Seymour had acted on his own accord. “YO CHRIS! Whazzup?” What cruel fate. Chris must have pissed someone off upstairs. “What do you want, Laguna?” That joy Chris felt inside, it was now a dark and cold void. He had wanted his sweet angel, not this idiotic buffoon. “I’m in enough pain without you making me feel like a drill is going into my brain.” Laguna seemed to have been ignoring Chris as he took the seat next to the bed. “Yeah that’s cool dog. So you going to eat that?” Laguna pointed to the pathetic excuse the hospital passed off as food. Chris shook his head and pushed it toward Laguna, who gladly chowed it down as if he hadn’t eaten in days. “So anyways,” Laguna started after finishing the last bite, “why did you let those suckers whoop your ass like that? That’s not like you. You’re the skull cracker, not the cracked skull.” Chris looked away, toward the white washed wall. He knew what Laguna would say, how he had gone soft. That was not the type of thing Chris needed to hear before going into a big match. Speaking of which, Chris had yet to prepare for the match. Pulling himself to an upright position, Chris threw the covers off himself. “Help me out of here,” he told Laguna, trying to pull himself from his bed. After having been out cold for so long, Chris barely had any energy to even pull himself out of the bed. The medications were making him light-headed, which only made the whole situation harder for Storm. “Dude, in your condition, I don’t think you should be moving around too much.” “That is exactly right!” cried the nurse as she entered the room holding a fresh set of linen. Placing one hand on Chris’ shoulder, the nurse was able to stop any plans Chris had of leaving. “We need you to stay overnight to run more test. You may have suffered a serious concussion as a result of that shot to your head. Further testing will be needed.” Aggravated, Chris brushed her hand off his shoulder and sat back up. “I’m fine, I’ve had worst. I’ve wrestled with a concussion in the past. And I never needed to go to the hospital!” “I can tell,” the nurse snickered, “you obviously have some brain damage from the experiences. Now be a good boy and get some rest. I’ve brought you some new sheets.” Chris turned away from her and signaled for her to just leave them beside his bed…with one finger. Waiting until the nurse left, Chris pulled the sheets off and started to get out of bed again, mumbling about how he needed to get to the gym. “I don’t think you shouldn’t be leaving, I mean I brought games for us to play!” And sure enough Laguna reached beside the chair he had been occupying and grabbed a booksack. “I’ve got Risk, and Monopoly, and Chinese Checkers.” “I think I’m going to hurl.” Chris started to look around for something. “Hey, have you seen my bedpan?” Taking a moment to look around the room, Laguna saw the bedpan near the bed but out of Chris’ reach. He picked it up and handing it to Chris. Who then proceeded to smack Laguna in the head with it. “Damnit I don’t want to play any Chinese Checkers! I want to get the hell out of here!” Laguna had to back away as Chris took another swing at him with the bedpan. Chris kicked the sheets to the floor and pulled himself out of the bed. He would be damned if they would stop him from competing for the North American title. This was his first title opportunity in the UWF and he would not lose it now. (Granted he had no idea why he was placed in the match to begin with, as his record was less than stellar. He figured it was because of past accomplishments and name value, which is good in his books.) Laguna moved between Chris and the door, though it would do little good as Chris could easily move Laguna even with the stitches and mild concussion. “The nurse said you needed to rest, big guy. If you’re cranky I can read you a story or something.” “You’re an fucking moron, Laguna! Now get the hell out of my way. I’ll be damned if anyone is going to stop me from showing up at Sunday Supremacy.” Hearing the commotion, the nurse and doctor enter the room to see Chris out of the bed and trying to gather his belongings. “Now see here, mister” the doctor told him, trying to get him to put away his things. Chris wanted to part of any test the doctors in the place had thought up for him. The doctor grabbed his shoulder and Chris spun around sharply to brush his hand off but the turn had been too sudden for Storm. A blow of light-headedness had overtaken him and stumbling, he fell to the floor backwards. He head caught the corner of the food cart that had been brought in for him early, clipping the metal. The staff and Laguna rushed to Chris’ side and together was able to lift him back into the bed. A few seconds passed and Chris was able to regain his bearings. “Ah, my head. Fucking hurts.” Instinctively Chris touched the part of his head that hurt him. Looking at his hand he saw his finger tips now covered in blood. His heart skipped a beat at the sight of his own blood. “I told you this would happen if you did not behave yourself. Now we’re going to need to fix the stitches. All because you have to act like a baby,” the nurse lectured, though Chris paid her no mind. “Honestly, act your age.” “You need to relax and calm down.” The doctor kept a soft tone to his voice, speaking to Chris and getting him to stop struggling. “You’ll be fine. You’ve been hurt but you will live to wrestle again.” The doctor’s voice started to pick up in volume. “However! If you choice to be stubborn about the situation and decide to wrestle, you may suffer a worst injury than what you currently have and may end up losing your career. So before you do anything else, ask yourself this, is your career worth this one match?” Chris remained silent and slipped further into the bed, hoping inside that if he crawled far enough into the bed that he would become invisible and somehow not have to address any of these issues. Chris was always horrible at dealing with important issues, he never really knew how to handle them growing up. As he laid there in the hospital bed, his eyes started to grow tired. He didn’t want to fight the sleep, rather he embraced it. He welcomed the peace he would find, where he could be alone with his thoughts. Did he really want to risk his career just for one match? Was it worth it? There were no promises that he would even win. He had five challenging opponents. What made him think he should roll the dice here and now? But Tessa… Maybe, just maybe, if he could pull out the win and become the North American champion, she might see that she was wrong about him. That he had changed and that Seymour Almasy was feeding her bullshit. Chris Storm has been and still is a winner. He may be in the middle of a slump, but like the Boston Red Sox, he would hit the homerun. Chris had decided, doctor’s orders or not, Chris was going to be at Supremacy and he would be wrestling for the North American title. His opponents be damned, Chris Storm refused to be stopped. “Let’s give them hell,” he said as he slowly faded away into his slumber.
