(Regal Rumble) Day 26: The Eighth Sin

13 Aug 2004

'The Renegade' Rich Rollins — The 'Windy Sin City'

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Day 26 One o’clock AM. Derek Jenkins had been a taxi driver since he moved to Chicago four years ago. The pay was enough for him to support himself, and himself was all he had. He’d made decent tips off of the downtown crowd, but since he started working the North Side, things had been getting more than a little lean. On this particular evening however, he was about to receive the tip of his life. Derek had parked his cab at the corner of 67th Street and Vermont, taking a few minutes to munch down a cheesburger he picked up at the nearest McDonalds. The late crowd was usually a rowdy one, so he liked to get a meal in before things started getting screwy. He hadn’t flipped off his “Off Duty” sign for more than twenty seconds before his first fare of the evening climbed into the back of his cab. The man said nothing. “Got someplace to be, bub?” Derek asked, his thick Brooklyn accent making his words sound more cheery than they should at one in the morning. Most people found it charming; this man still said nothing. “You know how this little marriage works, right? You tell me where to go, and I go there. You pay me, and we divorce our separate ways. Capice?” “Haddonfield,” the man replied. The tone immediately made the hairs on the back of Derek’s neck stand on end. He laughed, trying to hide his sudden unease. “Um, that’s three countys away, you know that? I’m a Chicago cabbie, not a train ticket.” From the dark recess of the backseat, a wad of cash crumbled onto the empty passanger’s seat next to Derek. Picking it up, the first two hundred dollar bills were all it took to change Derek’s mind. “Haddonfield, it is. You got a name, man?” Derek pulled away from the curb, mixing in with the traffic. Silence was all that greeted him. “Well, I figure we’re gonna be good friends for the next two hours or so. Not much for small talk, eh?” Something about this man raised a red flag in the back of Derek’s mind, but for this kind of cash he’d tote the Anti-Christ around. Rent was due in two days, anyway. “Just drive,” were the only words that came from the dark back seat. Trying to catch a glimpse of the man’s face, Derek adjusted his rear view mirror down a notch, but the man’s brimmed hat cast too many shadows to make any distinct impressisions. Taking a deep breath, Derek Jenkins turned his attention back to the road. “It’s your money, man.” Derek began humming the old Beatles song “All You Need is Love” absently to himself, trying to take his mind off of the odd vibe this quiet customer was practically radiating. He nearly got through the second chorus when the quiet customer finally spoke up. “Have you ever… felt rage?” The unexpected question startled Derek. “Excuse me?” he said. There was a pause, as if the man in the back were grinning. Repeating the question, the man enunciated the words much slower, almost mocking. “Have you ever… felt… rage? Jealousy..? Beaten a man for his ignorance?” “What the hell kind of question is that?” Derek replied, trying to keep his attention on the highway onramp. As if he had been talking about local sports or the weather, the figure in the backseat instead chose to ignore him. “You’ll want to turn off two exits up here, and take the turnpike. It’s faster,” the man in the back intoned. Conversation is conversation, Derek told himself. And he wanted to keep this man talking, creepy or no. “Yeah, I beat up my landlord once. He was an asshole who always stared at my girlfriend’s ass.” “Where is she now?” the man pressed. “None of your business, man,” Derek shot back. “Did she leave you?” “Look, man…” Derek started. Another wad of bills fell from the bullet-proof window that led to the backseat. With a glance, Derek started to sweat when he noticed the number of zeros next to Ben Franklin. “What is this all about?” he said, trying to keep the strain from his voice. He was only half successful. “I’m inquisitive. Indulge me,” the voice replied. Derek had to wipe a bead of sweat from his brow. He sighed, more than a little uneasy now. “She was cheating on me, and I kicked that bitch to the curb.” “Did you know the man?” Such a simple question. So loaded with condescension. “Yeah. He was some wrestler guy… thought he was some badass renegede or something.” “Did you beat him, as well?” “No, Mr. Twenty-Questions, I didn’t,” Derek responded, his annoyance getting the better of him. “So… you could beat your landlord for looking at your woman, but not the man who fucked her behind your back.” It wasn’t a question. It was a statement. And Derek could almost swear he heard a contained chuckle. Both men refused to speak. Derek sat in silence, taking the toll ticket and driving on into the bleak darkness of the countryside, not another car in sight. Nearly ten minutes passed by before the strange passanger broke the stillness. “So tell me… just how is Lacy doing these days? “What the fuck did you just say?” Derek nearly lost control of the cab. The figure in the backseat rattled off facts as if he were making them up, but Derek knew better. “Lacy Reinking, the ‘bitch’ you kicked to the curb. 1105 East Davenport, owner of Lacy’s Pawn downtown…” “You son of a bitch!” Derek didn’t even put on his turn signal as he swerved the car to the side of the road, parked awkwardly along the shoulder of the turnpike. Fuming with rage and confusion, he began to turn around. “Who the hell do you think you…” Before he could have turned his head half way around to look his passanger in the face, two thick hands came through the small window. The clear plastic bag was over Derek’s head and pulled tight against his throat before he could say another word, and those thick hands held fast. Paniced, Derek began to thrash wildly, flailing for the car horn. He was just barely aware of his passanger’s head, now next to his, the stranger’s lips pressed into his ear. “You can take this to the grave, my friend,” the voice softly whispered, “Rest assured… that 'bitch' and her new boy-toy will both see you in Hell. Isn’t ignorance such… sweet bliss?” Thirty seconds later, Derek Jenkins stopped kicking. At that exact moment, in the slums of South Chicago… … […static…static…static…static…] […static…static…] … [A pale orange glow brings the camera’s lens out from the depths of static and snow. Adjusting to the near pitch blackness, the entire view rotates ever so slightly, the edges of the room coming into sharper focus. Rich “The Renegade” Rollins, his hair a disheveled mess hanging down the nape of his neck and shoulders, sits with two legs of an old wooden chair rocked back on the floor. A bucket lays at his boots, a pile of old, rusted darts at arm’s length.] [He stares at his reflection in a dirty mirror set inside a dresser, all the drawers long since pulled out and forgotten. Where a fire usually sits in his eyes, now there is only bleak calmness.] [In his hand, a zippo lighter. It’s pale orange glow plays over every inch of the small confines of the room. Small details become clearer; torn posters of random movies and music bands are pinned all over the peeling plaster that passes for walls, and cracks run from the floor to ceiling. All except one wall.] TTHHWACK! [Plucked from the pile on the floor and dipped in the clear liquid filling the small bucket, a dart sails from Rollins’ fingers and lodges itself in the final wall, still just barely coming into focus. As the final details clear, so too do The Renegade’s intentions.] [The entire back wall is covered, floor to ceiling, with promotional posters.] [UWF superstar posters, to be exact. And sticking like a rotted feather, dripping clear liquid down the plaster wall beyond, a dart sits between two eyes.] [The eyes of Seymour Almasy.] TTHWACK!! [Jamie Shock, sporting a needle in the cheek.] TTHWACK!! TTHWACK!! [Joshua Dunn and Kenny Mercury, both with a dripping dart staining their foreheads.] [Wearing a pair of crumpled blue jeans and a light blue tank top, The Renegade rocks his chair back onto the floor and stands, picking up the bucket by its curved handle. The contents slop over the side as he rises, splashing unceremoniously to the hard wood floor. Solomnly, ever so solemnly, he walks over to the shine of UWF memorabilia, his cold blue eyes flickering in the light of the zippo like a man possessed.] Renegade: And so it ends. [Without hesitation, The Renegade upends the bucket and tosses the liquid across the entire wall, splashing everything in sight. Only then does the camera catch the empty can of kerosene on the floor.] [Turning to face the camera, Rich Rollins’ face becomes the definition of serenity, a quiet calm washing over his features like a wave of relief. Licking his lips, he holds the lighter out for the lens to see.] Renegade: I came to a conclusion tonight. That conclusion was that I am not a bad man. Nobody ever thinks they are bad by nature, I know… but I didn’t like the man I saw in the mirror two hours ago. I didn’t like the Rich Rollins who looked back, his eyes rimmed with tears that haven’t been shed since he was a teenager. And maybe they should have been shed. (he takes a deep, ragged breath) I’m sure the UWF fans don’t know it, but The Renegade has a family. Or rather, he had one. But my work… (he laughs, a choked gurgle of bitterness)… if that’s what I can call it, alienated me from them, and them from me. A man is nothing without family, boys and girls. Winning and losing in the ring doesn’t mean jack shit if you’re a loser in life, and in that… in that, ol’ Rich Rollins was a renegade through-and-through. And it kills me. [Bringing the zippo up to eye level, his attention focuses on the tantalizing motion of the flame, its seductive dance reflected against the pale blue of his eyes.] Renegade: And so, I’m quitting for good. Just the other day, a good friend of mine told me to let go of my past… but I’d rather make amends. And in order for me to do that, I have to hang up the boots… once and for all. What makes this all so hysterical is that Richard Hayes thinks he’s getting under my skin with this Television title bullshit, when he’s really doing me a favor. When I stood in that ring this last Sunday and Hayes announced I’d be putting my Television title up for grabs in the Regal Rumble, everything clicked. You want my reaction, Hayes? You want me to tell you what’s on my mind, get angry, do something stupid and then get fired? [He laughs.] Renegade: I’ll make it easy for you. (he looks dead-on into the camera) I quit. [His arm reaches out, the flame dancing closer and closer to the kerosene-drenched wall…] Female Voice: (off screen) Rollins? Rollins, are you there? [Surprised, The Renegade drops the zippo. The metal cover snaps closed as it bounces across the floor, the flame dissipating instantly and harmlessly. Physically rattled, Rollins walks over and behind the camera’s field of view, and in an instant the entire image bounces as he picks it up and walks through his bedroom door into the abandoned bar beyond that he calls home.] [Enter Lacy Reinking.] [Standing near the swinging double doors that serve as an entrance, a slender young woman in athletic pants and a tight neon red belly shirt comes into focus, a bundle of letters cradled in her arms.] Lacy: Christ Rich, when was the last time you checked your mail? You’ve got dozens of things all stuffed up in a coffee can outside. [Her immediate smile is melting. Brushing a wayward strand of blonde hair from her eyes, Lacy swaggers over to the bartop across from Rollins and the camera, dumping the entire pile next to an empty pyramid of beer mugs.] Lacy: You want me to go through these with you? Renegade: (off camera) Sure. I could use the company. Lacy: All right. (she pauses) Are you… okay, Rich? You look a little tired. Everything…? Renegade: (off camera) I quit, Lacy. [Silence.] Lacy: Come again? [The camera jolts as The Renegade places it on a shelf behind the bar, encompassing most of the room. Walking back into view, he leans his elbows on the counter as he starts to pick through his mail.] Renegade: I said I quit. I want to find my family, Lacy. Lacy: (astonished) So you… just like that? It’s over? Renegade: Yup. Lacy: But… you agreed to participate in Primetime Central’s Survival! Renegade: I’m dropping out. Lacy: What about the pay-per-view this Wednesday? Renegade: Not going. Lacy: Your Television title?! Renegade: I’m vacating it. Lacy: The Regal Rumble?!!! Renegade: Don’t care. Lacy: Bullshit! [Rollins knew she’d act like this. Refusing to meet her accusing stare, he instead focuses on the task of rummaging through his mail.] Lacy: How can you just - decide - to give up on your life, Rollins? Wrestling is your family, it’s your passion and it always has been. You’ve got too much talent and too bright a future to just shrug it off like none of it ever existed! Damnit Richard Rollins, look at me when I’m talking to you! [But Rollins still doesn’t respond. Instead, he holds in each hand a single letter, his entire back straightening as he pulls away from the bartop. Tossing one of them on top of the pile, he instantly shreds the second open and pulls a single hand-written pieces of paper out. For fifteen eternal seconds, his eyes furiously scan the contents.] [And then he drops it.] [Without looking at Lacy, without so much as acknowledging her existence, Rich Rollins turns around and walks back into his room, slamming the door behind him.] [Shocked, Lacy regains her composure for a few moments, staring at his exit path. Slowly, she reaches for the letter he just read, quietly reading its message out loud to herself.] Dear Richard, I know how much you hate being called Richard. Life can aggrivate you like that sometimes, can’t it? I just wanted you to know that I received your most recent letter, but I didn’t open it. I never open them. What makes you think you can just buy your way back into my life with your money and success? You abandoned us when we needed you the most, when we were hungry and living from welfare check to welfare check. But it’s always been like that with you. This is the last letter you’ll ever recive from me. In case you care, mother died of cancer a year ago. Her funeral was beautiful. But I’m sure you were too busy to give a shit, like you always are. Enjoy your career. Enjoy your shallow life filled with shallow people who’ll stick around as long as you’ve got the cash, but don’t even entertain the notion that I’ll be waiting for you when you crash. There was a time when I called you my brother. But my brother is dead now. He dug his own grave, and I hope you find it just as comfy as the shallow pillows behind your head at night. We don’t want your money or your pity. Don’t write again. -John Rollins [Lacy’s hand suppresses a gasp as she sets the letter back down on the bartop, her eyes furrowed with sympathy. Almost afraid to look, she slowly reaches for the first letter, the one that Rollins didn’t even bother to open. Written in large red marker across the envelope is a simple phrase: ] Return to Sender [Her stomach tied in knots, Lacy rips open the flap and reels out the paper inside, unfolding it and reading it to herself once again.] Dear John, I’ve never asked anything from you or from mother other than to be accepted. I wanted the both of you to understand me and why I had to leave, and love me for who I was. I’m not so naïve as to actually believe any of that, but that’s all I ever wanted. I thought I wanted fame and money and all that social bullshit that everybody thinks they need… but I was wrong. All I needed was my family. I’m writing you this letter to let you know that I’m leaving the wrestling business once and for all. I’ve saved every red cent I ever earned in this this godless place, and I’m coming to find the both of you. We’re going to start over, and I want to give you all the things I never could. Maybe say the things we never said, if you’ll just talk to me. As much as I love this business, it pales in comparison to how much I’ve always loved you. ‘Sorry’ just doesn’t seem to cut it, does it? But I’ve got our whole lives to make up for my own shortcomings, if you’ll just let me get a foot through the door. -Sincerely, Rich Rollins [A single tear smears her mascara as Lacy places the letter down on the counter. Her moist eyes dart back toward the door that Rollins had slammed, but she doesn’t dare walk toward it. What may or may not be on the other side of that door, Lacy doesn’t even want to fathom…] BANG!! [Lacy almost falls off the barstool as the gunshot renders her almost deaf, her irises bulged to the size of saucers.] Lacy: Rich… NOO!!! [Completely on instinct, Lacy jumps over the bartop and runs into the liquor shelves, knocking our faithful camera from its perch. The view spirals out of control as the camera free-falls to the floor.] […static…static…] […static…] [The lens cuts back in, the view now tipped on its side. A minute crack runs from one side of the screen to the other, but it doesn’t obstruct the visual of Lacy pounding on Rollins’ door, tears streaming down her face. The old door finally gives way, almost spilling her on her face into the room beyond.] [Sitting back in his wooden chair, a smoking pistol laying in his lap, is Rich “The Renegade” Rollins.] [The mirror in his dresser is shattered, the victem of a single well-placed bullet hole that shows the crater of plaster behind it, the bullet lodged in the wall and still smoking. Turning to face Lacy, who is now sobbing on her knees in the doorway, Rollins looks beyond.] [He looks straight at the camera.] [His reflexes like lightening, The Renegade rushes through the door and snags our faithful cracked camera, completely ignoring Lacy in the doorway. The view bounces as he hauls it back into his room and places it facing away from his shattered mirror on the dresser, the lens taking in the entire UWF-covered poster wall. Raising his zippo once more, Rollins snaps on a flame as he locks eyes on the camera, his face carved from molten granite.] [He begins to speak, slowly at first. His voice is distant but strong, fortified by an anger that no words could hope to describe. With Lacy still sobbing in his doorway, The Renegade addresses the camera.] Renegade: Every time I think I have it all figured out, Mother Nature never fails in proving me wrong. It takes the power of Nature to prove me wrong. Did you hear me? I’m speaking to every employee of the Universal Wrestling Federation, from the wrestlers all the way down to the office jockeys sitting in their plush little chairs and overpriced suits. I… I have nothing left. No past, not present, and maybe not even a future worth living for. But I’m damn sure willing to stick around long enough to find out. Hayes wants to fuck with my head, stack the odds against me and strip me of a title I’ve made important again? [He laughs then, a bitter, mournful sound. A tear rolls down the length of his cheek.] Renegade: If I can’t conquer my own personal Hell, then I’m packin’ up and bringin’ that Hell with me. And this time… this time, no force of Nature is going to stop me. Prove me wrong. [Without breaking his gaze on the camera, Rollins tosses the ignited zippo over his shoulder. It bounces off the poster-covered wall, instantly spreading a snaking flame across the kerosene-drenched UWF posters. Faces begin to blister, to swell and crackle. Ezra Kazee, Tsunami Oni, Misfit, The Plauge… they all fade away against the power of Nature. They all begin to lose out against the intensity of the fire.] [As the flames start to lick the ceiling, Rollins’ eyes gleam with an unholy energy. He doesn’t smile.] [Digital Mortality, Sean McClancy, Moraless… all victims to the relentless wall of fire.] Renegade: I’ll see you in Japan. Each and every last one of you. […static…static…static…] […static…static…] […static…] [Fade to black.] … … … Two Hours Later… It didn’t take long for the media to leak the news of last weeks’ incident along the turnpike outside of Chicago. That’s what local authorities preferred to call it, an “incident.” The media, on the other hand, preferred to call it something else. It screamed across the headlines of the Chicago Tribune not two days later: Massacre on Mile 88 Oh how the media loved to label. Officer Jordan McKnight had been under heavy fire since he found the old Firebird along the shoulder on that fateful night. His nerves had been on high alert ever since, and it was only his sense of civic duty that kept him on patrol the next day. His captain had suggested he take a few days off to clear his head, but McKnight insisted that his head was fine. It was his stomach that wouldn’t stop turning knots. As Officer McKnight pulled to the side of the turnpike to investigate the stalled taxi cab, those knots got even tighter. “Dispatch, Unit 17 here. I’ve got a Chicago taxi stalled out on mile 70. No owner in sight.” A handful of heartbeats passed in silence as he waited for the response. Finally, a tired female voice came through his CB: “Copy that, Unit 17. Proccede with caution, we’ve got Unit 23 en route.” “Copy,” McKnight almost mumbled, his eyes fixed squarely on the silent frame of the taxi. Flashlight in hand, he made his way around the rear of the vehicle, peering into the darkened windows. Not a driver or passanger in sight. The angle at which the cab was parked suggested a quick and unplanned stop, which didn’t do anything to ease the knots in his stomach. His heart sank when he saw the front windshield. Written in white shoe polish, a simple message greeted McKnight: IGNORANCE: THE EIGHTH SIN Without realizing it, McKnight started counting the days to his retirement. When he popped the trunk and found the strangled body of Derek Jenkins, he knew that those days might as well have been an eternity. Retirement be damned. ~Fin~
