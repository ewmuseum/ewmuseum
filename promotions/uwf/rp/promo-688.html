UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #688

Keepsake

9 Sep 2004

'The Renegade' Rich Rollins — Chicago

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Lacy Reinking was used to the alone-but-not-alone feeling that owning a business in Downtown Chicago afforded her. Day in and day out, thousands of nameless faces passed her tiny pawn shop. Businessmen, entrepreneurs, hobos and whores; a sea of individuals she would never know, right at her doorstep. When business was slow, she would grab a cup of coffee and just watch the tide flow by, admiring the mind-numbing barrage of people’s lives parade past her window who likely didn’t even know she existed. To them, she was a bright green neon sign that read “Lacy’s Pawn.” To her, they were a collective symbol of everything she would never have. On nights like this, she usually enjoyed watching the throng of humanity pass her by. But not tonight. Tonight, she was trying to take her mind off the front page of the Chicago Tribune. The words sent a chill down through her toes. High-Speed Execution: Second Victim in First Week The victim was her ex-boyfriend, Derek Jenkins. The man she left for Rich Rollins. In so many words, the newspaper article claimed that this series of murders along the freeway just outside the city was in no way related to a similar incident two years ago, when a man the media dubbed the “Slipknot Assassin” strangled women for almost three months before disappearing. He was never found. Lacy would have been his final victim, but became the only survivor. And she knew damn well this sudden “unrelated” series of incidents were most certainly related. Shuddering, Lacy pulled herself away from the window and prepared to close up shop. Had she watched the wandering citizens of Chicago just a few more seconds, she might have noticed the Man looking back, grinning. At that moment, in South Chicago… [Absently slipping the cab driver his fee, Rich Rollins steps out into the quiet rain, his boots squishing in the mud surrounding the massive storage building. The neighborhood is a towering cemetery of industrial structures that at one point had served one purpose or another, but now were nothing more than makeshift homeless shelters and roach motels. The landscape may be less than standard, but it's what The Renegade used to call home.] [He stands there in the soft drizzle, pulling the tabs of his black leather duster tight around his cheeks. The darkened silhouette of the old Love Box warehouse looms before him, practically calling to him, beckoning him to return.] [In two years, Rich Rollins had not been back to this place. Even now, he doesn’t know why he came. Shrugging off the invisible grip on his shoulder, The Renegade pushing past the length of security fence and walks in through a security door.] [The lights blink on, and a flood of memories assault him as the near-empty contents of the storage space appear to greet him, old friends welcoming him back like he had never left at all. Piled boxes with blue plastic tarps pulled over them litter the walls, an abandoned weight set with multiple coats of dust in the corner. Rollins walks casually, taking in the familiar musty smell of the warehouse, the damp residue of the evening’s rain making the air smell like rusted copper.] Renegade: Hello? [Silence.] [Just like old times.] [Compelled by some incomprehensible sense of purpose, Rollins walks over to one of the piles of covered boxes and whips the blue tarp back. Producing a pocketknife from his jean pocket, he sets the top box on the floor and slices the flap open, his curiosity beating the insides of his skull like a boxer on his heavy bag. Reaching inside, he pulls out the item nearest the top.] [An old “NC-17” promotional poster. A pay-per-view event from his GCW days, an event where he won the World Heavyweight Championship from The Jhub.] [More memories.] [He sets the poster aside, rooting through the rest of the material that probably hadn’t seen the light of day in half a decade.] [A copy of his semi-autobiography, “Rich Rollins: A Life of Hard Knocks.” A few scattered photographs of Rollins and other wrestlers backstage, people he knew as friends. As peers. As equals.] [He stops, clutching one particular photograph that grabs his attention.] [Scuffling in the middle of a backstage hallway that could have been anywhere in America, The Renegade is hunched over in a mock-headlock applied by John ‘Moraless’ Edwards, sheepish grinning faces all around.] [Rollins had completely forgotten the picture, along with half of the stuff in the box. An entire library of his life was crammed up in this warehouse. That was the reason he avoided the place, whether he would admit it to himself or not.] [Still looking at the picture, Rollins grins.] Renegade: Look at us now, eh John? [They had always had a professional relationship, Rollins and Moraless. It was a shame that now, three years later, that professionalism would truly be tested. The squared circle was an unforgiving mistress, and she spoke with brutal honesty. Come Sunday, that honesty will tell everyone just who GCW’s last remaining warrior truly was. A match three years in the making.] [And in the span of three seconds, it would all be over.] Renegade: One more time, for the old days. Let’s dance, Mory. [Rich “The Renegade” Rollins closes the top of the box and pushes it back against the wall.] [He stuffs the picture in his pocket, a keepsake and reminder of just how crazy life can be.] [Fade to black.] ~Fin~
