UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #579

Day 9: Black Math

27 Jul 2004

'The Renegade' Rich Rollins — Slums of South Chicago

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Day 9 [Nine days.] [Two hundred and sixteen hours.] [Twelve thousand, nine hundred and sixty minutes.] [For Rich “The Renegade” Rollins, the last seven hundred and seventy thousand, six hundred seconds have been the apex of the last two years of his life. Two years on the sidelines, and after five short days, the still-holding Television title reign has been nothing short of spectacular for Rollins. A championship is a championship, and in another twelve hundred hours, a record will be broken.] [Fifty days, to be more specific.] [These thoughts tickle the brain of Rich Rollins, who sits alone in his abandoned bar in South Chicago, the sounds of late-afternoon city life invading his quiet musings. His long brown hair tied off in a ponytail, The Renegade wears little more than usual; the tattered blue jeans hugging his legs look like they haven’t been washed in weeks, and his grease-smudged red tank top smells like the pants look. Nursing a full glass of warm beer, he leans forward on his elbows against the empty bartop, letting his mind wander.] [Dave Storm proved to be less than advertised, a paperweight wrestler more in love with the sound of his own voice than his work in the ring. How shocking. Another notch in The Renegade’s bedpost, another win for his record. Another night in the ring for The Renegade, and low and behold… another one bites the dust. You’d think they would learn.] [Just another step along the road to immortality.] [Fifty more days.] [Seventy-two thousand minutes.] [Four million, three hundred and twenty thousand seconds.] [Setting the not-so-frosty beer aside, Rollins suddenly gets up and walks behind the bar and through a door frame that lacks an actual door… his bedroom. Absently tugging on the end of his ponytail with one hand, The Renegade scans the room quickly, his mouth puckered in a contemplative frown. Tossing aside a pile of old magazines and wrestling memorabilia - the UWF shirts just kept coming, despite his protests - Rollins finally spies the object of his search.] [A small handheld camcorder, taken from the UWF Arena.] [After spending the first four days of his title reign at the Arena itself, Rollins had gotten to know a few of the media technicians who spent their week chopping together video packages for the next week’s shows. One of them had offered Rollins to put his homemade “documentary” on the air, if he could ever get it finished. The idea appealed to The Renegade, so he kept the camera in case he ever felt the need to record something memorable.] [Sometimes, just sometimes, the word “memorable” doesn’t quite capture the moment.] [But the word “controversial” does.] [Setting the camera against the decomposing plaster wall on the other side of the room, Rollins mounts it on its tripod stand. Scanning the room once more, his deep blue eyes search frantically for something else, anything else, that would make a good fire-starter. Suddenly struck with an idea, he dives back over to the pile of wrestling memorabilia and starts flinging shirts and posters across the room, items that may never again see the light of day. Grinning like a madman, he finally finds his treasure at the bottom of the heap.] [A promotional picture of Fosta, used on the “upcoming match” splash-screens on UWF television. A wry grin snakes across his face.] [He won’t be grinning long.] [Clutching the picture firmly, Rich Rollins flips on the power to the camcorder.] … […static…static…static…static…] […static…static…] [The lens starts to squeak as it adjusts itself in the dim lighting, focusing on The Renegade’s unshaven face. His wild blue eyes. His dirty clothes. His intensely intimate visual lock on the camera. All of these things come into focus within seconds as Rollins begins to speak.] Renegade: I have a confession to make. [The words echo dull against the bare walls.] Renegade: When “The Shocker” Dave Storm and I locked up last Sunday, and he tried to superkick my teeth into the back of my brain, I almost enjoyed it when I caused him to nearly break his leg. Letting his flailing foot connect with the security rail instead of my chin. (he sighs) No, that’s a lie. I sure as hell did enjoy the look on his face when he knew - at that exact second - that I was faster, smarter, and infinitely more dangerous than he could have ever hoped to become. He knew it. Jay Vyper knew it. And now… [He holds up the picture of Fosta for the camera to see.] Renegade: … Fosta will be joining the club tomorrow night. You think I’m “missing” something, pal? You think you’ve got what it takes to put the ol’ Renegade down for the three-count, just because you have some sort of ellusive secret bundle of energy that nobody else can tame? Oh I’m sorry… I forgot. You had a brother who died a while back. Doing what, you say? Robbing a bank? (he chuckles) Tell me, was he robbing a bank to make rent between welfare checks, or was he just trying to support his cocaine addiction? Or… both, perhaps? Let me let you in on a little secret, bub… nobody gives two shits about him, you, me, or anyone else in this world. My mother loaded herself up on painkillers and liquor for ten years, trying to raise my brother and I. Where are they now? Hell if I know, or care. But I do I cry about it, playing for the camera and the crowd by justifying my actions just ‘cause I got dealt a bad hand? Hell no. [The Renegade stops, realizing too late that he had started yelling at the camera. His fist was clutching the picture of Fosta like a piece of crumpled trash, shaking with intensity and strain. Taking a deep breath, he tries to shrug off his outburst by grinning, but the grin doesn’t reach his eyes. His cold, searing blue eyes.] [Without any special ceremony, The Renegade drops the picture of Fosta on the floor.] Renegade: Let me tell you something about an enemy, my friend. Or better yet, let me clear a common misconception about enemies. [Turning his back slightly to the camera, Rollins positions himself over the picture, talking over his shoulder.] Renegade: Have you ever heard the phrase, “I wouldn’t piss on you if you were on fire?” I know I have. And that little quote is a load of bullshit. Do you know why? [Taking out a silver zippo lighter from his jean pocket, Rollins snaps the flame into existence and drops it on the floor. In seconds, the picture of Fosta starts to crackle and curl, small licks of fire spreading across its surface.] [His back still turned to the camera, The Renegade unzips his pants.] Renegade: Because urine is flammable. [With that, The Renegade lets loose with his bladder, igniting a flash-flame that billows almost into his face. Jerking back, he zips himself back up and watches as the small picture quickly becomes a minature inferno, curling into a little black ball of smoke and fire.] [In fifteen seconds, the picture is gone. In its place are Fosta’s smoldering remains, permanently imbedded into Rich Rollins’ floor. Still focused on the ruins, The Renegade finishes.] Renegade: Tomorrow night, Fosta. Day ten. And after you, fourty-nine to go. [His face snaps up, drilling a hole into the lens like a white-hot torch.] Renegade: Try not to wet yourself before the match. I’d hate to have a zippo handy. [And with that, he reaches for the power switch…] […static…static…static…] […static…static…] […static…] [Fade to black.] ~Fin~
