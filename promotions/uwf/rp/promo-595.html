Day 15: The Darkest Night

3 Aug 2004

'The Renegade' Rich Rollins — Slums of South Chicago

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Day 15 Officer Jordan McKnight had seen his fair share of tragedies along the turnpike outside of Chicago over the years. Human stupidity never fails, he always said. From speeding maniacs to truck stop hijackers, he’d been involved in more high-speed chases along the twenty mile stretch that was his jurisdiction than anyone else in his unit. They called him “Lead Foot” in the office. And he hated it. It scared the daylights out of him every time some teenager thought he was invincible, pushing some suped-up rice rocket to the limits in the hopes of escaping some minor offence. Sometimes their common sense caught up with them before Fate would, and those chases were frightening enough. Other times… well, Fate could be a cruel mistress, and those chases were down-right terrifying. Despite the tolerance Officer McKnight had built up over the years, nothing could have prepared him for this. It was almost three o’clock in the morning when McKnight pulled to the side of the turnpike, investigating a stalled vehicle. His siren lights bounced through the musty windows of the old ‘88 Thunderbird, splintering the red and blue flashes like a prism into the dark countryside. He recorded the make and model of the red Thunderbird, entered the license plate into his dashboard computer, and waited for the response from Dispatch. …loading…loading…loading… Wiping a bead of sweat from his eyes, Officer McKnight wondered when the office would get around to fixing his air conditioning. The thought had only a moment to pass through his head when the simple response came through: Reported stolen by owner. “Copy, Dispatch,” McKnight responded into his CB radio, and exited his squad car. Unclipping his flashlight from a belt loop, he carefully examined the outside of the motionless Thunderbird. The paint was cracked and chipped in half a dozen places, and the triangular back right window had been smashed out. It was obvious that whoever had left the car here had no intention of coming back for it. Reaching for a sticky slip to tag the vehicle for towing, something caught McKnight’s eye. Walking around to the front of the car, McKnight shined his flashlight across the front windshield. Written in simple white shoe polish, a message had been scribbled on the inside of the glass: MTV MADE ME DO IT That’s when he noticed the smell. Like a can of tuna left out on the counter for three days, the smell was thick and nauseating. The hairs on the back of his neck stood up simultaneously, an eerie feeling creeping up the back of his spine. Reaching for his shoulder-mounted walkie-talkie, he sent a reqest to Dispatch. “This is Unit 17. I’ve got a possible hot-spot here on mile 88, and want to request forensics immediately. Somebody’s written messages in what appears to be shoe polish along the windshield, and… hold on, Dispatch.” “Copy. Forensics on their way, Unit 17,” was his simple answer. Walking around to the trunk, McKnight knew he didn’t want to lift it once he realized the smell was getting stonger. He knew what he would find. Thirty seconds later, he was no longer calmly requesting for forensics. He was screaming. Day 15... Three Hours Earlier [With his long brown hair tied back in a ponytail and his hands stuffed into the pockets of his black leather jacket, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins surveys the dismal surroundings of South Chicago. He had been down this street countless times for just as many reasons, but tonight was different. Tonight was his night for gloating. Tonight was his night for redemption.] [Tonight was The Renegade’s night to get his money back. Five thousand dollars, to be exact.] [The building had no distinguishing marks that would set it apart from the other forrest of renovated, bum-dwelling shelters that lined the street. Nearly five stories high - most of the top floor had been knocked out - and lacking a single window with a pane of glass that hadn’t yet been broken, Rollins saunters up to the front “door,” a piece of plywood that had been propped up with duct tape and wire. Reaching for the wire, The Renegade pulls off a good foot in length and kicks the plywood barrier down, palming his treasure.] Renegade: Anybody home? [His words bounce around the dark recess of the interior, sounding hollow and metallic. His boots crunch shattered glass under his heels as he walks deeper, allowing his eyes to adjust to the dimmness.] Renegade: I’m looking’ for a man named Becker. Anybody seen Becker? [Silence.] [A wry smile reaches Rollins’ eyes as the thought occurs to him that he was just talking to himself. Here, alone, with a small length of wire in one hand, was one of the UWF’s most promising new talents in the middle of Chicago’s worst ghetto.] [Talking to himself.] Voice: Who da hell wants to know, jackass? [Rollins stops, still not sure of where the voice came from. Scanning frantically, nothing but pitch black nothingness greets The Renegade’s searing blue eyes. He can hear his own heartbeat in the endless silence.] Renegade: Who wants to know? Somebody who’s about to be five grand richer, that’s who. Are you Becker? [Silence.] Renegade: Look pal, I didn’t necessarily come out here to cripple anyone… yet… so let’s make this easy. If you know where Becker is, let him know I’m here. I can wait all night. Voice: Becker ain’t here no more. He left town two days ago. Now leave. [Rollins responds with a dry chuckle.] Renegade: Talk to me like that again, and I’ll take the five grand out of your ass. I’ll ask one more time… where the hell is Becker? [A glint catches Rollins’ eye slightly to the left. A small shuffle emits from the darkness, and in an instant that glint becomes the business end of a switchblade, cutting through the damnable blackness like hot butter. The man is fast.] [But The Renegade is faster.] [Swinging the length of wire between both hands, Rollins catches the wrist of his would-be attacker, jerking hard enough to get a startled whimper from the man. The switchblade immediately drops the floor, along with the rest of the man’s body. Rollins is on top of him in a second, swinging the wire around his neck and pulling tight.] Man: (gurgles) Ppp…pplease… ss…sto…ppp… Renegade: Who’s the jackass now, fucker? Where is Becker? Man: (gurgles) Gg…gonn..ne…. Renegade: Wrong answer. [The Renegade wrenches back even harder, causing the man’s legs to kick up in desperation. Rollins holds tight, feeling the slick lubrication of blood around his fingertips as the wire digs deeper into the man’s throat.] Renegade: Where is Becker? Man: (gurgles) Ddd…onn’t… knn…oww…. I sww…earrr…. [The man’s writhing starts to slow down, and The Renegade releases him. He emits a few short gasps as his head hits the ground, gagging. The man may or may not ever be able to eat solid foods again, but Rollins doesn’t care. Leaning down on his hands and knees, The Renegade whispers into the terrified junkie’s ear.] Renegade: (whispering) Let him know I’m looking’ for him, would ya? He’ll know who you’re talkin’ about. [The man’s only response is a whimper, followed by many short gasps of air. Rollins can’t see his eyes in the dark, but he knows how big they must be. Fright usually did that to people.] [Not that The Renegade would know first-hand. He was just a good observer.] [Tossing the length of wire by the man’s head, Rich Rollins turns on his heels and crunches back through the broken glass, heading for the light of a streetlamp through the open doorway he had kicked in. Despite himself, a twisted smile reaches the corners of his mouth as a cool night breeze whips at his ponytail.] [Most people worked out in a gym to get ready for a match.] [Rollins preferred “on-site” workouts. It was one hell of a ride.] [A ride Justin Morris won’t soon forget.] [Fade to black.] ~Fin~
