UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #810

In the name of..........Eric.

Dec 3 (year unrecorded)

Malcolm Kennedy — Unknown

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Remorse. The room was dark. One single light bulb suspended by a wire swayed ever so slightly in the middle of the room. Beneath the bulb sat a small two foot wide round table. On top of the table were six picture frames. Each frame obviously holding a picture. The camera panned to the left upon hear the sound of boots scuffing against the dirty, dusty floor. From the shadows emerged a man. Alone, both in the room, and in the world. It was “Top Notch” Malcolm Kennedy. Well, it was the shell of that once arrogant man. A shell of the man who’s ego was matched by no ones. A man who had more confidence in himself that ever imaginable. A man who could carry a knife into a gun fight, and think he wasn’t playing fair towards the others. Malcolm wore a pair of tattered green cargo pants. His upper body was covered by a gray hoody with the letters E. A. K. On the front in black. Those were the initials of his dead brother. The hood was pulled up over his head, and his hands both rested in the front pocket of the hoody. He slowly strolled up to the table, and stopped at its side. His eyes immediately locked on a picture of him and his brother. He pulled his right hand from his pocket and picked up the picture. A single tear quickly fell from his left eye. With his left hand he ran his fingers through his jet black hair, and at the same time, pulled the hood back from his head. He sighed heavily as he rubbed his fingers along the scar on his forehead. He was flashing back to the night of his car accident. The night he wrecked his corvette in a drunken stupor. The night he last saw his brother Eric. The very last time he talked to Eric, in a vision. “Eric was everything you could ever ask for. He was a great man. He took pride in his job. He took pride in being a father. He took pride in everything he did. He loved his family. He loved my family. My family loved him. My kids looked at him and saw the hero that he truly was. They saw the man for who he really was. Not some arrogant trigger happy southern fuck tard, eager to squeeze of a few rounds in the back of the first Iraqi he came across. Not some southern fried moron who supported the war in Iraq “Just cause dubya is from the south”. Eric never wanted to be there. Eric never asked to go to Iraq. But he did, because he took pride in the marine corps. He took pride in the fact that people looked to him and all his fellow soldiers for help when they needed it most. He took pride in the fact that he could make something right, after so many years of being wrong. No matter what the cost. Well the cost was his life. And that is the biggest sacrifice anyone could ever make.” “Eric was three years older than me. He always told me when I was young, to follow my dreams no matter how much our parents hated them. When I told him I wanted to be the richest man in Nevada, and that I wanted to run the biggest, most elegant casino in Reno. He told me that was a great dream to follow, but to follow it because I really wanted to succeed. Not because of the money.” “When Eric died, his death taught me a lot. It taught me to live my life for the minute. And not dwell on the past, as well as not to worry about the future. His death taught me that there are more important things in life than money. And for that, I am forever great full.” “Eric was a good man. Any time I ever get down, I remember the night I last saw him. I remember the times when we were young and he told me to follow my dreams. I remember how much I loved him. Everything I do from here on out, everything I succeed in. Is in my brothers name.” Malcolm rubbed some dust off the front of the picture and set the picture back down on the table. Only he set it face down, in front of all the other pictures. The other five pictures were of him and his wife and his family. He glanced from picture to picture, a slight grin just dying to escape, but never succeeding. “No could ever tell me I didn’t truly love my family. I still love them till this very day. But I cant live this family life with such a big hole left inside me. I cant continue on every day with them when I cant sleep at night because of the pain that burns inside me. I left the UWF because I lost my brother. My brother died nearly a year and half ago. And I came back to the ring 2 weeks after that. But I never truly returned to the ring as my former self. I had so many demons to fight inside me. So much I had to overcome before I ever could truly succeed in the ring. It couldn’t have been more evident then at the Regal Rumble. I entered at the number one spot. I lasted till the final four. But I couldn’t get the job done. I was caught off guard and eliminated. I was dying on the inside. I was crashing inside, and I looked to ring for hope. I banked on my wrestling skills to cure my pain. When I felt I couldn’t go on, I convinced my self that a victory would make me feel so much better. The only problem was, I couldn’t win. So when I finally realized I had to leave. I did it in a hurry, without any notice. I went home, and looked to the family I walked out on for refuge. But no refuge was found. I was alone even when I was with them. I was alone no matter who I was with. I was always.............alone.” “Now that I’ve finally accepted that my life will never be bac to where it was. Now that I’ve accepted that my brother will never come back. I can move on. I can go back to find at least a little bit of joy in my life. That joy will come at the pain and anguish of others. That joy will come from my destroying opponent, after opponent, after opponent in the ring. All in my brothers name. Everything I do now, I do for him. He was my inspiration to follow my dreams, and he is now my inspiration to continue down my path to glory. Every one I face from here on out, will taste a little bit of the pain and suffering that is my new life. Craig Wright has already written my off. So be it. He will fall apart worse than the others when Im done with him. The pain we experience will leave him with nothing left to do, but to fall, and fall, and continue falling until there’s no chance of stopping. Craig Wright, will be the first to loose it all. But others will follow. “My family meant everything to me. They picked me up when I was down. They made my days brighter when there was no sun in sight. They made the rain cloud that followed me around disappear with a quick smile, or a soft giggle. But all that is gone. My life is now one great big rain storm. Never ending. Never stopping. So Ill bring my flood of sorrow with me into the UWF. I will fight along side of the one true friend I have left in this world, and I will give my blood to him to make sure he stays where he is in the UWF. I will make sure Oni’s reign never ends. World title around his waist or not, this is my solemn vowel to stand by his side.” “See that. See all that joy in our eyes in those pictures? That is all long gone. My kids don’t look at me the same. My wife doesn’t look at me the same. The sad part is.....I don’t even care. I lost the joy I shared with them. I don’t even miss it. You see, loss became a part of my life. I lost my friends, I lost my family. As my relationship faded with my family, my friendship with long time friend Jamie Shock, turned to bitter hatred. I was alone. Fending for myself with no one there. I was brawling night after night with a man I thought was the best friend I ever had. I beat someone I thought I loved in the back of the head with a crow bar. I was alone, and I was outnumbered. So I ran off. But I don’t care anymore. I have one true friend. That’s all I really need. My family will still be there when I come home from the road, and my love for them will still continue. But it will never be the same.” “NEVER!” With one swipe of his arm, he knocked all of the pictures of him and his family to the ground. They shattered on impact. Glass littered the floor. He grabbed the lone picture of him and his brother and stood it in the middle of the table. “Eric is gone. He’s never coming back. But his memory will always live on. It will live through me, and my victory’s in his name. No mercy shall be given. No punches will be pulled. I loved my brother more than life itself. I have dedicated everything I have left to him. The UWF will be my stomping grounds before I am through, all in the name of Eric Allen Kennedy!” Malcolm stared into his picture of him and his brother. Into his brothers eyes. Into his brothers soul. Tear began to slowly stream from his eyes. He pulled the hood back over his head, and returned his hands to the front pocket of his hoody. He slowly began to step backwards into the darkness. His heavy work boots crumbled the remains of the pictures of him and his family to dust. The pictures smeared with dust from his boots. His eyes never broke from the eyes of his brother in the picture. He never wiped away a single tear. Slowly and quietly, he re-submerged into the darkness of the room. His footsteps slowly faded off until silence returned. Fade to black.
