UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #631

Preparation and Rumination

14 Aug 2004

Mike Montgomery — The Office of the Executive Vice President of Talent Relations

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The scene opens in the office of the Executive Vice President of Talent Relations...and let's be honest, Commisioner-in-exile, Mike Montgomery. He is behind his desk, looking over paperwork that, upon closeup, we see is the contract of UWF Superstar Moraless. He reaches forward slightly, thumbing the intercom to his secretary. 'Ms. Allen?' 'Yes Mr. Montgomery?' 'Would you please set up a meeting with Mr. Edwards' agent? Tell him I'd like to offer his client a ten percent pay raise.' 'Of course Mr. Montgomery. Your schedule is empty tomorrow afternoon. Shall I schedule the meeting for then?' 'Well, I don't have any plans so that sounds like as good a time as any.' 'Alright then. Sir, your ten o'clock has just arrived. One Danny Write.' 'Right...right...the Action Replay taping. Send him in.' The offices double doors open to reveal reporter Danny Write, a kid barely into his twenties, and a camera crew. Write walks toward the desk and Montgomery stands to greet him while the crew sets up. 'Mr. Montgomery, thank you for taking the time to see me.' Montgomery favors Write with his most disarming smile, ushering him to the chair on Write's side of the desk, 'Please, call me Mike. I hate formality, and it's nothing really. Anything for the UWF's fans.' Write nods in agreement before turning toward the camera crew. The camera sits on a tripod, a technician checking the connections. To either side, a studio light stands on it's own tripod, both lit and pointing toward the desk. 'We're ready to go whenever you guys are,' the cameraman says, stepping behind the lense. 'Alright, let's get this started then. We'll of course need you to be in character for this.' 'Of course.' Write keeps one eye on the director, watching the count down. '3...2...' at one the director simply points at Write who turns toward the camera. 'Thanks Andrew, I'm here with the UWF's Vice-President of Talent Relations, former commissioner, and I guess once more the leader of the Player's Club...' At that, Montgomery, now appearing much calmer and cooler, leaning back in his chair, holds his hand up to cut Write off, 'Let me just stop you right there, and in fact, I'm going to help you. I'm going to give you the scoop that will make your career.' 'Really?' Write leans forward, clearly surprised. This wasn't in the script, 'And what would that scoop be?' 'Simply this. The Player's Club is dead.' Dead silence fills the room for a moment. Write's mouth opens and shuts for an instant but no sound comes out. Finally, he manages to get something out, 'Wow. That's...that's certainly quite the statement to be making. I don't understand though. You recently assumed leadership of the Player's Club, and now you're saying it's dead?' The camera switches back to Montgomery, sitting back, fingers steepled, 'Exactly. One year ago, it was my group that won Stable Wars. One year later, it is again my group that won Stable Wars. Studog Escobar is the only other man in UWF history who can claim that sort of dominance. This was one of the primary goals of the Player's Club. For too long, the talents of men like Joshua Dunn, Nick Persia, Ezra Kazee and Kevin Starr have been ignored. Starr was used to put over main eventers...Dunn constantly held back...Ezra and Nick left to toil in obscurity...' 'Then you came along?' Write asks. 'My dear Danny, you're learning. That's exactly right. Joshua and I struck a deal. He'd gotten himself into quite a jam at my hands, but I know talent when I see it. We swore to do what we could to advance each other's careers, and look at us now. Before the Club, we toiled in obscurity. Now...both of us with World Title wins, me two titles from the Grand Slam, Dunn with the longest title reign in UWF history by holding onto the IC title for nearly a whole year. He brought in Ezra, who went on to a simultaneous IC title and tag title reign...I followed with Nick Persia grabbing the hardcore title, a man who is now recognized as the future of this business' 'And of course your accomplishments as a group cannot be ignored.' 'Of course. The Prime Alliance? Once the most powerful triumverate in the business..and we destroyed them in one night. Not only did I take Jamie Shock out of this business, I took his job. We all went from total obscurity to main event status because we did what we had to do in order to take each other there. Now though, it's time to move on. As the old saying goes, always leave 'em wanting more.' Danny checks his notes for a moment before looking back up, 'Speaking of taking Jamie's job as you put it, that's actually where I'd like to go next. Several weeks ago, Ms. Preest, needless to say, put you in your place regarding the commissionership of the UWF. In fact, that had to be...' Again, Montgomery holds his hand up to stop the UWF reporter, 'Again I will have to stop you there. Now, first of all, I will admit that I do owe Ms. Preest an apology. I have no idea why I thought her name was Patti, but that's neither here nor there.' 'So you still contest her position as the UWF Commissioner?' Now it is Montgomery's term for an instant of silence. He pauses, looking into his steepled hands for a moment as he considers his answer, 'Yes and no. You see, it was Ms. Preest's own actions by...putting me in my place as you put it...that actually gave me the ammunition I would need for a wrongful termination suit.' 'But according to Ms. Preest you vacated your position as the Commissioner meaning the position was vacant when she was brought in.' Montgomery raises an eyebrow, 'Do I look like a fool to you?' 'No, not at all!' Write quickly squeeks. 'Smart answer Danny. Glad to see you recognize the value of not insulting the man who signs your checks. You see, I never vacated my position. As you're no doubt aware, my leg was broken in a title match with Digital Mortality. This of course required me to take a two month medical leave in order to recover. At best, Ms. Preest can consider herself temporary commissioner...one with a *very* tenuous hold on her power.' 'I'm sorry Mr. Montgomery, but I must say I find that hard to believe. You've lost your world title, your commissionership, and you no longer have stock in the company. Even then, it was never controlling stock in the first place.' 'Mr. Write, Mr. Write, Mr. Write. I see you've bought into Ms. Preest's propoganda. Let me make it clear for you. This past January at the Clash, it was announced that the Universal Wrestling Federation had been sold to it's wrestlers, correct?' 'Correct.' 'And I'm a UWF Wrestler, correct?' Write nods, 'Right again.' 'Therefore giving me controlling shares in the Universal Wrestling Federation, correct?' 'Uh huh...' 'So, we've established that as a wrestler who was given part ownership of the Universal Wrestling Federation, I did indeed own controlling shares, despite Ms. Preest's claims to the contrary. Now, this is what you need to understand, and what Ms. Preest is clearly not aware of. As with all other members of the Player's Club, my financial portfolio is handled by the personnel at Asylum Industries.' 'Not by Mr. Todd as with the rest of the UWF's personnel?' 'No. I prefer to maintain control of my finances as much as possible myself. Now Mr. Hayes did indeed contact our brokers and made a rather impressive offer. However, the offer was rejected by the Player's Club and we still own that stock.' 'If I could just clear up one point there. Now with your various holdings, you can of course afford not to sell, but I don't believe that Dunn, Ezra, Kevin or Nick can make that same claim.' Montgomery simply smiles, relaxing once again, 'Well, like you said, with my various holdings I can afford to do an awful lot. That includes making sure that Mr. Hayes doesn't make an offer that I can't match.' 'So you paid the rest of the Player's Club the same amount of money not to sell that Mr. Hayes was offering them to sell.' 'Exactly. Of course though, on the other hand, I do have to thank Ms. Preest. You see, without her efforts to 'set me straight' if Ezra had decided to pursue his own lawsuit, then I could easily have used Ms. Preest's own words to completely absolve myself of any responsibility regarding Kevin and Joshua's recent work on the UWF rulebook.' 'I'm glad you brought that up actually. Thanks to their efforts, as well as your pronouncement when you made your return in your own home town of Halifax Nova Scotia, it's now going to be you versus Joshua Dunn, Ezra Kazee and Kevin Starr for the World Heavyweight Title, despite Ms. Preest's desire that it be otherwise.' 'Quite simply Danny, that's nothing more than further proof that Ms. Preest doesn't recognize a quality match when she sees one. Despite the awards committee's views, the last Regal Rumble saw what had to be the match of the year as Kevin, Joshua, and I battled for the Intercontinental title in a ladder in a cage match. 'This year, I will not allow anyone to settle for a match of that calibre. This year, it will be taken to the next level. An Ultimate Submission Iron Man match. In a cell no less. Most submissions wins. My God, can you imagine the possibilities in a match like that? The mind absolutely boggles.' 'Be that as it may, our commissioner has named her enforcers, Tropical Storm, as the guest referees. I think it's safe to assume that, despite the cell, the odds cannot possibly be in your favor.' Montgomery pauses for a moment, considering this, 'You know, people have a habit of underestimating me. When I stepped into the ring with Jamie Shock, they all said I didn't have what it took to win. I won. They said I'd never be able to beat Beelzebozo. They said I didn't have what it took...and you know what? It did take every single move in my arsenal, but I won. Quite simply, I am *going* to win. People will see things they never thought possible. They will see things that they'll still be talking about for years to come. And you know what else they'll be talking about?' 'What?' 'They will be talking about how I came back from injury, how I participated in the greatest match they've ever seen, and, most importantly, they *will* be talking about how I got my title back.' 'Well thank you for your time Mr. Montgomery,' Danny Write turns back toward the camera, 'it should be quite a match this coming Sunday in Tokyo! Andrew, back to you.' Both men sit silently for an instant before, 'And...we're clear,' the director calls out. The crew quickly begins to take down their equipment, 'Thanks for your time Mike, that was quite the interview.' 'Any time Danny.' Montgomery watches as the crew soon finishes packing up and makes it's way out of the office. Opposite his desk, on the far side of his office, sits a TV and VCR. Montgomery walks over, selects a tape labeled 'SAS, August 8th, 2004' and pops it into the VCR. He walks back to his desk, picks up a remote, and watches the tape of his tag match with Ezra Kazee against Joshua Dunn and Nick Persia. He leans forward, elbows on his knees, and closely studies the action on the monitor. Montgomery thumbs the intercom one more time, 'Ms. Allen?' 'Yes Mr. Montgomery?' 'Get Nick Persia on the phone for me, would you? I need to discuss his participation at Terror in Tokyo.' 'I'll have him in a few minutes.' 'Thank you. Let him know that I'd like to meet with him this afternoon.' 'I'll relay the message.' 'One last thing Ms. Allen. I am not to be disturbed for the next several hours.' 'Of course.' With that, Montgomery shuts the intercom off, presses play, and studies every movement that unfolds on the screen. 'Once more from the top...'
