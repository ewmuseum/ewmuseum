UWF (2001–07) Universal Promos — #704

As the Hammer Falls...

16 Sep 2004

'The Renegade' Rich Rollins — Love Box Warehouse, South Chicago

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A mistake. A miscalculation. A single moment of hesitation, the end resulting in disaster. Since entering the Universal Wrestling Federation, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins has done nothing but succeed. Hell, they don’t even have a word for it; Rollins purely dominated. “The Shocker” Dave Storm, Fizbane the Fabulous, Shane Stone, Johnny Roxx, The Real Deal… all victims to the unstoppable embodiment of ambition that is Rich Rollins. Brody and Ash O’Hara, Jamie Dermont, Bryn and Danny Jaye, Nick Persia, Slasher… men who met their respective ends in the Royal Rumble alone, their last cognizant memory being that of The Renegade tearing a rampage through the ring, using their careers as a catapult for his own. In one night, Rich Rollins chalked up more eliminations than anyone. Save one man. Moraless. Or, John Edwards if you prefer. A friend. A foe. A walking contradiction in the wake of Rollins’ re-awakened career, John “Moraless” Edwards finally put a rock in The Renegade’s well-oiled killing machine, earning the honorable distinction of being the first man to legitimately pin Rich Rollins. A distinction that, truth be told, not many men had bragging rights to. A single mistake. A minor miscalculation. And before The Renegade knew it, he was mortal again. The illusion of invincibility was far too easy to embrace, making it that much more difficult to swallow its inevitable betrayal. These things swirl through The Renegade’s thoughts as we fade in… The Love Box warehouse, south Chicago… [Sitting with his head in his hands, “The Renegade” Rich Rollins is already staring straight into the camera as it comes into focus. His eyes sparkle with an unspeakable inner conviction, two determined orbs of blue fire gutting holes through the camcorder’s lens.] [Eyes of fire. Aura of death. Soulless, dangerous eyes.] Renegade: Failure… [The single drawn-out word is hollow. For some strange reason, it doesn’t resonate within the spacious confines of Rollins’ private warehouse.] Renegade: … has never been an option for me. How can it be? Do you think that people walk out their front doors every morning, thinking to themselves, “I wonder how bad I can fuck my life up today?” Well boys and girls, if you truly believe that nobody looks at life through such a desolate lens, then you’d be wrong. [His words are still hollow, barren any shred of emotion. His eyes, still intensely focused infernos of inner fury.] Renegade: You see people like that every day. People like prostitutes and pimps are easy targets, but I’m not talkin’ the general shady scum in the shadows of every McDonalds around America. No… I’m talkin’ about average, every day people you see in your homes. In your businesses. In your street-corner coffee houses on a lunch break. The people you consider “normal.” For every smiling, second-rate secretary you see chatting merrily on her cell phone, there’s an equally unhappy lawyer contemplating pulling the trigger for defending a convicted rapist somewhere. In the time that that same happy secretary takes a swig of her five-dollar Starbucks cappuccino, ten suicides just “couldn’t take it anymore.” [Pause.] Renegade: And that’s just Chicago. [Silence.] [Rollins leans back now, brushing a wayward strand of frazzled brown hair out of his eyes, tucking it behind one ear. He absently licks his lips, taking a breath.] Renegade: People have lost their ambition. They’ve lost all hope for redemption, and most people’s lives aren’t the melodramatic soap operas they claim to be. People are desperate for a good drama, something to spice up their lives and give them meaning again… but most times it just backfires. As a result, they don’t have the ambition to pick themselves up and plough on. Instead, they’re the kind of the people who look underneath a Pepsi cap that reads, “Sorry, try again” and get frustrated for losing a contest they didn’t even know existed until they heard the snap-fizz of the carbonation being released from the bottle. They have no discipline, no desire to improve their station in life, especially if they have to actually - you guessed it - earn their way to the top. [The Renegade nods ever so slowly, inattentively rubbing the knuckles on his right fist with his left hand, forcing a strained pop from the joints.] Renegade: It all comes down to paying dues. I’ve paid my way through a successful career ten times over, but does that mean I get handed professional acclaim and the respect of my peers on a silver platter? (he chuckles) Not in this fucking lifetime, I can tell you that. Just so you hear it from me first, John… Moraless, whatever you prefer… I respect you for keeping true to the business. You earned your victory over me, I won’t discredit you for that. You and I, we’re fossils of the wrestling world. We’ve been around and seen it all, but we’re both still looking’ for that one big break, aren’t we Johnny? [He leans back in now, the camera’s view encompassed by The Renegade’s stoic features.] Renegade: But it’ll only happen once, I damn sure promise you that. The next “break” you get from me will be your neck, so help me Christ. I climbed my way up the UWF ladder, ploughed through the competition like they weren’t even there, and then… then, Johnny-boy, you took me down a peg. Knocked me down a rung. And I’m not speakin’ metaphorically, either… literally, I destroyed Real Deal one week, fight you the next and lose, and find myself across the card from Real Deal once again. I can feel my wheels spinnin’ underneath me and it isn’t a feelin’ I care to let linger, if you catch my drift. [Standing erect to his full height, Rich Rollins forces the camera to pan wide as he slowly saunters over to a waist-high collection of opened storage crates. Standing up on matching polished wood backboards, two items glint in the fading light of a Chicago sun that streams in from cracked skylight windows.] [The World Heavyweight titles of both the Cyber Wrestling Union and Global Championship Wrestling.] [Rollins reaches behind the stacked crates as he speaks.] Renegade: I’ve kept these treasures under lock and key for too long. My biggest claims to fame in the wrestling world, hidden even from myself. But you know, I hate people who use their past accomplishments as a crutch for the future. [Without ceremony, The Renegade hefts a fifteen-pound sledgehammer from behind the crate, swinging it idly in the grip of one hand.] Renegade: Fuck the past. We define our own future. [Without a word, Rich Rollins cuts the musty warehouse atmosphere with the sledge, shattering the CWU title in one blow that sends shards of the polished backboard spiraling in every direction. The storage crate buckles under the blow and sags as he hefts the fifteen-pound metal mallet again, sending the GCW title to a similar fate with a second swing. Bits of goldtone metals and wood rain down like faded, glinting snowflakes. The crate finally gives out with an unceremonious crack, spilling its payload of random photographs and newspaper clippings all over the gray concrete floor.] [His face unflinching, The Renegade nods.] Renegade: The UWF is my future. [He looks at the camera now.] Renegade: And it’s unfortunate that The Real Deal doesn’t have one. [Fade to black.] ~Fin~
