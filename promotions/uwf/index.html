Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The UWF carried itself like a real company: a standing fictional network in CCTV, a weekly flagship (Sunday Afternoon Supremacy) plus a weekly B-show (Wednesday Night Universal) sustained for years, a pay-per-view roughly every six weeks, and arena cards billed from the Tokyo Dome to Madison Square Garden to the Houston Astrodome. Its commentary team, Rick and Larson, gave the fed its house-religion oath ("Dear Rod!") and its exasperated conscience. Its title ledgers, weekly shows, and roster database survive nearly whole — 197 served shows and 759 served roleplays, the deepest single record in the collection.

The character work is what survived best. Seymour Almasy, "The Final Fantasy" — a sustained Final Fantasy X cosplay gimmick and more recorded UWF title reigns than anyone else in the fed's own ledger — and the geometry-pun absurdity of Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid ("H.I.T.") are both Sean Simon Williams' (1985–2018) own creations. Around them: Kenny Mercury the technical wizard; Mike Montgomery, who sued the fed for fifty million and became champion; the hardcore stable drowningPOOL and the death-and-rebirth of Ben Jericho into Unforgiven; a wrestling chimpanzee who held the World Title; a hardcore belt spelled out, deliberately, as T-H-C; and an annual "World Jobber Title," carrying no holder ledger of its own, that the cards played as a pure gag — famously "held by Mr. T," with El Turtlé as guest referee, the same El Turtlé who genuinely held the Television belt for a week in May 2004. The joke was always load-bearing.

The UWF's founding runs earlier than its own main site's own later telling of it: a separate site, uwf.org.uk, ran alongside the uwf2k1 Yahoo group from the very start (Sep 2001), and per the promoter's own recall the site must have belonged to the handler behind Gary Clark's own on-page Commissioner role, before the promoter took the fed over onto the other domain — that earlier handler's authority giving way to the promoter, whose later, fuller uwfonline.com corpus is the bulk of the fed's own surviving record. The founding-era title histories agree with the later, revised ones on every reign checked; nothing was retroactively rewritten, the record just kept growing.

No closing post or farewell show survives, but the last card the Museum holds gestures at the end anyway. Sins of the Past, 1 Aug 2006, opens on-air with Rick and Larson trading rumors of the fed being "nearly declared bankrupt before a mysterious benefactor" stepped in, and closes with a stripped North American Champion wondering aloud what happens to his belt next — commentary calling it, in as many words, "the end of an era for the UWF." Nothing after that card was ever captured: the site's own later domain, uwf.rpcommunity.com, carried dated content into 2007, but no further show script survives behind it. The fed didn't announce its own death on record; the story was left mid-sentence.

Era-typical material Some UWF promos are genuinely period-typical late-attitude-era heel ugliness. They are served whole anyway, behind the content notice — every recovered promo with surviving text is on the shelf, unedited (145 board pages recovered; 122 carried text; all 122 are served — the remaining 23 were video-only pages whose media did not survive). What the Museum's own writing declines to do is repeat the ugliness in its placards and summaries. The documents speak for themselves; see Curation.

Storylines

Two weekly shows on a standing fictional network, and a pay-per-view roughly every six weeks — the fed itself stated the cadence out loud: "SAS, SAS, WNU, SAS, SAS, PPV." The arcs below are what four years of the fed's own awards said mattered most, recapped from the shows themselves.

The Coronation Written by AI, 2026

The richest single fully-scripted show in the corpus: a title-overhaul special where, as the fed put it, "FOUR TITLES HAVE CHANGED HANDS TONIGHT!" It is also the fed's cleanest screwjob-that-backfired. Lew Spectre, working the booking chair, stripped the "injured" Local NVA Company of the Tag Titles mid-show, put up a vacant three-way, and had his own Black Star Revolution jump Misfit two-on-one — then got legitimately double-crossed when the referee counted fast for Misfit's surprise roll-up instead. A booking-power screwjob that came down on the head of the man who called it. Almasy won the World Title the same night. The full script is served in the Card Archive.

The Resurrection vs. The Player's Club Written by AI, 2026

The fed's major multi-month storyline. Mike Montgomery's backstage-power stable, the Player's Club, against The Resurrection — Moraless and Digital Mortality, a revenge narrative built on the death of Digital's girlfriend Justine after Joshua Dunn's betrayal. The Tag Titles chain through both stables and then the drowningPOOL, with the Formorians queued behind them — a clean multi-stable title war with a built-in next-owner line.

Ben Jericho → Unforgiven Written by AI, 2026

The death and rebirth. drowningPOOL co-founder Ben Jericho, addicted and self-destructive, betrays Misfit for a contract bonus and joins Black Star Revolution — and in-fiction he is narrated as literally dead, with "Unforgiven" the man who returns in his place. Hanging off the same arc: the fire-for-fire retaliation between Misfit and Kenny Mercury, casket-burning answered with a man set alight. The lineage's third death-and-rebirth beat, after the IWF's Carnage and the GIWF's Gabriel.

Digital, Dunn, and the wrestling chimpanzee Written by AI, 2026

Two of the fed's signature beats. Joshua Dunn, brainwashed heel by the Local NVA Company's "magical brain washing syrem [sic]," kidnaps and turns his tag partner Digital Mortality's girlfriend — the betrayal the Resurrection is built to avenge. And Pete the PG Tips Chimp, a silent gimmick narrated entirely by his manager-fan "Little Timmy," won the World Heavyweight Championship off Jamie Shock in a manner no commission report could print — a reign that ends only when Almasy stands up to the Chimpanzee Tyrant and takes the belt. Larson, at the booth: "OKAY, I CHANGED MY MIND! I WANT THE MONKEY BACK AS CHAMPION AGAIN!" The fed's own 2003 Annual Awards named this pairing Angle of the Year outright — "Jamie Shock loses to Pete the PG Tips Chimp" — over the Black Star Revolution angle and that same year's Stable Wars run.

The Bunny Wars Written by AI, 2026

The fed's great recurring comedy-stakes gag: a costumed-bunny-army invasion, beaten back "with a flamethrower and an all-you-can-eat buffet" and reduced to "burning BBQ goodness," with a second uprising forever threatened in the news columns. Seymour Almasy and Aerith won the World Tag Titles, per the Hall of Fame ceremony itself, "after torching the Bunny Army of Doom with a flamethrower." Not every great UWF story was grim; this one was rabbits — and the ceremony agreed: the Slaughter of the Bunny Army of DOOM placed a co-runner-up for Angle of the Year 2003, the same year Almasy's own Final Fantasy gimmick swept Wrestler, Best Gimmick, and Best Mic-Worker of the Year.

The Feuds of the Year, ceremony by ceremony Written by AI, 2026

Naming a Feud of the Year is the fed's own annual verdict on what its year actually was, in its own words, and the four ceremonies together trace the fed's own center of gravity moving year over year: 2002's own winner paired two men still mid-rise, billed by the ceremony script itself as "the masked man bent on world domination and the guy who thinks he's in a Playstation role play video game" (Tsunami Oni vs. Seymour Almasy, 50% of the vote, over a three-way field that included the Bum Boozler's own return angle); 2003's went to Danny Jaye vs. Jamie Shock, over Kevin Starr vs. Almasy and Bruce Gordy vs. Mike Montgomery; and 2004's went to Mike Montgomery vs. Tsunami Oni, over Misfit vs. Kenny Mercury and Jamie Shock vs. Malcolm Kennedy. Full vote breakdowns, every nominee, and every other category from all four ceremonies (2001–2004) are live on the Awards page, filtered to UWF; the ceremony scripts themselves are served whole at 2003 and 2004 ("the Impies").

Tsunami Oni vs. Seymour Almasy — Feud of the Year, 2002 Written by AI, 2026

Both men spent 2002 climbing the same title lane before the ceremony ever named them. Oni won the Television Championship within weeks of his UWF debut and then dropped it to Almasy — commentary called the exchange plainly: "that belt got both of those men noticed, and thanks to their reigns, they've both moved to the top of the card within months." Oni answered by taking the Intercontinental Championship off Digital Mortality at Live and Loud 2 (6 Oct 2002). The two collided directly in a four-corners main event with Danny Jaye and Kenny Mercury on 24 Nov 2002 — "one of the most entertaining matches in the UWF," per the call, and Mercury shocked the field by pinning Almasy clean for the win. Oni turned the loss into a promo the following week, informing Mercury on camera that beating Almasy proved nothing: "Did you pin Tsunami Oni? No. Did you make Tsunami Oni submit? No. Then you did not defeat Tsunami Oni!" — a line that folded Mercury into Oni's own "EVILUTIONARY WAR" angle rather than crediting him a win over either man. The feud took Feud of the Year at 50% of the vote, over Bum Boozler/Roger Rogers vs. Local NVA Company vs. The S.A.S. (30%) and Inner Circle vs. PWW (20%); the same ceremony gave Almasy's Final Fantasy gimmick Gimmick of the Year (45%) and Oni both Newcomer of the Year (45%) and Best Current Mic-Worker (36%). The two shows carrying the direct collision are served whole: 24 Nov 2002 SAS (the four-corners match) and 27 Nov 2002 WNU (Oni's follow-up promo); the title exchange is called out on 13 Oct 2002 SAS, and Oni's IC Title win is on Live and Loud 2.

Iron Curtain — Stable of the Year, 2002 Written by AI, 2026

Membership shifted across the stable's own run — Joshua Dunn represented it as Hardcore Champion, Mike Montgomery was billed as its "newest member" at Halloween Hell 2002, and on at least one card the Local NVA Company and Tsunami Oni's own Storm Front are folded into the same banner ("the rest of the Iron Curtain") against The Legends. Its signature bit ran straight through the broadcast booth: at Halloween Hell 2002, the Local NVA Company's World Tag Team Title defense against Thunder & Lightning was called under a running gag where color commentator Jimmy Stapp openly rooted for "my comrades in the Iron Curtain" over broadcast partner Jason Lane's on-air objections — the stable's Cold War heel gimmick played as a full commentary bit, not just an entrance video. The Local NVA Company lost that title match clean (Thunder & Lightning won it) the same card Digital Mortality, the stable's most decorated member, worked the main event. The group's own year still closed with the fed's own verdict: Stable of the Year, 45%, over Inner Circle (40%), The Asylum (10%), and the Youngbloods (5%) — the same ceremony that gave Digital Mortality both Wrestler of the Year and Angle of the Year. Both cards are served whole: Halloween Hell 2002 and 2 Oct 2002 WNU.

The Regal Rumble — Match of the Year, 2003 & 2004 Written by AI, 2026

Back-to-back winners, two different Rumbles. The fourth annual Regal Rumble happened at Coronation (2003) — Kevin Starr's surprise win launched him straight into a World Title challenge a month later at Live and Loud 3, commentary crediting the Rumble outright: "after winning the Regal Rumble, he's gone on a tear." It beat the Live and Loud II Cruiserweight Finals, Almasy vs. Kevin Starr, the THC Prom, and the second Poisoned Pyramid Death Match for 2003 Match of the Year. The following year's Rumble, at Terror in Tokyo (2004), ended with Tsunami Oni's hand raised — "And you winner… of the Regal Rumble… TSUNAMI ONI!!!" — after a sprawling, chaos-heavy stretch run: a Herbert the Polar Bear brawl, a mid-match GIWF cameo from Eric Cameron chasing down a stolen gimmick, Killer Dude's returning-legend entrance. That stretch run survives in two forms — the final show script, and an earlier working draft carrying the same beats verbatim under "Ideas:" scratch notes, one of the only documents in the corpus that shows the fed's own writing process before a card went final: the Regal Rumble elimination log. It beat Wes Diable vs. Nick Persia, Joshua Dunn vs. Almasy, and Tsunami Oni vs. Mike Montgomery for 2004 Match of the Year — the same Tsunami Oni who'd go on to win the World Title itself that November.

The earliest captured snapshot (October 2000) titles the site "UWFonline.com — United Wrestling Fedaration" [sic], before it settled into "Universal Wrestling Federation." Nobody ever fixed it, and the museum does not either. The dates and the typos are both part of the honesty.

Affiliations

Primetime Central (PTC)

Member of UWF (2001–07) (as of 31 Mar 2002) — The UWF's own news archive (uwf.org.uk) dates the alliance precisely: "This morning, the UWF's negotiations with Primetime Central... finally came to a close... we happily joined the PTC family" (31 Mar 2002), with Commissioner Lew Spectre quoted on the terms.
UWF’s own affiliates/link-exchange pages banner-link Primetime Central (“The World’s #1 Interfed” / “Spiffy Interfed of Horror!”) — direct confirmation of UWF’s PTC membership, previously known only through storyline (Almasy’s Golden Turnbuckle Tournament run, Jay Phoenix’s PTC Global Championship reigns). UWF sent a delegation to PTC’s own “New Era” interfed supercard, 20 Apr 2003, whose Superstars page groups the whole card by member fed. The event’s own site was even uploaded to and served from UWF’s own domain, ftp.uwfonline.com/httpdocs/i-ppv — a member fed hosting the alliance’s own event site.

4 Winds Wrestling (4WW)

Affiliate of UWF (2001–07)
Banner link-exchange on UWF’s own affiliates page (“Gassiest Wrestling Promotion in the Tri-State Area”). Independently corroborates Jay Phoenix’s 2003 UWF comeback as a billed 4WW star: 4WW’s own record carries his North Wind Championship win on its first night, 13 July 2003.

Xtreme Ultimo Wrestling (XUW)

Affiliate of UWF (2001–07)

No Pirates Wrestling (NPW, 2004)

Crossover booking with UWF (2001–07)
Tsunami Oni's own served UWF origin promo names No Pirates Wrestling as the promotion he worked before the UWF, under promoter Zebediah Love, alongside Buck Naked and Esinem — the only surviving evidence that NPW, a splash-page-only mockup that never itself launched, ever had any roster at all.
