A Puerto Rico-set promotion that crowned its first-ever champion in an eleven-man Puerto Rican Heavyweight tournament before closing within three months of its own debut. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Pro Wrestling of the Caribbean introduced itself in May 1999 with a hype reel built from the region's own wrestling history — archival clips of Bret “The Hitman” Hart, Scott Hall, Carlos Colon, and Abdullah the Butcher — before cutting to its masked president, Oliver Santiago, promising “a new venture in an old, familiar place… the Caribbean.” Its own introduction describes Santiago as a former masked wrestling star who built the promotion on his retirement savings after growing disillusioned with the “mockery” the bigger companies had made of the sport, and it committed itself to just two titles, the PWC Caribbean Heavyweight Championship and the secondary PWC Puerto Rican Heavyweight Championship, “in an effort to escalate their status.”

It ran two shows a week: PWC Horizon, a Wednesday-night results show from the Eugenio Guerra Sports Complex in San Juan, and Legacy, a Sunday-night, full play-by-play main-event show from PWC Arena in Bayamon. Miguel Sanchez hosted both, joined by Eddie Raslo on Legacy.

Its own Puerto Rican Heavyweight title tournament closed on Legacy, 27 June 1999, before 10,000 fans, when “Son of a Bitch” Sam Hill beat “Britain's Nightmare” Alexander Lawson with a jumping DDT to become the first titleholder in PWC history. The moment he raised the belt, “Colossal” Ron Walker, a debuting giant with no match scheduled that night, stormed the ring and choked him out, dropping the title across his body as the broadcast cut to black.

The following day's news bulletin left the top prize, the Caribbean Heavyweight Championship, still vacant, promising it would be filled on 11 July in a “Puerto Rican Rumble” throwing the entire roster into one ring, alongside a new PWC First Blood Championship due on 4 July and a tag division still to be named.

How the Puerto Rican Rumble or the First Blood tournament played out isn't in its surviving record. Its founder closed the promotion that July, citing the demands of graduate study.
