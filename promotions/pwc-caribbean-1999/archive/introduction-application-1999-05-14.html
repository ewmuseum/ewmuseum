[Santiago gives a big thumbs up as the scene fades out.] INTRODUCTION Professional Wrestling of the Caribbean [PWC] is a regional promotion operating primarily out of Puerto Rico but claims the entire Caribbean as its base of operations. PWC runs on a shoestring budget, it's highest paid wrestlers receiving the base salary for those in the "big leagues." However, what it lacks in glamour and glitz, it hopes to make up in pure, unadulterated professional wrestling excitement.
PWC is the brainchild of Oliver Santiago, a former masked professional wrestling superstar and proud Puerto Rico native. A bit of the traditionalist,
Santiago grew disenfranchised with the mockery of the sport offered by the larger conglomerates in existence. With the rudimentary savings accumulated from his wrestling career, Santiago has put PWC together and serves as its innaugural chief executive officer/commissioner/president.
THE LOGISTICS PWC is a booked e-federation, meaning that all cards are scripted by hand with results determined by a system [which will be explained below] and not determined by a computer program. This system hopes to allow for both fairness and spontaneity, facets often overlooked in computer-based federations.
With all booked e-federations, roleplays are of the utmost importance. It is
PWC's assumption that its handlers are skilled in the art of roleplaying, so no explanation of this should be necessary. Roleplays and angles are what will keep the league going.
PWC will maintain a roster of 20-25 singles wrestlers at any given time [PWC does not currently have a tag team division but will look to open one in the near future]. A small roster will enable reasonable cards, better written cards and increased competition. Since that is the case, a handler will only be allowed one (1) wrestler.
While PWC hopes to have a small-time federation look and appeal of an ECW, we are not an extreme league. Specialty matches will occur on a rather infrequent basis. Hey, when you're a league that operates on a shoestring budget it gets kind of hard to pay for a cage construction!
The goal here is realism. I hope to make PWC as close to the "real" thing as humanly possible. Keep that goal in mind when playing in PWC.
ROLEPLAYS Again, roleplays are the bread and butter of any good booked league and that is definitely the case with PWC. Therefore, roleplays are mandatory. In order to maintain a wrestler in the PWC, a handler must roleplay at least once per week, the exception being in cases of personal business [hey, I certainly hope you all have lives outside of the e-federation business; I do]. Unless I am told of any absence, not roleplaying will result in a wrestler jobbing a match for the first offense. Second offense equals a one-week suspension from action.
Third offense means contract termination. Again, I can understand being too busy to roleplay; all it takes is a one-line e-mail to let me know.
Roleplays should be mailed to [detail withheld] . Roleplays will be distributed twice a week, once as a part of PWC Horizon and again as part of
Legacy [see below]. Therefore, anything above two roleplays a week may be obsessive and, more than likely, won't be able to be distributed until the following week. The current format allows for a wrestler to make comments and respond to his opponent in a week's time, much like the "real" sport itself.
Anything goes in your roleplays! Go anywhere, do anything. Cursing will be edited, if not by you then by me. However, when doing a roleplay, I ask you to keep the following in mind:
1. The scheme of PWC - Again, PWC is a small-scale league minus the budgets of the larger leagues. In all honesty, do you think PWC would have graveyard scenes, huge press conferences and the like? Let me answer that for you. Nah.
Does that mean there won't be exceptions? Certainly not. I don't want to be a stuff-shirt about things. All I ask is that you be true to what the PWC is about.
2. Realism - Self-explanatory, I hope. STRATEGIES Strategies are optional in PWC. Strategies are definitely not required for any match on PWC Horizon. However, if a wrestler is involved in a match on Legacy, a strategy is optional. Sending in a strategy gives the card-writer a better sense of what your wrestler hopes to accomplish but will not guarantee a win necessarily. Wins are determined by roleplays, angles and how the wrestler's match up in terms of statistics sent in on the application.
Not sending in a strategy will have no effect on the outcome of the match, either. Again, however, if a handler wants the card writer to know more about his wrestler's approach to the match.
Do send in a strategy if you wish your wrestler to attack someone during a card, interfere in a match. All attacks and interference must be realistic and may not be used if it does not further an angle or have any merit. If a wrestler is interfering or attacking someone in PWC, it will definitely be for a good reason.
THE SHOWS PWC will run two shows a week - PWC Horizon and Legacy. PWC Horizon is the weekly house show for PWC and will be telecast on regional television, Wednesday nights, 11:00 p.m., from the Eugenio Guerra Sports
Complex, San Juan, Puerto Rico. The Sports Complex is a relatively small venue that will showcase the up-and-coming talent of PWC. PWC Horizon will be a results show with interviews [roleplays] from the week, detailed mid-card match results, rumors and other PWC news. It is hosted by Miguel Sanchez, whose biography can be read on the Non-Player Character page.
Legacy is PWC's main event show, broadcast live the Sportatorium, on regional
Caribbean television, Sunday nights, 8:00 p.m. Legacy will be a full play-by-play show featuring interviews from both active and in-active participants, rumors and other PWC news. It is hosted by Miguel Sanchez and
Eddie Raslo, both of whom have biographies available on the Non-Player
Character page. PWC does not currently hold any pay-per-view specials as the budget does not allow for it. However, there will be an occasionally special episode of Legacy that may run longer than the one and a half-hour program slot PWC has paid for.
TITLES PWC will recognize two singles titles - the PWC Caribbean Heavyweight Championship and the PWC Puerto Rican Heavyweight Championship. The PWC Caribbean Heavyweight Championship is the premier title of the league. While it does not have World title status, it is, nonetheless, the top belt in the league. The Puerto Rican Heavyweight Championship is a secondary title.
The PWC Caribbean Heavyweight Championship must be defended at least once a month, the PWC Puerto Rican Heavyweight Championship at least twice. All PWC
Caribbean Heavyweight Championship title matches will be held at Legacy while the PWC Puerto Rican Heavyweight Championship title defenses can be held at either card.
PWC only recognizes two titles in an effort to escalate their status and the level of competition around them. With only two titles to fight for, things around the league should be quite competitive.
