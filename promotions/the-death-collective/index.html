A London-based promotion built around two linked shows -- a weekly televised soap opera and a European touring circuit with its own prize-money ruleset -- founded by the same handler who wrote NFW's own Castor Strife. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Death Collective's own recruitment pitch, posted 6 October 2017, opens without the usual welcome: “People are going to hate this fed. Creative freedom is the top priority. We're going to be playing for real money.” It presented itself as a Castor Strife production based in London.

Two linked shows were announced the same day. Black Market TV, a weekly televised program from York Hall in London, ran a “freeform” format that judged wrestlers on roleplay and character work rather than a decided match outcome, fought for a Heavyweight Championship under one-fall, ten-minute rules with no countouts. The Silk Road, a touring European show recapped by a Paris-based talk program, paid real prize money per fall under its own modified boxing-style ruleset, building toward an annual tournament worth €500,000.

An event poster for the debut broadcast, Black Market TV 01, set for 21 October 2017 at York Hall, billed four matches: Castor Strife against an opponent left unnamed, Simon Lineker against Nuckelavee, Ill Fortune against Money Talks, and Haircules against Nagasaki Fire.

A press conference the same day as the poster formally signed Simon Lineker, billed as Great Britain's freestyle wrestling gold medalist from the 2008 Beijing Olympics, to a three-year contract, with Talent Relations figure Robby Leland and a co-sponsoring wrestling association's own chairman praising him as a national hero. The event was crashed in person by his own first opponent, Nuckelavee, posing as a reporter before threatening him to his face as the broadcast cut away.

No results document survives for that 21 October debut, and no later show or notice is on record after it.
