Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

WLWA describes itself, across three surviving 1999 documents, as a three-year-old “booked” promotion — a federation running since roughly 1996 by its own account — even as each document announces a fresh start: a “Grand Opening” post on 30 January launches a new web site and opens applications (mentioning, in passing, a WLWA board game), and a Free Agents call on 20 July, after “a three month layoff,” announces yet another new address and a renewed recruiting drive.

A Rule Book posted 12 July 1999 sets out WLWA's actual structure: a single weekly televised card, Wednesday Night's Main Event, run in the style of “Monday Nitro, or WWF Raw is War,” plus pay-per-views roughly every two months — House Arrest I (28 February, Franklin Square, New York), Battle of the Belts I (21 March, the Silverdome, Dallas), WrestleFest (16 May), Summer Heat (8 August, San Diego), Birthday Bash (6 November, Madison Square Garden) and Year End Blast (26 December) — plus occasional interpromotional “Federation Warfare” specials.

Wrestlers were required to be “100% original,” the rule book warning explicitly against copying a real star under a different name; two weight divisions, cruiserweight and heavyweight, contested five championships — World, US, TV, Tag and Cruiserweight — with pushes decided partly through a points system scoring handlers' own flashes, interviews and strategies.

No card result, roster, or further document survives past the 20 July recruiting call; whether any of the six pay-per-views the Rule Book announced for 1999 ever took place is not recorded.
