Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

A 29 November 1997 post announces the Big Apple Wrestling Federation as “a hot new local promotion based out of the New York City area,” opening under the influence of the National Wrestling Council alliance. Its debut card, Bad to the Bone!, ran a month later at St. Raymond's Catholic High School in the South Bronx, built around a ten-man battle royal and two semifinal rounds of a Big Apple Title Tournament whose own final was never recorded.

That battle royal ended on a strange note even by the fed's own standards: Sephiroth, already eliminated, vanished from the ring in a lightning strike moments before the winner could be announced, leaving The Sycho to be declared the survivor over a stunned commentary booth.

Four championships ran through the promotion's roster of more than sixty named wrestlers across the fourteen shows its own record carries, most of them editions of a weekly Friday Night Heat broadcast out of the Jacob Javits Center in Manhattan: the Big Apple Heavyweight, Television, Manhattan Heavyweight, and Tag Team championships.

Its own place inside the National Wrestling Council turned into open war that May, when a planned joint supercard with the alliance's Texas region, Lone Star Wrestling, was delayed by a public feud between BAWF Commissioner “Magic” Mark Greene and his counterpart there, staged out in dueling on-air statements before the two regions finally met at the United Center in Chicago for Mayday Massacre.

The Big Apple Heavyweight title carried the promotion's own record to its end: champion Sephiroth, also billed as Kasey Arrows, and challenger Jimmy “Pretty Boy” Floyd traded the title match across three broadcasts, one thrown out by Greene himself after Sephiroth was legitimately hurt mid-match, before a steel cage rematch settled it — Floyd missing a dive from the top of the cage, Sephiroth answering with a Brainbuster for the pin. Its last surviving document, self-dated 22 May 1998, ends there, with Sephiroth still champion. No further record survives.
