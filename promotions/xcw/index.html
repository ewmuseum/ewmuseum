A twice-relaunched original-character e-fed whose champion's own invasion angle against HCW is its best-attested storyline, and whose December 1997 relaunch is the only one of its own eras to leave a complete card behind. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Xtreme Cyber Wrestling was already running by early 1996, went dark that year, and reopened by mid-1997 on rec.sport.pro-wrestling as a booked fed for original wrestlers only, pitching itself past ordinary roleplay: “You Create the Application form, Roleplay, Choose how you wish to wrestle in a match and many more advanced features are available.”

Its best-attested storyline that year starred its own champion, LOCO, staging his own banishment from HCW in a filmed promo, calling himself “the disgraced and humilated person you see before you... branded a broken man... banished for crimes not my own,” closing on six words — “STILL LOCO WITH OR WITHOUT YOU” — that the promotion's own community argued over for weeks afterward.

The fed relaunched again that December, billing itself in one recruitment post as a brand new promotion built on an old one's bones: “XCW is a brand new fed with that old style flavor... Be apart of the foundation and the history.” Its one surviving complete card, Monday Night Madness, ran live from the West Palm Beach Auditorium on 22 December 1997 as the opening round of a World Title Tournament: six matches, six winners — “Reckless” Randy Richards, Sniper, Michael Martin Murphy, Great White, Patty Babes Carter, and “2 Smooth” Setzer Van Strife — with a Friday card already booked to carry the bracket forward.

That Friday card is not in the surviving record, and the promotion's own next broadcast, Wednesday SHOWDOWN! on 15 January 1998, opened by admitting the relaunch had nearly come apart: “The Monday Night Maddness card was not able to be completed. The state of the fed was in jepordy, but now we are back in business,” settling from there onto a single live show a week.

LOCO's own year-end campaign post, two days after Monday Night Madness, already billed him as holding both the World and Intercontinental titles for wins over Eric Draven and Jared Irons — whether that stood alongside, or ahead of, the tournament the same week's card had just opened isn't resolved in the surviving record. How the promotion continued after its January 1998 broadcast, and how it ended, isn't in the surviving record either.
