Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The North American, and Intercontinental Wrestling Federation's surviving handbook dates its own first writing to 24 July 1997, revised into the copy that survives on 15 March 2000 — headquartered, in its own fiction, in New York City and modeling itself explicitly on “successful promotions such as the World Wrestling Federation and NWA.”

Its weekly show, Monday Madness, ran eight to ten matches under commentators Jason Sanders and Nick Martinez; a second show, Friday Night Fights, was added after its April pay-per-view, Judgement Day, itself billed as “our version of WrestleMania,” a two-day event.

Six championships structured its own roster: a Triple Crown World Title — unified, in the fed's own lineage, when “NAIWF legend Angel Reaper” combined it with his Mexican and Canadian belts — an Intercontinental Title, a Television Title, a Light-Heavyweight Title for wrestlers of 225 pounds or under, a hardcore-styled Warfare Title, and a World Tag Team Title.

Its front office was headed by President Victor Benfort, who the handbook says purchased the promotion on eBay from a previous owner, John “Direwolf” Pintha, and Vice President Dan Jacobs, a former Television Champion, Triple Crown Champion, and 1999 “Quest For The Ultimate Fighter” winner aligned, by 2000, with the sitting Triple Crown Champion, Vincent Visage.

No card result, title change, or further document of NAIWF's own survives beyond this handbook. What became of the promotion after March 2000 isn't in the surviving record.
