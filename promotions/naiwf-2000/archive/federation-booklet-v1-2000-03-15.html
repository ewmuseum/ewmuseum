Character Application --------------------------------------------------------- * Introduction... Welcome to the official handbook for the North American, and Intercontinental Wrestling Federation. The NAIWF is one of the near infinite number of fantasy wrestling promotions that suffuse the Internet. This federation exists to provide wrestling fans with a creative outlet. It gives you the ability to create a wrestler of your own, create angles, and to [potentially] win the
NAIWF Triple Crown World Title. The NAIWF relies on active involvement from the handlers, and cooperation between the handlers. For the most part, it is up to you to make the matches, create angles, and keep things interesting. The federation's front office will do its part to keep things moving and create angles, but in the end it is up to you.
The ultimate goal that is sought by all of us is to make the NAIWF different from most of the fantasy wrestling leagues out there. We are not only an outlet for its handlers, but also it is to be a coherent and exciting organization in which the wrestlers/handlers participate. This federation attempts to please an imaginary audience, so story lines, angles, and long running feuds are required to keep the action fresh. If you are looking for a federation where you challenge a wrestler, see the results, and just go on to the next wrestler, you would be wise to find a different league.
The NAIWF is not only a federation where the wrestlers roleplay to be successful, but the federation itself is going to be role-played. The federation is a fictitious promotion with it's headquarters based in New York
City. It is following in the footsteps of successful promotions such as the World Wrestling Federation and NWA. It is a world-wide federation, holding it's events in vast arenas all over North American and the rest of the planet..
The NAIWF follows the trend laid down by the original National Wrestling Alliance. Truly hard-core, traditional wrestling. In it's glory days, the National Wrestling Alliance was a better 'traditional' wrestling federation than World Championship Wrestling, and a better 'hard-core' wrestling federation than both the World Wrestling Federation and Extreme Championship
Wrestling. The difference lies in the fact that to the National Wrestling Alliance, their traditional wrestling was more hard-core than anything found today. The NAIWF has the specialty matches similar to the World Wrestling Federation, but of course also has many more traditional matches without blood-loss or anything like that. However, every time you step into an NAIWF ring, you run the risk of serious injury.
As for championships, the competitors here understand that these titles are something to be honored and fought for with every fiber of their being. Here, the champion does not give credibility to the title, the title gives credibility to the champion! You are not walking into this federation and getting a title without a fight. You are not going to become the number one contender without a little old-fashioned blood, sweat, tears, and dedication..
but that's what real professional wrestling is all about, isn't it? --------------------------------------------------------- * Codes of Conduct... There are several 'no-brainer' rules in e-wrestling, and I am hoping you are atleast somewhat aware of what I mean when I say that. Therefore, I will only cover those rules which the NAIWF stresses more than any others. However, these
'no-brainers' should be recognized as unwritten rules, and should be followed.
[1]. This is number one, because it is the most important... have some fun! If you come into this promotion looking for nothing but fun and excitement from role-playing, as well as good angles and cards, then that is exactly what you will get.
[2]. If you have a match, you *must* role-play to win. It does not matter to this federation if you are the biggest e-wrestling 'name' on the Internet. If you do not flash, then I guarantee you will not win. Simple as that. I do not say this to be mean, but roleplays are the threads that keep a federation together. I can only do so much to keep this place running, flashing is what you guys can do to keep us from closing down.
[3]. There is no specific length your role-play has to be, as long as it is quality. If I were going to guess, I'd say 20-25 lines is a good average. This gives you enough dialogue to get your point across, but not too much to where you repeat yourself over and over... These are not official minimums and maximums, they are just suggested guidelines.
If you have any questions about these guidelines, feel free to contact either committee member [name withheld] [hey, that's me~!] at
[detail withheld]
, or Dan Jacobs at [detail withheld] . Primarily, the NAIWF does not go around suspending everyone. We will definitely be quite lenient, but if someone disobeys to an extreme extent, they will be punished. See, now the rules weren't that bad were they?
--------------------------------------------------------- * Federation Events [a]. The NAIWF's number one weekly show airs every Monday night at 8:00p.m. EST, and is known as "Monday Madness". Hosted by NAIWF announcers Jason Sanders and Nick Martinez, this show may consist of upwards of eight to ten matches, however this is not always the case. Title matches can happen quite regularly on this show, so champions should be aware of this.
[b]. Starting after the April pay-per-view, "Judgement Day," the NAIWF has decided to bring back its second weekly show. "Friday Night Fights" will feature the same quality-type matches you get to see on Madness, and perhaps a little extra before pay-per-views. This show may have a few title matches thrown in at times, but they are not a regular occurance.
[c]. Over the course of it's existance, the NAIWF has been notorious for its pay-per-view events. Dating back to the very first NAIWF supercard, they have always been a place where all loose ends get tied, feuds come to a conclusion, and new feuds are spawned. These are definitely shows you do not want to miss.
[note: the next NAIWF supercard, Judgement Day, is our version of WrestleMania. A two-day event that has *always* delivered spectacular matches and compelling stories. A must-see for any wrestling fan.]
--------------------------------------------------------- * Role-plays and Angles... I mentioned role-plays already in the rules, but let me touch upon them once again. Role-plays should be done entirely in character. They should not be for the most part shoot comments. Personally, I do not mind a shoot role-play every once in a while if it is in good fashion, but let's not get too carried away.
The NAIWF gives an inch, let's not try to take a mile. Due to the federation's open-flash system, you can flash as many times as you wish. Also, we are not forcing anyone to do a role-play, but trust me, if you have a match and do not role-play for it, you will *not* win! If you don't have a match, it would still be in your best interest to do a role-play, just because it surely cannot hurt you. It can only help you. If the federation sees you trying even though you don't have to and we smile and your name just might get a nice push. Everything you get out of the NAIWF revolves around what you put in.
Now, I can honestly say angles are my favorite things in any federation. I am going to say right now, sometimes an angle, if it is good may make you lose a match even if you did have the better role-play. Sometimes that is just the way the cookie crumbles. But hey, it is all in good fun. Now on angles.. if you are a wrestler in this fed and feel you are going nowhere and need a good angle or feud with someone let us know. We will be more than happy to help you. Hell, that is one of the main reasons we are here. We want the wrestlers to succeed right along with this promotion.
--------------------------------------------------------- * Championships... The NAIWF currently recognizes five singles and one tag team championships.. If you lose a title, a rematch will be signed for two to three weeks after the time you lose it. The rematch will be against whomever holds the title, not the person you lost it to, some times in a three-way match, but 90% of the time in a one on one match. Title holders do not have to accept anyone's challenges.
However, it would be in your best interest to accept reasonable ones. As advice, it isn't a good idea to go straight for the Triple Crown or
Intercontinental titles. Start smaller with the Television, then work up to the higher belts. The more you work, the better you get. That is a huge rule here.
* Triple Crown World Title.. The ultimate goal of all wrestlers in the NAIWF. It's become known as the Triple Crown when NAIWF legend Angel Reaper won the World Title, and unified it with his Mexican and Canadian title belts.. This is the top of the mountain, the ultimate bragging point. The Triple Crown champion is required to defend his title against one of the wrestlers in the top five every month on his own.
In addition, President Benfort may also appoint a challenger for the Heavyweight Title. Only a wrestler in the top five may challenge for this title. * Intercontiental Title.. This belt could be considered a stepping stone to the triple crown belt, and the holder of this is considered to be the top contender for the Heavyweight
Title. The Intercontinental champion is required to defend his title twice every six weeks against a top ten wrestler. Also, just like for the heavyweight strap, President Benfort may also appoint the challenger for the title. Any wrestler in the top ten may challenge for this title.
* Television Title.. This belt could also be known as an endurance title. The holder of the Television Title must defend between two to four times a month, at the discretion of President Benfort. This belt is often considered a stepping stone to the Intercontinental Title, as the IC is considered to the Triple Crown. No competitor ranked beneath the top fifteen in the federation may challenge for this belt.
* Light-heavyweight Title.. This title signifies the best of the best for wrestlers under the 225lb weight limit. While the title only has to be defended two to three times a month, the champion usually is booked by the committee to make title defenses just about every week, depending on the number of viable contenders in the division at any given time period.
* Warfare Title.. Once a UFC-style division, under the administration of President Belfort the division has been transformed into more of a hardcore, anything goes type battle field. The champion will be allowed to dictate the stips in *all* defenses, and has total discretion and approval from the president. It is defended once or twice a month, and is open to anyone in the division to challenge for. This title is the most free form and creatively open title in the federation, and all competitors should strive to hold this title.
* World Tag Team Title.. The ultimate goal of tag teams in the NAIWF. The champions are required to defend the tag team titles against one of the federation's top teams every month. In addition, the Championship Committee may also appoint the challenger to the titles for either weekly or monthly cards. In this division, like the
Warfare, any tag team in the federation can challenge for the titles. On a side note, if two title holders go up against each other and the lower ranked title holder wins, then that lower title will be vacated. A tournament will be set up involving the wrestler who lost his belt, and seven others. It will come down to two wrestlers, the winner gets the belt. If, however, the
Television champion fights against another champion, and the Television champion wins, then the loser of the higher belt receives the TV title by default. If it was a 'controversial' win [this will be decided on a case-by-case basis], then a tournament will be set up with the aforementioned guidelines.
--------------------------------------------------------- * Federation Personalities... President, Victor Benfort [ [detail withheld] ]: Very little is known about this shrewd businessman. It is known that he has a considerable amount of his own financial backing for the NAIWF, as well as that of the newly re-appointed Vice
President Dan Jacobs. Since purchasing the federation from the former owner/President John "Direwolf" Pintha on E-bay, President Benfort has displayed a very worker friendly attitude, focusing on fairplay and sportsmanship, but as of late the President's actions have been questionable to say the least.
Vice President, Dan Jacobs [ [detail withheld] ]: A former Television Champion, as well as winner of the 1999 Quest For The Ultimate Fighter Tournament and former Triple Crown World Champion, Dan Jacobs is an NAIWF Veteran with a track record of screw jobs and "moral crimes" longer than most rap sheets in downtown
New York City. He is a dirty player who likes getting his way, and has no qualms about doing what it takes to get it. Possibly, depending on who you ask, considered one of the most hated men in the history of the promotion.
Currently, Vice President Jacobs is aligned with current Triple Crown Champion Vincent Visage in their quest to return the sport to it's glory days when wrestling was "REAL" and not "spoiled by the cheesy gimmicks and angles of today".
Commentator, Jason Sanders: Recognized by some one as one of the best play-by-play men since Gordon Solie, this kid has been dreaming of becoming a professional wrestling since he was in his early teens, and is now the main man behind the microphone for Madness.. He is liked by virtually all wrestlers and wrestling fans. He pulls no punches, and excels in telling it like it is.
Commentator, Nick Martinez: A former prowrestler himself, a serious back injury sidelined him for over a year which led to his decision to retire from the sport. He has been the color man for all NAIWF broadcasts for the past year or so, and isn't ashamed to cheer for the bad guy, or even offer to help out! His mouth can get him in trouble sometimes, and holds the world record for backpedaling..
