A Las Vegas e-fed that cycled through three formats between late 1995 and mid-1996: an open real-wrestler roster, a real-wrestler match simulator that crowned its own four champions, and a fully-booked fantasy relaunch whose first show named the first LVWF Television Champion. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Las Vegas Wrestling Federation opened 29 October 1995 as a real-wrestler e-fed: managers picked five wrestlers apiece, two of them tagged together, and results were simulated and posted as cards. Its first surviving card, posted 25 January 1996 after a self-described delay — “Midterms have kept me busy” — ran twenty-seven matches and crowned an International Champion (Vader), Tag Team Champions (Hakushi and Jushin Liger), and a TV Champion (911) through a run of battle royals and tournaments, the World Championship left vacant.

A follow-up bulletin the following week staged a full World Championship tournament, complete with Jerry “the King” Lawler proclaiming himself “the LVWF's first and only royal Champion” in a pre-match interview before losing his own opening-round match to Terry Funk; the LVWF's own final results named Taz champion, having outlasted Bret Hart seven falls to six in a death match. The card's closing news notes reported the Dangerous Alliance bought out and renamed the Corporation, and the Legion of Doom rebranded as the Dark Forces under Paul Bearer.

The LVWF relaunched again on 15 April 1996, still built on real wrestlers but now run with a co-booker and a role-play requirement, and named LVWF Friday Night Chaos as its weekly card and a PPV cycle roughly every six weeks. That format didn't hold: by 12 August 1996 its own announcement declared the real-wrestler fed closed outright — “it got boring so I closed it” — and reopened it as a fully booked, original-character fantasy federation, its own creation sheet asking for invented names, gimmicks, and finishers rather than real wrestlers, and describing the new venture as its own fed while also associated with the DPW.

That relaunch's first card, LVWF Friday Night Chaos of 16 August 1996 (posted 19 August), opened with “Wing-Man” Tyler James defeating Psycho Gil with a top-rope splash he called the Helli-Kitty, and closed with a ten-way dance for the vacant LVWF Television Championship, refereed under commissioner Johnny Wills (billed the same night as a current DPW announcer) and won by The Stud Muffin, who eliminated The Terminator with a top-rope clothesline he called the decapitator to become the Television Championship's first holder.

No closure notice for the relaunched federation survives; its last dated document in the surviving record is that same 19 August 1996 broadcast.
