[FTB] ________________________________________________________________________ ________________________________________________________________________ Message: 2 Date: Fri, 04 Jan 2002 19:48:38 -0000 From: [detail withheld] < [detail withheld] > Subject: RAW Report 4th Jan RAW Report [Note: DDI vs Neil was written by Lane, Jake wrote all other singles matches except OCD/Damien and Kirk/Mitchell - thanks to both.] DARREN WALKER and ELI STEERPIKE vs IVAN KINSKI & THE AMERICAN PATRIOT This was a match of unusual pairing, as Walker and Steerpike, who'd battled at Aggression before putting aside their differences to team in the battle royal, took on Kinski and Patriot, who didn't appear to have even spoken before this one.
Kinski and AP's lack of communication showed, as Ivan was double teamed by Steerpike and `Total Carnage', with AP unable to make the tag in. Eventually, Kinski hit a desperation double clothesline, and tagged in the former WAR! legend, only for Steerpike to chase him out of the ring. The two of them brawled up the aisle, with Steerpike fending off headbutts from Lulu, leaving us with only two men in the ring. Patriot tried to hold his own against the RAW rookie, but
Walker smelled blood, and quickly dominated the match, hitting a shortarm clothesline then a piledriver to get the two and a half count. Patriot mounted something of a comeback, even trying for a
Patriot missile, but Darren ducked that, dropped a leg, and then hit the Devastator turbuckle powerbomb to get the three count.
Rumour has it that AP's 2nd RAW stint may be over soon, and with displays like that, it's hard to see anyone missing him. Kinski may find a niche, but doesn't look too special at the moment. The other two, however, are different prospects - Steerpike impressed for the third match in two shows, and Walker finally got the big win he's been waiting for since coming into the fed.
FREELOADERS vs MERSEYSIDERS vs DEBONAIR. This was a match which pitted four newcomers in Debonair and the Freeloaders against an old team newly reformed, Lee Roberts and Alan Mangan. The Freeloaders were on the offensive straight away, taking it to Mangan, who you'll remember was the cause of their friend Darren Walker being eliminated in the first round of the tourney at the Internecine pre-show. Debonair, on the other hand, were quite content to sit and wait, with the match reaching the seven minute mark before either of them tagged in, and their only action up to that was a double stomp on David Reaume to break up a pin attempt on
Mangan. However, Bryson looked impressive when he did come in, working over the somewhat ring rusty Lee Roberts with lots of technical holds. Roberts did eventually manage to make a mini comeback, tagging in Alan Mangan again, which convinced Bryson to make the tag out to Lonewolf... a move which quickly backfired, as
Reaume double clotheslined the two Debonair members, buying Lonewolf just enough time to DDT Roberts, hit the Wolfbite, and take advantage of the confusion on the outside as the former KoH threw himself at the recovering Bryson and James to get the three count.
So, BIG win for the Freeloaders, although Debonair displayed themselves well. Merseysiders were understandably on the receiving end of a lot of punishment, but the reason for that was probably the ring rust of Lee Roberts.
WHIRLING DERVISH vs MURDOCH. Whirling Dervish, the 5'10, 211lb light-heavyweight from Grand Forks is one of the few people that can called evil. Not just nasty. Not just horrible. But evil. Cruel and a player of mind-games, he may be low on the lower card at the moment, but he has consistently been an interesting one to watch outside the ring, as has the situation
brewing between himself and Ernest Wall. Inside the ring he would find his stiffest challenge in RAW yet - former Light Heavyweight champion, and current Unified Tag-team champion, the immensely popular Stevie Ray Murdoch.
This match was exactly what a Light Heavyweight match should be about and did the division proud. Not only that, but it was a match between pure evil and pure good which, combined with all the high- spots, had the fans on their feet, cheering SRM's heroics and booing the cheating of Dervish. At one point, Dervish went over the top rope with a slingshot, but was caught by Stevie Rat Murdoch who hotshotted him onto the barrier then nailed Dervish with the Flying
Clusterfrick. Another high point would see Stevie Ray Murdoch would miss a flying legdrop and Dervish capitlaize by doing a leaping double foot stomp onto Murdoch's face.
The great match ended in surpringly quick fashion. Murdoch hit Dervish with a flying crossbodyblock, but Dervish used the momentum to carry it over into a small package and hooked the tights to secure the 1-2-3 victory. A big win for Dervish there, showing that he can - and will - do anything to win.
UNDERWOOD vs McCRAY Simon Underwood, the 5'9, 210lb 22yr old from Los Angeles, a hate figure indeed, was due to meet a man who has known more than most what it is like to be a true hate figure - Doug McCray. The
Corruptor, right now, is in the middle of a popularity renaissance and the line was clear - love McCray, hate Underwood.
Underwood, sleep deprived and all together screwed up in the head, seemed to be competing in a trance-like state against the former
World Champion, really not seeming to be...stable. A wild animal he appeared to be, rather than the young man with intese ability.
It was a messy match, often little more than a brawl, with Underwood thrashing around and proving very difficult for McCray to do anything against. Even when McCray was able to hit a clean move, Underwood would come back up, punching, kicking, biting, clawing, tearing away. All in all, this was a shame for the fans who, on another day, with Underwood in a more stable state, this match could have been a classic. As it was, it was an interesting match with Underwood making a bit of a name for himself against McCray.
In the final moments of the nine-miunte match, Underwood shoulder- tackled McCray then hit him with the Transcendent Exposure. Rather than pinning McCray, Underwood began gnawing on McCray's leg. The ref began to admonish Underwood, but quickly found his head smashed into the canvas. McCray, still out of it from the Transcendent
Exposure, came to quickly when Underwood tugged on McCray's hair - even pulling some out. McCray sprung back into life and battered Underwood with punches and kicks, clotheslining Underwood over the top rope to tremendous cheers. Underwood was up quickly though and began licking hijs arm profusely...then pulled out a sharp metal object - a steel CLAW with sharp sharp points. Underwood proceeded to chase McCray with it half running, half-crawling.
Seeing this, the recovering referee DQ'd Underwood. Distracted by the bell, Underwood was momentarily vulnerable. McCray seized the opportunity and hit him with a Devil's Deal, the fans raising the roof with approval. IF there was a roof. But this was San Marco square, and it had been a very strange match indeed. While McCray was announced as the winner, Underwood got up, cowring, literally like a dog, away from McCray.
DEREK IRVIN vs NEIL MCDOUGALL The crowd was in for a second in an old school/new school encounter as one of RAW's longest running superstars Neil McDougall entered the ring to take on the current capital champion Deadly Derek Irvin. With the title not being on the line both men were out to simply prove their worth. Both men went into a brief feeling out period temporality locking up for brief moments and breaking before either man got a clear advantage.
However once things were set into motion they didn't cease to stop. During a lock up McDougall gained an advantage and after a quick go around took down the capital champ with a full nelson face buster.
McDougall quickly capitalized with a a springboard moonsault off the ropes. Perhaps in a hasty decision McDougall went for the early pin.
Irvin kicked out at 1 1/2 and got to his feet and McDougall laid a few chops into the upstart champion and Irish whipped him into the ropes. Irvin dodged the running closeline attempt and quickly managed to get behind McDougall and take him down with a release German suplex. Irvin kept on the offensive looking to keep the Flying
Scotsman grounded. Irvin stomped on the knees of McDougall and went for a figure four leg lock. McDougall counters with a modified karate sweep and followed with a drop kick to the head of Irvin for good measure. McDougall quickly went back to work and after a series of palm strikes too Irvin to the corner and took him up top for a frankensteiner. McDougall got onto the shoulders of Irvin only to have Irvin quickly turn it into a top rope powerbomb. Irvin went on the offensive and followed through with a vertical suplex. Irvin went for a second vertical suplex only to have McDougall manage to counter the move into a a tornado ddt. McDougall quickly went to the top and nailed a sick 720 splash.
The time keeper announced the the match was at the ten minute mark and McDougall not one to waste time quickly went to work on putting away his opponent. Irvin if nothing else was proving to the crowd he could take whatever the former RAW Power/North American Champion could dish out. McDougall pulled out a Northern Lights Driver and followed with a standing moonsault only to yeild a 2 count. McDougall then went for a piledriver only to have Irvin counter it into a backdrop driver. Irvin quickly went for a dragon sleeper but
McDougall managed to grap the rope and break the hold. Irvin kept going and went for a powerbomb only to have McDougall turn it into a hurricanranna. McDougall went for the St. Andrew's Cross as the crowd went wild. Irvin broke the hold with a quick eye rake and managed to capitalize with a side suplex. The time keeper announced the match was in it's last minute and both men quickly began to pull out all the stops. Irvin went for a boston crab only to have McDougall sweep him with another modified karate take down. Once again McDougall went for the St. Andrew's Cross, but Irvin seeing an opportunity in the hasty McDougall quickly reached up and managed to apply a headlock to the Flying Scottsman and pulls him down and into a cradle for the 3 count, just 2 seconds short of the 15 minute time limit!
A huge win for DDI, but it will surely only be a temporary setback for RAW's longest serving wrestler, as Neil will aim to bounce back in time for his World title match at Aggression.
OMEGA vs BIG GAY BILL If you ever wondered how Omega would react to being dry-humped by a 458lb Canadian male, this would be the match you wanted to see. Omega, locked in conflict with Gigantor & Rick Styles, was presented with an opportunity to prepare for his fight against Gigantor at
Aggression by taking on the large and powerful Big Gay Bill, also known as Juggernaut, also known as William Bennett and god knows what else.
Omega was intensely serious and cold in the ring before the bell rang and as soon as it went, Omega charged into Big Gay Bill and pounded away on him. It set the tone for an incredible and fiery match between two men who could hit and take immense powermoves. The match even went vicious outside the ring in a brawl that went up the aisle- ramp, but when Omega suplexed Big Gay Bill onto the steel, he dragged him back for a pinfall that BGB just escaped. Both men were bleeding and drained by this point of the match and as the clock passed the fifteen minute mark, the money was more and more on Omega to win, having better cardio-vascular conditioning.
In a desperation move, Big Gay Bill managed to hit the Gay-bomb on Omega, but was too exhausted to cover. He did, however, manage to dry-hump Omega which sent Omega into a rage which saw Big Gay Bill take as big a beating as he's received for a long time. He held on for a bit longer though but only to be amazingly nailed with the
EndGame powerbomb into a bostoncrab. The referee was not conscious though, having just moments previously been squashed between Omega and Big Gay Bill.
The match ended in the fourteenth minute with Big Gay Bill reversing a whip to the ropes and backdropping Omega over the top rope. Big Gay Bill fell to the mat, the last bits of his strength in this intense match used up, but Omega suffered the same fate, only
outside the ring, with the consequence being a count-out. Omega showed himself to be a top level competitor today, going toe to toe with a RAW legend. Big things are waiting for him - immediately named Gigantor. A great match for the San Marco crowd in Venice to watch.
KIRK MACLEAN vs ADAM MITCHELL This was a match which had been eagerly awaited, but to be honest, didn't really go as well as you'd have expected from two wrestlers of this caliber.
Maclean dominated, working the legs of Mitchell with stomps, ankle locks, and lots of kneebreakers, while Mitchell rarely got out of second gear, apart from a nice implant DDT on the Hurricane. The end came when Kirk hooked on a figure four to take the submission win.
A routine match for Maclean, even though he's concentrating on tag team action more these days. As for Mitchell, he's struggling at the moment, but his slump almost certainly won't last long.
OCD vs DAMIEN This was held over from earlier in the show, with OCD looking like he wasn't gonna turn up, but when he did, he came prepared to fight!
Damien, on the other hand, showed his lack of experience, and was no match for the RAW vet.
Okkid threw the Suicide King around like a beachball, much to the delight of the RAW fans, finishing after four minutes with a
Persistence of Memory followed by a Descent Into Madness. But that's when the fun REALLY started.
As OCD celebrated, the other two Suicide Kings hit the ring, cutting short the fans jubilation pretty quickly. Spiky attacked him from behind with a baseball bat, sending him straight into a LaGrange
Trail Driver. only for the cavalry to arrive! With Ric Beauty out of town, Kings' enemy Neil McDougall charged out, steel staff in hand, and hit the ring to clear house! Spiky and Travis scattered, but
Neil flew straight after the elder of the Barnett brothers, dropping the staff and hitting a plancha to start a brawl which quickly went over the guardrail, as fans ran from the two cruiserweights.
LaGrange, meanwhile, retreated to what he thought was safety in the back. only to be tapped on the shoulder by a hand which shot out of the curtains, and to turn around and find a grinning Doug McCray, and a DEVIL'S DEAL! DEVIL'S DEAL!
