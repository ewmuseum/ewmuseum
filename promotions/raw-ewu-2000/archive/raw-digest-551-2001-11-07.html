Lineup for 16th November 'POWERSCOUSE' ALAN MANGAN vs ...WALLMAN! While there's no doubting Mangan's ability, he's struggled recently in RAW. Wall, on the other hand, is one of the fed's most impressive newcomers for quite some time, not withstanding his dodgy knees. The fact he's started dressing up in a cape and is trying to tempt away
Tony Quint's dog shouldn't take away from what he can do in the ring - because that's a lot, as Mangan found out in this opener.
Wall's strategy here was obviously to wear down the younger Liverpudlian, as he put on lots of rest holds, trapping Mangan in a bearhug and a front facelock to name just a couple, and managed to escape to the outside for a breather whenever it looked as if his opponent was going to take control. It certainly didn't endear him to the crowd, many of whom chanted 'Boring' in the same way they do at
Doug Simpkins, but just like it does for Dirty Doug, the strategy worked well. Working over Mangan's legs with numerous impact moves, followed by a leg grapevine, Wall eventually put on a figure four for the submission win at the twelve minute mark - although the move seemed to take it out of him almost as much as the Englishman.
Post match, Wall climbed the turnbuckle to celebrate, before leaping off like a luchador. Well, the leap was luchadoric... the landing was less so, as his knees gave way from underneath him and he landed flat on his backside, much to the amusement of the RAW fans.
VALEC CORSO vs STARMAN Starman took home his first TV victory in RAW on the inaugural Aggression, going over Corso with what he proudly proclaims to be RAW's smallest package. The Destroyer of Heroes, though, looked very impressive on his TV debut, and here had a chance to take back the victory.
Technical wrestling might not be the first term you associate with Starman, but he more than proved his worth here, coming up with some great suplexes, although Corso demonstrated some impressive counters, in particular flipping out of a German suplex attempt and then blasting Starman with a vicious karate kick. Corso's martial arts training was evident after the five minute mark, as he peppered Quint with a variety of chops and punches. Quint made a good comeback, with some great high flying moves, including a flying cross chop and a somersault kick, but try as he might couldn't get the small package on, with Corso obviously having scouted the move well. However, it was a small package attempt from Starman which led to the finish, at the 14 minute mark... he went for it, only to have Corso, who was playing possum, reverse the move and go for a suplex - which Starman in turn countered, showing incredible agility to flip over Corso's back, and hit a dragon suplex for the win.
Starman comes out the winner again, then, to continue a great start to his RAW career, but Corso was unlucky once more - and is surely another guy with a great future ahead of him in the fed.
KHAN vs LITTLE LOUIE Khan has been pretty impressive since making the step up from RAW, while Louie is looking just as good since returning to active competition. This match was set to be great, and didn't disappoint.
High flying action all the way, the two competitors looked like they were trying to provide a sneak preview of tonight's main event, as both men pulled out moonsaults, cross bodies, and senton bombs with abandon. Each got more than one count of two and a half, with Louie getting 2 and four fifths after one particularly impressive sequence, in which he set Khan up for a kneebreaker, landed it, then pulled him up again, slammed Khan's knee into a turnbuckle, back suplexed him, and finally jumped to the second rope, coming off with a vicious double stomp onto Khan's leg. Khan, however, just kicked out, and from this point started to take control of the match, hitting a
Russian leg sweep before catapulting Louie headfirst in the turnbuckles. With Louie dazed, the former WC-RAW champion went for his patented Snakecharmer finisher... only for LL to catch the thrust kick, step out of the way, and dropkick Khan in the opposite knee.
Still holding the leg, Louie pulled himself up, and applied an Indian death lock for the submission.
Louie looking very impressive, but again, a match in which the loser shouldn't be looked down upon - Khan gave it his all, and on another night, might well have walked out with the victory.
LUKE LUCENT vs TONNAGE This was expected to be a complete mismatch - Tonnage took the big win at Aggression to earn a World title shot at Internecine, while Lucent was wrestling his last match for the company after handing in his resignation due to differences with the management. Many of the smarts in the crowd knew this, and when they heard Euphoria's entrance music play, a chant of 'Raph-io!' broke out.
Sadly for those smarts, the Lord did not appear, although it's doubtful whether he could've done a more complete demolition job on
Lucent than Tonnage did, anyway. The big man was totally in control, hitting Luke right from the start with a devastating clothesline, then a huge legdrop, and actually gorilla pressing him from near the centre of the ring to the floor at one point. Better men than Lucent have fallen to the Beast before, but this was one of his quicker victories, coming after just a couple of minutes via the Gigaton crush.
So, Tonnage, with an easy win, carries on rolling, rolling, rolling, towards Travis LaGrange at Internecine. Lucent will start afresh in pastures new, presumably... maybe somewhere where the competition is a little more to his level.
MARCUS WILSON vs KIRK MacLEAN (Written by [name withheld]) Marcus Wilson made a promising start to his RAW career and many were predicting him for big things, but his initial momentum has worn off.
The match against Kirk Maclean for the Capital Championship presented the popular martial artist with a great chance to get going again.
Kirk Maclean meanwhile has continued to be an exemplary Capital Champion, a true champion for the people. Wilson got his share of pops, but the crowd really came to life for Kirk Maclean.
The height, weight and power advantage was with Wilson, but Maclean's speed and skill was a good counter. Overall this was a good match displaying why people tipped Wilson for big things, and also displaying why Maclean has been a great Capital champion. There was good sportsmanship through, and the match never got dirty. There was good trading of locks and holds and submission moves, as well as high flying moves - hit and missed!
Over the eight minutes of this encounter, however, Kirk Maclean was significantly the more in form wrestler. The match ended with Maclean moving out of the way of Wilson's 'Final Blow' then flooring him with a bulldog and then the 'Eye of the Storm' top rope somersault for the
1-2-3. The crowd went ballistic. They love Kirk Maclean and will be rooting for him all the way against Mike Masters. Marcus Wilson on the other hand needs to get into form and make the most of his potential.
ADAM MITCHELL vs STEVIE RAY MURDOCH Adam Mitchell made a very impressive debut on the last RAW Report, taking apart the 'Weapon of Choice' Marcus Wilson in next to no time.
Murdoch, however, is a level above Wilson any day of the week - especially when SRM is as angry as he was today, due to the attack on his manager Lil' Johnny by Mike Masters at Aggression. Johnny was at ringside, in full body cast, accompanied by two busty nurses, giving the fans - and LJ - something to look at which was nearly as good as the match.
Nearly, maybe, but not quite. This was an incredible display of athleticism and dirty tactics, with Mitchell busting out a 450 splash, an Arabian Facebuster, and a full nelson suplex, all of which got over
2 and a half, but Murdoch matching him every step of the way, with SRM's usual spots, including a Kickstart My Heart and a Nameless Leg Lariat, in addition to a host of biting and choking from the less than happy half-Mulleted One. Eventually, however, all great things must come to an end, and this match was no exception. On the quarter of an hour mark, Mitchell went for his Marksman finisher, only for Murdoch to counter with a kick to the groin, and then a tornado DDT, before finishing the match with the Full Frontal Lobotomy.
A good win for Stevie Ray, who's been through a bit of a rough patch recently - but Mitchell's display was impressive, and losing to
Murdoch is no disgrace, particularly since SRM was only named instead of AsH as Adam's opponent five days ago.
SPIKYJIM vs [name withheld] (Written by [name withheld]) If things had been different, it could have been [name withheld], The Flying Scotsman, defending the Light Heavweight title. Or the North American Title. Or even the World title. Time and time again, McDougall, who may be RAW's best wrestler pound for pound, could have been champion, despite his relatively small size. Each time recently though, he has failed. The chance was his yet again though to seize glory in the shape of the now very prestgious Light HeavyWeight title that SPIKYJIM, the current champion, is so keen to get rid of.
Needless to say, the fans were behind McDougall, but were all out of their seats for the champion, Spikyjim, just recovered from his injuries suffered at the hands of the disturbed Despair.
Ring rust showed in Spikyjim's performance during the early part of the match, with McDougall clearly in control of the proceedings and almost getting a few near falls with a roll-up, a back slide and a flying headscissors into a cradle. Spikyjim, although wanting to lose the title, was provoked by McDougall's attack to fight back, and fight back he did into an exciting exhausting back and forth encounter that had the crowd on the edge of their seats.
This was truly a match that showed why the Light Heavyweight Division was created, and nobody could imagine it going onto the twenty minute mark, but it did. The end of the match however was sudden. Having been in control, McDougall went to the top rope. Spikyjim managed to get up though and desperately leapt, shaking the ropes. McDougall crotched himself on the top turnbuckle, Spikyjim pulled him off and hit The Living End on the Scotsman for the 1-2-3. A great match adding yet more prestige to a glorious title that Spikyjim just does not want. [name withheld] on the other hand, just didn't seem to want it enough. Another title chance presents itself to him on Aggression, while Spikyjim gets his chance in a tag-match to take it to Despair and the world champion, Travis Lagrange.
