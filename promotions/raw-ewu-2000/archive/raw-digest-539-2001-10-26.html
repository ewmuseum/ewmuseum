Raw Report Lineup From: [name withheld] < [detail withheld] > ________________________________________________________________________ ________________________________________________________________________ Message: 1 Date: Thu, 25 Oct 2001 17:21:53 -0000 From: [detail withheld] Subject: RAW Report 25th October RAW Report 25th October From Spokane Arena (Spokane, Washington) [Quick note: Thanks to Jake, Gene, and Frank for helping with this.] Khan vs Ric Beauty This was an incredibly hot opener, with Khan showcasing the skills which made him one of Windy City RAW's top wrestlers, and Beauty matching him almost all the way. The fans were definitely behind the 'Twenty Four Hour Man', less than impressed by the posturing of 'The Franchise', but as Khan would no doubt point out, anyone who holds victories over the likes of Despair is probably entitled to boast. The presence of twins Vicki and Val, though, meant that the crowd would've probably cheered Beauty against just about anyone in the fed, and one older lady who he presented his trademark red rose to was definitely taken by the young man.
Khan started off the better of the two, immediately downing Beauty with two lightning quick dropkicks, but Ric was up almost straight away, and put Khan down with a Russian leg sweep. The match continued in a similar vein for more than quarter of an hour, with both guys showcasing some great moves - Khan put Beauty down with a front Russian legsweep, only for Beauty to come back with a running bulldog when he got up. Beauty flew from the top rope to take Khan's knees out, but Khan then did his own high flying, taking out Ric with a huge flying lariat. Khan dropped Ric with a back breaker for 2 and three quarters, just a couple of minutes later, Beauty got an ankle lock cinched on, and Khan only just made it to the ropes.
Later still, Khan had Beauty down with a backbreaker, but fell victim to an eye gouge as he tried to scoop him up. The fans seemed to be wishing it would never end, but end it had to... and it did as, with both men exhausted, and Beauty on the ropes, Khan managed to summon up enough energy to knock him to the outside with his patented 'Snakecharmer' thrust kick before sinking to the mat, too tired to get up, but picking up the countout win.
Alan Mangan vs Gigantor With Mangan already in the ring for the start of this one, Gigantor stomped down the aisle, to the terror of some of the younger members of the audience, who were visibly intimidated as he glared at them when he walked past. Throwing his chains down to the ground, he slid into the ring, and attacked the Liverpudlian from the getgo.
Showing his strength, Gigantor threw his opponent to the turnbuckles, then attacked him with a big boot, leaving his foot against Mangan's throat to choke him. Although the ref counted, the big man seemed to be intent on ignoring this, and looked prepared to take the DQ loss to inflict more damage, until Rick Styles instructed him to break the hold. Gigantor then threw Mangan
to the opposite corner, and pummeled him with roundhouse rights, repeating these attacks for a couple of minutes.
Eventually, though, Mangan managed to make a comeback. Booting Gigantor in the head as he came running in, Alan then charged out with a clothesline, staggering the big man. Unfortunately, a couple of blows to Gigantor weren't enough to knock him down - and with
Mangan in the middle of the ring, Gigantor locked on the fearsome Iron Claw! Quickly, the ref stopped the match, awarding it to Gigantor, but the big man refused to break the hold, instead roaring at the crowd and the referee. With the ref pleading with Styles to call Gigantor off, eventually, the 'Top Dog' nonchalantly climbed into the ring, and motioned for the huge man to release the claw - which he immediately did. Returning to the dressing rooms to a chorus of boos from the fans, Gigantor came close to attacking one or two young men in the front row, with Styles having to keep between him and the crowd at more than one point.
Marcus Wilson vs Adam Mitchell The fans here were firmly behind 'The Weapon Of Choice' Marcus Wilson, booing former SEWF World Champion Mitchell right away, but that didn't faze the cocky 28 year old, who's been used to it for much of his career.
Attacking Wilson right away with a superkick, then putting him down with an inverted DDT, Mitchell quickly made it obvious that he was in control of the match. Wilson hit back with a few suplexes, but then went for a spinning heel kick, only for Mitchell to manage an impressive reversal, catching his leg and hitting a head and leg suplex throw.
From there on, it was fairly academic. Mitchell hit a faceplant, then a sit out powerbomb, and pulled Wilson to his feet, before taking him straight back down with the Marksman. Making the cover, he chalked up a debut win in RAW in little over 3 minutes.
Ernest 'Brick' Wall vs Starman (Written by [name withheld]) The next match was a classic battle between experience and, well, bizarreness as crafty veteran ERNEST "THE BRICK" WALL took on the laser-brained STARMAN. The crowd entertained themselves early on with a "We want Stardog" chant which seemed to confuse the quaint Mr.
Quint, who soon found himself on the receiving end of some old school offense, as Wall's gameplan focused on Starman's lower back. Using his forty pound weight advantage and forty years experience advantage
(I kid, I kid. I think.) to maximum effect, Wall pounded away at the high-flyer's back, reasonably reasoning that his top rope moves would be somewhat limited by a sore back.
Belly to back suplexes, nice, crisp backbreakers -- even an honest-to- God bearhug. Wall did his best to set the sport back twenty-five years. But you can't keep a good space cadet down, and Starman battled back, sunset flipping out of an attempted powerbomb, following up with a slingshot legdrop, and connecting with an impressive powerdriver elbow from the top rope.
The ten minute mark found both men still going strong, neither able to gain much of an advantage, but there was a cheer from the crowd as video footage of Stardog - watching from some unknown location - appeared on the big screen. Strangely, though, Wall, seemed to be pleased by this, giving Stardog a thumbs up after one particularly well hit sitdown powerbomb. Ring Announcer Richard Anderson -- hey,
I just figured something out. His initials are -- oh, never mind -- let the crowd know that fifteen minutes had expired, pretty much giving away the ending right there.
Starman mounted a nice comeback at the eighteen minute mark, becoming a veritable house o' fire, peppering the Canadian veteran with a series of punches, staggering him enough to take to the top rope.
But the masked idiot missed a moonsault, as Wall rolled out of the way. Wall was winded -- nearly twenty minutes had taken their toll.
He slowly got to his feet, dragging Starman up for a tombstone piledriver -- but his bum knee gave out, sending Starman crashing down on top of him. Darius Smooth counted to one -- and the bell rang.
The twenty minute time limit had expired, rendering this match a draw and leaving the Washington fans hopeful that the rematch would happen the next time RAW was in town. As the two men left, Wall looked at
Stardog on the big screen, with a half pleased, half sheepish, expression - as if to say 'I nearly had him.'
Doug McCray vs 'Gelid' Garland Frost (Written by [name withheld]) "The Corruptor" himself, Douglas McCray, returned to television action against Garland Frost, the man who with the help of current World Champion Travis LaGrange, put him out of action a little over a month ago.
Frost was obviously still suffering from the massive amount of weight he had to lose to participate in the
Round-robin Light Heavyweight Tournament at The Big Card. The "Gelid One" dropped the weight, but was too weak to actually wrestle at The Big Card, but was forced into action here and was clearly not ready for active competition, something that McCray made him pay dearly for.
McCray took control early in the match with a series of chain- wrestling manuvers that had Frost ducking out of the ring for cover. The Corruptor followed after and the true carnage began. A few guardrail whips, a chairshot and some brawling in the crowd bloodied Frost and appeared to have futher sapped his already low energy level.
Frost managed to battle back at the midway point of the match with a few takedown manuvers into submission holds, but it seemed that even offense took something out of the
Canadian mat technician. McCray took advantage after a reversal of a russian leg sweep and applied the
Devil's Deal to Frost. Instead of a cover, McCray fed by the rage of gaining revenge on the man who helped put him out of action, went for another Deal. Then another. The cover was made, but it was clearly academic as McCray got his second straight win over a quality opponent.
After the match, McCray continued the assault as paramedics and officials rushed in and held off the Corruptor.
Frost was carted off on a stretcher and his condition is unknown. [name withheld] vs Stevie Ray Murdoch The fans were excited by the prospect of this right from the start, and who can blame them? Two of RAW's premier high flyers locked up, and as you'd expect, the action was fast and furious. Although possibly not as furious as the young lady who Lil' Johnny chatted up before the match, resulting in a slapped face for Murdoch's erstwhile accomplice - maybe he's getting over excited with the return of Leah
Maynard to RAW. Seriously, though, this was an incredible display of aerial ability, with more high spots than you can shake a stick at. McDougall's best moves included a top rope hurricanrana which got 2 and three quarters, an incredible sunset flip powerbomb which put SRM through a table on the outside, and nearly resulted in a double countout - referee Darius Smooth's count seemed on the slow side, possibly because he was afraid the fans might riot if he let a match like this end with that sort of result, and also the St Andrew's Cross, which
Murdoch broke somewhat bizarrely, locking his teeth onto the ropes for Smooth to tell McDougall to let go.
But that list shouldn't deceive you into thinking the Flying Scotsman had it all his own way. Murdoch, much to the delight of the Loony
Bin - many of whom appeared with half shaved heads in order to show solidarity with their leader - took it to Neil at every opportunity, with some of his highlights being a tornado DDT which almost drilled
Scotty's head through the canvas, a 360 splash which got 2 and four fifths, and the requisite Flying Clusterfrick to the outside, another move which nearly resulted in neither man being able to beat the 10 count.
Murdoch's high flying, however, seemed slightly less polished than normal - just how aerodynamic IS half a mullet?!? - and it was this which led to the end of the match. Stevie Ray missed a Full Frontal
Lobotomy, and Neil took his chance to pull Murdoch to his feet, and plant Ess Arr Emm with a Mongrel. Post match, the fans cheered as the two men shook hands, and left together - although Murdoch almost immediately had to turn back and rescue RAW Carolina's newest interviewer, Shona Maynard, who'd been watching the match from the front row, from the amorous advances of Ell Jay.
At the same time, Thomas Creighton came out, sarcastically applauding McDougall. The two glared at each other as they passed, but a posse of security were quickly sent out to ensure that they refrained from actually coming to blows.
MAIN EVENT NA Title Match: Ronny Ramirez vs Tommy Creighton (Written by [name withheld]) When it comes to popularity, Thomas Creighton and Ronny Ramirez will never win any awards. But they've both won the North American championship at some point. Ramirez is the currenty holder, and considers himself the greatest North American champion. A bold claim. Creighton was a distinguished North American champion himself and was prepared to do whatever it took to win this match.
Creighton got in the first blow. But it wasn't a physcial one. He dug into his toolbox and revealed a Ronny Ramirez action figure. 'You see there? That's a toy North American belt with your toy. Just so you have something to remember it with..."
Ramirez didn't like that one bit and charged at Creighton, falling into Creighton's trap of an early brawl, in which Creighton, brawler par excellence, really slugged the life out of Ramirez. In the ring, out of the ring, Ramriez was getting battered from pillar to post to apron to barrier. It wasn't looking good for the champion who was smacked head first into Creighton's tool box, busting himself open on the sharp edge.
A sneaky low-blow from Ramirez however managed to keep him in the game, and soon it was Ramirez who was causing trouble for Creighton with some serious impact moves, and almost got the victory with a double arm neckbreaker.
Plenty of near falls from both men ensued, and plenty of blood too. Creighton showed his trademark vicious streak, at one point clotheslining Ramirez from behind outside the ring with his toolbox.
Neither man would go down, and although the crowd weren't in favour of any man, they were getting pulled in by the action.
The match ended up going near to the thirty minute mark, an epic struggle between two men who refused to be defeated. Both men were barely able to stand. The referee was also only just back into the match. Creighton had pulled the referee in front of him when Ramirez was about to hit the Iconic Gesture forearm. He hit it alright, but on the referee.
In the twenty ninth minute, both men bloodied and straining for breath, muscles in agony, joints in pain, Thomas Creighton hit
Ramirez with the double-arm DDT. He made the cover, but a slow count from a disorientated referee saw him only get the two count.
Creighton was frustrated. Ramirez was all but dead. But then he signalled for the Dirty Deeds! The fans popped for the knees-first frog splash. As he turned round though, he walked straight into a
Dreamland from Jason Midnight who had stormed the ring! Midnight's attack was clearly provoked by Creighton's interference in his match with Doug Simpkins. The referee amazingly saw none of this, attending to Ramirez who still seemed in a disorientated state. But he wasn't disorientated enoguh to not notice Creighton out cold. He pulled up one last ounce of energy and leapt...DIRTY DEEDS ON CREIGHTON!
Ramirez covered, and got the three, just before the time limit expired. An incredible match between two top competitors.
Post match, Midnight leapt back into the ring, knocking Creighton to the outside with a series of martial arts kicks. Following him, he hit his 'Magic Trick' to put Tommy C through a table, before showboating for the crowd. So, Midnight ends the RAW Report on a high note... but will Jay Mid be regretting it when he and
Ramirez have to go head to head with Creighton and Doug Simpkins at this week's RAW Aggression? Only one way to find out... tune in!
