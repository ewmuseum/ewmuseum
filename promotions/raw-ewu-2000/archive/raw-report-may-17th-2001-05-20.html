Raw Report May 17th

********************************* RAW WRESTLING FEDERATION presents Raw Report Fargodome, Fargo, North Dakota, USA May 17th, 2001 ********************************** The crowd in Fargo has gone a long while since they have seen a major wrestling show, and they were out in full force tonight, with many signs, and banners, and painted on chests. The wonderful musical stylings of RAW's new attempt at crowd interaction, D.J. Dom, kept the crowd, well, confused in a happy way, and even the attempts at comedy by erstwhile referee Shirley
Shock were greeted with less boos than usual.
*************************** THE MOCK Vs. ISAAC TOGO *************************** The night started off with this little match. The Mock has shown signs of rising beyond Showcaserdom, but seems to have fallen off the training wagon once again. Isaac Togo, on the other hand, has been in hot water with RAW management, because he continually skips interview sessions. Mocky had a definate edge in crowd support in this match.
For about five minutes, this match was back and forth, with nearly nothing doing much damage to either of the wrestlers. However, Togo then made a costly mistake. He whipped the Mock into the ropes, and on the return trip, the Mock hit Togo with a quick forearm to the chin, that looked as if it was stolen right out of Ronnie Ramierez' playbook. Mocky then motioned to the crowd, pulled Togo up to his feet, and hit what he would later call "The
Mildly Annoying Imp's Transaction," risking retribution from the World
Champion. Togo was covered, and three slaps to the mat later, the Mock had once again won a match.
This gives the Mock some momentum going into Raw At War, where he faces
American Patriot, and hopefully will serve as a final wake-up call to Togo.
Winner: The Mock, "Mildly Annoying Imp's Transaction" 7:06, * **************************** WOLFGANG Vs. GLITTERIS **************************** 'I want it all' started up over the PA, and the fans went popped like a bowl of very loud rice-crispies. The 7'1, 319lb super-popular former World War!
Champion, Wolfgang was making his in-ring debut for RAW, and the fans, like he, were loving every minute of it. His opponent for this encounter was the high flying, fast moving Glitteris, one half of the tag-team champions.
Glitteris was clearly at a height, weight and power disadvantage, but this didn't scare him too much, keeping in with a shout and nearly a victory in this match with a variety of hot moves like a Top Con Hilo, a missile dropkick and an asai moonsault press...not to mention some sneaky throat chops and eye-rakes to keep him in the game.
However, the conditioning of Wolfgang was awesome, and he looked like he could carry on all night. He looked like he was enjoying it, and although
Glitteris posed a problem, he was never going to overcome an irresistable force as Wolfgang, who displayed so many moves and made clear he had a triple threat - on the mat, or standing or in the air. Glitteris was hit with tilt-a-whirl drivers, chokeslams, suicide dives over the top rope, uninage suplexes, t-bones suplexes, boots to the head and more.
If anyone has looked like the complete package in RAW, Wolfgang is it. No one could watch and not be impressed. The ending came with his Wolfdriver - a northern lights bomb and the 1-2-3. Glitteris will not be the toughest opponent Wolfgang faces, but he certainly possesses all it takes to make it to the top. He has the pedigree, he has a history of success...can he make it happen again in RAW?
Winner: Wolfgang Time: 7:32 By: Wolfdriver ** ************************************************ SUCCUBUS Vs. 'HARDCORE HERO' TOMMY PAYNE ************************************************* Andrazoon Succubus took to the ring, and was keen not to suffer the same result that Glitteris had earlier in the night. The fans got onto his back straight away, but it didn't bother him. There was a mixed pop for Tommy
Payne, a rookie who has yet to make a loud mark in RAW.
What an interesting match this turned out to be as both men used their vastly contrasting styles to create a match of intrigue, mystery and many near falls. While Succubus impressed with fast paced aerial moves, Payne often found chance to impose himself physically on Succubus. As it turned out though, Succubus was much more effective in his deployment of offense, and also his cunning trickery, skullduggery and general nasty boo-generation cheating shenanigans.
But it was Succubus's strengths that ended up being his weakeness as Tommy
Payne raised his knees for a swanton bomb from Succubus, stunning one half of the tag-team champions. If the air wasn't knocked from his sails, it was surely after Payne hit Succubus with his finisher, 'Bring the Pain', an elevated spinebuster. As Succubus tried to remember what oxygen was, Payne covered and got the three count. Payne now needs to build on this victory and make a charge for the Capital or Power championships, and begin to make a definite mark in RAW.
Winner: Tommy Payne Time: 5:28 By: 'Bring The Pain' *** ***************************************** TOMMY LIONHEART Vs. JASON MIDNIGHT ***************************************** Two fan favourites were on the scene for this match, two men who have formed part of the RAW Power division over the past few months. Could either of them break out of it though? A win here would certainly set them on their way. Tommy Lionheart came out to a "Your country's deputy prime minister punches people!" pop, with the fans still aware of his match against Despair a few weeks back, but obviously not too impressed with the English bloke's poor recent showing. His opponent, Mr Jason Midnight, also emerged to a polite pop, fans obviously wondering if he had stopped focussing enough to
Williams to take control of the match. The match itself wasn't exactly amazing. Midnight from the start was with the big kicks, a few roundhouse kicks topped off with a savate kick netted him an early two count.
Lionheart attempted to gain back on track by taking to the ropes, but a low blow from Midnight topped off with a DDT soon paid pay to Lionheart's
mid-match air-based assault. Midnight then continued to pound the ass of his opponent with a nice urange, and a cheeky little vertical suplex. However, it was the ending of the match that provided the real talking point for those of us who enjoy talking about points. Midnight grabbed Lionheart before shouting "This one is for you
Tonnage!!!!", before DreamLanding Lionheart into, um, dreamland. The 1-2-3 was a formality from then on.
Why the shout out to Tonnage? Or was Midnight saying "This one is for your tonnage", in case any ships wanted filling up with Lionheart's prone body.
Only time will tell. Tag team action continues to pick up unabated in RAW, and this match was no exception. Bedford and Roberts entered first, picking up a decent amount of heel heat, with their manager "Deadly" Doug Simpkins being singled out for particular venom by the fans. S&M then made their way out, and their reaction easily matched that of other RAW face teams such as The Alphas and
Scum of the Earth.
The match was a hard hitting, heavy hitting match, in which the hits were hard and heavy. From the off, Bedford and Roberts double teamed Spyke with all manner of classic Old School moves, double lariats and big axe smashes both earning early two counts. When Spyke managed to turn things around, it was on Bedford, with a big German suplex nailing two, and a brainbuster ending up with a similar number.
Tide soon turned though, as a Roberts tag in led to a pier four brawl, during which Simpkins managed to take out Spyke with a chair, and the double teaming was back on the agenda.
A double vertical suplex was one of the highlights of the match, and even the night. Cameras flashed all over the arena as it was executed. However, a two count was still all we got. Bedford and Roberts then set to brutalising
Spyke viciously, and when Simpkins decided to put the boots in as well, referee Chad had no option but to call a for a disqualification. The beating continued for a few minutes, and when Karl Mercury managed to make his way back to the ring, he too found himself manhandled and beaten down. S&M took the actual victory, but Bedford and Roberts took the actual one.

Blake, replacing Keenan versus Muldoon, both of whom no-showed the
Fargodome. The fans hadn't been expecting this on the card so there was relatively little pop for either of these low-profile competitors, but by the end of the match, Blake had the boos coming in for him. Whyno stagged to the ring, moaning and complaining about everything and anything, which amused the fans. Then Blake appeared, a dark-skinner man, with a scowl, intensity and a big sack with something heavy inside.
Before Whyno reached the ring, Blake had rushed up the aisle and began hammering Whyno with lefts, rights, ax-handles, kicks, punches, stomps. The attack was sharp and brutal, Blake displaying all the qualities of a top-notch, natural brawler. The referee tried to intervene to get the match in the ring, but Blake simply pushed him out of the way, before throwing
Whyno into the ring and whipped him from post to post, then nailed him with a cracking clothesline that spun Whyno 360. Mercilessly he punched away at
Whyno, finally eliciting boos from the fans.
Then, after about two minutes, Blake hit his first proper move of the night - a modified snap sideleg trip takedown called The Snakebite. An impressive looking move, the Snakebite ended the match, putting Whyno out of service, and enabling Blake to get the 1-2-3. But it didn't end their, because Blake reached into his sack and pulled out a huge black snake and threw it onto Whyno, who, despite being out of it, suddenly screamed and ran out of the ring, and Blake was left victorious in the ring, a chorus of boos accompanying him.
"Blacksnake" William Blake Snakebite, 6:47 ** ******************************** ARRON IRON Vs. JERRY GORDON ******************************** The crowd reacted with a rain of boos when Jerry Gordon entered the Dome, mostly because of his association with Larry Spikowski and William Bennet.
However, the crowd was confused when the Jamacian Sensation Arron Iron entered, wearing a WAR! t-shirt. Gordon looked quizzically at Iron, who just smiled.
The match was back and forth for a while, until Gordon slipped through the ropes after an Irish whip, and the match spilled out onto the floor. Iron took control, sending Gordon into the safety barrier. Then, with the referee's count climbing, he set Gordon up on top of the timekeeper's table, and hit an Iron Hammer, sending Gordon straight through to the ground. Iron, then gave the crowd a salute, and left the arena, to the continued confusion of the crowd, and the sound of the referee hitting 10. Gordon had to be carried off on a streatcher, his status is unknown at this time.
Double Count Out 5:14 * *********************** WAR MACHINES VS THE BINKIES *********************** "War Machine" by Kiss boomed around the arena and the fans jeered. "Awesome" Dan Anderson and "BETTER THAN YOU" Matty dee walked down the aisle with supreme confidence. They stared at the fans as "Don't Send In the Clowns"
played. Murder King Joe and Knife-Happy Roy strolled to the ring to a mixed pop from the crowd. All four men stared each other down as referee Darius
Smooth made sure that Joe and Anderson started this one.
The bell rang and it was all Dan Anderson. He started to wear down Murder
King Joe with vicious chops and repeated headbutts. Joe came back with a kick to the midsection and a swinging neckbreaker. Then Knife Happy Roy came in to help. Matty Dee was not happy about this and Darius lost control as a complete brawl between them took place for a matter of minutes. Matty Dee clotheslined Roy to the outside and along with Anderson started to brutally attack Joe. They nailed him with a double brainbuster but somehow Joe kicked out at two and three quarters. The War machines then hurt Joe with "The War
Machine Split" a double wishbone split.
This angered Roy. He came flying in with a chair and waffled everyone in the vacinity including his tag partner Joe. Roy had a smile on his face that was quickly stopped by the unrushing flying forearm from Dan Anderson. Anderson took Roy to the cleaners with a massive spinebuster bomb as well as a sidewalk slam. Dan then took Roy out of the ring and began smashing his head repeatedly on the steel steps. Meanwhile Matty Dee came to his senses and hit a spinning piledriver on Joe for a two count. He followed that up with a tiger driver and double underhook backbreaker. On the outside Roy was being destroyed and was even given a powerslam on the ramp.
Both Binkies inside and outside of the ring were virtually dead. Matty picked up Joe and gave him an amazing cradle DDT. He then called for Dan to get in the ring. Dan of course excepted. Dan picked up Joe and placed him on his shoulders. Matty gave him a chair and he set up to cover the face of
Joe. Matty then climbed to the top rope as Darius Smooth started lecturing
Dan. Matty Dee flew off and hit a leaping sidekick right into the chair that smashed in Joe's face. He crashed to the ground and Matty covered for the three count.
War Machhines Chair and foot to face (4:09) *1/2 ******************************* MAIN EVENT NORTH AMERICAN TITLE MATCH 'THE FLYING SCOTSMAN' NEIL McDOUGAL(C) Vs. AKUMA ******************************* This was always set to be a fast paced match, with two of RAW's premier high flyers taking it to each other in a battle for McDougall's NA belt. The fans were buzzing for it, although they suffered a little from not knowing who to cheer for, the challenger and his ever popular manager Harvey Skipper gaining a respectable pop, but the champion had just slightly more appeal to the fans, especially the males, with
RAW junior interviewer Tanya Maynard accompanying him to the ring, furthering speculation about a blossoming romance.
The two men started with a sequence of flips which at one point looked dangerously like giving the fans motion sickness from the speed their heads had to move to follow them, but quickly moved into more high impact manoeuvres, and we saw a whole lot of them from both guys. In fact, both busted out many of their signature moves, with McDougall hitting the Mad Dog ľ facelock bulldog, the Highland Fling Twisting Top Rope
Somersault Legdrop, and an incredible Asai moonsault from the apron to a table, and Akuma coming right back with a Phoenix 450 Splash, a Stardust Press twisting
450 splash, and a Rolling Thunder forward roll into standing splash. Akuma also somehow managed to counter the St Andrew's Cross, shooting out from under
McDougall's legs as he tried to lock it on, leaping up onto the second rope, and coming off with a bulldog.
However, there could only be one winner, even in a match this action packed, and for a long time, it looked like the loser would be the first to miss a big spot. This proved true on the 33 minute mark, as
Akuma climbed the top rope, and leapt off with a frog splash, getting incredible altitude. but landing on
McDougall's suddenly brought-up knees. The Scotsman then stumbled to his feet, pulled up his opponent, and hit him with the Mongrel for the three count. Post match, he offered his hand to Akuma, in recognition of a great match, and the crowd applauded as the Japanese high flyer accepted it.]
