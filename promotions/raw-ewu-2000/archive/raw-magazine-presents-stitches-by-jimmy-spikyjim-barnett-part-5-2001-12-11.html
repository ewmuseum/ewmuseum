This Instalment: PART FIVE - "BIG TIME CHARLIE" ============= So there I am, crashed out on the couch and missing rehab, not a penny to my name. My kid sleeping in a cot that blocks my view of the TV during my sleepless nights, and my wife sharing a sofa with me. My parents asleep in the next room, trying not to look disgusted with what their son has become. I left for Japan with no scars, no tattoos and a clean bill of health. I arrived back with blue hair, more scars than brain cells and so many tattoos that I looked like I'd been on a tour of duty in Vietnam. I was a mess. Not the bright student anymore. Not the great prospect. Not the doctor of psychology that my Mom wanted me to be. Not the professional football player that my
Dad wanted me to be. Just a washed up, deranged nut with no desire to do anything or help anybody.
So they should have been the last people to get me back into wrestling. AT least they kept the booze away from me - a minor miracle considering my state of mind - and kept a roof over my head.
Maybe they were looking after their grandchild more, but who cares - they had cable. Keiko found a job in a restaurant, and then at a school. She now works by teaching people Japanese from home. I sat in that sofa for days on end, and it took a sharp cuff around the back of my head to wake me from my eyes wide open slumber.
"Get up, ya piece of crap". Hello, brother. Damien was at college, and had returned to see me. I turned away, ashamed of what I'd become. He put a tape in the video recorder, as Mom and Dad joined him in the room. He hit play, and it was a tape of one of my last matches in the USA. I was drunk, and it showed... but my family didn't seem to want to lecture me. The tape was filmed from a handheld camera, and it panned across the crowd as I pummelled my opponent. Who did I see? My much younger brother and slightly younger parents, totally going nuts for me whilst the rest of the crowd hurled abuse. They whistled, whooped and jumped for joy. I can't even remember them being there. I looked at Damien.
"What's your point?" He laughed. Collectively, they said to me... "You're going to wrestle again". At the time, I was touched. Almost to the point of tears. But at the end of the day, they were right. Sure, I was a little f*cked in the head... but all my other injuries had healed by now. I had even worked out a little. My knee ached after strenuous exercise, but it had never been right. Physically, I could wrestle again.
Psychologically, my brother pointed out my new advantages. "So... you feel less pain, you fly into rages, you have a steel plate in your head, you've got blue hair and you're a frightening daredevil?"
I nodded. I shrugged my shoulders, too. "So? In Japan, that would have been cool. Here... well, I'm not from
Charlotte or Venice Beach, of you see what I mean."
Damien laughed. Worryingly, my parents laughed too. My dad spoke up first. "Son, you really haven't been home for a long time, have you?" He grabbed another tape and hit the button... and my lord, there was carnage everywhere. This was just an average telecast... but my sweet
Jesus, I couldn't believe it. The stuff I used to do, the stuff that was considered terrible and out of place in a wrestling ring five years previous... was now the norm. Chairs were swung like they were going out of fashion. And the musclemen were gone too, leaving the best men around as guys who could cut it in the ring, not steroid ripped gargoyles with no inring skill who quickly get found out when they step into the Japanese ring. Hell, I was starting to think I had a chance when Damien stepped into my view with something under his arm.
I looked at him, he looked at me. He smiled, and handed me what he was holding. A pair of boots. Size 11, black shiny leather with
"Spikyjim" written on each in blue lettering, and blue flames creeping up from the toes. They were great then, and they are great now. I still wear them and if a hungry bear was to run off in the forest with one of those bad boys in his mouth, I'd gladly kick his ass around his cave until he gave it back to me. My Mom gave me a knee brace, and we were the picture of the unusual family. It was only interrupted by a brief argument between my parents and Damien, when he chose that moment to say that he wanted to become a wrestler too. But that aside, it was them that made me train for two weeks solid after that, and it was them who made me tryout for every federation that was looking for talent.
I had a lot of doors slammed in my face. The first three places that
I went to said that I didn't have the necessary edge to get on there.
Shame. I had been advised my a few people to aim as high as I could, and I wasn't really aiming that high... and I was failing. The next two places turned me down as a result of my medical history. I'm sure the history of alcohol abuse wasn't great, either. The sixth place I went to was my last choice. Just goes to show how dumb I can be from time to time. That place was the CAWF, run by a guy called Joe Lorn.
I sat with Mr Lorn in a hotel lobby, and discussed why I wanted back in. I was shy, and hesitant... but he smiled at me as if to say "I know you want to scream the whole goddamn house down right now, so go on... do something crazy." A waiter brought me the wrong drink... and
I seized my chance.
"What the hell is THIS? A Mai Tai? A MAAYYYEEEE TAAYYYYEEEEE? What do you think I am, a WOMAN? Do you think because my eye twitches that
I'm a RETARD? Huh? Are RETARDS funny to you, Mister Waiter? Huh?
LOOK AT ME....." I threw the glass back at him, surprised and embarrassed by my own rage. I calmed down and continued to talk to Mr Lorn, who was open mouthed and smiling. He shook my hand firmly, and promised me a deal.
I had no idea what I had done, and he hadn't even seen me wrestle. I rang home, and Aki answered. He could just about talk.
"Hey little guy... is Mommy home?" I said, not knowing whether Keiko would laugh or cry. "Yay. Where are you, Daddy?" He replied. "Daddy has just got himself a job, Aki." I was sure my son would be proud. "Woohoo!" He was. I thought. "Daddy is a train driver! Daddy is a train driver!" I heard him yell until he was out of hearing range, and Keiko picked up. We talked long and hard about it, and I think she was in the main pleased about me returning to wrestling. At least
I was doing something. My first piece of work for the CAWF was to record a promo to hype up my debut match... which was so buried in the undercard, you had to look below ground level on the posters to see my name. I had never recorded a promo. Ever. I had wrestled in the
Indy's, and in Japan, Europe and Mexico... and had never even opened my mouth. I was determined to not sound like every other wrestler on the planet, with the same raspy voice, growling like a surly mongrel.
Nope, I was gonna be me. Problem was, I was terrified. The setting was fun... it was Aki's nursery. He wasn't in camera shot, as he kept saying "Daddy" every five minutes. I had to read them a story, and be a little weird. I went beyond that. I had a full blown panic attack and started using a voice that I had never heard in myself before... a high pitched, guttural squeal. That's my voice now. The voice I use when I start to flip out. And I do flip out, because I'm allowed to. The audience dig that, apparently. My first match in the CAWF was against a guy called Monty Miller. He was kicking my ass, so I clocked him with a chair, and got disqualified.
Nobody in the crowd knew what to make of me. I started to like talking. I struggled for a catchphrase, and then blatantly ripped one off. When I had been wrestling in Europe, I was in London and had watched TV one night. There was an advert for a cellphone company. Their catchphrase was "The Future's Bright, The future's Orange" - with Orange being the name of the company. For some reason, it came back to me during an interview and I thought that relating it to my hair colour was dumb at first until I realised that people thought I was using the term "blue" in the metaphorical sense.
I wasn't, but if people want to take me for somebody smart, then please feel free. The CAWF wasn't doing as well as it should have been, though. I made a couple of friends there, and even the fans were starting to like my antics and blatant catchphrasing (it sells the shirts). All the wrestlers were called into the locker room one day as it was announced that we would be merging with the HWF. I thought that this was the worst possible thing that could happen. I had just become number one contender for the hardcore title in a federation with a nationally syndicated TV show. I was on top of the world. Twice the federation means twice the talent and I was afraid that I would get lost in the mix. But I was wrong, because the night the IWC was born, I met an evil genius. His name was James Hall.
Mister Hall and Mister Lorn came up with the list of guys that they thought should be in the inaugural title tournament. Somehow, I was in there. I had wrestled about five matches, and I was in there. I was shocked. Of course, I saw myself as a front row troop, there as cannon fodder for those guys that were bigger, better and more well known than me. But I won. Then I won again. Then I was in the semi finals, with a match against one of the biggest names around... Chris
Courtade. I'm not lying, but I got the hell beaten out of me. I've spoke to people who thought that I did well in that match, but I didn't. I sucked. He pinned me fair and square, and I hated that.
He was a big star, and I lost to him. His star would keep rising and my bubble had been burst. I hated that feeling. I decided that I didn't want to feel it again.
So for nine months, I didn't. The IWC had a Cruiserweight Title that needed a champion. I was that man. I had some great matches with Ground Zero, another guy who has become a friend through the many wars that we have had. I must have been wrestling him every week, because he seemed to be the only one who wanted to come back for more. He never beat me, but he came real close. But I wanted more. With every win I could feel my confidence coming back. With every win, I could feel the faith that James Hall had in me being paid off. With every win, I could see myself being on top of the world after the journey from hell that was my career. I started to talk about Japan in the ring, and the fans would be marking out as I started freaking out, clawing at my own face and screaming blue murder.
I lost the Cruiserweight title to Kyle Combs in a match that saw seven separate instances of interference towards me. That was how psyched I was back then. But in the locker room afterwards, covered in blood and sweat and looking at the space on my shoulder where my belt used to be, I started to smile. And laugh. It didn't matter. I didn't have to defend my title every week anymore. Instead, I could go after another title: The big daddy of them all, the IWC International
Title.
It was held by Wes "The Eliminator" Coleman. A great wrestler. An innovator. An asshole. I hated him from the very moment that I clapped eyes on him. Arrogant, buffed up and worst of all, top of the tree. I had to get to him. And I did. I got my chance at being number one contender, and somehow managed to beat Chris Courtade for that privilege. I held onto that moment of glory for a long time, milking it for all it was worth. Wes was in my sights. I was tag team champion at the time with my Brother in Law, with him wrestling as The
Great Mishima. We had an argument, and things got ugly. So we split... and the winner of a match between us would hold the belts and could choose a new partner. I won, driving my then hated Brother in
Law through a flaming table. All the fun of the fair. And you all know who I would have chosen as my new partner, right?
Right. Wes was my new partner, and it took me all of five minutes into a match to dissolve the partnership with him getting double teamed by two seven foot monsters. The stage was set. We were enemies, and we would meet at Sole Survivor 2000. Dang. The venue was announced... the Eggdome, Tokyo. I was in two minds. I could go and have a chance of winning a big prize... or I could stay home and avoid going back to that place, the place that made me hear voices and have nightmares. It was only when Keiko pointed out the obvious - that I could go, spend the minimum amount of time in that place and maybe, just maybe I would freak out to my own advantage - that I decided to go. And I won in a fantastic ladder match that was the match of my career until recently. I was champ, and even though I had got ready in a motel and celebrated afterwards in a bar, with around 4 minutes of me not wrestling and being in the dome... I had managed to conquer my fear.
The rampage continued. I defender my gold against Throm the Black and
"Roman" Thomas Lawrence... before unexpectedly, the IWC closed. I was gutted. I had learned to love that place, and the prospect of working elsewhere - even though it may have been easier to get a job now - was not worth thinking about. Luckily, the closure didn't last long...
but on the order of the IWC's new financial backers, I was not to be champ when it re-opened. So, I was stripped of my gold and placed in the tournament to decide the new champ.
Oh dear. Having used the Spikebuster as my finisher for all this time in the USA, I decided to increase the risktaking a notch in my matches once again. Angry at being stripped of my title, I started to use The
Living End again. In my first match back, I broke a neck. Did I feel bad? A little. At the time? No. I was amazed at how much I could hurt somebody... little me, just 215 pounds back then... taking bigger guys down and ending their careers. I loved it. But once I was out of the "zone", I guess I felt bad. A little. But they would have done the same to me.
In the second run of the IWC, I had the most fun. An old friend from
Japan, "Union" Jack Worth, got an IWC contract, and hooked up with me.
We made a hell of a double act. I won the IWC title again, beating three other men for the gold, and hitting The Living End on all three.
But me and Jack, we found a new friend. Loaded. Loaded is an enigma. You love him or hate him, and the split is about
99% hate right now. But to me, the guy is a legend. And when we started the original Rudeboys back in the IWC, we had a blast. F*ck protocol, that went out the window. Screw manners, they went too.
You insulted the IWC, or us... whoops. Not only did we have the skills to back things up in the ring, but we pulled some of the best skits and stunts ever. I watched a tape of them the other day, and they were truly, truly hilarious. The three of us even had a match against each other, ordered by a moody Mister Hall... and we turned it into a farce. And you know what? The fans loved it... but not as much as we did.
Jack had visa problems, and Loaded and myself became the forefront of the stable. But tensions grew. Loaded wanted to be champ. I didn't want him to come within 50 metres of my belt... because deep down, I knew he'd beat me. I was getting arrogant, too... and being younger than Loaded, I guess it rubbed him up the wrong way. When Jack failed us both in a tag team match, he took it out on him and broke his jaw with one punch. I was pissed, and we argued. He made his true feelings known during a title match between myself and Shawn Viper...
by hitting me so hard with a chair that it knocked three of my teeth out. And he hit me in the BACK of my head. I lost my gold, and was not pleased. Then, he pinned me to become number one contender. He ended my streak. I had seen enough. I hired some muscle... dyed my hair red and started pouring out some spite and hatred. And the fans booed. Aaah.
I still feel bad about what happened next. I used my new-found favourite status with James Hall to get myself into the Viper / Loaded matchup. And I seized my chance... I caught Loaded perfectly and landed the Living End on him... but he didn't move. I watched him lay there, and everybody just stopped. A drink hit me in the head, thrown from the crowd. Viper left the ring. I just stood there. The EMT's came out and strapped him to a gurney, and I knew I was meant to feel something, but I felt nothing. I heard people saying that he'd never walk again. Nothing. When I hit Viper with the same move later on, I wanted to do the same to him. I was a monster.
Speaking of monsters, a third IWC title reign was put on hold when a guy called Christian Bridges interfered and cost me the match. I beat the hell out of him later on. By the time James Hall had walked away from the IWC though, it was in decline... and my final title reign there wasn't as special as it could have been. I kept the belt (and still have it), but I was disillusioned. The IWC closed permanently, and I was out of work, longing for the glory days again.
I'd made friends. Jack (I ended his career too, but we're cool now) was great. James Hall showed faith in me and our love / hate relationship spurred me on. Ground Zero was a worthy adversary, and is a firm buddy now. Brian Storm was a guy I looked up to (and still do). I've got a lot of time for a few guys. But the rest? Don't care for them, never have and never will. This sport is about the winning, and if I have to slay my very own grandmother to succeed, I still will.
But we're back to where you came in. The IWC closed down, and I headed back to Japan, the memories of past glories fading by the minute, and my tired mind fuelled by alcohol and painkillers. Life's a bitch, and wrestling is an unfair mistress. My job. A means to an end. I needed to quit. I needed somebody to beat me until I quit. I needed out. I came back from Japan and found what I needed, the fear, desire and agony in one package. I was gonna go out with a bang, and wrestling would miss me. I would never return to the ring, never ever again. I could be a sensible family man, with a nice job coaching football somewhere. I would never bleed for dollars again.
Or at least, that was the plan. ====================== COMING NEXT - The final instalment of this abridged version -
