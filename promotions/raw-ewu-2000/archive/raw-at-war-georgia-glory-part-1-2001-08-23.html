**************** ALAN MANGAN Vs. JUDAS PRIEST *************** The fans were mild with anticipation for the last Raw at War before
BattleZone. As the security warned a few of the rougher type dagos in attendance , the lights went down. The tron flashed and everything calmed down as
Judas Priest
made his way to ringside. The fans gave him a good ovation but nothing stellar.
Then the powersider himself, Alan Mangan strolled down to the ring and started shouting about being English to a chorus of boos ( All americans hate us, unless of course, we know the Queen of England).
Both men used their own style of wrestling to good effect. Mangan nailed a powerslam and an inverted backbreaker. Mangan wasn't in complete control however, Priest used his technical skills. He was after the submission win here. He locked in every type of submission hold ever but always
Mangan got to
the ropes or used dirty tricks to escape.
The end came when Priest hit swanton bomb on the impressive Mangan. The problem was that Mangan was on his feet. He held onto the body of Priest and nailed a rikishi type driver for the three count. Both men were very impressive but again Alan Mangan looks like one to watch out for.
******************************* BATTLEZONE ROUND 2 QUALIFIER DOUG SIMPKINS Vs. WOLFGANG ******************************* The next match on the card was a second round BattleZone match featuring "DIRTY" DOUG SIMPKINS and WOLFGANG, two former WAR! World Champions looking to advance into the final round of the tournament. With the fans solidly behind him as he made his way to the ring, the 7'1" tall Wolfgang stepped over the top rope and right into an attack by Simpkins before the bell. Using an inverted
DDT, Simpkins was able to take his much bigger opponent off his feet and keep him there, effectively neutralizing any offense Wolfgang might have mounted in the opening moments of the match.
Wolfgang, however, had no intention of staying on the mat and eventually powered his way back to his feet, putting up a tremendous fight and scoring a near fall on two occasions, the first after a suplex following a
Diamond Cutter
attempt by Simpkins and the second after hitting his patented finisher, the
"Wolfdriver." Simpkins, meanwhile, was able to score with a few cheap tricks of his own, once after throwing Wolfgang out of the ring and nailing him with a chair from the Announce Table, and, later in the match, when he used a microphone wire to choke down the popular former WAR! champion after the referee was had been accidentally knocked unconscious.
Finally, after a hard fought match by both men that could have gone either way right to the end, it was the much more experienced Simpkins who took home the win with a handful of tights at the eight minute mark and advanced to the final round of the tournament. Living up to his nickname of "Dirty" Dick,
Simpkins
quickly escaped the ring to a chorus of boos from the crowd while a stunned
Wolfgang was left watching his defeat on the instant replay.
***************************** BATTLEZONE ROUND 2 QUALIFIER JOHN SMYTHE Vs. TONNAGE ***************************** The Battlezone tournament continued with perhaps the most attractive match-up of the second round, as former WAR! World Champion and general all-around professional wrestling legend Tonnage took on perhaps the most gifted ring technician in the entire RAW Empire, "Blacksmith" John Smythe.
Tonnage arrived to a mixture of cheers and boos from the Atlanta crowd; the WAR! versus RAW versus New Breed feud that has engulfed RAW has polarized crowds around the country. Once he got to ringside, he gave Smythe a quick once over, before bursting into laughter. It was obvious he didn't take the
Blacksmith seriously. Smythe just smiled smugly – Many men had made that mistake; fewer still lived to tell about it.
Smythe had the gall to start the match with a collar and elbow tie-up, which Tonnage playfully slapped away, smacking Smythe across the back of his head a few times for good measure.
Tonnage continued to toy with Smythe, cuffing him, like a large dog batting around a puppy. After a minute or two of this, Smythe was PISSED.
Charging straight at the Beast, he went for the old schoolyard two-hand shove
– which Tonnage laughed off, his 550 plus pound frame barely budging. Now
Smythe was REALLY pissed. Winding up with everything he had, he caught Tonnage across the nose with a haymaker. Tonnage staggered a bit, and came up with a face full of blood. Not liking this one bit, he shoved Smythe across the ring into the corner, and followed him in with a meaty elbow right to the mouth.
Tonnage began working over Smythe's back, a given considering that his various finishers all do their damage to his opponent's back. But he also got in some shots to Smythe's neck, well aware of the damage Doug Simpkins had done there in recent weeks.
But Smythe was nothing if not tough on this night in Georgia. Every time Tonnage would get him down, Smythe seemed to bounce back with some sort of hit and run technique – given Tonnage's 300 plus pound weight advantage, Smythe felt confident his was the quicker man. And he was, staggering the
Beast with
running kneelifts, quick axehandles from the second rope, things of that sort.
After staggering Tonnage with a series of European uppercuts, Smythe bounded off the ropes, looking to follow up with a clothesline – only to be caught mid-run by Tonnage, who lifted him off the mat by his FACE in a sort of claw hold, spun 180 degrees, and then slammed him with what can only be described as a clawslam.
Pulling the Blacksmith off the mat, The Beast fired him into the corner, chest first. Tonnage followed Smythe in, looking to end the match quickly with a
Gigaton Crush – but found no one at home, as Smythe sidestepped out of the corner. As Tonnage stumble backwards, Smythe quickly hopped onto the second rope and came off with a nice Tornado DDT that planted the big man.
Smythe backed Tonnage into the corner, and began laying the boots to him. The educated kicks didn't appear to be hurting the Beast, but on the other hand, he wasn't fighting back at all, so maybe Smythe was starting to wear him down.
Referee Tony Peroni gave Smythe a five count, and then began forcing him out of the corner. Smythe didn't like that one bit, and shoved Peroni to the mat hard. As he turned back to the corner, Tonnage exploded to his feet in an unbelievably fluid motion, knocking Smythe to the mat with a stiff shot from his massive right hand.
Smythe bounced up with a roundhouse left of his own, which Tonnage sidestepped, delivering a heavy roundhouse kick to the small of Smythe's back, then flooring him with a mammoth clothesline. Peeling Smythe off the mat, Tonnage whipped him into the corner, and followed him in with an awesome Gigaton
Crush.
The match was over. Tonnage knew it. Peroni knew it. The Atlanta crowd knew it. And if Smythe's brain was still functioning properly, HE knew it.
But it WASN'T over. For fun or for his own sadistic pleasure, none can say, but Tonnage wasn't through. He followed up with ANOTHER Gigaton crush. And then a third. And then, as Smythe collapsed to the mat approximately the width of a dime, Tonnage simply picked him up and shook him like a ragdoll in the
Gigaton
Clutch. Peroni didn't even bother asking – he called for the bell, awarding the match to Tonnage. Tonnage and Louie left the ring to their usual mixture of cheers and boos, leaving Smythe in a crumpled heap on the mat. And then, after all that, the moment everyone in the arena knew was coming came: "Dirty" Doug
Simpkins
arrived. Without so much as a passing glance for bitter old WAR!
rival Tonnage in the aisle as they passed, Simpkins made his way to ringside, and climbed in the ring – but not before grabbing Smythe's sledgehammer from where it rested against the ringpost.
The crowd buzzed; this didn't look good. Simpkins called for the house mike, holding the sledge in the other hand.
