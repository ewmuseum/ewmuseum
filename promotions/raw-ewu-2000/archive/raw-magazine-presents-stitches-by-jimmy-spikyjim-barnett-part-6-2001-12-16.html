RAW Magazine Presents "Stitches" by Jimmy "Spikyjim" Barnett - Part 6

16 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
RAW/WAR! Magazine is still proud to present another instalment of
Jimmy "Spikyjim" Barnett's autobiography, "Stitches". Published by
Faber and Faber of New York, this book will be released in its full and unabridged form on Monday December 17th. Pre-order now at
www.geocities.com/spikyjimsworld and beat the Christmas rush! THIS FINAL INSTALMENT: Part six: "Epiphany". ===================== I'd been down. I'd been out. And as you will have read at the start of this book, I had promised to retire. Knowing how my screwy brain would react to a knock-down drag-out fight and have me fight back until one man was left standing, I chose my opponent wisely. I had my former boss James Hall come up with the location, pulling a few strings and using his contacts. We were going global, with a retirement match at the Master of the Ring 4 tournament card, with our match as a special attraction. I was pleased when my opponent was announced. I knew he's beat me until I stopped moving and they would have to carry me out. Loaded.
I still hated Loaded... although nowhere near as much as he hated me at that point. Through the drink and the rage, I'd forgotten about him, wrote him off as another victim of the Living End. But he came back. He wanted revenge. We booked the retirement match... no rules.
Me and Loaded. Loser retires. He was gonna wipe me off the face of the Earth. But like I say, my brain is all screwed up. As soon as Loaded started to hit me - and boy, did he hit me - I started to retaliate. I knew I would. I hoped I would still be out of shape, but I wasn't. I did surprisingly well. A thousand video tapes have had the tape snap during the part where Loaded threw me through that pane of glass. You could never have even the remotest idea of how much that hurt. But he couldn't put me away. And I couldn't put him away... stubborn bastard, kicking out of everything I threw at him and more.
For nearly seventy minutes, it was about sixty - forty of Loaded kicking my ass. Then I saw my moment. I went up for the Living End and... something went wrong. I nailed Loaded, but I landed on the back of my head and the next thing I know, Keiko's in the ring with me, Paige (Loaded's wife) is in the ring with him and the referee is checking my pulse. I lay there and turned my head to face Loaded. I smiled at him. He smiled at me.
We made some emotional speeches that night, and we BOTH retired. That was it for me, I would stay at home with the kids and start my own wrestling school. I even made friends with Loaded again... strange how two that have been at war could find peace through the destruction of each other, but it worked for us. I was calm, in control and happy. I rented an old warehouse in Cleveland and started my school... Barnett Puroresu. I still run it, although I'm away a lot more now. Damien and my Dad help out too, as does Danny Graham.
Danny is chief instructor, I'm more like a special guest. Besides, he teaches the basics better than me. My Dad is there all the time, splitting time between being a handyman and our accountant. We train guys aged between 18 and 25 only, and they have to pass a few tests first. I refuse to have my good name associated with the lame.
Every morning I would have Keiko drop me at my school, where I would spend my time working out, training the guys, doing publicity or tending to my website. I was NORMAL. I watched TV, paid my taxes and played with my kids. We even moved house to somewhere a little nicer... we needed more space now that Daddy was at home full time...
and Keiko was expecting our second child. I earned a little money from my school, but most of money from my past name. I may not have been the biggest star, but people in Japan loved me, and the hardcore fans in the USA totally dug what I did in the ring. I sold my tapes and merchandise through my website, and I started to deal in other wrestling merchandise too, shamelessly shilling whatever I could have made up to make a quick dollar.
I had been out of the ring for so long, I started healing up. I could move my legs freely. My back didn't spasm. My head didn't have a cut on it. My son didn't think I was a monster. I could look at a beer without wanting it. I was a machine, a tool of suburbia. As Keiko prepared to start at kindergarten, I joined the kindergarten PTA. Now living in a middle class district, my hair dyed black and my ring duds locked away, I remember my first and only meeting. We sat around a table, smiling falsely at each other. I thought that I looked smart, with my short black hair and my casual clothes on... I think I was wearing a pair of Dockers and a golf shirt, for crying out loud. I laughed when I was supposed to, and I pretended to be interested in kids other than my own. We drank a little punch, and ate some of Mrs
Rearsby's home made apple pie.
Life sucked. Keiko looked at me as she drove home, and she KNEW. She knew what I wanted, and she called a few of my old contacts for me the next day, as I pretended to be uninterested. I missed the travel. I missed the atmosphere. I missed the competition. And most sinister of all, I missed the violence. I missed hitting people in the name of sport. I missed hearing the disgust in an old-school crowd as I hot an opponent with a flaming chair. I missed the attention. I was a screaming kid on Christmas Day in a Buzz Lightyear costume, screaming "Look at me,
Look at MEEEE!!!!"... but nobody was listening. I had to make them listen again. Keiko gave me permission with a solemn speech.
"Honey, if you go back... then this is the last time. I will leave you, the kids will leave you and they'll HATE you if you start drinking again. You said that you wouldn't prostitute your name and this will be going back on your word... but if you play this family, then that is the end. Wrestle until you can't wrestle anymore... but stop as soon as you should".
She wasn't meant to say that. She was meant to stop me. But I guess my wife likes a little part of me when I wrestle... the dangerous, surreal and famous side of me... the side that screams blue murder, claws at his own eyes and will break a neck at the drop of a hat.
Even she wanted to see that again. I read stuff on the internet, and the fans wanted to see me again. But nobody wanted to see Spikyjim again as much as I did. I needed to break my promise.
I'm such a whore. I started to look for work again. This time, it wasn't that short on the ground. I had three good offers, and took them all. To compete in RAW as a solo wrestler. Took that, still doing it. To wrestle in
AVW with some of my old comrades in an all new Rudeboys line-up. Took that too, enjoying it. And to wrestle in the Master of the Ring again
- in both MOTR 5 and 6. 6 is still to come, but I guess I did OK in 5. Yet I'm busted up, banged up and every day I wrestle could be my last. If I had a dollar for every time a two bit journalist asks me that same question... "Do you think you'll be wrestling for much longer?" Then I wouldn't need to wrestle. I'd be a [name withheld] man. They always ask me why I keep on doing it, why I enjoy doing what I do so much...
well, something happened to me way back at the beginning of 2001. When Loaded put me through that glass at MOTR 4, I saw something out of my blood filled eyes that haunted me throughout the time I was retired. I saw a face in the crowd that made me feel sick. I lie. I saw more than one. And I saw signs, too.
I always thought I was popular. Even when I was acting like a psycho and wiping out heroes, I was popular. Wrong. The fans don't care about me. Truth is, you're reading this book because you want to hear about all my horrific injuries. You'll show off the colour photograph section to your friends. You'll regale them with stories about me.
Think it's glamorous being a drunk? Think it's fun being set on fire, or blown up, or cut to shreds, or thrown head first through a pane of glass? It isn't.
Do you know what I saw? What I heard? Fact was, that match was meant to be my swansong. The fans were meant to weep at the thought of me not wrestling anymore. In my foggy headed naivety, I thought that people actually cared about me. I was wrong. The biggest pop of the night didn't come from my entrance, or myself and Loaded's sporting handshake, or the seventy minute epic match... no, it came when I got thrown through a pane of glass. I could have tapped an artery, and you people CHEERED. You clapped. I looked up and saw the face of a sixteen year old kid in the front row of the crowd... ticket paid for by [name withheld] parents, wearing my merchandise. You would have thought he was a fan of mine, but he didn't look disturbed at what he saw. He looked pleased. He was holding a sign. It read...
"Spikyjim fan. Will bump for food". Screw the fact that I'm a wrestler. Screw the fact that I've held championship gold. Screw the fact that I have an amateur background.
Screw the fact that I have a personality. Screw all the hardship I've been through. That's all I am to him, to all of you. A bumping machine. The guy you pay to watch suffer. The guy that you want to see bleed. I don't care if you little bastards throw yourself off the roof of your houses, or break lightbulbs over each of your heads in your backyards to be like me... you think I enjoy being thrown around?
I'm out there busting my ass... I used to do it for you people, but not anymore. Now I'm much more dangerous... because I'm doing it for me.
All the time I was out of wrestling, the look of delight on that kid's face haunted me. When I came back to wrestling, his face was what I saw every time I closed my eyes, tattooed on the inside of my eyelids.
Mocking me. Taunting me. Time to take my dignity back. You dumbasses have cheered me in RAW, in AVW and MotR. You know what?
When I smile in the ring, I'm not smiling at you people. I'm not smiling because I enjoy the reaction that I get. I laughing at you all. You don't have the balls to get in the ring. You people could never overcome what I have overcome. Every chant you start using my name doesn't make me feel proud, it just makes me laugh. Claim to love me all you like, because nowadays I don't give a shit about any of you.
Selfish? Yes. Is that wrong? For all the entertainment I have given you people over the years, it's time I took something back. What have you ever given me? Your love? Yeah, right. You don't respect me, you don't feel sorry for me... I'm just another Jackass reject, hurting myself so that you can all have a good laugh. Never mind that every goddamn match I'm in is a five star special. Never mind that
I'm a better wrestler than any of your heroes will ever be. I'm just there for the bumps and the pratfalls, and maybe a little comic relief on the mic. Tough. That is all ending, RIGHT NOW.
Phase one is complete. I stepped out of my retirement and you losers went insane. You bought my stuff. You went to my website and spent all the money you earned flipping burgers on cheap Korean made crap that I had knocked up, anything with my name on it was fair game.
You've spent the last few months trying to read a whole book, struggling with all the big boy words and now you're at the end, you probably still think I'm joking. I'm not.
Just like I'm about to mess with all your heroes, just like I'm gonna make you hate me like you never thought possible... this book has been part of my revenge. You idiots have paid $20 a time to read a load of stuff you already knew, embellished with a few photographs you've already seen and a few quotes that I may as well have made up. Look at you. You're pathetic. You spent that money and the punchline isn't a well used catchphrase that you can chant in an arena, or put on a T-Shirt... the punchline is in your hands.
Thanks for nothing. Who's laughing now? ============================== www.geocities.com/spikyjimsworld
