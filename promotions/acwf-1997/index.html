Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The ACWF opened with a Saturday card the week after 15 November 1996, conceived plainly in its own founding post as a feeder promotion to the UWF, hybrid-booked — “using TNM to decide some results, but any major trends in the fed are booked.” A newcomer's own debut promo that December pitched exactly what it offered: “Some of you may have seen me wrestle before in some of the bigger leagues like the AEWA, GWF, and PWF... I was looking for a smaller group where I would have a greater opportunity to showcase and develop my skills.”

Four championships ran its roster across its own run — a World Heavyweight title, a Canadian title, and two successive Tag Team title lineages — and its surviving broadcasts, staged out of Tallahassee, Florida, carried full match results under its own three-man announce team.

That August a well-known broadcaster from its own parent promotion fronted a cross-federation supercard, the 1997 King of Wrestling, a sixty-man qualifying tournament narrowing to a thirty-man battle royal at the SkyDome in Toronto.

The promotion closed by 24 September 1997, mourned by the UWF's own owner in the same breath as a fellow regional fed: “It's just too bad the CWAA has gone into hibernation, it'll be sorely missed... RIP ACWF + CWAA.” The last title change in the surviving record is dated 23 August 1997; nothing later than the closure notice itself survives.
