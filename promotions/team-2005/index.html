A forum-hosted federation whose premise was the crossover dream match: a single-elimination Invitational Tournament that drew wrestlers from across the fwrestling.com hobby and beyond to compete for a trophy named for one of the circuit's founding figures. 272 wrestlers, 58 shows and seven annual awards survive from its 2005–2010 run. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

TEAM Wrestling opened on 26 June 2005 with a cluster of founding posts — staff announcements, a roster call, and an in-fiction founding vignette, “One lucky ticket...” — built around a single premise: a crossover dream match. Its own first Invitational Tournament drew thirty-two wrestlers from more than ten promotions across the fwrestling.com hobby and beyond, seeded into four regional brackets in Philadelphia, Calgary, Greensboro and St. Louis before a Final Four at Madison Square Garden.

The tournament's own prize, the Chad Merritt Trophy — named for one of the circuit's own founding figures and originally designed as TEAM's own most-valuable-wrestler award before being repurposed as the tournament's prize — went to Karl Brown, who beat Victor Molotov in the final on 9 April 2006 to become its own first champion.

From 2006 on, the promotion ran an annual interfed team tournament of its own, the Dupree Cup, named in memory of Chad Dupree and open to teams of four from feds across the fwrestling.com and A1Wrestling.com hubs and several outside alliances; New Era of Wrestling won the inaugural 2006 Cup, and its own HAL and rival The Spoiler shared that year's Most Valuable Wrestler trophy. Alongside the Dupree Cup, TEAM ran a numbered series of Supershows, a Tournament of Champions, and further Invitational Tournaments, naming a Championship of Champions, a FREE FOR ALL Championship, a Challenge Championship and a Lethal Lottery Championship as further prizes across its own run.

Its own founder stepped away in January 2009 after three and a half years running the promotion, handing the reins to a volunteer successor rather than close it down; the promotion continued for another year under that arrangement.

On 25 January 2010, the founder posted an open casting call stating that his successor had “kinda disappeared” and asking anyone willing to finish the promotion's own unresolved tournament, or to carry TEAM forward at all, to come forward. No reply or further document survives.

An interpromotional tournament league

TEAM Wrestling did not run a numbered weekly show. Across its own life it built its calendar around a small number of annual, multi-week tournament and special-event series — the TEAM Invitational Tournament, a Tournament of Champions, the Chad Dupree Memorial Cup, and a numbered SuperShow brand that hosted their finals — and drew a large share of its own roster from wrestlers whose home promotion was elsewhere on the fwrestling.com hub or beyond it, invited in for the dream-match format specifically. The museum's own holdings currently cover its founding and its first Invitational Tournament (26 June 2005 to 9 April 2006); the fed's own run continued for four further years, through 25 January 2010, not yet processed here.

Founding and the first Invitational Tournament (2005-2006)

The fed opened on 26 June 2005 with its own founding myth: a Philadelphia man, Jess Chapel, wins a lottery jackpot, loses most of it and remakes it in a single reckless stock trade, and calls on a fellow veteran promoter to help him build a wrestling league built entirely around dream teams and crossover matches. Within a week TEAM had a president (Chapel), an EPICENTER broadcast anchor, a color commentary team for its GAMEDAY calls, and an open call for talent. By the end of October the fed had signed thirty-two wrestlers — drawn from A1E, MBE, Empire Pro Wrestling, CSWA, GXW, UXW, Primetime Central, UCW, the Atlantic Wrestling Council, New ERA of Wrestling and elsewhere — into a single-elimination bracket across four regions (Philadelphia, Calgary, Greensboro, St. Louis), racing toward a Final Four at Madison Square Garden and a trophy, the Chad Merritt Trophy, named for one of the circuit's own founding figures.

The tournament ran three rounds per region before its own Final Four and Final: an English technical wrestler, Karl "The Dragon" Brown, won all five of his matches — over Trevor Hawke, Steven Shane, Hida Yakamo, Mr. Entertainment and, in the Madison Square Garden final, the Russian shootfighter Victor Molotov — to become the first tournament champion and the first Chad Merritt Trophy holder. Along the way the bracket produced the tournament's own defining upset (rookie Joe Average over former A1E World Champion Ken Cloverleaf), its own refused match (Dan Ryan forfeiting a semifinal to Molotov by walking out of the arena rather than face him), and the debut of a masked mystery figure, "The Black Scorpion," whose confrontation with Mr. Entertainment went unresolved by this era's own close. Sixteen documents from this era are held here: the tournament's own complete bracket and studio preview show, seven broadcasts recapping all thirty-one contested matches (plus one bye and one forfeit) across four rounds, the tournament final, the fed's own founding vignette, its founding administrative posts, and a member-submitted bio-card compilation covering most of the tournament's own entrants.

Later eras (collection continuing)

The museum's own accession of this wing continues in later eras, none of them read for this pass: a first annual cycle (2006-2007) introducing the Tournament of Champions, the numbered SuperShow brand and the Chad Dupree Memorial Cup; the corpus's own densest year (2007-2008); a third annual cycle (2008-2009); and an operator step-back and wind-down (2009-2010) after which no further dated activity survives. A standalone 2007 Fantasy Draft side-event, drawing on the wider e-wrestling hobby rather than TEAM's own continuity, sits outside the numbered eras entirely. None of this material is served yet.
