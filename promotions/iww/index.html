A real-wrestler-portrayal fed that fired almost its entire roster after its first card and rebuilt twice before its own corpus ends. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Insane World Wrestling launched on 27 November 1997, recruiting handlers to portray real, contemporary wrestlers for two cards a week and a monthly pay-per-view. Its own launch post listed four titles standing vacant at the start: the World, World Tag Team, US and Violent Championships.

It held its first major card in early December, described in its own following post only as producing “some good results” with no card or result document surviving to say what they were. By 13 December its president had fired nearly the entire roster and reopened the promotion's biggest names as available all at once — DDP, Sting, Bret Hart, the Legion of Doom, Mankind, Goldust, Owen Hart, Sabu and more, offered up in the same recruitment call.

A second card followed on 21 December, run on a rebuilt roster of eight stables that still leaned on real-wrestler portrayals: Roddy Piper, Ric Flair, Rey Mysterio Jr., Eddie Guerrero, Bill Goldberg and Sting among the names still listed as available.

By 7 February 1998 the promotion had rebuilt again, and this time changed its own stated preference: “I threw away all the loser deadbeats,” its recruitment post declares, asking from then on for original, created wrestlers rather than real-wrestler portrayals, with real names kept available only where needed. No document after that survives, and no closing notice is on record.
