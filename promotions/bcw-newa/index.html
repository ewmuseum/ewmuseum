Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

British Championship Wrestling billed itself "N.e.W.A Great Britain" — a member territory of the NeWA hub, its own home page naming a sister promotion (Limestone City Wrestling, across the ocean in Canada) and a developmental partner. Its own news page, signed by an operator known only as "Sam," describes running both promotions at once ("the president of the BCW and V.P of the LCW") and records BCW forming further partnerships with the "GWO" in June 1999. Five titles are attested across its surviving record: a National Championship and a JuniorWeight title (both changing hands at a Gaslaw Gala card, 16–17 May 1999), a BCW/NAeWF Atlantic Coast Championship contested for by Dan Cross and The Wraith, a National Tag Team Championship won once by Angels of the Night, and a London Territorial Championship whose own four-hop chain (Brent Morris, John Cole, Scott Simpson, R.J. Draper) is the clearest title history the record supports, reaching as late as 11 July 1999. This John Cole is presumed the same wrestler already on the sister LCW roster (matching finisher, and BCW's own news narrating his move straight from this loss to winning the NeWA Canadian title days later) — the two feds' own records are read together rather than duplicated.

The National Championship and JuniorWeight tables are not structured here: the surviving table renders several rows ambiguously and, for the JuniorWeight title, disagrees with the fed's own fuller broadcast script about who the champion actually fought — both readings are recorded on the served Title History page itself, neither resolved. No wrestler's real name appears anywhere in the held record; the operator signs only as "Sam" or by handle.

Affiliations

Limestone City Wrestling (LCW, 1997)

Sister promotion of BCW (1999) (1999)
BCW’s own home page carries a graphic link captioned “Our Sister Promotion,” pointing at the address independently identified as Limestone City Wrestling. BCW’s own News page (18 June 1999) confirms it in the operator’s own words: “we have formed a relationship with long time LCW Promoter Adam Wemp... he runs the NeWA Ontario promotion in Canada,” and a 24 June 1999 post has the same operator ordering a title match “as both the presidne of the BCW and V.P of the LCW.” BCW’s own Gaslaw Gala broadcast (16 May 1999) bills LCW’s own Beast outright as “the LCW Superpower” on his BCW debut, and BCW’s own 4 April 1999 card credits LCW’s own John Cole with his established LCW finisher, the Gator Bite.

World Class Championship eWrestling (WCCW)

Sister promotion of BCW (1999) (1999)
Both are member territories of the NeWA hub — BCW billing itself “N.e.W.A Great Britain” on its own pages, WCCW hosted at the same NeWA-hub GeoCities address the hub’s own shared pages use. No surviving document names the two together directly; the connection is drawn from their shared, explicit NeWA-hub membership.

NeWA Mid Atlantic Wrestling

Sister promotion of BCW (1999) (1999)
Both are member territories of the NeWA hub. NeWA Mid Atlantic’s own home page links directly to a shared NeWA-hub GeoCities address, the same one already used for WCCW, and both NeWA Mid Atlantic and BCW independently crown a tag team billed “Angels of the Night” — read together as the same touring unit rather than a coincidence, per the hub’s own explicit cross-territory roster movement (BCW’s own news: “seeing some new stars from either the GWO or the LCW come across the ocean to the BCW”).

National eWrestling Alliance (NeWA, National, 1997–2002)

Member of BCW (1999) (1999 or earlier – 1999 or later)
BCW’s own held record already self-describes as ‘a member territory of the NeWA (National eWrestling Alliance) hub,’ billing itself on its own pages ‘N.e.W.A Great Britain’ — matching the hub’s own Membership Listing entry ‘NeWA Great Britain.’
