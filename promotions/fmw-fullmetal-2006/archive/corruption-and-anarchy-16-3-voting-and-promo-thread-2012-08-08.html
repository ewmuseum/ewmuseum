I really hate hospitals. Mary Jackson was fading fast. Her time was coming and she knew it. Her morning had went well by her estimation, or as well as any morning can go when you are bed ridden in a hospital with a terminal illness.
Her eldest son Brett, only barely in his 20s, was quickly becoming the man she had hoped he would be. He and her younger children had come to see her en mass that morning. They showered her with love and had steadily been planning what they would all do when she got better. She could only nod in agreement, knowing what they did not.
She would never see anything outside this hospital room again. There would never be another walk in the park or a lazy day of siting in the window and watching in wonderment as the storms rage outside. No this was it. She would not make it through the day but she thought it best to let them have those last few moments.
Why does she have to go? It's just her time. Does it hurt to see this much pain all the time? I see no pain, only love. I don't understand. It's time. It started with a cough. Dense and heavy, the result of smoking a pack a day for almost thirty years. Then came another and another and another in rapid succession. When Mary pulled her hand from her mouth her delicate long fingers were painted in crimson. She summoned the doctor and he assured her that she'd probably just coughed to hard. He handed her a basin in case it happened again and left the room, never seeing the look of fear and desperation in her eyes.
Brett: You okay mom? Mary: Yeah Just need to.... Her voices trailed off as she felt another cough rising. She raised the basin to her face and out came more blood. And then more and more and more...
The children began to cry. Brett froze in a terror he had never before known. Mary coughed and coughed and gasped for breath. Then suddenly the coughing subsided. She took long deep breaths, trying to calm herself down, hating the taste of each. They were not crisp and clean breaths no they tasted of clinical grade disinfectant and the slightest tinge of copper.
So this was what death tasted like, Mary thought. Mary coughed again and knew she had a choice. She could fight on, trying to breath in a battle that she knew she would lose, or she could say goodbye to the souls she had brought into the world. Is that ever a choice for a mother? Her face filled with a terror that Brett had never seen. In a last ragged breath Mary managed four simple words.
Mary: I love you all. Her eyes rolled back in her head and she gasped. Brett knew even before the flat line siren shrieked that his mother was gone.
Brett and his siblings filed out of the room as the nurses and and doctors rushed in. The next half hour was spent with Brett and his siblings consoling one another. The room was filled with grief and sorrow and regret. Regret for the things left unsaid and for the things that were said in anger and for not taking the extra moments with her when there seemed to be no shortage of them left. Then in a flash it is all taken. They talked about her courage as she underwent the chemo and physical therapy. The fear and agony she felt when the her hair began to fall out and the situation suddenly became so real. Anger at themselves for not being supportive enough when she needed it the most. All that was left was the regret and the hope that maybe one day they could become half the person she was.
Brett was brought back from his thoughts by a hand on his shoulder. Finally it was Brett's turn.

It's been a fun ride FMW but this weekend is going to mark the end of my career (for now) as a professional wrestler. Why you ask? The answer is quite simple. Nick Bryson - Why are you the first to be addressed? You and me have unfinished business that from now on. Morpheus - I find it fitting that in my last match in this company I get to have it with a man I truly respect. I got nothing to say to you that I already haven't said previously my friend. Let's steal the show.
