A weekly Usenet booking promotion that ran Tuesday Night Thrashings and Saturday Night Rampage alongside monthly pay-per-views from March 1996, kept its own weekly power rankings, and spent 1997 fending off recruiters who kept trying to found a second federation under its own name. Eighty-three shows, twelve title lineages and 152 wrestlers survive. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Universal Wrestling Federation dates its own birth to 23 March 1996, the date and arena its own president gave for the promotion's first-ever card a year later, at Gold Rush 1997: “It was March 23rd, 1996 when GR '96 was held.”

It ran two weekly shows, Tuesday Night Thrashings and Saturday Night Rampage, alongside monthly pay-per-views, and its own president posted a running win/loss/score/rank table after nearly every card, covering the singles and tag divisions separately.

Its flagship, Gold Rush, returned yearly to the Pontiac Silverdome; the 1997 edition filled it to a self-reported 93,000 fans and opened on a recap of the year behind it — Serge Annis chokeslamming Alex Kidd, the Maloney Brothers turning on the Masters of Motion, Humanity unmasking as Brett Greene, Tom Landis winning the World Title, and the stable known as the Pride being born out of Scott Daniels and Brett Greene's own return.

Its own name outlived it as a target: a full year into its run, a Usenet fan warned a would-be founder of a second “Universal Wrestling Federation” away from the name outright, noting the original “has been well established” since the spring before.

It debuted its first female wrestler, the Lady in Red, in October 1997, wrestling male opponents by her own request; its own president said plainly he had no plans for a full women's division — “the UWF doesn't really have any plans to add a full division... heavyweight, cruisers, tag team and skyweight are about all it can handle” — while backing equal opportunity to compete against men in principle.

Its own record runs for over a decade past that: shows dated as late as 28 April 2007, posted months after the fact, with a link-only card notice from that November the last trace of it. No closing statement survives; the record simply runs out.
