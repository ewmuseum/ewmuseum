UWF Card Archive

Overload

21 July 1998
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Fade up from black to vintage clips of Tuesday Night Thrashings past. Title change after title change, surprising appearance after surprising appearance, and upset win after upset win. Eddie Fantastic and Tim Vexton trade wins over each other to claim the U.S., at the time International, championship title... The Maloney Brothers win the World Tag Titles from Fire N' Ice... The same brothers are defeated by Mad Season a short time later for the gold... Alex Kidd wins a 25 man battle royal to claim the U.S. belt... The Sweet Saviours win their first tag championship over the new Maloney Brothers... Blaze takes the Cruiserweight Championship away from Julio Sanchez... Jobbio Vega is knocked about and then declared the U.S. champion by "Badboy" Randy Acorn in a controversial win... Rebecca "Ginger" Wilder chases Ashley Wilder off the set of Thrashings while Moe Owens looks on... The caption "R.I.P. TNT... July 1998" appears on the screen shortly, and we fade to black. The scene starts off on a two shot but fades into a medium close-up of Moe.] MO: Hello fans, and welcome to the start of a new tradition here in the Universal Wrestling Federation! I'm Moe Owens and this is Rebecca "Becky" Carlisle to my left. And this is UWF _Overload_! As you can see, the setting and format for Overload is much different than Thrashings was. Each week Overload will bring you the news of the UWF, as well as reviews of events of the last week, and previews to the coming week. BC: And each week Moe and I will be joined by two guests here in the studio, to participate in a roundtable discussion. This week two such gentlemen are here, and I have a feeling their opinions and views will be as different as night and day. First up, please welcome the man who showed the world at Steel City Meltdown that he is 100 percent natural, and 100 percent kickass. Ladies and gentlemen, please welcome "The Natural" Kyle Backwood! [As "Gone Away" by the Offspring is piped in over the studio speakers, Kyle Backwood emerges from the shadows, and walks onto the set wearing a pair of bluejeans and his "100% Natural... 100% Kickass" t-shirt. He walks with a slight limp from the brutal match with Rick James, and sits down in a chair next to Moe. He smiles for the camera, and then shakes hands with Moe, leans across the table and gives Becky a quick peck on the cheek to say hello.] MO: Welcome to Overload, Kyle. KB: Thanks Moe, it's great to be here. Hiya, Becky. BC: Hi Kyle! [turns to the camera] And our other guest this week is a member of the Pride, and is the partner of "Romeo" Matt Richards in the tag team No Fat Chicks, welcome "Slick" Ricky Reno. ["I Touch Myself" by the Divinyls starts up just as Backwood's music did before, and as Moe and Kyle each bury their head in their hands in embarrassment over the music, Reno walks onto the set from the opposite direction as Backwood. He's wearing the brand new baseball cap that has the "Pain is Temporary... Pride is forever" message, plus a pair of cut-off denim shorts and a black t-shirt that reads "I went to the Daimyo Theme Park, and that Miki is one great ride" in red writing. He immediately goes to the side of Becky and gives her a big kiss(much bigger than Backwood's friendly greeting), walks around the table and slaps Moe on the back, and then swats Backwood in the back of the head. Racing around to the other side of the table before the natural one can get to his feet, he sinks into the chair next to Becky and grins like an idiot.] RR: You asked for the Pride on this first show, well you got it. MO: Obviously becoming a Pridesman has agreed with you, Ricky. RR: Of course it has, you roly-poly dancin' machine. The Pride is an institution in the UWF, and to be inducted into it means you've reached the top. BC: Wait a second, that's pretty strong talk coming from somebody that's never held a UWF championship before. RR: That's a rotten thing to say about Kylie there, Becks. BC: I didn't mean- hrmph. KB: Are you sure it was a good idea to have the both of us on together, Moe? MO: Don't look at me, I'm not the producer. Well, now that we're all aquainted, let's get down to business. First order, the stunning return of Xavier Lee Jackson this weekend on Warfare. It was announced that someone was returning to the UWF, but nobody expected that it would be XJ. Afterall, he retired shortly after Gold Rush '98 and ended what we thought was a great career. As it turns out, the bug bit the 40 year old and he returned. KB: You know, XLJ is one tough man, and I respect him. He is great, and his return here to the UWF will send heat waves right through the UWF, it will be great to see, if he still has it, and I think he has. RR: Jackson's a lunkhead, and a stupid one at that. I think the damage I did to him in our two matches must have caught up with him. MO: If I recall correctly, you lost the second match with XJ, quite handily in fact. RR: I don't remember that. BC: Despite what Mr. Reno says, I think Xavier Lee Jackson adds a lot to the UWF. It gives the fans a veteran superstar to cheer for, and Sebastian Jericho an ally. Being the heavyweight champion he hasn't had many of those lately, and with Alex Kidd out here sneaking up on people, especially Sebs, Xavier Lee could put a crimp in the Wonderkid's plans. KB: Xavier Lee and Sebastian won the tag team titles before, if they were to team up again they just might take them back from Sweet Madness. RR: The only teams that are gonna get their hands on the gold are the Sweet Saviors or No Fat Chicks. There aren't any other teams with the tools to wear the belts, and that includes those Land of the Rising Sun losers in the Daimoo. BC: Well taking a look at the tag team rankings, there appears to be a logjam at the top with longtime teams the Masters of Motion and the Darknight Express apparently the most consistent. You have to consider both of those teams very dangerous, especially considering each have worn the World Tag and U.S. Tag championships already. RR: Ever heard of the survival of the fittest? When was the last time either of those teams were competitive? We ended any hopes of the California boys' dreams of wearing the gold when we kicked them out of the Pride last year, and the Darknight Express are about as scary as a can of Klik. KB: What's Klik? RR: Imitation Spam, greenhorn. KB: I think the most dangerous team going today to the world tag belts are Twister. Face it, they've got the U.S. belts, they're on a huge winning streak, and the fans are behind them. It's a lot easier to go out and use the momentum from the fans than it is to fight their derision. RR: You ever take a breath? MO: Gentlemen, please. With all the tag teams competing in the UWF, it could be any one of them to come from nowhere and snatch up the gold. But look at who the champions are, Sweet Madness. You have Sho Satsuma who's won numerous tag team championships around the world, and the current AEWA world titlist Randall "Madman" Osbourne. It's going to be tough to go out and take the belts. BC: Kyle, would you ever consider going after one of the tag team championships, perhaps with Rock Ramon as a partner? KB: [grins] Well, that _IS_ an option, and it would be great, to team with Rock, he is one hell of a wrestler, and maybe the only person, I could trust, so if your asking is it a possiblity? Sure, of course it is, don't cross the t's, or dot the i's, but yeah, it could happen. RR: Ooh, there's a threat to the division. Mr. Happy and Mr. Stereotypical Cuban against the other teams? I hope your insurance is paid up in full. MO: Another tag team title that's apparently just been sanctioned is the CWAA titles, which have become official thanks to some petitioning from Max Robertson at the doors of the UWF offices. Kore's the official champion, but sooner or later not having a partner is going to catch up with him. KB: All, I can say is he is one tough brother, to have two belts, around his waist. Although, maybe that is the way to do it around here, all by yourself, who knows? RR: No Fat Chicks would be happy to relieve Wrench of his burden. And with the Romeo and Slick Mister Reno, first we'd show him how to wrestle, then we'd show him how to get the gals. But no cellulite please. [Reno holds his hands out in front of him, not giving much room for girth of a fantasy woman] You must be this slim to get on this ride. [Becky looks like she's about to gag, and Moe makes the save.] MO: Uhm, okay, weeeeell... what about Alex Kidd's transition from probably the most popular athlete in the history of the Universal Wrestling Federation, into one of the most hated? The fans were shocked with his blowup at Tom Landis and the UWF a few weeks ago, but every time they see him now they're openly hostile. KB: You know, it just makes me sick, what Alex did. I hung out with Alex backstage, and his whole attitude has change, he has become a real jerk to work with, let alone what he did to the fans here in UWF, and myself, I would like to set him straight, but I am sure others do as well. [Reno, from across the table, feigns tears in his eyes.] RR: It makes me sick too... [sniffle] Alex Kidd was my role model, I wanted to be just like him.... And now... now... [Reno starts to sob as he lowers his head, but after a few seconds it's apparent that he doesn't mean one bit of it, jerking his face back up in a wide grin] now he's trying to be a copy of the Pride! Ha ha ha! Now that's poetic justice for you. Alex Kidd's career has gone from top of the heap to bottom of the barrel! BC: Spoken like a true Pride representative. Alex's story is tragic, but at the same time he really doesn't deserve any pity. Sure, he was jerked around by the Chalmers Corporation and the Pride, but his contract is safe again, and he's still whining. He's getting his world title shot at Glory Days, but he's going to face the champion, Sebastian Jericho, and Sebs has revenge on his mind. MO: He's been jumped by him twice, and hasn't ever really been able to celebrate that title win in public. KB: That's not his style. Sebastian Jericho is low key, he'd rather take the belt home and celebrate with his family rather than an arena full of strangers. He's probably got the right idea, but when I win a title I'll be a sucker for the limelight. RR: _WHEN_ you win a title? Pal, you'd be lucky to hold Vortex's cup, let alone a piece of leather and gold. KB: Hey Reno, you're one to talk. Did "Hotspot" and "Sweet" Shawn let you and Richards take turns holding one of their belts? MO: Settle down guys, this isn't supposed to be a physical confrontation. Let's talk about Glory Days now. From the beaches of Waikiki on August 22nd, 1998, the UWF will bring Glory Days. We've already found out that Jericho and Kidd will square off in the main event, but there's also going to a twenty-five man battle royal in the same vein as last year's Summer Heat Wave. RR: So ol' tightwad Reed decided to spend some dough and take the crew to Hawaii? 'Bout time, the guys in Portland did it last year. BC: [doing her best to ignore Reno] For those who didn't see it last year, the "Road to the Gold" styled battle royal involves a random drawing before the event, in which each wrestler draws the name of another wrestler. When all twenty-five men are in the ring and the match begins, the only person each wrestler can try to eliminate is the man who's name they drew. MO: Once that person is eliminated, they move on to the next opponent, taking the name of their first opponent. This continues on until two men remain, and have each other as the next target. When that happens, pinfalls and submissions count, just as well as being tossed over the ropes. KB: Sounds fun. RR: Sounds like an easy win for the Pride. BC: You guys stack the deck with any more members and it might be a Pride-only event. Kyle, what do you think of the event being held in the tropics? KB: Well, you give me a free trip to Haiwaii, puts a smile on my face. My wife, she will be coming along, the kids, the coconuts... can't ask for more. I'll probably be fighting too! [Backwood grins, and Reno rolls his eyes.] RR: Can you walk and grin at the same time? [Backwood's grin turns to a glare at Reno] Like I said earlier, any time Reed springs for a trip it's fine with me, unless it's to a third world country like Canada here. [good thing there's no studio audience, but the looks from the crew Reno's in for it after the show] The Pride likes the warm weather, the string bikini's, and the open bar. MO: Nobody said anything about an open bar. RR: Trust me, when you're as necessary to the buyrate as the Pride is, you get the red carpet treatment. BC: Kyle, what are your thoughts on the schedule for Glory Days? Do you have a preference on where you compete and in which match? Do you even want to fight that night? KB: Well, it doesn't matter to me, the shot for the gold would be great, but being a part of the UWF, and a pay per view spectacular, that's what it is all about, I go out, to put on a show, and if I do that, in Waikiki, then, I'll feel good about myself. It gives a chance for anyone to prove themselves, leave the talk at home, and show up with your fists and fury. RR: Spoken like a true geek. That's why guys like you will _never_ win the main event. You're too naive, country boy. KB: Reno, so far all I've seen from you is talk. We didn't really settle much on Lightning, why don't you take this attitude into the ring one on one? MO: Whoa! Hang on a second, the purpose of this show isn't to- RR: Cram it, Owens. If this little greenhorn wants to duke it out, I'll be glad to oblige him. Next week's Lightning, Backwood. Stop the talk, and walk the walk. BC: While the testosterone in this room calms down, let's go to the Road Trip segment. Ladies and gentlemen, earlier this summer a contest was held to find special correspondents for the UWF, specifically for Overload. The contest winner and his two friends have embarked on a summer long journey to travel with the UWF, and report back to us each week. Match #1 - "Wasted Youth" Judas Steele vs. "The Ultimate Man" Joey Roy ---------------------------------------------------------------------- [Clips include Steele jawing with a fan, and Joey Roy trying to garner some reaction at all.] AG: "I don't know why I wasted the time to put Roy's nickname. Steele comes out, playing the whole disenfranchised Gen-X'er thing, ignoring the crowd, which brings a decent pop out. We went nuts, remembering his matches with Youth Gone Wild. Hopefully, there's something in store for Steele in the future. I think that's the plan, because he pretty much dominated Roy bell to bell here. Roy got a few punches in, but mostly it was high-impact from Steele, who hit a nice springboard dropkick before finishing Roy off with a top rope splash. Post-match, Steele went outside and grabbed a chair, and clobbered Roy as he tried to stand up. Steele then glared at the crowd, then left. Definite potential." Match #2 - Vortex vs. "Cardiac Kid" Joey Valachi ------------------------------------------------ [A clip of Valachi's arrogant entrance is followed by clips of the match.] AG: Valachi comes out in a Pheonix Coyotes jersey. How this guy hasn't gotten lynched yet I'll never know. Good back and forth match, with Vortex hitting a couple of spinning headscissors before Valachi takes control with a low blow. Fair amount of high spots in this match, including a somersault plancha from Vortex to Valachi on the floor, leading to a cool floor segmet where Valachi tried to slingshot Vortex from the floor into the guardrail, but Vortex jumped on the guardrail, and jumped back with a flying bodypress that missed, causing Vortex to hit the floor hard right in front of us. Valachi then rolls Vortex into the ring, and tries to take off his mask. This gets Valachi rolled up for the 1, 2... 3?!? What the heck? If my memory serves, Vortex has now won the last three or four matches between these two, but if this means anything, it hasn't gone anywhere yet." Match #3 - U.S. Tag Team Title Match - Twister vs. nCWAA -------------------------------------------------------- [Various clips include Kelli Perich's entrance, Conley and Langford hi-fiving each other, and the CWAA tag titles at ringside.] AG: "That's how this was billed, and I think we all thought it would be McNultey and Anderson vs. Twister, take 6,432. But the internet sheets say McNultey and Anderson are gone, so who are we getting? Kore Wrench and Max Robertson come out, and Robertson announces that Wrench will win the U.S. Tag belts on his own. So it's a handicap match. These are never as good as they're supposed to be, but these guys know each other, so they make it work all right. Twister begins with some double team moves, then when Conley steps out of the ring, Wrench starts to annihilate Langford. The tide turns when Wrench charges into the corner, and Langford makes the hot tag. Twister his a double dropkick, double elbow, and double leg stretch in quick succession, and seem to be on the way to winning, when Robertson jumps up and clobbers Conley with his Skyweight belt, causing the DQ. Wrench then powerbombs Conley, and is about to do the same to Langford, when the Reformer charges out to make the save. File that little bit of info away for now. It'll be important later." [One clip shows the Reformer brawling with Kore Wrench, while another pictures Twister looking on in shock.] AG: "Twister tries to shake hands with Reformer, but he just ignores them. I guess he hasn't decided if he's a face or a heel yet. We're getting ready for our next match, when who should come to ringside but Alex Kidd. He's in street clothes, and he wasn't scheduled to wrestle, so we have no idea what he's doing. (We boo anyway.) He grabs a house mic and lets us know what a bunch of ungrateful jerks we all are. He says that stunt the Reformer just pulled shows how pathetic things were getting in the UWF, and that the UWF was much better when he was the champion, but nobody appreciated it. Not the UWF front office, not the other wrestlers, and not the fans. Well, he's sick of giving the best years of his life, his family, and his career just to be tossed on the trash heap because he's not as trendy as he used to be. The Winnepeg fans react about like you'd expect, and throw a ton of trash in the ring, a trend that really needs to stop, if you ask me. Kidd looks at all the crap in the ring, then tells the crowd "You didn't deserve me as your hero," before storming off in disgust. Great interview, and it's nice to see that current angles are appearing at the house shows." Match #4 - UWF Skyweight Title Match - Max "The Professional" Robertson vs. Scott "Dynamite" Goldstone --------------------------------------------------------------- [Clips include Max Robertson coming out to a lot of derision, one sign in the crowd reading "Cameo deserves to get locked in a room with Dynamite!".] AG: "Skyweight division is heating up nicely. Giving Robertson the belt was a good call, and Goldstone got a really good pop from the Manitoban crowd. And Alex Kidd thought traditional faces were passe. Good match, with Goldstone holding the advantage through most of the match, getting several near falls with a sunset flip, small package, flying body press, and a sweet springboard headscissors into a cradle. Solid mat wrestling from Robertson, showing that you don't have to be a high-flyer to be a skyweight. The end comes when Robertson puts a figure four on Goldstone, who reverses it. Before getting to the ropes, Max pulls a foreign object out of his tights. When Goldstone stands, he gets clocked with a rabbit punch and pinned. Max puts a foot on the ropes, just in case the fans in the cheap seats didn't see him cheat." - Intermission - [Clips of the concession stands fly by, showing some of the hotter selling merchandise in one clip, and the bargain bin items such as Working Class wristwatches, nWd paperweights, and replica copies of Richard "Moxy" Blue's Golden Boot.] AG: "Took a quick look at the souvenir stands. Gold Rush videos are going strong, and it doesn't look like Steel City Meltdown is out yet. Pride t-shirts seem to be the top seller, with Reformer and Steve Ryott doing all right, and it seemed like every female fan I saw was wearing a Lady in Red t-shirt. Speaking of which, back to the action..." Match #5 - Six Man Tag - Daniel Kidd and No Fat Chicks vs. Crimson Joe Reed, the Lady in Red, and... KYLE SPARTAN?!?!? -------------------------------------------------------------------- [Clips of the fan favorites' entrances, and then to NFC's attempted flirtation sessions..] AG: "Okay, they billed this as Pride vs. Reed, Red, and Tim Vexton. Once again, rumor said Vexton's knee wasn't 100%, and he was passing on the first part of the house show tour. But this is a six-man, for Pete's sake! You can protect the man. J.D. said Spartan was a decent worker, but anyone who couldn't get a push in the ACWF, training ground for jobbers... actually, Spartan's skill doesn't matter much, and the first ten minutes of this match are Reed and Red pounding the snot out of Danny Kidd, which the crowd just eats up. There's a great spot where Reed gorilla presses Danny, tosses him at the Lady in Red, who catches him and turns it into a powerslam. Kidd and NFC get a few shots in on Reed, who's playing the face in peril for this one, but not one of these guys has the power to keep the big man down. Finally, Reed makes the hot tag to Spartan, who does about two moves on Reno before all hell breaks loose. Red clotheslines Nelson before being clocked from behind by Kidd. Danny boy threatens to strip LiR like she did to him at Steel City Meltdown, and Reed blows a gasket. The two of them start pounding Kidd into a smear on the mat, but meanwhile, Spartan gets hit from behind by Nelson, and NFC teams up for a spike piledriver to get the win for the Pride. Reed and LiR leave to huge cheers, and the Pride does a funny bit as NFC tries to help Dan Kidd to his feet, but he keeps falling down. Everyone comes off looking good, except for Spartan, which sounds about right." Match #6 - UWF Cruiserweight Title Match - Steve Ryott vs. Cosmos 3000 ---------------------------------------------------------------------- [Clips include Ryott doing a somersault plancha right into the audience, and Cosmos 3000 perched on the top rope only to miss Ryott entirely.] AG: "You know, something had been bugging me about house shows for a while, and I've finally figured out what it is, as Steve Ryott comes down to the ring without any scantily clad bimbos by his side. This, in a word, is wrong. And by the way, why no Kelli Perich earlier? We're telling the world, guys, you'd better bring the cheesecake next time. The reason I'm talking about the girls, or lack thereof, is it beats talking about the match. Cosmos is talented, but that and 50 cents will get him a cup of coffee, because nobody thought to push him. After a brief offensive flurry, Ryott takes control, and puts Cosmos away about five minutes later than he should have. The one thing that did make this a keeper, however, was Ryott's tirade on the mic after the match was over. He insults each and every fan in the house, announces that Chris Scottsdale won't be around anymore because his hormone injections were wearing off, and he was turning back into a woman. Then he said he had to go to Disney World, and make real women out of a few of the Mouseketeers. Damn, he's funny." Match #7 - Tom Landis/Youth Gone Wild vs. Sweet Saviours -------------------------------------------------------- [Clips show Wild diving off the guardrail and crowd surfing before the match, and Landis standing on the ring apron only to be thrown off as the Saviours attack from behind.] AG: "This was billed as a tag team title match when the card was first announced, but of course that ain't the case now, is it? Nevermind, Legion/Pride has enough drama about it without titles. Vexton and Daniels ask Legion "Where's Alex?" prompting a pre-bell attack that spills outside the ring, resulting in chairshots, irish whips into the guardrails, Tom Landis slamming the ringsteps down on Vexton, and Wild setting Daniels up on a table, then climbing to the top rope. Vexton pulls Daniels off the table before Wild jumps, and we're deprived of a wicked table spot. The match continues, with Vexton putting some restholds on Wild to give the Saviours time to recover. Daniels hits a couple of good suplexes, then Landis comes in, and hell breaks loose again. Landis hits the Chicago Thunderbomb on Daniels, but Vexton makes the save with a chair. Wild grabs the chair, tosses it back to Vexton, and hits the superkick into the chair into Vexton's face spot. Wild then climbs the ropes, when guess who shows up? That's right, it's Alex Kidd, and he starts to go off on Wild and Landis. The two current Legionnaires stand in the ring, and trade insults with Kidd, who suddenly grabs Wild by the legs, pulls him out to the floor, and challenges him to a fight. Wild shoves him, Kidd shoves back, and the crowd is going nuts at the prospect of this fight. Unfortunately, the Pride intrudes, as Daniels hits a belly to back suplex on Landis right on the chair.
