UWF Cards

Saturday Night Rampage

3 January 1999
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Fade up from black. "Erase and Rewind" by The Cardigans starts to play as we fade in on a black and white shot of the original members of Legion; Tom Landis, Tim Vexton, Youth Gone Wild, and Alex Kidd. The voice of Peter Phillips begins a voice-over.] Voice-Over: Since the incarnation of the Universal Wrestling Federation, one group has always stod high as the ideal representatives of teamwork, comradery, and above all else, friendship. These are the men of Legion. [The shot fades and another of Humanity, followed up with one of Black Diamond rise up.] Despite dissapointments from other members, Tom Landis, Tim Vexton, Alex Kidd and Youth Gone Wild have always been together for each other, through thick and thin. [Clips of Legion's past wars with the Pride are shown, along with the Chalmers Corp.] One friendship that was centered around trust was of Alex Kidd and Youth Gone Wild. [Black and white stills of Kidd and YGW in better times are shown. One of them cutting an interview together... the other with the two of then holding the the UWF Tag titles, during their short lived Gen-X period... and the other of Alex Kidd hitting the sumersault Bulldog on Brett Greene, as YGW holds Greene in place.] Wild and Kidd seemingly saw it all together in the UWF, from the birth of Pride, to the rise and fall of the Chalmers Corp, to their short tag team championship tenure together as Gen X. [A clip of Kidd and Wild, raising their hands in victory is next.] The storybook friendship between Alex and Youth Gone Wild took a turn for the worse however in early 1998 when longtime nemesis, Randolph Chalmers IIIrd bought out Legion's contracts from their shortlived manager, Nelson Taylor. [Action clips of Nelson and Chalmers holding out the contracts are shown, and followed by a shot of Legion all looking mighty unhappy, especially Alex.] That was when the trouble started... [Clips of Chalmers selling their contracts over to the Pride are next to run.] Scott Daniels and the Pride bought out Legion's contracts and immediately made life a living Hell for the Legionaires, and seemingly targetted "British Wonderkid" Alex Kidd the most. [A clip of Alex's beautiful wife, Alicia Powers frowning quickly flashes by.] Daniels immediately fired Alex Kidd's wife and manager, Alicia Powers from her managerial duties in the UWF, and subsequently, separated the happy couple. [Clips of Daniels ordering around Kidd, whom looks incredibly worn and stressed run.] Separated from his wife and social life, depression hit the "Britsh Wonderkid" hard. Scott Daniels used Kidd as an example to everyone else in Legion to keep in line. Legion heeded their warnings, while Alex Kidd suffered the blunt effects of being the example. Alex appeared night after night, without any breaks to fly home to see Alicia or his family as Scott Daniels had booked Alex Kidd's schedule monthes in advance. [Clips of Tim Vexton and Brett Greene defeating Scott Daniels is next, which freed Legion from their Pride rule. Everyone is celibrating in the ring, except Alex Kidd whom still looks worn and tired.] Things looked bright for Legion once they were freed from their contracts after Tim Vexton and Brett Greene collaberated and beat Scott Daniels with the stipulation that if he was beaten, he would have to free Legion. Legion rejoiced the news, and celebrated with Greene, making him an unofficial, but honorary member of Legion... [A close up on Alex Kidd shooting Greene a scowel as he rides around on Legions' shoulders.] Everyone, but Alex Kidd. Kidd felt rejected. After spending months trying to free Legion from the Pride, Alex Kidd's worst enemy had stepped in and done the job. [A clip of Alex pushing away Tom Landis and Youth Gone Wild runs next.] Kidd rejected Greene, and soon Legion. He felt betrayed that they would rely on a man that the British Wonderkid had so much sheer hate for. Add that to the emotional strains of being on the road for four straight months with no holidays, and from being away from his wife, Alex Kidd finally snapped. [Clips of Alex grabbing the microphone and running down Legion are shown. A clip of Tom Landis trying to help Alex is next, followed by tlhe clip of Alex turning down Landis.] Within a matter of days, Alex Kidd had quit Legion and proclaimed his independence from the group. All the while, chastising his friends for not being there when he says he needed them the most. Most people disagreed with Alex, but offered a hand to help him as he was going through his emotional crisis... until Alex Kidd, the man once beloved by all and heralded as the franchise of the UWF completely snapped.] [A shot of Alex Kidd attacking Sebastian Jericho and hitting the Full-Nelson legsweep onto the UWF World title.] Kidd turned his back on everyone that offered help, and adapted a self-caring attitude, proclaiming that he didn't need anyone anymore. Alex violently struck out at the new UWF World champion, Sebastian Jericho, whom was not connected to Kidd or Legion at all. Kidd claimed that he made Jericho an example, and continued to make 'examples' out of superstar after superstar until he got his UWF World title shot at Glory Days against Jericho. [Clips of the match run by, with highlights of Alex Kidd hitting the Sumersault Bulldog onto Jericho, and breaking his neck in the process by accident.] But in the end, when the smoke had cleared, Alex Kidd walked away with the UWF World title after he defeated Sebastian Jericho at Glory Days. [Clips of Alex and Tim Vexton both cutting seperate interviews are shown.] At that time, Tim Vexton was named the new president of the UWF, after retiring after a tragic car accident. Right away, Tim used his authority to try and help 'make a man out of Alex Kidd', as Kidd cried conspiracy against the new president. [Clips of Alex wrestling Brett Greene, Kensei Tokugawa, Scott Daniels, and Vortex are all shown.] After Vexton seemingly threw contendor after contendor at the World champion, Kidd kept on emerging with the UWF World title still around his waist. Meanwhile, Youth Gone Wild had captured the LIghtning Cup in a sixteen man ellimination tournament, and won a battle royal to be dubbed the number one contendor. [Clips of the two, Kidd and YGW wrestling their Steel Cage Match from the original Fright Night, in 1996.] Alex and Wild had met once in the past, in a special match at Fright Night where the winner would receive a shot at Brett Greene's U.S. title, which was Kidd's until Wild gambled it off in a bet with Randolph Chalmers. However before and after that match, Alex and Wild were still the best of friends. Now, things are different. [Clips of Wild and Kidd standing nose to nose are shown... each with a cold look of focus and intensity.] As Youth Gone Wild and Alex Kidd come into Live and Interactive Two, both are riding an incredible string of wins and each seemingly has momentum on their sides. This match up has easily become the most personal UWF World Title match in federation history. [Clips of Kidd cuting an interview are shown, with the UWF World title around his waist.] Despite the recent passing of Paul Reed, a father like figure for Alex Kidd, Alex has seemingly pulled himself out of a deep depression, and has a new focus in wrestling after an near emotional break down during which he no-showed for his main event match against AEWA World champion, Jimmy Dangerous. [Clips of Wild cutting an interview in the ring are next.] Meanwhile, Youth Gone Wild has been preparing more and moe for this match. It's been five months in the making, but Wild has been ready since day one. AK: And as God as my witness, I will never give up to you Wild. I'll be damned before I let you count my shoulders down for three, or make me say the words "I quit". [CLips of Wild and Kidd once again standing eye to eye, nose to nose.] VO: And now we are moments away from the most important match in UWF history, as "British Wonderkid" Alex Kidd defends the UWF World title against his one-time partner and best friend, Youth Gone Wild... Not an eye will blink, nor will noise break, untill one man walks out of the match, as World... champion.
