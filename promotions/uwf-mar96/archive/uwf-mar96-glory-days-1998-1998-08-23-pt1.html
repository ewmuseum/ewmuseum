Glory Days 1998, Free For All

23 August 1998
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Fade up from black to colourless images from Steel City Meltdown back in June of 1998. "I Don't Wanna Miss A Thing" by Aerosmith kicks in while we see the histories between Alex Kidd's and Sebastian Jericho's roads to Glory Days and their main event matchup. Moe Owens does a voice-over.] MO: Two men, one ring. Tonight, Sebastian Jericho is put to the ultimate test as he steps into the ring with Alex Kidd for the UWF World Heavyweight Championship Title. [Fade through clips of the early days of the UWF, as Alex Kidd is chokeslammed repeatedly by a young Serge Annis at the first UWF pay per view.] MO: Alex's story is the stuff legends are made of. From enduring the first championship tournament only to be eliminated in convincing fashion in the finals... [A new clip of Alex, this time holding the championship belt over his head in Toronto after a successful win.] MO: ...to his first championship win over Kelly Maloney on New Year's Eve 1996. Alex Kidd became a two time champion last August during the inaugural "Road to the Gold" battle royal event. Lasting farther than 39 other men, Alex reclaimed the vacant title. [Cut to clips of Sebastian Jericho dealing with Damien Nightfall, and executing the Kamalazoo Clutch on Jack Riley.] MO: Sebastian Jericho too has had a storied career since entering the ranks of the Universal Wrestling Federation. He debuted with a series of victories over other newcomers, then settled into his role as one of the toughest wrestlers around. [A shot of Jericho executing the Kalamazoo Clutch to win the United States Championship over Eddie Fantastic in a mini-tournament final last September.] MO: Sebastian wasted little time in grabbing some gold, as he took home the title vacated by "Enforcer" Tim Vexton, and then fortified himself as a lethal challenger when he teamed with Xavier Lee Jackson to win the World Tag Team Championship. [Footage from their match with the Fabulous Ones to snare the gold.] MO: And earlier this summer, Sebastian Jericho outlasted sixty-three other men to become the undisputed world heavyweight champion, battling through numerous rounds and opponents to take home the most prestigious belt in our sport. [Clips of Steel City Meltdown, of Sebastian pinning Kensei Tokugawa to take the crown... and of Alex Kidd attacking him from behind.] MO: Alex Kidd shocked the world the night before Steel City Meltdown as he verbally abused the fans, the UWF, and even his friends in Legion. Alex Kidd was a changed man, and he proved it one night later, as he jumped a tired but victorious Sebastian Jericho from behind, beating him with the very belt he'd worked so hard for. [More clips, of the various run-ins between Kidd and Jericho. MO: Since then, Sebastian and Alex have been on a collision course. Everytime we had seen one of them, the other was sure to be close by. [A clip of Alex jumping the champion at a house show, and another one of Brett Greene bringing Sebs out to back him for a match with Alex.] MO: Tonight, these two legendary competitors will meet in the ring, and determine who is the real flagbearer of the UWF. But is it Alex Kidd, the once beloved and now despised, embittered challenger who hopes to wear the championship for a third time? Or will it be the proud warrior Sebastian Jericho, the champion who wishes to prove once and for all that his legacy will be made inside the squared circle? The world will know once and for all, tonight at Glory Days! Just yesterday in Toronto, Paul Reed's resignation as president was tendered to the UWF Executive Committee, and as it stands Paul Reed is no longer president. However, impaired driving is the apparent cause. Tim Vexton's wrestling career is over, as Tim suffered a broken shoulder, torn ligaments in his forearm and bicep, and his wrist was broken in four places. JD-P: Normally, this match'd be a toss up in my mind, and I wouldn't really care. But I like Bolton, a little. He was truly bad in the ACWF, but at least he's doing something to get noticed now. DB: I'm not sure I do. There are some things that should just go unsaid, and I think he crossed the line. [Cosmos slips into the ring, and as he does Bolton pounces right away with a clubbing blow to the back. Finally, Bolton picks him back up and just hurls him by the neck back down to the mat. Cosmos gets back to his feet fairly quickly, but he's soon downed again with a vicious clothesline. Bolton, with some time bought from his early dominance, struts over to the corner for a little bit of showboating.] JD-P: Like I predicted, Bolton is putting this thing on cruise control. DB: I wouldn't say that quite yet! [Amy sees what Dwight doesn't, that being a resurgent Cosmos. As Bolton turns around from his hotdogging, Cosmos is right there to stun him with a series of jabs to the jaw. Cosmos then sends the man careening towards the opposite turnbuckles, and follows right in after him. Bolton thinks on his feet, grabbing the turnbuckle and vaulting up into the air to avoid the Mexican... however, Cosmos is also thinking on his feet and he just stops dead, catching the "Shooter". Beware the re-re-reversal, though, as Bolton lets go and just drives Cosmos back into the mat with an impressive reverse DDT.] DB: That has to leave a mark! JD-P: Bolton seems to be just one step ahead of Cosmos here in the early moments of this match. DB: I have to say I'm impressed. Dwight Bolton is showing me some talent I didn't think he had. I might be changing my story on him, even if he did cross the line with that speech. [Bolton gets right back on the attack, picking up a stunned Cosmos and labelling him with an atomic drop. As Cosmos sells the man-pain, Bolton follows up with a standing dropkick that knocks the Mexican backwards and out over the top rope to the floor. Bolton again has some time to show off, and this time he doesn't have to worry about a sneak attack as much, so he heads to the top turnbuckle and raises his arms triumphantly to irk the fans. He then turns his attention away, however, to a rising Cosmos on the outside... and without warning, he leaps off at him! The luchadore is slow to recognize the danger, and a bit too groggy to react quickly, so Bolton is able to hit square on with a top rope plancha, spilling Cosmos to the floor in a heap with Bolton's fall largely broken.] DB: How about that! Dwight Bolton flies like we never thought he could! JD-P: I had a feeling about this guy the whole time. [Bolton picks himself up a bit gingerly, and looks at the quivering mass that started the match as Cosmos 3000. Shrugging and smiling to himself, he picks the limp Mexican up and rolls him back into the ring, following soon after. He hauls Cosmos back to his feet and hooks up around his tights, preparing for a suplex. He then lifts the man up into vertical suplex position, but drops him down instead, right onto his head.] JD-P: Brain buster! DB: According to my notes, he calls that the "Straight Edge". JD-P: I call it a win. [In about three seconds, we all will. One... Two... Three!] TD: Here is your winner, the "STRAAAAIGHT SHOOOOOOOOTER" DWIIIIIIIIIGHT BOOOOOOOOOOOOOOLTOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOON! ["I Am the Man" starts again, and Bolton just rolls right out of the ring to pick up the house mic yet again, as the fans start to warm up... or, I should say, cool off... to him, letting him have it with the boos.] DBolton: Just like I promised, I squashed him like a grape. Who do you want me to squash next? [Bolton tosses the mic back to a techie as he heads to the aisle, drawing some decent heel heat as he goes. Cosmos 3000 is getting up in the ring and shaking out the cobwebs, but no one's paying much attention.] DB: I have to say, Dwight Bolton looked dominant against Cosmos 3000. JD-P: I think that shows some progress, because these two men were on the same level in many people's minds coming into this match. Dwight Bolton may have moved up an echelon.
