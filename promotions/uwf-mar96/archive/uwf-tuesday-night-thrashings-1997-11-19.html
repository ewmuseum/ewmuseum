UWF Tuesday Night Thrashings, 19 November 1997

19 November 1997
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Part One] [Fade up from black to the imposing image of the triple cage tower of doom from Fright Night 2. As the background music, an instrumental version of "Turn Back Time" by Aqua, plays on, the voice of David Rogers is picked up in a voice-over. Images of the many memorable matches from Fright Night are seen, beginning with the Free For All contests.] DR: October the 31st is always one of the most special days of the year, but 1997's version could go down as one of the wildest ever. The Universal Wrestling Federation showed the world exactly why it is among the top organizations of professional wrestling. [The clips start rolling, first the Pride interferes in the Joey Valachi-Unknown match and causes Valachi to win with the Heartstopper... Next Tom Landis and Luis Garavello exhibit some of the agility and technical ability that dominated their match, only to be interrupted by various sources.] DR: The Pride flexed some muscle on the Free For All, first guaranteeing in their own way that the Cardiac Kid would move past the first round of the Skyweight tournament. And only a short time later, they would again appear to cause grief, this time working with the members of the KO Corral to cause dissention between the Kid and the members of Legion. [A clip of the United States Championship match's conclusion, as Luis levels both men and puts Cogburn on top of Jericho.] DR: The Kid extracted a small measure of revenge on his attackers, but the United States title eventually remained with the champion after an assist from Tim Vexton. Luis Garavello meanwhile, shocked the crowd with the announcement that he is leaving the UWF. [Footage from the amazing Tower of Doom match plays, first Alex Kidd making it to the middle cage first, then Daniel Kidd diving through the trapdoor and freefalling onto his cousin.] DR: The main event tested the wills of every man who entered it, and there were some incredible moments that will surely never be forgotten. [The spot in the match where Youth Gone Wild jumps down the one trapdoor, lands with his feet on either side of the next one, and does it again in a matter of seconds, is shown, followed up by Joe Reed held by the hair from reaching the cage, but eventually falling to the canvas and onto a weary Cogburn.] DR: The squad consisting of former World Champions Brewster Cogburn, Alex Kidd, Joe Reed, and their partners, Scott 'Hotspot' Daniels and the Reformer went on to the sudden death battle royal portion of the match, and it too featured some devastating UWF action. [Scott Daniels is crotched along the top rope, and clotheslined to the floor by Joe Reed... Brewster Cogburn is hurled from the top turnbuckle by the Reformer...] DR: And then the man seemingly with the least chance to win it all gave the crowd one of it's biggest thrills. [Reed and Alex Kidd are pushed by the Reformer over the top rope and hit the floor almost simultaniousy, the crowd exploding and fireworks shooting off while the Reformer raises his arms in victory, hardly able to believe it.] DR: For the Reformer, Fright Night 2 had to be the sweetest victory he's tasted... so far. However for the Epitome of Evil, October 31st marked a very different occasion... [Fade into the Holy Hell match between Serge Annis and Bishop, each man stunning the other with hard shots, but each taking the hits and asking for more. Then cut to both men standing on the top rope, and falling from the perch through a table.] DR: Serge Annis has always been a question mark. Can he be hurt? Is he mortal? Fright Night proved both answers to be yes. [Clips of the medical team working on Serge and placing the neckbrace on him, then wheeling him out to the ambulance and the picture of it speeding off fills the screen.] DR: Tonight, the UWF returns after a hiatus with eight action packed matches, plus full updates from the incredible pay per view a couple of weeks ago. All this and more on Tuesday Night Thrashings! [Fade to blackness, and then the UWF logo begins to spin in the background, growing from the center of the screen until it engulfs the entire thing. Chumbawamba's "Tubthumping" plays as the opening graphics for TNT are seen, and then we fade up to the UWF's mobile studio truck outside the Compaq Center in Houston, Texas. Another fade takes us inside the truck, and the control center is cramped for space as not only the regular host, Ashley Wilder, is seen, but so are David Rogers and Moe Owens. All three are wearing merchandise from Fright Night 2.] AW: Hello fans, welcome to Tuesday Night Thrashings, MY show. [She looks at the other two with an eyebrow raised, daring them to say otherwise] As I'm sure you can all see, I've been saddled with these pieces of dead weight tonight. But I won't let it get me down. DR: Why thank you Ashley, it's a pleasure to be here. MO: Don't complain Miss Wilder, even with all of us and all this equipment, it's still bigger than the locker rooms I usually get. AW: Owens, I heard you share a room with Jobbio Vega and Lights, Cameras, Action. MO: That's not true Miss Wilder, I get my own room. It's just really tiny. AW: You're a dull boy, Owens. I could get used to that Miss Wilder stuff though. DR: You're going to have to, because Paul Reed, the esteemed president of the UWF has decided that Moe here is going to be your co-host from now on. [Ashley leaps to her feet in protest, while Moe gives one of his trademark goofy grins.] AW: You can't do this Rogers! Not him! DR: I didn't do it, Ashley. Take it up with Paul Reed. Congratulations Moe, I think. MO: Thanks Dave. Wow, my own show again! I worked with Cameo before, I can definitely co-host with someone of your talents Miss Wilder. AW: Oh shut up Owens. Why is Reed doing this? The male 14-34 demographic is the highest it's ever been! DR: Yes, but the entire female audience has dropped by wide margins. But before we get started with tonight's show, we've got some information to pass along to you concerning the next Universal Wrestling Federation Pay Per View, which will come to you on December 28th, 1997. AW: Wait a second Rogers, you skipped a month. This is November. DR: Precisely my point, Ashley. Because of the extra long hiatus that the UWF went on following Fright Night, President Paul Reed has decided that it would be in the best interest of the league not to present another pay per view so close to the last one. The December Pay Per View has been labelled UWF Live and Interactive by the executive committee thus far, but they will not as of yet release the details surrounding the name. We'll keep you posted on that one fans, but it's time to go to the footage from tonight's show. Just a few short hours ago the UWF presented it's first live event since Fright Night 2, and the crowd really enjoyed it, at least judging by the applause they gave. MO: Yup, but we're going to kick off the post-Fright Night era with a few words by the man standing victorious at the end of the night- the Reformer. [Camera fades in on the Reformer in the locker room after Fright Night 2.] Reformer: You all have now seen the light. The light that is now shining on my path to the UWF World Heavyweight Championship. I survived through the nine of the best wrestlers in the UWF today. I went through that doublecrosser Bishop! I went through Scott Daniels, Youth Gone Wild, Alex Kidd, Brewster Cogburn, Sebastian Jericho, Tom Landis, and Daniel Kidd. Not one of them stopped me in my quest for the chance at the UWF gold. I beat the best there is here in the UWF, and now there is only one more step for me becoming the UWF Heavyweight Champion. That step is to beat the champ, Mr Randy Acorn. Well, Mr. Acorn. Your time will come when we will get in the ring and you and I will have it out. The championship will be mine. Oh yes it will be mine and there is not one thing that you can do to stop me. I have a special surprise in store for you Mr. Acorn. I also offer this stipulation to this match Mr. Acorn. I say that we have a lumberjack match. I will pick my five men to surround the ring, and you pick yours. Do you think you can defend the title against me under those stipulations. Do you have the courage to wrestle me under those stipulations. I don't know if you have the guts to do it. But I still offer that match to you. I have been in this federation since it began way back when, and to this day have not held the UWF World Title. That will all come to an end when we meet. But like I said before, do you have the guts to take me up on that challenge. Can you defend that belt against me under those stipulations? I will be waiting your reply. The Reformer has spoken! [Camera fades down, then to the truck.] AW: What a jackass. He only won the match because of the people he was put on the team with. Scott Daniels should have won that, or Brewster Cogburn even. Instead, Joe Reed ruins everything by taking Hotspot out, and Brewster was a victim of unfortunate circumstances. Well, at least it wasn't Kidd or Reed that won. That would really be horrible. MO: I'm glad to see the Reformer win. He's been here since day one, more or less. He's finally gonna get the recognition. He'll get his chance at the next pay per view event to wrestle for the World Heavyweight Championship. DR: Quite right. Let's get to the first contest of the night, featuring the team of Armed and Dangerous. MO: [looks into the camera and grins again] Roll it! I've always wanted to say that. AW: There goes the intelligence demographic. ===================================================================== ARMED AND DANGEROUS versus 'LUKEWARM' STEVE JOBSTIN AND TMTC JOBBER ===================================================================== Jobstin and TMTC came out first to a very tepid response from the crowd, and before their opponents came out Jobstin kept giving the finger to his partner. He also took off the belt around his waist and threw it around the ring, and in the process lost his jean shorts, which fell around his ankles. TMTC was too busy to notice, standing on the middle rope and repeatedly asking the crowd whether to climb to the top or not. The sound of their opponent's music scared TMTC enough to make him fall off the ropes altogether. "Wang-Dang Sweet Poon-Tang" by Ted Nugent blared over the PA. The incredible looking Tawni and Shawni, or Nasty Habits, came out from the curtains wearing red leather tops with lace ties going down the center. Each had long, white lacy skirts with slits up the sides, and thigh high, red, high heeled boots. As they strutted their stuff, the cheers from the crowd were decisively lower in pitch than the boos. Once in the ring they were handed the microphone by a drooling Tom Davis, who for his troubles received a peck on the cheek from Tawni. Tom nearly fell down, and was laughed into the corner of the ring. Tawni: Hi! [Tawni waves to the fans] I'm Tawni and this is... Shawni: Shawni! We are... Tawni and Shawni at the same time: Nasty Habits! At this point the men in the crowd had trouble maintaining their whistles and catcalls, so a large pop just errupted instead. Tawni: We want to introduce the man who stole our hearts away from the milli-second Kidd. He's the words most dangerous man, Jimmy Dangerous, and his partner, the ever unstoppable "The Samurai" Kensei Tokugawa-- Tawni and Shawni at the same time: Armed and Dangerous! "Armed and Dangerous" by Anthrax played as Dangerous and Tokugawa made their way to the ring. As Dangerous entered the ring, the Nasty Habits rushed him and gave him a huge kiss. "The Samurai" for his part, entered very cautiously, not taking his eyes off the opponents. He put down his kendo stick in the corner and took off his samurai armor, putting it there as well. The Habits exited the ring as Dangerous hi-fived Kensei, and then the slaughter began. Jobstin managed to get in one finger before taking a spinning roundhouse kick to the face from Kensei, while JD leapt at TMTC Jobber and frankensteinered the huge lump of human waist(yes, that spelling) over into a pin. Spotted up in the crowd was the tandem of Chris Nelson and Matt Richards, the Twin Ninjas, looking rather displeased. Shinobi was not present(or visible at any rate), and the Ninjas watched emotionless. They were not seen at all by A&D, as Jimmy ended the pin prematurely and along with Tokugawa whipped Jobstin into the ropes for a double clothesline, and then as Kensei stood next to Jobstin, Dangerous bounced off the ropes and leapt. His foot was caught by Tokugawa and Jimmy gained even more height, somersaulting 360 degrees in midair and coming down in a huge splash on the Lukewarm one. He covered Jobstin for the win, and following the match lifted Jobstin up and prepared him for a piledriver. The whole crowd heard him scream "No! Not again! My neck!". Dangerous took pity on him and let him go, as Armed and Dangerous left the ring. WINNERS: Armed and Dangerous [Cut back to the mobile truck. Ashley's sitting in the corner, arms crossed, and putting on her best pout for the cameras. Dave shakes his head at her, while Moe sits on the other side of the broadcast team's elder statesman sheepishly.] DR: Now is a talented team we just saw, but somehow I have a feeling that Jimmy Dangerous isn't content with staying as one half of a duo for long. He's hogged the spotlight from his partner the whole time, and before the event started, the Daimyo staged a press conference this afternoon outside the arena. Let's take you now to that shocking piece of footage. [Cut to a shot of outside the Compaq arena. A whole slew of reporters are seen standing in a pack in front of a podium, and the first recognizable face belongs to Mr. Tsuburaya, the money man behind the Daimyo. At the podium is Mr. Tsuburaya. Behind him sits Jimmy Dangerous and the Nasty Habits, Tawni to his left and Shawni to his right. The room is full of reporters.] Mr. Tsuburaya: I would like to get down to business. I'm not here for my incredibly talented tag team, the UWF's United States tag team champions the Fabulous Ones, I'm here for Jimmy Dangerous. [The room starts buzzing, as they try to guess what the announcement could be.] Mr. Tsuburaya: I've just completed meeting with the UWF's board of directors, in a renegotiation of Mr. Jimmy Dangerous' UWF contract. It seems that there wasn't...lets say any room for him to grow in this organization. Thanks to their help, and of course the true talents of Mr. Dangerous himself, we've come to a new agreement. Mr. Dangerous will be getting title shots that have been passing him by, thanks to the old contract. Reporter #1: Bill Apter, PWI. Who will he be wrestling? Mr. Tsuburaya: I was just about to get to that. His first title shot will be for Daniel Kidd's cruiserweight championship. Then, onto the United States championship, and finally the UWF's world championship. Reporter #2: Dave Rosenbaum, Wrestle America. What gives him the right to get these shots, when there are other wrestler more deserving? Mr. Tsuburaya: More deserving? [Mr. Tsuburaya points to Jimmy] This man has given his blood, sweat, and tears to this organization and has been overlooked for title matches. He's getting these shots as a reward for his services. [Jimmy approaches the podium and Mr. Tsuburaya steps away.] JD: I've worked my ass off to be considered for title shots. I was passed by for these shots due to my old contract. I was stuck in a no-win situation. I dominate the singles circuit of the UWF for over a year and what do I get for it? "Ooh, I'm sorry, but so-and-so has the next title match." I bust my balls, and I get... "We're unable to give you that match because so-and-so is feuding with the champion right now." What the hell?! I've feuded with a few champions and what did they say... "He has a mandatory title match with so-and-friggin' so." Well, that ends now! I'm going to get what I deserve. Bill Apter: Who would you rather wrestle for the world championship, Scott Daniels or the current champion Randy Acorn? JD: It doesn't matter to me because they both pale in comparison to my talents. [Mr. Tsuburaya steps back up to the podium.] Mr. Tsuburaya: I would like to address that last question. Jimmy won't have to worry about Scott Daniels, because he has the next title shot. We didn't go into the renegotiations to play around. We went in with a set plan to get this man the title matches he so deserves. Reporter #3: Brandi Mankiewicz, Your champion. What about Daniel Kidd, then? JD: I'm going to school his Pride ass. I taught him who was better with women. [Tawni and Shawni smile] And I'll teach him who's better in the squared circle! Reporter #4: Vic Venom, RAW magazine. How about the US champion Sebastian Jericho, who in this reporter's opinion is the best young wrestler in the UWF? JD: First of all the key words are "young wrestler". I've got the experience, I've got the looks, I've got the talent, that he doesn't! I realize Eddie Fantastic thought the same thing at WITD2 during the tournament, but he's nothing compared to me. I've got more talent then him in my pinky finger. [Jimmy waves his finger in the air] Just think, after I win the US title, the Daimyo will own all of UWF's United States titles! Dave Rosenbaum: When will these matches take place? JD: ASAP! I'm calling these title matches "Tour De Dangerous". So, when you see those words in lights, you can be sure that a belt is going to be changing hands. Brandi Mankiewicz: What about your tag team combination with the Samurai? Mr. Tsuburaya: He's fine with it. He knows that Jimmy deserves these matches and supports him one hundred percent. JD: Don't worry about it! Armed and Dangerous will still be wrestling! Vic Venom: One last question, what exactly was Paul Reed thinking when he signed this agreement? Mr. Tsuburaya: Perhaps you should ask him when he finds out. This was not negotiated with him, it was done with a gentleman who ranks higher than Mr. Reed. [The footage ends, and we return to the truck.] DR: I'd like to know just who it is that supposedly ranks higher than Paul Reed to sign a deal like that. The only person I can think of is the owner of the UWF, and he hasn't been seen in months. Who else could it be? MO: I'm sorry, but that's just garbage there. Jimmy Dangerous does NOT deserve any title shots, at least not right now. I mean, who's he beaten in singles competition? He's won a couple of tag team matches with Kensei, but it's not like he's dominated the singles divisions like he thinks. He doesn't even fit into the weight category for the Cruiserweight title! AW: You two are overlooking the biggest piece of that story. Who gave Vic Venom a pass to that conference? MO: Who's Vic Venom? AW: Shut up. Let's watch a real athlete in action, as the Lady in Red displays her talents against some idiot in a mask. [The camera stands in front of the locker room door. The door opens, and "Crimson" Joe Reed and the Lady in Red walks out of the locker room. Each one is wearing their wrestling outfit. Totally ignoring the camera, they continue their conversation. The camera follows as they walk down the hallway.] CJR: All right, you know the book on Vortex. LiR: Of course. I scouted him for you, remember? He's just another luchador-style wrestler. Expect to get hit with an air attack, try to keep him on the mat, and let him kill himself with mistakes. CJR: Right. He likes to play mindgames, but ignore them and concentrate on putting him away. [Shakes his head, then faces the camera for the first time.] After a while, the faces all start to blend together. I've seen hundreds of them- young arrogant kids that think that they're the best. So they target the big guns- like me. Hell, several years ago I WAS one of the punks. But I got better, and smarter, and more experienced, until I became the wrestler that you're seeing now. So go ahead and talk, Reno. I'm sure you will. Talk and talk about how you're going to beat. Dozens have tried. Dozens have failed. [They start to walk away, but then the Lady in Red turns around and faces the camera.] LiR: And Acorn... if you show up tonight, one of us will show you how to REALLY use a chair on someone. [They walk off, and the camera fades.] =============================== VORTEX versus THE LADY IN RED =============================== The Lady in Red was accompanied to the ring by Joe Reed, not exactly a shocking development. Then Vortex's music started, and as the LiR watched her opponent walk out, he walked to the ring, and then the bell rang to start the match. Before they locked up, the masked luchador stood back and shook his finger at the Lady in Red, and at that time the lights dropped out. When they returned, Vortex had vanished out of sight. They dropped a second time, and the second time they came back on, Vortex was perched on the top rope and leapt down onto Reed with a spinning corkscrew twist, knocking the former champion off his feet. As Reed picked himself up and the LiR shouted at Vortex to get his ass into the ring, the skyweight headed up the aisle, taking the countout loss. WINNER: By Countout, the Lady in Red DR: That man is one of the strangest I've ever seen, in all my years in this sport. MO: He's downright nutty. AW: Too bad that chicken ran away instead of staying to fight. The Lady in Red is a great role model for little girls everywhere, as am I. If only she'd dump the slug, she'd be all set for a run to the World title. DR: Excuse me? You like the Lady in Red, but not Joe Reed? That's odd. AW: Not really Rogers. I respect the LiR, but Joe Reed is an ingrate. Here he is wrestling for the UWF, all the time ripping it and claiming allegiance to that other promotion. But when payday comes, he's always one of the first lined up with his hand out. DR: Let's step away from that subject, shall we? Fans, the last time we saw Serge Annis was after his long awaited contest with Bishop, and that nasty fall both men took. Bishop went on to the main event match, but unfortunately Serge suffered severe injuries and was swept away to the hospital. Thanks to Fright Night taking place in Portland, Oregon, the other wrestling company that Serge competes for, the IIWF, assisted our officials on hand in getting him the proper medical attention. A little over a week later, he wrestled at the IIWF's November 8th pay per view, Ring Wars IV and won a violent barbed wire match against two other men. MO: Yeah, and both of the others have previously held titles during their careers. Serge Annis showed the desire to pull off the win, not to mention his sadistic side. He took out the eye of a man named Creed for god sakes! DR: He is a twisted individual, but unfortunately he spent three more days in the hospital after that match. Serge Annis is a lunatic, but he is mortal and not even he can absorb punishment like that. We haven't gotten any further reports on Serge Annis, or when he can be expected back. BOBBY RAIN! Bobby Rain: Make it quick missy, or else I just MIGHT get Rachel on ya. AW: Well ... I was just wondering, is it all over between you and Johnson? [A sudden devilish and childlike grin (usual Jerry Lawler expression) appears on the brow of Rain who promptly responds.] Bobby Rain: Hehe ... guess we'll just have to find that our for ourselves now won't we? The interview ended and Landis came out to his expected huge pop, and was seconded by Tara 'Sunburst' Marshall, also expected. Landis unwisely forgot about the match and went over to investigate, but when the lid was opened, a handful of salt was thrown into Landis' eyes, and he stumbled back, blinded. Rain was there for the rollup and made the pin, and following the match Greg Smith(having been inside the casket) punished Landis with five Icepicks before Youth Gone Wild ran in for the save.
