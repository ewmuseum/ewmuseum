DR "Anyways, his match is first." ==================================== CHRIS SCOTTSDALE VS JOBTIMO DRAGON ==================================== "The Distance" by Cake rumbled through the arena and the cornerman for Ricky Johnson, Chris Scottsdale, made his way out to the ring. He slapped hands with the fans, who didn't seem relunctant at all to cheer for the unknown superstar. He got into the ring and immediately targeted Jobtimo Dragon for pain. A single leg takedown led to a version of the Regal Stretch, and then he changed to a front facelock into a DDT. Dragon got up and swung wildly, then Scottsdale dropkicked him over the top rope. Dragon hit the floor, while Chris stood on the inside of the second turnbuckle, then hopped over straight to the floor and a splash onto the prone Dragon. The crowd was pumped and cheered for the talented newcomer, as he threw Jobtimo back into the ring and climbed the ropes. A flying somersault bodypress delighted the crowd, and he immediately latched onto Dragon with a boston crab. Dragon reached the ropes and Scottsdale let go, only to place him in a headlock takedown. From there he went around his opponent and rolled him up from behind, but followed the roll through and ended up in a powerbomb. The crowd loved his moves, and when he again climbed to the top rope, they all stood waiting to see what was next. He thrilled the crowd once again with a beautiful flying moonsault elbowdrop. All that was left was to debut his finisher, a running powerbomb. The referee counted to three(actually, he could have counted to one hundred, Dragon wasn't going anywhere) and Scottsdale won his first UWF match. WINNER: CHRIS SCOTTSDALE DR "What an amazing talent! An incredible addition to the UWF, and a dangerous competitor." LF "I told you, I found him and I brought him here." SD "Who, Jobtimo Dragon? It figures." Now we are going to get a chance to see the leader of the Street Gang in action, and also a top contendor to the UWF title, The Darksider." =================================== THE DARKSIDER VS STERLING JUSTICE =================================== "Unskinny Bop" by Poison came over the speakers, signifying the arrival of the Eyes of Justice. Sterling Justice came out to a moderate pop from the crowd, and fireworks in the ring added to the entrance of the defendor of good. "Graveyard Symphony" played, and the lights went down. Lighters popped up around the crowd and the Darksider walked down the aisle, raising the lights with his hands. Justice was eager to avenge a loss to the Darksider's stablemate Rock Ramon a short time ago, and met the Darksider outside the ring. Justice displayed surprising ferocity, and hammered away at the Darksider, still clad in entrance attire. He brought the dark-man back into the ring and executed a single leg takedown, setting him up for a few kneedrops to the thigh muscle. The Darksider winced at the pain and was belly to belly suplexed, then covered for a two count. Justice made a mistake in bringing the Darksider back to his feet, and paid for it in the form of a backspin DDT. Quickly the tide changed as a fallaway slam, jumping DDT, and enzuigiri(yep, from the Darksider no less) led to a pin attempt by the Darksider. Sterling made a kick out at two, and ducked an elbow thrust. An armbar submission hold caught the Darksider by surprise, and held him in place for a while. Even so, the Darksider whipped Justice out of the ring by that same hold, and followed to the floor. He tried a chokeslam, but instead received a palm blow to the forehead. That staggered the mighty man from the darkside, and led to Sterling tossing him back into the ring where he belonged. He stepped up on the apron, but the Darksider rose quickly and suplexed Justice onto the top turnbuckle sideways. Justice clutched his stomach as he fell to the concrete floor, and the Darksider looked down emotionlessly at him. The referee almost counted Justice out, but Sterling managed to crawl into the ring and avoid the loss that way, only to get hammered with a legdrop. A bulldog headlock from the Darksider was countered and Sterling hoisted the Darksider up on his shoulders, trying to lock in the Unconditional Surrendor. The crafty street gang member broke out of the hold, punched Sterling low and readied him for the RIP Piledriver. The momentum from Sterling carried him over and the Darksider was in position for a piledriver from Justice. He kept him on his shoulder, climbed up a couple of turnbuckles to the second highest, and powerslammed him to the mat. The force of the blow knocked the wind out of the Darksider, and Sterling scored a surprising upset. WINNER: STERLING JUSTICE SD "Damn, I've never seen a move like that before. A second rope powerslam. I like it. Give the devil his due, Justice got the job done tonight." DR "That match could have gone either way, but the powerslam really ended it well. Daniel Kidd against Robbie Greer in a No Disqualifications contest." ========================================== DANIEL KIDD VS "THE FALCON" ROBBIE GREER ========================================== "The Ocean" came out from the arena sound system and Robbie Greer emerged along with his valet "Hawk" Jennifer Shards. The crowd started to cheer for them as they came down to the ring, and then the Chalmers Corporation made their entrance. The entire Corp., consisting of Chalmers, Daniel Kidd, the Scholar, and "The Ultimate Man" Joey Roy. On his way to the ring, Chalmers stopped by the announcer's table and avoided both Daniels and Fredericks. He proceeded to give David Rogers a gift, then walked off. The 4 on 1 looked to be a disadvantage early on for Greer, but he was the first to land a punch, dropping Danny Boy with a left hook early. He took a shot right back, and was set up on the top rope. Kidd went up, but came down differently then he wanted to, in a top rope frankensteiner. Kidd went down in a heap, but after a trip from the Scholar, was able to get into a nice rhythm with a double underhook backbreaker, followed by a fisherman suplex. The referee counted to two, but Greer escaped the pin attempt. Kidd was brazen enough to slap Greer in the face, before a faceslam led to a shoulderblock to the mat. Kidd latched on with an armbar, but was hurtled to the ground by a toss from Greer. Greer came back with a spin kick, then a vertical suplex before executing an Asai moonsault. Kidd could only watch and take the hits, until distraction by Joey Roy led to him backdropping Greer out to the floor. Greer wasted little time outside, returning after a four count. Kidd was waiting with an elbowdrop from the second turnbuckle. He gained a two count from it and then used a figure four leglock taught to him by Chalmers, the master, followed by a kneelock submission hold. He tried to get back at Greer with a top rope frankensteiner of his own, but Greer shoved him to the mat. He couldn't capitalize, as Roy again interjected himself, pushing Greer from the top turnbuckle to the mat right next to Kidd. Kidd got up and faceslammed him again, and caught the Falcon unaware with a shoulderblock. He tried to fold Greer up in an octopus hold and was rolled right into a small package that gave the Falcon a two count. Greer climbed up and flew at Kidd with a somersault headbutt that connected and put Kidd out cold. Greer picked up the unconscious Daniel and delivered his signature Crash Landing, then hooked the leg and waited for the three. It never came, as the entire Corporation stormed the ring and proceeded to beat the tar out of Greer. The bell didn't sound, as it was a no DQ match. Out from the dressing rooms ran Ricky Johnson, Chris Scottsdale, Alex Kidd and Tom Landis. They stormed the ring and took control, as Landis and Kidd worked over the ACWF branch, and Chalmers was taken care of by Scottsdale. That left Daniel at the mercy of Ricky and a half dazed Robbie Greer. From each corner of the ring, a member of the Corporation was flung towards the centre, where a group meeting of the skulls took place. They all rolled to the floor and hit the highway, while the group of fan favorites took stock of the situation and left one by one, Greer being the last to leave and winning by of all things, countout.
