A women's-only independent promotion based in Carson City, Nevada, running weekly "Unleashed!" and "Beauty Calls" broadcasts toward its second supercard in the summer of 2003. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Diamond Star Wrestling billed itself, in its own July 2003 handbook, as “a small, indepdenant women's ONLY wrestling federation based out of Carson City, Nevada,” run on “a very small budget, financed only by revenue and our owner's cash flow” — no real-world wrestler admitted, only original female characters. Its own studio desk was anchored by Freddy Nuckels, a comedic, lecherous host who fronted its Beauty Calls review show between matches.

Its own ownership changed hands that same June: the promotion was sold by its founding owner, Brenden Bowmer, to a new owner billed on screen as “The Walking Contradiction” Sabbath, an active wrestler in several other promotions who took over “for an unknown sum of money” after Bowmer's own burnout.

Three championships ran the roster. The Diamond Star Championship passed from Polaris (won over The Bloodsucking Editor in a gauntlet rumble, 16 January 2003) to Dalbello Rage by forfeit at High Stakes, then to Iris Galiver, who beat Dalbello Rage on UNLEASHED! on 10 June 2003 and held it into the summer. The Shooting Star Championship went to The Bloodsucking Editor, who beat Scarlett McCain in a ladder match at High Stakes; the Gemini Tag Team Championship went to Extreme Measures, who beat Sakura Asahara and baby-I at the same show.

Its surviving broadcast, Beauty Calls of 3 July 2003, ran two matches — Ophelia Johnson over Lisa Drake, and Tara Marshall over Nina Grimsson — alongside promo segments building toward the following week's UNLEASHED! and a second supercard, Clash of the Valkyries, announced for 24 August 2003 with title defenses promised from both champions.

How Clash of the Valkyries played out isn't in the surviving record: no document survives here past DSW's own 4 July 2003 update.
