Introduction of PICO PICO (short for Professional Independent Combat Organization... but we call it PICO for short because it sounds cool!) was officially formed on August 6, 2003, in a very brief press conference held by Hiroshi Yamasaki. Yamasaki, the older brother of Project BATTLE*DREAM owner Kayoko Yamasaki and the owner of a very successful chain of restaurants, announced that he would be forming a small pro wrestling league based out of Tokyo, Japan. While not being specific about a roster size, Yamasaki said that he is looking for having around ten regulars with a few rotating members. --- 2. Staff of PICO (in character) Blow-by-Blow Announcer: Shinya Tsukioka. Tsukioka, in his mid-30's, has participated in several small organizations before, as well as performing radio broadcasts. He takes his job very seriously, and it is his goal to make sure that each fan watching is very aware of the physical aspect product. Color Commentator: Patrick Rhodes. An English-born wrestler that speaks fluent Japanese, Patrick is the uncle of P*WIN co-founder Jeremy Rhodes. Patrick came to Japan in 1974, and has lived there since 1981. He is an expert on human anatomy, so whenever someone is having a body part worked over, expect Patrick to explain just what is happening. Ring Announcer: Kazuhiko Tanizawa. He announces the wrestlers. Duh. Referees: Takashi Arai (head), Yutaka Nakagawa, Yoshimi Iijima. Takashi Arai, head referee for PICO, is also the head referee for Project BATTLE*DREAM. He is 29 years old, but has been refereeing since he turned 18. Yutaka Nakagawa is 24 years old, and is a protege of Arai. He has been refereeing for two years. Yoshimi Iijima is PICO's lone female referee. She is 22 years old, and this is the first promotion she has refereed for. Matchmaking Committee: Hiroshi Yamasaki, Kazuhiko Tanizawa, Takashi Arai. These men determine who faces who. If a wrestler wishes to challenge another wrestler, he must appeal to the matchmaking committee. Even then, they can opt to refuse to book a match. Their word is final. --- 3. Staff of PICO (out of character) Fedhead: [name withheld]. Hmm, where to start. I've been in e-wrestling since 1997, and have ran a couple of ill-fated feds before. It's been about three years since I've ran a fed, mostly because I swore that I would never run a fed without being inspired. I've ran P*WIN since September 2002 (well, publicly, anyway) and Project BATTLE*DREAM since January 2003 (those are sim feds, though, but they're fun!). I'm also responsible for Shane Destiny, Michelle Bailey, Momoiro Tenshi, Nightlife (and so on, and so on...). I'm a very laid- back person in terms of e-wrestling (those of you who are business majors could call me a "Theory Y"-style fedhead). Writing Staff: [name withheld]. Well, not just me, of course. It wouldn't hurt to have a few volunteers here and there. After all, I'm not planning on overwhelming myself with matches. Writers must be learned in Japanese wrestling and how things are run. The last thing I'd like to see in PICO is an American-style match... that's not the style I'm shooting for. Contact me if you'd like to help. --- 4. Titles of PICO - PICO Singles Title. PICO has no defined weight classes, so there's just one title to shoot for in the singles division. That'd be this one. The title will be defended as the matchmaking committee sees fit. - PICO Tag Team Titles. Just like the singles title, but for tag teams! Challengers for these titles must have a certain amount of experience in the promotion under their belt to qualify for shots at the title, so don't expect teams randomly paired together and sent to the tag titles! --- 5. Shows of PICO - PICO TV. No fancy names here! PICO TV is aired on Samurai! TV twice a month, and will have three feature matches. One hour. - PICO Battle Station. A reaction-based show hosted by Shinya Tsukioka and Patrick Rhodes, where wrestlers talk about what happened on PICO TV. 30 minutes. - PICO commercial releases. Occasionally, PICO will run a large show and release it commercially on DVD and VHS. This is where you can expect to see title matches! Usually two to two and a half hours. --- 6. Rules of PICO (in character) - All participants in PICO are encouraged to fight cleanly. Countouts and disqualifications will be rare, as matches are expected to begin and end in the ring. Fighting outside of the ring is discouraged, and referees have the power to threaten fines to wrestlers who either refuse to return to the ring or attempt to foul their opponent. - Intentional abuse of PICO officials and out-of-ring staff will not be tolerated and will be considered grounds for termination. You may be fined for threatening a PICO official, or stripped of your title. Referees are referees, wrestlers are wrestlers. Let's keep it that way. - Wrestlers will be issued five-counts for climbing onto the turnbuckles. If a wrestler does not dismount the turnbuckles by the count of five, he will be disqualified. - Referees will call for immediate breaks of illegal holds, such as blatant chokes. --- 7. Rules of PICO (out of character) - Here's the big rule... winners and losers are determined by a number of factors. Heat, angles, and strats are most important, with RPs counting towards heat (after all, you can't be very popular if you aren't active in the media). If you lose, it's for a reason, and it's not intended to be punishment. If you are unsure about why something was booked the way it was, come ask me about it calmly. If you throw a fit, chances are that I'll cut your character from the roster and move along. - There is no need for romanji or "Engrish". Just act as if the English being used is really Japanese. The only reason someone should be subtitled is if they are actually speaking English. :) - RPing rules are very simple. For PICO TV, a small oncard hyping your match before it happens is expected. For PICO Battle Station, just a reactionary piece will do. - Strats are expected to be a basic gameplan, suggested spots, what you would like the commentators to mention, and an entrance. - If you apply with a gaijin, you're basically shooting yourself in the foot. There will only be room for two gaijins on the roster, and even then it'll probably be a revolving spot. - If you are confused with anything or need help with something... just contact me. - If you're not familiar with Japanese wrestling, this may not be the fed for you. :) --- 8.
