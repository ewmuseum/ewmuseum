Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

A federation bearing the name American Championship Wrestling Alliance reappeared on Usenet on 10 January 2000, headquartered, its own handbook said, in Charlotte, North Carolina, and following “the trend laid down by the original National Wrestling Alliance” — hard contact without the bloodshed of the era's more extreme promotions. Its own operators, writing in the handbook's own words, described bringing the promotion back for the new millennium after first opening it in August 1998.

The relaunch handbook promised a weekly broadcast called Breakdown, a flash show called Talk Zone, house shows three nights a week, and a bi-monthly pay-per-view slate under the banner Special Edition. Four titles were laid out — World Heavyweight, World Tag Team, Television, and Lightheavyweight — with a closed-flash system limiting each competitor to one submission a show. The promotion's own self-presentation claimed an exclusive national broadcast deal and events staged across North America, Europe, and Japan.

A revised booklet followed on 25 May 2000, restructured around four weekly and bi-monthly shows and a slimmer two-title, one-belt slate. The revision retired the handbook's own direct voice in favor of an on-screen president, a retired competitor once known as Hybrid in the NAIWF, flanked by a roster of announcers, an interviewer, and a head of security. The same posting also carried a sample battle royal script, offered to the newsgroup as a demonstration of the promotion's writing.

The name and acronym were far from the promotion's own alone. A held record elsewhere in this museum already carried an unrelated, circa-1996 federation of the identical full name, and a further, unrelated claimant of the same name is separately attested in 1999; a rival operator on the same newsgroup, that same year, counted roughly five federations sharing the bare acronym ACWA. No document here links any of them.

No further activity survives. The promotion's own last-dated document in this record is the 25 May 2000 revision, and no closing statement of any kind was posted after it.
