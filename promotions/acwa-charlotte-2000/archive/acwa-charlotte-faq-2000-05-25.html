Character Application --------------------------------------------------------- * Introduction. [- What is the ACWA? -] The ACWA is one of the near infinite number of fantasy westling promotions that suffuse the Internet. This federation exists to provide wrestling fans with a creative outlet. It gives you the ability to create a wrestler of your own, create angles, and to [potentially] win the ACWA World Heavyweight Title.
The ACWA relies on active involvement from the handlers, and cooperation between the handlers. For the most part, it is up to you to make the matches, create angles, and keep things interesting. The federation's front office will do its part to keep things moving and create angles, but in the end it is up to you.
The ultimate goal that is sought by all of us is to make the ACWA different from most of the fantasy wrestling leagues out there. We are not only an outlet for its
handlers, but also it is to be a coherent and exciting organization in which the wrestlers/handlers participate. This federation attempts to please an imaginary audience, so story lines, angles, and long running feuds are required to keep the action fresh. If you are looking for a federation where you challenge a wrestler, see the results, and just go on to the next wrestler, you would be wise to find a different league.
The ACWA is not only a federation where the wrestlers roleplay to be successful, but the federation itself is going to be role-played. The federation is a fictitious promotion with it's headquarters based in Charlotte,
North Carolina. It is following in the footsteps of successful promotions such as the World Wrestling Federation and National Wrestling Alliance. It is an international federation, holding it's events in vast arenas all over North
American and occasionally Europe and Japan. The ACWA follows the trend laid down by the original National Wrestling Alliance: truly hard-core, traditional wrestling. In it's glory days, the National Wrestling Alliance was a better 'traditional' wrestling federation than World Championship Wrestling, and a better 'hard-core' wrestling federation than both the World Wrestling Federation and Extreme Championship
Wrestling. The difference lies in the fact that to the National Wrestling Alliance, their traditional wrestling was more hard-core than anything found today. The ACWA has the specialty matches similar to the World Wrestling Federation, but of course also has many more traditional matches without blood-loss or anything like that. However, every time you step into an ACWA ring, you run the risk of serious injury.
As for championships, the competitors here understand that these titles are something to be honored and fought for with every fiber of their being. Here, the champion does not give credibility to the title, the title gives credibility to the champion! You are not walking into this federation and getting a title without a fight. You are not going to become the number one contender without a little old-fashioned blood, sweat, tears, and dedication..
but that's what real professional wrestling is all about, isn't it? [- Who runs the ACWA? -] President, Ian Hayes: Formerly known as Hybrid in the legendary NAIWF, this former Rising Star and Intercontinental Champion has given up his flurescent tights for Armoni suits, silver Rolex watches, and black stretch limousines. At only thirty years of age, Hayes is one of a dying number of professional wrestling purists out there. While he feels there is a place for all the pyrotechnics and glitzy outfits, when it comes down to it you had better be able to perform in the ring or you are outta there. He believe is fair play and sportsmanship, but understands that not everyone wants to see that.
The ACWA is also being backed financially by a yet to be named business associate of Mr. Hayes. No one as yet has been able to discover whom the mysterous investor is, however Mr. Hayes has been quoted as saying we will
"find out who the backer is when we decide to tell." So until Mr. Hayes or this financial backer feels like it, it would appear that we will remain in the dark on the subject.
--------------------------------------------------------- * Codes of Conduct... There are several 'no-brainer' rules in e-wrestling, and I am hoping you are atleast somewhat aware of what I mean when I say that. Therefore, I will only cover those rules which we stress more than any others. However, these
'no-brainers' should be recognized as unwritten rules, and should be followed. All matches are determined through the Human Judgment System (or HJS). Several things are taken into consideration before a winner is decided upon. To further insure that your character comes out on top whe he steps into an ACWA ring, make sure your flashes are of the highest quality you can make them, and try to be fairly consistent when you flash. Sending in one flash one week, then two the next, then no flashes for three weeks will not help you get ahead.. Also, remember that your characters heat going into the match will sometimes effect the outcome. Your heat is determined by the consistency of your flashing, as well as participation in other aspects of the federation. If you openly talk with other fed members and administration, work on ideas for future angles, or even assist in writing matches once in awhile, your wrestler could be pushed.
This is not to say that you MUST do these things to get anywhere in the ACWA, but those who play ball will get smiled upon.
Flash guidelines are few, however they are important. First and foremost, role-plays should be done entirely in character. They should not be for the most part shoot comments. Personally, I do not mind a shoot role-play every once in a while if it is in good fashion, but let's not get too carried away.
We try to give an inch, let's not try to take a mile. Due to the federation's closed-flash system, you are permitted three flashes a week (one per flash packet), as well as a backstage or in-ring interview during Homebrew.. Please understand that we are not forcing anyone to do a role-play, but trust me: if you have a match and do not role-play for it you will *not* win! If you don't have a match, it would still be in your best interest to do a role-play, just because it surely cannot hurt you. It can only help you. If the federation sees you trying even though you don't have to and we smile and your name just might get a nice push. Everything you get out of the ACWA revolves around what you put in.
Now, I can honestly say angles are my favorite things in any federation. I am going to say right now, sometimes an angle, if it is good may make you lose a match even if you did have the better role-play. Sometimes that is just the way the cookie crumbles. But hey, it is all in good fun. Now on angles.. if you are a wrestler in this federation and feel you are going anywhere and need a good angle or feud with someone let us know. We will be more than happy to help you.
Hell, that is one of the main reasons we are here. We want the wrestlers to succeed right along with this promotion.

--------------------------------------------------------- * Federation Shows The ACWA's number one weekly show airs every Sunday night at 11:00p.m. EST, and is called "ACWA: Homebrew." Hosted by ACWA announcers Shannon Jamison and Nick
Martinez, this show consists of upwards of six to eight matches, however this may not always the case. Title matches can happen quite regularly on this show, so champions should be aware of this.
Homebrew is not the only ACWA show for the week, oh no. There is also "Noise Pollution," a show where all the ACWA talent can speak their minds on whatever they want to. This show is hosted by Shannon Jamison and Nick Martinez, and comes your way every Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday night at 11:00 p.m. EST.
While on the whole, Noise Pollution is a show for wrestlers to discuss upcoming opponents and other things. However, dark matches from the previous week's
"Homebrew" broadcast may be shown as well. It all depends on how many wrestlers wish to speak their mind, and if there were any dark matches from Homebrew worth airing on national television.
Now, as far as special events such as pay-per-views go.. The ACWA is not one of these federations that holds a super card every four or five weeks. Here, we feel that super cards should have some significance to them. Far too many pay-per-view events these days tend to be lackluster, to say the least, and we do not wish to follow this trend. Instead, the ACWA holds only four super cards a year: one every three months. In doing so, new feuds are able to expand to greater proportions.. and on going feuds have a much greater climax if drawn out over three months instead of three or four weeks. It is our duty to entertain our fans, and we feel using this "old school" approach to super cards will be much more satisfying in the long run than holding one every month. This of course, is not to say that the ACWA will not hold off on pay-per-view caliber matches and the like for these super cards. The first Sunday of every month, Homebrew will be that much more spectacular than the others, while still leaving something unresolved for the super cards. You could compare these shows to the old 'Clash of Champions' cards the NWA used to hold on an off-month from
pay-per-view. --------------------------------------------------------- * Championships. The ACWA currently recognizes two singles and one tag team championship. If you lose a title, a rematch will be signed for two to three weeks after the time you lose it. The rematch will be against whomever holds the title, not the person you lost it to, some times in a three-way match, but 90% of the time in a one on one match. Title holders do not have to accept anyone's challenges.
However, it would be in your best interest to accept reasonable ones. As advice, it is not a good idea to go straight for the Heavyweight Title. Start lower with the Television strap, then work up to the higher belt. The more you work, the better you get. That is a huge rule here.
World Heavyweight Title. The goal of all wrestlers in the ACWA. Once held by such wrestling icons as Angel Reaper and Mark Andrews, this is the top of the mountain. The ultimate bragging point. The heavyweight champion is required to defend his title against one of the wrestlers in the top five every month on his own free will.
In addition, President Ian Hayes or Vice President Wickberg may also appoint a challenger for the Heavyweight Title. Only a wrestler in the top five may challenge for this title.
Television Title. This belt could easily be known as an endurance title. The holder of the ACWA Television Title must defend between two to four times a month, at the discretion of President Ian Hayes. This belt is often considered a stepping stone to the Heavyweight Title, however it is more widely considered a way for rookies to prove themselves, by defending it almost non-stop. No competitor ranked beneath the top fifteen in the federation may challenge for this belt.
World Tag Team Title.. The ultimate goal of tag teams in the ACWA. The champions are required to defend the tag team titles against one of the federation's top teams every month. In addition, just as with the World and Television straps, President
Hayes or Vice President Wickberg may also appoint the challenger to the titles for either weekly or monthly cards. In this division, any tag team in the federation can challenge for the titles.
On a side note, I would like to stress that the ACWA would greatly appreciate it if members handling singles wrestlers would talk with one another, and form tag teams. You would not have to team with each other all the time, however we would really like for the Tag Team Titles to actually mean something.
--------------------------------------------------------- Commentator, Shannon Jamison: Recognized by some as one of the best play-by-play men since Gordon Solie, this 28 y/o kid has dreamed of being a professional wrestler since he was in his early teens, and is now the main man behind the microphone for "Homebrew." He is liked by virtually all the wrestlers and wrestling fans. He pulls no punches, and excels in telling it like it is.
Color Commentator, Nick Martinez: A former professional-wrestler himself, a serious back injury sidelined him for over a year which led to his decision to retire from the sport. He has been the color man for all ACWA broadcasts for the past two years or so, and isn't ashamed to cheer for the bad guy, or even offer to help out! His mouth can get him in trouble sometimes, and holds the world record for backpedaling.
Head of Security, Zack Brody: You cannot describe a man as a 'mountain' without including this guy. Zack tried to make it as a professional wrestler, but did not possess the extreme cardiovascular conditioning to make it in the sport.
Luckily, he made friends with Mr. Hayes during a scouting session down in Florida, and Mr. Hayes decided to hire him. He was appointed the head of security, and also acts as the owner's personal bodyguard. There has yet to be anyone to get the better of this big monster, and at 6' 10", 310lbs. he is one of the biggest [and most intimidating] men in the sport.
Ring Announcer, Autumn Long: The beautiful voice you hear every week on "Homebrew" introducing ACWA superstars belongs to this young lady. She, like head of security Zack Brody, are the two extremes in the federation. While Zack is the big monster, Autumn is probably no bigger than his leg! At about 5' 2",
110lbs., Ms. Long is dwarfed by virtually everyone in the ACWA, men and women alike. Fortunately [or maybe unfortunately for all the guys], she is the significant other to the big man Zack Brody, and he has been known for severe acts of violence towards anyone who so much as looks at her wrong.
Interviewer, Greg Porter: If you want a story, this is the man to get it for you. Port (as he is known to everyone) is one hell of a reporter. There are very few wrestlers in the back that can avoid his onslaught of questions. He gets pushed around a bit when he's got a microphone and camera-crew with him, but this never discourages him from giving it his best shot at getting the big scoops.
