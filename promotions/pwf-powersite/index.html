THE PWF POWERSITE names itself outright as "the Power Wrestling Federation" and builds its whole roster through WWF No Mercy's own N64 create-a-wrestler mode, "resurrecting" in October 2000 after a self-described two-year hibernation under an owner-character, Michael Pellington, who opens its broadcasts in person, and whose two surviving broadcasts chain three of the fed's seven titles across a gap this capture never closes. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

THE PWF POWERSITE'S own REBIRTH broadcast, dated 1 October 2000, opens by naming the promotion outright: "For two years, the POWER WRESTLING FEDERATION has hibernated amidst its fame and popularity... Welcome to the new era of the POWER WRESTLING FEDERATION." The broadcast crowns an entirely new cast of Powerstars, none carried into the 2001 roster below, ending with Jason "The Goat" Doods climbing a ladder over Soniq and Inferno to become the new and undisputed PWF World Champion. A second relaunch follows at a new address, whose own home page names its own method plainly: "The PWF is a Fantasy Wrestling Federation based on the Nintendo 64 game, WWF No Mercy" — its own submission form asks applicants to build a wrestler in the game's own create-a-mode and describe its in-game colour codes field by field.

The 2001 relaunch's own Saturday Night Havoc broadcast, 24 March 2001, opens on Michael Pellington, billed on-screen as "the owner of the PWF," thanking fans for their support through his absence before announcing a tournament to crown new World, US, and European champions — interrupted on-air by Antonio Gambino's own demand that the World Title be handed back to him instead, as the fed's own pre-hibernation champion. The same broadcast crowns the Challenge Title's own first champion, Crusader Chrome, who goes on to win the World Title itself two months later at an unheld show, War Games (27 May 2001) — named only on the OutKasts' own bio card, whose formation the same match produced. No document in this capture records how the World Title passed from Doods to whoever Crusader Chrome actually beat that night; the Challenge Title's own further reigns (Power G, Rye Hazwaki) run on into April 2001, the ledger's own last entries.

The Rebirth tournament, and the champion who never got to celebrate Written by AI, 2026

THE PWF REBIRTH runs its whole card as a single elimination bracket toward a new World Title. Toby DiRizzo submits Ambrose Fleetwood in 26 seconds; Soniq outlasts a stalker billed Dark Cloak when the arena lights cut out and leave him alone in his own sleeperhold; a mystery match between two returning veterans, Wolfgang Rainier G. and The Hatawman, ends a draw when Jason "The Goat" Doods runs in with a lead pipe and knocks both men cold; Havoc, billed the former PWF champion, finally learns the identity of the mystery attacker who's been ambushing him for weeks — Inferno — and loses the rematch to the Carnage Crush. Doods beats "Bad Boy" Marcus T. in a First Blood match to reach the final, then climbs alone over Soniq (dragged from the ring by Dark Cloak's own return) and Inferno (blinded by a thrown ladder from Havoc, ringside) to become the new and undisputed PWF World Champion. He doesn't get to enjoy it: Marcus T. jumps him backstage, takes the belt, tosses it back with a warning — "NEXT TIME, THIS BELT GOES AROUND MY WAIST" — and no further document in this capture ever shows Doods again.

Gambino's grudge, and the throne nobody can agree is vacant Written by AI, 2026

Antonio Gambino's own bio card claims a pre-hibernation PWF World Title win over Diablo, lost back to him in a Last Man Standing match before the fed's first closure. Five months after Doods's own Rebirth win — a show Gambino doesn't appear on — Gambino opens Saturday Night Havoc by demanding Michael Pellington scrap a newly-announced tournament and hand the World Title back to him outright, naming Josh Resnick, General Leo, DeMoN and Firestorm as the tournament field he considers beneath him. Firestorm answers him live — "Remember all of the matches we had together the first time around?" — and the two settle it in the main event, Gambino escaping a top-rope Big Elbow Drop to win with the Gambino Facebuster. Whether Gambino's own claim on the World Title has any more standing than the tournament he's demanding scrapped is never resolved in this capture; Crusader Chrome wins the actual belt two months later at an unheld show, from an opponent this capture never names.

The gWo's hunt for Rye Hazwaki Written by AI, 2026

The gWo's own leader Matt Van Dam opens Saturday Night Havoc with a live callout for anyone willing to face him; Genecide answers and beats him after Rye Hazwaki runs in and crotches MVD on the top turnbuckle mid-move. The group — MVD, Power G, The Real McCoy and Jennifer Van Dam — storms Hazwaki's own locker room, spots him on a monitor signing autographs in the parking lot, and chases him there too; the broadcast cuts away with Hazwaki still driving off, uncaught. Two months later, at War Games, Hazwaki wins the US Title with an assist from Havok — the interference that also wins Crusader Chrome the World Title and forms the OutKasts — a stable the gWo's own chase never touches.
