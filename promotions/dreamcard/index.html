Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Dreamcard was a card, and then it was six cards. Its promoter asked rec.sport.pro-wrestling for match ideas, posted a full lineup, asked for results, and made every booking decision himself. The first went up on 11 November 1991; they came monthly after that, and between the fourth and the fifth the promotional duties changed hands.

What it booked were dream matches between television wrestlers. Dreamcard V, from the Tokyo EggDome, put Ric Flair against Jushin “Thunder” Liger for the World Championship, Hulk Hogan against Sting, the Ultimate Warrior against the Great Muta, and Lex Luger against Curt Hennig. It also put Ronnie Garvin against Steve Armstrong for a World Jobber Championship, which drew the only complaint the record holds: “AAAAAH!!! Ronnie Garvin is NOT a jobber!!!”

Underneath the dream matches ran a joke about the newsgroup itself. There was a World Bandwidth Championship and a North American Bandwidth Championship, defended by the people who posted the most, billed under nicknames — the Joker, the Flammable, the Tidbit, the Teaser. The card that carried them called the World Championship “(dare I say it?) the REAL World Championship. :-)”

Its last card reached outside itself. Dreamcard V's main event was arranged between two other federations and staged on Dreamcard's own show: the EMWA's World Champion against the FWF's, title for title, “no fewer than the three largest wrestling promotions on internet access” represented on one card. The card promised results by 25 April 1992. None survives.

By November the newsgroup had turned against it. Arguing about whether another federation belonged on rec.sport.pro-wrestling, one poster counted Dreamcard among the things “pretty well established as off-topic”, and another asked the man complaining whether he was the same person who had once told readers to killfile Dreamcard if they didn't like it. A sixth card ran, recorded in the FWF's own ledger and nowhere else.

Affiliations

RSPW Federation

Shared continuity with Dreamcard (1991)
The league's RSPW World, North American and World Tag Team belts were booked on Dreamcard V in March and April 1992, "courtesy of" the league's host; the Dreamcards' lineup update of 13 April 1992 has Ric Flair still holding "the RSPWF World Championship".

Recreation Sport Professional Wrestling Federation (RSPWF)

Shared continuity with Dreamcard (1991)
The call of 16 January 1994 that started this production proposed bringing back "the RSPWF Bandwidth titles", the posters' titles the Dreamcards had awarded in 1991 and 1992; its own FAQ tells those Bandwidth supercards as its first run.
