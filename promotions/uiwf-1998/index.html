Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Universal Internet Wrestling Federation opened on 23 May 1998, its own president describing a roster already taking shape: “just a few days I advertised for my fed and recieved a few responses…3 tag teams in good standing…12 singles wrestlers.” It ran as a closed-flashing, booked league — matches decided 80 percent on roleplaying, 20 percent on strategy and angle — with every wrestler signed to a one-month contract renewed, or not, on the promotion's own monthly cull of inactive talent.

Its own first pay-per-view, Summer Shutdown, ran on 4 July 1998 behind a video package recapping its own Heavyweight title picture: Jack Daniels winning the belt at an event called Anarchy in April, and his challenger, Duke Turken, beating Big Bong with a huracanrana cradle to earn the shot. The same broadcast introduced the promotion's own new Vice President, Brandon Stateler, on air, questioned live by the broadcast team about a career the crowd had never seen.

By that autumn the Heavyweight title had changed hands again: a ratings sheet the promotion issued alongside its weekly news bulletin lists Evil Alex Roberts as champion, at an 8-4-1 record, ahead of a ranked field that still carried Turken as inactive. The promotion ran a rotating slate of weekly shows through this stretch — an interview program it called Trash Talkin', and a rotating action broadcast under names including Thursday Night Terror and TNT — publishing its own wrestler ratings alongside each.

The surviving record continues into 1999 with a match-writers' handbook, a promo pack, and further TNT broadcasts, running to a Sunday Night TNT dated 28 February 1999. Nothing records how, or whether, the promotion continued past that broadcast.
