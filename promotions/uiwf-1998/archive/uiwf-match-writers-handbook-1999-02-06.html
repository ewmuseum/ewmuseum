UIWF Match Writers' Handbook

6 February 1999

*LAST UPDATED : 02/06/99* PRELUDE ... To start I'll let you in on the perks for writing for the UIWF ... THERE ARE NONE! Well unless you count the respect of the handlers, other writers, and our "fans", and the never ending thankfulness of me, the fed head. :) Here's the low down on how we do things, also known as the UIWF Match Writers' Handbook. \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ \/ Welcome everyone to the UIWF Match Writers' Handbook. This is a basic overlay of the what the writers need to know about the UIWF, and what y'all need to know to be able to write for us! 1. The UIWF 2. Cards A. Tuesday Night Terror (TNT) B. Supercards 3. Announcers A. [name withheld] B. Rex Roads C. Bob Sharpe D. Jim Jones 4. Our Writing Style A. ABS, what it is, and how we modify it. 5. Extras 6. Conclusion =================== 1. About the UIWF The UIWF, first off, stands for the Universal Internet Wrestling Federation. We're your standard booked e-fed. The UIWF began on April 1st 1998 and held it's first card on April 17th 1998. We then closed down in September of '98. It's now January of '99 and I, president Casey, am ready to roll again. That's why I need you guys! The UIWF is a regional promotion based out of Southern Ontario. We frequently tour the North Eastern United States as well. Although we're slowing building momentum in hopes of challenging that 'other' Canadian promotion one day. :) The UIWF is not a hardcore league. So unless specified by _ME_ or by a handler in his/her strat don't include much hardcore in a match. These are a few things things to help you when writing matches for the UIWF. I hope you can at least stay awake while reading the rest of this handbook. =-=-=-=-=-=-=-=> 2. Cards The UIWF has one card a week, and a Supercard every 2 months. The weekly card is Tuesday Night Terror. TNT usually has 5-8 matches. We don't skimp out on any match. Every match receives the same attention any other match would. No summaries or anything of that matter for the " lesser " matches. Everything we do is modified ABS. The only match which will receive a little extra attention is the Main Event. A. Tuesday Night Terror Tuesday Terror is the RAW/Nitro of the UIWF. This card will have around 5-8 matches. The card has the best quality matches seen anywhere. Usually their is a title defense of some kind. Often matches which would headline normal cards are just a normal match here because the UIWF doesn't try to fool it's fans into watching an inferior product. I will give out the winners on Friday Night, and I need the matches by Monday afternoon. The announcers for Tuesday Night Terror are [name withheld] and Rex Roads. Bob Sharpe and Jim Jones are the color men and provide comments hear and there. Keep this in mind. This is not a 4 man team. On average for every two or three comments CE and RR make BS and JJ should make one. B. Supercards The UIWF has 6 Supercards a year, or one every two months. The Supercard is where the writers should give their best work. Only the best writing is allowed on the Supercard. The Supercards are the big cards of the UIWF. This card will have anywhere from 8-12 live, play-by-play matches on it. The Supercards will often feature the ending/beginning-of-new angles. Here is a list of UIWF Supercards - Winter Blast ( January ) - March Madness - Anarchy ( May ) - Summer Showdown ( July ) - Slam-a-thon ( September ) - Awesome Autumn ( November ) I will be writing at least 2 matches, and I expect each writer to be able to write a minimum of a match. You will get a 4 day advance to write the matches, and all matches will have to be 15 k or more. The bigger matches ( Ones which involves heated feuds or titles ) must be at least 20k in length. The Main Event will be around 30k. This is why I'm giving you a 4 day advance on writing the matches. Now don't get me wrong, I'm not a K-mark, and I'm very loose on the "rules" I set, I'm just trying to give you guys an idea of what I'm looking for size wise. If you feel is the best it could be a little below the "minimum(s)" I have set by all means leave it that way. Don't sacrifice quality and excitement for size. The announcers for the Supercards will be [name withheld], and Rex Roads. The color commentators will be Jim Jones and Bob Sharpe. =-=-=-=-=-=-=-=> 3. Announcers The announcers of the UIWF play an important role of the card. What would the IIWF have been with out Steve Roberts? What would the SCRA be without Jack Hazard? Often times it's the commentators which make the "middle" of the match, and the "lesser" matches worth reading. The 4 man system, in away, makes the UIWF very unique as it is the only fed, to my knowledge, to make use of it. A. [name withheld] [name withheld] is the president of the UIWF and presents a completely unbiased opinion when commentating. He will ( and what president wouldn't ) hype up feuds and new comers as often as he can. He will also throw in plugs for upcoming Supercards. Evans is often times a mediator when Roads pisses of Sharpe or Jones. B. Rex Roads Roads is a pure heel commentator who often cracks jokes. Roads also has certain favourites. Meaning not only does he like heels but he likes some heels better then others. From the UIWF's very first days he has always supported the stable known as Legalization Nation. He will often blatantly cheer on heel and boo a face. Roads has high hopes the LN will return in the UIWF's second incarnation. C. Bob Sharpe Bob Sharpe is a former wrestler and that is the point of view he presents. When adding in his two cents he will often " recall " what it was like back in " his days ". He also will enlighten the viewer as to what he thinks is most likely going through a wrestlers head at a certain point in the match. D. Jim Jones Jones is a technical man. He can explain almost any wrestling move and that is why he has been hired by the UIWF. Jones doesn't support heelish actions but isn't really a goody, goody. He appluads hard work and good wrestling, regardless of who does it. *Remember when writing. Jones and Sharpe are color commentary men. Not actual commentators!!! On average for every two or three comments CE and RR make BS and JJ should make one. This is crucial. ( Well not really, but I like to pretend. )* =-=-=-=-=-=-=-=> 4. Our Writing Style The UIWF has a preferred writing style. All writers will be expected to learn it, and at least try to use it. If you don't do a match in the standard of the UIWF ... well ... I guess I'll just have to live with it :) A. ABS, what it is, and how we modify it. ABS is short for Action Block System. Basically all, or a lot, of the match's action goes into brackets. More on this below. ABS style means that you put all the wrestling action in brackets. That means you do a series of moves enclosed in brackets [ ], and then stop every so often, to get a word in from the commentators. I guess you could say the UIWF uses modified ABS. We do have action in brackets but a lot more commentating then normal ABS would have. Here's an example of how UIWF matches should be written up. It's taken from a recent IWE match I wrote. it's been slightly modified to fit the UIWF's "format". [Doomsday, after sliding back into the ring, and Hopper lock up in the middle of the ring. Doomsday has the height advantage, this time Doomsday gets the advantage from the tie up in the form of a side headlock. Hopper slowly reaches out towards the ropes, not quite reaching them. Chris stretches even further ... even dragging Doomsday and inch or two and grabs the ropes. Referee Billy Jones calls for a break.] BJ : Hopper wary, and he steps off the ropes... MASSIVE CHOP! Doomsday with a huge hand across the chest of Chris Hopper. [Loud "WHOO"'s can be heard throughout the arena.] BJ : And again! MJ : WHOO! [Hopper is sent into the ropes and grabs on to them, sticking half his body to the outside. Doomsday waits again, arms raised as Billy Jones watches. As soon as Hopper steps off the ropes, Doomsday hits him again with a chop, the sound echoing across the confines of the IWE Coliseum.] MJ : WHOO! [Matt Jones seems to be synchronizing his "WHOO"s with those of the crowd.] BJ : Look at the chest of Chris Hopper... it's beat red! MJ : Red's a good color. If I see red in a wrestling match it usually means blood has been shed. *THIS*, is a good thing. [Doomsday steps back... and this gives Hopper the opportunity to catch him with a kick to the gut! He grabs Doomsday and hurls him up against the ropes, and begins laying into the big man with hard blows! Doomsday reels before Hopper whips him to the opposite ropes sides steps him, grabs him as he walks by, lifts him up and crashes him down into a back breaker. Doomsday picks Hopper up and delivers another crushing backbreaker.] =-=-=-=-=-=-=-=> 5. Extras Of course, this is not all that makes you a great writer. It's a good start but not all There are other things that great writers add on that make them the great writers they are. Things such as: * Crowd interaction Make the crowd participate. Write "Big Face/Heel Pop" when a great move is executed, or a wrestler does something that the fans like, or dislike. Have the crowd chant, "ROBERTS SUCKS" or " Let's go Turken " during the match. Don't have a dull crowd during your matches. * Using [] Call me a nit picker, but it looks nice when everyone in the fed uses a certain type of brackets. I prefer [] over () or <>. * Abbreviating names like so CE : Abbreviate the characters name to 4 spaces or less. Space the colon to the 5th space. Here are some examples. N : CE : CKC : FDLM: * Using a Spellchecker Using a spellchecker helps. I spellcheck things as it is but if you spellcheck your work before sending it to me it cuts down on work for me :-) There are endless amounts of things that make a great writer. Use your imagination. Please include some extras in your matches, and help make the UIWF become the best that it can be! =-=-=-=-=-=-=-=> 6. Conclusion In conclusion, I want to wish you a good luck in your hopefully successful writing career with the Universal Internet Wrestling Federation. After reading this, if you're still interested, please e-mail me at: [detail withheld] because the UIWF is always interested in picking up more match writers.
