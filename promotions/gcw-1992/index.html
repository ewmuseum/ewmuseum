An e-mail wrestling league of about forty singles wrestlers and twenty-five tag teams, running from September 1992 on rock-star and cartoon gimmicks with belts in four divisions and no televised wrestlers at all — and not one post of its own survives. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

On 23 November 1992, in the middle of an argument about whether fantasy wrestling belonged on rec.sport.pro-wrestling at all, one poster listed the federations then running: “I don't see what it's held on a pedastal above the EMWA, UeWF, PWA, NEWA, ILIW, GCW, SWL or WeWF.” GCW is the sixth of the eight that sentence sets beside the FWF, and the seventh of the nine it names in all. For four years that sentence was the whole of this wing.

It is Global Championship Wrestling. The federation's own writer dated its start to September 1992 — two months before that list — when he canvassed every other e-mail wrestling administrator on the newsgroup for the same fact about their leagues, and offered his own as the worked example. Six months before that he had written the hobby its first directory, five leagues one after another, and given GCW the second entry: “This promotion is just finishing up it's first year.”

What that entry describes is a league of roughly forty singles wrestlers and twenty-five tag teams, sending out a telecast and a mid-week update at most twice a week and a SuperCard every month to six weeks, with eight belts across four divisions — World, Pan American, Euro-African and Austral-Asian, in singles and tag. Six men held the World title in eight months without a repeat; one tag team held the World tag belts for all but seven days of the first six. Not one of the seven is named. The federation booked no televised wrestlers at all, by its writer's flat policy, and put Elvis Presley, Jimi Hendrix, two Simpsons, a Smurf and H. Ross Perot in the ring instead. Its roster size swung so hard that it went from the largest of the five known leagues to the third largest inside a week. And it had, its own writer noted, yet to admit to its viewers that wrestling is fake.

In August 1995 a handler asked the newsgroup what had become of the names the old-timers would remember, and put the Smurfs on his list. The answer came back: dead, and hopefully the federation's full weight fell on them when it finally collapsed. Papa had retired; Hefty and Jammin' Jason were last seen being destroyed by the Headbangers at one of the Road Warrior Cups. That is the only GCW match anybody wrote down, and it is a joke told at a wake. The following year the community's own hall-of-fame ballots were still grouping GCW with the UeWF and the UFWS — great in their day, now dead.

Not one post of this federation's own survives. The complete rec.games.pbm thread index this project holds — 19,369 threads across fifteen years — carries no GCW report thread, and neither does the rec.sport.pro-wrestling index. Everything above is somebody else writing about it: a directory, a canvass, a thread of old-timers, a ballot. The federation ran for something over two years and left the paperwork to other people.

The same three letters run through the newsgroup that year in another sense entirely: readers reminiscing about Georgia Championship Wrestling, its tag titles, and a secret hair-removing-cream angle nobody had topped since. Those are the real promotion, not this one. Six other federations on this project's frontier have carried the same acronym or the same full name since; none of them is this one either.
