The Story of The E 1.1 Why You Should Join Non-Kayfabe Hello, allow me to introduce myself -- although it is irrelevant, because you already know who I am, and are fully aware of my qualifications -- My name is
[name withheld] and I ran LAW for 3 years, from the AOL font leagues, to the big time 'Net feds, and I had a tremendous amount of fun, joy, and headaches :)
I suppose, just as Wrestling goes, no retirement in E-wrestling is a permanent retirement, unless your an Adult or in College, so I am back. A new franchise has risen from the depths. I call it American One Wrestling, based out of North America, but mainly the Mid-West. Your asking yourself "Why should I join?", well let me tell you why. Because I will try my hardest, to insure that you have a good time, and remember that it's only a game, and that we're in it for the joy, and not for the headache of fighting over losing a match. We also have a back-up plan to insure that we go for a long time. With a field of writers, and a Vice-president, A1 Wrestling won't be just run by me, so you are guaranteed a good show.
1.2 How did it get created? Kayfabe American One Wrestling is a fantasy wrestling federation, created by Millionaire Christopher Chicago III, who resides in Pepperpike, Ohio. He gave 80% of the company to his wife Shannon, who will run the show. The decision was made to open A1, when Christopher got tired of his money sitting there and collecting dust and decided that it was time to see how far he would go with it. He got a TV Deal, Direct-TV PPV Deal, and opened up A1 for fans in North America.
1.3 How Do I Know It Won't Close In Six Months? With the large staff of writers, and the Vice President I have helping me, and as long as you know that you have to role-play, and keep active with the promotion, then there should be no closing in Six months or anytime soon for that matter.
1.4 Is this a simmed or ABS fed? As always, this is in ABS format. ABS (Action Block System) means that the lion share of a match is summarized move for move within square brackets, and announcer comments are used to break up the Paragraphs. For example...
DF - Irish Whip into the ropes goes Bo Bo. LM - Strong clothesline by Mighty Joe on Bo Bo! [Mighty Joe lifts Bo Bo to his feet, and lifts him upside down on the turnbuckle. Mighty Joe runs to the other corner, jets off and connects with a dropkick in stereo, sending the limping body of Bo Bo to the mat in a heap of pain.]
DF - Oh my, somebody call his dentist for an appointment! Bo Bo took a bad dropkick to the face while hanging freely on the turnbuckle corner!
LM - Unbelievable, Dick! The actual league style used is similar to that of the WWF or WCW, with great emphasis on storylines and vocal characters. Those who have the Heat with the fans are generally going to be more successful.
Thanks to UWF's own Mike Beeby for use of that ABS info. 2. The Staff 1.1 Who Calls The Matches 1. Steve Capshaw Steve Capshaw, 24 years old. Prescription glasses, brown hair, average look. Steve is a former Hockey League announcer for the Long Beach Ice Dawgs in California. He is well liked, and has been seen on many local TV News station, and has gotten lots of acclaim for the way he calls Hockey games. It should be interesting to see if he can bring all that energy to the AOW.
2. "Bags" Brown Thomas "Bags" Brown. Chubby, blonde short hair, dark eyes, mustache. Tommy is New York's filthiest comic. Getting the nickname "Bags", from his routine in New York, where he spends his whole time bagging on everybody in the room. 1.2 Who Runs The Show? 1. Shannon The Bosses Wife. 5 foot 5, 105 pounds, long blonde hair, blue eyes, a hint of freckles, a devious, sexy smile. She owns most of the company, and will do whatever it takes to get respect from the Locker Room [nudge, nudge, wink, wink]. Remember, she signs the checks, makes the matches, and runs the show.
1.3 Who interviews the wrestlers backstage? That job is secured by Tony Senera, the "Italian Rapper". Seems he has a Rapping background, as he rapped at several LA Party Clubs, and is most popular with his freestyle. He tells it straight, and sometimes that gets him in trouble.
1.4 Who rings the bell? Ring Announcer Martin Taylor rings the bell, and announces the wrestlers as they make their way towards the ring. He has had jobs before, ring announcing
Boxing Fights, LAW Cards, and many other performances. 1.5 Who counts to 3? Author Daniels; Middle-aged, a senior referee for the now defunct British Wrestling Federation. Has lots of experience in the wrestling game, and looks to train other referee's as they head to the AOW.
3. Titles 1.1 Singles Titles A1 Wrestling World Heavyweight Title The cream of the crop in the AOW. Only the ones who work hard, sending role-plays, strats, in-rings, really contribute to not only the show, but the character earn it, bottom line. The World Title represents greatness, and wearing this title effectively makes you the top dog.
AOW Wrestling Commonwealth Title Secondary title. Before you become top dawg, you get elevated, wearing this title. The title will be defended every week on AOW Television and PPV's.
AOW Wrestling Triple Crown Juniorheavyweight Title For those, who fit this description, you earn three titles. The BWF Lightheavyweight Title, the LAW Cruiserweight Title, and the AOW Skyweight. This is held by the best in the division. 1.2 Tag Team Titles AOW World Tag Team Titles Much like the singles belt, the best team in the league is the one possessing these belts. Any two wrestlers can wear these, whether or not the team is established, or if a pair of singles wrestlers team up. 4. Special Shows 1.1 SuperCards SuperCards are a special kind of Television Taping with titles in it. The names are randomly chosen, and happen every month, usually with a big batch of
Pay-Per-View Caliber matches. The SuperCard matches will air on Sports Net TV. AOW TV will air on Saturday's (sometimes :P), and the RING REPORT, which is a basic show where angles are pushed and wrestlers are elevated will air usually on Monday's and run down AOW TV's show with summarized matches as well. More info will be given at the Press Conference sometime during the middle of May.
1.2 Pay-Per-Views Pay-Per-Views are once every month starting in June. In May, we will have a Press Conference (as stated above.) , with a Q & A, and at the very end of that Q & A, Christopher Chicago III will announce the first show, in which two titles will be up for grabs.
6. The Commandments Thanks to Mike Beeby for allowing us to use this very important guide that should help you to a fun stay with the AOW.
1.1 Role-playing The EMPIRE does not impose a limit on the number of flashes from each character per week. _HOWEVER_, chances are if you are scheduled for action and don't send in a flash, you won't be getting a check mark
In the win column. Anyone who fails to produce flashes regularly, or remains silent for long periods of time, will be ejected from the league.
In actual flashes and role-plays, swearing should be kept to a minimum. Unless absolutely necessary (and by that, agreement on the management's part is required), nothing stronger than ass should be said. If necessary, use [Bleep] in place of most swearing. Nothing that could get a show kicked off the air by the FCC should be used (racism, constant swearing, etc.).
Wrestlers are _NOT_ permitted to physically attack any and all officials, ring announcers, television announcers or interview personalities _UNLESS_ you receive permission.
Stylized text is _NOT_ used in the AOW. One exclamation point is usually enough, and adding extra ones will only hold up the shows as they're edited out anyway.
1.2 Strategies Strategies, or strats for short, are needed in order to win matches. It's up to you which kind of strategy you send in, a long drawn out one or a simple paragraph or two. "I wanna win, 'cause it'd be cool," is _NOT_ a strategy. Focus on your opponent's weakness, and explain how your wrestler plans to wrestle against them. Failure to send in strats for long periods of time will result in a league ejection, much like non-flashing wrestlers.
1.3 Angles The mark of a good league is how well it's storylines and angles are thought out. Angles are what determines in most cases how and what matches are scheduled for the week. Flashes and strategies are dependent on good angles, otherwise proposed attacks may not be run unless they're for a specific purpose.
1.4 Match Booking The easiest way to get matches: ask for them! Sending in challenges to other wrestlers are looked favorably on, and in most cases will be scheduled whenever possible. Again angles come into play, and those challenges that seem to "make sense" will be given preference in schedule dating. Title matches usually are given to those who prove themselves in past matches, and simply asking for a title match without reason (or storyline) won't get you one, guaranteed.
