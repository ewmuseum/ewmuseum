TSW FAQ

First draft of the TSW FAQ. Yes, I realize that there is no material concerning tag teams in the FAQ. We are accepting tag applications;
however, I'd rather not have the division, at the start, if there isn't a handful of teams.
------------- Tri-State Wrestling FAQ v. 1.0 ------------- a. What is TSW? b. Who's in charge? c. Who's in charge, kayfabe? d. What's the scheduling, which shows does TSW put out, and how are things done? e. What are the rules? f. What can I get my grubby hands on? g. How do I sign up? ------------ a. What is TSW? TSW, or Tri-State Wrestling, is a regional promotion centered in the
New York/Pennsylvania/New Jersey tri-state area. A newcomer to the wrestling world, TSW was founded by Mister Frank Albright, a steel foundry worker that just happened to hit the New York State Lotto to the tune of nearly half a billion dollars. He's sinking a lot of his newly-found money into the fledgling promotion, in the hopes that he can fufill his dreams of being a star in the business by getting TSW off the ground. The promotion has recently signed a television deal with Esper Sportsnet for distribution nationally, as well as being in the process of dotting a PPV contract.
------------ b. Who's in charge? TSW's fedhead is [name withheld]. At the current moment, he's manning the ship alone, but is looking to add staff in support capacity following
TSW's getting off the ground.
------------ c. Who's in charge, kayfabe? Albright, a long-time fan, has assumed the role of general president of operations of the promotion. He has, however, hired on his son,
David Albright, to handle the entertainment, booking, and management aspects on on the wrestling end of the promotion.David's official title is "Vice-President of Production." A tough kid, growing up in philly, David's a little slow, but he's a hard worker that doesn't mind getting his hands dirty. David is also the play-by-play announcer.
Sittng alongside David in the announce booth is George Douglas, TSW's color commentator. An former football standout for the Syracuse
Orangemen, Douglas had a brief stint as a sports announcer before agreeing to help out his childhood friend Frank. Although he's a bit nervous trading barbs with his boss, George tends to speak his mind around a developing sense of humor.
TSW has a crew of five hired officials, headed by head referee Stanley
Kane. Stanley's Irish-Catholic, short, and a bit rotund, but he has sharp eyes, and is an excellent official (as is the rest of the crew).
As of now, there is no official on air interviewer personality, as
Frank Albright is experimenting with letting the wrestlers tape their own promotional material.
----------- d. What's the scheduling, which shows does TSW put out, and how are things done? Okay. I'm tentatively working on a two shows per month schedule, with two flash packets distributed between shows. The first packet of each cycle is mandatory. A few days after the first packet arrives in your mailbox, you'll be asked to strat for your character, followed by a second, non-mandatory, though encouraged, flash packet. Basically, this give you about a five day time frame to flash after a card, followed by a two or three day period to get in strats, leaving match writers and myself a week or so to get the card put together and out to you.
TSW's flagship show is TSW Kill Switch, and its promotional show is entitled TSW Malicious Intent.
---------- e. What are the rules? I'm trying to be as lax as possible with the rules. The only thing I really ask is that you flash and strat. If you can't, let me know.
Don't let extended periods of time pass without notice to me. As far as everything else goes, use your noggin'. Don't get too frisky with characters you don't handle unless you have permission. Cursing is allowed, and no, you don't have to censor yourself, but that is NOT a license to turn yourself into a bad Def Comedy Jam.
--------- f. What can I get my grubby hands on? The TSW Heavyweight Championship This is the big one for singles. The belt must be defended once a month, and at all PPVs (should PPVs materialize). The champion chooses his defenses, although the contendership is decided by higher authority.
The TSW Triple Crown Championship A wild card, some might consider this belt a little more prestigious than any other, as it's contendership is open to all weight categories. Must be defended once a month, and at all PPVs, with the same stipulations as those attached to the Heavyweight championship.
The TSW Superlight Championship The equivalent of a light heavy/cruiserweight championship, this belt is defended with the above stipulations.
