And to All a Good Night

21 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Christmas The snow fell in dreary flights, laying to rest atop the pavement. The car was off and silent, sitting still as the powder from above came to rest, burying the vehicle in a grave of white sand. Inside, mist rose into the air, emanating from the warm breath expelled from the lungs of a weary young man. He looked at it drift away into the sky, and imagined it as his life flying away, never to return. Devin closed his eyes, and tried to remember this holiday’s name. Chris mass? Was that what it was called? The cars had all long since stopped driving through the streets. The city itself seemed to be forsaken. Forgotten as the day of all hopes and dreams was among the world. The lights had all been turned out. The dreams of a million slumbering children wandered through a land of toys and fun, where no one ever told them to stop. Parents smiled and sat together, with only love and a fireplace to keep them warm. The pendulums of a million clocks clicked with the countdown. And when they all awoke, the tree would be haven to all those fantasies, locked up inside a prison that only the hands of a small child could save them from. The shackles of wrapping paper would be thrown aside, and tossed out like the days spent waiting for their dreams to come true. Inside they would find it all, waiting for them, shining as the day it was made. A million glowing teeth will shine through, and a million kisses embraced with loved ones. A feast would be served, the smell of a plump bird filling the air. The table would be decorated with plates, utensils, glasses, wine. A smiling family would sit around, laughing and saying Grace, and giving thanks to God for everything they had. And there Devin was, staring at the world outside, cold and desolate, all of humanity warm in their beds. Their dreams lift them away into heaven, while others sit alone, their dying watches reminding them how close the end is, and how much longer they have. In the corner of the back was a small, lifeless little Christmas tree. Days earlier, he had stolen it off the lot of an unfamiliar little business that overcharged anyway. He had taken it, walked away with it almost effortlessly, and stood it, tall and proud, in the back of his station wagon. A few ornaments of fake pearls he bought off the street and cutouts from a newspaper he found decorated his tree. His eyes stared at it, lost in its wonder, like an unmarked tombstone. His heart ached, knowing another holiday went by without happiness. His finger wrote the words clearly on the window. Merry Christmas to all And to all A good night Crying would have been the normal thing in this case, but Devin decided that it was too cold for that right now. Instead, he leaned back against the door of the backseat, and wrapped himself in the red blanket he had used for all the years of his life. His eyes gave way to sleep. But when he awoke, there was nothing there for him under his tree. The snow had stopped by now. But, deep inside, Devin could still feel it falling.
