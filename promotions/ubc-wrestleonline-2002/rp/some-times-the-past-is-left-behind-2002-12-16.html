Some Times The Past Is Left Behind

16 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: The Summer of '92/The Spring of '93 The victory had been short, but ever so sweet. His celebration with his new friends was just more excitement added to his already blissful mood. The sun was setting, and his time for his departure unknowingly nearing, but none of that mattered. He was sitting with his friends, eating some hot dogs and drinking some sodas, talking through the setting sun. The park was now shadows lit dimly by white neon lights. The stars were probably out, twinkling away, but who could tell. In the distance, the beautiful buildings towered over the world, their million eyes blinking on and off. It was ten o’clock now. The boys were leaving one by one, and there was only him, Ben and Sam left when he remembered that he had to meet Wendall at eight. “Shit guys, I gotta leave.” “See ya Devin.” said Ben, before returning to his already set conversation with Sam. Devin began to run, before Ben yelled back to him. “Think you can come back tomorrow Dev?” he asked. Devin heard the question, and realized for the first time that he wouldn’t be coming back. Boston was next on their list, and he wouldn’t be seeing New York City for God knows how long. He wouldn’t be coming back to the park tomorrow, or the day after, or the week after. He didn’t know if he would ever come back. “Yeah, I’ll try guys. See ya later.” Devin turned around, and walked through the park. Wendall was on a bench, waiting for Devin with a slightly aggravated face. The two got in the station wagon and drove away, leaving the park behind. A year would pass. Devin would return to New York City in late spring, and run into the park. To the same spot where he had knocked that guy done. What was his name again? Oh yeah. Jake. Except this time, there was no one their. Ben wasn’t throwing the football with any of the guys. Sam wasn’t running like the wind to catch the ball. Only two people, well in their twenties, played catch with a Frisbee. Devin walked away that day, a memory of victory still hanging in his mind.
