Some Times The Past Is Warm

16 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: The Summer of '92 It was the summer of 1992. A warm day, Devin recalled. The dark and lonesome future was only a few years away, but for now, the young and somewhat happy Devin Stillwater was only a thirteen year old teenager. The world was still a good place. The hatred of his heart was still too well hidden to make any emotions just yet. There was only the naïve innocence of a boy, a mind unscathed by humanity, and there was still an eternity left to enjoy. Central Park was green and beautiful. Devin had been there once before, but he was only seven at the time. Far too young to see it for what it was. He had never seen a forest so big within the confines of a city. It was like something out of a dream. He wanted to run around and explore. He wanted to see the wonderland from every point of view. He wanted to dive into the fountain and climb the trees. He wanted to live there forever. “Go run off Devin. I’m sure you can take care of yourself for a few hours. Look at your watch Devin. What time is it?” “Noon. Can I get going yet?” “Meet back here at eight o’clock. Ok?” “Yeah, yeah whatever. Can I or what?” “Yeah, sure. Go have fun.” Devin didn’t really hear the whole sentence. His feet were on the move half way through the “Yeah”. He thought that he heard Wendall say something else to him, but his ears didn’t pick it up too well. The park was alive. The citizens of New York City were all around him. Walking, running, talking, staring, waving, smiling, kissing, laughing. They were all human, and, as far as Devin could understand, all of them were good people. No sins were ever committed. No evilness existed in their hearts. They were all good people. Every last one of them. Devin ran around for a good hour, taking in all the sights the park had to offer a young man. It was everything that he imagined it would be and more. His eyes caught the sight of a large group of kids his age tossing a football around. One of them, a large, brown haired kid, stood tall over all the others. His body looked ripped. His face red with rage. His actions as violent as a professional. He was playing for keeps. Devin sat down and watched them play for a little while, each time this one kid either catching the ball or ramming a stampede into the kid that had the ball. One of them, one much smaller then he was, got the ball, and he knocked him some ten feet. The phrase “Fuck man! The fuck’s your problem?” came from the mouth of the kid who had thrown the ball to the shorter one. A few words were thrown back and forth, and a fight almost broke out, but the other boys were able to split them up. The smaller boy was hurt. He couldn’t walk on his left leg. The other boys on his team got into a conversation, and tried to figure out what they would do. The leader of the team looked over around, and stopped when he saw Devin sitting in the grass, staring down at them. “Hey you!” Devin looked over at him, surprised and a little appreciative of the attention. “Me?” he asked, already sure of the answer. “Yeah you man. You want to play?” “Sure!”
