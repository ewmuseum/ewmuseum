Going Home

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/9 Nick Walker’s big black Escalade can’t do 140, but he’s trying. On this crystal clear night in Florida, he is driving an unmarked stretch of highway just southeast of Miami. There is no speed limit, so he is free to drive as fast as he wants. Of course, he’s flooring it, doing 90, posing no danger to other cars on the road only because there are none. We see him at the wheel. Periodically, he sips on a 20 oz. Pepsi. He’s focused. That same far off look in his eyes that he gets when he strides to the ring is present now. The CD in the SUV’s player is Pearl Jam’s Live On Two Legs, one of the best live albums ever. Nick isn’t typically a big fan of music like this, he prefers rap, hip-hop, but this music can soothe him in a way the latter cannot. Track 11 finishes, and gives way to Track 12, Nick’s favorite PJ tune ever. Well, OK, maybe that’s not true. He loves “Evenflow”, and considers “Last Kiss” and “Given to Fly” under appreciated classics. But this one is up there on his list. And as the beginning is played softly on the guitar, we are treated to a view before unseen by UBC viewers: Nick’s mind. He is sitting in a dark room. He sits on a cot, surrounded by cold concrete walls. Suddenly, pictures appear on the wall, and they give the room light, one by one, as they shine. Eddie Vedder’s voice begins the song, and as the song progresses, we progress through somewhat of a timeline of Nick Walker’s life. We see a photo of Nick and his brother, arms draped around each other, each in their respective pee-wee football uniforms, each sweaty and smiling to beat the band. (Once divided...nothing left to subtract...) Another picture, this of him in his high school basketball jersey, holding a trophy. The man to Nick’s left is unknown, but one might guess that when he reaches his 40’s, he’ll look like that. (Some words when spoken...can't be taken back...) We fade out. Dean’s lost in thought. The images in his mind have long been repressed. To him, there was no childhood. Only the period from late-1998 on remained clear to him in his memory. The pictures startle him. He steps harder on the gas pedal. (Walks on his own...with thoughts he can't help thinking... Future's above...but in the past he's slow and sinking...) Back to Nick’s mind. This photo is from Sports Illustrated, sometime in February of 2000. Nick, ludicrously attired in a green and orange duck outfit, the bill, and feathers, peers out at the reader. The headline reads: “THE HURRICANE: Primetime Walker may be the best collegiate player ever.” (Caught a bolt 'a lightnin'...cursed the day he let it go...) Then, a shot of Nick taking a walk on the Miami campus. This image moves. He walks along, waves to a few people. Most simply watch him go by. It is 2000. Dean was 22. He was supposed to be making millions. He was making barely enough to pay rent. (Nothingman... Nothingman... Isn't it something? Nothingman...) A door opens in Nick’s dark room, inside his mind. The room shines brighter than ever. A beautiful brunette walks in. His eyes protrude from his head. She gets to work hanging more pictures up on the wall. Most of the pictures are of Nick living the good life. There’s a picture of him with her. There’s another. There’s one of him with two teammates and a handful of drinks. (She once believed...in every story he had to tell... One day she stiffened...took the other side...) Nick’s jaw hangs. He wants to call to her, wants to wave to her. Most of all, he wants to embrace her, to tell her how he really feels, to have her back, for good. The words barely creep out of his mouth as a faint whisper. “Jessica…” She turns. For a second, she glances at him. His eyes are full of hope, but that sense is deflated. Jess turns back, hangs two more picture frames, and leaves. (Empty stares...from each corner of a shared prison cell... One just escapes...one's left inside the well... And he who forgets...will be destined to remember...oh...oh...oh... Nothingman... Nothingman... Isn't it something? Nothingman...) He looks at the new pictures. He reaches out and touches some of them. His facial expression is that of a lost soul, a troubled soul. He takes one down from the wall. It’s Nick and Jess after a baseball game. His hat is askew on his unruly black hair, and his uniform is completely plastered in dirt, meaning he must have played third base for that particular game. Jess is dressed in a new, clean, red dress. None of that means anything to her, and the picture shows her wrapped around him, their lips locked. Lightly, slowly, tears begin to fall upon the photo. (Oh, she don't want him... Oh, she won't feed him...after he's flown away... Oh, into the sun...ah, into the sun... Burn...burn...) In a fit of angry sadness, Nick spikes the picture on the hard floor. The frame splits in two. He then notices that the door was left slightly askew. He moves ever so slowly towards it, but stops short, looking at the two picture frames. One is him, on the cover of the College World Series program for 2001. The other is a simple picture of the UBC World Title belt. (Nothingman... Nothingman... Isn't it something? Nothingman.. Nothingman... Nothingman... Coulda' been something... Nothingman... Oh...ohh...ohh...) Smiling ever so slightly, he grabs both pictures off the wall, and exits the room. Back at the car, the real Nick also cracks a slight grin, and without applying the brakes, throws the car into a u-turn, driving off in the other direction. It’s time to head home.
