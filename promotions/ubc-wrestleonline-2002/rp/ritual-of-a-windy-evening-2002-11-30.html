Ritual of a Windy Evening

30 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11.30.01 Ritual – (rich-oo-al) n. a procedure regularly followed The wind, a drifter all its own, roamed the cursed streets of city. The only lights that were on were the streetlamps and the occasional bedroom lamp. The ghosts of headlights danced on the pavement into the abandoned alleys. The sewer caps lightly smoked into the winter air. Night was upon all, and would not let its grasp go easily. The heart of Devin Stillwater beat slowly, echoing only through the small area of his station wagon. A pen in his hand and a piece of paper on a box in the back seat, Devin wrote his blood and tears in a few mundane words etched in black ink. Crying away my nights Stripping away my frights From my soul I gaze Into a moon ablaze The life I lead A wound I bleed A fight I lose An array of blues The hours burn away Lost in another day The years ramble on Singing their lonely song I only wish upon this evening star That I could be who you are A man with a life Without a worry or a strife The sensation of a woman’s love The eyes of a God from above Making love through summer sunsets No pain or agonies or lusts or unrests Nothing to lose in your lives The world at your feet and the hope in your eyes The desire to be thee The envy running inside of me Run away from the edge Devoured below by this ledge I fall away into abyss Never again to be missed Eyes never watched me live Hands never anything to give You pushed me into the deep To drown in my eternal sleep Die in my anguished tomorrow Strangled by my ravenous sorrow I cry away all my dreams And fall apart at my crude seams Someday I will awake Only to meet my miserable fate Devin sat there, staring at the paper, taking it all in. He would remember it forever, but see it nevermore. His left hand rolled down the window, while his eyes remained locked onto the paper in his hands. He stuck it out the window, kept his grip as tight as he could momentarily, and then let it go. The wind took it as its own, and brought it along for its eternal ride. Devin watched it fly away, and then lost it as it reached the horizon. He wondered if anyway would ever read it. He hoped that someone would.
