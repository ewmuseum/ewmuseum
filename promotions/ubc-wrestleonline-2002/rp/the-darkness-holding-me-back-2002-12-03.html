The Darkness Holding Me Back

3 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12.3.02 Outside the confines of a symbolic prison, the sun’s long desolated haven lay in darkness. The buildings, silent and beckoning, watched with their glowing eyes. The pedestrians of nighttime were out and about, their voices carrying in the wind. No eyes watched the actions of the man inside. No souls carried any sympathy for his feelings. Nobody in the world could stare into his eyes and feel the pain he held deep. And that was something that he had to live with for as long as his lungs exhaled air. There were assorted things in the station wagon – a few pens and/or pencils here and there, newspapers of forgotten days, pictures he had cut out from discarded magazines, clothes that he wore, pillows that helped him sleep, blankets that kept him warm, shades that kept the outside world blind to his existence. And then, of course, there was the lifeless ghost of a human being that saw nothing when he looked at the world – Devin himself. His eyelids shut out the light of the nearby Store 24. Only the ears sensed the world, with the voices of the people walking in and out and the occasional ring as the door opened. Devin was going to buy some food that he had pick pocketed off some yuppie, but now he wasn’t that hungry. His mind needed some food of its own, and he wasn’t sure how it needed to be prepared. What do you want? What do I want? What do I need to end this hunger? How many fights must I win? How many fights must I lose? How many times must my back hit the ground? The life I always wanted, but could never have – maybe that’s what I need. But how…? What the fuck do you want from me? I am but a man, with no skill. Nothing to hold onto. No one to hold my hand down this darkened tunnel. This world has eaten those like me, spitting them up when they ravaged their stomachs. They have beaten them away, never again to feel the touch of love or hatred in their hearts. They have buried their body six feet under, waiting for the day when their soul can be carried away into oblivion. And yet, I see something that no one has given me – hope. There it is, shinning in the distance, calling my name, its cry echoing through the darkness behind me. I can see the glimmer in its eyes, groping the fears of my mind, and telling me that everything will be all right. I want to run over, and embrace it in my arms, but then I realize that I first must conquer the blackness behind me. How will I destroy this evil? This sinister hangover that hold my shoulder? I fear it. But somehow Someway I must end it And then maybe Just maybe … And slowly, as the midnight hour beckoned the city, Devin’s eyes gave way and his mind fell into a safe slumber, hidden away from all the evil in the world. His dreams told him that everything would be all right, as long as Devin could blind the darkness holding him back. Michael McCormick was one of its names. It would be the first he would encounter. The first he would have to end. The first soul he would have to destroy if he wanted any hope in reaching his life locked in the unknown forthcoming days. Devin was afraid. Devin was ready. Ready to take it all Or die trying. The fight would begin, and in the end, Devin would step out of the shadows and into the daylight of life.
