Nick Walker Biography Nicknames Primetime Hometown Miami, Florida Birthday June 12th, 1978 Height 6'4' Weight 245 Biography NCAA All-Time Records- ALL-TIME LEADERS: Most Homeruns, Career Walker, Nick (119), Miami, 97-01. Most Hits, Career Walker, Nick (598), Miami 97-01 Most RBIs, Career Walker, Nick (612), Miami 97-01 CWS CHAMPIONS: 2000 Miami def. Stanford, 3-2. MVP: Nick Walker, Miami Quite a resume for a kid who grew up in Miami, FL, with only his father to support him. When Nick was 8, his mother was killed in car accident. His father was left to manage the family, which included Nick and his brother Brandon. He had big dreams. He grew up playing baseball in the summers, football in the fall, and basketball in the winter. He continued this all the way up to Sunshine High School, in Miami. Nick lettered all four years: 3-point specialist for the basketball team, quarterback for the football team, and power slugger for the baseball team. Baseball, however, was Nick’s true calling. After high school, he committed to his hometown college, Miami, to play baseball. And play he did, lettering all four years. Nick was put in the starting line up at centerfield, earning the title of “Primetime”. Whenever ‘Canes games were close, Nick always seemed to be at the plate, and it was all “Primetime” from there on. His first two years, the Hurricanes were in the process of a rebuilding period. It culminated in 2000 with a College World Series title victory over Stanford. Nick, who got the winning homerun in the title game, was named the tournament MVP. The next year, it looked to be more of the same. Scouts drooled over Nick's golden bat and his tremendous glove. He went the entire regular season without striking out or making an error. The Hurricanes made it back to the CWS title, facing LSU. With the ‘Canes up 6-5, there was a man on second and third and the batter hit the ball, what should have been a routine catch for Nick, but he dropped it allowing the runners on third and second to score securing the victory in the bottom of the ninth. Little did he know as he walked off the field, head down, that it would be for the last time. Nick went in the 1st round of the pro draft: number 1, to the Minnesota Twins. But his agent was unable to swing a deal. He became a free agent, but he had already gained the unfair reputation of being hard to negotiate with, and went the whole year unsigned. Nick’s career was over. The next few years are a blank. Shortly after the 2001 College World Series, he and his father got into a heated argument that saw him leave home for the final time. He drifted around the state of Florida, working in baseball training facilities; secretly hoping for the big chance that would never come. On a whim, he enrolled himself at a dojo in Miami, expecting to keep in shape by doing some lifting and treadmill work. What he found was a wrestling ring, and a new dream to pursue. In late 2002, UBC came calling after seeing Nick perform in indy shows in Miami. After a brief stint in another league, he was promoted to the big time. Nick’s future certainly looks bright. After all, he's been to the top before.
