Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

MBC's surviving record opens mid-run: its 17 May 1998 broadcast from Philadelphia numbers itself “Up Your Arse 5: Overkill,” implying an earlier, undocumented run behind it. The surviving reading covers only the show's own hype and its card rundown — an AWC World Heavyweight Championship challenge, a Team Bastard Championship tag defense, a Stanley Cup tournament final, and the fed's own namesake title, the Mighty Bastard Championship, defended by the Disgruntled Postal Worker against Simon O'Neal — breaking off before the first bell. No result from that night survives.

The promotion staged its own end on camera. A broadcast its own retrospective dates to December 2001 and titles “Final Night” closed with its wrestlers and broadcast team eulogizing it live: “Feds come and feds go, but MBC is different,” one said; another, “like the sun, we know the MBC will rise again. It always does.”

It did. At another broadcast that March, the wrestler Jessica “Fatality” Marshall announced on camera that she had bought the land MBC once stood on and would reopen it under her own ownership. That August, MBC returned as promised, its own broadcast noting most of the old production staff gone and Marshall installed as President and Owner. The reopening card crowned Satin Sheets by submission over her opponent, saw “Hentai” Ryu Osawa and Uriah Hawkins win a tag bout, and put Comedy over Felicity Malone for a shot at the Women's Championship.

Broadcasts recur in the record through at least 13 July 2006, that night's own card giving a hardcore-circuit newcomer, Johnathan “The Beast” Cable, a debut win, and putting over Mariko Shimada and “The Fantasy” Brett Young elsewhere on the show.

No closing statement survives after that night.
