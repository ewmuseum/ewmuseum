Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

SCWF was born on 4 March 2004, the same night the European Wrestling Alliance closed at its own final show — a new promotion built from its ashes by EWA's own co-owners, Joe Stock and Mark Harrington, explicitly billed by Harrington at launch as “taking the WWA on… in the ratings wars and in the ring.” It ran its own weekly “Glory” show, its own “Pain Games” gimmick battle royal, and its own SCWF Championship, won at debut by Rocky Adams and later held by Armaan. No independent SCWF site was captured in this pass — its recovered record survives entirely as coverage on WWA's own site, plus four promos one of its wrestlers saved from his own time there.

The rivalry was short-lived: cancelled outright on 1 June 2004, its remaining roster redistributed, and on 14 July 2004 folded — alongside the Northern Wrestling Conference and LDA — into one unified banner by WWA's own operator. Whether that ending reflects a genuine rival finally absorbed, or a spin-off that shared an owner all along, the surviving record doesn't settle (see Provenance, and this wing's own entry in data/promotions.json, for both readings).

Its most complete surviving artifacts are four promos by one of its signed wrestlers, working through a month of confusion, rage, and a one-man strike over being left out of its own battle royal — an artifact of what it was like to be a small, disgruntled part of SCWF's own roster while it lasted. See Roleplays for the full texts.

Crossover names checked

“The Bastard” Al Crud is name-dropped in one of the four surviving promos (“A Plea For Help”) — already a registered entity, referenced not present, alongside Shane Stone and the Phenomenons in the same breath. “Movement” names GIWF's own Evil as a rival who took a main-event slot Eric wanted, alongside the Phenomenal Army; “Hooligans and Shenanigans” names WWA's own Brad Polaris as the wrestler Eric imagines got “switched” with him — already-registered entities from Eric's own prior and concurrent careers, referenced not present. That same promo introduces two flavor names that stay deliberately unminted — Draco and “the Chosen One,” penciled-in opponents in a card whose own shape keeps changing mid-conversation — neither crosses the dead-only-rule/registry threshold, and “the Chosen One” happens to share a bare name with an unrelated, already-registered 1995 CSWA wrestler, a same-name coincidence rather than a shared referent. SCWF's own co-owners, Joe Stock and Mark Harrington, are recorded here as named kayfabe business figures — the same business-role sense a promotion's own credited operator is named elsewhere in the registry — not as registered characters; whether either shares an identity with the already-registered WWA characters of the same name (a referee active 1999–2000; a WWA authority figure whose own later dialogue names him a former WWA owner) is unresolved on the corpus in hand and left for a dedicated identity review. No other SCWF-side name (Rocky Adams, Armaan) matches anything else in the registry.

World Wrestling Alliance (WWA, 1997–2019)

Rivalry with SCWF (Squared Circle, 2004) (4 Mar – 14 Jul 2004)
Billed at SCWF’s own 4 Mar 2004 launch as “taking the WWA on... in the ratings wars and in the ring” — staged competition, not a shared house brand. The two were reunified under WWA’s own banner on 14 Jul 2004; sources disagree on whether the rivalry sat over genuinely separate ownership or the same one.

EWA (European Wrestling Alliance)

Shared continuity with SCWF (Squared Circle, 2004)

Eric Cameron's own promo "Movement" independently narrates the same handoff in fiction (his booking transferring from the just-closed EWA to the newly-announced SCWF).
