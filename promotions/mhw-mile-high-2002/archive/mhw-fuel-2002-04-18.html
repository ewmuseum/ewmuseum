[MHW] Fuel [4/18/02]

18 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
--Mile High Wrestling Mailing List-- MHW Fuel April 18, 2002 Taped from the Gerald R. Ford Amphitheatre Vail, Colorado *************** Xu Jing Segment *************** [The camera shows the main entrance for the wrestlers. "The Asian Sensation" Xu Jing approaches two security guards. He shows them an official Mile High Wrestling ID and they let him proceed.] [As he walks through the corridor, he is approached by William Waterford, his manager. Waterford and Jing exchange a few words in Chinese. Then, Jing walks away, presumably toward his locker room.] WW: Training? Getting accustomed to the altitude? He hitchhiked fifty miles to get here? [groans] This guy doesn't seem like my kind of wrestler. I don't want him giving me a run-around like this all the time. [Waterford then walks off screen.] *************** [The scene fades in with a shot of a tree-line in a forest. It's about Midnight. A silhouette approaches the camera. He has just finished pouring an unknown liquid on the ground, making a long trail as he went.] Voice: The time has come for the MHW to make its presence felt. [The man lights a match.] Voice: The time has come for Mile High Wrestling to ignite the fuels. [The man tosses the burning match into the liquid. The liquid, which we realize is gasoline, quickly starts to burn. It follows the path the man set for it and quickly gains speed as it twists and turns around the forest floor.] Voice: A new era in professional wrestling has begun. Welcome, to the MHW's Fuel. [The burning trail of fire turns toward the camera and races towards us. It quickly engulfs the screen. From within the flames, a logo is barely seen. It is the logo of the MHW. The scene then cuts to shots of various MHW stars as they conduct their promos. The Prodigy's "Firestarter" is heard thumping out its breakbeats in the background. The scene then cuts to the studios at MHW Headquarters. A man and woman sit behind a desk. The man, in his mid 50s, is well dressed in a brown suit and sports coat. The woman, young in age, wears a black t-shirt with the MHW logo on the front.] Man: Good evening everyone, and welcome to MHW Fuel. I'm Monty Hemrick. Woman: And I'm Nancy Fletcher. Monty: Tonight we'll show you what happened at the MHW tapings. This week they were from the Gerald R. Ford Amphitheatre in Vail. Nancy: Considering our current roster size here in the MHW, there were only two matches this week on Fuel. Monty: First off, "The Asian Sensation" Xu Jing faces off against "The Educator" Thane Sloan. Nancy: We just saw as this show came on the air that William Waterford and Xu finally caught up with one another. Personally, I don't blame Waterford for feeling angry. I mean, Jing is lucky. and he has just spent the past week wandering around the woods or whatever. I just think Waterford has every right to be angry with him. Jing better learn to show a little respect. Monty: That happened earlier today as the two men were entering the Amphitheatre grounds. We're about to show you the first match of the evening. But before we do that, Sean Malcomb had this to say... ******************** Sean Malcomb Segment ******************** [The lights in the amphitheater raise to blanket the whole arena in a bright, white light. "Walkin' on the Sun" by Smashmouth begins to play. The crowd gives a large pop, as decent as it gets with a light crowd. The lights die down to normal lighting and Sean Malcomb walks out of the enrtryway and heads toward the ring. He smiles to the fans as they cheer him. He walks to the ring and climbs inside. He then claims the microphone from Dave Madison. He smiles out to the crowd again before he speaks.] Sean Malcomb: Damn it feels good to be back in a ring! [The crowd pops a bit louder.] Sean Malcomb: I believe this is my first trip to Vail in my career. But looking around, I don't know why it's my first. Looks like Vail has some great wrestling fans out here, I can feel your energy tonight. [The crowd pops very loudly.] Sean Malcomb: And I'll need your energy tonight folks, I may have had my share of glory before, but I've been away for a bit. Sorting out everything outside of the ring. But tonight, I'm up against someone who looks like a good athlete, and I may need every bit of energy you fans are willing to share with me tonight. A man like River Rockwell has to be respected for his confidence to say the least. [A light round of boos rolls over the crowd.] Sean Malcomb: I may be the bigger man, but I've fallen before. I may be the stronger man, but I've been worn down before. I may even be the most experienced, but I've been dropped by upstarts before. All I can promise is that every match, every single time you see me out there, I'll be giving it my all. Everything I've got, for myself, my family, and you, the fans. [The crowd cheers again.] Sean Malcomb: But you see, the way I work is on focus. If something troubles me in my life, I can't focus on the ring as much. If I'm at peace, I can focus and move fluidly to defeat my obstacle. However, when something in my life is threatened, in any way, but what I see in the ring...rage sets in. I'm in this profession to work hard and earn my spot as an athlete. I've been around long enough to know that nothing is sacred in your life if you become a celebrity. Well, I don't back down from anything that comes my way, and I won't give up. Now, Rockwell may be all foolish talk when you see him, maybe he's just that big of a moron. But I'll mention this once, and only once, Rockwell... Don't come near my family, or you'll end up hoping you stayed out of this way of life. You can joke all you want, you can do a song and dance about how you'll beat me from ringpost to ringpost eighty times on a single broadcast. Hell, I don't care if you beat up a midget dressed up like me. But your comments have driven me to give you this warning, and it's the only one you'll get. [Malcomb's face has now distorted into one of mild anger. That expression sits on his face for a few seconds then slowly lightens up to a smile.] Sean Malcomb: But tonight isn't about that...no, tonight is about starting one hell of a trail, blazing from one end to another, all over the great state of Colorado. Some of this fresh air is nice after all my time in Florida. Hell, I kind of feel like I'm vacation. But tonight won't be any relaxing get away. Tonight I enter the ring to wrestle once again, and with you fans behind me, charging me with your spirit tonight... [The crowd pops loudly again.] Sean Malcomb: ...I know I can make it back into my old form again. Now let's get on with the show, you people wanted some action tonight, and it's about time we started it all off, don't you think? [The crowd roars with anticipation.] Sean Malcomb: Alright, then let's get this going! ["Walkin' on the Sun" by Smashmouth begins to play as Malcomb hands the microphone back to Dave Madison. Malcomb then steps out of the ring and smiles to fans, slapping hands with a few as he exits. The crowd continues to cheer until he's disappeared into the entranceway.] ******************** *********************** Match 1 Xu Jing VS. Thane Sloan *********************** [The scene then cut directly to the ring introductions for the combatants in the first match. Dave "The Rave" Madison introduced Thane Sloan first. "Peaceful World" by John Mellencamp played as Sloan made his way to the ring. Madison then called down Xu Jing, who came out to "Hard as a Rock" by AC/DC and was accompanied by William Waterford. Jing tried to use his martial arts background from the beginning. Surprisingly, Sloan was able to block a swift kick to the gut. Sloan held onto Jing's leg but was soon hit with an enzugiri. Sloan quickly got back up but was met with a standing dropkick. Jing worked the crowd a little, performing various martial arts stances as he waited for Sloan to get up. As "The Educator" rose to his feet, Jing went for a high roundhouse kick. Sloan ducked it and tripped up "The Asian Sensation." Thane then pulled him up for a side suplex. The action went both ways for several minutes. The small gathering surrounding the ring was really into it though. Thane had locked on an ankle lock. But Jing quickly rolled through, connecting with a heel kick in the process. He then tried to apply an ankle lock of his own. However, his inexperience in technical wrestling prevented him from properly executing it. Sloan quickly made it to the ropes to break the hold. The two exchanged pinfall attempts for a while. Then Sloan whiped Jing off the ropes and connected with a spinebuster. He then pulled "The Asian Sensation" up and into a torture rack, preparing him for The Educator. But as he rotated it into the piledriver position, Jing flipped out and landed on his feet. He quickly locked in The Chinese Death Grip (a double trapezius nerve hold). Sloan had no choice but to submit. Winner: Xu Jing *********************** [The scene cuts back to Monty Hemrick and Nancy Fletcher at MHW Headquarters.] Monty: That was a great match. I thought Sloan would've won it with experience. But I guess the heart of Xu Jing proved stronger. Nancy: That's very true Monty. It's like William Waterford said during his first promo... "Experience goes hand-in-hand with old age." Perhaps Sloan is just getting old and can't handle it anymore. Monty: Are you trying to say that Jing isn't talented enough to beat Sloan? Nancy: Not at all. Jing is a very talented young man. I was just reiterating a thought that Waterford had. Monty: Mmm-hmmm. Next up folks, "The Atomic Son" Sean Malcomb will face off against "Premium" River Rockwell. Let's show you what happened. ******************************* Match 2 River Rockwell VS. Sean Malcomb ******************************* This match should been on a Pay-Per-View. Or at least a Firestorm. It was that good. These two men gave it their all. It was a fast-paced matchup from the word "go." Rockwell was first to enter the ring. He didn't wait long for Sean Malcomb to make his entrance. As Malcomb stepped into the ring, Rockwell attacked him. Rockwell was merciless in his attempts to keep Malcomb grounded. But "The Atomic Son" fought his way back, landing hard elbows that sent Rockwell reeling. Malcomb followed up with a vicious clothesline that nearly decapitated his opponent. He then pulled Rockwell up only to hit a snap suplex. Malcomb then droped the elbow and made a pin attempt. He only got two. Rockwell proved why he is called "Premium," using everything in his arsenal to hault Malcomb's offensive onslaught. Rockwell connected with a hurricanrana and then went to the top rope for a guillotine legdrop. Malcomb rolled out of the way however. Malcomb regained the attack from here, dominating Rockwell with several power moves. He executed a brutal German suplex and then locked on a Texas Cloverleaf. Rockwell was almost in tears as he struggled to escape the hold. The harder River struggled, the harder Malcomb tightened the hold. Rockwell was just about to tap out when he made one final lunge at the ropes. He made it, forcing Malcomb to relinquish the hold. Malcomb complied without complaint. He then pulled Rockwell to his feet, planted a boot in his gut, and buried his head in the canvas with a piledriver. "The Atomic Son" then went for a pinfall. Again Rockwell was able to escape, shocking the crowd. At this stage, you're probably counting River Rockwell out. Well don't. He fought back with a vengeance. He whipped Malcomb off the ropes and nailed him with a spear. From there, he hit a springboard moonsault using the middle rope. He immediately went for the pin, using a lateral press. At two Malcomb was able to get a shoulder up. Rockwell showed signs of frustration as he pounded the canvas. But he was quick to regain his focus, putting Malcomb in a sharpshooter. Malcomb tried his damndest to get the ropes. But each time, Rockwell would pull him farther away. Malcomb was thought to be unconscious as the referee raised his hand in an attempt to end the match. The arm went down once. The ref raised it a second time and it went down. Then the crowd exploded in an attempt to give Malcomb their energy. The referee raised the hand the final time. This time, it stayed up. The crowd got even louder. Malcomb pushed himself up with his hands, trying to ease the pressure on his legs. Slowly, but with great determination, he crawled over to the ropes, forcing Rockwell to release the hold. "Premium" was irate. He almost went as far as to hit the referee. However, Malcomb surprised him from behind, rolling him up for a pin attempt. Yet another two count. Rockwell was up first as Malcomb was feeling the pain in his weakened legs. Rockwell backed him into the ropes and tried to whip him across. It was reversed. As Rockwell bounced back, Malcomb hit him with a kick to the gut. He then executed the Atom Splitter (a double underhook piledriver) and went for the pin. Unfortunately, they were too close to the ropes. Rockwell wisely put his foot on the ropes and the count was stoped. Malcomb lost all focus and started pacing around the ring, shaking his head, wondering what else he could do to finish Rockwell off. As he turned around, Rockwell connected with another big spear. He then waited as Malcomb struggled to his feet. Rockwell bounced off the ropes and hit the Resolution (an inverted Evenflow DDT). He hooked Malcomb's leg and got the one - two - three.] Winner: River Rockwell ******************************* [The scene fades back to Monty and Nancy at MHW Headquarters.] Monty: Wow. What an incredible match. Nancy: Yes it was. I see big things for both of those men. They really put their heart and soul into that match. Monty: Yes they did. No question about it. Folks, we're out of time. Don't miss next week's Fuel. I'm sure it will be even better than this one. For Nancy Fletcher, my colleague, I'm Monty Hemrick saying... "good night." [The camera zooms out from the duo as they chat amongst themselves. The Prodigy's "Firestarter" again plays as MHW programming goes off the air. --(c) Copyright 2002, Mile High Sports Inc.--
