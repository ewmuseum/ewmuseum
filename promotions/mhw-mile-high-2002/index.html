A Denver, Colorado e-mail fed of 2002 that alternated a weekly dark-match show with a play-by-play flagship, building a win-loss chase toward a first pay-per-view its held record never reaches. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Mile High Wrestling opened in Denver, Colorado in March 2002, an e-mail fed that promised never to venture past its own state lines. Its first announced card, a free-for-all tournament called the Mile High Rumble, was cancelled within a day of being posted; a self-described sample card, written solely to show what its founder could do, followed in its place.

Two shows carried its held record forward. Fuel, its dark-match program, opened with Xu Jing submitting “The Educator” Thane Sloan with the Chinese Death Grip, and “Premium” River Rockwell pinning Sean Malcomb with the Resolution. Firestorm, its flagship, followed at the end of April: a triple threat opened the card, Xu Jing outlasted “Servant” Robert in eleven minutes, and a tag main event between Sean Malcomb and Thane Sloan against Breaking and Entering ended in a draw when Quentin Avery drove Thane Sloan off the top rope to the floor.

That same Firestorm closed on a swerve: a returning “three-time world champion,” Frank “The Falcon” Peregrine, ran in as if to help Sean Malcomb, then turned on him alongside Breaking and Entering, before Malcomb fought back and put him through a steel chair with the Atom Splitter.

Its owner announced a mini-tournament, run on every result until a first pay-per-view, Hostile Intent, to crown a first Heavyweight Champion. Recruiting calls continued into late June 2002, naming a Heavyweight title, Tag Team titles and an Unsanctioned title and promising a Rocky Mountain title to come; none of them is ever contested in the surviving record, and no document survives past 30 June 2002, a week before that pay-per-view was to be held.
