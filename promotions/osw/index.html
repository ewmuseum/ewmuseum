Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

OSW's debut Slam! broadcast aired live from Easton, Pennsylvania, around 2 February 2002 — the fed's own Jane Reagan thanking the crowd "for being with us here in Old School Wrestling for the debut broadcast." Its first PPV followed five weeks later: Crowning Glory, 10 March 2002, which crowned Sebastian "Switchblade" Toomes as OSW's first Heavyweight Champion, decided Rhett Locke as its first International Champion over Drew Hostyle, and put the first World Tag Team Championship on Civil Disobedience, Seth Masters and Trevor Hawke. Crowning Glory became an annual fixture — a sequel billed for Tijuana, Mexico the following March — and Slam! ran weekly behind it, broadcasting from a moving circuit of arenas rather than one fixed home; a 9 February 2003 episode, for one, aired live from The Pyramid in Memphis, Tennessee.

The front office ran its own season-long mystery angle: Commissioner Ed, standing in for owner Jane Reagan during an on-screen absence, was revealed mid-run as the masked in-ring saboteur "the Vax," with Howard Porter — a wrestler-turned-executive recruited back into the fold — installed as acting CEO in the aftermath. Howard Porter's own arc continued past that reveal: a later ledger records him as a corrupt Commissioner who "lied, cheated, and slithered his way into winning the World Title" outright. A shared talent pipeline, PWA (Primetime Wrestling Academy), fed new names into OSW's own roster throughout — Tom Walczak's own bio card describes graduating PWA and "waiting to showcase his talent in the new OSW" almost verbatim.

OSW staged its own crossover traffic honestly rather than as a mere namedrop: its 9 February 2003 Slam! main event put the World Tag Team Championship on the line in a Falls Count Anywhere match, champions Heaven & Hell (Hessian and Propaganda) against a challenge from Politically Incorrect — William S. Powell and Reginald V. Lovecraft, themselves the reigning Tag Team Champions one wing over. Heaven & Hell retained, after an in-ring intervention from "the Reverend" Jon Hull; OSW's own follow-up card preview two days later confirmed the result in its own voice ("Politically Incorrect are livid after losing their tag title match Sunday").

The fed's own results archive index, likewise preserved by the predecessor ewmuseum.com's restoration, tracks Slam! by the numbers — 32 episodes between 2 Feb and 7 Sep 2002 alone — and fills out the Heavyweight and International title pictures beyond their own opening nights: Doug Troy took the Heavyweight belt from Switchblade on Slam 16 (22 May 2002), immediately Polish Powerbombed through a table by Tom Walczak in the title's own aftermath; the International strap changed hands repeatedly that summer between Rhett Locke, Propaganda, and Walczak himself. A September 2002 champions snapshot, from the same restoration, shows a further-along title picture: Howard Porter as Heavyweight Champion, Frankie Bones as International Champion, Corg as Cruiserweight Champion, and The Throwbacks as World Tag Team Champions. The Cruiserweight belt's own next holder, Kid Wonder, closed out a nearly four-month reign live on the 9 February 2003 broadcast by publicly dropping the belt, then reclaiming it on the spot and lifting its weight restriction unilaterally, an angle staged as his own act of defiance against PCW's Tyler Nelson rather than a title retirement. The domain went dark sometime between 2004 and 2007, its address later repurposed as an ad-park redirect — an ordinary end for a site of its era.

A full read-and-serve pass across the fed's own match and incarder boards — 194 fragments, spanning Slam 1 through 25 — closes most of the World Tag Team Championship's own early chain: Civil Disobedience lost the belts the same night they won them at Crowning Glory, three weeks in, to Heresian Bloodlust (Hessian and Azrael Cain), who beat back two challenges from Riptah and Short Circuit before dropping the belts to that same team on 23 June 2002 — the same date, closing the gap this pass had previously left open. Hessian himself held the belts twice with two different partners, Cain first and Propaganda later as Heaven & Hell. The same corpus carries OSW's own "Red Army" angle in full: Ivan Stanislav, arriving as a PCW crossover with manager Alexei Ruslan, made war on a rotating cast — Reno Davis, Doug Troy, and El Drago among them — alongside fellow Russians Tom Walczak and Michel Krieg.

Storylines

A full read of the fed's own remaining fragment board — the roughly 150 incarder and match-report pieces this pass had not yet opened — surfaces three arcs the earlier structured pass's own PPV-and-title-change sample never fully traced.

The three-way tag war Written by AI, 2026

Heresian Bloodlust (Hessian and Azrael Cain) spent the summer of 2002 fending off both other tag teams in the division at once — Da Westsidaz (Riptah and Short Circuit) and Civil Disobedience — in a running war of run-ins and DQs rather than clean finishes. The feud's own signature beat is the "Culmination" angle: Da Westsidaz kidnap Heresian Bloodlust's own managers, Minion the Imp and Caleb, and the ensuing confrontation in the arena rafters — all three teams present at once — ends with seven men falling onto the roof of a Hell in a Cell rigged for that night's own main event. The war closed at Ascension (23 June 2002): Da Westsidaz took the titles from Heresian Bloodlust, the same date this pass had previously left open as an honest gap in the title's own chain.

Ivan Stanislav's Red Army, risen and disbanded Written by AI, 2026

Ivan Stanislav arrived as a PCW crossover invasion, his manager Alexei Ruslan and a rotating cast of "Red Army" communist-gimmick allies in tow, and spent the spring assaulting whichever OSW names stood against him — Doug Troy, Trashcan Man, Ed, Reno Davis among them. A romance with Tempest ran alongside the invasion, billed on-air as OSW's own "First Couple," until Tempest broke it off over a lie Stanislav never named on camera; Stanislav's own paranoid response — accusing Amy Campbell of conspiring with Reno Davis to end it, then beating her on camera for it — is the angle's own turn toward genuine villainy. By July, Stanislav disbanded the Red Army outright and re-cast himself as a solo "protector of the people," a reversal his own column, "Think Red," narrates from the inside as a declaration of independence rather than a retreat.

Corg, the Cruiserweight title, and his brother Written by AI, 2026

Corg won OSW's first Cruiserweight Championship from Amy Campbell at Ascension, then spent the following months defending it while running an escalating campaign of attempted assault and abduction against Campbell herself — stopped, each time, by Reno Davis. The same night he won the title, Corg attacked the newly-crowned International Champion, Rhett Locke — his own brother in kayfabe — and stole his signature nose ring; Locke's later on-camera revenge (a chair beating in the boiler room) and Corg's own refusal of a reconciliation handshake after the Cruiserweight win play the estrangement straight rather than teasing a reunion. OSW's ownership tried to fire Corg outright over the Campbell assaults; a legal injunction, staged as its own angle, kept him on the roster and the title on his waist.

Titles

Four championships survive in the record: the Heavyweight Championship, the International Championship, and the World Tag Team Championship, each opening at Crowning Glory in March 2002, plus the Cruiserweight Championship. None has a complete chain — each carries several confirmed reigns with real gaps between them, honestly marked rather than guessed at. A fifth honor, the Queen of Extreme, ran separately — a points-scored women's tournament rather than a defended belt, settled at a PPV rather than carried card to card. Affiliations

Primetime Championship Wrestling (PCW, Primetime, 2000–2004)

Shared continuity with OSW (Easton, 2002)
Jane Reagan is PCW’s Personnel Director and OSW’s owner — a mid-storyline reveal that the same in-fiction person runs both front offices. Roster raid traffic ran both directions (Kid Wonder moved from PCW to OSW), and PCW’s own champions challenged for OSW gold: at OSW’s 9 Feb 2003 Slam! broadcast, Politically Incorrect challenged for the OSW World Tag Team Championship and lost, with Heaven & Hell retaining. Off camera, the two were also sister companies under one real-world operator, alongside a wider family of promotions that included PTC, PURE, and others — the operator isn’t named here.

Primetime Championship Wrestling (PCW, Primetime, 2000–2004)

Rivalry with OSW (Easton, 2002) (as of Apr 2002)
OSW's own text calls itself "PCW's greatest rival" verbatim (K-Dub/Kid Wonder signing angle, Apr 2002). Ivan Stanislav's Red Army storyline is explicitly framed as a campaign against PCW owner Tyler Nelson ("I am here to educate the younger talent of OSW and lead my army into battle against Tyler Nelson!"); a Howard Porter promo frames OSW stepping "out of the shadows of PCW and their overbearing dictator." Recorded as a second, distinct edge alongside shared-continuity above because both are independently load-bearing in the source material, not two readings of the same fact.

Primetime Wrestling Academy (PWA)

Academy — feeds into OSW (Easton, 2002)
PWA appears constantly across OSW’s own record, most often as a shared developmental academy feeding talent into both OSW and PCW — multiple bios describe wrestlers training in PWA before “graduating” into OSW (e.g. Tom Walczak: “about a month after joining PWA he was one of the first to graduate... waiting to showcase his talent in the new OSW”). Amy “Aimz” Campbell and PCW’s own Rent-A-Hero are shown reminiscing over a shared PWA-era photo, tying the academy directly into the OSW/PCW crossover romance thread as well.

Primetime Central (PTC)

Affiliate of OSW (Easton, 2002)
OSW’s own site nav links Primetime Central/PTC on every page, and in-fiction dialogue groups OSW roster alongside PCW and DRWF wrestlers as co-equal “PTC members” (“fellow PTC members Jack Hendrix of DRWF and Miyagawa of PCW”) — but PTC’s own FAQ explicitly excludes OSW (alongside PCW, NTICW, RUSH, XWA, PWA) from its formal “Channels”/rankings/network tier even while hosting OSW’s own board on its Ikonboard. Two senses of “PTC member” coexisted: an administrative tier that formally excluded OSW, and a looser narrative one OSW’s own writers used freely. Off camera, OSW and PCW (along with several sister promotions) were also run by the same real-world operator, who isn’t named here.

New True Internet Championship Wrestling (NTICW)

Crossover booking with OSW (Easton, 2002)
Jane Reagan personally recruited NTICW World Champion Scott Taylor into OSW mid-title-reign, a storyline beat that made both NTICW’s and OSW’s own news. NTICW appears frequently throughout OSW’s own record as a genuine peer/crossover fed, separate from PWA’s academy role.

Puroresu Ultimate Ring Entertainment (PURE)

Crossover booking with OSW (Easton, 2002)
PURE’s own rumour headline “PURE To OSW To PURE?” (17 April 2002); its own column reporting “PCW/OSW additions to the JR Heavyweight division of Great Dragon, El Drago, and El Diablo” and naming “OSW Queen of Extreme competitors Bethany Yamoto and Brandi Wilson” for a proposed PURE women’s division; and, from OSW’s own side, a storyline asking “WHAT is the correlation between Inphino Blitz, Aki-yankah, Rent a Hero, and Rent a Villian?”, naming PURE’s own Asian National number-one contender.

Crossover booking with OSW (Easton, 2002) (2002)

Roleplays

A romance thread running through OSW's own roleplay corpus pairs Amy "Aimz" Campbell with PCW's masked Rent-A-Hero, the two shown as an established couple with a shared PWA-academy backstory. A separate, darker thread in the fed's own incarder board — the six-part "Tears" series, all dated 29 June 2002 — has Corg abducting Aimz and preparing to kill her at a junkyard trash compactor before Reno Davis, working through an injury, tracks them down and pulls her free.
