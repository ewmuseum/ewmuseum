Never Competed In A Specific Type of Match? Train For It!

25 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/25/02 According to the big book of words written by Noah Webster, or just plain and simply known as the dictionary, the word training means 'To prepare physically, as with a regimen: train athletes for track-and-field competition.' Breaking that down further, it means to prepare one's self for an upcoming event or obstacle. This is where the scene opens up, the first thing shown is the back alley behind the motel where our last adventure was taking place. The new personal bodyguard/manager of Scott Taylor, Randolph, is seen standing next to a run down brick wall with a stop watch in his hand, dressed like always in a pair of slacks and a white tee shirt. He looks down at the stop watch and then back up to Scott. Scott is wearing a pair of black shorts and a white Reebok tee shirt. He looks back to Randolph. 'Randolph! Are you ready?' inquires Randolph's employer. 'I think so, Mr Taylor. But, could you explain to me again why we are doing this?' 'It's very simple Randolph. My match this Saturday is a cage match. And ho do you win a cage match?' 'Wait, I know this one. You have to be the first person to climb out of the cage! Right?' 'Correct. That is why we're going to be doing this. Now, are you ready with the stop watch?' 'Yes sir. Go!' Randolph presses one of the many buttons on the official brand name of whatever sports company stop watch and Scott Taylor begins to run, making a straight bee line across the alley. As the view switches to give a better view we can see that Scott is running towards a large chain link fence. As Scott gets closer to the fence he leaps up into the air and grabs onto the fence as his body connects with it. As soon as he has a grip on the fence he begins to quickly climb up the fence, once at the top he uses his leg strength to push his body over the top. Scott falls and lands on his feet in a squatting position on the other side. Seeing this, Randolph presses another button on the stop watch, ending the time. Scott stands up to his feet and grabs onto the fence, leaning against it. 'Well, how did I do, Randolph?' 'Total time was twenty-three.six seconds sir. Much faster than last time.' Scott leans back, away from the fence and runs his arm across his brow wiping away the sweat. His hands move to his hips and his head swishes to the side throwing a few loose strands of hair back away from his face. Still breathing heavily, he calls out to Randolph again. 'Not bad. It is better than the previous times. I told you that this would be a brilliant idea. This is the perfect training for my cage match on Slam. The experience of climbing this big fence is going to help me be able to climb the cage. 'It was a brilliant idea. But, why do you need training for a cage match? Aren't you an NTICW Legend and a future OSW Legend?' 'Yes, yes I am a very accomplished performer, but I have never competed in a cage match before. This is new ground for me. Do you undersstand?' Randolph nods his head as Scott jumps up and begins to climb the near ten foot chain link fence again. The scene begins to fade out as Scott hops over the top of the fence.
