La casa Manteniendo

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Wednesday, May 1/02 Our Scene the Holiday Inn hotel once again, this time the front lobby and the formal introduction to El Drago. Drago sports a white hotel robe and simple black ski mask covers his face. The story, well read on… El Drago waits in line for the front desk, people passing by stare at the masked man. “Why’s he wearing a mask mommy?” A small child blurts out and the mother quickly rushes him along and into the elevator. The woman in front of Drago finishes and he steps cautiously up to the desk. A middle aged man with greased back black hair in the green hotel uniform greets him with a smile, “Hello sir, how can I be of assistance?” “Apreciaría tener uno de esas damas que limpian suben a mi habitación y limpian el lío mi traductor hecho. Tengo que preparar para mi igual y apreciar también el acceso al gimnasio.” El Drago tells the man who with each word gets more confused. What El Drago wants is his room cleaned and a pass to the training gym. A moment of silence lingers as both man waits for the other to say something. It’s broken as an average looking high school girl buts in, “I may be able to help out, I’m taking Spanish in school right now.” She explains to the man behind the desk before turning to Drago. “Permita que mí traduzca para usted.” She says in a poor accent to Drago. Drago nods in understanding before giving the girl a challenge, “Apreciaría tener uno de esas damas que limpian suben a mi habitación y limpian el lío mi traductor hecho. Tengo que preparar para mi igual y apreciar también el acceso al gimnasio.” The girl blinks and thinks hard with each word and in all likely hood understands only a little bit but she’ll be damned if she’s going to let the man at the desk know that. She nods at Drago then turns back to the man and translates loosely, “He wants to make sure he isn’t disturbed because he is in training.” As you can tell the girl isn’t the best translator and this is destined to be trouble for El Drago. The man smiles and pulls a door hanger from under the desk and hands it out for Drago who grabs it and stares at the “Do not disturb” door hanger and nods. “Just tell him to hang it on the door and no one will bother him in his training.” “Si usted cuelga esto en la puerta usted será capaz de entrenar en la paz.” She translates for the Mexican Hero. “Si usted dice tan.” Drago says with a shrug as he turns and walks back towards the elevator. This ends the second of many stories in the life of the mysterious El Drago. We shall catch up with him tomorrow as he starts his training.
