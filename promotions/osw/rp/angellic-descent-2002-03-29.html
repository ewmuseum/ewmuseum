Angellic Descent

29 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 29th, 2002 'How many children have you fathered in your years here on this fine planet?' You wish you'd taken the plane like your father told you. But no, you insisted the bus ride would be cheaper...Clara wouldn't have to worry about you...you know how she hated flying. 'Mister? I believe I bestowed upon you an inquiry to which I must demand an answer.' 'Do you need to be so rude?' Your voice quivers with varied discontent. You look over at him, the man interrogating you with that foolish grin on his face, beaming with pride over his extended vocabulary. He shakes his head and buries his face in his hands. For a moment, you swear a tear trickles down his round and rosey cheek. The man pulls his oversized glasses from his face and brings the lenses to his sleeve. He begins wiping them without abandon, as if he were riding high on a see of cocaine. You shake your head and rest back against the head of your lumpy bus seat. But, your rest is quickly interrupted almost as soon as it begins. 'I apologize, sir...I just despise being ignored...a most abominable trait of mine...stinks like you wouldn't believe.' You turn to look back over to him, hoping to gather some satisfaction from his attempt at an apology and notice his face. Once gallant and pompous, now embarrassed and ridden with guilt. You can't help but feel sorry for lashing out at him, contradicting yourself with your own words. 'It's alright...I apologize for calling you rude when I wasn't being too polite myself.' You flash that grin of yours, the one Clara informed you won her over. He smiles back, his entire exterior illuminating as a direct result of your own apology. He brings one of his little hands out and pulls back the few whisps of rusty hair falling forth from his scalp with the other. Your eyes follow that confirmed tear you thought you saw as it falls from his chin. His lumpy body, blessed with five foot four inches of stunting growth, struggles to gain some comfort, knowing that you are looking it over. You shoot your gaze back to his own eyes, sensing the discomfort he must be experiencing and remember his hand still by its lonesome in front of you. You bring your own limb out from your body and involuntarily move down and grasp his hand. He smiles joyously, the handshake seemingly shooing any looming feelings in the air amongst the two of you to the luggage compartments built into the underside of the bus. 'The name's Maurice.' He tilts his glasses as he says so, spawning a polite grin upon your lips. 'Gerard. You can call me Gerry, though...with a 'G'.' You grin like an idiot again, perhaps a show of embarrassment for sounding how you just did. 'Married, Gerry?' You turn your head and look out the side window to the countryside as it passes in a blur. With that smile still pasted to your face, your mind plays out old memories of your children and your wife. Past memories. You dare not confront those recent ones. Not right now. Not when you're talking to Maurice. 'Yep...and to answer your earlier 'inquiry',' his face crunches up in response to your assumingly witty comment, '...I have two children. Twin girls. Becca and Jenna. They just turned one...would you like to see a picture?' You're beaming with a pride of your own now. That of your own children. You struggle in your back pocket, cursing at yourself inside your head. Maurice blinks as he lay in wait, little thumbs twiddling over his index fingers in a consistent manner. He drags his tongue over his lips and politely smirks in your general direction. 'Hmph...wonder where I put...oh yeah...' You bring your hand to the breast pocket of your grey business suit and retrieve one of your most valued possessions. 'Italian leather...nice, huh?' Maurice nods slowly as he peers at your billfold, thick with money you've spent two years of your life acquiring. 'Becca and Jenna...' A few flips through the plastic coated pictures centered within your wallet. 'Ahh...here they are.' You hold out your treasure, taking a moment to let your eight hour friend's eyes gleam at its splendor. Maurice smiles wide at the sight of your children and looks up for a moment. 'Gorgeous young ladies you have there, Gerry...it's a rewarding prize in your life, I'm sure.' 'Sure is.' You sigh and close your wallet, placing it back in your breast pocket, and adjusting the lapels of your blazer. 'I believe I would like some children of my own some day. But not now...I have the ability to impregnate...' Your brow shoots up in surprise at his blatant and unrequested report on the status of his fertility. '...but not at this very moment. I still feel, at times, as if I were still a child...and I'm going on 30. Myra wants them, but I know I'm not suited for fatherhood...not yet.' You reach across the walkway of the bus and place a reassuring hand on his shoulder. 'Believe me, Maurice...I'm sure you'd make a great father. Don't be afraid of such things. If you're dedicated to your family, you will never regret the decision of bringing children into this world. They're what make you better and define you as a man. You're never truly a man until you experience fatherhood. Absolutely amazing.' Maurice's thumbs continue to revolve around his index fingers as he turns his head down, sheepish grin and reddening cheeks now providing the decoration for his oddly proportioned body. He looks back up to you and blinks a few more times than could be considered normal and licks his lips once more. 'I'm sure it is. Maybe...maybe if Myra and I discussed the financial and emotional accessories that are sure to accompany a newborn, we could come to some means of agreement. I need to be more secure with myself and learn to compromise. After all...don't they say 'Marriage is compromise'?' He looks up at you with his round, emerald eyes, letting you catch a glimpse of what he meant by him being a 30 year old child. You simply nod. 'Yes...but we were talking about fatherhood...marriage is a whole different topic.' Again, that million dollar smile of yours brings light to Maurice's heart. 'Oh, yes, yes...but we'd come to a decision to wait until we are initially married before we attempt to conceive. Children were to accompant marriage...and I saw burdens accompanying children...but I believe I may be viewing things from another perspective now. Thank you, Gerard...' He again shakes your hand, desperation leaving him like a careless father. Your mouth always made you the popular one. 'Really, it's no problem...just speaking as a man of experience.' Maurice bites his lower lip and claps, bouncing up and down in his seat with excitement. He swings his stubby little legs and then looks back to you as those memories of the mentally handicapped peers at your high school come to mind. Restraints inside don't even attempt to hold back your mouth from curling upward with hidden arrogance again. 'I've told you about Myra...well...her name...what's your wife's name?' 'Clara.' Those restraints shoot into you like a tetnis shot, strapping you back to your seat and pulling your mouth downward, causing your skin to pale to a mild manilla. Maurice wimpers, knowing he's hit a sore subject, and diverts his eyes from you. His lips move for an apology, but you will have none of it. You aren't here for pity. 'No, no...don't apologize. You had no way of knowing.' His little round head slowly rotates upon his neck and turns back towards you. 'May...may I be so bold as to ask what the situation that I inadvertantly brought to your mind was?' His words confuse you. You weren't a stupid man...but you lacked the vocabulary in which he obviously excelled in. And his vague wording didn't help any either. Your eyes say it all as you look over at him, his thumbs twiddling again as he tries to reform his question. 'Oh, I'm sorry...um....did...something happen to Clara or are you two on the sidelines of your relationship?' You smile again at his latter comment and shake your head. 'No...we're great. We always have been...but yes, something did happen.' With Maurice's curiosity peaked, he's not about to give up his line of questioning. However, he seems to keep it as gentle as possible, knowing that what you must be going through is something beyond his comprehension. He hadn't any idea. 'May...may I ask what happened?' You swallow hard as your mind replays everything that has happened in the last two weeks of your life. You remember Clara's parents phoning you at work that night and informing you that she'd never made it to their residence for the dinner they had planned nearly three months prior. They told you they tried at your home as well and received no response. You remember calling into the police department immediately and all the feelings that rushed to the surface of your body. 'She was kidnapped...two days she was gone. Brought back to me with every finger on her left hand broken...and her pinky was just...gone.' Maurice's face nearly instantaneously shrouds itself in a white mask of that pity you didn't want. He turns his head back down to the floor of the bus and breathes in heavily, awkward moment cloaking you both. 'I'm...sorry to hear that, Gerry...how is she doing?' For once, he brings out that courage that he lacked of so much in his pittling little past. Sighing, you try your hardest to fight back emotions you know don't usually hang by your side. 'She's better than she was...' 'Do they know who did it?' Maurice's questioning begins coming quicker now...and it's not wearing well on your good side. 'They? The police didn't say a word about it...they didn't do a damn thing but take her to the hospital. Their reports were no help. They were half hearted and lacked any real effort...you figure they'd try their hardest, ya know? But no...they didn't do shit.' Your anger was starting to get to you and rear its ugly head. 'Policemen aren't as noble as their image implies.' You nod and excitement blends with that rage, forming words that turn heads within the bus. 'HELL YEAH!!!' A clenched fist slams to the empty seat to your left as Maurice's mouth gapes open. You slowly look over to him, blushing slightly in embarrassment and clear your throat, muttering words of apology. Maurice quickly shoos them off. 'No...I agree. They should have tried harder and conducted more effective means of rescue. They need to find whoever did it and put them away for a good long time.' 'They said they didn't know who did it. Clara knows.' A gasp of suddening interest scurries out from Maurice's lungs. 'Who...who was it, Gerry?' He leans forward as he asks, putting his weight on his left wrist. You shake your head and sigh. 'A guy she went to high school with...someone she told me about a thousand times before. He was the reason it took so long to convince her I loved her. Sometimes...even after all this...I still think she's got feelings for him. Why she would...I don't know...Ethan...I believe that was his name.' 'Hmm...I'm sorry to hear that such a tragedy has befallen you...why do the nice ones get all the negativity floating around?' Maurice shakes his head in disappointment, sucking in a full breath of air and releasing it with a less-than-desirable sigh. 'All the good people...all of em...they're the ones who get all this junk...this left over garbage.' You nod, but know inside you aren't one of those 'nice guys'. You wish you could change, but everytime you make someone like you, your organs throb with a sense of accomplishment and swell with pride. Superiority complex. You believe that's what you had. One hell of a complex at that. 'Yup.' You lie through your teeth. 'WHAT THE HELL IS HE DOING IN THE MIDDLE OF THE ROAD?!?!' The bus driver...you heard her voice when you boarded. The horn sounds several times from the front of the bus as you sit up a bit, looking over the seat in front of you. You stretch your neck higher, but you're both too lazy and tired to stand up to see what's going on. Your eyes suddenly catch Maurice as he bounces again. This time, under a power belonging to something other than his muscular system. The bus shrieks as you pull your head up. Everyone inside empties their lungs at once, their immediate fear childishly gripping them and dragging them out to the surface. Your body shifts a bit, but you clutch the seat tightly and lock your seatbelt in place. For once, you think about someone other than yourself and shoot your gaze over to Maurice. Glass shatters and sprays out just before your eyes meet their target. More metal screaming. More people crying for help that isn't present. Transportation haults and your trip to Miami suddenly cuts short as the bus begins to turn. The tires beg for relief with their rubber bodies trying to keep gripped to the pavement. The victor of their short battle is instantly decided as you finally catch that 20 seconds too late glance at Maurice. His pudgy body is pulled from his seat and thrown straight to the metal frame of the bus from the force. You hear him howl in pain upon impact and feel the bus begin to slowly tilt to its side. Maurice reaches out hands, grasping for air and face contorting into worriesome bawling. Your guilt is beginning to rise. So is your side of the bus. The seatbelt clutching you safely around the waist grinds against your pelvis as the bus comes to its side in a shower of sparks, its body sliding across the asphault and raising the pitch of that horrid noise that started it all. Sides of the bus cave in and people are thrown about the inside like pinballs. You count as they pass. Two old ladies, reading and prescription glasses preceding them, float past you. Their eyes meet yours and you want to help. You want them to like you. But there's no way. Your pride begins to empty, knowing your helpless state. You hear the women make contact somewhere behind you, what remaining glass there is cracking and exploding like fireworks. One teenager, hair flowing behind him is your next visitor. He manages to clutch at your seat for but a moment, his panic gathering adrenaline and blessing him with the strength to take a piece of the seat with him. You try to turn and look up, but the seatbelt keeps you in the same position. Three women and four children. Two of the women hit Maurice's seat, limbs instantly bending in deformities that silence them immediately, a surprising response considering the situation. Your attention turns away from them as you witness the terror flood through the three children's eyes before the fourth's head makes contact with the steel railing above you. His neck whips like a thin noodle and you watch his eyes roll back into his head, forehead caved in and bits of skull creeping out from his crimson scalp. A few drops of his blood spray back on that new suit of yours as his limp body falls beneath you. Like some twisted form of adoption, Maurice's first child falls in a bloody heap directly upon his lap. You stare down at the little boy resting motionless on the odd man's body and listen to your heart pound out through your ears. The bus has stopped and all you hear is your heart pounding. Sparks fly loose and the lights within the bus flash as cables and baggage spill out of their respective compartments. You look around and suddenly remember you're still being suspended by that seatbelt. Slowly peeling it from your skin, you manage to rest it further down on your thigh so the pain doesn't come in such bulk. Numerous tries at the buckle come to no avail. You decide to stick where you are. From your suspended position, all you can see is Maurice, unconscious fifteen feet below you. You stare at him for a moment and remember how just ten minutes ago he was speaking with you, displaying emotions in every manner possible. Now he lay there, every emotion gone from his face, dead child resting on his body as if he were Maurice's teddy bear. Sickening thoughts run through your mind and you shake your head to try to scatter them. You grunt as you struggle to look above you, the lights suddenly going out with a loud pop. A few of the people in the bus still conscious let out their last few screams in response. When the lights leave you...the cries for help start. Too many come at once. You stay silent and listen, trying to make out individual tones from the grouped jargon your mind processes. You begin to wonder how many people may be dead, how many could be injured...how many survived without so much a scratch, like you. You look down at Maurice and then glance down to your unscathed body. He was right. The nice ones get all the garbage. The irony playing in your head is all too quickly interrupted by another scream from the bus. This time, no sparks, no glass shattering, no lights blinking...just metal...and that screaming. The screaming of a manmade material that will forever haunt you while you slept. It stops after a few moments, but is looped in your mind. Your fear begins to grow, hoping that the bus isn't going to collapse under its own weight and bring you into its crumpled grasp. It heightens again when you hear an inhuman growl. You hope for a moment it's some sort of help, but your head tells you differently. A few more sounds spring forth within the confines of the darkened bus, in rapid succession. A few people audibly gasp as the sounds continue. Hulking, heavy thuds. One after the other. Increasing in volume. 'Please...help...' A weakened plea escaping an elderly woman's lips. You hear it clear as day. 'Please...help me, sir...get me out of here.' Your eyes shift from side-to-side as you listen. She whimpers and starts to sob. You swallow, credibility trying to remain distant from your mind. This isn't happening. Another bark of a cry starts with her weak, little voice. 'Please...do some--' Silenced with a sound you've only heard at the packing plant you worked at three years back. Those noises return. More heft and bulk. Even louder than before. It's getting closer. And the entire bus, once filled with screaming for help is now silent as if they were at a funeral. Perhaps their own. 'Please help me, sir...' You're starting to believe there's someone here for your rescuing...someone sent just for you. Your guardian angel. 'I don't want to die here.' This voice belongs to a child. Those sounds stop for a moment and the child quiets. You close your eyes as silence looms in the air once again like sticky, thick, irritating humidity. No one says a thing. No breathing anymore. Maybe you've tuned it all out in your own mind. Maybe you were making the silence. Yet...no screams. No pleas. No cries. You want a sound. You want peace. Ages pass with that maddening silence ever present. You clutch your eyes tighter and grit your teeth, angry at the quiet and cursing at it to end. Arms flail with rage, legs kick in fury, eyes burst open once more. Your heart chokes your throat. That monster stares up at you from below. That halo you pictured floating above his head is nowhere to be seen. Those wings protruding from his back, abandoned. The sounds start up again. Women scream. Men begin pleading. Children cry. Your savior's here...and he's bringing you your peace.
