Knowledge is Power

14 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/14/02 Knock, knock. 'Who's there?' 'Mike.' 'Mike who?' 'Lasandra.' 'Oh, hey, come on in.' Fade in. The scene opens in the cluttered, cage-walled, portable office of the Hardcore Legend and OSW Commissioner, Ed. Contained within are several battered chairs, a few framed photographs, a suprisingly verdant, leafy plant, and a shiny new cardwood desk purchased at Wal-Mart for just under one hundred bucks. Sitting behind this piece of near-Chippendale-quality furniture is none other than a bruised and aching Commish, the bulging blue form of an ice pack sticking out from behind his back. The door opens, and in steps Mike Lasandra, clad in a slightly new garment -- a pair of baggy black jeans (with pocket chain), a black 'Slayer' T-shirt, and a rather cumbersome neck brace. 'Been shopping with Aimz?' Ed asks, grinning slightly at the sound technician's threads. 'Nah, man, my rubber suit's at the cleaners,' Lasandra replied. 'Well, the car wash.' Ed shrugs. 'Close enough. Have a seat, Mike.' With a nod, the lanky technician pulls the chair closest to the Commissioner's desk back, and drops his scrawny frame into it. After a moment's consideration, he leans back and drapes his leg over his left knee. 'What's up?' 'I wanted to talk to you about something Meanstreak said on Slam. The pyrotechnics thing,' Ed adds, noting Mike's confused expression. 'Oh...the thing about...what was that? Burning your face off?' Ed nods. 'Specifically, about how you didn't get the job done.' The technician blinks, then shakes his head with a short, nervous laugh. 'Hey, man, you don't think I--' The Hardcore Legend replies with a chuckle of his own. 'No, no. I'm not accusing you of anything.' Somewhat more assured, Mike leans back, as Ed places his elbows on his desk and leans forward. 'I trust you, Mike,' Ed continues. 'And that's no small thing. This is a dangerous work environment, and I don't mean just in the ring. I can't be sure who's in Jane's pocket and who's in mine, and I need your help.' Lasandra raises his eyebrow inquisitively. 'I know you're not one of Jane's lackies,' the Commish explains. 'And I know you know who's the good guy in this little power struggle. I need your help.' 'What with?' Mike asks, after a long, thoughtful pause. 'You're 'cool' with almost everyone on the payroll, right?' The technician nods, as does Ed. 'I need you to put your feelers out. Find out who's on Jane's side, and who's on mine. I'm going to need all the help I can get to take her on.' 'Sure, Ed,' Mike replies with a slight, confident grin. 'Mind if I put my feelers up one of the QoE girls?' Ed snorts with good-natured derisiveness. 'Good luck. I'm going to need you to keep a low profile, too,' he adds, abruptly getting back to business. 'This is political subterfuge, not holy crusade. I need you to keep low, so, if need be, I can get you into Jane's inner circle.' 'Does that mean I have to have foursomes with her, Toomes, and Streak?' Lasandra asks, screwing his face up. Ed laughs. 'I hope not, but I'd lube that rubber suit up just in case. Thanks, Mike. I knew I could count on you.' The technician nods, then pauses. 'What about Streak?' 'What about Streak?' Mike shrugs. 'I heard you bragging on the radio about how you were going to get inside his head and f*ck with him, but how are you gonna do that, man? Shit, how are you going to do that before he kills you?' 'Easy. I find out what's going on. Why he is how he is. What makes him tick. It'll take some work, but I've already got a starting place.' 'Where's that?' Ed eyes the technician speculatively, then nods to himself and pulls open one of his drawers. With a practiced motion, he procudes a thin, almost empty manilla folder and tosses it over. Mike catches it, pulls the top open, and withdraws a single sheet of paper. 'That's all the information we have on Meanstreak from before his career. Printed in 10 point Times New Roman. It takes up half a page.' Lasandra arches a lime green eyebrow, and nods. 'Not exactly much to go on.' Ed gives an agreeing nod. 'By the time I get done, that folder's going to be thicker than any other in my drawer.' Mike raises his eyes to meet the Hardcore Legend's determined stare. 'How are you planning on that?' 'Read it.' The technician glances again at the Commish, then raises the paper and begins reading aloud. 'Former pit fighter...began wrestling approximately two and a half years ago...no formal training...released from Shady Hills Sanitarium when stated facility closed...' He raises his eyes in realization, and Ed gives yet another confirming nod. 'Shady Hills Sanitarium, located in upstate New York, about two hours' drive from Albany. I have a good friend who came from there. It's my starting point.' Mike shakes his head. 'According to this, the place has been closed for years. What are you expecting to find there?' 'A forgotten file. Notes scribbled on a wall. Meanstreak's diary.' He sighs. 'Frankly, Mike, I don't know. But it just feels right -- like the answer, or the path to the answer, is right there. And besides, I have to start somewhere.' Lasandra swallows, feeling a definite dramatic moment going on. 'When are you leaving?' he finally asks, after a pause. 'Tonight. My plane's at eleven, and I should be back for Slam. Keep me informed -- here's my cell number,' he adds, handing over a small piece of paper with a few blurred-out numbers scribbled on. 'Remember. Low profile.' The technician nods grimly, and takes the paper. He examines it, then returns his stare to the Hardcore Legend's eyes. 'Good luck, Ed.' 'Thanks, Mike. I'll need it.' Fade out.
