Date: 12.06.05 Note: I used this RP as one of my First Round RP's in RPG3, but it fits perfectly to what I want to write about... --------------------------------------------------------------------- Pause. Silence. Another moment dropped off. Left Behind and. Hanging still. -American Head Charge, “Just So You Know”. That pretty much describes the atmosphere. The shadows of the early morning sun cast mysterious light across the room. A gap in the blinds and rip in the cheap fabric created ample room for the sun’s own batch of light to shine bright lays across the room. With an air as still as the night sea and the room’s participants the same, the scene seemed to be one of peace and tranquillity. That was not the case. Not in Michel’s head anyway. As the giant German slowly rose from his bed, sitting upright slowly like a modern age Neanderthal, the bed creaked ever so slightly, as most do, as he swung his legs around the side of the bed. Burying his head in his hands for a second, Krieg got his thoughts together and made his way over to the dresser, where some of his clothes from the previous day and an open suitcase with various items of clothing lay. Trying to keep the noise down, Krieg dresses himself in a pair of dark jeans, a plain black t-shirt and a pair of heavy boots. Tugging a hooded top over his head, Krieg completed his outfit as he crept across the room and slowly opened the room’s door, letting it make the slightest of creaks as he quietly made his exit from the hotel room, leaving his mother motionless as she snored slightly, still in bed. The digital clock on the wall beeped for the fourth time since he had got up. “6.36” its display read, the seconds flashing by in smaller digits on the right. The corridor was peaceful, just as expected as he made his way down towards the fire exit at the other end of the corridor, Best avoid the crowds at the main door. Don’t want to start any conversations. I’ve got things to do. The metal bar clicked as its mechanism opened the door, the massive European striding through its threshold. As soon as the door opened, a deafening amount of cheering could be hear, as fans in mid-scream shouted at someone on the opposite side of the building. Smiling slightly, Krieg had achieved his first goal. As Michel strode across the rear car park, a couple of cars could be seen, dotted around the car park as he walked right past them. After a minute’s walk, he reached his method of transport. A 1979 Harley Davidson motorcycle. Michel produced a set of keys from his pocket and unlocked the seat compartment, pulling a dark black and blue helmet out from it before closing it over silently once more. The engine purred as Krieg jabbed the keys to the right, looking appropriately dressed for riding a motorcycle in his jeans, boots, heavy top and protective helmet. Now on his cycle, the road was his. But the open road wouldn’t be the only thing he’d be tearing up today: His mind was in for some as well… The scene fades out and then in again as a deserted beach comes into view. The camera pans around as numerous sun loungers sit unoccupied as the time shows in small white digits at the bottom of the screen. 6.57. A engine’s noise gets closer before the camera turns to the left and catches view of Michel Krieg heading down the quiet beach road towards the camera. Pulling up about 10 metres short of the camera, Michel stops the bike and switches off the engine, sitting on the motorcycle upright, looking out at the sea, his inner thoughts showing a more dark/reflective side to him… …This is peaceful, right? If people knew what I’ve had to put up with, this would look like nirvana. The beach, the sea, the atmosphere, oh so quiet. This is not real life. This is what they call Californication. The whole outlook of these people is to have fun and not generally worry about the consequences. This is not how it is meant to be, not how I look at things. Yeah, maybe I’m strange. Yeah, maybe I take a more sceptical take on things. But this isn’t right… Slowly lifting his leg over the engine, Krieg gets off of the bike and takes off his helmet, holding it at the side of his body under his arm. His slow movements give the impression of a thinker, someone indulged in his surroundings. Maybe that’s the case… Walking down a short stairway down to a reasonable sized beach-side walkway, taking each step as if it were his last, Krieg steps onto the concrete of the walkway and sighs deeply, his chest visibly rising under his jumper. Two steps forward and his feet touch the white sand of the beach, the sand most Californian’s love and enjoy on a regular basis. The soft sand rose around the base of his boots, creating a faint mark at its highest point as he steps further into the beach. After walking for a minute or so, he stops again, standing next to a closed beach bar. …These bars, they’re a regular site in any Ameri-kun town. Its almost like their landmarks or trademarks of towns, like mini Statues Of Liberty, or shrunken White Houses. These Ameri-kuns take pride in their bars. I mean, they’re people lying homeless in New York and women having to sell their bodies because they can’t get jobs. And one of their top priorities is to make their bar look pretty or have some fancy memorabilia on their walls or hanging from the front of the bar fascias? Is there no pride anymore? No standard? No morals, or beliefs? What’s happened to Martin Luther King’s great dream? Don’t these people care for their own? And if they do, does their generosity extend to their immediate family or people who will make them a profit? This isn’t how it’s meant to be… The waves rolling up the shore broke Krieg’s chain of thought as he realised where he was, opening his eyes ever so slowly. Holding his helmet by the rim in his hand, he tosses it off down the beach, the protective helmet landing 50ft or so from his position. As he turns round and walks back towards his bike, a yellow cab pulls up just past his bike as he reaches mid-way up the stairs. Not taking much notice, he continues to walk up the steps. The taxi door slams, its participant standing next to Krieg’s bike, his back to Michel. As Michel approaches the bike, he places his hand on the man’s right shoulder and slowly he turns round to face Michel. A look of confusion strikes Michel’s face as he looks at the man, studying his face hard. After a minute or so, Krieg stands there, still looking confused as the visitor breaks the silence, “Michel,” he says, his voice sounding slightly wary, “Yes. I am Michel. Who are you? How did you know I was here,” asks Michel, “I followed you...Michel…I’m your father,” These words, ones Krieg thought he might never hear, took a second or two to register with Michel as he continued to study the man, still looking puzzled by the man’s presence. This situation wasn’t one of the one’s Michel had planned over and over in his mind, like what he was going to do if his mother didn’t approve of his friendship with any of his OSW comrades, or what how he would react if he had to choose between certain people as allies. He’d never planned on meeting his father… After a slight pause and another deep sigh, Michel asked one question, one word, “Why,” Michel’s father was prepared for this though, obviously having been preparing to tell his son the reason for his departure for the past 20+ years since he left Michel and his mother, “It was extremely difficult to live with you and your mother,” he replied, Lifting his head to the side, Michel shook it slightly and then rubbed the left side of his face before answering back, “How so? Why couldn’t you stay with us? For my sake, for the family’s sake,” “Michel, it is difficult to understand, I know, but there were a whole lot of different factors to me leaving, some which your mother may not have mentioned,” “Oh, she just mentioned the fact that you were sleeping around with numerous women, but if that’s not a valid fact, just let me know,” replied Michel, the anger in his voice more than prominent, Clearing his throat, Mr. Krieg seemed to be getting a little choked up with seeing his son again, “Look Michel. I was sleeping around a little, I admit that. I know I was wrong. But do you not think there were reasons why I left? Why I slept around,” “I don’t know why you did that, or why you felt it best for us for you to leave, but all I know is that I’m very confused and I need time to think,” Nodding, Mr. Krieg patted Michel on the shoulder, “I understand, so-“ Mr. Krieg stopped in mid-sentence as Michel looked around at his father’s hand on his shoulder and then at his father. Slowly taking his hand off of his son’s shoulder, Mr. Krieg put both his hands at his side as he stood slightly nervous, “I do not want to tell you how much I think I hate you, because I know there is a part of me who has always wanted to meet you and get to know you better than I did the 5 years before you left. I also know that I want to believe mother’s stories about you, but my heart tells me to let you have your say,” “Ok, ok. I completely understand the situation. I came here because I heard your name on the radio the other day as I drove out of Los Angeles, and I wanted to support you and give you all the help I could in this tournament, because no one deserves to win this prestigious thing more than you. It may not seem like it, but I’ve watched you develop all the time and I know how hard you’ve worked for this,” “I suggest we meet soon. Today, I have to think and work out what I’m going to do with my life…father,” Smiling slightly, Mr. Krieg once again nods, “Ok son. How about we arrange to meet outside your hotel tomorrow morning? I was the one who called yesterday about the extra person to stay in the hope you’d do some research and find me. But I thought it best for me to find you, the fact being I left you, not the other way around,” said a regretful Mr. Krieg, “Ok. I shall meet you outside in the car park at 8.30am tomorrow morning. I need to ask you one thing before I go…What is your name? Mother never told me…” “Michel,” Nodding, Krieg stepped onto his cycle and started up the engine once again, the vintage cycle sounding like a jaguar in the wild. Pulling away from the curb slowly, Michel gives his father one last look before speeding off down the road, leaving his father standing there for a second before he gets into the taxi waiting for him and heads off in the opposite direction… The scene fades to black as only Krieg’s voice can be heard, his thoughts once again becoming audible: I never thought I’d say this but I met the man who left me. The man who deserted me 21 years ago. The man who thought it best to leave us in the lurch as he went off with other women. Or was he the man who left because it would have made my childhood hell if he stayed? Was he the man who moved out without much notice because it was caused more bad than good if he helped raise me? That’s just something I’ll need to find out… The scene fades back in again as Krieg pulls up in the rear car park of the hotel and dismounts his bike, parking it close to where it was originally sitting. Opening the fire door, Michel stepped back into the hotel, the hot air of the radiators hitting him as he came in from the brisk cold. The corridor was a bit more noisy now, a couple of maids clearing out rooms of people who’d left the previous night. His room key chinked in the lock as he slowly opened the door with a slight creak and entered the room, presuming his mother was still asleep. As soon as he closed the door and turned round, the bold outline of his short mother could be seen across the room, “Vhere have you been, Michel,” she barked, her hands held rigidly on her hips, “I’ve just been doing some thinking, that’s all mother,” “Ah…Thinking is an Ameri-kun’s game. Wrestling is ours,” she spoke, a slight smile cracking at the side of her mouth, “Today, we shall prepare you for this tournament Michel. We will practice like never before…I have a question though Michel,” “Yes mother,” “Who is the other person in the room? There’s three beds and only two of us,” Pause. Silence. Another moment dropped off. Left Behind and. Hanging still. “…I don’t know mother. No idea,”
