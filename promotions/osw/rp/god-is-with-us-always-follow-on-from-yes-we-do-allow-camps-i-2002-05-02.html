God is with us always - Follow on from 'Yes, we do allow camps in Christianity'

2 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2nd May 2002 If you read my roleplay entitled 'Yes, we do allow camps in Christianity' then you would know Propaganda and Eric Sagar were scheduled to go on a balloon trip. Well after a few complications the trained 'expert' has fled. They are in a bit of a pickle. -Enjoy, THIS WAS YOUR IDEA!' We hear a roaring southern accent echo. 'I… I thought he knew what he was doing.' Moans another humbled southern accent. The scene fades in to see Eric Sagar and Propaganda arguing in the capsule of a hot air balloon. 'How was I supposed to know the instructor would flea at the first sign of terror?' The two men hastily consider their difficult situation. Wind speed was higher than they had expected, and if they carried on in the same direction they could be swept miles off the coast by the time darkness fell. A ground landing was much too unpredictable. Instead, they would try to bring the balloon down now, on a beach if possible or near one. Sagar began brining it down. They could have only imagined this horrible situation. 'Did I ever tell you I used to be a balloonist before I got the call?' Sagar asks. Propaganda just glares at him. 'I knew what to do, I just have to remember!' he adds. Sagar remembers, he would push the two red ignition buttons to fire the explosive bolts and release the balloon from the capsule. If the balloon remained attached to the capsule, it would either drag uncontrollably across the sea or tun the capsule into a submarine, dragging it below the waves with its either, and almost certainly drowning the pair. If all went according to plan, however, the balloon would be released, leaving the capsule to float safely on the water until they were collected. The capsule bounced on the water with a resounding thump, shaking them both. Sagar punched frantically at the ignition buttons for the explosive bolts. 'They haven't gone off!' Sagar exclaims 'Get out, Propaganda! Get out!' Sagar prised off the canopy and clambered onto the top of the capsule, Propaganda close behind him. The capsule was bouncing and skidding along the tops of the waves like a stone skimmed by a giant hand, the balloon flapping and billowing ahead. Then Sagar was shouting 'Jump Propaganda, for goodness' sake jump!' - and Sagar was gone. God's Ultimatum pulled himself to the edge of the rail and looked down. The balloon was rising upwards like an express elevator: 75, 100, 200, 500 feet above the water. It was too late to jump now. Looking down onto the grey water he could see no sign of Sagar. The Power Himself clambered back through the roof into the capsule, slumped in a seat and tugged at the handle of the burners to give himself time to think. The balloon was ascending at what seemed to be an astounding rate, into thick cloud. Above him the burners gave off a thin watery light. Propaganda then realised he was finding it hard to breathe. That meant he had risen further and faster and than he thought. He pulled the oxygen mask on and took several deep breaths to cal himself. Thing Propaganda, think. He would parachute out. The Power Himself strapped on his parachute and his liferaft and clambered back onto the roof of the capsule. Was this going to be it? For a fleeting moment, Propaganda thought he could meet his demise. He pulled himself back into the capsule and then he gave himself a mental slap. He had god on his side. Just to give himself more reassurance he dropped to his knees closed his eyes and grasped his hands in front of him. 'Our Father, You art in Heaven, Hallowed be thy name, Thy kingdom come, Thy will be done, On earth as it is in heaven, Give us this day our daily bread, And forgive us our trespasses, As we forgive those who trespass against us, Deliver us from evil, And not into temptation, For thine is the kingdom, The power and the glory, Forever and ever, A-men.' He prays humbly. Then he clambered back out again. The metal on the capsule roof was freezing cold and damp to the touch. Propaganda shivered. He felt very, very alone. Peering over the edge he could see only thick mist. It was impossible to tell whether he was over land or water, be he thought that somewhere he could hear the chatter of helicopter rotor blades. He did not want to jump. A feeling of nausea came into his throat. He had to hump. But wait. Supposing he jumped, landed in water and wasn't seen. Wouldn't everybody assume that he and Sagar were still in the balloon? Nobody would be looking for them in the sea. Eric would drown, if he hadn't drowned already. He would down too. It made more sense to stay in the balloon; that is what the rescue services would be watching out for. He must bring it down himself, then he could tell them were to look for Eric; then they would both be sage. Shivering, Propaganda pulled himself back inside the capsule. How long had he been up here by himself? Ten Minutes? Twenty? It seemed like an hour. Propaganda settled himself into his seat. He was breathing evenly now, tugging the levers, which controlled the burners, and venting the balloon. Everything was going as planned. The balloon was descending slowly now; the cloud thinning. And then it was clear. Below, riding the waves like a bathtub toy, God's Ultimatum could see a ship - a Navy ship. And there was a helicopter. And another. Propaganda took off his parachute, checked his life-vest and pulled himself back on to the capsule roof. The water rushed up towards him. Propaganda looked into the void. And Jumped.
