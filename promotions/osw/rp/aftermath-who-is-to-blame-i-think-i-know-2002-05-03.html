Aftermath: Who is to blame? I think I know.

3 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Friday, May 3rd 30-something people dead. Hundreds more injured. Well over half of a billion dollars worth of damage. No real numbers have been released yet, but that’s what’s being thrown out right now as a result of the Orlando riots earlier this week. Thursday night, I caught a plane and flew back to Orlando. At first, I wasn’t going to. But after thinking about it, and briefly consulting Amanda, I decided to fly down…alone. Why? It came to my attention there would be some sort of get-together on Friday. Not that I’m interested in those sort of things, but I thought it could be important, so I wanted to be there. I arrived home in the midnight hour on Friday. After taking a cab to a hotel and catching a few hours sleep, I woke up and headed for City Hall arriving just in time for some sort of town meeting. “Ugh,” I said to myself as I entered the building. “I can’t believe I’m actually here.” I slowly walked down a hall and entered a large room. The room is loaded with occupied chairs. After looking around the room, I just took a seat in an end chair in the back of the room and sat back. “The entire police force needs to be fired!” shouted one lady as she stood out of her chair. She obviously doesn’t look happy. “Because of their incompetence, my daughter is in a coma!” In the front of the room, the police commissioner stood up out of his seat. He got some heat from the people in the room. I, on the other hand, didn’t know nor care who he was. “Ma’am,” softly said the commissioner, “if we fired the entire force, then there would be nobody to maintain order if this situation ever arose again.” “Nobody maintained order this time!” shouted the lady. “There really was nothing any of us could do, ma’am,” the commissioner told her. “Nobody would’ve been able to control what happened those three days.” “That’s a load of bullshit!” the lady shouted out. I couldn’t help but to roll my eyes as she started getting out of line. Typical ignorance from narrow-minded assholes. She proposed some of the dumbest things. Fire the entire police force? If we did that, the common criminal would do whatever they wanted. We’d have a riot way more scary and serious than the one a bunch of teenyboppers put on this week. Bring in new officers from other cities? Heh. Why should we deplete another city’s police force? This is Orlando, Florida. As far as I’m concerned, we’re supposed to be the greatest city in the world. We don’t need people from Tampa or Miami to keep Orlando safe. And we sure as hell don’t need any 2nd-rate officers from 2nd-rate states like Georgia or North Carolina here. They don’t know how things work here. They’d just screw things up worse. Sigh I took out a handheld roulette game from my pocket and started to play it. It was the only real thing to do. I get tired of hearing complaining. After all, being surrounded by egomaniac wrestlers over 200 days of the year and having to hear them make excuses for every little thing that goes wrong is annoying in itself. I don’t want to have to come home and listen to this crap. Instead of making excuses, we should be looking for solutions. We should be looking for a way to prevent this from happening again. “Ugh,” I quietly said. A woman sitting next to me looked at me and told me to be quiet. Sigh I just sat and played my game. In the back of my mind, I listened to the complaining. Especially this one complaint by an old Japanese woman. She spoke rather fast and incoherently. “Slow down, ma’am,” the police commissioner told her. The woman had a hard time talking. “I lost all things,” she said in between tears. “Husband, two sons, store all go.” I stopped playing my game and started focusing on what she was saying. The lady was really clueing me in on what really happened. I had nothing to be scared of even though they did burn my house to the ground. These teenyboppers targeted certain people. They targeted fat, obese, overweight men. They targeted prostitutes, or dirty whores as I look at them as. And most of all, they targeted the Japanese population. The Japanese make up about 1% of the Orlando populace, but they were definitely the hardest hit. They took the brunt of the teenyboppers’ rage. And as I sat there listening to her, I really got to thinking. “Fat people. Dirty whores. Japanese people,” I thunk to myself. After a few moments of thinking, it finally came to me. “I got it!” I quietly exclaimed to myself. “I know what to do!” It all made perfect sense now. Now I know why these people did what they did. Now I know how to fix it. Now I know what to do. It’s all so clear now. “Time to get to work,” I told myself. There was no reason for me to be there anymore. I knew what I had to do, and dammit, I’m going to go and do it. Fade out
