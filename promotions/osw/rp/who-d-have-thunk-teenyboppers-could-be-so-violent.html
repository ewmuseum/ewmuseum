Who'd have thunk teenyboppers could be so violent?

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Monday-Thursday Monday It's been 10 years. I can't believe it's been that long, but it has. It was the closest thing to armageddon that I can remember. It's been 10 years to the day since the Los Angeles riots. Wow. 10 years. But that was then. That was when times were really tough over there. I mean, they still are, but it was much tougher back in the day. And after those bastards got off, it just added fuel to the fire. And while I don’t condone what happened 10 years ago, I do understand it. It was just like what that Xavier Kannon bloke from that glorified dump talks about. Oppression or something like that. But, again, times have changed and that should never be a problem. Rioting should only occur in places like College Park after winning a championship. That’s why I was shocked when I woke up this morning. I woke up around 7:30 like I do most mornings. I woke up and turned on my TV. And what I saw was just shocking. So shocking it made my jaw drop. On one of the news networks was my hometown. My hometown of Orlando, Florida was having problems. Why? What was going on? What could possibly be going on in Orlando that would be newsworthy? Did Boda finally kill himself? Did Bobby Stormbringer finally grow a set? What was going on? And then I saw it. On a street corner stood about fourscore of girls. They all ranged between the ages of 15 and 19 years old. None of them looked happy. “Is it that time of the month?” I questioned. Amanda, who was sitting next to me, smacked me for that one. But she didn’t think much of it after that. She just sat next to me and watched. We watched, almost in horror at what was going on. As the day progressed, things started getting worse. More girls started joining in. And as more girls came, more violence erupted. If someone even a tad bit fat would walk by, half a dozen of the girls would go after him. They’d throw things at the guy. They’d mock him. They would torment the poor individual. Then when the fat guy would try to run, the girls would run after him, tackle him to the ground, and beat him. They’d scratch, bite, claw, and do whatever they could humanly do to hurt the guy. And on a few instances, a girl wearing high heels would come along and step on the man’s testicles. I cringed. Then I would keep questioning what was happening. “Shouldn’t these girls be in school or something?” I’d ask. Amanda, on the other hand, wasn’t concerned with where they should be. The only thing on her mind was what they were doing. “I don’t believe it,” she’d keep saying, not being able to come to grips with what was going on in our hometown. “Someone has to stop them.” But, sadly, there was nobody there to stop the rampaging girls. As afternoon turned into evening, there had to have been close to 500 girls there. Some of the girls looked to be no older than eight. There just because their sister, mother, or babysitter was there. Others look to be almost as old as 40. They were completely oblivious as to why this was happening, but looked at it as a way to let out some pent up anger and frustration and get away with it. “They’re gonna destroy Orlando!” Amanda shouted out. I did nothing to console her. I couldn’t. I was too wrapped up in the television. My eyes were glued. This is where I was born and raised. There was no way I was looking away from it. “When is someone gonna stop them?” Amanda frantically questioned. “Just calm down,” I’d tell her time and time again. “Everything will be OK in the end.” Tuesday A day passed. Amanda and I hoped things would be calm after that, but sometimes you don’t get what you hope for. Things just got worse. Why are they so mad? What could have possibly happened to piss these ladies off? Lance’s rocket hasn’t blown up with him in it yet. Why are they so upset? It was around noon when we found out that the cops had all left. The Orlando police department is gone. “How could the cops leave at a time like this?” shouted Amanda in a mixture of anger and disbelief. Both of our emotions were going, but I was able to keep mine in check. I just sat back and watched what was happening from the Detroit hotel. The crowd had grown to about 800 girls by this time. There was no way the cops could stop them, so what better thing to do then to get out of dodge. There were times when I was contemplating going home. “Screw OSW,” I’d tell myself. “I need to be in Orlando right now.” But the logical side of me prevailed. I stayed in Detroit. That was the safest place to be. And I knew Amanda didn’t want to go back home right now, so that helped make the decision a bit easier. Plus, with the way these girls have been acting, the sight of any man could make them go off more. And being the man I am, I didn’t want to get torn apart at the hands of enraged teenyboppers. Again, afternoon turned into evening. That’s when the most violent event of the rioting to date took place. A truck was driving up the road. The man, a rather large, fat guy was minding his own business. As he drove along, one of the girls jumped into the middle of the road, ripped open her shirt, and flashed the guy. The man, trying to avoid the girl, ended up tipping the 18-wheeler over onto its side. The truck slid across the road and right into the crowd. It hit a couple dozen of the girls and slid right through a store window. “Oh my god,” Amanda shouted in horror. “That…that…” Amanda couldn’t even finish what she was saying. Instead, she just ran out of the room. As for me, I just sat there. While you don’t want to see that sort of thing happen, you can almost say that they had it coming. Oh well. Shit happens. Nothing you can do about it. Maybe this would be what was needed to stop this nonsense. Oh how wrong I was. Instead of stopping after watching a couple dozen of their peers get run over, the girls got worse. About eight girls dragged the fat man out of his car and beat him to death. Meanwhile, some other girls checked on the people that were run over. While that was happening, one girl went into the store and walked out with a tube of Loreal lipstick. And as she came out, others, having seen what she did, started going in. The looting had now begun. The crowd grew even more. They estimated about 1250 girls, but I could’ve sworn there were much more. Some of them brought bats so they could smash windows, limbs, and anything else that would break. Most of the girls stopped the acts of violence in favor of looting. CDs, clothing, posters, toiletries, cosmetics, and anything else the ladies could get their hands on, they took. It didn’t matter who they were hurting. As long as they were getting what they wanted, they didn’t care. Dolls, mirrors, frying pans, it didn’t matter what they took. I don’t even think they cared what they took. Most of them just looked to get something, anything, so they weren't left out. But just because they were now looting, that doesn’t mean the violence stopped. On a street corner, a man stopped his car. Yeah, he was a fat man. He stopped his car in favor of a prostitute. The guy wanted some loving. But a few dozen girls were standing in the shadows. They ran up to the car and beat the prostitute to a pulp. Then when the fat guy complained, they drug him out and, yeah, beat him to a pulp. “Why does this remind me of something?” I asked myself while watching that sequence. “A fatass and a whore getting beat senseless. Seems vaguely familiar to me.” I just shrugged my shoulders and kept watching. And as I did, I kept trying to piece together the puzzle. Why were they doing this? It’s gone on for two days. There has to be some reason for doing this. Wednesday For the past two night I have slept on the couch. Having fallen asleep while watching my own home be turned into rubble. I don’t wanna see it happen, but can’t help but to watch. And once again I found myself glued to the TV. I was so into what was happening in Orlando that I didn’t even notice Amanda come back. “So have they nuked Orlando to death yet,” she said sarcastically. Amanda seemed much calmer than she had been the past two days. But, still, I ignored her. I just sat and watched what was taking place in front of my very own eyes. The crowd in Orlando had escalated to around 1600 girls. Not a single guy amongst them. These were teenyboppers, no doubt about it. But what could get these people so riled up? Teenyboppers don’t act like this. The worst behavior I’d ever seen from a teenybopper was when I called Nick Carter a little baby to one’s face. And the worst I got out of that was a smack. Now they’re rioting. It just doesn’t make any sense. “Why are you ignoring me?” Amanda would ask. I wouldn’t respond. So Amanda just plopped onto the couch and wouldn’t even look at me. While Amanda is important to me, what’s going on in Orlando is more important. This is my home. These angry teenyboppers are starting to burn it to the ground. Yes, with the cops still away, the teenyboppers have now learned to play with fire. And where do they go to test their newfound ability than to Little Tokyo. Little Tokyo is where the majority of Orlando’s Japanese industry resides. It’s mostly businesses and what not, but that doesn’t stop the girls. Instead of being nice, they’re into violence, looting, and burning things now. They’d take some bats and smash store windows. If the storeowner attempted to stop them, the ladies would beat them up. They’d take everything they could grab from inside the store. Once done, they set the place ablaze. That appeared to be the theme of the day. Break in, loot, and set on fire. And if someone tried to stop them, the girls beat them to death. And their main focus was on the Little Tokyo neighborhood. “They’re really laying waste to the Japs,” I said to myself. “Oh really. I’m glad you noticed that, James,” Amanda said in a frustrated tone of voice. “It’s about time you noticed something since you haven’t noticed me in days.” I just ignored Amanda’s quibbling and watched as events unfolded on my television. Amanda, who wasn’t too pleased with me, got up and left again. Again, as day slowly turned into night, the rioting continued. Despite the crowd dying down to around 200 or so, they marched on. They marched up a hill until reaching a large, beautiful white house at the top. “Wait a minute,” I said to myself. “That place looks familiar. That’s…” I paused for a moment and watched in shock as the remaining girls lit the house on fire. My jaw dropped. “That’s my house,” I shouted out. I don’t believe it. They’re burning down my house. “No!” I kept shouting. This wasn’t supposed to happen. I thought they liked me. Why are they burning my house down? What did I do to them? A million thoughts started to go across the mind. As I watched the house slowly burn to a crisp, I did the only thing I could do. I cried. “Why?” I kept asking myself. “Why me?” Everything that could go wrong has gone wrong. The teenyboppers didn’t just destroy a good chunk of Orlando, but they culminated it all off by destroying my house. All my personal possessions, the statue of myself from Shazbotonia, the Alan Kriegman action figure, and even the severed head of Christina Aguilera are being burned with my house. As I was wiping my tears, Amanda came back. My head, which had been in my hands, slowly raised up and looked at her. I was almost embarrassed to look at her, but managed to bring myself to doing it. “What’s wrong?” she asked me sounding a little concern. “Our…our house…” I had a hard time talking since I was all choked up. “What?” she asked trying to get an answer out of me. I took a deep breath. After wiping my tears, I looked her in the eye. “Our house is gone,” I said. Amanda gasped. She ran over and looked at the TV. Sure enough, she saw their house being burnt to the ground. “Oh my god,” she exclaimed putting her hand over her mouth. “I don’t believe it.” “Neither do I,” I said dejectedly. “Neither do I.” For the rest of the night, Amanda and I consoled each other. Knowing that we now had no place to go home to, there was nothing else we could do. Just contemplate over what to do next. Thursday After three mind-numbing days, the violence has ended. The rioting has stopped. Police officers are back in control, but it’s too late. Fire fighters are back to putting the fires out, but it’s too late. Our house is already gone. And while there’s a chance we might be able to salvage some stuff from there, there’s no way we can go back. A once great house has now been turned to dust. Three days after it started, there were still questions that needed answered. Questions that I, along with many others were probably asking themselves. What just happened? Why did this happen? How were a bunch of teenyboppers able to cause so much damage and destruction? And then there was the serious stuff. No numbers were released yet, but it was safe to say there would be a high death toll as a result of the rioting. And it’s safe to say there were hundreds of people hurt in some capacity. And as for how much damage was caused, I couldn’t even begin to imagine. I’d just guess that it was more than even Tyler Nelson could afford. That doesn’t even include my own stuff. Items that are irreplaceable that were taken away from me with my house. “There’s gotta be something we can do,” I told Amanda. “There was a reason for all of this. And whatever the reason was, we need to do something to fix it.” Amanda didn’t say anything. I think she was at a loss for words. I would be too if I was her. But having dealt with many issues, and having been around people who have dealt with serious issues, I just knew I had to do something. I had to say something. “We can’t let this kind of thing happen again,” I said. “Whatever the reason was for it this time, we’ve got to do something to fix it.” “If…if you say so,” she quietly said. There’s not much else to say. All we can do is look on for now. Look on and wait for questions to be answered. Whatever was done to cause this, I’ll do my damn best to fix it. And if I can’t do it, I’ll kill myself trying. That’s all anyone can ask for. Isn’t it?
