Preperation

23 May 2002

Date: 23rd May 2002 ‘Damn’, Ian Wright had just banged his head against the wall of his hotel room, it was not surprising considering that he was in an uncommon position, trying to lean around the back of the TV to plug the AV line from the VCR into the aerial input of the television. He had rented the video player from reception that morning, it was fortunate that this hotel offered this facility because he had meant to watch these tapes before he left home but had not had the time to get through them before his flight, the past 5 days had taken him from Ontario to his adopted hometown of Kirkland Wa. and then back to the centre of Canada, it was not all that far from Calgary to Winnipeg Wright thought. He would normally have just travelled straight from the location of last week’s Slam to this week’s but he had not counted on being booked against Jesus St. Crux in a match this week, in fact he had planned to be preparing to challenge for the OSW International title but St. Crux had put paid to that by interfering in his match. It was only fair though, Wright had after all cost him a shot at the Cruiserweight title by distracting St. Crux and allowing Kid Wonder to pin him. St. Crux was the reason that Wright had to take the trip home. He wanted to view the video tapes that now lay on his bed. All of them were marked with the logo of the UWA, and on each of them was written the date, venue and participants of the matches that they featured, nearly two dozen in all and they all had one thing in common, on their labels were written Wright verses St. Crux. Wright really shouldn’t have needed to go home to get these tapes, after wrestling St. Crux so many times he should know more about St. Crux’s in ring style than Jesus did himself, but he was nothing if not thorough in his preparations for his matches. St. Crux was the greatest opponent that Wright had faced in his entire career and if you asked them both you might, just might get each man to admit a grudging respect for each other’s talents, Wright would need to be on top of his game to ground the high flier. Every match that the pair had ever had was in front of him, from the first time that they ever wrestled each other in a mixed tag match with a pair of UWA cameramen as their partners to their final battle at Spring Break 2001 where in a brutal match Wright had taken the Canadian title from St. Crux. That was the night that both men had sealed their places as UWA greats when after having already wrestled for half an hour they both got to the last 5 men left in the 2 ring Lord of the Rings battle royale, Wright had failed to become a double champion that night, but St. Crux managed to triumph, winning the battle royale and the subsequent match for the UWA heavyweight title. And there had been many matches in between, too many to mention really, each of them unique, both men were constantly varying their styles in an attempt to gain the upper hand. St. Crux had tried to use his normal high flying style, he had tried to brawl with him, he had tried to mat wrestle him, Wright never knew what he was going to get when he climbed into the ring with St. Crux. Similarly Wright had used power against St. Crux, he used brawling and he too had used a technical strategy, but a decisive victor had never emerged. That was a situation that Wright did not wish to let happen in OSW, it was the reason that he had returned to Washington for these tapes, he was going to study every match that they had ever had, every combination of styles that they had used, and he would find something in them to give Wright the edge, St. Crux is a great wrestler, but he is not flawless. And if Wright wanted to take the UWA heavyweight title that he knew belonged to him that Jesus was currently flaunting he would have to find that flaw. Wright placed the first tape into the VCR and pressed play on the remote. He sat down on the edge of his bed and stared intently at the screen as a bell rang that signified the beginning of a rivalry that had never ended, and perhaps never would.
