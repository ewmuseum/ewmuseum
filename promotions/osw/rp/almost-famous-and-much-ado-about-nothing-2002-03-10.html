Almost Famous and Much Ado About Nothing

10 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-10-02 Our scene opens backstage at the Nokia Dome. Yeah, yeah. I know it’s not the most original setting, but where the hell else would wrestlers be after the show went off the air. It’s not like they could mystically teleport so some magic elf land where the houses are made out of mushrooms and the rivers flow with milk chocolate. If they wanted to go there I’m sure it would be a good ten, fifteen minute drive from the stadium. And who wants to waste gas on that garbage when plenty of fun could be had just hanging around backstage after Slam. Well I think that’s a decent enough defense of the clichéd setting, so we might as well just get to the plot. Seth Masters and Trevor Hawke are currently walking down one of those winding corridors you see so often in those backstage vignettes. This isn’t your typical Civil Disobedience post-Slam promo, however. This week’s is different from all of the others you’ve seen so far. For on this day they are not on their way to the hospital, nor are they soaked in their own blood, nor are they limping from being tossed through tables. No, today they are upbeat, chipper, and...shockingly look unscathed. Could it be they came out on top tonight with a post-match beatdown? For those of you that saw Slam, you’ll know that they have! Hooray! Hooray for this glorious day! As they make their victorious way down the hallway, their voices come into range and we can hear their topic of conversation. “And then I fucking kicked the bastard in the throat,” Seth is saying excitedly, kicking his foot in the air to illustrate. “It was like...BLAM!!! and he was eating fucking concrete, man.” “And remember when I leaped off the guardrail with the...” Trevor starts. “Oh shut up, Trev. Nobody cares what you did,” Seth interrupts. “What the hell are you talking ab...” Trevor starts once more, but is quickly cut off. “Quiet! And then I fucking took that steel chair and went fucking Jedi on his ass.” Seth says, pretending like he’s holding a lightsaber and swinging it around. “Bzzzz, Bzzzz, Snick, Waaaaa! Shit, I was fucking Seth Kenobi and he was Darth Anal Invader.” “Seth...Vader killed Obi-Wan in Episode 4,” Trevor says after a sigh. “Didn’t I tell you to shut up, dude? Quit trying to make me look bad,” Seth says, pushing his partner away. Trevor just brushes his shirt off and raises an eyebrow at him “Oh yeah, I forgot. You need to impress your legions of fans, right? Where the hell are the drooling masses, anyway?” “Uh...I don’t know,” Seth responds, changing from his cocky tone to a more sheepish one. “Hey, wait a minute. I know that sheepish tone,” Trevor remarks wryly. “It’s the tone you always use when you’re trying to get off a subject.” “No it’s not,” Seth answers in the same tone. “There, you used it again! Alright, something is going on. Enlighten me, Seth.” Trevor folds his arms, waiting for an answer, then a look of contemplation comes over his face as he realized what he just said. “Hmmm, would’ve never thought I’d be asking you that.” “I don’t really want to talk about it,” Seth says, looking off in the opposite direction, taking off his hat and running his hand through his hair. “Why, what happened? Your fame wear off or something?” Trevor asks with a smirk. “No!” Seth shouts back defensively. “Then where are all your fans?” presses Trevor. “They...uh...they must be waiting in the parking lot. Yeah, that’s it. The parking lot!” Seth states defiantly. “They’re not waiting in the parking lot, Seth,” Trevor tells his partner, placing a hand on his shoulder cockily. “Face it, you’re just a normal Joe again.” “I know,” Seth admits softly, hanging his head sadly. He raises his fist in the air and screams. “Oh God, why must the people be so fickle!? Why must they BE SO FICKLLLLLLLE!?” There is silence for a moment as Seth pauses and stares up at the ceiling. About ten seconds pass and then the two resume their walking once more. “Eh, it could be worse. We could be laying in hospital beds right now,” Seth says, trying to look at the bright side of the situation. “Don’t speak too soon. We’ve still got a PPV to get through, bro,” Trevor answers back. “Aw, don’t tell me you’re not going all pessimistic and shit on me,” Seth shouts, before Trevor cuts him off. “No, no. I was just joking, dude. There’s no way we’re going to lose tomorrow night. I mean, the match is our specialty, right?” Trevor laughs, getting a confused look from Seth. “The Suburban Death Match?” he asks hesitantly. “Yeah, that’s the one,” Trevor nods. “Um, I may not have been paying attention or some shit, but...what exactly is it again?” Seth asks, a questioning expression coming over his face. “You mean the rules? Well, uh...they never did clarify on the details,” answers Trevor, shrugging his shoulders. “Well...how can it be our specialty if we don’t know what the hell it is?” Seth shouts, flailing his arms in the air. “Damn, don’t get all pissy about it!” Trevor shouts back. “Sorry, this whole fame episode has gotten me really moody,” Seth says in a apologetic tone. “Anyways, I figure that since the match has got Suburban in the title. How can it not be our specialty. It’s not the Heresian Deathmatch, whatever the shit that means. Suburban. Us. Specialty. Right?” Trevor says, looking to Seth for approval, which he doesn’t get. “Sheesh, and they call me the dumb one,” Seth mutters, rolling his eyes. Trevor doesn’t reply, just shrugging back. This kicks off another long period of silence before the two men finally make it out of the arena and into the parking lot. It’s a pretty big parking area; big enough for the opposite end to be out of sight. There are only a few cars left in the gigantic lot dotting the terrain, as most of the OSW wrestlers and personnel have already left. The ones that are left are mainly the bigwigs and the ring crew. Standing in the doorway to the back of the arena, Trevor places his hand above his eyes, peering out into the distance. Sweeping his gaze around, he looks confused. “Seth?” he asks. “Where’s our car?” “You’re standing right next to it, dumbass,” Seth says, nudging Trevor and pointing to their right. It seems as if his claims are valid, as sitting just fifteen feet away from them is the duo’s beat-up gray Chevy Celebrity. “Whoa-hoa-hoa! Nice parking spot, man! How the fuck did you pull that off? I thought the parking lot was full we when got here?” Trevor exclaims as he runs to the car. “Shit, it was no problem at all, man. After I dropped you off at the front and came around back here to park the car I thought I might as well check to see if there were any open spots. I sure as hell wasn’t going to park a mile away. Sure enough I drove all the way up here and this was the only open spot. It was primo...” Seth reminisces, smiling and nodding at the thought. “Seth, I think I know why it was open,” Trevor says, tapping his partner on the shoulder. “Check it out.” Trevor points at the sign sticking out from the ground in front of the car. It bears the words ‘Reserved for: Ed’. Seth stares at it for a while and then smiles slightly. “Man, I don’t know how I missed that one,” he says, then laughs. “Oh well, that sucks for the Commish. Anyway, are you ready to leave yet, Trev?” “Yeah, sure,” Trev answers, moving to get into the passenger side. “Whoa, hold on. Who the fuck is that?” Trevor is staring off to the left, where a small figure is approaching in a sort of waddle. As the person comes into view it is revealed to be a boy of about fourteen, with heavy acne, duct-taped glasses, and a Pokemon T-shirt. As he runs up to the car, he’s panting heavily, but he manages to speak through his deep breaths. “Oh my goshhhhh!” he screams, his voice distorted by his mouth full of braces. “It’s Seth Masterssshhh! It’shhh really you!” “Shit, it’s one of those losers who don’t realize a fad is over until four months after it becomes unpopular,” Trevor whispers to Seth, who is busy shielding himself from the spittle flying from the kid’s mouth. “What fad are you referring...oh, you’re talking about me,” Seth says, shaking his head sadly. As the kid keeps jibbering at him Seth and Trevor jump into the car and back out quickly, running the nerd over in the process. The car speeds out of the parking lot, stopping only for a moment as the members of Civil Disobedience laugh at Ed’s car parked a good five minute walk away from the arena. The scene fades out.
