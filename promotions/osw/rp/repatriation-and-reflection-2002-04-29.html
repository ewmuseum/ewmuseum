Repatriation and Reflection

29 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-29-02 Days after Head on Collision, Ivan Stanislav was nowhere to be seen. Sunday night he was reported to have entered into his office and had not exited since. As one would near knocking on the door to check on him the fear of the repercussions were too much and resulted in a retreat, lest they rouse The Russian Bear. Some tried to call him via telephone, thinking that perhaps talking to him in that way would be safer, but he did not answer his telephone. Some members of The High Command feared that perhaps he had done something drastic such as taking his own life and urged that the door, which was locked, be knocked down. However, others, notably Alexei Ruslan, said otherwise and pushed for patience to win out. He knew that Ivan would not do such a thing and urged that The Russian Bear merely be awarded some time to himself. Nevertheless, the Hierarchy of Russia was gossiping over what Praporschik Stanislav was doing locked up in his office for days on end. Never before had Ivan done something so drastic and hasty. Hastiness was not in Praporschik Stanislav’s nature and this worried them greatly. So what was Ivan Stanislav doing locked in his office having no contact with the outside world? After learning that Tempest was indeed gone, Stanislav began to take inventory of his priorities. With Tempest away he found a void in his life that needed filled and he could not find anything to fill it. As far as surviving in his room, Stanislav was pretty well off. He slept in his chair, ate one meal a day from rations he had saved, and had used the bathroom in his office for water and other personal matters However, this sort of behavior could not go on forever. Stanislav had obligations to the State and those above him would not harbor a hermit Praporschik forever…. Ivan Stanislav sat staring at a small red feather that rested on his desk. The feather had actually come from one of Tempests many boa’s in her room. This one in particular had attacked Ivan as he came over for dinner and inadvertently attacked Ivan and wound up in his pocket. It was the only physical object he had to remember his “Little Storm.” He had sat there for hours, literally, staring at it. When he found it he had nonchalantly placed it on his desk along with his identification card. But as he walked and paced in his office he found himself spellbound by the feather and would stare at it for hours on end. This was just another time when he could not pull away from it and he thought about Tempest. His beard, while normally scraggly, had grown out in a tangled mess. His gray hairs grew longer and more prominent in his beard and stuck out like jagged pieces of wire. He was paler than normal and his eyes appeared tired. He looked old….drained. Suddenly, Ivan tore his eyes from the feather almost violently, as if he had fought to do so for a long time, and rose to his feet. He turned from the desk and put his back to the camera. Stanislav was far too preoccupied with his thoughts to hear Alexei Ruslan fidget with the door to his office and unlock it. Ruslan slunk into the office and stared at Stanislav for a moment. He cleared his throat and shifted his weight. “Praporschik?” muttered Alexei. Ivan did not move, but a long sigh exited his body, “Is that you Alexei? I was wondering how long it would take before they confronted me.” Alexei swallowed nervously and shut the door behind him. Ivan continued, “Am I still Praporschik?” “Da, Ivan.” “Am I still respected?” “Da, Ivan.” “And am I still, a comrade?” “Of course Comrade!” Ivan’s head dropped slightly, “What do the others say?” Alexei shifted his weight uneasily, “About what, Comrade?” “My failure to gain World Title is start. My humiliation that millions of people have seen! My lackluster performance! My….” Ivan trailed off, his back still to us. He balled his hands into fists. Alexei slowly responded, “They are…concerned Ivan. They are also…disappointed in your…failure.” “As they should be….” Whispered Stanislav. Ivan could not bring himself to face Alexei. He was embarrassed and ashamed of himself. He decided to ask the next question with his back to Alexei as well… “What do they think of entire…Tempest ordeal?” Alexei had hoped this question would not arise but he knew the chances of it coming were quite high. He pushed his hands into his pockets and looked at the ground. “They are pleased that she has left. But they are hoping you will return to normalcy.” Normalcy was a word that Ivan had been hearing a lot lately . In actuality, he did not care what they thought. What he did care about was what Alexei thought. “And what are your opinions on entire matter?” Alexei closed his eyes as he had also wished this question would not have been asked. He decided, however, to be honest with Ivan, “I….” He paused as the words got caught in his throat, “I….I think you should not have given her that book.” The book in question was the original book of the “Communist Manifesto” as was written by V. I. Lenin. At this, Ivan whirled around, “It was heirloom of mine! I could do with it what I wanted!!” “That is correct,” answered Alexei, “but did you have to give it to her? A non-communist?” “Alexei, who else would I have given it to? I know no one else outside of Russia!!” Ivan took a step closer to Alex. “Besides, she needed it! I wanted to give it to her!” Alexei crossed his arms over his chest, “And now? That book which was over seventy years old, is now lying in ashes somewhere with your name burnt away with it!” Ivan growled as some of his old anger began to emerge, “I do not care what happens to it! I passed it on and it is out of my hands! I could do anything I want with it!!” Alexei gasped and recoiled, taken aback by such an individualistic thought! “Who do you work for, Praporschik?” “Russia.” Responded Ivan. “And why do you fight for Russia?” Ivan swallowed, “Because I must protect the State….which has, or should have, complete control.” “And if that is the case, Ivan,” continued Ruslan, “who owns you?” Ivan blinked as his shoulders sunk, “R….Russia. Alexei nodded, “Good! And therefore, your belongings as well as your actions are the property and responsibility of Russia, right?” “Yes.” Ivan answered weakly. The mantra that he swore to uphold was now crushing him. “Therefore, the book was Russia’s! Now perhaps you can understand our concern!” Ivan nodded slowly. He then looked up at Ruslan with newfound fight, “When Meanstreak attacked, my first instinct was to indeed run and let he and Doug Troy eliminate one another.” Ivan swallowed hard as the memory overcame him, “But then I turned to help Tempest and you stopped me from saving her! Why did you do that Alexei? Why did you stop me!? This would never have happened otherwise!!” Alexei fired back, “Because your orders were to do so! They were NOT to protect your opponents, which she was!” Ivan grabbed the side of his head, “Orders, objectives, goals! Why must it always be like that??” “Praporschik, because…” Ivan interrupted Ruslan, “SHE does not live by such words and she has survived! She fights for what she wants and is content AND the people love her for it!! They despise me!!” “IVAN SERGEIOVICH!!” Alexei screamed, “Listen to yourself! If you fight for yourself and no one else that is greedy and capitalistic!” Ivan looked at the floor. “She willingly entered that match and she paid the price! She was NOT your responsibility!” Ruslan paused for a moment, “AND afterwards you saw how much she cared in that parking lot when she refused to make attempt to save what you had!” The words hurt. Ivan did not want to hear this. He thought that she was his responsibility since Meanstreak was after him. But if the State thought otherwise, they must be correct. Ivan put his hands in his pockets and backed against a wall while still staring at the floor. “Tempest left you after a so-called error on your part, Ivan,” Alexei swallowed, “Russia has not abandoned you.” Ivan’s face twisted. He didn’t know what to do. Part of him, a part of him quite new, wanted to throw his badge and medals across the room and tell Ruslan he was wrong. But another part wanted to accept it. This part Stanislav had possessed for his entire life and yearned to simply accept the words of Alexei and end this terrible ordeal. Ivan turned away from Alexei and put his hands on his hips. “I do not understand what is going on, Alexei.” Ivan paused, “I feel a great emptiness. I wish to agree with you, but I will not deny that I am hesitant!” Ivan paused, his voice seemed to waver, “I miss her, Alexei. I wish she had never left…” he swallowed as his head lowered, “I miss seeing her smile, and hearing her giggle….and having her fall asleep in my arms….” Alexei walked closer, “She left you Ivan. The capitalist took advantage of you and left! The communists, however, have stayed.” Ivan closed his eyes. “I am sure she did not work alone, Ivan. We can use this injustice put against you to our advantage.” Alexei continued to press his thoughts as he tried to kill the portion of Ivan that was individualistic. Ivan nodded painfully. “Forget about her Ivan. It is painful but now you know who truly cares for you, da?” Ivan kept his eyes closed. He thought Tempest cared for him…he thought she may have truly enjoyed his company….she was the only person that gave Ivan the chance to show her who he was. And it hurt to think that she did not care for him. It hurt deep inside of him. “Comrade, am I right??” Ivan thought long and hard and then slowly opened his eyes, “Y…yes..comrade.” He answered raspily. Alexei smiled, “Good! I have some reports for you.” He walked to the door and turned, “It is good to see you back, Praporschik Stanislav!” Alexei smiled as Ivan nodded slowly. Ruslan turned and walked out the door, leaving Ivan alone. Ivan turned as the door shut and leaned against the wall. He turned and faced a nearby window and pulled open the shade, revealing a beautiful blanked of snow on the countryside. Stanislav stared out of the window as his brow furrowed. He put his hands on his hips and stared, the only sound was his breathing. His eyes closed as his lower lip quivered and he bit it in an attempt to stop the memories. He thought of his first meeting with Tempest and how beautiful she was. And how nervous HE was. When he preached communism and she fell asleep during the movie in the Congo. The fateful match in which Ivan made perhaps the most damaging mistake in his life. He swallowed, his massive Adams apple bobbled as he opened his eyes and blinked quickly. A small smile curved across his face…. He thought about how she came to him to heal his head wound. No one had ever come to help him with anything of the kind. How compassionate she was towards him after barely knowing him. And how he had actually gone to help her when she was hurt. He remembered his words on that night… If you show that you are worried for someone, then that is something that can be exploited. He shook his head and sighed again, his lip stopped quivering. His temples moved as he ground his teeth together and tried to think of something else. But the memories crashed into his mind again. He remembered how he wished Tempest would come to Russia…after the first time when he had a concussion that appeared to be worthless. He had asked her to visit and she did. For the first time in his life, someone outside of Russia had come to visit him. It was there that he wished her a happy belated birthday and held her in the Alexandrovsky Gardens after giving her the most prized possession he ever had. It was there he felt something he never had before…his heart beat harder on that beautiful day as he held her in his arms. He felt….alive. Ivan’s lip quivered again as he caught his breath suddenly. His muscles tensed in his arms and neck as he bit down hard. And that dinner, that dinner with that god awful hot food… Ivan chuckled at that and smiled, but it was a sad smile all the same as it melted into a frown once again. He stood still again for a long time, not really thinking about anything, just staring out into the snow as another phrase crept into his mind… There are some things that will never become history... Ivan shook suddenly. He opened his mouth and shut it as he sucked in a gulp of air and slammed his jaws shut. His eyes quivered, his muscles tensed, his breathing quickened as he reached out and grabbed the side of the window to steady himself, feeling he may faint. Why did he do it? Why was he such a coward? Why did he hurt her? He remembered her crying backstage at the Pay Per View. He had made her cry. He had made her cry. He closed his eyes again and breathed in deeply through his nose. He felt so empty. His life seemed so worthless now without her and knowing that somewhere, she was probably crying herself to sleep made him want to kill himself. Sleep that wouldn’t come because he had abandoned her when he said he never would. He had hurt her when he swore he would stop those that yearned to. He straightened himself and let go of the wall. He lifted his head proudly and regained his composure and stood. And stared. And stared. And stared. He thought of humming her to sleep. Humming her the Russian Lullaby during that horrendous storm. He had rocked her to sleep in his arms. She nuzzled against him completely safe in his grasp. She trusted him. He would give the world for her and she was gone. He had ruined it. He had failed himself. He feared that forever, she would be haunted by his painful deeds. Forever the memory of Ivan Stanislav would be one riddled with pain and suffering. Of lies…. What he would give to hold her in his arms and rock her to sleep again. Standing up in Arkhangelsk only proved how lonely he was in his cold, secluded office. He had questioned what ‘love’ was as she laid with him and he had figured it out. He new what the definition of love was….. She hated him for he was a monster. She hated him and wished he had never met her. When she said they were based on lies….she was correct. He steeled himself and ground his jaw again… He would never have her again. What happened happened and it was over. The damage had been done and to dwell on the past is to hurt for the future. The memories were too much for The Russian Bear. All the training in the world could not prepare him for the emotional thoughts that flew through his head and culminated in one completely alien thing to him… A tear. A single, solitary tear welled up in the corner of his left eye as he continued to stare out the window. His cheek twitched and his lower lip shook violently as the tear grew in size and began to roll down the angular cheekbone of The Russian Bear. The tear traveled down the side of his rugged face and neared the scraggly beard around his granite chin. The tear cut through the steely hairs of his beard like a knife through butter, as if nothing in the world could stop it. It rolled along the hairs to the cleft in his chin and slipped down a single gray hair that stuck out stubbornly and hung there. A soft…whimper…escaped Ivan’s body as a shiver rolled up his spine and he snorted. The tear struggled to hold on to the hair and then gave way, dropping silently off the hair and plummeting down to the floor where it splattered violently. As it hit the floor, Stanislav breathed in deeply and exhaled erratically. He closed his eyes for a long time as he struggled to push the memories from his mind. After all, she was gone. Alexei was right in the fact that Russia stood by him after his mistakes, while Tempest seemingly did not. Alexei was right….he owed his allegiance to the State, not to himself. Alexei was right….Ivan does what the hierarchy says and does not question it…ever. Ivan swallowed hard and stood tall again, slowly he opened his eyes as the memories subsided and he stared at his Russian homeland through the window. He felt a soft pain in his heart as it broke into countless pieces, only to be hardened by a steel that may never be breached again. It was at that moment that something very young and fragile, yet very vibrant and joyous, died inside of Ivan Stanislav. It was at this time that this ‘something’ that he loved dearly and did not know it, that this ‘something’ that yearned to flow through him again, was eclipsed by words such as “normalcy” “the State” and “objective.” He exhaled again and blinked as his features hardened as they had countless times before while he allowed that portion of him to be locked away deep within the pit of his stomach so that nothing could ever awaken it again. The streak from the tear began to dry on his pale face as he grabbed the window and pushed it open, allowing the icy air to blow into the room. The cold Russian air blasted his face. He swallowed and stared out the window, stoic in his poise as he had been in the past without even the slightest hint of emotion. The breeze flew through the open window past Ivan and whirled around the papers on his desk, rustling them. The boa feather struggled to remain atop the desk. However, the breeze was too powerful. It surrounded the feather and lifted it from the desk, blowing it in a maelstrom of air, whipping it off the desktop. As the little wind storm died around the feather it dropped into a half opened drawer in Stanislav’s desk, hidden within the shadows of his Communist books…
