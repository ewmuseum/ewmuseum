Jailbait.....

27 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-27-02 The ordeal following the arrest of Ivan Stanislav was one that the Winnipeg Police Department had not been prepared to handle. Logistically alone it was a tremendous undertaking. Handcuffs could not fit him, he barely was able to fit in the back of a squad car. The facilities at the station were not enough to house Stanislav, for after his violent outburst at the airport, they did not feel safe with him in their little private cell and thus, chose to move him to the nearby jail in order to hold him in a much more secure area. But the problems didn’t necessarily stop there. There was no pair of clothes or shoes big enough to clothe Stanislav. They would all have to be specially made for The Russian Bear, and considering the chance that his stay would not be permanent, it was folly to go through all the work so, after thorough searching, they decided to leave him in his current clothes. Throughout the entire tribulation, Stanislav was extremely uncooperative. Often muttering to the men in Russian and generally making their lives far more trying than it had to be, getting him finally into a cell was a welcome outcome to the day. But Ivan’s troubles only STARTED there… The bed itself was entirely too small for the huge height of Ivan Stanislav and when he laid in it, his legs from the knees down fell off the side. And then, after a few minutes of uncomfortable rest, the bed’s legs snapped and crashed to the floor, giving way to his tremendous weight! Because of this, Stanislav walked over to his steel chair and table. But after sitting in the chair for a few minutes, the metal legs slowly began to bend inward and the chair flattened under itself. When he tried to support himself from the fall the entire table twisted and broke as well…. And thus, Ivan Stanislav sat on his broken bed, knees brought up high to his barrel chest, and seethed. He seethed for a long time, merely staring at the wall ahead of him which was covered with phone numbers, scrawled writings, and scratched out phrases. Alexei would come and get him out of this, he knew it was only a matter of time before he would come. Tomifei would get him out of this mess and set things right. Minutes turned into hours as Stanislav sat on the bed. He finally rose and walked over to the bars. The screaming of other inmates was deafening as guards patrolled the halls. Stanislav brought his hands up and grasped the bars. He could probably bend them if he wanted. He could probably tear the entire set out of the wall..but what was the use? He’d only create more problems by doing so. Stanislav looked down at his shirt, where one area had a hole burnt into it from the stun gun that was fired at him. He growled and rubbed the hole, his skin blistered underneath his shirt, giving his pale skin a very red patch in the dead center of this chest. “All of this because of a stupid animal!” Growled Stanislav as he turned from the bars and walked over to the only area that could give him a decently sized seat: a steel toilet. He sat on it, still entirely too low for him, and rested his head on his hands. “What am I doing here? I should be in Russia preparing for next Slam! I do not even know what I have in store for myself yet!” He paused for a moment and looked at the floor, “A Russian Warrant Officer in Canadian Jail….what have things come to?” He digested his phrase for a moment and looked at the collapsed bed and table. They’d get him out of this, he knew they would. The Army would not want Ivan in another country for a prolonged period of time. Yet, sitting in the jail gave Ivan a secure area to think about things. Things that normally he either did not have time to, or was too preoccupied to handle. Things such as his place in the Russian Federation. Things like Alexei’s response to his conspiracy theory. But he trusted Alexei. To him, Alexei was in the same predicament as himself, but perhaps even more so being so close to volumes of sensitive information regarding Russia. Ivan stood again and sighed as he walked over to a marked up wall and ran his hand over the wall. It was grimy, dirty, and disgusting. “How does a Russian Soldier get thrown in such confusing predicament?” Ivan muttered as he contemplated this, questioning if he was talking about his physical surroundings, or his professional situation. “More important…how do you get out of them? How do you get out of the cages that they put you in…and you yourself…fall within?” He turned and walked over to the fallen bed and sat on it until he reclined and laid down. He put his hands over his face and sighed. Here was a Russian soldier, one who shook hands with Gorbechev, fought in wars, saved lives of many…. …stuck in a cell for criminals….anarchists. They’d get him out of this…and then he’d fix them all….each and every one of them…
