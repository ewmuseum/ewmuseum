H.G. Wells is Rolling in his Grave

22 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-22-02 Our scene fades in with a disturbing shot of a huge bloodshot eye filling the entire screen. It’s really frickin’ big, I say. The pupil is the size of my fist. Considering the fact that I have large hands, that’s saying something. Yep, it sure is a big eye all right, and not a very pretty one, either. Bleh, yucky. The capillaries are swollen, visibly branching across the surface of the sclera, not unlike many tiny rivulets breaking off from the mighty mother river. Now how’s that for an analogy? Pretty damn sweet, huh? No? Oh well, then... We pull back to reveal a shocking fact. The eye wasn’t some freakishly giant abomination of nature at all, but instead a normal sized eye in an extreme close-up. The wonders of cinematography these days never cease to amaze me. Pulling out even further, we see that the normal-sized (though still disturbingly bloodshot) eye is attached to the optical nerves of none other than Civil Disobedience’s promo co-star as of late, Keanu Reeves, who seems to be looking rather worse for the wear...whatever that means. Keanu is currently sitting on a makeshift stool composed of adult magazines, staring up at a clock on the wall of Seth and Trevor’s living room area. He flinches a bit as every second ticks away, never blinking, and with a demonic smile painted across his face. Judging from the looks of the man, he has obviously gone a bit insane, and has been so for quite some time by now. His arms crossed, hands pressed underneath his armpits, Keanu rocks back and forth, never moving his gaze away from the clock. In a way, this is possibly more disturbing than that yucky eye we saw earlier. Hmmm...nah, that eye was really icky now that I think about it. Keanu must have been staring unmoving at that clock for at least...ten hours, thirty two and three quarters minutes...when he suddenly jerks his head away at the sound of an opening door. Spinning around, he hunches down, craning his head to the side as he watches Civil Disobedience member Trevor Hawke trod into the room from the foyer. Reeves looks paranoid as he initially surveys Hawke, but what’s left of his mind registers Trevor’s face and he relaxes a bit, though that doesn’t stop him from spasming slightly every few seconds. “Hey Keanu, what’s up?” Trevor asks nonchalantly, apparently not observing just how lost the actor’s marbles really are. Keanu looks to the ceiling in fear, crouching down and placing his hands over his head, as if bracing himself for some torrent from the sky After, seeing that the only thing on the ceiling is mildew, Reeves shakes his head, embarrassed, and smiles wickedly at Trevor. “Oh, nothing much Trev!” he says happily, through gritted teeth. “Just watching the old time tick away, is all! Tick tock, tick tock, tick tock! Whooooooo!” Keanu laughs insanely at his own comment, falling on the ground in hysterics. Despite this show of madness, Trevor’s casual expression doesn’t change. “Yep,” Trevor nods in agreement. “That clock is always the big attraction when we have company here. They just sit and stare at it for hours. I don’t know why, though. I don’t see anything so special about the thing. Though perhaps the fact that the only company we’ve had so far was a couple of acid-tripping hippies had something to do with...” “Guess what!?” Keanu shouts, cradling his legs in his arms while he rocks on the floor. “Two days, seven hours, nineteen minutes, and eleven seconds until your next show!” “Ha!” Trevor says with a chuckle. “That’s funny that you’ve got that all figured out, buddy. It’s almost as if you’re eager to get out of here or something. Ha ha!” Trevor playfully slaps Keanu on the back, then exits the room through the doorway leading to the TV room. Reeves stares after the departed wrestler, before giggling to himself and turning his attention back to the clock. “Almost out...heh heh...almost...” he mutters happily to himself. “Seth and Trevor make me go cuckoo. Cuckoooooooooo!” Keanu giggles madly again, as the scene cuts to the TV room that Trevor has just entered. There is a case of beer sitting the corner of the room, which Trevor reaches his hand into, pulling out a can in one fell swoop and continuing his walk without missing a step. Popping the tab, he begins to sip from the alcoholic beverage as he makes his way out the back door of the house, into the back yard of the house. What? You didn’t know the house had a back yard? It’s the suburbs, man. Everyone has a back yard. Strutting across the grass, Trevor soon comes to a stop in front of a heap of machinery situated in the center of the yard. A tinkering sound can be heard from the opposite side of the contraption. Seth’s head pokes out from around the corner of the hunk of junk, thus confirming that some idiotic act is currently underway, possibly too stupid for the normal mind to comprehend. I’m sure we’ll find out if that’s the case soon enough. Trevor sips his beer as he stares at Seth, who looks to be hard at work, using various tools to hammer, drill, screwdrive, wrench, and any other action word that can be related to the name of the tool used to perform it. Eventually he decides to throw out a comment, if for no other reason than to move things along. “That looks...interesting,” Trevor says, taking another sip of his beer as he watches casually. “Oh, it is,” Seth says, standing up to look over the top of the mechanism he’s been toiling over. “I bet you’re just dying to know what it is, huh?” “Eh,” Trevor shrugs. “I got nothing better to do. Shoot.” “Alrighty,” Seth nods, walking around to the front of his odd looking creation, wiping grease off of his hands with a towel as he does so. “Remember at that party a few nights ago when you said something about Keanu using a time machine to go back in time and bring Genghis Khan to the future...” “Yeah, he did that in Bill and Ted’s Excellent Adventure,” Trevor says, recalling the conversation his partner is referencing. “What about it?” “That’s what I’m doing,” Seth states matter-of-factly, gesturing his arms toward the machine with a flourish. “I’m not following,” Trevor replies between sips of his beer, a confused look appearing on his face. “I’m making a time machine!” Seth shouts, jumping in the air and pointing at his project. “Can’t you tell by my intricate design schematics?” Trevor looks at the contraption, which appears to be more of a Frankenstein’s monster of various other apparatuses than any single recognizable machine. After staring at it for about ten seconds, he nods. “It looks more like a slushie machine on top of a port-o-pottie to me,” he remarks, slapping his hand on top of the Big Gulp rotating slushie cup that is sitting on top of the large box to illustrate his point. “Well technically you’re right,” Seth says, using a wrench to tighten up one of the screws connecting the two pieces. “Technically. But imaginatively, it’s a time machine. In any case, it will serve its purpose.” “Which is?” “To supply us with an endless plethora of possible plotlines by opening up the entirety of history for us to explore,” Seth states, as if he were repeating the Boy Scout Oath, or some other oath or pledge not unlike it. Trevor takes another sip of his beer and nods understandingly. “This is all because you’re worried about losing the current unending stash of plots that is our public access television show, isn’t it?” “No,” Seth responds, shaking his head. He grits his teeth in anger. “Fuck no! There’s no way those Team Annoying bastards are going to beat us.” “But this is just in case...” Trevor begins, trailing off before stating the words that neither man want to hear. “Well it wouldn’t hurt to have two unending supplies of storylines, ya know,” Seth says, trying to validate his efforts without conceding any of the confidence he has concerning his pay-per-view match up. “True, true,” Trevor agrees. “That’s cool and all. I can see only one problem with your whole setup here.” “And what is that?” “Well, the whole time travel thing...it’s kinda IMPOSSIBLE!” Trevor shouts, throwing his empty beer can at the machine. “How stupid do you have to fucking be to think this could actually work? Bill and Ted’s Excellent Adventure was a movie! A MOOO-VIIIIIEEE!!! As in not real-life!” “My aren’t you a violent drunk,” Seth says calmly, before chuckling to himself. “Well, it goes to show just how much you actually know. After devoting hours of effort into it, I believe that this time machine is finally functional. We’ll see just what is possible and what is unpossible.” “Im,” Trevor corrects. “Huh?” “Impossible, not un,” Trevor explains in a lecturing tone. “Whatever, man,” Seth says, shaking his head. “You won’t be acting so smart when I prove your ass wrong in a minute. Just watch and see.” Cracking his knuckles, Seth shoots a confident smile toward his partner before opening the door on the inferior port-o-pottie portion of the mechanism. Strutting inside, he takes a seat on the toilet inside and shuts the door behind him. Some electronic noises are heard emanating from inside, before Seth shouts out excitedly. “This is it, Trev! Witness the wonder of time travel, in all of its GLOOOOOOOOORY!” A count-down begins from a pair of speakers nailed to the side of the outhouse. Trevor taps his foot impatiently, crossing his arms as he waits. Five! Four! Three! Two! One! Zero! ZZZA-ZZZA-ZAP! A lightning bolt shoots down from the sky, striking a metal rod sticking out from the top of the outhouse, and...this causes delicious slushie foodstuff to begin spewing from the spigot of the machine onto Civil D’s back lawn. Other than this nothing happens. Besides Trevor erupting in laughter, that is. “Oh yeah, Seth! I sure am the stupid one. How could I be so foolish as to think time travel was impossible,” Trevor says sarcastically, delighting in his mockery of his partner. “Or maybe it IS unpossible after all.” “It’s neither, damn it!” Seth says, scrambling frantically out of the machine. He runs to the back and begins shuffling through a mass of wires. “It has to be possible! I could have sworn I had all of the wires in the right places. What could have gone wrong?” Trevor continues to laugh to himself, while Seth continues to search. As this goes on, neither of the two men notice Keanu Reeves emerge from the house until he’s almost on top of them. Pushing Seth aside, he begins to tear out wires and tie them into knots before sticking them back in completely different places. He then proceeds to give a good kicking to the base of the thing, muttering various insane phrases as he does so, before leaving as mysteriously as he came. “Does something about Keanu not seem right?” Trevor asks. “Lately...I just don’t know about that guy.” “Does something not seem right?! He ruined my fucking machine!” Seth screams, holding his head, infuriated. “Eh, it’s not like it worked to begin with,” Trevor says, obviously not caring too much. “Say, you wouldn’t mind if I...you know...dropped off my load?” “What?” Seth’s face scrunches up in puzzlement. “Nevermind, just get the fuck out of my way,” Trevor says, pushing Seth to the side as he walks inside of the port-o-pottie and shuts the door. Seconds later grunting sounds can be heard from inside, when suddenly.... ZZZA-ZZZA-ZAP! Another lightning bolt comes down from the skies and the contraption, with Trevor inside of it, is gone with a flash. Seth is left staring in shock, with his mouth open wide enough to land an airliner in. Of course that’s exaggeration, but surprisingly not as much as you would think. He’s on the verge of freaking out when a lightning bolt hits the ground in the same spot and the time machine reappears. Moments later, Trevor stumbles out, confused. “Dude!” Seth shouts. “Did it work?! Did it work?!” “I don’t know,” Trevor mutters. “You tell me.” Trevor turns around to reveal the writing on his shirt. It reads ‘I was at the storming of the Bastille and all I got was this lousy T-shirt’. Seth stares in amazement at Trevor’s shirt. “Holy shit! You were at the storming of the Bastille?!” “No, but I WAS at the first store that let you custom design your own T-shirt. Pretty sweet, huh?” “But...but...” “Yeah, I just thought I’d freak you out by getting this shirt made,” Trevor says, laughing at his still stunned friend. “If I was at the storming of the Bastille, do you think I’d actually stop and get a T-shirt. Hell no, they’d chop my frickin’ head off.” “But it works, right?” Seth asks, toning out Trevor’s running commentary. “Oh yeah, it must have taken me back to the mid-eighties...if not earlier!” Trevor states with a nod. “Eureka!” Seth shouts, pumping his fist in the air. “Keanu Reeves is a genius.” “Yes,” Trevor says, his voice taking a concerned tone. “Brilliant, yet disturbingly insane.” “Is there something wrong with that?” Seth questions. “Nah, I’m cool with it,” Trevor says, his face brightening up again. “Well, at any rate it’s nice to know we have back-up plots waiting for us.” “Hell yeah. I’m way to lazy to think up interesting things to do in such a limited scenario as the present,” Seth agrees. “Now that that’s done, do you want to go take Keanu to another party?” “We do only have one more day with the guy. Maybe we can talk him out of that crazy mood he’s currently in.” “Sounds good to me,” Seth says, throwing a blanket over the top of the time machine. The two members of Civil Disobedience turn around and walk back inside their house. The tortured screams of Keanu Reeves split through the air seconds later, and it is with this sound that we fade out.
