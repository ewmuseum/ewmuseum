Deportation: Part IV

24 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/24/02 Tom Walczak has been in Poland for the past couple of days, and he has yet to learn of any significant information surrounding the case. Since his arrival on Tuesday he has sat by the phone every evening only to learn of minimal advancements in the case. Tonight he once again waits by the phone hoping that tonight he gets his big news. As the hours pass the phone finally rings. Walczak fervently picks it up and presents himself. “Hello who is this?” “Well Mr. Walczak this is the United States Deportation Department, and we have good news. There has been a court date set for your case. It will occur on Friday, August 29. Also you can come back into the country this upcoming Monday. This will give you a chance to get your information together.” “Ok, but can I commute to SLAM! this upcoming Saturday?” “Well, the deal that has been struck with the organization is that if your case is not settled by Friday then you will be able to attend the Companies weekly show as well as the PPV, and you will be able to communicate with your employers, but until that time you have no right to communicate with them, and Monday is when you can first enter the country.” “Are you kidding me? I get paid per week, and if I don’t show up then I don’t get paid. Do you people realize that you are absolutely ruining my life? Do you know how much anguish you have already caused me? I have spent the past week waiting by the phone instead of enjoying myself and visiting with old companions.” “Well, than maybe the next time that you try to illegally enter a country all these things can be avoided if you just think of what may happen.” “That is not the point. The point is that I have all that I need to show you at home, and that all this could have been avoided if you fucking Americans would just pay some attention to people.” “Sir, we all have our opinion, and there is no harm in expressing yours, but you to have to realize that you can’t just disrespect the people who are trying to help you get back into the country.” “Wiesz co? Ja mam dosycz z wami. Do mnie jusz nic nie znaczy, i ja muwie prawdo jak cos takego do mnie jescze raz sie zrobi to bedzie cos do placenia.” “Whatever you say sir. Also there is no more need for you to wait by the phone. You can go out and do whatever you want. Your plane leaves at eight in the morning from the airport in Gdansk.” Walczak does not answer the man all that he does is hang up the phone, and sit there contemplating what had just happened. As Walczak sits there thinking about it the scene fades out…
