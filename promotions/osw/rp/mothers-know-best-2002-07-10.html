Mothers Know Best

10 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 10 2002 A concussion can be one of the crueler injuries you can endure. Nevermind the constant headaches, the intermitten bout of nausea and the general fuzzy-headed feeling that makes you feel as if you've lost touch with the world. On top of all that, doctors usually advise the poor concussed victim avoid sleep for at least one full day. Dave Gibson's doctor had advised just that following the baseball attack by The Cult's new--though still unknown--member. Only now, he couldn't sleep. Son of a bitch. No matter how tired he was, Dave couldn't sleep. When he did close his eyes, he could only focus upon one thing. The final moments of his match at Slam! Dave Gibson, standing alone in the center of the ring... a tap on his shoulder... turning to see the black-cloaked individual swing the bat at his face... the growing sense of horror and--as much as he hated to admit it--fear as the blunt object approached his face. Then there had been a sharp pain... and nothing. Dave never told the doctor, but he really couldn't remember how the match won. Since then, Clay had praised his young partner for the guts he showed getting back to his feet, even as blood poured from his head. Dave didn't remember it. He didn't remember ANYTHING. Except the man with the bat. A ringing phone broke Gibson's train of thought. He realized he'd been running his hand over the large set of sutures in his head, then rolled over, grumbling, and answered the phone. 'Yo,' he said. 'Yo? Is that how you were taught to answer the phone? Why, I bet your mother would hate to hear such a lack of manners.' Gibson smiled despite himself. 'Hey, mom, how are you?' 'Worried about you. See the doctor today?' 'Yeah. He said I should be able to fly tomorrow.' 'I hope he gave some tips for in the ring too. It looked like you could've used them against those two monster movie rejects...' Gibson smiled again. That was his mom: His biggest fan and, at the same time, his worst critic. It was just another reason she was the most important person in his life. 'Yeah, I'm heading to Miami, actually,' Gibson replied. 'Clay and I have to find a way to combat those two, otherwise I may have the shortest-ever tenure in Old School Wrestling.' 'Just try not to get this federation closed down too, dear.' 'Ha. Ha. Ha.' There was a lengthy silence, both mother and son consumed by their own thoughts. Gibson head a brief click on the other end of the line--mom lighting a cigarette, no doubt--followed by an exhale as she took her first drag, then broke the silence. 'Still having trouble sleeping?' 'No, not at all. Slept like a log last night.' Dave's mother laughed, a sharp cackle. 'You never could lie to me, boy. What's wrong?' 'I don't know. Just thinking about a lot of stuff, mainly that asshole who came out and bashed me with the bat Saturday night. I mean, that's not supposed to happen to me. In IOW... in jOlt... I was the one causing the trouble, orchestrating the attacks. I didn't lose once in jOlt, and only lost one singles match in IOW. Then I go out and lose my very first match in Old Sch--' 'Would you like some cheese with that wine, dear?' 'You're spending all this time complaining about all the things that haven't gone right since you came to OSW. Maybe you should focus on what HAS gone right, instead.' 'Yeah...' Gibson said, instantly thinking of his part in the Mount St. Hell's End Match. The shock of the crowd as he bludgeoned Hessian and Vampir with the chair... and the roar that followed. 'OSW signed you for a reason, son. They know how great you can be, just like I've always known. Now it's time for you to realize it too.' 'Yeah...' Gibson found himself running his hand over the sutures again and stopped. 'Yeah, I guess so. Well, I guess I'd better go.' 'OK, have a safe flight tomorrow.' 'I will. Need any money?' 'No, I've got a couple of customers paying me tomorrow.' Dave shook his head. His mother had worked out of the house as a seamstress for as long as he could remember. He'd tried to set mom up in one of those big houses in the suburbs of Charlotte. A gated community. She'd turned him down each time, and Gibson didn't feel like opening that can of worms. 'Fair enough, I'll talk to you later,' Dave said, then suddenly, 'Mom?' 'Yes, David?' Dave paused, squeezing the phone cord with his hand. 'Do you think he was watching?' Dave looked at the phone, confused. Where the hell did that come from? 'Your father?' his mother said after a lengthy silence. 'I have no idea, David. I try not to think about him. Neither should you...' 'I... I know,' Dave said. 'I'm gonna go. Love you.' 'Love you too.' Dave hung up, his mind racing. What the hell had he been thinking? Dave's father--who left shortly after Dave's birth--was not a topic Dave and his mother discussed often. In fact, he hadn't been mentioned for years. He was a deadbeat, a drifter and they were both better off without him. They knew that. But now... why was he thinking of him? Dave laid back down on the bed, closing his eyes. He opened them again with a sigh. Now, something completely different was preventing 'Old School' Dave Gibson from sleeping.
