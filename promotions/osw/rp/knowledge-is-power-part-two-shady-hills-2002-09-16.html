Knowledge is Power, Part Two: Shady Hills

16 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/16/02 (released 9/20/02) We hear the sounds of a car badly in need of a tuneup...or a brand new engine. Loose gravel skips and rolls along an unpaved road as tires rotate atop them, creating a sound much like the tide coming in. Fade in. The scene opens just outside of a battered, 1988 Plymouth Reliant as it slides through the unrelenting gravel surf. Gently rolling hills blacken the dark, night sky, and the moon shines down brightly from behind a thin veil of clouds. Upstate New York, a knowledgable observer would realize -- and in a very empty tract of land. We move up from the spinning tires and flying pebbles to find ourselves looking through a window at the vehicle's driver: OSW Commissioner, Ed. No goofy grin or broad smile marks his face; instead, there is only a determined, thoughtful frown. A shape rises in the distance, like an ebony dagger slashing upward. As we come closer, it's more apparent what this object is: a massive, Victorian-era building, with conicle 'towers' and red brick walls. The concrete blocks, however, are stained with rust -- the emerald roof is missing several tiles; in fact, several large holes gape in invitation to any rain or wild animals who may wish to enter. The place is abandoned, has been for years...but it's also easy to guess what purpose it served. Shady Hills Sanitarium, the cruel asylum that produced Meanstreak. The clamor of bouncing stones slows so that we can hear each one in turn, rather than just a single, constant wave. Headlights that once faced forward begin a slow turn to the right, and we pan back to show the Reliant pulling into what must have once been a parking lot, before it succumbed to the forces of nature. A snake quickly slithers out of a disturbed clump of weeds as Ed's car rolls over them. The brake lights brighten; the car stops; the headlights cut off; and the Plymouth Reliant shudders to a halt. A short pause later, the door creaks open, and out steps the Hardcore Legend. He pulls a small, dark object from his button, and a small 'click' issues forth as he depresses the button. 'April 16, 9:37 PM Eastern time. I've arrived at Shady Hills Sanitarium. It looks to be as abandoned as reported. I'm preparing to go in.' With that, he clicks it off. Keys jangle with an eery loudness as Ed pulls them from his pocket, selects the proper one, and inserts it into the car's trunk. It pops open with a thunk and a hiss, and a small backlight flickers on to reveal the contents. With one hand bracing the trunk door, Ed pulls out a large, bulky backpack and a thick, equally bulky flashlight. The first he slings across his shoulders, with the effort of one not used to wearing them, and the second he flicks on before slamming the trunk shut. Battered Nikes crunch down upon gravel as Ed advances across the ruined parking lot, stepping onto a path of cracked pavement which, in turn, leads to a pair of massive, double doors, both of which have long since been stripped of glass and locks. Click. '9:39 PM. I'm at the front doors. Nothing to report yet, but hopefully I'll find something inside.' Gently, he pushes the doors open -- not so gently, the rusted hinges give way, and the reinforced wood gives way, crashing to the ground with a heart-stopping volume and sending clouds of dust high into the air. Ed coughs, waving the dust away, and stepping into the darkness. Click. '9:40. The lobby has two main hallways, in addition to a a small one behind the check-in desk, which I assume leads to the doctors' private offices. I'm going to check that out first.' His footfalls click ominously on the forgotten tile floor; more clouds rise with each step. The walls are all either cracked and torn, or covered with a coat of simple white plaster; the desk is comparitively new, bending in a half-circle and colored in the dull salmon that was so popular in the 1980's. A simple half-door, barred from within but not out of reach, gives access to the hall beyond, and as Ed turns his light towards it, dust dances and swirls, almost in annoyance at having its play revealed. Click. '9:42. Nothing on the check-in desk, except for a few phone cords someone didn't think were important. Moving on to the office hallway.' The pale tile abruptly gives way to a dirty green carpet, as if the metal barrier that usually divides the two was removed in the moving process. One thing remains the same, however: with each step, a small cloud rises high to irritate Ed's senses. The first door on the right contains nothing, save the shattered remnants of what must have been a one-way mirror that allowed access to the neighboring hall. Aside from broken glass, the room is empty; even the carpet was torn up and taken. The next holds a bit more: a single bookshelt, dryrotted by a leaking ceiling directly above. A quick inspection shows that it holds nothing, save perhaps a few half-starved termites. Click. '9:47. I've searched two of the offices, with no results. There appear to be three more rooms on this hallway.' The following two yield results similiar to the first, and Ed's cautious step turns to one of impatience, as he places his hand upon the doorknob, turns, and pushes it open. Then, he gives a slight, suprised gasp, and immediately pulls out his recorder. '9:52. The last office is occupied. I've found a candle still burning, and an open copy of a book...a Beverly Cleary novel...on the desk. There's also a large bundle of straw and other things in one corner, and they appear to have been recently used. Apparently, someone's still here.' A sudden cough startles the Hardcore Legend, and he spins around, arms raised in a combat stance. 'Find what you're looking for, son?' asks a withered, leather-skinned old man with long, wispy gray hair and a similiarly unkempt robe. 'Need any help?' Ed stares blankly with his mouth agape, then suddenly shakes his head to clear it. 'Who...who are you?' The old man tut-tuts him, as if the information is of no imortance. 'What brings you to Shady Hills Sanitarium? I'm afraid visiting hours are over for the night.' 'Ah...' Ed stammers, after taking a moment to realize that the old man was completely serious. 'I'm sorry if that will inconvenience you, sir, but rules are rules,' he concludes with an apologetic tone. 'Can I get you anything? Coffee? Coke?' 'Erm...I'm fine, thank you.' 'Good, good. I'm Marvin, the caretaker,' the old man suddenly states, extending his hand. 'Just call me 'Marv' for short...that's what is says on my nametag, after all.' Ed glances at the faded, torn scrap of paper pinned to the battered strips of cloth that serve as Marvin's shirt, and finally seems to realize what's going on. 'Marv, how long have you worked here?' 'Why, ever since they converted this old mansion to a nuthouse, back in 1972.' 'You've been here for thirty years?' 'Really? My, time certainly does fly.' Ed blinks slowly, soaking it in. Obviously, Marvin is a bit mad, but he seems a nice enough guy. 'Would you mind answering a few questions for me, Marv?' 'Sure,' the old man agrees with a smile. 'Ask away...ah...' 'Ed.' 'Okay, then...ask away, Ed.' Click. '9:59. I've come across the caretaker for Shady Hills Sanitarium, who goes by the name of Marvin. He seems nice enough.' He turns to the kindly, if mad, old man. 'Marv, do you mind if I record what you say?' 'Not at all, Ed,' he replies, testing out the sound of Ed's name. 'I didn't know they made those things so small.' 'Science marches on,' the Hardcore Legend replies with a grin. 'Okay...Marv, did you know of or have contact with a patient named Ethan Giroux?' 'Giroux, Giroux...' Marvin repeats, rolling the name around. 'No, I don't recall--' 'How about 'Meanstreak'?' The old man's eyes widen in recognition, and an unmistakable twinge of fear. 'Oh...him.' Ed nods, motioning for Marv to continue. He heaves a shuddering, reminiscent sigh. 'Big kid. Had a real bad temper, went off at the slightest provocation.' He pauses, then sighs again. 'Little bastard broke my arm.' Ed winces and shakes his head in sympathy. 'I'm afraid I don't know much about him, Ed. Something went wrong at home...his mother died, in a crash of some kind. I don't know the specifics, but he went off...I saw some nutcases here, but he was one of the worst. Absolutely off the deep end.' 'Who was his doctor?' Ed presses. 'Was anyone good friends with him?' Marv laughs bitterly. 'Friends? That's a good one...although there was another big kid who got along with him pretty well. He had these real big, wide eyes...they called him Crazyeye, I think. I don't know what happened to him.' 'And his doctor?' 'Well, that's a tough one. He had several doctors; a psychiatrist, an observer, a rehabilitator -- social, not physical, and the doc in charge of his surgery.' Ed's eyes widen in sudden suspicion, and a determined look crosses his face. 'What kind of surgery?' Marv thinks carefully for a moment, then shrugs. 'It was kept fairly hush-hush. A really complicated procedure, I heard, and it was supposedly an expirement in helping patients overcome their madness. It was some kind of...ah...' 'A lobotomy.' It's a statement, not a question. 'Yeah, that's right. Anyways, it didn't work. It just pissed the guy up; as soon as he woke up, he started lifting weights...well, when he wasn't talking to himself.' Ed nods. 'Yeah, he still does that. What was the name of the doctor who performed the surgery?' 'Let's see...hmm...oh, that's right. Dr. Karris. Dr. Ted Karris, though he liked being called Theodore.' Again, Ed nods. 'Thanks, Marv. You've been a huge help.' 'No problem,' replies the old man. 'Now, if you'll excuse me, I'd better get to sleep. I've got to get up early and mow the lawn...' Ed stands at the same time as Marvin, shakes his hand firmly, and steps back. 'Take care, Marv. As soon as I'm in the area again, I'll stop by to say hello.' 'I'll look forward to seeing you, Ed. Good luck with whatever you needed that information for.' 'Thanks. But I don't need luck... I need Karris.' Fade out.
