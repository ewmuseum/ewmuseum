'Duty calls. . .'

4 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/4/2002 Wrestling is the least of one’s priorities. Well, at least for Maverick, that is. When one has a profession, that profession should be the top priority of that one, but Maverick had other obligations. The snow falls silently on the street of New York City. Thick steam pours from the gutters and manholes in the street as a black Lotus drives in violently interrupting the soft steam. The shiny black paint job brilliantly reflects the neon lights of the red light district. Chrome stripes light up the sides as neon lights under the care light up the snow. Snow spins up behind the wheels of this slick vehicle. It stops abruptly in front of a ringing pay phone. A tall figure steps out of the monster of a car. A white male with just a shadow for hair on his head wearing a black wool trench coat, black pants, and a pair of black gloves creeping and carefully watching his back cautiously steps over to the phone. He picks the phone off of the dial. “Hello.” “Let’s make this quick, this line might be tapped. The cops have been up my ass all month,” says the voice on the other phone, quickly ending the man’s greeting, in a more integrated Italian-New Yorker accent. “Alright, I know you were expecting a little time off by now, and of course you have more than earned it. But duty calls first. I need you hear. The cops have been thicker than ants lately, and I can’t have one guy gone right now.” “But Don Leone” “No buts kid. You have done your job well up until this point. I don’t want to be disappointed. You do me right and you just might find some more jobs from others. But if you screw me like the little cocksucker the other guys believe you to be, then I will make sure that not another breath from your lungs is breathed. Capesche?” Don Leone threatens. “Capeshe,” the other figure gives in. Obviously, this is a disappointment. “Good kid, Maverick, you are. If you do my work for me, then I will make sure you get taken care of. Now I need you here, so stop by the joint on your way home, I have a small errand for you to run for me. Now get off the phone before the feds catch onto you.” Maverick hangs up the phone and agitatedly gets back into the Lotus. His wheels smoke as he speeds away. Another wrestling gig missed. For what?
