The Story Plods Onward

31 January 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1-31-02 Our scene comes back in, showing Seth Masters paused in mid-sentence with Paul the Jew standing a few feet to the right of him, fixed in front of the ominous looking Christian church. For those of you who missed the last edition of this promo, what happened was that the two men came out from the woods and Seth was in the middle of saying something when we cut off again. I don’t know about you, but I think it was the most thrilling installment to date. None of that bullshit fluff, just got right down to business. Speaking of that fluff jazz, we had better stop filling this promo with it right now and get on with the story. Lights, camera, action! “...ace with the holy water junk, right?” finishes Seth, staring up at the cathedral as the scene unfreezes. “Yep, this is it. Hope you’re ready for some action, bud?” asks Paul, slapping Seth on the back before continuing his trek toward the building. Seth remains looking at the structure, in a state of semi-awe. It is quite an impressive construction; certainly not the type of thing you’d expect to see in the small town of Aurora. Intricate archwork, gothic design, combined with the massive size set it in a class of its own as far as the town’s architecture was concerned. Rising high above any other structure in the city, the imposing structure had been said to have rested on this site long before settlers had decided to build a town in its vicinity. Rumors circulating stated that missionaries had built it back in the seventeen hundreds as a place of worship to bring the Indians under the heel of their foreign religion. Other rumors said that it had been overtaken by Satanic druids in the eighteen hundreds, who used it to convert the now Christian Indians to their dark ways of worship. Still other stories said that the now Satanic Indians were in current talk with the parish to purchase the building and use it for a casino, to be used to convert the town’s white people to the most sinister worship of all: slot machines. Seth, however, had heard none of these stories. In fact, the look of semi-awe on his face was not from pondering the building’s appearance or history at all, but was because he was watching a pretty pretty butterfly flap above his head. Come on, did you really expect him to be appreciative of a church, or of any magnificent work in general? He smiles gleefully as the butterfly circles around, then jumps up to try to catch it. He misses, and as it flies away, his grin fades. Seth turns around to see Paul the Jew is almost at the base of the church, so he runs after to catch up. In a short time he pulls even with him and relaxes his pace to match Paul’s stride. “Man, Paul the Jew...butterflies kick ass!” exclaims Seth with smile. Paul gives Seth an odd look. “What the hell are you talking about? Wait, are you one of those guys that ate glue when you were a kid?” “Why the fuck would I eat glue when all those delicious paint chips were just laying around?” replies Seth with a sarcastic tone in his voice, before turning serious. “No, I didn’t eat glue, dumbshit. I’m just saying butterflies kick ass. Is there anything wrong with that?” Paul shakes his head unbelievingly, looking to the sky in dismay. “Oye, of all the guys in the world that could have offered me a slice of ham, why did it have to be this stupid fuck?” “You sure do cuss a lot for a holy man,” remarks Seth, ignoring the insult. “Nowhere in the Torah does it state anything denouncing profanity,” replies Paul the Jew. After thinking for a minute he adds, “And even if it did, nobody is perfect.” “Well once I get my hands on that holy water, I’ll be pretty damn close.” mutters Seth to himself, grinning at the thought of the superior physical properties he will be granted when he finds the stuff. “Are we going to get going or what? This is going to be our only chance to infiltrate the compound. The clergy is preoccupied with the picnic, so the path will be basically clear. Even so, there’s no way we could enter in the front. We need to find a way in the back. This opportunity isn’t going to last forever, though, so we need to hurry our asses up.” Seth nods in agreement, before turning to face the church again. He stretches his legs out, running in place for a few seconds, before leaping out forward in a dash to the compound. As he puts distance between himself and Paul the Jew, the other man reaches an arm out toward him, shouting out at Seth to stop. “You fool! Stop running! You don’t know what you’re doing!” It’s no use, though, as Seth just keeps up his dash. As he nears the church it looks as if Paul was making a big fuss over nothing. Seth has almost reached the building without anything going wrong, but just as he’s within fifteen yards...BOOM! A large cloud of smoke flies from the ground as Seth is thrown back almost fifty feet to where Paul the Jew is standing. Seth lays in a heap on the ground unmoving, his skin covered in a cloud of soot, while his clothes are now torn around the edges. When he opens his eyes, he sees Paul the Jew staring down at him, shaking his head again. “I suppose I should have told you about the landmines ahead of time,” Paul says, turning to survey the stretch of land between the church and them. “That would have helped.” Seth responds weakly, as we do a scene wipe out. ======================================================== Our setting transforms to a small stream surrounded by trees, next to which we see the second half of Civil Disobedience, Trevor Hawke, walking next to Lisa, the cute but naive Christian girl who thinks he is a great holy man. As the two walk along the winding water, they talk softly, though Trevor seems to be concentrating more on starting at the girls breasts than the conversation. In fact, that’s exactly what he’s doing now. “I feel that the church, although it has been struggling to change to meet the times, isn’t moving fast enough to keep up with the culture. It needs to do so if it’s going to continue to thrive in the twenty first century. That’s what I think at least. Don’t you agree, Trevor?” she asks, though Trevor is a bit too preoccupied to answer. “Trevor? Trevor, are you listening to me?” Looking up from her boobs, surprised, Trevor nods in agreement. “Oh of course I am. It’s just I was pondering your words so thoroughly, as they touched me deeply, that I must of lost myself in the..beauty of the whole situation.” Lisa smiles at him approvingly. “Do you really think what I said was beautiful?” “Almost as beautiful as you,” Trevor replies smoothly, seeing that he’s getting closer to sealing this deal. “Though out of all the things I’ve seen, nothing can quite match your loveliness.” “But you told me earlier that nothing can match the loveliness of God’s glory. You’re surely not comparing me to the Lord, are you?” she asks, confused. “Well...ah...I’m allowed to make multiple uses of hyperbole, aren’t I? Cut me some slack, I’m just trying to be poetic,” responds an agitated Trevor. “I’m sorry. I didn’t mean to make you angry,” says Lisa apologetically. “It’s just that I’m a humble woman. For myself to be categorized with the divine makes me feel uncomfortable. You do have quite a way with words, though. I’ll try not to insult you with any more objections.” “Don’t worry about it, sweetheart...I mean, my child,” Trevor says, slipping an arm around her shoulder. “All sins can be forgiven. Isn’t that what Christianity is all about?” “Well actually...” she begins. “Nevermind, I didn’t mean it in the literal sense. The last thing I need is another lecture.” “I have been speaking at great lengths,” admits Lisa. “Perhaps you’d like to say something about your take on the state of the religion today?” A look of panic crosses over Trevor’s face, as he has been talking out of his ass this whole time not really knowing what he was talking about. “Um...I don’t know...it’s hard to really make any comprehensive statements, but actually...uh...I think it’s a bit preachy lately.” Lisa looks a bit skeptical upon hearing this. “But that’s the point of religion. To preach!” Trevor, trying to change the subject, grabs her by the shoulders and turns her so she’s facing him. A look of shock crosses her face, but it quickly fades back into a smile. He grabs her hands in his, and speaks soothingly. “Yes, I agree, but don’t you think there’s more to it than that?” he asks softly. “Like what do you mean?” she asks back. “Isn’t one of the big rules is to love each other as you love yourself?” questions Trevor slyly. Lisa swallows deeply and nods in reply. “Yes, yes it is. What are you getting at?” Trevor decides to go for it. “I’m saying...I love myself...and you love yourself...so maybe we should love each other?” He moves his head closer and closer to Lisa’s, opening his mouth slightly. She closes her eyes in anticipation. Just as their mouths are about to make contact, a scream is heard in the background. “Oh my God!!! They’re holding hands!” Both Trevor and Lisa’s heads snap to the left, facing the direction where the voice emanated from. It seems as if a crowd has formed, with about twenty people staring at the two in startled anger. Mouths hang open all down the line in surprise at what they have found. Silence hangs thick in the air. You could hear a pin drop, if...you know, anybody carried around pins that could be dropped. Trevor unclenches Lisa’s hands and takes a few steps back, staring at the people in unease. A wrinkled old woman covered in a black dress on the outskirts has a finger pointed accusingly at him. “You! Who are you!? Where are you from!? And why are you defiling that poor young girl!?” the hag screams. Trevor, sensing imminent danger, tries to put up his holy man act once more. “Oh, my name is Trevor uh O’Toole. Yes, I am Trevor O’Toole from St. Joseph’s Cathedral in London. I’m touring the world in my quest of religious enlightenment and...” “There ain’t no St. Joseph’s Cathedral in London.” spits the old woman. “He’s a liar!” Trying to save himself, Trevor tries to plead with them. “Oh, there is too. You must be mistaken in your...” “I ain’t never mistaken bout nothin’, specially cathedrals.” crones the hag. “You’re a liar and from the looks of ye, you’re a heathen too. Let’s get him!” Trevor looks over to Lisa for help, but she just shrugs helplessly in reply. Turning back to the crowd, he can see that they are now following the old woman’s advice and advancing on him. Some of the man are pulling out lighters and trying to put together makeshift torches. Trevor backs off slowly at first and, when they draw nearer, turns tail and runs off through the forest. The mob soon speeds up as well, in hot pursuit of our protagonist. This is the scene we’re left with when we are given another scene wipe and the picture disappears. ======================================================== We’re now treated to a shot of Seth and Paul the Jew, who seem to have finally picked their way through the field of land mines. Well, Paul seems to have, at least. Seth is looking rather worse for the wear than he did when we last left him, if that’s possible. His clothing is charred black, while the rest of him doesn’t look much better. His hat is gone, revealing his hair...or what’s left of it. Most of it seems to have been singed thoroughly. He’s still walking, though...somehow, and the two men are now making their way around the back of the church looking for a way in. They trek around the exterior, not finding anything. A concerned look comes over Paul’s face. A few seconds later an equally concerned look comes over Seth’s face, caused by the original concerned look on Paul’s. Seth starts to panic. “What’s the matter? Please don’t tell me you don’t know the way into this place.” he pleads, gripping his hands together and falling to his knees. Paul’s face scrunches up as he contemplates something. “Well...er...I guess I won’t tell you that, then.” “Noooooooo!” screams Seth. “I got my ass tore up by all those landmines for nothing! What a fucking crock! I don’t believe this shit!” Seth is throwing the proverbial tantrum, as he’s up and kicking the wall in his frustration. Paul watches him go on like this for awhile before putting a hand out to calm him. “Are you done throwing your little hissy fit?” he asks. “Hissy fit? Hissy fit!? What? You drag me out here through a fucking field of explosives only to tell me that we did this for no reason because you can’t find your damn way into a fucking church. Do you expect me to be happy about this, you dumb fuck!” “Settle down,” responds Paul calmly. “I never said a way in didn’t exist. Just because there’s no way in the ground floor doesn’t mean there isn’t one on...say the second floor?” Paul pulls up his shirt to reveal that he has a grappling hook hanging from his belt. Seth laughs, realizing that there still may be a chance for him to get his hands on that damn holy water everyone keeps talking about. Pulling out the device, Paul the Jew whips it around his head for a few rotations before letting it fly to the top of second floor ledge. It catches perfectly as he pulls it back to cinch it in, and he smiles as he offers the rope to Seth. “You first. I’ll cover you,” Paul tells him. “You know, in case there’s need of coverage.” Seth grabs the rope from him warily, yanking on it to test its hold. It seems to be to his liking, as he leaps up onto it, wrapping his legs around it. He pulls himself upward and gets about...one inch higher. Another upward pull gets him a bit farther...two inches. Ten pulls later he’s about three feet above the ground, until he slips and slides back down, that is. His hands scrape against the cord, causing a nasty rope burn. He jerks them away, flailing them in pain. “Ow ow ow ow owwww!” he moans. “I can’t climb that damn rope. It’s impossible.” Paul shakes his head in a show of his disappointment in Seth’s persistence. “Do you want that holy water or not?” Seth sighs before leaping back on the rope again, inching his way up and falling back down. We pan to show a shot of the sun hanging in the sky, a bit to the east. The words ‘Four hours later’ appears on the bottom of the screen, as the sun magically transverses the sky; now almost setting in the west. Panning back to the scene we can see an exhausted Seth Masters finally pulling himself onto the second floor roof. As he makes it over the side he rolls onto his back, breathing in and out trying to catch his wind. Sitting up, he wipes the sweat from his brow with his lacerated hands, wincing slightly as the perspiration stings his cuts. After resting for a short while he examines his surroundings to find...that there’s nothing on the roof. No doors, no windows, no anything. It’s just a flat roof with a rising section of the building with with no entrances about ten feet from the ledge. He screams out in frustration before peering over the edge down at Paul. “There’s no fucking entrance up here!” “What? That doesn’t make any sense.” says Paul, squinting up at him. He brings his gaze down to the ground floor, looking to the right. “Oh wait! There’s an entrance over there. Hmmm, wonder how I missed that earlier. Get your ass down here, boy. We’ve got work to do.” Seth looks as if he’s about to cry as he realizes he’s done all that work for nothing, but manages to hold back his anger. Sliding down the rope, screaming in pain as he does so, he follows after Paul halfheartedly as he makes his way into the building. As the interior comes into view, we see a large room full of seats with a huge pile of crates stacked on top of each other near the back. The boxes are labeled with the words ‘Magical Holy Water’, surprisingly enough. “Hmmm. I wonder what’s in those boxes.” Seth whispers to Paul. Paul ignores him, pushing him out of the way as he approaches the pile of crates. Pulling one off of the top, he yanks it to the ground, prying off the top with his hands. Inside sit about fifty bottles of what appears to be purified water. Paul snatches one up and twists the top off, sniffing the fluid inside before replacing the cap and tossing it to Seth, who catches it with a wince. “Drink that,” orders Paul. Seth raises an eyebrow in question. “Why? I’ve already gotta take a piss. All I need is to be carrying a heavier tank.” “Just drink the water, damn it!” orders Paul again, this time with a hint of anger in his voice. Seth shrugs and opens the top again. Turning the bottle upside down, he proceeds to chug the entire thing. As the bottle empties he tosses it over his shoulder without thinking. “What? Was that supposed to do something to me or some shit, because I don’t feel anythi...” Seth is stopped in mid-sentence by a shimmering aura that surrounds him. The aura intensifies in power until finally it fades away into nothing. It leaves behind Seth, who seems to have all his physical injuries cured. No longer do burns mar his hands and skin. His hair has become unsinged, while his clothes are not torn anymore. He looks exactly like he did at the beginning of this little adventure. Glancing down at himself, he smiles and nods. “That’s fucking awesome! We need to stockpile this shit, because we could make a damn fortune.” “I don’t think so. Only the Christian church may make money off of the Christian church,” a voice shouts from the left. Seth and Paul turn in fear to see that a crowd of priests have come in from behind them, blocking their exit from the way they came in. They are all wielding chains and baseball bats, waving them menacingly at the two would-be thieves. Paul voices the only reasonable thought that could be voiced in such a situation. “RUN!!!” Both Seth and Paul make a break in the opposite direction, in hopes of leaving the church through the front. Racing through the rooms, they look like they may possibly make it out, but as they enter the next room they run into a familiar face. Seth collides with Trevor Hawke, who is running in the opposite direction, and the two men fall sprawled out on the church floor. Seth glances in the direction Trevor came from to see a mob has been chasing him as well. As people close in on them from all sides, Trevor, Seth, and Paul all stand up back to back. It certainly doesn’t look good for them, knowing how vengeful the church can be. Will they make it out alive? Or...well, isn’t that enough of a cliffhanger for you? What more incentive than possible death do you need to watch the next installment? Well, whatever...until then, this is... TO BE CONTINUED
