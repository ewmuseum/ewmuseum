The Truth Hurts

18 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2002-07-18 Lillith bolted upright in bed with a small scream, as the nightmare she was experiencing already began to fade away. The third one that night. She tried to calm her breathing as her eyes darted around the dark room, searching for an answer, any answer. But the darkness told no tales. The only sounds were from the streets outside her window. Pulling the thin sheet aside, Lillith got out of bed and stretched to try to loosen her cramped muscles. She felt like she'd fought off 10 men in a championship judo match, which only made the nightmare more ominous. What would make her fight so much? She left her bedroom and walked down the lightless halway towards the kitchen, suddenly needing a drink. She opened the fridge and pulled out a bottled water, then closed the door. As the light went out in the fridge, the room suddenly began to spin. She turned suddenly to grab the counter, when her surroundings suddenly shifted for an instant. For the briefest moment, she was not in her apartment, she was in... something else? Her knees gave way and she crashed to the floor, the bottle of water dropping to floor and rolling across the room. Her body was already trembling with an unknown fear, and a small cry escaped her lips. She looked around to reassure herself that she was indeed in her kitchen, and as her eyes fell upon the open hall closet, her view flashed again, and she literally screamed. For a moment, she wasn't in her apartment. She was in a coffin. The nightmare suddenly flooded back into her mind with full force, the mental impact momentarily paralyzing her. She was buried alive in a coffin, and even though it was pitch black, she could see the white silk covering the inside of the lid. Her arms and legs were sore because she was beatin on that lid, tearing down the soft material, screaming for help in a voice that no one would hear. Her eyes stared straight ahead in shock as the scene replaced in her mind, the hours of fighting until her body felt like lead. Screaming until her voice was hoarse. And finally - giving up. Unable to save herself, her will to live exhausted by the stuggle. Accepting death. And that was when she woke up. Lillith's arms began to twitch, slowly reaching down to the floor to push herself up. Once on her feet, she took stock of herself. She began a breathing exercise she leared years ago from her first sensei. As her heart slowed down, she opened her eyes once again, and looked around. The room was quiet and dark, with no signs of any images resurfacing. She took a step towards the bedroom, and her eyes fell on the closet again. And the trembling began. She was swallowed up by it, taken into it, and the door closed behind her, and she was trapped, again! Just like in the coffin, unable to get out, unable to move, unable to breath! With a sudden lunge, she threw herself across the kitchen into the hall, grabbed the door to the closet, and slammed it shut, so she wouldn't see inside it. The quickly backed away from it into the living room, tripped over an endtable, and landed on the couch, her eyes still riveted to the closet. She didn't want to see in there. She didn't want to go in there. She was afraid. Suddenly, tears began to fall from her eyes, blurring her vision. She began to sob, and pulled her knees up to her chest, and cried, knowing now what the problem was. She was sick, and it wasn't something a pill could cure. It wasn't coffins she feared. It was the lack of freedom. And as she told herself what was really wrong, she burst into full-fledged crying, because there was nothing she could do. Lillith... was clausterphobic.
