Homesick

27 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/27/02 Ring……..Ring……. Jim Axtell paces nervously in his hotel room as he presses his cell phone tightly to his ear. Looking like he is just getting ready for bed, Jim wears a pair of plaid boxer shorts and no shirt. “C’mon….answer the phone!” begs Jim. Ring……Ri- “Hello?” answers a sweet sounding voice on the other end of the line. It’s a voice that Jim loves to hear, and his face lights up with joy. “Hey babe,” coos Jim lovingly. “It’s me.” “Hi Jimmy!” replies Jim’s wife, Kami. Her voice fills with happiness. “How are you, sweetie?” Jim smiles broadly as he paces around his rather plain hotel room. The room is a single bed room, with a color scheme that seems to have come from the sixties. There is a rainbow of reds, blues, greens, and yellows splattered all over the room, almost making Jim dizzy as he wanders around the room. “I’m good….still have a few aches from the match with Kanaida, but I’ll be fine. How are things back home?” asks Jim as he walks over to the sliding glass door leading to the patio. It’s nearly pitch black out, save for a few street lights and neon signs along the road in front of the roadside hotel. “Things are good….I miss you,” says Kami with longingly. Jim presses his head against the cold glass of the sliding door, sighing as he speaks. “I miss you, too,” says Jim quietly. “This is the hardest thing I have ever done, Kam, but it’s something I have to do. I hate being away from you and Kima, you know that.” Almost on cue Kima, Jim and Kami’s German Shepherd, yelps aloud. “Heh,” Jim chuckles as he tries to fight back tears. “Give Kima a treat for me.” “I will, Jim,” Kami promises as she chokes back tears as well. “I saw what happened this week on SLAM! with that Kory Storm guy. What’s his problem?” Jim walks away from the sliding glass door and moves over to the bed, plopping down next to the night stand. “Hell, I don’t know. I guess he’s just one of those guys that needs all of the attention on them all the time. He didn’t like the fact that I was getting interviewed and he wasn’t. Jealous I guess,” Jim concludes. “Well, those are the kinds of people you need to be careful of,” Kami warns. “It’s people like that who don’t give a care about anyone else but themselves. You have a family to think about, Jim.” “Don’t worry, hon,” Jim says soothingly. “I would never do anything to jeopardize what I have worked so hard to get. You, the house, Kima…..it’s the most important thing in the world to me. You know that!” “I….I know,” concedes Kami with trepidation in her voice. “I just don’t want you to get hurt. You’re so far away from home and I miss you so much…it just amplifies my fears.” Jim repositions himself on the bed, kicking up his feet and leaning back against the headboard. He reaches over and picks up a sheet of paper from the night stand. As he looks it over, a smile finally returns to his face. “We need to talk about happier stuff, babe,” says Jim. “Okay,” Kami agrees, the faint sound of sniffling being heard through a hand covered receiver. “I got booked in a match this week!” exclaims Jim as enthusiastically as he can. “An actual match, on the card even!” “That’s great, Pooh!” musters Kami. Pooh is her nickname for Jim, as in Winnie the Pooh….a big, lovable, cuddly teddy bear. Jim begins to fidget as he gets a little excited at the prospects of having a legitimate match on SLAM! “Yeah, it’s a tag team match….and I’m tagging with “Classic” Clay Vendetta,” continues Jim, the excitement building in his voice. “We’re booked against Kory Storm that new Truxell fellow.” A short pause on the other end of the line. “Hon, you there?” wonders Jim. “Yeah, babe,” responds Kami. “I’m here. I’m just not used to all this yet. Those two guys have been around for a long time in that business.” “So has Clay,” Jim interrupts. “But you haven’t!” Kami says, the fear quickly returning to her voice. “You don’t even have one real match under your belt yet! The one match you did have you got really beat up bad, and I don’t want that to happen again.” The gravity of her words smack Jim in the face. “I know you are scared, honey, and I kind of am too,” Jim says trying to comfort his wife. “It’s new and different and unknown, but it will be okay. I may not have had any real matches yet, but I can hold my own with anyone….you know that. I thing the fact that I haven’t wrestled before is an advantage in itself, because no one knows what to expect from me. They don’t know what I am capable of in that ring. This Saturday night I will show them all that I am ready for Old School.” Jim’s confidence serves to quell the fear his wife fears….for now. “Okay, Pooh,” Kami says with a bit of a yawn. “I have to get up early in the morning tomorrow to set some things up at work.” “Okay, I’ll let you go and get some sleep. I love you.” “I love you too, Jimmy.”
