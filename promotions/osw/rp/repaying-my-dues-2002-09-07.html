Repaying My Dues

7 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 7th, 2002 “Bring it back!” I turned around to see the little girl take off with the boy’s lunch pail and gallop into the jungle gym with it. The boy’s face told the entire story – he was pissed. He grabbed a large rock from the ground and proceeded to chase after her, that is, until I stopped him. I caught the rock in my hand and held it behind my back as he leapt at me to retrieve it. “Give it back!” He hollered, “Give it back!” He hollered, trying desperately to reach around me. “What do you think you’re going to do with this?” I asked with a cynical look on my face. “Hit her with it!” He replied angrily. “She took my favorite lunch pail!” I glanced back over at the jungle gym, watching the little girl hiding underneath the slide, still clutching the pail to her side. I grinned sheepishly. Weren’t the boys supposed to harass the girls? Kneeling in front of the boy, I shook my head. “Son, what’s your name?” “My name isn’t son.” “I know that, I meant…” “Then why did you say it?” “What I meant was…” “You should say what you mean. That’s what my dad always says.” I resisted the urge to throttle the boy and bit my lower lip. “What I mean is what’s your REAL name?” “Jeremy,” He stated simply. Smart ass kid. “Jeremy, we don’t chase people around with rocks. Especially pointy rocks like this one. It could poke someone’s eye out.” He pouted for a moment. “But she took my favorite…” “Your favorite lunch pail, I remember the story, Jeremy.” I sighed. “But we don’t go around trying to hit people. When someone takes something that is yours, we tell the teacher, okay?” “Okay…” He responded flatly. He walked over to the teacher on duty in the yard and proceeded to give a detailed report while I made my way over to the jungle gym. She sat there in the sand, still underneath the slide, staring at her feet. She had shoulder length blonde hair, although they were tied up in pig tails, and she wore a white skirt with brown bears on it. Her eyes were innocent enough, although they didn’t have that fun loving look in them that belonged to a child. She had seen too much. “Hey there,” I said softly, sitting beside her. As I did, she scooted away from me nervously. “What’s wrong?” “I’m not supposed to talk to strangers,” She replied quietly, still staring at her feet. “That’s true,” I said, nodding my head. “But I’m not a stranger. I work with your teacher, Mrs. Burle, and I’ve been here for a week now helping your class.” She was still quiet, but was slowly being convinced that I was not a stranger. “So what are you doing?” “Nothing.” I cocked my head to the side and gave her a squiggly eyebrow. “Nothing, huh? That’s not what I saw. I saw you take Jeremy’s lunch pail.” I looked over at it, which she clutched at her side. “That’s not yours, you know?” Her head hung low. “I know…” “And you know you’re not supposed to take things that aren’t yours…” “I know…” “So why did you take it?” She stood up and stamped her foot. “Because Jeremy’s a poop head! He always makes fun of me and never plays with me.” “Well, I’ll talk to him about that…” Her eyes drifted back to the jungle gym and back to me. She handed me the lunch pail and began to sob. I sat next to her and put my arm around her, trying to comfort her as much as I could. “There now, don’t cry. It’s okay.” As I touched her shoulder, she winced in pain. “What’s wrong?” I asked. She shook her head and got up quickly to run away. As she did, I noticed a bruise on her shoulder where I had touched her. A bruise that couldn’t have come from a regular fall or bump that kids take. A much larger bruise. Bruises that I find on myself after I’ve gotten out of the ring.
