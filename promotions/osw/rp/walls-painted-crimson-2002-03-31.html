Walls Painted Crimson

31 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Mar. 31, 2002 A Mother's Love... ... A son's trust... ... Turned to ashes... ... And dust to dust... ... 'GOD D*MNIT! I HATE YOU! DO YOU HEAR ME YOU F*CKING B*TCH! I HATE YOU! I HATE YOU!' 'Johnny, son...what are you doing!?! 'Go to hell!' 'John...please...this is your mom you're talking to! You're scaring me, honey...plea-' 'SHUT UP! SHUT-THE F*CK-UP!' 'NO! PLEASE DON'T! PLEASE!' ...*Click, click*... 'NO! JOHNNY, DON'T!' 'DIE B*TCH!' *BANG!* *** 'Ladies and gentlemen if you're just tuning in, I'm Bethany Witherrowd. I'm standing a mere five feet away from the scene of an absolutely brutal crime scene, in Decatur, that happened just a mere five hours ago, early morning at approximately 4:00 AM. Where investigators say that a teenage delinquent, Jonathan Howard, shot his mother five times- three bullets to the chest, and two to her gut. It truly is a shame, and I hope you hold the rest of the Howard Family in your hearts. Back to the studio.' If you were in Atlanta, and happened to watch the local news, you would've heard such a story. As Rhett Locke took a drive through the city on the way to the next arena, he himself heard the story on the radio. A shame...but oddly intriguing, correct? Perhaps it wasn't what happened that was intriguing...but the background story. Why did the child hate his mother so much? Did she abuse him? Did she not understand him? Was he a prick that just couldn't stand not getting what he wanted? Or was he insane? You could ask so many questions... Murder on Easter Sunday...What a way to belittle such a historic and religious holiday. But alas, there seemed to be no way of finding out...and our Anti-Paladin drove without a second thought of it crossing through his mind. Locke never really could focus on tragedy that didn't involve anyone he cared for...the few that he did care for... But something made that child explode. Something lit the fuse in that boy that set him ablaze... Though, as Rhett drove into the area of Decatur...the thought crossed his mind once more as he saw yellow tape around a small tenement block, Police cars parked up and down the street, Curious souls that looked on, a body bag that covered a hardworking mother's now dead body, and a wall painted crimson. Rhett rolled down the window, and he could almost smell the fresh death in the air as he inhaled deeply. But as he exhaled... he could taste it. ~
