A Mistake

1 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/01/02 Have you ever been in a taxicab? They’re not the most pleasant place to be. They smell of a million bodies that’ve been in and out. The floors are sticky, the seats torn. It disgusts most people. In this particular taxicab is SLR, the man who’s been seen recently as a part of the debut roster of Old School Wrestling, the newest efed around that’s sending shockwaves through the community. Everything from it’s controversial new owner to it’s bare bones roster has the efed world buzzing. Amongst that buzz is SLR, the 33-year-old veteran of the game who looks to come to OSW and re-invent himself. To OSW and their fans, SLR is a mystery. He’s small, tattooed, and smokes constantly. But as each day passes, he’s turning heads. There he sits, in the back of a taxicab, smoking a cigarette, something that most cabs won’t allow but this driver seems oblivious. SLR wears what we saw him wearing last in the promo entitled ‘Breakfast,’ that outfit consists of a black leather jacket, a black t-shirt that reads ‘Ringmaster,’ blue jeans and black boots. SLR gazes out the window, no doubt contemplating where his life has taken him in the recent months, rising from obscurity to become a cornerstone in what could be an efed revolution. The cab pulls to a halt. “Here ya are man,” the cab driver turns around to look at SLR, “That’ll be $25.67.” SLR reaches in his pocket and pulls out a few bills that he hands to the driver. “Keep the change.” SLR steps out of the limo and looks up at the building in front of him. The sign reads Hardcore Henry’s House of Hardcore Supplies. SLR smiles as he stomps out his cigarette and lights another before walking into the store. He shoves open the door and a bell above it rings. SLR slowly walks through the aisles, aisles lined with every conceivable piece of plunder used in hardcore wrestling. Tables, ladders, chairs, barbed wire, thumbtacks, florescent light bulbs, kendo sticks, you name it, it’s here. SLR can’t help but snicker as he strolls to the back of the store. Suddenly a look of concern comes over his face, “Henry?” We now see what concerned SLR, an old man sits behind the counter, presumably Hardcore Henry, the proprietor of the shop. He sits, stroking his chin, his showing great sadness. He looks over at SLR and smiles a forced smile and when he speaks, he seems on the verge of tears, “SLR my boy, how have you been?” “What’s the matter Henry? Is everything ok?” asks a growingly concerned SLR. “Actually SLR, just when you think everything is ok, you realize that very few things really are.” SLR looks overwhelmingly disturbed, “Henry, please, tell me what’s the matter.” Henry looks at SLR with a crooked smile and motions to the stool next to him, which SLR sits on. “SLR, do you know why I started with the hardcore wrestling?” SLR shakes his head as he lights another cigarette. “Back when I started wrestling, there were very few black men like me and we were never allowed to rise to the top of the card. So, the only way we could keep our spot on the card was by becoming a novelty. We were given degrading gimmicks playing off our ethnicity and some of us, like me, were forced to wow the crowds with the hardcore stuff. We weren’t good enough for their main events but we were good enough to get ourselves thrown onto barbed wire and thumbtacks. At the time, I felt I was lucky because I was being obviously exploited but as I look back at it now, it was just as bad.” A long silence. SLR stares at the floor, not sure to make of what his longtime friend is saying. He’d called a few days ago to tell Henry he’d be in town and the two had arranged to meet and have coffee, after all they hadn’t seen each other in a few months since SLR wasn’t an active competitor but this wasn’t what SLR was expecting. He expected to be trading jokes with his friend and mentor, not sitting, watching the man bare his soul. Henry continues, “The sport has changed, or so I thought. We’ve seen champions of every color now. It seemed as if we’d moved past the bad times, not just in our sport but in society in general, it seemed as if we’d grown as a people but every now and then, a glaring reminder is given to us. We’re reminded of the past, of the ignorance, of the discrimination. I used to get angry but now, it simply saddens me.” A single tear runs down the old, scarred face of Hardcore Henry. “Henry, what are you talking about?” asks SLR, obviously having no clue what troubles his friend. “I uh, just caught a promo by your opponent, Toomes. It’s a real piece of work, you should check it out,” Henry says dryly. “Hey, I know we said we’d get together but I really don’t feel like it today. Can you lock the door and turn the open sign off when you leave?” Henry speaks as he heads to the back, leaving SLR shaking his head. SLR heads back through the store, shuts the sign off, locks the door and closes it behind him. SLR then stands on the sidewalk as people, of all race, sex, religion, and sexual orientation walk by. SLR stands there, concerned for his friend and confused at his state of mind. He reaches into his breast pocket and pulls out a cell phone. He dials a few numbers before lifting the phone to his ear. “Yah, can you give me the number for Jay Bolger…oh, it’s Ray? You don’t say. Ok, thanks.” SLR dials another number and waits for an answer. “Yah, Bolger? This is SLR. Can you do me a favor? I understand that Toomes has put out another promo. Can you drop a tape of it off at the hotel? Thanks a lot pal.” SLR hang ups the phone and hails a cab. The camera fades out and fades back in SLR’s hotel room. In his hands he holds a videotape. His jaw is clenched, his eyes distant, his nostrils exhaling smoke. He looks straight into the camera. “Toomes, I just watched your promo, the one where you go to a t.v. station and cause a raucous. I’m not sure if those were actors or real people. I’m not sure how much of that was staged for ‘entertainment’ purposes. I’m not sure if you meant to offend anyone or if you were just trying to be funny, but understand this, that promo, that was a mistake.” SLR’s eyes pierce the screen as he inhales two lungfulls of smoke. “I don’t care if you have got personal heat with Drew Hostyle, you’ve made a mistake.” SLR lowers his head. “Instead of using this week to concentrate on me, you’ve let your focus go elsewhere. That’s a mistake.” “I know I said before that I wouldn’t make any predictions but the truth is, I knew from day one I’d beat you, that wasn’t a question in my mind but before, it was just about me establishing myself in this neck of the woods. It wasn’t you I was beating, it was simply a body. But this last promo of yours, you fucked up pal. You made our match go from a business affair to a personal one and that was a mistake.” SLR’s head remains lowered as he drags on his cigarette. “Before, I was beating you for me, now, I’m beating you for other reasons. Now I’m beating you for Henry. I’m beating you for the world. I’m beating you so maybe I can beat the goddamned ignorance right out of you.” SLR raises his head, his eyes mean now, sadistic, unrelenting. “Racism is a cancer that ate away at our society for a long time and even though it still exists in our society, it disgusts most people. You disgust me.” Fade to smoke.
