Through the eyes of a child...

15 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 15, 2002 'That's spelled wrong.' Jesus St Crux looks up at the boy, maybe ten years old. '¿Qué?' The boy points at the diagram on the papers in St Crux's lap. 'The name... the mountain is called Mount St. Helen's. I did a project on it once. I even used vinegar and baking soda to make it erupt.' Jesus smiles at the kid, a sort of wry smile that indicates he knows the boy didn't understand what he was looking at. 'No, mi amigo, it's spelled right. This isn't that volcano. But in about eight days, I get to climb this.' The boy cocks his head to look at the pictures of sheet metals forged into a crude mountain. 'It doesn't look like a very big mountain.' As Jesus continues to smile, again the sort of smile wherein he knows the boy doesn't understand the seriousness of the mountain. 'It may not be your Mount St Helen's, but I have to play King of the Mountain with a lot of other guys... most bigger than me.' 'Davey,' calls another woman, who seems a bit frazzled, as if she's had a long trip with rowdy kids. 'Don't bother the man. He's very busy.' Jesus grins softly towards her. 'It's ok. He's not bothering me.' Davey looks at his mom, and then turns back to the Mexican wrestler. 'Why are you going to play King of the Mountain?' 'I'm a wrestler with Old School Wrestling. We have a big match coming up and the winner gets a shot at the Championship.' Davey starts getting really excited. 'You're a wrestler! Can you throw me? Can I throw you? Mom! He's a wrestler!' Davey grabs Jesus' arm and seems to start trying to give the crusierweight a hip toss; the attempt is futile, but this narrator has no interest in explaining the mass ratio of the two figures. Jesus starts chuckling as the woman pries her son loose. 'Davey... this isn't the place.' When the boy looks like he'll start arguing, she quickly segues over. 'But, if you ask the man nicely, maybe he'll give you an autograph.' Davey pouts for a second. 'Ok... but Susie doesn't get one!' The woman looks to the stoller with the not-even-a-year-old girl, and slowly smiles. 'I think Susie can live with that.' Leaning back in his seat, Jesus just continues to smile at the antics of the young boy. The boy turns to him, and half sheepishly, yet half enthusiasically, asks, 'Could... could I get your autograph... maybe... please?' 'Sí, mi amigo pequeño. Uno momento.' The boy, apparently not fully understanding Spanish, stands dumbfounded for a few seconds as Jesus fishes through the papers in the folders beside him. Eventually, Jesus draws out a promo photo of him and Caridad. He writes a short message on the photo, and then hands it to the boy, who immediately reads it. Seconds later, he looks up excited. 'Jesus St Crux? My friend Jimmy talks about you all the time. Wow! This is so cool! He's going to flip!' A horn outside the airport waiting area interrupts Davey's excited utterances. Davey's mom looks outside, then starts gathering her stuff. 'Ok, Davey, Gramma and Grampa are here to pick us up. Thank Mr. St Crux for his autograph.' Davey slings his backpack over his shoulder and turns to Jesus one last time. 'Thank you, Jesus!' Davey's mom reaches for his hand, and leads him out of the waiting area as she pushes Susie's stroller. As the boy and his family leave, Jesus leans back in his seat, letting the papers slide to the wayside. That's what I have to get back to, he thinks. The sheer excitement and enthusiasm of his when he started. Lose the cynicism and jadedness from betrayal after betrayal... and just have fun. Caridad comes back from the convenience stand off to the side, drinking some bottled water. 'Everything figured out, Guapo?' Jesus smiles as he looks up at his grilfriend. 'Sí... I think so.'
