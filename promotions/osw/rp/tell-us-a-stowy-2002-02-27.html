Tell Us A Stowy!

27 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-27-02 The warm sun beats down on the sidewalk. A bluebird whistles on a tree branch. Small children play in the street, dodging oncoming traffic...most of the time, at least. What do these things signify? Well, I could say that they mean it’s a great day in the suburbs. I could say a lot of things, for what it’s worth. Like perhaps there are NEVER any great days in these suburbs. Perhaps the dark mysteries contained within them make even the most hardcore ghetto seem like Wonderland by comparison. Perhaps the pleasant exterior is just a mask; a facade to cloak the diabolical underbelly of this so-called middle class community. Perhaps the horrors that take place beneath these roofs are like excerpts from a Lovecraft tale, the macabre nature of even the most mundane act making even a Hessian promo look like a ray of sunshine. Or perhaps not. Perhaps Seth Masters and Trevor Hawke could only wish that such exciting things were happening underneath their noses. Perhaps I should stop the excessive usage of the word perhaps. Perhaps I will do that. Whatever the case, as things currently stand, it does in fact seem to be a great day in the suburbs, as I could have stated at the beginning, but instead chose to draw out incessantly. Spring-like weather permeates the air, bringing out the neighborhood’s residents for the first time since the harsh winter reared its ugly head. These residents include one Trevor Hawke, who as we all know by now is one half of Old School Wrestling’s zany tag team, Civil Disobedience. Garbed in his usual jeans and t-shirt, Trevor is presently walking down the street, enjoying the fresh air. Actually, he’s more enjoying that fact that he currently has a reprieve from hearing Seth’s inane rambling than the outside conditions. If he would have had to listen to the man talk for another minute about the disco disappointment of last Saturday night, he might very well go insane. Well...he probably wouldn’t have gone insane, but he would have been very, very irritated, that’s for damn sure. It is a rather enjoyable outing for Trevor. Seeing an old friend from high school on his walk, he has a nice conversation with him before the two part ways. Stopping at the park, Trevor manages to get into a game of pick-up basketball with some people he knew from the gym. The details of these things are all very fun and joyous, but are nothing that will add to this wrestling promo. All that is necessary is to know that they happened, and Trevor has been out for a considerable amount of time. Walking back toward his house, he’s in a pretty good mood, and why shouldn’t he be. He hasn’t been away from Seth for this long of a time period in...well, ever since he started sleeping on the man’s couch. He had forgotten how nice it was not to hear that voice ringing in his ear. Of course, all good things must come to an end, and Trevor’s sabbatical is no exception to that rule. He turns the corner, still almost two blocks away from the house...two more Seth free blocks, he thinks...when his hopes are shattered as he sees the very man he’s been so glad to be free of standing mere feet in front of him. Not only that, but he seems to have surrounded himself with about six or seven neighborhood kids. They look to be in about second grade by their height, for they only come up to about Seth’s navel area. Currently the buffoon himself seems to be wrapping up a story he was telling them. “...And then Trevor admitted that he was the biggest idiot ever. That’s a true story!” Seth finishes, placing his right hand over his heart and raising his left in a show of honesty...even though, if you couldn’t tell already, it is one of the most falsified tales that has ever escaped a man’s mouth. The children don’t know that, however, and they immediately begin talking amongst themselves in wonder. It wasn’t every day they got to talk to a professional wrestler, after all, as Seth usually stays at home and watches porn all day. Trevor, sighing, figures he had better do something to right this terrible wrong that Seth seems to be committing...that wrong being the defamation of his character. He walks up, slipping through the crowd of kids, and delivers a mighty smack to the back of his partner’s head, effectively knocking off his baseball cap. Seth has a look of shock on his face at first, but when he turns to see that it was Trevor who delivered the blow, the sly expression returns. Picking up his hat and placing it backwards on his head, he regards Trevor with a laugh. The two take a few steps away from the crowd of young uns, and Trevor regards his partner with a furious gaze. Seth laughs again in response. “What’s the matter, Trev? Don’t you remember that night?” he chuckles wickedly. “I believe it was the same night you were sodomized by Pauly Shore, wasn’t it? Heh heh.” “Are you still high from that Global Title Tourney promo?” Trevor asks in a condescending tone. Seth seems to think he’s joking around though, and responds in like. “No, that wore off hours ago...buh-uh-ddy!” he snickers. “That was the worst Pauly Shore impersonation ever, Seth.” Trevor says, shaking his head sadly. “You know I’m just messing around. Pauly Shore would never sodomize you. His standards are too high. Like he’d stoop down to your level. Pfffff.” Seth breaks into laughter again, while Trevor regards him with scorn. “Are you done yet?” Trevor asks his partner, crossing his arms impatiently. “With what? Talking about washed up Mr. Shore?” Seth says, still smiling with amusement. “No, with acting like an idiot,” Trevor answers tersely. “And filling these little kids minds with your senseless drivel. You know the only reason you talk to them is because whenever you start on one of your idiotic tangents with me, I just ignore it. It’s sad, really.” “Oh, shut up. You’re just jealous that I’m a nice enough guy to donate my time make these kids’ days worthwhile. If it wasn’t for me they’d be off selling drugs somewhere. I’m a pillar of this community, dammit!” Seth says, dismissing his partner’s comments with a wave. “Now if you’ll excuse me, I was just about to tell another story.” “It doesn’t involve me, does it?” Trevor sighs. “No, no...well yes, but I swear that I won’t make any sodomization references involving you.” Seth says, while Trevor regards him skeptically. “Just like you swore to those kids that that last story you told was the truth?” “So what, I swear a lot. You know that. Fuck, damn, bitch,” Seth laughs at what passes for his wit, stopping when he sees that Trevor is not amused. “Seriously though, I swear for real this time.” “I think I’ll just stand off to the side to make sure you keep that promise,” Trevor says, his glare not lightening up a bit. “Do you what you want. It is a free country after all,” Seth states with a shrug, as he walks back to the circle of kids. As he walks to the center, one of the children in the circle waddles up to him and tugs on his pant leg, looking up at him hopefully. “Mista Seth, are yousa gonna tells us anotha stowy?” the child asks, looking and sounding just so gosh-darn adorable. “Sure I will, Little Tyrell. Sure I will.” Seth says, patting the kid on his head. “Now gather round children, for I have a tale that will put all other tales to shame.” “Is it about the time you fucked Tyrell’s mom?” pipes up a blond haired kid on the right. “What!?” the child known as Tyrell shouts. His mouth drops open in stunned disbelief. “No, no, no. While I would love to tell that story, it’s unsuitable...” Seth starts, but a glare from Trevor makes him change his tune. “I mean, no, I never fucked Tyrell’s mom.” “And why not, Seth?” Trevor asks from off to the side, looking smug. “She shot me down,” Seth says, looking down in shame. The chorus of laughter from the group of kids makes his head hang even lower. “Ha ha ha ha ha ha ha ha ha ha ha ha ha ha!” “Wow, Mr. Seth! Tyrell’s mom is really fat and she wouldn’t have sex with you? You’re a loser!” states the same blond kid who brought the topic up in the first place. “No I’m not!” shouts Seth, putting his hands over his ears. It doesn’t shut out the laughter though, as the kids continue to mock him. Glancing at Trevor, who has an entertained look on his face, Seth begins to get red in the face. “Shut the hell up, you little brats! Do you want to hear the fucking story or not?!” The children shut up immediately, while Seth glares at each and every one of them. Excluding Seth’s angry breathing, absolute silence fills the air...besides Trevor’s chuckling off to the side, that is. After Seth feels that he’s glared at them for a sufficient amount of time, he starts speaking once more. “Ok then. Since you little fucks think you’re so fucking funny, you don’t get the spectacular story I had saved up for you. Instead you’ll just be stuck with the story of what me and Trevor did on Monday night,” Seth says, before stopping to glare at all of the kids again. “Awww...” they moan simultaneously, because, like you, they are obviously disappointed about having to hear more about the lameness of Civil Disobedience. “Didn’t you just tell us that story?” complains a fat kid to Seth’s left. “No, that was the story of the Monday before last Monday. Now keep your mouth shut, Fatty, and listen to me talk,” Seth snaps at him. “I don’t appreciate being called Fatty, Mr. Seth. You oughta start showing us respect, seeing that the only reason you talk to us is because you know we won’t ignore you like Mr. Trevor over there does whenever you start on one of your idiotic tangents with him, and...” he pauses as Seth stares at him angrily. “I’ll shut up now.” “Damn straight!” barks Seth, before returning his attention to the rest of the crowd. “Now as I was saying, on Monday night me and Trevor over there were rather bored sitting at home. You know, because there’s absolutely nothing to do in these stupid suburbs.” The kids all nod their agreement before Seth continues. “Somehow, during our excessive boredom, the topic of our wrestling match next Saturday night on Slam came up. After some discussion we thought it wouldn’t hurt to go talk some strategy with our tag team partner for that very match. So off we went to the ABC Motel to pay a visit to none other than Meanstreak.” At the mention of the Monster, the kids are abuzz with amazement. They recount all of the crazy things they’ve seen Meanstreak do over the years, to the point where they’re no longer paying any attention to Seth at all. He has to clear his voice to bring their attention back to him. “*Ahem* So yeah, we went to Meanstreak’s motel room, and...” Seth doesn’t finish his sentence, for as soon as he says Meanstreak’s name again the kids begin to chatter wildly. I tell ya, that Meanstreak character sure can get a pop out of any crowd, even one as small as Seth’s. He’s beginning to get irritated now, as he feels that he should be the center of attention, considering that he is actually there in the flesh and all. “Quit talking about Meanstreak. We’re in the story too, you know.” he says, glaring at the kids once more. They don’t stop talking however, and he’s forced to talk over them. “SO ANYWAY, WE WENT TO HIS MOTEL ROOM AND HAD A GRAND OLD TIME. WE ORDERED ROOM SERVICE, AND WATCHED PAY-PER-VIEW, AND SWAM IN THE SWIMMING POOL, AND...” Seth realizes that the kids have lost complete interest in him. They’re completely wrapped up in their own private conversation about Meanstreak, marking out big time as each one tells a story in turn about what the man has done. Obviously their stories are much more entertaining than the ones Seth was telling them earlier, for as far as they’re concerned, he doesn’t even exist. Letting out a sigh, he walks over to where Trevor is standing, his head lowered disappointedly. “They don’t care about us, Trev. All they’re doing is talking about Meanstreak. We’re completely overshadowed.” Seth pouts to his partner, as the two men begin walking to their house. “Just like in real life, huh?” Trevor answers, not seeming to be bothered by it at all. “Oh well, at least we’re in the main event. Look at it as an opportunity to show everyone how frickin talented we are. There are guys that will never grace the main event spotlight, and we get to after only a month in OSW. That’s something to be happy about, right?” “I guess,” Seth says unhappily, shuffling his feet. “It’s just not fair. We should always be the center of attention. Well I should, at least. What’s the big deal about Meanstreak, anyway?” “Well...he’s good for one. Damn good. And he isn’t annoying, which is one major thing he has over you,” Trevor say, smiling. “Plus he can easily kick your ass...which he might do after the shit you pulled on Monday.” “I didn’t do anything so bad,” shrugs Seth. “Just because he wasn’t at his motel room when we showed up doesn’t mean we should have been miserable. I mean, if it was the other way around and he was in our shoes, I wouldn’t mind.” “You’re telling me you wouldn’t mind if he added a few hundred dollars to your hotel bill in your absence,” Trevor asks him, to which Seth shrugs. “Why? Do you think he’ll mind?” Seth asks. “For your sake I hope not, Seth. I hope not.” Trevor says solemnly. Hearing this, Seth starts to look worried. This seems like a good place to fade out, so we do so.
