FUN

24 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 24th, 2002 Beep…Beep…Beep…Beep… … … ………………………………. The scene comes into to show the form of Riptah, tearing wires and straps off his body. He sits upright, looking around the room, surveying it in disgust. He glares at the darkness and flips on the lamp beside him. “…You assholes took my business away from me. I CAN’T ENJOY WRESTLING ANYMORE THANKS TO YOU TWO!” The final speech ever given by Ed echoed through Riptah’s mind. Ed was not a man known for his intelligence, wisdom or even coherent thought. But if there was anything Ed knew about, it was wrestling. His final speech was wise. It was right. Riptah closed his eyes tightly, reminiscing. Then he looked at himself. Where was he? He was in a hospital. They told him his back could be injured. It could lead to a shortened career. It could even lead to a wheel-chair. It could end Riptah’s enjoyment. But that was not the end Riptah was worried about. “…You assholes took my business away from me. I CAN’T ENJOY WRESTLING ANYMORE THANKS TO YOU TWO!” The one thing Riptah feared most wasn’t his fun being ended by an injury. He didn’t want to end up the way Ed felt before he died. He didn’t want to end up in sorrow because a group of thugs destroyed the thing he loved most. But the sorrow was still evident even in Riptah’s life. In Primetime Championship Wrestling, Da Westsidaz were known as the fun loving partiers. They were known as the life of the party. Even two months ago, Da Westsidaz loved a party. When ever they stepped into the ring with Civil Disobedience, it was fun. Even when they locked horns with Heresian Bloodlust, who they despised in every way. Their lifestyle, their looks, their beliefs, even their name. But whenever Riptah was in the ring with them, a fire sparked up inside him. It was fun. He stepped out of his bed, pain shooting up his spine like being whipped. He grunted and fell to one knee, even the big man could not take the pain. He felt the sorrow when he brought himself off the ground. He felt the sorrow creeping over his shoulder. He remembered Ed’s words… “You went and pulled that bullshit with The Vax… Hurting people, ending careers, all the political bullshit. You guys took it too far, and you killed all the fun there was in it. I’m not taking you guys on because it’s so much fun anymore… You guys have taken something I loved and ruined it…” “NO!” Riptah yelled outloud, breaking the tranquility and emptiness of the cold room. Riptah promised himself he wouldn’t end up like the shell Ed was before his end. He vowed on Ed’s name he would never let the business run him. If he ever got like that, the fun disappearing and the sorrow taking over, it would be defying everything he stood for. He sat back down on the bed, laying his head on the uncomfortable pillow, staring up at the ceiling. “Sorry, but your not doing this mind-game shit to me. I ain’t falling for it. I ain’t giving up. The Tank think they got me reeling, but they just opened my eyes.” He drifted off in his thoughts. In know conceivable way would he feel sad. The hospital bed, the loss of a friend, even the thought of missing his title. It just opened a fire inside of him. The Think Tank awoke his soul. If he didn’t want to give into the sorrow, then he would have to begin the search for fun. One would think that fun didn’t come in the form of Alan Kriegman, Howard Porter, Herbert Dorkiman, Trashcan Man, Armour and John Mammoth. But fun is a word that gets thrown around too much. Playing a game is fun because it is a challenge. The challenge is to beat the game. Being a heel is fun because it is a challenge. The challenge is to be hated. Going up against the Tank would be fun because it is a challenge. The challenge is twisting the sorrow. The challenge is exacting your revenge. “Think Tank… let’s have some fun.”
