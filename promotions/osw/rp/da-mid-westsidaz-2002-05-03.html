Da Mid-Westsidaz

3 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-03-02 We open with a shot of the suburbs. Big surprise, huh? Well, actually these aren’t quite the same suburbs you’re used to seeing with the opening of one these scenes. You see, Civil Disobedience’s hometown is far far away from these suburbs. About two thousand miles away, in fact. A bit more than a stone’s throw, so to say. For right now, we’re no longer in the suburbs of Chicago. We’re in the outskirts of that most notorious of cities. No, not Montpelier, Vermont. I’m talking about motherfuckin’ Los Angeles and shit. Site of violent riots, rampant criminal activity, and smog so thick you could cut it with a knife. Luckily we’re in the suburbs, which is plagued by considerably less of that above stuff. At the moment, our shot is zooming in on a basketball court, where about six African American teenagers are currently playing a game of pick-up basketball...as you would expect, seeing they’re on a basketball court and all. It’s a gray court, with white paint outlining the three-point arch and free throw line. The basketball hoops rest on poles on either end of the court, looking to be at regulation height. As you can see, it’s relatively normal stuff. Things are going about as well as you’d expect. Nothing out of the ordinary is taking place at all. This is before the sound of a gunshot ring out in the background. The kids on the court all jerk their heads in the direction of the sound, nervous expressions of their faces. A gunshot is nothing to take lightly, after all. The looks eventually settle a bit as music begins to play. It appears that it wasn’t an actual gunshot after all, but merely the opening to the song “Natural Born Killaz” by Dr. Dre and Ice Cube. The source of this unexpected music soon reveals itself, as onto the court trots none other than Seth Masters and Trevor Hawke, the two numskulls that make up that lovable team of Civil Disobedience. The two suburbians don’t appear to look their usual selves, however, as their wardrobe has apparently been...tweaked. Presently they are garbed in black and red FUBU jerseys, respectively, with JNCO jeans that sag waaaaaaay down. Seth is still wearing his backwards Cincinnati Reds baseball cap, but he has furthered his accessorization by wearing a big gold medallion hanging around his neck with a clock on the end of the chain. Trevor is wearing all sorts of rings and necklaces, almost blinding any who try to stare at him with the sparkling glare shining off of him. As the two men walk out onto the basketball court, Seth carries a boom box on his shoulder, which is currently ‘bumping’ the aforementioned song. The kids on the court all look at the two members of Civil D oddly, as Seth sets down the ghetto blaster and nods at Trevor. Serious looks appear on both men's face as they wait purposefully. And then it happens. When Dre kicks in with the lyrics, so do they, their voices overlapping the rapper’s as they begin reciting the song dead on. As they flow with the lines, Seth and Trevor flail their arms wildly to embellish themselves, making sure to make eye contact with the kids as they do so. Trevor walks into the middle of the court, Seth standing behind him, and begins his performance. “Join in with me into da mind of a maniac, born to be a killer since I came out da nutsack,” wraps Trevor, shaking his head cockily as he does so. Trevor leaps backward, and at the same time Seth jumps forward, taking the spot that his partner just left. “I'm in a murderous mind-state with a heart full of terror. I see da devil in da mirror.” Seth grabs his crotch as Trevor jumps back up to take a position to the side of him. “Buck buck, lights out! Cause when I grab my sawed-off, niggas get hauled off!” Trevor sticks out his index finger like a gun barrel and makes a motion of firing it in the air. “Barrel one! Shot yo’ motherfucking flesh. Barrel Two! Shot yo’ fucking heart out yo’ chest.” By this time, one of the kids has had the sense to walk past the rapping idiots and has pressed the stop button on the boom box. The bass kick stops, but that doesn’t stop Seth and Trevor from continuing to make complete asses out of themselves. “Terror, illustrates my era. Now I can't hang around my mama 'cause I scare her.” “I want the baddest muthafuck. ‘Yeah! What's up?’ It feels like I'm bustin' a nut when I open you up.” It’s at this point that the Civil D guys finally notice that the music is no longer playing. Looking rather sheepish, they both scratch their heads, shuffle their feet, and turn their gazes to the group of kids whose game they interrupted with their pathetic act. The six kids all have their arms crossed as they gaze at Seth and Trevor with confused looks on their faces. “Excuse me, sirs, but just what purpose does your presence here serve?” one of the kids on the right asks politely. “We were having an innocent game of basketball before you arrived with your radio. Do you want something from us?” “Hold up, Trev, I gotz dis one,” Seth says, putting a hand up to stop Trevor from walking forward. Straightening his shirt, Seth swaggers up to the kid, looking him in the eye. “Yeah, I want sometin’. Some fine ass pussy is what I want. Ha ha ha!” Seth begins to crack up laughing, though the guys standing in front of him see no humor in the situation, their expressions unchanging. Looking back he sees that Trevor is laughing his ass off as well. “But since there ain’t none of that around, me and my dawg Trev back there...” Seth says, sounding serious again as he points back at his partner. “We’re just here to prove a motherfuckin’ point.” “And may I ask just what is this this point you are trying to prove?” one of the kids asks skeptically. “Shit, you tell ‘em Trev,” Seth says, shaking his head in disbelief. “Heh. We’re here to prove we be the most hardcore motherfuckin’ niggaz on dis planet, yo. Just check dis shit out,” Trevor says cockily, snatching the basketball from the kid nearest to him. He dribbles the ball between his legs rather sloppily, almost losing it at some points, and then delivers the worst-godawful three point shot you’ve ever seen. It misses the backboard entirely, bouncing off of the pole underneath the hoop and rolling back to rest at his feet. Trevor shifts his eyes back and forth, not sure what to say. “Er...yeah. Uh...holla back, youngin!” he finally says, shaking his head back and forth confidently. A kid on the right steps out, seeming to have heard enough of this joke of a promo, and addresses Seth and Trevor condescendingly. “Excuse me, but do you two realize how stupid you’re coming off as?” “Stupid?! Shit, Trev! I know this lil bitch ain’t just gone an called me stupid! I’ll cap his motherfuckin’ ass!” Seth looks all sorts of offended as he tries to walk up to the kid to get in his face. Trevor grabs his partner before this can happen, though, and puts himself between the two in hopes of staving off any potential conflict. “Whoa, settle down, Sethdawg,” Trevor says, holding his partner back “Leave da kid alone, yo. Dis punk just don’t be knowing who he be messin’ wit.” “Oh, please enlighten me,” the kid says, a superior intelligent air in his voice as he rolls his eyes. “Just who do I ‘be’ messing with?” “We’re the motherfuckin’ MID-WESTSIDAZ and shit! Repping Aurora, Illinois up in this bitch!” Trevor says, loud and proud. “Mid-West SIDE!” Seth shouts, crossing this middle and ring fingers on each hand. He then flips his right hand upside down, making a sign that resembles the letters M and W. “Shit, everyone knows the mid-west side is the best side,” Trevor says matter-of-factly, cockily chuckling a bit at his words. “Are you two done with your pathetic little parody, yet?” one of the other kids asks, sounding exasperated. “Wha?” Trevor and Seth ask simultaneously. “You’re obviously trying to rip-off the Westsidaz, only by doing so your presenting forth racial stereotypes that I find rather insulting,” the kid says, shaking his head in disgust. “Wha?” Trevor and Seth again ask simultaneously. “Isn’t this performance a little juvenile for two men your age? You should be ashamed of yourself,” another kid chimes in, looking at Seth and Trevor as if they were two misbehaving children. “Whatever the case, I think you should get out of Los Angeles and go back to whatever bigoted community you came from,” the first kids remarks solemnly. “Shame on you. Shame on you.” “Hey now, we’re not rascists!” Seth shouts. It sounds as if he’s going to argue his point with the kids before Trevor tugs on his arm, pointing back in the direction they came. “Let’s just go, Seth.” “But...!” Seth protests, but Trevor just shakes his head, pulling him away. “Let’s just go. He’s right, we’re acting like children,” Trevor says, causing Seth to hang his head sadly. “Let’s go home and make fun of celebrities like usual. Nobody takes offense at that. “Alright...*sigh*...” Seth agrees. The two turn around and walk back in the direction they came from, Trevor picking up the boom box as they leave. As soon as Civil D gets off screen, the kids beging to crack up laughing, some even falling on the ground from the sheer hilarity they’re experiencing. “Shit, dawg, can you believe dem motherfuckin’ crackas?” one of them says, almost crying from laughing so hard. “Honkey-ass bitches be thinkin’ they got a fuckin’ chance against Riptah and Short Circuit,” another one says, trying to stop his chuckling. “Those niggaz ain’t shit.” “Well, it’s been fun, my brothas” another one says, wiping tears of laughter from his eyes as he stands up. “I don’t know about you folks but I feel like going home and fuckin’ my woman wit my giant, giant penis.” “Shit, man. I was about to say da same thing,” a fourth remarks with a smile. “That stereotype is da best one!” Fade to a politcally correct color.
