Near the Breaking Point

12 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-12-02 “You know,” Trevor says as he leans forward onto the table. “I really can not believe the shit that went down last Saturday night.” “I know,” Seth agrees, flipping his sunglasses down to the tip of his nose. “I was there. I saw it go down in front of me. It blows.” The team of Civil Disobedience are currently sitting in what appears to be some sort of shady cafe in the middle of urban Cleveland. It’s a funny story how they arrived here, really. Having arrived about a day early for the July 13th edition of OSW Slam, the two men were left with nothing but time to kill. Hence, here they are. Alright, so the story wasn’t that funny at all, or even really much of a story. In any case, the two men are tucked away in a booth located in one of the back corners of the restaurant. It’s a dimly lighted organization, with no windows to the outside. It’s not as if their presence would have helped light the room anyway, as presently it is about midnight. That being the case, it certainly explains the lack of business. Aside from a few regulars up near the bar in the front, Seth and Trevor are the only patrons in the room. Which gives them plenty of privacy to discuss the pressing matters of the day. “What the fuck is going through Porter’s head? Some fucking scholar he is, when his hired gun nearly cripples one of the fed’s biggest draws,” Trevor mutters. Reaching into the pocket of his pants, he pulls out a pack of Marlboro’s. He pulls a single cigarette out, then replaces the pack, looking back at Masters. “You got a light?” After a second of fumbling around, Seth holds out a Zippo lighter. Striking it, a flame billows up from the top, as Trevor sticks the end of his cigarette out to bathe in it. As smoke begins to float out of the lit end, Seth closes the lighter back up, while Trevor lifts the cancer stick to his lips. He takes a puff, exhaling out of the corner of his mouth, before shaking his head in disbelief. “Armour? Who the fuck saw that shit coming? It was supposed to be Toomes versus Porter, but did Porter wrestle? Noooo, it was some six nine son of a bitch built like a tank, that wrestled like one too. There’s no way we could have even seen that coming. You know, the fucking Think Tank, and their fucking diabolical schemes...it’s all bullshit, that’s what it is. A couple of wily son of a bitches who are more concerned with sniveling behind their master plans than fighting their own battles. Bullshit. And tomorrow night, you can bet your ass the bullshit is only going to get deeper,” Trevor rants, taking another puff of his cigarette as he finishes up. “Um, I hate to interrupt, but will you two be needing anything else tonight?” Seth and Trevor turn to their right to see that their waitress has returned, eyeing them both uneasily. After hearing the end of Trevor’s profanity-laced rant, to say she is eyeing them uneasily could very well be an understatement. Seeing the expression on her face, Seth forces a smile in an effort to reassure her of her safety. “No, we’re just about finished here.” “Uh...alright. I’ll be back with the check in a minute,” she says, before turning on her heel and walking back to the kitchen. Trevor scowls as she departs, inhaling from his cigarette once more, before flicking the ash off the end. “I’m definitely not arguing with you,” Seth tells his partner. “Porter did cross the line. Toomes is laying in a hospital bed right now, and it’s all his fault. And without a doubt, PHD is going to attempt to cross that same line tomorrow night. We just have to make sure to not let him.” “Phhh. I’ll make sure to not let him when I kick his buck teeth back into alignment,” Trevor seethes, making a fist with his free hand. “But that Think Tank shit’s not even the worst of it.” Trevor trails off, looking across the table at Seth as silence hangs in the air. “You talking about Amy?” Seth asks, already knowing the answer to the question. “You know damn well I’m talking about Amy. It’s just that...argh, everything is just getting so...out of control,” Trevor mutters, rubbing his face with his hand. “This Corg shit is completely out of hand. Everything in OSW is getting completely out of hand.” “What do you mean by that?” questions Seth, raising an eyebrow. “Corg, Porter, more than likely PHD...nobody’s safe anymore. It’s like a friggin evolution is taking place and we’re right in the midst of it,” Trevor says, staring down at the table as he takes another hit. “I’m still not following you, man,” Seth says, shaking his head in confusion. “Can you explain to me what the hell you’re talking about?” Seth leaps back in surprise as Trevor pounds his fist down on the table in the blink of an eye. He grinds his cigarette into the ashtray with a vengeance, as if venting his anger on the roll of tobacco. Looking up, Trevor stares at partner, his eyes ablaze with some unknown quality. “This sport. It’s not even a sport anymore. Not with all these fucking politics and power struggles and...and...well fuck, man, you know what I’m talking about,” Trevor says, his voice full of emotion. “It’s not even about winning anymore. It’s not even about entertaining the damn fans. It’s about who looks better, and who’s standing tall at the end of the night. It’s fucking bloodthirsty shit now. Just shit. And I’m sick of it. I hate what my career is turning into.” As Trevor finishes his tirade, he is greeted with another stretch of silence in answer. “I think you’re overreacting, dude,” Seth says, after about a minute. “Things are pretty cruddy at the moment, I won’t deny that, but things aren’t all that bad.” “Not bad? Not bad?” Trevor mutters, shaking his head. “Amy and Sebastian both went to the hospital after last Saturday, Seabass with a class three concussion and Amy with stab wounds. Stab wounds! That’s not bad?! What’s going to happen tomorrow night, Seth? What’s PHD going to pull to top last week’s display? Is he going to bring a gun-toting hitman to put a fucking bullet in my head? Is that what’s going to happen?!” Trevor is nearly hysterical at this point, his eyes gleaming with what very well may be the beginning stages of insanity. A frightened look appears on Seth’s face for a moment, but it is quickly dashed by one of determination. Leaning forward, he slaps a hand across his partner’s face, grabbing him by the collar of his shirt. “No, that’s not going to fucking happen! Get a hold of yourself, man! You’re cracking up! You’re not going to pull this shit on me now. Not now...” As Seth glares into Trevor’s eyes, a change occurs. Visibly relaxing, Trevor sighs deeply as the fervored expression disappears from his demeanor, replaced by a depressed frown. Releasing his partner, Seth watches Trevor settle back into his seat and cup his face into his hands. “I’m sorry man...” Trevor moans apologetically. Seth frowns back, concern for his partner visible on his face. “Look, dude. The stress is just getting to you. You need to relax. Don’t worry about Dorkiman. There’s nothing we can do about it until it happens. There’s no point in us examining the endless amount of possibilities. We’ll deal with it when we have to, but until then, you need to relax. Do something. Let loose. Enjoy yourself, if only for one night.” “Here’s your check.” Both men look up in surprise to see that their waitress has returned with the bill. She waits expectantly as Seth reaches into his pocket to pull out the twenty that will cover their night’s expenses. As she makes change, Trevor gazes up at her, licking his lips thoughtfully. She’s a pretty attractive girl, despite the dreary settings she’s gotten herself caught up into. About five foot six, a hundred and five pounds. Curly brown hair. Decent body. Maybe all he really needs is a night to relax after all. “I think you may be right, Seth,” Trevor says to his partner, before turning to the waitress. “Say, are you doing anything after work tonight? Because I was wondering...” The woman looks taken aback by the question, visibly startled. She looks as if she’s about to turn him down, but then a smile appears on her face as she returns Trevor stare. The scene fades out, leaving us with this shot.
