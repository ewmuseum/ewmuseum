American Upbringing

17 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/17/02 Walczak is seen sitting in a park down by a lake. He is lying back in a lawn chair, and has a cooler next to him. The cooler is obviously filled with beer, which in case you haven’t noticed by now is Walczak’s favorite liquid refreshment. The day is rather hot, and there are various other peoples in the park. Some of them are just walking through, others have set up a grill, and still others are actually bathing in the lake itself. Walczak is staying in America for a short time while his house is being renovated, but he has not been particularly pleased with his stay. “These damn Americans, are so annoying. I cannot believe how stupid that they actually act. Look at these people swimming in this lake. They swim in it, and yet they do not realize the filth that is present in this water. They do not realize that there could possibly be some sort of toxins in the water. Plain and simple this lake is not a lake for swimming, because if that’s what it is for then would there not be a life guard on duty, or at least some sort of safe house? Of course there would, and that just proves my point once again that Americans are once again the stupidest people in the world.” Walczak takes another beer out of the cooler, and laid back on his lawn chair. Still thinking in amazement at just how stupid these people actually are. Then he sees another thing that to him proves his point just a bit more. He sees a child walk by wearing a Reno Davis T-shirt, but that is not what amazes him. The child is walking hand in hand with his father, and the father too wears a nearly identical shirt. This is what makes his eyes glance over twice. “I thought that I had enough examples to get my point across, but this one, well it simply just takes the cake. Look at it. Normally it would have been a beautiful scene, father and son walking hand in hand through the park, but when it is visibly seen that the son’s mind is being poisoned by the ideas of the father, that is just sick and cruel. I cannot understand why someone would willfully acknowledge Reno Davis as a human being let alone become a fan of his. Even if such a travesty occurs than the least you could do is keep it to yourself, and allow the rest of humanity to remain normal, but to implant the likeness into the mind of your offspring, that is just disgraceful. If the communist regime had taken over this pathetic country, than this would not be a problem, and more likely than not Reno Davis would cease to exist.” Walczak looks over once again at the pair and turns away with a look of disgust. He lays his head back and as he nods off the scene fades to black…
