The Truth of Faith - The Old Testament

8 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04-08-2002 Lillith stood outside the stone ediface before her, staring up at the twin spires rising into the sky. The signpost on the neatly trimmed reads 'Our Lady of Wisdom Catholic Church', which was a fitting name in Lillith's eyes - perhaps they would honor a woman unlike so many other churches. She pushed open the left side of the oaken double doors and walked into the cool shadowy interior. She walked down the center aisle, her hand caressing each oiled pew in succession, until she stood before the altar. High above and behind was probably the most common religious symbol in the last 2000 years - a Jew crucified on a cross. The man who claimed to be the Son of God, was executed for it, and is believed to have gotten back up after three days. 'Can I help you. my child?' Lillith turned to her left to see a middle-aged man with grey hair enter from a side room. Lillith was slightly surprised to see that, although he wore the black shirt and while collar typical of priests, he also wore blue jeans and tennis shoes. He had a friendly smile which seemed to warm the room, if not Lillith. 'Actually, yes,' she answered, 'I have a couple of questions about your faith, and wanted to know if you could answer them for me.' 'Certainly' he said, 'please sit down and I'll help you as best I can' He invited her to sit in the front pew. She hesitated for but a moment, but took a seat. He too sat in the pew, but a good 10 feet away, so as not to make Lillith feel too uncomfortable. He folded his hands in his lap and waited patiently for her to begin. Lillith glanced around her surrounded for a few seconds, and her gaze fell upon a bible set at the side of the pew. Picked it up, she ruffled through the pages with her thumb, and then spoke without looking at her host. 'You believe everything written in here?' she asked? 'Most of it,' the priest replied, 'although some of it is more of a guideline than actual fact. Like the story of Jonah and the whale.' 'Um-hmm' muttered Lillith as she flipped open to the book of Leviticus, and began reading. 'If a woman having received seed shall bear a man child, she shall be unclean seven days, according to the days of separation of her flowers.... but she shall remain three and thirty days in the blood of her purification. She shall touch no holy thing: neither shall she enter into the sanctuary, until the days of her purification, be fulfilled.' Lillith looked up at the priest, 'So, if a woman gives birth, she's unclean for 33 days?' Looking a little taken aback at her choice of passage, the priest strggled for an answer, 'Well, you see, that book was written a long time ago, and not much was known about medicine, and-' 'Wait a second,' interrupted Lillith, as she had flipped to a different book, the Song of Soloman, 'Here's another one.' 'I sleep, but my heart waketh: it is the voice of my beloved that knocketh, saying, Open to me, my sister, my love, my dove, my undefiled: for my head is filled with dew, and my locks with the drops of the night. I have put off my coat; how shall I put it on? I have washed my feet; how shall I defile them? My beloved put in his hand by the hole of the door, and my bowels were moved for him. I rose up to open to my beloved; and my hands dropped with myrrh, and my fingers with sweet smelling myrrh, upon the handles of the lock. I opened to my beloved; but my beloved had withdrawn himself, and was gone: my soul failed when he spake: I sought him, but I could not find him; I called him, but he gave me no answer.' Lilith turned accusingly at the priest, 'That's erotica!' Not wanting to comment on it, the priest stayed silent, blushing slightly. Lillith closed the book and set it aside. She stood up, and looked at the man in the pew, 'Thank you for your time. Your silence was answer enough.' She turned away, and walked up the aisle.
