It all started with a can...

20 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/20/02 We see Tom Walczak walking through a grocery store. He has a shopping cart with him, and it is already more than half way full. Walczak is walking aisle-by-aisle making sure that he does not miss anything. Then he comes to aisle number nine, the soda aisle. Walczak walks down and stops in the middle of the aisle. A small smirk appears on his face. “To think that this little thing could be so huge.” Walczak picks up a can of soda, and begins to look at it almost examining it. He turns it around, and even examines the back. “This small piece of scrap metal has led to my Heavyweight title shot on the upcoming Pay-Per-View. I thank you.” Walczak tosses the can into his shopping cart, and begins to ride away. He pulls up to the shortest of the lines, and waits for his turn to unload. “Troy, although the can has caused my rise to superstardom it will surely lead to your fall. To think if you had just given me that can of soda this would have never happened. This coming Sunday you would not have to face me, and maybe you would have had a shot at retaining your belt, but now you don’t. The tides have turned, and they are turning towards Poland. We meet on Sunday, and one person comes out with the belt the other will be lucky to come out with his manhood in tow, and Troy I always figured that you would be better as a woman anyway.” Walczak looks to see if there is any movement in the line ahead of him. There is some room for him to start to unpack, so he starts unloading his cart. “I can’t wait for Sunday Doug. This will be a match for the ages. There is one thing I don’t understand though Doug. Instead of being enemies there was a time where we could have been friends. You see when you made that whole trust through carbonation speech I actually believed you. No doubt I was confused, but I trusted in you nonetheless, but then you had to go and fuck shit up. You turned your back on me Troy, and now I will put you on it. There is no doubt in my mind, oh and by the way Good Luck!” Walczak unloads the last of his things onto the counter, as the scene slowly fades out…
