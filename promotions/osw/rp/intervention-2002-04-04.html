Intervention

4 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-4-02 Alexei Ruslan sat in his office deep beneath the ground floor of the Kremlin with a look of worry on his face. He sat at his desk and stared at a small manila folder that was opened for all to see, however it was written in Russian and therefore unintelligible to us. However, to Alexei, it was anything but unintelligible…. It was a notice from one of the Chiefs of the Russian Military, General Leonid Tomifei, asking for an audience with Ruslan regarding the status of the United States based project, hatched by Ruslan, regarding the slow integration of Russia into the United States, as well as a way of spurring economic growth within Russia. It had been a while since Alexei had talked with the powerful Russian General, but it was not long enough. Tomifei was the type of man that kept a close eye on any project that came under his jurisdiction, but rarely was the type to intervene…. Unless there was a snag of some sort…in the system. Ruslan fidgeted in his chair and checked his watch…it was about time. He got up from his seat and walked over to a nearby mirror and gazed at himself. He fixed his hat and tie nervously and donned his brown trench coat as if it was some sort of armor to protect him from some huge adversity that was about to befall him, and walked out the door. He walked with a calm about him that few people had seen. While Ruslan believed that it conveyed a look of confidence, the other Russian workers that passed him only knew full well that something was wrong for Alexei Gregorovich Ruslan to be calm. He walked to an elevator and called it. The two huge steel doors opened almost immediately as he walked into the elevator and hit the ground floor of the Kremlin. A few moments later, the doors opened and Ruslan walked out into the massive Kremlin. After walking through a large, illustrious foyer and down a pipeline of hallways, Ruslan came to a large wooden door that served as the entrance to a conference room. He grabbed the brass handle, turned it, and walked into the room. Inside, a huge round conference table sat in the center of the room as well as a few desks lined up against the wall and a large blackboard. Standing in the center of the room, clad in his military uniform which was decorated with several Russian medals, stood General Leonid Tomifei of The Russian Federation. Standing about 6’3” tall, Tomifei was an imposing figure to say the least. White hair covered the battle scarred soldiers head just under his hat, and the lines of his hard, square face showed that he had been through an awful lot to get where he was today. As Ruslan walked into the room, Tomifei let out a small sigh. The line between government and military had been breached. There was no love loss between Ruslan and Tomifei. The two merely stomached one another. While Ruslan was government and intelligence, Tomifei was military. It is a strange marriage, Ruslan and Stanislav, for generally these two camps do not get along, but lined up against Tomifei, it was extremely apparent. “About time you got here, Ruslan,” barked Tomifei, his voice higher pitch than perhaps he let on, “I don’t have all day, you know!” Ruslan nodded and walked into the room, “I had other matters, General,” muttered Alexei, “I was as prompt as could be.” Tomifei scoffed and walked closer to Ruslan. To Leonid Tomifei, Ruslan was nothing more than a pencil pushing bureaucrat. One must realize that the military in the Russian Federation is almost a political party in and of itself. He walked over in front of Ruslan and looked down at the far younger Russian, “Lets get through this as quickly as possible, Ruslan, I have things to do.” Ruslan raised an eyebrow, “Da, I do as well!” He took a step back and sat at the table, feigning confidence as much as he could, for while Ruslan and Tomifei were almost equals, Tomifei was a man to be afraid of. Old school, hard lined General that disliked the young guard of the Russian Federation almost as much as he hated the rest of the world. Tomifei stood across the table from Ruslan, not willing to lower himself to the same level as the director of the FSB, “I wanted to talk to you about your current project that you’ve been spearheading in United States…” Ruslan swallowed and nodded. Tomifei brought his hands behind his back and stood taller, “I believe that the major objective of that mission was to dominate the Old School Wrestling Federation and bring it under our control….” Ruslan nodded while Tomifei continued, “..yet that has appeared to have eluded you.” Ruslan caught an opening to avoid this entire situation and stood, fixing the front of his jacket as he did, “With all due respect, General, Praporschik Stanislav can handle these inquiries much better than I can.” Tomifei stood stone faced, “Praporschik Stanislav is incapacitated at the moment.” Ruslan stood stock still, realizing that yes, he would have to handle this himself. “Well..perhaps it is better if I handle this whenever….” Tomifei burst into a yell, eliciting a jump from Ruslan, “Perhaps you should listen to me! You pencil pushing bureaucrat!” Alexei glared at Tomifei, but complied and remained silent, “There are few things that are causing trouble within your….Red Army.” Alexei swallowed as Tomifei turned his back to Alexei and walked across the room, “Who is this Tempest girl?” Alexei nodded, knowing he was going to hear this sooner or later, “Tempest is..an acquaintance of ours…that is all…” Tomifei turned on his heels and glared at Ruslan, “I do not call Ivan Stanislav’s lips touching hers an ‘acquaintance,’” Tomifei ran his tongue behind his lips and stood stock still, “I consider that something more….and I think you consider that something more….” Alexei shrugged and put his hands in his pockets, “Well, I do not know….” “I think you do, Ruslan. You are not stupid. You can see it as much as I can see it.” Alexei turned from Tomifei to relieve the pressure, “Tempest is merely helping the Red Army, that is all. She is a morale builder, she is a friend of the communist….” Tomifei burst into a roar, “DO NOT TALK OF THAT GARBAGE!!” Ruslan jumped and stood still while Tomifei continued, “We are not Soviet Union anymore! We are Russian Federation, which WE work for! Do not speak of the past like it is attainable!!” Alexei paused with his back to Tomifei and shifted his eyes back and forth, and then continued, “….she is a….” Tomifei walked closer to Ruslan, “She, Ruslan, is a distraction.” Tomifei walked again, closer to Ruslan, “Distractions are not welcome here, you know that.” Alexei spun around and looked at Tomifei, “What exactly are you saying??” Tomifei crossed his arms and looked down at Alexei coldly, “You were sent to America with Praporschik Stanislav as an advisor for him. He is too stubborn for even I to speak to, but he listens to you,” Tomifei growled, “make sure that Tempest becomes nothing more than a fly on the wall. I do not wish for her to be in any relationship with Stanislav.” Alexei shook his head, “I do not think that is best course of action, General.” Tomifei turned on his heels. “Since Stanislav joined the Military of Russia years ago, he has been devout Russian. But now, there is a small wall being built….something within that overgrown skull of his is misfiring, and it better get back in place before he becomes too….” “Independent?” Asked Alexei, grimly. “Say whatever you want, Ruslan,” muttered Tomifei, “but it is time for you to act as the advisor that we have you there for. Get that woman away from him, before he is distracted too much.” Ruslan shook his head and walked closer to Tomifei, swallowing his fear, “I am Ivan Stanislav’s friend, and because of that I am reluctant to take any action like that!” Tomifei spun around quickly and pushed his finger into the chest of Ruslan, “You listen to me Ruslan, you may be his friend, but you are also a Russian! This may have been your plan, but it is Russia’s duty to be successful!” Tomifei stood tall and looked down hard on Ruslan, “You are an organ of Russia, and if you malfunction, Russia will take over!” Alexei nodded slowly and seemed to lose some of his confidence, “I will see what I can do, General.” “You do that, and do not fail again, Ruslan.” Tomifei started to walk away and stopped at the door, “I want rid of her, Ruslan, or else I will find someone that can get it done themselves. Ivan Stanislav is a soldier and nothing more…..he can be replaced.” Tomifei grabbed the doorknob, “And so can you.” Tomifei opened the door and left Ruslan alone. He stood there and looked at the floor and then stared up at the great wall of the Kremlin and saw his flag. He nodded and muttered something in Russian, and walked out the door….
