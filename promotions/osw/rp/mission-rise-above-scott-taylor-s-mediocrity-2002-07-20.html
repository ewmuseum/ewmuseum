Mission: Rise above Scott Taylor's mediocrity

20 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 20th Throughout Kid Wonder’s brief 22-year life, losing has not been in option. It’s become accepted when it happens, but is never an option when doing something. Everything Kid Wonder does, he has to do just right. K-Dub has to be the best at whatever he is doing at that particular moment. Half-assing, or mediocrity of any kind, is unacceptable. Lately, however, that’s the highest Wonder has been able to get in his career in the ring. Mediocrity. He’s never been able to win a title that matters in a promotion that matters. He has been made to feel like an outcast in a group of Outcasts. He was unwanted in a DAMN group that wanted him. And, all in all, Kid Wonder has dropped below the competition that he is, on a talent standpoint, clearly above. Yep. Kid Wonder has been the picture of mediocrity for well over a year now. But it’s about time for all of that to change. It’s about time for “The Quintessential Teen Idol” to take his place among wrestling’s elite. Sure, some do consider K-Dub one of wrestling’s elite, but hardly has he ever lived up to those standards. But that is going to change and change now. Kid Wonder’s mission, if he chooses to accept it, is to rise above the dreaded mediocrity stick. He is to rise above his weaker foes and insert himself into the elite where he has always belonged. Last Saturday was a step in the right direction. A young man with a lot of nerve, a man named Scott Taylor, took it upon himself to run down Wonder, as well as one of his greatest friends/enemies in the business, Doug Troy. So Wonder, who was cheated out of a win against the same Scott Taylor a week earlier, got a measure of revenge against the newbie and beat him soundly in the middle of the ring. It wasn’t much, but Kid Wonder’s win against Scott Taylor was a step in the right direction. A few more of those against the mediocre mid-carders of OSW and the elite will have no choice but to take notice. Serious notice. Kid Wonder’s mission to rise above mediocrity is far from complete, but he’s getting there. Soon enough, he’ll be where he should’ve been all along. And soon enough, all those tags and labels that have been given to him over time will be shed away for good.
