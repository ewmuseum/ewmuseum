A Couple o' Jedi Pimps

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/10 to 5/11 Pre-Slam Late night on Friday is usually an eventful time for the boys in Civil Disobedience. Usually, that is. Usually they would be busy drinking, smoking, straight mid-west coastin’ at some bumpin’ party around this time. Usually they would be busy macking hos, backing bros, and cracking those...who mess with them, that is. Usually they would be doing the one thing they do best: pretending to be cool, and do it with such style and flair that no other posers would ever dare try to match the posing greatness displayed by them. Not tonight however. Tonight, they are about as far away from the heart of cool society as they can get. When you are standing in line to get into Star Wars Episode II: Attack of the Clones almost a full week before the movie is set to open, you can rest assured your finger is far from the pulse of the trendy community. This is hardcore nerd-dom at its best, no matter what refutes the accused may give. Seth and Trevor know this, of course, but as far as their line of thinking goes they figure that since they are former...and future...OSW Tag Team Champions, that places them in a category of cool all of their own. Considering this, backed up by their major egotism and flawless self-images, they feel they can indulge themselves in such dorky pleasures every now and then. And it certainly doesn’t get any more dorky than this. Around the movie theater are all kinds of freaks, geeks, and weirdos. And those are just the employees of the cinema. The actual people standing in line are ten times as worse. Most of them are donned in full costume of some character or another from the Lucas-verse. You got your Boba Fetts, clad in Mandalorian armor. You got your CHEW-baccas, with costumes ranging from nice replications to the poor slobs who ended up shaving their dogs and glueing the hair to their skins. Then there are the assorted lesser known aliens. You got your Bothans, your Sullustans, your Ithorians, your Biths, your Ross Perots, your Olson Twins, and any of twenty other crazy races, each zanier than the last. And two thirds of the way toward the back of the line stand Seth Masters and Trevor Hawke, albiet looking a bit worse for the wear. God only knows how long they’ve already been standing there, but judging by the full length beards growing out from their faces, it’s been a while. They’re getting pretty smelly as well, as the odor lines that you’d expect to see in comic books are flowing out from them. That’s damn smelly, if you couldn’t tell. Of course, with the company they’re currently keeping, Seth and Trevor seem like candidates for the next cover of GQ by comparison. Again, it doesn’t seem to faze Seth and Trevor. They’re happy to just be there. “Oh man...just six more days until the movie opens, man!” Seth shouts excitedly, looking in anticpation at the movie theater. He presses his face up against the glass, looking like a puppy at the pet shop trying to get through his sole impediment between himself and happiness. “I just hope waiting in this line for two weeks is worth it,” Trevor mutters, busy scraping some plaque off of his teeth. “I haven’t brushed my teeth since the toothpaste ran out three days ago.” “Sacrifices must be made if we truly wish to meet our goal,” Seth replies, taking a steadfast tone in his voice. “If you want to make an omelet, you gotta break some eggs. Be strong, Trev. Be strong.” “I’ll try,” Trevor mutters, running his tongue along the surface of his teeth in disgust. “Do or do not. There is no try,” Seth answers back, in a crappy Yoda impression. “I bet you’ve been waiting days to say that,” Trevor remarks with a sigh. “Hey, so what,” answers Seth with a shrug. “I’ve almost quoted the entire script of Empire in the time we’ve been here. I only have three hundred more lines to go.” “My, won’t that be fun,” Trevor mutters sarcasticly. “ It will be,” Seth says, still doing the Yoda impression. “It will be.” “You got the line wrong, dicksmack,” Trevor says, shaking his head. “It’s ‘YOU will be.’” “Hey...I...shut up!” Seth yells at his partner. “Don’t ruin this for me, Trev. This type of thing is only a once in a lifetime opportunity and...” Seth trails off, as he overhears a bit of conversation taking place in front of him that doesn’t settle well with him. Directly ahead of him, a man in street clothes has stopped by the line and has pulled aside a man in a Darth Vader mask, in what appears to be a heated argument. “I can’t believe you think Star Trek is better than Star Wars,” the guy in the Vader suit says in disbelief. “What the hell is the matter with you? You’re not one of those guys that think that Episode One ruined the whole series, are you?” “No, I think the whole series just overall lacks creativity. Why you people are standing in a line like this for weeks seems absolutely insane to me. It’s not just Episode One that sucks. They all do. Why can’t they come up with a believable alien, like Mr. Spock. Instead they have Chewbacca and Jar Jar Binks. Give me a breeeeea...HEY!” The man doesn’t have a chance to finish his rant, as Seth has already pulled him off to the side and is holding him up by the collar of his shirt. “How dare you speak your blasphemy, you Trekkie fuck!” shouts Seth angrily as he slams the guy’s head into the brick wall. “You’re lucky I don’t have a lightsaber, or else I’d fucking cut you open and eat your soul.” Thirty-two hits later, the man is unconscious, laying on the ground bleeding. Trevor looks down at Seth’s handiwork with disdain. “Now that was productive,” he says sarcastically, eyeing Seth as if he were a misbehaving child. “Hey, he was dissing the Force, man...” Seth starts, only to be stopped by a raised hand from Trevor. “Just be quiet, Seth,” Trevor pleads. “I don’t know how much longer I can take this insanity.” “Fine, be like that,” Seth mumbles, kicking the unconscious man before he sits down on the ground beside him. “I’ll be quiet. You know I’m right, though. No one fucks with Star Wars like that and gets away with it.” Twelve hours later, vacant stares are painted across Seth and Trevor’s faces. Seth looks almost catatonic as he stares at a wasp buzz around his head. When the little critter lands on his eye and injects his stinger into it, Seth doesn’t even flinch. Trevor’s mouth is hanging open as he stares, unblinking, straight up into the sun. Prolonged periods of inactivity do this kind of stuff to people. Make them totally out of touch with reality and stuff. Suddenly Seth shakes his head, regaining consciousness from out of nowhere. Pulling the wasp stinger from his eye, he stands up, looking worried. He reaches into his pocket, pulling out a letter that he had apparently forgotten he had, then shakes Trevor out of his comatose state. Trevor blinks, causing a layer of eye crust to crumble and fall to the ground, but soon he’s showing signs of life, despite being a bit groggy. “Trev...do you ever have the feeling that you were underappreciated?” Seth asks, a bit apprehensively. “What the hell makes you ask a question like that?” Trevor asks back, sounding uninterested as he cleans the last bits of crust from his eye. Seth does one of those shifty eye things that people do when you know they’re lying. “Well, uh...you see...it was like...er...well, I was having sex with this girl the other night,” he says, pausing and nodding as if he is satisfied with the way his fabrication is going. “Yeah. And she so did not know she was having the fucking of her lifetime. I kept trying to drive the concept home to her, but she just didn’t figure it out and...” “Don’t lie to me, Seth,” Trevor interrupts, turning his gaze to rest on his partner. “What makes you think I was...” “Do you really expect me to buy all that crap?” Trevor asks in a lecturing tone. “You fucking a girl? Give me a break.” “Oh. Right,” Seth says, hanging his head sadly. “So what’s the real reason you’re asking a stupid-ass question like that?” “Um...well,” Seth rolls his eyes, trying to think of a decent answer he could give his partner, but seems to come up with nothing. The truth looks like it will have to suffice. “Well I was hoping you might be able to ease my feelings about this letter right here, but I think you had better just look at this yourself.” “What is it?” “Just read it and find out, damn it. Do I have to explain everything to you.” “Touchy,” Trevor mutters to himself, rolling his eyes. “Geez, you’re pretty ornery today? What’s the matter? Got the shits again?” “No!” Seth exclaims defensively, his hands reflexively shooting to his ass before he catches himself. Shaking his head, Masters points to the piece of paper in Trevor’s hand, his eyes narrowing. “Just read that and you’ll be orned up too...whatever the hell that means.” “Fine, fine. Let’s see here,” Trevor says, seeming not too excited as he snatches the piece of paper out of his partner’s hand and begins reading it out loud. “Date...four, twenty four, o-two. From Ed at oldschool dot com. Hmmm...” After perusing the sheet for a few seconds, the expression on Trevor’s face turns from disinterest to shocked disbelief. Bringing the paper down from his eyes, he brings his gaze upon Seth once more. “What the shit is this, man?” he asks unhappily. “See, I told you it was fucked up!” Seth shouts in a vindicated tone. Trevor looks down at the paper and begins reading it out loud. “Blah blah blah. Civil Disobedience was doing a good job, but they went to do their own thing and even they are out of control now.” He shakes his head, looking perturbed. “What the fuck?” “Keep reading, man. It gets better,” insists Seth. Trevor obliges, and it isn’t long before he’s reading another passage out loud. “Just make sure the Tag Champions and Civil Disobedience know you’re here to mess them up.” Trevor drops the letter down from his eyes once more. “All right. Fuck this letter.” “Fuck it up its stupid ass,” agrees Seth. “Even though it doesn’t really have an ass...or any other body part as far as that’s concerned. I’m talking about its figurative ass, of course.” “I thought Ed was cool with us, and then he goes and pulls this shit? The world is turning upside down, my friend, and I am not a big fan of standing on my head,” Trevor comments, crumpling up the piece of paper and tossing it off to the side. “I can’t believe Ed would write something like that. What’s with this two-faced shit, anyway?” “Well actually, he’s denying he ever wrote it,” Seth explains, but he doesn’t sound too sure of himself. “Do you think he’s telling the truth?” “Well if he is then there is definitely someone out there that has an ass-kicking waiting from them,” Seth exclaims wildly. Trevor stares at him blankly. “Courtesy of this foot!” Seth exclaims again, pointing down at his boot. “Oh, I get it now,” Trevor says with a nod. “But what if he isn’t telling the truth?” “Then the whole fucking world is against us,” Seth says, turning in a circle as he regards all of his surroundings with a glare. Trevor sighs in response, kicking the crumpled up email further down the sidewalk. Suddenly, Seth stops his sweeping gaze as a look of horror crosses his face. “Aw, fuck man!” he shouts. “We can’t stand in this line until Thursday. We have a fucking appearance to make on Slam!” “You’re not telling me we just wasted our whole week waiting in this line only to lose our spots, are you?” Trevor asks, though he already knows the answer to his question. “Damn it!” Seth shouts angrily, kicking the unconscious body of the Trekkie laying on the ground next to them. After a few more angry kicks, Seth’s eyes light up. It’s almost as if...he has an idea. “I’ve got it,” he shouts, dragging the Trekkie’s body into their spot in line. “This guy’s going to save our spot for us!” “And just how will that work?” Trevor asks skeptically. “Don’t you think an ambulance will pick him up eventually? Or what if someone just cuts in front of him? It’s not like he’s going to fight back. And what about the blood seeping out of his ears? ” “Nah, that’s no problem,” Seth answers. “An ambulance won’t pick him up because people sleep all the time in staked-out lines like this one. And as for cutting in line, nobody here will dare take our spot. Leaving him behind is like leaving a coat in your seat at the movie theater. It’s like a reservation. And as for the blood...” “Yeah, what about the blood?” Trevor asks, tapping his foot as he waits for Seth’s answer. “You can’t expect me to solve every single problem in the whole fucking world,” responds Seth, throwing his arms up in the air. “I’m not going to spend all day arguing with you. We’ve got an appearance to fulfill up in Toronto, so quit jibba-jabbering and let’s get moving, damn it.” “Whatever,” Trevor says with a sigh, leaving his place in line to follow Seth away. “I just hope that guy’s still there when we get back.” “Worry about that after Slam, man,” Seth says as the two reach their beat-up gray Chevrolet Celebrity. “We’ve got enough people shitting on us as it is, and things just keep getting worse. This kind of crap is not good for our image, bro.” “Whatever,” Trevor says, sighing again. “As long as we don’t get our asses kicked tonight, I’ll be happy.” “Agreed,” Seth says as he climbs into the driver’s seat. “Getting our asses kicked most certainly does not kick ass.” Seconds later the two are driving off, on the road to Toronto. And as the viewer’s who caught Slam know already...so much for them being happy.
