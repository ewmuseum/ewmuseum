The Nemesis

16 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 16th, 2002 My face was flush as I walked into the men’s room of a local 7-11 convenience store, hauling with me a large chunk of beef jerky that I had just purchased at the counter. I could feel a tinge of pain as I unzipped my pants and began to relieve myself, feeling somewhat lax in concentration. A few moments later another man came in to the stall next to me and I suddenly became much more aware. There’s something about urinating in a public restroom that makes you dilly dally just a little less. That’s when I realized that I wasn’t quite alone. I turned my head to the left and saw a very pale man standing at the stall next to me. He wasn’t urinating, he wasn’t doing anything but staring at me. I would usually be disturbed by this, unfortunately, I knew the man by name. “Alejandro?” I questioned, still not believing what I was seeing. “What in god’s name are you doing here?” He was approximately five foot ten, with neatly combed black hair and hazel brown eyes. He wore a leather jacket, which probably would have looked tacky on anyone else, but he had the body to fill it out. “God?” He replied with a sinister grin. “God and I haven’t seen eye to eye in quite some time, Mr. Lexian.” As he grinned, I could see his fangs longing to show. That’s right, I said fangs. The guy was a living, breathing vampire. Well, not living. After all, vampires are dead. “How’d you find me?” I asked, trying my hardest not to sound scared. “You’re the ‘Executioner,’” He said laughing. “You’re not hard to find. Even though you don’t wear the amulet anymore, we can still feel you…and you can feel us.” He was right. In the back of my head, I could feel a tingling sensation whenever I passed by someone who wasn’t quite human. You know the type. Drifters, loners, and all of those individuals that just seem to fade into the background never to be noticed. They all have a secret to hide. I was dubbed ‘The Executioner’ by fellow vampire killers. It basically meant that I was their ace, number one vampire hunter and that vampires had to fear me. Trouble was, they didn’t. Not unless I was wearing the amulet, a piece of Executioner jewelry that gave me added sense of awareness, agility and strength. Why wasn’t I wearing it right now? Long story. “I’ve put those days behind me,” I retorted, turning my body to be able to block him if he decided to attack him. “Right,” Alejandro said, nodding his head sarcastically. “You really expect me to believe that? No vampire killer could ever step away for any length of time. The drive to fight, the drive to kill…” “You’re the killer,” I stated hatefully, staring into those dark, abyss-like eyes. As I did, I got caught in his stare. I immediately pulled away, feeling weak at the knees as he tried to roll my mind. The amulet protected me from them coming into my thoughts, and without it, they could come in whenever they pleased. He was pleased. “Look at yourself. Nothing more than a shell of yourself. I should kill you.” “That’s always been your goal.” “Ever since that night you came into my life…embarrassed me in front of Alexandria…” He gritted his teeth, anger fuming from his chest. I guess vampires have memories, after all. “You could kill me, but where is the fun in that?” I replied. “I’m your nemesis. You need me to keep your fire going.” I leaned in to make my point very clear. “What else would you do with your day if I were dead?” He huffed at me. “Probably finding someone new to torment.” He snickered under his breath. “Besides, don’t give yourself so much credit. You wouldn’t be that difficult to kill.” I still remembered the last time we fought. He had nearly ripped my arm from the socket, although I badly scarred his face after raking it with a small knife. Looking at it now, his face appeared to have healed perfectly. Good for him. I guess a night in a coffin does wonders for your complexion. “Try me.” Alejandro mocked my attempt to be brave. “You wouldn’t be a challenge without the amulet. Now you’re just a defenseless human.” He looked over at the stall next to me, noticing the man there watching us intently. Alejandro quickly hissed and flashed some fang, causing the man to hurry and leave. “Was that really necessary?” “He looked agitated,” Alejandro said with a shrug. “I think he overheard our entire conversation.” I sneered. “Are you worried he’s going to run and spill all of your vampire secrets?” “No, but he had a look of fear in him. You humans are so unpredictable when you are afraid.” He looked at me. “We follow your career, Lexian. We never left you, even when you decided that you didn’t want to be the Executioner anymore. We were there on Wrestle Vessel when you made the announcement. We were there on the first day when you stepped foot in OSW. We even rubbed shoulders with Disorderly Conduct.” “If you’re getting close to a point, make it.” “We’re in contact with a man who is very much like yourself,” He said, crossing his arms. “Does the name Michael Strickland ring a bell?” I shook my head, having no idea what he was talking about. “Should I?” “He used to work for New Wave Entertainment. Apparently he was well on his way on being a star there, but he found some disturbing things about the company.” Disturbing? What could he be talking about? “What do you mean?” I inquired, feeling curious. A smile crossed his face. A very sinister smile. Why were vampires so good at that? “Oh, I wouldn’t want to spoil the fun for you. Do a little research for yourself. Let’s just say Mr. Strickland has hit upon some hard times and wants a little revenge for himself.” “Just stay out of my affairs,” I warned him. I zipped up my pants and turned my back on him. Mistake number one. As soon as I did, he was in front of me, and I staggered back in surprise. The look on his face showed that he was pleased with himself. I found myself with my back to the stall in a very vulnerable position. “Our destinies are star crossed,” He said. “No matter how far you try to distance yourself, we’ll always run into each other. It’s fate. Now, if you’ll excuse me, I think I’ll have a word with the man at this stall now.” He turned around to walk away and I found myself trying to be a good guy again. “Stop!” I ordered. “You can’t kill that man. I won’t let you.” “You aren’t in any position to stop me,” He replied, not even bothering to turn around. “You don’t want to die now. Not when you’re so close to having that match with NC-17. Don’t disappoint all of those nerdy wrestling fans who nearly creamed in their pants waiting for this match.” “You can’t…” “I can,” He said simply. “And you can’t stop me. Not now, at least. Take care, Lexian.” And then he vanished out the door. I hung my head in despair, knowing that he was right. I said a prayer for the man who he was pursuing and went about washing up my hands at the sink.
