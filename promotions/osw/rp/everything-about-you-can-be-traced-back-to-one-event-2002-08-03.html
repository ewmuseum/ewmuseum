Everything about you can be traced back to one event...

3 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/3/02 Days used to tick by like seconds on my dad's Tag Heuer. Summer afternoons in an upper middle class neighborhood in upstate New York, the fruits of childhood. My brother, 'the man', treated me better then an older brother should. He was quick to start teaching me the ways of the world, the way he saw it at least. Our house was big, our yard bigger... And things were good. The days were warm and the nights cool this summer. It seems like such a short time ago. We just had a barbecue at our house that day, the entire neighborhood was there. Beer flowed like water, chicken and burgers were plentiful, but laughter seemed to be in greater supply then anything. We loved where we were, and we were loved... So we thought. That night was cooler then most, for a time. It was late, my parents were asleep. Me and Preston, my brother, were still awake, just goofin' off. The night seemed to heat up in a heartbeat, a burning, searing heat... Our home was on fire. Me and P. had to wake up mom and dad. We frantically grabbed the few valuables we could and ran out on the lawn. Ran on the lawn...to watch our home burn to the ground before the fire department arrived... Things were not good. The next day we went through the rubble, nothing was recoverable. The police said it was arson, and they had no leads. My family and I spent the next few days in a hotel room. Everyone moped around, nobody wanted to believe what happened. Even at that young age, I realized my parents had no idea what we were going to do. They started arguing with each other a lot, about nothing really. Me and P. just sat and watched, our happy life, crumbling before our eyes... It was getting worse. My folks copped out, they flaked. They took the stereotypical route, and possibly the correct one, and blamed the fire on white people. They decided that our white 'friends' in the neighborhood secretly hated us and wanted us out, just because we were black. I didn't buy it, hell I still don't. But they did, and they were pissed. They decided we weren't going to be in a community that did not want us. They decided we would go to a place where minorities were in abundance, and accepted. We moved to Brooklyn... It was on. My father quickly began teaching me and Preston the 'real' ways of the world. I wasn't sold on it, but Preston ate it up. Some people find it easier to blame others for their trials and tribulations. Everyone in our community felt the same way. Our big house was now a tiny apartment, our huge yard was now a run down basketball court. I was not happy, I was not adjusting... I was in hell. My parents' ideas of finding nice city jobs lasted about three weeks. They ran into trouble landing a good job like they used to have, blamed it on white people, and decided to take the easy route. My folks sought quick money, they began dealing and using crack. It was hard to believe that a month ago we had everything. My life was gone, things were different, I had to cope. I blocked it the same way any kid would, I acted like everything was the same... Convinced myself everything was fine. My new days were filled with finding ways to entertain myself. My parents were either not home, or stoned out of their minds. Preston was busy hanging out with his new 'friends'. I wondered why they all wore the same colors, it seems so funny now. So I sat in front of the television, surrounded by shitty toys, and I played. Alone in my mind, not much different then the way things are now. So this was the way things were, for what seemed like an eternity... Then it all ended. The day was like any other. I woke up late, school was a non-issue, and made my way to the living room. I stopped by the kitchen on the way and looked in the fridge. It was completely empty. It's funny how one's fridge seems to mirror one's life. When you are happy and doing good, your fridge is full, and vice versa. So I plopped in front of the television, with an empty stomach. A putrid stench of smoke filled the room. The kind of stench that can only be made by crack, smack, crank, or some other ghetto drug. Cheap, nasty fixes, that's what my parents thirsted for, and they were always thirsty. The day was carrying on like any other... But it couldn't have been more different. The phone rang during 'Fat Albert' as it usually did. My father answered with a distant, 'Yello, yeah, get yur ass over huur.' I knew it had to be 'Uncle Escobar.' Uncle Escobar was a Hispanic man who dressed like a pimp and supplied my parents with their fixes and the drugs they sold. What was it Tony Montana was told in Scarface? Oh yeah, 'never get high from your own supply.' My parents didn't take that advice to heart. My parents told me that Uncle Escobar was a relative, and the expected me to believe it. I never did, no matter how much I tried... Shorty there after, there was a knock at the door. 'Yeah Esco, door's open!' yelled my dad. What else was he gonna do, get his lazy ass up? The door exploded open with a bang, causing me to drop my toy and look. Five armed police officers rushed in, guns pointed at us. They were yelling at my still seated parents, 'Where's Preston?!? Where is he?!?' My mother simply nodded at his bedroom door. The cops ran and kicked in the door. Preston was climbing out of the window, but he wasn't quick enough. The last time I saw my brother, he was being dragged from our apartment, cuffed and cussing. 'The man' didn't seem like much of a role model anymore. My parents simply sat on the couch and watched... This day was turning sour. About an hour later there was another knock on the door. Uncle Escobar must have seen the cops and stayed away for a while. Again my father yelled for the person to enter, and this time it was Uncle Escobar. My parents demeanors changed drastically. The blank stares turned into blank smiles. There fix was there, and that made them happy. Uncle Escobar carried a duffle bag in one hand as he approached us, a smile curling his lips also. He walked by me and patted my head, 'Hey there little Frankie. Your gettin' big.' They all began to have a conversation that I cared not listen to. I paid attention to my toys, and the television. I almost didn't notice the conversation turning into an argument... But it had. The next few moments are embedded beyond control. They move in slow motion every night while I try to sleep, I cannot shake them. The voices, ever growing, yelling incoherently. Something about money owed and drugs used. I turned to catch the end of it. The sound of the zipper on the duffle bag made me look. The bag was slung open with a flutter. Bags of random drugs and loose money filled the air of the apartment. The bag fell to the floor, the only remaining thing in his hand was a sawed off shotgun, aimed at my parents... The next few seconds took an eternity. I flinched at the first two gun blasts, then I just watched. I watched as round after round was emptied into the chests of my parents. Well, my shells of parents. What's odd is the looks on their faces did not change. The same blank stares. My parents were dead to me long ago, but now they were truly dead, grotesquely dead. Uncle Escobar turned quickly, putting the barrel of the gun about an inch and a half from my forehead, my eyes starring directly into his. I remember wanting him to do it, damn that's so fucked up. He simply muttered 'ahh shit,' and walked out... I sat and starred at my dead parents. I turned to yell at Preston, then remembered he was gone. So I swallowed my breath and turned back to my folks. I starred at their mangled bodies, at their blank faces. Shooting images of our life in the upstate hit me like the bullets still lodged in their bodies. Images of laughter, happiness. Me and Preston playing. Him teaching me how to shave my head. Images of my parents laughing with each other, playing with us. Soon these images faded with reality, I passed out... Gentle voices and taps brought me back to the real world, bastards. I woke up changed, a different person. My house was full of EMS workers and police officers. They were all quite, seemingly shocked at the scene in front of them. They asked me a few questions, I answered them. I don't remember what they were, didn't seem to care much then either. Everything was different now. Was it worse? Hell no, it couldn't be worse. Was is better? Heh, how could something like that make anything better. It was just different, and so was I. I was unable to live in a dream world anymore. Unable to convince myself that everything was okay... It would never be okay again, and I accepted that finally. I often wonder why I do the things I do, why I am the way I am. It all seems so obvious when you think about it. But then again, I don't like to think about it... Would you?
