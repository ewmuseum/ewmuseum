Being Famous Is Hard Work

7 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-7-02 Our scene opens up with a shot of a taxi cab hurtling down the street at about sixty miles an hour. It cuts a rough turn to the right, slamming into a lightpost, but it keeps going in spite of this. A second later we can see the reason for the insane driving, as two police cars whip around the corner in pursuit of the fleeing cab. The chase continues down a long narrow road, as the cab weaves in and out of lanes in hopes of dissuading its pursuers. It’s no use though, as the police don’t seem as if they are going to give up very easily. The cab runs through a red light, slamming into an oncoming car’s front end, spinning it around. The taxi driver must not know he’s supposed to exchange license and registration after a collision like that, because he just turns to the left and keeps on going. The police swerve to avoid the impacted car and do likewise, following him. Traffic begins to get heavy on this road; so heavy that it’s now impossible to maintain any sort of high speed. Hearing the police behind him, the taxi driver realizes that he can’t afford to just sit and wait in a traffic jam. Something must be done...and fast. He slams on the gas, pulling off to the right up onto the sidewalk. He barrels down the side of the road, getting odd looks from the commuters on the road. The looks change to stares of horror as the cab strikes first one pedestrian, then another. Soon over a dozen people have been turned into roadkill in a horrific display. Pulling into another intersection, the taxi sees a roadblock of police cars straight ahead. It turns sharply right, only to find its path is cut off in that direction as well. It seems as if the jig is up, as there’s nowhere left to go. For some reason this notion goes over the head of the driven, as he guns on the gas once more, racing toward the roadblock. He collides full speed with the front car, the impact sending the cab flying up in the air. It peaks at about five feet in the air before crashing down on its side and rolling almost two rotations to end up sitting upside down. The engine catches on fire shortly after, and the driver of the cab crawls out of the door weekly. He takes a few futile steps away before the taxi explodes behind him, sending him to the ground in a bloody heap. The word ‘WASTED’ appears on the screen as the camera pulls out to reveal Trevor Hawke sitting on a chair with a Playstation 2 controller in his hand. Seeing his dead character on the screen, he slams the controller to the ground in frustration, as he face twists in an angry expression. “Damn Grand Theft Auto 3! I hate the fucking popo!” he shouts, glaring at the controller on the ground. He soon gets over his little fit, for it is only a few seconds before he sighs deeply and reaches down to pick up the controller. Pressing the O button, he brings his character back to life at the hospital. Soon he’s become entirely engrossed in the game once more. Minutes later he’s in the midst of shooting down police helicopters when his tag team partner, your favorite idiot and mine, Seth Masters walks into the room, carrying a small pedestal under his arm. He struts past the TV screen, then places the pedestal on the ground before climbing up on it. Seth is followed in by about eighteen people; a various assortment of men and women, some carrying microphones, and some carrying cameras. One of them unplugs the controller as they walk past, and as Trevor struggles to plug it back in, he’s once more wasted as an FBI car slams him into the next time zone. “Aw, you stupid fucks! What the hell are you doing in here?!” he shouts at the people, shaking his fist in anger. They seem not to even acknowledge his existence though, just surrounding the pedestal, chattering among themselves as if they’re waiting for something. It isn’t long before another man walks into the room following them, carrying a mic and boom. This man circles around the crowd of people, taking a spot next to Seth, who seems to be composing himself. After fiddling around with the boom, the man situates it so the mic is hanging above Seth’s head. He gives a thumbs up to the crowd while Seth proceeds to test the microphone. “Is this thing on? Hellooooooooo? Testing...1...2...3,” he states, before nodding in satisfaction. “Alright, I think we’re ready to go.” The crowd turns its attention back to Seth, as all the cameras focus in on his face. The people with microphones look on attentively as well as they wait for Seth to begin talking once more. Trevor on the other hand, still with the Playstation controller in hand, is watching this scene unfold before him with a look of disbelief painted across his face. Come to think of it, he’s usually sporting one of those looks whenever Seth is involved. Anyway, it seems as if Seth is about to speak. “Eh-eh-hem!” Seth utters, clearing his throat, before . “ Now I know you’ve all been clamoring to get a few words with Seth Masters ever since the last OSW Slam, and now you all finally have your chance. I’ll be speaking briefly, giving you all my State of Seth Masters Address, my first speech as the famous guy that I have become. Following that, I’ll be taking questions from the gallery. With that stated, I think this is a good place to begin.” Seth pauses here, and the crowd is absolutely silent. They don’t move a muscle, just waiting there anticipating his next word. Nodding his head, Seth continues. “Those of you who have been following my career closely will have noticed that I am in the midst of the greatest ascent to the top of our business in the history of the known universe. This may sound like the usual self-righteous rant you have heard from numerous wrestlers over the years who ended up amounting to jack-shit, but unlike those pieces of garbage I have the facts to back up my claims.” “Fact. In the first round of the Global Title Tournament I beat former NTICW World champion Xavier Kannon. No, not just beat him, but beat him rather effortlessly. Fact. I strutted through the second round of that tourney just as easily, destroying some BMW smuck in the process. Fact. I am a breath of fresh air in the polluted environment of professional wrestling today. Fact. I am currently undefeated in singles competition. Fact, and this is the big one. I was Red Scared through a table by Ivan Stanislav...THEE Ivan Stanislav on the last Slam. If that has not gotten me famous, I don’t know what will do it for me.” “So now that my fame has been solidified in all of your eyes, it appears that interest in me has peaked lately. I can’t seem to have anytime to myself with you damn paparazzi following me around constantly. It’s only been going on for three days now, and frankly, I’m already sick of it. I’ve set up this forum today for you to get all your questions out of your system, so I hope you utilize your time wisely. Well I’ve said my piece. You may ask away now.” “That was quite the egotistical speech,” shouts a man on the right. “Don’t you think the whole ‘full of yourself’ schtick is kind of played out these days? Where’s your creativity?” “Um...no,” Seth says, shaking his head. He ignores the man’s further pressing of the topic, turning to call on a woman to the right. “Next question.” “Are you ready for the ass-whooping you are sure to receive from Hessian this Saturday?” she asks, readying her pen for his response. “Um...no,” he answers, shaking his head once more. “Next question.” “Yeah, I’d just like to say that you and your tag team partner, Trevor Hawke, come off as really gay,” another nerdy looking fellow on the right asks. “I mean, I can understand the wrestling together, but living together? It just seems kind of superfluous. Superfluous meaning gay. So yeah, you guys look like the type of people that would suck each other off back in the locker room. Meaning you’re really gay. That’s all.” “That wasn’t even a question!” an angry Seth Masters shouts, getting only a shrug in response from the kid. “Do you care to comment on the rumors that you’re an item with Alanis Morrisette?” questions a woman near the back. “Ew, god no!” he shouts, disgusted. “What about Michelle Branch?” “Hmm...maybe,” he says, looking contemplative. After a few seconds he shakes his head once more. “I mean, no. Next question.” “Are you sure your time wouldn’t be better spent getting ready for the beating Hessian is going to give you this Sunday instead of giving this lame press conference?” a man from the left side asks with a puzzled look on his face. “Um...no,” Seth states, sounding irritated. “Wait, wait, wait. Hold on a second, here. Do any of you people have any questions I’d actually be comfortable with answering?” “Yeah, what’s up with that stupid hat?” asks a guy from the back, raising his hand. Seth glares at the man who dared to insult his choice in headgear accessory, then sweeps his glare to encompass the entire crowd. His face reddens as he points to the door in anger. “Alright, this press conference is over. Everybody out of my house!” he screams, as the various reporters begin to bicker among themselves. They try to shift the blame onto each other, but it is all useless since Seth manages to shuffle them all out of the front door with no trouble at all. Coming back inside, he grabs the boom that was holding the microphone, walks back to the door, and tosses it into the yard. Slamming the door shut behind him, Seth walks back into the room, where Trevor is still sitting with a shocked look on his face. “Man, Trev. You wouldn’t think it, but being famous is hard work,” Seth says, shaking his head exhaustedly as if he’d just undergone the most trying ordeal of his lifetime. And with that, Seth turns around and walks off into his room on the left. Shutting the door behind him, he leaves Trevor sitting alone once more. “But...you’re not famous!” Trevor shouts at the closed door. He is obviously still not buying all of Seth’s talk, despite the lame interview proving to be evidence to the contrary. He stares at the room, but gets no answer from his partner. He must not care that much about proving his point, because only a few seconds pass before he picks up the controller again and begins another run of anarchy in GTA 3. Ah, there’s nothing like beating a dead prostitute with a baseball bat while running a drug smuggling mission for the Yakuza. Good times. Good times.
