Time to step up

11 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/11/02 There comes a time in everyone’s professional life when they need to step up. No matter what the job is, at some point an employee needs to step up and take charge. Be it going from fry cook to cashier or from intern to executive, every employee must step up or risk being stuck at the bottom forever. In the wrestling business, there are also many forms of stepping up. A guy in the Academy steps up into the big time. A guy in the lower card steps up into the upper card. And a guy in the upper card has to step up his performance to hold back all contenders to his spot. But the stepping up Kid Wonder has to do Saturday night isn’t related to those. Well, it is in a way, but, at the same time, it’s not. While Kid Wonder must step it up to beat Rhett Locke, he’ll also be stepping up in another way. Kid Wonder is trying to step up to the second level of the mountain. Sunday, June 23rd, OSW holds their Ascension Pay-Per-View event. The main event will occur on a monstrosity of a creation called Mt. St. Hell’s End. The match will feature every OSW superstar brave enough to compete with the victor becoming the #1 contender to the OSW Heavyweight championship. The purpose of the match: Be the only man at the top of the mountain at the end of the 60-minute time limit. Now Kid Wonder would have no problem starting at the bottom of Mt. St. Hell’s End if the situation called for it. But with the chance of stepping up to the second level there for the taking, why not go for it? While being on level two means a longer way down, it also means you’re closer to the top. And in this enormous match on June 23rd, the top is where you want to end up. Beat Rhett Locke and Kid Wonder is one level closer to the pinnacle of OSW. Now don’t get it wrong. Kid Wonder isn’t selling Rhett Locke short by any means. Sure, Kid Wonder has likely accomplished more in his career than Rhett Locke, but just judging from OSW standards, Rhett Locke has been on the big end of the stick more often than the “Quintessential Teen Idol”. Only an idiot would be willing to deny that. But at the same time, Rhett has been in a war recently with the always-dangerous Graphic Violence. While that has happened, Kid Wonder has been relatively idle. That makes him much fresher than his Saturday counterpart. And in a match with as much meaning as this Saturday’s, a fresh Kid Wonder always means a dangerous Kid Wonder. And a dangerous Kid Wonder, combined with an unknowing mental state in regards to Graphic Violence and where he’ll be, are only gonna cause the budding superstar more problems than K-Dub will have to deal with. Saturday night, Kid Wonder has every intention of stepping up to the second level. He won’t care if he has to go through Rhett Locke, Graphic Violence, or anyone else who decides to come down that ramp to the ring. When an opportunity like this arises, you seize it. “I’ll be ready,” we hear from Kid Wonder’s voice. There is, however, no sight of the teen idol. “I start my climb Saturday. I continue it through Ascension. And I end it against Doug Troy. I’ve waited long enough. It’s my turn to stand on top. It’s my turn to be the man. It’s my turn to be…champion.” Fade away
