Date: 5/6/03 Tom Walczak is seen walking through a town in Suffolk County in New York. Normally he would never be in such a forsaken town, but his purpose there today is to get ready for his match against El Drago. You see if you go deep enough into Suffolk County then you will find that there are Mexicans on street corners looking to find work for the day. The point of Walczak’s visit is to find out as much as he can, not only about their culture, but also about their wrestlers. He is on foot, so that he can spot the dispersed groups easier. After about half an hour of walking he spots a group of about five Hispanic males. Walczak goes over to them, and he begins to speak to them. “Do any of you speak any English?” “Si, we speak some sir. Do you need us for any work?” “Yes, I am prepared to give you a hundred dollars each as long as you agree to answer some questions.” The men talk amongst themselves in Spanish with some words of broken English in between, and then after about five minutes of deliberating they come out with a decision. “Sir we will answer all that you want as long as it is done hear, no funny business, aye?” “What did you think I was going to do? Beat you with sticks?” “It has happened before.” “Ok, why the hell did you not stand up for yourselves?” “We did not know what to do, and we have been very cautious since.” “Maybe, you should go get some respectable jobs, like some of the other immigrants that have come to this country.” “We would, sir, but we find this just so much easier.” “OK, well, I was wondering do you have any knowledge of the Mexican Lucha-Libre?” “Si, Senor, we watch it whenever we have the chance. To see those men go through the air with such ease gives us such splendor.” “Would you be familiar with a wrestler by the name of El Drago?” “Indeed I would, he is known throughout Mexico for his grace in flying even though of his immense size.” “Did you say immense?” “Yes, although he is no where near your size sir, he had an advantage over all competitors. He outweighed them by over a hundred pounds at times.” “This is great, all this time I thought that he actually had some competition, but now I realize that he has faced nothing but a bunch of futile paper weights. Thank you for your help, and here is your money.” The men rejoice when given the money, and do a dance to show the jubilation. Walczak walks away from the scene smiling as the scene fades to black…
