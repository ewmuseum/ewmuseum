Devotion and Payback

29 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-29-02 Alexei Ruslan hurriedly walked through the hallways of the Kremlin, leading directly to the office of General Leonid Tomifei himself, the man that would get Ivan Stanislav out of the accursed jail that he found himself in in Winnipeg, Canada. Alexei walked up to a desk where a secretary sat. Basically, this poor woman handled all of the Chiefs of Staffs work and appointments, of which Tomifei was a part. A younger woman, she looked up and smiled as she saw Alexei, knowing full well who he was. “Alexei Gregorovich,” she said as she put down a pen, “what can I do for you?” “Hello Anna,” responded Alexei as he stopped and smiled at the attractive young girl, “I am here to see General Tomifei. I have an appointment for this time. Shall I go in?” Anna shook her head, “General sends his apologies, Mr. Ruslan, but he is quite busy at the moment. He said that when you came, however, to send you to see Lieutenant Karpov and discuss any matters that you may need handled.” She smiled up at Alexei, who worked a smile back at her, but beneath the smile was a hatred for Karpov. He nodded to her quickly, “Thank you my dear, I will go to his office.” She nodded, “I will tell him to expect you.” Alexei turned and walked out of the office and in the direction needed to get to the Lieutenants. Lieutenant Igor Karpov, a man of the same type as Alexei, but with a different set of values. Ruslan knew Karpov through his ties in Moscow, even from a young age when the two went to University together. But Karpov was unlike the Communists of the time…he was a revolutionary of another sort. A capitalist. As Ruslan walked through the hallway towards Karpov’s office, he thought of the few meetings the two had together. While they were warm on the outside, the mind games behind the exchanges were always heated. The two hated each other more than anything else. Karpov taking the way of military, Ruslan taking the way of intelligence. Karpov joining the Federation, Ruslan staying with the Communists. He knew that if Tomifei was sending him to Karpov, something wasn’t right. Ruslan walked up to the door to Karpov’s office and knocked loudly. “Enter.” Came a voice from within as Ruslan grabbed the knob and walked into the room, which was a spacious office to say the least. At the far end of the office sat Lieutenant Igor Karpov of the Russian Federation. For years, Karpov had wanted the Communist system of the Soviet Union to fall and the capitalist upswing of the West to envelope his homeland, and it had. Since then, he enjoyed seeing the communists squirm in their attempts to regain power. Most of all, he enjoyed seeing Ruslan himself squirm and meddle in the political affairs of The Russian Federation, only to find that Russia was decidedly capitalist, and would not change. He looked up at Ruslan, still wearing that brown jacket and hat, an old style of dress that dated the communist era, some thing never change. Karpov appeared to be rather young for a man of his rank and in reality, was actually a year younger than Ruslan, who could not be more than thirty five years old. Alexei walked into the room as Karpov stood, as per etiquette, and extended his hand to Ruslan, who shook it. “Mr. Ruslan, a pleasure to see you,” said Karpov, all smiles, “Anna told me that you were on your way!” “Yes,” Ruslan answered, “I spoke with her. She said that General Tomifei was indisposed at the moment and that you would handle any affairs that he would normally have to listen to from me.” Karpov nodded and sat back down at his chair, offering Alexei a chair facing him. “So, with that in mind, what is it that you need?” Karpov said as he folded his hands in front of himself, he knew exactly what Alexei was here for. Ruslan sat down and fixed his tie, “As I am sure you know, Praporschik Stanislav has landed in a bit of a jam, and requires Federation’s help in getting out of it.” “Ah yes,” answered Karpov, “you are referring to his imprisonment in Canada, I presume?” “Precisely.” Ruslan replied coldly. Karpov leaned back in his chair and looked up at the ceiling for a moment and then leveling his gaze at Ruslan, “You see, Alexei, the Chiefs of Staff have decided that perhaps it is best to….distance themselves from Ivan.” Alexei blinked, “What?” “You see,” Karpov continued, taking great joy in his slow response, “Praporschik Stanislav has always been somewhat of a loose cannon within the ranks. He never truly followed protocol to the fullest, and that cannot be tolerated, you know that.” Ruslan sat upright, angry, “Praporschik Stanislav was fighting for Russia before you were even considering the military, Igor! He has always fought for the people, for you, and for me! We should be embracing him!!” Igor sat in his chair and raised an eyebrow, “Alexei, I am sure you have seen the news as of late, eh?” Alexei nodded slowly. “Then I am sure you know that we are now a Junior Member of NATO.” Alexei grumbled to himself as Karpov continued, “Amazing, is it not, that we are now a part of the organization that was originally created to combat Soviet Aggression? Amazing that we are moving closer to our western neighbors…as opposed to moving away.” Alexei scoffed, trying to ignore what he was saying. To Ruslan, this was indeed painful to hear. “That being said, we do not believe that it is wise to cater to a communist of which Stanislav not only is, but also upholds loudly.” Karpov paused, Alexei opened his mouth but the Lieutenant interrupted him, “Tomifei himself said this.” “T..Tomifei?” questioned Ruslan as the color faded away from his face almost instantly…making him paler than he already was. “R..Russia is not going to help him?” Karpov smiled, “Nyet, Alexei. This is Ivan’s mess, he can clean it up.” Ruslan stared at the floor, shocked, appalled, the system was against Ivan in this respect. Karpov chose to dig the knife in more, “Ivan is old, Alexei. He is not much use to Russia now that we are making these moves towards capitalism. To a communist, Ivan is the greatest thing in world, but to us, he is nothing. He is loud and nothing more.” “You son of a bitch….” Muttered Ruslan. “Alexei, this is a place of professionalism, please, no need for harsh words.” Continued Karpov as Ruslan stared at him angrily, “It must hurt to be you, Ruslan. To find out that all you have worked for is wasted. Your entire life is nothing more than a futile effort to hold on to the past. While I have looked to the future, you have looked to the past and now, you have lost. The ruling stands, Stanislav must get out on his own power. His bail must be paid outside of Russia somehow, which means YOU cannot either, you understand?” Alexei glared back and stood up. “Yes, I understand. Let it be known that when Ivan Sergeiovich returns, he WILL find you.” “I will be here with a smile on my face, Ruslan.” Karpov stood as Alexei did. Ruslan turned, paler than ever. Was Ivan right? Was the conspiracy theory of Ivan Stanislav correct? He had to find a way to get Ivan out of jail….and he had to find a way out fast. The longer away from Russia, the more damaging it would become to himself…and his friends.
