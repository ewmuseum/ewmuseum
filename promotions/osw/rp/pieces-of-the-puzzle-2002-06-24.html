Pieces of the puzzle

24 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-24-02 The fall….the huge fall that Stanislav took from the top of Mount St. Hells End, was not one of the healthiest trips he had ever taken. Sometimes, weighing 400 pounds can be more of a burden than an advantage, and this was one of the times that this rang true. Twenty feet Stanislav fell. Twenty feet that landed him hard on his back. He doesn’t remember much after the fall, save for the fact that the great height that he was now was high above him. His head had hit the back of the second tier and had left a dent that only the skull of The Russian Bear could have created. Now, he rested. For once, The Russian Bear rested in a bed. Lying in a massive bed, actually in a nearby hospital for the time being, Stanislav was at ease. Or at least, at as much ease as was possible after, as he called it, being ‘robbed’ of his World Title shot. And it was at this time, while his head was bandaged and his body was numbed, that his mind began to think. While conscious, Ivan Stanislav suffers from what some may say a ‘poisoning’ of the mind. Outside pressures, outside beliefs, and outside people all worked to make up his mind, his decisions, his rationales. The Communist side of Ivan Stanislav, the side that made up the majority of his decisions and his beliefs, had the power while he was conscious. Stanislav did not think much about himself, or in the terms of his own self, but while he lay in that bed after such a taxing night of fighting, Stanislav’s mind was allowed to function without that burden. Ascension may have been the most taxing night of wrestling in his life. He may have had more brutal matches against Meanstreak, or more tiring matches against Jeremy Howard, but at Ascension, so much more was at stake. He was not fighting for just the State or the Country, but for himself. His pride, his conscience, his being was battling that night. He was emotionally charged, and that emotion was released at the end of the night. In his mind as he rested, the image of Tempest with her beautiful, porcelain face and happy sense of humor could be seen. While awake, he could eventually convince himself to block her from his mind. His communist mind had told him that an individual such as herself was not capable of changing his life. He felt that she had made a tremendous impact against him, but his mantra, his belief, his system told him otherwise. And yet, peacefully asleep, he thought of her without a second thought… Communism taught that the individual could not make a difference. Communism taught that the collective, the pooling of resources, the coming together of groups made the world change. Communism taught that individuals were not capable of making such a change. She is not capable of changing my life. She is not capable of changing my thoughts. She is an individual. The above thought ran through his head. It droned on and on, like a monotonous song that bore into his brain. But a tiny thought rang out in the background… Lenin was an individual. Stalin was an individual. Khrushchev was an individual. Did they change the world? Did they change your life? Did they make a difference? His face twisted and contorted as he slept. The individual of Ivan Stanislav wrestled with this thought. The communism droned on, the voice in the background grew louder as it gave way. No politicians changed his mind. No rationales outside of his own swayed him. The picture of Tempest in his mind grew brighter, it was something he enjoyed, someone he cared for, something he cared about. Individuals did change the world. Individuals did change lives. It was possible. It was possible. It was possible. Like the Berlin Wall crashing down, this great realization sent a shudder through Stanislav’s body. Tempest had played a pivotal role in his present and perhaps in his future. He continued to think, however, for his mind was not at rest. He continued to try to figure out the inconsistencies, the irregularities in his life. For one, after defeating Reno Davis, Stanislav was overjoyed, overwhelmed with happiness at his victory. He had done it. He had defeated Reno Davis, the man that had taken away Tempest. He had done what he set out to do and thus, his pain would be over. Yet, as he sat backstage after the match, as his adrenaline had slowly calmed and his nerves had eased, the pain returned. Something else was bothering him. What could it have been? He had gotten rid of the cause of Tempests’ departure, had he not? Reno Davis was the reason she left. The voice said in his mind. But it also came in various other forms too…. Reno Davis was the reason she left. Meanstreak was the reason it happened. Tempest would not listen, and it is her fault. The fans caused her to leave. And then the injustice done to him by El Drago. That stupid Mexican that had dared to get in his way. He had cheated. The timekeeper was paid off. It was a conspiracy. He had help. Yet once again, there was more to it. The people around him, the literature, the media, they weren’t here to skew his mind. They weren’t here to make him throw up barriers. No, it was just Ivan Stanislav. If there was one person he could be truly and completely honest with, it would have to be himself. His wardrobe had cost him the match. As absurd as it sounded, his suspenders, in a way, had caused him to fall from that mountain. He knew why he wore his suspenders… A belt constricts you. It chokes you. It uses itself to squeeze you and make you stable. But suspenders, they rest upon your shoulders. It is your own shoulders that leave you stable. You are responsible. Drago had pulled his suspenders. Drago had pulled him down. He had grabbed the bulwark of Ivan’s mantra. He had used Ivan suspenders. Ivan’s suspenders pulled him down…. Drago pulled my suspenders and I fell. He grabbed my suspenders and dragged me down. My wardrobe caused me to fall. My suspenders caused me to tumble. I caused myself to fall. I am why I fell. A backlash of ideological jargon shot through his mind, free from the Russian Military, free from the Russian Federation. Free from the pressures….. I control my destiny. Because of myself, I fell from the mountain. I caused myself to fall. I defeated Reno, I should be happy. I defeated Reno, I should feel happy. Reno was the problem. Drago was the problem. My suspenders were the problem… The words blew through his mind as he raced to make sense of them. His face twisted, he sweated, his breathing quickened…. I was the reason I fell. Meanstreak was the reason I left. I thought Streak would beat up Troy. I thought Tempest would understand. I thought no one would find out. I thought he would end the problem. I am the reason I won. I am the reason I lost….. I am the reason she left. Somehow, someway, the purely physical occurrence on top of Mount St. Hells End caused a repercussion within Stanislav’s mind. He had somehow come up with a solution for his pain. Reno Davis was not the reason for his pain. It was there all along. It was right under his nose. It was him! Ivan Stanislav was the reason. He was responsible. The change….. …would have to come from within. With a startled gasp, Stanislav awoke from his slumber and sat straight up. Michel Krieg told him that the ‘times were changing.’ Major things were going on and Stanislav was unaware. The dissention within The Red Army, the taking of money from Storm, it all began to make sense! A nurse ran into the room quickly. Stanislav pointed at her, redoubled by the last thought. “Get me Alexei Ruslan on telephone!!” he rasped. “Praporschik Stanislav…” she answered. “Get me him now!! IT IS IMPORTANT!!” She nodded and gave him a phone. He dialed the number…. “Alexei, we must talk. Something has got to change….”
