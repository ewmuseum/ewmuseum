Forsaken

26 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 26th, 2002 Raymond Burris typed furiously away at his keyboard, trying to finish up his latest report on his personal project: Mr. Thomas Lexian. Or Legend Inc., as he was better known as. Burris never knew the real reason why Lexian was so wildly popular. Was it his good looks? Perhaps. But he wasn't movie star good looking. Definitely not his charisma, because the man was as about as warm as a snow cone. He was witty and funny, but still a private man. He didn't endear himself to many. So what was it? The higher ups in New Wave wanted a progress report on how Lexian was doing. They wanted to know why he was arrested one evening for disturbing the peace, and how he allowed it to get into the newspapers. I can't control everything the man does, Burris mumbled to himself, as he backspaced a million times after misspelling the word, 'Lexian,' yet again. But the higher ups would have one answer for that: tap him. And Burris knew that the risks ran high on such a bold move. They had tapped their last star, and it nearly put the company out. But the controversey was quickly masked and that man is no longer employed. Knock Knock. Burris closed his document and cleared his throat. 'Come in.' Lexian stepped into the office, wearing a Nike t-shirt and a simple pair of jeans. The look on his face told Burris that he had a lot on his mind. That was good. It would be easier for Burris to get his point across now. 'Lexian, I've been expecting you,' Burris said, motioning to the seat in front of him. 'Take a seat.' 'Let's make this quick,' Lexian replied, seeming uneasy. 'I need to get down to the gym.' Burris laughed. 'I saw the card for this week. I don't think you need to train to hard to beat Disorderly Conduct.' Lexian gave a half hearted attempt to see the humor in the comment. 'I don't take teams lightly. This is my job, remember?' The tone changed. 'I remember, alright. Maybe you DON'T remember the fact that you lost to NC-17 a few weeks back. Or did that also slip your mind?' 'We got our revenge.' 'Psssh.' Lexian shifted in his chair uneasily. 'Listen, you control my public relations, but leave the wrestling to me. Just like any other sport, there are some powers beyond my control...' Burris stuck a finger in Lexian's face. 'I'm a business man, Lexian. I thrive on having complete control. Now if you're telling me that you can't handle your business in the ring, you need to do so now, because I'm not here to represent a mid carder...' 'What did you say?' Lexian questioned in shock. 'You heard me.' Lexian stood up in anger. 'I don't have to take this shit. I'm a former champion in more promotions than you can count on those fat hands of yours, and you're going to sit there and insult me?' 'You told me you were the best.' Lexian answered simply, 'I am. The Extreme Legends are the best team in professional wrestling today.' 'Then prove it.' His eyes saw the challenge and answered it. He knew Burris wanted him to prove that he was worth all of the money he had been promised, and Lexian was not a man to take without giving back. 'I'll prove it by destroying Disorderly Conduct at Slam! Then I'll see what you have to say.' 'Then do it, because I don't have time to waste...' Burris turned back to his computer, not paying attention to Lexian. Lexian could only watch on, turning back and heading out the door. But before he could leave, Burris added, 'Oh, and Lexian?' He stops momentarily. 'Yeah?' 'Try to stay out of the county jails. I'm tired of bailing your ass out. Once more and you'll be spending thirty days in the slammer and I'm not lifting a finger.' A few moments later Burris heard the door slam and finished the rest of his report: 'He's prideful. He'll do what he can to assure that he is the best, and that will be where we will have him. The lost to NC-17 was a momentary obstacle, but I have the utmost confidence that his potential is still limitless. He should not be tapped until he proves himself more of a nuisance. End report.' Burris took a deep breath and hoped his recommendations didn't fall onto deaf ears as he clicked the send button.
