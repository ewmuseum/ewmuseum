Wounded

25 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-25-02 Sitting in his locker room minutes after Ascension went off the air, Trevor Hawke slouches on a bench running along the wall, a tattered white towel draped over his head. His back still glistens from the buckets of perspiration sweated earlier in the night. The sheen goes well with the large patches of dried and not so dried blood covering his body. The OSW medical staff had done their best at closing up the major gashes with stitches, but there were a few minor ones that had not warranted them. Grasping his head with both hands, a pained expression comes over Trevor’s face. A fitting expression, really, as it has been a rather painful night on all fronts. All in all, the night of Sunday, June 23rd may have been the most physically demanding of Trevor Hawke’s career. Over the course of the night, he logged nearly two hours of match time. Two hours of hardcore brawling all over the American West Arena. It began with the match for the tag team titles. A Weapon of Choice match takes quite a lot out of a person even under the most positive of circumstances. Tonight, however, circumstances were far from positive. After around twenty minutes of fast-paced, heavy-hitting action, Seth Masters was the first man in the match eliminated after missing a high impact maneuver, leaving Trevor all by himself to face the other five men in the contest. For over twenty minutes he fought alone, no one to watch his back, more often than not on the recieving end of a double-team beatdown. He was pulverized, his flesh was shredded, he was busted open, but he refused to give up. Eventually his perserverance awarded him with the uneviable task of being left to face a two on one assault care of Da Westsidaz. Despite the odds, he weathered the beating and managed to at least take out Short Circuit before finally being pinned by Riptah after forty three minutes of hellacious action. Following this, Trevor recieved a break of about twenty five minutes as the World Title match took place, before he was shoved back into action for another straight hour in the Ascension match-up itself. This match was not a joyride by any means, and along with his partner Seth, he gave and recieved tremendous beatings during the course of the sixty minutes. The blow which eventually knocked him out for the night was a brutal powerbomb from Hessian on the second tier which sended him cascading some twenty-odd feet down the ramp. Any schmuck can see that Trevor’s body has been torn to shreds. For his getting shit on by the fans as of late, Trevor Hawke sure the hell busted his ass in the ring tonight. Almost two hours of hard work and nothing to show for it but a series of cuts and bruises running down the course of his body. Nothing to show for it, but his pain. And the fact that there’s nothing to show must only contribute to that same pain. Because the thing about Trevor Hawke is, he doesn’t like to lose. That might seem like a stupid thing to state. Of course he doesn’t like to lose. NOBODY likes to lose. But for a man who runs off of ego such as Trevor, losing takes on a whole new light. To be reminded of one’s own mortality and fallibility is enough to deal a striking blow against any egomaniac’s psyche. When you get to the level Trevor competes at on a nightly basis, it gets to be that your life begins to revolve around competition. Losses are cause of untold amounts of mental anguish. After giving everything he had out there and still coming up on the short end of things, it’s no wonder Trevor’s less than happy with himself. Especially important losses in front of pay-per-view audiences. Especially important losses that strip you of what hopes you had of finally achieving perfection. But Trevor will get over it. No matter how much pain might be contained in his expression, Trevor will get over it. It’s not as if he has a choice one way or another. He has to get back on track once more and make another drive toward the limelight, or fear losing who he is. And he will. Like his physical wounds, these mental wounds will heal, and his pride will swell once more. This blow to his ego will cauterize itself, and in the end he will come out stronger than he was when he began. Such is the evolution of Trevor Hawke as a wrestler...as a man. But to label his pain as two-fold, as physical and mental, seems to be leaving something out of the equation. There’s definitely a third element involved here. An element with which Trevor Hawke has had little experience with as of late. For tonight his trauma isn’t just physical and mental. It is also emotional. For he has to deal with the knowledge that his friend and stable mate, Amy Campbell, was nearly raped by that sadistic son-of-a-bitch Corg in the Ascension match, and it would have never happened if he hadn’t abandoned her. Just the thought of what Corg did to Aimz...his ally...his friend...just knowing that part of the blame lies on his shoulders...knowing he could have prevented it and didn’t...it all just...just... “FUCK!” Trevor screams, tearing the towel off of his head and whipping it hard across the locker room floor in a fit of anger. The door to the locker room swings open, allowing Seth Masters, the second half of Civil Disobedience, entrance into the locker room. He takes half a step inward before watching the thrown towel skid to a rest at his feet. Bending down, he picks it up, before bringing his gaze to bear on Trevor. Seth doesn’t look very happy at all. “What the hell is this?” Seth asks in angered disbelief, holding the towel in front of him for Trevor to see. Masters takes a few steps toward his partner. “Don’t tell me you’re throwing a fucking tantrum.” “Shut the fuck up, Seth,” Trevor mutters, glaring up at him from his seat on the bench. “Tonight is not the night for you to give me shit about anything.” “You’re such a fucking baby, ya know? Things don’t go your way and you throw a big hissy fit. Grow the fuck up!” Seth spits, tossing the towel back at Trevor in disgust. The towel strikes Trevor in the chest, then falls to the ground. In an instant, Trevor is up, grabbing Seth by the collar of his shirt and slamming him up against the nearest wall. With his eyes open wide, sweat streaming down his bruised face, and a seemingly endless stream of anger manifesting itself in his expression, Trevor almost doesn’t even resemble himself. He brings his face within inches of Seth’s, screaming at his tag team partner. “Grow the fuck up!?” he screams, spittle landing on Seth’s nose. “If anyone needs to grow the fuck up it’s you! You go out there in an important fucking match like that and take stupid pointless risks!? What the FUCK were you thinking!? You were the first man eliminated in that match! I had to face five men BY MYSELF!!!” As Trevor glares into Seth’s eyes, Masters’ initial expression of fear slowly fades into one of contempt. He pushes Hawke away from him, before putting some distance between himself and his partner. “Don’t try to push shit on me, man!” Seth yells back. “I was trying to win the fuckin’ match. And it’s not like you were so fucking great out there, either. You could have won that match by pinning Riptah countless times, but I didn’t see you pulling out some gut effort. It’s as much your fault as mine.” “But the fact that I had to beat both of the fucking Westsidaz to win the match was totally your fault!” Trevor screams. “Some fucking Elite you are, Seth. You can’t even stay in the fucking match long enough to make an impact!” “Get down off your fucking high horse, Trev,” Seth states bitterly, pointing his finger at his partner in anger. “Don’t try to pretend like you’ve never made a mistake, because I know for a fact you’ve made plenty.” Trevor looks as if he’s about to shout something back, but upon hearing this last comment, a vacant expression comes over his face as he retakes his seat on the bench. “Like leaving Amy behind?” he asks in a bitter mutter, looking up at Seth. Seth nods in vindication.“Yeah, like leaving Amy be...” he starts, before realizing what Trevor just said. The anger suddenly drains out of his face as well, replaced by a downturned expression similar to Trevor’s. “Shit...” “Look, Seth...” Trevor says, his voice taking an apologetic tone. “I could give a fuck less about the tag match. I mean...I do care that we lost, but it’s not that big of a deal. We’ll get back in the running for those belts eventually. It’s just that...” Trevor pauses, not quite sure what to say. Seth finishes for him. “We fucking blew it, man.” “Yeah, I know,” Trevor says, staring at the ground sadly. Suddenly his voice fills with anger once more, his eyes filled with vengeance as he jerks his head back up to look at Seth. “No! How the fuck were we supposed to know what was going to happen? We were just trying to win the match, damn it! How were we supposed to know Corg would...would...” “There was no way we could have known,” Seth answers. “But that still doesn’t make things right.” “God!” Trevor screams, driving his fist into the wall in another fit of anger. “Everything is so fucked up and we’re here, sitting in this fucking locker room arguing about pointless bullshit!” “It is most definitely not the time to be placing blame,” Seth agrees. “But getting all pissed off about shit isn’t going to help much either.” “Yeah...” Trevor mutters, staring at the ground once more. “I can’t have this on my conscious, Seth. We’re going to have to make things right, you know.” “So much for being the bad guys,” Seth says, managing a weak laugh. “But you see...we are the bad guys,” Trevor answers back. “That’s the whole problem here. We...fucking left her behind!” “Quit dwelling on it, damn it. If we’re going to fix things, then let’s fix them,” Seth says, pulling Trevor up off the bench and pushing him toward the door. “It’s been a shitty night. Let’s just leave it all behind us and move on to a better future.” “It’s been worse than shitty...” Trevor mutters as Seth leads him out the locker room doorway. “I don’t even think there’s an adjective bad enough to describe this night.” Seth nods, turning off the light, then closes the door behind them. We’re left in blackness, hearing footsteps echo off down the hallway. All wounds heal, even the unseen ones. It’s just a matter of time.
