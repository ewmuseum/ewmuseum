Comfort

19 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-19-02 a long day for Praporschik Stanislav. Today, the soldiers of the Arkhangelsk Base in Russia decided to do everything wrong, and nothing right. Starting from four o’clock in the morning when Stanislav woke up bright and early to lead the revelry, the men decided that they’d sing off key, and slouch slightly… He was quick to fix that. But the problems were only to begin. A freak accident while two men were working on a jeep resulted in one getting his chest crushed, and the other breaking a hand. A fight ensued shortly afterwards in the mess hall in which of course, Ivan was there to break up, and then had to fill out the paperwork for the resulting injuries that had occurred during the scuffle. Those few incidents, along with the need to verbally abuse a bunch of ruffians and then lead three drills for a sergeant who had been discharged earlier that day had given Praporschik Stanislav more than enough to do for one day. As Ivan walked into his dimly lit cabin, his own personal living quarters on the Arkhangelsk base, he felt tired, which is a rarity. However, Stanislav was not physically tired. It would take an insurmountable amount of physical exertion to sap his seemingly limitless strength, but mentally, Ivan Stanislav was ready to lay down in a warm bed and relax for once. He found himself wanting to relax slightly more than usual nowadays. But in that respect, it could be understood why Ivan Stanislav would be a bit stressed mentally. He had been through perhaps the most taxing mental experience of his life during the last couple of weeks and they seemed to only continue to grow. Stress was something that Ivan Stanislav rarely allowed to affect him, but it was taking its toll on The Russian Bear, he could not deny that. The front door to his cabin opened as light from the outside lamp streamed into Ivan Stanislav’s cabin that rested on the outskirts of the Arkhangelsk base, far away enough that he could rest in peace when need be. The massive form of Ivan Stanislav eclipsed the outside light as he walked into the place that he called home, a loud groan exiting his huge form as he shut the door and left us in darkness for a moment. About four heavy footsteps later, a lamp across the room was lit via a match and light flickered throughout the room, as well as against the angular features of Praporschik Stanislav, dressed in his military uniform. Stanislav rubbed the side of his head with his right hand and walked deeper into a corner of the room. He lit a match and tossed it into what appeared to be a fireplace, which seemed to instantly ignite and send light flickering all around the area, showing us for the first time the living quarters of Ivan Stanislav. Stanislav stood in his dining room portion of his cabin, with a table in the center of the hard wood floor, a fireplace behind him with a mantel above it. Above the mantel hung a large Soviet and Russian Federation flag, while atop the mantel were several pictures. Some of them, black and white pictures, were of Stalin and Lenin, and a few others, some in color but most in black and white, were of Ivan Stanislav himself. Neighboring the dining room was a sort of study, complete with large armchair, massive desk, and an enormous bookshelf which housed a tremendous amount of books. The fire crackled behind Stanislav as he pulled off his hat and set it upon the mantel and sighed again. He scratched his scraggly beard as he walked into the study and turned on an old radio. A Russian on the radio jabbered on quickly, prompting Stanislav to nod in recognition of some of his words and exit the room via a hallway. The room itself is enormous, and to a person of ‘regular’ size, we would find ourselves stuck in the house of a giant. The chairs, tables, and doorways were all built to accommodate the enormous size of Ivan Stanislav. In relationship to the furniture, various ‘unfixable’ objects, such as the radio, plants, and pictures, look remarkably small and out of place. A short while later, Stanislav walked back into the study. Instead of his black shirt and suspenders he had discarded them and for another brief moment, we see the hulking body of The Russian Bear without a shirt on, including the massive scar on his back and the tattoo of a hammer and sickle over his heart. Stanislav walked over to the fireplace, stretched a hand out, and heated it for a moment before turning back to the study. He walked to his large chair and eased himself into it, letting out a sigh as he did. He brought his massive feet up and set them on a nearby footstool and reached to a side table and brought a book up to read. He sighed deeply. He was indeed tired. His home was not one that you would call ‘warm’ by any stretch of the word. Very drab, it served its purpose, and little was present within the rooms that showed any sort of ‘character’, save for the few pictures upon the mantel. He was tired…it was after midnight and in four hours, Ivan would have to get up again just to sing the national anthem of the Russian Federation. He had gotten Davis though..it felt good to destroy that heap of junk car of his and then manhandle him on Slam. But he wanted more. He wanted to do more than just hurt him..he wanted to do something that would do more than physical pain… ..he wanted hurt his soul, just as Davis had to him. Ivan closed the book and set it back down. He leaned back in his chair and closed his eyes. He was tired. Far more tired than he had originally thought. A low sigh exited his massive chest as he scratched at his scraggly beard. The fire crackled in the background. The massive Russian soldier began to slowly drift to sleep after an arduous day of work. As his breathing slowed he drifted deeper to sleep…. …and then there was a rumble outside. His eye opened slowly as another rumble, the sound of thunder high above in the clouds, echoed through the atmosphere. A voice, deep within his memory, reverberated through his head… I hate storms. 'It's just something overwhelming, something I can't stop or control or even effect. I guess I feel like I'll just be swept away by it. It was her voice. The voice of Tempest of whom he had not heard for such a long time. A voice that was soothing and kind, and happy and….. Ivan growled as the thunder knocked the memories from his mind. A pain was felt as he laid there for he had sworn to protect her, and now, more than ever, she would need him now…..but he wasn’t there to protect her, she was somewhere else. He slowly got out of his chair as the thunder rolled again and sleet began to hit against his window. There was going to be another storm. He walked over to the window and reached up and grabbed a black shirt, hat, and a heavy military jacket. He peered out the window as the sleet pelted the window more. He walked over to his door and opened it only to get blasted by a gust of wind and sleet as he walked out into the cold. He grabbed his hat as the storm whipped around him and his jacket quickly became drenched. His hat blew off of his head only seconds after exiting, but he made no attempt to retrieve it. Rather, he walked deeper into the storm. He had failed her and he had left her, but he still had an obligation. While he tried to forget, the thought of her being afraid chilled him to the bone deeper than any other Russian snow ever could, and because of that, every time there was a storm, Stanislav did the same thing. Lightning shot across the sky, lighting up the area around him as his face turned red and he stared up at the sky. He whispered to himself… “You are safe….Tempest…do not be afraid.” The wind howled as the weary, tired Ivan Stanislav stood in the middle of the storm and stared up at the sky. He wished she was not afraid, but he knew she probably was. He remembered how she found safety when she was near him, and was even capable of sleeping whenever he was with her…now he was gone and she had to handle it alone. His voice grew in intensity, “You are safe Tempest! Do not be afraid!!” BOOM! The thunder echoed around the area in defiance of the comforting words that Stanislav yelled, “YOU ARE SAFE TEMPEST!! DO NOT BE AFRAID!!” He roared loudly into the storm, hoping that his words would reach her by some far off chance. This had been the third storm this week. And try as he might, Stanislav could not sleep whenever the thunder rolled in. Some soldiers peered out of their windows as they saw their Praporschik screaming to the sky, others had grown used to it and tried to ignore it. However, within the mind of Ivan Stanislav, as he screamed for literally hours on end, another feeling, other than pain and regret was building within his body: Hatred. Reno Davis was the cause of this. Reno Davis was the person that had caused him all of this pain. And because of that, Reno Davis will pay for his crimes against Stanislav and Tempest. Four hours into the future, Ivan Stanislav would still be standing in the middle of the storm, screaming to the sky in an attempt to combat the storm and vent some of his grief…. ….it was going to be a long week…with a big payoff on Slam.
