Walczak's Good Deed (Part 1)

26 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/26/02 Walczak is seen walking down the street, but he does not seem to be his happy go lucky self that he was just a while ago. No, the smile once visible has diminished, and his usual straight face has returned. What could be the cause of this? Could it be that he has gotten some bad news? Maybe it is just that he got tired of smiling, because we all saw how much effort that took. He is clothed in a pair of black sweatpants and a tight white wifebeater. He just walks down the street and pays attention to no one, he occasionally bumps into a person, but does not look back, and no one has the gonads to stop him and make him apologize. The Walczak stops as if he heard something, but he is not sure about it. He clears his ears out and continues to walk, but the murmurs do not subside, and he turns around and sees two old ladies talking between themselves and occasionally pointing their fingers towards Walczak’s direction. Walczak walks over to the ladies, and whence he arrives they just look up at him and smile. “Good evening ladies, I noticed that you were gazing over in my general direction and I was wondering if you needed help with anything.” “Oh, no son, we were just admiring you for your valiant efforts this past Saturday. You see we were watching the wrestling program with our grandsons and we noticed that you saved that poor boy Timo from certain injury. We were amazed that you rushed in there risking your own health at the risk of another. Until that moment we thought that wrestlers were just a bunch of compassionless buffoons, but you obviously proved our assumptions wrong.” “Well, I thank you for the compliment. It truly means a lot to me. However, I must be on my way now, I don’t want to keep you ladies from your daily duties.” Walczak says good buy to the ladies and walks away. He goes to sit down on a street bench at the corner of the street. “Damn! Now all the people that saw me on SLAM! are going to think that I am nothing more than a pussy that helps people. What is this world coming to when a supposedly vicious man such as myself is depicted as a pussy?” Just then Walczak hears something in the distance. A lady comes running down the street hysterically crying. “Help! Help! Help! Help! Help! Help! Please Help! My son, he is stuck in a tree, and the branch is about to break, can someone please help me.” She sees Walczak and immediately runs over to him. “Sir, I saw you help Mr. Bolamba on Saturday, and I would really appreciate if you would come and save my boy. He is crying and I don’t know of anyone else that is strong enough to catch him.” “Just leave me alone lady it was one good deed.” “Please, I am begging you?” Walczak builds up some sympathy, and goes with the lady. As he nears the tree the scene fades out. What will happen? Will he save the boy? Will the boy fall? No one knows, but soon all will be known.
