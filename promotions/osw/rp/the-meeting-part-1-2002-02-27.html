The Meeting (Part 1)

27 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-27-02 The scene opens up about an hour from where we last left off in the hanger. Nothing much is known now. The smell of fish, it covers the air and that of salt water. A nasty combination when mixed, but we obviously were caught in the middle of what has come to be. The sound of rolling tires along wood planks is heard in the background as a black pick-up truck pulls along past us. It turns into the edge of the dock and stops. Still running and finally being turned off. A brief silence is heard and no commotion from anywhere. When the door cracks open and the fully pops open. A man flips his cigarette away, into the water, and pulls his trench coat around himself. He pulls down his hat, so that we are not able to see his face. He makes his way by as, so mysterious and quick. He walks over to a building to our back, when he comes to the door; he turns and looks around in all directions. After seeing that no one is watching, he opens the door and walks inside. Inside of the building, are huge boxes. Pilled and stacked on top of each other, one on top of the other. Boxes of all shapes and sizes, but this man seems to be able to find his way through the maze of boxes. Making a left, a right, straight ahead, and then suddenly a rapid move to the left. On he moves so quickly through this plethora of boxes. Slowing down tremendously, he comes into contact with an area that is almost barren, with a single solitaire table sitting in the middle of all of those boxes. The man looks around and we catch a glimpse of his face, but not enough. He slowly eyes the table. On it rests a couple cans of beer, cigarettes, playing cards, and chips (no potato chips either, poker chips). The man removes his hat and tosses it onto the table. Now revealing himself to be none other than “Bad Attitude” Jesse Ramey. Jesse eyes his surroundings very heavily, and then finally takes off his jacket and tosses it onto the table as well. He is wearing a black wife beater and a part of baggy black jeans that cover his boots, but don’t sag off of them. He jolts as the sound of something coming shifts through the air. In a hast, Jesse takes and jumps up onto several boxes and hunkers down at the top of the pile. Four men walk into the area, where Jesse was last. They look around and notice the coat and hat. Suddenly they begin to jolt around and look in a frenzy. When all of a sudden, Jesse stands up and leaps down onto one of the men. Jesse knocks the man to the ground. Jesse grabs the man and pulls him to his feet. As the other three men begin to come closer Jesse reaches into his pants and pulls out a gun. He places it to the mans head and the others begin to back up. Jesse slowly begins to back up, but out of nowhere, then man brings up his left leg and smashes it against Jesse’s crouch. Jesse drops the gun and the man flips Jesse over his shoulders in a single move. The man hovers over the, now holding his balls, Ramey. “Tisk, tisk, tisk. You never learn do you, Ramey? How many times do we have to teach you how these things work?” The man reaches down, extending his hand to help Jesse up. A smile comes across Jesse’s face as he reaches up and grabs the other man’s hand. “Yeah, I know, I know. Just tell me one thing. Why’d ya have to go and knock me in the nuts like that?” “I dunno, just thought it might make you think a little more before you act?” He pulls Jesse up. “Nice reasoning. I guess. Man that hurt though.” Jesse adjusts himself and then walks over to the table. “So we going to play, or what?” “Hell yeah!” All four other men say concurrently. As all of the men sit down, everything cuts into a black screen and the words To Be Continued pops up.
