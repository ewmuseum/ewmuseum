Thoughts of a beaten winner

19 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-19-2002 “50 pounds of gold and silver.” That’s what Pgod called it, back in the day. “50 pounds of gold and silver.” The World Title. Didn’t really matter which world title he was referring to. Different promotion, different “50 pounds of gold and silver”. It always puzzled Doug to hear someone talk about world titles in that context. Until November of last year, it always seemed like a nod at the holder. “Hey,” people would say. “This guy’s the World Champion. He must be pretty good.” Until he won the PCW World Title in November of 2000, Doug had always thought of it like that. An indicator of being “pretty good” or “lucky”. Granted, luck was what had got him his first World Title. If it wasn’t for the yellow Humvee taking out both Meanstreak and Ryan Knox, Doug never would’ve been World Champion. Even while he had the World Title then, Doug had felt uneasy. He basically had the title handed to him on a silver platter, and that didn’t sit quite right him. During his short title reign, he had the nagging feeling that something wasn’t right, and it was made immensely clear to him when Preacher, a notorious Satanic Jobber, defeated him for the World Title. After his loss to Preacher, Doug was back on ground zero. No #1 contendership shots, no title shots, no nothing. Fast forward to a couple months later, at PCW’s first Shanghaied. Doug had come from out of nowhere to become then PCW Champion Reverend Jon Hull’s greatest threat. During the match, Doug built and held momentum against Hull, evening putting him down twice with The Take. After the second Take, Doug went to make the cover… But history had other plans, as did the head office of PCW. Or rather, the NEW head office, consisting of Tyler Nelson and his Media cronies. The pin was broken up, the match thrown out and Doug’s big chance it earning the title the right way was squashed. From then until the end of PCW, Doug became lazy, almost despondent. His one chance to earn what he wanted, the redeem his earlier failures, ruined by some front office assholes. Doug quit the PCW and that was going to be the end of the story. Except it wasn’t. Just three months later, Doug signed on with newcomer fed OSW as an announcer, which lasted for one whole show. Doug found himself wrestling actively again, but still with the same despondency that he had at the end of his PCW tenure. It wasn’t until he lost his #1 contendership match against Ivan Stanislav that he started to realize what he was doing to himself. He wasn’t applying himself like he should be, even with a World Title sitting there, being held by a man who earned it much like Doug earned his first World Title. Time to right wrongs. Doug, seeing his chance, stepped up. He made himself more visible, speaking out against the same management that awarded Toomes the OSW World Title, acting like it was a status symbol that they gave out to friends. Like it was 50 pounds of gold and silver, a commodity to trade. Doug looked at Toomes and saw himself, just a year and a half ago. Same ill-gotten title, same smug reaction, same superiority complex. So Doug applied himself, talking trash to management while winning high-caliber matches. People took notice because Doug gave them no chance to avoid him. Fans cheered louder and louder for Doug as Doug won the #1 contendership. Finally, Doug got his shot. The fans cheered. The punches flew. Toomes had the extinguisher. Hawk swooped in. Doug took the chance when it was offered. 1 2 3 And now, sitting in his hospital bed, Doug stares at the OSW World Heavyweight Title. Toomes’ nameplate is still there, soon to be replaced by Doug’s name. The gold, which had been recently polished, glinted back at Doug while he pondered on the situation. Right now, he had a concussion, several bruised ribs and a large hole in his back. Battle wounds that would take a while to heal, and may in fact stay with him his entire life. But was it all worth it? The title no longer was 50 pounds of gold and diamonds. Not to Doug. Right now, it seems like everything he’s fought for for the past 4 years. Journeying from shitty indy federation to shitty indy federation, joining up with DAMN, who would later he both his greatest assets and greatest hindrances, making his way to the MWF, his wars with Porter for the MWF Title that always eluded him, his move to PCW and his assisted title win, his subsequent loss to Preacher, his failed attempts at regaining the title… Was it all worth it? Right now, looking at the title, draping over a chair sitting across the room from him, he thought about his battles against management. He thought about his successes, his failures. He thought about each and every federation he joined on his way to this moment. Everything in his whole career, it seemed, was building up to this moment. Was it all worth it? Sure, there would be opposition. There always is. But right now, Doug was at his apex, the plateau of his personal mountain. Some would try to knock him off, but he’s learned from his mistakes. Walczak, Stanislav, Toomes, Hawk, Spinks, any wrestler on the OSW roster right now wants what Doug Troy has. Some of them think of it as the summation of their life’s work, others think of it as the 50 pounds of gold and diamonds. Was it all worth it? Bring them on. Doug learns from his mistakes, and he doesn’t care to repeat them. His life’s work is sitting on a chair in a small hospital room, and woe to the poor bastard who tries to take it away. Was it all worth it? You bet your ass it was. The blood, the pain, everything that lead up to the gaining of the belt was instantly vindicated when Doug’s hand was raised in victory. Some would say nothing is worth that much pain and suffering. Some would say that it isn’t all about the belt. Some would even say that the belt means nothing. Those people are liars. The belt means everything to those who realize what it truly represents. Those who don’t realize it don’t deserve it. With these thoughts, Doug drifts off to sleep, aided by the lulling beep of the heart monitor.
