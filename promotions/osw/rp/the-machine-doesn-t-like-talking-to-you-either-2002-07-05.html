The machine doesn't like talking to you either

5 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-5-2002 The scene opens to a room shrouded in darkness. The only light is provided by a window letting in soft moonlight. The fuzzy quality of the video and the date/time display in the corner show reveal to those watching that this is the feed from a security camera. Whoever’s watching the feed right now isn’t immediately apparent, but the viewers aren’t given enough time to piece it together. The door to the room opens as Doug Troy walks in. His appearance is different than normal. He looks disheveled, his hair in a mess and six day’s growth of beard on his face. His walk is unsteady, nearly drunken as he enters the room. His dull brown jacket flaps as he shuts the door behind him. A cigarette dangles from his lips, dropping the large collection of ash at the tip due to Doug’s neglect to shake the ash off. He drops his keys on the floor with a *clink*, not caring where they end up. He looks around his room and notices something that catches his attention: his answering machine. He regards it like it’s an alien lifeform for this room, something that he’s never seen before. A monkey faced with algebra, you might say. However, in the monkey’s defense, he’s probably not stone-drunk when he faces the math problem, so he at least has a chance of comprehending it. “Th’ ‘ell?” Doug plucks the cigarette from his mouth and stabs it into an ashtray by the phone. He pushes the button on the machine and listens. “You have 23 new messages. Message 1. July 1, 2002. 1:34 am.” Doug starts to take his jacket off as the machine continues. “Doug, this is Frisco. Just wondering if you saw the bullshit that went down at the Pay Per View. Heard you had similar problems the night before. I tell ya, you torture a guy for weeks and what happens? Some other fucktard comes out of the woodwork and ruins it all. Anyway, just wondering what you were doing for the Fourth. Me and my political group are going to launch SAMs at clay pigeons just for the hell of it. Oh, and you must meet my gunner. Hardest DAMN working heavy weapons guy in the Americas, I tell you. You’re welcome to join if you feel like it. It’ll be at the Colorado estate. I hope to see you there.” *click* “End of message. Message 2. July 2, 2002. 10 am.” “Hey Doug, it’s Damon Trask, Wrestlingtattler.com news. Just wondering if you wanted to comment on the stories that you’ve been skipping house shows and blowing off fans. Oh well, I’ll try back later.” *click* “Message 3. July 3, 2002. 3:15 am.” “Doug, it’s Trask again. Just wondering if you had a comment. As an editor, I must first get a quote before I can alter it to make you look like an idiot, so please call me back as soon as possible.” *click* “Message 4. July 3, 2002. 5:01 am.” “Doug, it’s Trask again. Still wanting that quote. Call me.” *click* “Message 5. July 3, 2002. 7:37 am.” “Trask again, wondering if…” *click* “Hey Doug, Trask here… *click* “Doug, it’s Trask again.” *click* “It’s” *click* “Me” *click* “Trask” *click* “I” *click* “Think” *click* “You’re” *click* “A Jackass” *click* “And” *click* “I” *click* “Will” *click* “Make up” *click* “My” *click* “Own” *click* “Quote” *click* “Doug, it’s Nate Dumas.” Doug has his hand poised over the button again, ready to skip past the message. However, he keeps listening. “I know it’s been a few days, but I need to talk to you about what’s going on. You seem to be under a lot of stress, and that’s understandable. I lobbied with Reagan and Ed to get you off this week so you could get your personal affairs in order, but please give me a call when you get a chance. Nothing professional, I’d actually like to meet at a bar or someplace we can go over old times. Just gimme a call when you can.” *click* Doug has his head in his hands, a look of absolute tiredness hanging over his entire body. He looks at the machine. One message left to go, then he’d turn in for the night. He’d give Nate a call in the morning. Doesn't wanna blow off the only friend he has in OSW management now, does he? “Message 23. July 5, 2002. 8:30 pm.” “I swear it I’ll make my own quote up asshole I…” The message stops as Doug pulls the tape out of the machine and chucks it against the wall of his room. He yawns loudly before walking off camera as the feed fades to black.
