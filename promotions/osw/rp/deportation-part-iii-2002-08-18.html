Deportation: Part III

18 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/18/02 Tom Walczak sits on the plane staring out of the window, looking down at the country that was once the place of his residence. He is waited on by a private flight attendant, and is given treatment deserved by only kings, yet he still sits there nearly motionless with an angered look on his face. Just then the phone rings, and Walczak quickly picks it up. “Hello who is this?” “Well, I am an agent of the United States government, and I am calling you to let you know why this has happened to you, and what you can do to get the problem resolved.” “Thank God, because I sure as hell want to know what the fuck is going on. I have been put on this plane, and I do not even clearly know the reason why, other than you assholes cannot find papers documenting my citizenship.” “I know that you are angered, but that is no reason to result to name calling. I am not the person who sent you out of the country, and I am not trying to keep you out. In fact I am trying to help you. You see it is not even that we cannot document your citizenship because you were legally adopted, but we cannot find the papers that your foster parents filled out to legitimize your residence in the United States.” “Well, I have all the papers that you need at my home in Copiague, and had you contacted me earlier I could have went and gotten them for you. You could have saved me a lot of time and trouble.” “You see all this was reported by the customs agent, and she did mention that you claimed to have the papers at your home, so what I plan to do is ask you where they are, and we will go and search for them.” “No one is entering my house. I don’t give a fuck who you are, but unless you are family or friend you do not have any fucking right to enter my house.” “Well, that is respectable Mr. Walczak, but if you do not comply with us than the process could take months, but if you just tell us where in your house the documents are you can be back in the country in a couple of days.” “Even if I do tell you how do you expect to get into my house?” ‘Your foster parents have agreed to let us into the house with your permission, and show us what we are looking for.” “Well, than ok. The documents are all in a file cabinet inside the basement, and the key to the file cabinet is in the draw next to the refrigerator in the kitchen. Is that what you were looking for.” “Yes, thank you very much we will contact you again once we get the papers, and if we do find the papers than you may not even have to go to court to defend yourself.” “Ok, I just have one more question to ask you.” “What would that be?” “Well, I have a social security number, so couldn’t you just go into a computer, and find the number, and in so doing prove that I am indeed a citizen of the United States?” “Well, this is the news Mr. Walczak. You did at one point have a social security number, but once you turned eighteen you had to make a choice as to which country you wanted to live in be it The USA or Poland, and you never turned in the papers that said that you chose to live in the United States.” “I never got any fucking papers.” “It will all be resolved soon enough, and when it is all of your troubles will be over.” The man hangs up the phone, by which Walczak responds by throwing his. Walczak sits angrily with his arms crossed as the scene fades out…
