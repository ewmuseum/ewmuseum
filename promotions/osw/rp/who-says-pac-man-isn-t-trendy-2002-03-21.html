Who Says Pac Man Isn't Trendy?

21 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-21-02 Our scene opens at the South Lake Mall, one of the few locales that exist where you can tell which wrestlers are going to cut their promo from it by taking a quick glance. Obviously this one is coming from Civil Disobedience, as if you couldn’t already tell by the title. Knowing this, you realize it is only a matter of time before one of those numskulls interject themselves into this scene. I think we have about five more seconds before that happens....four....three....two....one...Alright, maybe I was a few seconds off. Zero...negative one....negative two...negative three...negative fou...Ah-ha! Someone’s coming! Right on cue...I mean a few seconds off cue, Seth Masters steps onto the scene from the right. He’s wearing his typical black t-shirt, jean shorts, backwards cap, with his quickly becoming typical OSW Tag Team Championship belt strapped around his waist. He pauses triumphantly in front of a spurting fountain, looking upward as he inhales a deep breath of mall air, while onlookers point at him excitedly. “Mommy? How did that man escape from the mental hospital?” a small child asks, tugging on his mother’s skirts. She has no answer that could appease him, so he is left to huddle behind her in fear until Seth moves along. As he makes his way through the mall, Seth peers into various stores on one side or the other, until he stops in front of the resident mall arcade. Looking inside, he almost turns to continue his trek when something catches his eye near the back wall. Past all of the fabulous new arcade games, he can see a man furiously wiggling the joystick of the old, broken down Pac Man game. Yes, I said Pac Man. Boy, isn’t that a blast from the past. From the nonmoving nature of the man’s hair, it could be nobody but Trevor Hawke. Seth transverses the room, on his way passing the crowds of people around the more popular gaming machines, eventually stopping behind his partner to glare over his shoulder. Deep in the bowels of the room, Trevor and himself stand alone. “Trev, what the fuck are you doing?” Seth asks softly, trying not to draw attention from people who may be in the front of the arcade. “Getting a new fucking high score,” Trevor answers back, not taking his eyes off the screen. The little circular sprite on the screen makes his way around a corner, almost running into a ghost before switching his direction to move in the opposite direction. “But...you’re playing Pacman!” Seth says, pushing Trevor away from the machine. Trevor glares at him for a split second, but his hands are quickly back on the controls, fast enough to keep his game from ending. “Your point is?” Trevor asks, as he runs Pac Man around the corner to snag one of the power pellets. He goes a ghost munching spree as Seth slaps himself in the forehead. “My point is...is...IT’S PACMAN!” Seth exclaims, clearly not understanding why his partner has chose to spend his time in front of this machine. “And just what is wrong with Pacman?” Trevor questions, raising a questioning eyebrow toward Seth before turning back to the screen, where he makes a complete rotation around a T-block, picking up all the pellets. “Nothing...if you’re living in the eighties,” Seth remarks sarcastically. “And just because Hogan and Curt Hennig are back in the WWF doesn’t mean we’re trapped in a time warp.” “Just because something is old, doesn’t mean its not good,” Trevor offers as his counterpoint, wiggling the joystick rapidly. “Oh yeah?” Seth asks, setting up what looks to be a lethal counterpoint of his own. “What about Michael Jackson? Mick Jagger? Dare I say...Cher?” Game, set, and match. It seems as if Seth has won this war of words, if for no other reason that he pulled out the brutal weapon that is inflicting the mental image of Cher. Trevor starts to choke in disgust, spasming slightly. This lapse in concentration causes his last life to be taken away by those damn ghosts, which in turn causes the words ‘Game Over’ to flash on the screen. As Trevor regains control of himself, he glances at the score near the top of the screen, then smiles in satisfaction. He has just cleared the high score by a good thousand points. He enters his initials proudly while Seth shakes his head in disapproval. “Trev...you’re one half of the most trendy team in Old School Wrestling. We’re supposed to be on the cutting edge of pop culture. We’re supposed to be the ones who trim that edge. As far as arcade games go, just look in the front of this arcade to see what is hot. Tekken 4, Capcom vs. SNK 2, and even Dance Dance Revolution...those games are on the cutting edge, and Pac Man...well, it’s most definitely not on that edge. It went out of fashion like ten years ago, if not more. Even Mrs. Pac Man couldn’t pull his ass out of the gutter. Get with the times, man. You’re fucking embarrassing me. I mean...Pac Man! For Christsakes, dude! I...” “Oh, shut up, Seth.” Trevor responds calmly, cutting his partner off. “You’re attacking Pac Man for being out of date, yet you don’t even comprehend what the game truly is.” “What’s to understand? You run that stupid yellow circle guy around the screen and try to eat up all those stupid dots. It doesn’t take a brain surgeon to figure out how to play Pac Man, dipshit.” “You’re looking at the game through the eyes of a child, dude,” Trevor says, shaking his head in a pitying fashion. “There is so much more to the game than that farce of an explanation you just offered.” “What kind of idiotic explanation could you have to justify your defense of this dumbass game?” Seth spits angrily. “Bro...Pac Man isn’t just a game. It’s a metaphor for life,” Trevor states solemnly. “Phhh...give me a break,” Seth says, shaking his head in disbelief. “No really, check it out,” putting another quarter into the machine, Trevor soon has a new came started. “Alright, look you start here,” he says, pointing to the Pac Man sprite in the middle of the screen. “You’re right in the middle of things. Just like in life, you have to start somewhere.” “Uh huh,” Seth nods. “From your humble beginnings you have to go out and make something of yourself. That’s what these pellets represent: money, fame, power...whatever you want in life. And people are so greedy for these things that they’re left no other alternative than to gobble them up whenever they’re found.” Trevor says, moving Pac Man around the screen to illustrate his point. “I guess that makes some kind of sense.” says Seth, still sounding skeptical. “But what about the ghosts? What are they?” “The ghosts...they represent everybody else in the world. They see you eating up the sweet things in life, becoming happy and satisfied with yourself, and they naturally become jealous as hell. So what do they do? The same thing that all bullies do. They try to kick your ass and take you down a few steps. And what’s life without a representation of the jerks, ya know. God knows there’s fucking enough of them,” Trevor says, with spite in his voice. “Yeah, the ghosts make sense too, now that you explain it.” Seth admits grudgingly. “But you’re leaving out one important thing. The power pellets. Pac Man gets those things and all hell breaks loose. There’s no real life explanation for those.” “Sure there is,” Trevor states matter-of-factly. “Drugs.” “Of course,” Seth says, wondering why he didn’t think of it. “Damn right. Those ghosts split when Pac Man gets a hold of that shit. Think about it. If you were picking on some dude and he took a hit of acid, then started coming after you trying to bite a chunk out of your head, you’d run too. Yeah, Pac Man is one hopped up hippie, that's for sure,” says Trevor with a nod “Whoa. That is possibly the coolest thing I’ve ever heard,” Seth says, now awestruck. “You know, Trevor...you were right about Pac Man all along. This game is awesome after all.” “See, you should listen to me more often.” Trevor says confidently. “Want to finish my game?” “Sure!” Seth exclaims, reaching for the joystick, before Trevor pushes him away. “That’ll be a quarter, bitch.” Trevor says, holding his right hand out toward his partner while still maneuvering the joystick with his left. Seth reaches into his pocket frantically before pulling out a quarter and slapping it into Trevor’s hand. Trevor steps away, making a gesture for Seth to take over, which he quickly does. As Seth starts to play, Trevor begins to make his way out. Seth calls after him when he’s a few steps away, causing him to stop in his tracks. “Say, Trev. Where’d you find out about all that Pac Man shit, anyway?” he asks. “Eh, I pretty much just pulled it out of my ass a few seconds ago. The only reason I was playing the game is because it’s the only machine here that costs less than a fucking dollar. Damn games are expensive.” Trevor begins to chuckle as Seth looks at him in disbelief. Trevor continues his exit again, on his way slapping a group of kids at the Tekken Tag Machine on the backs. As they turn to look at him, he points over at Seth, who is still playing out his Pac Man game. “Hey guys, look at that fucking loser playing Pac Man back there!” Sneers form on all of the kids faces as their eyes fall upon Seth. If there’s anything they hate, it’s a Pac Man player. Trevor is laughing loudly as he takes his leave, while the kids immediately storm to the back of the arcade, pelting Seth with quarters and insults. I mean, come on. Only losers play Pac Man.
