Drowned Sorrows

20 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-20-02 The bar was empty, save for a few tables occupied by a couple of bikers and a figure in the corner of the bar itself. The bartender wiped the counter and looked over at the figure. “You done boss?” said the bartender. A low growl exited the corner where the figure sat, “Nyet.” The bartender shrugged and returned to his table, wiping off a puddle of beer that had been left by a construction worker. The bikers tossed a few dollars on the table and stood up, nodded to the bartender, and exited the building, narrowing the occupancy of the bar to the figure, and the bartender. The figure growled and belched before he threw a bottle on the floor with a CRASH. The bartender turned quickly, “Hey buddy, you mind?” “Be quiet and get me one more vodka.” Said the figure, who’s voice was in a heavy Russian accent. “I hope you’re paying for all of this.” Replied the bartender as the figure shifted and threw money all over the bar. “Here, have my money, I do not need it.” Said the figure as the bartender collected the money. “Here, keep your money for now, I’ll collect it afterwards pal.” He put the vodka down as a large hand grabbed the bottle, popped the top off easily, and began to drink it. A flash of metal from the figure’s torso reflected off the light for a moment. The bartender checked a few of his taps and looked over at the figure, “So, you’re not from around here, are ya?” “I am from another world,” replied the figure, “a world that you would never understand.” After a moment of digesting the reply, the bartender wiped out a glass with a rag, “We all are from a different world, in a way, would you not say?” A chuckle came from the corner, “Not like mine. You would not understand.” A low sigh came from the figure, “I come from world unlike any other.” The bartender nodded and walked to the corner where the figure sat and replaced a light bulb on a wall light. Light flooded the area, revealing the figure to be none other than Ivan Stanislav. Stanislav, however, did not sit straight in his barstool. As a matter of fact, Stanislav had teetered to the side hours ago and laid into the wall with his huge broad shoulders. Four hours and over thirty vodka bottles later, Ivan Stanislav still sat in the Southside Bar, far worse than he had come. Pouring his heart out to El Drago had relieved some of the pain within his heart. Knowing that Drago was ignorant to his words had made it possible to lessen the weight that was upon his shoulders. He had exited the hospital an emotional wreck. Though his face was grim, his mind was twisted in knots. He had returned to his hotel room but found it impossible to rest, and thus, landed himself in the bar. The bartender watched as the hulking Russian downed the rest of the vodka and set it down next to a large pile of empty bottles beside him. “So, what’s your story?” A slow blink came from Stanislav as well as a snort. He looked horrible. His hair was disheveled, his shirt untucked, and stains could be seen on his black shirt from missing the bottle with his enormous maw. He chuckled, “My story? You are joking. You wish to hear MY story?” The bartender took a step back, fearing that perhaps this massive man would get unruly. He questioned if his double barreled shotgun would bring this bear of a man down, “I meant no offense, of course buddy.” The Russian Bear pushed himself from the wall and focused on the bartender, waving a large finger clumsily in front of him, “Do you know of irony, my friend? How such things that might normally be impossible, suddenly become true?” “Uh….yeah….I think.” Answered the bartender as he was unsure how to reply. “Were you ever surrounded by friends, only to find that most of the were enemies?” “Well, of course. I am a bartender, that happens a lot.” “Why do I do it? Why do I fight so hard, my friend?” Stanislav growled and swayed to the right to the point where he caught himself, his jaw slack, “Why do I even give a damn about anything anymore? I have been hit, directly in face, by reality and I do not like it.” The bartender swallowed and sat down on a stool a few seats from Stanislav, “You don’t seem like the kind of guy that’d, give up easily.” “DYAAHAAHAA----urk” replied Stanislav, grabbing his stomach. He paused and puffed out his cheeks for a moment and looked over at the bartender, “You are correct. I do not give up easily, but sometimes, the most powerful men are unable to defeat the most powerful enemy…..” he trailed off, staring at something on the floor. The bartender leaned forward, “….which is?” Ivan growled and swallowed, licking his lips a few times, “One’s self.” The bartender nodded in response, sharing a moment with the inebriated Russian. Ivan shook his head slowly, “How stupid I am, to believe that I could fix such a situation. How naïve I am!! I was doomed to fail from beginning…..” Ivan slowly lowered the volume of his voice and growled. The bartender licked his lips, “So….what are you fighting for?” Ivan shook his head slowly, “I do not know.” “Y’know,” began the bartender, “we all fight for something, whether we know it or not. Sometimes, it’s something outside of us, or perhaps, it is for our own good.” He paused, “I’ve seen a lot of people come through here, people that have given up. People who have allowed the world to swallow them up and spit them out and think that drinking this fucking liquid’ll fix it all.” Ivan looked at the bartender and blinked. “But y’know what, they’re all wrong. Alcohol doesn’t fix problems. It only makes them worse. Now I don’t know who you are, but I have a feeling that this isn’t where you need to be.” The bartender paused again and leaned forward, “You need to find what your purpose is buddy and hold on to it. Giving up isn’t the answer, alcohol isn’t the answer, but the answer is near.” “Where?” asked Ivan. The bartender pointed at the barrel chest of Stanislav, “There, my friend. Within yourself. You don’t belong here sitting at a bar, you belong on your feet, proud, and fighting.” Ivan’s eyes suddenly lit up as a realization hit him and he stood, staggering into the bar, “You are correct! I do have a fight! A fight that I had forgotten about!!” Stanislav took a step past the bartender and lurched to a stop. He turned and tossed him a wad of money, “Keep the change, my friend!” Ivan staggered into the doorway of the bar and out into the nights sky. The bartender stood up slowly and began to sweep up the shattered vodka bottle. As for Ivan Stanislav, perhaps he had found something to fight for. If anything, he had remembered about Kory Storm. He didn’t wish to destroy Storm because of orders from the Motherland, but because of what his own heart wanted. That alone was reason enough to head directly towards Pittsburgh.
