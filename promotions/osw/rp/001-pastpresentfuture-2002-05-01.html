001. Pastpresentfuture

1 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 1, 2002 Sometimes even the smallest thing can be the biggest vice on someone's personality. For gamblers, it could be a matter of the next dollar bill to waste against innumerable odds. For the nail-biters of the world, it's obvious. But for many people, the biggest vice is a little cylindrical roll of nicotine and poisons known as the cigarette. The thought of the dangers posed by this cigarette, like so many others, is a fleeting one in the mind of our subject, who, like many other Canadians, was reveling in the fact that the Eastern Conference playoffs of the NHL were being dominated by the Great White North. And, just like his fellow citizens, he was living it up in the time-honored fashion of fellow Canadians. Our subject, OSW newcomer Jason Winters, was getting drunk off of his ass. Stereotypical? Perhaps. But to be a professional wrestler, one has to accept that you're surrounded by stereotypes. People from a specific country are made to represent the entire nation as a whole, as if Nikolai Volkoff was your everyday Soviet Russian, or the British Bulldog was the true representation of all things United Kingdom. Anyway, Jason was sitting in Montreal, a bit out of place with a Senators jersey, slowly getting drunk. For the first time in a while, he was by himself, reflecting on his career as a whole. Being a professional wrestler wasn't the easiest career in the world. You were constantly moving from place to place, working for half-empty auditoriums, praying for your chance to break through. Jason was lucky. He never went through that. His father and uncle were both professionals before him, affording him the opportunity to start at the big leagues. But, being in the big leagues were themselves a big disappointment. His entire career up until that point had been filled with mostly disappointments. With the exception of being the UWA's Canadian champion for an incredibly long time (in part due to a lack of competition), there wasn't really any other claim to fame for Jason. The fans seemed to love him when he was at his greatest. They hated him at his cruelest. He was living the dream, but not receiving the rewards. Every shot at the big titles was a loss. He always wound up 'getting hurt' at pay-per-views, allowing Jesus Saint Crux, Kanaida, Doctor Hardcore, or whoever was holding a title at the time to get the win. He had one of the best records in UWA history, and despite the consistent passing over at pay-per-views, he loved working there. Nothing could ever seem to eclipse his work there. Nothing was the same. And since the UWA, he had little career to speak of. Whenever anyone stopped him, he was always asked... 'Hey! Aren't you that guy who died in Epic?' A fan of professional wrestling, perhaps a newcomer to the world of marks. They never can tell when to shut up. 'Yeah...but I am defined by a bit more than dying in Epic Wrestling you know,' was the only reply that seemed to work for Jason, still sitting at his stool, looking cockeyed at the young man who interrupted his reflecting. 'Well, of course you are,' said the young man, 'I do remember you from the UWA. But let's face it...every career is defined by moments. That's one that the public won't forget any time soon.' Moments. Careers in this sport were just a bunch of moments. Rising to the occasion and taking a bump off of a steel cage and into a laundry bin. Being sued by the PBS Networks for unlawful use of the 'Teletubbies' franchise. Teaming up with your archenemy and getting thrown off a rafter one week later. 'Making love' to another man's wife during a show. Looking back upon defining moments were never good in Jason's eyes. 'You know what,' said Jason after a slight pause, 'you're right. Thanks.' The young man look startled, but left. Jason had a moment of realitization while lost in those moments that stick out greater than others. Before now his career had been simply defined by a few embarassing, awkward moments that never showcased his true talent. But now, he realized he had a new opportunity, a new chance to rise above the stupid things he had let others talk him into doing on national television. The future was in the hands of Jason Winters, and in OSW, perhaps he could finally be happy with his chosen line of work.
