Thoughts & Sweat

3 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 3rd, 2002 The punching bag jolts violently and dust flies off of it. It pauses for a moment, until the screen pans out to show Andrew ‘Riptah’ Washington start unloading on the bag. Each punch is filled with emotion, as perspiration drips down his baldhead. Each punch seems to be an angered outburst as he looks back on the past weeks. One thing keeps going through his mind. Think Tank. Last week, Short Circuit didn’t think. He was so mad about the Tank’s actions, he went ahead and did something stupid. He assaulted the Think Tank, alone. Da Westsidaz have never successfully done something alone in their entire careers and this past Saturday was just another example. John Mammoth destroyed SC. He physically took him apart. But there was something that bothered Riptah after he watched the tape over and over and over… Mammoth had no emotion, no expression. He just destroyed Short Circuit. He had no motive other than that Dorkiman told him to. No rage, no passion, no fury. Just a dull, focused look in his eyes. Riptah drilled the bag some more, shaking his head as his tapped fists landed into the hardened leather. His staple white beater had turned from it’s usual self to a damp, discolored shirt. Over an hour had passed, but Andrew still punched the bag, awaiting Slam. A night that would hold more than a simple Tournament match, but it would be a step. A step toward the time that he could step in the ring with a member of the Think Tank so he could exact his revenge. Civil Disobedience? Porter’s scum. He figures, ‘Wow, Civil D and Da Westsidaz sure want to kick the shit outta me, huh? I guess I’ll pit ‘em together to at least make sure that’s one team we don’t have to worry about.’ Well Porter’s gonna have another thing coming. I don’t care which one of those nerds we lock up with, just so long as we do. This tournament isn’t about the titles. At this time in my career, the tag titles mean nothing to me. All that I want now is to have fun, so tonight I’ll do just that. Ever since last month, I’ve had nothing but respect for Civil Disobedience. Those two boys pushed me n’ SC to the limit. It was tough, to say the least. I’m glad that I get to fight ‘em again, but not like this. This match is a match that should be the finals at the Pay-Per-View. I bet Trev and Seth feel the same way. But, instead of enjoying the fight, I hafta be pre-occupied with how long it’s going to be before I get to step into the ring with the Think Tank. It’s bullshit. A few minutes passed and Riptah’s fists started to numb. He still pummeled the bag and the white tap around his fists and knuckles started to darken to a crimson color, but still he hit the bag, in anticipation for the hours ahead. He looked to the mirror in the locker room, a frown on his face. “Trevor. Seth. This ain’t about who advances in the tournament. This is about who gets to kick the Tank’s asses first. And may the best team win.”
