Deportation: Part I

18 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/18/02 Tom Walczak sits on sidewalk curb in the middle of the night. He holds a paper bag with an alcoholic beverage inside, and he just sits there with his head down. Every now and then he sips from the bottle, but other than that he sits there motionless. “Why? Why did Stanislav come into the ring? Why did I never regain the World Title that is rightfully mine?” Taking a pause from all the questions Walczak once again goes back to his trusty friend the bottle. “I do not know the answer to any of these questions, and I don’t think that anyone else does. Stanislav, once my most trusted ally has been pussy whipped by Tempest, and now has gone on a reckless trail of destruction. Who cares though that is not what matters. What matters is that once again I was screwed out of a shot at a championship belt. Seriously Vampir resorted to tactics of a girl. He fucking dug his nails into me. All matches that involve me and in some way a type of belt should be called screw the Polack matches because in the end that is what eventually ends up happening.” Walczak goes back to start drinking, but is stopped by a tap on the back. A police officer stands there with a pair of handcuffs. “Are you Thomas Walczak, the native of Gdansk, Poland?” “Yes I am, and if this is about me drinking I am sorry I will leave just give me a couple of minutes to clear my mind ok?” “No this is much more than just drinking. You see by order of the United States government you have been deported to your native country of Poland, because according to the government you never received Citizenship, or permanent residence.” “This is unheard of I have my citizenship. I was legally adopted by a family of American Citizens, and that made me a citizen of this God Forsaken country. So you better be leaving me alone.” “Well you will have a chance to prove yourself at a later date, but for now you will be deported back to your native country. You are lucky enough to have your employer at OSW provide you with the company’s private jet, or else you would have had to pay for your own ticket.” “You seem not to be understanding me. I am not leaving. I have all the papers that I need, and if you just give me a chance to go and get them I will be more than happy to clear this up.” “As I stated earlier this will all be brought up to a judge in a court of law if you want to, but I have your deportation papers with me, and if you do not come then not only will you be deported, but you will be sent to jail for resisting arrest.” “This is a complete bunch of bullshit. How did you even come up with the idea to search if I was a citizen or not? Did someone tip you off, or some shit like that? Because if that is the case then I better not find out or else I will tear their head off and shit down their neck.” “This is not a time to be quoting lines from Full Metal Jacket, it is just a minor situation, and if you do indeed have the papers that you claim to have then you should be let back into the country very quickly.” “Oh I will, and when I do come back there will be hell to pay. I was angered at all you damn Americans before this incident, and now that this has occurred, well I am just plain and simple pissed the fuck off.” “Whatever you say Mr. Walczak, but all that I want you to do is to comply with me, so as to make my life easier. It will only be a couple minutes in my car until you get to the airport, and then you can go. Just think of it as a visit to Poland, you can visit old friends, and then you can come back and settle the matter in the court.” Walczak complies with the officer although unwillingly, and heads into the car. The officer shuts the door behind Walczak and proceeds to get into the driver’s seat. “Just hold tight, and in no time at all we will be at the airport. I do wish you all of the luck in the world Mr. Walczak.” “Ty nie masz prawa do mnie mowic. Jak ja w zyczu cie jescze raz zobacie to ja nie wie co ja zrobie. Ale jedna ziec wiem, i to jest zie lepie na cibie zeby cibie nie zo baczylem.” “Whatever you say Mr. Walczak.” The car drives off with Walczak looking out the window of the car awaiting his flight back to Poland…
