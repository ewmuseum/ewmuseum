Return To Form

29 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/29/2002 :: intro :: The feel of it all just doesn’t fit. Returning to what was once a lifestyle. I left this business for my reasons, and when I did return, my home was gone. So I’ve found a new home. I left Five Star Wrestling with that knee injury, but when I came back it wasn’t there where I expected it to be. But then I’ve stumbled across a new home. I have found a new place to spread these wings to once again show men how this eternal quest for eternal bliss and happiness is never rewarded. I hate to give my sadness to others, but what should I care. They’re the cause of all this. I’m the victim in all of this. But instead of whining like I used to, it’s time for redemption. Time to make it where I belong. :: A Downtown Chicago Apartment :: The walls are painted with a brick design, covered with metal posters for underground and somewhat-mainstream bands. Some tattered photos barely hanging on by a thumbtack reside there as well. In the distance, drumming is heard. The pounding is coming from somewhere in the back of the apartment. The drumming picks up faster, louder, and heavier. Suddenly it all stops in the crashing of a cymbal. A door creaks open and from around a corner walks in Jack Miller. He’s still the same person. Yet the sternness in his face is colder, darker, but with a little light. The same person, but a different man. His absence from wrestling has been a grueling few months, but necessary. Jack grabs a shirt off the floor and exhales deeply after putting it on. Jack walks over to his fridge, opening it wide enough to see inside. The fridge is bare except for some cheese, bottled water, and a lot of Coke. He pulls out a bottle of water and takes a large drink. After finishing off nearly half the bottle, Jack sets it down on the counter. “It’s been too damn long,” he says while exhaling. “For the past seven months I’ve deprived myself of the only thing I’ve truly enjoyed in my life. Well, aside from sex and music, it’s the only thing I’ve enjoyed. It’s what kept me sane. And let me tell you. Those seven months were worse than anything I’ve endured since my childhood. All the waiting and suffering through the solitude. How many people do you think took the time to call me up to see if I was still alive in these seven months? No one!” He takes another sip of water, “but I finally got a call. Old School Wrestling called me up and asked me if I wanted a chance to grace the ring again. What do you think I said? I jumped at the chance. Some may think I’m desperate, but I’m not exactly going to pass up a chance like this. This is a chance to get back at what I need to be doing in life.” He picks up a dented chair with blood smeared across it. “Such a great souvenir this will be. I’m thinking of framing this and hanging it up above the television,” he chuckles. “For those of you angry with my actions… well, you have every right to be. But to me, it was justified. For months when I wrestled in Five Star Wrestling, I begged the president and commissioner for a chance to take on the Epitome of Evil. I was denied the chance every time. Sure, it seemed like I was happy there, but I never got my hands around his neck. Now, I took the initiative and blindsided him. He deserves every single stitch.” “As for Nick Casey… well, I don’t understand why he’s so upset. So I shattered his dream of defeating EOE himself, but I’ve been waiting longer than him. You missed out on your chance, Casey! So give it up! Maybe you should have tried to get a win sooner. Besides, you’re so busy pleasing the fans and not yourself, you’d never have gotten the job done. If it weren’t for my intervention, EOE would have gotten that win over you. Face it, Casey; I helped you at Head On Collision. Instead of yelling your discretions at me, maybe you should be thanking me instead.” Jack takes another deep breath and walks over to a table in what appears to be the dining room. He sets the mangled chair down next to a shovel, a chain, and a fire extinguisher. He looks back at the camera, “memorabilia. I just moved in here, and these are some beauties I have yet to grace these walls with. They remind me of my glory days when I was the extreme champ back in Five Star Wrestling. When I was respected backstage. The days when wrestlers didn’t bother to get in my way because they knew I wasn’t one to mess with. It looks like in Old School Wrestling I’ll have to prove myself like I’ve had to in the past,” he smiles. He picks up a piece of paper and lets out some laughter. “I’m facing Drew Hostyle at Slam? I guess they want to let me get warmed up in my first match. The man lost to a woman at Head On Collision4. Now, I have nothing against women, and especially nothing against Amy, but Drew certainly proved all he is in this federation is a big talker. I should have no trouble dealing with him. Just another stepping stone on my path to MY inner peace.” He walks over to his stereo and turns on a Testament CD. He walks over to his door and opens it, motioning for the crew to leave. Everything fades. :: conclusion :: It’s finally time to go somewhere in my career. Time to show everyone what I really am and how worthy I am. It’s time to make an impact.
