The Greatest Answer

5 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 5th, 2002 I stared into her eyes, those beautiful eyes that held all that was gentle and sweet in my world. Whenever I looked into them, I fell victim to her aura, one that I would not escape. In fact, I was happy to be captured. She carefully read over the contract, her lips slightly moving as she read it to herself. I didn’t have the heart to make the decision in the office, so I tucked the document into my pocket and walked out with my head held high. I didn’t want them to know that the choice was racking my brain, so I gave them the best bluff I could and ran out with my tail tucked between my legs. Cowardly? Perhaps. Smart? Absolutely. Natalie Dischner, the one true love in my life, sighed and looked up at me. “Interesting.” I blinked once. No, twice. “That’s it? You’ve been reading that contract for the past ten minutes, and all you have to say is…” “…interesting.” She said, finishing sentence. “There’s nothing special about it, Tom. It’s all cut and dry. You would be contracted to New Wave Entertainment and they would promote you and whatever projects you decide to undertake.” I snatched the contract back, quickly skimming through it again. “That’s impossible,” I snapped back. “There’s got to be some loophole, some sort of angle that they put in this contract to undermine me later…” Natalie shrugged. “Not that I can see. Maybe they are being sincere about this offer.” “You don’t know this man, Nat,” I replied. “He’s a slime ball. Scum of the earth. I wouldn’t trust him to hold my ice cream cone during a hot day, not to mention handling my business affairs.” “I don’t doubt he’s a slime ball, but that doesn’t mean he didn’t hand you a legitimate contract.” She perked an eyebrow up. “Even slime balls need to earn a living.” Suspicion was the only word I could use to describe my emotions at the moment. Not only did I not trust Burris, but I let that mistrust cloud my own instincts. “But I’m not an actor,” I said. “I am a wrestler. What could I possibly bring to the rest of this world if it doesn’t involve me knocking the hell out of someone or leaping off of the top rope with a clothesline?” She rubbed my check with the softest touch, trying to comfort me. “Tom Lexian, I don’t think you give yourself enough credit. Now, who is that other wrestler that has made such a splash in the entertainment business?” I rolled my eyes. “Oh please, don’t mention him.” “No, no, he has made a huge impact,” She insisted. “Now, what was his name? The Boulder? The pebble? Oh god, I know it was some inanimate object…” “The Ro - ,” I began to say. “Oh, forget it.” She made a low growl in her voice, frowning disapprovingly at me. “The point is that you could do it too. You have everything that these hotshots in the entertainment business want.” “So you want me to do this.” Her eyes drifted away. “Tom, I know you love the business. It’s been your life for so long, sometimes I don’t think you know anything else. But honey, this could be a career waiting for you after wrestling is over.” I didn’t want to think that far ahead. I was a guy – sue me. Men don’t think ten, fifteen, twenty years into the future. I lived in the present. But her face told me that she wanted me to have something else on the side; something that would keep me honest. “The high life,” I began to say, seeing twinkling stars and broken dreams in my eyes. “Watching those Hollywood fluffs come out in their limousines and their fancy dresses. I’ve never wanted to be one of them or pretend that I was a part of their elite.” Natalie wasn’t even looking at me anymore, already anticipating my response. “But for you – I would.” Natalie looked up at me with wonder and surprise on her face. “What?” “There’s nothing in this world I want more than you,” I replied, taking her hand. “And our life together means more to me than my hatred for the entertainment world. More than all of the titles and accolades that I’ve won, more than the fame I’ve garnered in the ring.” “More than Mike Extreme?” She teased. I shrugged. “Almost.” She playfully tugged at my chin. “But I don’t want you to do this if you feel uncomfortable with it. That last thing I need is for you to come home bruised and battered from the ring, and then spitting venom about the Hollywood fluffs you hate so much.” “I’ll find away around it,” I said. “Besides, it gives me a chance to work on my mic skills. Maybe a few Hollywood scripts will improve my methods in scaring the holy beejesus out of my opponents.” “Let Mike Extreme do the scaring. He’s better at it.” Oh sure, take his side. Not like I wouldn’t go with the six foot eight inch gorilla either. I got up from the table, the contract in hand. My signature was elegantly written on the bottom, signing away my very dignity that I held dear to me. “I guess I’ll have to talk to O’Brien about this and let him know what’s going on.” She gave me a disputatious look. “O’Brien’s always the last to know, isn’t he?” “What else is a manager good for except to screw around with?” I answered with a joking smile. “It’s good for him. Keeps him on his toes.” “He’ll be pissed.” “O’Brien’s always pissed, sweetie,” I replied, making an exaggerated motion with my hand. “He’s Irish. They’re always upset.” “Stop being an ass.” I paused, noting my crass generalization. “Okay, I’m sorry. That was wrong of my to say. But O’Brien is always angry, and it always seems to be directed at me. Plus, he’s never gotten along with Colin, so I have no idea how that’s going to go over.” “I guess O’Brien will just have to put up with it some more.” She paused, smiling to herself. “You know, if I didn’t know any better, I’d say he loved you like a son.” “That’s good, because he’s old enough to be my father.” I leaned over and kissed Natalie on the cheek. “Wish me luck?” “Always.” She kissed me softly on the lips, one that could have stopped any man right in his tracks and forget about everything going on around him. She had on a white skirt that came up above her knee, revealing her beautiful, long legs that were tanned to perfection. A moment later I grabbed it with my hand playfully. She slapped it away. “When was the last time I told you that you’re beautiful?” “Maybe an hour ago.” I chuckled. “How thoughtless of me.” “Don’t let it happen again,” She retorted. I grabbed the knee again, but this time she allowed it. She smiled seductively at me and with those beautiful dimples of hers nearly brought me under her spell again. “Why do I ever listen to you?” I mocked, trying my best not to laugh. “Because you know I love you,” She whispered back, running the back of her hand against the side of my face. That was the answer I loved to hear. The greatest answer I could have hoped for.
