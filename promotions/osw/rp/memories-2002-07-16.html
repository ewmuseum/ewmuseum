Memories

16 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-16-02 The news of his suspension was something of a shock to Ivan Stanislav. How could the country that he had sworn to protect for so many years turn their backs on him? How could they punish him for merely turning over a new leaf? At the very least, Stanislav thought that by distancing himself from his communist beliefs, Russia would smile on him, but in stark contrast, he was punished, and to Stanislav, very severely. One may say that now, he could focus on wrestling solely. He didn’t need to return to Arkhangelsk. He didn’t need to worry about soldiers and paperwork and drills. But it was as if a small part of his life was ripped from his body. Like a parent had turned their back on a child. As if…he was truly alone. Nevertheless, Stanislav returned to his office. It was strange when he entered the base. The soldiers had to have already known for while they treated him with great pride, they seemed dejected. Here was the man that had led them for years, suddenly wrapped up in the machinery that he helped to create. Eugeny Narobi, a Colonel in the military, was asked to watch over Arkhangelsk in Stanislav’s absence and he had already began moving into Stanislav’s headquarters. The older Colonel stood outside of the building, snow falling slightly around him, as Ivan approached. He was afraid, certainly, that Stanislav may crush him then and there and gave him a wide berth, of course, saluting as is required. Stanislav nodded to him, only to receive a somewhat dejected nod back from the Colonel. He didn’t want his pity, Stanislav did not regret what he did, he rarely regretted anything he had done. The Russian Bear walked through the quiet hallways of his building to his office. He opened the steel door and shivered, the interior quite cold from his absence. He turned on his lights, which flickered a few times and then whirred to life, and looked. His office was the same. Soviet Flag and Federation Flag on the wall. File cabinet, computer, desk, library, pictures, plaques. Memories. He slowly walked into the room and to his desk. He produced a small list from his pocket. He wanted some of his books, mainly his communist ones. He also required some extra pairs of clothes, a few pins, and a uniform. Narobi would not be moving into this office, so his personal belongings would not be touched or tampered with. At least, he hoped not. Ivan walked to his desk and fumbled with a few papers, and then grabbed at a drawer, the one that housed his books. He pulled once, but it seemed wedged shut. He gritted his teeth and wrapped his huge hand around the handle of the drawer and pulled again, jerking his entire desk and forcefully getting the drawer open. There, sitting in his drawer, were his coveted communist books. He bent down and fished one out, setting it gingerly on the desk, and then another, and finally, he grabbed a third. As he lifted the book out of the drawer, something caught his eye that startled him. With a gasp he dropped his book, which fell on the ground, and recoiled from the drawer. He had seen something in his drawer that appeared to terrify him. Ivan Stanislav crept back to the drawer and peered in and stared at the cause of his terror…. ….a red feather. He knew immediately what the feather meant. It was a feather from a red boa. But not just any boa, but a boa that Tempest had. That single, solitary feather had pulled from the boa and stayed with him and when she had left it was all he had to remember her by. He remembered sitting in his office, for over a day, staring at that feather and he had thought that it had blown away with the wind, as the rest of his happiness had, but nevertheless, the feather must have gotten wedged in between the Communism. Stanislav reached into the drawer and pulled the feather out gently, holding it in a shaking hand. He stood up straight and fell backwards into the wall behind him, leaning against it as he stared. He wanted to smile, but he couldn’t. With the memories of Tempest came happiness, and much sadness. For Ivan Stanislav, symbols were very important. Hammers, sickles, flags, and songs are just a few to describe. This feather, it was a symbol. A symbol of a time in which The Russian Bear could be The Russian Bear, without any care in the world. For the better part of a half an hour, Ivan Stanislav leaned against his wall in his cold, dank office and stared at the feather in his hand. And then he gently put the feather in his pocket, and quickly exited the room. He knew what he had to do….and only one person was capable of helping him…..
