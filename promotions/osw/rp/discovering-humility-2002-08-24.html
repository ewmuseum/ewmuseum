Discovering Humility

24 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 24th, 2002 “What do you have to say for yourself?” She asked me. I couldn’t even reply. My eyes darted back and forth from the ceiling to the floor, but eye contact was a definite no-no. Besides that, it’s hard for a guy that just landed himself in the slammer to realize what an idiot he was. Yeah, that’s right, the slammer. The cell. Good ol’ Shawshank itself. “Well aren’t you going to say something?” She asked again, this time seeming more irritated. “What do you want me to say Natalie?” I replied, the resignation in my voice being clearly expressed. Her man was in the slammer and she wanted an apology? Shouldn’t she be supporting me? Tom, when things are as bad as they seem right now, nothing will work in your favor. It’s rule number one of life. Funny, I thought rule number one was to find a mate and proliferate quickly. I guess this would be rule number one of a practical life. She shook her head, still in disbelief. “How many times have you been arrested in your life?” I thought it over for a second. “Do you count the time my fourth grade class visited the local county jail?” “Don’t be a smart ass.” I allowed myself to chuckle. “I can’t help myself, Nat. I’ve got that smart ass gene in me that has a mind of its own sometimes.” She reached over the table and touched my hand. Her touch immediately eased some of my tension and I began to relax. I had made the call this morning, some time around eight o’clock am. I had been brought to the station at two in the morning, but I didn’t want to wake her. That just would have brought a meaner, angrier Natalie to the station that would have raised Hell and gotten Lucifer himself to pack his bags and move back to the pearly gates of Heaven. Yes, it would have been that bad. “So the officer said you’re free to go once the paper work is finished,” She continued. “You’re lucky this is your first offense, otherwise you would have to be arraigned.” There were only bits and pieces of what happened last night. I left a bar, which was probably my clearest memory, and then I went parading around in the streets shouting for NC-17. I wanted Graphic Violence and Silence to appear from some street corner and take me on. I didn’t care if it was a two on one, because the way I was feeling, I could have taken on the combined arms of the Netherlands and still felt I had a chance to win. “I should have never gone to that bar,” I admitted. I rubbed my cheek, which felt sore at the touch. I couldn’t remember clearly, but I think I got in a fight in the cell last night as well. “You don’t even drink,” She said. “You should have known better.” Her eyes darted back at me. “It was because of the match wasn’t it?” I didn’t answer. “Tom, you’re not a rookie anymore. Winning and losing shouldn’t be like this…” “But it is,” I snapped back. “Natalie, do you understand the implications of a match of that magnitude? Not only were we going to be a step closer to the championship, but…” I trailed off, not finishing my sentence. “But what?” I shifted uncomfortably in my seat. “It was to prove who was the best, Nat. We have been two of the most dominant teams in the history of this sport, and we were finally clashing. And now what? It’s all been for nothing?” I hung my head in shame. “Tom, you can’t let your pride be hurt,” She said, still not understanding why I was so upset. She paused and stood for a second. “I’m going to go check on the paper work.” As she left, she looked back and said, “Oh, and Burris from New Wave called. They need to speak with you soon.” I let my head drop again. God dammit. That’s just what I need.
