Becoming The White Mexican

2 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Monday, September 2, 2002 Step 10: Working with out the Mask… It was strange the first time I stepped in a training ring unmasked, it’s weird but I felt as if I was naked. My timing was off and I was constantly distracted by the expressions on my face. I had to learn to wrestle again, to learn the American way. Step 11: Starting Over… I felt like I never wrestled a day in my life when I started out at the Academy. Losing the mask had incidentally shattered my confidence. But I was determined and I stuck at it, and eventually I was confident enough to wrestle with out the mask. Step 12: Finding a New Identity… As if in the reverse of things in the past, the wrestling was down but I need an identity, many hours I thought and tormented with names and gimmicks but nothing took. It was only with a phone call back home that the answer came. An answer that was right in front of my face. I was a White Mexican. Step 13: Remembering the past… Despite what many people may thing, the past is very important in my life. I was planning to become a different person to fit in as an American wrestler, but before I did this I wanted an everlasting reminder of who I was. Deep down I was still the Dragon and I wanted to show it, even if it wasn’t with the mask. So I came up with a sketch of a tattoo. An arm band with the eyes of a Dragon and after that was crafted to my arm, I was ready to move on. Step 14: Changing… Just like before in Mexico, I had to look the part and here was no different. The hair was bleached an almost see thru white. My skin professionally bleached to lose all resemblance of a tan. A pair of black pants with some red and white shapes was the finish to my attire. When I looked in the mirror I was no longer El Drago… I was the White Mexican. Step 15: Debuting… I was the White Mexican and an American federation by the name of Primetime Championship Wrestling and they wanted me to go to work right way. After the debut, much like in Mexico I slowly build up the crowds support and got more and more comfortable wrestling with out the mask. Step 16: Dealing with Success… Unlike before now when I left the arena the fans would recognize me. They would ask for autographs, to pose for pictures and many other strange requests. It was all-new to me and something I wasn’t great at handling. No longer could I go to the store and buy food with out getting spotted. No longer could I walk to the LC and purchase a bottle of Tequila with out expecting to sign a couple bottles in the store. It took a while but eventually I realized the price of fame was giving up your privacy and perhaps for the first time I understood why Mexican wrestlers hid from their fame. Step 17: Leaving on Top… Many things changed during the many months I was in PCW, I had gone from a virtual nobody to a top draw and in a major feud with the then owner of PCW. I won my final match and announced my retirement from PCW, but truth be told now the only reason I left was because I could not see myself working with the management any more. Step 18: Remembering the Times Like many things in my life I want to make sure that important things in my life do not get forgotten. My time in PCW was no different and it was back to the tattoo parlor to have the three letters that made my face famous etched on my arm. Then it was on to the next phase of my life.
