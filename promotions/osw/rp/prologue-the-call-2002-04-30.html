Prologue: The call

30 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 30th 2002 **The night was young when it happened. To this day, I still can remember the scene...** 'ANDREW ACE HAWK!!!!' 'THE LEGEND FROM THE CCW!!!' 'NOOOOOOOOOOO!!!!!!' 'BOCA!!!!!!!!!!' 'Toomes blinks and drops his title in shock as Andrew Ace Hawk yells at Toomes!' 'ANDREW ACE HAWK IS THE MASKED BIKER!! THE MENTOR OF SWITCHBLADE HAS BEEN THE ONE TRYING TO DESTROY HIM!!!' **I was sitting in my chair, beer in one hand, remote control on the other. There was I, relaxing after a rough week from work. The children were already sleeping, they had school in the morning. My wife, not a very big fan of wrestling, was arguing in the back, from the kitchen, saying I should get some sleep, since the other day would be a big day. The night was dark, and although I live in downtown LA, in a small cozy house in the suburbs, I could not hear any movement in the streets, showing people were as ecstasic as I was. I knew he would call me, I knew he would need me, but I didn't know, I wasn't sure. So, I got up and instead of preparing myself to sleep, I prepared myself for a day of work. I walked into the bathroom, turning the lights on. The cold chill from the cool breeze coming from an open window, freely sliding on the bright white tiles, made me realize I was wearing nothing more than an old pair of brown pants. Grabbing a dirty white muscle shirt from a laundry vine basket on the floor, I began shaving myself. Looking at the mirror, I could see the difference between today and the glory days when I was a CCW announcer, times when I followed Ace's career in CCW. So many stories, so many interviews. It was almost a year, but the rumors led me to believe the call would come. He had fired his manager, after he found out she was stealing his money. He was venturing himself in a new ground, and he needed some help. And I knew he'd trust me. It was just a matter of time.** **Even though the time went by, after I finished shaving and finished ironing my best suit and shirt, and grabbed my best hawaiian tie, the time I spent just sitting in my couch, side by side with the phone, made me realize how much I missed the road. The chance to stop on different places, to meet different people, to see different settings. The road was always a part of my life, since my days of wrestling in the indy scene in the 1970s. The sensation of never seeing the same sight every day was something wonderful. It was time for me to go back to the road again. The family life was killing me, and Ace was gonna be my savior. And I remember to this day that, as I finished that thought, and almost started to give up, the call came. I remember jumping on the phone, tripping on a toy on the floor and crashing the phone table down, making a huge noise that by a hand of god didn't wake my three children. I picked up the phone, and my face lit, as I recognized the voice on the other side of the line:** 'Hey, there, old man...', said Ace, in a cheerful voice. 'Ace. It's been quite some time...', I answered, trying to not show my true emotions. To this day I can't understand why I simply wasn't honest and talked openly to Ace. And yet, even though I was trying hard to hide the happiness from being remembered, the man said, in a sarcastic way: 'Come on, Dick Strang, I know you for long, you can't fool me with that voice. I know you!' 'Oh really?', I poorly tried to answer, barely enclosing my smile. 'Then tell me why I have that voice.' 'It's simple', answered my old companion. 'You just watched OSW Head on Collision and is wondering what the hell I was doing there...' 'Well, that's not it, but I wouldn't mind if you gave me some answers...', I remember answering, trying to play with him, trying to get out of the conversation on top. Obviously, Ace wasn't in the mood for games. 'Look, you'll get your answers together with the rest of the media. Now, what I wanna ask you is this: Do you or do you not wanna be my manager? I could use a trained pair of eyes to control my interests backstage.' **My eyes glowed, and even though I wanted to answer to that question like a young girl answering to a proposal from her loving man, I refrained myself, trying to sound as much of a professional as I could.** 'I'll have to think about it, Ace. You know I have a family and a job... I'd have to drop all those things...', I said, trying to stay at the top of the topic. But, once again, Ace turned things around... 'Hey, dude, if you're considering leaving your family, that means you have already accepted it. I need you to catch a plane in twelve hours, I have a press conference back in LA and I need you to start setting things over.' 'Ahn, ok, ok, Ace... I'll see what I can do', I answered, without realizing Ace had hooked me badly. **After that moment, the whole night is a huge blur in my mind. I remember arguing with my wife, because I was going back to the road. I remember saying bye to the kids in the morning and leaving earlier than them. A limo was waiting for me on the outside. As I walked on that black stretch limo, as it took me to Ace's home, I remember thinking about what the future was reserving for me. I knew that Ace was an awesome wrestler, a young guy who had already accomplished a lot, and was not yet at his prime. I knew he was ready for primetime, and that the OSW was the right place for him. The big stars were there, the big stages were there, the big venues were there, and the big crowds where there. Even though OSW was a young community, it was building into a colossus of sports-entertainment, and for Ace being a part of it was really exciting. And I kinda sensed that in Ace's semblant, as I walked into his home in the Hollywood hills on that afternoon. I remember walking in that huge living room, rich and filled with engraved wooden furnitures and expensive paintings on the walls, and looking at my left, where, at the end of the room, near the big windows, with the afternoon lights barely breaking through the heavy curtains, sitting in a colonial wooden chair, with the small, skinny figure of his friend Danny Jackson to his left and the gigantic monstrous figure of his bodyguard Johnny Atlas to his right, a happy, smiling Andrew Hawk, the CCW living legend, the Ace of wrestling, simply putting his hand forward, as I approached him, and saying:** 'Welcome back.' **That moment stook into my mind. There, in my front, a wrestler who had been in very different situations and places, who have seen it all, happy like a rookie, smiling, and I could see myself through him. I knew that was a new beginning, and a bright one, one with a meaning, one with a goal. And that was when I knew that I, Dick Strang, former CCW senior commmentator, was gonna be joining him in a journey I'd never forget. That's when I decided to keep this log, to keep a track of every fact of every action of every moment Ace would have in that new life. I hope this serves a purpose, not for commercial reasons, but for historical reasons, so that people realized, in the future, who my friend is all about. Feel free to listen to this and broadcast it, because everything listed here is nothing more than the truth, the truth about the CCW legend turned OSW star, Andrew Hawk...** **Until a next time** End of Transmission
