A Restless Night (Part 1)

1 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/1/02 Walczak is seen lying in bed. His eyes are shut, but Walczak is anything but relaxed, as he turns around in his bed tumultuously. He clenches the covers, as unintelligible words come from out of his mouth. What could Walczak be thinking of? What could be troubling him so much before such a big match? Is it something about Doug Troy, or maybe it is something about the Red Army? Now we enter into Walczak’s head to see what is the cause of all this trouble. A smaller version of Walczak is seen playing with some trucks. There are various types, such as fire trucks, and eighteen-wheelers, but all of them look used and are missing various parts. Walczak has a red shirt on and a pair of jeans, both of them given to him by the orphanage. His hair is brown, and uncombed. Walczak sits there playing with the trucks by himself as all of the other children play together. Walczak is not playing with the trucks in an ordinary kind of way, no instead of just driving them he crashes them into each other, and causes pile ups, but does this all with a straight face. A sister who has been watching him for quite a while finally has enough, and goes over to Walczak. “Thomas, why is it that you have to play with the trucks in such a manner? If you cannot play with them in a more delicate, and non-violent manner than just put them away is that understood?” “Why do I have to play with them in a different way? Why is it that all of the other children get to play with the toys the way that they want to, but I can’t play with the toys the way that I want to play with them?” “Because Thomas, if you keep on playing with those toys the way that you do, then you will break them. If you do that than you or the other kids won’t be able to play with them, and what will that accomplish?” “Well, the toys are already messed up, and they have scratches all over the place. I really don’t see why I can’t crash them. They aren’t going to get much more messed up than they already are.” “How dare you talk back to me like that Thomas? Do you realize just how much I, along with the rest of the sisters in the orphanage have done for you? Do you know that had it not been for us you may be lying in some abandoned street now at this very moment, or even worse dead?” “You always tell me that you and everyone else do so much for me, but you do nothing. All that you do is tell me how worthless I am, and how I will not amount to anything. You give me no support whatsoever, and than you expect me to be grateful to you, and act as if I owe you everything.” After the comment made by Walczak the nun rears her hand back and slaps Walczak clear across the face, and in doing so sends him flying to the floor. Walczak gets up clenching his face, as if to show that she has only hurt him a bit, but as soon as he stands he gets sent to the ground with another fierce blow. “How dare you speak back to me Thomas? I warned you once already, but that was not enough for your pathetic kind. You are to go to your room for the rest of the night, there will be no supper today and no breakfast tomorrow. If there is one word that comes out of your wretched little mouth than the punishment will be put to a greater severity.” Walczak walks upstairs without saying anything, and as he goes over to his bed the sister closes the door on him. He sits alone in a dark room weeping, and as he weeps the scene fades out…
