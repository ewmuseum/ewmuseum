The sun has risen over Denver, Colorado, and the city is well into its day. At the Denver airport, commuters make their way through the bottom level of the three story terminal, a sea of humanity, each with their own destinations but together an unorganized throng of unconsiousness. In the background, voices pass on the overhead speakers, warnings remind the less intelligent of the travellers of the dangers of exiting a moving walkway. An incessant chatter fills the halls, rising not only from the bottom floor but from the second, as well. Outside, the loud roar of planes taking off and landing manages to power its way through the walls and into the throbbing head of Kimbusa. Kimbusa doesn't remember how he got to Colorado. He doesn't know why he's still at the airport. All he knew was that he had to get away from the American metropolis. He had found refuge from the swarms of American travellers on the third floor of the airport, where only the highest of classes ventured. Most of the frequent flyer incentive rooms were here, board rooms and sitting areas. The traffic here was almost non-exhistant, as the regal American business travellers refused to venture out with the public until the time for their depature had come. Kimbusa warily sat up from his bed of five chairs in the hallway. Shaking the stiffness from his head, he turned to look out the window. Daylight streamed in from beyond the airport tarmac, where below he could see planes refueling and deboarding. A large American Airlines jet slowly taxis by, momentarily providing Kimbusa's eyes with some welcome shadow. The jet finally passes, and Kimbusa again looks out beyond the tarmac. The rocky mountains lie in the distance, still covered in snow despite the warming climate. With a half grin, Kimbusa turns back towards the airport. He had found his next destination. But first, he needed more sleep. As he slumped back into his sleeping position, the sumo heard a creak. Then another. A loud tearing sound finally caused Kimbusa to sit up with a start, quickly scrambling back to his feet. The leather of the chairs had begun to stretch and break under the stress of Kimbusa's weight. He would be forced to find sleep elsewhere. He needed to find himself. For the moment, Kimbusa felt entirely defeated. Kid Wonder had now gotten the best of him on two occasions. He was disoriented, he had no direction. He simply didn't know where he was going. There was so much of this new country to see, but every time Kimbusa saw it first hand, he was disgusted. He decided to head downstairs. Kimbusa pulled his jet black hair back behind his shoulders, and adjusted his new black trenchcoat. With a few light steps, Kimbusa began the journey downstairs...
