003. Waking Life

24 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 24, 2002 Jason's eyes stirred open with a start, but he wasn't totally conscious. He was out of it...and he had absolutely no idea where he was. It was as if the last...however many days it was...had been a total blur. He tried to get up, but as he moved from a laying to a sitting position, his blood didn't quite move with him, so he laid back down and collected himself, trying to get the feeling of total nausea to leave him. As he was laying down, Jason looked up at the sky. It was easily the middle of the night...the Moon was near full, and there were thousands upon thousands of stars in the sky...he had never seen so many in his life. It had to have been really late in the night...Jason took a glance at his wrist watch, and did a double take. The numbers were unreadable. Jason tried to sit up again, and this time, he nearly stumbled over as he suddenly left the ground and began to sail away, up to the stars that were penetrating through the sheet of darkness overhead. ---------------------------------- Suddenly, Jason's eyes popped open. That couldn't have possibly been a dream...it was too real. But it wasn't exactly the first thing on his mind...it soon moved to the back of his memory, and faded much in the same way the Moon does at daybreak. Jason got out of his bed, and stretched. The Best Western people over in Winnipeg knew how to make a good room. Jason walked over to the mirror, brushed off the fact that he was totally dressed, and left the room. Jason suddenly found himself at the bottom of the staircase...it was weird feeling to say the least...wasn't he just in the corridor outside of his hotel room? Everything had been so surreal for the last few days...he didn't even remember checking in to the Best Western, but he simply accepted the fact that he was there. As he started walking again, the thoughts had left his mind once more. Leaving the hotel, Jason walked out into the streets of Winnipeg. He was minding his own business as he took in the sights of the city, keeping to himself as usual. He walked by a nice looking cafe, and saw a man sitting there all by himself. He was a total stranger, and yet Jason felt drawn to him. Without even questioning it, he sat down across from this strange man. 'Jason, I've been expecting you,' said the man. 'What? How do you know my name?,' asked Jason. It was the only fitting response he had for this man. 'It really doesn't matter, how I know your name. Questions like that are totally unimportant. Your name is unimportant. So is mine. What's important is that you and I are here right now, sitting face-to-face, and ready to talk.' Jason replied, 'I'm sorry. I don't know what we're 'ready to talk' about.' 'Oh...you don't? Well, that's also quite unimportant in the great scheme of things. You see, Jason, this life is full of challenges. You've already had several in your short life. Whether you realize it or not, you'll have many more ahead. But, you must realize that life should not be defined by these moments. You keep cycling over little, unimportant things about your past. You need to live in the present, live in the now. It will pay off if you forget your past and move on. Just because others can't doesn't mean that you should live back in the UWA.' 'I don't really see your point,' replied Jason. As he said this, things became fuzzy, even more surreal than the situation already was. 'Look,' said the stranger, 'you need to start over with a tabula rasa...a clean slate. Forget everything you know, or you thought you knew. In the end, it will help you more than it could possibly hurt you. The benefits outweigh the consequences.' 'Wait a minute,' said Jason. 'You're telling me to forget everything? Forget Kanaida's weaknesses, his strengths, our heated history, and everything that will help me in every match that I have with him? And you want me to do the same for Jesus, Ian, and anyone else that may waltz in in the next few weeks? You want me to forget my history...forget everything that makes me who I am?' 'This is where you're wrong,' said the man. 'History doesn't make men. Not in professional wrestling. It's a single, fleeting moment that defines who you are. Live for the moment, and then forget it. Move on to the next moment. Trust me, and good luck.' Jason didn't really have much of a chance to absorb this conversation. The stranger spoke quickly and feverishly, as he told him how to succeed and how to live. Before Jason could even repsond to his last statement about history, before he could even comprehend what the stranger was talking about, this man said 'Move along.' ---------------------------------- Jason's eyes popped open, and the scene was decidely less surreal. But, as he looked over at his digital clock, he realized he was in for another rude awakening...for lack of a better term...
