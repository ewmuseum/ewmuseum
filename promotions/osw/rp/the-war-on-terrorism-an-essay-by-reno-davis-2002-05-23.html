The War On Terrorism: An essay by Reno Davis

23 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 23, 2002 On September 11, 2001 a tragedy occurred in New York City, NY. The World Trade Center buildings were attacked and completely abolished by airplanes. The suspected terrorist behind the attacks was Osama bin Laden, leader of the al-Qa’ida terrorist group in Afghanistan. His brilliant move involved the theory of somehow uniting the Muslims of the world together to overthrow the United States, who interferes with his drug trades like a regular old tv cartoon bad guy vs. good guy scenario. This started up the 8 year search for Osama bin Laden once again, as it began in 1993 when he was suspected to of bombed the car garage underneath the towers, killing 2 people. The “War On Terrorism” was immediately started as George W. Bush Jr. took a move right out of his father’s playbook and began a massive fuss over the events. The hypocrisy plaguing the “War On Terrorism” is fairly overlooked, which is a terrible thing when it comes down to it. Although North American television wouldn’t ever say it out loud on air, The United States are Internationally known as the leading terrorist state in the world. In the world! But why has everyone overlooked the United State’s shaded past? It’s due to an age-old method of playing with words and truths known as “propaganda”. Propaganda really made it’s first stink during and before World War 1, when pamphlets and posters were dropped and posted everywhere in an attempt to either get citizens to join the war, or the other side to surrender. This continued on throughout the “last war ever”, World War 2, and then throughout the other wars that the US promised they’d never take part in, like Vietnam and Korea. Today, people think that they can block out propaganda because they are educated. Well, with all due respect, so are the people who make the propaganda, so who knows how much of it you’ve been exposed to. You are being exposed to propaganda every time you open the newspaper, turn on the radio or tv, in fact you are being depicted to propaganda as you read this essay. The role in propaganda in the “War On Terrorism” is more or less America’s talent of playing with words. An example would be how when the US had those attacks on Iraq, it was referred to as using “Intelligent bombs” for some reason. When America stuck their noses in Kosovo’s problems, it was referred to as “Humanitarian Intervention.” So let’s say you pick up a newspaper and it says: “US’ Humanitarian Intervention in Kosovo...etc,” it sounds like the US is being good guys and helping out. Okay, you want some more examples of “Humanitarian Intervention”? We’ll go back to pre-World War 2. We have: · Japan’s Humanitarian Intervention in Manchuria · Mussolini’s Humanitarian Intervention in Ethiopia · Hitler’s Humanitarian Intervention in Suedetenland But what really was is... · Japan’s invasion of Manchuria · Mussolini’s invasion of Ethiopia · Hitler’s invasion of the Suedetenland So with that comparison, the second collaboration of events sounds much worse, but what the people heard was from the first collective. So the translation of the United State’s “Humanitarian Intervention” is called “Invasion” or better yet a “Crime against Humanity”. The connection between the above demonstration and the “War On Terrorism” is that what the media calls the events taking place in Afghanistan is not a war. According to Webster’s Dictionary, “War” is described as: “Armed conflict between nations,states, or factions/ A determined struggle for a specific goal.” Now I’m not sure if I’m the only one, but the last time I checked, sending over airplanes and bombing innocents in an attempt to maybe hit your main target was not war. It was called “terrorism”. A counter-argument to this not being “War On Terrorism” was that what the United States was performing a determined struggle for a specific goal, that being the end of terrorism. True, to an extent but what isn’t realized is that in order for ‘Terrorism” to ever end, the leaders of the fight against it will have to throw down their missiles and bombs. As stated above, the leading terrorist state in the world is the United States of America. From the end of WW2 until 2001 there have been well over 65 attacks on foreign countries, 36 of which have been since 1980. So you can see that in order to end the “War”, the US would have to destroy themselves. The reasoning behind the use of the word “war” is fairly simple. When the word ‘war’ is sounded, a country sticks together immediately and has blood in their sights. Add a bit of revenge to the mix and you have a stark raving mad country ready to abolish whomever they are told to. Or, it’s supposed to work that way. The people in New York, as admitted by the New York Times, were not as angry as the newspaper said they were. The majority just wanted peace, or the ones who were educated on what was happening anyway. Unfortunately, the rest of the country in majority is patting President Bush on the back and saying: “Go get them tiger!” Another reasoning behind the word “war” was to show how serious the event truly was to America. September 11, 2001 was the first time since the war of 1812 that the American mainland was so seriously attacked. Some say that Pearl Harbor was an attack on United States mainland, but it was not. On December 14, 1941 suicide bombers attacked military bases and ships on 2 US colonies in Hawaii, thus it was not a direct attack towards the true American soil. That means that in 189 years, the United States was never attacked. The media has convinced the public that war is black and white with a good guy and a bad guy, or in this case a good country and an evil country. Osama bin Laden is now playing the role of the evil mastermind, whereas President Bush and the United States of America are trying to straighten his terrible ways. Unfortunately, with war and even with life, there is now black and white. Instead, there are certain shades of gray, each with their own evil and good deeds blended together. NOTHING can justify the action taken towards America in the form of the September 11 incident, but people should seriously stop pretending that they never expected something like this to happen. As I’ve stated throughout the entire essay, America has done their share to the rest of the world, and had something spiteful coming their way. As usual, they reacted violently and flexed their muscles in the form of airplane attacks, showing the world just how much they’ve come along as a nation since the earliest wars ever. My main question towards the new “War on Terrorism” is what America would like everyone to do instead of acting with violence. I mean, the world can sort of say that they’ve learned how to solve problems by example from the US and when a country tries to take a peaceful action, they are shot down that way as well. A prime example of such an event is the United States and Nicaragua clashes throughout the 1980’s. The United States bombed Nicaragua, killing over ten thousand people in the grand scale. After the terrorist attack by the United States, the country entered an economic war and the events were so severe that they make September 11 look like a paper cut. Nicaragua still to this day has not recovered from the mess that America made and most likely never will. Their solution to the US attacks? Take them to the World Court. The court ruled in their favor and told the US to pay considerable amounts for the damage they caused, and to take their military out of the country. The United States dismissed the judgment and replied with a heavier attack. The Security Council was called upon and proposed a resolution calling on states to observe the international law. America vetoed it and went to the General Assembly, where a similar resolution was made and passed. It took that much effort to get the leading country in “Anti-Terrorism” to stop committing terrorism in a much weaker country who only led a small revolt in response to the million-dollar soldiers of the US. This so called “War on Terrorism” is nothing but a hype machine for the current President, George Bush, to leave his mark on the world and hopefully be elected again for a second and final term. We, the people have to realize that the sole point of this “war” is to do nothing more than pump up George Bush’s bandwagon, and have to also realize that this “war” is nothing more than a crime against humanity no better than what Osama bin Laden called forth to happen to the twin towers. Remember that almost every crime ever committed has had some sort of reasoning behind it. Whether the reasoning was correct or not is not the issue, but rather that no one just wakes up one day and says: “Hey, let’s ram some planes into a couple towers. Just for fun.” America obviously upset someone with a lot of power at some point, and they paid the repercussions. Those shades of gray are always there to look at, just not always so easily seen, as we’d like. It’s time that the people took a step up from allowing our leaders to handle such delicacies like sending us off to die for a cause that could have been prevented. Everything isn’t as it seems, so before supporting a movement as big as the “War on Terrorism”, make sure you know what you are truly fighting for. This essay was originally written by Eric Bourque on October 12, 2001. © All rights reserved.
