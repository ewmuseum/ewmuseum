Afghanistan 1982

29 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-29-02 Afghanistan 1982 ~~~ It was hot in Afghanistan. Hotter than anything Private Stanislav and Private Groshinko had ever experienced in The Motherland. For four years the Soviet Union had invaded into Afghanistan, poised to destroy the Mujahideen regime and install it’s own puppet government. But as for Stanislav and Groshinko, they and the rest of their platoon were merely supposed to help control and attain a nearby Afghan village. “It is hot… too hot…” growled Stanislav, a young eighteen year old Russian just entering into the Soviet war campaign. The Russian Bear as his childhood friends would call him, Ivan Stanislav was an impressive 7’ tall and weighed 340 lbs by the time he had gotten out of training camp. While perhaps not suited well for combat as he was a large target, Stanislav was a morale booster nonetheless. A proud Russian and the first to crack a joke followed with a boisterous laugh. Groshinko was not nearly as loud or statuesque as his Russian counterpart. He merely was in this war because he had to be. The conscription law forced him into his service and held him here for four years. Stanislav was different, his father was a proud soldier as was his grandfather, it was customary to be in this position. All Groshinko wanted to do was live. “Da Ivan, it is hot. How can these creatures live in this heat? I bet you they have never seen snow!” Groshinko exclaimed. Stanislav shouldered his Kolishnikov rifle that barely stretched across his broad back and wiped his brow with his sleeve, “Probably not, Groshinko. Come, we’re lagging behind!” Stanislav quickened his pace as the two men walked along a rocky outcropping that rose to the right. To their left, a vast expanse of desert stretched as far as the eye could see. The two men trudged over the rocky rise and down the rocks, ahead of them roughly twenty other men, walking in a rough formation could be seen. All of them wearing their yellow and gray camouflage. Camouflage meant for northern European mountain battles but considered adequate for Desert fighting… one of the many grave mistakes Moscow would make. As Stanislav and Groshinko ran down the hillside to their comrades, they were already engrossed in an in depth conversation. Sgt. Gromov led the group of young soldiers, some conscripts, some enlisted as they neared their destination. Gromov turned and growled at the two soldiers, noting their tardiness, “Welcome back Stanislav….Groshinko. Have fun looking at pretty mountains?” Gromov spit on the rock and continued to walk, he didn’t expect an answer. Stanislav grinned and leaned over to the platoons communications engineer, a man named Krivosheev, “Nyet, big bag of wind, I was looking at pictures of your mother….DYAAHAAHAA!!” Krivosheev joined Stanislav in his almost contagious laughter, “HAA HAA!! That is good Ivan! That is good!!” Gromov turned and glared at the two, instantly causing them to straighten up and look presentable. Corporal Nawroz wiped his head with a handkerchief, “Sergeant Gromov, is it always this hot in this barren land? How can anything live here?” “They are animals,” Gromov growled, “they can live virtually anywhere.” “Not in Siberia,” Stanislav chimed in, “I give them five percent chance surviving in OUR Motherland!!” The tall, young soldier spoke with pride as the other men cheered and raised their hats in the air. The platoon marched onward throughout the desolate land. The sand stung the Russians eyes, a feeling they were unaccustomed to. Slowly but surely the Red Army contingent walked up a rise in the sand and stopped just at the peak. “Groshinko, take a look.” Gromov growled as he lit a cigarette, hardly showing concern. Groshinko scrambled up the hill and peered over the rise. Sure enough a small Afghan village, about 1000 meters away, thrived before him. “800…1000 meters Sir…we’re right on our path.” Groshinko said as he slid down the hill and squatted near the rest of the platoon. “Good…” growled the Sergeant. “You people know your orders. We are to take this shithole village and claim it in the name of the Soviet Union. We do not believe that the enemy has any standing force in the vicinity of the village, but that doesn’t mean you can be lazy. These people will do anything to protect their land….or whatever you want to call it.” Gromov ashed his cigarette, “We need this village because of nearby oasis and the flat land, we can use as landing strip. We attack in a few hours, I need to use the radio…” Gromov took Krivosheev’s radio and distanced himself, leaving the young, scared soldiers to their leisure. Privates Stanislav, Grau, Koz’ma, and Groshinko sat together in a circle, chatting with one another while Gromov continued to radio back to the main force. “So,” Koz’ma said, an older ex convict that actually volunteered to be in the army, “you younger boys might have a future after this war, if it ever ends. Me? I’ll be here forever and I’ll probably die here, but what does the Motherland’s ‘finest’ have in store for the rest of the world, huh?” The three other men sat nervously and wondered, finally Grau, a hardened Russian that loved the fight spoke up, “Whenever I return home, I’m going to go into politics! Who knows, twenty years from now I may be Premier of entire State!” The men chuckled, but Grau was adamant, “I’m going to be a hero here my friends, I assure you! Everyone will know who Knut Grau was, I assure you! Watch me whenever we butcher those dogs over the hill, you’ll see! No one can stop me!” Groshinko produced a bottle of vodka and took a swig of it, “To Premier Grau! May we all vote for him, and may our five year plans succeed! Ha ha ha!” “What about you, Bear?” Koz’ma said, indicating to a silent Ivan Stanislav, “What are your great plans for Mother Russia.” Private Stanislav ran his huge boot into the sand and kicked it, watching it flitter into the wind. He shrugged, “I do not know, my friends. I am a simple man. I do not like politics and I do not like war.” He swallowed, “I want to live a simple life in my Motherland, perhaps with a farm. I think that would be nice… with some animals and a garden.” Grau snorted, “You’re fighting in Afghanistan, don’t be thinking about stuff like that.” “I’m not a fighter really… no matter how big I am. Those politicians, those soldiers, they wish to fight forever.” Ivan paused and shook his head, “I do not wish to fight for the rest of my life.” “For a big boy,” Grau growled, “you sure are yellow.” “Shut up Grau.” Groshinko retorted. “Perhaps I am Yellow,” Private Stanislav sneered back, “but there is a lot of Red inside of me too. I love Russia! I would do anything for her! But… but… I don’t want to kill for her. Kill these people, no. And I don’t want to fight a global war, bomb innocent people’s homes, no, I do not wish to do that. I want to live a quiet life with a family. Two little boys and a wife. That is all. I will fight now, and whenever I get out I will make Russia proud, but in a simple way.” Ivan thought for another long moment, “Maybe I will be a singer. I want to make my country proud. Perhaps I will sing my national anthem and become famous in that way! Eh? Make the little boys and girls of Russia hear my voice singing about our Motherland. Yeah… I would like to be a singer. Grau scoffed, but Koz’ma smiled, “You have a big heart Ivan, it shows. Not all men are fighters, no matter how big they are.” Ivan smiled, it meant a lot to him. He had thought at one time that maybe he’d be a politician, but he didn’t like it. He disliked the capitalistic governments, but he didn’t hate the people like everyone else. To Private Stanislav, they were just like him, people caught up in a big politicians war. Just as another conversation started, Sergeant Gromov returned. The men gathered around to listen to him. “All right. They want us to attack now.” Gromov whispered. “Attack now?” Yelped Krivosheev as he took back his radio, “It’s the middle of the day Sergeant! They can see us coming down the hill!” “Damnit I know boy!” Gromov hollered, perhaps too loud for his own good, “Headquarters says it’s mainly militia, nothing more and nothing less. They are said to merely have busted flintlock rifles and one jeep in their possession.?” “Is there a gun mounted on that jeep?” Grau interjected. “Nyet,” answered Gromov, “They want the place cleared of civilians and militia. Clean them all out.” “Why not bomb it?” said a soldier. “That would be too easy,” said another, “they want their boys to shoot them up instead.” “Quiet!” Gromov barked as he wiped his eyes, “They don’t know we’re here so we have the element of surprise. The entire village must be secured.” Gromov nodded to the men as they stood up and got ready. They were scared, almost all of them for most of them had never been in actual combat. Stanislav remembered seeing the tapes of Russians that had been captured, how tortured they had been by the savages from Afghanistan. He’d never let that happen to him. “Alright….CHARGE!!!!” Gromov roared and burst up the hill and over the other side. The other soldiers followed suit, struggling up the uneven terrain and over the side! All twenty of them barreled towards the rickety village who housed no more than fifty people. Twenty Russian Red Army soldiers charged against an unbeknownst Afghan village. The men of the town weren’t ready for the Russians. Why would a superpower like the Soviet Union attack their town. But as the women screamed and grabbed their kids, they knew it was coming. All they had built and lived for was about to be smashed, like a massive hammer on an anvil. “Fight Comrades!!” screamed Nawroz, “Fight for the glorious Soviet Union!!” Grau opened fire with his AK-47, riddling a small clay house full of holes as he neared the western side of the village. An Afghan male exited the building with a rifle of some sort and fired, but a quick shell to the chest that followed with three more to the stomach stopped him dead on his feet and knocked him into the wall. Chickens scattered around Private Stanislav and Groshinko as the first shots were fired, but the Soviet soldiers had descended upon the village too quick for the Afghans. From behind a well two soldiers popped up and fired, barely missing Stanislav and Groshinko. Groshinko leveled his sub-machine gun and fired at the two men who ducked behind the stone well. Stanislav dove to the right behind wooden cart. Krivosheev rushed towards Stanislav. But Krivosheev never made it. The soldier from the well popped up again and fired. The bullet hit Krivosheev in the throat and knocked him onto his back, sending blood spurting from the wound high into the air, his tortured gasps for air turning into gurgles as he flailed about. An explosion rocked a nearby wooden house, blasting its windows out and sending two men rushing from it clearly on fire. Stanislav watched in shock as the men flailed about and fired wildly. They screamed in a way that Ivan had never heard before! Gunfire echoed through the area while Stanislav snapped out of his trance and turned to Groshinko, who stood with his back against a wall and looked back at his massive Comrade. He nodded to Ivan and Ivan nodded back. Stanislav shot to his feet and flipped the cart over on its side. Bullets fired toward him and hit the cart, but the rifles weren’t strong enough and the strong wood stopped them before ever hurting the young Russian. Ivan roared and shoved forward, using the cart as a shield against the bullets and drawing the fire of the enemy, which gave Groshinko enough time to pop around the corner and silence both Afghans, who were not paying attention to him Ivan turned and hollered, “Krivosheev!!!” He lumbered to his flailing friend as did Groshinko, but they both knew it was too late. Krivosheev shivered uncontrollably, his body painted in his own blood. His eyes open and his breathing labored, he was a dead man with a pulse. Ivan put his hand over Krivosheev’s throat, tears in his eyes, “It is okay my friend, it is okay….” Krivosheev’s hand shot up and grabbed Stanislav’s arm. He coughed violently and opened his mouth to say something, but thick, frothy red blood came out and dripped to the ground before his hand slowly slid down the large arm of Stanislav and fell. He was dead. It was the first time Ivan Stanislav had seen someone die. “He’s gone Ivan, come on!” Groshinko screamed. Ivan turned and saw the jeep begin to pull away, but Koz’ma tossed a grenade into it’s rear end and blew it apart, sending the frame flipping in the air and smashing the man inside. “SECURE THE AREA!!” screamed Sergeant Gromov from somewhere throughout the village. “Farewell Comrade Krivosheev, I will not forget you.” Ivan whispered as he put his bloody fingers over the eyelids of Krivosheev and closed his eyes. “Let check that building there Ivan! Come on!!” Groshinko screamed and ran towards a large adobe building with a large wooden door. The two men shouldered the door and knocked it clean over, charging into what appeared to be a kitchen of some sort. “I’ll check upstairs Ivan, be careful!” Stanislav lumbered across the room and found another door of some sort. He kicked the door in revealing a closet. Hiding in the closet was an Afghan woman, her face covered in a shawl, and two little girls with her, both probably around five or six years old. “NOOO!!!” she screamed, terrified as she clutched her two children close to her. “Nyet,” Stanislav said in an attempt to not look dangerous, “it is okay, I will not hurt you.” Ivan shouldered his rifle as footsteps could be heard in the background. “Kill them, Private Stanislav.” It was Sergeant Gromov. “They are merely civilians, Sergeant.” Stanislav said as he turned, “They are not a threat to us or the Motherland.” “Our orders are to kill them all, Private.” Gromov glared at Stanislav, blood splattered on his face, “Do it.” Ivan turned and looked at the woman, tears streaming her face. Her children sobbed, horrified. “Sir….” “DO IT PRIVATE STANISLAV!!” Gromov pulled out a revolver and pointed it at Ivan Stanislav, “In the name of the Soviet Union! In the name of the People’s Republics, you WILL follow orders! Kill them like the dogs that they are!!!!” He turned slowly and faced the woman as she stared at him with pleading eyes, horrified eyes. He raised his rifle as she screamed again and hid her children’s faces, clutching them both close to her chest. Stanislav stared straight into her eyes and tightened his grip…. Ba-BAM!! Ba-BAM!! Ba-BAM!! Ba-BAM!!! The bullets echoed in his memory. The tortured screams forever a reminder. Krivosheev’s lifeless body. The jolt of the gun. Gromov. The warmth as he stood there and watched, warmth of the liquid as it drained over his face. Ba-BAM!! Ba-BAM!! Ba-BAM!! Ba-BAM!!! Ba-BAM!! Ba-BAM!! Ba-BAM!! Ba-BAM!! Ba-BAM!! Ba-BAM!! Ba-BAM!! Ba-BAM ~~~ Alexei Ruslan forced in the door just as Ivan Stanislav shot up from his bed, covered in his wet, hot sweat. “Ivan!? Wake up!!” Ivan breathed heavily, tears in his eyes, he gasped deeply. It was just a dream. A flashback. It was twenty years ago. He looked at Alexei, his eyes full of pain and fear. “Ivan? Are you… okay?” Alexei whispered. He had never seen his friend like this. Ivan rubbed the sweat from his face… so warm and thick… just like then… he swallowed, “I… I am fine, Alexei. I am fine.” He looked at the clock and nodded in his cold tone, “I am fine. Come, I slept in… it’s time for our next fight.” He pushed it away like he always had and kept it away from everyone else. But those screams echoed in his mind constantly…
