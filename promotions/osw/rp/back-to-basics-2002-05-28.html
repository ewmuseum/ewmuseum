Back to basics

28 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 28, 2002 It had the same look as many of the buildings on Toronto's waterfront. It was three stories of brick, with only a few windows.. rather featureless. It had been built by a small shipping company at the turn of the century, and had served them well through two world wars. But changing economics had put them out of business, and the warehouse had been unused since the seventies. Hobos and rats, drug dealers and gangs were her only contents through 25 years, and the city was looking to raze the site. But in mid 2000, someone came forward with the deed, and a business plan. The city was impressed, and granted the license not only renovate, but to run a bar. It was hoped that the area could be revitalized by a new business. And the plan was a startling success. 'Ground Zero', as the bar was named, had proven to be a huge success. Two thirds of the ground floor was taken up by a sports bar and eatery, serving some of the best food in the city, at reasonable rates. Not fine dining by any means, but a solid menu, covering all styles of bar and pub food. But it was the downstairs that was Ground Zero's true attraction to Toronto's sports fans. On both levels, there were televisions everywhere. In each booth in the pub, there was a tv where the patrons could turn on any sporting event available while they sat. This was possible downstairs too, but on a grander scale. The walls themselves were composed of giant televisions, and were always showing the events surrounding the Jays, Raptors, and Leafs. Boxing, curling, the olympics, it was all available. This, coupled with all the interactive items like batting cages, hockey simulators, and free throw rooms, had instantly made Ground Zero Toronto's sports mecca. But at any given time, at least half the televisions were devoted to wrestling, showing the events of the week in the various federations, and the highlights of the past. And whenever there was a PPV, Ground Zero went into full wrestling mode.. nothing else went on downstairs. Only the playoffs could interfere, and at that, only by taking over half of the room. Ground Zero was serious about it's wrestling. And why not? Ground Zero was owned by a retired World Champion. His devotion to the life he'd once led, the life that had given him the money to create the hotspot, was understandable. And half the attraction for some wrestling fans was the fact that the rest of the building was devoted to living space for the owner. He wasn't seen very often, however. The closest most fans got to him was seeing one of his cars zoom in through the garage door. The upstairs windows were black, and the only access had a security system so strict that anyone entering an incorrect code was connected by videophone to the police. So when a black Grand Cherokee with tinted windows zoomed into the alley and headed for the quickly opening garage door one afternoon, the few patrons outside waved, and watched the door close before heading back into the bar. As the garage door closed, the drivers door of the Jeep flew open, and Kanaida stepped out. A few bruises were evident on his face from the beating he'd taken at the hands of Winters on Slam. It was ironic, he thought to himself. After what had happened to him in his first Slam match, he'd decided to take a week off, and get back into ring shape... but the tag match had thrown all that off. He needed to do something drastic to make a change. A talk with his best friend had only reinforced that feeling. With a shake of his head, Kanaida hung up the Jeep's keys along with the seven other sets on the wall, and reached for the elevator button. But he stopped himself, and grinned. 'Waste no opportunity' he grumbled to himself, and headed for the staircase in the corner. He took the steps three at a time, and headed for the top floor. He was just starting to feel a slight burn when he reached the top floor. He entered a code on a keypad, and opened the door to his living space. Home sweet home. He'd renovated the hell out of the wharehouse, making it into a gorgeous loft apartment. A gigantic master bedroom, two guest rooms, an office, a kitchen, dining room, three full baths, a formal living room, a tv room, and the nessecary laundry facilities. And of course, the trophy room. He had designed the place so that he could vanish here, and leave the world behind. And for a time, the seclusion had been wonderful. But the time to sort things out had only given him more issues to deal with, more things remembered from a shadowy past, and so he'd come back into the spotlight to busy himself once again. Maybe if he got back to living, he could put the ghosts to rest. Entering the master bedroom, Kanaida quickly changed into his gym clothes, and headed back to the second level, his gym. This floor had been left alone for far too long, as Kanaida had gotten out of his workout routine. It felt good to get going again, to get into shape again. And this floor had everything he needed to do just that. Around the perimiter was a track. A basketball halfcourt was there, for idle burning of calories. Free weights and fat-burning machines.. stairmasters and their ilk.. populated one corner, along with an assortment of heavy bags and speed bags. And at one end, looming over the rest of the room like a dark tower, was a ring. Any smile on his face quickly dropped off at the sight of that ring, and the memory of the embarasment of the past two weeks. He dropped his towel, and hit the track. Six hours later, he found himself taking a break. He stood at the window, and looked through the privacy glass at the patrons of his bar below. But mostly he stared at the nearby pier, where just over a year ago, he'd briefly killed Wippit Guud. Back when The Northern Wonder had been a force.. not a farce. Back in the day that he'd had what it took to be the best. And with a look of anger and determination on his face, he dragged his already tired body back to the stairmaster, and pushed himself harder... ever harder.
