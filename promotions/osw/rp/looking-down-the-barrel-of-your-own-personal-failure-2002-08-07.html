Looking down the barrel of your own personal failure

7 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-7-2002 Holy shit, that’s a long way down. Sometimes, before you die, you tend to make strange observations. Wonder how long I’ll fall for… When faced with eternal sleep, the mind tends to act like things are going like normal. Wonder if I should hock a loogie first? Oh wait a second. You people out there in the reading audience are probably wondering what’s going on right now. “Hey, this is a Doug Troy promo and they’re talking about death? Oh man.” Well, don’t worry. Doug isn’t sick or about to get into a drunk-driving accident or anything like that. But then I might land on the loogie. That’d suck, dying on top of a loogie. Nope, everything he’s about to do is going to be of his own will and cognizance. I’ll be the Guy Who Died On Top Of A Loogie. That’s no way to go out. From the top of the One First National Center tower in Omaha, the world looks like how a king must see his kingdom. He can survey it all from a comfortable position without even having to bend his head. If he only bent his head downwards, he’d be able to survey the workers milling about like ants. Doug Troy didn’t feel like a king from his perch on the roof of the One First National Center tower. With a dirty t-shirts, tattered jeans, a ratty beard and unkempt hair, he certainly didn’t look like one either. But there he was, King Shit of Turd Mountain, presiding over nighttime Omaha with his royal helpers Jim Beam and Jack Daniels (and foreign advisor Jose Cuervo). Those two were the ones responsible for getting him in this position and chances are they’d watch the situation end. Everyone would. The people down there would get the first look at the end of the drunken depressed situation that Doug’s life had turned into. They’d get a good long look at it. And then the news would bring Doug’s death into every home in the country, live and uncut with bunches of little stock tickers running underneath his legacy. In the wrestling arenas where Doug used to work, he’d be mourned for a few shows. They’d do a tribute, ring the bell a few times, and then forget he ever existed. The only people who’d remember Doug’s life would be the tape collectors and traders who’d sell off “The Show Where Doug Loses His Will To Live” for $15 plus shipping and handling. An entire legacy reduced to a ringing bell and maybe a few crude jokes from asshole recappers who fancied themselves to be the next George Carlin or Dennis Miller. Time to fly, Doug. Doug looked down and blinked a few times. He’d been thinking for the past few minutes on whether or not he’d watch the ground get closer before he went splat and decided that if he was never going to see again, then he might as see all he could right now. He looked again, then started to bring his right foot forward, dangling it off the ledge. Beneath his right foot lay 634 feet of air. After that, the concrete that made up Dodge St would be there to catch him with ungentle but never-missing hands. He put his foot out farther and his balance started to shift… It’s funny how the mind and body interact against each other when you most want them to be cooperating. For instance, the mind could be praying to the heavens above that you don’t fart in front of a very attractive lady while the body goes “Fuck it” and lets it rip. Or how when your mind tells you to “not eat that steak because it’s bad for you” but your body has already started shoveling forkfuls of steak into your mouth. Or when your mind is complete intent on ending its own life, but the body goes “Fuck you I’m staying right here.” In every conflict, the body wins because it controls all the important parts of the human being. In Doug’s case, the body exerted its will over his drunken mind with a sudden case of vertigo and balance loss. He started to lean backward, away from the open Omaha air and back toward the roof. The body finished it’s domination of Doug’s mind by making Doug’s legs go limp, dropping him ass-first to the rooftop of the One First National Center tower. Jesus Christ, I can’t even commit suicide correctly. Doug tried to stand up, but at the moment his legs didn’t feel like cooperating. After a couple attempts, he gave up and sat still. Suddenly, the roof access door opened up and a man in a security uniform stepped out into the night air. The man looked around for a moment, then pulled a cigarette from his pocket and sparked his lighter. He took a slow drag, savoring the flavor as he noticed Doug. “Hello?” he called out. “Who’s there?” The guard flicked his flashlight on and pointed it at Doug, who shielded his eyes from the glare. “Who’re you? What’re you doing up here, it’s off-limits!” The guard walked toward Doug, who tried to get up but his legs were still taking a nap. The guard stopped a few feet away from Doug and pointed his flashlight at the scattered empty bottles on the rooftop. “Throwin’ a one-man party up here, eh?” The flashlight panned back over to Doug, who shielded his face from the light. The guard looked closer at him, tilting his head a little bit. “Hey, you look kinda familiar. Yeah, I recognize you from somewhere…” “You…” Doug coughed out, a serious case of drymouth catching up with him. “You ever watch wrestling?” “Yeah… wait, are you Doug Troy? From OSW?” “Yes to the first part, ‘I dunno’ to the second.” The guard kept the flashlight pointed at Doug, but his posture changed to indicate that he wasn’t as tense as he was before. “So, what’re ya doing up here, Mr. Troy? Seems to be a little bit late for sightseeing, and the amount of booze you got up here would be enough to get all of UNO drunk.” “I was… I dunno, thinking.” “Thinking? About what?” “Stuff. I dunno… hell, I dunno about pretty much anything nowadays. I dunno what I’m good for, whether or not I still got a job…” The guard turned the light off and took a seat near Doug. “Thanks for turning that light off, man.” Doug said while rubbing his eyes. “Don’t mention it. So, why aren’t you wrestling anymore?” “I stopped caring.” Doug replied matter-of-factly. “Stopped caring? Why?” the guard asked. Doug fidgeted a little, trying to get comfortable on the very uncomfortable roof. “Well, it’s just… I feel like I just can’t win anymore. No matter how hard I try, there’s always some huge roadblock in my path that I just can’t clear. I lose and lose and lose, and now the fans don’t want me around anymore…” The guard turned to Doug. “Hey now, don’t say that. I gotta younger brother, about 10 years old. He used to idolize you, no matter what. He didn’t care if you jobbed to Hank or whatever it is that the weaselly lookin’ guy on PCW TV says. He wanted to be just like you.” “Who’d want to be like me?” Doug asked. “Who’d want to be a washed-up wrestler with a bad leg and neck at the age of 28? Who’d want to work as hard as they could for YEARS only to have someone play the system against you rather than actually wrestle?” “So… are you going to quit or something?” “I SHOULD. The longer I stay in wrestling, the worse things’ll get. It isn’t how good you can wrestle anymore, it’s all about how to play the system. I mean, look at Ed. He wrestled his entire life, busting his ass all his life… and now he’s dead. He’s dead because the system played him instead of him playing the system! And now the system wants my blood too…” Doug started dry sobbing, his drunkenness making it hard for him to shed tears. It’s his pity party and he’ll cry if he wants to. The guard, noticeably uncomfortable from Doug’s tirade, looked around the Omaha skyline. “Maybe, Mr. Troy, you should try again.” Doug turned to look at the guard. “I mean, but use a different plan. You can’t just expect the world to adapt to you. You gotta adapt to the world! You can’t fight the world, my friend. You can only hope to change yourself to fit its needs, not vice-versa.” Doug slowly stopped sobbing and turned to the guard. “So… you’re saying that it’s better to change yourself than to try to change the world around you?” “Exactly.” Doug nodded at the guard, who started to stand up. “And now, it’s time for you to go home and get some rest. I’ll clean up up here, you just go home and sober up. Alcohol is the devil when you’re depressed, you know that? Kick that bottle and you’ll feel a whole lot better.” The guard offered his hand to Doug, who took it. The guard pulled Doug to his feet and helped to steady him. “Thank you, Mr…” “Smith. John Smith.” Doug tilted his head quizzically at the guard, who chuckles. “Don’t worry, I get that look all the time. Now you head home.” Doug started walking to the roof access door, his head swimming with drunkenness. However, he was more concerned with the guard had told him. Better to change yourself than to change the world, huh? And with that thought, Doug experienced an epiphany. He knew now what had to be done. He knew how to get his life sorted out. He knew what he had to do to get back into the ring and WIN. No more drinks. No more pity parties. It was time for action. Tomorrow, Doug would be reborn into a better version of himself. Tomorrow. I’m back.
