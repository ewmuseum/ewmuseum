The three geniuses fled across the parking lot, and the angry mob followed.

9 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/9/02 The three geniuses fled across the parking lot, and the angry mob followed. The parking lot outside the Tulsa Convention Center was vast, stretching out under the night sky in what might have been an endless field of black concrete. Two hours ago, if you had told the three members of The Think Tank that they’d end up running for their lives from an angry mob, they’d have turned their noses up at you and called you an ignoramus. “Well THIS is a fine spot you’ve gotten us into!” shouted OSW Commissioner “The Scholar” Howard Porter. “We can play the blame game all night long, Howard, but it won’t save us from getting lynched!! Or perhaps you’d like to try and reason with them, again? We all saw how well THAT went!” Alan Kriegman responded, sneering to his old friend as he ran alongside him. Professor Herbert D. Dorkiman, who had been lagging behind slightly, cried out to his two comrades as he heard them argue. “Gentlemen, Gentlemen! If you two would PLEASE cease your bickering, you’ll realize that we can’t run from them forever! We need to think of something quite quickly!” Let’s go back a bit… It was immediately following the July 6th Slam. Alan Kriegman, known to some as ‘evil itself’, was walking down a hallway in the Tulsa Convention Center. Like the rest of the OSW roster, he was on his way out of the arena. He and his fellow masterminds behind “The Vax” were hardly traveling in opulence, as they would be riding to their next destination in PHD’s beige, 1963 Dodge Dart. Herbert said it would be fun. They could have meaningful, intellectual conversations, he explained. It would promote stable unity, he reasoned. Alan agreed because it saved him money on airfare, it was better than having to ride with the mundane, generally annoying public, and he could have the back seat all to himself. A voice called out from behind him as he walked toward the exit. “Alan, Alan I say, wait for me!” Recognizing the voice immediately, the evil supergenius stopped in his tracks and turned to face none other than his fellow Vax, Professor Herbert D. Dorkiman. Carrying a rather bulky duffel bag over his scrawny shoulder, PHD came trotting up the man in the black suit. “Alan, my friend, we would do well to stick together backstage. We’ve incurred the wrath of an absolute gaggle of inferiors. Any of us could be ambushed at any time…” muttered PHD as he suspiciously peered around the area through his thick black-rimmed spectacles. Kriegman returned PHD’s paranoia a cocky little smirk. “They all fear us, Herbert. When the villagers are frightened, they grab the pitchforks, light up some torches, and band together.” The little man in the labcoat and the Dr. Who t-shirt gave a nod to his friend. “Yes…let’s just hope they don’t come knocking on our castle door.” “Agreed. Where’s Howard? I want to get out of here before things get…tricky.” “I’m right here, my friends!” exclaimed a third voice from down the hall. Alan and Herbert turned towards the source of the voice, the final member of the Think Tank, Howard Porter. “I overheard your conversation, and I’m ready to depart” he said, indicating the brown leather satchel in his right hand. “Alan,” began PHD “where’s your bag?” “Oh I made Armour carry it for me.” the evil supergenius responded with a casual shrug. Seemingly a bit miffed at Alan, Porter raised an eyebrow to him. “Armour is not a butler, Alan. He’s a mercenary. More importantly, he’s MY mercenary! If he carries anyone’s bag, it’ll be mine!” Alan rolled his eyes, and in response, spoke in a high, girly voice. “*I’m Howard, I can’t share my goons.” “You’ll have your own soon enough,” stated the Commissioner, “I don’t see why you can’t wait.” “You two can be more disagreeable than a pair of Jem’Hadar shock troopers without ketrosel white! Now come, to the transport shuttle.” PHD interjected. “Let’s.” Alan said. “Quite.” Porter agreed. With that, the Think Tank set off down the hallway, totally unaware what they were getting themselves into. Before long they reached the exit, a sharp right turn (a “blind turn”, so to speak) in the hall which led to the bottom floor of the parking garage. Their method of transportation would be parked close by…in the nearest handicapped spot. That was Kriegman’s idea. He insisted that nobody else would need it. Besides, how many handicapped people worked for OSW? Alan was the first Vax to turn the corner. Before either Howard or Herbert could do the same, their diabolical friend suddenly flung himself back around the corner, knocking both of them to the ground in the process. His stablemates cried out as they hit the hit the floor, with Alan falling on top of them. “Gah! What the devil are you doing?!” exclaimed Howard as he fell. Kriegman scrambled to his feet, looking to his friends in panic as they got up. He gasped to them in deep, fearful breaths “Alright…slowly look around…the corner…” While Alan leaned against the wall, Porter and PHD both curiously peeked around the corner… The beige, 1963 Dodge Dart was parked right where they left it. Now, a rather large crowd of people stood around it, carrying torches, rotten vegetables, and you guessed it, pitchforks. Eyes wide open, they turned back to Alan, identical looks of fear on their faces. Kriegman nodded, his eyes equally wide. “Y-you don’t think they saw you, do you?” asked PHD to Kriegman. The answer to this question became fairly obvious when a plump tomato suddenly flew in from off camera and splattered on the wall nearby. Herbert looked to Porter, then Porter to Alan, then Alan to Herbert. The three Vaxes shared a simultaneous gulp. “Perhaps we can reason with them…” Porter suggested. PHD nodded. “It’s possible. Alan?” Alan fearfully peeked out around the corner again, then looked back to his comrades. “Fine…but I’ve got plan B ready in case negotiations fail.” he said while patting a rectangular lump in his suit jacket. They nodded to each other, and slowly turned the corner, stepping into the parking garage. The mob surrounding their car seemed to be made up of what Alan would have called “the mundane, generally annoying public”. “THERE THEY ARE!!!” shouted a female voice within the mob. “YER NOT GETTING AWAY THIS TIME, YOU NO GOOD VILLIANS!!” added another. The Think Tank stood roughly 15 feet away from the lynch mob before them. Professor Herbert D. Dorkiman was the first to make his move, boldly stepping forward with his right hand held up in the traditional Vulcan sign of respect. “Live long, and prosper.” he said to them in a confidant voice. “NNNNNNEEEEEEERRRRRRRRDDDDD!!!!!” bellowed a rather large member of the crowd, receiving several cheers from the others for doing so. Howard Porter was the next to step forward. “Friends, OSW fans, I---“ The OSW Commissioner was drowned out by a heavy chorus of shouts and boo’s, and threw his arms up when the rotten produce began to fly. Alan Kriegman scowled at the big unruly mob and stepped forward, reaching into his suit jacket. “Negotiations have failed! Setting PalmPilot to vaporize!” he yelled to his friends over the jeers. Knowing what he was about to do, Howard and Herbert both immediately tried to stop him. “No!” “Don’t do it!” But their pleas were in vein, when Alan whiped out his infamous weapon of choice, the PalmPilot…OF DEATH!!! To you newbies, the PPoD is your standard electronic personal organizer, which also serves as a multi-functional ‘phaser’. Literally the entire mob was cowering away from the weapon. “DIE, simpleton scum!” Kriegman shouted, and pressed the button which would reduce the entire mob to a fine vapor. Unfortunately for the evil supergenius, and quite fortunately for the angry mob, nobody was vaporized. The PPoD crackled with electricity, let out a bright flash of light…and shorted out right in Alan’s hands. Alan looked down at the malfunctioning PalmPilot…OF DEATH(!!!), then back up at the mob. “Now that’s really very interesting. You see, I wasn’t REALLY going to kill you all. I…” Even more furious than before, the mob advanced forward. Alan, Howard, and Herbert wasted no time, and immediately ran like the wind. Soon they were chased right out of the garage, running out onto the enormous parking lot of the arena. The three geniuses fled across the parking lot, and the angry mob followed. The parking lot outside the Tulsa Convention Center was vast, stretching out under the night sky in what might have been an endless field of black concrete. Two hours ago, if you had told the three members of The Think Tank that they’d end up running for their lives from an angry mob, they’d have turned their noses up at you and called you an ignoramus. “Well THIS is a fine spot you’ve gotten us into!” shouted OSW Commissioner “The Scholar” Howard Porter. “We can play the blame game all night long, Howard, but it won’t save us from getting lynched!! Or perhaps you’d like to try and reason with them, again? We all saw how well THAT went!” Alan Kriegman responded, sneering to his old friend as he ran alongside him. Professor Herbert D. Dorkiman, who had been lagging behind slightly, cried out to his two comrades as he heard them argue. “Gentlemen, Gentlemen! If you two would PLEASE cease your bickering, you’ll realize that we can’t run from them forever! We need to think of something quite quickly!” As they ran, Alan spotted something long and black not too far ahead of them. A limo. “Is that who’s I think it is?!” Howard nodded. “Yes, that limo was part of our arrangement! Forward, my friends! Safety awaits! To the limo!” Poor Herbert, who has been having trouble keeping up with his two more athletically gifted friends in their retreat until now, let out a deep moaning sound as he pumped his sedentary legs even faster. The three geniuses did double time towards the limo, quickly reaching it and pounding on the door without hesitation. “Let us in!!!” pleaded Herbert. The angry mob drew closer and closer to the Think Tank, mere moments away from getting them in their grasp. It was then that the door to the limo opened, and our three paragons of intellectuality wasted no time in piling in. The door, however, did not close. The lynch mob charged toward the open door. Imagine their surprise when the enormous mercenary known as Armour climbed out, and without the slightest bit of emotion on his face, stared them down. Their charge ceased, and they in fact actually shrunk away from the monster who less than an hour previously had nearly killed Sebastian Toomes. “Come on!! We can all take him!” one particularly bold voice in the crowd shouts out. “Armour,” Howard’s voice called from within “You know what to do.” Obviously, he did know what to do. As cold and emotionless as a robot, the gargantuan Armour calmly walked toward the pitchfork-wielding, torch waving mob. Believing strength lies in numbers, the small army of disgruntled fans let out pathetic-sounding battle cry, and advanced upon the Think Tank’s hired gun. About 10 minutes later, Armour re-entered the limo without a scratch on his body. The few crowd members that remained standing were staggering away from the scene of devastation, leaving the others laying in motionless heaps on the concrete. As the limo drove away, while Howard and Herbert smiled in contentment at Armour’s display of battle prowess, Alan Kriegman had only one thing to say. “You left my bag at the arena, you big dolt!”
