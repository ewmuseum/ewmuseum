You Can't Save Everyone

14 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-14-02 Our scene opens with a shot of Trevor Hawke, tossing and turning in bed. Suddenly sitting up from his troubled sleep with a start, he stares ahead blankly at the wall, sweat streaming down his forehead and uneasy thoughts whirling through his head. Thoughts of the events that took place earlier in the night. Reaching his hands up to rub his eyes, Trevor realizes that no matter how hard he tries to put them out of mind, these thoughts will be troubling him in the days and weeks to come. He remembers getting the phone call about an hour before Slam went on the air, a frantic Jane Reagan telling him how Sebastian was assaulted in the hospital by that freak of nature Armour. How his injuries were compounded. How he was expected to be out of action for over a month. How with Toomes and Amy out, that he and Seth would have to carry the Elite by themselves in the meantime. Some job they were doing so far. He remembers standing in the ring as PHD, that nerdy fuck, waddled down the ramp to taunt him and Seth. Him and his stupid ass dice and his stupid ass Dungeons and Dragons jokes. And his stupid ass surprise...which really wasn’t that stupid, considering how effective it turned out to be. John Mammoth. Who saw that coming? Nobody could have expected it. Trevor didn’t, that was for sure. He had sworn that no matter who Dorkiman brought out, he would have been ready for it. So much for that promise. Mammoth had torn them apart, and despite what revenge he and Seth may have extracted from PHD’s hind, the fact remained that they were crushed and humiliated in the middle of the ring by a single man. No matter how famous he was, or how frickin’ huge or powerful, he was still one man, and they were former OSW Tag Team champions. And they were manhandled. That is not a way to maintain a dignified reputation, that’s for sure. Dignified would be the last word he’d use to describe himself at this juncture. He remembers the main event on Slam, if you can call that farce a main event. It was more like attempted murder. If Ed didn’t pull through, perhaps the attempted could be removed from the charge. The Think Tank’s organized crippling of Ed was perhaps the most sickening act Trevor had ever witnessed in the fourteen years he had watched professional wrestling, and was the first he actually had to turn away from because it was so sickening to his stomach. He remembers Trashcan Man turning on Ed, striking him in the head. He remembers Ed being..crucified on his barbed wire chair. He remembers Ed’s limbs being crushed by the Think Tank and their cronies. He remembers the show closing with a cradle piledriver delivered off the edge of the stage. He remembers the feelings invoked in him. The rage. The vengeance. And above all, the disgust. Above all, the disgust. When he had feuded with wrestlers in the past, Trevor remembered feeling anger toward them. He still holds hard feelings toward Hessian for the lengths he went to when they were feuding over the Tag Team titles. He remembers wanting to put Da Westsidaz in their place, to show them that all their smack talk was delusions of grandeur. But it was never like this before. Trevor Hawke hated the Think Tank, possibly more than any other group of human beings he had ever come in contact with. They were ruining men’s careers, going beyond the bounds of common decency, and they were destroying the sport that he loved. He hated them, and if it was the last thing he would do, he would make them pay. With a sigh, Trevor lays his head back down on his pillow, looking up at the ceiling. Despite what feelings he may hold, he also knows that there’s nothing he can do about the situation. Especially now. In bed. Looking over to his right, he sees the woman he’s spent the last two nights with. It’s the waitress from the restaurant the other night. Her name’s Julie. She looks absolutely content. Last night, Trevor had thought about taking her away from all of this. A shitty apartment in downtown Cleveland. A shitty job in that shitty restaurant. Shitty friends and a shitty family. A shitty life. She was a nice girl . She deserved better than what she had. After tonight, he had changed his mind. He couldn’t bring her into his life. Not with the sport evolving as it was into a place where chaos and anarchy were the rule of the day. It just wasn’t safe anymore. For anybody. Hell, Vendetta’s valet was just kidnapped last night. He wasn’t going to chance that shit happening to her. Or anybody, for that matter. He wasn’t going to stick someone else’s neck under the headman’s ax. He had enough necks to look out for as things stood. He couldn’t save everybody. He’d be lucky if he could save anybody. It was all quite a bit for Trevor to mull over in his head. Too much for any one man to have to deal with. Yet it wasn’t as if he had a choice. Closing his eyes once more, Trevor tries unsuccessfully to drift back off to sleep. Sitting back up again, he climbs off the bed and begins pulling on his clothes. In a matter of minutes he’s out of the door, beginning to pull it shut behind him. Taking one last look at his would-be damsel in distress, he locks the door behind him. She’d get the picture in the morning. Better for her to think he was an asshole, anyway. At least she’d be safe from wrestling. A shit life was better than the one he was leading now. She was a lot luckier than he had originally believed. And he...well, he had better hope he was a lot luckier than he had originally believed, as well. Because now, more than ever, with the Elite in shambles and the Think Tank running amuck...he’s going to need it. Boy is he going to need it.
