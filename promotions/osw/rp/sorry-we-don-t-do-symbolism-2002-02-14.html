Sorry, We Don't Do Symbolism

14 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-14-02 Our scene opens at the public library. Seth and Trevor walk out onto a black background screen with confused looks on their faces. Both of them look forward and voice their thought simultaneously. “The public library?!” Yes the public library. What, is this not making sense to you here? Trevor scrunches his face up, raising a hand in question. “No, we understand what it’s saying. It’s just that...well, ya see...do you even know who we are?” Of course I do. You’re Seth Masters and Trevor Hawke: the team of Civil Disobedience. The Bad Boys of Suburbia, the team that everyone’s mothers warned them about, the... “Yeah, you know who we are, but do you really know WHO we are?” pipes in Seth. I don’t understand what you’re asking. I just told you... “I mean, do you know what kind of people we are?” clarifies Trevor. You’re wrestlers in the OSW. “No, you fool. You’re not getting the point at all!” shouts Seth, starting to frustrated with the narrator. And the point is? “We’re idiots!” screams Seth. “Speak for yourself,” says Trevor holding up a hand in protest. “I’m no idiot, but I still would sure as hell not be seen in a public library. That kind of thing is damaging to one’s reputation.” Well you two really have no say in the matter. I’m the narrator and I’m the boss. I dictate what goes on around here. “If you were a real narrator, you would know that you’re not supposed to make contact with the characters, you moron.” Trevor spouts, as he points a finger forward accusingly. “Geez, you’re stupider than I am.” chimes in Seth with a look of disgust. Hey, shut up! Now get your asses to the library! NOW! The black screen in the back fades to a scene of your typical generic library. The building, or at least the area that the public is allowed to use, is made up of basically one big room. The floor is coated with trampled green carpet, surprising only by the fact that enough people have transversed its surface to make it appear to be trampled. Tables sit in the middle of a room, with shelves of books lining both the walls and the spaces between them. On the left wall runs a counter covered with computers and monitors. Currently Seth and Trevor are the only people there, aside from a elderly woman sitting behind the librarian’s desk. The emptiness of the place is accentuated as a tumbleweed blows across the floor. Trevor and Seth sweep their gazes around the room’s perimeter. Trevor shakes his head in disappointment at having been placed in such a setting. Seth shakes his head as well, though his reason for doing so is because he is amazed at the sights that surround him. Having never set foot in a library before, he is quite amazed at the spectacle he is now witnessing. He takes a few steps away from Trevor, before dropping to his knees and holding his head in pain. “So many books! So...many...books...” Seth moans. “I don’t know if I can take it, Trevor. It’s driving me...insane!” “Oh deal with it, dumbass,” says Trevor as he walks over to Seth and kicks him in the ribcage. “Now get up. We’ve got...uh...hold on a sec.” Trevor turns to face forward and shouts, “Hey narrator! What’s our motivation here? You just plop us down in this setting and expect us to know what to do. What’s going on here, plotwise and all?” Oh, sorry about that. NOT! “Oh quit holding your petty grudge and do your job right, ass.” Trevor says angrily. Fine, fine. You two are at the library to use the computers, as you don’t have any sitting around your palatial estate, if you catch my drift. “Hey!” shouts Seth, getting to his feet. “You’re not insulting my house, are you?” Well actually I am, now that you mention it. Why? You have a problem with that? “Yeah, I do!” shouts Seth in a tone that suggests if the narrator had an actual physical form, he’d be all up in his face. Watch it, tough guy. You don’t want to cross me. If you think the library is bad, just wait until you feel the brunt of...the ballet. “Eeep!” says Seth, as he lowers his head. “Sorry sir.” That’s what I thought. “Excuse me,” interrupts Trevor. “You threatening my partner is extremely hilarious to me and all, but I was wondering if we could get back on the subject of why we’re here. You said we’re hear to use the computers. Um...just why would we use the computers anyway?” *Sigh* So you can visit Old School Wrestling’s website. All wrestlers promos get streamed onto video that can be downloaded from there. How do you think you’d be able to respond to other people’s promos otherwise? So get on that damn computer over there and look up your opponent’s promos before any more precious time is wasted. “Alright, alright...I get it.” Trevor says, already on his way over to the terminal on the far right of the counter. “You know, Seth, I think perhaps we should buy one of these computer things. It would be a good investment if we never had to come back to this place again.” He shudders at the thought. Seth follows along behind Trevor, seeming to be out of touch with reality. “Cum-pew-ter?” he pronounces, sounding confused. “What are you and that narrator guy talking about, anyway?” Trevor extends his arm to point at the monitor. “We’re talking about these things, Seth. They’re our links to the information superhighway, otherwise known as the internet.” “Ah,” voices Seth. “I think I heard them talking about this on the radio before.” Sitting down in the chair sitting in front of the terminal, Trevor flips the machine on. As it boots up Seth jumps back in fear, getting an incredulous look from his partner in response to his action. “What gives?” asks Trevor. “That screen...it just popped up!” Seth says, flinching away from it. “So what’s the big deal?” a confused Trevor asks, as Seth dives behind a bookcase for cover from the monitor. “Is...is it magic?” he asks, peeking around the corner. “Um...no.” Trevor says, trying to pick words that will not confuse his dimwitted partner. “In fact, I can’t believe you’re so fucking stupid to even consider magic as a possibility. You turn the TV on all the time and you know that’s not magic. Why would this be any different?” “Wait a second...” Seth says, appearing to be in deep contemplation. “Wait, you’re right.” “I knew you’d come to your senses sooner or later.” Trevor says as he clicks on the Internet explorer icon. “I never realized it, but the TV is fueled by magic too!” shouts Seth from behind the bookcase. “The whole world is mad! Mad I tell you!” “Shhhh!” Seth and Trevor look over at the elderly librarian, who has a finger to her mouth in a sign for our duo to tone down a bit. Trevor looks from her to Seth and shakes his head once more in disbelief, as he types in the site address for the OSW homepage. It is only a few seconds before the page comes up and Trevor waves at Seth to come look at the screen. Seth doesn’t seem to eager to do so, though, as he remains planted in his hiding place. “Come on, Seth. Get your ass over here and look at this. Hessian and Azrael Cain both have promos up on this site.” Trevor whispers loudly, as much of an oxymoron as that is. “No way. I’m not going to give the cum-pew-ter demons an opportunity to enter into my body.” Seth whispers back. “There’s not a decent exorcist in a five mile radius, and I’ll be damned if I’ll let myself be possessed for an extended period of time today. I have to watch Friends tonight, and I’m going to have to be in control of my body to completely enjoy that experience. Damn, that Rachel chick is a hottie.” Trevor looks annoyed at the show of ignorance from his friend, but suddenly his eyebrows perk up and he grins slyly. “Fine, stay there, if you want. It doesn’t matter to me. You can hide behind there all day. I’m sure those BOOKS will protect you from the demons.” “Good,” says Seth, still hiding behind the shelf. “Wait a second...” he stops in midsentence as he realizes exactly what the ramifications of Trevor’s sentence were. He was standing...right next to...books? Seth lets out a small yelp as he comes to terms with this, running away from the shelf. He joins Trevor at the computer following his short sprint, pulling up a chair and taking a seat, shivering. Trevor lets out a chuckle as he points to the screen, his index finger landing on an image of Hessian. “Alright, this is the first one. Let’s check this shit out.” says Trevor as he maneuvers the mouse to click on the picture. As he does so, the screen shifts to a video player, upon which Hessian’s first promo begins to play. As it goes on...and on...and on...Trevor and Seth watch with increasing disinterest. Ten minutes later, as it draws to a close, Seth has to shake awake a sleeping Trevor who has fallen asleep at the keyboard. As Trevor lifts his head up groggily he asks hopefully, “Is it over?” “Yeah, thankfully,” responds Seth, as Trevor gives a sigh of relief upon hearing it. The two sit quietly at the computer screen, contemplating what they just saw. Seth breaks the silence by voicing the thought that is in both of their minds. “Trev...” asks Seth, confused. “Why are gothic people so weird?” “No one knows, Seth. No one knows.” replies Trevor, placing a comforting hand on his confused partner’s shoulder. “I suppose years upon years of rejection from social circles can drive a man to recoil from humanity and seek refuge into a cultist type of Satanic behavior.” “Yeah whatever, college boy. What does that mean in English?” questions Seth dumbly. “It means that goths are really just stupid losers who have no other friends. They’re pretty much just hate-filled reject trash that are bitter about their lack of popularity.” states Trevor matter-of-factly. “Is that why Heresian Bloodlust hates us so much? Because they’re jealous? Because their minds are clouded with envy of our natural charisma? Because we have the popularity, now and forever, that they never had and ever will have?” “Sounds like a safe bet to me. But you forgot to say that they were jealous of our good looks.” Trevor says as he runs his hand through his hair. “Oh, how could I forget that. They are SO jealous of our good looks. Can you really blame them, though? Not only do we have looks that just guarantee us poster boy positions in the OSW calendars, but just look at how ugly they are in comparison. Not just in comparison to us, mind you, but in comparison to anyone. Everybody you can think of is more attractive than they are. Hell, I’m sure they’re jealous of Tom Walzak’s ugly mug, too. Did I mention they were ugly? Cause boy they are. Pug ugly, fugly, pug fugly, and just plain not pretty. Was that a good tirade, Trev?” Seth finishes, almost out of breath. “I’d have to say it was one of your better ones, Seth.” says Trevor with a nod. “Sah-weeeeeeeeeet!” Seth pumps his fist in the air excitedly. And for this action he gets another shushing from the librarian. “Shhhhhhh!” she shushes shushedly. Seth sighs and brings his arm back down to his side. “Go ahead and click on Azrael’s. I’m sure it’s not going to be much better, but we might as well look at it while we’re here. It’s not like I want to make a trip back here or anything.” Trevor obliges and, just like the first promo, the second one flashes across the screen. Seth and Trevor are equally uninterested in this one as they were in the first, but at least this one doesn’t last long enough to put them to sleep. As it fades out, a look of disgust appears on Trevor’s face. “Damn those stupid midget managers!” Trevor grumbles. Seth sticks a hand out in defense. “Hey! Be kind to the wee folk, Trev. They have no means of defending themselves.” “Those little fuckers do,” Trevor groans, putting a hand to his still swollen forehead. “Little shits knocked us the fuck out with those crutches. Bastards.” “Who cares, man. They have to hang out with Hessian and Azrael all day long. Isn’t that a much worse punishment already than anything you and I could dish out toward them?” “I guess you’re right,” says Trevor reluctantly. “I’d still like to kick a field goal with those little bastards.” “Why kick a field goal with them when you could do the same with Heresian Bloodlust’s nutsacs. That reminds me. Have those steel toed boots we ordered come in yet?” Seth asks hopefully. “Yeah, they came in this morning.” answers Trevor with a snicker. “Sweet. It’ll just be the third win to add to our flawless win column come Saturday night. We’ll teach those big ass dicksmacks a lesson. You don’t spit into the wind, you don’t tug on Superman’s cape, and you certainly, most definitely do NOT FUCK WITH THE SUBURBS!” The camera pans to show that the librarian at the desk has had enough of the two rambunctious men, as she stands up with purpose. Nobody talks loudly in her library, not even if they ARE the only two people in it. As she shushes loudly and angrily, Seth and Trevor become silent across the room. Her faces furrows up in an expression of extreme hostility as she composes herself. Finally it seems as if she is going to speak. The old woman opens her mouth...and is immediately floored as a book flies through the air and strikes her in the head. She slumps to the ground, unconscious, as from across the room Seth is pumping his fist in the air once more. “Score!” he shouts, as he picks up another book from the counter top. He tosses it up a little and catches it back in his hand. “These things aren’t so bad after all. I mean, they may not be comic books by any means, and they may not have any pictures, but you can’t go wrong with using them as projectile weaponry.” Trevor pushes his chair back and stands up, turning to face the exit of the building. “I take it that’s our cue to leave. You know, considering she’ll probably be contacting the proper authorities when she returns from her blackout spell. If you didn’t kill her, that is. Then we’d have a whole new set of problems.” Seth is already on his way out of the door. “Well let’s just hope for the best and get the hell out of here. I’ve already had way too much excitement today. I’m ready to go home and spend the rest of the day watching TV.” “Sounds like a plan, bro.” Trevor says, joining him. The two men exit the library and begin walking down the sidewalk in the direction of their home, alongside a busy street. It’s your generic suburban setting, trees spotting the landscape, homes lining both sides of the street, hobos passed out on the sidewalk, you know the drill. Seth and Trevor evade the drunken homeless as they transverse the city. They are barely walking for two minutes, however, when they are interrupted by two large men, dressed all in black. Both of them stand about six foot elevenish; one is wearing a Frankenstein monster mask, and the other has tattoos covering his arm. Both appear to be mildly retarded, as is apparent when the masked one opens his mouth to speak. “Dur...I am B..b...b...Bessian and this is my partner Cazrael Dain. We are going to beat you because we are big and evil. Durrr!” Seth and Trevor exchange an amused look before Seth walks up to both of them and grabs each one by the arm. “Sorry,” he says, as he walks them out into the street. “We don’t do stupid symbolism crap in our promos. That kind of stuff, like, went out with the nineties. Now can you please die and get out of our memories before you scar our minds forever?” “Sure thing!” Cazreal says excitedly, as he gives a thumbs up sign. Seth leaves both of the men smiling dumbly in the middle of the street as he joins Trevor back on the sidewalk. The two members of Civil Disobedience continue walking in the direction of their house as a semi truck whizzes down the road and strikes the two parodies of Heresian Bloodlust, sending their pieces flying all over the road. The scene fades out with the head of Bessian flying straight toward the camera. Now that was a fun promo!
