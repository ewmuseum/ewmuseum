Father knows best, but only sometimes

22 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/22/02 Darkness. If you’ve been reading Doug Troy promos since he started his OSW career, you will have noticed several patterns developing. Silence. This is no exception. Once again, a Doug Troy promo opens inside a large dimly-lit room. Dark silence. Why has this become a pattern? Is it the fault of lazy cameramen? Is it the fault of Doug Troy scheduling interviews in dark places? Silent darkness. Or maybe it’s because our author today is having a hard time thinking up new ways to open promos. A light activates. Sure, we can blame the author for being lazy. Hell, he probably is lazy. A figure is illuminated. It’s a cardboard cutout with various red circles drawn on it. But still… no, nevermind. Silent semi-darkness with a dummy in the middle. Who am I kidding? The author is just plain lazy. An explosion occurs offscreen. Milliseconds later, the left half of the cardboard figure’s head is torn clean off. More explosions follow as large holes chew apart the cardboard figure. The person causing the damage stands behind a small waist-high barrier. A large golden Colt 1911 pistol roars from the man’s hand. He holds the gun almost casually, like he’s not even trying to aim it as it continues its berserk assault on the cardboard figure. Right before the gun clicks on empty, the man ejects the spent clip and slaps another one in, resuming his barrage without skipping a beat. As he empties this clip, a door opens in the back of the room, behind the man. Doug Troy steps into the room and immediately covers his ears to protect his hearing. The noise continues until the gun finally clicks on empty. Doug uncovers his ears and starts walking toward the man, who is now twirling the gun around on his index finger like an Old West gunslinger. “Dad?” Doug asked. The man doesn’t respond, probably because the pads covering his ears to protect him from the sound of gunfire are also keeping Doug’s voice out. Doug walks up to the man and taps him on the shoulder. The man, quick as a flash, whirls around and presses the golden gun against Doug’s forehead. Doug yelps as the cold metal meets his flesh and the snarling face of the man comes into view. The face softens, however, when the man realizes who he’s about a second away from killing. “Doug? Jesus, how many times have I told you not to sneak up on me like that?” The man slips the gun into his back holster and takes off the earpads. Doug, who should be in a state of shock from nearly dying, is merely miffed that a gun had been pressed to his forehead. “Sorry Dad. I tried getting your attention by talking to you, but you had those pads on.” “S’okay. So what brings you to the homestead, my injury-prone boy?” Doug raises his eyebrow. “You watched Slam last Saturday? I thought you hated wrestling.” “Eh, yer mom made me watch it. She’s so proud of her little boy…” The man, aka Doug Troy’s father aka Castor Troy, steps over to another booth in what we now know is a large indoor firing range. Castor puts the pads half-on, with the front of the ears being covered and the back open to sound. “…even when he’s getting his ass kicked by large Russkies.” “Hey, he caught me offguard…” “…just like the last time and the time before that, right?” Castor picks up an MP5 sub-machine gun and takes aim. “You know, if you would’ve followed my advice in dealing with him, this never would’ve happened more than twice…” “But…” A loud burst of gunfire drowns out Doug’s response. Inside the target area of the room, another cardboard cut-out is torn to shreds by the hail of bullets. Doug covers his ears and grits his teeth in response. After a long burst of gunfire, the sound ends and Doug takes his hands off his ears. “Dammit, warn me the next time you do that!” Castor puts the gun down and turns to Doug. “Hey, I picked up the gun. That should be even indication that something’s gonna get shot, right?” “I know, but still…” “Whatever, son. Now, why haven’t you followed my advice in dealing with your little problem yet?” Castor says as he walks over to another booth. Doug cocks his head and crosses his arms. “Because killing him would be illegal, dad.” “Hey, how many times do I have to tell you? It’s only illegal if you get caught.” Castor picks up an M-16. Doug sees this and covers his ears as the room is once again filled with the noise of gunfire. Another cardboard dummy is ruthlessly slaughtered by the bullets, entire chunks breaking off as the bullets slam into them. The clip empties and Castor sets the gun down. “Jesus, how much do you spend on ammo for this room?” “Entirely too much, Doug. Entirely too much.” Castor moves to another booth as he speaks. “Anyway, do you have any other ideas about how to deal with him besides murder?” “You got me.” Castor picks up an M-79 “Blooper” grenade launcher, opens the breach and pushes a large shell in there. He flicks his wrist and the breach shuts right as he takes aim. Doug turns away and covers his ears right as Castor fires. The dummy target disappears in an explosion of fire, and when the smoke clears only the flaming head is left on the ground. “You know, psychological warfare is also very effective.” Castor says as he sets the M-79 on the barrier. “Psychological warfare? Well how can I do that?” “You notice how pissed off Ivan was when you tried to tell Tempest about what happened a couple weeks ago with Meanstreak?” “Isn’t that because I called him a few choice names?” Castor shakes his head ‘no’. “No, it was because he has feelings for her. She’s an important part of his life, and he doesn’t want her to know the truth. She’s an open wound on your little adversary, Doug.” Doug ponders this. “Hmm, you’re right. I dunno why I didn’t see that before. So, what should I do with her?” Castor moves to another booth and stops, thinking of an answer. “Kill her.” “What?! No, no way. Wait a second… hold on, I think I have an idea… Did you guys happen to tape the Slam a couple weeks back, where Meanstreak beat Tempest up?” “Nope, sorry.” “Shit. Well thanks.” Doug walks out of the room. “Anytime, son!” Castor says as he grabs the handle to the minigun that’s mounted to the barrier. A large smile crosses his face as he sights the soon-to-be-fucked-up cardboard cutout. Outside the room, Doug has his cell phone out and is dialing a number. Inside the room, the faint chatter of gunfire can be heard. With the number dialed, Doug puts the phone to his ear. “Hi, can I please speak with Ed? Thanks.” A pause. “Ed? Hey, it’s Troy! Yeah, I’m out of the hospital. They should probably name a wing after me, after all the money I’ve given them lately. Hey, I noticed that you got yourself a secretary now. Look’s like you’re moving up in the world, eh?” Another pause, followed by laughter from Doug. “Hehe, anyway, I was wondering if you could do me a favor for Slam? Yeah, could you give me a copy of the Slam before Crowning Glory? Yeah, I need it for a little something I’ve got planned. Could you just have it delivered to my dressing room before the show? Thanks man, I owe ya one.” Doug hangs the phone up and smiles as the promo fades to black.
