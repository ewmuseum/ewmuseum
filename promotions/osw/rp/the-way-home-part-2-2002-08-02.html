The Way Home, part 2

2 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/2/02 Healing may be a slow process for some, but for me it always moved quickly. Like slamming a trap door in my mind, I've always been able to forget the painful things and look forward with a sense of optimism. After all, if something bad happens to you, why dwell on it? Why not turn your eyes forward toward all those possibilities which are surely better than whatever has just made you unhappy. So I was fine not long after the big break with Ivan. Sure I was. And a few short weeks later I had the chance to prove it. ~Rewind, June 17, 2002~ June in Moscow is chilly. The sun keeps up a desperate campaign to warm the hard ground and infuse the people with a sense of life and its possibilities. After the long winter of communism, Russians found themselves thrust into a world of free market nightmares like McDonalds and television. But a few of these they grasped and fit into their world. Wrestling was one of these things. One only had to look at the popularity of Ivan Stanislav to see that. But other wrestlers and organizations had their followings too. Even the fWo in it's efforts to expand, decided a tour was worth doing. And so it was on a dusky gray morning that eleven fWo stars stepped off a bus into Red Square and were greeted with a resounding ovation. Tempest smiled and waved as the light breeze blew her hair about, with it's light blue tint. That tint also appeared in the blond locks of Poison Ivy, her good friend, who waved at the crowd and said to Tempest, 'Ah, violence, the universal language. I think I'm gonna like this town!' Tempest nodded and chuckled as she said, 'I know. What a great idea. I hope we get to do some shows here and stuff.' 'Bet we will,' Ivy said. 'They sure seem to like you.' She nodded to a crowd of teenagers eagerly waving, some adorned in fWo shirts, and a few in the briefly produced but very popular OSW 'Girl Power' shirts. Tempest blinked a few times and then resumed her thousand watt smile to the crowd. She sat with the rest of the visiting stars, eager to answer questions and mingle. The reporters struggled with English and the wrestlers had a few laughs trying to muddle their way through. And then it happened. With all the preparation, all the careful planning, the inconceivable... 'Miss Tempest, have you heard from Ivan Stanislav?' Tempest froze, as if the name were a slap in the face. 'I... um...' 'Will you give him another chance?' 'I...I...' 'You and Ivan were first couple in OSW. Do you think you will share a ring again?' Tempest merely shook her head and after a long moment of silence said, 'No... no I don't think so. I... I don't know Ivan anymore.' Ivy watched her, stunned and cut in, 'What Tempest is trying to say is that her contract with fWo precludes any more work outside it. So.... she's not gonna be working with Ivan or Boris or even Natasha.' Ivy passed the mic down to the next of the stars and hissed at Tempest, 'Hey! Snap out of it! Who the Hell is Ivan Stanikov?' ~~~ Inside a hall that was far more grand than their purpose, the touring wrestlers sat having lunch and chatting. A few of the more prominent reporters joined them, those that had arranged the tour. They soaked in the presence of the wrestlers like the rare treat it was. But uncharacteristically Tempest sat alone, picking at the meal before her, her mind far away as she mumbled, 'I can't face them again.' In the background behind Tempest a figure moved towards her. As the figure neared it's features could be made out. It was a woman in a blue, tight form fitting jumpsuit complete with several military badges. Her raven black hair sat straight on either side of her face as she neared Tempest and paused. 'Excuse me?' the woman said softly, her voice was heavy with a Russian accent, but her English was quite good, 'Is there something wrong?' Tempest looked up and blinked, then laughed at herself and said, 'Oh no... nothing. I uh... well I'm just nervous, that's all.' She looked over the woman's uniform and said, 'So... does every Russian wrestling fan adore Ivan Stanislav or does it just seem that way?' The woman laughed to herself and nodded, 'Oh, everyone adores Ivan Stanislav, miss. He does so much for his country... in a world without much hope, he brings it to them with every paycheck I suppose.' She grinned to herself, 'I adore him too, I suppose.' She blinked and looked at the table for a moment as her mood dropped. Tempest smiled nervously and said, 'I guess I'm just a little surprised. I heard he was having trouble over here. But the people don't seem to care. He really does symbolize something greater than himself here.' She shook her head and said, 'Did, you say you knew him?' 'He surely does symbolize something. He is a driving force against those that oppress our people for things they do not understand.' She licked her lips, 'Yes, he is more than just a man. Far more than that….' She paused, stuck in some sort of thought, and then snapped back and nodded to Tempest, 'Oh yes, I know Ivanushka quite well.' She sighed and swallowed, 'At one time….I thought that Ivanuska and I would be more than just…friends…Comrades as he would say. But… something happened, I suppose.' Tempest gulped, seemingly more nervous than before and said, 'What happened?' The woman sighed and sat down beside Tempest; the two looked to be around the same age. She spoke softly, as if not to let anyone else here, 'Believe it or not, there is a side to Ivan that few people ever see. A very honorable, very.. chivalrous side to him. I know you probably think I am lying, but believe me, sometimes he can be a very surprising gentlemen.. albeit a bit awkward.' Tempest smiled in spite of herself. The woman thought for a moment and looked down at the table, 'Ivanushka and I….I once thought that we had something. I thought that perhaps I was the….woman that would spend the rest of her life with him.' She shrugged, 'But that was not so. He has been wrestling in United States for over a year now and while he was over there he met this woman. It was quite recent, as a matter of fact.' She smiled to herself, 'He was so much different when he was seeing her. He was very…alive. Some people did not appreciate it, but most did. I was jealous.. but he was happy which I think he has never truly known.' She paused, 'But something happened and she left him after a misunderstanding. He was crushed. I had never seen him so upset. I thought that perhaps I could make him feel better and I went to see him.' 'I remember exactly what he said when I professed my love to him, 'Illyana…whether I wish it or not…my heart will forever belong to someone else.' ' She sniffed, 'He meant the woman…Tempest.. I believe her name was…..he is in bad shape, Ivanushka, he misses her dearly. Whenever it rains he cannot sleep because he fears she is afraid. That is how Ivan is.' 'I know...' said Tempest quietly. 'Did Ivan ever tell you what that misunderstanding was about?' Illyana Bukharin shook her head, 'He never told me specifically what it was about. Ivan had said that he tried to save something, and instead lost it all. I heard that he left her in wrestling ring and she got hurt, and then he lied to her.' She furrowed her brow, 'That doesn't sound like Ivan. He cared for her then, I believe. It does not sound like he would maliciously lie to her. Perhaps he was confused, that is what I think, at least.' Tempest nodded slowly and said, 'You know, his history in the ring hasn't been all that trustworthy. But you don't think he'd lie on purpose? That he'd scheme like that to keep someone he... valued?' Illyana shook her head, 'If Ivan wishes to gain something, or if he has something to fight for, he will do almost anything to obtain it, that is true. That is why he is so successful. He is resourceful, strategic, and willing to make bold decisions.' She thought for a second, 'But also, if Ivan cares for someone he'd die for them. I have no doubt in my mind. If he lied, he probably thought it was in the person's best interest. Not even in his best interest, I would say. Ivan is very giving and when it comes to things like that, he usually puts the person in front of himself.' She sighed, 'I feel bad for him. I think it hurts him that he thought he was doing the right thing. It appears that he tried to divert hurting the woman…but instead he accidentally hurt her more.' 'Yes he did...' Tempest said quietly. She stared at her plate and breathed in and out unevenly, trying to stifle an unbidden welling of tears. 'But all those people love him and have faith in him regardless of all that. They can believe in him when most of them have never even met him. And they all look at me so hopefully. I can't face them.' Illyana looked over at Tempest as it dawned on her who she was talking to. She blinked hard once, 'Y..y..you're…Tempest?' The smaller woman nodded slowly. 'Yup. Nice to meet you. I didn't realize Ivan had such good friends.' Illyana swallowed, dumbstruck, and shook her head, 'I..I am sorry, I did not realize that it was you. M..My name is Illyana. I hope that I did not cause you much pain.. I did not mean to.' Tempest shrugged and said, 'No, it's ok. I just... I thought he was out of my life and over with. I wanted it to be over with. But now...' She sighed with a small gesture toward the door to the square. 'What I see in those people's eyes is almost frightening in a way. There's so much faith in him...' She looked up and into Illyana's eyes. '...that I find myself asking 'Where was mine?' ' Illyana looked back into Tempests eyes, the eyes of the woman who had taken Ivan from her, and sighed. 'You are the only one that can answer that, I am afraid. But I know this, Ivanushka misses you. I believe he always will. Sometimes in order to see the true person, one has to look very deep. You may have never lost faith in him; I do not believe he lost faith in you.' Illyana breathed in deeply, 'After all the horrible things he has been going through, it is obvious that he cares for you.' 'I thought all his problems would be solved after I left. And I thought mine would be too,' Tempest said with a sigh. 'But it's not going to just go away.' She shook her head and said, 'What was it that you called him?' Illyana smiled warmly to herself, 'Ivanushka.' Tempest smiled a little and said, 'What's it mean?' 'It means….' Illyana trailed off for a moment, 'it means that you care for him.' She swallowed, 'We Russians have many ways to say our names. For Ivan…Ivanushka is used only by those that are extremely close to the individual.' She shook her head, 'I do not know of anyone else that has ever been close enough to Ivan to call him that…' she looked at Tempest and smiled warmly, 'but I think I have found someone that deserves to call him that more than me.' Tempest gulped and said, 'Not anymore I don't.' She looked toward the door where the wrestlers were beginning to file out and Poison Ivy was motioning to Tempest. 'I have to go out and face them, and I still have no idea what to say. I don't know if I can ever forgive Ivan, but... I don't want to fear him. And I'm not gonna be able to just forget him.' Illyana stood as Tempest did and nodded, 'That is understandable. I do not believe he tried to hurt you…but he hurt you nonetheless. Good luck with your wrestling, Tempest, I am glad that we got to meet.' She swallowed, 'If you ever do see Ivan again….be patient….he may be afraid he will hurt you again…..but he cares for you.' She smiled and nodded, 'Das vidanya.' Tempest nodded as well and took a deep breath before heading out into the sunny June day that seemed chillier than ever to her. ~ And so evaporates that thin layer of confidence supported only by a short memory. I had been telling myself that getting only two or three hours of sleep at night was normal for me. I had been telling myself all the nerves would be ok if I just stayed focused on wrestling and tried not to think about Ivan or anything like that. I told myself that mere days were all I needed to grieve for having lost him. I was wrong. I had dared to care about him, but hadn't dared to confront him, or even my pain from what happened with him. And so the clouds still roiled in the corners of my mind, waiting for a weak moment to break and wreak havoc. This was a storm I wasn't going to be able to weather just by seeking shelter.
