Clay Vendetta: Private Eye in... THE MALTESE CYBIE! (Part 1)

15 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/15/02 [in black and white.........] It still felt like an elephant had done the two-step on my ribs, and this was a pachyderm in need of some serious dance lessons. Sure, we I had beaten Twisted Metal with just a little help from the broad... errrr, I mean, Jesse Ramey... but I was still no closer to any answers and the judgement day for the mystery thugs was only two weeks away. It's times like these when I wish it was legal to drink and drive, 'cause I could really use a bourbon right now. Cruising down the interstate in his souped-up '67 Hemi 'Cuda, 'Classic' Clay Vendetta cuts a dark figure in sunglasses and a familiar fedora. Taking a long drag from a cigarette, he tips the butt into the ashtray with one hand as he keeps the other lazily on the wheel. What? Oh, the cigarette. It's stage. You can't do film noir without a cigarette... nah, I'm serious. I took out a book from the library: 'Film Noir for Idiots', one of those. Don't smoke, kids. Smoking's bad. The signs float hazily past on the Florida Expressway... finally, 'Miami: Next Exit' can be seen, as Vendetta flips on a blinker and heads over into the exit lane. My head throbbed and pounded like a... like a... hmmmmmmmmmmmmmmm... like an elephant stomping? Shit! That's rule two in the book; number one, you've gotta have a cigarette to do film noir, and two, you can't describe something without using figurative language, preferably a simile. Damn, it's hard to come up with good ones all the time. I need a writer. And a drink. Did I mention a drink? Drinks are rule three. Pulling into a parking lot right off the exit, Vendetta parks the car. It's an old, run-down dump of an ocean bar named 'The Palmetto'; and no sooner does Vendetta pull in than a drunk is tossed out the door with a THUD to pass out in the street. Vendetta steps out of the car, pulls out his wallet, and drops a few bills on the lush as he steps over him gingerly. Swinging the door open, the room is filled with smoke, old tables, and a dingy bar where a fat, large bartender is dispensing drinks to the inebriated clientele. When I needed information and all my other leads had run dry, Mickey was my source. Sure, he might have taken a few too many shots to the head back in his boxing days, but he's the best I had. And besides, now I could get that stiff drink. 'Bourbon and Classic Coke, Mick,' Vendetta says tiredly, sidling up to the bar and pulling up a stool behind him. 'Is Pepsi okay?' 'Don't insult me,' Vendetta replied, and Mickey chuckled. 'Pullin' your leg, Clay. So, what's with the fedora?' Ugh, not him too. Why the hell did I pick film noir when nobody appreciated it? Sigh. I was going to have to do something about Classic appreciation. 'Cripes, Mick, it's film noir! You know... Sam Spade, detective novels, roaring 20's... you'd think a bartender would have some idea behind the roots of bathtub gin, right? But no... man, that's the problem with everyone today, especially the kids; no appreciation for the truly Classic things in life. Nobody's ever heard of Humphrey Bogart but everyone can tell you what Dawson's latest 'angst' is or which rapper Jennifer Lopez is whoring herself out to. Not that I can blame them, of course. That J-Lo's one fine piece of ass.' Mickey fills Clay's glass and slides it to him. 'Sorry, Clay. Just not familiar, that's all. Didn't have a whole lot of time for old movies, anyway, and I hate that black and white stuff.' Ahhh, the irony. 'So, I only see your face around these days when you need to know something. Whaddaya need to know?' Clay takes a sip and pokes absent-mindedly through the trail mix on the bar. 'Well, Mickey, it's like this; I've been getting jumped by a bunch of thugs from out of town, and I've got reason to believe Tyler Nelson's behind it... but I think it's a framejob, Mickey. I think Nelson's a red herring.' Mickey looks confused. 'Gee Clay, a herring? I thought he was a wrestler?' Hoo boy. Sometimes I forget who I'm talking to. Clay takes another sip. 'What I meant was... I think there's somebody deeper behind this. What do you know about... robotic dogs?' With an astounded look, Mickey starts to polish a glass... 'Robotic dogs? Wow. Never heard of 'em, but that'd be a hell of an idea, right? Never piss on the sofa, never crap on the rug... I mean, damn, I've gotta hit my mutt all the time for doing stuff like that. Told the damn thing I'd whack it with a bat if it got outta line, though, and no more 'little presents'.' 'Well, that's... that's, uh, disturbing, Mickey. But anyway, what I really need from you is...' A click is heard as the jukebox picks a track... and quickly, the pulsing beats of techno fill the room. Oh, sweet Jesus, techno music was right up there with moonroofs and Rosie O'Donnell for things that should be destroyed on sight. Besides, I had a nice little noir groove going here! I needed Sinatra, not... Hopping off the stool and walking over to the jukebox, Vendetta notices the name flashing on the jukebox... ...Madonna. Mark my words, somebody was going to pay for this mortal sin. Walking to a nearby table, Vendetta notices a small, glasses-wearing man in a white shirt start to groove to the music. Strolling up to him, Vendetta can hear him singing... 'And I FEE-UHL! Like I just got.... URGGGGGH!' Picking the nerdy man up by his throat, Vendetta takes him over to the bar. 'Hey, uh, Mick? Yeah. What's your policy on me killing your patronage?' 'Well, as long as all his drinks are paid for... just do it outside so that I don't have to clean up a mess in here.' 'Can do,' Vendetta replies, and carries the singer outside before slamming him up against a wall. 'Now then; for your assault on my better taste...' but it's too late, as the man's already fainted, leaving a wet spot rapidly spreading across the front of his pants. Dropping him to the ground, Vendetta heads back into the bar, but notices that the man's wallet has fallen out of his pants, scattering business cards across the sidewalk. I should probably take his cash for making me listen to that drivel... well, hello... what's this? Reaching down and picking a card up off the ground, Vendetta read it aloud. 'Gee-Whiz Toys... I-Cybies Now In Stock!!' Hot damn... this is the break I needed? That damn robot dog's behind this... and if I can get to his animatronic brethren... finally I can get some of the answers I need... and end the anguish caused by the death of my brother who also was in the private eye... The voiceover trails off as Vendetta gets a confused look on his face. Walking over to the trunk of his car, Vendetta pops it and reaches inside. Fishing out an orange book, Vendetta opens: 'Rule 4: All film noir private eyes have dead brothers that haunt them to the point where they can't function as part of normal society and often spend chunks of voice-over time talking about this to beat the point into the viewer's head with a large metal stick.' Ahhhhhhhhh. That explains it. Now then... to the toy shop! Vendetta hops into the car and peels off as the camera fades away... ON OUR NEXT EPISODE of CLAY VENDETTA: PRIVATE EYE! Clay hunts for the root of his problems at the toy store! Will he get more information about the mystery men? Will he finally come to grips with the fact that he really doesn't have a dead brother? Will we finally get to see Niki Weathers in a skimpy outfit? Or will anybody read the next episode because nobody has an f-ing clue what 'film noir' is anyway? All these questions will (probably not) be answered in the next EXCITING episode of... CLAY VENDETTA: PRIVATE EYE!!!
