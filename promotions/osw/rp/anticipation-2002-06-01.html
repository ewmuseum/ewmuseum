Anticipation

1 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/01/02 Walking through the empty General Motors Place in Vancouver on this day is a surreal event all in itself for Jim Axtell. In just a few hours from now the arena will be packed to the gills with rabid OSW wrestling fans, their emotions swelling and ebbing with the action in the ring. Jim’s own emotions are wreaking havoc inside of him, his stomach a bundle of nerves from a combination of fear, excitement, and anticipation mixed with a healthy dose of adrenaline. Working as an OSW security guard has afforded Jim to walk the arena’s before the shows countless times in the past, making sure the building is secure for the pending presentation of SLAM! It never felt like this before. Jim makes his way through the stands, wearing his OSW security uniform and scouring the rows of seats for anything out of the ordinary. Down below on the floor the ring crew works feverishly to erect the forum for the gladiators of OSW to conduct their battle that night. There is also a plethora of other activity in the arena as the stage is set up, sound is checked, lights aligned and tested, and cameras set up. Jim has seen all of this before many times, but this time they are getting prepared for him. He is going to wrestle that night, in a scheduled match, against set opponents, with a tag partner that is one of the best in OSW. Things are different indeed. As Jim is wrapped up in his daydream world the sound techs begin to test the arena’s sound system, putting the state of the art Bose system through a rigorous regimen. “Momma Said Knock You Out” by LL Cool J beats through the speakers of the acoustically sound arena, sending a shiver up Jim’s spine. “Hey,” Jim mutters aloud. “That’s DT’s music!” Jim stops and listens to the rhythmic melody and the flow of Uncle L, bobbing his head and feeling like a mark. The music is abruptly cut off and the arena grows silent again. “Man, this is gonna be one hell of a night,” says Jim as he exhales a deep breath. “I can hardly wait.” Axtell goes back to his work, checking around and under the arena’s seats. The Vancouver Canucks security staff has done a great job of getting the General Motors Place ready for OSW, but it never hurts to check. Suddenly a ferocious guitar riff is blared over the PA, accompanied by an insane drum beat. The music stops Jim dead in his tracks. After a few seconds the deep, powerful, bass voice of Public Enemy’s Chuck D infiltrates the arena. ”BASS, how low can you go?” “Death row? What a brotha know?” The adrenaline rises and nearly causes Jim’s hear to explode as “Bring Tha Noise” by Public Enemy with Anthrax pumps over the sound system. “That’s my music!” exclaims Jim, although almost inaudible beneath the crushing sound of the sound system. “THAT’S MY FRIGGIN MUSIC!!!” The security guard slowly turns toward the center of the arena and something else catches his attention. Above the ring, suspended from the rafters, hangs the eight sided video scoreboard affectionately known to the Canuck fans as OrcaVision. The four Mitsubishi DiamondVision Mark IV video boards and four Whiteway matrix boards are in full action…..with images of Jim Axtell being flashed on them. “OH MY GOD!!!” shouts Jim as he nearly shits his pants. He watches on in awe as the video package rolls through images of his match with Kanaida as well as shots of him breaking up fights in the crowd and backstage, culminating in his arm being raised after his stunning victory over Kanaida a few weeks ago. As he looks on, Jim feels a heavy hand on his shoulder. Jim spins around, startled. “Wha? Oh, hey Moose!” Jim relaxes as he sees the head of OSW security standing behind him with a broad smile on his face. “How do you like that, kid?” asks Moose warmly. “It’s awesome,” is all Jim can come up with at that moment. “Heh,” chuckles Moose. “That’s just about what I said the first time I went through this. It’s one of those things that some people never get used to….seeing themselves up there.” Moose steps up beside Jim as the music fades into a staccato drum beat, then finally fades out completely. “I had the production boys put that together for you,” Moose says. “My gift to you on the night of your first official match.” Jim turns to Moose and avoids the urge to hug the much bigger Moose. “Thanks, Moose!” Jim spits as he chokes back a tear. “You didn’t have to do that, you know.” Moose waves his hands in dismissal. “Bah, it was nothing. The least I could do for you taking up for me a couple weeks back. That was something that I won’t soon forget, kid.” Jim pats Moose on the shoulder. “Aww it was nothing that any other self respecting guy wouldn’t have done. Don’t worry about it.” Moose gets a more serious tone in his voice as he changes the mood of the conversation. “Look kid,” starts Moose. “I want to make sure you aren’t getting in over your head here. These guys here don’t play around, you know? This Truxell character and that Storm fellow are full time assholes who don’t give two shits about you. I just want to make sure that you realize that it’s a war in that ring down there.” Moose motions the the ring down on the arena floor that is nearly complete in it’s setup. The OSW apron dangles around the bottom and the crew is just finishing up tightening the ropes. Jim pauses as he looks down at where he will do battle in a few hours. “I’m ready, Moose. I’ve dealt with guys like this before, back when I was doing those bare-knuckle fights back home,” recalls Jim. “There were tons of guys just like these two. The guys who thought they were the greatest thing to walk the face of the Earth, and some could even back it up. I know it’s gonna be a fight in there tonight, but I don’t quit and I don’t give up and I sure as hell am not going to let those two jerks get the better of me.” There is a little fire in Jim’s voice as he finishes off the last sentence. Moose gives Axtell a smile and puts a hand on his shoulder. “I knew that already, kid. I’ve been thinking,” starts Moose. “With you starting in the ring and having to worry about your opponents, I think it may be best of you took some time off from the security team. I don’t want you distracted while you prepare for your matches or while you’re out in that ring.” “That’s not necessary, Moose. I can handl-“ Moose interrupts. “Not another word about it,” says the head of OSW security sternly. Jim’s shoulders sag in disappointment. “Okay, but I’m going to finish out tonight because if I don’t you’ll be short handed.” Moose tries to interrupt again but Jim stops him before he can utter a word. “That’s the deal, take it or leave it.” “I guess I have to take it,” sighs Moose. “You drive a hard bargain, kid.” Moose and Jim smile as Moose pops the young Axtell on the arm. “Take care of business tonight, Jimmy. All the guys in the back will be rootin for ya,” Moose says as he starts to walk away. “I will,” Jim promises. “And thanks for the entrance! It was sweet!” Moose shoots a wave over his shoulder as he disappears around the corner. Jim takes a deep breath as he looks over the arena once again. As he exhales he starts back to his job of checking the seats.
