Doomed to repeat history

24 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-24-2002 Doug Troy wasn’t sure how long he’d been in his locker room. After he’d regained consciousness from his elimination from the Ascension main event, he had walked to the back immediately. No playing to the crowd, no trash talking the guys who hadn’t been eliminated yet, just walked right back to the locker room and sat down on a bench right next to his locker. He’s been there for who knows how long. It didn’t really matter to him. Not again. Not again, dammit. The only thing that mattered to him was his loss to Walczak. That was it, the center of his current existence. He’d been to the top of the mountain and once again the first real challenger had pushed him off. Over fucking soda pop. Over a can of fucking soda pop. As if to confirm his fears, the rattle of a pop machine giving up it’s caffinated contents shakes Doug from his reverie. He looks over and see Toomes with a can of Coke in his hand. Toomes was staring right at Doug. No, he was staring right through Doug, a smirk on his face. Toomes said nothing, but Doug heard everything that he didn’t say. “Now you know how it feels, Troy. Now you know how I felt when you did the exact same thing to me.” Doug turned back to Toomes, who was busy chugging the Coke down. He had been for the past thirty seconds. So who was it that said all that? I did. Shit, am I going insane? Toomes finished his Coke and crushed the can, muttering something about “Corg” with a snarl. He tossed the can into a garbage bin and walked off, again leaving Doug with the thoughts he didn’t want to hear. Doug looked at the clock on the wall and noticed that it’d been only thirty minutes since he’d sat down. It had felt more like three hours to Doug. With a sigh, he grabbed his duffel bag out of his locker and walked out of the locker room. His walk to the parking lot was a blur of faces. People talking to each other, discussing the Pay Per View, having friendly conversations. Occasionally, someone would say “hi” to Doug, but Doug didn’t even acknowledge them. It’s not because he was being an asshole, but because his thoughts had once again taken precedence over everything else. Besides, he knew what they’d say anyway. “Hey Doug. Tough break with the title and all.” “Hey Doug. Good match, too bad about those damn Red Army guys spoiling it for you.” “What’s up, Doug? How ya holding up after the match?” “Hey Doug, what’s it like to be a LOSER.” He needed to talk to these people like he needed a Rottweiler biting his balls. They’d be all artificial and mock-caring, acting like they really gave a damn about whether or not Doug won or lost. The fake sincerity turned Doug’s stomach because he knew what they all really wanted to say. “First Preacher, now Walczak eh, Doug? Man, you really suck.” They didn’t have to say it for Doug to hear it. He knew it’s what they wanted to say. He pushed through, almost desperately trying to get to the arena doors before the ridicule in his head could reach him. He opened the door to the arena and broke into a run for his car, a rented Toyota 4runner. He threw his gear into the passenger seat and sped off into the night, trying to leave his thoughts behind. For a while, he succeeded. All he was concentrating on was driving, all of his thoughts put toward going down the interstate. They caught back up after about 20 miles. Loser Stop it. So much for the OSW Title meaning everything to you, LOSER. Stop it. Preacher AND Walczak. “Stop it” Let’s face it: you’re done, Troy. “STOP IT!” Doug slammed on his brakes and pulled over to the side of the road. The dark night stretched over the desert for miles and miles. Doug’s car was the only point of light in the desert. Inside the car, Doug was holding his head, holding back tears of absolute defeat. His entire life had led up to the OSW Title, and yet even that was robbed from him. He fought a war against his brain, a brain that informed him over and over that he’d lost the thing he’d been fighting for all this time. Doug shut his eyes against the world, determined to shut out the thoughts. The car’s radio started to play his thoughts through the speakers, so he turned off the ignition so he wouldn’t have to listen to it anymore. The thoughts got quieter and quieter until a loud rapping at his window brought them back to the forefront. “GODDAMMIT CAN’T I GET A FUCKING MOMENT’S PEACE?!” Doug opened his eyes and found them assailed by harsh sunlight. The sun was starting to rise in the east, spilling light over all God’s creation. Doug looked to his window, the source of the noise. It was a cop tapping his nightstick against the window. Doug rolled it down. “There a problem here, sir? You okay?” Doug looked around and blinked the sleep from his eyes. How long had he been out for? “Yeah. Yeah, I’m okay. Just got tired while driving and decided it’d be better to sleep on the side of the road rather than drive into a bridge embankment.” “Yeah, well, you take yourself home, Mr. Troy. Too bad about the title. I was pulling for ya.” The cop walked away as Doug sneered. More false sincerity. He rolled up the window and started the car. “I need a drink.” Fade to black.
