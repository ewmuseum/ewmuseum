Roads to the future, Roads to the Past.

19 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 19, 2002 It was one of those gray days outside. The ones that never bring anything good with them. Rain poured violently down upon the countryside and one sole man, and his monkey, stood in the center of it. Reno extended his arm out, thumb up and aimed in the direction opposite that he faced. An old black van drifted up the highway, mists from the wet road expanding like a cloud around the vehicle. It appeared to float closer, getting bigger and bigger as it neared, passing Reno right by. He dropped his arm down and began to walk in the same direction of the van. It was a horrible day outside, with hard showers pouring down on Davis almost every time he began to dry off from the last rainfall. ZaC rested in the inside pocket of Reno’s trench coat, dry and protected. Reno appeared to be, although he didn’t realize it, actually homeless. He wore a blue hooded sweater underneath his dark gray trench coat with the hood of the sweater up. Random band patches were scattered over his sweater and beaten up jeans. Holes from wear and tear on the jeans had made them impossible to wear without long johns underneath, but the effect didn’t exactly look professional. Davis hadn’t slept since he left the Saddledome in Calgary, and managed to work his way to Winnipeg, Manitoba. The good thing about being a wrestler sometimes, is that if people recognize you, they will talk to you. So asking for a drive was normally easy. Reno had to sleep, but didn’t want to do so on the side of a highway. He refused to go back into Winnipeg city, since he’d made it at least 10 km away from it already on foot. “Shit...what a terrible day huh ZaC?” The Unwanted Angel asked ZaC, who merely shuffled in his sleep. ZaC slept a lot lately, but he was nearly 10 years old, so he didn’t have the energy he once had. An old brown Volvo slowed to a stop near Reno, and rolled down its window. Davis cheered in his mind and began to walk towards the window. “Good-day sir, how are ya today?” Reno asked. “It’s a piss awful day out, where you trying to go?” The man from the car asked. He was balding and chubby, at least in his late 40’s. “I’m trying to go to Washington, DC...But anywhere near there would be great,” Reno replied. “Washington eh? I can take you to Ottawa, but after that I’m going north.” “Great! Thanks a lot!” Reno exclaimed. The doors to the Volvo unlocked and Reno ran around to the back where he hopped in from the rain. The inside of the car was almost too hot to bear, but Davis said nothing since he was grateful to have a drive. When Reno sat down, he didn’t realize the other person sitting in the car. The Unwanted Angel didn’t say anything about him either, despite an odd odor emitting from him. The figure just sat there, playing with a pair of scissors. Davis stared at the rusted scissors with confusion, the man really weirded him out for some reason to begin with, the clippers just added to the paranoia. Long, greasy blonde locks hung down about as long as Reno’s except it seemed the figure had a headband on that pushed the hair up so it hung down overtop, covering most of the figure’s face. snip...snip...snip... The man wouldn’t stop snipping the scissors and the driver didn’t notice the events at all. It was as if the driver didn’t even know the man was in the car. The man wore a simple black t-shirt with wear and tear holes everywhere. The yellow lettering on his chest was unreadable due to the man’s position, but the letters seemed just as beaten up as the rest of the shirt. Simple jean shorts covered the man’s frail legs and were almost as beaten up as Reno’s, except without the long johns. Everything on the man seemed to have holes in them. He must be homeless, Reno thought. “So, what’s your name?” The driver directed towards Reno, who eyed the person next to him carefully. “Uh, Reno sir. Reno Davis.” The snipping stopped and the man’s fists seemed to clench. Reno placed his hand on the door handle and concentrated on the man’s movements. The driver simply nodded and continued to drive. “I’m Dave.” The driver stated after a few seconds. The man beside Reno still didn’t do anything but sit silently. Reno gulped and chose to take a chance. “What’s your name?” He asked the man beside him. No response came, but the man’s hand began to shake slightly and his knuckled became white. Reno started to regret his chance. Dave eyed the two men from the rear-view mirror and began to sweat. The man turned his head slightly and looked at Reno through his hair. It appeared as though the man wasn’t wearing a headband, but a mask. A wrestling mask at that, and one from which Reno had seen before, but from where? Reno’s eyes met the man’s for a moment, sending a chill down his spine. Then the man looked back towards the scissors and began to open and close them. Dave continued to drive and Reno just watched the nameless, but familiar man from the corner of his eye. It was going to be a long drive to Ottawa.
