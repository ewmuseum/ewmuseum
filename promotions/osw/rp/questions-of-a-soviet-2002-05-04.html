Questions of a Soviet

4 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-4-02 As I sit here, I contemplate my existence. As I sit here, I question my meaning within the great scheme of things. A soldier. That is what I am. I soldier. I exist to fulfill obligations, complete objectives, and protect Russia. I exist to make my superiors proud and prove to the world that Russia is most powerful and honorable nation in the world. I am NOT a compassionate man. I am NOT a nice man. I am not one that you would welcome into your home and ask to talk to. I am a man that you fear and yet, respect for you know that with my job comes responsibility that you cannot fathom and are afraid to comprehend. I am not meant to think, I am meant to act. For forty years I have done as my superiors have told, and I have done them proud. Throughout it all I have glorified my country and thus, glorified myself. And yet, there is an emptiness. A feeling of incompleteness that I feel somewhere within my heart. There is a feeling of unacceptability that I cannot comprehend, for the mantra of a soldier, of an officer, and of a communist cannot give me the answers to the question that I pose to myself presently: Who am I? Am I a soldier? If I am such a thing, why is it that at one time my heart felt itself beat with a warmth that could melt the Siberian snow? Am I a communist? If that is so, why am I so selfish now as to wish that I, myself, possessed something so dear to me that I would not share her with anyone else. Her The thought sends a shiver up my spine and a pain in my very being. The thought of her wracks my body with an anxiety and a feeling of regret from which I cannot even begin to understand. I questioned what love was once, I know what it is now, and as I think of the definition of love, as I think of her, I assure myself that I have answered my question. Yes, she enters my mind still, no matter how hard I try to shield myself from the pain. Still the memories of her invade my mind and try to rouse my heart and awaken it to what I had felt. Yet, do I wish to take that gamble? Do I wish to take that chance? Do I have the courage? The fleeting memories of days gone by, days that feel as if they were minutes ago, still haunt me. The memories still penetrate even my skilled brain and challenge me to question myself, and I allow it to do so. And then, suddenly, the words hit me. “Liar” “Beast” “Animal” It is these words that define who I am. It is these words of which the people of the world know me. The others, “Gentleman” “Hero” “Savior” Those words are ones in which I have tried to live up to and I have failed. I have failed in my duty to myself and to the people. When the people look at me, they do not see a gentleman, they see an animal. I must be getting to the plane soon….Saturday Slam is upon us. Then the people will see who I am. Then the people will see who Ivan Stanislav is. My peers, they smile when they see me. They salute me with more pride now as I walk by, and I am glad. Have I given up a piece of myself for them? Or was it just an act, a lie, a charade that I was just foolish enough to accept? I know what I must do. I know who I must handle. I know who is responsible. I will crush his skull with my boot heel and ensure that never again will he damage anyone like he did to me. Who am I? The question returns as I hear the clock chime, telling me to fulfill my obligation to my schedule and go to the hangar. I am The Russian Bear, Praporschik Ivan Sergeiovich Stanislav. Those people say I have changed? They are wrong. I have never been more the same….and I will teach them that… tonight.
