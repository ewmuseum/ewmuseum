Pondering Poland...

5 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/5/02 The scene is set near a polish deli. There are two men inside of the deli, one appears to be Tom Walczak, but the other’s identity is unknown. Walczak and the man seem to be talking, but what they speak cannot be heard. Finally words are heard from Walczak’s mouth. “Man, I remember a whole bunch of great times in Poland. Especially when I was about eleven. That was the year that I had my first beer, and after that it was smooth sailing. Sure I have had my tough times, but when the worst comes around I just turn to my trusty friend, and everything gets better.” “So wait you are an alcoholic? You should really get some help for that.” “No, I am not an alcoholic. You see, in order to be considered an alcoholic you need to become drunk constantly, but although I drink constantly I rarely if ever get drunk, so case and fact I am not an alcoholic.” “ I see, well the fact still is no matter what, drunk or not Poland is still great, and nothing can ever change that. I remember various times that me and my friends did things that here would be frowned upon, but in Poland were seen as a common childhood occurrence. Also the drinking age around her is preposterous. I am nowhere near the drinking caliber of yourself, but 21 to me just sounds outrageous. When my kid gets to the ripe old age of fifteen I will drink a beer with him, and I don’t give a damn what anyone else thinks.” “Damn straight, you can’t take this sh*t from anyone let alone these capitalist bastards. I mean if I did then I would have been out of this country a long time ago. You know all this talk about Poland, and how good it is makes me wonder why I even bother staying here. Why do I not go back?” “Well, I don’t see what is stopping you. I mean it is not like you don’t have the resources to commute back and forth for matches, and houses there are pretty cheap so you can still keep your home here.” “You know that’s not such a bad idea, but I still have doubt. I mean as great as Poland was it also had its setbacks, and now being used to this country, well it could be difficult to adjust.” “Are you getting soft? Is it possible that a tough Polish man such as yourself could possibly be scared? Do you think that you are taking a risk, and it might not pay off? What are these crazy words that I hear in my virgin ears?” “Are you out of your stupid a*s mind? Of course I am not scared, all that I am saying is that I need a little time to make the decision. I am not the rash and indecisive person that I once was. No, I will take everything in, and then, then I will make my decision.” “Hey man, take all the time that you need, just don’t puss out on it just because you are scared. If you do that then you will no longer be able to buy food from this fine deli. Do you here me?” “Loud and clear, and rest assured that I will not puss out, and if I decide not to go then well there will be a logical reason behind it, and not a pussy out reason. Well I have to get going see you tomorrow.” “Ok, you take care of yourself now, and remember to think that decision over ok?” “I will.” Walczak leaves the deli, and heads over to his bike. He gets on, and rides away as the scene fades out. What will Walczak decide? Should he stay or should he go, and will it effect his wrestling ability?
