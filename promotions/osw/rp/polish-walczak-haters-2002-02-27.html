Polish Walczak Haters...

27 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/27/02 We see Tom Walczak picking fairly heavy boulders and tossing them on what appears to be some sort of target, taped, or painted to the side of a mountain. As we get closer we see that it is a poster of Doug Troy with his trademark Sobe shirt. Walczak keeps throwing rocks at the poster, and with each shot the poster turns more and more tattered, until finally a rock takes the poster off the side of the cliff completely. Walczak sits down, and gets his breath together rather quickly. He gets up, and dusts himself off as he starts for the journey back home. “That right there was just a taste. A little sliver of flavor of what will happen to Doug Troy this upcoming Saturday. He will fall down in front of me just as that poster did, and with one last breath he will ask for mercy. He will ask for my forgiveness, but if he thinks that I will show any mercy whatsoever, than he is in for the surprise of his life. I will be a person of apathy in the ring, just like the grim reaper when he comes searching for you on the day of your death. I will make the ring a massacre and a mockery out of Doug Troy, and then the people will see the man that their American hero really is.” Walczak continues to walk down the road, which is just a plain dirt road with a few pebbles in it. A car passes by every couple of minutes, but other than that the road is relatively abandoned. Walczak continues to walk down the road as an old beaten up pick-up truck passes him by. All of sudden however, it stops and backs up. It pulls up right in front of Walczak and the man in the driver’s seat rolls his window down. “You are Tom Walczak, are you not?” “Yes I am, but what is it to you?” “Well, we are very nationalistic Poles, and most of the time that an athlete strikes it big we cheer for them more than anyone, but with you we feel that it would be a disgrace. You bring more shame to our country than you do pride, and you attack people’s characters for no apparent reason.” “No apparent reason? Do you not see how they treat us Poles on television? If you do then you will see exactly why I do what I do. I am a very aggressive person, and I wont take shit from anyone, especially worthless non-nationalistic pieces of shit like you. Just get your asses out of here before I pull you worthless carcasses out of the car and beat you near death.” The car pulls away, but shoots dirt all over Walczak in the process. Walczak begins to run after them, but realizes that his attempt would be in vain, so just slowly continues home as the scene fades to black.
