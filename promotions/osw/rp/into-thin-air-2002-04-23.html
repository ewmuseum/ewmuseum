Into Thin Air

23 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 23rd 'Allow the thoughts to fade. Feel them streaming from your mind, pouring out of every spare place in your head, fading into thin air...' Kimbusa sits on the ground, his eyes closed tightly. His mighty chest heaves up and down in a slow, even rhythm, the air rushing in and out of his mighty nose with great volume. The firelight dancing from the corner of the room shines on Kimbusa's face, causing his jet black hair to shimmer in the dim living room. Kimbusa is seated cross-legged on a large red mat, his sensai seated close behind in a small red chair. The room is modest and plain. Martial arts training equipment occupies most of the far walls, with traditional oriental art decorating the walls. The room itself is lit only by the fireplace and a few candles strewn about the room. Kimbusa is seated in the center of everything, doing his best to follow his master's instruction. Yoshi speaks with a soothing voice, but Kimbusa's eyelids begin to tremble with frustration with each word Yoshi speaks. 'Feel the anger dispersing, feel the fear dissipate. Banish the memories, send every mistake and every atrocity into exile. Leave the memories behind, remove them entirely from your mind. Refuse to submit to them, defy their reminders. Never-' Kimbusa's eyes suddenly flash open, a brief glimmer of angry redness in his eyes. He quickly cuts Yoshi off with a harsh snap. 'Enough of this.' Yoshi Kimawa is taken aback, sitting back in his chair. Kimbusa quickly rises to his feet and turns to the window, placing his hand on the handle to slide the door open to the balcony. Yoshi, however, is not about to give up. 'Are you submitting to your disgrace? Are you giving in to your past? Why did we come here? Why have we sought out this refuge in the mountains, joined this new company, shown ourselves to the Americans?' Kimbusa, slightly shocked by Yoshi's stern reply, turns his head slighty to the left. Yoshi's head and back are in perfect alignment, as Yoshi speaks sharply, showing no fear of the mighty sumo's incredibly size. 'Are you admitting defeat? Are you giving in to your anger? Will you really so quickly abandon all that I have taught you, the years of spiritual guidance, all because you are afraid of your past?' At the uttering of the word 'afraid', Kimbusa snaps around to face his master, his face growing with rage. Despite Kimbusa's obvious immenent explosion, Yoshi continues his tirade. 'Do not forget that you did not acheive victory in your first exhibition for the Americans.' Kimbusa raises a fairly meek voice in defiance. 'It was the girl's fault. I want nothing to do with her.' Ignoring Kimbusa's referral to his tag team partner, Yoshi continues. 'There are laws in America, Kimbusa. They will not protect you from prosecution to save their sport. This sport has no sacred protection. You are not considered honorable. You are not held in high esteem. But that is not what we came for. The fact of the matter is, Kimbusa, if you do not keep your anger in check, you will find yourself in the gravest of circumstances, and you will not be able to blame me!' A mere moment after Yoshi ends his sentence, Kimbusa shouts with all his might. 'SILENCE!' Yoshi is startled at the volume of the shout, and is quickly silenced. 'Enough of this! I will choose my own path! I will not be lead around like a dog on a leash! I will not hold back any longer! Enough of you, enough of your meditation! From now on, I follow my own path!' With that, Kimbusa throws open the sliding door and steps outside into the cool air, slamming it shut behind him. Yoshi sits in shock, reeling from Kimbusa's anger. After a moment, Yoshi's eyes grow thin, as he glares out at his fearsome sumo....
