Seal the Deal

9 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 9th, 2002 O’Brien struggled with his tie as he tried to straighten it out. One side was abnormally larger than the other, causing the long end to come out crooked. I would have helped him, but I decided to be smart and come in a casual suit – one that didn’t require me to deal with ties from hell. “Stop struggling with that,” I admonished, feeling a bit embarrassed to have O’Brien as my manager. People had begun staring over, watching his forty year old Irish Roman Catholic fiddling with a silly piece of cloth. “Blasted tie,” He said, trying to repudiate the fact that he couldn’t make heads of tails out of it. “ “Just leave it loose,” I advised. “It makes you look younger.” He looked at me with a face of utter vice, as if I had acted with any sort of turpitude that would have demeaned him. Let me reiterate that I hadn’t. If I had implied that the bastard was old, I didn’t. It should be obvious for everyone to see THAT. “I’m still not too sure about this whole thing,” O’Brien continued, shaking his head with an obvious uncertainty. “I can take care of myself.” “I didn’t say you couldn’t,” He replied. “All I’m saying is that you’ve got to watch yourself with these people. I don’t know what kind of a speech he gave you to convince you otherwise….” “Natalie and I made this decision,” I stated strongly. I didn’t like being so direct with O’Brien, because honestly, I liked him. But I didn’t feel like defending my position a million times over. He was my business consultant and a trainer – nothing more. “She wants what’s best for you. And so do I.” He seemed quiet, withdrawn from the moment. In some way, on some levels, I was like a son that he never had. Poor O’Brien. The bastard gave his entire life to the wrestling business, never finding the time to have a family. I was his family. “I know that,” I replied quietly. “That’s why I want you by my side when this happens. I need you by my side.” He patted me on the shoulder. “You know I’ll always be there for you. Through thick and thin, through the good times and the bad.” “We’ll hammer them for a deal that works for us, not them,” I answered, feeling confident that I could make good on my word. “I’m not about to play their games.” O’Brien opened the office door, ushering me in. “Then let’s go get them.” Let the negotiations begin. * * * The door to Raymond Burris' office finally closed, and after a two hour meeting with Tom Lexian and his very eccentric manager, only known to him as O'Brien, Burris' mind was nothing more than a wasteland of fatigue. He pressed a button on his desk, turning on an intercom. 'They're gone.' A side door to the office opened, revealing an older man wearing a navy blue suit and black tie. His hair is parted neatly to the side, tinged with silver throughout. He sits at a chair next to Burris' desk and crosses his legs. 'Your thoughts?' He asked Burris. 'They agreed to almost everything, except for the no-escape clause that our lawyers tried to slip in there, Mr. Clark,' Burris replied, running a hand through his hair. Mr. Clark just shook his head in disgust. 'I told the boys at 'Lockert & Packard' to be careful. Lexian's not stupid.' Burris shrugged. 'It got past Strickland. After two months of being signed, he was begging for a way out, but of course, no one could find him a loop hole.' 'Like I said, Lexian's not stupid.' Burris grabbed a pack of cigarettes from the first drawer on his right and opened it, offering the first to Mr. Clark. He declined. 'Suit yourself,' Burris mumbled, sticking it in his mouth and lighting it up. He took a puff and set it down. 'So what do we do about Lexian?' 'Keep him on a short leash,' Mr. Clark replied. 'He's going to see how far he can push our buttons and relieve himself of this contract. He just wants the money, and he wants nothing to do with New Wave.' 'So what do I do if he starts acting out of line?' 'Punish him.' The words struck Burris solidly. 'Punish him? As in monetary fines?' He let the moment sink in. 'For now.' Burris' eyes glazed over and he pounded his fist onto the desk, shaking everything on it. 'Dammit, Clark. We can't do what we did with Strickland. We can't go down that road again.' 'Calm yourself. And that is Mr. Clark to you.' He took a quick breath and held it in, already feeling the pressure from the stand off. 'All I'm saying is that we can't have another fiasco like the one we had with Strickland. It was too messy.' Mr. Clark only laughed, amused by the man who came so close to exposing New Wave Entertainment. 'Strickland is in the middle of an unemployment line scratching around for food stamps. He had a future here and decided to play hardball, and look where it landed him. He didn't have a case.' 'They found the phone tap devices and a few of the micro recorders in his home,' Burris replied in a subdued tone. 'Even after our men had gone through his house to remove them.' 'Then your men are fuck ups.' 'We cannot tap Lexian.' 'Not yet.' Burris let his head drop a bit. “And what about his partner?” “What about him?” He checked Lexian’s file, seeing the name again. “Colin Mitchell is his real name, otherwise known to the wrestling pundits out there as Mike Extreme. Quite a character, apparently. His reputation and temper precede him.” “Keep an eye on him too,” Mr. Clark ordered. “I don’t want him jeopardizing this company’s name anymore with bad publicity.” “I can put a tail on him twelve hours a day.” “Make it twenty-four. I want absolutely no bad press from Mitchell until we have Lexian signed to a full deal.” Burris whistled in disbelief. “That’s a lot of man hours dedicated to one guy, Mr. Clark.” Mr. Clark smiled. A power smile, one that Burris did not possess – yet. “Let me worry about signing the time sheets. You just make sure that Mitchell doesn’t interfere and blow the whole deal. Once we have Lexian signed, we can dump Mitchell.” “Lexian will never go for that.” “Then we’ll have to make him ‘go for that.’ I can be a very persuasive man, Raymond.” Burris let out a breath and rubbed his eyes. He was stressed out and tired. 'The upside to Lexian is too high for us to ignore. We need to play his game for awhile.' 'Fine,' Mr. Clark said standing up from his seat. 'But don't forget that I call the shots around here. And when I get orders that Lexian is to be tapped, believe me, he will be. Upside or not, we play this one by the book, just like we always do. Good day Raymond.' Mr. Clark coolly walked out of the room, exiting from the main door, leaving Burris to his own thoughts and to his cigarette. Not all that comforting, to say the least.
