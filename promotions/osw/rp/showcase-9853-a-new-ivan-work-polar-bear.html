A NEW Ivan work: Polar Bear

22 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Life in Siberia was certainly anything but boring. If anything, The Russian Bear had far more time for recreation than ever before. Of course, if by ‘recreation’ one means reading up on old Soviet novels, firing automatic weapons, chopping wood, and repairing an old hunting cabin in Siberia, then definitely one would be chock full of amusement. Exile to Siberia, at his own behest, had given Ivan Stanislav time to reflect on himself, including his belief system, code of honor, conduct in the past, and what plans he had for the future. Yet when you’re as old as Ivan Sergeiovich Stanislav, old habits die hard. Or, much like his stubborn self, they refuse to die altogether. The Russian Bear still maintained a strict weight training regiment, unable to accept a loss of a single ounce of muscle mass. His suspenders were all neatly hung in a row along his bedroom wall, his hair still cut high and tight, and his beard was still as disheveled as ever. Nevertheless, parts of Ivan had given way to the Siberian wasteland. Like the crisp falling snow that fell virtually every morning, Stanislav’s hair had quickly surrendered to age and relinquished its color, going from the feral mishmash of black and white to an almost angelic and pristine white. And while to his predecessors the beauty of Siberia did not lie in its ruggedness, but in its isolation, Ivan Sergeiovich agreed with this line of thinking. Yet he did not love it because of its mystical means of swallowing up people, but rather leaving those who have been swallowed immune to the tampering of other sentient life. On this particular day it was quite warm for Siberia. By his rough conversion, Ivan surmised that the western people would say it was approximately 35 degrees Fahrenheit. A heat wave, if you will. The Russian Bear stood outside in a black windbreaker, zipped up to his neck, complete with his rudimentary black pants and boots. A large, fuzzy hat sat atop his square head while he did battle with what was now the most tenacious of his enemies: timber. From this vantage point Stanislav stood amongst the trees of Siberia, waging his daily mêlée against the trees, a friend and foe alike. With his trusty axe in one hand and a cigar in the other, Stanislav looked more like a hulking polar bear amidst the backdrop of a Siberian forest. His cabin was a scant mile away, which was nothing more than a brisk walk for Ivan. With a hewn piece of a tree lying precariously atop two modified saw-horses, Stanislav raised his axe high in the air and slammed it down into the bowels of the tree. The tree let out a splintering wail and fragmented, sending pieces of its semi-frozen woodchips into Gods creation. Deep within his chest, Stanislav hummed the famed national anthem of his countrymen and once again attacked the timber with as much vigor as before. Exhaling loudly and sending a torrent of smoke from his lips, The Russian Bear looked around just for a moment and peered down the hazy path, catching the sound of something definitely foreign. The constant haze of Siberia had marred the vision of Stanislav for months, but now he had grown used to it. Even when it did not snowing, visibility was limited greatly as images seemed to drift in and out of the reach of the human eye. Like a bird of prey, however, Stanislav had grown accustomed to this strange phenomenon and had trained himself to see and discern more minute details. And Ivan had definitely seen something unheard of in Siberia: headlights. * * * * * “Why in God’s name does this guy have to live so far out in the middle of nowhere??” the promoter asked from the back seat of the jeep, contending with the roaring howl of the wind around him and the pained groaning of an aged jeeps engine. The driver, a Russian man making a mint off of the promoter’s stupidity and inability to understand the strength of the American dollar in Russia, rolled his eyes from beneath his wide brimmed hat, “Well, Sir,” he began in a heavily Russified English, “Comrade Stanislav prefers to stay as far out of the reach of the public eye as he possibly can. That is all.” The jeep bounced and jostled down the clay path, poised for a meeting with quite possibly Russia’s most notorious throwback. Trying to carry on a conversation with the driver had proven to be much akin to pulling teeth from a small child, which had left the obnoxious promoter relatively quiet throughout most of the drive. The driver grinned. Just what he wanted. * * * * * “What in Vladimir Uliyanov’s name is vehicle doing out here??” whispered Stanislav, the cinders of the cold sending a flood of steam from his mouth. Sure as ever, the jeep jostled and rollicked down the path towards Stanislav. The Russian Bear took a few steps to the side. He had stood head to head with various four wheeled vehicles in the past, but those were for titles or bragging rights… it would be folly to try to stop one now. He clutched the axe in his hand. If, by some off chance, it was a group of thugs or fools trying to make a name for themselves, they’d be hard pressed to take on the hulking Ivan Stanislav. The jeep galloped towards Stanislav, its twin headlights jerking lethargically to the uneven ground until finally slowing to stop just feet from Stanislav. Ivan squinted and peered through the windshield at the driver but did not recognize him. The driver stared back docilely, much like any Russian would. The back door of the military jeep flew open and out stepped the promoter. Stanislav gripped the axe and glared at the man like a feral dog. He screamed of American. From his spotless shoes, pressed pants, and refined suit jacket to the dark sunglasses and viper smile on his face, he was certainly an American. “Ivan Stanislav, I’m glad I—“ Stanislav’s gravelly voice boomed beyond the scope of any sound this man could muster, “You had best state your business clearly and succinctly, American. Ivan is in Siberia for reason and it is NOT to be bothered!” The promoter was taken aback by the forcefulness of “The Russian Bear.” He was everything that he had heard about. This Stanislav fella had the charisma, presence, build, and tenacity of a superstar! He saw it instantly. Staring up at this massive man clad in his rugged clothing and with a face lined with wrinkles and an almost glowing beard, this man was the ticket! “Mr. Stanislav, with all due respect, I merely wanted to discuss other options you could have with your life!” Stanislav looked around him, “Siberia offers many options. Options that some capitalist whose head is buried in his checkbook and swimming in the stars would never be able to fathom!” A strange flame grew in the breast of Ivan Stanislav. This was a flame that had not been ignited for some time. He advanced, axe still at his side. The snow gave way beneath the heavy boots of the Bear. It crunched, gasped, and wheezed in weak determination against the bulk of Stanislav and ultimately succumbed. “Yet here you are, Sir, invading MY privacy in MY home! A man that lives in Siberia lives here for reason and it is not to be found, but to be forgotten!” The promoter put his hands up defensively. This guy was bigger than in the photos. “I… I understand man and I don’t mean any offense! I merely wanted to help you…” “You wish to help yourself, wretch!” Ivan’s barrel chest rose and fell in rage as he stopped just short of the promoter. “How did you find me?” The promoter glanced over at the driver and then back at Stanislav, “I just asked around and that guy said he had an idea where you might be!” Turning promptly and walking back around the jeep to the front, Ivan closed one eye and squinted through the dirty windshield. As if silently commanded, the drivers door opened and a smallish Russian exited from the vehicle. He slipped his hands in his pockets and lowered his head. The man was a coward; Stanislav could see it from here. Unkempt as he was, he wasn’t even a military man. He wore an old, tattered Red Army trench coat, probably one he got from a military surplus store. His face was shallow, with high cheekbones and dead, cloudy eyes. Ivan snorted; even the jeep was of an older model and lucky to have survived this long by Stanislav’s estimation. This driver was probably nothing more than a freelance taxi driver, hoping to get this moron out into the middle of nowhere and mug him, and now accidentally came upon just who he was meant to avoid. The promoter rushed around to the front of the jeep near Ivan. The driver quickly entered back into the jeep. “Look man, I’m here representing the ACW. It’s a Denver based fed and it’s really a good place! I know you used to be one of the top, if not THE top guy in wrestling! Everyone knows that!” The promoter fumbled in his pockets and pushed a card into the huge paw of Stanislav. He shivered. Damn it was cold! “Look, I… I’m sorry I invaded your privacy man. I didn’t mean it. But you’re a piece of talent that guys would die to see back in the ring!” Stanislav stared coldly back at the promoter, who glanced at the very large axe in his hand. Perhaps mentioning death wasn’t the best course of action. He swallowed which caused his large Adam’s apple to bob up and down. “It would be prudent of you,” Stanislav muttered while holding the card, “to get out of here as quickly as possible. Lest Ivan Sergeiovich decide to send you out of here with his boot imbedded deep within your behind. I am not interested and I decline your request.” The Bear’s words were powerful and crushed around the promoter’s ears like two massive glaciers pulverizing a small fish. He nodded numbly, stunned by not only the cold but also the harsh turn of the conversation, and began to clamber into the front passenger side of the car. “I understand, but just remember that card if you’re interested! We’ll pay you big!!” The promoter slammed the door shut as the ailing jeep revved back to life and drove past Stanislav. The Russian Bear gave a meaningful glare to the nosey driver. He glanced down at the business card and the phone number of the promoter. In his hands, Stanislav could have been holding thousands upon thousands of dollars, fame, fortune, and titles. He dropped it into the snow Stanislav turned for just a minute and whirled around again, but the car was gone. The driver looked… familiar. With a shrug, the Bear had more important matters to attend to… like firewood. * * * * * An hour later, Stanislav returned to the secluded cabin in which he lived. After dropping off his freshly cut wood and throwing some into the fireplace in his living room, The Russian Bear walked into a small study that he had constructed after moving into the smallish cabin. Ivan sat down in a custom built chair and sat at his desk, clear of all but a few books that he had recently finished. Upon the walls around him hung the annals of his illustrious career. From war photos and pictures of himself with political dignitaries, to still shots of his wrestling career, the story of Ivan Stanislav was displayed in both black and white and color for only him to see. Photos of Ivan fighting that hellion Hessian, attacking Kremmen, and arguing with Jane Reagan brought back triumphant memories. Other photos, like those of the beautiful Tempest, brought back mixed feelings that he had not experienced in some time. His eyes wandered across his friends, Boda, Wang, and his dual Women. Then his eyes fell on a picture of himself and his greatest Comrade: Alexei Ruslan. Ivan shook his head and rolled his eyes as he considered the events of the day. The Russian Bear reached under his desk and turned on a tiny generator, which helped to power his small telephone. After dialing multiple phone numbers, Stanislav reclined into his phone and let it rest on his barrel chest as he waited for someone to pick up. “Da?” Came a voice from the other side of the phone. It was a high-pitched voice all the same. “Comrade… it was good to see you today… I wish it had been under different circumstances.” Stanislav replied to the faceless man on the other side of the receiver. “Well…” the voice began and then trailed off for a moment, “…I merely think that sometimes it is good to keep some blinders open to the world, this is all.” The voice was heavily Russian, but spoke English amazingly good. Ivan sighed, “True as this may be, I do not wish you to be disclosing my location freely. The last thing Ivan needs is the circus to come to Siberia.” “Do not worry about that Comrade, I will not do it again, I merely thought it would be funny to see the look on your face… and it was! Hee hee haa!!” Ivan sighed and shook his head, “Da Comrade, it was funny.” Ivan paused and swallowed, “It was also good to see you again. You did not think that disguise would fool me, did you?” “Of course not!” “Good.” The generator began to wane, “I apologize my friend, but I am losing some power here on my phone and I must go. It was good to see you old friend. Next time you choose to visit, be more formal about it.” “I will Ivan.” The voice replied, “Das vidanya.” “Das vidanya Alex.” The Russian Bear silently replaced the receiver and just softly hummed the Soviet National Anthem to himself. Russia was changing, Comrade Putin was compiling his power… fears and excitement of a communist state was beginning to echo throughout Russia. Stanislav was not deaf to these concerns. He had felt something today. An energy that he had not experienced for what seemed like a millennia. His eyes fell once more on a picture of the triumphant Russian Bear, pinning a downed Trashcan Man. He smiled. Even within the breast of frozen Siberia, a polar bear was unable to hide.
