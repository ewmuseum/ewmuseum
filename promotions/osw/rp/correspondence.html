Correspondence

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The following correspondence was mailed between the small ski town of Telluride, Colorado, and Hiroshima, Japan. Though written in Japanese, a reporter obtained these translated copies and gave them to Old School Wrestling. ------------------------------ Sensai: It has been several weeks since we parted ways, and it has been several weeks since I have had direction. I have found myself lost, a feeling of abandonment my only company in this strange new world. I have not been abandoned by you, I have been abandoned by myself. I remain in my firm position that your spiritual guidance will not give me what I seek. However, I do seek a few answers, as I attempt to bring closure to the first twenty five years of my life. I feel a few confessions are necessary. While I no longer seek your guidance, nor your company, I feel I owe it to you to be honest. Honest to you, honest to myself. While I seek release for my agression, I still fear what I may be truly capable of. After what happened that night in Tokyo four years ago, I have never been the same. The only real fear I have may be the fear of myself. The fear of not knowing who I am and where I am going. I am lost now, as I was then. In Tokyo that night, every measure of pain, suffering, frustration, and hatred was all released on her, and I simply cannot continue to hide from it. When I looked into young Ms. Yamoto's eyes, I saw her again, Yoshi-san. I could not bear the sight of her any more. Her father is a powerful man, and should anything happen to her as the result of my own lack of control, I would face hell. But I face hell now with the remnants of that night four years ago. You have shown me a path to peace, and for that I am greatful. But now, sensai, I fear that my ability to maintain that peace is wearing thin. The world itself works in this way, sensai. We all desire peace, but the path to peace may require war. Right now, sensai, I am preparing for war. This young American boy I have crushed, Kid Wonder, is this American culture I have grown to despise so much. I have seen this country now, I have seen its beauty and its ugliness. I have sought refuge in this quiet mountain town for the moment, but I fear that my memory has followed me, and I must move again soon. I fight again for the American wrestling company this weekend, and I anticipate another confrontation with America. I have no doubts that I can overcome my opponents. But for the moment, my doubts lie in my own ability to overcome myself. When I finally found my release, I was frightened by how much there was to be released. I was startled by the extent to which I was prepared to go. I was prepared to crush you, Bethany, Wonder, anyone within reach. I sought an outage for my anger, but I fear that one day I might find myself in the same position as I was in that night in Tokyo. I want to harm Kid Wonder. I do not wish to kill him. -Kimbusa ------------------------------ My Son: I will not waste my energy in attempting to sway you back to my side. It is too late for the two of us to continue down the same path. This will be my last bit of guidance for you, and it will be brief. You are indeed powerful. You are the most powerful man to step foot in an American wrestling ring, this much is certain. But their world is different than yours, their rules are foreign to our kind. Your old ways will find you nothing but trouble as you have forseen. Be cautious when confronting this Kid Wonder, for we know that you are capable of destroying him on a whim; what remains uncertain is if you are capable of defeating him in a manner acceptable to American law. Be wary, for your true vulnerability is certain to be seen by your foes. Do not underestimate your opponent's intelligence. The worst injustice you can do yourself is to deprive yourself of your rightful chance at true inner peace. Find yourself, Kimbusa, your place in the world lies somewhere in that country. You will find it, and when you do, it will spell certain doom for anyone who chooses to oppose you. You are powerful, Kimbusa. You are a foe to be reckoned with. They do not yet know the power you posess. But until you find the ability to contain those most primitive of brutish desires, you will find yourself in the shadow of those members of your profession who merely wish to entertain. You will always be my son. -Yoshi Kimawa ------------------------------ The following transcript was sent in return to Kimawa from what we assume to be Kimbusa, but it was written in a language that could not be translated in the past three days. The words 'death', 'regret', and the name 'Amber' were believed to be among the words in the letter, but as of yet this letter has not been translated.
