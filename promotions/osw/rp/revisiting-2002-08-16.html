Revisiting

16 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-16-02 We open with a car pulling into a vacant lot in back of what appears to be an abandoned building. It looks to be rather late at night, the moon almost straight overhead. It, along with the sole unbroken street lamp out near the road, provide scant illumination. Doors swing open on both sides of the vehicle, and as figures inside of them climb out and begin walking along the side of the building, we see that the occupants were none other than Seth Masters and Trevor Hawke, Civil Disobedience. As they walk, the shot pulls in for a close-up of Trevor’s face. Strands of hair wave to and fro, brushing up against the lenses of his sunglasses, which he wears despite the darkness. A serious, stoic expression covers his face, as his voice-over is heard in the foreground. Life is a popular subject for metaphors. And why wouldn’t it be, considering what it is: the representation of the collective past, present, and future all balled up together in one tiny four letter word. That really gives it an impact that few other verbal bundles could ever hope to attain. One can ponder the topic for hours without ever coming to any concrete answers to the questions of its nature, and yet even though the thoughts are fruitless, they are capable of providing their own sort of fulfillment just through having thought them. To seriously sit down and ponder existence, the process of living, is to wrack one’s brain thoroughly, for the concept is just so broad, so powerful, so inexplicable that it is near impossible to even begin to comprehend. The mysterious aura surrounding life in general has been fodder for many great thinkers throughout history, and even today it’s no wonder that people are always comparing it to one thing or another...or vice versa. Life to me, at least as of late, is similar to the feeling one would get while riding a roller coaster that became stuck at the apex of the loop. Just the feeling of pure, unadulterated exhilaration, stopped all of a sudden by matters out of your control. And then entrapment, hanging perilously, upside down, disoriented. The blood rushing to your head. Dizziness and confusion taking the place of what once was joy and bliss. It’s not quite as poetic as I would have liked to put it, but people have always considered me more of a Walt Disney than a Walt Whitman. I’m not sure how much truth lies in those words, but I usually let it slide. Those familiar with my career and more importantly, who I am as a person, know exactly why I feel the way I do. Turning a corner, the duo arrive at a boarded up doorway. Trevor reaches a hand up to start taking down the pieces of lumbar, but Seth waves him aside. Lifting his foot in the air, Seth stomps down hard into the center of the door. A few cracks appear in the restraining boards. A second stomp sends the whole thing crashing down. A wry smile appearing on Seth’s face, he motions for Trevor to go in first. Shaking his head, Trevor grins that same wry smile back at his partner, before walking over the wreckage and into the building’s interior. I, Trevor Hawke, am, at heart, a thrill seeker. Some would like to label me principally as an entertainer, but that never really sat well with me, the idea that I was gallivanting about for someone else’s amusement. That’s more Seth’s motivation in this business than mine. That is what he lives off of. I could care less. Walking through a narrow hallway, Trevor comes out into a wide open area. The absence of light indoors obscures the view of whatever may be in the room. This problem is quickly solved, however, with a wandering hand sweeping against the face of the wall. In less than minute, Trevor has located a series of light switches. Flicking them on, one by one, the fluorescent lights that line the ceiling cast sheets of light down into a room that most likely hasn’t been exposed to illumination in years. Now don’t get me wrong...if people like to watch me work and they want to cheer for me, then the more power to them. But they are not my motivation. I don’t risk my neck out there for them. No, I do it for me and me alone. And I do it because I need this. I need this because without it, I can feel nothing. I need this because without it, my existence would be mundane...trivial...pointless. As the darkness is banished from the room, the interior furnishing suddenly become visible. A large mat, rather dusty from who knows how many months of neglect, covers the majority of the floor. A heavy punching bag is positioned in near the far right corner of the room. Across the back wall the words “Aurora Dojo” are painted across the wall, marred somewhat by graffiti. Nothing else of note is located in sight. When I got out of high school, my parents asked me what I was going to do with my life. Needless to say, they weren’t very pleased when I told them I didn’t know. Yet, having faith in me as they did, they funded my college education. “He’s going to make a great doctor,” by mom said. “Nah, he’ll be an even better lawyer,” my dad countered. With their high expectation, it’s no wonder that when I came out of the place with a bachelors in Philosophy, I never thought I’d hear the end of it. I couldn’t help it if I was more interested in logic and thinking than making money. It just seemed so much more exciting to me to ponder the wonders of the universe than to argue some white trash couple’s lawsuit concerning possession of a trailer park residence. Their grateful, toothless grins just didn’t seem like they’d give me the same satisfaction as trying to figure out the meaning of this crazy existence we all live. At any rate, that was the end of the faith extension of my folk, and as they cut off their trust in me, their funds cut off as well. Broke and penniless, I was in no position to get my doctorate. And since I couldn’t get a job as a professor of philosophy without a doctorate, I was more or less screwed. So I was forced to abandon my hopes, my dreams and seek out a new calling in life, temporary or otherwise. At the time I was terrified I would become stuck in a menial position, flipping burgers, punching buttons a cash register, working a job that I hated to serve an ignorant public I hated even more. If I would have known what lay on the horizon, I would have slept a lot easier at night. “Wow, I can’t believe what happened to this place,” mutters Trevor out loud, his gaze sweeping across the whole of the room. “It seems like just yesterday we were sparring right over in that corner.” “Me kicking your ass of course,” Seth says with a laugh, before the stoic nature of the situation sinks into his head. “Man, it’s been a long time since we’ve been here. About what...eight, nine years?” “Yeah, it’s been eight and a half years since Goto Sensei passed and they had to foreclose on this place. It’s a damn shame, really,” Trevor remarks, shaking his head sadly. “It sure does bring back memories, though. Heh, if it wasn’t for this place, I wouldn’t have starting hanging out with you.” “Yeah, if you never started hanging out with me, you would never have become a pro wrestler,” Seth adds with a nod. “Boy, you sure lucked out when you started taking classes at this place. It’s funny how little things like that have a way of shaping your life.” It was a little over nine months ago that Seth, my best friend at the time, came to me talking about how PCW’s wrestling school was accepting students. I’m sure the look I gave him when he told me this was one of the most skeptical look ever directed to an individual. Why the hell was he talking about wrestling to me? I had never even watched the damn sport before. Seth, he was the one who was obsessed with it, watching hours worth of television shows, pay-per-views, tapes from Japan and Mexico. I had only caught the stuff one or two times, and I, frankly, wasn’t that interested in it. But he reminded me that I needed money...badly...and wrestling the indies was as good a way to secure a paycheck as any. So I accepted Seth’s offer to be his tag team partner, and a week later we were off to the academy, Seth with stars in his eyes, and me with low expectations. Expectations that were shattered so utterly and completely that my life has never been the same since. The two team mates begin wandering around the room, reminiscent expressions on their faces. Trevor’s trajectory ends up taking him directly in front of the heavy bag. He nearly passes it up as he walks past, but something makes him stop and stare at the leather practice tool, the sole piece of equipment not vandalized or stolen. A vacant expression comes over his face as he stares at the bag. As hollow as watching wrestling felt to me, actually stepping into the ring was a whole other story. A whole new world suddenly became open to me, one that’s provided me with everything my life had been lacking up to that point. Excitement, drama, and epicness beyond the wildest scope of my dreams. These past nine months, from the PWA straight up to the heights of OSW, have revealed to me my true calling in life. Once I hit that ring for the first time, I knew I could never go back. Forget about philosophy...I was going to be a professional wrestler. Blinking a few times, Trevor takes on a more determined look, throwing a few right jabs at the heavy bag. He visibly relaxes, becoming more comfortable as he lashes out at the bag. Soon he’s running through kata, almost appearing to be in some sort of serene dance as he flows from move to move. It’s hard to describe the feeling one gets out in that ring. The pure adrenaline rush felt when hitting a somersault plancha from the ring to an opponent on the arena floor. Your body being so hopped up on that damn hormone you don’t even feel it when your lumbar region splatters against the concrete floor. The thrill of performing in front of a packed house of rabid fans night after night sending a shiver down one’s spine. The crowd reaction making the knowledge that one day that same spine will be filled with fluid not seem so bad. Every night getting to go out there and give it your all, draining yourself of every ounce of energy, and sitting in the locker room afterwards knowing you had the best match on the card. Those are the types of things that outsiders can never understand. They may think they get it, but they’ll never truly be able to comprehend it in the same matter we do. It’s one of those things that goes along with experience. Suddenly, in the middle of the routine, an intense expression of anger comes over Trevor’s face. His eyes narrowing, a sneer forming on his lips, he stops everything, simply exuding raw hatred. And the Think Tank changed EVERYTHING! Rearing back with his right hand, he lets out a mighty blow, the impact of which sends the aged heavy bag flying off of it’s hook. Trevor is breathing heavily as he watches the bag bounce off the ground and come to a rest laying against the wall. They destroyed the excitement! They ruined my release! They stopped my roller coaster! And for what? The pursuit of power and control? Why? What would possess these men to do the things they’ve done? I know you’re supposed to understand the manner in which your enemy’s mind works, but truthfully I don’t want to know. All I know is that if there was ever a group to represent the evil, vilified side of humanity, it would be them. And although I never pictured myself as one to defend what is good and decent in this world, it appears as if that’s the role I’m forced to play in this whole ordeal. Seth comes up behind Trevor and lays a hand on his partner’s shoulder, not saying anything. The two exchange a look, nodding in sync. They both know what’s going through Trevor’s mind right now. Words would just seem superficial at this point. I don’t know what’s going to happen in the future. I’m not a fortune teller. What I do know is that this Saturday, me and Seth are stepping into the ring with the enemy for the first time in weeks. They’ve been avoiding us, they’ve been playing mind games with us, but that ends now! Semifinal match up in the tag team title tournament: Civil Disobedience versus Howard Porter and Armour. Call it what you will; the Elite versus the Think Tank, good versus evil, right versus might. Whatever. Just be forewarned that this one is going to be personal, for the Think Tank consists of the six individuals I loathe more so than anyone else in this industry. No one stops my roller coaster and gets away with it.
