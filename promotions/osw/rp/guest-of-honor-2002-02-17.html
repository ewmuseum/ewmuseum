Guest of Honor...

17 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/17/02 Tom Walczak is seen walking down a street, with a long black trench coat, and black sunglasses. It is raining outside, and the drops of water flow off of the baldhead of Walczak. The street is relatively uninhabited, seeing as more than half of the buildings on the street are abandoned, and the other ones have been closed for safety violations. Walczak comes up to a red brick building. It is one of the most decrepit buildings on the entire street, and appears as if it will topple over at any instant. Walczak looks around to see if there is anyone near him. He then proceeds to enter the building. Walczak walks through the door, and then steps over various objects that impede his progress. Walczak finally gets to the end of the hallway, and knocks on the door. A man standing at 6’6”, and weighing around 300lbs opens the door. He is dressed in a black suit, and also wears dark sunglasses. “Comrade Walczak, what an honor. Please come in I will lead you to the meeting room, and there you will be able to speak.” The man shows Walczak down a flight of stairs, which also look as if you will fall through them, and creek with every step. Finally when they get to the bottom the man opens a door. Walczak looks inside and to his amazement sees a newly renovated room. The floor is covered in carpet, and there are over two hundred plush seats that are lined up in rows. At the front of the room stands a man behind a podium. As Walczak enters the room all the people sitting down stand up and give Walczak the proper salute. Walczak walks forward and looks around the room. He sees pictures of such communist leaders as Mao Zedong, Joseph Stalin, Vladamir Lenin, and then at the front right behind the podium is the largest picture of all, and it is of Karl Marx. The man at the front begins to speak on the microphone, as Walczak gets closer to the stage. “My fellow Comrades, today our featured speaker as well as guest of honor is none other than Tom Walczak, one of the most outspoken communists on the entire planet, and part of a coalition that we all know and love, The Red Army.” Walczak nears the podium, and the people begin to clap. Walczak looks around the room, and sees that the room is filled with men, mostly between the ages of forty and sixty, some older some younger, and most of Asian, or Eastern European descent. Walczak then begins to speak to the onlookers. “Comrades, I have come hear today to talk to you about our united coalition. The one that we must carry out in order to beset the capitalist monster that currently controls the global economy. Now, when I walked in here I was mildly impressed with the amount of people that were here. I came in expecting no more than thirty people, but then upon entering the look of nearly three hundred faces put a smile on my face. However, when you look at the big picture this group means nothing. This is quite possibly the largest communist union in the world right now, but there is over six billion people in the world, and this group makes up less than one percent of that. In fact it comes nowhere near one percent. That is why we must spread the word. I am not saying that you should go out in public and give out fliers on communist gathering, but telling some of your closer friends to come to a meeting is a good start. In turn they will tell some of their other friends, who in turn will tell their other friends, and this will produce a type of chain reaction. Then once you have their attention the communist cause will become more global. Now, Ivan and I, we have our own agenda, and we feel that we are carrying it out fairly well. I have disposed of that insolent bastard, Trashcan man, and Ivan ran into a pest known as Meanstreak, but that is of no worry because Ivan will surely respond with force as he destroys him in what will surely be an upcoming rematch. The Red Army will march on and we will march through all those that oppose us. In doing so we hope to show all of the doubters that Communism is a force, and one that is stronger than ever. Thank you all for your time today.” Walczak steps down from the podium, and takes his seat in the front row. The man who introduced Walczak once again makes his way to the front. “Thank you Comrade Walczak for those words of wisdom that make yourself and Ivan two superior role models of Communism. We will now discuss other topics and Comrades feel free to state your opinion, because remember we are all equals here.” They begin to speak on various topics, and Walczak participates in many, but as the conversation drags on the scene fades to black…
