The Fight

17 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Feb. 17, 2002 Rhett took a long drag from his cigarette as he leaned up against the brick wall of the bar. 'Sweet jesus, that mother fucker is HUGE Dom!' 'Youz aint kiddin', but look here kid,' the short italian man, middle-aged it seems as he ran a hand through what little grey hair he had, stared at Rhett straight in the eyes. 'You're a big guy 'round these parts. Youz rememba when youz was just a kid, lookin' fo' a job? Youz rememba when ya took up dis fightin' bizness, an' nobody gave ya da respect youz deserved? An' now youz gettin' dat respect an' mo', an' you aint gonna fight? Youz betta be kiddin'.' Rhett sighed, and shook his head. 'It's just as hard on me, Dom. He ashed his cigarette on the sidewalk, and continued, 'But...I'm not a fighter anymore, man. I'm a wrestler. I wre-' Rhett was stopped mid-sentence by the back of Dom's hand. 'Youz a fighta! And youz always will be a fighta! Nowz you get in dat pit, an' entatain these people, ya hear? O' else you might find jaself swallowin' the barrel o' one o' Dom's pieces.' With a hand he tilted Rhett's embarassed head up by the chin, and looked him in the eyes. 'Now youz get in dat pub and fight.' In utter frustration, Rhett rubbed his hair, rubbing the gel out of the, at one point, spikes. He threw his cigarette down, ashed it, and walked in. Dom chimed, 'Ladies and gets. Allow meh to introduce t' youz, one o' da greata fightas in Dom's Pub t' date. He is, Rhett Locke!' The drunken men and women cheered and clapped their hands, the one not passed out. This was Dom's business, and he conducted well. So well that, in fact, it was hard for Rhett to push through the people to the poorly created boxing ring. And like so many fighters before him, he stepped through the brown ropes (Yes, actual ropes) with great ease, getting a slight rope burn as he grasped the top. Then, as he turned, his eyes widened slightly as he saw... A monster. Standing near 7 feet tall, scarred, scabbed, and bruised from previous fights. Rhett wore nothing but baggy black jeans and a pair of kick-boxing boots. While the monsterous man wore a full-body suit. The monster's teeth were grit. Rhett's bare fists were clinched... Rhett Locke was ready. The bell chimed three times, and like a raging bull the monster charged, crushing Rhett into the corner. Agony and pain taking the form of yells as Rhett is battered by the giant's boot, kicking away at Rhett's upper body. This was glory. This was notoriety. This was no-holds-barred fighting. Rhett Locke, in a desperate attempt, parried another kick with his own boot, and diving out of the way. The drunkards laugh, and the monster snickers. Rhett spits saliva to the mat. He was ready for the monster's charge this time, and side stepping him, the monster spilling over the ropes. Rhett soon began to realize how inexperienced the giant man was, and as the monster slid back into the ring, a swift kick to the temple was given, and the monster screamed in pain. Rhett fell to the mat, and began delivering punch after punch after punch to the giants face. And as the drunkards chanted 'Rhett! Rhett! Rhett!' and blood drooled down the monster's forehead. Rhett Locke remembered who he was... He was the fighter.
