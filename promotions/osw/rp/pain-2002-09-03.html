Pain

3 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9-3-02 By Any Means Necessary was a painful night for Ivan Stanislav. The Russian Bear’s match against The White Mexican was particularly brutal. His cheek had not healed completely from the gash opened up by the ring bell that just happened to hit his relatively thin line of skin over his accented cheekbones. His body ached constantly, perhaps not so much due to the overall offense of The White Mexican, but how hard he had tried to keep him down and had failed in doing so. But out of all that had happened, the blows, the foreign weapons, the submission holds, none of them were nearly as painful as Mexi’s words. Words that cut deep into the heart of Ivan Stanislav. Words that shattered The Russian Bear’s pride and bore through his smokescreen of valor into the core of what the fight was about. Being true to ones self. Indulging in what one would like as opposed to following orders at the expense of your own personal happiness. The words sowed wounds deep into the heart and soul of The Russian Bear. Never before had someone hit him so deeply into the core of what he was feeling. And worst of all… …Ivan knew that Mexi was indeed correct. For most men, to dare to speak of such a thing to Ivan Stanislav, particularly not only in the presence of the wrestling fans but also in the presence of Tempest would have warranted a sound and quick beating. And yet, while the extent of the match appeared to be quite brutal, Stanislav had held himself in check. He could have killed Mexi. Choked him to the point of death and no matter what he tried, the iron grip of Ivan Stanislav would never have been broken. He could have splattered his head against the mat like a potato under his boot and finished it there. But instead, he used a technicality to save him from the powerful words of Mexi. He found himself in a lose/lose situation. If he destroyed Mexi, he would be branded a monster. If Mexi defeated him he would have lost all credibility. So he chose the safest route… …retreat. It was time to get his priorities straight. To figure out what exactly he was doing with his life, or lack thereof. Ivan Stanislav found himself in his office in America, locked away from the prying camera’s of the OSW and the questions of the reporters. The words of Mexi reverberated in his mind like a drumbeat that never stopped, but continued to vibrate the reality of the matter. I feel the pain inside you from pushing the woman of your dreams away, from pushing away a true friend. He had considered El Drago a friend. And Tempest… was she the woman of his dreams? He could not answer that, or at least, could not admit it even to himself. That's your biggest problem... It's always business before pleasure with you. He had orders. He had to do what Russia stated, what his contract stated. Even with his suspension, he had an obligation, a charge for the Russian people which overruled anything. Even his personal life… …right? Ivan sat in his large chair, his back away from his desk and his arms propped on his knees. His eyes were closed tightly as once again, the drums rolled in his head… First it was Aimz you destroyed… His breathing quickened, it hurt seeing her bloody, mangled face after he assaulted her… Then it was Reno…. Reno deserved what he received… yet… Ivan had caused it… And now it will be me… …your friend. Ivan opened his eyes and stared at the floor just as the door behind him opened. He turned quickly to find his best friend, Alexei Ruslan, sliding into the room and shutting the door behind him. “Good evening… Praporschik.” Alexei said quietly, still wearing his trademark trench coat and hat. “I am not Praporschik,” rasped Ivan, “I am a just a Russian now.” “Ivan don’t…” Alexei began. “Nyet.” Ivan growled, cutting off Ruslan, “You know it as do I.” Alexei Gregorovich nodded, he knew it was better than to argue with Ivan. He had watched his friend for the last few weeks, almost distancing himself merely to try and analyze what The Russian Bear, the bulwark of Communism and the champion of Russia, was going through. Alexei shoved his hands into his pockets and walked deeper into the room, the desk serving as an inadvertent barrier between them. “I got letter in mail today from Russia… Ivan.” Ivan snorted, “Russia. What did they want?” “They want me to go back, Ivan.” Alexei paused as Stanislav looked up at him suddenly, “They want me to review something, do some sort of work, they would not tell me.” “How long?” Ivan swallowed. “How long will you be gone?” “I believe,” Alexei continued, “they only want me there for around one week, but I will not be at Slam, I am afraid.” This did not bode well with Ivan, Alexei had never missed a card before, “Alexei, you will be back, da?” “Of course Ivan, it’s just a formality thing. I have not been back for a long time and I believe they have work for me to do. Work that cannot be transferred through mail.” Alexei smiled a bit as Ivan stood, “I have to leave now, I am afraid.” “Now…” Ivan nodded, “…I understand.” He walked around his desk as Alexei sternly saluted him, but Ivan shook his head, “No, Comrade, I cannot be saluted.” Ivan brought his hand out and Alexei slowly, almost sadly, grasped it and shook, “It is only suspension Ivan, you are still Praporschik in my eyes.” “Your eyes,” Ivan said, “are not important. They belong to the State.” Alexei shook his hand and pulled back. He looked up at Ivan and spoke very softly, “Ivan… I have never left before,” he paused, “and I never thought I would say this, but please be careful. I do not wish for you to get hurt.” Ivan chuckled, “Hurt? You know no one can hurt me.” Alexei stared intensely at The Russian Bear, “You know what I mean Ivan.” Ivan looked, feigning confusion and nodded, “I can take care of myself Alexei. Do not worry.” Alexei nodded and turned. He walked to the door and looked at Ivan, “No matter what, Ivan Stanislav, you will always be The Russian Bear. Never forget that.” He nodded to Ivan and exited, shutting the door softly behind him. He contemplated exiting the room and facing the crowd… but instead turned and chose to remain inside, now truly alone. How long will you run away, Ivan Stanislav?? The answer was as painful as the question...
