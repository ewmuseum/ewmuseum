Hypocrites

24 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/24/02 Walczak is seen walking down the street; he wears a tight white shirt, and a pair of jean shorts that are held on his waist with a brown belt. On his feet are a pair of work boots that are untied. He walks down the street as if he has nothing more than a stroll planned. Walczak has yet to move back to Poland, and constantly finds himself wandering the streets, and finding nothing that pleases him. He no longer watches television because of the atrocity that America calls programming. Most of his time goes to working out which has helped him to get into top physical condition, but the fun factor from Walczak’s life has completely disappeared. “There is nothing to do in the shit hole known as America. I can do little to nothing to make myself feel good. The service in my hotel rooms is horrible, and there is nothing to do other than working out, and going on strolls. I always made fun of fairies for doing this, but I can see how easy it is to do this when no one wants to see or speak to you. The thing is not that no one wants to speak to me, it is that no one is worthy of speaking to me. You see it is not I that hate the Americans in fact if they realized the person that I really was than some of them could have been the closest of friends. They think of me as some big mean oaf, who no only has no feelings, but is a sadistic son of a bitch. While some of the facts may be true many of them are just exaggerated misnomers. The people are the biggest hypocrites in the world, and to tell you the truth that just pisses me off.” Walczak continues to walk down the street, and he looks at a couple of people, and they in turn gawk at him, but no verbal transactions take place. Walczak thirst begins to take over him, and he looks for a bar to quench his thirst. He finds one not to far away, and quickly makes his way inside. Once inside he orders a pitcher of beer. He is served, but as usual the bartender gives him an awkward glance while handing him the beer. “What are you looking at? Do I really look that abnormal to you?” “No, uhh it is just that I usually don’t see anyone come in here and order a pitcher of beer all to themselves.” “Are you accusing me of being an alcoholic? I assure you that I can handle my fair share of alcoholic refreshments.” “No, I just thought it kind of weird that’s it.” “Get back to doing your work you hypocritical son of a bitch.” “Whatever you say sir.” The bartender gets back to work, and Walczak grabs his beer and makes his way over to a table. “It is these kind of people that get me pissed off. I come in and I order a pint of beer, and then all of a sudden I am an alcoholic, when he is the one that works around drinks twenty-four hours a day. I am the alcoholic I don’t think so buddy. These damned Americans have the most confused minds of any nation on earth, and I know that I along with the rest of the Red Army are determined to set them straight. We will show the world what we can do Sunday when Comrade Krieg along with myself destroy Heresian Bloodlust.” Walczak continues to drink as the scene fades to black…
