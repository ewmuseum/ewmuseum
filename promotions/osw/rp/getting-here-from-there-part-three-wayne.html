Getting Here From There, Part Three: Wayne.

Date: 090902 In retrospect, I probably should have exercised a little more say in who Matt chose as his partner. I knew that he was a very…individualistic man. But I didn’t realize that every bridge he had ever crossed was left in flames. Four years in the business and he had not one friend. There are more living Beatles then there were people on his list of potential tag teammates. In a fit of frustration, I suggested that he start thinking about patching up differences with old enemies. Burying the hatchet…and this time, not in someone’s back. He shrugged it off at the time, but a few days later, he phoned me and explained that Jeffrey Wayne, a frequent ally/enemy of his in UCW, would be taking the spot. Gee, Matt. Thanks for letting me know. “The Legendary” Jeffrey Wayne, as I remembered from UCW, was a sniveling, whining, opportunistic brat of a man, who had a habit of stepping on whoever’s head it took to get to the top. In retrospect, he was a lot like Matt. Their last team ended when Jeff and two thugs put Matt in the hospital. It’d be an understatement to say I was less than enthused about the choice. Meeting him for the first time only did more to dampen my spirits. Though he still sported the “Legend” moniker, Jeffrey Wayne was very much a different man. Taking a page from his booker father, he moved to Japan and took over All-Asia Promotions, a company that does exactly what it sounds like it does. They’re not exactly New Japan, but then again, Jeff ain’t exactly Inoki. He knows how to sell. He’d been overseas since UCW folded. Came back for a month to win the Foley Cup tournament. All reports I read said he wasn’t the same Legend. Had curbed the whiny, conniving bit. Much more professional. He did his business, and stayed out of everyone else’s. That eased my mind a little. Both for his change in attitude and his taking home of the Cup. Meant he still had it. Meant I could work with him. Or so I thought. Jeff’s very much the businessman. Assesses his actions based on their commercial value to himself. He understands levels to the sport that most other wrestlers hire guys like me to take care of. In most case, it would make him very successful. As far as I’m concerned, it makes him dangerous. He knows about Matt and Cronus. Matt told him, I know it. He doesn’t trust Jeff, but he wants to. And a little sweet talk about “no secrets amongst partners” is all it would take for him to spill the beans on everything. That kid’s so gullible I’d sell him the Golden Gate Bridge if I didn’t think he already had the deed somewhere. Since the MET started, Jeff’s made Matt forget about the deal. He’s more worried about winning matches and less about this little underlying goal I’ve informed him of. I suppose it was ignorant of us to expect anything otherwise. In a clash of business mentalities, the stretched-thin capabilities of Cronus can’t fend off the battle-worn relations of Jeffrey Wayne. He knows Register more than we do, and he knows the game more than we do. Right now we’re splitting time. But sooner or later one of us is going to get pushed out of the frame. With Cronus breathing down my neck, I’ve got to try my damnedest to make sure it’s not us. Jeffrey Wayne was just supposed to be along for the ride. How the hell did he get in the driver’s seat?
