School is in session, por favor

6 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 6, 2002 The scene opens on the interior of a small restaurant. Inside the restaurant, various costumers mill about and chat over steaming plates of food. Based on the contents of the plates (tacos, rice, beans, etc), it’s easy to tell that we’re in a Mexican restaurant. The restaurant is currently full, filled with families out for a togetherness lunch and businessmen out for a quick meal. One set of customers stands out in particular. One of the members, a man wearing a Sobe T-shirt and jeans doesn’t stand out as much as the other: a man wearing a jacket over a spandex bodysuit. A black ski mask covers his face, making all facial features except for his jaw impossible to make out. Right now, the jaw is moving up and down, chewing the mouthful of tamale it had inserted not 5 seconds earlier. “Good, eh? Probably taste like home cooking to ya, huh?” The speaker: one Douglas Troy, part-time Sobe endorsement guru, part-time wrestler, full-time OSW World Champion; is dealing with his own food at the moment. Using his fork, he cuts off a piece of his chicken enchilada and stabs it. He dips the piece in a small bowl of chili set beside his plate and puts the piece in his mouth. He chews a few times, then opens his mouth slightly and waves his hand at it as he finishes chewing and swallows, sweat starting to bead on his forehead. “Damn, that Netties chili is hot stuff. Now I know how you can breath fire.” El Drago, not understanding any of Doug’s idle conversation, merely continues to eat his tamale. Doug shrugs and they eat in silence. Doug quickly polishes off his enchilada and chili (and several glasses of iced tea) while Drago finishes his tamales and rice. Doug wipes his mouth with his napkin and pulls a stack of index cards out of his jean pocket. “Alright, ready for some school, Drago? The first lesson is microphone work.” El Drago tilts his head quizzically at Doug in response, not quite understanding what he’s saying. “Um, er, ah… hold on.” Doug grabs a ballpoint pen and scribbles frantically on one of the index cards. He holds the card up. On the card is a hasty drawing of a stick figure holding a microphone. Above the figure is a speech bubble with the words “If you smelllalalalalalalalalalalalala…” “Like this!” El Drago nods, starting to understand what Doug’s saying. “Yeah, if you wanna get the crowds happy, you gotta do that. It’s essential.” El Drago tilts his head again. Doug thinks for a second, trying to find the best way to put it. “You know, man. ‘EL DRAGO! EL DRAGO!’ Cheers, a pop, applause.” A couple customers look at the two as El Drago nods, “Usted es un hombre extraño.” Doug has no idea what El Drago just said, but Drago’s nod let him know that he understood. “Yeah yeah, that’s right. If you wanna please the fans like I do, then you gotta rock the mic like I do.” Once again, El Drago tilts his head. “Nevermind… Now, in order to use the microphone properly, you have to speak English. Right now, I’m going to teach you some English that you will need as a wrestler.” Drago tilts his head again. “¿Por qué hablaría yo inglés?” Doug sighs. “Dammit, you’d think after all the time I spent around Frisco that I’d know at least SOME Spanish…” He pulls an index card out of the stack and holds it up for El Drago to see. On the card is a crude drawing of a pine tree. “Tree.” “Verdaderamente, ’s un árbol de pino. ’S lo que’s conocido como un árbol de Arbol de hoja perenne, llamó como tal porque’las agujas de s retienen un año verde del color redondea, mudo como.” Doug sighs. “No no no, it’s a TREE. TREE.” More looks from other customers. “¿Por qué no hace usted como un árbol y la hoja?” “What?” “¿Que?” “Tree!” “Trey!” “No Tree, treeeeeeeeeeeeee.” “Treeeeeeeeeee, treeeeeeeeeee.” Doug smiles and brings another card forward. It’s a picture of a dog. “Alright, good. Here’s the next one: Dog.” “Doug.” “No I’m Doug, this is a dog, dog.” “Allí no parézcase a una diferencia pero si usted dice tan.” Drago sees Doug’s frowning face. “Dog!” Doug smiles widely. “Fantastic!” El Drago looks puzzled. “¿Qué tiene que hacer esto con nuestro igual?” “Huh?” “Nevermind.” Doug pulls another card from the pack. This one has a picture of a bottle of Sobe on it. “Now, here’s the next one. Refreshing beverage.” A slight *ka-ching!* sound is heard off camera as El Drago looks utterly confused. “¿Que?” “Refreshing beverage that restores the carbohydrates you need to be the best you can be in the ring!” Another slight *ka-ching* sound. “¿Que? In Hell?” “Look, it’s Sobe!” “¡Mee la bebida!” “Yes you recognize it, now try and say it, SOBE!” “Sobe?” Doug smiles again, the smile of a proud teacher. “Excellent.” He pulls another card from the stack. “Now say ‘endorsement deal’.” “¿Puede decir usted el trabajo a la Madeja?” “No no no, ‘endorsement deal’.” “No, jobbed de Doug Troy a la Madeja” “Endorsement deal. C’mon, if you want to be somebody big in wrestling, you have to have an endorsement deal.” “Iré Madeja de llamada.” Doug sighs, exasperated. “Fine, we’ll go to the NEXT card.” More customers give Doug the Evil Eye, trying to communicate “Your loudness is making it hard to digest food” through thought waves. “Esto es suficiente, necesito ir idea un plan contra los Russians.” “What?” El Drago stands up and starts to walk away. Doug gets up after him and throws the card down. “Oh, so that’s what you’re gonna do? Don’t you wanna learn English, be an OSW champion like me?” El Drago turns to look at Doug, then walks away. Customers watch him go, then turn toward Doug. “Fine, I’ve got the tab. Just hold up, I’ve got some things you need to know about getting royalties from merchandise!” Doug looks at the customers staring at him. “What? Oh c’mon, don’t tell me you’ve never seen someone argue with a Mexican wrestler before.” Doug grumbles and walks toward the payment counter as the feed fades to black.
