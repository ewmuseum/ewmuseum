Training in the dark

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-8-2002 (1 am EST) Darkness. One of the most uncomfortable things for the majority of people to do is walk through a darkened room. Of the 5 major senses, two of them are considered to be the greatest producers of fear. Sight and sound. Silence. Darkened rooms, especially large ones, tend to create fear using both of those senses. In the middle of a darkened room, the only sight is black and the only sound is your footsteps. Sometimes, if you’re unfortunate enough, the ears will start ringing in the silence and triple your fear. Darkness and silence. Yep, being in a dark room can really do a number on an easily frightened person. Good thing this is Times Square. Doubly good that this is the window-covered set of MTV’s Total Request Live, where neon lights spread a small multi-colored hue throughout the room to chase all the shadows away. The dull sound of New York’s nightlife, while kept mostly at bay by the thick windows, still keep the room from descending into complete silence. Even in the multi-colored darkness of the room, a small beacon of light shines from somewhere in the back. Since the nature of most living things is to go to the shiny thing, we are drawn to the light. As we get closer, a dull steady bass thump can be heard coming from the room. Closer and closer we go, and another set of thumps can be heard. These, however, are nowhere near as steady as the dull bass guitar riff that overrides them. “Hot damn, ready to win, money in my pocket, wearin’ a grin.” As we get even closer, the focus on the picture shifts to show that the light is coming from a room in the back. “Whoopin' ass, knuckles brass, bitin fire and chewin glass.” The first thing we notice as we enter the room is a fairly standard boombox, something you wouldn’t expect to see in a highly technological studio such as MTV. The bass-heavy tune that the box is currently blasting out is ‘Fighting Stance’ by Big Dumb Face. The source of the other thumps is across the room. “Temper like a cyclone baby” A lone figure pounds away at a large ceiling-mounted punching bag like it owed him money, his movements too fast for us to see his face clearly. Jab. Hook. Feint. Another jab. “In a fightin’ stance, ambiance” Low kick. High kick. Feint. Roundhouse kick. “Temper like a cyclone.” A nonstop assault on an inanimate object. An object that, the lone figure hopes, will both multiply into two and be very animate in just a couple days. “Temper.” Suddenly, the music is turned up. The lone figure turns to the boombox to see the source of his lack of music. His face now clearly visible, we see that it is none other than Doug Troy. Doug wipes the sweat from his face as he looks at the trenchcoat-clad man who shut his music off. “Oh, hey Richard. Back from the Kumate already?” The man, Richard Blackthorne, long-time friend of Doug Troy, nods in response, his short platinum blond hair shifting as he did so. His eyes were obscured behind jet-black sunglasses, preventing them from giving away any clues. Doug turns back to the punching bag and continues his exercise. “Don’t tell me you got beat. I thought you were one of the toughest dudes out there.” The man cocks his head at Doug, then shakes it ‘no’. Doug looks at him, then shrugs and goes back to his exercise. “So,” Doug continues. “What brings you here then?” More silence, punctuated by Doug’s repeated assault on the bag. Doug stops punching for a couple moments and turns toward Richard. Richard sighs, his broad shoulders rising and falling under his trenchcoat. “I came when I heard,” he said. “Heard what?” Doug asked. “Oh, yeah that. Yep, it’s true. I’m getting back in the ring again.” “Against the very man who put you out of it. Not very wise, Doug. I thought I taught you better than that.” “’Taught me better than that’?” Doug smirks as he grabs a towel and a bottle of Sobe Power from a nearby bench. “Hey, remind me of what happened in Japan again, Rich.” Even from behind his glasses, you could see Richard roll his eyes. “No no, don’t say it. I’ll say it for you. You damn near broke my neck in my first match. My FIRST MATCH. You know what happened then?” Doug takes a swig from his Sobe before finishing his thought. “I came back and I beat you. I came back from a career-ending injury and beat the man who injured me.” “That may be true, but I am no Ivan Stanislav…” “History is just getting ready to repeat itself once again, Richard. It’s the truth. Ivan made a huge mistake by not finishing me off, and I’m about to show him just how big of a mistake that was.” Richard cocks an eyebrow at this. “Is that so? Can I ask you a question? Why?” Doug turns toward Richard and takes another drink from his Sobe. “I thought I made it pretty clear last Saturday.” “Refresh my memory.” “Well,” Doug said. “Mainly it’s for pride. Ivan’s been taking constant shots at my pride ever since he first entered the PCW. Secondly, it’s for the OSW. When I saw Tyler Nelson commit his crimes for the past year in PCW followed by Stanislav’s little temper tantrums that wound up putting people in the hospital, I got fed up and bailed. I never thought that one of them would actually come over to the OSW…” “Doug, did it ever occur to you that maybe Jane Reagen WANTS Ivan to be in OSW?” Doug shakes his head no. “No way. Not with how he treated her in PCW. But still, I do have my suspicions. I’m going to talk with her before the show on Saturday, try to get some of this straightened out.” “Right, ‘straightened out’ How about your partner?” “Huh? Oh yeah, we’re solid. Trashcan Man’s always reliable in the ring. He’s a crazy f*cker, but he’s reliable. Don’t worry.” Richard sighs again, before walking out of the room. Doug turns to him as he leaves. “Hey, gimme a call when you get done in Hong Kong, would ya? This Hollywood guy’s been bugging me about some script, and he wants you to see it too.” Richard answers back with a quick wave from over his shoulder as he walks off, leaving Doug alone in the room. Doug starts to take the towel off when something catches his eye: his knee brace. He stares at it for a couple moments in silence, then… “Karma’s a real bitch, Ivan. You’ll get yours.” Doug throws the towel so that it lands on the camera, pitching us into total blackness. “You’ll get yours.”
