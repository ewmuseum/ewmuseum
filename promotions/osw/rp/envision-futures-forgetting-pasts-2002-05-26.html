Envision Futures, Forgetting Pasts

26 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05.26.2002 A court is in session, A verdict is in, No appeal on the docket today, Just my own sin, The walls are cold and pale, This cage made of steel, Screams fill the room, Alone I drop and kneel, Silence now the sound, My breath the only motion around, Demons cluttering around, My face showing no emotion, Shackled by my sentence, Expecting no return, Here there is no penance, My skin begins to burn So I held my head up high, Hiding hate which burns inside, Which only fuels their selfish pride, We’re all help captive, Out from the sun, A sun that shines on only some, We the meek are all in one, - “My Own Prison” by Creed ][People change just as the sun rises. One minute you have a friend. The next minute you have an enemy. In an instant you fall in love and just as quick you turn to hate. This is why the human race have turned to using means of violence and hatred to make their ends, changing only to benefit themselves, not those who will no doubt suffer from their sudden metamorphosis. Our subject knows all about this bitter side of humanity. In fact, his life was forever altered because of greed and desire. Just because his parents constantly fought and battled over finance, didn't mean that the family had to be destroyed ... destroying him along with it. However, the burden proved too great and on that dreary night when the wife of Cory Rutherford pulled the trigger to her husbands shotgun, money truly proved itself to be the root of all evil. Now she was dead. He was dead. And the one innocent person in this battle, the dear grandmother, was dead as well. There was now a change, for as the young boy now known as Kory Storm watched the covered bodies of the only loves he ever knew being rolled away from him and his heart, he noticed the sick realization burning inside of him that he no longer had a family. A life. A home. Soon, however, another voice inside of the boy spoke asking him why a free spirit like the youngest Rutherford needed a warm home and a shoulder to cry on. For we're all trapped inside of our own prisons, our minds serving as the cages which protect us from the outside. As long as you can feel and hear the demons which plague the inside of yourself, life goes on. Sitting on the wooden bench inside of a rundown building which looks like a mechanical area, Kory Storm ponders over these very thoughts, the cloud of smoke which emerges from his cigarette keeping him from seeing the beast called the world all around him.][ 'Next!!' Screams an old man from a booth at the front of the main room. ][A ragged looking woman who was sitting on the bench beside Kory's stands, clutching a small baby in her arms. She speaks meekly, yet Kory Storm can still overhear.][ 'H-Hello sir.' Speaks the woman in a soft, reassuring voice. 'Yes miss?' He answers. 'What can I do you for?' 'I'd like one ticket to the San Diego Harbor,' she explains, 'my baby wants to see the dolphins jumping.' ][She reaches into the pocket of her brown overcoat and removes a mass of crumbled up dollar bills and change. The man looks down in disgust, slowly starting to count the money.][ 'Well isn't that touching.' The man says to her mockingly. 'I'd be overjoyed if I cared.' ][The woman looks shocked as Kory Storm stands up from his bench, slowly walking up behind the woman.][ 'It seems you only have enough money here for one ticket.' The man tells her in a slightly annoyed tone. 'Well it's really only me.' The woman speaks defensively. 'Surely my baby can't be subject to a fully charged ticket.' 'First off...' the man tells her straight up with a sheer look of hate in his eyes, 'it's not really only you! It's you and your lousy kid damn it! And as I own this company, your little brat can be subject to whatever the hell I want the runt to be subjected to!' ][The woman is now frantic. Tears erupting from her eyes, she explores the deepest nooks of her coat, finally pulling out a gold necklace with a ruby heart ... the glimmer of diamonds surrounding the symbol of love. She holds it out in front of the manager's face, slowly muttering.][ 'It belonged to my mother,' she cries, 'I know that it is worth something.' ][The baby begins to cry in her arms as the manager snatches the necklace from her open hand, examining it with his beady eyes. He laughs sickly and then processes two tickets from his computer. Placing them on the counter he stares directly into the woman's eyes.][ 'Get the hell out.' He says in a tone worse than a scream. It was a tone of loathing. ][The woman quickly grabs the tickets and begins to walk away, muttering in between cries of grief.][ 'I only wanted him to see the dolphins....' ][Now the woman is running away embarrassed and the man who stands behind the counter doesn’t care. Does Kory Storm feel remorse? In a way you could say yes. You see, he thinks only now of what money does to people. He cannot change the hurt feelings of the poor children he used to pick on in grade school. He cannot revoke the laughter he and his buddies in high school gave a beggar on the streets. But he can change himself for the future. Starting now. If only he would give this manic-depressive manager a piece of mind...][ 'Next!' The man yells again, feeling no remorse for the pain he just caused. ][This is the chance for Kory Storm to be happy ... to not hear demons ... speak his mind.][ 'Of course,' Kory answers, 'I'd like a ticket to get to the old Rutherford Estate.' 'Ah yes..' the man says with wonder, 'once a beautiful place but now hidden beneath dirty clouds of dust and murder.' ][Kory Storm manages a faint smirk, trying hard not to give the man any reason to be nosy. Glancing at the manager's name pinned to his shirt, Kory changes the subject to what happened earlier.][ 'I couldn't help but notice the woman you just had in here ...erm... Keith.' 'You could smell her too?!' Replies Keith with an angry look to him. 'I get those types in here all the time. With their poverty and welfare. Trying to steal food out of the mouths of the hard working American if you ask me! These little sluts walk in here smelling like a dumpster, with soiled kids fathered by crackheads, and expect my sympathy! Please!' ][The voice of a conscience boils up from inside of 'Perfection Personified'. What will he say?][ 'Fucking beggars..' Kory mumbles, 'they all should be thrown into jail! Walking up to me on the streets in their bare feet asking to wash my car or lick my shoes clean! They're all disgraces to society.' ][They are all disgraces to society?! Then what exactly is Kory Storm? The man who survived on theivery and the manipulation of others until his early twenties when he entered this profession. Now he survives on blood and violence ... hatred pays his way. Feeling slightly happy with his new customer, Keith the manager hands Kory Storm his tickets with a smile. Walking out of the building, the burning inside of Kory telling him to do something good dies down, taking a part of his already black soul with it. He is waiting in a line of different people, but he reaches into the backpack he carries and pulls out his portable CD player. If the thoughts of his past can't save him, maybe music by Flickerstick will. Slowly the line progresses, eventually ending up with Kory Storm on the bus in the seat directly to the driver.][ 'Hello,' says the driver in his imported British accent, 'I'm your driver, Albert Kirkmere, and I'll be taking you where you need to go on this fine May evening! Keep in mind that you are driving with The Spartan Bus Company! We'll get you there, even if it takes a fight!' ][Putting down the microphone and sitting down with a smile in his cushy leather seat, the driver puts his hands on the steering wheel. Kory Storm can't help but snigger at his state. Untucked shirt. Obvious fake British accent. Unshaven beard. Untied shoes.][ 'God ... someone call Jesse Ramey ... one of his relatives must have broken free and ended up in San Diego.' Kory says softly to himself in a slow, angry drawl. ][Albert must have gotten bored with keeping his eyes on the road, because soon enough he was speaking with Kory like they were old friends, using his wrestling career as a way to break the ice.][ 'So you're a professional wrestler?' Asks Albert. ][Kory Storm sits in awe of this man's ignorance. He's wearing an OSW shirt and no jacket, showcasing his bulging muscles. It isn't really that hard to tell.][ 'I guess you could say that ... that is if you're smart enough to string two words together. I haven't really had my first match in Old School Wrestling .. but I've been quite prone to confrontation lately....' 'Oh!' Exclaims the driver. 'I love Old School Wrestling! Especially that Jim Axtell, he gives hope to every working man out there. Shame on that guy Kory Storm though, raining on his victory parade like that. What a temper!' 'Well...' says Kory Storm trying to hold back his feelings of rage, 'since you like OSW so much, maybe you should pay a bit more attention to Kory Storm. If you did then your stupid ass would realize that he is me!' ][The driver goes completly red and slams on the breaks. Kory leans back in his seat and fights to remain calm. The driver, furious with himself for talking down on the six foot eight monster in front of the monster himself, changes the course of the exchange.][ 'So you're going to The Rutherford Estate?' Asks Albert. 'Yes.' Kory mutters back coldly. 'Well be sure to get a good look around, I hear there are secrets about that place that are so amazing only family knew about them!' The driver smiles back at Kory. 'Don't you worry there Al..' explains Storm, 'I know that place like the back of my hand.' ][The bus comes to a halt and Kory realizes that he has reaches his destination. That final remark from the superstar has left our faithful driver confused, and he says nothing as the bus door opens letting Kory Storm out in front of the open steel gate which would usually protect the house. Kory takes in a deep breath of air.][ 'Home sweet home!' He exclaims. ][Lighting a cigarette, Kory Storm begins the long walk to the house, seeing what has become of his old home. When he left, it was so majestic, but the rumors circulated so quickly that no childrens services agency in California would let him stay in his silence. He can still recall the whispering voices.... 'Did you hear about the young boy?' 'Yes, that's right, saw them murdered with his own two eyes.' 'I heard that he killed them all! We at the McFlaherty homestead always knew little Kory was a bad seed!' ....yet today they don't have the same effect they used to. When he was a boy, these moments of speech scared him. They made him see the truth of things. The truth in the fact that he would never see his home again. But it isn't Cory Rutherford III standing at the doorstep of the mansion he once felt safe in, it was Kory Storm. Same body ... two different mindsets. Now these only fuel him to compete with more fury and rage. Then when he finally gets the victory he worked for, the whispering ceases. Walking into the dusty hallway which leads to every room in the house, Kory hears nothing, not even the voices trapped inside of his own prison.][ 'As I've seen today...' Kory speaks, 'the days of kindness and giving are gone with the fads of The Beatles and free love. I witnessed a man like myself, out for himself, deny a woman a free ride. I wouldn't have even been that nice about it. I'd tell the stupid bitch about the only free ride I'd give her, and that's only if the slut would take a shower first. So it all comes together and proves to me that nice guys like Jim Axtell cannot thrive in a business with nothing except a blurred conception of the line between good and evil. He made a mistake coming after me, because I never forget. I remember the anger he felt, and I think it was a weak attempt at best. If you really want to take a shot at me Jim, take a shot at me with something you know will harm me. Not calling me sir or presenting me with a certificate of kindness. But with your fists. For just because I'm unable to be understanding and loving, doesn't mean that you being that way hinders my spirit. It simply proves to me that you aren't worth my time. Why? Because you're a nice guy. And when it comes to the violent games I'm used to playing, nice guys ALWAYS finish last.' ][Gazing at the untouched paintings on the walls and the broken shards of glass and police tape. Our proverbial savior speaks again.][ 'My father was a good man...' Kory whispers, 'and even though he sometimes let his stocks and bonds impact his relationship with his child, I still respected him. I didn't have a choice. My grandmother would smack me if I didn't act as he did ... with dignity and charm. My mother always told me that this was what she loved about him, even though my father always knew that it wasn't his charming smile, but his wallet that appealed to her. For a period of time we lived together. Really it was just me and the only person I ever truly loved, my grandmother, for my dear old dad was never home and my mother was usually living the high life along with him. We never expected that it would all go to shambles, nor did we see the depression mounting up. But I remember the muffled cries of my dear, sweet, old grandmother in between the shots fired by my own flesh and blood ... and I remember that on that same day I knew that I would never love again. Who is to say that it wouldn't be taken away from me, just like that bitch took it away when she fired those bullets. I remember the final whisper of my father telling me that he loved me, as he stopped breathing right in front of me and I, his son, couldn't feel remorse. He was a bastard. And even though I spit on my mother's grave for what she did, I knew all along that he deserved it. I KNEW!' ][Kory Storm, showing a side of himself that no one has ever seen before, reaches for a nearby vase and throws it at the wall, falling to his knees in front of the fireplace where at the very top mantle sits a picture of the family together.][ 'But I will continue on!' Kory screams. 'For I refuse to die like my father! And I will never give in to my demons like my mother! I will not let the beast inside of my own head devour me until there is nothing left. And I won't do this in memory of the one love I had. Nor will I do it for revenge against those who wronged me. I will do it until every athlete in the OSW hears my message and realizes that I am the only true human in their midst! I am the only man who inside the walls of my head carries the directions for living as all should follow! Never trust! Never love! Inflict pain! WIN! So join me OSW superstars, for I will lead you to yourselves. You may have your families and your friends, but who will be there for you when you lay sprawled bloody in front of me? Only yourself. Then you will realize that I was right and I guided you to being a better human. Simply because I kicked your ass. Why? Because I am the superstar ... the icon ... the paragon! I am 'Perfection Personified' Kory Storm...' ][Kory now lets the tears of pain and violence flow freely from his eyes, letting us all know just how dangerous he is. For men can express their feelings no matter what situation. But it takes a man who truly doesn't care about life to give away his weakness as Kory Storm has just done. He wallows in the cesspool of his hatred and laughs later, proven right again when he heard the news of that same woman and her baby. She indeed made it to the San Diego Harbor, and taking her baby with her, she jumped off of it, drowning. She ended her life and more importantly the life of another who couldn't even make a decision whether to live or die, all because of the pain. Is this what Kory Storm expects for the future of OSW? Death. Destruction. Only time will tell...][
