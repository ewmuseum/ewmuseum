The child saw it all...

18 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/18 The day started like any other day. A hot summer day in midtown Brooklyn. The older son, say 14 or so, sits in his room listening to his stereo way too loud. The young son sits in front of the television. The 6 year old child plays with a set of trucks his has aparently had for many years, or they were possibly the older sons at one point. He sits in his world, oblivious to the world around, oblivious to the nasty reality of life in the ghetto. He sees the Huxtables and reruns of the Cleevers, and is sure his life is no different. His parents sit on the couch behind him, oblivious in their own way. They are also oblivious to reality, crack cocaine has a tendency to do that. Dr. Cliff Huxtable is having a heart to heart talk with young Rudy on the television about going to the dentist. He mutters 'obey-kabey' over and over, causing the young child to laugh. He turns, 'Funny isn't it mommy?' he asks. 'Wha...yeah yeah, funnn....' she mumbles. He chuckles and turns back to the television show, thinking his mother ranks right up there with the great ones of all time, he is far too delusional for a normal 6 year old. Living in shit tends to make a small child delusional, or deluted, it's hard to tell. He has to think his life is just like the people on T.V., how could he cope otherwise? Just then there is a very loud, forceful knock at the door, 'Police, open up!' The parents frantically hide their 'candy', the child doesn't notice, or decides not to notice. The parents try to quickly 'sober up.' The kind of 'sober' that only a high person thinks is normal. His father approaches the door and opens it, 'Can I help you?' 'Is Gilmore in?' asks the Police man. Eventually realizing that Gilmore is his older son, the father answers, 'Yessssir he is, can I get him for you?' 'Yes please,' replies the officer. 'GILMORE!! Get out here!' yells the Dad. The music quickly stops and the door violently flies open. 'What?!?! Oh shit!' exclaims the young man as he closes his door even quicker then he opened it. The Policeman barges into the apartment and rushes to the bedroom. Upon entering he can see the kid trying to climb out of the window. He races to the window and grabs him just before he escapes. Minutes later the two exit the room, the kid in handcuffs. The kid glances at his younger brother as he exits the apartment door, giving his brother a quick wink and a smirk. The child was there, the child saw it all. The seconds drag into minutes, the minutes into hours. As the day drags on, it returns to it's pseudo-normalsy. The child gets lost in his world of television and toys, and his parents get lost in their own world. They decided to take the edge off of their emotions from the day's earlier events, so the result is their current stupor. Just then the door is kicked in. The child looks up quickly but is set at ease when he recognizes the man. A certain Cuban gentlemen who has been around the apartment a lot lately. The young man has been told he is 'Uncle Escobar.' The kid doesn't know exactly who he is or how he knows his parents, but he figures he must be like the kooky neighbors on his T.V. shows. The kid likes him because he dresses funny, has a crazy accent, and talks about candy a lot. The man has a bag in hand and begins carrying a conversation with both parents. The child blocks them out as they are interferring with his television program, but it is apparent that the level of the conversation is rising...and rising...and rising... The child turns to protest the noise... The bag opens... The bag drops, money falls from it, powder and rock scatter the carpet... BOOM! The child blinks... BOOM! Again... The child ducks under the flashes of light and the explosive sounds... BOOM! BOOM! BOOM! BOOM! BOOM! It goes on for what seems like eternity........ The child lays in a ball, fingers in ears...for minutes... He slowly musters the gusto to lift his head... .......... Blood never looks this red on television... Their faces, their expressions, as lifeless as they were five minutes ago, yet, this isn't drug induced... This is shotgun blast induced... He's seen the movies, he's seen the T.V. shows, he's seen the cartoons, dead people aren't supposed to look like this... He touches their faces, touches their wounds, touches their blood... He touches his own face, no tears, only trails of blood from his finger tips... The child was there, the child saw it all... Frankie was there...Frankie saw it all...
