Rising Sun, Fading Memory

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Storm clouds rumble in the distance, brooding as they gather over the mountaintops. Threads of lightening streak through the sky as a warning to those who lie in the storm's path, as thunder grumbles in response. The black storm slowly comes together in a violent rush of thunder and lightening, as two storms meld to make one. From this perspective, the storms collect and brood over a mighty sumo's head. Rain has begun to fall on the mountain ridge where Kimbusa makes his American residence, but the looming powers of nature do not drive the warrior inside. Instead, he stands upon his balcony, staring out at the valley below, watching as the rain intensifies a few miles away. His dark black eyes reflect the brooding of the storm clouds overhead, busy with thought and meditation. He stands unmoving, motionless, as if made of stone. Even his eyes remain unblinking, his chest unmoving as if he has stopped breathing entirely. But the storm now envelops the mighy yokozuna. One in the valley, and one in the mind. For it has been only a few short weeks here in America for the sumo, and yet his past still follows him. His face hard as rock, Kimbusa's mind trails back to a time he has tried so hard to forget. He was once a mighty Yokozuna. The highest of all honors in the art of Sumo. For sumo itself is an art form, not a form of combat. But Kimbusa made the mistake of combining the two, and found himself disgraced and dishonored. Yoshi Kimawa was, perhaps, Kimbusa's saviour. For without Yoshi's spiritual guidance, Kimbusa would have most likely fallen in with the yakuza as he did once before, or found himself in prison. The mighty warrior was once the greatest of sumos in the world, but one night caused a fall from grace that was not unlike an emotional armageddon proportional to the size of the mighty warrior's body and soul combined. Yoshi gave him direction. He gave him guidance. This is why he is in America. This is why he is in OSW. It is an escape. A change. But change was not what Kimbusa had in mind. Despite the oriental home in its secluded mountain terrain, and despite the added attention and financial success OSW has already afforded him, Kimbusa's eyes tell the tale of his miscontent. As the lightening flashes in Kimbusa's eyes, so does a brief but fierce flash of anger. An anger he has come to release. It was this anger that caused his exile from the art of sumo. The sumo are, by nature, a peaceful race. As with most of its oriental relatives, sumo is an art form as opposed to a form of combat. Kimbusa was a loyal student of the oriental arts of jujitsu, ninjitsu, judo, among others. But by his very nature he craved more. But the things he craved most were the things first forbidden by the life he had chosen. Kimbusa desired the honor of his sumo arts, yet the battle of his ancestor's wars of ancient times. A primal bloodlust resided in Kimbusa's heart and soul, a lust for destruction that was unparalelled before his time. A lust that was momentarily quenched... on the girl... 'Come inside.' Kimbusa is not startled by the sound of his master's voice. Yoshi Kimawa has silently slid the back door open and moved just behind his pupil's massive frame. Yoshi examines the storm clouds beyond, before turning to examine his student. Kimbusa pays Yoshi no mind, his thoughts still elsewhere. 'Your thoughts dwell on the past. Let us now turn to our future, and leave behind us these turbulant memories.' Kimbusa, still ignoring his master's words, remains focused on the storm. Yoshi shuffles forward, his sandals scraping against the wooden balcony, to take a place next to Kimbusa. Yoshi looks up to focus on Kimbusa's eyes, but does not find his look returned. 'That darkness still resides inside you, I can see. But tomorrow is a new day. The new day is upon us. We are embarking on a new journey, Kimbusa-san. Your thoughts are required here for you to find what it is you desire.' Kimbusa, now slightly disturbed, quickly turning to face his master. 'I desire peace!' The two share a heated glare for a moment, before Kimbusa turns back to the storm. The rain begins to pick up slightly, now falling in slightly bigger drops onto Kimbusa's oriental robe. 'Do you? I would be quite happy to hear such a thing, if I could believe it to be true. Kimbusa-san, you have always desired battle. This is the source of your current turmoil, is it not?' 'I desire peace of mind, peace of soul, sensai...' Yoshi considers this for a moment, before turning his glance upward. The storm has begun moving across the valley, and lightening is beginning to streak its way forward. Yoshi seems worried at the intensifying storm, though Kimbusa remains solid. Yoshi speaks with a growing intensity in his voice, but cares more for his student than the storm. 'How can you attain peace of mind without peace of body? Your thoughts dwell on the past. But can you not let go because of their horrors... Or can you not let go because of their pleasures?' Kimbusa, angered, turns back to his sensai and locks eyes with him again. 'I wish them to be gone! I wish the memories were banished forever! They plague my soul, they assault my mind. I crave the peace that they have disturbed.' Yoshi smiles, continuing with his point. Kimbusa, however, remains intense, locked onto his master. 'They are of your own doing, and each memory holds its own lesson. Until you have heeded their message, they will plague you. To find the peace you seek, Kimbusa-san, you must prove to yourself that the past is now history. Can you unlearn what you have learned? Can you deny yourself those pleasures that we both know you crave? Can you channel the anger towards your new career, and funnel it away from the places they damage so badly? The peace you find can be attained through your own enlightenment, Kimbusa. But you must first channel the anger somewhere that it might serve some good. The storm is looming, Kimbusa-san, but the rising sun is already moving to break dawn upon a new time for you and I. And when the rising sun strikes your face, so will it be time for you to strike down upon those who oppose you, and forever quell the demons that haunt you. Come inside, Kimbusa-san.' With that, Yoshi turns and shuffles his way to the door, sliding it open and closed as he passes. Kimbusa, now feeling beckoned inside by his master's words, glances upward to the sky above. The clouds have moved in and become menacing, daring Kimbusa to scorn their power a few moments longer. Kimbusa reluctantly backs away from the railing towards the door, taking one last look at the sky. 'The storm is here, sensai... And it is winning...'
