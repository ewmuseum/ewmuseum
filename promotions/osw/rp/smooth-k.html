Smooth K

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Toomes looks around the diner, dressed in a tan trenchcoat and a black fedora. He sees several elderly people eating their lunch in peace, subjecting their failing gastric systems to whatever ‘special’ they serve here. But no, the former OSW Champion wasn’t here to ponder the digestive powers of elderly people… Oh no. He had a mission. He strides over to a formica table, and sits in the red upholstered seat. Across from him, a woman, probably in her 40’s from the looks of it, sits, smoking a cigarette. Toomes pulls a manila envelope out of his coat and lays it on the table. The old woman’s eyes widen, and she reaches for it, only for Toomes to put his hand on the envelope. He locks eyes with her. “You have information for me?” She nods, stubbing out the cigarette. “Yes… I have some information on Alan Kriegman… I saw him on your wrestling show a couple of weeks, and I’ve been grappling with the decision to tell anybody… But I must. I’ve got to get this off my chest…” Toomes nods. “Go on.” “Well… She begins, as the screen becomes wavy… ------------------------------------------------------- It was 1983, and I was attending Pleasantville High School. It was towards the end of the school year, and Prom was coming up. I didn’t have a date, but in those days, I was a little more… rubenesque than I am now… So naturally, with Daisy Duke on television, and all the skinny little bitches in the school getting all the choice guys, there were some slim pickings. But I never though I’d actually go to prom at all. I mean, who would ask me? I was overweight and ugly, not like those rich girls who had Daddy’s money to get make-overs and such. And then, in Math Club, one of our last meetings in the school year of ’82-83, I saw him… Alan Kriegman was the school’s most gifted and intelligent students… But… He never really recovered from the Seventies. I mean, 1977 came and went, and John Travolta was like anathema to the image of the good-looking guy. The fashions were out and the music was universally reviled… Not so for young Alan Kriegman… Or should I say… Smooth K. ====================================================== “Smooth K?” Toomes interrupts? “Yes…” the woman says mournfully. “Smooth K is what he called himself. He was a self-styled Master of Dance. He wore these horribly tacky outfits of polyester and tweed… He put Afro-Sheen in his hair and wore platform shoes… It… It was horrible. I’m surprised more people haven’t come forwards… But we were young and foolish then… Perhaps, in an attempt to save their sanity, they’ve blocked it out, haven’t made the cognizant decision to put two and two together… But I have. I’ve wrestled with the knowledge for years, and its been like a poison… A cancer in my heart. I need to tell it…” The woman stops, grabbing a napkin to mop away her moist eyes. Toomes looks concerned. “Do you want to take a break? I know how hard this must be for you…” She shakes her head, choking back a sob. “No! I need to be brave… I need to tell you this story…” “Hey, hot disco mama!” he said to me. Even though I looked nothing like what those women were wearing in the dance clubs during my junior and elementary school years, he called me ‘disco mama’. He rolled up to me on skates – old roller skates, with the leather two-toned boot and thick, dirty, almost translucent apricot wheels. He wore thick brown corduroy bellbottoms, so large at the bottom they swayed when he skated towards me. He wore a thick terrycloth sweatband and wore this brown and tan jacket with that fringe stuff like you see on old Westerns on it. He had “ABBA 4-EVER” stenciled on the back of it. It was… jarring. He rolled up to me and gyrated to a stop. “Hey, disco mama, this is Smooth K… Since we’re in Math Club, hows about a mathematical equation for that pretty head of yours?” “I’m fat, not gullible, Alan.” I fairly spat at him, but what the hell. At least it was male attention. Alan’s face looked pained. “Burn, Baby, Burn! Hold on, baby! Smooth K is trying to romance you with his brain, an organ almost similar in weight, size, and circumference as another organ, conveniently yours if you wish to partake of it on Prom Night, Disco Mama!” He grinned, his braces glaring in the light shining from the windows, where I squinted out the football team in those cute little yellow and blue uniforms, practicing. “Total ugh, Kriegman…” I said out-loud, but no one in the room came to my rescue. The thin, nerdy girls were too engrossed in their Calculus books, already asked out by the desperate guys who are inbetween the stoners and the jocks and the freaks and the burnouts. But no one was asking me, except… Smooth K. He got to his knees, gripping the edge of the lacquer-covered compressed particle board desk with his spindly little fingers, and begged me… But not like, ‘Oh please, go with me to prom to at least fool my parents into thinking I’m straight” or anything like that… He… How can I put it? He laid his Smoothness on me. He got this weird glint in his eyes, and then began talking in this low voice. “Tabitha, sweet baby doll, Smooth K knows what you need. Smooth K, he… He knows your innermost desires and fantasies… Smooth K will take you to the prom…” I shuddered, but he kept going on. “Smooth K will show up at your parent’s abode at five o’clock sharp. Not one minute variation, even with the traffic. No, no amount of gridlock will keep Smooth K from taking his princess to the prom, her very special night. Oh yes. “Smooth K will show up in his finest attire, complete with corsage, bought only from the most discriminating of tuxedo and corsage dealers. If they offer me something I suspect that will not please you, I will demand something that will, not that would happen, for Smooth K is inside of you, he knows what you want. “Smooth K will lead you by the hand to the blackest, shiniest limosine in the Tri-State area, and open the door for you – not the driver, oh no. The driver is not going to the Prom with you, oh no, Tabitha… Smooth K is, and Smooth K is the one who will open your door for you. “Smooth K will then offer you the finest selection of wines and spirits, to ease your understandable tension, for this is your special night. He will lead you out of the limo, and take you into the gymnasium, where Smooth K will dance when you want to dance, to romance you on your very special night. And then, when the dance is over, Smooth K will take his very special lady to a pre-arranged and pre-booked hotel room, where Smooth K will lay the boogie and proceed to plow your virgin furrow with his Smooth Disco Beat. You like that, Tabitha? You wanna be the dancing queen? So young and sweet, only seventeen? Dancing Queen? Feel the beat from the tambourine? You can dance, you can jive, having the time of your life?” I stared at him. “That’s a line from ABBA, Alan…” “Are you wet yet?” he asked. Well, truthfully, I was. No male had ever paid attention to me like Alan did that day in Math Club. And, true to his word, he showed up at my house 5 o’clock sharp in a black shiny limo. And we danced… Oh, the dancing. I was too drunk to do anything but sway around, trying not to puke, but Alan… I remember his dancing. Like a blur in his powder blue tuxedo, I think I picked out every move John Travolta did in Saturday Night Fever in that powder blue blur. And then, he took me to a hotel room. I don’t remember anything past getting through the door. But I have an idea… ======================================================== “Alan Kriegman popped his cherry on a drunken fat chick who was too fat and ugly to be asked out by anyone else.” Tabitha cried into her napkin. “Eww!” Toomes utters. Tabitha looks at him, her mouth open. “I’m trying to tell you the single-most horrible event in my life!” she sputters. Toomes shakes his head. “I’m sorry, it’s just… It’s just too true to form… Please, go on…” Tabitha sighs, wiping away her smudged mascara. “Not much to tell, really… I woke up in a puddle of my own puke on that hotel bed. I felt sore and sick. Then I heard Alan say ‘Good morning, sweet thang! Smooth K just took a shower… Looks like you had a little too much Smooth K for your own good!’, and then I raised my head out of the puddle of bile and expensive wine, and looked at him in a terrycloth robe, his Afro-sheened hair tussled and smoking a pipe. I screamed and ran out of the hotel room naked, running the six miles back into town. After the commotion died down, I went into therapy, to deal with… You know…” Toomes nods. “I understand. I know re-telling this…horrible story must have been hard… You’ve earned this.” Toomes slides the envelope across the table, where the woman grabs it and stuffs it in her purse. “I… I’ve got to go.” She says, and rushes out of the booth, heels clicking on the tile as she barges for the door. Toomes sits there, and pulls the tape recorder out of his pocket. “That should do it…” he mutters, and lays down a ten for the coffee the woman drank… Six cups to work up her courage, apparently… I got your number now, you son of a bitch… Porter’s next.
