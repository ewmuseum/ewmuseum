Reflections over fermented hops and barley Part 1

18 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-18-2002 Taped: 7-16-2002 The scene fades up inside a bar on the outskirts of Omaha, Nebraska. The bar is clean and tidy, creating a mood of relaxation for those who choose to drink there. A jazz quartet plays a mellow tune from a small stage in the middle, giving suitable mood music for the few patrons that chat amongst themselves quietly. The bar is nowhere near capacity, the majority of the tables with the chairs resting atop them. At the bar counter sits Nate Dumas. Nate is dressed in his standard OSW gear, an OSW T-shirt and jeans. Sitting in front of him is a slightly drained mug of beer. He looks around the joint, obviously waiting for somebody. He checks his watch a couple times, and then sighs. “Not again, Doug…” “Hey,” comes a voice off to Nate’s right. There stands Doug Troy, dressed in a Sobe T-shirt and jean shorts. His clothes are pretty clean, but his face looks like it could use some serious attention. The chin stubble displayed a couple weeks ago has been replaced by a scraggly unkempt beard. Dark circles lay under his eyes, which are bloodshot from either too much booze or not enough sleep. Maybe both. Nate looks up at Doug. “Jesus, Troy. You look like hell,” he says. Doug scratches at the unkempt beard. “Yeah, I guess I do.” “You heard of that Mach 3 thing? You ought to look into it, Doug.” Doug chuckled at Nate’s joke and sat down. The bartender wanders over to Doug. “What’ll you have, sir?” “Ah, make it a Heinieken. I’m getting tired of Busch.” Nate sniffs in Doug’s direction and scrunches his nose up. “Smells like you didn’t wait until you got to the bar to start the night off.” “Yeah, I had a couple before I walked over here.” “Walked?” “Yeah, the motel I’m staying at here is only a couple blocks down the road.” “Motel? Aren’t you staying at your home?” “Nah, got tired of being around my parents all the time. They’re trying to convince me to get into their business again…” The bartender clangs a mug filled with beer down on the counter in front of Doug. Doug nods and grabs the handle. “Mmmm, that’s good. Am I the only one getting déjà vu right now?” Nate looks confused for a second. “Huh? I… wait…” The look disappears and is replaced by a knowing grin. “Oh yeah, the night you offered me a spot in DAMN.” Doug took a good-sized swig from his mug. “Bingo. Too bad you turned me down.” “Ah, the good-old-days. Man, a lot of stuff’s gone down since then. Lot of faces, lots of matches…” “Mmm, like that first Carnival Of Carnage?” Nate nodded his head. “No way could anyone forget that match. That was one of the greatest matches I ever witnessed in my life right there.” “Ah, my favorite from back then is still the Kmart match I had against Homocidal Anthony Cay.” ”The Kmart match? Yeah, that was a sick ass brawl. First time I ever saw someone get stapled to a piece of wood in a wrestling match.” Doug took a deep deep drink from his mug, polishing it off. “Back then man, it was all about innovation. All of it. We set the standards that they’re trying to surpass today. Did you catch Gold and Glory a few weeks ago? That last PCW pay per view?” Doug asked as he pointed to his mug. The bartender took the cue and started to fill another one. “Yeah, I caught the tail end of it. What about it?” The bartender placed the mug in front of Doug, who grabs it. “Saw this real interesting thing involving this Mexican guy, Juan something…” “Juan Borro. Yeah, I heard about that.” “That’s him. Anyway, he weeps about it being his last match and how he lost his belt, then he pulls a knife on this Chinese guy who beat him…” “Charlie Loc.” “Bingo. The lights go out and when they come back on, Borro’s dead, shot fucking dead on live TV. The cops arrest this other guy who’s the Spanish guys brother or something and the Chinese guy has this big shit-eating grin on his face. And here we have the fucking announcers acting no differently than if someone had just been put through a table! A man fucking died on national TV, everybody and their mother knows who did it and the fucking announcers act like it’s part of the fucking show.” “I know. They handled that completely wrong, I think. Then again, when your federation has someone like Clyde in it, you get desensitized to things like that.” Nate took another drink. “It’s a sad state of affairs, I know.” “That’s not wrestling, goddammit. Guns and knives aren’t wrestling. Murder isn’t wrestling. So why do they insist on putting bullshit like that on a wrestling program? It’s not right. That’s the difference between then and now. Then, we innovated to make the audience clap. Now, they shock to boost the ratings.” “That’s business as usual, Doug. Business as usual.” “Business as usual…” Doug took another large drink from his mug. “You know, I used to love this business. I used to love everything about it. I used to love the travel, the competition, the roar of the crowd. Now… it’s like I don’t even know the business anymore.” “I know what you’re saying, Doug. Remember Fedwars? Man, for about three months, I hated wrestling and everything about it. That place had so much infighting and backstabbing that it just boiled my blood…” “No shit? Pissed me off to, especially when the roster theft started up. I swear PCW had the highest turnover rate of all of those federations during the Fedwars era. All those guys coming in, thinking they were going to get an easy ride to the World Title. All those guys coming in and getting sent right back out because they tried to get ahead on the value of their names alone. The PCW revolving door.” “Like Kurt Ragnarok?” Nate asked before taking another swig of beer, finishing off his first mug. The bartender descended on it with great haste to fill it again.. “Fuck. Yeah, like him. How the fuck did a waste of evolution like him get hired in the first place? Dude was a complete asshole to everybody. Hell, him and his entire 24Gay crew. It’s things like that that sour me on this business, when useless assholes get hired just to pop a rating.” Doug took another drink from his mug. “That’s what pissed me off about PCW, Nate. The guys who worked their asses off get screwed and pushed aside because they want to ‘pop a buyrate’ or ‘shock people.’ Maybe if things weren’t so political, they wouldn’t NEED to do shit like that to gain a tenth of a ratings points.” “I agree with you 100%, Doug. Once the Media got in, it started going downhill. Sure, PCW made more money, but the guys stopped being wrestlers and started being circus monkeys.” Doug nodded as he finished his mug of beer. “Speaking of animals, I gotta go drain the lizard. I’ll be right back, man.” Doug stood up and walked away from the counter and the feed fades to black.
