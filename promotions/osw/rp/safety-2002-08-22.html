Safety

22 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-22-02 For forty years I have been what I am, and for forty years it has been perfect. How could one person, one individual, teach me that the flawlessness of my life… was nothing more than major imperfection? The last time that I stared at this building it was colder and yet I find myself shivering. The last time that I stared at this building, I was a hated man, a despised man. The last time that I stared at this building… I was scared. …and I still am. Ivan Stanislav stood staring at a large apartment building in the middle of New York. The apartment building that housed one Tempest, a woman who had forever changed his life. It was dark outside, probably around midnight as he stood across the street and stared at the building. He wore a heavy black trench coat, wide brimmed hat, and black pants as he watched, fading into the shadows the crept around him. A few passerby’s had noticed him, which was not much of a surprise, as the hulking man who towered over all those around him stood stock still with his back to the convenience store behind him. However, even Stanislav looked tiny when compared to the building that he gazed at. He stared high, several floors above him, to a small landing with a sliding glass door. The light was on… I wonder if her room is as busy as it was the last time I was there? Busy is good word, for it was not messy. It was not out of any true order. But rather… active in strange way. It broke my heart when I entered her apartment last, devoid of all belongings and objects that made it such a beautiful living area. She had moved out. She had moved out because of me. He swallowed as a shadow played across the door that he stared at. I probably have the wrong window. That probably is not her. Why would it? How could she get same room from before? Once again I find myself chasing some sort of dream that will never become reality. Ivan bit his lip absently and furrowed his brow as two teenagers walked by him. She said that she had admired me for fighting for something. But for what? For something that never will be. Just like we will never be. I should leave. I should not have come here. Stanislav turned to leave just as a silhouette appeared behind the window of which he was watching. He froze and watched the form for a moment. He knew it was Tempest. His first instinct was to walk away, but he couldn’t. From this point I can see her and not harm her. I can see her and not make her uneasy at all. I could go into that building and see her and fix it all, right now. Everything would be fine if I did such a thing… Ivan closed his eyes slowly and shook his head…. …but I cannot. The door to the landing opened slowly above him as Tempest stepped out into the windy night. She shivered from the wind, her black t-shirt with a glittery silver star on the front not much to shield her from the biting cold. She brought her arms around her body, walked to the banister, and looked down at the ground below. Ivan froze, petrified under her gaze. Those blue eyes… those beautiful blue eyes. So powerful they could melt the Siberian snow… capable of piercing any tanks armor… able to… quell a raging Bear… Ivan looked up at her, fearing that perhaps she would notice him. Did she? He was unsure. She did not seem to stare at Stanislav much, but rather glance around the area. She looks worried… I wonder if it is because of me? Ivan looked down at the sidewalk and swallowed. Of course it is because of me. I have caused her worry ever since she laid eyes on me. I am a monster. A monster that has done nothing but terrorize an innocent girl. As Ivan raised his gaze up to Tempest again, she brushed a stray purple hair out of her face and looked to the sky. A car horn blasted in the background and the sky was cloudy, but to Ivan Stanislav, it was a truly beautiful moment. He absently raised his arm as if to reach out for her and then paused. I once heard of play… Romeo and Juliet….. was not the fair woman on balcony and Romeo… was on ground beneath her? I think he confessed his feelings for her… and it ended in tragedy. Tragedy. Suddenly a flash shifted through the clouds above the apartment building. It was almost beautiful in a way, had it not been for the sudden rumble and jagged bolt of lightning that pierced the sky seconds later. Ivan gasped audibly and recoiled his hand. A storm… no, no, no, no, not a storm…. Tempest brought her hand up to her mouth and looked around, a sudden look of fear over her eyes. Ivan whispered, “Do… Do not be afraid… Ivan is here…” She quickly recoiled back into her apartment as another bolt of lightning webbed across the sky and a torrent of rain fell from the sky. Ivan raised his hand as she shut the door and roared, startling a woman scurrying under a now opened umbrella. Just as his roar grew in strength another crash rocked through the sky, drowning out even the mighty Russian Bear. Tempest was safe within her apartment as the rain fell, but Ivan Stanislav remained outside, staring up at the bland, overbearing building in front of him. I remember how she was inside when it rained. She shook like wet cat in Red Square. I will stay here, at safe distance, and watch after her.” It rained for three more hours, and Ivan Stanislav remained outside her building, watching. Even when the light shut off and the blinds were drawn, he would not leave until it stopped raining. When the rain did finally ebb and ultimately stop, it was late into the morning. Ivan Stanislav’s pale face was red from the rain, he shivered within his coat and turned on his heel and walked down the quiet, soaked street. Another storm defeated Tempest, I hope there are not many more to come…
