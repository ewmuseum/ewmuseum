Binge

1 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 1st Think back into your childhood, I know this was most likely a long time ago. But, think back still. Do you remember that one day when you were bad as hell, but your parents still took you out to Chucky Cheese's? Millions upon millions of people have done research on why things like this tend to happen, some results being that the parents were too lax or wanted to have only the best for their children. But I've found it conclusive that those facts are bull; and that the reasons parents give children what they want, no matter their behavior is because they deserve it. They deserved to get that lollipop after threatening to turn you into social services. They deserved to watch the cartoons instead of the damn news. They deserved to eat chocolate instead of brussel sprouts at dinner. And things will be the exact same way once these spoiled ass kids grow into adults. They cry and weep and demand for things that they deserve. Running their mouths about things they can't even imagine, not paying attention to values and qualities they haven't learned as a child. These adults are usually people in the form of 'Black Ice' Matt Blaze. Whiners, complainers, moaners. But people always change their ways. Maybe Matt is changing his, he hasn't whined yet. He hasn't bitched..wait, I take that back. He's practically begging for attention. Press here, press there. He's firing comments here, answering questions there. He's not ready to face me. Even if given the opportunity, he'd still not be ready. It just so obvious. I see it all coming, no Black Ice, just a clear road. And I'm drivin my car straight down it. No stoplights, no yield signs, no nothing. Just me, getting to where I need to be. The top of the card, the main event, and the biggest attraction. That's all I want. It's not so much to ask of you, but no matter, I'll have it anyway. The screen fades to straight darkness, and you can soon hear what sounds like the typical mother/daughter argument over what she can and can't where. 'I don't care what you say, Ally, that shirt is simply too short and no daughter of mine will be dressed as some..some houchie!' The mother says, matter of factly. 'It's not a houcie dress, moooom. It's what everyone is wearing. You're ruining my life! We don't live in 1962 anymore! George Bush is President, not George Washington!' The teenage girl retorts then throws the mini, mini shirt in her hand down at the ground. 'Watch you're mouth young lady. Maybe if you were more involved in your studies, you've find that George Washington wasn't even President during my childhood. It was..' The mother begins. 'I know, I knooow. Can we just go home then, I'll ask Daddy to come to the mall and buy it for me.' Says the girl as she cuts her mother off, rudely. 'You're father knows how I feel about these kinds of things.' The pale skinned mother, with light blue eyes, wavy blonde hair, and seeming to have lipstick everywhere except on her lips, then turns around and looks up with scorn as she sees the black shadow cast over her. Not yet making her way up to his face, she begins to walk away and yanks her daughter with her. Her daughter, however digs the nine inch heels on her shoes, into the rough store carpet, in order to talk to the man to whom the shadow belonged..OSW wrestler Drew Hostyle. 'I think I recognize you..Hey, aren't you that guy from the OSW?' she asked, teething because he already knew it was true. 'Yeah, that's me.' Drew Hostyle answered back, not making her struggle to get the simple answer. 'Really? I never thought I'd meet Drew Hostyle..can I have your autograph?' Drew slightly chuckled to himself, and signs the paper with his own name, Drew Hostyle written on it, visably. 'Thank you' the girl says and smiles. 'You're welcome little girl.' Drew says with a smirk back. The mother turns back around and looks at her daughter with a face full of disgust once again. 'Ally May Hudson! You get your body over here right now.' she yells throughout the entire store. Suddenly a short, tiny little black man with black Roc-a-wear shorts sagged down to his ankles, an exagerrated limp in his stride, and a sideways matching Roc-a-wear metallic black hat. He bends down, so that his boxers becaome visable and picks up the mini, mini shirt that the girl has thrown down. He then smiles, and makes his way over to the mother, with the skirt still in hand. 'Yo, did you drop this lady?' 'Well I..it just..when I...' 'Well it just turns out that we got a new policy round here. You drop it, you cop it.' Says the short man, as he winks at the girl, who's now glowing at the fact that her mother will have to buy the skirt now. 'Cop it? I don't want to bring the law enforcement into any of my affairs, young man' 'Yeah, you gotta cop it..buy it, woman. It means you gotta buy it.' 'Really? Well I've never had such terrible service in my life, and I certainly will NOT...' The woman stops mid sentence as both the men look up at her with confused looks on their faces. Looks that say no one's ever said no to them before. 'What you say?' The sells man asks again, seeming more angry this time. 'I said..I don't know the way to the check out line..sir.' The woman statters as the smaller man points. She then walks away with her head held high in the air, like she wasn't going to let this little altercation shape her dignity. But when she's more then hearing distance away, both men burst out laughing. 'Why'd you have ta go scarin white folk like dat?' Drew asks the little man. 'I aint' scare that old winch, you heard her hollerin at that girl, I was just gonna help her out.' 'Pookie, man don't try to convince me, your boss saw the whole thign go down. Here he comes now.' A middle-aged white man, with a serious problem of acne and glasses that are so thick, they can see into the future, walks over to Drew Hostyle and the man who's some distant kin to him, Pookie. 'Pookie, there's been some things brought to my attention as of late.' 'Oh, no' Drew muttered to himself, this was the fourth job that Drew had gone out of his way to get Pookie into. 'You've made the most sells than any other employees, I don't know how you do it, but you're EMPLOYEE OF THE MONTH!' Pookie's worried look changes into a wide smile and he slaps the hand of the partner to his left, Drew Hostyle. 'Fo real? I don't know..somehow peoople see dis bright smile and they wanna let me sell dem the best clothes they can afford!' 'That's the kind of attitude we like to hear, at THIS store. Congratulations Pookie!' The manager of the store, then hands Pookie an envelope and a pink hat to wear. 'Uh..thanks a lot, man' Pookie says, stuffing his pink hat into Drew's hands. 'No problem, and you get the next 2 days off. Have fun!' The two men then walk off into the lobby of the huge mall they're in. Pookie finds that in his envelope he was given $50 extra. They both agree to spend some money in the mall and make their way over to a shoe store called, Foot Fungus. 'What the..foot fungus?' 'Yeah Dog, dis the ghetto side of the mall..the otha side got Athlete's Foot, dis side got Foot Fungus.' 'Well I don't trust no store called Foot Fungus, let's go to the otha side.' Both men then laugh as they make their way slowly but surely to the other side of the store. When they get their they are surrounded by tons of whites and appear to be the only black faces in the crowd, the man however make their way to the Foot Locker and prepare to buy themselves some shoes. 'Hey mane, you like des ones?' Pookie asks holding up a pair of white DC's. 'They're aight I guess.' Pookie and Drew keep looking for different shoes until they're both satisfied with what the other has picked out. They then ask the nearby employee to get their correct sizes and as they wait to try on the shoes, Pookie starts up a conversation. 'So..Drew you ready for yo match tomorrow?' 'Oh, fo sure. I'm pass bein ready, for this match, I'm ready to show somebody how I take put punks that don't even bother to put up a decent fight, against me.' 'He's been showing up, mane. He's just been talking to the press about you, I even heard he said he don't know you Dog' 'Oh, he don't know me? He'll know me well enough when my foot is imprinted in his punk ass. I'm sick of people talkin bout they don't know me, yet my name and only mine is what comes outta they mouth in every other sentence. It's obvious that people know me, I've already got their attention, but tomorrow, I'll show them why I have it, and just why they decided to pay attention in the first place. 'Black Ice' isn't a challenge. Hell, Reagan probably signed him because she was afraid of what he'd do to her with all those circulating in his bloodstream. But I'll cut his hype down to size. Expose him for the wanna be that he is, and my job will be done for the first night. I'm lookin to make a positive impression. There's no need for idle threats, he'll actually SEE this ass kickin comin, if his eyesight is even 20/20. Speaking of 'Black Ice', ice of any kind on the road is something I'd wanna avoid. And this dude sure as hell ain't cool. Nor is he talented. This hometown hero is not going to get no fame and glory on Slam, he's gettin somethin he must never have had. A reality check. He ain't no hero, the only thing that comes close to one these days is people like Jane Reagan, givin spoiled ass punks like him a chance at the big time. We'll we real folk earn our's the old fashoined way, through hard work and sweat. 'I hear you man, and..since I'm off, I censee you too! I'll have some front row seats at Slam Dog!' 'How'd you get those tickets man? By any chance, do you get them legally?' 'Hell nah..' I didn't think so..man let's leace before you do somethin else wrong. I don't feel like goin through no more drama today. Just then, three police men from each direction of the mall, enter Athlete's Foot and ask both men politely to step out of the store and not to sturrgle while they hand cuff each of them. Drew, still confysed about the whole sitaution goes willingly, yet wonders if not if he, but Matt Blaze will be ready for their match at Slam! He'd get Pookie and himself out of where ever it was he was going..somehow. 'Where are they taken us, not to jail, right? RIGHT POOKIE?!' Drew asked, still confused and a little upset. 'That ain't it mane, they don't execute no brothas for shopping...you think we being booked for shopliftin'...?' Pookie answers. 'Well, that depends...when I told you to put those damn undressable eye-glasses back on the shelf...did you listen?' Drew asks..sounding suspicous. 'Damn Dog, I was thinkin' that....' Pookie is cut off. 'DID YOU OR DID YOU NOT?' Drew shouts. 'No..I didn't. But it can't be THAT bad can it? I mean what's the worse they can do?' Pookie says, still calmly as if he'd been in this situation tons of times before. 'Throw us in jail maybe...' Drew says, dreding the thought. 'But fo how long?' 'Don't matter how long. They already know recognize this handsome face in there.' 'Heh, heh...mane, they probably gotta go to the bathroom when they think about your ugly ass face.' 'Just shut up fo once, damn. I mean we all know what kind of effect I have on the tricks of America...no just think about the men, probably jealous ass boyfriends that can't satisfy they women..or worse yet, they might be planning on saving me for themselves, yo.' 'Yuck...That's sick man, stop that shit.' Pookie says, sounding like he's beginning to get sick himself. 'I'm just jokin wit you, Dog. Cept about that trick thing..you know I be..' 'HOW WE GOIN' GET OUTTA HERE BEFORE THE MATCH!' Pookie says, finally getting Drew's full attention. 'Who you gettin loud wit'?' Drew says, shutting Pookie back up. 'You, dumb son of a...' At that precise moment, one chunky police officer followed by two more, grab Drew and his relative by the neck and shove them with exagerrated force into the rear of the poilce cars. Drew and his nephew then glare at the officers through the window, however they ignore them and go on to make the typical black men hold up, call back to the main dispatch. Officer: We're now on a Code 243, I repeat Code 243, all incoming officers can now turn around, the situation is now in control. Yes sir, we got em, and wooo eee, was it a struggle. They almost took down Officer Marshall and Officer Rascoe, until I stepped in. Yes siree, we got em in the car now. We should be at the dang station in a few minutes sir. Uh huh. Yes siree, I'll be sure to bring back some donuts, for ya sir. What kind do ya fancy? Chocolate specked spinkles? You say ya never heard of it? I'll see what I can do then. Okie dokie, alrighty, bye bye. Heh,. that was cold. We all know that wasn't what happened, but they could care less. What about Slam!? Will he be there in time? Will he be there at all? ....Don't ask me.
