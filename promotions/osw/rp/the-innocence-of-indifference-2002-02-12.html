"The Innocence of Indifference"

12 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-12-02 'Depression is merely anger without enthusiasm.' - Unknown It's really late on a bitterly cold night in St. Cloud, Minnesota… We watch as Lennox walks across the doorway and into his home. It has been quite some time since he's been able to return to his St. Cloud suburban home. What a necessary mini-vacation, a few cherished moments in time to find some relaxation and comfort. For soon, very soon Lennox Lanthrop will take part in a match, marked one of the most physically demanding stipulations in all of wrestling. An all-too-talented wrestling veteran does nothing to ease or lighten this load. He flips on a light and drops his gym bag with a loud thud on the tile floor. He stands there for a few moments, surveying his living room as if it were some unusual foreign stranger. The sheer curtains and the inviting set-up. It's extremely extravagant, and expensively furnished. During the days of LoW, when Lennox watched his mother defiantly walk out on him. She just deserted him, and married one of Lanthrop's bitter rivals, Chris Goebbles. Lennox then indulged himself with the expensive; a visible victim of extreme materialism. It all took the pain away, but it was shortly after that this newfound happiness would just slip away, and Lennox was left once again with nothing. Now, Lennox treads across the thickly plush carpeting…stopping at a corner wall (near the kitchen); Lennox catches a glimpse of himself in the mirror. He approaches the mirror eagerly. His eyes are nothing but a glaze as he looks at his reflection in the mirror. The light in the room is dim, and only a small portion of his face is clearly visible. His face turns to frustration once more as he tries to focus at the image he is staring out. Surely it is not his. It's another stranger, one unknown to Lennox…the salon-style spiky hair, the well-trimmed goatee. He looks at his clothes…and nearly chokes at the sight of his unusual attire. Wrestling made me this way…the fans…the front office…my publicist and agent…they would never let me think for myself. It was always 'In my best interest.' Pssh. They tried as hard as possible to make me 'marketable & likeable'; when in bitter truth they used me as an instrument for financial gain…how patheticly sick. There will be no more of this, that's for sure… Lennox's stern face gives a slight nod as his right arm casually swings to the side, accidentally knocking a picture frame off of its proper place; on top of the table. It lands and breaks with a loud crash on the floor. Lennox shudders at its sudden impact, then realizes indeed what it is that he has dropped. He stoops down and picks up some shards of glass and disposes of them in the trash. He picks up the slightly torn picture... His vision seems angry as he looks at the image. It's a picture of him with a small group of mentally retarded children. Two are dangling from his muscular arms on both sides, and Lennox gives a million dollar smile and an innocent wink. After all, his publicist said, 'It would be a great ploy to quickly boost your status as a friendly wrestler, one that the fans and media are sure to love.' Surely enough, the media ate it up. They flocked around Lanthrop like a flock of mindless sheep. I would have done the right thing and done the gig anyways…but my publicist just had to charge $150 an hour for a public appearance. Lennox lets out a deep sigh as he indifferently rips up the picture and tosses it away…. …Into the garbage. fade. View Lennox Lanthrop 's Biography
