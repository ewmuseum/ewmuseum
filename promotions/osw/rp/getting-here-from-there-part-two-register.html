Getting Here From There, Part Two: Register.

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 90902 Matt Register has been a professional wrestler for about four years now. He didn’t start training until he was out of school. And even then, it took him time to get a job, thanks to that temper and attitude that some of you might be familiar with. He paid his so-called ‘dues’ managing and wrestling under a hood as a luchadore who shall remain nameless. Then, in the year 2000, he finally got what you could call his big break. And that is when we at Cronus started watching him. I had been told to look for a wrestler. Somehow it linked into this massive strategy of theirs. Another piece of the puzzle. And from the sound of it, this was a pretty big one. Since I wasn’t learning anything from my superior, I turned to more underhanded methods to find out what was going down. Whatever this grandiose idea was, it needed someone within the public eye. A person who could be noticed by the populous if they were to do or say something peculiar. Politicians come with too many strings, and besides, they were already being controlled by enough people. No, for this scheme, they needed someone in the entertainment industry. It couldn’t be an established star. Their careers are too fickle to bank on, and previous work could jeopardize the light in which they were received by the masses. It needed to be someone new. Someone who Cronus could dictate too. And the more they thought about it, actors are too flakey and unreliable to carry in this situation. It could take someone seven days to hit it big, or seven years. Not worth the trouble. It was this type of logic that led them to settle on professional wrestling as the source of our new friend. Less respect. Less intelligence. Lee independence. A down-on-his luck wrestler would be easy to control, at least in their eyes. So I was told to keep my eye on the indies, looking out for and up-and-comer who had a shot at the big time, who also would be an easy subject for the experiment. Someone who cared more about advancing his career and less about trivial things like ‘morality.’ Still working as a manager, Matt and his charge, a one-trick pony inmate gimmick named Crazy Tom, found their way to a developmental fed for a Texas-based company named UCW. Tom, understandable, did not impress the officials. Matt did. They offered him a deal, provided that it’s only him along for the ride. He wasted little time in agreeing. The developmental was closing, as none of the talents had panned out. The last show featured Crazy Tom, with Matt in his corner as “Jim the Orderly,” challenging for the Heavyweight title. It ended quickly. Tom bounced off the ropes, Matt tripped him. Tom stumbled forward into his opponent’s finish and a three-count. By the time he realized what was going on, Matt Register was long gone. It was all about him now. That story was all over the dirtsheets the next morning. I saw that and called it in immediately. This was our Prometheus. The idea was that he would have to contact us, to keep him quiet about the project and uphold his end of the eventual deal. Matt’s career was reaching an apex in UCW, and we all know that professional wrestling is much like a drug habit. Extreme highs followed by extreme lows. We had an associate in the company plant our contact information on him. Sure enough, months later, UCW closed, and he was without a job. He spent some time playing the tournament game, trying to land a job with a host company to no avail. Then, somewhere around the quarterfinals for RPG3 in LA, he found the number and dialed out of desperation. From then on, he was ours. Cronus dispatched to me the foggy details of what Matt’s task would entail. In return for access to the bottomless pit of money and clout that they supply, he had to become famous. Yeah, that’s right. His part of the deal was to be famous. Of course he agreed. The idea was that he had to place himself within the public eye before his real return favor could be called in. Once he was a strong enough figure, at least recognizable by the average person, they’d contact him about part B. Once again, they do the secretive thing. They really seem to enjoy that. Regardless, this public venture of the Titan Project, whatever it would be, started to take shape. Register was Prometheus. He was to bring fire to the people. As RPG3 came to a close for us, I applied Matt to Old School Wrestling’s Most Extreme Team competition. If his rise to the top was going to work, we were gonna do it the way all the really good stars did it – with a team. I left the second member on the application blank. It was up to him to find a partner. Prometheus never did a damn thing without his brother. Now we needed an Epimetheus.
