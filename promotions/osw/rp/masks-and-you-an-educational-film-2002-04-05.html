Masks and You (an educational film)

5 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-5-2002 Blackness. Blackness transitions to grayness. Grayness transitions to the sudden sounds of an old-fashioned movie projector. The grayness takes on a grainy quality as a simple bouncy tune fills the audio. The words “DAMN presents” appear on the screen, followed by the words “A Doug Troy Film”. The words fade out to gray once again, followed by the words “Masks and You.” This picture fades out to black. After a few seconds of black, the picture fades up to a black and white house. The home is set up like a stereotypical 50’s nuclear family household. Inside this house, the stereotypical nuclear family father figure is putting candy into a pumpkin-shaped bucket while smoking his pipe. The stereotypical nuclear family housewife enters the scene as the narrator starts to speak. “Ah, Halloween! A time for spooks, scares and candy! Here we see mom and dad preparing for the neighborhood kids by filling a bucket with candy. Soon, kids will be knocking at the door for the candy, and mom and dad don’t want to disappoint.” The dad turns to the mom and mouths some words. The mom shrugs her shoulders. “Hey, speaking of kids, where is little Jimmy? It’s time for trick-or-treating, and little Jimmy needs to do it before the child molesters start making their rounds!” The mom makes a shocked face at this, and a picture of Ivan Stanislav with the words ‘Child Molester’ above it appear in a thought bubble over the mom’s head. Suddenly, Jimmy appears in the room with a Godzilla body suit on. “Oh wait, here he comes now! But wait! Something’s wrong!” The picture zooms into Jimmy’s uncovered face. “Oh no! It seems that Jimmy doesn’t have his mask!” The mom makes a shocked face again, while the dad makes a ‘five across the eyes’ motion at the cringing Jimmy. “Jimmy doesn’t know why he needs a mask! It’s time for Dad to explain some very important things about masks to little Jimmy!” The dad grabs the suit and shoves Jimmy onto the couch. Jimmy rolls around and tries to get away, but Dad descends upon him and puts him in a headlock. “Little Jimmy would rather go trick-or-treating, but there are certain things you must know about masks before you can go trick-or-treat!” The dad shakes his finger at the struggling Jimmy as the picture fades to a man holding a large stone mask. “The mask was first invented in the year 10,000 BC, when a caveman named ‘Gork’ first had the idea of covering his face to strike fear into the hearts of predators.” The picture fades to a picture of Gork being eaten alive by a sabertooth tiger. “Needless to say, there were some bugs that needed to be worked out. Masks continued to flourish during the ages. For instance, tribes in Africa used to use masks for tribal rituals. Masks were often attached to helmets during battles in ancient Rome. Historical experts believe that this was done so Russell Crowe would have a way to look bad ass in the film ‘Gladiator’.” A picture of Russell Crowe in a battle mask fades in, followed by the words ‘Badass’ with an arrow pointing to Crowe’s face. “In more recent times, masks have been turned into a tool for anonymity. Burglars oftentimes used masks to hide their identity during bank robberies, so as to avoid having their faces seen.” A picture of a man in a stocking mask fades in. “The stocking mask was the most common burglar tool until recent times, when a more creative type of crook came into the scene.” The picture of the man in the stocking mask shrinks to fill half of the screen while a picture of Patrick Swayze and his President Nixon mask from ‘Point Break’ fills the other half. “Besides being used by crooks and degenerates, another widely-known use for masks is in the field of Professional Wrestling.” The picture fades to a picture of Ultimo Dragon and his mask. “In wrestling, the mask is used for several different reasons. The first reason is to create an air of mystery about the wrestler. If nobody can see their face, then it automatically makes them more appealing and mysterious to average Joe fan.” The picture fades to a picture of Super Duper Masked Jobber King and his paper bag mask. “Another use for the mask is to hide the shame of a jobber. If nobody can see someone’s face, then it makes losing all of the time less degrading for the wrestler.” The picture fades out again, only do be replaced by two pictures: that of the four masked OSW invaders and the Phantom Phive. “The last use is for groups of wrestlers to look intimidating during invasions. In all cases of this use, the group always winds up failing miserably and retiring in shame, mostly from humiliation in their opening match.” The picture fades back to the father and son having their discussion. “’So you see’, dad says. ‘Masks have a very long and vibrant history, and that’s why it’s important that you wear masks on Halloween.’” Jimmy nods his head. “Ah, and it seems that Jimmy understands dad’s point! But what’s that?” The dad turns to the door and opens it, revealing a kid with no mask at all. The dad makes a ‘hold on’ motion, then closes the door partially and walks over to the candy. The dad picks up a carmeled apple from the bucket and shoves a razor blade into it before walking back to the door. With a big smile, the dad hands the razor apple over to the kid, who skips away in glee. After dad shuts the door, both dad and Jimmy burst into laughter. “Oh, those kidders. Anyway, it’s time for Jimmy to get his mask so he can trick or treat! And with the power of masks, he’s safe from both evil communist child molesters and masked wrestlers with tampered candy! Now that he, and you, have learned about the relationship between you and masks, you may now go to any Mangelsens or Nobbies in your area and pick out a mask in pride! Wear your mask with pride, knowing full well the elaborate and amazing history behind masks. God Bless America and God Bless Latex.” The feed fades back to the grainy gray screen as the words ‘A Doug Troy Film’ appear. After a couple seconds, the picture and the music fade to nothingness.
