Fool me once...

5 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/5/02 Alan Kriegman did not take the loss to Tom Lexian and Mike Extreme very well. After a good five week-long reign of terror, you sort of get used to the whole utter dominance thing. So as you can imagine, he and Trashcan Man’s swift smiting at the hands of the Extreme Legends came as quite the unpleasant surprise. His reaction to this unfortunate turn events was something that could be described as far more than a simple temper tantrum and slightly less than a total breakdown. He screamed till’ he was pink in the face; he spat and cursed; he pounded and kicked the mat; he may or may not have cried. Well, he DID cry. Like a little child, but that’s between you and me. What I’m trying to say here is…well… Remember in cartoons when the evil villan would throw his black hat to the ground and stamp it flat after being beaten by the hero, shouting “Curses! Foiled again!”? This wasn’t one of those moments. Frankly, it was pretty pathetic. A 37 year old man openly weeping. This all, of course, was his initial response to the happenings of the main event. He eventually regained his composure. Placing the shock and rage inside him aside, the evil supergenius looked past his immediate emotions and came to a grim realization. His plans had been brought to a crashing halt; something he hadn’t been prepared for in the least. Why hadn’t he seen this coming? The 99% probability of a swerve was right there in front of him all along! Of COURSE the newly crowned OSW President was going to pull something! How could he not have considered that possibility? Alan had gotten too full of himself. He actually began to think that he truly was “Untouchable”. Literally getting away with murder will do that to a man with evil intentions, I suppose. His overconfidence was a mistake. A fatal one, at that. It had opened the door for ‘Mr. President’ to deliver a heavy blow to the Think Tank. In other words, Alan Kriegman had gotten so cocky that he allowed himself to be outsmarted by a man in a George W. Bush mask. A costly mistake, indeed, but as all great minds do, Alan learned from it. It was not something he planned on letting happen again. The President’s little coup was a pure stroke of luck. He couldn’t have possibly known for sure that Alan would be unprepared for his power play. He took a chance, and it just so happened that it worked marvelously. Alan Kriegman had unwittingly stumbled right into a clumsily-laid trap; something he would have spotted a mile away had he not been wearing a blindfold called “overconfidence”. Luck, plain and simple. Too bad for the President that his luck had officially run out. Untouchable? Not at all. If Alan had one fatal flaw, it would be overconfidence. He learned that the hard way. But learn is exactly what he did from that mistake. With Alan once again seeing clearly and thinking like the evil supergenius he was, there would be no more luckily successful tricks played by the Think Tank’s newfound foe, Mr. President. That was the last swerve Kriegman would let himself fall victim to. To err is human, but to repeat one’s mistakes is idiocy. Fool me once, shame on you… Alan had been silent for the past hour as he sat with his Think Tank comrades in their white limo, riding away from the airport. None of them had offered consolation; nor did he deserve it. They knew as well as he did that Mr. President’s plan was successful only because they had grown too confident. It had been a quiet ride, until Alan broke the silence by whispering to himself. “Fool me twice, shame on me…” His friend and fellow genius Howard Porter, who had also been occupied by his thoughts, looked to Alan. “Pardon?” he asked. “Oh, nothing. Just going over what happened in my head,” Kriegman sighed. “Try not to beat yourself up too much. I should have seen it coming, too. So should have Herbert,” said Porter as he motioned toward PHD, who had fallen asleep in his seat. “It was a mistake, but one we won’t make again. Not that there weren’t…contributing factors to your defeat. Ivan, Sebastian and the others…” Alan nodded, and his eyes brightened as the image of Ivan Stanislav and Sebastian Toomes working together to hold back the Think Tank during the main event suddenly flashed into his mind. Stanislav and Switchblade… “Howard?” Howard, who had noticed Alan’s face light up, raised an eyebrow. “Yes?” An evil grin spread across the dark man’s face. “I’ve got a plan…”
