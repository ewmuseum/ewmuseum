Book Report

31 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-31-02 There were several inconsistencies running through Ivan Stanislav’s life. Fighting the Think Tank was one of them. The hidden identity of the new President of the OSW was another. El Drago remained a mystery as it was difficult to communicate with a man that doesn’t speak the same language as yourself. But the biggest inconsistency of all came from the most unheard of place: Russia. Stanislav had quietly accepted his suspension from Arkhangelsk. The charges were that his behavior was unbecoming of a soldier of The Russian Army and that his boasting against the Motherland was detrimental to the Federation. In a life where so many question marks suddenly appeared, it was time that Ivan Stanislav put a period on the end of his suspension and restart his life in Arkhangelsk. Thus, his travels led him back to Moscow. It had been a while since Ivan Stanislav had been in Moscow, as a matter of fact, weeks. The only other time he was that far away from his Motherland for such a long period of time was during wartime. But then again…was he at war with…Russia? Stanislav stood outside the office of Lieutenant Igor Karpov, the constant thorn in the Praporschik’s side. He waited for a few minutes before the secretary nodded to Ivan. “Praporschik Stanislav, the Lieutenant will see you now.” With a gruff nod Stanislav walked through the door into Karpov’s office. Karpov sat at his desk and leaned back in his large chair without a care in the world. The Praporschik stared at him and saluted. Karpov nodded, not even giving Stanislav the respect of a salute back, and let the Russian Bear remain standing. “How are you, Prapor..I mean..Ivan.” Karpov chuckled at his slip up. “I am fine, Lieutenant.” Ivan responded coldly. He glared down at Karpov for a moment. Karpov stared back up at Ivan and leaned forward, “So, what brings you to Moscow?” Ivan blinked just once, almost slapped by the ignorance of the man. Why was he here? He knew damn well why Ivan was in Moscow. “I am here to discuss my suspension, of course!” Karpov ran his tongue along his bottom lip and nodded, “Hmmm..suspension..yes.” Stanislav quickly became animated as he discussed his progress, “I have done much since the suspension! I have won the fans over to my cause and Ivan has also brought much revenue back to the Motherland! The people have hope now in the wake of controversy! Also, Ivan has downplayed communism!” “Hmm….” Karpov paused and looked at Ivan, “We have been reviewing your current status…but we need more time….” Ivan shook his head, “More time?! How much time do you need Igor?” Ivan looked down at Karpov’s desk and saw a copy of his recent article that had hit newsstands. It had quickly sold out everywhere as wrestling fans flocked to learn the truth about Ivan Stanislav as a person. This particular copy was in Russian, and it also had a large X over the front cover of it, in red. Ivan swallowed and made eye contact with Karpov, who absently pushed a few papers over the article to hide it. “Ivan,” Karpov began, “it is admirable what you are doing in America, it really is. But there are still certain factors that we here in Russia do not appreciate. For one, the Mexican, El Drago I believe his name is?” “Da, that is his name.” “Well, you seem to be helping one another,” Karpov paused and raised an eyebrow, “with a Mexican.” “It helps to promote our free thinking Lieutenant!” Ivan fired back. “One must have allies in wrestling federation.” “And this woman, Tempest? She has returned, has she not?” Karpov grinned, he had Stanislav. But Ivan remained steadfast. He balled his hands into fists, “She came and helped me, but that is all! Leave her alone Karpov! This mission was made for wrestling! Personal matters are not of consequence!” Karpov stood up and put his hands on his desk, “Oh, I think they are, Ivan. You are becoming too…too..emotional. Too charged in what you are doing. It worries us! I am afraid that you’re just going to have to sit out your suspension longer.” Karpov grinned. Ivan breathed in heavily and out his nose, “Very well…..very well…..” He turned and walked to the door. He paused and reached into his right pocket and produced the red feather. He replaced it, shook his head, and walked out of the room. Stanislav would remain within the Kremlin, but rather sink into the ground towards an office deep within the recesses of the building: Alexei Ruslan’s office. Ruslan sat in his chair and worked on a document when his door flew open and Stanislav barged in. “I cannot believe this!! I cannot believe this!!” screamed The Russian Bear. Ruslan hopped out of his seat, startled by the sudden influx of emotion into his quiet office, “Ivan, what is the matter?” he asked. Ivan growled and paced across the room and back, visibly agitated. Ivan paused and looked at Ruslan with his hand out, “What are they doing up there?! Why are they doing this to me!! WHY??” “Calm down Ivan…” Ruslan walked closer to Ivan, but he snapped. “NYET! I will not calm down!” Ivan jerked his arm away from Ruslan, scaring him, “I have done this for too long! Too long have I done what those stupid officers say and fall in line!” Ivan stomped his massive boot on the floor, “This is WRONG, Alexei. This is wrong and you know it! They are not just fooling with mission, but my life!” Ivan paused. “MINE!” Ivan scoffed and pointed at himself. Alexei swallowed, “Ivan…I…I don’t know…..something is not adding up, I agree.” Ivan roared and knocked a bottle of vodka off of a nearby stand, shattering it against the wall. “GGRRAAAAHHHH!!” Alexei yelped at the violence. “C…calm down Ivan….calm down…..people can hear….calm yourself..please.” He walked over to Ivan, a look of worry on his face. Ivan’s breathing slowed. He looked at Alexei, “I have given them my life, Alexei. I have given them my body….my mind….my blood. I have shed my blood…my humanity….my livelihood…everything for them. And I am nothing without them.” “You are just going through trying time Ivan. Do not worry about it. I will see what is going on. You should relax, eh? Without having work in Arkhangelsk, you should take advantage of free time.” Ruslan smiled at Ivan. Ivan remained silent, however. It crushed him, the reality of the situation. Too many things were happening too quickly for him. The OSW, his sudden fan base, Russia’s lack of support, El Drago, and most importantly, Tempest had all been so sudden that he was overloaded, perhaps. He leaned against the wall and shook his head, “Alexei…why does this have to happen like this?” Alexei shrugged, “I do not know, Ivan.” “If I do not have Russia…..” Ivan swallowed as he absently put his hand in his right pocket, “what will I have? Who will I have?” Alexei watched as Ivan dipped his hand into his right pocket, “You’ll have those, Ivan, that care for you.” He smiled at his larger comrade. “After giving them so much, they are treating me like I am a stranger, Alexei. How could they do this?” “Praporschik,” Alexei said grimly, “this happened to Trotsky too.” Ivan shot a stare up at Alexei. Leon Trotsky, Lenin’s best friend and a true communist, had been killed by Stalin whenever the dictator feared Trotsky would take away his power. “I am no Trotsky, Alexei, and if it comes to that…..I will get by….I know that Ivan will.”
