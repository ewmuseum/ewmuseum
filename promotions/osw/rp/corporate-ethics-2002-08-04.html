Corporate Ethics

4 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 4th, 2002 Routine is not easy to change, especially when your life revolves around a set of events that dominates your day. My routine for the past two months had been quite simple: Dominate PCW. It was simple enough to accomplish. Get in the gym, work out for a few hours, watch a few hours of videotape, and get a good meal and plenty of rest afterwards. I was a professional, you see, not your average, run of the mill brain dead grappler. Wrestling is an art form, and I am Van Gogh in a world full of impressionists. Not even a Continental title victory could satiate my hunger to be at the top where men like Big Allan Steele and Frisco had taken up space as the top wrestlers in the promotion. It pained me to see such lumbering hulks of mass carrying the banner of such a proud organization, but I vowed to bring a new light to that dark and sinister era. But apparently, if you piss off the wrong people, you find yourself staring up at the ring lights much more often than you would like. Duke Williams, are you listening? All of these thoughts raced through my mind as I made my way through the New Wave Entertainment office, being fashionably late to an eleven o’clock meeting with one Mr. Raymond Burris, a talent executive who had befriended me in my last days at PCW. Well, strike that. He wasn’t my friend – actually, he was a leech, one that I continually tried to brush off my arm. Straight ahead was a front desk where a very attractive young woman sat, typing furiously at her computer. She was either preparing a very important document that needed someone’s attention within the next fifteen minutes, or she was finishing up a rousing game of Tetris. Either way, I would be interrupting her. “Excuse me,” I said, a charismatic smile crossing my face. “My name is Tom Lexian. I’m here to see Raymond Burris.” She turned to face me, closing whatever document she had been working on. Dammit, it wasn’t Tetris after all. You lose four brownie points for not wasting company time, missy. “We have been expecting you, Mr. Lexian. You’re late.” “I know,” I replied, trying to hide the sly grin that was demanding to get out. “Mr. Burris hates not being on time. He says it puts his whole day off schedule.” “I’m sure he’s a very busy man.” “Just go right ahead and head over to his office,” She said, pointing over her left shoulder. “He’s just finishing up a few business calls.” “Thank you.” I made my way to his office, whose door was open just barely. The nameplate on the door read: Raymond Burris. Chief Executive Officer of Talent Relations. I pulled off the name plate and stuck it in my back pocket. Shiny pieces of metal weren’t meant to be put on old, creaky wooden doors. I pushed the door open, peeking in to see Burris turned around in his chair chatting on the phone. His gel ridden, slicked back graying hair wasn’t making him look any younger. Neither was his pinstripe suit, for that matter. “Listen sugar lips, I want to see you tonight too,” He continued. “But the ol’ wife is having a few of her party friends over and I have to be there. But I can come over later this week, maybe have a few drinks at your place…” “Ahem,” I interrupted, crossing my arms as he turned to see me at his desk. He panicked and rushed to hang up the phone. “Sweetheart, I’ll call you back. I have an important client demanding my attention. I’ll talk to you later.” He made a few kissing noises which almost made me gag and he hung up the phone. That oh-so not genuine smile made it’s way onto his face as he greeted me. “Lexian, looks like you finally made it.” “I love the part where you made the fake smooching noises,” I replied, taking a seat. “Do you do that with your real wife too? Pretty romantic.” His demeanor told me that he was not pleased I had overheard his conversation. “Listen Lexian, we all have our weaknesses. Mine just happens to be women. And if you saw this girl, you’d be doing the exact same thing.” “Is this corporate ethics I hear?” I said, a note of sarcasm in my voice. “Do what you can as long as you don’t get caught?” “That’s the name of the game, kid. Look at all of the scandals that have been going down the past few months. Enron, WorldCom, the list goes on and on. Men in power seek the most risks, and the risks seek them out.” “So what are you seeking?” He smiled, showing his hands to me. “I’m about as clean as a business man that you’ll ever meet. I want to help you find your dreams, and I want to make you a star.” I chuckled. “You want to make me a star? Is that what this has all been about? Your hyperstatic wet dream to put your name on the next big thing? You can count me out.” “Now just hold on there for a second,” He replied, becoming serious all of a sudden. “That’s completely false. Lexian, I took you out of the hellhole that was PCW - the burning inferno that threatened to engulf your career. Even you have to see now that you could never win. There were too many forces trying to hold you down.” “So I’m in OSW now,” I said. “I’m happy, and I’m back together with Mike Extreme. You’re right, things couldn’t be better at the moment.” Burris unleashed a devilish grin. “Legend Inc. and Mike Extreme, the Extreme Legends. You can cut the electricity with a knife when you say that name out loud. There’s a certain ring to it, you know? Sounds like…” He paused to make it sound more dramatic. “…like dollar signs.” “I don’t care about money.” “Are you really that dense, Lexian? I’m not asking you to burn every bridge and cheat the poor out of their savings for the sake of lining your own wallet. I’m telling you that you could be the next big star of the entertainment business, and if you play your cards right, we could both make a lot of money.” “I’m a wrestler, Burris. Not an actor.” “I’m not asking you to stop wrestling,” He said, standing up from his desk. He stared out his window overlooking the San Francisco skyline. “But I’m asking you to start thinking past the ring for a moment. Do you really think you can wrestle forever? What happens when you can’t wrestle anymore? What if you suffer some freak injury and you have to hang up the boots for good?” “That won’t happen,” I answered arrogantly. “And if it ever comes to that, I’ll take care of it.” “Like your old friend Sean O’Reilly took care of it?” “Fuck you,” I shot back angrily. “Don’t you ever mention him to me again. Do you understand me?” He had me on the ropes now. Sean O’Reilly, once my trusted confidant, who betrayed me during the RPG3 contest and nearly cost me my name and reputation, lost his own career to an injury. I could have never have guessed how he would turn out, only three years after he finally retired. “Think of your future. Think of that family you’ll have. You have the look, the charisma, and the marketability to be as big a star as you have been in the wrestling ring. All I am asking you is to take a chance.” I looked over at him, his eyes gleaming with dollar signs in them. “All I am concerned about right now is winning the OSW Tag Team tournament. That is my number one priority.” He sighed, running his hand through his hair, which unsurprisingly, didn’t budge an inch. “I didn’t want it to be this way, Lexian. I really thought you would see it my way. But never forget who organized your rebellion away from PCW and into OSW. I was there to put it all together.” “What is this? Blackmail?” “No, not blackmail. But for all of the good men like myself that look out for guys like you, there are a hundred more that would like nothing more than to try to bury you and your career. Under the New Wave Entertainment banner, you would be protected.” He slipped a contract onto the desk and placed a pen next to it. I stared at it as if the devil had just asked me to sign away my soul for a lollipop. “You expect me to sign this?” “I don’t expect anything. I wouldn’t be a good business man if I did. Have it back to me by the end of the week, or the deal is void. Good day, Tom.” He walked out of the room, leaving me to my thoughts. I stared at the piece of paper that had my name on it, tying my reputation with that of New Wave. I had never wanted the mainstream appeal that some had dreamed for, but now that it was sitting right in front of my face, I wasn’t sure if I could pass it up. The Dark Age and Duke Williams may have seemed like a handful, but corporate ethics could damn well drive a man insane. My sanity was all I held sacred nowadays.
