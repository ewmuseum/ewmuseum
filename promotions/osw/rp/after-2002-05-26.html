After

26 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Sunday, May 26th, 2002 The oak-furnished door loomes back at him. 'Suite seven-forty-three, this is it.' The bell-boy recites the number of the room back to himself, to make sure he got it right. He stands there clad in the fancy hotel's standard uniform: a longsleeved, red, cross-button suitjacket of sorts, black pants, and polished black loafers. He hates it. In his hands are the items requested in a room service order: One glass of 'Jose Cuervo Tequila,' pre-poured, with the bottle capped and set in a bucket of ice. Between balancing the brimming cup of alcohol in one hand and the bottle-in-ice in the other, things are getting pretty damn heavy; he is tired of waiting. 'Room Service.' He calls out again, this was the third time. Finally, he hears footsteps on the other side of the door. When the door opens, Griff Truxell stands in the threshold. 'I have your or...' The bell-boy is cut off as Griff reaches out and snatches the glass from his hand. Setting it down on the nearby counter, Griff quickly takes the bucket of ice as well. The door then slams shut, leaving the bell-boy standing there, dumbfounded... ...remembering why he hates his job. Took god-damned long enough. Inside the suite Griff Truxell puts the bucket of ice on a nice, glass coffee table in the middle of the living room. The suite is almost as big as an entire apartment, furnished with everything. Yet, Griff moves about more agitated than usual, which is strange, since he won his match on Slam!. What can't be seen is what is bothering him. Underneath his shirt, his ribs and abdomen are layered with gauze and tape, thanks to a Bearhug from Kimbusa. Fat piece of shit, surprised he didn't have a heart-attack in the middle of the ring and drop dead on the canvas. Peon. I should have broke his leg. Left him there to drag his five-hundred pound carcas to the hospital. Beating him just wasn't enough. People like that need to learn. People that take a horrendous flaw that they have and use it to make up for their general lack of pure skill and ability. People that throw their fucking obesity (or their ability to jump around like a monkey, or their ability to run their damn mouth) around the ring in a quote, unquote 'attempt' to not make a fool of themselves. It downright disgraces Wrestling Itself. He swirls the amber liquid in his glass a bit, looking down into it. He takes a sip. Griff's lip curls quickly, wincing subtley in pain, his left jaw visibly tightneing up... ...[In slow motion Griff sees Clay Vendetta's fist coming at him a inch away from his face. It's too close, he can't dodge it. He bites down hard, tensing for the collision. It knocks his head to the right, saliva flying out of his mouth. Flashbulbs flicker and blend in with the cheer of the crowd as everything blurs together.]... He walks towards the glass sliding door that leads out onto the balcony. He drinks again, pulling the door to the right. Griff looks out over the Canadian skyline. This is where he is used to being; on top of the world. The boy must have a death wish, standing toe to toe with Greatness. Most people couldn't have handled that alone, piss their pants or run away, or both. Don't think I'm giving the little shit credit though. He's not brave, he's ignorant; and no matter what anybody says, ignorance is not bliss. Ignorance can get you hurt, it will get you hurt, evenutally. Standing up and trying to face down Greatness is a stubborn thing to do. It's been done before and has never, ever worked. But people keep trying, while others keep egging them on. Cheering them, wanting them to do the impossible. Because believeing in the impossible is a lot easier than facing the Truth. That's why I hate Clay Vendetta. I don't know him and I don't want to know him. The only thing I do know about him is that he's just like those pissants in the stands: believing in the impossible. He's fighting for a unachievable cause, a lost hope, and he's involving me in his petty little Crusade. I don't have time for Clay Vendetta. I'm better than Clay Vendetta.
