Belated Birthday

9 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/9/02 Dear Tempest, I apologize for not being able to speak to you in person or through the telephone, but I had to return to the Motherland quickly after Saturday Slam. However, there was something that I would have liked to talk to you about, preferably with you beside me. Unfortunately, due to my work I am unable to return to the United States for a period of a few days, thus I am inquiring if perhaps you would be able to accompany me in the Motherland. I did not do you justice the last time you were in Motherland, being unable to show you the beauty of my country, so perhaps I will be able to take this chance to show you around Moscow. I understand that this is at short notice and you may be unable to attend, but if you are able I have enclosed a plane ticket that will land straight within Moscow, via a Russian military plane. The directions to the hangar of the plane are also enclosed, as well as all other information you may need. I look forward to seeing you, Praporschik Ivan Stanislav P.S. You may wish to dress warm. The engines of the old plane growled away the time as Tempest watched clouds and ocean flit by beneath her. She smiled and looked around the empty interior, wishing that the plane could fly just a little faster. She'd already hidden in one of the overhead compartments and scared the co-pilot. She wasn't sure what he had said, but it was some very excited Russian. She was glad to feel the plane's eventual descent and peered out the widow, hoping to catch sight of the scenes she missed on her first jaunt. As the plane cleared the cloud cover she gaped at the teeming cityscape below. In a series of slow circles, the plane descended and eventually touched down roughly. She bounced in her seat and resisted the urge to yelp. It wasn't flying that was scary, just the landing, and she knew this better than most. She pulled her black velvet coat, with it's fluffy purple color around her and nodded to the pilot, who seemed to be rather intent on making a good impression as he led her to the gangway. Too bad she had no idea what he was saying. The pilot pulled a lever and pushed a button as the door slowly slid away, revealing that a set of five steps were already set out on the landing pad. Two young Russian soldiers stood on either side of the steps and saluted Tempest while Ivan Stanislav stood a few feet from the end of the stairs in a heavy black jacket with red fur around the neck and wrists, a fuzzy brown hat, black pants, and boots. He suddenly smiled wide as he saw Tempest. Flakes of snow flew through the air, his face red from the cold. 'Tempest! I am so glad that you could make it!!' Stanislav rushed forward to help her down the steps. She smiled wide and glanced at the two saluting soldiers. 'Ok, who the heck did you tell them I am to get that kind of reception?' she asked and giggled as she held out her arms toward him. Ivan grabbed her hands in one of his massive paws and wrapped his other massive arm around Tempest and hoisted her off the top of the steps. He laughed loudly and spun her around, 'DYAAHAA!! I told them a friend of The Motherland!!' He put her down and smiled warmly at her, 'I am very happy that you are here, Tempest.' She laughed and said, 'Well me too! I'm looking forward to seeing Moscow (sp?) with it's most notable citizen. How are you feeling?' She looked up at him quizzically and squeezed his arm. He smiled warmly, 'I am fine, it was only a little bump on the head,' he brushed a stray hair out of her face, 'besides, I had someone to take care of me, eh?' Behind Ivan, the soldiers grabbed the stairs and wheeled them away while the door shut from inside the plane. Ivan pointed to a nearby jeep, 'Come, there is much that I have to show you.' He smiled and offered her his arm. She wrapped her arm around his and scooted closer as they walked from the airfield and into the heart of his homeland. ~~~ The scene fades back up within Red Square, the sprawling square of black cobblestones that serves as the center of most Russian cities. In the distance, the Kremlin can be seen looming high, it's spires almost touching the heavens. People bustle everywhere, various small shops are open and are selling wares to the passers by. A few Russians on bikes ride by. Stanislav and Tempest stand beside the jeep while a few Russians walk by, not even looking at them. 'Behold Tempest, Red Square in all it's beauty! Many times have I marched upon these stones in front of my leaders! During Afghanistan War, when Ivan was only 18, he walked with rifle over his shoulder, proud to defend his Motherland!!' Ivan spread his arms out proudly, grinning ear to ear. Tempest looked around and said, 'At 18? Wow, that's young. Is that what they do here though? March armies?' She looked around the vast space and it's red brick walls. She shrugged and said, 'Seems like a great place for a party... but that's just me.' She chuckled and looked up at him. Ivan smiled at her and nodded, 'Yes, all Russian males must enter military at age of 18 for four years, it is like school here. At 18, I marched to do my Motherlands wishes.' Ivan smiled to himself, remembering that day, 'Red Square is used for political demonstrations as well as large marches used to rally people.' Ivan looked over at Tempest, 'You know, 'Red' did not come because of Communism. It is called 'Red Square' because the Russian word for 'red' is 'Krasny' which means 'beautiful' as well as 'red.'' He points to the Kremlin, 'That is The Kremlin, the capitol of Russia. I spend much time in there working if I am not in Arkhangelsk at my home office. Alexei's office is within the Kremlin, deep beneath it.' Tempest chuckled and said, 'Beneath it? Geez, poor Alexei, sounds like he's a mole... and I don't mean like a spy.' She looked around and smiled as she said, 'You're right though, it is a beautiful place. So Red comes form the Square? Does that mean that Communism began here?' Ivan smiled, happy that she has shown some sort of interest, 'Yes, Red comes from describing the Square. Communism began over there...' Ivan pointed to a nearby red and black brick square building. Two soldiers stood at attention with rifles at the door and turned some people away as they came near them. Above the large doors are Russian words, but we are unable to understand what they say. 'That is the Lenin Mausoleum, where Lenin, the founder of Communism, lies.' Ivan smiled. 'I will show you him, da?' Tempest nodded and took his hand as they walked. 'But... it' didn't begin with his death did it?' Ivan grinned, 'Oh no! Lenin led very rough life! He lived most of his life in Germany, as matter of fact. He was in jail many times, risked his life on several occasions, but it was he that started Great October Revolution. Without him, none of it would have existed within Russia. Marx and Engels actually thought up idea, but Lenin put it into action.' Ivan walked a few steps and looked to his left and saw a long line of people walking up to a shop, he sighed at the sight, deflated. Tempest followed his gaze and said, 'What are they waiting in line for?' She smiled at a little girl fidgeting as those around her stood still. Ivan lowered his voice and looked down at the ground, 'They are waiting for food....and heating oil.' Her smile faded a bit and she said, 'So that's why you send your money back here.' She thought a moment and said, 'You told me Lenin led a rough life early on, right?' Ivan nodded solemnly, 'Yes, that is why I send my money back....and yes, Lenin led a rough life not only early on, but throughout his life....' Ivan looked at the people as they continued to walk, 'I wish there was something else that I could do, but there is not...' She patted his arm and said, 'You also lead a rough life early on, right? I mean being in a war at 18 is no fun.' 'Da,' replied Ivan, 'my family life is somewhat of...mystery...' Ivan swallowed hard, what small amount of color on his face draining away, 'Yes, at 18 I learned what death really was, and I learned what true pain was. It was not easy.' Tempest nodded solemnly and said, 'Well, I didn't have a very good early life either. But ya see, I have a great life now, and I think you do too.' She nodded toward the line of people and said, 'And someday they will too. It's hard to change, but I bet they're strong enough. After all, you've made it his far, right?' She smiled up at him encouragingly. Ivan looked over at her and put his arm around her shoulder, 'Yes, you are correct. Russian people are made of something unlike any other. I just do not like to see the children suffering...many of them do not survive....' Ivan cleared his throat as they neared the Mausoleum, 'Yes, but we are not here to be sad, are we Tempest? And besides, Ivan does not wish to make you sad! We are here!!' Ivan nodded to the two soldiers who saluted him and opened the huge double doors to the building. Ivan led Tempest inside the building, which was quite cold inside. Large black slabs of what appeared to be onyx covered the floor as well as huge chandaliers that hung from the ceiling. Along the marble walls hang various pictures and plaques regarding Lenin and his life and at the far end of the Mausoleum was a small alcove that was roped off, with two soldiers standing in front of it. Tempest shivered and pulled her coat tighter around her, feeling suddenly much smaller than before. Ivan walked ahead of her and turned to her. His voice echoed through the quiet, sacred room. 'Tempest, this is the Lenin Mausoleum! Vladimir Ulyanov, also known as Vladimir Lenin, the father of Communism and the father of Soviet Union, is buried here!!' Tempest blinked in the low light and said, 'Wow... not exactly a romantic get-away, but still... interesting.' She looked around at the plaques and documents on the walls. 'It's interesting that he's still so revered even though the country isn't communist anymore.' Ivan nodded and walked over to her. He took her hand and led her closer to the small alcove, 'Yes, there are those that wish to have this building taken down, even though it was Lenin that saved Russia from it's demise.' He paused as they neared the door to the alcove. Ivan nodded to the two soldiers, who promptly got out of the way and saluted him. Ivan looked over at her, 'Would you like to see him, Tempest?' She looked at him startled and said, 'A guy who's been dead for 80 years? I don't think so...' Ivan swallowed, almost as if he was delivered a swift blow to the face, 'He uh... he is well preserved...' Ivan looks at the floor. Tempest watched his face and thought a moment before she raised her index finger and said, 'Oh yeah wait a second, he's one of the few modern mummies around. I remember seeing something about that.' Ivan smiled widely, 'Da! That is true!! Come, I will show you!!' Ivan took her arm and led her into the room. It was quite dark in the room, with a small lighted walkway leading up to a glass wall. On the other side of the wall, lying in his casket, was the leader of the Bolshevik Revolution, Vladmir Lenin, dead for 80 years now. Ivan gasped as they walked up closer to him. Lenin lay as he had for 80 years, his goatee trimmed to perfection, a solemn look of eternal rest on his face. A small light lit the area, slightly reflecting off of his bald head. 'Do you see him Tempest? It is the savior of my homeland....' Tempest nodded with a sympathetic smile as she gazed at the departed leader. She cleared he throat and said, 'It's kinda strange that he looks so peaceful when his life was anything but.' Ivan talked softly, 'Da, da....Tempest, you may not understand gravity of situation...you are looking at the man who has decided my life...I have devoted my life to the beliefs of this man....I would die for this man....he determined who I would be without even knowing it....without him, I am nothing.' Ivan swallowed, not realizing that perhaps while it sounds admirable, it is almost pathetic in a way. But Tempest smiled and squeezed his hand as she said, 'Maybe... I don't understand all the important things about him or you. But I do know that if he had a hand in determining who you are, then he did a pretty good job.' Ivan smiled at Tempest, extremely glad that she had seen Lenin. He brought his head down to Tempest and whispered softly, 'Come Tempest, I want to show you why I really brought you here.' He squeezed her hand and led her out of the Tomb of Lenin... ~~~ The scene opens up within the Alexandrovsky Gardens that border The Kremlin. Home of beautiful gardens encased in glass greenhouses, as well as plants that can survive the harsh climate of the Russian area, the Alexandrovsky Gardens are one of the most beautiful places within Moscow. Right beside the Gardens, the large wall that serves as the border of the interior of the Kremlin looms in the background, and the Kremlin's spires tower in the background behind the wall. Our focus is on a white cobblestone bridge with a pearl white stone banister on either side. Beneath the bridge runs dark, black, yet somehow pristine water flows beneath the bridge. In the background some trees are trimmed to perfection. To the left side of the bridge it ends in a circular cobblestone area. A large fountain flows despite the colder Russian weather, shooting water into a large basin. Around the perimeter of the basin are benches where one can sit. It is an altogether breathtaking sight. Ivan Stanislav and Tempest stand on the bridge. Stanislav holds Tempests hand and waves around him, 'Tempest, these are the Alexandrovsky Gardens! One of most beautiful areas within Russia!' Tempest looked around grinning and said, 'Oh you're right, It's gorgeous!' She hopped up onto a bench and looked around. 'And here in the shadow of the Kremlin... what a weird irony.' Ivan cleared his throat and walked over to Tempest. He suddenly stood very straight and at attention and brought his hands behind his back, 'Tempest, if I may, I have a few things to say to you.' Ivan swallowed and stared at her. She spun around still standing on the bench an chuckled as she said, 'Ok, can I stay up here? I forgot to bring a step ladder to look you in the eye with.' Ivan nodded slowly and took a step back. He breathed deeply, as if calculating something. He looked down at the ground and began, 'Since I have come to OSW, I have been very busy man. I have not had much time to devote to personal matters, which in my case is my work, and thus is not much problem.' He looked up at Tempest, 'However, throughout all of this I have neglected to pay attention to an important matter regarding someone else....that is quite dear to me...' Ivan paused, his eyes darted around as he looked at the ground again as if trying to figure out what to do next. Tempest smiled encouragingly and said, 'Aww, whatcha mean?' Ivan cleared his throat and his hands fidgeted, 'In Russia...we have been taught not to show emotion, for if you show emotion your enemies will know your motives. Therefore, we tend not to show what we feel on outside, and keep it on inside instead, and I am no exception.' He paused and breathed out heavily, he looked around again and then up at her, his eyebrows raised, 'During beginning of this month, I had forgotten about...about your birthday. It is not customary to celebrate much regarding one's birthday, and I apologize for being so ignorant, but I have something for you....' Ivan looked down and began to unbutton his jacket. His hands shook as he did, however it was not terribly cold..and Ivan did not seem fazed by the cold before. Tempest rocked on her heels and said, 'Well, you did have a concussion. So that's reasonable grounds top forget a woman's birthday.' She laughed and added, 'But next time you'll have to find a missile attack as an excuse.' Ivan nodded, 'Nyet, it was not a valid excuse to forget your birthday.' He finished unbuttoning his jacket and reached into the inside pocket. He pulled from the pocket a 3 foot by 1 foot, thin box, wrapped in red wrapping paper. He blinked as he looked at it and handed it to Tempest. 'Please, open it.' He cleared his throat as he waited. Tempest's face lit up and she said, 'Oh thank you!' as she ripped into the paper gleefully. 'But really, don't think I mind something like forgetting when you're hurt. I'm just glad you're ok.' She took a moment to stop tearing paper and leaned forward to kiss him on the cheek. Ivan smiled as she kissed his cheek. As she tore off the paper she revealed a wooden case. The corners of the case had gold encased around them, and a small golden latch, forming what appeared to be the mechanism used to open it and a keyhole, was sealed on the front. A glass display served as the top of the case. Inside of it was a deep red fuzzy fabric used to protect the contents: On the left sat an old, tattered brown book with Russian letters on the front, on the right, a brand new, black book of some kind rested to the right of it. The interior of the case recessed slightly, allowing the books to rest deeper within the display case. Ivan looked at the case and then up at Tempest and swallowed. He handed her a small key and pointed to the old book, 'Please Tempest...open it.' Tempest sat down and looked at the contents with a curious smile. She took the key and opened the look on the small case, carefully lifting the lid. she reached out and ran her fingers over the worn leather cover of the older book and whispered, 'Wow... It's beautiful. I wish I could read this. What is it?' Ivan sat down beside her and let her lift the book out of the case. He let her open the inside cover. Inside the cover were what appeared to be three, three word sentences listed in the upper left of the cover. Each sentence ended with the same word, however. On the first page were more words, however all of them were written in Russian. Ivan pointed to the book and spoke very softly, 'That, Tempest, is an original copy of 'The Communist Manifesto' as well as the other works of Vladimir Lenin.' He paused and pointed to the list of three sentences, 'This book has been in the Stanislav family for over eighty years and has served as a guiding light to my family.' He pointed to the first sentence, 'That is my grandfathers name, Igor Nikitovich Stanislav, he was a Bolshevik and fought in the great Civil War of the 20's. However, he was killed in battle and my father who fought with him, for my grandfather had him at very young age, was given this book. My grandfather told him that this book would tell him of the collective, and that he had been given to him by Lenin himself.....' Tempest stared wide eyed at the weathered pages and held it carefully. 'Ivan... I... I can't accept something like this, it's too valuable, too personal, too...' She sighed, but smiled and said, 'I'd be afraid I'd drop it.' 'Shhh....Tempest, please, allow me to finish.' Ivan answered softly and pointed to the second name, 'The second name, Tempest,' he paused and swallowed, 'is the name of my father, Sergei Igorovich Stanislav. My father was a soldier, just like my grandfather, and a devoted communist. He disappeared for long periods of time, and when he did he left the book with my mother. He told her that if for some reason he would never return home to give me the book.' Ivan paused, 'He left one day and never returned, I was four years of age. When my mother found out she gave me the book with a note that told me to learn the literature, for it would teach me all I needed in life, as well as how while he had abandoned me, I would never be alone again.' Ivan paused and stood up. He walked away from Tempest and continued to speak, unable to look at her. He stared at the sky, 'The third name, Tempest, is my own, Ivan Sergeiovich Stanislav.' He paused and turned towards her, 'I have been charged with keeping this book moving throughout my family and to continue to teach the way of the collective...however, I have failed. I have had no one to teach, for all that I know of communism. I have no children, nor do I believe I ever will, and therefore I have killed my family line. I will be the last Stanislav to be on Earth, and someday, when I am dead and buried, I will be nothing more than name in book.' Ivan swallowed, very moved by what he is saying. He took a step closer to Tempest. 'I have never had anyone to give it to, until now. Your twenty sixth birthday, Tempest. You are so young and have so much opportunity. And yet, I feel that there are times when you feel alone, Tempest, but you do not let on to it.' He smiled warmly and blinked. 'Please accept this book and if you so choose, you may sign the inside cover with your name, for it is yours now. I have no need for it now for I have achieved what it set me out to acquire, and now it is time for it to teach someone else. The other book is written in Chinese, so that you can understand the literature.' Ivan breathed in heavily, stood broadly, and at complete attention, 'Tempest, on this day I make a pledge that with that book in your possession, Ivan Sergeiovich Stanislav will NEVER abandon you. You will NEVER be alone, ever again in life. If at any time you need someone, Ivan Stanislav will be there. It is not a simple thing for a Russian to do this, particularly to an outsider, but you are different.' He swallowed and couldn't help but furrow his brow and smile, 'Happy Birthday, Little Storm.' Tempest stared up at him for a long moment and then gulped as she looked back down at the book. She said softly, 'I don't even have a name to put in there you know... So I hope you understand that what you said...' She took a deep breath and looked up at him smiling in spite of her eyes being a bit wet. 'I just hope you understand that it really means a lot to me.' She stood and laid the box carefully on the bench and hugged Ivan tightly. Ivan wrapped his arms around her and pulled her tight towards him, 'It is all right, there is nothing wrong with not knowing your true name,' He held her for a long time and kissed her on the top of her head and spoke in a whisper as the water trickled around him, 'Tempest is a far more beautiful name to grace cover than anything anyone else could come up with.' Tempest sighed and smiled up at him. 'If you're not tone let your emotions show, you sure are making up for lost time,' she said and chuckled. 'But I'm glad I'm here to see it. In spite of all the outward differences... make me happy.' She reached up and then laughed at herself and said, 'You do realize however that I can't get up there to save my life?' Ivan blushed heavily and smiled, 'Yes...well, sometimes I suppose Ivan gets carried away. Here, let me help you.' He reached down and lifted Tempest by the waist until she was eye level with him. She wrapped her arms around his neck while he continued to hold her. He brought her closer and kissed her passionately on the lips, sighing heavily as he did. In the background the sound of water splashing softly continued and the soft, Russian snow flurried around them as they kissed in silence.
