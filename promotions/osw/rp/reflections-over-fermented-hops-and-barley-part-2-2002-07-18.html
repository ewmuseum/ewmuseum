Reflections over fermented hops and barley Part 2

18 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-18-2002 [b]Taped 7-16-2002[/b] “I noticed that you went to go see Toomes last week. I never thought that you cared for the guy.” Here we are, back in the same jazz bar from the first segment. Remember that old description? Good, because I’m not going to repeat it. Nate Dumas and Doug Troy are sitting at the bar counter of a lightly populated jazz bar. Doug shrugged his shoulders. “Not really. He’s kind of an asshole, and this whole thing he has going with Reagen pisses me off. But still… he got a really raw deal from that Armour motherfucker.” Doug’s words are slurred, evidence that he’s already well on his way to Drunksville. Nate raises his left eyebrow at Doug. “You traveled out of state because you think he got a raw deal?” “No, not just that. When I saw his getting screwed over and played with by Think Tank, I saw… well, myself. They screwed me over a few times in the past, if your remember correctly, especially that assbag Porter.” “Hrm?” “That’s the only reason I won the OSW World Title. That’s the only reason I even got a chance at it. Not because I earned it, not because I deserved, but because Porter wanted to make some kind of political powerplay. That really just pisses me off, Nate. I was a patsy for their bullshit political power struggle. A puppet, man…” Doug took a rather large drink from his current mug of beer, which sat closer to him than the 4 others that had met similar fates not long ago. By contrast, Nate only has two empty mugs in front of him. “I really don’t think I like this business anymore…” “You’ll snap outta this, Doug. You’re just in a bad place right now, but you’ll get your shit together. Maybe you should try to get DAMN back together or something…” Nate paused as he finished off his mug. The bartender takes it and slides a filled one in its place. “DAMN’s dead. It’s been dead since The Phantom Booker killed it in 2000 when he fired all of us. Every time you saw it past then was a facsimile, a sham, a pseudo-DAMN. Spinks can crow all he want about killing DAMN, but I know that the real killer was The Phantom Booker.” “Really? I was under the impression that you liked The Phantom Booker.” Nate said, holding the mug inches from his mouth. Doug took a swig of his beer. “Like? Hell no. The man’s a scumbag, exploiting wrestlers for his own purposes. And then, when he was forced into a bad position, he sold PCW out to Tyler Nelson in a heartbeat.” “That’s not very fair, Doug. PCW must’ve meant everything in the world to him.” “Fine then, TWO heartbeats. But he still did it. I don’t like him at all.” Doug paused and took another drink. Empty. He waved the bartender over again for a refill. “That being said,” Doug continued. “I respect the man. He wasn’t afraid to talk with wrestlers after the show. He gave feedback, real honest feedback. He was at all the shows and tried as hard as possible to stay off camera. He didn’t wrestle, he didn’t run in, he didn’t take part in screwjobs… I respect him immensely as a booker. I would never call him my friend, but I loved calling him “boss”. Nate gives Doug a quick smile as Doug takes another drink from his replenished mug. Doug set the mug down and continued. “I wish we had a boss like that in OSW. Someone who stayed off camera and just did their job instead of trying to hurt the careers of actual wrestlers. Someone who put over the talent instead of themselves night after night.” “Jane’s not that bad about that…” “Yeah, but she’s still a cold-hearted stupid bitch with no business sense. She sold out her own damn husband just to get herself ahead in the business. How cold is that?” “Not as cold as you’d think. I’ve seen colder. And you, on the other hand, are in serious hot water with her. That no-showing stunt you pulled last Saturday had her throwing fits. She even called you a primadonna, you believe that?” “Awww, shit, what does she think she can do to me? Suspend me?” “Lots of things. Hell, she could even throw a lawsuit at you claiming breach of contract for missing Slam. It took lots and lots of convincing and debating from me and a few other guys to keep her from just firing you, you realize that?” Doug sits speechless for a moment, then “Thanks for sticking up for me.” “Hey, I consider you a friend, and I look out for friends. However, I don’t think I can hold her off forever. She’s already talking suspension, backstage. My advice is that you get shaved, get sober, get your head clear. You’re no good to anybody in this state of mind, and you’re going to need to make a hell of a case for Reagan to not up and fire you.” Nate puts his hand on Doug’s shoulder as Doug slumps to the bar. “You take care, man. Give me a call this weekend so I can help you get back on track.” Nate stands up and pulls his wallet out of his pocket. He pulls a couple bills out of his pocket and sets them on the bar. “Keep the change. See ya, Doug. Try to get a good night’s sleep tonight.” Nate slaps Doug’s back as he walks by. The bartender watches Nate as he walks away, then turns back to the slumped over form of Doug Troy. He grabs the empty mugs and pulls them off the counter as a voice rises from Doug. “Keep them coming, man.”
