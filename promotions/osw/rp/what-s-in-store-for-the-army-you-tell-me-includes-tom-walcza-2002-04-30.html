What's In Store For The Army? You Tell Me...[Includes Tom Walczak]

30 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 30.04.02 Sitting in their hotel room, Krieg sat on the edge of his bed while Walczak sat on a wicker chair over near the window. Deep in thought, the two men hadn’t spoken for a few minutes now, but Krieg decided to push the point, “Tom, what is wrong with Ivan? He is acting very strange,” questioned the German, “Michel, I am in the dark as much as you are, Comrade,” replied his Pollack counterpart, Both men went silent again, Krieg slowly pushing himself up off of the bed and walking over to the window. Drawing the curtains back slightly, he looked out as some light poked its way through the crack in the fabric. Walczak now broke the silence as he turned in his chair slightly, looking at Michel, “Comrade, what is going to come of the Army? Are we going to crumble as Ivan weakens? I am not scared Michel, just worried,” “I understand, my friend,” replied Michel as he patted Tom on the shoulder, “But you have right to be concerned. I, also, am concerned for Red Army’s stability. Our careers, never mind our honour, dignity and reputations, could be torn to shreds if we don’t do something about this,” “I feel the same way Michel. I’ll give Ivan credit where credits due: he sure managed to match us two, didn’t he,” “Yah. We like the same things, both share a sense of humour, agree on the same subjects. Even if the Red Army was to crumble, which it won’t I can assure you, we could still work as a team, dah,” asked Krieg, “Yes, yes, my German buddy,” smiled Walczak, showing an unusual sign of friendship, Both Walczak and Krieg now stand at the window, looking out to the sea as it smashes off of the rocks on the beach, early on a Tuesday morning. Standing side by side, the two European giants had their arms folded behind their backs, “Krieg, I think I have answer to problem,” claimed Walczak, “Yah, Walczak. What do you think, Comrade,” Turning round slightly so the light catches the side of his face, Walczak looks at Krieg as he proposes his idea, “We need new members, Michel. New Communists to lead the revolution of the Army,” “Dah, you are right, Walczak. Let’s set about recruiting already,” With that, Krieg walked over to the side of his bed and pulled open the drawer of his bedside cabinet, revealing various personal items like his German Youth Army medals, which he carries with him at all times, his Swiss watch and some other items of jewellery. From under all the bits and pieces, Krieg pulls out a small folder with a binder through it and places it on his bed as he closes the drawer. Walczak looks over in interest and begins to saunter over to Krieg’s bed, “This is my guides to wrestling, Tom,” said Krieg, a little grin spreading across his face, “Ah,” answered Walczak, who was getting interested in Krieg’s idea, “Look at this, Walczak,” said the German, as Walczak sat down next to him and the two Europeans looked through Krieg’s notes, which contained information on wrestler’s from all over the world which had caught Krieg’s interest, “Now, he’s from the PCW,” said Krieg as the scene started to face out and only to two Communist’s voices could be hear as the scene changed to black, “Dah, he is one big man,” claimed the Pollack, “Yah, and he will be one of us. Oh yes, he will....”
