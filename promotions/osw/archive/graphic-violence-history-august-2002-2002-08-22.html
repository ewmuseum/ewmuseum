Graphic Violence — history (August 2002)

22 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
History There are some nights when I go to sleep where memories of a past long ago come back to visit me. Sometimes I do not even need to sleep, for the whole nightmare can pass right before my eyes, just like I’m back at the old house. Steven Shadows? Yeah, that was his name. But that name has been dead for over eleven years now. If it weren’t for me, Steven would be dead right now. There was no way that he would have been able to stand up to his parents if it weren’t for me. Who can live a normal life when you’re father likes to abuse you and your mother has schizophrenia? There was only one alternative for Steven: to listen to me. I helped him through his time of need. I was there for him when no one else was. Eventually he let me take over and he became the voice in his head, which was I… Graphic Violence Shadows Home – 1989 'Here you go, boy. Your food for the week, come and get it.' John Shadows stood at the top of the basement steps. Steven, beaten and afraid to climb up, stood at the bottom. 'Fine, you ungrateful little shit!” John threw the plate of food down the steps towards Steven. “Stay down there!” The door slammed shut and was locked. A single bulb hanging from the ceiling gave Steven his only light. He had been locked in the basement for months, maybe longer. He had lost all track of time. He picked the sandwich up from off of the dirty floor and began to eat. He must have finished the sandwich in less than twenty seconds. He only received one a week. He then grabbed one of the seven bottles of water and began to drink that rather fast. You’re pathetic, Steve. Steven gagged on his water, spitting out what he could before it, too, could go down his throat and help out in the choking process. 'No,' he moaned. He threw the bottle down, fearing the return of the voice that wouldn't leave him. 'Go away. Go away!' A recycled tear ran down his cheek. Pathetic. I’m ashamed to be here, Steve. Steven pulled on his hair. It was his natural reaction from the voice coming from inside of his skull. 'Why are you talking to me?” His body fell to the floor in an invisible struggle. “What are you talking about?' This wasn’t the first time that he had had the encounter. It was pushing him closer and closer to an insanity that his parents could never do. Look at you, eating food off the floor. You’ve been trapped in this basement for seven months now. I, for one, am getting tired of watching you sit here barely living. 'What am I to do about it?!” Steven pulled his hand away, throwing the collected strands of hair as far from him as he could. “Father has locked me down here. There is nothing I can do.' Stop being a pussy, Steven. You’ve always been a pussy, and you always will be as long as you let your mother and father hit you, abuse you and lock you down here. 'Watch… your…” Steven gasped for another breath of air. “…Language.” You are becoming your parents Steven. Look at you. There is no one in this room but yet you’re talking to me. Do you want to be a freak like your schizophrenic mother? He didn’t want to be anything near what his parents were. The thought of such caused the emotions to boil in his head. His head raked across his forehead, hoping that pain would cause it end. 'N-No. I don’t want to be like them at all.' He latched onto his bangs. I have always been here for you, Steven. Whenever they hit you, I feel that pain. Whenever you’re starving, I’m starving too. If it weren’t for me, you would be dead right now. Steven threw his hands away, pulling with them more locks of his mangled hair. 'Go away,” he screamed. “I don’t want to hear you any more!' Steven began to bang on his head. What are you trying to do Steven? Get me out of your head? That’s not going to happen. You need me. You need me to survive this life of yours, and you know that. It’s time for you to stand up for yourself instead of always getting pushed around. Right, Steven? “No,” he screamed. Tears began to stream down his cheeks. “No,” he repeated, but with less of assurance than before. “No,” he whispered, but the tears clouded the word’s effect. Say yes. On last time, Steven recited “No.” He then cried. Just say yes and you can be free. You can be free from all of this, Steven. Let me out, Steven. Just say yes… “N-“ Steven couldn’t finish the word. “Y-“ he began. That’s it. Do it, Steven. “Ye-“ Steven stopped. He wiped his eyes clear. 'Yes,” he meekly said. But once more, “Yes,” he said. He spoke it from the depths of his chest. “It’s time to stand up.' It’s time for you to show your parents whose boss now, right Steven? 'Yes.' Steven stood up. It’s time for you to forget who Steven Shadows was. From this point on there is no more Steven Shadows. There is only… “Graphic Violence.” He looked around at his surroundings. 'We were the same, Steven.” He spoke softly to himself. “You were me all along, but only now do you accept Me.' His eyes then slowly traced up to the basement door. With a strong stride, he took the first step up towards that encasing. * * * The reports stated that they were found dead at the bottom of the basement steps. It was never determined whether it was homicide or accidental. A culprit was never pursued and the two were buried in partnered coffins three days later. Flowers were placed on the grave. A scripture that spoke of love, forgiveness, and the after life for those who lived their life as they should, stood guard at the top of the tombstone. It was a terrible tragedy to a couple who had lost their only child seven months earlier. His body had never been recovered. At last, they would be able to join him in Heaven. * * * There they rested, her body atop of his. Neither breathed. There had been a scream, but that was their last words as they fell down the basement stairs. From up top, the lights shown an outline of a bald head. The person looked down at the wreckage before him. They were dead. Pulling back, Graphic Violence slowly closed the door to the basement. Death didn’t have the mental atrocity that would one would connect with people’s inner thoughts. Sure, those people down at the bottom of the basement were parents at one stage in their life. But they weren’t his parents. They were the parents of a helpless kid named Steven Shadows. That person was dead, too.
