Great Dragon — character application (June 2002)

24 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Great Dragon Real Name: Horito Yakama Nick Name: G.D., The Dragon Catchphase: "..." Character Handler: Tomer C. MSN Account: N/A AIM Address: [detail withheld] Theme Song: "Wake up" by Rage Against The Machine Date of Birth: 11/5/1966 From: Osaka, Japan Height: 5'10" Weight: 187 Lbs. Wrestling Style: Technical High Flyer Signature Moves: Dragonrana, Dragon Speed [Superkick], Dragon Chaos [Tornado DDT], Powerless to Resist [Chicken Wing Crossface], Dragonsault [Moonsault/Asai Moonsault], Dragon Pain [Brainbuster DDT], Dragon Driver [Michinoku Driver] , Splitting Headache [Enzuigiri] , Dragon Breath [Mist Spray] , Dragon Kick [Tajiri Kick] , One Moment [Tiger Driver] , No Conciousness [Kata-hajime] Finisher Move: The Dragon's Wrath [Cobra Clutch Sleeper Hold] Set-Up Move: Kick to the gut Current Team: N/A Past Teams & Groups: Team Gunmen (fWo Survivor II), Team LoW (LoW), Team Future Foundation (fWo Survivor II) Title History: 1x KOYASHI Champion 1x LoW Lightweight Champion 2x LoW World Heavyweight Champion Awards History: fWo Survivor II Participant Description: Great Dragon is a short man, wearing black wrestling tights and boots, as well as a green and black mask. His green eyes reflect off the light, and he look ready most of the time to wrestle. History: The following transcript was recorded onto videotape between Horito Yakama, the man known as ‘Great Dragon’ and Dr. Charles Helmsley, Psychoanalyst and professional Hypnotist during a regression session on Monday, December 10th, 2001: “Mr. Yakama, please tell me of your childhood, to the best of your recollection.” “I…entered this plane of existence on a cold day in late Autumn. I believe it was November fifth, nineteen sixty-six. I do not recollect my past, besides my name. I was brought into this world, only remembering…THIS!” “Yes, Mr. Yakama. What does that mean exactly, though?” “It is Japanese for ‘Rebirth’, Doctor.” “I see. Why would it be on your left leg, however?” “I do not know why it was written. It is an enigma that I have tried to solve, yet it appears I have a ‘hole’ in my mind…” “Of course. Do you have any other distinguishable marks?” “Besides a scar on my right hip from a childhood mishap, I do not know of any other birth marks or childhood injuries that I have sustained.” “Please, tell me about your childhood, from what you remember.” “I was orphaned at the tender age of three. I know I grew up in the poverty of a Japanese orphanage in the town of Jukai-Oshi, about 80 km from Tokyo, where I was never called by name, but merely ‘you’ or ‘him’.” “And how did this make you feel?” “It degraded my self-esteem for many years. Rarely in my childhood was I very happy, Doctor.” “What happened in your childhood that was your turning point in life?” “I would have to say when I…well, I cannot say what I did, or I would be extradited back to Japan for trial. However, it was a somewhat brutal crime that forced me to leave the Orphanage at eighteen.” “I understand. So, what did you do then?” “I went for several months to various towns and cities, working odd jobs, but never lasting long because it was not my goal nor the place that I wanted to be in. So, I headed to the big city, Tokyo.” “What did you find there, Mr. Yakama?” “As I entered Tokyo, I was bedazzled by the large arena near outer edge of the city…” “Which arena was this?” “The Tokyo Egg Dome, of course.” “Of course.” “I worked in the arena itself as a Janitor for a year, watching shows and practicing the moves that I saw men like Stan Hansen, Johnny Ace, The Funk Brothers and so forth executing with flawlessness.” “What happened after a year?” “Well, one time, I was caught practicing some moves in the ring, and was about to be fired for failure to do his job and using arena property without consent…” “That’s not a good thing.” “No, it is not. But then a man appeared in my defense, and saved me from termination of contract.” “Who was this man?” “This man was Kiato Mutari, a man of respect in the Puroresu scene, being a former wrestler himself in some of the smaller ‘Indy’ Promotions in Japan, who saw potential in me. He went on to teach me how to perform different manuevers and how to better myself.” “That’s a fine thing, when a master teaches his student the arts…” “Yes, it is. I mastered the arts of Technical and High Flying wrestling, like my mentor had learned before me.” “That’s good to hear.” “I knew being a Pro Wrestler would not pay much at the start, but I was not in it for the money, but to show to the world I had the skills to gain respect, something of which I had lacked for my whole life.” “Ah, so it was because you lacked respect that you went on this course to…Professional Wrestling.” “Yes. I began my Wrestling career in a Training Center called 'The Dragon's Lair', a small, but highly respected training facility in most of Japan. There, Mutari taught me his knowledge of Technical wrestling, teaching me various submission and very painful maneuvers if applied in the correct manner. I learned most of these maneuvers within my first three to four months of his training career, almost using the moves as an appendage to my basic functions in every day life.” “That is even good in self-defense, I heard.” “Mutari then went on to train me in the area of High Flying maneuvers. He taught me everything from a splash to an Asai Moonsault, and I once again absorbed the knowledge of how to flawlessly execute these maneuvers with virtually no difficulty.” “Interesting…” “It was as though Wrestling was my hidden purpose that I had unlocked.” “Indeed. That is a good thing.” “Well, after watching, and subsequently studying some American Professional Wrestling to see how America utilized Professional Wrestling, in case I was to ever enter the sub division of USA style Wrestling, I watched many tapes of WCW and WWF matches. One man that intrigued him was 'The Million Dollar Man' Ted DiBiase. DiBiase had utilized a very powerful submission move, a Cobra Clutch Sleeper Hold, which he dubbed 'The Million Dollar Dream' as his Finisher. I loved both DiBiase's attitude as well as his efficiency in executing his finisher, and his in ring skills and psychology overall. I then decided to borrow the move from DiBiase and dubbed my Cobra Clutch Sleeper Hold 'The Dragon's Wrath'.” “I remember Ted DiBiase. I think his theme went something like ‘Money, Money, Money…’” “Yes, yes. That was the theme song. Anyway, the origin of my Kayfabe, or in laymen’s terms, ‘gimmick’ name, Great Dragon, came from a legend in Puroresu and US Wrestling: Ultimo Dragon.” “He was a good wrestler, from what I heard of him.” “Yes, he was a great wrestler. Now, after watching many awesome matches by the Japanese legend, and seeing the great looking mask that he donned, I decided to 'borrow' the Dragon last name and change Ultimo to Great. Afterwards, I got myself the mask that I wear to this very day, nearly a decade and a half later. I became almost instantaneously, via recognition of my signature mask and the snappy green and black tights the man that is simply known as 'Great Dragon'.” “That is awesome.” “Yes. After being taught the basics by Mutari and getting my Kayfabe identity prepared, Mutari went on to manage me, his 'idol' pupil, as I began his career in the Puroresu Promotion 'SPW', or Shuriken Professional Wrestling.” “That sounds like a Ninja name.” “Shuriken is a fighting star, yes, but its not a damn Ninja promotion. Now please let me continue.” “All right, all right.” “It was in this two year span that I would learn my 'Extreme' or 'Hardcore' Wrestling mannerisms, having fought in dangerous Death Matches and other 'Extreme' matches, as this promotion was nothing more than a 'Minor League' version of Frontier Martial arts Wrestling, or FMW. However, after two years, and having barely reached the Upper Mid Carder level in the promotion, I decided it was time to move on to bigger and better things, and left SPW after my two year contract was completed.” “That is sad.” “Yes, but it gets better. In May of 1990, I joined KOYASHI, a relatively large Puroresu promotion. When I entered, I was an established Superstar, and quickly won the second highest belt the promotion offered, the KOYASHI Junior Heavyweight Championship in an awesome bout against Jiusi Makiri, which lasted nearly an hour long, having both of them sacrificing their bodies for the audience.” “Wow, an hour long match. Quite intense.” “Oh, it was, my friend, it was. Finally, after eight years, and several stints as Six Man and Standard Tag Team Champion, as well as another short stint as Junior Heavyweight champion, I won the KOYASHI World Heavyweight Championship in December 24th, 1998, and set a name for himself in Japan as a 'Legend' there.” “You must have been proud.” “Of course I was. It is to this date my crowning achievement in the wrestling industry. However, sadly, KOYASHI went bankrupt in early 2001, and it seemed to have been doomed to history like many other promotions before it.” “That is a sad thing to hear.” “Yes. However, luck intervened. The Legends of Wrestling hired me after Tony Hooch and Zac Hawkes created a proposed contract for the last KOYASHI champion.” “Did you agree to it?” “Of course I agreed to the terms, knowing that I would succeed once again.” “That’s a good thing.” “Here, I won the Legends of Wrestling Light Weight Championship and the Legends of Wrestling World Championship, not once, but TWICE within little more than a month. I had begun to show the United States what he had shown in Japan: that he was a 'legend' in the field of Professional Wrestling, and that there was little anyone could do to stop it. However, that promotion closed sadly shortly after my second reign began.” “Another abysmal failure, huh?” “Yes. I was then about to join the ranks of KAGE fighting after the owners had contacted me in secret about being part of it, but it was shut down by some federal agencies before I was allowed in.” “I…see…” “Finally, the fans Wrestling organization had recognized this slowly rising star's potential talent, and decided to 'test' me by having him appear in the annual 'entrance' program, 'fWo Survivor'. I would use my mind and body to overcome obstacles and perhaps get an fWo contract. I was placed on the blue team, known simply as 'Team Gunmen', along with established talent like jolt superstar Graphic Violence and Golden Glen Miller, and so my next great quest began.” “I heard about Survivor. Didn’t Ruben Ross win it last year?” “Yes. And unfortunately, I couldn’t win this years one. After 10 rounds, I was eliminated, and now am looking around for future prospects to prove myself further.” “Well, then, I wish you luck, Horito.” “Thank you, Doctor.” Kid Wonder Kimbusa Michel Krieg
