Slam! 27, 3 August 2002

3 August 2002

Seth: You know, Trev, this is such bullshit. Porter books these ridiculous brackets for this tag team title tournament and Jane says we have to go to this president guy in order for them to get changed. Our scene shifts to a shot of the backstage area of the Mountain Dew Center in Erie, PA, where we are greeted with the team of Seth Masters and Trevor Hawke who are collectively known as Civil Disobedience. They’re currently en route to some place or another as they with a purpose through the corridors, clad in their usual wrestling gear with nothing looking too out of the ordinary about then. We come in during the middle of a conversation the two men are having as they venture forth. Hawke nods in agreement to his partner’s complaints. Trevor: Oh, I know, Seth. I know. She can’t touch the brackets, she says. That’s something that only the president or commissioner can do, she says. Seth: Don’t get me wrong. I have no reservations about giving Da Westsidaz the beating of their lives. After all, we never did have an opportunity to have a fair match back in the day, and a clean win over them would give me some closure on that issue. But the only reason we’re facing each other in the first round is to give the Think Tank the benefit of not having to deal with both of us throughout the course of the tournament. Cowardly shit like that...man, I just can’t stand for it. Trevor: I think the feeling is completely mutual, my friend. It’s a frickin’ joke that we have to face Da Westsidaz, who though inferior to us are at least somewhat capable in the ring, while they get opponents like...Disorderly Conduct? I mean, what the hell. How fair is that? Masters cracks his knuckles, shaking his head skeptically. Seth: All I know is that this president guy better change the damn brackets to make this tourney fair...because if he turns out to be a dicksmack, I’ll be pissed as hell I had to waste my time searching for his stupid ass. Trevor: Well look on the bright side, Seth. Jane did handpick this guy to come in here to stop the Think Tank. She trusted him enough to give him the position of OSW president. He’s got to be at least somewhat reasonable. At least reasonable enough to let us have a piece of those Vax fucks in the first round. I... Rounding a corner, the two members of Civil Disobedience nearly run smack dab into their opponents of the evening, as Riptah and Short Circuit of Da Westsidaz appear to be traveling in the opposite direction. An awkward backpedaling by both groups as they see each other leads to an exchange of angry glares as they take each other in. Riptah decides to break the silence, pointing angrily at the two suburbanites. Riptah: Just where do you punk-asses think you’re going? Hawke returns his angry tone, cocking his head with an air of superiority as he addresses Da Westsida. Trevor: Well not that it’s any of your damn business, but we were just on our way to see our new president about these lame ass brackets for this tag title tournament. Masters interjects himself into the conversation before either member of Da Westsidaz has a chance to respond, stepping in front of his partner and copying his tone and poise. Seth: Don’t get the idea that we’re scared of you, though. I’d be more than happy to take you boys to school again. You do know you guys just got lucky at the pay-per-view, don’t you? Riptah, his face turning up in an enraged expression, raises a fist and takes a step forward threateningly. Riptah: You piece of shit...I’ll show you luck... Putting an arm out, Short Circuit stops his advancing partner with a few calm words. Short Circuit: Cool it, Rip. Turning his head to face Seth and Trevor, Short Circuit removes his goggles to regard the duo with an irritated, but agreeing, gaze. Short Circuit: I’m not too sure your head’s on straight with all this talk about taking us to school, but me and Rip here are totally there with you on the bunk-ass nature of this tourney. In fact we were just on our way to see the Prez as well. We wanna get our hands on them Think Tank bitches as soon as possible. No one fucks with da Westside and gets away with it. Cupping a hand to his mouth, Hawke leans over to Masters and whispers a comment. Trevor: (Muttering) Well, besides us that is. Riptah raises an eyebrow at the smaller man. Riptah: What was that? Hawke raises his arms innocently, backing up a step. Trevor: Oh nothing. Just a cough. Riptah and Short Circuit look extremely skeptical as Civil Disobedience snickers to themselves. Short Circuit shakes his head in disbelief as he points an accusing finger at the duo. Short Circuit: You boys better watch your mouth or me and Rip might change our minds about wanting to rough you up tonight. Masters waves him off, uncaring. Seth: Yeah, yeah. Whatever. Riptah: Well what you fools waiting for. Let’s get going so we can find this political bastard and get this shit fixed up. A’ight? Hawke and Masters exchange a questioning look and after a few seconds both shrug and nod. Seth and Trevor: A’ight. The two teams begin walking together, and, as if my some miracle of fate, it only takes them seconds before the man they’re looking for appears in the hallway before them. Though apparently in the midst of carrying out one task or another, the OSW President, still wearing his George W. Bush mask and “Mr. President” T-shirt, pauses to acknowledge the two most popular teams on his roster with a wave and a nod. The sound of the crowd roaring in approval at the man who DVDed Howard Porter last week, reminds us that the fans in the arena are viewing the activities on the Jumbrotron. Mr. President: Oh hey, guys. How are you doing tonight? Turning back to face the other three men, Trevor Hawke whispers an order. Trevor: I’ll do the talking. Turning back to face the president, Hawke doesn’t see the sour expressions appear on Da Westsidaz’ faces. Instead, he puts his hand to his mouth and clears his throat. Trevor: *Ahem* Hello, sir. I’m Trevor Hawke, this is Seth Masters, and these... The president cuts him off with a wave of the hand, speaking with a friendly tone. Mr. President: Oh, I know who you all are. Do you really think I’d be unfamiliar with my roster? Trevor: Heh, of course you wouldn’t. I was just hoping perhaps you could introduce yourself to us. Mr. President: I think for now it would be best if you guys just referred to me as Mr. President. Hawke strokes his chin thoughtfully at this non-revelation. Trevor: Hmm...not quite as informal as I would have like, but it’ll do. Crossing his arms unhappily, Riptah takes it upon himself to interject his presence into the conversation. Riptah: Can we cut with the chit chat and get down to bud-ness, already! Facing the former tag champ, the president nods in agreement. Mr. President: Oh, of course. That is what we’re here for, isn’t it? Business, I mean. So just what is it that you boys need from me? As soon as he asks this question all the men begin to talk at the same time. After hearing it all come out in jumbles, they all exchange glances and Short Circuit is the first to get out a statement. Short Circuit: The reason we came here to ask you if it was possible for you to change the brackets for this tournament Porter’s been organizing. Masters nods, then adds his two cents. Seth: I’m sure you’re aware of just how totally unfair the first round pairings are. I mean, our great commissioner is playing us against each other while he and his buddies take the easy road out of the first round. Obviously that is complete bullshit. The president nods, and one can only wonder what face lies pondering underneath that mask of his. Mr. President: I agree with you, there. You two certainly shouldn’t have to face each other the first round, especially with the grudges you have against the Think Tank. Matches between you and them would be compelling television. In addition to that, I’m sure all of you would be rather satisfied with the situation. And a happy roster is the first step to a successful federation. Both Civil Disobedience and Da Westsidaz smile in agreement at the president’s words. Mr. President: That’s why it makes it so hard for me to turn down your request. And with that, the smiles fade to frowns of disbelief. Riptah: (in a Gary Coleman-esque voice)Whatchu talking about, “turn down our request”? Mr. President: Well guys, it’s like this. It would be as easy as pie for me to manipulate the brackets, pitting you guys against whoever I wished to. It’s all part of the perks being the president. Of course, if I were to do that...I really wouldn’t be better than Porter, now would I? I’d be taking advantage of the federation to carry out personal vendettas and that’s not really the type of morally correct presidency I’m trying to establish here. Hawke spreads his arms, blatantly pleading for him to change his mind about his judgment. Trevor: But...but come on, man! Where’s your sense of justice? The president visibly sighs, putting a comforting hand on Hawke’s shoulder. Mr. President: If there’s any justice in the world, Porter will get his in the end whether you face him in the tournament or not. Now is there anything else I can do for you? Seth: Technically you didn’t DO anything yet. Mr. President: Well mince words if you wish, but I’m a busy man as of late. If there’s no further issue here, I’ll be getting on with my affairs. The other four men are silent. No responses are voiced and the president shrugs his shoulders in reply. Mr. President: No? Well then I will talk to you fellows later. As he walks off down the hallway, continuing on his path, the two teams of men shake their heads, disappointed. Riptah: That’s fucking bullshit, man. Trevor: You know, Rip...for once I think I agree with you. At this moment it dawns on both teams that they’ll be facing each other in the ring in just minutes. Their expressions shifting to confrontational glares, all four men stare angrily at each other. Riptah: Well I guess we don’t have any choice now. We’ll see you punks in the ring. Hawke wiggles his fingers in the air in a mocking manner. Trevor: Likewise...dawg. Turning away, Da Westsidaz and Civil Disobedience storm off in opposite directions. Sour faces are the popular look of the night. Seth/Trevor/Riptah/Short Circuit: Un-fucking-believable The two groups continue to move away from one another, as the scene fades to black. . . . . . . The scene opens inside a small office suite inside the arena where Jane Reagan sits behind her desk smiling sweetly. Jane: Well, we really are glad to have you back. In spite of everything you’re a great talent and it’s only going to be good for the company that you’re here. She turns a set of papers around on her desk and pushes them toward the opposite side as the camera pans around to see Tempest sitting there. She smiles and starts signing things. Tempest: Thanks Jane. It’s good to be back, even if I still don’t understand the whole thing with you and Sebastian Toomes. Jane smiles a sort of stony smile and folds her hands as the two exchange a tense glance. Jane: I don’t need you to understand. Tempest shrugs and continues signing the contracts. Tempest: Too bad I couldn’t sign these earlier in the week and help you with your little Think Tank problem in a match though. Jane: Oh I’m sure you can help me with it regardless. After all, without a match you’ll be bored… and you’ll just have to entertain yourself somehow. She smiles wryly and Tempest grins ear to ear. Tempest: Good point! I’ll just have to find something to do. She winks at Jane, who folds her arms. Jane: One thing I don’t get though Tempest... Why are you here? I mean the fWo wouldn’t have fired you. I just never thought I’d see you walk through that door again. She sits up a little straighter and smiles proudly. Jane: Of course I know I’ve built the OSW into a premier organization. Thinking it’s a better bet perhaps? Tempest rolls her eyes, but still smiles at her. Tempest: Don’t flatter yourself too much there Jane. It is a great organization, but I came back because of Ivan. The rest is secondary. Jane shakes her head. Jane: I just don’t understand what you see in him. Tempest looks up and smiles mischievously. Tempest: I don’t need you to understand. The two women rise and exchange a cordial handshake before Tempest exits the office. . . . . . . Nate: Ahem… as I was saying Disorderly Conduct… Steven: PINK SPANDEX!!!! Nate: Disorderly Conduct… Steven: PINK SPANDEX!!!! Nate: DISORDERLY CONDUCT… Steven: PINK SPANDEX!!!! Decker connects with a right hand shutting Steven up. Nate: Thank you. Disorderly Conduct will face off against Alan Kriegman and Trashcan Man in a first round tag team tournament match. Robbie: Wonder who’ll win that match… Nate: Also on tonight’s line up we have NC-17 against Redemption Song, Howard Porter and Armour vs Scott Taylor and Kid Wonder and Civil Disobedience vs Da Westsidaz, which are all first round tag team match ups. Nate sighs as we should cut to commercial but, “Genius” by Pitchshifter suddenly thumps onto the arena PA system as the arena immediately fills to the rafters with jeers. Before long the ring entrance is filled by the smirking guise of the OSW Commissioner himself, “The Scholar” Howard Porter. Nate: CUT TO A COMMERCIAL!! QUICK!!! Porter casually strolls down to the ring amidst the beer cups and insults, both of which are flying at him. Porter gives them no mind as he reaches the ring and climbs in, beckoning Earl Heinrich for a mic. We get a close up camera shot on the scene as Hebner crosses his arms and turns away, refusing to oblige “The Scholar.” Porter: Earl, if I had a badge that said OSW Commissioner, I’d point to it right now. Earl sighs and hands Porter a microphone. Porter: That’s better Earl. Howard then walks to the middle of the ring to address the on hand Pennsylvania crowd. Porter: Greetings Erie! The token boos come from the crowd at Porter, who’s mouth drops open in feigned shock. Porter: How? How can you boo your beloved Commissioner? How can you boo your beloved Commissioner who has made this the greatest Slam in the short history of Old School Wrestling? Steven: Like this. Boooooooo! Robbie: Not only are you gay, you’re a friggin’ idiot. Porter: What have I done you ask? Well, there are many subtle changes I’ve brought about in OSW that have improved vastly on the tripe that Jane Reagan was shilling out. But all that is piddance compared to my recent actions. Ladies and gentleman, I may have single handedly solidified the future of Old School Wrestling as a frontline promotion. Nate: What the hell is he talking about? Porter: I have signed two individuals who I personally think will not only have an impact on OSW, they will change it altogether. They bring instant credibility, instant talent, and an instant impact on the main-event scene here in OSW. Nate: Oh Lord… Porter: Ladies and gentlemen, let me introduce to you…the men, the myths, the legends themselves…Tuff Tom and Mean Mike…DISORDERLY CONDUCT! Some generic neo-heavy metal music blares across the sound system as the crowd immediately resumes their booing. Robbie: How can they boo Tuff Tom and Mean Mike? These guys practically reinvented the sport! Nate: Aside from the fact that they suck…it’s probably because they know Porter simply brought them in as cannon fodder for Kriegman and TCM. Robbie: How dare you insult our Commish’s integrity! He only signed Disorderly Conduct because it was in the best interest of OSW to do so! Porter: Ladies and gentlemen, I give you the next great thing in OSW, Disorderly….CONDUCT! Tuff Tom and Mean Mike thrust their arms up once again to the fans, who only boo louder. Tuff Tom hands it back to Mean Mike. Mean Mike: And then after we’re done, we’re gonna kick ‘em some more! Mean Mike, again, hands Tuff Tom the stick. Tuff Tom: Yeah! Tuff Tom gives it back to Mean Mike. Mean Mike: So don’t beg for any pity, because Disorderly Conduct don’t give none. Robbie: Whoa, no wonder these guys are in the main event. Nate: Shut up! Porter cringes as he takes back the microphone. And I’m sure this will be just as bad….We need to head to a commercial. We will be right back after these messages. . . . . . . PHD sits fiddling with a remote controller.
