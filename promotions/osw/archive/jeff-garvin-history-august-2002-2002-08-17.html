Jeff Garvin — history (August 2002)

17 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
History Professional Wrestling was not as main stream as it was in the nineties. It was 1986 and the World Wrestling Federation was just showing signs of potential. The other kids in Jeff’s classe were taught that wrestling was for trailer park trash, people who couldn’t read or write. He was often teased in the playground while he sat on the swings. It hurt him to hear these kids putting down his father, the man he idolized. But Jeff was taught that violence wasn’t the way to solving your problems, so he took it all with stride. His father had made it clear that he didn’t want Jeff to be associated with wrestling. It was a tough life that few could handle and he wanted something better for his son. He wanted him to get a college education somewhere nice and to get a high paying office job. Just at the sight of his various welts that decorated his upper body made him cringe at the thought of his beloved Jeff indexing that. But as time went by, he knew he couldn’t keep Jeff from the business. Jeff loved it even more than he did. Gerald’s brother, and Jeffrey’s uncle, Ron Garvin… or ‘Rugged’ Ronnie Garvin to those who followed his wrestling career played a heavy part in his boys life as well. Ron was a big name in the WWF and was celebrated as one of the best technical mat wrestler’s in the industry. While Gerald was a nobody who’s awesome wrestling talent was being wasted in small arena’s with only a hundred or two hundred people watching. This wasn’t the way he wanted to be remembering, he didn’t want to be known for his brother Ron’s fame. But there was no way out of it. Then would come the biggest tragedy in Jeff's life... His father's death. Gerald was attacked after a show by a local gang. Jeff would see him pass away in his hospital room, and thus would begin a new chapter in Jeff's life. He had a mission. He had to carry his father's name and the traditional ways of wrestling on with him. Jeff would be adopted by his Uncle Ron and would learn what happiness was once again. But deep down... Jeff was boiling up inside, now even his uncle was slowly fading away… The people. They were the problem, it was their approval that aloud these new promotions to change the face of Wrestling for the worst. The music, lights, pyros, scripts, goofy gimmicks, steroid freaks, hardcore match types, and the name ‘sports entertainment’ were all the doings of those moronic hill billies who didn’t want to see wrestling… They didn’t want the sport… They wanted over blown entertainment. Jeff stopped watching all together, he just sat at home reviewing old tapes from the 50’s, 60’s, and 70’s and realized this is what wrestling was when it was in it’s prime. None of that filler shit they call promos, just one hundred percent pure, natural wrestling. Two guys settling a score in the middle of the squared circle. No kidnappings, no love triangles, no flaming tables. Just fifty or sixty minutes of technical wrestling. This new era has destroyed his family. His Uncle Ron was out of a job. And wiry little Mexicans and chinks were taking their place. Beer guzzles and Goths were taking their place. Sports entertainment was killing pro wrestling So at the tender age of eighteen, fresh out of high school. Jeff knew that wrestling needed a savior, a general to fight the war and fend off this new breed of sports entertainment and bring back what Pro Wrestling really stood for… Wrestling! So Jeff trained with Dean Malenko and his Uncle Ron Garvin. Learning every single wrestling hold and move in the book. He was like a soldier preparing to go into battle. He needed every weapon and every tool. Finally he was ready… Adopting the name, ‘the Original’, meaning he was representing the Original Way of Pro Wrestling and mastering Dean’s move the Elevated Texas Clover lead and stealing Ron Garvin’s submission hold name the Hammer Jammer, adding the 3000 to the end. Jeff was ready to spread his message across the land. It was on UNCUT where ‘the Original’ Jeff Garvin showed up, jumping from the crowd and staging a sit in. He preached his message and was thrown out of the building before he could tell the world who he was. The next vary week, Jeff returned. This time with a contract and his own theme music. He introduce his series of segments, he called… Wrestling 101. Air time he would use to teach the fans one move and to spread his message to the people. He called the security guards down and degraded them, using one of them as his dummy to teach the people a simple Arm Bar, breaking the guards arm. The next week Jeff did the exact same, only this time the dummy for Wrestling 101 was a teenager who he found backstage bringing coffee to the staff. Jeff would once again make a huge impact, shattering the kid’s leg with a Leg Grapevine. The host of Wrestling 101 was climbing the ranks. Contradicting himself along the way. Saying that promos were one of the big problems in wrestling, and then going out and doing his Wrestling 101 on his air time. People were starting to really hate ‘the Original’ Jeff Garvin… But did he care? Not one fucking bit. Cause he knew, that when he was done, and this new age of entertainment was obliterated, they’d all thank him, and cheer for him. Garvin would stay with 21w for nearly six months, winning both pay per view matches against Jacob Utsler at Soul Survivor and even the extreme legend himself, Ninja K in a thirty minute submissions match. Within two months, he had ended Softcore wrestling and given Kenshiro two back to back concussions. But the thing was, a week before his match at Titania with Ninja K, a stipulation was set. If Garvin did not make Kenshiro tap out in the match, then Softcore would return. But if Garvin won, then Kenshiro could never again wrestle in a match under hard core rules. And as an added bonus, would get Kenshiro’s vary own mask. At Titania, the two men wrestled for a half an hour. Ninja K would scale to the top of the turnbuckle and attempt to him Jeff with a moonsault. But at the last second Jeff caught him and drive his head down into a steel chair with a tombstone piledriver. After which, he put his long time rival in his finishing submission hold, only to have Ninja K pass out. The referee call for the bell just as the time limit expired. At first Jeff was elated, only to find out that he had not submitted, and there for… Softcore would return. With that, Jeff’s dreams had been shattered, he threw away his Submissions Championship belt and left Titania a winner…or possibly, the loser on a higher scale. Weeks later, Jeff would do something that would change his destiny forever… He would betray his uncle and mentor Ron Garvin by beating him down in the middle of the 21w ring. Seconds later, Jeff introduced his new manager… None other then former PIW interviewer and manager, Jenny Jiggles. The next month went by in a haze, with antics here and there. Finally, Jeff would get his at super stardom when he faced 21w World Champion Glen Miller. Jeff would eventually loose the match and would go to the back in a fury. Only to announce later on that he was leaving 21w forever. Now Jeff is here in OSW… And what does ‘the Original’ have planned this time around?
