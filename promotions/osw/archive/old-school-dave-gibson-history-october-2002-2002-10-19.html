'Old School' Dave Gibson — history (October 2002)

19 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
History Dave Gibson didn’t have what you’d call the ideal childhood. His father left soon after Dave’s birth, and that left him without any male role models. He soon found some... courtesy of the wrestling he watched on television in the 80s. Gibson was drawn to one style of wrestling--that of basic, tough-as-nails competition--like a moth to a light.. When Dave was 10, some frugal saving habits led to a life-changing event: He attended a live show in Charlotte, watching some of his heroes up close and personal. Right then, Gibson knew what he wanted to do. On his 15th birthday, Dave began knocking on doors at the local wrestling school, only to be turned away. He expanded his search to the entire state of North Carolina, then South Carolina, Georgia and Virginia. Each time, after a few classes, Gibson was dismissed. 'You’re too small!' they said. 'You don’t have the look! You’ll never make it in the business!' Gibson proved them wrong. A keen natural athlete, he took the snippets of instruction provided at the various wrestling schools to develop his own technique, a throwback to the old style steeped in psychology and mat wrestling. Gibson is equally content to twist an opponent into submission, or suplex them around the ring with ease. Occasionally, he’ll even go up top. And he’s never afraid of a slugfest. Despite his growing skills, Gibson refrained from joining most organizations. The hardcore, high-flying, soap opera of 'sports entertainment' that pro wrestling had evolved into disgusted the young North Carolinian. So, he kept working on his own, and waiting... waiting for the organization that would call to him. That organization proved to be IOW. There, Gibson quickly shot to success, becoming the first--and only--Television Champion in IOW's short, troubled history. From there, Gibson was signed for jOlt's reopening. Gibson has enjoyed equal success there, winning the Revolutionary Championship and becoming embroiled in a feud with another former IOW grappler, Sam Potright. Now, Gibson finds himself here. In Old School Wrestling, a federation that, for obvious reasons, appeals to the young North Carolinian. Gibson is ready to make sure that EVERYONE in OSW remembers wrestling’s old school legacy. As for those that choose to forget, or worse, disgrace this legacy, Gibson plans to teach them respect... through pain. Old school is here, and it's making a comeback. Whether you like it or not.
