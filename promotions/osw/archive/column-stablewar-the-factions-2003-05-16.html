Stablewar: The Factions

16 May 2003

by Nate Dumas

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is The OSE site's own column (#4) by Nate Dumas, dated 16 May 2003, from the OSW site as it stood in the Wayback capture.
Hey OSW Fans! It’s Nate Dumas here with a column entitled “Stablewar.” Things in the OSW have changed drastically since it first opened up over a year ago and they continue on this trend. The OSF, OSE, President Trashcan Man, and other situations have turned the OSW into the Old School yet revolutionary federation that we now know and love. However, there is a storm brewing, and I’m not talking about Tempest. Ever since Head on Collision, which probably should mark the time when all tension would be released, instead has created a time of instability that I for one am not comfortable with. Quite frankly, a war is breaking out in the OSW. A war that can shift the balance of the political, physical, and psychological power forever and it appears that three large wrestling forces are on a collision course for disaster: The Asylum, Power Play, and The Red Army. The amazing thing about these three forces is the diversity within and around them. Not only this, but each has it’s own set of goals and strategies, it would appear, that play to their own advantage. The Asylum Leader: Trashcan Man Weight: 1212 Active Wrestlers: 4 Other Members: 1 The Asylum is an interesting group. A rag tag group of misfits on the outside, within them is instilled a strange sense of brotherhood to one another that gives them the poise to face any sort of challenge. Under the leadership of Trashcan Man, a founding father of the original Asylum, he proves to be the epicenter and the soul of the group. Strengths Versatility: The Asylum possesses a wide range of talent. Trashcan Man is the hardcore brawler while Timo Bolamba adds a more exotic and highflying nature to the group. Azala Zameer, though lovable, has the strength of an elephant and the ferocity to match while Six Finger Cisco has the technical prowess to get the job done. Heart: It could easily be said that the four men that represent the Asylum fight with total devotion to their cause. And they do it in a way that it is fun for them and entertaining to the audience. Political Power: As crazy as it sounds, The Asylum has the power of the President behind them and for once, it’s not going to be abused! This advantage can prove particularly disastrous for Power Play and The Red Army, who can and will suffer greatly from this advantage. Weaknesses Too Versatile?: Is it possible that The Asylum are too crazy for their own good? Though they all work and excel in different areas, could this hurt them when working together, such as in a tag team format? Enemy Number 1: While The Red Army and Power Play undoubtedly hate one another, they are both focusing on The Asylum exclusively. Power Play wants political power, The Red Army wants to eradicate any vestiges of the political machinery that Trashcan Man possesses. Can they fight a two front war? Too Good for Their Own Good: Having political power is nice, but having to obey the rules of the game can be difficult to manage. As the poster boys of the OSW, The Asylum has to be careful not to stoop to the level of PP and The Red Army. If they do, they’ll lose legitimacy and fans and fall back into their “Asylum Stigma” that they could acquire. Goal The Asylum is strictly defensive in this war. It is their job to fight off the oncoming Power Play and Red Army, who wish to overthrow them. With Trashcan Man leading them, this should be easily done unless the pressure becomes too great for them. Power Play Leader: Dave Gibson Weight: 907 Active Wrestlers: 4 Other Members: 0 A group that is comprised of foreigners to the OSW, Power Play quickly gathered together and formed a bond that no one thought possible. Not only that, but if there is one area that Power Play is especially lethal, it’s on the mat and in a submission hold. Wily as ever, it’s been shown that Power Play will do anything to gain a win, but in a way that you wouldn’t normally see coming. Led by Dave Gibson, a longtime OSW wrestler and flanked by three young, hungry stablemates, Power Play is nothing to shrug off. Strengths: Mat Masters: Every member of Power Play are lethal on the mat. Neither The Asylum or The Red Army have that sort of masterful use of submission moves. Psychological Predators: Part of submission fighting is beating your opponent psychologically, and that is certainly a tactic that Power Play has employed. From EDH appearing to be a fowl up to the two faced nature of Dave Gibson, “Shut up and wrestle” has turned into “Shut up and tap out” whenever they strike. On the attack, Power Play seems to come out of nowhere and be successful. A frontal attack is something they dislike, but catching their opponent unawares works like a charm. Nothing to Lose. All to Gain.: Power Play came out of nowhere and has tried to take power for themselves. They don’t have fans to lose. Title to shed. Or feelings that can be hurt. This puts them in a position where they could do anything at any time. The Asylum has to live by their Presidents code of conduct that he protects while The Red Army is walled in by their ideology, but Power Play has free reign. Weaknesses Too One Sided?: Power Play does indeed have technical ability, but they lack raw power or high flying abilities. This makes the susceptible to the other two stables, who are a bit more diversified. Keep Your Friends Close…: Sure, Power Play doesn’t have anything to lose, but they’re in it for power and traditionally that’s led to the destruction of stables. What stops one from attacking the other if they near their goal? I could see it happening. I’m sure Gibson sees the possibility too… Isolationist?: The Asylum have political power and fans. The Red Army have a wealth of members and possible others. But Power Play? Who do they have? All stables need wildcard’s that will help them in a jam. Does Power Play have that sort of relationship with the others? Comprised of wrestlers from other feds, are there Power Play sympathizers? We’ll have to see. Goal Power Play appears to be hell bent on taking monetary and political power and have it for themselves. This places them directly up against The Asylum, who is the source of that power. It will be difficult for Power Play to succeed, however, as their guerrilla tactics may be more difficult to implement. However, as the wilier team of the three, they have many opportunities to come out of nowhere and strike. The Red Army Leader: Ivan Stanislav Weight: 1294 (2306 Total) Active Wrestlers: 4 Other Members: 7 Historically overwhelming, overpowering, and overarching, The Red Army emerged out of nowhere and instantly threatened the entire OSW. Hell bent on the destruction of all that is Old School, Ivan Stanislav has called upon old friends, new friends, and his love to battle against the OSW itself. The raw power of Stanislav matched with the brawling abilities of Boda and the versatile Politically Incorrect give The Red Army a wide range of talents. And one must not forget the fighters in the army and his entourage as well… Strengths Overwhelming Force: The Red Army currently numbers in at a total of eleven warriors. Six being athletes and five being a diverse group of peripheral talents. An attack en masse against any other stable will decimate them. The Red Army knows this and have already used this to their advantage and will continue to. Unity, Unity, Unity: The Communist Ideology of The Red Army has solidified them into a singular unit of eleven members. Ivan Stanislav seems to stand in the center, being in love with Tempest, friends with Krauser, Ruslan, Wang and the entourage, and being a partner with Boda in the past. Every member works equally well with one another and they seem to be able to read each others mind when the time arises. The other stables don’t seem to possess this relationship with one another. Ivan Stanislav: Lets face it. The Red Army only exists because of Ivan Stanislav. He holds the soul of The Red Army in his hand, as he’s the loudest preacher of communism and the largest member. Stanislav is ferocious, tenacious, and incredibly charismatic and it has shown as wrestlers have flocked to his army. Weaknesses Too Much Power at Center?: Lets face it, Ivan Stanislav likes power. He may not say it, but he does. It’s shown in the past his oppression of his own members in The Red Army that even Boda can attest to. If someone goes against Stanislav’s wishes, will he end up bleeding on the floor with Ivan standing over them? Can they trust The Russian Bear? Or does he have another agenda all his own… Big Threat=Big Target: Sure The Red Army is a serious threat, but everyone also knows it. Some may ignore Power Play, but they won’t ignore The Red Army. Asa Fountain has made it clear that The Red Army is wrong and surely others see it too. If The Red Army wants to fight not just Asylum, but all of OSW, who’s to say the OSW won’t fight back? Ivan Stanislav: As much of a strength, Stanislav is also a weakness. What if the Russian Bear is eliminated? The Red Army would probably crumble. And Stanislav, though he admits it or not, is his own worst enemy. Tempest has pledged her love to him, but not her love for communism. Why is it she is noticeably missing whenever the Red Army beats down someone? If Boda was missing… would Stanislav ignore that too? Goal Simple. The Red Army wants to eliminate the OSW front office. Ever sine Jane Reagan this goal has been yearned for. The Red Army can do it too, that is what is scary! No one knows if someone is going to be a member as each week new members join. A Red Terror seems to be building as The Red Army grows in size. However, at the same time, The Red Army is loud, boisterous, and arrogant and because of that, when push comes to shove, many people will band together regardless of who they are to push back. I guarantee it. Conclusions Asylum/Power Play/The Red Army. It’s a three-pronged war that I’ve never seen before. All have their goals, strengths, weaknesses, and each stable will do everything to preserve itself as well as what they stand for. I cannot say who will win, for I feel they all have an equal chance at it. While sporadic skirmishes have occurred between all factions, it appears that at any moment a complete war will break out. However, this is just the surface. There is far more to these groups than mere overarching facts. I’ll get back to you, OSW fans, with another column outlining the individual members! See you on Slam! Nate Dumas
