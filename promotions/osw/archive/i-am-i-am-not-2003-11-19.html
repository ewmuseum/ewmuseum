I am. I am not.

19 November 2003

by -Six Finger- Cisco Milloy

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is The OSE site's own roleplay post (the site calls them Promos) (#223) by -Six Finger- Cisco Milloy, dated 19 November 2003, from the OSW site as it stood in the Wayback capture.
I am Cisco Milloy. I am not my win-loss record. I am not a World Title. I am not full of empty threats. I am not the American Dream. I am not Scott Taylor. I am not the money I make or the things I own. I am the reason you should watch. I am rock bottom. I am full of hate. I am crazy. I am Asylum. I am the truth. I am not phony. I am not here to put on a show. I am not a part of every day routine. I am not my career. I am not a team player. I am not who you should be looking up to. Was this supposed to happen? Am I supposed to be one of the elite in OSW and up towards the top of the foodchain? I am not Ivan Stanislav. I am not Scott Taylor. I am not fighting to make sure my win-loss record is impressive when I retire. I am not fighting to stake claim to worthless pieces of shiny metal that have nothing to do with who is truly the best. There’s always someone better than you....you’ve just been lucky enough not to have run in to them yet. The gold is worth about as much to me as the dirt it was dug up from. Some of my coworkers amaze me. They wrestle for one reason. They wrestle to eventually climb that mountain and snatch the coveted prize that is supposed to signify greatness and invincibility. They live, breathe, eat, and sleep for titles. They shed tears of joy when another check is marked in their win column and shed tears of pain when that check is instead marked down on the ladder side. When they retire, they go home to their big house. To their big families. To their false sense of a happy life. They reflect on their career, and they are fooled into thinking that they actually made a difference. They worked up an impressive win-loss record while winning a few titles here and there, and that’s enough for them. It’s not enough for me. I am not a championship belt. I am not my win loss record. I am not my fucking wonderful family. I am not my perfect life. I am the difference. I am outside of the box. I understand it, because I am enlightened. I am not the products I used to endorse or the person I used to be. I am not someone who will give up when I am challenged. I am a finisher. I am not a quitter. I wrestle and have confrontations with other human beings because I want to make a difference. I hurt other people because I want to make a difference. I don’t wrestle to entertain other people. I am not an entertainer. When I retire, I will not know my win-loss record. I will remember those who I have broken, and those that have broken me. I will remember my title reigns, but they will be insignificant to the big picture. I’ll look back on those times simply as battles with materialistic motives as the reason for fighting. I’ll remember those that I’ve grown to hate. The few that I’ve grown to love. And all that I’ve gone to war with. None of them I will remember as people I respect. To them, I was a check in the win column or a check in the loss column. I was another rock in the mountain that they had to climb over in order to claim that ultimate prize. I am not the mountain. I will make a difference. I will be remembered. But it won’t be for the wrong reasons. It won’t be for my impressive win-loss record. I’ve found plenty better than I. It won’t be for the titles I’ve won. I’ve been in the right place at the right time more than once. It will be for what I represent. The sport at its purest. Me vs. You. Who can hurt the other one the most, and who will demonstrate the most brutality in order to hurt their opponent. Not who can pin the other. Not who can knock the other one out. You haven’t lived until you’ve seen someone’s eyes filled with a mixture of pain and fear. Fear for their life. Fear that you were going to end their existence with one more smash to the head. That’s what I strive for. That’s what I wrestle for. It puts anything and everything in life into perspective. What would you regret in your life if I ended it right now. What would I regret if you ended my life right now. For the first time in my life. I get it. I would regret nothing. And that makes me very dangerous.
