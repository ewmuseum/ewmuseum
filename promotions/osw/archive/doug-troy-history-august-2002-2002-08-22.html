Doug Troy — history (August 2002)

22 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
History Douglas Troy, the son of well-known international freelance “problem solvers” Castor and Sasha, was born in an Afghani prison on August 11, 1975. After a week or so in the prison, he and his mother were removed from the prison and transported to Omaha, Nebraska, the place where young Doug would spend most of his young life. Doug showed a fascination with wrestling at an early age while watching such legends as Harley Race and Bob Backlund work their magic in the squared circle. In the Mid ‘80s, Troy watched as Hulkamania ran wild while he parents did “business” in Latin America and the Middle East. His scholarly career went normally enough for him. He graduated in the Top 50 of his class (despite a small debacle in Chemistry class that will not, for legal reasons, be elaborated upon) and traveled to Missouri to attend Truman State University. At times during his college tenure, he attended a small Missouri wrestling school. In 1997, he received his Bachelor of Arts degree in the field of Film. After graduating, he briefly pondered a job in the movie industry, but instead got a letter from a small martial arts and wrestling dojo in Japan. Excited at the opportunity to train abroad, he jumped at the chance. Doug picked things up very quickly at the Tendo-run ‘Anything Goes’ School of Martial Arts and Wrestling, and experienced his first real wrestling match in 1998. His first opponent: Toshiaki Kawada. Needless to say, Troy didn’t fare too well in his matchup. The next couple months were spent in a hospital for young Troy, his neck in a brace after a brutal Kawada powerbomb. When he came back, he quickly decided to AVOID Kawada and instead logged some weeks in the NJPW and some smaller Deathmatch federations. It was there that he developed his finisher, which was found to be extremely effective in hardcore matches. Many an opponent left matches with Troy with their faces and torsos scarred. Even during his Japan days, though, Doug quickly learned the art of playing to the crowd. In late 1999, Doug Troy came back to America to ply his learned skills in a place where hyped-up Japanese announcers didn’t scream “LARIATOOOOOOOOO!” He quickly found a spot in the ACW, a small indy fed known for its quirkyness and brutal hardcore matches. It was there that he joined the stable known as DAMN, a decision that would factor very heavily into his later career. After the ACW folded in early 2000, Troy bounced from federation to federation, each one folding soon after he got there. Convinced that he was an inadvertent death sentence upon any established fed that he joined, he joined the fledgling Primetime Championship Wrestling after being approached by Professor Herbert Dorkiman. Doug reached the apex of his e-wrestling career in November of 2000, when he beat Meanstreak and Ryan Knox in the same match to claim the PCW World and Extreme titles, respectively. After losing his titles to the Preacher is one of the more interesting screwjobs in PCW history, DT fell into a slump that he didn’t get out of until February of 2001, when he entered the World Title scene as a dark horse and went into Shanghaied a serious contender for Reverend Jon Hull’s World Title. Alas, it wasn’t meant to be on that night, as the Media formed and won’t up costing Doug the title as he had the pin on Hull. From that point on, DT seemed to be a shadow of his former self. This and the nagging injuries that he suffered would soon spell the end for his career. In November of 2001, he finally retired from wrestling. DT lived in the shadows for a while, rehabbing his injuries and getting his affairs in order. In December, DT was contacted by former PCW Personnel Director Jane Reagan to be the color commentator for OSW. DT quickly accepted, having grown bored of life without wrestling. DT sat at the announcer table, ready to deliver his verbal barbs. The job lasted for one whole week. After an attack on the recently signed Ivan Stanislav, Doug was signed to OSW as a full-time wrestler and replaced by Robbie Decker on commentary. Doug faced both Ivan and the newly signed Meanstreak at the first OSW PPV, Crowning Glory, and surprisingly left as the winner of the match. Doug and Ivan crossed paths many more times over the next couple months, culminating in Doug's defeat by Ivan in a #1 contendership match. While Ivan went on to Head On Collision as the #1 contender, Doug was thrust into a matter of large importance for OSW: an invading group of masked wrestlers from PCW. At Head On Collision, Doug teamed with fellow superstars Jesse Ramey and Clay Vendetta, as well as long-time veteren and OSW chief of security Moose Malone, against the invading quartet. The OSW wrestlers prevailed, and Doug soared in the huge fan response right into his State of OSW Address, wherein he railed against management. His attacks on OSW management continued, heading up with a tirade against OSW World Champion Sebastian Toomes. After a #1 contenders match against Andrew 'Ace' Hawk, Doug had a date with destiny. Unlike his previous date with destiny at the first PCW Shanghaied, Doug came out on top in the end and took home the OSW World Heavyweight Championship. Doug wants to be known as a proud champion, a champion for the fans, and is willing to take on all comers. How long can the new champion hold on to his belt? Only time will tell.
