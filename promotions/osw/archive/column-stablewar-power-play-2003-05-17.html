Stablewar: Power Play

17 May 2003

by Nate Dumas

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is The OSE site's own column (#6) by Nate Dumas, dated 17 May 2003, from the OSW site as it stood in the Wayback capture.
Stablewar: Power Play Well OSW fans, my latest column dealt with the analyzing of The Asylum, and to continue that trend I will be mapping out the four members of Power Play, the rag tag group of technicians that want to gain as much power as they possibly can. Dave Gibson Info: Height: 6’1” Weight: 228 lbs. Style: Technical/Submission/Old School Brawling Title History: OSW Tag Team Champions (w/ Clay Vendetta) Awards:None He entered the OSW as “Old School” Dave Gibson and his mantra, “Shut up and wrestle” was heard loud and clear. Tired of the Graphic Violence and the Sebastian Toomes’ of the OSW, Dave Gibson was a scrapper with a cause. Then, just as suddenly as his entrance, he changed gears and became “Sell Out” Dave Gibson. Now, he leads Power Play against The Asylum in an attempt to wipe them out, and take as much power as he can. Dave Gibson is the personification of Power Play. Style While “Old School” may not be in his name, that doesn’t mean that his style has completely changed. Dave Gibson knows the power of a technical background and is versatile in it. He can stand up toe to toe with any other technician in the OSW but instead opts for the quicker, blunter, more illegal form of attack now. When push comes to shove, however, Gibson has proven that he can use his repertoire to his advantage when the time comes. Strengths Smart: Not that other members aren’t intelligent, but Dave Gibson is very smart in the ring. Gibson can read opponents, allies, and situations like a book and make relatively intelligent decisions based upon the facts. Resourceful Traditionalist: Dave Gibson has an old school background, something that many wrestlers don’t pay attention to. The high flying and flashy moves of today may make the fans cheer, but tried and true methods of wrestling will never fail. But Gibson adds more than just being an old school wrestler, he’s always got a trick up his sleeve as well. Group Fighter: Dave Gibson once was part of the tag team “The Throwbacks” and went from being relatively unknown in the OSW to being a major contender and then champion of the OSW Tag Team Titles. His operation in that team was flawless and it shows that he’s able to work alongside anyone he needs to and in a slew of situations. Weaknesses Two Faced: Dave Gibson has turned his back on his allies several times. Who is to say that he won’t do this yet again whenever the going gets tough for Power Play? Misled: I won’t lie. Dave Gibson has talent. He has a lot of it. But he’s bogged down in his cheating ways and it’s holding him back. If “Old School” would return, he’d kick ass again and stick to his strength: wrestling. But “Sell Out” is walking the wrong path, plain and simple. Ming Info: Height: 6’6” Weight: 225 lbs. Style: Martial Arts/Lucha Title History: None Awards: None Veteran WO wrestler, Ming has fought a multitude of matches. Going from the PCW to the FSW and now finding his home in the OSW, there’s no doubt that Ming has what it takes to remain a contender in this federation. His screwball antics are funny at times, but behind the façade of Ming is a warrior, plain and simple. Ming is the clown prince of Power Play. Style While he is a decent technician like the rest of Power Play, Ming excels in martial arts and his lucha libre style of fighting. This is a good thing, because too many technicians would seriously injure the stable as a whole. Ming brings a great dynamic to Power Play’s overall fighting style. Strengths Seasoned: Yes, Ming is a turkey, but he’s been through a lot as well. This is the third organization that he’s worked for, and he’s worked with and against many different types of people This’ll help him in the long run of this stable. Stable Player: Stable fighting is no new thing to Ming. He was a part of the Asian stable “The Elements” back in the PCW and operated quite well in that capacity. He was also part of Mob Rule in the FSW. He too knows how to be a team player. Commie Fighter: Ming was in the PCW when the first incarnation of The Red Army ran roughshod through the federation. And it was Ivan Stanislav with Boda that headed that attack as well. He knows the Red Army’s tactics, and he knows what they stand for. That’s a big asset to Power Play. Weaknesses Clown Prince: Ming’s antics are funny, and they’re amusing, but they mainly get him into trouble. His focus sometimes is more on himself than the group, and that can hurt himself and the image of Power Play. Wiry: Ming breaks the mold of being short for a Chinaman, but he doesn’t when it comes to being lightweight. At 6’6”/225 lbs, Ming is a tall, lanky fellow. In the heat of a battle, he runs the risk of being knocked clean out of the park! Mike Marvelous Info: Height: 6’3” Weight: 254 lbs. Style: Technical/Submission/Blatant Cheating Title History: None Awards: Voted Sexiest Man in OSW by Female Fans Mike Marvelous has been in the PCW and the FSW as well as being part of the OSW for a brief stint early in its history. A delusional wrestler to the end and arrogant to boot, Mike Marvelous thinks he’s a gift to humanity. He’s sadly mistaken. Mike Marvelous is the voice of Power Play. Style Mike Marvelous is the largest member of Power Play, tipping the scales at a still trim 254 lbs. Like the majority of his stablemates, Mike employs cheating, technical ability, and submission tactics to get the job done. One difference between himself and his friends, however, is that no matter what he has a smile on his face that you always want to wipe off with a good punch. Strengths Confidence: Marvelous likes to shoot off his mouth and boast, but that’s not really a bad thing. At least he has faith in himself. Like him or hate him, you can’t deny that he trusts himself. Delusional: In his own mind, Marvelous believes everything he tells himself is true. They say if you lie to yourself enough, you’ll take it as true. It seems that this is a fact in Mike’s case. So if he really thinks something happened, and he convinces himself of it, why hesitate? Size: Marvelous is a larger submission wrestler, by Power Play’s standards. He can mix some power moves in with his impressive technical arsenal and really inflict damage. Weaknesses Strike Out: Marvelous’ record is stricken with missed chances. In PCW he disappeared. In OSW he died out. And now it appears to be happening again. As much of a mouthpiece as he is, that can’t be good for morale of Power Play. Ego Coming Through: Marvelous has a huge ego. In a stable, egos must be kept in check so that all are comfortable with one another. Will Marvelous let his ego slide so that others can share his spotlight? “Exceptional” Dave Hurst Info: Height: 6’2” Weight: 200 lbs. Style: Technical/Submission Title History: None Awards: Defeated OSW World Champ to gain OSW Contract “Exceptional” Dave Hurst may call himself “Exceptional,” but a lot of words could describe him. Idiotic, insane, delusional, inane, crazy, and foolhardy. But there’s no denying that he did fight his way into the OSW and has rightfully earned a spot here.Dave Hurst is the pride of Power Play. Style EDH is built for submission and technical wrestling. He’s compact, quick, smart, and deadly. He’s mastered his trade and it shows in his matches. Sometimes leaping before he looks, EDH has fallen into his own blunders in the past. In a stable war, the chances of getting hurt are enhanced tenfold. But he’ll keep fighting. That’s what he does. Strengths Technician: All members of Power Play are technicians, but not as focused as Dave Hurst. He doesn’t hit the top rope, or the power moves, or brawl. No, EDH knows where he’s strong at and he remains there. Annoying: You would think that this would go as a weakness, but I think not. EDH is simply annoying. He grates on my nerves and I’m sure he does for a lot of other people too. That leads the enemies into a false sense of security, however, for EDH may be annoying, but he’s also very dangerous. Learning More and More: It’s true, EDH fought his way into the OSW and since then, he’s learned a lot of tricks. Being probably the most inexperienced member of Power Play, he’ll learn a lot from Gibson especially… but is it a good lesson to be learned? Weaknesses Small: EDH is small as well, and that can hurt him. Barely hitting 200 lbs, he doesn’t have a lot of stopping power. He may be able to hurt wrestlers of his size with his submission skills, but a leglock or armbar on the likes of Azala Zameer or Ivan Stanislav is going to do nothing except make him look stupid. How Exceptional?: Considering that Dave Hurst is relatively new to the world of wrestling, just how exceptional is he? He may think he is, but if he tries to spout off the stuff he thinks in front of a true veteran, he’s going to get a big piece of humble pie for his trouble! Closing Remarks Well OSW fans, that’s about it for Power Play. They may be a bit one sided in terms of technical skill, but they are no doubt crafty and experienced. My next and final analysis will be The Reds themselves, The Red Army! Nate Dumas
