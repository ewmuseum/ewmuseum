Advanced FAQ

20 June 2003

What this is The OSE site's own the Advanced FAQ: applications, roleplaying, segments, matches and angles, from the OSE main site as captured in the Wayback on 20 June 2003.
Applications Q: Are applications open? A: OSE Applications are always open, however we are not always hiring. What this means is we keep all apps on file and contact the applicants when spots open up. Q: How do I apply as a tag team? A: There is a tag team application form up just underneath the singles form under the Info tab on the site. Q: Why is the Application so Big and do I have to fill it all in? A: Because Old School Entertainment is an established fed we have a pretty stable roster. While that’s great for the members it means as a person wanting to join it takes more to get accepted then it might in other feds. The application form is your chance to impress us so we make it big and long to give you that chance. Q: How long does it take to get a response back from my application? A: You will get an auto-response with in the first five minutes of submitting your application. This is to let you know that your app was received. The process we use for reviewing applicants varies on several things which I’ll get into later. We keep every app on file and will only respond to apps when we have an open spot, but if you don’t get a response back to begin with you could get one a week later, a month later or even further down the road. Q: Why don’t you respond to apps and let people know they are on the waiting list? A: We don’t inform people when they are on the waiting list because we feel it’s not fair that someone sit and wait for a spot in OSE when they could be having fun in another fed. Rest assured even if you go to another fed, if a spot does open up you would still be offered the spot then and it would be your choice if you wanted to accept it or not then. Q: Is there a set limit for roster size? A: No, there is not a set number that we’ll never go above, when hiring new characters a large factor in the decision to hire is how would they fit into the fed, with the current angles and storylines. That being said we limit the roster size to a number that we feel is appropriate at the current time (Not more then we can keep everyone active with enough TV Time). Q: What does Character Exclusivity mean? A: It means that your character can only be in OSE. This does not restrict you as a handler to only the one fed, but just the character being used here. Q: Why do you have a Character Exclusivity Policy? A: OSE operates with a certain degree of realism. WWE wrestlers don’t appear on other federations’ shows unless there are special circumstances and OSE works on that principle as well. Q: Can I apply as a wrestler and a fighter? A: You certainly can but you could not be accepted with both. The only way one handler can handle more then one character in the fed is if the two characters are a member of a tag team. Q: Can I switch my characters and stay in the fed? A: You can switch your characters but you would have to apply with the new character and be accepted with that character. Q: Can I form a stable? A: Certainly but you need to have it approved by a booker. Q: Can I bring in a character to be a tag team partners? A: Once again this can be done with the approval of the bookers. Q: Can my character jump from the fighting division to the wrestling division or vice versa? A: For special occasions this could happen, but if you are hired to one division you should expect to stay in that division. Roleplaying Q: How often do you have to roleplay? A: In OSE you roleplay to further your stories, sometimes you will find that you are posting a roleplay every couple of days and other times you may go two or three weeks between posting. Roleplays are secondary to the stuff that occurs on the cards and there is no set limit. Q: Is there a maximum number of roleplays I can post in a day? A: You can post as many roleplays as you like as long as they either further your angle or further your character development. Q: What happens if I don’t Roleplay? A: If you are still active with segments on cards you’ll be fine, if you’ve stopped doing both, it is likely that you will be released. Q: How long do I have to be inactive before I get fired? A: Generally if you go inactive for a couple weeks we try to contact you, if you don’t respond we’ll play out any angles with your character and depending on the length of time you’ve spent in the fed have your character injured or released. Q: Will roleplaying move me up the card? A: It’s not the only determining factor but it will definitely help you get noticed and help your chances of being pushed. Q: How do I post a roleplay? A: Members have a backstage login, from this area they can post their roleplays, post news, rumors, submit matches and segments for the cards and update there roster pages and a whole lot more. Q: Do Roleplays have to be in a specific format? A: Yes they are to be written in a novel format. Q: What is Novel Format? A: “An example would be something like this,” Jeff said as he type up the FAQ. Segments Q: What is a segment? A: A segment occurs on a card, such as Slam or Assault and is simply put an in card roleplay. These could include but are not limited to, backstage and in ring interviews or interactions with other characters, brief stories to push the feud and just about anything done on a show. Q: Is there a limit for the number of segments I can use on one show? A: No, there are no limits. Q: Do Segments determine how high up on the card I am? A: To an extent, but they are not the only determining factor. Q: Do Segments need to be in a specific format? A: Yes, Any segment that appears on a show needs to be done in Script format. Q: What is Script Format? A: Jeff: This is script format. Matches Q: Who books the matches for each show? A: To a certain extent the handlers themselves do. The bookers are there to approve the matches and fill out a card with extra matches if needed. Q: How do I win a match? A: Winning a match should not be that important, if it is then you are better suited for a roleplay fed instead of an angle fed. Match winners are often determined by the handlers involved to fit with their feud plan, in cases that a winner is picked by the bookers, there are many factors that determine who will be picked. Q: Who writes the matches? A: 9 out of 10 times the handlers involved in the matches will write them to best do their angle justice. Q: Do matches need to be written in a specific format? A: Yes, matches should be written in present tense, script format and have commentary. Angles Q: What is an angle? A: An angle is the reason we are here and playing the game. In a sense it is basically a story told between two or more people in the Sports Entertainment Industry. Q: Who makes up the angles? A: The handlers involved come up with the angles and then bring them to the bookers in a feud map to be approved. Once approved the bookers use the map to book the proper matches and it’s the handlers’ responsibilities to play out the angle. Q: Can I use anyone in my angles? A: There are many staff characters that can be used in angles pending the approval from one of the bookers, on certain cases real life celebrities could be used in angles if a booker approves it. Q: Do angles determine how high up on the card I am? A: Yes, it’s not the only factor but how you work your angles and your reliability in working them will determine how much of a push you receive.
