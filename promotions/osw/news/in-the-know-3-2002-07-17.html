In the Know #3

17 July 2002

by Nate Dumas Hey there OSW fans!! Welcome to another edition of In the Know with yours truly, Nate Dumas. Its been a wild ride since Ascension with all kinds of ups and downs on both sides here in OSW, with faces coming and going at a mind boggling pace. The Vax/Think Tank Despite the facts they are the bane of everything that Old School Wrestling stands for, you have to look upon this just say “wow” at the level of talent in this group, from top to bottom. First and foremost, you have core of one of the top stables in PCW history, The Think Tank, with “The Scholar” Howard Porter, Alan Kriegman and Professor Herbert D. Dorkiman reunited again. Couple that trio with the individual talents of what internet smarks are calling the “Hired Guns” featuring two time PCW World Heavyweight Champion, Trashcan Man, the gargantuan John Mammoth and the man who has been banned from every promotion on the face of the earth, Armour, and what you have is, perhaps, the greatest array of allied talent ever to grace a wrestling promotion. Yes, they are better than New Breed. Yes, they are better than Asylum. Yes, they are better than even Tyler Nelson’s almighty Media. If Porter, PHD and Kriegman can keep the “Hired Guns” in check, the Think Tank will be dominating, and perhaps ruining, OSW for months to come. fWo Influx? With the closing of the Fans Wrestling Organization, OSW workers were as nervous as fans were excited at the possibility of out-of-work fWo superstars making their way to OSW. As of now it hasn’t happened, but I can tell you that it will not be that way for long as I have it on good authority that OSW has inked at least two former fWo workers to contracts. That’s right folks! Two fWo superstars are on their way to OSW!!! I say, woopty-frickin’ do! All these guys can do is upset the already delicate balance of the OSW locker room atmosphere, which has been surprisingly good so far. No matter how these guys debut, whether it be main eventing, or jerking the curtain to open events, egos are going to flare, and I for one see little or no need for it. Our ratings are up, our talent is happy, and our fans are pleased. Sure, sure, I can see the appeal of adding some big name workers to the mix, but at best they will add a short term spike in ratings, ticket sales (which are already at 95% of capacity) and merchandise sales, but these guys will need to do a bit more than live off their name in order to have any sustained, positive effect. On the flip side of the coin, it only takes one bad booking move involving new blood for a negative backlash from both fans and OSW workers alike. Overall, my opinion is OSW officials are gambling from a position where they already held all the chips, and that, my friends, is a mistake. The Cult This group, despite its stereotypical demonic goth themes, intrigues me, as both Hessian and Vampir Nosferatu are impressive individuals. Add to their massive statures a dark charisma and blessings from Ole’ Scratch himself, and these guys are a force to be reckoned with. I expect this group will grow to be a major force in OSW. They have already added one “Cultist” to their ranks, and I expect many more to follow. Grading the New Blood Over the past several weeks, several newcomers have joined the OSW ranks, some good, some not so good. Below is my take on each individual, and their grade thus far. “Old School” Dave Gibson has been used perfectly so far, and his pairing with Clay Vendetta was bright booking. OSW hasn’t pushed the former jOlt and IOW worker to fast, and the sky appears to be the limit for this gritty, well-liked professional. Grade: B+ Sepiroth Du Luc has had a bit of trouble getting going here in OSW, but its not his fault. Being paired with a couple of legends-turned-duds like Griff Truxell and Gregor Xaxon hasn’t exactly done much for his work rate. Despite his opponent’s short SDL, who had reportedly been a spotty working in the past, has carried himself well and OSW officials are pleased with what they see. Grade: B- Scott Taylor has been good in his role as the dark to Kid Wonder’s light. More importantly, he has helped elevate Kid Wonder’s game, which is something that OSW officials are pleased with, because a happy and hardworking Kid Wonder is a ratings boon for the company. Grade: B Armour is little more than a thug who likes to hurt people. He doesn’t belong in a wrestling promotion, and frankly, Howard Porter should be ashamed for bringing this guy in. He is a danger to himself, his opponents, and the fans at ringside. Grade: B+ for being what was expected of him by Porter. Porter gets a big, fat F for bringing this Neanderthal in here. Most Extreme Team Competition Boy, this has be a bust so far. Half the teams are gone after Task Two, and frankly none of the teams that are left interest me. The two teams I felt had the most potential, The Faculty and Adam and Eve are gone. All About the Benjamins do nothing for me, nor does Vice Versa, who look like they belong doing modern interpretive dance on Public Television. The Black Dragons are stereotypes, and the American Ronin, while impressive, really aren’t a tag team as much as they are two talented individuals working as a dysfunctional team. The problem is, OSW is full of talented individuals already. Do we really need lackluster tag teams clogging up the roster when we could be signing better talent or developing more teams from within, like the Cult and the Throwbacks? Exactly. Well, that’s all for this edition of In the Know. See you soon! Nate Dumas
