Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

American Wrestling Alliance announced itself on 21 September 2007, in the voice of its own newsdesk: an owners committee, a temporary site up while a contract with an un-named Internet Media Development company went through, a signed-roster page and a route by which a prospect could send in footage to be judged. Over the fortnight that followed the same newsdesk reported cable negotiations stalling with Comcast, talks resuming with TBS Superstation over a Sunday prime-time slot, a Canadian network already agreed to carry its shows, and a listing taken out on a wrestling top-sites board.

The wrestlers arrived the same way. Rush was announced as its first signing, then a second man who had asked to speak to one of the co-owners directly and whose name the newsdesk withheld. Six more — ToK, Johnny Legend, Brock Metzer, El Phantasmo, Astro Creep and Juan Valdez — were signed to a pay-per-appearance deal for the first show. Ninja K, billed as a formerly globally known wrestler, signed off an unnamed approach to the offices. Nixx came in as a road agent to work with the younger wrestlers on ring work and interviews. Its own application page asked a newcomer for a ring name, an alignment, a height, a weight, a background, a sample roleplay and a sample match; its backstage section put each application in front of the membership for a yes or a no.

The first card ran at 10:00am on 6 October 2007 at the National Guard Armory in Lawrenceville, Illinois, general admission five dollars and front row ten, before a crowd its own transcript puts at 276. Five men in casual clothes opened it, Carl Lucas — billed as the former Lusus — speaking for the ownership group and cutting himself short because the crowd had come to see wrestling. Johnny Legend beat ToK with a bridged full nelson suplex and was powerbombed by Greatness before he could finish celebrating. Half Sliced and Rush beat a three-man team billed out of Mexico and Columbia, Rush taking the fall with a frog splash. Mid-show Carl Lucas announced a first bi-monthly supercard, The Conclusion 2007, for 25 November, and Ninja K walked out behind him. Matt Fury closed the night by blocking Brock Metzer's Independent Clash and hitting three consecutive powerbombs, in a match announced as a qualifier for the Heavyweight Championship tournament.

Two championships were described on their own pages and neither was ever awarded: a Heavyweight Championship called the centre piece of the promotion, and three Trios Tag Team Championships with a rule set of their own — a partner who leaves the ring is replaced without a tag, breaking up a pinfall is legal, and the team that breaks one up then has eight seconds to run a move together. Six supercard brands were drawn up as logos. The tournament that would have crowned a champion, the supercard announced for November, and how American Wrestling Alliance ended are not in the surviving record.
