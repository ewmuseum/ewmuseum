Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Hosted on a Hong Kong homepage and billed in its own later posts as the Asia Wrestling Association, the promotion blended original wrestlers with real-world ones under their own ring names from its earliest surviving document. Its 6 May 1998 pay-per-view result headlines “Unbelieveable that Kojiro win the match”: Tiger Kojiro won a no-time-limit World Title triangle match over Lawrence Shamrock and Stun the Zvil, on the same card as a tag match pairing the real Undertaker and an original wrestler, Politika, against Shawn Michael and the Mad Bomber.

By December 1998 the promotion describes itself growing “more and more” toward becoming, in its own words, “like a real fed”: a PPV called “Slaughter in the Hell” ended Dream King's four-month Heavyweight Championship reign, crowning Marvelous Mike in his place.

A Chinese New Year pay-per-view, a Battle Royal held 16 February 1999 in Hong Kong, carried five more title matches: Bob Peil took the Asian Championship from Canadian Thunder; Attitude, Inc. kept the Light Heavyweight title by disqualification after a prank — a “red pocket” gift that turned out to hold a condom instead of money — drew in an ally, Big Poppa Pump; and an I-Quit match pitted Lawrence Shamrock against Shane Jeckins and Ironbreaker under a stipulation that a losing challenger stays only if he also wins the Battle Royal itself.

A first-anniversary post, 25 May 1999, marks roughly a year of operation and describes its own owner as active in e-wrestling since May 1997: by this point the promotion runs two weekly cards, “Thunder Challenge” on Wednesdays and “RAW” on weekends, plus a monthly pay-per-view, across a Heavyweight, Intercontinental, Asian, Tag Team and Hardcore championship alongside a women's division, with match outcomes decided entirely by the quality of a competitor's own guestbook roleplay.

The last surviving episode, “Saturday Night Slaughter,” broadcast 12 September 1999 live from Hong Kong Polytechnic University, carries a tag title defense by Dream King and Big Shot over Jagpreet Ghuman and Hitkid and builds a feud between two brothers, Jack and Kerry Morris, toward a confrontation the surviving record does not reach.

No later document or closing notice survives past that September 1999 broadcast.
