 Members: This is tutorial for those of you who are having trouble, who are interested in improving, or are simply interested in what I have to say about how to roleplay. These are my views and my opinions. With that said, I would like to communicate to you that I have been playing this game for 7 years, all of them handling and fed-heading more than once. So, I believe that I am an authority on the subject. This is simply to put information at your disposal on roleplaying. So, that's my introduction, now some information which will be followed by sample roleplays from characters that I feel are very good and perform at the level I one day hope to have as a the standard in the GWC. I will also include several of my own roleplays that are from recently. Finally, I will give you a URL for the Keening School of Grappling Arts. This is a website set up by Patrick Calnan. He is a handler and a man I had the privilege of "wrestling" for. It has tutorial on many e-wrestling related topics, roleplaying including. I encourage you to not only explore the site's roleplaying resources, but the entire site. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> ~ My Experience-Based Roleplaying Guide ~ <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> As previously mentioned, I have handled for the 7 years I have been playing the game. I have been moderately successful. I feel that I am a good roleplayer. By no means am I great and by no means do I expect you to feel the same. All I'm asking as that you strive to be good. If you have the drive by no means am I asking you not to be great. If you have it in you, it would be a great experience for everyone in the Grand Wrestling Corp. to experience a great character and a great handler. Moving on, I feel that the key to a good character is good roleplaying. The keys to good roleplaying are in my opinion these: -- Creativity. Don't bore people. You want them to read your work, not skip over it to get to the one they care about. You want your character to get noticed by your fed head so that you may be advanced...put in matches that will get you ahead. -- Always Your Best. From the get, you need to give your all, all the time. First impressions are everything, you've been hearing that your whole lives. When you enter a new fed, you want to get noticed, you want to get on the top of the heap and be successful in staying there. But first, you must get noticed. Put your best effort forward in YOUR FIRST ROLEPLAY. Don't wait, because a fed head will pay attention to a characters initial roleplays more so than the future. You get labeled by a fed head and it's much harder to break away from that label after you've already been branded. Get it? -- Originality. Don't be repetitive, don't send your fed head the same roleplay week after week. Again, boredom sets in. You want to stay fresh, stay interesting, and stay winning. All of these things are attained by the combination of all the aspects I have and will mention, along with originality. -- Roleplay regularly. Don't be stingy. Roleplaying when you should is far better than roleplaying when you feel like it or when you have time. If you don't have time, you shouldn't be playing. -- Intelligence. Ignorance will be your downfall. Do not be ignorant in your roleplaying. Constant cursing, small insignificant roleplaying where you simply say "I'm gonna kick your butt and that's the bottom line 'cause I said so!" will get you nowhere. Handlers will tear you apart if you go this route. Be smarter than this. -- Spelling and grammar. This is somewhat related to intelligence, however in an out of character manner. If you send a roleplay out meant to be good, meant to have impact and you misspell three worlds per sentence, your opposition will jump all over you. More importantly, you look bad as a handler and your character looks just as bad. I suppose if you are somehow incorporating these errors into a character and they are intentional (such as a hillbilly high-school dropout who married his cousin) then it's acceptable. PROOF READ. -- Content. Content is very important. Don't just roleplay for the sake of roleplaying. Have a purpose. Don't be repetitive. Say it once, say it the best way you can, get your point across and STOP. Quality over quantity is the golden rule of roleplaying. Embracing this will bring you success. In closing, let loose, be creative, and most importantly...HAVE FUN WITH IT!!! This is a game that you are devoting your free time too, so don't spend it being frustrated, mad. It blossomed into something so utterly debilitating that it drove him to his knees, the air from his lungs, the thoughts from his brain. It was unbelievably unbearable. Wild-eyed, with his red-black hair blazing around his head, he stared around. He could taste the blood on his tongue. He thought it was his. The blood that blossomed at the back of his skull was his. He didn&#8217;t even feel it as he fell. And awoke, a scream on his lips, blood on his tongue. He was still in jail, he knew, as his eyes frantically rolled like a wild animals in his skull. The dream, of championship gold and fame and money was just that. Just a dream. This was reality- the cell, the cage, the torture. The cell was dark, no light, he couldn't even see the bars. He flew up out of bed, ready to lash out at anyone there. No one was there. The room was empty. Sanity returned. He wasn't in jail. He was out on parole. It wasn't a dream; it was reality. At times he couldn't distinguish the two, especially when the nightmares haunted his sleep. It was just a dream, more of a memory than a dream, to be honest. He could remember the moment vividly, just like in the dream: the insult given so easily, the ensuing fight, the guards blaming him, and the solitary for the next week. Azrael Ravenell, known to many as Shadow Fire, began to pace. He knew sleep wouldn't return. So he sat, feet brushing lightly against the smooth, hard floor. He reached to the night stand and quickly lit a cigarette, contemplating a shot of Jack. It was a little late. He probably shouldn't. He made up his mind and stood, watching the smoke curl in front of him. He needed that shot of Jack.
