Now, after [name withheld] finally woke up, and realized that L.A.W will haunt him forever until he ends it appropriately, he brings it back with plans of innovating wrestling once more. What is to come? Well, the first time L.A.W runs a show, and the first league in Los Angeles to gain popularity. The show is Sandblast '96, and takes place on the Santa Monica Bay. The main event, features big time stars Eric Cooper, Tommy Rifles, Submarine, and James Pillow all competing in a Bunkhouse Battle Royal for the Heavyweight strap. The undercard featured an eray of unknown talent, and was probably one of the most unrecognized show in that year. But what onlookers, and locker room participants, didn't expect, was a revolutionary league. Los Angeles Wrestling, or LAW for short, started on the AOL-Fed outlook, but bloomed into a 'net fed, and brought in King Apples, Andrew Tucker, Eric Cooper, James Pillow, Johnny Glamour, Scott Jamison, Tim Beck, and several other stars who showcased their stuff, and broke the LAW. [name withheld], I, was indeed pleased with the contracts, and money arriving. However, a downfall accured. Several stars were heading back to college, and getting very busy, unable to compete in LAW anymore. The LAW was broken, and possibly unfixable. After several attempts at a return, and failed attempts at other promotions, I, had what you call, a mental breakdown. I shutdown, flew up to a cabin in the far sea's, and stood away, until finally reviving and entering the GSWP to start managing. Since then, I have been waiting for a chance to bring back the beloved LAW, and I feel it's as good a time as any. The LAW firmly believes that it can Thrill you, Shock you, and make you stand up and chant "L A W, L A W" all night long. So put those hands up, and make the floor shake, because we're gonna Break... The... LAW! 1.5 TELECASTS When the L.A.W opened up, they had a show: WRESTLEWARS, which was an underground TV show, based only on one TV network, KCAL Channel 9 at 12:00am. But now, with a budget of Thirty-Million Dollars, the L.A.W will travel around the California Border with BREAKDOWN. Our main goal, is to provide something different from HARD-CORE style. We want to broaden our horizons, and provide key wrestling matches, that fans will enjoy, and hopefully tune in every Saturday Night at Ten Thirty (10:30) on local cable access stations across the California Border. Check with your local cable company for more info. Also, on Mondays, the L.A.W presents RING REPORT, where we take a look at current standings, taped matches from House Shows, and updates on current angles. On Wednesdays and Fridays, we present L.A.W RINGSIDE, where the L.A.W's talent mouths off to us about their opponents and other standings. 1.6 MATCH SYSTEM On RING REPORT, Jeremy Stone and John Kelly will host the show, and present matches from House Shows, throughout the month, in edition to other things. He allowed me to use it for my last fed I attempted to run. IIWF Forver... How to set up matches If you wish to challenge another wrestler to a match, you may do so through the Ringside Report (See TV Shows.). However, in order to ensure that the match is booked, and to confirm the card on which it will appear, you must contact the President with your challenge and ask for the match to be booked. How to set up angles The best angles are those which involve more than one handler working together to form a coherent, exciting storyline with a few twists. If you would like your wrestler to be programmed with somebody else in the L.A.W, try and conceive of an innovative way to get the attention of the wrestler in question, and it never hurts to contact the handler of the prospective ally or enemy directly. All the handlers' email addresses will be available after the month of May or you can email the President and he will send you a list. Ground Rules For Role-playing Nobody wants to stop handlers from having fun in the L.A.W, but it's important that the following ground rules are understood and taken into consideration out of respect both for the wishes of the fed head and other handlers: Don't: Don't ask for your wrestler to hospitalize another wrestler. Sneak attacks are fine, but attacks with barbed wire baseball bats or double-barreled shotguns will not be accepted. It's unfair on the handler of the victim to have his wrestler laid up in hospital and miss out on action in the ring without his consent. Don't attack announcers in your flashes. Unless you have the express permission of the fedhead, don't have your wrestler brutalize any of the L.A.W's staff, with the exception of the numerous jobbers, although you should still respect the above rule about hospitalization. Don't use excessive profanity. Often it is realistic and within reason for wrestlers to use obscene language in moments of extreme emotion. However, words stronger than "ass", "bastard", "hell", "crap", "asshole", "son of a bitch", "shit" or "bloody" will usually be edited out of flashes, because such language is contrary to what is acceptable in everyday speech and on the majority of television programs. It is still possible to express anger or discontent without recourse to a string of four-letter words. Don't submit three long flashes a week. The primary forum for flashes is the weekly Ringside report, which gives every wrestler in the league a chance to be heard. Short flashes will be accepted for the Monday show (Ring Report.) and the weekly card, but only if they fulfill one or both of these two criteria: firstly, they should build heat for current storylines, and not simply babble on badmouthing every other wrestler in the league; and secondly, they should relate to the wrestler's match on that card (if the wrestler does not have a match, the flash is unlikely to be used in any case, and will be held over until the next Ringside report). Don't take things too seriously. Every once in a while, some handlers seem to forget that fantasy wrestling is only a game, and heat generated between characters can rub off into real life. If you are unhappy with the way things are going, send a message to the fedhead explaining your concerns. You will always be met with a prompt and reasonable response. Don't pass on the mailing list. Most L.A.W handlers might not appreciate receiving junk mail, or invitations to join other leagues. Kindly refrain from spamming the L.A.W's mailing list. Do: Do take an active interest in your wrestler. Your wrestler is more likely to succeed if you follow his progress and not only submit flashes, but also keep the fed head apprised of developments in his character, successes elsewhere and so on. Do send in strategies for individual matches. One of the most effective and useful forms of roleplaying can be sending in a strategy for a particular opponent. You might be surprised at how much of an effect this can have on the match; strategies give the writers a more detailed impression of the character, and lead to a more "realistic" depiction in the ring. Do ask for matches. As the rosters become more and more crowded, the only way to ensure that your character is noticed is to ask the fedhead to book matches for you. If you sit back and expect to be given title shots or a push, you'll be disappointed. Activity is essential. What makes a good flash? There are no rules about what makes a good flash, but several guidelines should be considered: Keep the angle interesting to all members of the L.A.W and develop your character(s). Remember that many people read the reports. The best way to ensure future feuds is by making sure that others know about and are interested in your wrestler(s). Be creative. Experiment with new settings to add to the personality of your wrestler(s). Longer flashes are not necessarily better flashes. Make sure your wrestler(s) get his/their point across and do not repeat it endlessly. Overkill forces readers to skip flashes. Flash regularly to maintain interest in angles and your wrestler(s). Handlers will be required to submit at least one item, be it a promo, interview or news item, for each of their wrestlers at least once a week. Consistent roleplays is essential. Keep flashes free of profanity and swearing. Think of the L.A.W as sports entertainment and bear in mind the limits under which actual wrestlers must operate on television. Strong language, if submitted to the report, will be edited. 2.0 [APPLICATION FORM(s)] Special Thanks to [name withheld] and [name withheld] for allowing me to use their
