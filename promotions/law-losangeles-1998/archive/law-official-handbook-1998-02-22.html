Roleplaying Tips [Special Thanks to Kevin Riley for his permission] Some of you more experienced e-wrestlers out there have probably seen sections similar to this in other e-wrestling handbooks, but this is a little bit different. And for all you newbies, it'd be wise to read through this before continuing on into the LAW. I'm sure you're wondering by this point, "How do I succeed in Los Angeles Wrestling?", well it's simple. There are several ways to reach the top of LAW, and here they are ... A. Flash or Roleplay A flash or a roleplay, is a way your wrestler may speak his mind. It is written up by you alone and can be any type of interview, commentary or 'video production' in which you describe the setting and your wrestler speaks using dialogue. If your wrestler prides himself on his work on the mic, then this is your ticket to sucess. If you aren't sure what a roleplay or RP is, well I'll fill you in now. Roleplaying can be easy. The main goals of it are to stay true to character, be descriptive, and to get your point across with out babbling. In the LAW, your flashes are judged by quality, not quantity. I know, alot of feds say this, but Los Angeles Wrestling means it. You want to try and RP so that you keep your audience interested. No one wants to read endless flashes that go on and on. B. Characters Before entering a league, it's a good idea to ask for a few cards to see what else is out there. It gives you a feel of what type of people are out there and will prevent two people from running characters with the exact same names or gimmicks. Do we really need 5 Undertaker clones running around? Before you pick a gimmick, here's a few things to consider : * Can I effectively run this character? Is this the kind of person I would be interested in writing for? For example, I once came up with a Russian Olympian type character. I had moves and an outfit and everything ... then I had to write interviews and came to the realization ... I couldn't come up with a believable stuff for this guy to say because I didn't know Russian and couldn't fake an accent. * Is this character believable? I personally like characters that are realistic. A guy who magically disappears or other such crap can really ruin the game. A human element helps certain characters. People have a tendency to go overboard when pushing a character (usually applies to heels) and then you get McMahon syndrome. Like a guy who was supposedly a drug dealer like character or a nuclear mutant suddenly becomes a face. Yeah, he was eating children yesterday, but now he's a fine upstanding individual! * Be careful with comedy gimmicks. They can be good and bad. Some guys can write comedy, others can't. Also, what you might think is funny, others might consider really lame. And in case you're wondering, I hated Doink (as a heel and face.) As a wise man once said "Dying is easy, comedy is hard." * Keep in mind, not all faces are friends and the same holds true for heels. Some of the biggest (and hottest) feuds in e-wrestling have been between guys of the same alignment. C. Interview Notes * Try to keep them coherent. Spell check them if possible. * Say what you want to say, but you don't have to write an epic. A lot of the best interviews I've seen have been relatively short (one to two screens long). Who wants to read through 80 pages of ranting? * Try to stick to your alliance. Faces in general shouldn't be egomaniacs and should generally try to be nice guys or at least likable. One of the many reasons I hate Hogan. * Try to avoid "cheap heat". Cheap heat can be something that's in bad taste. Try to avoid things that are racist or in bad taste (such as beating up women). There are more effective ways to get characters over. I won Heel of the Year for a character who wrestled cleanly, but he had an incredibly huge ego and really was good at getting under other wrestlers' skins. I can't really give tips on being a face as I've found it very difficult. Most of my characters are heels, I've had only a handful of faces and there's only one of them which I thought was interesting as a face. This is something which is partially interviews, partially GM responsibility. One of the things I tend to hate is when players give their characters mega pushes as a face or a heel. If everyone in the game wasn't so lazy :-), I would like to see a somewhat frequent poll of the players to see who they think is good at getting their character over or not. There are several levels of characters : Over Heel: Ric Flair, most ECW guys. The marks hate them, but they've got a style to them that they get some cheers. Not Over Heel: Jeff Jarrett or the Honky Tonk Man. This character sucks. Everyone hates them. Open chants of "YOU SUCK" occur when character is around. Over Face: The guy is over. Popular as can be. Guys like Sting and Bret Hart. (I would include Hogan, but he is now the anti-Christ.) Not Over Face: pre-turn Bob Backlund, Lex Luger. Yeah, yeah, yeah. Shut up. You've got the charisma of a rock. From there, the GM can gauge how over the character is. That way you don't have characters whose interviews really suck or doesn't act like a face getting huge crowd pops. D. Help! I Have No Charisma! * Winning on Wrestling Ability In real life, there are wrestlers who don't give extensive interviews, yet still suceed. Take Dean Malenko for instance. Malenko did not gain his reputation by working the mic. He did so with his moves in the ring. So, in Los Angeles Wrestling, we offer you the option of doing this. How so? Well it's simple. If you aren't the best RPer in the land, you can do things such as writing "Showcase" matches for yourself (see section on showcase matches), sending in strats (see section on match strats), and plenty of Challenges (see section on challenges). * Challenges If you would like to challenge a wrestler, you could do it in a few ways. Either do it in an interview (flash or in-ring), or simply send in a 'contract' to the president. All this is, is a message saying "My wrestler (whatever his name is), would like a match (specify on what kind of match) with this wrestler (the guy you're challengeing) on Tuesday." I will then forward the 'contract' to the man your challening, and if he agrees, the match is signed. v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^ 4. Strats What is a STRAT? Well, guys, if you don't know already... STRAT is short for Strategy. For any match that you compete in here in the LAW YOU are entitled to 1 match strategy. This STRAT will tell us how to play your character in the matchup, and it will help your chances of winning. For instance... [Bob is set to wrestle Joe on the 23rd of January. He knows that Joe has out roleplayed him and he has very little chance of winning the matchup. In order to help him in his matchup, Bob decides to write a strat. His strat is very simple and it consists of attempting to go at Joe's knees. Here is his STRAT for you to use as an example.] Dear President Kevin, I know Joe is going to kick my as* on the 23rd, but I will come at his knees with a variety of manovers consisting of Figure Four Leglocks, Grapevines, Clips, and more. At one point in the matchup, my guy Bob will attempt to take him outside of the ring for some action and attack him with a chair. That's my STRAT, sir. Thank you! From, Bob Members: Sending in STRATS is a great and easy way to add fun to the matchup and you can get a chance to see how your strategy matches up against your opponents. You are required one strat per match. So for_YOUR_upcoming matchup... get it to: [detail withheld] and [detail withheld] . Thanks, guys. Now check out the next section on In Ring Interviews! B. In-Ring Interviews * What Is An In-Ring or On-Card Interview? Well, guys, have you ever seen a WWF or WCW contest where at a certain point the broadcaster says, "Let's go to pre-recorded comments from ____." ? If you haven't, you mustn't be a very avid wrestling fan! That is what an On-Card interview is. Sometimes your wrestler will want to make last minute comments about a matchup and I'll publish the on the card. Or, sometimes your guy will want to grab the mike and rant about his problems in the ring. * Will It Help My Roleplaying Standing? Of course it will! In Ring or On Card Interviews are treated at the same level as roleplays, and sometimes higher level. Each card we put out should have at least _TWO_In ring or On Card interviews.
