A Los Angeles-based fed that states its own origin in 1996 and relaunched at least twice in 1998, sanctioning three titles and running a weekly TV show, an August pay-per-view and a recurring flagship show through the rest of the year. Its own founding-history handbook and an earlier relaunch handbook survive here. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

L.A.W. dates itself, in its own words, to “the summer of 1996,” opening before a crowd of 340 with a card called Sandblast 1996 — Jack Manson fighting Alan Cool, Anthony Brigano battling the New Avatar among its first bouts.

Its founding handbook, reissued 11 August 1998 after a relaunch, named three championships: the L.A.W Heavyweight Belt, billed as “the most important [belt] in L.A.W,” awarded on record and roleplay rather than an immediate tournament; the L.A.W Western States Belt, representing “the Western state, including California”; and the L.A.W Universal Tag Team Belts, reserved for “the best recorded tag team in L.A.W.” That same handbook recounted its founder's own detour to Japan, “where he met a new culture of wrestling... EXTREME wrestling,” before he brought the promotion back to California “with more power, and more guidance than ever before.”

By September 1999 its storyline centered on “The Cornerstone” Jeffrey Drake, who by his own account had spent three years “putting all I've ever had into this thing” without a major title, chasing the reigning L.A.W. Skyweight Champion, “Dangerous” Devon Case, freshly billed with a contract at the UWF. At a card called Breakdown on 19 September, Case avoided a clean finish and kept the belt; Drake answered from a filmed Beverly Hills mansion, casting himself as “the judge... jury... and the executioner,” and set a rematch for 3 October 1999.

Its surviving record runs at least to that promised rematch, whose outcome does not survive here. No closure notice follows.
