Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Bad-Ass Hardcore Wrestling opened on 24 February 1997, the same day its own signup sheet went out billing itself as “a completely booked league” with “no simulators involved,” original wrestlers only, and results “decided upon review of activity and quality of roleplaying.” Its own warning to prospective wrestlers was blunt: “this league is extreme, the faint of heart should not apply.”

Its founding supercard, Hardcore Haven, crowned four champions in one night, called by an announce team of Kendall Cunningham, Jesse Stevens, and Brian Kelly, with “Bullywug” Glenn Hudson sitting in as guest commentator. Ring introductions on the card's own career match, “Mirror” Mike Adams against J.T. Magnum, were handled by Michael Buffer, whose presence the broadcast played for a joke of its own — Hudson asking on air whether Buffer's name on the marquee was really his, and getting a deadpan “yes” back before the subject was dropped.

The promotion ran on two weekly cards, Wednesday WARzone and Saturday Nite, plus a monthly supercard, out of the Orange County Civic Center in Orlando, Florida, later moving a card as far as the Pond in Anaheim, California. Five championships ran through it — World Heavyweight, United States, Television, Cruiserweight, and Tag Team — and a sixth, the Tough Man Championship, was created on the spot for a single Mayhem pay-per-view card, restricted by its own rules to No Disqualification matches only. Rick “The Reaper” Erickson held the World Heavyweight title from the founding card on, and got his own homepage that May.

An interim president ran the fed's day-to-day business at least once while its own president was away on what a recruitment post called “a Bussiness deal,” and its tag team division closed to new applicants within a week of opening in early March “due to the incredible number of new applicants”, only to be advertised again as short-handed and rebuilding a month later.

Its last surviving document, a fan-board reply dated 6 July 1997, comes from the fed's own former president, by then already running a further promotion elsewhere, still naming Rick “The Reaper” Erickson among the wrestlers under his handling. No closing notice for Bad-Ass Hardcore Wrestling itself survives.
