A December 1997 Usenet fed staging its own take on Extreme Championship Wrestling, whose four-hour launch weekend ran a 32-man World Title tournament to its own live semifinals. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

DECW opened recruitment on 18 December 1997 as, in its own words, a “roleplay fake wrestler fed,” run by three co-presidents so that a card would go out “no matter what.” It was already at 23 wrestlers with plans to close membership at fifty, and every title stood vacant. A follow-up four days later, from the same operator, described the promotion's three co-presidents as “former fed heads” and pushed recruits to sign in time for a tournament starting that Wednesday.

Its first show, Monday Night War Hour, aired 23 December as an explicit period homage to Extreme Championship Wrestling — an announcer billed as Joey Styles opened a taped segment alongside “DECW's Florida state promoter, Terry 'The Destroyer' Brunk,” introducing “The Franchise” Alex Gowan, fresh off winning a since-defunct title elsewhere in a match against Rob Van Dam, against newcomer Sturm Brightblade. The match spilled out of the building entirely and ended with Gowan driving Brightblade through the windshield of his own car with a Death Valley Driver.

The same broadcast opened a 32-man World Championship tournament that ran through its first three rounds that night, wrestler after wrestler falling to a signature finisher — a Tombstone Piledriver, a submission called Da Ice Death Grip that claimed four straight victims, the “Pantherbomb,” the “Gas Chamber.”

The tournament resumed the next day, 24 December, working through its remaining rounds to a semifinal between Gowan and Brightblade that left the building again — into the stands, out onto the street, through a parking garage, a head smashed through a car window, a dunk in a locker-room toilet — before ending back in the ring with Gowan's own finisher, the Primetimebomb, called on the broadcast itself “the bloodiest match in DECW history.”

The final was set for a New Year's Bash '98 supercard, Gowan against Da Iceman of a stable called the dWo. No document later than that 24 December broadcast survives. Whether the tournament ever reached its own final, or who if anyone became the promotion's first World Champion, isn't in the surviving record.
