A Connecticut-based Usenet booking promotion that drew 1,355 viewers to its own debut broadcast in Fairfield before running a weekly Yahoo Group-hosted card slate through at least three shows in the spring of 2004, crowning its own first Television Champion in a ten-man battle royal. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

North East Wrestling Coalition staged its first Danger Zone broadcast on 4 April 2004 at the William H. Pitt Center in Fairfield, Connecticut, drawing 1,355 live viewers to what its own newsdesk called a debut that built “a very solid fan-base... in the Fairfield/Bridgeport area.” Headquartered in New Haven, its weekly bulletins tracked an eleven-man roster through wins and losses card to card, opening with “The Rich One” Steve Greedy defeating Bradley Vision and Misery defeating Nefarious.

Its second Danger Zone, moved to Boston's Cabot Center, crowned the first NEWC Television Champion in a ten-man main-event battle royal, won by a surprise entrant who hadn't appeared on the federation's roster before that night: “The Walking Contradiction” Sabbath. He carried the title into a third show, at Manchester, New Hampshire's Verizon Wireless Arena, retaining it against Steve Greedy with an assist from a rival, Misery, that nearly turned into a backstage fight of its own. By its fourth bulletin the promotion had also created a new NEWC Tri-State Championship, built around the New Jersey, New York and Connecticut wrestlers on its own roster, and had begun qualifying four men toward a Four Corners match for its still-vacant NEWC Heavyweight Championship, with Alexander Anderson the first man through.

The roster carried its own running grudges into the ring: “The Jackal” Bryan Vincent and “Sick & Twisted” Zack Sharp fought each other on nearly every card despite an official bout only once, escalating into a Last Man Standing match booked for the promotion's planned pay-per-view; a newly signed Trashbag Jackson was named, unconfirmed, as the man behind an unprovoked attack on Neige Thirteen.

That pay-per-view moved on its own record from a first announced date of 6 June to 4 July, Independence Day, planned for the Wachovia Center in Philadelphia, and was still unnamed when the promotion's fourth bulletin went out on 12 May 2004. Whether it was ever run, and how the promotion's two tournaments and unresolved feuds resolved, isn't in the surviving record.
