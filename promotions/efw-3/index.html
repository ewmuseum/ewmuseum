A real-wrestler roleplay federation that ran one card a week and a monthly pay-per-view behind a self-billed “most extreme” house style, closed and relaunched within a year, and split its year-end supercard into a five-part broadcast. Fifteen wrestlers and ten shows survive from its 1997–1998 run. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Extreme Fantasy Wrestling opened with a taped “Friday Night Extreme” broadcast on 27 October 1997, presenting itself, in its own commentary team's words, as a show “so wide open” it aired “on some obscure cable TV station at 2 am,” carrying what it billed on air as an “RH” rating — “Really Hardcore.” Its opening card ran through two rival stables, the Horsemen and the H.O.A. (the Harbingers Of the Apocalypse), and closed on the debut of a masked, cane-carrying figure billing himself “The Face of Fear,” carved “Horsemen IV Life” into his own arm before he had even joined them.

Its December 1997 cards staged a parody stable, the Gay World Order, whose tag team Sweet Stuff (Juicy Fruit and Butt-A-Cup) upset the Horsemen's own titleholders for the US Tag Team Championship on the stable's 16 December debut, managed at ringside by Coach Dike; the group lost the belts back to the same Horsemen team thirteen days later at the Season's Beatings pay-per-view.

At its Extreme Halloween special (4 November 1997) the Impaler took the US Heavyweight Championship off Glass Joe in a brawl fought through the concession stands and a restroom, Death and Destruction reclaimed the World Tag Team belts from Suicide and Primetime, and the reigning Cruiserweight Champion, the Dragon, fell twenty feet from the top of a steel cage onto the arena floor and still retained the title. Four weeks later, at Extreme Survivor Series, Unleaded beat Stupendous Steve for the World Heavyweight Championship in a match refereed by an authority figure the show's own commentary had already flagged as compromised.

Its last surviving pay-per-view, Armageddon 98 (17 May 1998), closed on Gregory Brians, the World Champion previously known as the Golden Boy, retaining his title over the Impaler in a main event that spilled out of the ring, through the crowd and the concession stands, and into the parking level, while the Horsemen surrendered the US Tag Team Championship to Casey Collins and DOOM as the promotion merged its tag divisions. Collins closed the show by naming herself the Impaler's new manager and booking Brians into a Hell in a Cell rematch the following week.

That rematch isn't in the surviving record. The next documents this collection holds are two “Extreme Monday Night” episodes from December 1998, seven months on, built around a new stable, the Children of Darkness, contesting a North American Championship and a Six Man Championship the earlier record never mentions. What happened to the promotion between those two points, and how or whether it closed afterward, isn't in the surviving record; its last surviving document is a card announcement for a Season's Beatings anniversary show, posted four days before the date it names, with no results ever served for it.
