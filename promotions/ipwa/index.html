Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The International Pro Wrestling Association opened on 26 August 1998 with a Handler's Handbook run section by section under its own slogan, “It's not knowing that we are the best; it's having everybody know that we are the best!” Rather than a single booker, the Association put every ruling to a vote of its own Executive Board, chaired by a figure the Handbook credits by role but not by name.

Five titles anchored its roster: a World Heavyweight Title reserved for main eventers, an International Title reserved for wrestlers born or billed outside the United States, a United States Title for Americans, a Television Title the Handbook called a stepping stone for hard workers and mid-carders, and Tag Team Titles.

Its cards ran two a week — Wednesday Night War And Havoc for dark matches and rising talent, Saturday Night Mayhem as the flagship show — against a calendar of six named pay-per-views, each with its own format: Grand Massacre (February, a battle royal), Alarma (April, entirely hardcore matches), International Incident (June, staged outside the United States), Summer Splash (August, often set on a beach), Lethal Lottery (October, a spin-the-wheel gimmick-match card), and For The Power And The Glory (December, a King of the Ring format).

The Handbook's own sample roleplay, offered as a model for new handlers, staged “Jade 'The Blade'” threatening an opponent, “Ian 'Human Torture Rack' Walsh”: “I will reign victorious in the most violent of fashions… Ian Walsh, prepare to die.”

Whether that match happened, or how the promotion itself ended, isn't in the surviving record.
