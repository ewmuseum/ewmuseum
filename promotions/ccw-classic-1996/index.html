A Usenet fed founded by two co-presidents in late 1996 that crowned first champions in four titles by the spring of 1997 and folded, then returned in the winter of 2000, after NCCW, with its old broadcast team and a new World Title tournament. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Classic Championship Wrestling was announced in November 1996 by two co-presidents pitching a simulator system with a twist — member participation itself would move the percentage points behind match results — and a schedule of two cards a week plus pay-per-views, styling the whole venture “The Future of Wrestling.”

Its recruitment posts sold that pitch directly against the genre's own frustrations, promising an alternative to “hardcore feds with no future, really late cards, and pointless 8-10 page flashes,” and named its first signings: “The Bad Boy” Mark Bagwell, “The Fantasy” Brett Young, “GQ” Geoffrey Quartz, and “The Bullet” Ricky Revolution.

Its own first show, First Night, aired 18 January 1997, headlined by Young against Bagwell alongside a four-man tournament for a new West Coast title. A World Heavyweight Championship followed that March at a pay-per-view called Explosion!!, an eight-man single-elimination tournament staged, its own broadcast declared, live from the Joe Louis Arena in Detroit, its opening graphics borrowing footage of the Hiroshima bombing for its own “8 Men, 1 Belt” tagline. Desmond Harrison held that title before losing it to X.Plosion.

By that July, in a scene staged inside a gym, Harrison was preparing to challenge X.Plosion for the belt a second time, worried less about the man across the ring from him than about the “goon squad” his own rival faction might send after him instead.

The promotion collapsed within weeks of that broadcast: a rival federation's own notice that August, cancelling a memorial pay-per-view of its own, cited “Classic Championship Wrestling's financial fall out” as the reason. Nothing of its own survives past that July card.
