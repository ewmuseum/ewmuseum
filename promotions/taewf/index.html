Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

No founding document for THE AMERICAN E-WRESTLING FEDERATION survives; its own earliest surviving broadcast is Summer Blast, staged 10 August 1996 at the Rose Bowl in Pasadena, California, opening on an angle in which Alice “the Outrageous One” Lamour unveiled a nude painting to expose “Ice Man” Steve Snow as the wrestler he had been daring into a match.

That card's own title results: “Flashfire” Dean Ferris won the United States Championship from “Rude Dog” Alex Reed; The Executioner retained the World Heavyweight Championship in a four-way Texas Tornado match; and “Quickstrike” Chris Quigley retained the North American Singles Championship.

By February Fury, in 1997, Quigley held the World Heavyweight Championship itself and defended it against longtime rival “The Masterpiece” Kurt Manning in a match its own broadcast team, Vic MacInnis and Mel Blake, called the biggest main event in the federation's history. Quigley retained, but the broadcast ended on the fallout: Kurt Manning, on discovering that Quigley was secretly dating his own sister Stephanie, turned on his family at ringside, attacking his brother, his father, and his mother in full view of the crowd.

Quigley, billed by then as the federation's longest-reigning champion, lost the World Heavyweight Championship at the Kauffman Cup that July to “Dreamlover” Trey Porter in a two-out-of-three-falls main event that turned brutal enough, by its own broadcast's account, to bring a fire and a real risk of electrocution into the ring — decided when Porter's own stablemate, Zack Malone, interfered to turn the third fall.

No TAEWF document after that title change survives.
