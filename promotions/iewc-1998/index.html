Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

IeWC's own surviving record picks up already in progress: its earliest surviving broadcast, Wednesday Night WarZone (22 April 1998, a sold-out United Center), opens mid-tournament, with one wrestler just fired for drugs found in his locker and a team already retired. Co-president Sykic and a fellow president ran the promotion's own front office on-screen, presiding over three title tournaments at once — World, National and Juniorweight.

Its first Tag Team Champions of the World, The Perfectly Perfect Duo, were crowned four days later on Saturday Night Showcase (25 April 1998), after the reigning Enforcers were let go from the roster entirely.

IeWC's own first supercard, Spring Fest 98 (31 May 1998, a crowd the broadcast put past 67,000), crowned its first-ever World Heavyweight Champion, “The Human Torture Rack” Ian Walsh, over Aries “The God of War” in a match that ran close to an hour, and a new Juniorweight Champion, Mad Goat, over the tournament's top seed, Jinsei Shakanuzi.

By Boston Massacre (27 June 1998) the Tag Team Titles had changed hands again, to Teen Angst, and the night closed on an eight-man War Games match pitting World Champion Ian Walsh's own team against a squad built around Lawrence Williams. The mystery fourth man Williams' team had held back turned out to be Creed, who armed Walsh's own teammates' opponents with weapons mid-match and handed the champion's team the loss.

IeWC's own last surviving broadcast built toward a supercard its surviving record never actually reaches: Summer Bash 98, announced for 26 July at the Pontiac Silverdome, with Lawrence Williams lined up for a World Title shot against Walsh.

Whether that match happened isn't in the surviving record. A community post from 29 July 1998 states that the promotion's own president was “out of e-wrestling completely,” and a separate community thread from 19 May 1999 independently confirms the promotion itself had closed by then.
