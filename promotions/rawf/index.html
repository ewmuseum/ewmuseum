A four-year-and-three-month-run e-fed whose surviving title ledgers track eight separate championships changing hands well over a hundred times between 1998 and 2003, closing its doors in January 2003 five days after crowning Darksider its own final World Champion. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Real Action Wrestling Federation's own closing notice, dated 28 January 2003, gives the fed's own life span in its own words: "After 4 years and 3 months of greatness... since October of 1998." Its surviving title ledgers are unusually deep for a small e-fed's own record — the World and Hardcore titles alone changed hands roughly seventy times combined between November 1998 and the fed's own final weeks, alongside six further championships (Intercontinental, United States, Tag Team, Television, Hardcore Tag, and Women's) that the ledger shows ending vacant, unified, or discontinued rather than carried to the close. Outcomes were decided by a stated combination of weekly roleplay and ongoing storyline "angles," per the fed's own Rules page.

Wednesday Wargames on 18 December 2002, billed the fed's own 3-Year Anniversary show, crowned Spawn the new Intercontinental Champion in his own second match in the fed — not the World Title, despite an earlier pass's own misreading of the same ledger; the show carries no World Title match at all. The World Title's own last recorded change instead belongs to Darksider, who won a Death Cube, Two Out of Three Falls match against Matt Vandal at November Nightmares (27 November 2002) for the title Brandy Sangria's own three-way elimination defense had left vacant, and carried it to the promotion's own close nine weeks later. David Wilson held the Hardcore Title at the same point, won the same night in a falls-count-anywhere brawl against The Crippler settled with a sledgehammer.
