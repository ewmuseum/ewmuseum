Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Northeast Federation of Wrestling introduced itself in November 1996 as “a spin-off of Northeast Fantasy Sports,” pitched explicitly at newcomers to e-wrestling rather than veterans, with one weekly show, Wednesday Heart Attack, and a monthly pay-per-view built around roleplay and character development. A later recruitment post, signed by a self-described founder and former president, listed the talent the promotion had already produced — Diamondback Chris Myers, Airborne Mark Fulcrum, Mondo Somatic, the Rude Dude, Ares God of War among them — and called it a fed where “handlers rag on their opponents” better than anywhere else on the newsgroup.

Its record from 1998 centers on a stable called M4, run by a manager known only as the Professor, against a rival team, Northern Exposure. At a May 1998 Wednesday Heart Attack, M4's Mondo Somatic and Dr. Meloncholy took the tag team titles from Northern Exposure's Chad Champion and Ringmaster after Somatic wrestled through a knee injury inflicted earlier that same broadcast by a fellow M4 member, Five Fingers of Death, in what the promotion's own broadcast team concluded was a deliberate sabotage the Professor had engineered. A July 1998 Wet 'n Wild Weekender saw Dr. Meloncholy retain the fed's Pete's Wicked Ale title by disqualification after M4 interference, while the Rude Dude — billed as its own highest-paid wrestler and a charter member of the promotion — walked out on a match rather than finish it.

Its own returning founder, Simon Pollinetti, rejoined its booking staff that November, and by 3 March 1999 sat at the broadcast table himself for a card staged, in storyline, on the Gettysburg battlefield: the broadcast opened with a parody of the Gettysburg Address crediting Pollinetti with having “brought forth on this industry a new federation” two and a half years earlier, and closed its television-title match, Joe Six-pack against Bloodlust, as a Civil War re-enactment fought across the actual field before ending, by way of outside interference, in a new champion.

That same broadcast previewed a card the following week at Washington College in Chestertown, Maryland. No later document survives to show whether that card, or anything after it, ever aired; the promotion's own record stops there, and no closure notice was ever posted.
