Boston, Massachusetts. The promotion is based in the New England area, and tours around that area while being primarily housed in the
Boston Fleet Center, and it's head office is located nearby. The official NEFW website is
www.nefwwrestling.com , operated and maintained by Downpour Productions, under contract with the NEFW. It will be run completely through e-mail.
************************************************************************************ a) Who runs the NEFW? The NEFW is operated by it's President, David Fineberg, and
Vice President, Jake Steed. Fineberg is a longtime wrestling personality, often associated with other small independent federations. He has experience as a booker, a talent coordinator, and several other aspects of the industry, and brings all that to the table with the NEFW.
Jake Steed is an NEFW mainstay, and has worked with the fed for many years both backstage and at the forefront.
Out of Character, David Fineberg has been a writer and handler in many feds, most notably in federations such as HGW
(Hardcore Garbage Wrestling) and AWE (Absolute Wrestling Entertainment). He has taken over the fed from a pair of destructive hands, and now seeks to restore the NEFW to glory.
Jake Steed is a web designer, and longtime handler and writer for the NEFW. His dedication to the betterment of the federation and willingness to take action has earned him the position of Vice
President. [name withheld] is another web designer, the pair of him and Steed creating Downpour Productions. His writing talent and superior handling skills make him an ideal candidate for Director of
Programming, and we can count on him to ensure that all programs make it out on time Finally, the owner of the Federation, Flashmaster, a.k.a [name withheld]. [name withheld] ran the fed for four years before a brief absence, but now has returned to sit behind the scenes and make sure nothing goes awry as happened with the last Presidency.
b) NEFW Mission Statement The guidelines of the North Eastern Federation of Wrestling are very simple. It was created to attract the greatest talent around in hopes of putting on a program that would appeal to everyone for athleticism and true competition. Primarily, Fineberg hoped to create a federation where the talent would have fun doing their work and be able to work together to create a fan-friendly environment that everyone could enjoy. Overall, the satisfaction of the fans comes first.
The NEFW is around in hopes of gaining handlers and writers eager to contribute and willing to compete with others in the great game of e-wrestling. The NEFW wants to put out good, solid programs that are delivered on time and with strong writing, and develop a sense of community and friendship within the handlers. The best way to explain the NEFW's plans would be the creation of a wrestling utopia.
c) NEFW Personalities These are personalities exclusive to the NEFW. The employees of the NEFW are few, as the promotion just suffered a series of setbacks. Listed here, they are:
Ring Announcer: Gregory "Doc" Hanson "Doc" Hanson is an experienced announcer who worked for many Boxing Events in the New England area. The NEFW is very lucky to have him aboard, as he is also experienced in dealing with various talent.
Senior Referee: William Yeath William Yeath is a 37-year-old former indy wrestler once known as "The Comet" Billy Yeath. Once age and injury caught up to him, he retired to a quiet life in Vermont, but quickly leaped on the offer to get back into the industry when approached by David Fineberg.
He referees the most important matches for the NEFW. Other Referees: Taka Torohito Taka Torohito was a referee for a Japanese federation until financial burdens caused the fed to close. His ring experience and fair judgment made picking him up an easy call for Fineberg, who also hopes to use Taka in the future to negotiate talent relations with
Japanese promotions. Evan Patterson Evan Patterson is a very young referee, whose size and lack of athletic ability kept him from becoming a wrestler. At times, it seems he is bitter towards those who actually are wrestlers, but
Fineberg has faith in his ability to be fair. It remains to be seen if that faith is well-placed.
Victoria Stevens A former boxing round-number girl, Victoria Stevens came to the NEFW alongside family friend "Doc" Hanson, and learned the referee business. She is very dedicated to her work, and though Taka Torohito has precedent, she might be second in line for the position of Senior
Official should William Yeath retire.
Alan Pryor Alan Pryor is a longtime friend of David Fineberg who often accompanied him as Fineberg worked for various promotions. When he became President of the NEFW, Fineberg immediately hired Pryor, who had some refereeing experience.
Announcers: Play-by-Play: Lester Holland The perfect straight man, Lester Holland is a weedy, nervous little man with poor fashion taste and a sense of humor that is almost never seen. He calls matches as he sees them, and abhors any kind of cheating and is quick to condemn those who do. He is often the target of jokes from other personalities, but he usually doesn't understand them.
Color Commentary: "The Encyclopedia" Jerry Brooks Jerry Brooks is a middle-aged former wrestler who has become disgruntled with the industry in general. He and Fineberg were friends years ago, and so he was persuaded to become Color Commentary man due to his experience and name value. Brooks, however, is almost the exact opposite of Lester Holland, prefering to take favorites in a match and call things in their favor. Brooks is called "The
Encyclopedia" for his expansive knowledge of wrestling techniques and holds. Backstage Interviewers: Raylene O'Patrick A local weathergirl, Raylene doubles as a backstage interviewer whenever her job doesn't take her away. A perky young girl who sometimes thinks up good questions, but mostly just asks for opinions.
Steven Jeffries Steven Jeffries is the main interviewer for the NEFW, and a good one at that. He tries to challenge the talent to explain their actions or plan out their next moves, and isn't above criticizing them. He sometimes gets in trouble because of his mouth, but he sees himself as a true journalist and refuses to back down.

************************************************************************************ a) Weekly Shows NEFW Impact Impact is the weekly show of the NEFW, in which as much talent as possible will be featured in the allotted time.. The show is one and a half hours long, and will usually feature up to six matches with time for backstage interviews set aside if necessary.
NEFW Verbal Warfare Despite the fed's now-limited resources, Fineberg allows wrestlers to use the camera equipment daily in order to film their own interviews, which are compiled and sent out to the other talent at the end of the day and also broadcast around the area You never know what's going to be said on NEFW Verbal Warfare.
As long as there are Roleplays to be sent out, Verbal Warfare will be seen every night. b) Pay-Per-View The NEFW's saving grace is Pay-Per-View as far as recovering financially is concerned. The NEFW strives to put out one
Pay-Per-View a month. These will be the crowning glories of the NEFW, topped off by their yearly Supercard, Wrestlefest.
c) Special Tournaments In the spirit of Puroresu, several tournaments will be held in the NEFW for trophies and other awards, in order to both generate interest in the fed and promote the spirit of competition. The details of these tournaments has yet to be revealed.
************************************************************************************ [3] On With the Show Now that the NEFW is ready to begin, it's important to note how the actual card will be planned and play out.
************************************************************************************ a) How the NEFW is Run All the NEFW Cards are booked by the President. Match outcomes are also decided upon by the President's judgment. The President judges all matches based on both the frequency of roleplaying by the characters involved and the quality of those roleplays. Quality especially is taken into consideration.
b) Handler Influence Handlers are, of course, the lifeblood of the NEFW. Their input is greatly appreciated, and they are free to send in strategies that will also weigh in the judging of their match if sent in.
Handlers can cooperate on their matches and plan them out themselves if they so wish. Any suggestions, ideas, or comments from Handlers on the running of the NEFW and its shows will be recieved and considered by the President.
c) Angles/Storylines The driving factor behind the NEFW will be the interaction between characters and what comes of them. The hope is that angles will begin and from them will branch larger, more encompassing storylines that will attract attention and increase interest and enjoyment for all involved. While the President will plan some of these out himself, the hope is that Handlers will also think up angles amongst themselves and present them to the President for approval.
Handlers are encouraged to work together in order to further their angles, and are free to turn to the President for suggestions, but they must have any and all angles approved before carrying them out.
Also, if one wrestler wishes to carry out an angle and does not have the cooperation of the Handler he intends to have involved, that angle
CANNOT be carried out.
d) NEFW Titles There are currently five titles in the NEFW. The NEFW Tag Team
Titles have been suspended indefinitely until such time as the Tag
Team roster is sufficiently bolstered to support a championship. The other titles are:
The NEFW Title: Formerly the NEFW World Title. This suffered a loss in status after the recent misfortnes of the NEFW, and is now simply representative of the Champion of the Federation rather than holding actual "world" status. Everyone who holds the NEFW Title will be given the difficult task of holding up to the dream of restoring the title to it's former glory. The NEFW Title may be defended on weekly shows, and will always be defended on Pay-Per-View.
The Unified Intercontinental Title: An old NEFW Title now restored to active competition, the UIC
Title is second in status to the NEFW Title, and competed for by upper-tier performers. The UIC Title is often seen as a stepping stone to the NEFW Title itself, and thus is in hot contention. A
Unified Intercontinental Champion will be expected to defend his title on most televised programs, and always on Pay-Per-View.
The Television Title: Another recently restored NEFW Title, the Television Title is only for those who crave constant battle. The NEFW Television title will be defended on every televised program without fail. This could, in the future, lead to the Champion competing up to twice a week, three times on Pay-Per-Views. A successful Television Champion could gain very high standing in the roster itself.
The Rage Title: A new NEFW title, the Rage Title is geared towards Cruiserweights, but not exclusively limited towards them. While it is meant to be used by the incredible Cruiserweight talent, it is very possible that they might find themselves proving themselves against the Heavyweights in order to solidify their claim to the title.
Intense, hard-hitting action is expected to accompany the Rage Title, as well as steady defenses and frequent gimmick matches.
The Hardcore Title Explanation unnecessary. Only for those with a desire to be hurt and hurt others, the NEFW Hardcore Title will belong to those individuals possessing the most bloodthirsty, domineering, extreme attitudes, and a willingness to back it up. Only the dedicated need apply for this title.
************************************************************************************ [4] Rules Every good system is based on rules, and the NEFW is no different. Please read these rules carefully before deciding whether or not to join the NEFW.
************************************************************************************ a) The NEFW Commandments. I) Have Fun. The basic rule of any e-fed is to enjoy yourself. Above all, remember that it's just a game, and that every game has winners and losers. The beauty of e-wrestling is that everyone has a chance to be both at some point or another, and depending how good you are, can have fun with either one. So above all, remember to just sit back and let the good times roll, and the NEFW will be good for everyone.
II) Cooperate with Others The NEFW is not a place to let egos play. I don't care who you may have been in another federation or how many years you've put into e-wrestling, or how much better than someone else you believe you are. The main point of the NEFW is to establish ties between e-wrestlers, not start rivals with people when you should be concentrating on their characters instead. While we understand that sometimes handlers get personally offended at things that are said and
"shoot," as it were, on one another, it is not a preferable
III) Role-Playing You are required to send in at least one Roleplay per week in the NEFW. Failure to meet this number will result in a warning, two weeks in removal from the cards. If a handler goes a month without meeting the one Roleplay per week limit (barring sending an explanation to the President) their contract will be terminated.
Also, there is a limit on vulgarity in flashing. It is perfectly acceptable to "bleep" words out, as is often done in e-wrestling, but as far as what should and should not be censored, just think of what we are allowed to hear on network television.
Anything stronger than the word "bitch" should be censored. If you don't see it on NYPD: Blue, it isn't allowed here.
A Roleplay should get your point across, even if it's just to be funny or make your character seem mean or vile. I'm looking for quality, things with substance. Scene setting, some action perhaps, something that stands out.
All Roleplays are to be sent directly to [detail withheld] . At the end of every evening, all Roleplays sent in will be mailed out in one Flash packet called "[NEFW] Verbal
Warfare." As long as they are delivered to [detail withheld] before midnight, you will see them the following day. Roleplays are sent to the NEFW Roster through a Yahoo! Group which you will be invited to join upon applying. IV) Decisions Ultimately, President David Fineberg has the final say on anything in the North Eastern Federation of Wrestling. He will try to be fair and even-handed, but he will put the good of the collective fed over the good of a single individual. If you are upset with a decision he makes, let him know, and together something can be worked out.

Also, any characters that have abilities that are grossly inproportional to their size (i.e; Andre the Giant-type characters that do handspring moonsaults, Billy Kidman-type characters that gorilla press people) will be rejected. Be original. You must fully complete the application and then follow up with a sample roleplay in order to be considered.

Alignment: (Pick One) Principled A principled character lives by a strict code of ethics. There is a definite right and wrong in the world for this type of character, and they attempt to right those wrongs. There are certain lines that cannot be crossed though, and these sorts of wrestlers will do all that they can within the rules to bring wrong do'ers to justice. A principled character is the "ultimate" face.
Scrupulous People of this nature know right and wrong, and believe that those in the wrong should be brought to justice... but if the rules are preventing this, they will bend the rules so that justice can be served. The adage of fight fire with fire can be brought to light with this sort of character.
Unprincipled The unprincipled wrestler understands that there are rights and wrongs in the wrestling world. However, as long as they can stretch a
"few" of those rules, and don't get caught, they're still okay. As long as the damage done isn't devastating, and the repercussions don't have a long-term negative effect. An unprincipled character will be a nearly "facish" character, as he knows the rules, and will tend to abide within them, with only the occasional 'line crossing'.
Taoist Whatever is fun or enjoyable is what is right. A person of this sort seeks out what they like and strives for that above other things.
It doesn't matter where something came from or how they got it, as long as they can have fun using it. If someone doesn't understand the humour of a situation, they're missing out on the purpose of the whole thing. Insults and "fun scenes" are more important to a Taoist than an assault or sneak attack on an opponent.
Anarchist To the anarchist style of wrestler, the social norms are simply roadblocks to allowing someone to be their best. The anarchist wrestler may or may not be a "me, me, me" type of person, as they may or may not care about their own best interests, as long as they're allowed to push the boundaries of standards and practices.
Self-Serving Much like an unprincipled person, only one who works within a system, and as long as the system benefits the selfish wrestler. These people are usually quite aware that their actions can cause misery, but it doesn't bother them. The rules apply only when they are are beneficial. When the laws in place begin to become a burden, they are discarded. The only thing that matters is how things can best serve the person, not the other way around.
Aberrant Honor among theives. People of this nature have a code of ethics that governs their lives. They abide by this code above what the accepted social norms are. They will not break their code, and have honor. That's not to say they won't cheat and use shortcuts. On the contrary, the nature of the sport clearly allows it, and they know it.
Miscreant A person of this sort is someone who causes grief for kicks. Someone who seems to enjoy making life miserable for other people. They do have some lines that they won't cross... but not many. Most
"heelish" e-wrestlers would probably fall in this category if they are true heels.
Diabolic Those few that fall into this catagory are evil, plain and simple. There is no level to which these people will go to achieve their goal. Backstabbing, trickery, or whatever else is necessary to get what they want are tasks done without guilt. And it may not be for any natural goal. The opportunity is there, hurt your opponent, and maim him/her intentionally.
