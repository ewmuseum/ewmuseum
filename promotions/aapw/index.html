Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

All-Atlantic Pro Wrestling was announced on 5 October 1996 by two bookers who had made their names on NCW's own PRIMETIME show and its Light-Heavyweight and Juniors divisions, pitching a fully-booked regional promotion “based on reality” — no giants, no thousand-pound wrestlers — around four founding titles, and explicitly disclaiming, in the same advert, any current involvement with Network Championship Wrestling.

That disclaimer did not hold: the fed's own record carries a worked feud with NCW itself, receipted from both sides, alongside the ordinary business of running a regional territory — touring real college arenas and named venues before building its own 2,790-seat arena in Secaucus, New Jersey, and putting out a serialized fan magazine, Wrestling Illustrated, across at least seven editions.

Its roster produced at least one storyline the promotion itself could not stand behind for long: a heel character's “Down's Syndrome angle” and a “Dating Game for Kids” segment split the fed's own audience — some nominated the character for its own Heel of the Year — and the promotion cut him not long after, its writer told, in his own paraphrase, that the act had become “too... politically incorrect” for the AAPW.

It closed out 1997 live from Madison Square Garden, opening PRIMETIME with a montage of real Garden wrestling history under the line “New York and the AAPW: Together at last,” then running a fatal four-way for the World Heavyweight Championship and defenses of its Light-Heavyweight and Atlantic titles on the same card. By then its own name had already drawn an imitator: a same-acronym upstart advertising on the newsgroup that June was told plainly by one of the fed's own audience to rename itself rather than confuse readers with an outfit “highly regarded as one of the better e-feds out there.”

Its own record continues unbroken into 1998 under the same announce team and the same rotating show titles — Perimeter, PRIMETIME, Spectrum, Invasion — through a Night of Champions VI pay-per-view in February and a two-night Desert Swarm supercard that June. Nothing in the surviving record explains how or when it stopped: its own last attested broadcast runs to 3 October 1998, still active, with no closing notice anywhere in what survives.
