The Worldwide Internet Wrestling Alliance, a short-lived USIW-led plan to federate six e-feds under one banner, known only from its own 13 January 1997 founding call before USIW itself closed the next month. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Worldwide Internet Wrestling Alliance survives as a single founding pitch, dated 13 January 1997, from the United States Internet Wrestling federation's own president: a planned six-member structure of USIW, MCW, NIWF, BWF, IFW, and AAWF, posted to rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy in search of anyone willing to sign on.

The same post made USIW's own case for itself. It ran something every day of the week — Monday Night Dynamite, an “Inside USIW” update show twice weekly, house shows three nights, and a Thursday Night Thunder, plus a monthly pay-per-view it hoped to double once the alliance came together. Its own tag champions, it said, had “beaten every other team and some of them twice,” with its two most famous of those matches both ending in “screw job endings.”

No further document survives naming the alliance. USIW, the fed that proposed it, is recorded elsewhere as having closed suddenly the following month, and nothing more is heard of the Worldwide Internet Wrestling Alliance itself anywhere in the record.
