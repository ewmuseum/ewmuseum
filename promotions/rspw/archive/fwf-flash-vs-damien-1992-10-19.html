FWF: Flash vs. Damien

19 October 1992
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Stilt Man returns to the terminal to continue the saga of the Fictional Wrestling Federation! Coming up from the FWF (if and whenever I get around to it): Doctor Death offers an open contract match, with the World Championship on the line. The taker: "Suicidal" Paul Stuart, a newcomer! Suicidal, indeed... The Mighty Annihilus accepts the challenge of Kane the Undertaker. The Invincible Titan Morgan breaks some new ground, defending his newly-won United States Championship against one half of the World Tag Team Champions, Aaron "Lightning" Murphy! "Hurricane" Andrew Simms takes on former I-C champion Sultan Sitan El Sagga. Okay, if I manage to get that all done within the next month, or even if I don't, today's match: Continuing an ongoing feud that shakes both the tag and singles ranks, one half of the new Blade Runners takes on the accomplice of he who broke up the first team. I am referring to "Flash" Johnathon Flann and "Greased Lightning" Damien Flayer. Comments: Flann: "No strangers here, Flayer. We've fought before, and we fight again tonight! The outcome means a lot to both sides. It's the speed against the speed, the agility against the agility, the 'Flash' against the 'Greased Lightning.' Well, get set to surrender, because there's not a lot of room for losers like you in the FWF, yesterday, today, or tommorrow! Dustbin city for you!" Flayer: "You know, what we have here in the case of John Flann is an ego out of control. He comes to the FWF, bails out a nutcase like 'Rock' Rockwell, and then sees himself as a serious challenge to a two-time former U.S. Champion. I've paid my dues, I've been in the FWF since day one, and I'm not about to let every last newcomer in the FWF walk all over me! It's time for you to be put to sleep, little dog, and the needle's been given to me! I hope you're not afraid of needles . . . " ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- The tune of Black Sabbath's "Iron Man" sounds over the PA system at Madison Square Garden, and a lone individual steps out from the ring ropes. He has blue eyes, pale red hair, and the image of a white flash blast emanating from his eyes is painted on his face. He walks with a springy stride, and has a smile that shines in the ring lights. Alfred Grinchley begins his work: "THE FOLLOWING CONTEST IS SCHEDULED FOR ONE FALL. COMING DOWN THE AISLE, HAILING FROM MINNEAPOLIS, MINNESOTA. HE WEIGHS TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY-THREE POUNDS: 'FLASH' JOHNATHON FLANN!!" He runs up to the ring as his introduction finishes and leaps to the second rope, springing up over the top into the squared circle. The fans cheer in appreciation, and the hardcores are virtually bubbling over with excitement. Two aerial masters, one a bit more experienced than the other, with good technical wrestling skills, stand against each other here. The possibilities are endless. The music shifts, and Spectre General's "Nothing's Gonna Stand in Our Way" begins its steady pace. A second individual with platinum blond hair and gray eyes in long green tights with a neon yellow lightning bolt down the leg steps from the ring curtain and starts for the ring. He, too, runs down the ring, suddenly turning into a series of gymnastic handsprings and flying high into the air, twirling and flying into the ring! The move is boasting, and humiliating to his slightly larger opponent. "AND HIS OPPONENT, FROM CLEVELAND, OHIO, WEIGHING TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTEEN POUNDS, HERE IS 'GREASED LIGHTNING' DAMIEN FLAYER!!" Flayer has a slight height advantage and a slight weight disadvantage over his opponent. The differences, however, are small enough that it matters little. Flayer is built more streamlined, perhaps indicating a little more dexterity, while Flann is probably a bit stronger and having more compact power due to his greater weight and smaller frame to distribute it on. Any way it looks, they are quite evenly matched physically. One inch and eight pounds in Flayer's and Flann's favor, respectively, are all the difference. The bell sounds, and the match begins. Flayer begins hopping up and down, doing a little dance with his legs to unlimber them at the last minute before the contest truly begins. Flann is looking about somewhat nervously for Stan Golding, Flayer's cohort. He knows, however, that Flayer is fresh off a defeat at the hands of Andrew Simms, and it gives him hope that being a new- comer is not such a detriment. His thoughts are interrupted as Flayer darts at him, and he just barely gets out of the way of a sudden roundhouse kick aimed at his head. The move was in defense, Flann realizes in shock. Flayer has a slight but definite speed advantage, Flann sees quickly. It is an advantage he hopes to diminish. He engages in a collar-and-elbow tie, and immediately Flayer's arm wraps itself around Flann's head. Flash goes to the ropes, and Flayer has little ability to stop him from shoving him away. Flash follows Flayer to the other side, only to be greeted with a shoulder that sends him to the mat! Flayer goes to the ropes quickly, and Flash rolls onto his belly to allow Flayer to pass over. In a blur of motion, Flayer's leg comes out from under him and an elbow descends accurately to the base of Flash's skull! Blackness creeps upon the prone man's vision, and he rolls instinctively over to protect his head. He is rewarded with a second elbow, slower, to the chest, and he springs to his feet to try to regroup, dazedly. Flayer keeps up the attack, grasping Flash in a headlock once again. He shifts from one arm to the other, spinning around to get an arm in a hammerlock, spins some more to get a drop-toehold, keeping the hammerlock on, hopping back into a bridge over the locked arm, imprisoning Flash quite effectively. Flayer's speed is being used well thus far, and Flash is quite disgusted with allowing it to happen. Flash tries to get to his feet, but a stabbing twinge of pain through his right shoulder stops him. Flayer has to shift to allow for balance, however, and raises one foot, losing his leverage. Flash turns himself onto his belly, flopping Flayer to the mat, and Flash turns out of the hammerlock, catching Flayer into a headlock of his own! The Flash sits on Flayer's back pulling up in the chinlock into a reverse camel clutch, keeping the smaller man firmly on the mat. Flayer slowly and successfully brings one knee, then the other, beneath his body, coming up to his feet and hooking Flash's legs under his arms. He takes a couple of steps, then falls away, smothering Flash beneath him! Flayer does not go for a cover, however, choosing to roll away and get his bearings after the shake-up. He watches Flash, as the other comes up to his knees slowly. Flayer rises to a squatting position, ready to move in a moment. As Flash comes up to a stoop . . . Flayer runs, trying a knee-lift, but instead Flash steps aside, scooping up one leg of the passing man and throwing him up into the air. Flayer's flight path takes him bottom-of-thighs first into the top rope, bouncing him further out. Flash watches in rapt fascination with the fans as Flayer hurtles un- controllably out of the ring, landing luckily with an arm against the security railing instead of his throat. Flayer grimaces on the thin mats outside, clutching at his left arm in pain. A cut on the arm begins to drip blood onto the mat, and seeps out from beneath the hand over it. Flash turns to argue with the official, who did not enjoy this questionable action. Flash points out that he acted by instinct, spur of the moment, and was not at fault if Flayer went rear-first flying over the ropes. The ref warns him to watch it, for Flayer might have been hospitalized if he had landed against the fence differently. If the arm took a fracture, he might have to be anyway. Flash raises his hands and acknowledges the warning, then turns and gets back to the business at hand. Flayer has risen to his feet, favoring the left arm, as Flash comes out of the ring. As Flash nears, Flayer buries a foot in his stomach, causing him to double over to the mat outside. Flayer obeys the referee's instructions and sends Flash back into the ring, following after. The moment he is back inside, he is caught in an arm drag by the injured left arm! Flayer screams in pain, and the official asks politely if he wants to go on. Flayer shouts defiance, gritting his teeth against the pain, as Flash brings him back to his feet, bounding him over the top rope with the arm! The zebra shouts at Flash again, but Flash's eyes are all for Flayer at this point, as he throws his weight in a cross body block over the top against Flayer on the fence! Flayer's eyes roll back in his head, and Flash shouts to the approving fans in the ringside seats, shaking a fist in the air with a whooping war cry! Flash puts Flayer back into the ring, and climbs to the top rope, waiting for Flayer to come to lie on his back in the middle of the ring before launching off the top . . . . . . striking with an elbow to Flayer's chest. The Garden erupts as the official's hand strikes the mat, one, two! And Flayer kicks out. Flash makes no bones about it, and resumes an armbar upon his foe. He brings him to his feet, and gives him a Japanese arm drag back overhead, flopping around with him and dropping a leg across the left shoulder! Flayer slowly turns his body around against the armbar, and finally gouges Flash in the eyes to free himself. The gesture by Flayer outside the ring is ignored by the fans, as Flayer puts Flash up and into a reverse backbreaker! He slowly goes to the ropes, and drops a knee across Flash's head! He stands quickly from the kneeling position, waiting as Flash instinctively rolls onto his belly to prevent a pin. A second knee to the back aggravates Flash's problems, and movement in the shorter man's body ceases. Flayer drags Flash by the ankles over to the ropes, and turns him over into a Boston crab as he gets there. He sits back, viciously putting his weight against Flash's spine, aiming to weaken or force Flash to surrender. He has a sly look on his face, and looses one of Flash's legs to grab onto the middle rope for help. Flash screams in agony, and the fans shout in protest. Flayer lets go as the referee turns to him, and the official asks why Flayer has only one leg held and the ropes shaking. Flayer points at Stan Golding outside the ring, saying he was playing with them. The fans do a double take, realizing that the Scorpion is, indeed, at ringside. The gesture by Flayer to the outside suddenly takes on some new significance, and Flash realizes he's in some serious trouble, as if he wasn't before. The ref turns back to Flash to ask for a submission, and instantly Flayer's hand grasps the middle ropes again. The official turns back just as quickly, and this time catches the heel before he can take his hand away. A disqualification count begins, and Flayer releases Flash at four. Flayer draws Flash up into the corner, and begins hammering away, with a left, right, left, uppercut right, knife edge chop. The beating continues, with kicks and fists until the official finally backs Flayer out with another DQ count. Flash sags in the corner, and Flayer turns back and lets off with a blatant choke hold, which the official immediately begins a count on. Flayer backs off at four again, and then launches another choke. He gets to three on the count, then digs away at Flash's eyes, stopping at another four on the count. Flayer glances at Golding, who finally at this point lets go of Flash's foot in the corner. Somewhere in an announcer's booth for the TV brodcasts, a mark commentator's shouting is barely audible. Flayer whips Flash out of the corner to the opposite side suddenly, keeping a hold on the top rope where he is to wait for the right moment. A few moments before Flash hits the opposite side, Flayer launches into a cartwheel, followed by a handspring back into an elbow that lands bare instants after Flash hits the buckles. The twin blows elicit a gaping gasp of pain from Flash, as he staggers out of the corner and falls forward a few feet out. Flayer shouts at a couple of fans at ringside, neither of them over 10 years old. The hardcores in the audience take up to arguing for him, and he gestures to them thankfully before turning back to Flash. He collects him in a full nelson, sitting down on his back to bend him in a horrific modified camel clutch! Flash squints his eyes, screaming in pain, as his already-sore back is nearly popped out of joint by the hold. The referee asks for a submission, but Flash refuses. He tries to crawl with his knees towards the ropes, but he has nowhere to go. A cheer comes from fans near the dressing room, quickly picked up by other fans around the arena. Steve "Rock" Rockwell is running to ringside, to even out the numbers here. The "Scorpion" Stan Golding shouts at him, and they exchange insults. Flayer releases the hold, some would say foolishly, to join in the shouting match. Flash slumps to unmoving prone position on his belly in the ring. Flayer turns back to Flash, taking him up and back into a reverse backbreaker again. Flash arches his back on the mat, as Flayer walks to the corner, drawing one finger across his throat sharply. The Lightning Stroke is on its way, the hardcores see . . . Golding leaps up to the ring apron, pointing at the opposite corner, where Flayer is climbing the turnbuckles. The referee turns to remove him, not seeing as Flayer climbs the turnbuckle, curling forward to spring out, and Rock whacks him with a steel chair from the second step! Flayer flies back into the ring, twitching spasmodically next to Flash, and Flash rolls over to cover the lighter man. The referee turns around to see the cover. One, two . . . Golding is not willing to allow the pin. He kicks Flash off of Flayer, and the official immediately calls for the bell. Then he realizes that Golding has the bell in his hands! Rock comes into the ring with the chair, but Golding brains him with the bell, leaving him in a tangled mess in the ropes. Turning contemptuously to his replacement, Golding slaps Flayer around, bringing his cohort back to his feet. Flayer waits for Flash be held up by the hair, then blows his "Storm Cloud" from his mouth into his foe's eyes! As Flash spasms around in pain, Golding lifts him into a one-handed torture rack, as Flayer climbs to the top rope again. Flayer springs back in the Lightning Stroke, as Golding kneels with Flash to allow Flayer's moonsault knee to splatter the imprisoned man's face around the ring! Flash slumps into unconsciousness. But Golding and Flayer's celebration is short-lived, as Rock comes back in with the chair, smacking Golding across the back! Golding crumples in pain to the mat, and Flayer, wide-eyed, leaps over the top rope out of the ring to save himself. Rock turns to his fallen partner, and Flayer reaches in under the ropes to pull Golding out by the feet. With a glare back toward the ring, he helps his partner back to the dressing room. The Grinch makes the call: "THE WINNER OF THIS BOUT, AS THE RESULT OF A DISQUALIFICATION: 'FLASH' JOHNATHON FLANN!!!" The fans cheer as Rock brings Flash back to his feet and raises a hand in victory. Rock points menacingly out to the Scorpion and Flayer, on their way back to the dressing room. The Blade Runners may have won the battle, but the war is far from over. ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ And so, Flash chalks up a DQ win over "Greased Lightning" Damien Flayer, and Flayer's losing streak comes to three, lasting to his loss of the U.S. Title to "Crimson" Joe Reed. That's about it from the Stilt Man for now.
