The founding vote, 4 December 1989

rec.sport.pro-wrestling was not created by decree; it was voted into existence, the way any Usenet newsgroup of the era had to be. A Call For Votes went out to news.announce.newgroups on 22 November 1989 stating the group's charter — an unmoderated forum to discuss professional wrestling as it aired on television and in arenas — and inviting readers to mail a yes or no vote to a volunteer vote-taker. This page is the museum's record of that process. It replaces a document that formerly served the vote-taker's own working list of who had voted, name by name and address by address; that list is retired from public serving in favor of the counts below.

The document this page stands in for was itself a checkpoint, not the final word: posted 4 December 1989, it acknowledged 137 individual addresses as having been credited with a vote so far, explicitly invited anyone whose vote had gone astray to resend it, and promised a second call before the tally closed. That second and last call followed on 10 December. The vote closed with a result posted 25 December 1989: 159 votes for the newsgroup and 48 against — a margin the vote-taker judged to clear both the 100-vote minimum-difference rule and the roughly 2:1 yes/no ratio that later became formal Usenet convention, even though this particular vote had been called before that convention took effect. The newsgroup followed within days.

The vote-taker posted from a personal UUCP-gateway account, forwarding each stage of the process — the call, this 4 December checkpoint, the second call, the result — through the moderated news.announce.newgroups, where a moderator approved each posting before it went out. The addresses acknowledged along the way show the mixed, decentralized shape of the pre-web Internet: routing paths threading through university computing systems, corporate and commercial hosts, BITNET gateways, and plain UUCP site-to-site relays with no domain of their own. A precise count by address class is not attempted here, because a UUCP routing path records the relay hops a piece of mail traveled through, not reliably the voter's own institution — counting hops as affiliations would manufacture a false precision this record cannot support.

Those 137 acknowledgements, and the 207 named votes in the final tally, are not served by name. Curation rule v109(d) holds credit- and roster-of-real-people documents back from serving by name by default, and this vote-acknowledgement list is held the same way: the fact of the vote, its date, its tally, and its mechanics belong on the wing's public record; the individual voters do not. The original document — unedited, with every name and address intact — remains collected in the workshop for anyone doing primary-source research into the newsgroup's founding.
