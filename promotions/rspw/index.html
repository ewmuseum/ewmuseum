Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

rec.sport.pro-wrestling was argued into existence in the winter of 1989. The call for discussion had gone out under a different name, rec.arts.wrestling, and a preference vote settled on this one instead; the call for votes of 22 November 1989 then put the group itself to the net under a charter of a single sentence — “To discuss the ‘sport’ of pro wrestling, as it occurs on TV and in arenas throughout the world.” It squeaked by, 159 for and 48 against, and the vote-taker said he would issue the newgroup on 27 December.

What it became is in its own paperwork. By the time the group kept a Frequently Asked Questions list, the list had a section on fantasy wrestling: what the Dreamcards were, who booked them, that the first ran on 11 November 1991, and what the Fictional Wrestling Federation was. One sentence posted on 23 November 1992 counted nine fantasy federations running on or around the group at once.

That was never a settled arrangement. The oldest of those federations had been asked to take its results off the newsgroup as early as 1990 — politely by some, not so politely by others — and its own founder later agreed the request had been fair. Posters kept naming rec.games.pbm as the proper destination. The FAQ of 1 November 1992 carried the fantasy section; its successor of 16 December deleted it as junk, and the Mini-FAQ that outlived them both kept one short answer to the question of what those posts even were.

The argument was resolved by the mechanism that had created the group in the first place. A request for discussion of 5 April 1994 named the fantasy leagues as one of the main generators of the unrelated messages, and the vote it led to failed, 96 to 59. A second attempt of January 1995 asked for something else: a shared home where all the e-wrestling federations could post their reports, advertise themselves, and find each other. That one passed, 179 to 60.

A newsgroup has no closing act, and this one has not had one. It carried fantasy wrestling before there was anywhere else to carry it, argued about carrying it for five years, voted it a room of its own, and went on talking about televised wrestling afterwards. Its last e-wrestling exchange on record is of June 2018.
