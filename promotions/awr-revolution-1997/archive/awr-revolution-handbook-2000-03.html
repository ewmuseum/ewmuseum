The AWR [American Wrestling Revolution] opened its gates for the first time in the fall of 1997. Another one of the pack, they all said. The AWR was "blessed" with bad luck ever since its starting moments. Problems seem to follow the AWR and whatever the AWR staff did, the problems keeps sticking to the fed. This spells in periods of greatness quickly followed by dangerous down falls. Dedication is what kept the AWR standing up until today. Another era starts now. New soldiers grin within AWR’s ranks. Time once again for a revolution!
2. Men running the show. Currently in charge of the AWR is president [name withheld]. He returned to the AWR after a two year break and in 2000 he promised the
AWR will climax and set new boundaries in wrestling. He signs the cheques, he prepares the surprises, he sets the rules and whenever needed "he lays the smack down". He likes to play by the rules and always stuck to the good side of things [as he likes to say].
By Lupsa’s side at this time is one of AWR’s trademark names, [name withheld]. The second VP in AWR history [after [name withheld]’s time in 1998] is committed, dedicated and ready to sacrifice for success. He is AWR’s top recruiter and a man of many ideas.
3. AWR History 3.1. The Tagg/Schilling days. The AWR opened its gates for the first time in the fall of 1997. The two men in charge back then were [name withheld] and [name withheld]. The AWR's roster soon grew as the fed was well run and was a promising investment in fun and work. Under [name withheld] and [name withheld]'s hands, the
AWR saw its first champions, its first feuds and created its first idols. Around December 1997 the fed was shut down due to some problems the presidents encountered.
But that was not for a long time. In February 1998, the AWR kicked back at full force gaining some new names and handling even tougher battles. The two weekly shows took place Wednesday, the show being called "Wrath" and Sunday, the unforgettable, "Slaughter". New title tournaments took place to determine the righteous of the AWR. The valid titles that the AWR had during this time were the AWR World Belt,
US Belt, TV Belt and World Tag Team Belt. That period of time is marked by many great events in the AWR: the giant feud that existed between
"The Show Stopper" Scott Jamison (World Champ at that time) and "The Steroid Boy" Flex Bicep (US Champion at that time) or the battles featuring Chris "Thunder" Lupsa and Andrew Peters, or even the formation of AWR greatest faction ever, The Silent Rapids. Things were going pretty well, until out of nowhere faith struck the AWR at the beginning of April. [name withheld] was unable to help [name withheld] in running the AWR, and [name withheld] just couldn't devote the same amount of time he used to.
3.2. The Lupsa days But this time, the AWR stood tall. [name withheld] and [name withheld], two of AWR's top names decided to take over. [name withheld] soon saw he couldn't spare enough time to lead the AWR to power so he left it to Chris. The
AWR by then was pretty weakened by all the changes and presidential shifts. Many handlers had left and the AWR was sick for quite a while.
Things started to look good again around the beginning of May. The cards were better than ever, the AWR had its own weekly newsletter, entitled "AWR Reporter" and many new starts joined to add up to the talent already on the roster. The cards were held Tuesday and Friday and were entitled "Turmoil" and "Fury". Major events came one after the other: the feud between LA Johnny Stylz, Crow and Flex Bicep that led to many bloody events, the cockiness of "The Legend" Chris Eaton and his fights with Alex "The All-American Pryde", the introduction of the
AWR RingMaster belt and more. But the AWR (as always) was in trouble again at the end of June. [name withheld] (due to personal problems) was forced to drop management of the AWR. He gave it to [name withheld], but he never really did anything.
3.3. The [name withheld] days So, the people of the AWR decided to have a board decide the matches. The board consisted of [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], and Mike. After a few weeks, it became obvious that the board wasn't working. [name withheld] disappeared (he later e- mailed and explained some family problems), [name withheld] quit, and [name withheld] quit.
At that point it was clear the AWR was in trouble. One man kept his cool and took over as president, [name withheld]. Things were going pretty well, until he graduated from college in December 1998 and lost computer access. He didn't want to shut the AWR down, so he did some asking and [name withheld] (handler at that time) took over.
Things didn't work out and the AWR was shut down for the second time. Luck just wasn't going to help the AWR. [name withheld] never gave up on the idea of the AWR. He contacted former handlers and got the federation back on its feet. The AWR restarted in June 1999. [name withheld] didn't have a computer at home so everything he did, was done from work. The cards weren't fully written out, but the love for the AWR kept many of the handlers at their place. The AWR had only one card per week during this time. Held on Monday, it was entitled "Madness". [name withheld] returned in the AWR life in November 1999 and took over the AWR on the 1st of
January 2000. 3.4. The Lupsa days [II] Lupsa’s return saw a new AWR. A revolutionary running policy, a mature way of dealing with the issues at hand and complete devotion to all AWR freaks around the world. During these times, the ratings grew sky high, the signings were more and more valuable, the fan response was huge. With stars like Kash, Keizmen or Stryker leading the pack the
AWR seemed to head strong. The dream collapsed when Kash left after an injury, a new force called the Blood Revolution resigned and president
Lupsa faced some legal issues. The string didn’t break and the AWR kept breathing. And now it’s starting to grow in speed once again… Join the ride…
4. Joining the AWR. To join the AWR you must first fill out the application. Try to make the best out of your app if you want to be a part of the AWR.
Devote time to filling the app, and the creation of your character. The apps will be sent to
[detail withheld] [President [name withheld]]. You will be notified via e-mail within 3 days if you are accepted or not. If not accepted, you will be given specific reasons and suggestions for improving your chances of getting in.
5. Match writing in the AWR The AWR encourages a mixture between the ABS [Action Block System] and PBP [Play by Play] styles of match writing. ABS means that most of the match is a summarised move for move within square brackets, and the announcers comments are used to break up the paragraphs. PBP means that the whole match [move by move] is called by the announcers.

7. The AWR crew. Note: More on these characters in the AWR Crew web page. 7.1. Commentators Deion "Money" Flash - as you have seen he likes to be an ironic, cynical son of a bitch. He always dresses sharp, adores women and loves hardcore. He never actually stepped in the ring as a wrestler but as a colour commentator he rocks the mic. He is the black haired, green eyes playboy type that only likes to hang with the shining stars.
"Lethal" Lance Vader - in comparison to Deion, he was a wrestler. Forced into an early retirement after a terrifying attack that affected his spine. That's why he likes fair things, mat technical wrestling and is not a fan of outside interference. He is a mark for fair-play and clean matches. He is your typical face commentator and stands for everything considered right.
"Sexy" Spade Ruff - both wrestler and a commentator. He took a break from wrestling and is now working the mic. He is not stupid...he is just ignorant and likes to play the fool. Always dresses crazy and has wild calls during matches. Doesn't really fall for something in wrestling. He is pretty much neutral.
7.2. Interviewers Jennifer Ann Mortimer - daughter of Roger and Ann Mortimer. This beauty has quite the family tradition to fall into. She was born on July 23,
1971, that would make her astrological sign that of a Cancer. Of which she exhibits to the tea. She can be very perplexing as she can range from timid, shy, dull and withdrawn to brilliant, friendly and flirtatious. She is full of human emotion and is very fun loving by nature. She appreciates the security of a relaxing home when the everyday life bears to much for her.
Roger Mortimer an executive at NBC for the last twenty years has rubbed off on his little girl immensely. Having had the opportunity to bring Jenny with him to his work at will. She has seen and adapted the purposeful, energetic, and shrewd business like intuitive attitude.
Having a very strict philosophy and view upon life, she is tenacious in defending her beliefs.
Her mother Ann Mortimer the retired secretary of Roger Mortimer. Left the everyday hustle and bustle of the news/media lifestyle and started writing romance novels at home. The romance novels were a great success and lead to a vivid career. Ann Mortimer tends to be on the jet-set allot while researching her characters for her novels. She has a script in the works as we speak and is attempting to get her daughter cast and away from her horrid job as a spot reporter for the barbaric world of professional wrestling.
Jenny is a single women who is shy and yet very seductive when she wants to be. She likes to tease and be treated like a lady. She awaits her knight in shining armour to ride up upon his firey steed and carry her away from it all. If her knight fails to reach his goal. She is quite content on playing the field and working on her up-start broadcasting career. [full bio on the page]
Jacob Keifer Daniels - son of Len Daniels, now a retired reporter, and Julia Daniels, a retired criminal prosecutor. He was the youngest of five children, three boys and two girls, which meant he always got beat up. He became interested in pro wrestling at the age of 10 when his brothers started doing moves that they saw on TV to him.
As he went to high school, he tried out for wrestling but just couldn't cut it. It was at this time when he decided if he couldn't wrestle then he would be an announcer or be a reporter for a federation. His father said it wasn't practical and wouldn't support his son's dreams. Len always wanted his son to follow in his footsteps, to become a world class reporter, just as his father was. Time rolled by and by the time Jacob was ready to go to college, he had followed in his dad's footsteps. He went to Harvard and got his degree in less than three years.
Jacob still wanted to do something with wrestling though. He sent out applications to major newspapers as well as to wrestling organisations. He got a positive reply from the Times and from the
NJWF. He decided to put wrestling off for a year or so, but then realised that the work for a newspaper wasn't what he wanted to do. He eventually left the Times and went to work at the NJWF. His father resented him for this but tried to be supportive. Jacob moved to Philly in early '98 where he first met with the AWR.
It would be an understatement to say that the AWR wasn't impressed with him. They didn't even give him so much as a second look.
This was a crushing blow to Jacob. But one day around a year or so later the AWR called and asked him to do a couple of interviews for them. He instantly jumped on this without even asking who the person was. Luckily for him that person was none other than the man no one else could do a good interview with, Adam Stryker. But the AWR saw his work and they were thoroughly impressed and with that they offered him a full-time contract in the late months of 1999. [full bio on the page]

9. AWR’s main rules. NOTE: More on AWR rules on the web page. 9.1. Roleplaying. Roleplays are sent out in packs by [name withheld] [Error! Bookmark not defined.]. There will be three roleplay packs per week. That does not mean you have to roleplay three times a week. It just gives everyone time to roleplay in a week’s time.
To win a match you MUST have good roleplays (RP). Do not expect to win any matches with one paragraph roleplays. Also, don't expect this week's roleplays to win you next week's matches. All RPs must be at LEAST 25 lines in length. If you have a difficult time filling 25 lines with just dialogue; describe things like what your wrestler is wearing, where he is, or what he was doing before the cameras found him. A good roleplay introduction is highly recommended and appreciated. Anything can be used as long as it can be justified in the roleplay. Posting a RP that is shorter that 25 lines can also have an adverse effect on your next match.
RPs must not contain any anti-Semitic, racial, or other such unnecessary comment. Sexual comments are allowed on a very stringent basis (you are not allowed to continually make sexual references). Any comment that personally insults a person's race, colour, or religion will be deemed null and void. If RPs frequently contain this problem, termination will result. Swearing is allowed but, PLEASE, keep it at a minimum. Cussing does not make a bad roleplay good. If you have to use a lot of cussing to make your roleplays good, then maybe you shouldn't join this fed. It's OK to threaten wrestlers, but do not threaten handlers!!! Also NO use of another wrestler or manager in a roleplay unless they agreed to it. Roleplays should be mailed out directly to everyone through the mailing list.
Spam mail is not acceptable. Thus you are not allowed to use the mailing list for sending messages. Informing the roleplay list of your e-fed, site, or anything else of that nature will result in your immediate termination.
All roleplays will gain you 1 point. 9.2. Strategies The AWR didn’t use strategies until now that is. It’s absolutely clear you need strat sheets in a mature fed. It improves the match and it definitely helps character build up. Strats must be though over and developed as it would be a matter of life and death. A simple sentence saying: "X wins because Y trips accidentally" is no strategy and will never become one. For each match you are about to fight, you have to fill out a strategy sheet which you will send out to
[detail withheld] . Strategy sending schedule. - for Wednesday’s card strats are due in Tuesday afternoon. - for Sunday’s card strats are due in Friday afternoon. Here is the strategy sheet: * Wrestler's name and match in which he is involved: * Ring Entrance: [Include Mike’s ring introduction and all the other stuff just as you would like to see in on a card] * Match Plan: [ an overall picture of the match and how your wrestler will go at it, also some certain spots you’d like to see.] * Winning/Losing Situation: [What your character will do in each] 9.3. Getting paid It's simple actually. Dealing with large sums of money is not only a pain in the ass for the board and AWR staff but for the handlers also.
Not to mention that who knows, one day we might actually believe that we are handling these sums and that would be BAD. So, the AWR translates these sums into points. And after thinking of the employment method, we chose a modified "per-work" basis.
What does this mean? It's simple actually. A wrestler can gain his points from roleplaying, participating in matches, taking bribes (well yeah - maybe you wanna buy out the big man instead of facing him) or being awarded special bonuses by the board. There are many other possible ways to extra points income so keep thinking and stay open to imaginative interpretation.
Losing points is also possible here. Lost matches, fines or bribing others will more likely result in point flow depletion. But don't worry. It is not that dramatic.
There is one thing not be forgotten here. TRANSFERING POINTS FROM ONE WRESTLER TO ANOTHER (even if the same person handles them) IS NOT LLOWED AND WILL NOT HAPPEN. Sorry. Ok… Here is the point scale for all involved in the AWR. Some are set for good and some vary depending on the handlers. If you have any questions about the scale or about the way some of these decisions will work just email the AWR president [name withheld] at
[detail withheld] and you will receive the answers: Each Roleplay - gain 1 point Winning a normal match - gain 1 point Winning a gimmick match or a TV title match - gain 2 points Winning a US Title match - gain 3 points Winning a World Title match - gain 5 points Losing a normal match - lose 1 point Losing a gimmick or a TV title match - lose 2 points (counts only for the one losing the belt)
Losing a US Title match - lose 3 points (counts only for the one losing the belt) Losing a World Title match - lose 5 points (counts only for the one losing the belt) Draw in a normal match - both gain 0 point Draw in a gimmick or a TV title match - both gain 1 point Draw in a US title match - both gain 2 points Draw in a World title match - both gain 3 points Fines - vary on the violation (count around 10 points with repeats causing escalation) Pseudo-attacks (voice blaring the speakers, simple appearance etc.) - lose 1 point Before/After match attacks - lose 2 points During match attacks and brutal interference - lose 3 points Counter attacks - lose 3 points Bribes - vary on the handlers (the bribe offer must be sent to both the AWR president and the handler you wish to bribe. If the bribe is accepted, the bribed must send an mail to the president notifying him of that.)
Bonuses - you might get some. Each handler will receive 5 points to start with!!! NOTE: The president reserves the right (in order to develop angles) to create interference that were not requested. This however will have no effect on your accumulated points
9.4. Attacks/Interference/Counter attacks Attacks must be sent in at least one day before the card to [detail withheld] . Attacks and match interference are always problems in an efed. They tend to piss off the handlers that are attacked and will sometime build up unnecessary heat between characters that should not be feuding at a certain moment in time. It is like having Barry
Horrowitz attack Kevin Nash just for the sake of it. Think well before sending in a sneak attack. Although attacks have their price (see the rule above) there is one more thing to be known. Just because you paid for an attack and you had it placed on the card, doesn't necessary mean you will effect the match outcome. Sometimes you will just show up and attack without influencing the result. If we feel your attack SHOULD decide the match outcome, we will go with that. Also, do not complain if sometimes you are countered. Counter attacks are pretty seldom and hard to write. But if there is a well developed counter attack (*must arrive before an attack*) you may find yourself attacking and getting your ass kicked. Develop your attacks, don't just send in a few lines.
9.5. Bribes There are things to be said here as well. Sometimes, you feel you have been out roleplayed or you just didn't have the time to prepare for a match. You own a considerable amount of points, and you want to win. You mail the other handler and offer him some points (you bribe the guy). The president [
[detail withheld] ] should be getting an email about the offer as well. If the offer is accepted, I need another two emails, one from both handlers stating the number of points the bribe is worth and the acceptance of the bribe. There are many other ways to use this bribe possibility. Mostly they all lead to lost points. But sometimes, you will find that it is worthy. Bribing officials, announcers, referees, managers, even the PRESIDENT are not out of the question. Be creative and this shall be most fun.
9.6. Getting matches You get matches in the AWR in many ways. The most usual way is you challenge for a match. The other is that you are booked by the president. If your challenge is not accepted you will receive notice of why this happened. Match outcome is decided by the quality of your roleplays and your strats.
9.7. Angles, feuds, storylines. In order for a good fed to work, it needs good feuds and angles. Remember that any well run feud or angle raises your chances in winning matches. Feel free to work together in developing angles. You can contact the President as well if you have angle ideas for both your wrestler or some other handler's wrestler. Basically, ideas for storylines are MORE THAN WELCOME.
