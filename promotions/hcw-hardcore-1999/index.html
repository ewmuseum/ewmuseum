Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Hardcore Championship Wrestling staged its own family saga through the Brentwood brothers of Flagstaff, Arizona. Dave Brentwood, the eldest, built his name across the promotion's earliest years before a forced retirement from a career-ending knee injury; HCW President Mike Watson signed his younger brothers Steve and Jack on Christmas Eve 1997 after seeing them wrestle in the independents, debuting them as masked men who were unmasked as the Brentwoods only after facing Dave himself and the Executioner at a card called Extreme Chaos.

By Big A$$ Bash, its own pay-per-view of 27 June 1998, Steve — billed as the “American Heartthrob” — had already won the Extreme Championship from the Ghost Face Killa and challenged “Big Daddy” Donnie DeCosta for the World Heavyweight Championship in a best-of-three-falls main event; DeCosta tried to have the finish thrown out and restart the match after losing the first fall, but Steve won regardless to become World Champion. On the same card, Jack Brentwood won the Extreme Championship himself in a table-and-barbed-wire match against “Sensational” Stevie Rhymes, putting both surviving brothers on top of the promotion at once.

The rest of that card ran through the promotion's own faction lines: the tag team of the Ghost Face Killa and “Suicidal” Johnny Flame, wrestling as the hWo, retained the World Tag Team Championship over Ironman and Scott Diamond after Diamond turned on his own partner mid-match, and “Cool Hand” Luke Jackson retained the Intercontinental Championship over Hotrod with help from a distraction by Hotrod's own valet, Beulah.

An earlier broadcast, Friday Night Aftermath on 6 June 1998, had built toward that same card, and closed with Cool Hand Luke Jackson retaining the Intercontinental Title over Venom.

By the opening part of a later pay-per-view, Random Violence and Low Moral Standards, on 23 January 1999, the World Heavyweight Championship had changed hands again: the Ghost Face Killa reclaimed the title from Cool Hand Luke Jackson, while elsewhere on the same card a returning former Extreme Champion destroyed the Extreme Championship belt itself with a sledgehammer mid-match rather than defend it further.

Only this opening part of that January 1999 broadcast survives past Big A$$ Bash; nothing establishes when Hardcore Championship Wrestling was founded, how the World Heavyweight Title changed hands between June 1998 and January 1999, or how its own record continues from there.
