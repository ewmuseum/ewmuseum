A single-operator fantasy federation renamed from the CWF (Coley Wrestling Federation) on 31 December 1994, running a real-wrestler-portrayal weekly "Spotlight" column and bimonthly pay-per-view pastiches through a self-announced closure on 3 May 1996 -- one of the earliest well-documented single-operator fantasy feds in the held record, traceable end to end from its own founding rename through its own clean closure notice. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The World Championship Wrestling Federation began as a rename: on 29 November 1994 its commissioner announced that the Coley Wrestling Federation would become the WCWF effective 31 December, keeping everything else — its fictional roster of WCW, WWF, ECW and SMW personas, its house rules — intact. He ran it single-handedly, maintaining its own mini-FAQ and writing every card himself.

Its weekly Spotlight column carried the promotion's own storylines, tangled enough by April 1995 that Keith devoted an entire installment, billed as “a Crisis on Infinite Storylines,” to untying them — Sean Waltman chasing a lost urn to reopen his own line to the dead, Virgil airing his career troubles to the WCWF's own resident psychiatrist, “Dr. Sigmund Fraud,” after being fired and saddled with the promotion's own Loser title.

Keith announced an end to “his version” of the WCWF that May, planning to hand the name to a fellow operator running a related federation and take a break before possibly starting fresh with a full booking committee. He kept running it anyway, into a second year, before closing it for real on 3 May 1996 with what he called “a little experiment in creative writing” — a multi-part capstone story, WCWF Alpha through Omega, resolving the Undertaker and Soultaker's fight over the fate of the 1-2-3 Kid. “Basically this is the last WCWF stuff I'll ever do,” he wrote. “Period.”

That August, Keith put a best-of compilation and a handful of old pay-per-views up on his own homepage for anyone still curious “what I was doing at the time,” noting dryly how many of his own angles the real WWF and WCW had gone on to use without him.
