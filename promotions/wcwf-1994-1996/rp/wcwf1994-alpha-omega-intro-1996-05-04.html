WCWF Alpha/Omega - An epic story of sorts by Scott Keith. Historical note: The following takes place shortly after WCWF Summerslam 1995, the last ever WCWF card. Following that card, Raven and Steve Richards had Sean "1-2-3 Kid" Waltman literally killed to prevent him from defending the US tag titles. Reader warning: The following contains EXTREMELY graphic depictions of violent acts of a detailed nature, and much harsh language. If you're under 18 or squeamish about this sort of thing, you might to skip this. I'm not kidding about this. And away we go... Prelude ------- Sometime around the year 1700... Captain Marcus Callous, the most disreputable and vicious pirate of the waterways, stood on the bow of his ship, surveying the sight before him. His crew was the roughest group of cut-throat bandits money could buy, and any one of them would engineer a mutiny in a second. So Captain Callous had one killed every week in a lottery drawing, just to keep them in line. Men feared Callous from shore to shore. His flaming red hair and deep, menacing voice were said to bring kings to their knees in terror. He was, they said, like a ghost, and at night you could see him walking along the ropes of his ship, supported by nothing yet keeping his balance perfectly through stormy weather and hurricanes. Callous had the pick of the merchant ships to loot. He was a rich man many times over, albeit one wanted by the law. Yet there wasn't a royal navy yet who could catch Callous, much less bring him to justice. And now he wanted something more. He wanted a country to rule for himself, and to do so he needed cheap labor. Slave labor. So he took his ship to Africa to find some. Callous always dressed in black from head to toe, like an executioner, and today was no exception. He looked at the island up ahead, and smiled. And when Captain Callous smiled, you knew someone was in trouble. "Ahoy, ye dogs. There be an island up ahead. Bring us in -- and sharpen the swords." Roughly five minutes later, the ship landed ashore of the island. Callous, walking behind an honor guard of about 10 of his most fiendish cohorts, stepped off the ship to the sands of the beach. He saw a most peculiar sight. There, on the beach, were about 10 natives, with the usual ceremonial trappings, chanting at a large cauldron, filled with molten bronze. One man stepped forward, one who was smaller than the rest, but had a skull painted in white on his face. The smaller man chanted more words of power at the cauldron, then the 10 natives tipped it over, pouring the metal into a mould on the ground. The skull-man, deep in meditation, sat next to it and began chanting, and amazingly enough Callous found that fairly soon he could make out the words... "Oh gods...bless this urn with the wisdom of Yourselves. Give it the power of the gods, and curse any man who would attempt to use it for their own purposes. Curse him...for eternity." Callous was not a man of many words, however. "You'll be coming with me, now, my friends." He pulled out his sword, blood red yet still gleaming, to show his sincerity on this matter. The skull-faced man suddenly stood up and snarled at Callous. "Leave this place now. You are not welcome here, and you would not like the consequences should you continue." Callous was unfazed. "I think that I'll be stayin', my friend. And I think I'll be takin' that urn there with me, as well." He smiled again. So did his compatriots. The urn began to do funny things. Rays of light emanated from it. Then, out of the clear blue sky, a bolt of lightning struck the mould, shattering it into a million pieces, revealing the urn, perfectly cooled and glowing with the power of the gods. Callous was almost fazed. And the skull-faced one began to change, too. He got bigger, and more imposing. The ceremonial paint on his chest set into his skin and became an intricate series of tattoos, and the skies grew dark. Callous was fazed. "I think we'll be going, now..." Another lightning bolt shot out of the sky, this one hitting Callous' prized ship, setting it on fire and burning it to cinders in the space of five minutes. Callous was fazed again. "You'll not be going anywhere, 'my friends'" the skull-face said, taunting Callous with his own words. The natives advanced slowly, and from out of the woods came another 100 of their friends, and from out of the water swam another 100, and the slaughter began... One slaughter later, Captain Callous' reign of terror was about to come to a screeching halt. He was lashed securely to a stake in the middle of the beach, wounds so severe that dying was completely preferable to anything the natives might have had in mind. But dying was not on the agenda yet. The skull-faced one spoke. "Know, oh callous one, that you have angered the gods today. You say you want the urn? Then you shall have it, forever. And know, oh callous one, that we shall meet again someday, and when we do, I shall destroy you. We, my friend, are now and forever more eternal enemies, and the annals of history shall sing the praises of our battles throughout time from now until Armageddon. And know this as well -- you shall be burned at this stake, until naught but your ashes remain. Those ashes will be placed into the sacred urn, and your spirit will NEVER be allowed eternal peace. You shall wander the Earth forever, alone. You will inhabit the body of the first man to open the urn, and you will be powerless without the urn. I take your soul from you, Marcus Callous, and the souls of all your descendents. May you burn in the fires of hell for all eternity for violating the island of Pago. The chief priest of the tribe steps forward to add a few more words. He places his hands on the temples of the skull-faced one. "Aboughadien, first born child of this generation and conduit to the gods, I name you high protector of this island for all of eternity. Be now known by the name Papa Shango -- the Soul Taker!" Another lightning bolt shot out of the sky then, igniting the fire around Callous, and engulfing him in flames, and the last thing he saw as he entered a torturing death by cremation was the laughing face...of the Soultaker. - - - - - - - October, 1995. It was all going to hell, and there was nothing the Undertaker could about it. But damned if he wasn't going to try. His corporeal form hovered above the body of Sean Waltman, cut down by a two-bit hood, who was just now slipping off into the shakdows after hastily verifying that his target was indeed dead. The assassin would be dealt with shortly, that much the Undertaker knew. Waltman's bizarre friendship with the pale warrior had at least earned him that much vengeance. But Waltman deserved more. Much more. He deserved to live again. "Oh please..." A deep voice droned out of nowhere, heard only by the Undertaker, "You're not on *that* subject again, are you?" It was, of course, the Soultaker. "Didn't I kill you?" Undertaker replied in a rather perturbed voice. "Aren't we the uptight one? You know, death is very liberating. Loosen up." Amazingly enough, the Soultaker had actually developed a sense of humor since joining the afterlife. It was pretty frightening, actually. "Go...away." He intoned, hoping his even-more-obnoxious-after-death eternal nemesis would take the hint. "Oh, I don't think so. This is much too fun. Watching you brood like this, over some pathetic little mortal." "Go to hell." "Been there. Done that. Bought the t-shirt." He held up a shirt that said "My soul was damned to suffer eternal agony at the hands of the Soultaker and all I got was this lousy T-shirt." "I liked you better before you died." Undertaker replied dryly. "Yeah, well, you're in the minority, pal." The thug who did in Waltman came skulking back onto the scene, because, incredibly, he dropped his keys. Undertaker decided now would be a good time to flex his muscles, so to speak. He started to make himself visible to the crook, when suddenly Soultaker reached out and did what his name implies -- took his soul. The man screamed in agony and fell to the ground. If looks could kill, Soultaker would be dead. Again. "What? Didn't I call that one?" He asked sarcastically, jiggling the soul provocatively in full view of his foe. "I wanted to avenge his death myself." "What, don't you know *any* other words? Vengeance this, rest in peace that, blah blah blah. Get a life." "I have no life to get. I am..." "Yes, I know. It's a figure of speech. Okay, look, I'll make you a deal. I'll flip you for it." He pulled a coin out of whatever passed for pockets in the afterlife. "I win, we arrange it so you never came to the WCWF. You win, and I never came to the WCWF. Either way, it should have a big enough impact so that Stick Boy there will live again in the new timeline. Sound fair?" "The deal is acceptable." "Good. It's a 50/50 shot, just like us, so you can't accuse me of fixing it. Either way has an equal chance. Just like us. We are yin and yang...alpha and omega...Rocky and Bull..." "Flip the coin and let us be on with it." "Call it." The Soultaker said. The Undertaker opened his mouth to reply as the coin went into the air, but was cut off as everything went nuts... Whatever forces provide our heroes with their power split the world into two very seperate and very different paths as the coin tumbled down. Since either possibility could happen, based on the flip of the coin, both were brought into existance, just in case. God takes no chances. One world had no Undertaker, the other no Soultaker. Yin and Yang. WCWF Alpha and Omega. And on both, the 1-2-3 Kid yet lived... [end Prelude.] First name Scott, last name Keith. Don't make me say it again.
