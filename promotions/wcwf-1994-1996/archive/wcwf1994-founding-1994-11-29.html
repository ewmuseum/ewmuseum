[Fantasy] WCWF membership drive

29 November 1994
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Well, kids, it's that time again. The CWF is expanding once again. Sort of. First of all, I'm adding a W to it. As of December 31 1994, it will no longer be the Coley Wrestling Federation, but the World Championship
Wrestling Federation. [Note: There is no wrestling content at all in this message, so hit 'n' now or forever hold your peace.] Basically, I'm changing the name and one of the titles, and everything else stays intact. But I want a few more active members, now that we've lost a couple.
So I'm presenting by way of intro [drumroll please] the CWF/WCWF mini-FAQ! This was created for my own pleasure, then trimmed down for [name withheld]'s fantasy database, and now rspw gets a sneak peek. :) Please read it before responding, and make sure this is really what you want to be involved in.
DO NOT FOLLOWUP ON THE NEWSGROUP! E-MAIL REQUESTS ONLY! Just common courtesy, folks. And please be at least semi-literate. Nothing personal, I just have high standards. ;) And now, without further adieu, the CWF Mini-FAQ... CWF Mini-FAQ version 1.0 Written and maintained by the CWF commisioner, Scott Keith ( [detail withheld] , [detail withheld] , [detail withheld] ) Q. What is the CWF? A. A fictional wrestling federation, populated by wrestlers from the real life WCW and WWF, as well as recent additions from ECW, SMW and the USWA. It is an alternative to the true federations, and a way to explore what betters way there might have been to book feuds, angles, title changes, etc. Having said that, the CWF generally stands on its own without angles from the real federations. The CWF is made up of the commisioner (myself), as well as a number of handlers, each of whom
"manages" a stable of 4 or 5 wrestlers. They can communicate with myself, or other handlers, about plans for feuds and angles. They can make trades, and sign matches. And if they offer is made, they can also write matches for pay-per-view cards.
Q. What shows does the CWF put on? A. The CWF stages a weekly CWF Spotlight show, which is much like the syndicated programs put on by the real federations. It features mainly interviews and special features, with some matches thrown in for fun.
:) Recently I have cut down drastically on the number of "squash" matches staged every week, for reasons I will cover later. As well, the CWF stages a major pay-per-view type event, approximately every two months. The following is a rough calendar of the events of 1994:
Winterwar in January, the Royal Rumble in March, Wrestlemania IV in May, King of the Ring in June, Summerslam 94 in August, Halloween Havoc in October and Starrcade 94 in December. Approximately the same schedule will be followed in 1995, except Winterwar will be dropped in order to more closely match the WWF and WCW schedule. It should look something like this:
Royal Rumble in January/February, SuperBrawl in March/April, Wrestlemania V in May/June, King of the Ring in June/July, Summerslam in August, Halloween Havoc in October, possibly Survivor Series in November, or if not then Starrcade in December. It is the handler's responsibility to sign matches for these cards, or else I will do it for you and you may not like the results. :)
Generally I like to have all titles defended on these cards, so keep that in mind if you manage a champion in the CWF.
Q. So who started the CWF? A. Not me. I'm the third commisioner in the CWF, following in the footsteps of [name withheld] (the founder) and [name withheld].
Brandon ruled the roost from its inception many moons ago until about November of 1993, when he moved to Illinois and seemingly disappeared off the face of the earth.
There was no action in the CWF for about a month or so, at which point [name withheld], a member of the booking committee, stepped in to restart the CWF again. He lasted from November 1993-May 1994, when he feared that his account would be revoked. So he asked for volunteers to replace him, and only one person answered. The rest is history. :)
Q. So what are the rules? A. Short answer: There aren't any, really. Ever since I took over I've just been sort of making them up as I go along. But with the creation of the new CWF in January of 1995, there will a written set of rules for the league.
Q. So who can join? A. Anyone I say can. I like to have a reasonable cap on the number of members (currently it's at 20) in order to preserve my sanity. If two or three members go AWOL at a given time, I post a message to rspw asking for new members to refresh the scene, and the cycle starts again.
It's limited to 2 or 3, first come first served. Q. How do I pick which wrestlers I want? A. Say "I want this guy, this guy and this guy." I might say "That first guy is already taken, but you can have the other two" and we'd negotiate from there. Again, as with most things in the league I'm easy to get along with. If you want to bring someone new into the CWF, by all means go ahead. As long as he is a real wrestler, go for it.
Q. Okay, so now I'm a CWF'er. What do I do now? A: Handlers must schedule matches for their men. They must also set up feuds and strategies for their men. NOTE: Every so often the unexpected happens and a feud starts without you having planned it - expect it. Also, it is good to run feud by me first because the men you intend to mess with may be already prepared to mess with someone else.
Q. So what happened to the rankings? A. Ah, yes, the rankings. Well, if you could follow the ranking system outlined by Bolt (Scott Keith), then you're smarter than me. I always
had trouble figuring out rankings, and when I officially took over I discovered just what a pain in the ass they really are. The main problem I had with them was that you couldn't stage a really interesting main event unless the challenger was in the top 3. That, to me, was bullshit. If I, as commisioner, want to give, say, Ric Flair a shot at upsetting Vader (as he did at King of the Ring), then I don't care where he stands in the rankings. If Vader is instead forced to defend his
World title against the #1 contender (I believe it was either Lex Luger or Mr. Perfect at the time) in a meaningless match, then the event loses something. I would rather see a mismatch with a hell of an interesting angle behind than a ***** match with no buildup any day. That's MODO, of course, but then mine is only one that counts around here. :) So I dropped the rankings. Besides, they weren't good for anything outside of title contendership anyways, again in MODO. (My own damn opinion, for those not fluent in Zimmer-ese). However, if you can come up with a better rankings system, or are willing to maintain the one outlined above on a permanent basis, then I'll reinstate the rankings. But I can't be bothered.
Q. So why so few squashes now? A. Because they too are meaningless. The CWF doesn't have house shows, and the PPV's are always sold out anyways, so I don't really see the point. I'd rather have a JTTS (jobber to the stars) system where you have wrestlers getting wins over established, but down on their luck,
wrestlers of equal ability. Since there's no rankings, there's no need to try and score two easy points, so to speak. Q. Can we make trades? A. Yep. Whoever you like. Just as long as I'm notified. Q. Can we "make up" our own wrestlers, like in those other e-wrestling leagues? A. NO! Definitely not. As booker, I have to know each and every wrestler in order to book matches and feuds for them, and I can't do that if they're just a fictional person. While I'm sure there are some very well-developed characters out there (Like the Doppelganger, and the
New Fabulous Ones, until I killed them off) I don't want them clogging up the CWF. For any wrestler you can create there's a real one out there with approximately the same characteristics. I'd rather you use him.
Q. So who's in charge of the league in CWF-world? A. The fictional president of the CWF is currently evil manager extraordinaire Hiro Matsuda, but he's just standing in. He took over the late Wendi Richter., who was killed in a subplot of The End. She took over for Bobby Heenan (Sousa's choice) and he took over for Rene
Goulet (Coley's choice). It will change again after The End. Q. What is The End? A. Ah! Good question. Well, given the fact that my CWF is so totally separate from the original CWF, I feel it's time for a name change. The End is a way pull off a cosmetic change while shaking up the foundations of the federation in the process. All I can say is that someone will stop the Soultaker, and after that the mysterious Master will reveal his identity, and he'll have to be stopped too, and the resulting damage to the CWF will be so great that a new federation will have to be formed. It will be roughly the same, with the same handlers
(with possibly new stables) but a different name and president. The Coley Wrestling Federation will close it's doors December 31, 1994. The new federation will open it's doors January 1, 1995.
Q. Who all are the champions of the CWF right now? A. Here are the CWF champions: CURRENT: World: Soultaker Intercontinental: Steve Austin United States: Cactus Jack Light Heavyweight: Shawn Michaels Tag Team: The Miracle Violence Connection Loser: 1-2-3 Kid Commisioner: Scott Keith :) Q. So what the heck is the "Loser" title? A. It's the title bestowed upon the "best of the worst." If you get pinned by the Loser champion, you're the new champ. It's a pretty dubious honor, let me tell you. The belt itself is a rope with a big ol' pink L on it.
Q. Anything else I need to know? A. If there is, drop me a line at one of my accounts ( [detail withheld] , [detail withheld] , [detail withheld] ) and I'll answer you pretty quickly. I read my mail all the time so you'll get an answer very rapidly. Mail to [detail withheld] will get the fastest reply.
