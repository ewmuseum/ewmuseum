[WCWF] Infrequently Asked Questions, V2.1

11 July 1995

WCWF Infrequently-asked-but-still-in-need-of-answering Questions List, version 2.1 Written and maintained by the WCWF commisioner, Scott Keith ( [detail withheld] , [detail withheld] ) Q. What is the WCWF? A. A fictional wrestling federation, populated by wrestlers from the "real life" WWF, WCW, ECW, SMW, USWA, NWA and whatever others may exist. It is an alternative to the true federations, and a way to explore what better way there might have been to book feuds, angles, title changes, etc. Having said that, the WCWF generally stands on its own without angles from the real federations. The WCWF is made up of the commisioner
(myself), as well as a number of handlers, each of whom "manages" a stable of however many wrestlers they can handle, usually around 4 or 5. They can communicate with myself, or other handlers, about plans for feuds and/or angles. They can make trades, and sign matches. And if the offer is made, they can also write matches for pay-per-view cards.
Q. What shows does the WCWF put on? A. The WCWF stages a weekly WCWF Spotlight show, which is much like the syndicated programs put on by the real federations. It features mainly interviews and special features, with some matches thrown in for fun.
:) Recently I have cut down drastically on the number of "squash" matches staged every week, for reasons I will cover later. As well, the WCWF stages a major pay-per-view type event, approximately every two months. It is the handler's responsibility to sign matches for these cards, or else I will do it for you and you may not like the results. :)
Generally I like to have all titles defended on these cards, so keep that in mind if you manage a champion in the WCWF.
Q. So who started the WCWF? A. Me. It was my idea completely, and it came into existance on January 1, 1995, with 100% transference from the CWF. Q. So who started the CWF, then? A. Not me. I was the third commisioner in the CWF, following in the footsteps of [name withheld] (the founder) and [name withheld]. Here's a history of the CWF in [name withheld]'s own words:
A Brief History of the CWF by [name withheld] (My memory isn't all that great, so I may get some small details wrong, but I hope I will get most of the important things correct.)
The CWF was born of the extreme boredom of two WPI freshmen, [name withheld] and [name withheld]. On one of our numerous trips to Toys R Us, we saw four WWF wrestling figures which had just came out, and naturally we decided to buy them. We got back to the dorm, and held the first two CWF matches, even though there really wasn't a CWF to speak of yet. So you could say that the charter members of the CWF were Hulk Hogan, Rick Rude, Randy Savage, and the
Ultimate Warrior. Over time, the WWF and WCW came out with more wrestling figures, and we bought them all. It didn't take long until we had enough figures to fill out a decent-sized card. I think every wrestling fan's dream is to book a real federation, so that's what we did. Brandon owned all the figures at this point, so he got to decide all the outcomes of the matches, while I was his captive audience.
The original name for the CWF was actually the BWF, which stood for Brandon's Wrestling Federation. Hulk Hogan was the first champion, and I believe Rick Rude was the first IC champion. The WWF had not released the wrestling ring yet, so our matches wer held on the floor of Brandon's dorm room inside a square made out of masking tape which we imagined was a ring.
We both enjoyed the BWF, and in time a few other people ont the floor also got a kick out of "playing with dolls." So it was decided to hold the first BWF WrestleMania. The Main Event was World Champion Hulk Hogan vs.
IC and TV champion Sid Vicious for all the gold in the CWF. Hogan won that match, but he had to vacate the lesser titles immediately. This threw the BWF into a state of title chaos which would ultimately never be resolved, as the
BWF was becoming really silly, and the end of the school year was approaching. In April of that year, we decided that the BWF would not survive the summer, so it ought to go out with the greatest card in wrstling history, which it undoubtedly did. The Death Rumble would have made FMW look tame by comparison, if we knew about FMW back then. The rules of the Death Rumble were simple. The only way to be eliminated was to be killed, and the match would go on until only one wrestler was still alive. Some amazing highlights of this match included Lex Luger receiving a spike piledriver from the ceiling of the arena, which also killed Arn Anderson who gave the piledriver as well as
Sting, who had the bad luck of being underneath the 'driver. Andre the Giant was killed by Jake Roberts' poisonous cobra, but in his death motions, fell on top of Roberts, killing him. This incident began the CWF tradition of having
CWF events come true in the real world, as Jake Roberts would eventually bring a poisonous snake into the WWF. The Death Rumble was won by Randy Savage, who bashed in Ric Flair's skull with the timekeeper's bell. However, he learned that Elizabeth had been killed in the match, and instead of celebrating his victory, killed himself in the middle of the ring.
And so came to an end the BWF. We returned to school in the fall of 1991, and we very quickly realized that the BWF would not rest in peace. Even more wrestling figures were released during that summer, as well as the WWF miniature wrestling ring. I bought the ring to be used for wrestling, which made me part owner of the new miniature federation. The name we decided on for the successor to the BWF was the CWF, which stood for either the Coley Wrestling Federation, or Collegiate
Wrestling Figures. CWF WrestleMania was a quantum leap beyond what the BWF was. In the first place, there were more wrestlers involved. We had an actual ring.
Brandon had subscibed to the WWF fan club in order to get the introduction music for the CWF wrestlers. CWF WM was actually an attempt to simulate the appearance of an actual supercard. We held it in the evening, turned off all the lights, and shone a lamp on just the ring, as Brandon acted out all the matches with the figures. I and [name withheld] provided the commentary for this historic event.
It consisted of the crowning of the three CWF belts there were at that time. The World title was decided by a round-robin iron man competition of eight competitors: Hulk Hogan, Ultimate Warrior, Randy Savage, Andre the Giant,
Ric Flair, Lex Luger, Ted Dibiase, and Rick Rude. After four rounds of preliminary matches, Hulk Hogan and Randy Savage were tied for the most number of points, and therefore should have wrestled for the World Title. Except that
Ric Flair, who was third in the standings, attacked Hogan backstage, injuring him and preventing him from competing. Flair thus earned the right to wrestle
Savage, and he took advantage, defeating Randy Savage to become the first CWF World Champion. Honky Tonk Man pulled off a huge upset in winning the Intercontinental battle royal, and Demoltion won the tag team tournament. The CWF was off and running. At this point the CWF did not yet have a weekly show, as the PPV's took quite a bit of time to set up and plan, so four of them a year seemed to be sufficient at that time. My memory is hazy with regards to that first year of the CWF with regards to the line-up for cards and title changes. The file that contains the title histories includes all title changes that occurred.
Also this year, the CWF began introducing non-wrestling figures as wrestlers. The WWF released Demolition, Smash and Crush as a tag-team package. We definitely wanted Crush, as even back then we saw his potential to be a great heel. Unfortunately, we already had Smash and Ax. So instead of having two Smashes, Brandon dressed up the older Smash figure with black electrical tape and some gauze, and voila! The Doppleganger was born. We also had a number of other duplicates, so the gimmick of the Doppleganger was the ability to perfectly mimic another wrestler. When he appeared as Hulk Hogan, we used the other Hulk Hogan figure. Due to the devious possibilities, he naturally began his career as a heel, and his first feud was with Hulk Hogan.
He won the first-ever CWF Royal Rumble, when he eliminated Roddy Piper, Hulk Hogan's tag team partner. This began the still-raging and bitter feud between Hogan and Piper, who believed that Hogan had eliminated him from the Rumble. The Doppleganger quickly became one of our favorite wrestlers, and his enormous popularity continues today, even though he has turned face.
Another important creation which occured in the CWF's first year was the Loser Championship. The number of wrestling figures was small enough that every wrestler who had a figure was wrestling in the CWF. However, that also included some of our least favorite wrestlers at the time, including The
Rockers, Tom Zenk, and Sting. So I had the idea to create a title for the worst wrestler in the CWF, which we would award to the wrestlers we hated. If a wrestler lost to the present champion, he would become the new Loser, and would be forced to wear a large pink L around his neck. Due to the unique nature of this title, and for logical reasons, I decided that the title must change hands on a CO or DQ, since no one in their right mind would wrestle a clean match against the Loser Champion otherwise. The first Loser champion was determined in a tournament of the 16 lowest ranked wrestlers. In the final,
Shawn Michaels lost to Marty Jannetty, his former tag team partner, to become the first ever CWF loser champion. The Rockers split up in the CWF months before they did so in the WWF, yet another example of life imitating the CWF.
CWF WrestleMania 2 would fit in here chronologically, but I really don't remember when it happened, or who won what titles from whom.
[end of Sousa's history of the CWF] [name withheld] was the first ever commisioner of the CWF, and he reigned from its inception until roughly October of 1993, when he inexplicably moved to Indiana.
There was no action in the CWF for about a month or so, at which point [name withheld], a member of the booking committee, stepped in to restart the CWF again. He lasted from November 1993-May 1994, when he feared that his account would be revoked. So he asked for volunteers to replace him, and only one person answered. The rest is history. :)
Q. So what are the rules? A. Short answer: There aren't any, really. Q. So what about ratings? A. Go away. Q. No, really, shouldn't you have some sort of quantitative system for determining who's in line for a title shot? A. Alright, already. There used to be a ratings system, but it sucked so I dumped it. Now I just give whoever I feel like the title shots. Q. So why so few squashes now? A. Because they too are meaningless. The WCWF doesn't have house shows, and the PPV's are always sold out anyways, so I don't really see the point. I'd rather have a JTTS (jobber to the stars) system where you have wrestlers getting wins over established, but down on their luck,
wrestlers of equal ability. Since there's no rankings, there's no need to try and score two easy points, so to speak. Q. Can we make trades? A. Yep. Whoever you like. Just as long as I'm notified. Q. Hey! I'm a participant in the DeJWRFP e-wrestling federation, and I've got a really cool wrestler called Max Lord, with his manager Oberon, and he's been World champion for 5 1/2 years now. He's got a 16 point powerbomb as his finisher, and can take 250 points of damage!
A. So what's your point? Q. So wouldn't it be cool to have him be part of the WCWF? A. No, not really. So don't even suggest it. Q. So who's in charge of the league in WCWF-world? A. Although I'm in charge in real life, I don't exist in the WCWF. Therefore we have a fictional president, like Jack Tunney was before he got fired recently. Wendi Richter was the last Madame President and she still is until further notice.
Q. What was The End? A. Ah! Good question. Well, given the fact that my WCWF is so totally separate from the original CWF, I felt it was time for a name change. The End was a way to pull off a cosmetic change while shaking up the foundations of the federation in the process.
Q. And who was/is The Master? And the Druid? What about... A. This is a slightly longer question to answer. You see, as part of the original CWF, [name withheld] included some fine wrestlers who, unfortunately weren't part of the real world. Here's a quick summary of who they were and what happened to them...
a) The Druid. The major player in The End, he was supposedly explorer Michel Levesque 500 years ago, when he was taken in by a cult of island natives and turned into a mysterious powerful wrestler named the Druid.
He later received a nasty knock on the head and developed a 2nd personality called the Master, with which he attempted to take over the
CWF. He died as a result of complications from removing the brain tumor causing the sickness at Starrcade 94. HOWEVER, it has recently been revealed that the "Michel Levesque" identity was just a delusion brought on by the tumor, and in fact "The Master" was someone else entirely who had hired the Druid to do his dirty work for him, only to have the crazed Druid start thinking *he* was the Master. He's better off dead, believe me...
b) The Doppelganger. Another major player in the CWF, the Doppelganger's exquivalent in the real world is Scott "Flamingo" Levy. In the world of the CWF, however, Levy suffered a hideous car accident 5 years ago that left him with a deformed visage and no will to live. His only motivation was his wife and his niece Tina. In order to hide his face, he covered himself in bandages, and disguised himself as the wrestler he was facing, in effect becoming his opponent during the match. At the end of 1994, he finally realized that he had no idea who he really was anymore, and wrestled in his retirement match against Ted
Dibiase at Starrcade 94. He is currently co-owner of the Chickens We've Fried chain of restaurants, with friends Scourge and Plunderer. He lives happily with his wife and niece Tina, despite constant rumors that he will be returning to the ring soon.
c) The New Fabulous Ones ([name withheld] and [name withheld]). The Fabs are the only wrestlers in the federation to be created in another e-wrestling league. They were created by CWF'er [name withheld], and had limited success. Finally, just after King of the Ring 94, they won the
CWF tag team title for the first time ever. However, at Summerslam 94, they were beaten so soundly by the team of Steve Williams and Terry
Gordy that they died from injuries suffered in the match later that night. Oddly enough, it turns out that they were treated by Dr. Vincent
Baats, a known quack, who diagnosed them as dead without actually doing any diagnosis. They turned up very much alive, albeit with amnesia, much later.
d) The Dark Pirate, Scourge. Not much to say about him, except he was another wrestler made up by [name withheld]. He was put out of wrestling after being betrayed by Hiro Matsuda around August of last year, and is currently co-owner of the Chickens We've Fried restaurant.
e) Misu Nagami. A fictional Japanese star, whose name comes from the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles III movie. He too had limited success in the CWF, and was unfortunately involved in the same attack that put
Scourge out of the wrestling. Nagami, however, did not survive that attack. f) The Plunderer. One of the best light-heavyweights in the CWF, the Plunderer suffered an injury at the hands of Shawn Michaels before Wrestlemania IV, and never really recovered enough to make a full comeback. Also a co-owner of the Chickens We've Fried restaurant.
