E-Wrestling Ringpost #2 (excerpt: the WLW years)

10 December 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[...] [name withheld]: E-Wrestling seems to be your fun as well. When did you start E-wrestling? Mark Rosendorf: About three years ago, I first became aware of E-wrestling when someone advertised their league on RSPW. It looked interesting, so I entered my wrestlers, "Top Dog" Rick Murano, and Gigantus. The league started off with a tourney for the title. Gigantus was counted out in his opening round match, Murano won his. That was the last I ever heard from the league. Soon, I entered the WLW, when I saw them advertising, only my characters went through a couple of name changes, as well as gaining a manager. [name withheld]: When did you first know that Rick Styles would become as big as he did? Mark Rosendorf: Honestly, originally, Gigantor was my main character. He won the WLW world title, while Styles won the US title. Later, in the GCW, Gigantor became world champion there, while again, Styles was fighting for the secondary title. In the DWO, Gigantor won the Extreme title, while Styles couldn't even get a match. Finally, in the UEW, I decided to concentrate more on Styles, since he seemed to fit the UEW mode a little better, and I just happened to stick with him ever since. Up until the UEW, though, Styles was always involved in the big stuff, like the Inter-fed wars, the War Games stuff, but Gigantor was my main character who I pushed towards title wins. [...] [name withheld]: What federation do you/did you enjoy most? Mark Rosendorf: Currently, the UEW. But the WLW was without a doubt, the best fed I've ever been in. It didn't have the names other feds had, but Adam and Dan, who I've lost contact with since then, ran it. This was their first time running a league or involved in E-wrestling at all, so they had no previous biases, favorites, or ideas of how it should run, but they were really creative geniuses. They came up with things that were really exciting, and everyone loved the fed. Every Sunday Night card was like a typical league's PPV, with interviews, league characters, angles, etc. A pity it only lasted 5 months since they burned themselves out, literally. [...] [name withheld]: Do you think that's where your path to greatness really started? Mark Rosendorf: Heh, I don't know if we can call it a path of "greatness", but it's fun for me. Anyway, I'll say I learned a lot in the DWO, but my start and what molded both of my characters I have to say was definitly, the WLW. Dan and Adam tended to take "liberties" with characters that always seemed to work, and stick. Initially, believe it or not, my college roommate's character (Kyle Kamikaze) was a Sabu clone. Dan and Adam changed his ring style and made him the character he is today. Let's just say, though, when my characters brawl, they got that out of the DWO. [...] [name withheld]: Where did the idea for the New Alliance come from? Mark Rosendorf: Ooh, long story. My first PPV: from the WLW. We knew it was coming for abotu 3 months. It all revolved around a big War Games. The initial teams had Styles captaining one team, and Spartan Troy Walters captaining the other. It started off 4 on 4 (my team was Styles, Gigantor and Mayhem Inc). Soon, Spartan got a 5th partner, so I recruited TWO more. Then they recruited and I recruited. Gigantor won a battle royale and go the title match at that PPV (which he won) so now we needed a new partner. When the event finally took place, it was 7 on 7.. The presidents wanted team names. Spartan picked The X-treme team, I picked the Alliance. The Alliance consisted of Styles, Mayhem Inc, The Executioner, The Black Ninja, the Supreme Punisher and, announced the night of the PPV, the rookie, Kyle Kamikaze. We won, using a strat of mine (My first big strat), but immediately following our win, Mayhem inc turned on all their team members and laid us all out..While I thought it was pretty slick, I declared that the Alliance was dead!! There would be no more Alliance!!.. A month later, when everything was forgiven and forgotten, they helped me out in a match, in return that I help them out in a match. We made such a good team that night (we both re-gained titles) that The handler of Mayhem Inc wanted to reform the Alliance with the promise of no backstabbing this time. I'm a man of my word, so the NEW Alliance was born. [name withheld]: Speaking of the New Alliance, a lot of people refer to Kyle Kamikaze as the "Arn Anderson" to your "Ric Flair". Is this true? Mark Rosendorf: Kyle Kamikaze's handler was my college roommate. I brought him in, and yeah, I helped him get his feet wet in E-wrestling, just as he helped me out in allowing me to bounce ideas off of him to see how they sounded. If you're comparing him to Arn Anderson in that he's a key team member to the New Alliance the same way Arn is to the Horsemen,, if you're comparing Kyle to Arn in that he works his ass off for the good of the stable, if you're comparing Kyle to Arn in that both have brought respect and titles to the New Alliance, then yes, it's an accurate statement. Right now, with the Suicidal Stallions and Alex Extreme, I feel we have a very strong stable, but since the beginning, it's always been Kyle Kamikaze and Rick Styles. Throughout all the teams in the past hat made up the New Alliance, Rick Styles and Kyle Kamikaze have always been the tradition of the stable in the same way Ric Flair and Arn Anderson were for the Horsemen.
