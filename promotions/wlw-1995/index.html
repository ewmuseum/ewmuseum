A hand-booked flash-and-strategy federation whose 1996 run the community twice remembered, months after its own closure, as one of the best e-feds ever run -- and whose founding operator spent all of 1997 failing to bring it back. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World League Wrestling launched on 31 December 1995 with a bare wrestler-creation sheet posted to the newsgroup, promising a computer-simulated season run on “simulator 5.1.” Two first-time operators turned it, within months, into a hand-booked weekly show with its own three-person broadcast team, calling cards from the Rose Garden in Portland, Oregon, under a banner reading “Where the World Comes to Play.”

Its own first Achievement Awards, held in April 1996, put a name to what the season had produced: Samhain won Best Gimmick and had already become the promotion's first recorded World Heavyweight Champion; Rick Styles took Best Trash Talker; Spartan, Most Popular Wrestler; Grave Digga, Best Newcomer; and Bret Young, Most Hated Wrestler, for attacking his own cousin on-air.

The promotion closed within the year, in its own presidents' own words: “As the Presidents of the WLW, we regret to inform you that World League Wrestling is CLOSING, effective immediately... The work load has become more than we can handle.” Two attempts to bring it back followed in 1997, in March-April and again in August, the first proposing an unusual five-person voting board to decide match outcomes; neither produced a surviving card, and the second attempt's own promotional reel promised only that the promotion was “coming soon.” One of the relaunch attempts drew a public accusation, from the handler of the original promotion's own television champion, of claiming credit for work that belonged to its actual former writers.

The promotion outlived its own closing notice in the community's memory rather than in any ring: a December 1997 interview with one of its former wrestlers called it simply the best fed he'd ever been in, run by two first-time operators who “were really creative geniuses,” and said it lasted about five months because they burned themselves out; two separate reader polls that same December still placed it among the internet's best e-feds, more than a year and a half after it stopped running cards. Nothing past 1997 is in the surviving record.
