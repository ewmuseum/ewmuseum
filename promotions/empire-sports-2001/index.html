A spring 2001 independent whose complete house handbook and recruitment campaign survive from a six-week launch window, with no card confirmed to have aired. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

EMPIRE Sports Entertainment announced itself on 28 March 2001, promising “a brand of wrestling never seen before,” headed by a president who had previously served as president of the WAO.

Its kayfabe staged a founding story for an in-character owner, Darren Donaldsworth, who decided wrestling had “gotten stale” and resolved to “make wrestling cool again.” The son of two Boston business lawyers and an owner of a local tavern chain himself, Donaldsworth flew to Hollywood and struck a deal with a production company, EMPIRE Entertainment, for a weekly broadcast from its own studio, plus a Sunday-night slot on GEN X-TV, the same network that had carried the WAO the previous year.

Its staff named head commentator Chris Foster, a former Boston sportscaster; color commentator “Maimin” Damon Taylor, a former shoot-fighter; and ring announcer Aaron Ashley Anderson, with a commissioner's hire still promised for the “first edition of Sunday Night Destruction” — though its own handbook table of contents names the same show Saturday Night Destruction, one of a few small inconsistencies in its own surviving paperwork.

Its rules laid out a two-to-three-month pilot season with no title matches and no pay-per-views: each week, four matches on its flagship broadcast put an EMPIRE contract on the line, the winners earning a further spot on its studio show, EMPIRE LIVE!, with the pilot set to end once thirty-two wrestlers were signed. Afterward, a money system was to reward wins — a thousand dollars for a pinfall, thirteen hundred for a submission — spendable toward title shots and storyline pushes. Five championships were named at launch: the EMPIRE World Heavyweight, Television, Skyweight, World Tag Team, and Skyweight Tag Team titles.

Its last surviving document, a recruitment plea on 12 April 2001, begs for applicants outright: “Please?”. Whether EMPIRE ever signed its thirty-second wrestler and closed out its pilot season isn't in the surviving record, and neither is how or when it ended.
