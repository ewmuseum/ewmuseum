A roleplay-booked newsgroup promotion that expanded from two singles titles and a tag title to an Intercontinental championship and a Lightweight division within two months, then relaunched in the summer of 1997. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Fan Friendly E-Wrestling opened on 28 May 1996 with a direct pitch: “This is the E-Federation that you have been waiting for.” It set a 20-singles, 10-tag-team cap, let wrestlers settle their own challenges — “You challenge someone....YOU FIGHT THEM” — and planned nightly events plus one pay-per-view a month, four in summer, built around two singles titles and one tag title whose champions had to defend twice a week and at every PPV.

Growth came fast: by the very next day it had “14 wrestlers and urge more,” promising an Intercontinental Title tournament once two more signed on, and its homepage went up on GeoCities within the week. By 29 July it had opened a Lightweight Division capped at 230 pounds — “NO REAL OR IMITATIONS” — scored on an eleven-category point-buy out of 200.

A roster dispute surfaced that June. On 8 June its own operator publicly warned other feds against an applicant calling himself Saber Jade, whose submitted stats — 7'10", 243 lbs, managed by Jim Cornette — he called “obsurd,” laying out his own three rules for a fantasy league: proper proportions, no rudeness, no real names. Four days later he reported the same man bribing wrestlers away from FFEW with “money, posters etc.” and reopened recruitment outright: “COME ONE COME ALL 'COS FFEW WANTS WRESTLERS.”

Its own web address moved from that GeoCities page to a personally branded Angelfire site by November 1996, when its Fed Head and owner put out a direct call for match writers to help run the promotion.

It relaunched under its own name on 20 July 1997 — “Old Fed.....New Beginning” — now describing itself as “a Booked E-Fed, with wins based on roleplays,” building toward a Royal Rumble-style card called Mega Rumble on 30 July. The new schedule ran cards Saturdays and Wednesdays with a monthly PPV, a Monday roleplay show for challenges and feuds, a Thursday recap show, and open permission for wrestlers to form their own stables.

No card, match, or title change survives from either its 1996 run or its 1997 relaunch, and no document after 20 July 1997 is held in this record. The only surviving trace that its title picture was ever settled is a mention years later, elsewhere, crediting a wrestler with once holding FFEW gold; how it was won, or how the promotion ultimately ended, is not in the surviving record.
