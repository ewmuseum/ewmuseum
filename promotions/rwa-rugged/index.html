Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Rugged Wrestling Association billed itself, in its own words, as "an e-mail wrestling simulation league" — results and rosters posted to GeoCities, matches decided over e-mail rather than a simulation program. It shut down in February 1998, its own closing notice directing members toward its operator's next e-fed, the ICWA.

Nuke and Trent Malady fought the fed's own World Heavyweight Title tournament final to a 30-minute draw that left the belt vacant; Malady won it outright the following week in a Triple Threat, and held it until Da Messiah's own interference handed it to Nuke off-page. Kontra then took it from Nuke in a Scaffold Match staged as payback for that same interference, and stands as the fed's own last-attested champion. Three more titles carry their own reign ledgers on the fed's own served Rankings page — the U.S. Heavyweight, World Television and World Tag Team Titles — each showing a champion the corpus's own held matches never crowned, an off-page title change every time.

Da Messiah steals the World Heavyweight Title Written by AI, 2026

Nuke and Trent Malady fight the fed's own tournament final to a 30-minute draw, both men kicking out of each other's finishers before Malady counters a piledriver into a head-spike victory roll for a 2¾ count — the belt stays vacant, and the two are rebooked into a Triple Threat with Pete Stevens the following week. Malady wins it there, reversing Nuke's own piledriver a second time and finishing him with the Maladjuster while Stevens lies spent in the corner; Nuke offers a handshake, and Malady walks past it without a word. He defends the title six days later against Axon Blade of Da Messiah, standing in for an injured Nuke — Da Messiah jumped him backstage before the bell — and retains by DQ when Kontra and Danix run in; Da Messiah turns on Malady with the belt itself until a staggered Nuke runs out to fight them off, and Malady leaves without acknowledging the save. Sometime in the weeks that follow — off-page, no held document records it — Da Messiah gets Nuke the title anyway: by the fed's own next broadcast, its own commentary calls it his "newly won" championship and credits the same stable with the win outright ("he never would of beat Trent Malady himself"). Nuke defends it there against Kontra in a Scaffold Match billed as payback for that very interference — the fed's own main event, and its own last word on the title before the served corpus ends. Kontra stands as champion on the fed's own later Rankings page; the match's own outcome is not part of the held record.
