North American Championship Wrestling OLD SCHOOL WRESTLING, NEW SCHOOL ATTITUDE History Basic Federation Explanation The Five Commandments of the NACW Titles Shows and Cards Staff On-air staff Behind-the-scenes staff Application HISTORY Well, in name, the NACW has been around since September of 1997. Only problem is, it's been closed, reopened, closed again, then reopened, and finally closed in July of 1999 when Justin Russell (that's me) wanted a fresh start. However, I feel that I'm finally read to run a federation I can enjoy as much as you guys do. After all, if Brian J. Blottie endorses it, by golly, it must be good, eh? Oh, the history.
Anyways, the NACW was started in September of 1997 by Shawn Coats as a federation to makeup for that fact that [name withheld] had closed the
NACW. Coats closed the federation in April of 1998, and was then reopened at the insistence of Justin Russell in January of 1999. After a lot (and we mean a lot) of fighting, the federation was closed back in July of 1999.
After a few failed attempts to run "different" federations, it always helps to go back to the old formula. And thusly, the NACW is back in action.
BASIC FEDERATION EXPLANATION The reason our slogan is "old school wrestling, new school attitude" is simple; I'm sick of seeing the new style in a lot of federations. Mainly:
overblown, unrealistic hardcore matches on every single card; about five or six titles per federation; way too many of the same "rich", "badass", or
"tough mofo" types that all get monster pops. Pick and choose; each federation has at least one of each. Let's face it, not everyone is like that. In fact, there should be something different. What we're offering is simple; the old school style with the new school attitude. You'll see what we mean, but we guarantee a fun experience for all.
THE FIVE COMMANDMENTS OF THE NACW Why the five commandments? Simple: there's five of them, and they are set in stone. That means, plain and simple, we're not going to change these rules for anyone or anything. If you don't like them.don't join. These are the rules that will not (and I mean WILL NOT) be broken. There may be more, as I gradually get more upset with the way things are going.
COMMANDMENT #1: NO "AOL/SHIT-FEDDERS" ALLOWED. The definition of an "AOL/shit-fedder" varies. For me, it means, someone who's characters flat-out steal the gimmick of a "Big Three" wrestler. The names are similar, the ring styles are similar, and the attitudes are similar. This also expands to include anyone who uses another wrestler's music (ECW doesn'
t fall in with this one, as they use real music. I'm talking about someone who puts "Triple H's music" or something similar in his or her app). I'm not saying all AOLers are barred from the NACW; that couldn't be further from the truth. However, if your character is a knockoff (deemed by me; you may not think that, but hey, you're not the one judging apps), then you aren
't getting in. This also includes the usual "AOL/shit-fed" tactics. In other words, using "smart" terms, using other wrestlers/commentators/managers/general staff in your roleplays to put you over without permission (such as having Bobby X be in your roleplay saying how good you are and how scared he is of you if you are fighting him on the next card, or attacking anybody), and the general stuff.
COMMANDMENT #2: NO COMPLAINING. This is another "AOL/shit-fed" tactic that needs it's own commandment. If you lose, it's because I felt you should. I may have an angle that is furthered by you losing (but if that's the case, I
'll tell you about it before hand). You may feel otherwise, but like I said, I'm the one making the calls. Don't worry, everyone loses. It's a fact of e-wrestling. Also, I have a thing about flash length. I don't care if you wrote the equivalent of the Odyssey in your flash; you might lose if it's boring. A 2K flash can beat a 20K flash if the 2K is more entertaining. You can write as much as you like, but if I doze off while reading it, sorry, it's not going to win.
COMMANDMENT #3: NO SLANDERS IN ROLEPLAYS. This should never be brought up because common sense dictates it: no racial slanders, no sexuality slanders
(meaning no slanders about homosexuality OR heterosexuality), no slanders on religion, and no gender-based slanders. None. Not even if it helps your gimmick. Plain and simple, if people put these in their flashes, who gets the complaints? Me, that's who, and I'm just not nice enough to do that.
So don't do it. I also don't care if the character your character is going against just happened to be a homosexual. If I see anything along the lines of "faggot" or "cocksucker", I'm asking you to remove it. And hey, guess what; that means you can't insinuate that there are sexual favors being exchanged. In other words, no saying "well, Billy Z is sucking Bobby X's dick so Bobby X will protect him". Unless you're Pat Patterson, it doesn't happen.
COMMANDMENT #4: CUSSING IN ROLEPLAYS. You can do it, but use common sense. If you can say it on network TV, you can say it. If you can't say it on network TV, you can do two things; mask it (a [BLEEP] works nicely) or use another word. You guys are creative.
COMMANDMENT #5: NO HARDCORE DIVISION, ULTRA-VIOLENT HARDCORE MATCHES, OR MATCHES WITH RIDICULOUS STIPULATIONS. None. I'm sick of them. Most hardcore titles are excuses to see how much your wrestler can bleed. Same with ultra-violent matches. These matches with unusual stipulations are, well, weird. I mean, we don't need to see turkey-anus-on-a-pole matches any time soon. That means we aren't having them. Don't worry, we'll still have something close to hardcore matches, but this is old school wrestling. It should be noted that these matches should only be used as "blowoff" matches to end a feud. Our "hardcore" matches are as follows.
-STEEL CAGE MATCH: Meaning just that. You fight inside of a steel cage, NWA Mid-Atlantic-style. That means pinfalls and submissions only. -I QUIT MATCH: Force your opponent to say "I quit" on a microphone. -"LIGHTS OUT" STREETFIGHT: It's a match unsanctioned by the NACW, meaning no titles are up for grabs. Come as you want, tape up your fists, and use whatever you can find. Knockouts are the only way to win.
-TAPED-FIST MATCH: Both fists are taped heavily, meaning punches are rock-hard. Knockouts are the only way to win. -SCAFFOLD MATCH: Throw your opponent off of a scaffold held about 15 feet about the ring. -LADDER MATCH: A title belt or a special prize is hung from the ceiling, and the only way it can be reaches is by using a ladder. -FALLS-COUNT-ANYWHERE MATCH: Meaning just that. Falls count anywhere in a designated area. -TEXAS DEATH MATCH: After you pin your opponent, there is a thirty-second rest period. If your opponent can't get up after a ten-count, you win the match. TITLES The NACW, in the past, has had several titles. However, with the shift to old school wrestling with a new school attitude, things have changed.
-NACW World Heavyweight title: Yes, of course, the main strap. This is for the best of the best: mainly, the people who have clawed their way to the top. It's the main goal for everyone. This title is defending once every
30 days, which times out to about once every four cards. It is also defended on every supercard/pay-per-view.
-NACW Television title: TV titles are usually regarded as passé nowadays.not to us. The TV title is a proud title to hold. Granted, if you hold the TV title, you're defending it every week against the #1 contender as deemed by me, but it's the springboard to the World Heavyweight title.
-NACW World Tag Team titles: Teams are welcome, but they must be teams. Two singles can't just pair up and shoot for the gold. They must be regular teams. If we get enough teams, the belts will be up for grabs. If not, the belts stay dormant. It's up to you guys.
SHOWS AND CARDS First, and I really should have explained this in the commandments, but there are no flash shows. You only get one chance to roleplay; that means flashes will air on the cards. Let me all-cap that; FLASHES WILL ONLY BE ON
CARDS. One flash a week, that's all I ask. The shows/cards are, as follows. -NACW Rampage: This is the weekly show. Usually headlined with a TV title defense and containing five or six matches (depending on whether or not I have enough writers or reasons to book a sixth match), as well as interviews with the wrestlers.
-Supercards/pay-per-views: um, I haven't named them yet. The usual fare, though. STAFF ON-AIR STAFF (in character) -Steve White: The "journalist" commentator. He's more concerned with calling a match than dealing with the commentator he usually gets saddled with, which is. -Dan Mazin: Dan's the joker, the arrogant jerk. He loves the rulebreakers almost as much as he likes verbally torturing Steve. -Drew Fortesque: The ring announcer. Sorry, but Drew's kinda bland, so he gets no bio. -"Supreme Being" Jeremy Rhodes: This is the commissioner. Jeremy is a former NACW veteran, as well as a retired wrestler. He is a manager for AGW and, with 13 years of experience wrestling as well as being the third generation of a strong Irish/English family, he's more old school than anyone on the staff.
-Mikey Johnson: The senior ref.and the only ref. Mikey's fair, impartial, and treats everyone equal. -Justin Russell: The owner of the NACW as well as a former three-time NACW World Champion.
