The NACW is looking for some talent. Take a look at our handbook. North American Championship Wrestling 1. What is the NACW? The whole thing that started the NACW began nearly 3 years ago, in a federation called the Hardcore Wrestling Alliance, run by [name withheld]. The HWA ran for about 8 months, before it fell to time constraints. The NACW was thus created by Shawn Coats, who ran it for about a year and a half before handing over power to his nephew, James. James cleaned house, and was left with almost nothing, so the NACW died. Coats, refusing to let it die, opened up the Global Wrestling Circuit not shortly after. The GWC closed recently, when a new NACW was brainstormed. 2. How is the NACW ran- AKA the FAQ. (FAQ Compiled by Justin Russell.) FAQ... Q: Who's going to be the president? A: There will be no president. There will be a board of seven people, and we will determine the match results. Q: Will staff members be allowed to have wrestlers in the federation? A: Yes, they will. Q: How will the staff members be impartial in voting if they have wrestlers in the federation? What if they are voting on a match they are in? A: Simple; staff members will not be allowed to vote on matches in which their wrestlers (or allies of their wrestlers) are involved. Therefore, the vote remains impartial. The Alternate board members will come into play at this time. Q: Is it true that Shawn Coats is returning to e-wrestling for this? A: Yes, it is. I cashed in a few favors to get his writing talents, and he is begrudgingly joining up as a NACW board member. Board members will also have to write at least one match for the card. It's a requirement. Q: Can I become a board member? A: Not at the moment. Q: What roleplaying system will this be under? A: A closed roleplaying system. Send RP's to one address (which will be released later). 3. Can you give me an over view of how the NACW looks at the moment? Sure. First, we'll discuss our card procedure. CARDS... -Sunday Night Slam Masters: This is our main card. Seven matches, interviews, run-ins, and so forth. -Supercards: A card held every month (on PPV months, the final card before the PPV). These usually contain up to ten matches, and sometimes have World title matches. -Pay-per-views: These are big, big cards that only happen once every two months. World title match is always in the main event. About twelve matches. Roleplay Packets will be sent out periodically. As for the Titles, here's a quick look at the divisions we have to offer. ~NACW World title. This title is for the best of the best of any weight class. It's a belt filled with legacy, being the former HWA world title belt as well. Here's a look at some who have held it- "Big Dog" Justin Russell Jason "Icefire" Deegan Jeremy Rhodes The Master Edge ~NACW US title. This title is generally considered to be one of the top contenders of the NACW. It's been held by all kinds of number 1 contenders, including Alan Soriano Billy Classon Jason Deegan Ray Garcia ~NACW Television title. This is the midcard title. It's commonly used a springboard into the front guard of the NACW. It's been held by former NACW greats such as: "El Diablo" Eddie Deegan Ray Garcia Master Edge Billy Classon ~NACW Cruiserweight gold. This gold was the hardest to obtain. And the reason for this was simply- two men had a stronghold on it. Eddie Deegan Billy Classon ~NACW Tag Team titles. The best Tag teams in the land. It's been defended by people who specialize as Tag teams, or two singles wrestlers forming into invinicible teams. Ghetto Boyz Justin Russell and Ray Garcia Love Makers Jeremy Rhodes and Chris Crow And you might want to know a little bit of the backstage production... FICITIONAL FOLK Owner- -Co-owners Shawn Coats and Paul Davidson see things from the owners box. They also serve on the board of directors and the championship committee, but they are the ones who sign the checks. BROADCAST TEAM -Steve White - Our lead commentator, and one of the most knowledgable men in wrestling. If you want a move name, someone's history, adn so forth, Steve's the man to talk to. -Dan Mazin - Every federation's got a heel commentator, and Dan Mazin is the best of the best in that category. Everything the heel does is great in his opinion. -Ross Howard - Our backstage man with a microphone on a mission. He's the one to turn to when a wrestler has something to say backstage. -Adam Pulsford - Our senior commentator, he'll only come in and handle something when he's needed. -Bryce Wilson - This is the best announcer in the business when it comes to bringing the men to the ring. Howard Finkel, David Penzer, eat your hearts out! REFEREE TEAM REFEREES... -Mikey Johnson - Mikey is our senior official, and a veteran referee of the e-wrestling rings for over two years now. Mikey will give you a fair shake in the ring, but if you break the rules and he catches you, you're going to hear about it! Mikey also has the power to overrule a referee's decision, so if you win a match unfairly, Mikey will probably end up reversing the decision. -Scott Whitman - Our youngest official, he loves to sit back and watch the action, even letting the wrestlers brawl through the aisles and use weapons at will. He also loves to let the fans see the extreme action, so if you draw Scott as your referee, then you can count on an all-out brawl. -Brian Stevens - A former wrestler by the name of Bryan Storm, Stevens is our biggest referee at 6'4", 260 pounds, and he's not afraid to stick his nose to the grindstone and break up a fight. He's also not afraid to DQ you if you break even the slightest rule. -James Deeley - Every federation has a referee that's in the pocket of the heels, and Deeley is our "Jeff Jones." He always ends up favoring the heels, so if Deeley gets pulled for your match, thank the Lord that you are a heel. His decisions usually get reversed by Mikey Johnson, but he can cover a few of them up.
