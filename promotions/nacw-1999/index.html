Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

North American Championship Wrestling traced its own history back nearly three years in the recruitment handbook that announced its latest incarnation, 16 December 1998: a first promotion under these initials founded by Shawn Coats, handed to his nephew James, who “cleaned house” until it collapsed; a follow-up, the Global Wrestling Circuit, that Coats ran and eventually closed; and finally this fresh board, organized without a president — “there will be a board of seven people, and we will determine the match results” — that opened its first card, Sunday Night Slam Masters, on 10 January 1999. “Big Dog” Justin Russell compiled its own FAQ and ran the promotion; Shawn Coats himself, per that FAQ, was talked into returning as a board member.

It ran a weekly Sunday broadcast, monthly supercards and a pay-per-view every two months, deciding matches by board vote on submitted roleplay, with any board member who had a wrestler or ally in a given match barred from voting on it. Six championships ran the card — World, United States, Television, Tag Team, Light Heavyweight and a Toughman title — the World belt billed as carrying the lineage of the original Hardcore Wrestling Alliance title before it.

Its own record turned physical more than once. The Television Championship changed hands by forfeit at its second World Tour pay-per-view when the promotion's own on-screen commissioner ruled a champion unable to defend it, “due to his recent arrest for assault... serving 75 days in a state pen back home in Cleveland, Ohio.” The same card's Toughman title match was fought on a burning-table-ringed scaffold lowered from the usual twenty feet to eight “because of the Owen Hart incident”, per its own broadcast team; the champion, the Flying Scotsman, retained by throwing his challenger off the rig, only for that same challenger to grab the belt and flee with it as the show moved on.

The promotion closed on 14 July 1999, and its own members said goodbye to each other on the newsgroup the same day. One thanked Justin Russell by name for running it despite feeling the booking staff's own wrestlers were overpushed; another, a board member, answered that the card-writing had in fact been split between three people plus a “commissioner” role, and that several of the wrestlers named as favored were “only a handler” rather than a booker's pet. A third poster, confusing this promotion with the earlier, unrelated NACW that Coats had first built, was corrected in the same thread: “Totally different federation.”
