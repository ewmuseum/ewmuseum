Founded in kayfabe on a rival's collapse and staged bi-weekly at the Hammerstein Ballroom, EWA ran Shatter Point out of New York in 2011 behind a World Heavyweight title and a 24/7 Underground belt defended in a board only its own champion could read. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The fed moved onto its own site on 15 April 2011, and said plainly that this was a move: earlier boards, on a host the record does not name, stayed up as an archive of the work already put in. What it built on the new site was a news module, and that module is most of what survives — thirty-one numbered entries between April and August 2011, announcements and hype and backstage reports, one of them written entirely as in-character Twitter traffic.

It ran four championships: World Heavyweight, International, Tag Team, and the Underground. The Underground is the one the fed advertised as innovative, and it earned the word — a 24/7 belt with out-of-character rules of its own, challengeable by anyone but the World Champion at any time by sending the word “Challenge” to the front office, first come first served, and defended out of a board only its own champion could read.

Its rules kept changing in public. It abolished the women’s division on the grounds that having one was the problem — men and women in the same matches for the same belts, “and none of this mixed crap”. It invented an open-roleplay mechanic where a promo posted “For response” became a booked match, and recruited a standing committee to judge them. On 23 July 2011 it stopped being a no-disqualification promotion, announced in capitals, with a note in the same breath that the change was not a storyline.

Shatter Point ran at least six times and Retaliation once. The results for all of them lived on a second domain of the fed’s own; only one survives, Shatter Point, 5 July 2011. The site was still live and still taking new threads in May 2012, with a pay-per-view called Rising from the Flames announced and never reported. No closing post was written.
