A roleplay federation founded in 1998 by a wrestler-rpg-dice booker and relaunched under a new on-air president in June 1999, with an equal-opportunity title-shot policy and five named championships. It reopened again on 1 March 2002 with a roster of returning legends, ten structured cards and six championships. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Legend Pro Wrestling introduced itself on 1 August 1998 as a dice-and-chart federation, its own founding post promising two weekly cards, a bi-weekly Sunday show and a monthly pay-per-view, all resolved by “special dice and charts from a real wrestling rpg gamebook” rather than roleplay alone, under the motto “Where the superstars of today become the legends of tomorrow.” A follow-up two weeks later refined the system: a poor roleplay gave the opponent “a slight temporary increase in his attributes,” nudging but never deciding a match outright. No card, roster reveal or match result from this run survives in the record.

By its own later account it was rebuilt at least three more times under the same founder-president before becoming a different, darker-themed promotion, and its founder retired from e-wrestling roughly two months before the events described here. In May 1999 a new operator announced Legend Pro Wrestling's return at a second web address “with his permission,” reopening 1 June 1999 with an equal-opportunity title-shot policy: “anyone can get a shot at any title, regardless of rank,” a promise its own announcement called something it had “never saw... any of them make” among the dozens of leagues its new operator claimed to have visited.

The relaunch listed four titles at first — World Heavyweight, a National title renamed for each champion's own home country, Cruiserweight and World Tag Team — and five by the time it actually opened, adding a Hardcore title, run across the same two weekly cards and monthly pay-per-view as the 1998 run. A World Heavyweight title tournament was announced to begin the week of 11 June 1999.

No result from that tournament survives. The relaunch's own last surviving document, posted the same week, reports a slow response to recruitment and raises the possibility of falling back to the promotion it had branched from if signups did not pick up; nothing further under Legend Pro Wrestling's own name is in the record after that.
