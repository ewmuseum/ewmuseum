RVRL — History/FAQ

River Valley Rumble League FAQ

15 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
RIVER VALLEY RUMBLE LEAGUE (RVRL) Little but a memory in the minds of many, River Valley Rumble League [RVRL] was once the penultimate wrestling territory in the mid- to late 1980s. The home of many future superstars and wrestling greats, RVRL, for many years, provided the states of Missouri, Arkansas, Mississippi, and Louisiana with some of the greatest mat wars the business has ever known. Unfortunately, RVRL would meet its untimely demise in 1996 when financial difficulties and the burgeoning success of Big League Wrestling [BLW] forced the league to fold and ultimately merge with the monopoly. Nonetheless, RVRL had left behind a legacy of wrestling not easily forgotten by true fans of professional wrestling. However, like the proverbial phoenix, RVRL has arisen from the ashes to bring to wrestling fans its unique brand of wrestling action! - - - - - - - - - - HISTORY - - - - - - - - - - In the Beginning (1978-1980). River Valley Rumble League originally started in 1978 as a showcase for a group of Midwestern and Southern based wrestlers trying to get noticed by the triad of major wrestling organizations: Global Wrestling Federation [GWF], National Championship Wrestling of America [NCWA], and, of course, the aforementioned BLW. Spearheaded by former 70s great "Diamond" Jack Tatum, RVRL, then called Missouri Valley Wrestling, primarily featured local talent wrestling in high school gyms and state fairs throughout the Southern Missouri/Northern Arkansas area. Known for its brand of unusual characters, foremost among them the massive Samoan known as King Konga, and thrilling action, Missouri Valley Wrestling quickly gained in popularity. By 1980, Missouri Valley Wrestling was a mainstay. Gaining Momentum (1981-1982). With significant profit gains and burgeoning competition from other regional based wrestling organizations, primarily Razorback Wrestling Association [RWA], Tatum chose to reorganize Missouri Valley Wrestling and increase its exposure throughout the state. Renaming it River Valley Rumble League, the league began spreading into the northern part of Missouri and southern Arkansas, establishing strong footholds in St. Louis and Little Rock . Tatum also sanctioned a State Heavyweight Championship and sponsored a tournament for the title. In June 1982, RVRL crowned its first RVRL State champion, the charismatic "GQ" Johnny Handsome. RVRL would later crown its first State Tag Team champions in the form of "Big" Brad and "Dangerous" Dan Baker, the Baker Boys. Breaking Down Doors (1983-1984). In 1983, RVRL absorbed the struggling RWA, merging its own State titles with RWA's Arkansas State titles. In an epic battle that still ranks as one of the best matches of all times, RWA's resident cheater and Arkansas champion "Dirty" Dick LeBaron defeated "GQ" Johnny Handsome to become the first ever RVRL Heavyweight champion. In a grueling match of no less intensity, the Baker Boys would unite the RVRL and RWA Tag Team titles by defeating the RWA titleholders, Mr. Puerto Rico II and Masked Menace. RVRL would only continue to grow, extending its territory well into the Jackson, Mississippi, area, where its remaining competition in the region, Delta Pro Wrestling [DPW] still stood strong. However, DPW, like its predecessors, would eventually fold into RVRL. By the end of 1984, RVRL had become the only wrestling body of significance in the entire region. Trouble Arises (1985). However, RVRL did not go into 1985 without its share of problems. Having flown under the radar for so long, RVRL finally was noticed by BLW, which, by then, had grown into the top wrestling organization in the country. BLW began raiding talent from RVRL, which refused to sign a talent development deal with BLW. However, this unfortunately did not stop BLW from raiding some of RVRL's top draws, primarily RVRL Heavyweight champion "Dirty" Dick LeBaron and RVRL Tag Team champions Bryan Random and Billy Vance, the heart-throb duo known as the Tampa Bay Express [later repackaged in BLW as Heavy Metal]. A Decade of Mediocrity (1985-1995). Having lost a great deal of its talent to BLW and to retirement, RVRL was a mere shell of its former self by the time the 80s ended. With a roster full of low-budget BLW copycats, past-prime performers, and lackluster rookies, RVRL slowly drifted into obscurity. No longer the top draw in the area, RVRL saw tremendous drops in attendance and revenue. The End (1996). By the end of 1995, RVRL could be likened to a snake with its head chopped off: It flailed about for a while, but eventually, it died a slow, painful death. Bankrupt and devoid of worthwhile talent, RVRL closed its doors, ending nearly two decades of wrestling action. The rights to the RVRL name and archives were auctioned off to BLW, and the league's remaining performers and staff sought out work in other venues. A New Beginning! (2006). Through some legal maneuvering and a few loopholes in the contract, Tatum has reacquired the rights to the RVRL moniker along with its full archives from BLW. Initially, Tatum simply sought to regain the vast revenues being generated from the purchase of RVRL DVDs and memorabilia; however, with a growing interest in RVRL abuzz in his ear, Tatum decided to do the impossible: to raise RVRL from the dead. - - - - - - - - - - RULES & INFORMATION - - - - - - - - - - RVRL is not an attempt to reinvent the wheel, per se. However, RVRL is also not just a standard, angle based, biweekly e-wrestling league; it is the chronicling of a legend. Championships-along with wins and losses-are relatively incidental; the main focus here is as follows: 1. To work together to tell a story of magnificent proportions. 2. To bring to life a unique brand of characters. 3. To produce a product unparalleled by any other in the genre. RVRL is being cast as a regional, relatively low-budget operation. As such, RVRL will operate on a smaller scale than a traditional organization. To participate in RVRL, a handler will be responsible for two (2) items each week: 1. Strategy. Handlers must provide the league with a brief synopsis of their wrestler's strategy for their forthcoming match. Strategies should not be longer than a full paragraph and should address the wrestler's overall plan along with a few specific spots. Below is an example of a suitable strategy: Wrestler X has been feuding with Wrestler Y for quite some time, so he would like to clearly put this feud to an end. As a result, Wrestler X will be particularly aggressive in this match. Wrestler X should rush Wrestler Y as soon as the bell rings, pummeling him with a series of punches and kicks. Wrestler X will then proceed to wear down his opponent with a few holds, such as a variety of suplexes and such. As the match progresses, Wrestler X will then break out the more forceful moves in his arsenal, looking to take control of the match. Look for him to try to capitalize on Wrestler Y's tendency to use the top rope by crotching him on the top rope and then delivering a crushing belly-to-back superplex. Wrestler X will then try to finish the match with his finisher. If Wrestler X wins the match, he'll then make a quick exit. If not, expect Wrestler X to attack Wrestler Y with a chair, getting his last bit of revenge. 2. Promo or Soundbite. Whether a handler produces a promo or soundbite will be determined by the card in which his or her wrestler appears. Promos-which for the purposes of RVRL are defined as full-length comments by a particular wrestler-are reserved for the full card whereas soundbites-or brief snippets of comments-are saved for the house show correspondence reports. Promos are a valued commodity in RVRL and should not be taken lightly. Because RVRL is not televised, all promos must be made in front of the audience, which means either in the ring or at ringside or somewhere thereabouts. While promos should contain full text and descriptions, it should also be relatively brief so as not to bore the crowd. Below is an example of a suitable promo: ["Bad to the Bone" by George Thoroughgood and The Destroyers cranks out from the arena loudspeakers as Wrestler X flings back the aisle curtain to a chorus of cheers from the fans. He pauses, basking in the fans' applause, before storming down the aisle, slapping hands with the occasional fan.] [As he enters the ring, his music fades out. He grabs a microphone from a ringside attendant.] WRESTLER X: Orlando, how the hell are ya? [The fans let loose with a pop.] WRESTLER X: Y'know me. I ain't the type to come out slingin' a bunch of trashtalk. Me? I'm a fighter, and that's what I came to do. I came for a fight. So Wrestler Y, get yer sorry ass down here and fight! [The fans pop loudly as Wrestler X stalks the ring, awaiting Wrestler Y. Wrestler Y never arrives.] WRESTLER X: C'mon, ya coward. I know yer yellow-bellied sorry ass is back there! Hell, I can smell that cheap ass cologne ya wear from here! [Another pop from the fans.] WRESTLER X: Well, I tell ya what: Since you ain't man enough to come out here, how 'bout I show ya how much of a man I am and come back there?! [The fans let loose as Wrestler X hops over the top rope and storms back towards the locker room area.] Again, the focus here is on brevity, consistency with character, and plot advancement. A promo does not need to be lengthy in order to get a point across. Soundbites. A soundbite is even briefer than a promo. A soundbite is essentially what amounts to a quote from a wrestler that will appear in the remarks of a house show correspondent. A soundbite, like a promo, should focus on brevity, consistency with character, and plot advancement. Below is an example of a suitable soundbite: After the recent houseshow at the Merritt Island Civic Center, Wrestler X remarked, "Let me tell ya somethin', son. Don't nobody stick their damn nose in my business and not expect to get it punched clean the hell off! Since Wrestler Y is feelin' a bit froggy, tell the bastard I said, 'Jump.' I got a little nose arrangement waitin' for him over here, and it ain't no damn Dr. 90210, neither!" Each week, wrestlers will be assigned either a promo or soundbite. More than likely, a wrestler will only perform a soundbite unless he or she is featured on RVRL's flagship program, Thursday Night Rage! - - - - - - - - - - TITLES - - - - - - - - - - RVRL HEAVYWEIGHT CHAMPIONSHIP The top title in RVRL, the Heavyweight title has an illustrious history. An amalgamation of the original RVRL State title and the RWA Arkansas State title, the Heavyweight title has been held by such superstars "Dirty" Dick LeBaron and "GQ" Johnny Handsome. The Heavyweight title will be defended at least once every month. RVRL MISSOURI VALLEY HEAVYWEIGHT CHAMPIONSHIP A tribute to the old MVW, the Missouri Valley title is the secondary title in RVRL. The Missouri Valley title will be defended at each RVRL Thursday Night Rage! RVRL TAG TEAM CHAMPIONSHIP Once held by the legendary Baker Boys and The Tampa Bay Express, the RVRL Tag Team titles have an illustrious history. - - - - - - - - - - SHOWS - - - - - - - - - - RVRL, a small regional operation, does not have a broadcast deal. As such, RVRL does not have a formal show, per se. Instead, RVRL action will be distributed in two forms: a detailed summarized card and a correspondent's report of a house shows. Thursday Night Rage! (TNR) is RVRL's main card. TNR! usually takes place in mid-level venues throughout the River Valley territory. RVRL features the top performers in RVRL. As such, TNR will only feature no more than three (3) promos per card. TNR will be distributed each Thursday evening/Friday mornings unless stated otherwise. The Blain Report surveys the landscape of RVRL by providing a summary of RVRL's house shows, which typically take place in smaller venues throughout the River Valley territory. The report will also feature RVRL's Casey Blain's thoughts on the action throughout the week, the week's schedule, soundbites from RVRL's wrestlers. The Blain Report will be distributed each Sunday evening/Monday mornings unless stated otherwise. - - - - - - - - - - STAFF - - - - - - - - - - "DIAMOND" JACK TATUM-RVRL's presiding president, Tatum is the silent voice of authority of RVRL. Tatum prefers to remain behind the scenes and only makes an appearance when strong armed tactics become necessary. Having a good rapport with the talent, Chavez is able to find out all of the inside dirt and pose the intriguing questions. ROY HARDEE-The official announcer of RVRL.
