A week-old rec.sport.pro-wrestling inventing its own overseeing body for dream matches between television wrestlers — a World Heavyweight Championship, a title lineage, and results posted card by card. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

RSWA arrived less than a month after the newsgroup that carried it. Its founding post went up on 22 January 1990, less than four weeks after rec.sport.pro-wrestling was itself issued: “OK...what we've all been waiting for! The RecSport Wrestling Association!” The idea was a monthly card of dream matches between wrestlers “at the peak of their careers,” submitted by the newsgroup itself and tallied by its founder.

The same post carried the first card — fifteen matches from a fictional Memphis broadcast, headlined by Bruno Sammartino defending an invented RSWA World Heavyweight Championship against Andre the Giant. A companion post laid out the fed's whole premise: RSWA sat above the real promotions as the only body permitted to sanction a genuine World Champion, with the WWF's, NWA's and USWA's own titles reduced to Federation belts underneath it.

Results followed four days later, on 26 January, along with a second card already booked for Tampa. In between, another poster entirely wrote up one match his own way — Nikita Koloff against Lex Luger — a comic blow-by-blow that disagreed with the founder's own account of who won it, the newsgroup already talking back to its own fantasy fed within days of its first card. Some of RSWA's cast were already dead by January 1990 — Bruiser Brody, David and Mike Von Erich — cast anyway because, in the founder's own words, they were still “fairly familiar to many of us.”

Nothing of RSWA survives past 28 January 1990. The Tampa card it had already announced carries no results post of its own in the record.
