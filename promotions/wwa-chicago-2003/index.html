Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The World Wrestling Association introduced itself in a founding post of 27 August 2003 as an experiment in reader-steered storytelling: having written every character himself, its creator invited fans to suggest angles and trades between two in-house brands, the East Coast Wrestling Federation and the West Coast Wrestling Federation, each carrying its own titles under a unifying Undisputed Championship and tag title, modeled, in its own words, on “how the WWE did with its undisputed championship at the beginning of their brands.”

Its first announced schedule (29 August 2003) set the East Coast brand's Mayhem at Madison Square Garden and the West Coast brand's Slamfest at the Great Western Forum in the same week, each running a full undercard, with a Week in Review broadcast promising the first look at the Undisputed titles ahead of an October pay-per-view, WarZone.

Its next surviving broadcast, House of Pain (11 August 2004), stages itself as “the first broadcast of the returning World Wrestling Association,” relaunched out of Chicago under president Tori Brooks and broadcast, for one week only, from a sports bar down the street from its own arena while the arena's control center finished construction.

That card ran a debut win for JAG over veteran “Naturally Gifted” Peter Hand, a triangle match Kid Kabuki won over Beaver LasVegas and Irish Devil, and a bloody no-contest brawl between Gravedigga and A Force of One that spilled from the ring into the aisle after A Force of One publicly demanded a spot in the main event. It closed on a tag match won by Scott O'Callahan and “Black Action Hero” Mike Means over The Outpatients, repeatedly interrupted by a rival stable, the Unstable, and on a cliffhanger: A Force of One tracking Gravedigga and O'Callahan down at the broadcast's own bar setting as the feed cut to black.

Whether that confrontation, or House of Pain's own promised return two weeks later, ever aired isn't in its surviving record, which ends with this broadcast.
