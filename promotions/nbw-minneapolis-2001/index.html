Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

New Blood Wrestling opened its own Player's Guide, posted 18 January 2001, with a sales pitch for the state of the industry itself: “The sport of wrestling has hit its peak. The decline is inevitable... For wrestling to remain mainstream, a change must take place.” That change was staged as the brainchild of two co-owners, retired wrestlers Dallas “Perfection” Reed and Jeremy Steele, who built the promotion around what they called the System: wrestlers locked inside a converted Minneapolis warehouse arena, the Cell, for hours before and after every show, ranked by winning percentage, and filmed everywhere backstage — every locker room, every hallway, with a reporter roaming for reaction — under contracts that waived their right to privacy inside the building.

Two weekly lock-in shows ran from the Cell: Seek and Destroy on Wednesday nights, hosted by Tommy Vaughn and Mitch Decker, and No Escape on Sunday nights, hosted by Vaughn and Tony Adams, each run under a strict lock-in and lock-out schedule. Three titles were up for grabs, each paying a bonus per diem to its holder — fifty percent extra for the Northern Title, seventy-five for the World Tag Team Championship, and a full doubled salary for the World Championship — and the guide staffed the promotion with a full broadcast crew: commissioner Barry Schwartz, play-by-play from Decker, color commentary from Vaughn, and ring announcing from Jack King.

Cards were written in a summary style twice a week, mixing commentary, analysis, and interview transcription, with only pay-per-views given full match-by-match coverage. By a follow-up call on 10 February 2001, after the promotion's first two shows, its roster stood around twenty, with only about half actively contributing, and its own president revised the format to two floating shows every five days and asked for more active wrestlers to fill out the card.

Those three posts, spanning less than a month, are everything that survives of New Blood Wrestling. No card, roster, or result is held, and no record of how or when it closed is known.
