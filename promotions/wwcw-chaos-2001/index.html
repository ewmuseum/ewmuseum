Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World Wide Chaos Wrestling introduced itself to the newsgroup on 23 March 2001 as a promotion already a year and a half old, and its own later account puts its actual founding in late November 1999, with a first card run on 1 January 2000. Every surviving recruitment call from its president describes the same persistent problem: a two-person staff trying to keep a promotion with, in its own words, “some real talent” running at all, hoping for new hands to help book, write and manage a growing roster.

On screen, the promotion staged a running power struggle for its own presidency: head booker Little Big Bob and president Dizz, a wrestler retired since 1990 after multiple neck operations, feuded across the roster through the summer of 2001 until settling it directly, in a loser-leaves-town match at the Reckoning Day pay-per-view (25 July 2001) that Dizz won with his own finisher, the Dizzifier, after interference from both sides.

The same card's Hardcore Championship rematch between Lars and Flood spilled out of the arena entirely, through a backstage storage area and up onto the video screen itself, where a hooded attacker — blamed for a string of blackouts hitting WWCW's cards all year — threw both men off the screen. Flood landed on top and was declared the new champion by pure accident.

The same night crowned a new World Heavyweight Champion: Red Dragon, previously the number-one contender and the promotion's own weekly columnist, won the title from the ICON in a triple-threat Hell in a Cell match that also involved Justin Vincible, turning on his own ally the ICON to do it.

By that August, Red Dragon's title reign had curdled into an on-air breakdown storyline: goaded by his own manager into a rebellious streak, the champion began attacking fellow wrestlers with a staple gun and was confronted on camera by his family and a psychiatrist sent to commit him, an angle that ended with Red Dragon fighting off the orderlies sent to take him and breaking down in tears in the ring.

By 1 September 2001 the promotion was, in its own words, in a “desperate situation,” down to two staff and pleading for help simply to survive rather than close. No further document survives, and how or when World Wide Chaos Wrestling actually closed is not known.
