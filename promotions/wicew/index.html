A GeoCities competitive-roleplay e-fed whose nine simultaneous championships and some ninety wrestlers moved through six documented cards over five months, including a Steel Cage War Games and a United States Heavyweight Title tournament left mid-bracket when the surviving record breaks off. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World Internet Championship e-Wrestling ran nine titles at once across three weekly cards and a monthly pay-per-view, per its own rules and home pages. Its own two captured pay-per-views crowned the fed from scratch: Max A. Million won the first World Heavyweight Title in a 33-minute battle royal on 4 October 1998 in Chicago, with Masta Gangsta named the first United States Heavyweight Champion as its own runner-up; a month later in Las Vegas, "Superstar" Steve Taylor took the World Title from Million the same night Volcano upset the European Title and the No Limit Soldiers stood revealed as a stable. By the fed's own 10 December 1998 championship snapshot, eight titles were held (the United States Title standing vacant), including Blizzard holding both the Internet and Cruiserweight Titles at once.

The surviving record runs six full cards deep and breaks off mid-story: a United States Heavyweight Title tournament reaches its quarterfinals with the semifinals scheduled for a house show the capture does not carry, and the fed's own last dated card closes on a disqualification finish whose losing side the surviving text never fully resolves — Black Mafia over Red Dogg and Darkstar, after Darkstar pulled a baseball bat from under the ring and turned it on the referee and both opponents before arena security restored order.

Storylines

The record holds six of the fed's own cards whole, October through December 1998, and two threads run clean across all of them — every quote checked against the served text.

The No Limit Soldiers, crowned twice over Written by AI, 2026

At the fed's own first pay-per-view, Max A. Million outlasts twenty-nine other men to win the World Heavyweight Title, throwing Masta Gangsta out last to name him the fed's own first United States Heavyweight Champion in the same motion. A month later at Black November, DaLinkwent has just retained his Internet Title against Jupiter when TRU climbs into the ring wearing a WICeW U.S. Champion shirt over one reading "No Limit Soldiers." Million runs down to break up the shoving match; DaLinkwent and Million tear the shirt off TRU together, and all three men leave the ring as one crowd looks on — the champion who opened the fed's own record, the champion who just closed a title defense on it, and the U.S. Champion himself, revealed as a standing stable in one motion. By 10 December the U.S. Title itself sits vacant on the fed's own championship snapshot, with no surviving record of how TRU lost it; the stable's own name survives the gap, resurfacing weeks later when President Gregory Joseph bars N.L.S. wrestlers from the U.S. Title Tournament — the same ban that catches Darkstar the moment his disguise as "Eclipse" comes off mid-match.

Traditional Wrestling Order, undone from within Written by AI, 2026

On the same Black November card, "Superstar" Steve Taylor and Sean "Iceman" Stevens team as the Traditional Wrestling Order to challenge Original Gangstaz for the World Tag Team Titles. Stevens turns on his own partner mid-match, cracking a chair over Taylor's head and rolling Gangsta New Jack on top of him for the pin, then takes a microphone: "You are a piece of crap Taylor!... this federation isn't big enough for the two of us," challenging Taylor to a loser-leaves-WICeW match the following Saturday that the surviving record never carries. T.W.O. outlives the split anyway — Jupiter's own bio card states outright that "he has joined the stable T.W.O.," and on 5 December, Duke Puzo and Jon Northcross wrestle with the winner's own T.W.O. membership on the line. Jupiter interferes on Northcross's behalf with his own briefcase and finisher, delivering the newest member into a stable its own founder no longer shares a locker room with.
