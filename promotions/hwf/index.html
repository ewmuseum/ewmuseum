Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Heartbreak Wrestling Federation, produced under the HeartBreak Productions banner, was a year old by the time its own president introduced it to the newsgroup on 21 December 1997. Roleplay decided, not simulated, it ran a weekly card styled after RAW and a Sunday pay-per-view, with a Rumble battle royal awarding the winner a shot at any title he chose.

Its own December 1997 record is dominated by a stable calling itself the Pack, styled as “the knights of the round table,” whose members — Malibu Mike Donovan, a character called the Knight, Batman, Irish Devil, and Bad Ass John among them — feuded across the roster; its characters routinely bragged up résumés in other federations (SCWF, WecW, CCW) as part of the promotion's own trash-talk culture, and a former Pack member, Ken Masters, spent the same month working the opposite side of that same feud.

Its headline turn that December broke the fourth wall on purpose: wrestler John Austin, deep in his own feud with the Pack, used a promo to announce a shoot against the promotion's own booker by name, Kris — “there is no more script, no more rules, and no more predetermined outcomes... it will not be because Kris told me to go out there and lay down” — framing his own rebellion as reclaiming the company: “This is my world, my life, and my federation.”

The same month's record staged a death: a backstage angle had one wrestler murdered, and Batman, found holding the weapon, was framed for the killing and drawn into the wider feud as its own self-appointed investigator.

By April 1998 the promotion staged WrestleCade II at Aloha Stadium in Honolulu, a triple-main-event card topped by a World Title match between champion Godly Ken Davison and Evil Lord Pelius, a blood feud settled under “I Respect You” rules between Natural Born Killer and Blade, and a grudge match between former Pack stablemates Bad Ass John and Irish Devil — called by guest officials including Jack Nicholson, Sarah Michelle Gellar, Tom Cruise, and Evander Holyfield, with Jerry Springer booked for a segment and the Backstreet Boys opening the show.

Its own last surviving document, a letter from its owner the next day, called the show a success and left the door open to new wrestlers. No further document survives, and how the promotion ultimately ended is not in the surviving record.
