A May-June 1997 Usenet fed that never produced a single card before closing twice in three weeks, one of eleven distinct 1997 federations sharing the bare acronym CWF. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

This CWF surfaces first on 17 May 1997 as a president looking for advice on how to actually run a federation — how many cards a week, whether to book by simulator or dice. Eleven days later a full application went out for a roleplay-based promotion running two cards and two reports a week, self-hosted on an Angelfire page, needing only three singles wrestlers and two tag teams to open. It had already advertised itself before any of that existed: a respondent two days earlier had mocked its own pre-launch pitch as “the greatest soon to be league” that “hasn't even started yet.”

Two separate replies on 30 May read the promotion less as a debut than as a pattern. One accused its own operator of starting roughly two federations a month. The other set its recruitment copy directly beside the IIWF's own application form and closed “Some people just don't have a creative mind,” noting the same poster had separately asked the IIWF's permission before reusing it for the FASW.

On 4 June the promotion relaunched at a new address as “CWF: Simply the Greatest,” the same application form unchanged beneath new copy billing itself “the most coolest internet fed on the net” and a fictional 900 hotline. A reply that same day, from a poster identifying with a standing community joke calling itself the Anti-SHEF, called a fed a month into overhyping itself with no card to show “DOOMED to fail.”

It closed the next day, 5 June 1997, citing “personnal problems and reasons,” with a promise of “another league opening up when I recieve time.” The replies it drew were blunt: one blamed the closure on nobody signing up, the other only that “the greatest fed ever didn't managed to get a single card in.” A further reply on 7 June needled the same recycled hype copy on the dead relaunch post and noted its link no longer worked, and by 12 June one poster wondered aloud whether the whole run had been a deliberate troll rather than a genuine attempt at a federation — a question the surviving record never answers.

No document later than that 12 June post survives, and no card was ever produced. Every document this promotion left behind is the newsgroup's own reaction to a fed nobody ever saw wrestle.
