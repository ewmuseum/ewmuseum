A twice-weekly booked-and-simmed newsgroup federation that opened in the summer of 1997 and instituted a wrestler-of-the-year award in an owner's memory that October. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

American Championship Wrestling opened on 27 July 1997, self-hosted on Angelfire, combining booked and simulated matches across two cards a week, Sunday and Wednesday, and claiming 900 hits inside its first month.

Its own heel stable, the Seneca Street Thugs, recruited openly to “wipe out the bottom feeders,” building toward a first advertised pay-per-view, Wrestlestorm '97, live from the Henry Levitt Arena in Wichita: a War Games-style Team ACW vs. Team SST match inside a double-ring steel cage, and, for the World Heavyweight Championship, a scaffold match thirty feet above the arena parking lot between champion Nitewing and Tom “Thunder” Cage, “with NOTHING between the scaffold and the concrete.”

Neither result survives. The next card served, a delayed Wrestlefest a week later, opens instead on other business: the Seneca Street Thugs' own Los Tiburones winning the World Tag Team titles, a barbed-wire match that ended with the loser, Nazeem, carried out in cardiac arrest and pronounced dead at the hospital mid-broadcast, and a main event closing on Mark Austin unmasking as Maximum Adrenaline to turn on his own stable and declare, “The Icon is dead.”

That October, out of respect for the recently deceased Brian Pillman, the promotion's management named its Wrestler of the Year award the Pillman Award.

By November the World title had changed hands again, to Mufsta, alongside a Television Championship (Vandal), a United States Championship (Great White), and Tag Team titles (Sniper and Moonhawk), and the fed answered talk of other e-feds folding with defiance: “No, we're not gonna fold up & die like some other e-feds... We'll be here until the end of time,” announcing a third weekly card and a Monday-night show planned for the following spring.

Its last surviving post, 30 November 1997, is a routine call for more card writers. How the promotion fared afterward, and how it ended, isn't in the surviving record.
