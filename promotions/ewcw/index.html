Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Extreme World Championship Wrestling had already collapsed once by January 1997, when its own president, Corey Collins, posted a call to anyone who remembered the old league: “My e-fed EWCW has gone kaput... if you wanna join, go here and fill out the form.” He relaunched it days later and played himself on-screen running it, a self-billed president appearing in its own broadcasts and roleplays under his own name.

The fed spent 1997 under siege from Dark Force Rising, a heel stable dominant enough that its July pay-per-view, Ring Wars, built its entire card around a seven-match “World Series of Wrestling” pitting a DFR representative against an EWCW loyalist in each bout, with the loser of the whole series exiled from the group; DFR won it five matches to two, and Professor Egon Ecton dropped the deciding fall to be cast out of the stable on camera. The same broadcast closed on a barbed-wire, table-breaking World Title death match, Chainsaw Charlie Dallas retaining over Iceman Scott Snyder by referee's count after a brainbuster through a table failed to finish him.

The Big One, its own November three-hour special, doubled every championship match on the card and delivered three title changes in one night: Pimp Daddy and the Hustler took the Tag Team titles from LFS, a debuting wrestler billed only as Bloodlust powerbombed Babyface Killer from the top rope to claim the Television title, and the Ghetto Boy retained the World title against Bull Dozier to close the show. A month later, at Wrestlefest, a returning giant billed as AzteK dethroned “Franchise” Shane Stud for the United States title in a mystery-opponent match built up across the card; Stud demanded a rematch on camera days afterward, the last word on that feud the surviving record carries.

No closing notice of the promotion's own survives; its last held broadcast is that December 1997 pay-per-view, still running, still selling its next feud, with no sign of the end that must eventually have come.
