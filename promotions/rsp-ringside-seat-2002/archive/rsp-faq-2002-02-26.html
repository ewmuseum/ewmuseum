CLOSING THOUGHTS --------------------------------------------------- ---===INTRODUCTION===--- Storyline: Ringside Seat Promotions came to life on February 13, 2002 as the brainchild of Theodore Stevenson, Esq. He had felt that a wrestling promotion would be a great money-maker, and being the shrewd businessman that he is, he got down to work. With a large bankroll, a love of the sport, he met up with a man by the name of Sean Christopher, who is an ex-wrestler. Stevenson agreed to give Christopher a 50/50 partnership in exchange for his knowledge . An agreement was reached, and RSP was born. The question lingered where would the promotion be based? Many locations were considered, although most of them (California, Florida, New York, etc.) were already cornered by other promotions. The answer finaly came...WASHINGTON! WIthout thinking, Stevenson applied to run his promotion out of Seattle, but quickly found out why there was little to no competition. The Washington State Athletic Commission is the toughest in the nation, and regulats wrestling very strictly. Stevenson was not to be denied. Stevenson made a below the table deal with the the commissioner. The deal was that in exchange for the promoters license being granted Stevenson would hire the commissioners son as a commentator. Thinking that this would help him keep tabs on the company from the inside ,with minimal effort, the license was quickly granted. RSP is forced to complie with several restrictions. Each wrestler has to apply for a licence to perform in the State of Washington, and could have it revoked at any time if they display behavior detrimental to the sport or to the state. Also, RSP was ordered to obtain a special permit anytime they wanted to sanction a match that was deemed "dangerous" or "extreme", and all performers were to acquire waivers to perform in such matches. With a contract in place, Stevenson and Christopher began their quest to find wrestlers who would be willing to accept such restrictions. Most big names won't even put themselves in this position, as most won't even return calls, so Christopher is left with the daunting task of landing underrated talent looking for their big break... ---===OUT OF CHARACTER===--- In a nutshell, this fed is run by two guys who have been in and around the e-wrestling circuit for quite some time. Myself ([name withheld]) has been in too many feds to count, and was even recognized at one point as one of the top handlers in the game with the character known as "The Bandit" Jason Blake. I've run a few feds before, with my earlier projects (ironically the ones I had assistance with) being far more successful. With this latest project, I'm looking more or less to uncover some lesser known talent, and try to make it a fun environment where a team effort is necessary to succeed, both as handlers, and as us front office types. The more we co-operate, the better this will be for everyone involved. I'm looking forward to working with some new faces, and possibly some old e-w acquaintances. [name withheld]---Been in e-wrestling since 96. Been involved in every aspect of the game from handling to running a promotion. Most noteable characters are K.C Hagan in KCW and Sean Christopher from ICW and IWF/WOW. Most recently ran AWP --------------------------------------------------- ---===RULES===--- Yep, every fed's gotta have them and no, in this case rules were NOT meant to be broken. Remember when applying your applying for a "license" to wrestle in the state of Washington and by doing so agree to abide by the rules set by the athletic commission and RSP staff. Any breaking of these rules will result in your license being suspended. 1.) THE HAPPY HAPPY JOY JOY RULE - First and foremost, have fun! that's what RSP is for. If you don't expect or even want to have fun this isn't the place for you. 2.) THE DUMBASS RULE - simple don't be one!! There's no use for it in e-w or in life. 3.) THE BIGOT RULE - No racist, sexist or religous bigotry. This is a _ZERO TOLERANCE_ rule. There is no room for this crap in e-wrestling and there is absolutely no reason for it to be used. If I see this in a RP or on-card you better PRAY that I'm in a good mood because there is a good chance I will just can you right away. Sorry if that seems a bit extreme but this is one of the things I simply can not STAND. 4.) THE *@#^% RULE - Try and keep the obscenities to a minimum. I have no problem with your character swearing when the time is right but please remember to *bleep* it out or censor it somehow. 5.) THE KAYFABE RULE - I am actually very lax about kayfabe. Try to refrain from calling your opponents jobbers and such, but referring to some of the "kayfabe-breaking" words shouldn't be a problem. But here is one thing that you should really try to remember: RCW, EMWC, UWF, GLCW, and all the rest of the e-feds on the planet...they exist in the world of RSP, so if you feel you need to mention them, be my guest. WWF/WCW/ECW, WWA, XWF...they do NOT exist in the world of RSP, so please don't mention them. 6.) THE WHINY LITTLE GIRL RULE - Don't be a sore loser. Don't e-mail me bitching and moaning about losing a match. If you lose and you want to know why, e-mail me or strike up a chat with me and we will talk it out. I have no problem explaining why I had your characters lose but I will have a problem if you start yelling at me about being stupid and not knowing how to run a fed. This is another way to find yourself fired from this federation quickly. 7.) THE ORIGINALITY RULE - Unlike these rules [which you may have seen in another feds handbook :)] try to be orignal with your characters and roleplays. These titles will be awarded to the members of RSP who produce the most consistent quality work. The titles will be the Western States Herritage Championship, The Television Championship, and the Northwest Tag Team Championship. Western States Herritage Championship- The main title for RSP. Right now RSP is based and only operates in and around the Seattle, Washington area. However as time goes by plans are to expand to all areas of the west coast. Television Championship - Public Access is all RSP can afford right now. The only way they can afford that though is through sponsorships of live events. So the television championship will be named after a different sponser each week. --------------------------------------------------- ---===HOW TO SUCCEED===--- Roleplays, Strats, and Angles all lead to success in RSP. ---ROLEPLAYING--- No roleplay..no wins. It's that simple. Send all roleplays to: [detail withheld] We don't ask for much around here. All that is required is one roleplay a week with a maximum of two allowed. Roleplays do not have to be long they just have to be quality. ---STRATS--- Strats are also sent to: [detail withheld] . Strats are another factor in determining who wins a close match. Strats for the most part are simple to do and should follow the format below. - Entrance (if it is different from thee one on your app) - General Strategy - Spots - A suggested finish. - What will your team do if they win/loose? If you have a question about strats fill free to ask. ---ANGLES/HEAT--- The more work you do the more heat your team gets..the more heat your team gets the better the angles get..the better the angles get the better you chances of having fun in RSP will get. --------------------------------------------------- ---===WEEKLY SCHEDULE===--- RSP ON TV-Weekly Show from various areas in and around Washington. This show will feature four to five live matches. This is where all the important stuff happens. He is really lazy, but knows he's got an easy job, so he plans on enjoying it. He's a huge wrestling mark, and hates "good guys", and it shows in his style of commentary, as he acts more like a fan than a commentator, and won't hesitate to pop for insane moves. He's not tough in the least, but wrestlers be warned, lay one finger on him and you're out a job, courtesy of the WSAC, as his father won't stand for any of that.... Colin Blackwell: At the ripe old age of 45, he's a true veteran of the sport. He's worked in many high profile wrestling companies such as AWMC and AWP. He's an old-school style commentator who was asked by Sean Christopher to apply his talents to RSP, and to add a sense of sanity on Television. He almost quit upon learning who his counterpart would be, but the temptation of a fairly sizable contract overrode the punishment of working with a brat. This promises to be the toughest test in Colin's distinguished career, but he's taking it head-on. (Note: All referees were brought aboard from the now defunct AWP) REFEREES: TOMMY "I'M NOT _THAT_ TOM JONES" JONES Always in tip top shape, and giving a 100%, this is the usual Main Event referee and RSP head official. BRYAN INFINITY Bryan always calls it straight down the middle...and is there to give the fans what they want...even if that means bending the rules a little. "OLD TIMER" STEVE MASSARO Steve is RSP's oldest ref, and perhaps the oldest on the RSP staff. Nearing 50 years old Steve has been around the ring long enough to know how to handle himself. not the fastest, or the most in shape, the most able to see, or even able to hear Steve does his best and doesn't back down from anybody. RSP Timekeeper: JAY HOWARTH Another young student of the game some say he has been weird ever since losing the Ring Announcer job to Anderson Michaels. Anderson and Jay seem to have harbored a training school rivalry which could explode as they sit together at the same table at ringside. Always trying to elevate his status and garner attention, Jay gets into the spotlight wherever he can...evening taking the mic from Anderson on occasion. RSP Ring Announcer: ANDERSON MICHAELS Anderson in his early 20's and is in good physical condition. Anderson is a young student of the game as he hopes to one day turn the mic in for a pair of boots. Anderson isn't afraid to take the lumps and bumps that may occur as he sits ringside. On a side note, he and Tonya Sanders have a bouncing baby boy. RSP Backstage Commentator: TONYA SANDERS The T&A of the RSP announce team. Tonya will handle all the backstage announce work as she uses her "talents" to dig under the sheets to get all the scoops. She's certainly learned her lesson, however, as she became pregnant. RSP Journeyman Reporter: James Wallace Newly hired reporter/columnist for the Seattle Times, who has a huge interest in wrestling, and is quite intrigued by a wrestling company setting up shop in his hometown. He has been given exclusive rights to conduct interviews with RSP talent in and out of shows. The Times has embraced this, as it gives them something over all the other Seattle papers, and are in the process of finalizing a contract with KCTS TV to televise interviews. No matter the outcome, this is Wallace's big break, and has vowed to be professional, so he can achieve his dream...
