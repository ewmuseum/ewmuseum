Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

A Seattle promotion opened in February 2002 by a co-founder narrated as a businessman and an ex-wrestler alike, the promotion built its own weekly television script around a wrestling license the state athletic commission made it fight for. Its earliest matches settled an eight-man battle royal and a three-group round robin into a first Western States Heritage Champion and a first Television Champion, both crowned on the same pay-per-view night.

Its own broadcast team, its referees and a wide slice of its early roster arrived from a promotion that had just closed its doors, carrying rivalries, finishers and even a broadcast partnership across the move intact. A president's own on-air favoritism toward two of his own wrestlers curdled by summer into a heel turn, revealed only once he refereed a grudge match himself and let an old rivalry settle a score in his own ring.

A cruiserweight title handed out by decree rather than won outright, a tag division built from a debuting team's tainted victory, and a stable calling itself the Reign of Terror all crowded onto the same weekly card by late summer, alongside a rival alliance of a Television Champion, a tag team and a bodyguard styling itself the Trinity.

Its own first pay-per-view turned over both of its top two championships and put its own president in a hospital bed, all recounted only after the fact on the broadcast that followed; no notice of the event itself, nor any closing announcement, survives in the newsgroup's own record.
