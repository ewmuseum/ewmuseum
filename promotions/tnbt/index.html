Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Next Big Thing staged itself as a reality-television competition rather than an ordinary wrestling promotion, opening “Season 1” with a Live Casting Special on 27 October 2004, filmed outside a mansion in Scottsdale, Arizona and billed as a FOX Sports production. Host “The Hack” Patrick Hackett introduced the show's own president, creator and executive producer, Dave Ace, alongside resident judges Sir Chauncey Billingsworth and Bosslady, and laid out the premise on camera: twenty-four contestants living together over four months, competing in judged promos and gimmick matches to become, in the show's own words, professional wrestling's Next Big Thing, with one contestant eliminated most weeks.

Its own structure split the cast into three groups, Platinum, Diamond and X, contested across a weekly show called Whup Ass Wednesday Night, with the match stipulation for each group's bout decided by online fan poll and every eliminated contestant symbolically asked to throw in a towel bearing his own name. Contestants were also set an early writing challenge: each had to work a specific assigned word into that week's promo. The casting special closed on a twist Ace himself hadn't signed off on: Hackett unveiled a surprise twenty-fifth entrant of his own choosing, Neige Thirteen, over his own boss's visible objection.

Its opening cast ran from straightforward wrestlers like Zen and the masked, boastful J.A.P.A.N. to stunt entries built entirely around the bit — feuding Brazilian twins Wayne Maynard-Demola and Zane Demola-Maynard, the self-reinvented “Phoenix” Adam Lazarus, and a single performer entered under three names and personalities at once, Peter Porkchop, Bam Bam Gigolo and “the White Trash Gangster” Charlie Sweatpants.

Its first regular episode, Whup Ass 1.1, aired 8 November 2004 and ran three group eliminations at once — a blindfold match, a strip-elimination bout judged on who stayed clothed longest, and a falls-count-anywhere “Good House Keeping” match — between them removing more than a dozen of the season's contestants in a single night.

No further post survives after that episode. Who, if anyone, the season ultimately crowned The Next Big Thing isn't in the surviving record.
