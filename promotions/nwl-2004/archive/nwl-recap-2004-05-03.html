NWL Recap, 3 May 2004 (Combat Episode 6)

3 May 2004

recent NWL Combat Ep. 9 Previous NWL Combat Ep. 8 Next NWL Combat Ep. 10 NWL Recap: 5/03/04 NWL Recap: 4/15/04 5/5/04 From: Elks Lodge in Queens, New York An important week for the NWL, as the WLS nears the end with Dakota Smith needing a win and for Watcher to lose for him to win the whole thing. We also had a lot of debuting talent, as well as a wedding.
Manny kicked off the show as normal, giving us a little run down. And I do mean a little run down, since he spots Shawn Hart in the front row. Hart claims he's here to see the "wedding of the century" and then kidnap Data. Great spot between Gonzago and Hart, and they kept it short enough so as not to kill the heat before the first match.
Backstage, President Medina is seen talking with...someone? A someone who knows about The Watcher's past. A someone with a familiar voice. A nice spot, keeping itself short whilst making it clear we're to expect something later in the night.
Next, we finally get a full run down of the card from Manny and Roger Kincaid. A lot of debutants, which shows the strength in depth the NWL has built up in a relatively short amount of time.
'THE REAL' MITCHELL BURGAN vs. THE JOSH ...or not. They get started, sizing each other up with a collar and elbow tie up, which The Josh wins, before Burgan hits a drop kick, and a man in a hood and sweatshirt makes his way out, attacking both men, finishing Burgan off with a powerbomb/piledriver combination, and The Josh with a Shining Wizard. The mystery man then pulls down his hood, and we see it's "Your's Truly" Adam Benjamin. He gets on the mic, ragging on the fans and the "few semi talented wrestlers in the NWL." He then even goes as far as to call Maelstrom overrated and his lap rat (by the way, I had an inkling this was gonna happen; I did follow MCW too, you know). Benjamin admits it was him who was sending Maelstrom the letters and notes, which makes sense since they were all signed "Your's Truly," but it did give Maelstrom something to do to build his feud with Smith. Benjamin then calls out Maelstrom for a shoot fight. Instead, Medina and some police officers come out to escort Benjamin from the ring. Medina also says he's sick of people like Benjamin and Hart, who don't come in to sign contracts but take up TV time anyway. GOOD. It does get repetitive sometimes.
OK, now THIS is getting repetitive too. Another segment, this time with Magellan and Finnegan. Lars is talking on his cellphone about how people have been waiting for this person to return. When he hangs up, we get a shot of Hart on his cellphone. Too... many... segments... not... enough... wrestling...
SAM 'ICE COLD' ICESHON vs. ARLO FERRALL And it's two more debutants. They lock up, Iceshon going behind, taking Ferrall down with an amateur wrestling hold, before Arlo counters into a hammerlock, before hooking on a headlock. Both men get to their feet, and Ferrall presses the advantage with a swinging neck breaker. Both men are up quickly again, only for Iceshon to get sent down again with a belly to belly. A two count is followed with an attempted suplex by Ferrall, but Iceshon blocks it, hitting a sambo suplex and a double arm DDT for two. Sam Iceshon goes for a chokeslam, but Ferrall hits a VICIOUS looking snap kick to the head. He scores with a cross body, but Iceshon kicks out at two. Sam is first up, hitting a knee to the gut, but Ferrall counters the Russian leg sweep attempt with some elbows, followed by a springboard drop kick, and one of the SICKEST looking moves I've seen in a while. Ferrall lifted Iceshon up for a powerbomb, throwing him forward and scoring a superkick before Sam hit the mat. He calls it The Abyss, and it's enough for the three count. An impressive match, if a little short, but you can blame that on too many segments early on. 2/5
RAPIER vs. 'POSEIDON' AODHAN LORIGAN We go straight in with the next match. Really, they could have had one of the segments in between these matches. Kincaid informs us these two have a history, and as Lorigan gets into the ring, he clobbers Rapier with a boot to the face, followed by an Irish whip and back body drop. He rushes in, but gets hotshotted onto the ropes. Rapier slams Poseidon, stomping away and getting a two count off the knee. He locks on an arm bar off a hip toss, and then tries a short-arm clothesline, only for Lorigan to duck underneath and attempt a German suplex. Rapier counters, looking for a clothesline, but Lorigan counters into an airplane spin (I LOVE these old time moves sometimes). Poseidon tries for the cover after regaining his balance, but Rapier kicks out, only to be taken down with a standing drop kick. Rapier reverses a piledriver with a back body drop, followed by a double arm suplex, and the blatant choke. He throws the larger man into the corner, throwing lefts and rights until Poseidon counters, smashing Rapier's head into the padding as the crowd counts along. After ten, Lorigan scoops Rapier up, hitting a running powerslam, but only gets two. Rapier surprises Lorigan, looking for the Cadaver, but Lorigan counters, hooking in an Argentian backbreaker into a piledriver (The Trident) for the three count. A good match, with some great pacing by the two, and both looked crisp in there. I'm looking forward to seeing them again. 3/5
Next, we get a segment with Medina, Watcher, and the man who was talking to Medina earlier... Larry Tact. Tact gives Watcher a little motivational speech, setting the bar higher for the Brown/Watcher match later in the evening.
Kincaid and Manny give us a little run down of the possibilities for the WLS as it stands now. Nice informational segment, but could have been used elsewhere.
RONNIE VALENTINE vs. THE DISTRIBUTOR OF PAIN DoP managed to sicken a lot of people in the build up with a prostitute skit, whereas Ronnie's a newcomer to the ranks. Valentine starts out with some punches to the head, really selling the angry opponent bit, before following DoP to the outside. He goes for a whip to the steps, but DoP reverses it, hitting some hard kicks and ramming Valentines head into the ring post. DoP then uses Ronnie to push the barrier back, before charging in, but Valentine side steps, smashing DoP in the head with a chair borrowed from a fan. That's something I like about NWL; the chairs don't look planted. Ronnie pushes DoP into the ring, but only gets a two count. A sleeper hold slows things down for a few seconds, before the Distributor distributes a belly to back suplex counter. A one count follows, Valentine scores with a kick to the head, followed by a drop kick and neckbreaker for two. A moonsault still only gets a two, and a hard spinning clothesline blocks a Russian leg sweep attempt. A Falcon's Arrow from DoP sets up a top rope leg drop for two, before he sets up the Devil's Cross. In a nice spot, Ronnie counters into a Rocker Dropper, followed by a spinning heel kick. A rookie mistake in the corner has him singing an octave higher, though, as DoP crotches him as he played to the crowd. DoP hits a stiff looking sit out powerbomb from the top, and it's all she wrote for Ronnie. Some good psychology from these two, but I'd have liked more time in the match. 2.8/5
Next, The American comes out (with some lawyers in tow), insisting that Pulsar hand over the Continental title and that the NWL be renamed the American Wrestling League. Nice spot, which gets The American over as an arrogant and annoying heel. I like him already. Pulsar gives him the evil eye as he makes his way out for his match.
Next match was supposed to be RYAN CRUZ vs. PULSAR, but Cruz no-shows. So, to fill up some time, The American slags Pulsar off some more, before getting clocked with a right hand. One of the lawyers thrusts some papers into Pulsar's face, and gets Black Holed for his trouble. This is shaping up to be an interesting feud, as you've also got The American walking out of the TV title decider, so Poe can always be added for more weirdness later.
CAMERON LOVE vs. JAROD POE for the TV title Love comes out and continues his Hometown Hotties tour, with Tiffany Saunders. He says that Poe likes virgin sacrifices, so later after the match, Love intends to make sure that both himself and Tiffany are suitably 'protected'. Man, I love this angle already. Both men lock up to start the match, with Love locking in a headlock, only for Poe to get a two count off a belly to back counter. Love holds onto the headlock, as Poe rolls him over for two again, and another roll up finally makes Love release the hold. A snapmare and elbow drop doesn't even get a one count for Love, so he starts chopping. And not a "Wooooh!!!" to be heard :) Love whips Poe to the ropes, and after some criss-crossing, gets HAMMERED with a sick looking clothesline from Poe. Poe scores with a DDT, then uses the claws in his fingers to lock in Blood Rush, as Love squirms and bleeds. Poe releases, taunting Love, before hitting a suplex and a second rope elbow for two. Poe brings Love to his feet with an armbar, but Love reverses into a Northern Lights suplex for two, and gets another two with a double arm underhook suplex. Poe tries a capture suplex as a counter to the Love Tap superkick, but Love reverses into a swinging neck breaker. Love hits a flying shoulder tackle as Poe gets to his feet, before setting up the 69 Slam, which Poe counters, flipping over and lifting Love onto his shoulder. He runs to the corner, but Love slips out, rolling Poe up for two. Love then goes for another running shoulder tackle, but gets hit with the Breath Stealer, followed by the Fading Light, which is enough for Poe to pick up the victory. Good work from these two, as Love looked like a threat to Poe's title, and Poe was able to keep his momentum by not looking too weak in there. 3/5
Post match, we get another run down of the many ways the WLS could go. This sets us up for the WLS section of the show.
DAKOTA SMITH vs. LARS MAGELLAN w/SHAMROCK FINNEGAN in the WLS Magellan comes out in a limo, and Manny's stilled miffed at the beating he took a few weeks back. Dakota kicks the action off with some punches, whipping Magellan off the ropes, but Magellan reverses, and neither man budges when Smith charges in with a shoulder block. Lars taunts Dakota, who runs the ropes again, but still Magellan doesn't budge. Lars ruffles the smaller mans hair and pats his cheek, but Smith uses the time to hit a DDT, following up with a hard knee to the head as Lars gets up. Smith looks for Deep Impact, but Magellan's too large for him, so he pounds away for a while, before Lars back drops him. A side suplex gets a one count for Magellan. Lars sends Smith off the ropes, ducking a clothesline, before Shamrock Finnegan pulls Smith down by the arm. Magellan gets a two count off of a stalling suplex, whipping Smith into the corner and using the big mans' foot choke for an unfair advantage. He tries to whip Smith to the opposite corner, but Smith counters, lifting Magellan up on the rebound and hitting the 336 (fireman's carry into a DDT) for two. As Lars gets up, Smith takes him down with a sleeper hold slam, and keeps the sleeper locked in. More heel work from Finnegan here, as he runs round, trying to get Magellan up, then jumping onto the apron to distract Smith. Smith walks over to the ropes, giving Magellan enough time to hit a German suplex for two. Some stiff boots set up a camel clutch, and we get some more heel tactics here, as whenever the ref goes to check Dakota, Finnegan helps Magellan by pulling him back to add more pressure. Finally, the ref sees it, kicking the hands of Lars and Finnegan, before ordering Lars to release the hold (4/5 for the kick there ref). A non-chalant cover gets a two count, and Lars thinks it's time to end it, setting up the Beach Bomb. Smith counters, hitting Megadeath after flipping out of the hold and spinning Lars round. Dakota only gets a two count himself, so he whips Magellan to the ropes, clotheslining him down. Shamrock tries to get involved, but gets knocked off the apron for his troubles. And it's here that the match descends into madness a little. Fireworks go off, as a man stands with his back to the ring, distracting Smith. Kincaid says it's Maelstrom, but Manny says it's the wrong shape. Meanwhile, Shawn Hart comes in and delivers Hart Attack to Smith. Policemen arrest Hart, as Magellan covers Smith for the two. Magellan tries to lift Smith off the mat, but Smith hits with a slingshot brainbuster. The Watcher now gets involved, walking down to the ring carrying a picture of Maelstrom pinning Dakota. Dakota's distracted, and Magellan rolls him up for a two count. Smith then counters a back body drop into a crucifix position, but gets distracted AGAIN as Maelstrom comes out, driving his motorbike over Magellan's limo. This allows Magellan the chance to hook Smith in the Beach Bomb, which is enough to pick up the win. Great wrestling from these two, but the ending was really too silly; it seemed like the creative team threw darts at a board for this one. 3/5, losing 1 point for the ending.
Post match, DoP comes out, attacking Magellan. Finnegan helps Magellan, then Watcher attacks Finnegan, as Smith runs at Maelstrom!!! Gonzago summed this whole thing up; clusterfuck. Referees, security, police officers, and President Medina, looking PISSED, come out to restore order. Medina then lays down the law. Finnegan is going to take on DoP in a cage match, the Watcher is taking on Lars Magellan in a lumberjack match, and Maelstrom and Dakota Smith now have to contend with a dog collar match!!! I am SERIOUSLY looking forward to seeing these matches. But that's not all; Medina also makes an open challenge, because of the actions of Benjamin and Hart, for people to sign contracts with the NWL, then challenge anyone currently on the NWL roster at the first pay per view in about two months time. Seeing Adam Benjamin in the ring in the NWL will be interesting, as he has a feud with Maelstrom to blow off, and it's widely known he can work with the only WLS member NOT involved in the end of the last match, Karl Brown. Meanwhile, Shawn Hart has a few feuds he can become involved in, and there are already a lot of angles and storylines going on. Things are going to be interesting to say the least. And we haven't even had the main event or the wedding yet!!
Next, before the main event (and I think in part to calm the crowd down a bit after the last match), we see The Watcher in the back stage area. He gives a promo, pulling Brown up on some of the things that were said, like Brown's insinuation of The Watcher playing mind games. The whole segment had the feel of being a late promo, but it worked well to calm the crowd. 3.5/5, since it was long enough and did a good job.
THE WATCHER vs. KARL "THE DRAGON" BROWN in the WLS A big match this one. If Watcher wins, he can still tie with or beat Dakota Smith in the points. If Brown wins, he sits in pole position needing Maelstrom to do the business next week. So say Manny and Kincaid, as they hype this match up no end. And during The Watchers entrance, Roger tells us he knows one of the people who trained Brown, and it'll be a shock to even Larry Tact. INTRIGUE!!!!! Both men come out to huge ovations, so you know the crowd are anticipating. Both men lock up, and Watcher makes the first move with a hammerlock, which Brown counters into a hammerlock of his own. Watcher counters with a snapmare, and a headlock on the mat, but Brown manages to get to his feet, twisting The Watcher's arm into an arm bar, which Watcher counters with an arm twist of his own, before whipping Brown into the ropes, and taking him down with a shoulder block. Watcher runs over Brown, running the ropes, as Brown leap frogs him on the rebound, and on the second rebound, Brown takes Watcher down with the now rarely seen Mexican arm drag. Watcher gets to his feet, but is taken down with another arm drag, and then another, as Brown locks in an arm bar. Watcher manages to get to his feet, connecting with a belly-to-belly suplex, and then catching Brown as he runs in with a powerslam, but only manages to get a one count. Watcher, keeping the match in a technical vein, locks in a cobra clutch as Brown gets to his feet. Brown backs Watcher up, and the ref calls for the break, only for Karl to turn and lay in with some hard chops, very reminiscent of the Japanese style (as in, stiff, not the "Wooooh!!" type), before whipping Watcher off to the other corner. Watcher reverses, sending Brown in, but Brown counters Watchers charge with a second rope leaping cross body block. Brown takes Watcher down again with a drop toehold, before floating over into a side headlock. So far, so good from these two, as they're keeping the pace quick without going too quick. Watcher fights to his feet, sending Brown down with a spinning back suplex, holding on for the cover. A count of two, and Watcher brings Brown up and down with a forward vertical suplex, which is powerful enough to make Brown bounce to his feet. Watcher lifts Brown up, only for Brown to slip behind with a sunset flip for two. Watcher charges Brown as he gets up, almost taking his head off with a clothesline. He gets a two count, setting up a full nelson, which Brown counters with a Russian leg-sweep, locking in the Sharpshooter/Scorpion Death Lock. Brown wrenches back, and Watcher sells the move brilliantly before just making it to the ropes, but he gets met with a T-Bone suplex for his troubles. Brown gets a two count, locking in a Dragon sleeper and looking to end it with the Dragon's Bite. The Watcher, however, had other ideas, as he reverses into a side slam, still selling the Sharpshooter as he moves round, looking for the superkick, Starbreaker. He goes in as Brown dizzily gets to his feet, but Brown has the same idea, as they both hit superkicks. This works brilliantly, as Brown's martial arts experience plays well, and The Watcher obviously has as much or more experience with those techniques as Brown. Watcher gets up at five, as Brown starts to stir, and The Watcher moves in behind, looking for an inverted suplex. But, in a wink of the eye, Brown manages to drop behind, hooking the dragon sleeper, then the Dragon's Bite, getting the one, two, three, and putting himself in first place in the WLS. GREAT chain wrestling from both these guys, as they put on a show and a half for the mat wrestling enthusiasts, and the crowd were really into the match. However, I do feel it could have had another few minutes, but like much of this show, it was hurt by timing the segments. 4/5
Post match, The Watcher and Brown shake hands, as Manny calls them the two best rookies in the industry right now bar none. For the amount of time they've been at this, I'd agree that they're two of the greatest rookies right now.
And now, finally, it is time for the wedding we've all been waiting for. Data, looking HOT as always, comes down the aisle, and the proceedings begin. Everything is perfect, and no one has a problem with them getting married. Hacker says, "I do." Data, Natalia, says "I", and then. the lights get cut!?! We hear Natalia scream, and when the lights come back on, Hacker's been crucified, tied onto the cross by barbed wire, with a barbed wire crown. Everyone in the church is screaming as we fade out.
A lot of happenings on this edition of Combat. The show was hurt by the amount of non-wrestling segments early on, which ate up time that could have been used to extend some of the matches later on. I really liked the Valentine/DoP, Love/Poe, Brown/Watcher and Smith/Magellan (until the ending) matches, and the others that took place were definitely a high standard, as we've come to expect. A lot of question marks over who kidnapped Data, as I can't see Shawn Hart doing it, despite what he said, and we have three first class matches already signed for next Combat. All in all, a great show wrestling wise (4/5 for the wrestling), but hurt by the time devoted to angles eating too much ring-time.
