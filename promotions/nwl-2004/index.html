A forum-hosted federation on the FW Central hub, founded by an operator returning to the business after an earlier promotion's collapse, whose three championships were all still in play when its own owner closed it seven months later to take a full-time job. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The National Wrestling League opened for business in January 2004 under owner and president Andrew Medina, staged in an on-screen interview admitting his previous promotion, the World Wrestling League, had “fell through” after he raised talent's hopes and then couldn't deliver: “I take full responsibility with that one.” Starting over from the ground up rather than trying to hold onto a prior legacy, he signed Maelstrom as his first name talent, followed by Shawn Hart and Jarod Poe.

Its founding week staged a feud before a single card had run: a new signing, Dakota Smith, the son of a retired former rival of Maelstrom's named Nevada Smith, cut a promo dismissing suggestions he was riding Maelstrom's coattails, and Maelstrom answered in kind, needling Dakota's “5 mph mind” before Nevada himself appeared to introduce his son as a threat in his own right.

The National Wrestling League ran three championships. Maelstrom is billed “The First NWL World Champion” by the fed's own roster document, having won the NWL World Heavyweight Championship from a wrestler billed only as The Dragon; Pulsar held the NWL International Championship as of the roster's own mid-May entry; and Jarod Poe held the NWL Television Championship, describing himself in his own title-defense promo, against the Distributor of Pain, as the only man ever to have held it.

By June, Maelstrom had staged a takeover of the front office itself, installing himself as boss under the name “El Jefe” while still holding the World title, booking the next Combat card's matches personally, firing his own secretary on camera, and needling the roster over their names and habits along the way. The segment closed with a pointed jab at Medina, “lying all broken up in the hospital” after he “slipped and fell onto those chairs” — the promotion's own account of how its founder left day-to-day control.

Medina announced the National Wrestling League's closure on 1 August 2004, citing a new full-time job rather than any in-fiction cause: “I can't keep you good people held back for long... the NWL is officially closed.” All three championships were still active and unresolved at that point; nothing in the surviving record says how, or whether, they were ever settled.
