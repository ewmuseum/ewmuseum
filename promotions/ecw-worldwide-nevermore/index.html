Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

ECW Worldwide is one author's diary of a fictional Extreme Championship Wrestling, kept in a Total Extreme Wrestling game world that follows real events until July 1994 and then goes its own way. Its opening page lists what changed: the WWF bought out World Championship Wrestling in 2000, Steve Austin, Chris Jericho and Rey Mysterio Jr. never left ECW, Kurt Angle joined it in 1997, and the author owns the company after taking over from Paul Heyman in 1999, with Heyman staying on as booker and on-screen authority.

The roster is split into two brands, Revolution and F' The Mainstream, each with its own on-screen authority, announce team and champions: Stephanie Mcintosh with Don Callis, Scott Hudson and Jim Ross on Revolution, and Paul Heyman with Joey Styles and Taz on F' The Mainstream. Every show is a script, with the announcers' banter, the ring announcer's introductions, a finish and a time for each match, a grade for every match, promos and angles, and the show's rating set beside the rival show's.

The first month builds to Wrestlepalooza at the end of June, a card with a triple-header main event: Tommy Dreamer and The Sandman in a Singapore Cane Match, Rob Van Dam against Sabu, and Raven against Goldberg for the World Heavyweight Championship. Kurt Angle meets Brock Lesnar after Lesnar clotheslines Heyman in the ring, and Steve Austin faces Rocky. A front section, written late, carries the title histories up to 30 November 2007 and profiles of the franchise players Shane Douglas, Sabu, Raven, Chris Jericho and Shawn Michaels.

The saved copy ends in the middle of the build to the Masters of Pain finals, in the third week of December 2007 on the game's calendar, with a seeding cage match announced and no results. It carries no closing post and no statement that the diary stopped there, and it names neither the forum it ran on nor a single posting date.

This federation’s fiction portrays the ECW.
