A single-operator hardcore-styled federation that opened as the Universal Wrestling Federation and renamed itself five days later, running four documented pay-per-view cards and five contested championships between February and November 1997. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The federation opened on 6 February 1997 as the Universal Wrestling Federation, recruiting original-character wrestlers by e-mail for a five-title slate. Five days later its founder renamed it the Extreme Hardcore Wrestling Association, explaining plainly that the first name “was already taken” and that he had “gone ahead and changed the name” rather than cause any trouble over it.

Its earliest surviving card, Sunday's Sacrificial Slam (25 February 1997), staged the Masters of the Apocalypse beating a rival tag team, a non-title match between the Extreme Television Champion Maverick and Extreme Internet Champion Ivan Thor Thunder after the promotion's own commissioner refused to make it a title bout, and a three-way dance won by Agony of the Powers of Destruction. The same card closed on Maverick retaining his Television title over the self-billed Playboy Ronnie D.

Maverick lost that same Television Championship weeks later when a ringside fan handed him a chair that backfired into his own face, letting Viglen finish him off for the title. The World Tag Team Championship changed custodians across the promotion's surviving cards without ever cleanly losing a match for it — held at different points by the Knights and by Eclipse and Cataclysm, the latter twice retaining only after outside interference voided the finish. Ivan Thor Thunder held onto the promotion's Extreme World Championship, called “the Internet championship” once and the Extreme World Title thereafter by the federation's own inconsistent usage, through every surviving defense.

At the promotion's year-end Ultimate Survivor Battle Royal (30 November 1997), its Internet Champion quit the federation on camera mid-show, vacating the title; the promotion announced that the battle royal's winner would receive that belt alongside a newly created US Title on top of the usual World Title shot. “Too Sexy” Christopher Charles won the match, eliminating the field down to himself and two others, and left the ring holding three championships at once.

No further card survives; how the federation's title picture settled after that night, or how the promotion itself ended, isn't in the surviving record.
