Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Rabid Wrestling Association opened on 11 June 1996 with a welcome-and-signup document carrying the tagline “YOU'RE GONNA GET BIT!!!” and presenting itself as a booked league in which “the angles and winners are chosen by the President based on roleplaying, character statistics, and whatever is best for the league as a whole.” It promised a weekly card, Tuesday Night UNLIMITED, and a Pay Per View Extravaganza every two months as it grew.

Two titles anchored the promotion from that first document: the RWA World Heavyweight Title, to be decided by a thirty-two-man single-elimination tournament (“That means no SCRAWNY FOURTEEN YEAR OLDS!”), and the RWA World Tag Team Titles, to be decided by an eight-team tournament. Both were required to be defended every two weeks.

A roleplay digest posted eight days later, on 19 June 1996, gathers the roster building toward that tournament. The New Horsemen — “The Enforcer” and “The Nature Boy,” with manager “The Ringmaster” — billed themselves former tag champions elsewhere, entering as the league's presumptive top team. The Holy Roller and the Archbishop staged a training vignette built around an uprooted olive tree; Osami Karelin introduced himself as a twenty-five-year-old Sambo fighter out of St. Petersburg with tournament wins claimed in Brazil and Japan; Genocidal Violator called out Hellshock, Nemesis and Layne Larson in the same promo; and Craig Masters, writing himself out of active competition on an in-fiction leg injury, installed himself as manager of the Asylum Association. A rivalry ran through the same update between Trey Porter and a trio naming themselves the Agents of Anarchy — Aaron Adams, Scotty O'Bawdy and Steve Salazar — while Ray Chavalier laid claim to a legacy from what he called “International Productions” and offered Porter a tag-team partnership in the same breath as a challenge.

How it ended isn't in the surviving record. Its last surviving document is that same 19 June update, still recruiting and still building toward the tournament its founding document had promised eight days before.
