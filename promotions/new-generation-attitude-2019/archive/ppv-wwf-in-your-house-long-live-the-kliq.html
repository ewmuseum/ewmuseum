A New Generation…..a new attitude? Pay-per-view

WWF In Your House: Long Live…..the KLiQ

Posted 4 May 2019
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

The blog’s images are not served; the posts’ text is given whole, as the author wrote it.

Week 4 April, 1996

El Generico Stadium (Memphis, Tennessee)

ATT: 45,000

Buy: 1.04

The show opens to a highlight package of the events that have led up to this evening, from the KLiQ assaulting Jerry Lawler, to the reunion of the Hart brothers and everything in between, before finally the In Your House logo appears on the screen with the words “Long Live The King” emblazoned underneath…..only for a graphic of a branding iron to come crashing down over the top, leaving the logo of the KLiQ behind. Long Live the KLiQ indeed.

Following that we go live to the arena, where Vince McMahon and Jim Ross are on commentary, no Jerry Lawler obviously, he has more important business to attend to tonight as be battles Shawn Michaels for the WWF Championship. The duo welcome us to the show, briefly running down the card before we go straight to our first match up and the arrival of Yokozuna.

VS.

Yokozuna vs. ??? (w/Jim Cornette)

Yoko has a lot of anger to work through here tonight…..a lot of it aimed towards his former manager Jim Cornette, but if he can’t get his hands on the Louisiana Lip, then he will be more than happy to lay a beating on Cornette’s newest client, his un-named weapon of mass destruction, who is now stalking his way menacingly to the ring. The bell sounds and these two behemoths come face to face in the centre of the ring, with neither man giving an inch, until finally Yoko has had enough and swings hard with a big haymaker that snaps the Giants head hard to the left. It doesn’t seem to phase the seven footer however, so Yoko does it again…..and again……and again…..with the same result each time. McMahon remarks that the blows seeming have little to no effect on this monster and the former WWF Champion seems to agree, because finally he just grabs at the masked face of his opponent, turns it to the side and delivers a hard headbutt to the big mans temple. That finally rocks the imposing figure and Yokozuna is quick to follow it up with a succession of two more that force his opponent backwards until he is propped up against the ropes. Yoko propels him off, Irish whipping him and using the momentum to deliver a big stiff chop to the chest of the monster. This still doesn’t knock his opponent off his feet, it does stagger him however and this time when Yokozuna delivers a series of right hands it has the desired effect, driving the monster back into the corner, where Yoko is able to utilise all of his massive weight, crushing his opponent with a big turnbuckle splash. Again in a repeat of Monday night, he follows this up with a big clothesline over the top rope…..and again the monster lands on his feet.

This time though, after landing feet first on the floor, Cornette’s Doomsday Machine reaches under the bottom rope and sweeps his opponents legs out from under him, before dragging him to the outside where he whips him hard into the steel steps. That turns the tides rapidly for the monster and with Cornette jumping up and down like a child at Christmas, he picks up the mammoth girth of Yokozuna and again drives him, knee first into the unforgiving steel. Back inside the ring, the damage inflicted on the outside is obvious, because tipping the scales at well over 600lbs means that the knees of Yokozuna have a lot of weight to support….take out the knees and you severely limit the big mans (already limited) mobility. Keeping his opponent on the mat, Cornette instructs his weapon to destroy his opponent….and the monster complies. It’s not pretty, it’s not exciting…..but it’s very, very effective as he slowly dismantles the former Champion. Yokozuna tries to fight back on a number of occasions, but his knees won’t let him muster any prolonged periods of offence.

The ending comes quite quickly in this one….and it’s about as ugly as the rest of the match was. Because during one of Yokozuna’s brief periods off offence, he made the mistake of trying to slam the red clad monster…..unfortunately his damaged knees couldn’t take the added weight and his opponent was able to slip out of his grasp, land on his feet…….deliver a Chokeslam that Yokozuna got about 3 inches into the air for…..and make the cover for the one, two, three.

Winner: Cornette’s Monster

Rating: C

With another victory in the bag, Cornette is on the microphone as quick as a flash, running down Yokozuna a mile a minute as only the Mouth of the Mid-South can. He tells Yokozuna that he warned him that this would happen…..“that when you have the nerve to place your nasty sushi stinking hands on Jim Cornette, only bad things are going to happen.” He tells the locker room to let this be a lesson, to anyone who thinks that the can push around the little manager with a big mouth….and if this wasn’t proof enough……

“I don’t call this man my Weapon of Mass Destruction for nothing.”

He turns to his monster and tells him to “finish the job.” As usual the monster complies, stomping at the already injured knees of Yokozuna, who cries out in pain with each new blow. The seven foot demon looks down on his prey, clearly not satisfied with the level of damage he’s inflicted. He pauses for a moment before heading to the outside where he picks up a steel chair. Back inside the ring he picks up one of Yoko’s thick legs and inserts it in the folding part of the chair. McMahon is shouting furiously on commentary that he’s going to break the mans leg…..and he may well have done, had it not been for the intervention of this man……

…..clearly still looking to “make a difference” Phatu comes running down the aisle with a chair of his own. He nails the monster hard in the back, but it has no impact. Slowly and methodically the big man turns to face his attacker…..he should have maybe turned a bit quicker though, because Phatu was more than ready and he smashes the steel over the masked head of the big man. Even this doesn’t knock the man off his feet, in fact it doesn’t look like it has any effect at all. Phatu goes for another swing, but before he has chance to make impact, the monsters arm snaps out and grabs him by the throat. One Chokeslam later and there are two bodies laid out on the mat, while Cornette and his client make their way victorious to the back.

Rating: B-

While the carnage is being cleared in the ring, we head to the back where Dok Hendrix is standing by with Hawk and Animal, the Legion of Doom. He asks the pair if they have a game plan in place to counter act whatever plan Paul Ellering has concocted to ensure victory for the Harris Boys? The pair look at each other for a moment, before Animal starts to yell.

“PLAN? PLAN? DO WE LOOK LIKE MEN THAT PLAN ANYTHING? WE DO WHAT WE WANT, WHEN WE WANT, HOWEVER WE WANT…..WE DONT PLAN, WE JUST DO….TELL ‘EM HAWK”

“WELL…..YOU SAY WE DON’T HAVE A PLAN……BUT I DO HAVE A PLAN….I PLAN TO SHOVE THIS FIST, SO FAR UP RON HARRIS’ ASS, THAT I’LL BE ABLE TO USE DON HARRIS AS A VENTRILOQUIST DUMMY…..AND DO YOU KNOW WHAT THAT DUMMY WILL SAY DOK?”

“What a rush?”

“……..DON’T EVER STEAL MY LINE AGAIN!!!!…..PAUL ELLERING, THESE TWO DINOSAURS ARE COMING, AND THE ONLY THING THATS GOING TO BE EXTINCT……IS THE HARRIS BROTHERS ABILITY TO BREATHE…….UHHHHHH…..WHAT A RUSHHHHHHH!”

Rating: B-

VS.

The Legion of Doom vs. The Harris Boys (w/Paul Ellering)

It’s tag team time and this one is going to be a pier six brawl, because no one has ever accused any one of these four men of being a technical genius. What they are, are four big burly brawlers so batten down the hatches. The Harris Boys come to the ring first, with Paul Ellering still giving them instructions as they walk down the aisle. The Doomers are out next, to a huge reception from this live crowd. They each mount a turnbuckle and pose for their fans, before both turning to stare down Ellering. You would assume that both teams have aspirations of gold in the future, and for the Harris Boys especially, a victory over a team as legendary as the Legion of Doom would certainly put them near the top of the list of contenders.

The bell rings and both teams charge at each other, fists and feet flying as the punch and kicks flow freely. If this was part of Paul Ellering’s plan however, it was a pretty poorly thought out plan. Ron and Don might two of the toughest men on the face of the planet, but I don’t think Hawk and Animal have lost a fist fight since kindergarten. They each nail their opponents one more time, before tossing them both through the ropes and onto the floor. Ellering regroups his team….telling them that this wasn’t the plan….you know what to do. Apparently they do, because Ron steps back into the ring challenging Hawk. Never one to back down from a challenge Hawk is quick to accept, but gets blindsided by Don from behind, just as Ron cheap shots Animal off the apron to the floor.

Ellering looks much happier now that his contingent is in control, clearly the plan was to isolate Hawk, presumably to counter the power house that is Animal…..but then Hawk is no slouch when it comes to the power department either and the Harris boys have to work twice as hard as they usually would to keep the advantage….not that they seem to mind, because they utilise the five count with every tag, ensuring that they double team the Road Warrior each and every time. At one point Hawk gets tossed to the outside by Ron, who then turns and spits in the face of Animal. Obviously infuriated by the action, Animal tries to get into the ring for a dose of retaliation, unfortunately all that does is draw the officials attention away from the fact that both Don Harris and Paul Ellering are on the floor, stomping the paint off Hawks face.

It’s hard to keep a Road Warrior down though and despite their best efforts, The Harris Boys double teaming eventually back fires, because after an Irish whip, Hawk is able to duck a double clothesline attempt, rebound off the ropes, and nail both Ron and Don with a double clothesline of his own, landing close enough to his own corner that he’s able to make the hot tag to his waiting partner. Animal hits the ring like a house of fire, peppering both Harris Boys with hard rights and lefts, before hitting Ron with a big boot and catching Don immediately after with a powerslam that drives him hard into the mat. Hawk is soon back into the action and the official loses all semblance of control as the match breaks down to a brawl. Paul Ellering decides to get in on the action, jumping up onto the apron and hooking hold of Hawk from behind, holding him for a charging Ron, unfortunately Hawk is able to move at the last moment and Ellering is sent crashing to the floor courtesy of his own man…..who soon follows him thanks to a clothesline from Hawk which sends him flying over the top rope and to the floor.

In the mean time Animal has picked Don Harris high up onto his shoulders. Hawk climbs the turnbuckles and comes flying off. One Doomsday Devise later and Paul Ellering’s master plan is out of the window.

Winner(s): The Legion of Doom

Rating: B-

We head to the back again, where Mr. Perfect is standing by with his guests, Hunter Hearst Helmsley and the ravishing sight that is Sunny. Perfect tells Helmsley that it’s a travesty that he should have to put his Perfect undefeated record up against a scrub like Marc Mero…..especially with virtually zero notice. Hunter agrees, telling Perfect that there has been a miscarriage of justice here….and that somebody with his pedigree and win record should be out there tonight competing for Intercontinental gold, not sullying his hands on one of the great unwashed masses in Marc Mero. Sunny says that it won’t matter, because after Helmsley dispatches of Mero, Gorilla Monsoon will have no choice but to grant him a shot at the gold….because that’s what Sunny wants…and what Sunny wants, Sunny gets.

Rating: B+

VS.

Marc Mero vs. Hunter Hearst Helmsley (w/Sunny)

It’s Marc Mero’s in ring debut tonight, although some might say that he could have picked an easier opponent for his first match. Hunter Hearst Helmsley has been on a roll here in the WWF since his arrival….many have tried to hand his his first mark in the loss column, but they have all failed. It’s time to see what Marc Mero can do. He comes out first, to a pretty decent pop, it’s safe to say that a good portion of the crowd recognises him from his previous (more flamboyant) life in the competition. Hunter is out next, with Sunny ever present by his side, tonight clad in a long elegant red gown, that’s low cut enough to still give the men in the audience plenty to appreciate.

The two men get face to face and Hunter can be heard telling his opponent that he “should have stayed in the crowd where he belongs and left the fighting to the real men.” Mero answers this with a left jab out of nowhere that puts Helmsley flat on his back. Luckily for him the official had yet to call for the bell, because had this match been underway then it may well have been over already, along with Helmsley’s undefeated record.

The referee orders Mero back to the corner while he ensures that Hunter is in any condition to continue. It takes a few minutes, but finally this one is underway. Still clearly disoriented from the knock out blow, Helmsley is no match for his opponent in the opening minutes of this one, with Mero running rings around him. Despite his build and size, Mero is deceptively quick and far more agile than anyone would suspect by looking at him. Even bailing to the outside isn’t enough to give Helmsley a breather, because the minute he turns around he gets nailed with a somersault body splash over the top rope. Just when all looks lost for Helmsley, Sunny does what Sunny does best. With Hunter on the mat and Mero on the apron, it looks like Marc is going for a springboard attack of some kind…..what kind we will never know, because the second he jumps, Sunny sweeps his legs out from under him and instead of landing on Helmsley inside the ring, he lands face first on the ring apron.

Having finally slowed his opponent down (albeit with some help from his manager) Helmsley turns to his favourite page in his playbook, arrogantly displaying his believed superiority over Marc Mero, following up each move with a lot of showboating and even more posturing to the crowd. He picks up Mero by the hair, telling him that this is a wrestling match, not some dirty backwater boxing match, before delivering a humiliating back handed slap to the newcomer. Mero is able to duck under a clothesline attempt and rebound off the ropes looking for a strike of his own, but he’s met with a big high knee that almost garners Hunter a tree count. Helmsley keeps up the offence and almost manages to catch Marc with a Pedigree, but he gets backdropped before he can deliver the move. Seeing the tide beginning to turn, Sunny is up on the apron like a shot….but she maybe should have learnt from Paul Ellering’s downfall earlier, because all the gets for her troubles, is the impact of Hunter Hearst Helmsley being propelled into her…..and the impact of the safety mats on the floor as she crashes back down to ringside.

Distracted by his managers flight to the floor, Helmsley finds himself rolled up with a schoolboy, barely getting his shoulder up at two and two thirds. Hunter is quick to his feet with a look of anger on his face, delivering a hard clothesline to his opponent, then rolling to the floor to check on Sunny. He would have been better served by keeping up the offence on his opponent though….because it’s no sooner than he helps his manager to her feet, that he gets nailed in the back with a baseball slide from Mero, which in turn forces him to collide with Sunny….again, which then sends her crashing to the ground…..again, much to the delight of the crowd. Back in the ring Mero is able to hit a hurracanranna for a near fall, followed by a springboard leg drop from the middle rope for another. McMahon remarks that while you might not like Helmsley’s arrogant attitude, it’s clear why the man is undefeated here in the WWF, because Mero just can’t keep him down. The newcomer bends over to pick his opponent up but Helmsley hits him with a low blow that should have caused a disqualification had it not been for the fact that Sunny had commanded the attention of the official, claiming that she needs medical attention…..clearly she doesn’t though, because as Mero collapses to the mat in pain she tells the referee to pay attention to the match. Hunter picks up Mero and positions him for the Pedigree, but again he is able to backdrop Helmsley to escape the move. Hunter is up quick as a flash…..but he’s not as quick as Mero’s left hand, which connects with a stiff jab straight to his opponents jaw. Marc Mero makes the cover and just like that this match……and Helmsley’s undefeated streak…..is over.

Winner: Marc Mero

Rating: C+

Mero celebrates his huge debut victory while Helmsley lies unconscious on the mat before hopping out of the ring, past a distraught Sunny on the floor and heads to the back. Sunny clearly can’t believe that her man has lost, screeching into the camera that “he cheated…..that’s not fair…..you can’t just punch a man in the face, he should have been disqualified.” She climbs into the ring to check on Helmsley, but he’s still out cold. She tries to bring him to for a minute, before having a biggest temper tantrum known to man, hopping up and down and screaming…..before tripping over her long gown and landing on her ass, much to the delight of the crowd as we go to the back.

Rating: B-

Dok Hendrix is standing by, with both WWF Light Heavyweight Champion Chris Jericho, and one of his opponents in the first ever Three Way Dance, Elimination match coming up, the 123 Kid. Dok remarks that the two men appear to have developed a mutual respect for each other over the last few weeks, but tonight they will have to put that respect to one side if either man wants to walk out with the gold. Jericho tells Dok that he’s right, he has a lot of respect for the Kid, that’s why he gave him this shot to begin with….because he comes to the ring and asks for a shot at the title, face to face, man to man…..no cheap shots, no ambushes….just straight to the point….but he warns the Kid that while he does respect him, make no mistake that when all is said and done, he will not hesitate to put him away and retain his title.

The Kid pretty much echoes Jericho’s words, telling him that he respects his as a man and as a Champion….he says he sacrificed himself a few weeks ago to save Jericho….but tonight it’s all about the Gold….it’s every man for himself….and the only thing he’s saving….is a spot around his waist for that WWF Light Heavyweight Championship, because tonight, he will sacrifice everything to walk away with that title.

The two men shake hands as Dok sends us back to ringside, where Scorpio is about to make his entrance.

Rating: B

VS. VS.

WWF Light Heavyweight Championship Match: Three Way Dance

Chris Jericho (c) vs. Scorpio vs. The 123 Kid

It’s time for the first of three title matches this evening, as Chris Jericho puts his Light Heavyweight Championship on the line against both the 123 Kid and the man making his way to the ring right now, former Light Heavyweight Champion, Scorpio. Jim Ross and Vince McMahon talk on commentary about the clause included in this one, that who ever wins we will be looking at a fresh face challenging for the gold after tonight…..because after threatening to challenge for the championship week in and week out until he is successful, Jericho had informed Scorpio that there will be no rematch after tonight.

Scorpio is accompanied by the Knights, Bobby and Nelson as he makes his way to the ring, The 123 Kid who’s out next and Chris Jericho who makes his entrance last are unsurprisingly alone. The three men face off in the middle of the ring and the commentators discuss the fact that unlike previous multi man matches that we have seen here in the WWF, there will be no one to save you from a pinfall here, because of the elimination rule. One man will have to beat two, for the right to walk out of here tonight calling himself Champion. The bell rings and the three men are instantly in the attack. Despite the mutual respect between Jericho and the Kid, there’s no teamwork or holding back….you would expect the pair to gang up on Scorpio since both men have their issues to settle with him, but no….it’s all action from the start.

Jericho and the Kid exchange blows, before embarking on a fluid Lucha style reversal of each other’s moves spot. Scorpio attempts to cheap shot the pair from behind, but gets caught. He gets sent running with a double Irish whip from Jericho and the Kid, after which the duo hit a high double back body drop that sends Scorpio flying over the top rope to the outside. That should have put him out of commission for a while, luckily for him he landed into the waiting arms of big Nelson Knight, who catches his stablemate and sets him down gently. The Kid complains to the official, but is informed that the Knights haven’t actually interfered in the match, or laid a hand on either himself or Jericho….yet.

The action continues in the ring and is a real showcase of what these Light Heavyweights can bring to the table. All three men utilise the top rope, all three men are acrobatic and the crowd are treated to a real aerial display. Lionheart and Scorpio have tangled enough times now that they know each other’s move set like the back of their hands, able to prepare a counter for moves that haven’t even delivered yet….which is why when, after hitting the 123 Kid with a reverse DDT, Jericho goes to deliver a Lionsault but Scorpio is able to toss the Champion out to the floor and cover the Kid himself. He only gets a two count and Ross questions the logic of not letting Jericho connect with the Lionsault on commentary, reiterating the elimination rules….to which McMahon replies that Scorpio clearly wants the glory of the pinfall for himself.

On the outside the Knights attempt to soften the Champion up a little bit more, with Bobby holding Jericho from behind for his partner to nail him. Before Nelson can connect however, the official spots the interference and evicts the pair from ringside, under instructions that Scorpio will be disqualified if they don’t leave. Scorpio is beside himself at the decision….so much so that the Kid is able to take him by surprise with a quick school boy roll up….and three seconds later we have our first elimination.

Now we are down to two….Chris Jericho and The 123 Kid….and only one of them can walk out tonight as Champion of the Light Heavyweight Division. With the distraction of the third man now gone, these two can concentrate all their efforts on each other, delivering a good show for the live audience. There are near falls aplenty as these two men assault each other with high flying moves, each looking for the final impact that will put their opponent away for good. The Kid goes on a roll, hitting Jericho with a number of martial arts strikes, a series of clotheslines, a roundhouse kick and a running leg drop, before signalling that it’s all over as he heads up to the top rope. He comes flying off with a big moonsault….but Jericho manages to roll out of the way and the Kids knees crash into the mat. The Light Heavyweight Champion sees his opportunity and quickly applies the Liontamer, smack bang in the middle of the ring….it looks like our second and final elimination is on its way…..that is until…..

…..the KLiQ contingent of Kevin Nash and Scott Hall amble their way to the ring. Ross and McMahon question their motives for being here, before discussing the bad blood that had existed between Hall and The Kid, after the Kid turned his back on his friend….McMahon suggests that Hall may be out here to rekindle that feud. The pair make their way to ringside and Scott Hall hops onto the apron, yelling at the official. With little choice but to try and remove Hall and Nash from ringside, he takes his attention away from the action….just as the 123 Kid begins to submit…..unfortunately for Jericho the referee doesn’t see the submission….which means he also doesn’t see…….

……Aldo Montoya sliding into the ring, Light Heavyweight Championship in hand…..which he nails Jericho in the back of the head with, before sliding straight back out of the ring. The Kid regains his composure and picks up the Champion, before delivering a move he’s not used before here in the WWF, a sitout facebuster…..not that it was really needed, Jericho is still out from the Belt shot as the official registers the three count…..this one is over….and in the most controversial of fashions.

Winner: ….and NEW WWF Light Heavyweight Champion….The 123 Kid

Rating: B

Scott Hall picks up the lifeless form of Chris Jericho and tosses him through the ropes to the outside. The official collects the Light Heavyweight Championship from the timekeeper, but Nash snatches it out of his hand before shoving him out of the ring as well. Hall raises the Kids hand in victory as Nash straps his newly won title around his waist, before the three men embrace. Montoya slides out of the ring to collect something from the outside that he had obviously bought down with him before his heinous attack. He rolls back in and hands the item to the Kid. Nash and Hall stand back and applaud as the new Light Heavyweight Champion unfolds a crisp new KLiQ shirt, which he promptly puts on, displaying his new allegiance. Montoya looks on in the background, before the camera picks up him asking Nash when HE would get a shirt. Scott Hall picks up a microphone and tells Montoya that

“Hey Chico…..you want a shirt? You gotta earn those colours man….remember, you came to us….we didn’t come to you….the Kid here, he’s been KLiQ 4 Life….before there even was a KLiQ. You need to worry about the match later….and not about IF, you’re gonna get a shirt.”

Rating: B

With the KLiQ still celebrating their new newest members Championship victory in the ring, we head to the back where Dok Hendrix is standing by with The Million Dollar Man and his last remaining Corporation member, Psycho Sid. Dok asks DiBiase his thoughts on the KLiQ growing stronger…..by adding a former Corporation member in the 123 Kid….as well as his thoughts on the up coming match between Sid and Tatanka.

DiBiase says that the 123 Kid is nothing more than an ingrate….who will find out sooner rather than later that a smart man doesn’t put friendships before money. He says that leaving the Corporation will be his downfall…..just ask Steve Austin….he’s not even got a match tonight…..and if that doesn’t convince you, then just wait about five minutes and ask Tatanka….because five minutes is all that this man mountain standing behind me will need.

Rating: B+

VS.

Tatanka vs. Psycho Sid (w/Ted DiBiase)

It’s time for the Native American to step into the ring with the last remaining member of the Corporation, Psycho Sid. Tatanka has vowed to regain his honour in the eyes of his ancestors after his stint as a Corporation member and finally put an end to The Million Dollar Man’s group……but that’s going to be easier said than done. This might be a Corporation of one….but that one is the size of two men and as angry as ten…..couple that with the fact that he’s about as sane as Al Snow and you have a dangerous combination.

Tatanka starts out strong but it really doesn’t take the big man long to take control of this one….Tatanka might be fighting to regain his honour, but he’s fighting a losing battle against an opponent who is twice the man he is (even if he does only have half the brains). The Psycho one brutalises his opponent with a look of glee flashing in his crazed eyes. He could have pinned the man on a number of occasions, but chose not to, instead toying with the Native American.

It almost comes back to haunt him though, because while showboating for the disapproving crowd and with Tatanka set up for the Powerbomb, the Lumbee Indian is somehow able to power his much bigger opponent up in the air, dropping him to the mat in a modified Samoan Drop, reminiscent of his “End of the Trail” finisher….unfortunately he hasn’t inflicted nearly enough damage on Sid, meaning the big man is able to kick out at a count of two. Hitting that big move however clearly gave Tatanka a second wind, as he starts hopping about the ring doing his war dance, hitting his opponent with jumping ax handle chop after ax handle chop. They rock Sid but not enough to put the big Redwood down, so Tatanka climbs to the top rope and dives off with another big chop, this time dropping Sid to one knee. Seeing his opponent drop gives Tatanka the encouragement to try the move again, this time when he flys from the top rope however, Sid catches him square around the throat and delivers a huge Chokeslam…..your ancestors are shamed Tatanka, Sid just pinned you.

Winner: Psycho Sid

Rating: B-

After that display by Sid, we head to the back where Mr. Perfect is standing by with WWF Intercontinental Champion Faarooq and the rest of the Nation. Faarooq tells Perfect that tonight, Ahmed Johnson will realise that it’s better to stand with the Nation than against them, because while “the man” wants to hold them down, keep them at each other’s throats…..the Intercontinental Champion only wants to unite his brothers……one Nation, under Faarooq…..there is strength in numbers……and if Ahmed insists on continuing to challenge the Nation, he’s going to find that out.

From there we go straight to Dok Hendrix, who’s stood by with the Challenger, Ahmed Johnson. Hendrix reiterates Faarooqs comments, saying that the Champion sounds like he’s planning on using the numbers of the Nation to his advantage. Ahmed shouts for a little bit, saying that he’s never needed help to get where he is today….and a real man fights his own battles, he doesn’t hide behind his cronies……he says that they might be a Nation of five men, but like Public Enemy said….

“it takes a Nation of Millions to hold me back.”

Rating: A

VS.

WWF Intercontinental Championship Match

Faarooq (c) vs. Ahmed Johnson

It’s time for the second title match of the evening, as Ahmed Johnson faces the uphill battle of prying the Intercontinental Championship away from Faarooq and the Nation of Domination. Ahmed has been on the hunt for the title since Goldust was the Champion, but he’s yet to taste gold here in the WWF, much to the crowds dismay…..because despite his clumsiness and terrible mic skills, Ahmed is still tremendously over with the live audience…..and it shows by the pop he gets when he comes out.

The Champion is out next, flanked as usual by the sea of Nation members. The whole group gets into the ring as one, fronting up to Ahmed, before all giving the Nation Salute as one. Faarooq barks at Johnson that it’s still not too late to do the right thing, all he has to do is pledge his allegiance to Faarooq and the cause and he can save himself from a world of pain. Ahmed’s answer? Is to punch Faarooq square in the mouth. The official orders the Nation from the ring, because this one is underway.

Johnson tackles Faarooq from the offset, taking him down to the mat where he pummels him with rights and lefts. Faarooq scrambles away, but gets nailed with a big clothesline before he can get too far. Ahmed picks up his opponent and whips him into the corner, preparing to hit him with a big splash….but Kama reaches into the ring and drags Faarooq to the outside out of harms way before the challenger can connect. The Nation huddle together around their leader on the floor but Johnson doesn’t care….he runs the length of the ring and flys with a dive over the top rope to the floor that takes out all five Nation members. Faarooq is driven hard into the ring post and Ahmed charges with a clothesline attempt….but again Kama is there to save his leader….pulling him out of the way as Johnson connects with nothing but steel, wrapping his arm around the post.

Back in the ring, the Intercontinental Champion goes to work on the damaged arm and shoulder region. It’s all power and no finesse, but then Faarooq is a power house so what do you expect. There’s not many WWF superstars that could manhandle a 320lb mass of muscle like Ahmed Johnson, but Faarooq makes it look easy. Ahmed ducks a clothesline attempt, but gets caught on the other side with a big spine buster. Faarooq hits a big shoulder breaker, following that up by folding Ahmed’s arm up his back and slamming him down to the mat with the limb still pinned there. Utilising the Nation, he tosses Johnson to the floor, before drawing the attention of the official so that the four Nation men can beat him “like a government mule” as Jim Ross remarks on commentary. The Nation back away, damage done, and Faarooq tells the official to “count that clown out”…..and he nearly does, but Ahmed manages to roll back into the ring at a count of nine.

Feeling that the victory is in the bag, Faarooq slows the pace down and starts toying with Johnson. He whips him hard into the corner, so hard that Ahmed rebounds back to the centre of the ring, straight into a big backdrop. Faarooq goes to the corner again, this time using it to ram Johnson shoulder first, causing Ahmed to shout out in pain. Faarooq dusts his palms off in a “job well done” motion, before telling Scorpio at ringside to “watch this”. Going wayyyy out of his comfort zone, Faarooq decides to climb the turnbuckles, all the way to the top, before diving off with what looks like a diving shoulder block……overconfidence was his downfall though, because Ahmed was able to impressively catch the man and hit a huge powerslam to the mat.

The Nation look on in shock at ringside as Ahmed begins to mount a comeback. He’s still clearly favouring the shoulder though….not that it’s slowing him down, as he’s able to hoist Faarooq high in the air and plant him with a one armed spine buster…..a series of lariats with his good arm, followed up with a kick to the gut and a scissor kick for as near a fall as you could possibly get. Fighting through the pain, he indicates that it’s over and somehow, despite the bad arm, manages to hook his opponent and deliver a big Pear River Plunge before making the cover……..for what would have been the three count……had Bobby Knight and Scorpio not hopped onto the apron, drawing the official over to them.

No one ever accused Ahmed of being the sharpest tool in the box….which is why with the official distracted, he still sits there holding Faarooq down for the pin….unfortunately that leaves him open for a big boot square to the face courtesy of Kama. Kama rolls him to the outside, before propping him up against the ring post, where Nelson Knight charges, using all of his weight to crush the Challenger against the steel. Ahmed crumples in a heap, dropping to the safety mats, but Knight isn’t done, delivering a big splash on the floor that crushes Johnson like a bug.

Back in the ring, Scorpio and Bobby drop down from the apron, as Faarooq motions to the official to do his job, count the man out…..and that’s exactly what he does, because Ahmed is going no where. Faarooq wins….Faarooq retains the Title….but he didn’t do it clean….and he certainly didn’t do it alone.

Winner: ….and STILL WWF Intercontinental Champion…..Faarooq

Rating: B

It’s time to head to the back, where Dok Hendrix is standing by with WWF Tag Team Champions Rick and Scott Steiner…..and their partner for this evening….who is currently engaged in a conversation only he can hear just off to the left of them….Al Snow. Dok starts by saying that Kevin Nash and Scott Hall of the KLiQ have made no secret about the fact that they are coming for the titles currently held by the Steiner Brothers….and have shown with their actions as of late that the KLiQ don’t care what they do, or who they do it to….in order to get what they want. Rick is about to answer, but gets cut off by Snow

“I can tell you right now Betty, that there is no way……..what?……yes I’m telling him……let me finish…….there is no way, that Kevin Nash and Scott Hall are taking our WWF Tag Team Titles, tonight or any other night…..”

Dok looks at the Steiner’s for a second who just shake their heads. He tells Snow that first of all he’s right, the KLiQ won’t be taking the titles tonight, because it’s not a Title match…..but he’s not sure what he means by “our” Tag Team Titles….Rick and Scott are the Champions…..they are Tag Team belts…..two champions, not three. Snow pauses for a moment with a look of confusion on his face, before returning to his internal conversation.

“………did you know about this?…….it’s what now?……no I’ve never heard of that…….ok, ok, I’ll tell him…….Betty, let me tell you something, there’s a little thing in wrestling, something you’ve probably never heard of, not many people have, it’s called The Freebird Rule……that means that any two of this fine trio you see before you can defend those titles, at any given time………”

Scott interrupts Snow there, he tells him that he’s pretty sure that Dok is well aware of the Freebird rule, but they have been over this, he’s not a co-holder of the tag team titles and this isn’t about the gold…..

“this is about Kevin Nash, Scott Hall and that wannabe KLiQ lackey Aldo Montoya….this is about putting a stop to their little reign of terror…..a stop to them pushing around guys like Al Snow….because just like Roddy Piper say’s, we’re here to kick ass and chew gum…..and we’re all out of gum…..lets go.”

With that the Steiner’s walk out of shot, leaving Hendrix and Snow alone. Al looks at Dok for a moment, before pulling something out of his tights.

“……I have gum……you want gum?”

Dok shakes his head in disbelief…..

“Do you keep that in your tights?…..you know what, I don’t want to know.”

Rating: B

VS.

The Steiner Brothers & Al Snow vs. The KLiQ & Aldo Montoya

This match came about due to a strange combination of events, involving of all people, Aldo Montoya and Al Snow, two people who just last month would have been lucky to have been competing in the dark matches here tonight, let alone teaming with The KLiQ and The Steiner Brothers respectively. But one month later and here we are. Nash, Hall and Montoya come out first. Hall and Nash have their sweet black and white KLiQ shirts on (available at the merchandise stands kids…..tell your parents) and while I’m sure Aldo would love to be wearing one too, apparently he hasn’t earned it yet. He is clad in new ring gear though. Gone are the gaudy yellow/red and green tights, instead he is decked out in a pair of cut off jean shorts with black boots. The Steiner’s and Snow are out next, Rick and Scott with the WWF Tag Team Titles around their waists. Snow has obviously drawn inspiration from his new found friends, because he’s also in new gear, ditching the Avatar outfit for a more traditional singlet…..but enough about outfits, this isn’t a fashion show, it’s a fight.

And fight they did, as soon as the bell rings these six men go to work on each other, pairing up against their opposite number, with Scott and Nash, Rick and Hall….and finally Snow and Montoya all pairing up to beat the hell out of each other. The faces are fired up for this one….and it doesn’t take them long to gain the upper and and send the KLiQ contingent bailing to the floor. Rick runs around the ring barking like a dog and is about to drop to his hands and knees below Scott in that signature Steiner Brothers pose…..but Snow gets down there first. Clearly in a good mood Scott ruffles his new new pets hair, before Rick barks at Snow, then crawls over and cocks his leg as if he is urinating on him, much to the amusement of the crowd.

Nash is less amused on the outside, kicking the ring steps in frustration and generally pacing about looking surly. He grabs hold of Aldo by the scruff of the neck, telling him that if he wants to earn his colours, he’d better show him something tonight….before tossing him into the ring……where Rick Steiner runs through him with a Steinerline, sending him back to the floor. Nash says if you want something done right, then do it yourself, before stepping his big seven foot frame up onto the apron. Scott goes to charge him, but Nash tells the official to keep him back so that he can enter the ring. Both Steiner Brothers and Snow back up at the request of the referee, giving Nash the chance to step one big leg over the top rope…..unfortunately they had backed up so far, that Rick and Al get their legs taken out from under them as they are dragged to the outside by Hall and Montoya. Scott turns momentarily to see what happened, which is when Nash connects with a big boot straight to the jaw.

Rick and Snow attempt to get back into the ring, but the official has decided enough is enough, Scott and Nash are legal and everyone else is ordered back to their corners. Big Sexy punishes Steiner in the corner, choking the life out of him with the heel of his boot straight to the throat, before tagging out to Hall. Razor Ramon was always a cocky S.o.B….but since his rechristening as Scott Hall, that cockiness has gone up to another level. The Bad Guy stands over Scotty, paint-brushing the back of his head with arrogant slaps, before whipping him into the ropes and catching him, hitting a big fall away slam before tagging in Montoya. It’s quite a sight to see, the figure of Aldo Montoya beating down on a fallen Scott Steiner….certainly not a sight anyone was expecting, but since pairing up with the KLiQ Aldo has developed quite the aggressive streak. The three men make frequent enough tags that there is always a fresh man in the ring and each man gets ample time to lay a beating on Steiner…..Nash uses his power, Hall showcases some rarely seen technical moves, while Aldo utilises his speed, as well as the top rope…..at least until he comes flying off with a cross body attempt, that Steiner catches, reversing it into a huge overhead belly to belly that sends Montoya flying long enough or Scotty to tag into Snow.

The thought of Al Snow being the subject of a hot tag would have been unthinkable a few weeks ago, but the crowd get behind him here as he charges into the ring, swinging wildly at the man who turned on him on Raw. Montoya may have a new found aggression but Snow seems to have an entirely new move set. Aldo swings a left at his opponent, but gets his arm caught under the armpit of Snow, he tries with a right hand but gets the same result….and with both arms trapped, Snow unleashed a series of headbutts that daze his adversary. Nash and Hall hit the ring….but so do the Steiner’s, as much like the opening minutes of the match, we again descend into a six man brawl. Snow hits Montoya with a scoop brainbuster, which Jim Ross informs us has been christened the Snow Plow (quite how he knows that is anyone’s guess.) That should have been enough for the three count, but Hall breaks up the pin….only to get clotheslines right over the top rope by Rick Steiner. Scott peels Aldo up of the mat, hoisting him up on his shoulders for his Brothers patented Bulldog from the top….before Rick can leap however he is distracted by a lone figure that comes running down the aisle way….

…..it’s the 1,2,3 Kid (who has apparently grown a beard since earlier this evening…..creative licence there people) he reaches ringside and traded insults with Steiner, right up until the Dog Faced Gremlin is sent plummeting all the way to the floor on the outside, as Al Snow, who Kevin Nash had whipped hard into the corner, crashes into his midsection, head first. Scott is left in no position to defend himself with Montoya still upon his shoulders, which means that Kevin Nash’s incoming Big Boot connects square in the jaw. Aldo lands on top of the fallen Steiner and three seconds later we have a winning team…..unbelievably, Aldo Montoya just pinned one half of the World Tag Team Champions.

Winner(s): The KLiQ and Aldo Montoya

Rating: B+

After another victory for the seemingly unstoppable KLiQ, we head to the back where Dok Hendrix is standing by with the reunited Hart Brothers and their Brother In-Law The British Bulldog. Dok says that it’s nice to see the family getting along again. Owen says that it’s amazing what bonds a common enemy can rebuild. He says that Bret, him and Davey have wasted the best part of two years feuding with each other, while the real enemy has been building from within. That the KLiQ have done a lot of bad in a very short amount of time…..but the one good thing they did? “They gave me my big brother back.” Owen pats Bret on the shoulder, before the Hitman begins to talk.

“You know, it’s been pretty clear around here since Wrestlemania, that Shawn Michaels and his little Boy Toys Kevin Nash and Scott Hall, they’re willing to run through anyone and do anything to get what they want….and it’s not just the three of them anymore….Chris Jericho found out first hand earlier tonight that you need to be very careful who you trust…..It’s a war Dok….and in a war, you need to watch out for each other…..you need to rally together and rely on each other…..you need fighters……not cowards, who are going to slink away like snakes in the night at the first sign of trouble……yeah, I’m talking about you Jeff Jarrett, I haven’t forgot that you left me to the wolves last week……..you need……family…..and this family…..this Hart Foundation……we’re going to put an end to the KLiQ, once and for all…..starting with tonight…..when I stand in the corner (Bret shakes his head in disbelief) of Jerry “The King” Lawler.”

Rating: A

VS.

The Undertaker (w/Paul Bearer) vs. Mankind

It’s time for the Semi-Main Event of the evening, as The Deadman goes one on one with the Madman. We have a brief recap video before hand, documenting the build up to the match, before the eerie music of Mankind starts to play, as the deranged competitor makes his way down the aisle. Mankind rolls into the ring and sits rocking in the corner, awaiting his opponent…..and what an opponent he is…..the lights go out as the Gong sounds and The Deadman makes his methodical walk to the ring. A large part of the Undertakers mystique, is the fact that his entrance alone is enough to psyche out most opponents…..but Mankind isn’t most opponents….and he deliberately sought out the Undertaker upon his arrival to the WWF…..and he seeks him out tonight……

Before the Undertaker is even halfway to the ring, Manking has dropped to the floor and charged to meet his opponent in the aisle way, launching and landing with right hands that knock The Undertakers trademark hat flying. Mankind whips the Deadman into the steel barrier, following up with a running knee to the gut that drives Taker again, hard into the metal. The Undertaker staggers back into the middle of the aisle way and Mankind charges again, this time however the Deadman delivers a big back body drop and the deranged one lands hard on the floor. Undertaker picks up his foe, dragging him over to the In Your House set and slamming his head into the wall of the display house. McMahon mentions on commentary that this is NOT a no holds barred match and in fact the only reason that the referee hasn’t counted out or disqualified anyone, is because the match hasn’t even yet officially started.

Not that these two men care right about now. Taker goes to pick up Mankind, but his grounded opponent grabs a handful of tights and yanks the Deadman hard, driving HIM into the set, before pulling him up and Irish whipping him hard into the front door of the house, which shatters into pieces on impact. The two men continue their battle on the floor, trading blows and generally inflicting damage on each other, utilising their environment to its full potential. They eventually make their way down to the ringside area, where the Undertaker is whipped hard into the steel steps. Mankind charges at him, looking to drive his entire body weight into the Deadman, but The Undertaker moves and Mankind collides with nothing but steel. The official drops to the outside to try and restore some order, get the men in the ring, and get this one officially underway, but The Undertaker shoves him to one side, more concerned with inflicting damage on his opponent.

He picks Mankind up from the floor and bounces his head off the announce table before going for a suplex but his opponent is able to reverse it into a hip toss. Mankind pulls the safety mats up from the floor, exposing the bare concrete…..the official tries to stop this from happening, but again he is shoved to one side, this time by Mankind. Looking to inflict some serious damage, Mankind positions The Undertakers head between his legs, hitting a pulling piledriver onto the exposed floor. The Deadman is down and hurt but that’s not enough for Mankind, who climbs to the middle rope before diving off with an elbow drop to the floor, which hurts him as much as it hurts the Undertaker. Both men slowly make their way to their feet (with help from the crowd barriers) and continue to rain down blows on one another. For a third time the official attempts to intervene between the two men and for a third time he gets shoved away, this time by both men simultaneously, as they send him sprawling to the floor….third times the charm for this official, who informs the timekeeper that he’s ruling this a No Contest, just before he heads to the back out of harms way, leaving the Undertaker and Mankind to continue their battle at ringside.

Winner: No Contest

Rating: B

And continue the battle they do, although ironically they have finally made it into the ring. Mankind advances on the Undertaker, looking to apply the Mandible Claw, but the Deadman is able to block it and deliver a huge Chokeslam right in the middle of the ring. Having finally put Mankind down, Taker runs his thumb across his throat, signalling that he’s about to put him away for good. He picks his opponent up and gets him into position to deliver the Tombstone……which is when the lights go out……the darkness remains for a moment until suddenly a huge pyro of fire erupts from all four corner posts, which brings the lights back up……and these men out from the back……

……it’s Jim Cornette, dwarfed by the figure of his Doomsday Machine by his side. The Undertaker drops Mankind and turns his attention to the aisle way, where Cornette has a mic. He tells the Undertaker that…..

“Since Wrestlemania I’ve been coming out here with this monster by my side….I warned Yokozuna that he would make him suffer….which he did…..but I also said that he originally came to the World Wrestling Federation to inflict his pain on another……I just didn’t say who that “other” was. I’ve also called him a lot of things……my Monster…..my Doomsday Machine…..my Weapon of Mass Destruction……but what I’ve never called him by, until now….is his name…..and this is where it gets interesting Undertaker…..and this is where you want to listen real close…..because this man right here….this seven foot monster…..his name is Kane….and that “other” is you!”

The Undertaker is stood rooted in the spot, with a look of disbelief on his face….a look of disbelief that appeared at the sound of the name Kane, clearly it’s a name that means something to him, although quite what it means we will have to wait to find out…..because it’s at that precise time that Mankind decides to nail the Deadman in the back with a steel chair. The Undertaker goes down in a heap and Mankind is on him like a shot, locking in the Mandible Claw. With the Undertaker convulsing under the devastating hold, Kane slowly makes his way to the ring. He steps over the top rope and looks down at the suffering Phenom, before positioning the steel chair that Mankind bought in, into the centre of the mat. Finally releasing the hold, Mankind shuffles back to the corner of the ring, while Kane lifts the Undertaker to his feet. He stares him in the eye for a moment, before hoisting him up, and shockingly delivering a Tombstone of his own, driving the skull of the Deadman into the steel.

Kane stands back and admires his handiwork. From the outside Paul Bearer frantically calls on the power of the urn to revive his Deadman…..and slowly, with much effort…..the Undertaker does indeed sit up….only for Kane to pick him to his feet and once again drive his head into the chair with a second Tombstone. Again Paul Bearer waves the Urn at the Deadman…..and again it looks as though he may rise….but Jim Cornette strides over and snatches the gold artefact out of his hands, rolling into the ring and presenting it to his Monster. With The Undertaker half laid and half sat up, Kane holds up the Urn, staring at it transfixed…..before smashing it over the head of the Phenom, finally leaving him down and out. He takes two steps back and raises his arms high in the air, before bringing them down violently, as the fire again erupts from the corner posts as we head to the back.

Rating: A

With the backstage crew, medical team and security all out trying to clear the chaos that is going on in the ring, we are treated to a split screen, with a close up of Vince McMahon in the arena on one side and The King, Jerry Lawler on the other. Vince talks briefly about what we have just witnessed, before turning his attention to Lawler, asking him if he is ready for the biggest match of his career. It’s your basic response from Jerry…..he says that he can’t think of a better place in all of the USA to be fighting for the WWF Title, than right here in Memphis Tennessee. He says he’s won some of the biggest matches of his career here and tonight, he’s going to add one more to the list…..and the icing on the cake? Bret Hart stood right there at ringside to see me do it. He says its like he’s always told McMahon……it’s good to be the King…….but it’s going to be better, to be the WWF Champion.

With that Lawler walks off to prepare for his entrance. Vince is about to sign the interview off when suddenly he stops, telling the camera man to zoom in on the background….

…….where Mr. Perfect and Billy Gunn are huddled together, deep in hushed conversation. Perfect suddenly spots the camera man, telling him to stop filming them and to get out of their face, before the two men walk off together.

Rating: B+

VS.

WWF Championship Match

Shawn Michaels (c) vs. Jerry Lawler

It’s time for the main event of the evening, and the WWF Title is on the line. The King is out first and being that this is Memphis, he gets a huge reception as he walks down to the ring. He soaks up the reception, basking in the cheers, before his music comes to a stop and the Champion makes his entrance. Shawn walks out from the back….and surprise, surprise he’s not alone….Kevin Nash, Scott Hall and The 123 Kid are all following behind. Michaels gets into the ring and raises the Title high into the air, smirking at the King and indicating the the KLiQ contingent that surrounds the ring. The smirk soon fades though, as the Hitman’s music starts to play and the entire Hart Foundation makes its way to the ring.

Lawler and Michaels go face to face in the ring, as the KLiQ and The Hart Foundation position themselves on opposite sides at ringside….this one Ladies and Gentlemen, is underway. Jerry Lawler might only be a part time performer and in the twilight of his career….but this Memphis crowd has him performing like it was 20 years ago. If Michaels was expecting an easy ride against the “colour commentator”, he soon realised that he was mistaken. Every punch Shawn throws is blocked, while every one the King throws connects. For as unprofessional as HBK can be in the back, he bumps like a pro for the Kings offence, backing off with a look of shock on his face. Lawler Irish whips the champion hard into the turnbuckle….so hard that Michaels is turned upside down and staggers back into the middle of the ring where he’s levelled with a clothesline. Jerry tries again with an Irish whip to the ropes, but Michaels ducks a clothesline attempt, rebounds off the opposite ropes and goes for his trademark diving punch/forearm….but Jerry drops safely to the mat out of harms way and The Champion crashes to the canvas. The King climbs the second turnbuckle, looking to connect with a fist drop, but the 123 Kid grabs hold of his ankle, giving Michaels time to climb to his feet and hit a dropkick that causes Lawler to tumble over the top rope and down to the floor.

The KLiQ circle Lawler like a pack of hyenas, but true to his word, Bret is over like a shot, with Owen and Davey in tow, and in a blink of the eye the Hart Foundation and the KLiQ are trading blows. It looks like we could have a repeat of the previous Mankind/Undertaker match, as the two factions begin battling on the floor, but before it can go any further Gorilla Monsoon is out with a microphone. He orders all six men not actively competing to the back, stating that if he sees a Hart Foundation member out at ringside again during the course of this match, then NONE of the group will be eligible to challenge for ANY title for one full calendar year…..and likewise, should any member of the KLiQ decides to interfere in this match, then THAT entire group will be denied the opportunity to compete for Championship gold…..but on top of that, Shawn Michaels will be automatically disqualified…..and stripped of the WWF Title.

Michaels is furious at hearing this, but having little other choice, both factions disappear to the back, leaving HBK and The King to battle it out for the gold. Michaels has the upper hand, as Lawler is still suffering from his tumble over the top rope….and he goes straight on the offence, pummelling his opponent to the mat with right hands, before stomping a hole in him. He whips Lawler into the corner, before hitting him with a high back body drop. Shawn goes over to the corner and takes the padding off the turnbuckle, revealing the steel underneath. The King has gotten back to his feet and swings wildly at the Champion, but Shawn easily avoids it and delivers a back suplex, reminiscent of his original finisher, the Tear Drop Suplex. He goes for the pin but only manages a two count before Lawler kicks out, much to the delight of the crowd. Michaels looks frustrated….he stomps Lawler a few times, before heading up top, presumably looking for that patented diving elbow drop….but the King manages to get to his feet and staggers forward, knocking Shawn’s feet from the ropes, unfortunately for Michaels he lands crotch first onto the steel turnbuckle that he exposed earlier.

Feeling his second wind Lawler climbs to the second rope and delivers a big Superplex to the Champion that knocks the wind out of both men. Lawler is able to drape an arm over the Champion, but he only gets a two count. With both men still down on the mat, the official starts his mandatory ten count, The King has taken the brunt of the punishment tonight, but with the crowd cheering him on he is able to make it to his feet by a count of nine. Unfortunately so has Michaels….the King is on a roll though, and he counters all of HBK’s strikes with one of his own, backing the Champion into the corner, mounting the turnbuckle, and delivering the customary ten punches as the crowd counts along. Michaels staggers to the middle of the ring, before dropping face first to the mat. The crowd roars as Lawler drops the strap on his singlet….they know what that means…..McMahon remarks that we might be about to witness the crowning of a new champion (pun intended in sure) as Lawler picks up Shawn and positions him for the Piledriver. Before he can hit it though, we have an arrival at ringside…..

…….it’s Aldo Montoya! McMahon shouts out that the KLiQ were barred from ringside…..but Jim Ross points out that they may well have found a loop hole here…..because Aldo Montoya is not an official member of the KLiQ. Montoya screams abuse at Lawler from ringside, which is enough of a distraction that The King releases Michaels from the Piledriver and turns his attention to Aldo. He walks over to the corner and begins hurling insults of his own down at the young man. Being the veteran that he is, Lawler should have known better than to take his eyes off his opponent though….because Shawn charges and drives a knee into the back of the challenger, which knocks the wind out of him. Michaels picks Lawler up and slams his head into the turnbuckle three times…..the exposed turnbuckle…..meaning Michaels slammed Lawlers head into solid steel three times. The official backs The Champion up and checks on Lawler…..who is busted wide open, bleeding profusely from a gash in his head. Michaels advances on his wounded prey, but again the official backs him up, wanting to check that the King is fit to continue. Lawler waves him off and staggers into the middle of the ring…..straight into a Sweet Chin Music from Michaels. Three seconds later and we have a winner.

Winner: ….and STILL WWF Champion…..Shawn Michaels

Rating: A

The King is helped from the ring by medical personnel as the rest of the KLiQ join the celebrating Champion in the middle of the ring. They congratulate Michaels and Michaels in turn slaps Montoya on the back in a job well done, before all five men raise their hands in celebration.

This brings the Hart Foundation out, who stand at the top of the aisle way with a look of disgust on their collective faces. The KLiQ see the trio and the two factions stare each other down as the show fades to black lol

Rating: B+

Final Rating: B+

Final Thoughts

So In Your House is in the books and I have to say, for a secondary Pay Per View the reviews came back really positive….especially for the main event. It probably helped that we were in Memphis, but Shawn Michaels and Jerry Lawler put on possibly the match of the year so far….to a red hot crowd. Those Memphis fans were firmly behind the King and it really helped the match over all. Mankind’s stock has really gone through the roof after his no contest with the Undertaker. I was a little bit worried about the booking, because considering that Taker is a bonafide Main Eventer and Mankind…well let’s just say he was a solid midcarder at best….I had visions of having to talk the Deadman into taking a draw…..The Undertaker proved me wrong though…..and showed everyone in the back why he is considered a leader in the locker room, because he was perfectly happy with the finish, stating that he and Mankind could have some stellar match ups going forward, especially if the audience saw them on the same level……See that Shawn? See that Razor? That’s called professionalism you pair of Jackasses.

There was someone not so happy about tasting defeat at the Pay Per View though…..and for once it wasn’t one of the kliq…..Yokozuna was less than happy…..in fact he was downright furious at being asked to lose to…as he put it “Lawler’s jobber Dentist with a mask ok.” It took me a long time to convince him that while he might know that it’s Isaac Yankem under a mask, as far as the vast majority of the audience knows, it’s a brand new big bad, looking to tear the WWF apart….he agreed to take the loss in the end, but he wasn’t happy about it, even when I guaranteed him that I have big plans for him going forward to make up for it……yeah, I lied……I do have plans for Yoko don’t get me wrong…..just not especially big ones. His weight is starting to become a problem now, to the point that he’s out of breath a few minutes into his matches…..I’m genuinely worried he’s going to die on live TV……although it would probably pop a good rating (I joke, I joke…..sort of)

Anyway, that’s April over and done with, May here we come.
