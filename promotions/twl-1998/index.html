A roleplay-board-run fed whose weekly Monday Massacre and Thursday Night Rewind cards carried a heel World Champion's reign into a mid-run leadership handover, before the promotion moved from a roleplay board to an e-mail closed-flash system in October 1998. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

TWL traces its own opening to 19 June 1998, recalled in a retiring wrestler's own words on a later broadcast: “I came to TWL on June 19, 1998. The first day TWL opened.” It ran five championships — World Heavyweight, Intercontinental, Television, Cruiserweight, and World Tag Team — through 1998 into 1999.

Its own pay-per-view A Cold Day In Hell, 26 January 1999, turned three of those titles over at once. Billy Joe McCallister, a three-time World Heavyweight Champion, lost the belt to his own former Pain Incorporated partner Q-Ball, who vacated it himself soon after; Sandman, billed “the People's Champ,” dethroned Palimetius the Historian's Intercontinental reign, itself won by catching a dropped title belt off a ladder at an earlier event called Helloween; and the promotion's own president, Bill Collins, ruled the World Tag Team Championship vacant on the same card after one half of the champion pairing, Torture and XeRo, failed to appear.

Those vacated tag titles went to Sex Sells, the pairing of “Simply Irresistible” Jeff Jacob and “Dollar Man” Matthew Hutchinson, in a five-team ladder match the following month. A further event, Armageddon, cost two champions their belts to conspiracy finishes: Adrian Blades, billed the promotion's own longest-reigning Television Champion, was cheated out of the title by “Delicious” Dave Davidson, and Anthony Levert lost the Cruiserweight Championship to a masked luchador, El Puerco, after a rival's interference.

The surviving record shows the promotion closing briefly in the last days of April 1999 and relaunching under the same operator within the week, its own recruiting post naming Sex Sells — by then finished eighth on a newsgroup's own tag-team rankings — as proof of what it could still offer. The record runs to 5 May 1999; no further closure notice survives.
