An annual invitational tournament drawing rookie wrestlers from other Usenet federations to determine the year's best newcomer, reaching a third edition by the end of 1999. Its second and third editions survive across two full broadcasts, fifteen structured matches and an award with two recorded bestowals. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Kozak Newcomer Invitationals opened before this record's own window begins: its earliest surviving document, a pre-show card dated 2 February 1999 for a second edition, already takes for granted that readers know a first tournament ran and crowned a winner in 1998. It billed itself as a pay-per-view special under the house name Kozak Sports, promising “the GREATEST ROOKIE TOURNAMENT OF THE YEAR” and “The BEST just got BETTER.” It was built as an invitational, not a standing roster of its own: each edition drew a sixteen-man field from wrestlers already competing elsewhere, in federations including the UEW, the OWF and the EWO, inviting them to fight for a single trophy rather than fielding a cast it signed itself.

Its second edition ran across two broadcasts five months apart — a five-part script posted 23 February 1999 opening the field of sixteen, a results recap posted 20 April 1999 closing it. Prince Judife, Judas Lasher, Duke Putrid and Candyman were among the names who fought through two rounds and a semi-final; commentary on the opening bout called Candyman “a candyass” and predicted Judas Lasher for the whole tournament. The final came down to Candyman against Prince Judife, and Candyman won it, taking the tournament's trophy, the Kozak Cup, for its second year running.

Its own July 1999 video-script recap named both champions to date — “Heartbreaker” Steve Wallace, the first edition's winner, and Candyman, the second's — before opening entries for a third edition, “KNI 3: The Third Spectacular,” open to any wrestler who had debuted after December 1998. A bracket sheet posted 22 December 1999 seated the new field: Brian J. Blottie, Devon Case, the Warlock, Nyx Dunne, Devil Elvis and a dozen others drawn up against each other for a trophy the tournament had now awarded twice.

That same December, a public dispute over in-character comments made about one entrant led a different competitor to withdraw from the field shortly before the bracket was finalized. How the third edition played out from there isn't in the surviving record: the bracket sheet is its own last dated document, and the result it was building toward falls after this record's own year boundary.
