A Canadian-only hardcore fed that reversed its own membership restriction within hours of announcing it, and never advanced past recruiting. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Canadian Hardcore e-Wrestling Federation announced its homepage on 13 February 1997 with online signup and, in its own original terms, membership restricted to residents of Canada only — no AOL, Netcom, CompuServe, or other American internet service allowed.

The restriction lasted two days. A community reply, posted under the name MULFOOF, mocked the policy in full capitals — “GET REAL! AND GOOD LUCK. PEOPLE ONLY FROM CANADA???? WHY?” — and the fed reversed itself the same day it was quoted: “Seeing as this has pissed people off...Canada only? FUCK IT! the CHWF is now open to anyone, anywhere.”

Its character-creation questionnaire, posted 17 February, ran to forty-three questions on a TNM-simulated engine — a wrestler's finisher, its counters, its weight ceiling, whether it worked off a cross-corner whip or a turnbuckle setup, alignment as hero or heel — the fullest surviving statement of what the promotion actually wanted from a roster it never finished building.

Its last surviving document, dated 22 February 1997, admits the federation “desperatly needs wrestlers,” promising one card a week growing to two, and two pay-per-views a month, once enough of them signed on. No card, roster, or further record survives to say whether they ever did.
