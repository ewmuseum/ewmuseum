A multi-year, multi-pay-per-view Usenet e-fed broadcasting its weekly "Conquest" show and specials through the RAW Sports Network, independently voted the newsgroup's own best women's-division federation of 2007. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Shootfire Pro Wrestling's own February 2006 newsletter announces “a brand new season” of its weekly show, Conquest, following what it calls “our wildly successful Shootfire: Violent Ascension Tour,” broadcast free through what it names the RAW Sports Network. That episode's own main event put “AsH,” billed “The Cruiserweight Icon,” defending the SPW World Title against Adam “FRIGGIN” Cage, and introduced a debuting tag team calling itself the RAW Deal, Ric Beauty and Chris Scarlet, alongside a rundown of prizes a group called the Shootfire Army had claimed for winning a Deathwish Cage Match over the Founding Fathers of Extreme at an earlier show, Charity Carnage.

Its pay-per-view that March, Culture Shock, staged from the Toronto SkyDome, put five championships on the line: the World Title, Ash defending against Larry Gionet; the World Tag Team Titles, Jeff Keenan and Spike Nelson against Chris Scarlet and Ric Beauty; a RAW World Title ladder match letting fans bring their own weapons, Vile Vince Viper against Jason Storm; a Universal Title international challenge, “Deadpool” Ian Christoph against “El Savaje” Joey Savage; and an eight-woman Queens Chalice tournament run across two elimination matches.

The same card advertised a five-way King of Snakes match and a Nexus Death Match between JDM Superstar and “The Nexus One” Rex Caliber. No result from Culture Shock survives.

The promotion's own broadcasts continued for years afterward — voted the newsgroup's own best women's-division federation of 2007, and still running house shows as late as December 2009 — though no further document from either stretch survives here.
