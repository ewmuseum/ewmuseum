A continuous recruitment-only e-fed that ran for at least fourteen months without ever posting a card, a result, or a title change of its own. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

CyberRealm Wrestling's own dated posts place its start on or about October 1996 — a year-anniversary notice would later put it “1 year old october 1, 1997” — and every one of its 1997 documents is the same kind of message: an appeal to fantasy wrestlers on rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy to join, never a card, a result or a title change. Its earliest surviving advertisement, from 18 February 1997, names the goal directly: “CyberRealm Wrestling goal is to get the top 100 role players, we have half of that,” hosted at a Tripod address and run, by its own later admission, on an engine it named only once, in passing, as “TNM.”

The presidency changed hands that November. A wrestler billed as Great Scott announced himself the new president, writing that “the old zuel guy quit last week” and that he was looking for “20 of the BEST wrestlers on the net” to rebuild around — the only named operator the record gives after the founding host, Zuel, drops out of it.

Two more recruitment pushes followed within weeks. “Join Before Christmas!” went out on 21 December, urging new signees to “copy and paste the address below” and join before the holiday; eight days later, on 29 December, CyberRealm Wrestling posted twice in the same day — once declaring itself “going into the second year” and still asking for joiners, and again admitting outright that “CRW has a mess up” after “16 months without TNM messing up,” announcing a restart with “NEW AND OLD WRESTLERS.”

How that second year went isn't in the surviving record. The next and last document is dated more than a year on, 29 March 1999: an announcement that CyberRealm Wrestling would “return to the net after being gone for months,” billed as “working on 4 years of wrestling in TNM,” and built around a World Champion, Great Scott, defending the position against a returning Trazan — the only mention anywhere in the record of a title actually being held. No further document survives.
