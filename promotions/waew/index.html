Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The World Association of e-Wrestling traced its own idea to 8 January 1997, a day after its founder stepped down as co-president of a different e-fed, and had briefly operated under another name, CeWF, before this one — per its own founder's aside, “my league WAeW [formerlly CeWF but we had to change names].” Its FAQ, issued 26 January, set one rule for new characters up front: “BE ORIGINAL. Do not enter any Sabus or Shawn Michaels…BE CREATIVE. Don't rip off a real life character either.” It staged out of a St. Louis studio, sanctioned three titles — the World Heavyweight, North American and World Tag Team Championships — and planned three pay-per-views for the year: Inagural Bash (30 March, Seattle), Sunwave Meltdown (4 July, St. Petersburg) and Autumn Armageddon (5 November, Pittsburgh).

Its weekly Wednesday magazine show, the Rewind Report, debuted the same month under co-hosts Kelly Summers and Ronny Wallace and built its first World Heavyweight Championship tournament through a run of taped promos: a self-styled noble, Lord Foul the Despiser; “Killer Kid” Brad Slack, closing the show with a promo aimed at Lord Foul; Casey Stanfield, who had wrestled as “Lord Nikon the Impaler” before, in the broadcast's own words, finding his roots back in the United States under his own name; and the Punisher, dismissing both men's confidence in one promo.

Elsewhere on the same broadcast, a bongo-drumming heel, Sinister Sam, taunted a wrestler named Power with his own “Sinister Death March”; a masked, executioner-coated Damien Dahmer threatened a rival, El Burrito; and Stormtrooper of Death vowed to take the WAeW Championship from Jack Deniro. The show closed by previewing a 23 February Chicago card, including a debut for the Cherokee Chief and a match pitting a hometown favorite, Aerial Man, against a disturbed, candlelit-basement gimmick calling himself The Phenom.

No card, match, or reign of any of its three titles survives; its qualifying tournament for a first World Champion was still building when its record stops.
