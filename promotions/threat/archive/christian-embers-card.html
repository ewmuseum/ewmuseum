Christian Embers

Real Name: Christian Embers Nickname: "Shoot Sensation"

Born: 6.4.70 Place of Birth: Des Moines, Iowa Hometown: New York City and Des Moines

Height: 6'2 Weight: 257 Lbs. Blood type: O- Eye color: Blue

Trainer: Rickson Gracie (Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu), Rick Bassman and Christopher Daniels (Professional Wrestling)

Theme song: "Vasoline" Stone Temple Pilots

Style

Competing style: Brazilian Jiu Jitsu, Greco-Roman Wrestling, various kicks, knees and uses of forearms (fights rather on the ground than facing his opponent, like a BJJ fighter would)

Signature moves:
- Rolling German Suplex w/ or w/o bridge
- Wheelbarrow Suplex w/ or w/o bridge
- Wheelbarrow Smash (Wheelbarrow Suplex into ropes, which causes the opponent to reverse directions, falling to the mat face first)
- Rolling Armbar
- Armbar (Can be performed almost everywhere, from a school boy to Christian jumping and grabbing the arm)
- Face Kick
- Dragon Sleeper
- Half Crab
- Figure Four STF (A figure four submission hold with the legs, but instead of dropping to the floor, Christian will flip his opponent to his stomach and then bridge backwards, putting pressure on both knees and the back)
- Shoulder Slam (Instead of a spinebuster, per say, Christian will not slam the opponent on his back, but rather on the left arm)
- Roaring Elbow
- Rolling High Knee (With the opponent laid on one part of the turnbuckle, Christian will run from the opposite, rolling (much like RVD for the Rolling Thunder) then springboard off the ropes attacking the opponent's face with his right knee)

Finisher: 1a. Crucifix Armbar(Submission) - A submission in a grounded crucifix position. One arm is locked between the legs, while the other arm is locked between the near arm
1b. Crucifix Arm Bar Takedown(Submission with attack) - A submission in a grounded crucifix position. One arm is locked between the legs, while the other arm is locked between the near arm. If the opponent does not submit to the streching of the pectorial and deltoid muscles, Christian will repeatedly smash their face with elbow or forearm blows
2. Dragon Wing Suplex (Submission) - Butterfly (Double Arm) Suplex in which Christian maintains the double arm lock and rolls over from the ground position to end up sitting on the opponent's thighs and wrenching the neck with the double armlock
3. Ember-assed(Grapple) - (Sitout Northern Lights Backbreaker) Christian will flip the opponent in a Northern Lights Suplex Fashion but instead of bridging back, he will land on one knee, sticking the other out, landing a massive flipping backbreaker on the opponent. Christian will usually drive an elbow in the sternum for more impact during the flipping.

Historical Info

Other/Previous promotions: Icons of Wrestling

Other/Previous teams: Gracie Jiu-Jitsu

Other/Previous managers: Rickson Gracie

Biography

Glory… a rise to fame … pride… I had it all with me one year ago. I was unbeatable, unstoppable… Frank Shamrock… David Abbot… Matt Hughes… those people were nothing compared to me just one year ago. You see... I was the king around the MMA… I was the tamer of the lion that was the UFC… I was the dominator in the dominated that was Pride Fighting…

SNAP.

One instant… just one submission… and that… all I had done… lost forever…

I was born in Des Moines, Iowa thirty-two years ago on June 4th. I don’t have many cherished memories of my pre-pubescent years. School was a breeze and sports like hockey and baseball were almost natural to me. But, there was resounding memory… it was the first time that I saw the art of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu… I was marveled at the athleticism and thinking skills that were involved in the sport. I thought to myself at the time, “I can do this… right?”

WRONG.

Countless videos and hours of Jiu-Jitsu led me to nowhere… but I wasn’t discouraged… I didn’t care how long it took. I wanted to succeed so badly… I wasn’t going to let anyone stop me. From then on, my early teenage years were spent on Jiu-Jitsu there after and my goal to be the greatest Jiu-Jitsu champion.

At the age of 15, my family and I moved to Manhattan in New York City. My father’s job as a day trader brought us great wealth but unfortunately for me, it also cost me an opportunity to know him better… back then… I did not care… my obsession with Jiu-Jitsu put my mind aside of everything…

I was accepted into Stuyvesant high school, and upon arrival, I immediately joined the wrestling team. I had thought to myself that my knowledge of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu would help me succeed in wrestling.

Not only was my presumption true, it led me to the city championship.

At first, my grappling skills were poor compared to the top contenders for the city MVP award, but my submission skills were highly touted and revered. This was now where my transition from Jiu-Jitsu to wrestling changed. I now spent hours with my wrestling coach then in front of the TV watching videos. I now spent hours training and working on my body then jumping on my big brother and locking an armbar on him.

My love for Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu was still in me however. For me, wrestling was just another skill to master… Jiu-Jitsu was my true love… my calling. It was when I was 17 that I found Rickson Gracie’s, the grandson of the “father of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu,” and part of the famous Gracie family. I attended his academy and in within one year, I was awarded with a black belt, a record time from anyone to knowledge till this date.

I graduated with a 3.6 GPA, a fantastic score for a great school like Stuyvesant, and went back to my roots in the mid-west and went to school at the University of Minnesota. I was accepted into Cornell, but I had no wish to stay in New York. I had a black belt in Jiu-Jitsu and was great grappler…

Arriving at the University of Minnesota in 1990 was almost like a life’s dream to me. The University of Minnesota had a great wrestling program and was a NCAA Division I school… I knew that I could’ve gotten BIG exposure in this school.

After some practice rounds and training sessions, I was accepted into the wrestling team. The coach saw me as a great prospect and believed that I was to make a great captain for the wrestling team in the future.

His prediction however, did not come true. I was not a team captain… I was not even a good wrestler… HELL, I WASN’T EVEN A WRESTLER.

On my first match, my Fibula and Tibia bones were broken from a wicked illegal kick followed by a spinning leg grapevine. The doctors told me I could not wrestle anymore.

SCREW THAT.

My recollection of college was the same as my childhood… nothing. Just a vast stretch of space from 1990 to 1994 that was void and meaningless. My will was broken and my spirit was diminished… even when I was fully healed, the coach did not accept me back to the wrestling team. He said it was “for my health.”

When I graduated school in 1994, I left with nothing gained except for a degree in business. I went back to New York to be with my parents and opened up a Jiu-Jitsu dojo in Westchester. From 1995 to 2000, I taught nearly 100 students in the art of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu.

It was on my birthday, my thirtieth, that I received a call from Rickson Gracie about Pride Fighting. Previously I had watched a couple of pay-per-views of UFC and thought it to be rather bland. I talked to Rickson for about to hour and before I knew it, I was to be booked on the next venue that was to take place it the Bukodan Hall in Japan.

July 9, 2000… I arrived in Japan, three days away from my bout against an unknown opponent. I met Rickson at the Hilton hotel and talked to him once more… for the next three days I trained, ate and planned with Rickson…

Three hours before my match, Rickson came up to me. “I’m confident in you Christian.” I shook my head in silent joy at Rickson’s confidence in me.

Two… One… it was showtime… for the first time I knew who my opponent was to be… it was a Korean fighter named Joe-Son. Joe-Son was highly skilled in the art of Joe-Son-Do, an ancient Korean fighting technique that had been kept secret to the rest of the world for centuries.

In within one minute, the secret of Joe-Son-Do was out… it was no match against my skills in grappling and submission. I defeated him in 59 seconds to a crucifix arm-bar, a move that had been a trademark submission through out all my years of grappling in high school.

From then on, I defeated every one of my opponents… quickly and effectively, almost usually with my then labeled “unbeatable” crucifix arm-bar.

In within a year and a half, my MMA record was a lopsided 12-0-0. Twelve victories in just a year and a half… it was a record that dropped jaws everywhere. The UFC however was my true calling. When I first saw this place during college, I was disgusted by the poor quality of fighting from people like “Tank” David Abbot but enjoyed the skills of Don Frye, Dan Severn and Ken Shamrock. Frye and Severn were excellent grapplers and Shamrock was a great submissionist, but neither were both.

I WAS.

My UFC record was an uncontested 7-0-0. It not only looked impressive for a “rookie,” but also looked just shy of a “legend.” I was almost there… the status of legend… the status of a god in MMA… of UFC… fighting in general.

But on one day… the twenty-eighth of May, my “almost legend” status washed downstream and rushed right into the ocean, disappearing into the far reaches of the deep void that is the bottom. It carried my pride, my self-respect… the confidence in myself that I had always had.

I stepped into the ring and within 20 seconds, I was defeated. Not only was my arm practically broken from my opponent’s arm bar… my pride was shattered into pieces. My “faithful” fans were amazed at my opponent’s ability and cheered for him. They now not only hated me because I failed as their hero… but as a person… they wanted me out…

My opponent was to be their new god… my opponent was Tito Ortiz.

I was done for.

He was in…

I… I was out.

With the fans backs turned and promoters laughing at me… I was forced to retire from the MMA with a more than respectable, almost god like record of 12-1-0… but that one dark loss put me out forever… into the void and darkness once more…

Now I turn to wrestling… professional wrestling, as my stepping stone to fame once more… to come out on top… to vanquish my “almost legend” to “legend” status… to make my fans turn back to me… to have the promoters be envious of me…

To be the best…

To be ME once more…
