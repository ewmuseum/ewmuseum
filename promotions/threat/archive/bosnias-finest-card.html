Bosnia's Finest

Wrestler 1: Risto Dizdarevic Birthday: 5/15/1984 Hometown: Sarajevo, Bosnia Weight: 253 lbs.

Wrestler 2: Nikal Dizdarevic Birthday: 11/10/1969 Hometown: Sarajevo, Bosnia Weight: 246 lbs.

Combined Weight: 499 lbs. Entrance Theme: Godinama by Dino Merlin Wrestling Style: Technical / All-Around Common Spots: Double Belly to Back Suplex, Double Gutwrench Suplex, Double Suplex, Boston Crab - Elbowdrop (Top Rope)

Bio: Risto Dizdarevic was born in 1984, more specifically May 15th of 1984, into the town of Sarajevo, Bosnia. He was born as a Muslim which most of the population of Bosnia was and still is. His mother and father weren’t well to do exactly but they weren’t poor either. His father was a lumber jack which was pretty well paying, also Bosnia’s biggest agricultural value. While his father was off cutting down lumber, his mother was home being a home maker. Risto had a brother the first year of his life but horribly he was found dead as he choked on a small piece of plastic leaving Risto an only child. His parents were heart broken for sometime but it even increased their outstanding love for Risto as they saw him as their only offspring. Risto was enrolled in school as a normal child would be; he was an quick learner and spoke Bosnian almost as well as an adult with in his first 10 years. He was very intelligent to say the least. His father was not only a lumber jack, he was also a pretty well known amateur wrestler, he was quite a strong man but yet not the largest, he was well known locally for being quite talented, sort of the towns pride. He was debating trying for the Olympics for many years but never tried as it would take him away from his family for training and he really was afraid to leave Bosnia in a whole, sadly the winter Olympics were held in Bosnia in 1984 but that was the winter Olympics which he was not to fond of at all, and really had no relevance to him. Risto was fascinated by his father’s favorite hobby and his father agreed to show him takedowns and such things. Things grew to more as his father began to work with him with strength as well as early as the age of 6 in 1990. This wasn’t long lasted but Risto grew large knowledge being very intelligent and some conditioning. As 1991 rolled along the Croats and Muslims of Bosnia and Hercegovina feared Serbian domination so they voted for a declaration of independence from Yugoslavia in October, 1991. The next year in April the European Community and the United States viewed Bosnia and Hercegovina as a separate country. As this occurred Serbia and some local population of Serbs were outraged and began to “ethnic cleanse” the country of Muslims, the Muslims were unarmed and frail to this attack. This event paid a major event in Risto’s life as it took the life of both his parents and many friends. One night as the Dizdarevic family was rather tense on the situation they were attacked for the fact they wouldn’t leave their house. A grenade was thrown by a Serbian which was angered and it killed both his parents as he hid under a near by table. A piece of shrapnel hit Risto as his forearm blocked his face, it barley touched his face but yet left a hefty scar on his forearm and a light one on his face directly under his left eye. He was never captured but he had to go through the pain of seeing his parents first killed, then dragged off, he spent the night shriveled up under the table with tears in his eyes, balling for hours on end. It was a horrid event, to be quite vague.

That next morning he snuck through the shadows to his uncle ’Nikals , he was his father’s younger brother. Nikal was a loving caring man. Risto’s father and him were very close in younger years and still were till his now passing. Nikal being the younger brother usually was protected by his older brother. He was only 15 years older then Risto so he was by no means an old man, their birthday only lied 2 days apart Nikal’s being the 17th. Nikal had not the life experiences as Risto’s father and they were very different. Risto was less known, less famous so to speak, and was usually in the shadows which he secretly hated. He was never the one to settle down, the closest thing he’d ever had to a son was Risto, which gave him a love as a brother or father would have for him. He worked with Risto’s dad cutting lumber and at night was quite the out going drinker. He tried to maintain status as a good athlete in younger years but failing to cover his brother’s shadow he gave up. He would of done anything to be known to be famous to be a sparkling star. He wanted to be “big” and he had no way to do it, which left him in a hole. He didn’t want to amateur wrestle like his brother that was too, well boring. He was just left empty.

They were able to stay alive in Sarajevo till late 1994 when Risto was 10 years old. They stayed in the country for about 2 years longer in a local town while they both worked until Risto turned 13. They were able to leave the country and get to America. America was different for both of them, Nikal spoke a bit of English and Risto spoke none. They were able to be located in Chicago, Illinois, by the point when Risto was 14. Chicago was supposedly the location of Nikal’s cousin, they were unable to find such a relative, mostly for the fact it was a rather large city. Nikal was able to secure a pretty well paying factory job with the help of a new fellow Bosnian descendent he met. Risto basically stayed in their small apartment most of the time soaking up old Bosnian novels or videos which Nikal was able to come across. Nikal’s English grew quite a bit, yet Risto’s intelligence grew but not on the subject of English. He barley knew any English at the age of 16. He was rather starting to form endurance and strength as he had nothing better to do then run or work with some weights which his uncle had picked up off a friend. At the age of 16 he got his first job at the factory his uncle worked at. His English improved some what but still lacked context, and he had a huge accent. He missed his parents, as well as Bosnia.

One day he came along a poster which read, well he didn’t know what it read, but it had a wrestler on it, which brought back memories of his father. He was interested in this so he wrote down the address which he didn’t understand but he knew how to navigate his way there. He arrived, but his attempts to speak in English failed and he ended up cursing in Bosnian. A highly respected trainer at the wrestling school thought he had a look of something special and he understood what he said. He grew up with the language, knew it well, his parents emigrated from Bosnia them self’s. This mans name was Dan Magas, he ended up making Risto. He took him under his wing and trained him quite well. He spoke mostly in Bosnian to Risto but yet used American terms for the moves. Risto caught on fast as his factory job paid just enough for him to enroll. He informed his uncle and he was very supportive, thought he had a chance to make something of him self in this foreign country.

Envy, the envy of Risto’s uncle quickly had him in the ring as well after a month, before Risto even found a federation Nikal was enrolling him self. Nikal wasn’t athletic for the most part having 15 years on Risto and not training what so ever through those years, he was getting a bit “lumpy” and had no idea how to wrestle what so ever to begin with. He had tuned into the fWo lately on their television in their small apartment and realized the glitz and glimmer of this wrestling. He loved it, and wanted in. So he enrolled, and they would make a tag team. This was Nikal’s chance to be something, to be bigger then his brother ever was. Risto wouldn’t argue even though he thought it was quite dumb, and didn’t want to. He respected his uncle and the fact he was the only family he had made him cling to the idea of a tag team. Nikal had one advantage though, his English was quite good, he could fool some as a pure American, he wasn’t as intelligent but his vocabulary was larger, and he was pretty good with people. After a while Nikal became an alright wrestle but still wasn’t even half as good as Risto, Dan Magas really didn’t care for him, the tag team would mostly end up clinging on Risto’s talent. Nikal had this plan that they would be famous in a year or so and be called “Bosnia’s Finest”, would it work? Who knows?
