Biography

You just don't get it, the life your living isn't like the one everyone else is. People are dying in Africa before they even reach their twenties or thirties. In the Middle East, starvation is common and women are mistreated. In China, the government kills your second child if you have one. But of course your living in America, and yes, Finland isn't an economically challenged country like most countries are over the world, but living on the Kemi River in Northern Finland is hell.

I was born on the cold frozen ground of the road from my parent's farm on the way to Kemi, the nearest town. December 18th, 1965, was the day I was born. Only 5 years after the United States lent money to Finland for Reconstruction after WWII. Most of Kemi and farms nearby had been wiped off the map by the Russians about 15 years earlier. The money was meant to build up industrial capacity and to rebuild what had not been rebuilt. Nobody knew it, but Finnish officials had been promising money through all the years but never delivered. My parents weren't even going to a hospital in Kemi, it was burnt down, they were going to anyone who had ever had a baby.

They didn't get there.

After my mom got over the pain on the sideroad, they headed back to the farm as work needed to be done. My mom still did all the work despite her just having me. I lived in my parent's lousy farm with the worst land in the world until I was 17, I had just finished my 9 year school course after failing once. After I got back from school I would work the land so the wheat and potatoes we grew would be fitting for selling while my dad helped out and switched back and forward between livestock and crops. I had 4 sisters, all worth nothing. I could've sworn he would've preferred for all them to been boys. He still loved them more, even if all they did were lousy house chores. I was bullied by them when I was barely a tot to when I was 18 years old. They youngest of my sisters was 6 years older than me yet they all never left my mom and dad.

I did, at age 18, two weeks after my birthday on January 3rd, 1983.

I left at about three thirty in the morning, one and a half hours before my dad woke up to start working on his farm. I thought about taking the truck, but then I just left a note and started walking down the road asking for a ride. I hitchhiked my way to Tampere where I arrived at about nine in the morning. I took a job with a lumbering company and I had to start right away even after having no sleep the night before. It was poorman's pay, four of us workers had to live together in a one kitchen one bathroom and one room apartment and we barely ever ate anything more than milk and bread. While I still suffered from Winter winds, the Summers were nice. The biggest change though were the 3 months of snow here and the eight through 9 months in Kemi.

Although I was anxious to get out of that crowded apartment, it took me twelve LONG years of cutting down trees and transporting the lumber down the river to Tampere before I got enough money to go to Helsinki and take a boat to Edinburgh, England. It was cheaper than an airplane and I was going to send the rest of my savings to my family who I had left 12 years before.

A month after sending the money to my parents in Kemi, I got a letter from my mom and my sisters thanking me for the money... than they wrote to me about my father.

He had died of pneumonia two weeks before I had sent the money.

They sent me a picture of him for me to remember him, I still yet to have a day when I don't carry that picture with me.

Since I arrived at Edinburgh, due to my limited English, I was used just to load and unload at an old warehouse. Four years of good pay compared to what I earned as a timberman, I looked to put my father's death behind me so I bought a ticket to the United States knowing I would easily get paid plenty enough to make a living for barely even doing any work. I went to New York City a day before New Years Eve took place, December 30th, 1999. The city was up in commotion, all these crazy lunatics were predicting the end of the world. When the New Year began, I bolted out of the Big Apple. I don't know where I went, but it was the only place bus tickets were still available to. When I got there, it was perfect. It was nice, cold weather which is nothing compared to Finland but still pretty cool. I interviewed for a job with some thing called PIW. I figured it meant Packaging Incorporated World or something considering there were some pretty big guys there. For some reason, they were all wrestling.

It didn't take a second before I figured that the W in PIW was for Wrestling. There had been some small wrestling organizations in Europe visit Tampere so I was not unfamiliar with it. But on the first show, the magnitude of the crowd in the arena was shocking. I didn't really like the wrestling thing so I just punched and slammed my way to victory that night. I stayed in the Pinnacle of Insane Wrestling for almost 2 years before it closed down. It was a nice ride. With all the money they gave me, I settled in Pennsylvania for awhile. I hadn't done much in PIW besides winning the TV title and the Hardcore title but it gave me the thirst for more wrestling. I went to other federations only to be disappointed with an abrupt one week end to them.

After about seven months of inactivity, in the summer of 2002, some jackass calls me up says he wants to hire me to wrestle.

He must've never seen me wrestle... heh.
