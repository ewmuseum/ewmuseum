Biography

I was born on the 8th of June 1975. My mom had told me that it was one of the warmest summers the Russia has had in the past few decades, and that it was a good day to remember for the Simunek family. I was put into the children’s wards with the rest of the children as usual and stayed there for the rest of the night while they stabilised me. Mom had told me that the doctors had found a minor brain problem but it was no real problem. I was released two days later with my mom and we went back to the family home.

Our home wasn’t big at all. Mom didn’t work at all, and dad’s job wasn’t exactly spectacular. He would go out late afternoon and wouldn’t return till late in the morning. Mom was the only one I really knew for the first five years of my life. She used to feed me the little food that we had, clothe me and take me to school. Ohh my school days were awful. Why? Because my dad forbid me making any friends. I never understood why, but mom always used to tell me that dad never liked having other children around the house. He didn’t even let mom have any friends back then, he just wanted it to be the three of us.

Dad was an evil character. Mom had told me that he had, had such a terrible childhood, his dad and parents disowned him at the age of 10, and he was then brought up by a couple of psychopath adults, who were later killed because of their hatred of Stalin. He had terrible scars across his face that used to scare me when I looked at them. I never understood dad. I never liked dad. He would always suddenly strike my mom or I because he would get angry for no reason. He would just look at us and punch us. He was terrible at times, loving at other times. He always told me that I should never trust Americans. I don’t know why, perhaps he just never understood them, but he had such strong believes about the cold war, that he refused us watching anything to do with American’s, unless it was something to do about them being murdered.

When I was 11 I moved up to the second state of school. By then I was so depressed and scared that I used to cower in the corners hiding the right side of my face. For some reason dad always used to hit me that side, saying that I was a two faced brat, and that I was useless. He never let me have a life, and I had really become to hate him. Mom was to scared to get a divorce, her parents wouldn’t accept it, and he would come and kill her probably. By 11 I had come to realise that dad was just using us as boxing bags, and that he wanted me to follow the same life that he had. If mom ever told him to calm down she would get a beating that would send a shiver down my spine.

School itself was just as bad as home. I always held my head down, trying to avoid showing the scars on my face. All the kids would pick on me, screaming names that I just couldn’t accept. I found that sitting in a corner and avoiding everyone else was the best way to get away from them.

When my 15th birthday came along I had such a great party with dad, mom and myself. It was the first time that any of us had actually been together as a proper family without fighting for years now. Everything was so normal that day, until he told us why he was so happy. He took me out of the home for a walk. It seemed normal to me, and of course I wasn’t going to argue, as he would lash out at us again, and I didn’t want that. We walked down some back alleys and he showed me to his gym. It was a tatty place, I’d never been there before because he said that I wasn’t ready, but I realised that this was his birthday present to me. I remember his words clearly that day, because for the next three years I would be inside of this same gym fighting for my life. “You’re going to grow up like a real man Vladimir,” he said to me.

By 16 I no longer could be bothered to go to school, and my depression was so high that I would literally wish that I could die. Every night for the past year I had been going down to dad’s gym and toughening up against the thugs of Russia. All of them were ten years olds then me, and would kick the crap out of me if I ever tried to fight back, but in the end I couldn’t stand there and take the beating, I had to respond. Every night mom would have to treat me for yet another battered bone, or cut in my face, and dad would never let me see a doctor, only his special doctor who I knew was nothing by a phoney guy in white clothes sprinkling some water over my face before I went another round with a guy 6’6 tall and built like a monster. Ohh god I can still remember what he put me through. I was never to forgive him.

Two years later it was my 18th, and I finally got my own passport, I had lost all love for this world, and I no longer wanted to live with my parents. For the past two years I had discovered although my dad had brainwashed me against the American population, that I should fly there whenever I possibly could, as there could be a job opportunity for me. I was one of the stubborn Russians who believed something that was never going to come true. I had saved up every tip that I had got from a fighter, which my dad hadn’t taken. I had got myself enough for a one-way ticket.

When the 18th of August that year came, my mind was so messed up that I had started to attack my own mother, because I was so angry. She understood why, and I just couldn’t live doing the same thing anymore. I packed my bags and kissed me mom goodbye, and promised that one day I would get her out of there. I waited in the shadows of my living room for my dad to return in the morning. 2am he walked back from his usual gym, and slammed the door. The memory is still clear to this day. I remember watching him walk into the living room and then I pounded. With everything that I had I began to batter him down to the ground, punching and kicking and eventually knocking him out with a firm blow to the head with a huge glass vase that my mom loved so much. I remember laughing hysterically as I left, slamming the door leaving him in a pool of his own blood. It finally dawned on me that I loved fighting. He had turned me into something that I should have not been at one stage of my life. Instead of the dream of being the president of Russia back in my early days I had turned into a gruesome bloodthirsty fighter.

I caught the plane to Texas, America, in the afternoon of 19th of August, handed in my ticket and vowed to never return to Russia again, because if I did I swore that I would find and kill my father, I knew my mother would be dead for what I had done.

I managed to get myself a job as a bouncer at a regular American club, wearing a white mask over the right side of my face to cover up the scars that I had. The guys always used to call me two face, but I never got angry about it. They were the first friends I ever had. Finally when I had enough I got myself a small apartment in Texas and went looking for fighting venues where I could fight to get rid of the frustration of the ignorant American people that I had met at the bar. They were exactly how my father had described them to me, fat, ignorant and rude. Finally I found a small underground fighting place. They offered me 200 dollars every fight, it was a lot of money for me back then. I accepted and every Sunday night I fought the local thugs and youths that stepped into the caged ring. Ever since then I continued to fight, slowly growing more dependent on fighting as I continued.

Now I find myself in thReat, a new venue...
