'The New Disorder' Sean Clayton

Real Name: Sean Clayton Nickname: "The New Disorder"

Also known as: TND, The Human Vehicle

Born: February 12th, 1976 Place of Birth: Detroit, Michigan Hometown: Detroit, Michigan

Height: 6'5 Weight: 289 Lbs. Blood type: O Eye color: Brown

Theme song: "Fuck Off" by Kid Rock

Style Competing style: Brawler/Powerhouse

Signature moves:
1. Twister [Swinging Neckbreaker]
2. Violation [Tazzmission]
3. Crossline [Clothesline From Hell]
4. Fallaway Slam
5. DDT

Finisher: Running Spear Finisher name: Disordered Drive/The Double D

Historical Info Other/Previous promotions: [Indy Promotions] - Golden Glory Wrestling

Other titles won: Golden Glory Regional Champion, Golden Glory National Champion

Biography November 25th, 1975

"Practice makes better, as no one can be perfect."

Those words were marked in the head of Henry Clayton ever since he had heard his father say them to him when he was a child. But Henry could be perfect, right? After all he was only in his early twenties; there was enough time to be the best at what he did. However, thoughts can be very deceiving as less than two years later, "The Human Disorder" Henry Clayton, a professional wrestler at the time, was rushed to hospital after he suffered a major injury in the ring whilst taking a bump in a very unorganized manner. He had attempted to land the move to get a few cheers from the crowd, also known as showing off. This all backfired in a matter of seconds, as Henry had suffered a broken neck with several internal damages, and without a doubt it was to cut his career short at the tender age of twenty-eight. Henry Clayton became paralysed; at what was also during the time his wife was pregnant. Henry had absolutely nothing in life to look forward to, except one person.

February 12th, 1976

This day marked a new beginning in the Clayton family. Mariah Clayton gave birth to a nine-pound baby who was to be named Sean Clayton. Henry immediately saw himself in the eyes of Sean. He saw stardom. He saw someone big.

However, Sean grew up in a whole different direction compared to how his father did. Raised up in Detroit, Michigan, Sean actually cared about getting good grades; he cared about having a good future. Because, of course, without education there is no future to look forward to, is there? Well that's what Sean believed, and it was a good belief according to his mother. His father didn't entirely believe this, however he allowed Sean to do what he wanted to.

At the early age of eleven, Sean's mother passed away due to an on-going heart problem that had bothered her for several years. It was finally her time, and Sean accepted it and continued to move on with life. Of course his grades lacked severely ever since that day, and his teachers understood. They offered him time to catch up with work after school. Some days Sean would attend them, but most of the time he simply didn't care.

December 7th, 1987

Sean had always known that his father was a famous wrestling superstar back in his time, but it was something that was never really beyond his interest. Sean had always seen wrestling as a 'violent' sport, and violence was not something that fitted his character.

Sean quiet often watched the wrestling events in his time, as they were a little less violent than the older days. However, he still saw it as something that he never wanted to get involved in. When his father had explained what 'blading' was, Sean thought it was ridiculous. He felt that when you bleed, you bleed in a real fight and not to cut yourself just to get some fans to chant your name.

But his father, Henry, had tried to change his perspective on wrestling. It took several days before Sean agreed to sit down and watch 'wrestling' from back in his father's days, and the wrestling in the modern days. Sean still did not deem wrestling as a prospect for the future, but he found great interest in just being a fan.

August 16th, 1990

By now, Sean was fourteen years old and was still studying to his full potential. Wrestling was just a side hobby, and Sean even found time to join the school wrestling club after his father recommended that he do it to 'pass time.' Sean was not the strongest in the club, in fact he found himself never entered into any of the tournaments. He found this very upsetting and decided that he'd work harder.

Weight lifting and bodybuilding was his solution to becoming stronger. His father was very pleased with Sean's determination and self-inspiration, and he always motivated his son to work harder and harder. Within one year, Sean's body had reacted very heavily to the intense working out. Offers were flying in to compete in amateur wrestling tournaments. Sean agreed to a few but had to reject many due to lack of time for preparation.

The first highlight of his wrestling career, though still looked at as a hobby, was when he won a junior amateur league contest, claiming the number one spot. Following this achievement, many other awards came his way as Sean quickly began climbed the ladder to the top.

June 2nd, 1995

It was on this date that Sean first realized that he wanted to continue wrestling as his profession. He sat down with his father, who was more than happy to see his son follow in his footsteps and continue the family legacy. By now, Sean had dropped out from college, pretty confident in his success in the wrestling industry. He continued to receive several offers from amateur leagues all across the country, but he wished to stay in Detroit with his father.

Sean's big break came when a small wrestling promotion based just outside of Detroit offered him a spot on their talent roster. Sean was hesitant in his decision, as his strength was only in amateur wrestling and not 'fake' wrestling. However, Sean decided that this was a great opportunity for him to learn the professional game and be even a bigger star than he had already hoped.

He accepted the offer from the league, which was to be called Golden Glory Wrestling. GGW held a weekly event at a local hall that had about two hundred seats for attendance; however, the average amount of attendants was fifty-sixty people. With extra guidance from his father, Sean adapted to the professional style of wrestling very quickly, and he found the brawling technique a perfect style for him as his enormous body structure and strength fitted the technique very well.

Being one of seventeen people on the roster, Sean was without a doubt the biggest wrestler there. Standing six foot five inches tall and weighing two hundred and eighty-nine pounds, other members of the roster shook with fear upon entering the ring against him. Sean knew that he was the strongest out the lot, and he smelt fear from whomever he wrestled, just like his father. Henry attempted to decrease Sean's level of confidence, as he knew himself that he was like that at one time, and was defeated by his own cockiness and arrogance.

Sean accomplished everything that there was to accomplish. He had defeated everyone on the roster, in what everyone classed as uneven match-ups due to Sean's incredible size advantage. Sean simply brushed off the negative comments he heard, claiming that he was, without question, the best that GGW fans had seen.

October 25th, 1997

On this day, Sean's father sat him down and asked him to relax from wrestling. Confused as to why his father would say after convincing him to enter the sport, he began to ponder the fact that his father was trying to slow down his success rate. He felt as though his father thought that Sean was becoming too good, or in other words, better than his father. Sean ignored his father, and continued work at GGW.

February 13th, 1999

It was a day after Sean's twenty-third birthday when his father, Henry Clayton, was tragically killed in a car accident. Sean felt as though everyone he ever had was lost. Although he didn't have much people to look up to anyway, his father was his idol. He new that he was following in the exact same footsteps as his father, but he believed that there was still a hell of a long journey to travel to get anywhere close to the level that his father was at.

Sean, wrestling for a small promotion in New York at that current time, decided to call it a day at that time. He decided that he would leave wrestling for a year or two to get his head together, and that was exactly what he did. He stopped training, and lost his shape. Sean began to become a little lazy, as he stopped receiving offers. He realized that finding a job is much harder when you have been out of the game for several months, or even years.

Sean felt that he was once again ready to continue in his father's trail. He began intense working out again and it took a lot of time and hard work for Sean to be as fit as he once was. But he managed to get there, and he was once again in perfect shape to work as a wrestler again.

May 19th, 2002

Now at the age of twenty-six, Sean has received all the necessary learning that he needs to succeed at a professional level, and all he awaits for is the right offer to come along. His father was a legend in his own rights, and Sean wants to be just as good, if not better.

People looked up to Henry Clayton as a role model. They chanted his name every time he walked down an aisle. Even in Sean's mind, Henry was a legend. Now Sean thinks, with a huge bulk of confidence, that he can gain that kind of spotlight. He wants to be in the same status that his father was in.

Sean looked around several places in desperate need of a wrestling promotion. After hard weeks of searching, he was directed to a long-living wrestling promotion in the name of World Wrestling Revolution. Immediately, Sean caught an interest in WR. He saw people of a great work rate, he saw a large fan base, and he saw huge publicity. Sean knew that this was one place that could challenge him to walk that extra mile. He decided that if there was one place he wanted to be, it was WR.

August 1st, 2002

Word went across to Sean that WR were currently hiring new workers to add to their current roster. Sean knew that it would be hard, but why pass up on a great opportunity? If he didn't make it, he didn't make it, but Sean was desperate to make it because here was the place where he could switch his status from 'nobody' to 'legend.'

Sean put his name down to be considered as a possible candidate for WR. Still following his father's footsteps, he even named himself after his father. From, "The Human Disorder" Henry Clayton, Sean decided that he was the next member of the family to continue the tradition, so he would become, "The Newly Disorder" Sean Clayton.

However, WR was short-lived and Clayton never really got much of a chance. He then moved onto a promotion called Threat Wrestling which was opening. Clayton took a great interest in this promotion in which he saw tons of potential.

Now, Sean only has one goal in mind. If he is to make it into Threat Wrestling, he is going to work day and night to become better then his father. His father tried but failed. Many other people have tried but failed. But Sean will not accept failure. He wants to only be one thing.

And that is to be the best. And at the prime age of twenty-six, who is to say that Sean cannot climb the ladder of success to becoming the best ever superstar the world has ever seen? According to Sean, no one has the right to claim that, especially if "The Newly Disorder" is going to be around. And now, Sean will begin his legacy, simply ignoring the one message that his father left for him.

"Practice makes better, as no one can be perfect."
