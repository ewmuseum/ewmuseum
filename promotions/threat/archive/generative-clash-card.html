Generative Clash

Wrestler 1: Stephen Martin Birthday: 7/4/82 Hometown: South Berwick, ME Weight: 200 lbs

Wrestler 2: Zoe Antoinette Birthday: 1/10/82 Hometown: Boston, Mass., through South Berwick, ME Weight: 162 lbs

Combined Weight: 362 lbs Entrance Theme: "Baby's Got A Temper" by Prodigy Wrestling Style: High Flying Madness with a technical edge. Antoinette takes the flying route. Martin is a bit more grounded. Not that they both aren't insane -- they pretty much are. Willing to try any move that requires the flight of their bodies.
Common Spots: Monkey Flip Senton(flip one so they hit a senton), Over-Partner Diving Tackle (you know, one runs into the ropes, comes back, the other one ducks and the runner throws himself over the ducker and into opponents), Bulldog into Six-Inch Dropkick, Double Anklelock.

Their finisher would have to be The Miyazaki: simultaneous Lionsault (Martin) and springboard spinning legdrop (Antoinette).
Bio: Today's generation. Disenfranchised from the world. Uncaring of the past, the present, and the future. People without a point to their lives, who have nothing but their own interests in mind.
As Marvin once asked... "What's Going On?"

There are two people in this generation, though, with thoughts beyond that, thoughts of making a crowd "pop". One's a bi-sexual Italian with a changed last name, the other a descended French-Canadian.

Talk about a culture clash. Yet they've linked from things that don't involve their basic ethnicities; racism is nothing to them. They've spent enough time together to know each other's beliefs, and each other's thoughts, practically. They communicate nearly without speaking.

For two people that have only known each other since, say... the year 2000, that's intensive.

When Zoe and Stephen met, it was from the most random thought in Stephen's head: he'd call an acquantince of one of his friends to make a prank call. When he tried the Antoinettes, though, he got Zoe. And as such, he didn't hang up for an hour. And they talked the following day at school. From there, they became somewhat of a brother and a sister. Closer than most could be.

However, this was in the year they graduated. They went their seperate ways. Zoe went to Boston University; Stephen simply spent time in his town. Worked to get money to do something with his life... anything. But he didn't know what.

He sat down to watch TV one night -- his favorite show, Sunday Night Slaughter. He had a cigarette burning. He didn't inhale for the entire first five minutes of the show -- in fact, he barely ever moved the cigarette to his hand. He crumpled it out to the point where ashes were creating bomb-like smoke clouds as he spun the end across smooth plastic, all the while keeping his eyes on the TV.

Wrestling. Professional wrestling. It was interesting. He was a lightweight -- somewhere in the vicinity of 170 pounds -- but his thought process had locked onto this. No way was he going to turn away, change the channel, do anything of the sort.

He was hooked.

It was a week later that he called up Walter Kowalski's school in Massachussetts. Within two weeks of that call, the money he had saved up went straight into Mr. Kowalski's bank account, and he spent hours before and after scheduled classes, learning exactly how to be a great wrestler. Slamming, being slammed, throwing himself over the top rope onto other students. Kowalski would sit back and clap. Martin was beginning to look like his best student since Paul Levesque.

Stephen graduated from the school in six months, thanks to his intensive training. With that graduation came an extra fifteen pounds of muscle, making him 185 pounds, and a slightly sore back, which still nags him -- but he still pushes himself, even with it always being there.

At the same time, Zoe was on summer break. She spent most of the time sleeping.

Stephen called her at about three fifteen in the morning at some time in July. Their conversation was a lot of catching up. And then, finally, Stephen Martin popped a peculiar question: "Would you want to go into wrestling with me?"

She refused immediately. Swore at him, saying he couldn't possibly expect her to want to get into something as boorish and childish as pretending to hit someone.

He didn't give up. For three and a half hours, the conversation went on. It was seven in the morning, with the sun shining down like an interrogation lamp, that Zoe conceded to Stephen's idea. Two days later, she took a leave from school.

He took her to Kowalski. He looked her over -- she wasn't Joanie Laurer, that was for sure -- and thought it over.

In the end, she paid half-price because Stephen Martin was his prize student of the moment. She spent eight months with both Stephen and Kowalski drilling her. She spent hours in the gym with her best friend, turning her body from a sleek ex-gymnast and ballerina look to something rougher, tougher.

They went onto the independent circuit together, and Zoe discovered the simple joy of putting on a show. She had been a major dramatist in high school, doing about eight productions for the school in the four years there. This fit into that mold perfectly.

Zoe's graduation from wrestling school was in October 2001. For sixteen months, the two worked the independent circuit.

Now they've got a chance at the big time. Stephen Martin, Zoe Antoinette -- calling themselves Generative Clash, because it sounds cool -- are shooting for the star.

However... there are dark shadows that creep around the back of their heads, reminders of some moments in their past, both together and alone, that could come up at any moment and create chaos. If only because fate makes the best things bittersweet, is why these things will not leave them be.

They've got a journey of journeys in front of them.
