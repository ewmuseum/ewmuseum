Biography

I came, I saw, I conquered. That's bloody all you need to know.

...

Oh, stop your whining, fecks. I'll release my life biography whenever the sodding hell I want to. Here's a little preview to whet your appetites;

At a time in my life, I spent six to seven months in the Australian Outback. The site for Survivor 2, and also the homebase of the Outback Fighter's Guild, one of THE toughest fighting arenas in the world.

I had acheived a pretty high degree of success in the OFG. I managed to outlast 14 other men to be crowned Annual Desert Challenge 2001 Winner, a huge feat for someone who had only been fighting for three and a half weeks.

It was then that my long-lasting feud with Aristida began. A feud that would last to the very last second of my OFG contract. We battled each other tooth and nail for about five and a half months, each of us being able to oust each other every single time. If this guy was in the Underground Fighting Federation...

Well, he would have become huge. He would have developed himself into an unstoppable force.

Of course, nothing is unstoppable. Aristida learnt that first hand. He tried to teach me a lesson, but in the end, I taught him the meaning of living for the moment. I thought him just how tough one can be when pushed to an extent. In the end, I proved myself to be the better man. Some would say I did it by cheating. Well, life isn't fair. God didn't tell me that my life would be rosier than a rose garden. Nobody was promised a fruitful and fair life.

Deal with it, my motto is.

My life was rocked by a series of devastating events a few years ago. Events that would change the course of my life. Mainly because I dealt with it wrongly. I lashed out at innocent bystanders.

But if I had not done that, I would still be a pawn in this chess game for life. I would still be playing by the rules set by the gamemaster, and following the paths offered to me blindly. That's not what I'm about. Not after experiencing some of the darkest hours of my life.

Now, I play by my own rules. It didn't start out that way, though. I started out as a good guy in the Underground Fighting Federation. I wanted to have friends by my side, and generally make a name for myself. That got me nowhere when it came time for the big Ultimate Heavyweight Title fight at Revelations 1. I was duped by Phoenix and lost the chance to make history. Jason Slade beat me to it.

That's when I really started thinking... does being a good guy have any advantages? I constantly changed my stand on the good and the bad theory in the UFF. Until I finally got what I wanted. What I should have won at Revelations 1.

The Ultimate Heavyweight Title was finally, after a year and a half, around my waist. I lived through it all. I lived through the invading UFL boys, I survived the plague that was the Horsemen Of The Apocalypse. I survived three months of no fighting due to a contractual clause.

I survived it all. I became the best, after overcoming insurmountable odds. Challenge after challenge, I knocked all of them down and plowed my way to the title.

And then, I found my answer.

Being bad was the only way for me.

Without it, I may have well donned a pink g-string and paraded around in Madison Square Garden during a New Yorks Knicks game. What has being good ever done for me? In the long run? Sure, for a short while, I lived a good life. There were hardships, but I overcame them.

I overcame the odds better when I decided to just fuck everything. No more pretenious bullshit. As a bad guy, I'm making some big money, and I also get satisfaction. Satisfaction of seeing the look of bewilderment on the faces of law enforcement officials. Satisfaction from seeing the look of despair on the faces of opponents, when they realise they've been outplayed and outfought. When they realise that being good does nothing for them.

Poor tards.

Oh, and my record for the OFG?

40 wins. 6 defeats. 4 draws. In the space of seven months.

... Yes, fear me.
