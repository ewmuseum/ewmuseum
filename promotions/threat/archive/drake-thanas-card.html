Historical Info

Biography


Growing up in upperclass America isn't as easy as it sounds. Sure, it has its definite pros, but only to those on the outside looking in is it so glamorous.

Take Drake Thanas for example.

The son of a wealthy, yet indifferent father, Drake usually could have whatever he wanted. Only, he never asked for much. Drake was content with what he had. When his friends at his local private school would sit and talk about the newest stuff they had or boast about how rich and wealthy their family was, Drake would stand off in the corner. He was always a happy-go-lucky kinda guy, and always seemed so innocent.

Enter boxing.

Drake's dad was always very fond of boxing and, naturally, instilled in Drake's mind that REAL men fought to the death. Tooth and nail. Drake began to take courses and after a few years entered the Golden Gloves. He never won much, but he didn't care. Drake would just do it for the thrill of fighting. Championship didn't matter to him. He just loved the sound of crowds roaring. He loved being apart of the grandeur of it all.

But Drake would soon learn that boxing really WAS as corrupted as it is known to be.

Thanas was told by his dad that winning was everything and to never give up. He cherished these morals. So when he was demanded to take a dive to a young hotshot boxer out of Philly, Drake refused. In the process, he was severely beaten by many inside the business and was left bleeding on the cold street. He was lucky to be alive, and hailed forth from that day he would never enter a ring again.

He was wrong.

After getting a referral from a friend, he was told he "might look good" in a pro wrestling ring. Drake laughed hard at that one. I mean, fake wrestling? Come on, was this guy serious? Indeed. Drake went to a local show anyways just for shits and giggles. Only he wasn't giggling after the night was over. He was plastered with hysteria. He loved the feeling of being there, in the crowd. Here, the fans would roar even MORE than in boxing. There was more energy, more excitement. What was he missing? He soon started taking a wrestling course. Although tough at first and not used to abandoning his boxing principles, he was always told

You're a natural.

Drake brushed it off and soon started to genuinely respect the business. He also started making serious waves among the local scene. Soon, he wasn't local - he was national. Drake signed a lucrative contract with the WWA and soon dominated the tightknit intersection of feds. He tore through opposition night in and out. And although some nights he would leave with a leather strap and gold, he didn't care. He did it for each and every fan that would pop for his bodyslam. For his moves. For

Him.

Drake was just about to completely accomplish any goal he ever had in wrestling. But he would soon experience something that in the three years he's been in the business he's never had before. His act was getting stale. His moves became repetitive. His popularity waned. The fans

Booed him.

The equivalent to a twelve-inch stake through your heart, Drake was devastated. He would try harder than ever before to gain their support. But everytime he landed that classic Roaring Elbow, the crowd exploded into rage against him. He was hated. It was consuming him. It was almost an obsession to figure out WHY the crowd hated him. But before he could, his anger and frustration ate him alive.

He spat at them.

He loathed them for.. loathing him. He began to curse them at first, but in time he just ignored them. He was a hollow wrestler, just doing it for the money anymore. In the end, he left because the amount was too paltry for something you are tired of. And Drake was tired of wrestling. So, he hung up his black boots and ill-fitting spandex to pursue a career in Business; after all he did have a degree from USC. And so it was that way for many months. He had he job in his office and everything was just right.

Until one night.

His friend was a big fan of wrestling. BIG fan. He had bought tickets to a local indepedant fed, brand new on the scene, and told Drake he HAD to go with him. He knew of how Drake had been a pro wrestler - he saw all his old gear after he helped him move in. So at this show, which Drake REALLY didn't want to go to, there are just filing in. Drake didn't think twice to remain incognito, and this would alter the course of his destiny. His friend started acting like a jackass and Drake had to restrain him. The scuffle made a scene seconds later

Hey? Isn't that DRAKE THANAS!!

Before he knew it the arena was chanting his name, ignoring whatever match was going on. For a small period of time Drake OWNED that arena. And as he left that night, he knew he had to go back. Like aldrenaline in his blood. His blood still boils to this day, his craving for nostalgia higher than ever. He wants to loved again. And the fans are willing to love him.

The only question is, Can Drake make an even bigger mark this time? Or will his attempts to be loved backfire?
