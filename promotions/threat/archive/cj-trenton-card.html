Biography


From riches to rags.

I wouldn't have it any other way.

---------

You think being born into the lap of luxury is easy?

Well, you're right. The only child of my family, I was naturally spoiled every waking minute. My father - a high-profile lawyer following the family lineage - coupled with my mother - a successful model - made enough money to live the grand life. The three of us lived atop the highest hill in the city in our mansion. But little did my parents know how I truly felt; I hated it. In my adolescence they would send me to the best private school around. I looked just like all the other kids there in my dorky sweater and loafers, but I wasn't. They all talked about how much money their parents made while I stood off in the corner and played by myself. I was the outcast. And I suppose it was about this time that my parents began to realize something was wrong.

Years passed. My life, although extravagantly lavish to those who didn't know me, had been the same mundane routine. I had grown hollow on the inside. It was in the last nineties that my Senior Year of Sacred Heart High School was coming to a close. I was happy about that, because it was all about cliques, cars, drugs, money, and women at Sacred Heart. I slaved through the years figuring one out of five wasn't bad. Of course my parents never understood why I only had a handful of friends - the kids who felt the same way I did. Anyhow, the year was over and my father began to increase the pressure on me. He would attempt to coax me into following his footsteps - his "legacy" as he would often refer to it - and practice Law.

Unfortunately, I had radically different ideas. In my youth I stood out in a major way from the other kids. I listened to Metal while they listened to whatever was trendy. I learned electric guitar while they learned the piano. But I also watched Pro Wrestling. My whole life I had watched, and my whole life I had been enamored. I can't describe the mystic allure of it all, but it was truly mind boggling. I always told myself I wanted to be a Pro Wrestler, and even in my late teens I ran the idea over in my head. At first it seemed like a silly childhood dream, but as I watched more and more, and as pro wrestling grew to it's climatic best, I somehow convinced myself it's what I wanted to do.

So one day, I announced it to my parents. At first, they didn't take me serious. But as reality set in, they seemed taken aback and even letdown in a few ways. A just sort of left it at that and waited a few days to bring it up again. I was relieved when they told me they loved me and would support me, even if they didn't exactly agree. I knew on the inside though, my father was completely crestfallen he wouldn't have the torch to pass to me.

I enrolled in Les Thatcher's Heartland Wrestling School. I struggled for the first year or so, but eventually broke into it. I was told I was pretty gifted as most other guys had quit long by the time I was still going at it. I gave it all I had, and as a result I walked around beat-up for a good three years. After learning initial and then more advanced training, I began working matches. I began wrestling on the Independant Scene. My wrestling career had taken off, which was all I could ask for. And I slowly began to develop into a person that I'd always want to be. After living and working hard for the past three years, and then looking where I came from, I was disgusted. It came to me just how averse to the wealthy life I truly was.

I had discovered myself.

And subsequently, I had discovered my own philosophy on life and how I was going to live it. I left Ohio and moved to New Jersey. I traded in the Mercedes I had received for my 16th birthday for some decent living quarters. I started working the wrestling scene over there to make a solid income. I grew my hair long and started bathing less frequently. The transformation was complete when in 2003 I officially changed my name from the much reviled and snobbish "Christian-Jakob Blakeney" to "CJ Trenton". I had forged the new rules I would now live by. As for what the rules are?

There are no fucking rules.
