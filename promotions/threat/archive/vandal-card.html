Biography

Ever since Darnell Corteia was a little boy, he had a dream..

To play in the NBA. As a youth, he idolized the play of Kareem Abdul Jabbar, trying to emulate the legendary center’s patented sky hook at an early age. Nothing could get him off of that goal, not the constant reminders of how hard it was to make it into a league that, not even his learning disability that forced him to struggle in school. Darnell knew that he was one of the larger children around his area, he knew how strong he was against the people his age, so unlike most kids he showed a determination he never gave his class work by playing against men 10 years his senior. As the level of competition raised, so did Darnell’s intensity, while he was on the court he wasn’t a youth he was a man. In the classroom, Darnell fell behind and was frustrated, and his parents were forced to make a tough decision, keep Darnell in school with the “regular” kids or send him to the Child Development Center (CDC).

While his energy levels were extremely high on the court, the moment Darnell stepped off the court he was something that strangers wouldn’t expect.. He was quiet, and kept to himself, and was timid to the point where he could barely look a person he didn’t know in the eyes. At the young age of 13, while Darnell competed on the blacktopped courts of Coatesville’s Ash Park, people were in his ear, telling him how great he was, telling him that he’s the best they’ve ever seen, and the voices got to him. The usually tame Darnell demanded that he be allowed to go back to the regular school, and his main threat towards them was that he would run away. The threat hit his parents, Brian and Georgia Corteia hard and they allowed him to make the switch.

After years of being on the front cover of the Daily Local News, and having more attention brought to him than the Red Raiders high school team, Darnell finally arrived in high school. And like was expected, two things took place he was placed on that Varsity basketball team, and he was pushed through school even though his education lagged behind. But all that mattered, was his play on the court, he was obviously a man among boys even when playing against guys who were three years older than him. Many people who’ve seen him play, have said that he was possibly one of the most dominant high school players ever to play. Everything came easy, the scoring, the rebounding, the shot blocking, everything.. Everything on the court. As he stepped off into the days, people were in his ear telling him how great he was and after all the years of the people saying it, the words finally broke through, and Darnell began to believe it. That summer he graduated, and declared for the 1988 NBA Draft.

The media buzz around the 18 year old phenom was as ground shaking as one of his pivot’s dunks, but he could barely hear any of it among the voices around him. And as people were talking about him being the first to make the jump since Moses Malone, people around him were pushing him and grabbing at him for a hope of bringing them up for the ride along with him. But for as many coat tail riders, and bandwagon jumpers there were, there were a fair share of people willing to take advantage of Darnell. And with a lack of a guidance, he was peer pressured into using drugs, to celebrate the great moment. Darnell was projected to be a first round pick…

After the physical his stock dropped, his character and intelligence questioned, and then no one took the risk of drafting the powerful 18 year old until the nearby Philadelphia 76ers selected him in the third round. People in Coatesville managed to attempt to turn the negative around into a positive, by saying that Philly was the ideal place for Darnell to land. As he got to play nearby, and he could help put the team over the top, but that wasn’t to be. Darnell sat on the end of the Sixers bench for a season, and his attitude was different and his motivation was sapped. Even though his dream came true, and his chance to step up and earn a starting spot, Darnell was still torn up over his decision, and torn up over not being a first round pick. The next fall, Darnell was released, never to see the inside of the NBA again.

Darnell arrived back home as perhaps the biggest disappointment in the history of the steel city. No matter where he went, eyes seemed to follow, in another life people would have followed asking for contracts, but in this one he had no job and was forced to live off the rest of his rookie salary. At first he refused to leave the house, but as he began to grow bored of the same life around the house, and he began to roam around and eventually the wrong crowd flocked around him again. This time the trouble Darnell got involved in, several breaking and entering cases, and assault, resulted in him getting time in jail.

The moment he got out of incarceration, his uncle Claude Mitchell took the chance on using him in [name withheld]’ FWA, the role that needed to be filled was that of a large silent enforcer, and from that point the Demolitionist was born. What Claude didn’t know was Darnell easily grew disgruntled, he came in to be the enforcer, and quickly he wanted to change his role and get more control over a character that was becoming a joke. The control never came. And it wasn’t long after when Darnell went on his own way, where he ended up in Y2KWF. After a disagreement with the way his character was handled, Darnell exited wrestling, and left for overseas with the dream of fighting his way back to the NBA. The L was calling, or so he thought, as his year and a half performing over in the European League got him no closer to the league than his drug binge did.

Now Darnell Corteia arrives at the doors of thReat, his dream destroyed by his own hands, and now he needs to find a direction a new dream, but can he stay away from the people out to harm him?
