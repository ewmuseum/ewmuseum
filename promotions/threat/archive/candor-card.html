Biography

April 22nd, 1972 welcomed the birth of Uchenna Jibril Rahid (a traditional Yoruba name) to parents Isa and Asante Rahid. His grandparents had been amongst the traditional leaders to cooperate with British colonials in the empire’s attempt to expand. Though Nigeria had gained independence almost twelve years earlier, connections still connected his parents and Britain.

At 6, Uchenna was sent, with a British family friend, Ulysses Calder, off to London, where he was to be schooled until he had graduated from university, at which point he could carry on in his father and grandfather’s footsteps, taking the government position of Import and Export Trade Counselor.

Rahid was a smart boy, learning as quickly, if not more rapidly, then his (for the most part) British-raised peers. His parents’ wealth allowed him to attend a private school, one of the best in England, where he was the only child of color in his class. This did not deter him, yet it began to set the gears in his already astute mind going: why was he the only dark boy in the class? Why did all the other boys and girls have nice British names, while he had “Uchenna Jibril Rahid?” As he progressed through middle school, this question of self-identity plagued the boy.

He turned out to be as brilliant on the football (American soccer) field as he was in his teachers’ eyes. Kicking a ball for the first time in Grade 7, Uchenna quickly picked up the game and was said to have had the skill to play on the Junior National time, but the troubling issues of his differences continued to force him to delve deeper into books and other sources of knowledge for the answers.

As whispers about “the Nigger” could be heard in the corners at his school, Uchenna, dubbed “Chenny” by perhaps a lone few friends and his classmates at large, he was now taunted mercilessly for his very dark skin and comical name. Skipping school became commonplace and any book on Nigeria, where the aging Ulysses Calder had told him to look for answers, became a refuge from the torture of daily life.

After Grade 11, Uchenna fully dropped out from school, devoting his life to his studies on exactly what and where he came from. Results of his research shocked him. He learned that these same people with whom he shared a country at the moment were the same people who had come into his country and forced men and woman off their cropland and into poor conditions. The British, until some 30 years ago (he was now 17), had been keeping his people crippled by controlling their government. The young man was furious. As Uchenna stormed to Calder’s room, he found the old scholar dying before his eyes. Rahid watched as the final seconds of Ulysses’ life passed him by, his eyes rolling up into his head. Uchenna had nothing left to do here, so packing his bags full of as many books as he could carry along with a few small personal items and all the money Calder had kept around, Uchenna set himself up a life in Britain.

He tried to dismiss the past as simply that, the past, but it continued to haunt him, entering his dreams and keeping him from focusing. He bounced around various trivial jobs, before a cult-like unknown immigrant from Zimbabwe somehow got a hold of him. What exactly happened is unknown, except that Uchenna disappeared from the streets of London and from anyone’s memory for almost seven years. When he emerged from the depths, he called himself no longer Uchenna, but simply referred to himself as “Candor.”

Most who had once been close to him claimed he had become a raving lunatic at the age of 29 and now was utterly brain dead, spouting useless fragments. One such man (Caucasian, as luck would have it), who had only hours before been speaking of Uchenna’s life deteroriating, was found, already having bled to death. Eight of the ten toes cut off and seven of ten fingers were eventually found a mile from his bloodied corpse. The case remains unsolved to this day.

Having been off the radar for nearly seven years, the man now known only as Candor had picked up a great deal of martial arts knowledge. In London, he recruited his own gang of associates, who decided to follow him to preach his word in the United States; Atlanta, Georgia, to be precise. He set up shop there and trained himself, readying to reach a mass market in no other way possible: the world of sports entertainment. He trained himself readily in basic technical grappling and Greco-Roman wrestling, gaining a knowledge of them both.

From there on out, his quest has been clear: to rid America of all those who represent the filth of the world. He has called for war between those pure and those impure, even for a nuclear holocaust to wipe out all but Africans so that the world, in Olorun’s image, might be wholesome again.

First stop: thReat.
