Atken: What made you come out of retirement? McCluskey: Well, once you've been in a business like this for as long as someone like I have, it's hard to get away from it. You've seen so many people who have said they were in retirement come back out of retirement lately. I've heard rumours that I was really just waiting for an excuse to come back, but that's not true. I came back because I have some unfinished business and I cannot rest in the knowledge that it isn't resolved. Atken: You used to be known as "The Cluck" in your popular days. Why change it now so late in your career? McCluskey: Change is sometimes good, and in this case, it's necessary. When I went under the moniker of "The Cluck", I was fun-loving, fool-hearted, and I lived under the illusion that the fans actually cared about the people they came to watch; that we actually meant something to them. Well, I've come to realise that at the end of the day, the fans don't care about you. Maybe they did back in the good old days, but not now. All they care about now is the next high-spot or person getting beaten to a pulp in some unprovoked revenge attack. The fans these days don't have any honour; they just like to see people getting hurt. For the fans, it isn't about watching men and women give all they have to fulfil their dreams, they like to see people struggle all their careers only in the end to see their dreams shattered. They hate people to be better than them. Atken: Does this apply to all wrestling fans, or just Americans? McCluskey: Well, back home [Northern Ireland] during my "retirement", I was recognised in the street quite a bit. People asked me for my autograph all the time. Little kids wanted to have their photograph taken with me. The last time I was in Canada, wrestling in the Bell Centre, I was cheered. People came up to me in the street to shake my hand. That all happened less than three months ago. But here in America, the fans just don't care about you. An autograph to them is something they can sell on e-Bay to make money to buy drugs or guns or alcohol. Unlike the rest of the world, where an autograph means something; and is something you cherish all your life, American fans don't care. They just don't care. Atken: What is "Neverwhere" supposed to mean anyway? McCluskey: To be honest, it's the title of a book I recently read. It's about London, England, and how that on top, there is this world where, to be straight to the point, everybody thinks it's great. But underneath that all, there's a world that most of them don't know about, that would turn their sentiments of the world they live in upside down if they knew about it. I've put that a bit simply, but I think that premise somewhat applies to this business. Most of you wrestlers live in a world where the fans love you, and the fans care about you, and the fans think you're God among men. Well, simply put, that's all bullshit, and I apologise for swearing. That's where Neverwhere comes from. Atken: ... Cookie? McCluskey: Chocolate chip? Atken: Who do you think we are, fWo?! Anyways, we had quite a bit of history. In a previous indy promotion, we were at one time about to murder each other. Why, and why don't you hate me now? McCluskey: Phil, that's all in the past. You ever heard the proverb, "we may be through with the past, but the past isn't through with us"? Atken: Proverb? McCluskey: Basically Phil, any animosity that I felt towards you is long gone. That proverb doesn't apply to us. What was in the past stays in the past. I have bigger issues to deal with now. And when I'm finished, I'll expect a "thank you" from you. Atken: Ohhhkay. Why were you so negative towards the fans at Flaw 19? I'm still a little confused about the whole thing. McCluskey: Have you not been paying attention? Though I suppose you weren't actually in the building at Flaw 19. Well, as I said then, I saw some things on the Internet that upset me. The fans who had cheered me to my face my entire career were spitting on my proverbial face. They had forgotten me like I was just another meaningless wrestler, and now that I wasn't around, they couldn't get their "fix" off of me. Atken: Fix? McCluskey: Yes, fix. They couldn't see me beating some poor guy, or some guy beating on poor me. When I heard the fans cheer as I beat guys all over the arena some nights; I thought there were cheering me on, however they just wanted us two men to injure each other as much as possible, because they enjoy the bloodshed. For the fact that I was retired, they ignored that I existed, because I wasn't around to give them that anymore. They were only worried about the "free agents" who could possibly come back and fill their lives with pain and suffering again. Atken: Then, is it only the Internet fans you dislike? McCluskey: Phil, Internet fans are just wrestling fans with a computer. There's no difference. They're all wrestling fans at the end of the day. Atken: What do you want and hope to accomplish in thReat? McCluskey: To be honest, I don't have a set plan right now. I realise that there are some people out there who might still like me, and who might be innocent. But there are hoards of fans who just want to see men kill each other. That's wrong. But that's the American way it seems. I'm going to give those fans something to think about. At the end of it all, they don't like me. But when I become a success in thReat, they're going to be envious of me. They're going to really hate me. I'm going to make the fans hate watching me wrestle, because they know deep down that I care as much for them as they care for me. They're not going to enjoy me beating people in matches, they will absolutely loathe it. If the fans aren't going to remember me for the right reasons, they're as sure as Hell going to remember me for all the wrong ones. Atken: You returned the week after The Vagabonds were defeated by The Resistance at Malevolence, which meant thReat became a strictly wrestling company. Is there any connection between the two events or is it just coincidence? McCluskey: Purely coincidental. I had an offer on the table to come to thReat. I didn't have to go around looking for a job at other places, when I had one right here to take if I wanted it. Atken: Sum yourself up in five words. McCluskey: Going to open your eyes.
