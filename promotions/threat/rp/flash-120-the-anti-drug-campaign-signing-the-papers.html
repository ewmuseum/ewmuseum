The Anti-Drug Campaign: Signing The Papers
Starring: Twizzy

Twizzy had been up for four hours before the phone rang. He had woken up at 9:30 am on this Thursday morning and grabbed a T-shirt. He went to his apartment’s gym and stayed for an hour and a half. He benched, squatted, cleaned, used the lateral pull, and worked on his calves. All this was after taking a Twistodium steroid, of course. He then went back up to his apartment and made breakfast. He had three strips of bacon and two sunny sides up eggs with a glass of orange juice. He crushed a Twistodium pill and sprinkled the dust into the eggs. He then took a shower and got dressed in a white Old Navy shirt and a pair of blue jeans. Twizzy watched CNN in the living room for a few minutes before turning to HBO and watching Fools Rush In.

In the middle of the movie he received a phone call that he answered on the second ring when the caller ID came through and he saw that it was his agent, Robert Schoch.

“Hello,” he said. Even with the caller ID Twizzy never acted like he knew already whom he was talking to, even though he obviously did.

“Hi, I have great news!” Robert usually did. He was a good agent and booked autograph sessions and things of that nature very well. Some of the time Twizzy could do that stuff on his own like in the meetings to become the Twistodium spokesperson.

Twizzy was curious. “What news? I didn’t know you were trying to get me something out of the ordinary anyway.”

“The executives at Drugs N’ Stuff,” the company that produced Twistodium, “and I came up with a great idea to promote Twistodium and help others in the process. Everything’s good, we just need you to say yes, and I’m sure you will. Listen to this.”

Twizzy was listening.

“DN’S and Twistodium are going to sponsor an anti-drug campaign and you, being Twistodium’s spokesperson, are going to head it.”

Twizzy’s eyebrows raised in excitement. “How am I going to head it?”

“The works man, the works! It’s a double-whammy! Twistodium gets promoted, you get your face on more posters than Victoria Secrets model’s tits, and we get people off drugs!” Robert shouted. “You’ll be helping people Twizzy. This campaign is going to be huge!”

Twizzy’s smile was broad.

“All we need is for you to come down to my office and sign the papers. This is big man. Big.”

The two said their good byes and Twizzy hung up the phone. He grabbed his wallet, his keys, and a bottle of Twistodium. He jumped out the door, locked the top lock (the bottom one locks automatically), and rushed to the elevators. He pressed the button, waited, and got in. He went to the apartment building’s garage, asked the attendant for his car, and waited four minutes. He got into his car and drove off for his agent’s office on Madison Ave., by Bryant Park. He looked the paper over and they looked good. He faxed a copy to his lawyer and then Twizzy and Robert Schoch went out for a late lunch at a small diner down the block. Robert had a ham sandwich and Twizzy had a chicken cutlet. The contract was back in Robert’s office when the two got back with Twizzy’s lawyer’s approval. Twizzy signed.

Ladies and Gentlemen, you will now see Twizzy’s face everywhere. From posters to billboards. From television to radio. He will be everywhere you look telling you not to do cocaine, heroin, marijuana, or smoke cigarettes and he will be telling you that Twistodium is an alternative because you do not want a person telling you that there is a problem that needs to be fixed and then has no solution themselves. He will be everywhere.

He will be the headliner for the big, new anti-drug campaign.

The Co-Dependant will tell you not to be dependent on drugs.

The Lost Soul will tell you not to be lost in that world.

The Addict will tell you not to get addicted.

The Druggy will tell you not to do drugs.

My God, what the fuck is going on?
