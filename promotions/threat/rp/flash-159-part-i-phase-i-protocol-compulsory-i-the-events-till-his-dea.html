Part I; Phase I - Protocol; Compulsory - I - The Events Till His Death

25 August 2003

Part I; Phase I - Protocol; Compulsory - I - The Events Till His Death
Starring: Rejection

As seemingly endless as the bottle seemed, he finally had managed to drink the last gulp of it. They say that 85% of the last swig is backwash, but that means there's about .75% alcohol in that last sip. However, it actually would depend on how much alcohol you drank.

For the man known as Rejection, that was one Rolling Rock. That's it. One beer.

But it didn't matter. He wasn't out to get drunk, or to drown his sorrows away. He simply had not had alcohol in a very, very long time. His tolerance to the burning liquid hadn't changed, and he was sure he wouldn't feel the effects of it at all. Just a slight, stinging coolness going down before it came to a halt. This was his night out.

He wasn't out because he needed to find some solace in the ending of his relationship with his long-time girlfriend. And he definitely wasn't out because he lost his match at Double or Nothing. He just wanted some time away, to clear his head. He wasn't thinking, trying to sort things out, he was just there to appreciate the silence his mind had achieved.

Of course, he couldn't help but think about all that shit. The cloth sling that held his lifeless left arm was a constant reminder. If he wasn't looking at it, it was looking back at him. It didn't hurt his shoulder, not that he knew of. If it did, he couldn't feel it anyway. Only his face, and a bit of his leg were functional. He walked with a limp, with the sleeve of his coat hanging loosely, absent of an arm.

He nursed the empty bottle with his hand, the green glass causing the wooden bar-top beneath him to have a green ellipse wherever the bottom of the bottle neared. He smiled a little, when he saw the emerald oval, dancing across the wood. But then the bartender came over.

"You all finished here?"

He didn't pay any attention to him at first. He just kept moving the bottle, noting the way the shape and color differed with the different angles of the neon lights. The bartender coughed, aritificial, and Rejection finally looked up at him. His smile had now faded.

"What?"

"Are you all done? Or can I get you another one?"

"No thanks. But I'll take this bottle though."

"Sorry, can't do that."

"Trust me, you can."

He got up, shifting in his pocket. The bartender started to raise his voice, as he yelled at him to give him the bottle back. From his pocket, he withdrew a nickel from the change he had been given.

"There. That should cover the bottle."

The bartender continued to ramble in frustration, but soon cut himself off. He couldn't really stand there and mouth off to the guy. He was in far worse shape than the bartender was. With a resilient attitude about him, he proceeded to wipe down the counter.

 

Outside, he strolled the sidewalks. Not in a relaxing manner or in a casual stride. He just needed to walk this way to get home to his apartment. He wasn't as happy as he had been before. Not drunk happy, just amused at all. And now it was gone, but not forgotten, as the bottle in his back pants pocket pressed against him everytime he took a step. He knew that it was doing that, but at the same time he didn't. He could scarcely feel it, and even then, he didn't know if it was the bottle, or him telling himself that he felt it.

He seemed so despondent to the few people he passed. Most nodded; one smiled, a female, but no one had made any connection with him. His appearence didn't exactly present a happy attitude. He just stared down at the sidewalk, the light grey of the concrete in contrast with the black pavement.

Only know, just a few blocks away from his residence, he realized that there were no people around anymore. Not that it meant anything, but he couldn't help but feel that it did. It was through this blanket of silence that he had heard her voice.

"I said stop!"

And he did. Looking around to see where it came from, only to then realize, there was no one there. No one was talking to him.

But there was someone down the block. And this time, instead of hearing a voice, he heard one loud footstep, and then a flesh-on-flesh boom. He squinted his eyes a little more, and saw the woman leaning against the car. Her pink hair color shined near the street light, giving away her position. And the man with the long blonde hair and leather trench coat helped just a tiny, tiny bit.

So he kept walking. Down the sidewalk, across the concrete tiles that made up his pathway. The cracks getting worse and worse as he walked, and then suddenly disappearing altogether, only to come back again as a tree root seemed to grow over-top of the sidewalk. And he stared at the grain so hard that he could almost make it out, as he started side-stepping faster. His left leg started shooting out straight and half-dead, as he quickened. He moved more agile, his stride using all of the energy in his only good leg. Until after enough speed and enough distance in a time quicker than he expected and had planned...

...he was right next to the blonde man, un-aware of his friend, standing a few feet away.

"And just who the fuck are-"

The smash of the bottle over his heap stopped his sentence, his forehead trickling just a little bit near where he was hit.

The other man didn't move.

He punched the blonde man hard, and then again. And before he knew it, that was all that he was doing. He let his body drop to the ground, kneeling not a possibility, he collapsed using the blonde man to break his fall. His fist didn't give off any feeling that it was in contact with someone else's face. He didn't feel his knuckle cut just a slight bit, his blood mixing with the blonde man's. All he knew, was that when he finally stopped, his hand was covered in blood, hardly any of hit his. And the blonde man's face was nothing but a wreck, of ripped skin, and a large, wide cut on his forehead. When he finally stopped, his right arm no longer with any feeling, he looked up.

The other man was gone.

The girl with the pink hair, was laughing. Not the out loud, bellowing from the stomach laugh. Just a slight chuckle. And the sadistic tone that she gave off wasn't one that you could become afraid of. It just lingered in the air like a moment of triumph, or victory. She had no idea what was going on, or who this man was. Why his arm was in a sling was something she didn't wonder to ask. Why he was there was a question that escaped her. So she kept laughing.

This was Reanna Hart, and this was how she met Trevor Wilson.
