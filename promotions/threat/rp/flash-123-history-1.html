History [1]
Starring: Chris McCluskey

I was always the good guy.

I learned first-hand what it was like to get knocked down when I was a mere boy at primary school, or “elementary” school, as some people call it. Every single day of my young life, I got punched, kicked, and beaten to the ground. I could have gone home and cried about it to my parents, or complained to the teachers. But I didn’t. Even at that age, I figured I was simply paying my dues. Maybe that was something a person had to go through in life, which would ultimately make me stronger. I always tried putting up a good fight – but I never ever won. I always went home from school with a deflated heart and deflated bicycle tires. It seems that in today’s world, the bad guys get the last laugh, even if it’s not funny. But still I tried.

Each and every lunchtime, I got bullied relentlessly, and for what? Why? Because I let a bunch of people walk all over me, because I didn’t respect myself enough to make it so that they couldn’t. But, being beaten silly for five years as a young boy had its perks.

The main one being that finally, after all the bloody noses, all the bruised limbs, and all the tears cried; I figured it all out. You just can’t let anybody take advantage of you. It would be a crime to do so.

I remember watching wrestling on a Saturday afternoon on TV. Maybe I was getting the crap beaten out of me each day, but some of these guys weren’t. I paid attention to those wrestlers who won, and how they won, and I took that to heart.

So, one day soon after, I grabbed the ring leader out of the blue, before he had a chance to hit me. I was smaller than him, but that didn’t stop me wrapping my arm around his neck and squeezing until he fell unconscious. Surprisingly, the rest of his gang just stood and stared. They didn’t pick on me from that day onwards.

And what was the lesson I learnt? From that day forward, anybody who didn’t like me could cry about it.

It was with that attitude I stepped into the world that is professional wrestling. It seemed like the natural thing to do. After years of me “losing” fights, I decided that it was my turn to start winning them. Plus, the money wasn’t bad, particularly for somebody with talent. After all, wrestling is a business; it pays for your home and your food and your family. You’ve got to treat it with respect or else you won’t be treated with any.
