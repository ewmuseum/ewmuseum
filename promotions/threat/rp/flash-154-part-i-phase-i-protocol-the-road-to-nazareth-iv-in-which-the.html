Part I; Phase I - Protocol; The Road To Nazareth - IV - In Which The Pavement Ends
Starring: Rejection

The metal teeth joined together, the black metal of the zipper combining both sides of the bag together. After that, the shoulder strap was re-adjusted, and fastened on to the opposite end of the bag. Slinging in the air, Trevor placed the bag across his chest, hanging it on his right shoulder. The Rejected one reached into his pants pocket, and pulled out the plane ticket. Opening the inside of it, he glanced across the words reading his name over, and over, and over again. Now, only a few people knew it. And no one would ever call him Trevor again.

Maybe.

He looked up into nothing, as a woman's voice came over the PA system in the airport. But they weren't boarding his row yet, so he moved to the opposite side of the opened area, and leaned himself against the wall. Inside his duffel bag, tucked away beneath clothing and ammenities, was a small book. Un-read, but with a full view of it's synopsis, he planned to read it most the while he was on the plane. Those few off moments when he could get the chance to read it. It didn't matter how much, page by page, passage by passage. Either or neither would suffice. In the grand scheme of eternity, he truly understood how much time he had.

The woman's voice came back on the PA. This time, they were "ready" for him. Putting pressure back on his left leg, he started to walk to the gate. With a pleasant smile, and an exchange of paper, he was on his way through the tunnel to board his plane. Straight to Vegas. He knew the departure and arrival times, but he had done the math and estimated it down to about seven hours, give or take. That meant one frozen meal, and possibly two bad movies.

He figured that he could probably read about a fourth of the book. He couldn't read that fast, but he never found himself having comprehension problems, so he figured that he'd get a good portion of it done. He also had a small notebook with him. Small little phrases seemed abundant that he figured would be useful later on. He wasn't looking to sound pretentious, or even sophisticated or intelligent. He already was those things, and a few sentences from some well-known book weren't going to change anything.

He looked around at the different labels, trying to find his seat. Looking left, right, left, right, left, right, he went forwards, and forwards. He was always going forwards. Then he spotted his blue window seat. Dropping the bag, he kicked it over against the side. He slipped into his seat, already knowing that eventually it would get uncomfortable no matter how many times he changed his position. Pulling the bag between his legs, he parted the metal teeth with the black metal zipper. Digging around the articles amongst the bag, he removed the book. Pulling down the tray from the seat in front of him, he placed his hands and the book on tray.

It had been a long time. Not for any particular reason, but it just never worked with him. His past never allowed for it to happen really, save for the time he spent with Lucretia. It was only about a year and a half, but it seemed like it meant something for a little while. He didn't really care anymore. There wasn't a chance for that to resurrect itself, only to be replaced by something else. It didn't matter.

He was happy, and he opened his book. Seeing the type and the font, he realized that he might get through it that weekend.
