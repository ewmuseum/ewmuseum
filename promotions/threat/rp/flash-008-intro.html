Intro
Starring: Eric Morgan

Eric Morgan is the name.

I haven't lived long enough to legally consume alcohol, yet I seem to have endured more than a man of a thousand years. The usual type would sit here and brag about how much he's gone through, how tough he is, how much he's suffered. But you've heard it a thousand times, and I'm not one to cop sympathy. I don't need it, I am alive and more satiated than any willl imagine with just this. If you breathe, and breathe easy. If you don't count your breaths, wondering how many you have left, then you're living a charmed life in my eyes. Thank the Lord that I live one.

Today, I ate at a restaurant, without planning it in advance. I just walked in. I did so without getting dressed up. It wasn't on a Sunday. I remember when i used to have to become regal only to share a meal someone else at the McDonald's in Watts. I've gone to restaurants before, but I just still can't get over the feeling of being able to order whatever I want. I can't get over the waitresses being nice to me; I can't get over them not yelling "what you want and then muttering "peckerwood" under their breath as they walk away. I can't seem to get over not having to look through my food for saliva and worse. I remember when things were worse than that. I remember when I'd eat things out of garbage cans because they didn't look too rotten. Now, I see this beautifully prepared and succulent delight before me, and I feel like breaking into tears. I feel like bowing and worshipping. I feel like kissing the young waitress that smiles at me as she brings me this. Homecoming is always touted to heaven. Have you ever had to cook the rat you were going to eat at home?

They say you can never go home, as if it’s a bad thing.

I never want to go home. Although, I suppose I haven’t told you why. I guess now is the time.

My name is Eric Morgan. I’m from Watts, California. I was born and “raised” within Watts. I guess “raised” could be supplanted with “suffered”, but it kind of goes without saying. Every kid imagines his hometown to be the worst place imaginable, and want nothing but to escape it. The only difference in my situation is, I’m right.

Watts is a blue collar town and probably the roughest of them all. Watts is the center, essentially, of gang activity in South Central. Basically, it is hell. The steps between many of its citizens and the penitentiary and/or death are very few and not very large. I am more than lucky to have escaped; I am blessed.

I won’t bother telling you what it was like for a “peckerwood” to grow up in the world’s worst black gang neighborhood. You can use your imagination, because I don’t like to use my memory anymore.

I won’t bother telling you how I got there, nor why, nor how I survived. These are stories for another camp fire, when the tears are drier.

I survived to become what I am, that is all that’s important right now. I’m a man free. I’m a man alive. I have a life ahead of me, not death. Amen.

I never want to go home, but I’ll always know that where I’m from made me what I am today. The things that some people forget the significance of, the precious things that they see as trifles, are precious to me because I’ve only recently become acquainted with them. I’ve lived my whole life with their dearth, and now I hold them dear.

Even though I may have had the worst place in which to mature, it made my life succeeding filled with more reverence for its greatness than if I had lived my life in Suburbia.

Amen.
