Past And Present [4]
Starring: The Cursed One

A light, barely visible blue immerged out of the corner of his eye. In an hour or so, the sun would wink divinely in the morning sky, reflecting gracefully in the morning due, and waking the people peacefully for their morning lives.

But for him, it was just be another day of self-pity mixed in with a little uncertainty.

“So what else happened with this tournament thing? Besides that guy named Twizzy.”

“I thought Twizzy would have been the end of the tournament for me because I didn’t realize how important the fucking thing was to me until a few days ago. But it wasn’t. Somehow I was able to defeat him, and I advanced to the quarter finals. It was there, a few days before Flaw 25 came around, that I began to realize that I wanted to win this.”

“I can’t really understand why you didn’t care in the first place. Something like that could get you a lot of money and fame, so why the hell wouldn’t you want that?”

“I don’t know. None of that really appealed to me then. And most of it still doesn’t appeal to me now. Now, it’s something… something else altogether. There’s a bigger reason for me going through with the tournament.”

“What are you talking about?” asked the man, giving him a strange look.

His head leaned back slowly, and his eyes stared into the sky, now catching samples of the dawn swelling just over the horizon. The darkened night was slowly bleeding away, and what little glimpse of the stars that had remained was hidden away by blissful morning twilight. He knew that he would have to get on a plane for Las Vegas in a few hours, but that was later in the day.

For now, he had to remember what had happened.

And why it happened.

“I guess I’m talking about ending my self-loathing, which is just what I the tournament was for before I realized it.”

-----

With the night in full effect and the only source of light climbing from the street lamps below, he looked over towards the edge of the building, where the source of the two feet could be clearly seen and made out into one image of sorrow.

A young woman, aged somewhere in her early twenties, sat on the brick wall that surrounded the border of the rooftop. With the snow falling from the sky like within a snow globe, she sat there with her beautiful read hair swaying and dancing in the wind. Her head never moved an inch and her eyes remained still, gazing into the street below. The crowd was standing there, but he was sure she couldn’t see them.

But for him, it was just another image of self-pity mixed in with a little uncertainty.

Without hesitating or thinking about what he was doing, he pulled out the camera that rested in his pocket, easily opened it, and snapped a picture.

“What are you taking a picture for?” asked a man standing next to him.

“I’m a member of the press.” he said, thinking up an excuse rather quickly and proudly.

The man nodded, and looked back over towards the young lady, who was still sitting there, looking at the snow filling street, waiting for something that was only apparent to her and her train of thought.

He knew, right then and there, that he was going to walk over, trampling through the fresh snow collecting like sand atop to roof, and attempt to talk to her. There would be seconds of hesitation, of course, but he knew that those would pass and he would go on, despite his better judgment. As he stood there, he was already contemplating what he would ask her. Things like what was her name and where she lived and what her thoughts were as she sat there on that ledge. Those questions would come.

But first, he had to know what had happened.

And why it happened.

“Mind if I go talk to her?” he asked.

“Just don’t say anything stupid.”

“Yeah, I won’t.”

His head leaned back slowly, and his eyes stared into the sky, which was white and dreary, snowflakes drifting down from the sky. He looked back at her, and slowly stepped over.

She never looked back to see who was coming.
