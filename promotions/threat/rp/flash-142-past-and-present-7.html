Past and Present [7]
Starring: The Cursed One

The faint patting of his boots echoed down the corridors and the hall as he walked upon the old, creaking wooden steps. His eyes tried to adjust to the mellowed darkness of the tired apartment building’s hallway, and were alleviated by the impending sunlight, now only an immature blue shining through the dirty windows. But, in his own mind, he felt no fear about tripping over his own foot or taking a fall down the stairs. Maybe it would help him a little if he got hurt before the big event.

Relief comes in all forms, he figured as he marched down the staircase embedded in dying darkness.

“It didn’t help any that this tag team called ‘The Faithless’ were up my ass every time I had a match in the tournament.” he spoke, his voice carrying on and the man walking down the stairs behind him waking from his unperceptive daze.

“Ah, what was that?”

He shook his head, contemplating whether it mattered enough to repeat or whether he would just let it slip into the past. He decided against the latter.

“Marcus Escobar and Geneva – they’re the Faithless in my fed, or so that’s what they call themselves. You see, when I came into this federation, I was going to make it my personal mission to rid the federation from the ‘filth between the cracks’, so to speak. So I came in, with no one knowing my name, and told them all that I was going to show the truth behind all their lives. The sinners would pay for their sins. Shit like that.”

The man nodded, never lifting his eyes and never removing his hairy arms from the deep pockets of his blue bathrobe. He grunted a little at the man, who was getting a little too tired to remain observant for much longer.

That didn’t matter.

The evening was falling asleep serenely, and the day was now slowly opening that one good eye, etching light across the distant edge of the world. For him, he would be moving on soon enough, and the world would just be tailing him and his misery-filled soul. There was nothing left for him here except the past. And the present.

“I thought of myself as their judge, jury and executioner. I came into the federation, and I chose Malik Roland as my first target straight off. I knew that he wasn’t supposed to be there, and I elected myself to teach him a lesson. After him came DeVante Stevenson, but with him it was different. In a sense, I knew that I would choose him anyway, but he was the one that got the upper hand at first. He attacked me in the ring first, even though in the end I was the won that walked out victorious.

“These things keep changing for me. Now I’m the target, and I am the one that is suppose to be taught a lesson. The tables of fate have seemed to completely turn on me, and now I have to stop them instead of it being the other way around like it had always been. That’s the thing about the future – you never know what it holds for you.”

The man nodded again, and then actually had a little feedback to go with it.

“I can see why that would piss you off, and I wouldn’t want to take their shit either, but what about the tournament? How’d that turn out?” Maybe he was paying attention after all.

“Thanks to R.A.T’s no show the previous week and the moving of the match, I would have to say that Flaw 26 was one hell of a night.” he said, reaching the bottom of the staircase and heading for the door.

“And Marcus and Geneva were there to make my life a living hell as well.”

He stepped into the street of Chicago, feeling a slight breeze flowing from the direction of the lake. The moon was still out, despite the imminent dawn kissing the horizon. Dim and as faint as a whisper, he didn’t see the same moon he saw that night. It was different. Weaker and more delicate then before. That’s how he saw it – it came in strong, full and bright, and then disappears when the sky gets to be too much for it.

Out of the corner of his eye, he saw something, light with black spots. He looked over immediately upon discovery, and realized that it was a book. A book missing a cover. He picked it up, and flipped through the pages of the novel, reading every little word he could along the way. But on the page of the front, nothing but blank paper.

He laughed and smiled, and flipped through the pages again.

“What’s so funny?”

“Nothing. Just reminds me of something.”

-----

“I was only fifteen when I met David.”

He stood behind her, his right arm gently wrapped around her neck and his left hand grazed her long, curly red hair. He stood above her, listening to her voice speak what she wanted to get out and feeling her heart beating slowly and calmly in her chest. He thought he could feel her tears falling from her eyes, but he could some how tell that she wasn’t crying now.

“I met him here, on this very rooftop, in the late 80’s. He was just sitting up here with his friends, talking and tossing a football around. Or a basketball, I can’t really remember. He lived on the third floor during this time, and my best friend, Robin, lived on the fourth. We came up here with her little sister because we were bored.

“And the first time I ever met David would be because I was bored.”

He smiled to himself, thinking that was probably the reaction she would have wanted out of him. His hand kept traveling up and down the strands of her curls, and his eyes kept staring down at the same spot he figured her eyes were locked on. And all this time, the snow kept falling.

“So this is where I met him for the first time. And I thought he was the funniest guy I have ever met, even though his friends thought he was being a ‘moron’. Robin wasn’t impressed either, but that didn’t bother me. Not at all.

“David was all that mattered, and I knew right then and there what the future held for me.”

He knew the rest of the story before she said it.

“We were a couple all through high school and through college. All my friends had relationship after relationship, saying that they meant something each and every time and then forgetting about them when the next came along. Even Robin – she left with her family after this guy that meant nothing to her got her pregnant. But David and I…”

“There was never a problem between that we couldn’t work out. We didn’t see any reason to leave each other, so we never did.

“That’s why we got married and left college after a few years.”

He nodded, and smirked a grin of pity. All of this pain building up inside of her due to the love she held deeply in her heart. It was something he had seen some many times before, and something he knew he would see again. The only question was what form the love would take that time around.

“We each thought that we had enough knowledge to make it on our own. I went into designing dresses, and he went on to become a reporter. We moved into the third floor after his parents moved out, and steadily began to work our way up in the chain of our professions. The idea was to be out of her after our first year or so.

“It’s now been nine years. And we’re still here.”

His right hand lifted off her shoulder, and he rubbed her hair once again. He leaned his head against hers, and then he could her that she was crying again. The tears ran down faster now then they had before.

“You love him, right?”

“Of course I love him. Of course I do.”

“Then that’s all that matters.”

She turned around, trails of water ruining the make up on her face, and smiled. He wiped away the tears once again, and she suddenly hugged him unexpectedly. He just held her head close to his chest and calmed her. It was then that he truly realized what he should have known in the first place.

He knew now that she wasn’t going to jump. The man standing behind him was quite right, she was just depressed. She decided, all by herself that sitting on the edge of the building where her story began was just. A place where she could sit and cope over the agony that filled her soul.

Relief comes in all forms, he figured as her stood there, comforting a young woman named Nicole, with the snow falling on their tired bodies.
