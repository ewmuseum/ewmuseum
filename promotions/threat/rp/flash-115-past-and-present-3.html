Past and Present [3]
Starring: The Cursed One

“So, it has been a rather ‘interesting month to say that least’, quote on quote,” said the man who had come to the rooftop and decided out of the blue to stand next to him. “And this is all because of this tournament thing or whatever. I still don’t see much of a reason to come all this way just to sit on my blasted roof.”

The irritation buried in this man’s voice spoke plenty to him as he sat there, swaying gently with the summer breeze. He couldn’t really give any solution that would solve the problem this man had with him being there. Truth was that he himself didn’t know why he was there. In a weird way, the past lead him there, and he couldn’t piece together the when and the why.

As he sat on the edge, he couldn’t help but feel the irony of the situation he was in. The human mind was always quite a mystery. Always ticking inside the empty box known as the skull. Always speaking words of wisdom, jealousy, fear and compassion. The simple thought of irony came to him as he sat on the edge of that building. The irony that it was the thoughts in your own head that tried to understand that actions of that same mind.

Sort of like the irony that it took the present to understand that past.

“Well, the tournament was sort of an eye opener, you could say. Twizzy came along and represented what I thought would be a real challenge. I had no idea what he was like in the ring, so I could only imagine what he was capable of. You should never go into a match like that, but I did anyway, despite my hidden apprehension. Twizzy gave one hell of fight. One that caught me off guard. It was amazing that I got out of it unharmed, I’ll tell ya. But, well, that was a few weeks ago.

“I was trying to figure out what I thought this tournament meant to me, and what would happen if I won it. At the time, I didn’t give a shit. Not one bit. At the time, it meant absolutely nothing to me. Now I know. Now I realized that, deep inside, it meant a lot to me. I wanted to win it then, just like I wish I could win it now. But I think I screwed it all up right then and there.

“Now I can’t go back and fix my mistake.

“I guess you can say that I’m here because I’m trying to understand what led me here. Does that make sense?”

“I suppose,” he responded, sucking up a glob of spit and shooting the repulsive liquid off the side and into the empty street. “Has it done anything for you so far?”

“Nothing. Nothing at all.”

-----

His feet a bit sore after a day of walking, he pushed himself up the stairs and towards the rooftop door, of which he could literally picture in his mind waiting for him to come and open. The hallway, dark and dank, seemed unwelcoming. He could feel hostile eyes staring at him from doors creaked open slightly, blade thin. Part of him wanted to run back, but his mind and his thoughts of the unknown got the best of him. And his understanding of this particular state of affairs, he knew, would be one of the only ones that could do anything of importance.

As he climbed those steps, he couldn’t help but feel the irony of the situation he was in. He knew, deep down, that this person wasn’t thinking about doing anything rash, like the crowd below thought. He or she was only trying to contemplate their life and what it all meant – the same thing he had done only moments ago on the edge of a lake. It was irony that a man that had the same thoughts would be the one to make sense of the entire situation.

Sort of like the irony that it took the past to understand the present.

He reached the peak of the mountainous stairs and saw the door closed, just as he suspected it would be. His hand reached out and gripped the cold knob, turned it quickly and stepped through, where small mounds of snow fell to the surface of the rooftop.

A man stood there, his hands buried in his pockets and his glazed over face staring at the person sitting on the edge. He hadn’t yet stared over at that person just yet, but instead was too enthralled in the reason this man was just standing there, not attempted to do or say anything but just waiting for something to happen.

“What are you doing here?” he asked curiously, yet almost crossly as he stepped up behind the man.

“Watching her and making sure that she doesn’t do anything. Don’t worry. She’s just a little depressed. She’s not going to do anything.”

“Well has she done anything before this that would tell you different?” he couldn’t help but ask.

“Nothing. Nothing at all.”
