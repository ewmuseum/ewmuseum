-- Part 1 -- Army Days
Starring: Lawrence Neumeyer

The past.

The face of a familiar man. It was Lawrence Neumeyer, the man that had taken out the one known as General David Sanders at Flaw. His face was younger, and his hair military style.

Lawrence was enlisted in the army a mere week ago. Still getting his boots wet in the swing of things, he sat in his tent. He was writing in a journal of sorts, recapping the events of a day gone by. He silently closed the old book. The tent was lit up by only candlelight. It was a muggy day, and the physical training had been finished.

Lawrence kept to himself in the army. After his family moved to America freely, he felt he should give back to America, hence the joining of the army. Besides, he enjoyed the idea of fighting for what he believed.

He placed his small journal in a dark green fabric case. He blew out the candle on the box. He was sitting Indian-style writing on this soapbox until now. He got up in the darkness and opened the tent. He saw about four guys laughing about thirty feet in front of him.

They all had cigars in their mouth and were playing some poker. They were having an old-fashioned good time.

“I’ll raise you five bucks!” one of the men shouted. The minimal money they had brought with them was being used. A log in the center of all of them held a couple bucks, some change, and a nice cheap wristwatch that one of the men threw it. Eventually, he the man who bet the watch took in the pile after placing down a royal flush.

The men hollered out in enjoyment.

“You know,” Lawrence overheard one of the men say, “I’m tired of just sitting here in camp sites. I want actual action. You know, blow away some Japs or something.”

“Still dreading over World War II, Sanders?” The commander of the current squad came over to the poker game and also heard the young men talking.

“No, sir. I’m just looking forward to some militant action. After all, the physical and mental training you put us through can get us through anything.” Private Sanders –- David Sanders –- was kissing up to the commander in a joking manner.

“Thank you for the compliment, Sanders, but I take it as a good thing when the army isn’t needed. It means out country is safe from any harm for the time being. While you dread your time here, I enjoy it.”

“Enjoy it?” Sanders talked on. “How could you enjoy it? It’s always damp. The food, quite frankly, stinks. And we go through hell every day on these training courses you’ve got here.”

“That’s the beauty of it, Sanders. Someday you’ll understand.”

The men chuckled as their poker game ensued. This time, David Sanders had won and took in the pile.

“God damn it.” One of the man shouted. “That’s it, I’m out. I’m just about broke.”

“Awww, come on!” All the men growled.

“Sorry, guys. I’ve got a wife to aid financially.”

The men laughed.

“Alright, well we need a new player.” Sanders took the lead in saying. “Hey you!” He yelled over towards Lawrence. Neumeyer made a notion of “Me?”

“Yeah?” He said.

“Come on over and play some five-card draw.”

“I’m not very good.” Lawrence said.

“Even better!” Sanders laughed with his pals. “Don’t worry about it. No one here is a mastermind at this game, albeit myself.”

“Heh.” Lawrence chuckled as he sat down on a plastic chair around the men. He was dealt his five cards. He drew two more after placing them down.

“What ya got?” Sanders asked as two of the four men put their cards down.

“Three kings and a pair of fours.”

The men all looked at him.

“Must be beginner’s luck. You win, though.”

Lawrence grabbed the crumbled dollar bills and put some in his pocket as he threw all his cards in the middle.

“So,” Sanders said, as one of the other mates shuffled the cards, “Neumeyer, what’s your story?”

Sanders read his name from the tag above his left breast.

“Nothing really. Joined the army because I wanted to give back to this country.”

“The great U.S.A. I’d give my life for it.” Sanders said, as others around him agreed. “But basically I’m here to fight. I’m here to take out any threats to the United States, and I’ve vowed to do it.”

“Hey, it’s getting late.” One of the men said. “They’re walking us up at five-hundred hours.”

“Yeah, I’m retiring for the night. Since Figueroa is broke, looks like we found ourselves a new card player. What d’ya say Neumeyer?”

“Sure. How much harm could it be?”
