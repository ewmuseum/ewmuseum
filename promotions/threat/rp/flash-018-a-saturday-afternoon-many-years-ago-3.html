A Saturday Afternoon Many Years Ago (3)
Starring: Bobby Knickerson

It was 9:45 pm, and Robert was already changing into his pajamas, getting ready to retire to bed. Not by choice, but by force. Bobby had heard stories of boys & girls his age being able to stay up to catch the late-night Saturday programming, or being able to head to the mall to waste money on enjoyable arcade games. He'd also heard stories of teenagers galavanting around the neighbourhood until the wee hours of the morning, making a ruckus and drinking booze at the same time.

How Bob wished he was experiencing that right now.

Instead of grumbling at the notion of going to bed so early. And it wasn't as if he had a chance to possibly read more comics before going to sleep. Hardly. His parents had locked the bookshelf of comics. They didn't want their precious son to be reading anything in the dark, and hence, impair his eyesight.

"Life's so unfair!" Robert Knickerz moaned as he jumped into bed and pulled the covers up, turning on his right side.

It really was. What kind of child gets restricted like this, really? Probably many other kids go through a similar experience, yes, but for Bobby, it was even tougher. He just couldn't express himself the way he wished to. And he had NOBODY to confide in. Not a single soul. At school, he was almost like an outcast. Nobody liked him. Maybe it was the way Robert gelled his hair?

Or maybe, the fact that his parents didn't believe in using deodorants had something to do with that?

In any case, tears were streaming down his cheeks now. Bobby couldn't understand why he had been dished out such a life. All he wanted was a normal life, like all the other normal kids. Instead, he had overprotective parents and a not-too-smart brain to boot. With each passing day, Bobby felt like just giving up.

And let the netherworld embrace him. They'd accept him for who he was, Knickerz thought.

The door to his room creaked open again, and the sobbing immediately ceased. It was more silent and held-back now, as Bobby choked on his own tears. He couldn't risk Momma & Papa finding out he was crying. The two 'parents of the year' walked towards the bed, whispering agitatedly.

Over what? Bobby didn't know, and didn't WISH to know. But finally, the conversing stopped, and it was Papa who leaned in and kissed his cheek first.

"Hey son, have a good night's sleep."

Then, it was Momma's turn, who also stroked his hair lovingly as she placed a peck on his cheek, not knowing it was wet due to her son crying.

"We love you, Bobby. Get some good rest, and be up early tomorrow for your swimming lessons, okay?"

With that, they left the room. Continuing their secret conversation, which seemed hate-filled. But all Robert could care about was wanting to end to end his miserable life. Imagine, an eight year old child, having these kind of notions swirling about in his head and in his heart.

What else could Bobby do? He didn't dare to stand up to his parents.

But he would have to, if he wanted to change his life. HIS life. It wasn't anybody else's life. It was the life of one Robert Knickerz, and he was now vowing one very simple thing.

"I will take control of my life."
