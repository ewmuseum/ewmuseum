Why We Do What We Do: Part 1
Starring: Levond Daniels

Bad things like when I was 16. It was just a couple of days after my birthday, and I had just got off my cell phone with Shawn. Had to get him to come over for a bit so we could talk about something really important. Shawn smiled and let out a slight chuckle as he came through the wooden gate connecting my backyard to a huge set of woods. We always used those woods to get back and forth, because right on the other side was Shawn and Jessica’s backyards.

Shawn’s changed a lot since back then. He was an odd ball back then, always dressed in black. Black baggy jeans and a black t-shirt with flames on the bottom, and a no fear logo written on the center, wasn’t anything I would’ve been caught dead wearing. That and his curly dark hair. I can remember why he dyed it and spikes it up all the time now, his hair back then was the ugliest damn thing I had ever seen.

When Shawn finally got past the last set of bushes, and into my backyard, he was greeted by the sight of me doing my normal daily practice. I would run around, kicking at everything in sight. Trying to kick something higher or harder every day until I was actually good. Obviously, this game plan would never work. That’s what Shawn was chuckling about.

He got there just in time to see me completely miss the tree branch my last kick was aimed for, and I came down hard onto my ass. I stared up at the tree limb for a bit, not even really noticing that Shawn was there yet, but after talking to him, I’m sure this is how and when he showed up.

They all said I wasn’t very talented back then, but the energy I had would make me great someday. They said that energy was so obvious they were jealous. Me? I wasn’t trying to be great. I wasn’t trying to make anyone jealous. I damn sure wasn’t trying to impress them, or get respect from them. Definitely not about bullying around a bunch of kids to get what I want like most fighters either.

For me it was about the memories. Before dad left, we would sit together and watch one of the endless supply of Kung Fu movies he had collected. Those are the only good moments I really recall having with dad. I guess it’s only natural that I kick now, to try and bring back those memories.

Shawn said he knew I had a lot of potential to be great, but doubted I had it as a fighter. He told me if I trained with him once I was 18, I could be a really great wrestler. I could become a legend someday. He told me that all the time, but the thing is, I don’t want to be a wrestler. I don’t even want to become famous, or a legend. I just want to be able to kick things, have fun, and remember my dad.

I slowly pushed myself back up to my feet. Today would be the day Shawn and I would have our first serious conversation. I dreaded telling him what was going on from the second I realized I would have to do so. It’s a really scary thought, never knowing what your friends will think of your decisions in life, and never knowing if they’ll care enough to still be your friend afterwards.

My family never landed on a huge pile of money like his and Jessica’s did. After dad left mom with me, my brother, and sister, we struggled for a long time to get by. We would all get jobs whenever we could, but nothing was enough to save us from the debt that was taking over our lives. Even with dad paying child support, it wasn’t enough.

I was truly scared of Shawn’s actions today, but it was too late. I had already agreed, and already gotten the loan.

No. Not Money.

Marijuana. I was so determined to help my mom back then, that I sunk to becoming a drug dealer.

I turned around to see Shawn standing behind me, still chuckling at the missed kick.

“How long have you been standing there man?” I already knew the answer slightly, as I heard the bushes moving during my practice, but I wanted to make sure. I slowly made my way towards Shawn so we wouldn’t have to yell, dusting off the ass of my pants and back of my shirt as I walked.

"Oh. Just long to see you bust your ass, as usual. I always seem to come over here just when things are starting to get funny." Shawn smiled and ran a hand back through his hair. Throughout the years he had always done that when he didn't know what to say or do.

“Yea. Got a knack for making it in time to pick on everyone else. Someday we’ll get you for that. Anyways man, I asked you to come over because I need to talk to you about something.” I let out a slight sigh after pausing for a moment and lowering my head. “Kind of important, and I just don’t want to keep anything from the family, you know?”

Shawn quickly responded back, reassuring me with a simple “Yea, I know.” He had always had a knack for reassuring people like that. With just a few quick words he could make you believe he understands everything you’re saying, even when half the time he was barely holding onto the attention span you had caught.

“I’ve decided to help my family fight out of debt Shawn. I just wish I had more than one option about this.” I let out a heavier sigh this time and lifted my head back up to look at Shawn’s face. “A friend got in touch with me today Shawn, and he dropped off enough drugs to get me a couple hundred after paying him back. I’m…”

He quickly cut me off. That was another time he could do better than anyone. When he was mad at something someone had said, he would never let them finish what they were saying. Looking into his eyes with then, it felt as if they were piercing through mine. “You’re what Levond? Taking the easy way out? Doing things the dumbest possible way? What if you get killed man?”

“Yea Shawn. I’m going to be selling soon.” I paused for another moment. I was trying to figure out a better way to explain why I decided to do what I told him I would be doing, but nothing would come to mind, except for the truth, which I knew wouldn’t be enough. “I just have to make sure my family doesn’t wind up homeless, or even worse man. You know you could do the same if it was your only ch…”

He again cut me off halfway through my statement. “There as always other choices Levond. Have fun, just don’t think I’m going to support you with this, help you with this, or even talk to you when you’re doing this. Fuck, that’s if I talk to you at all anymore after today. You’re not taking me down with you for this shit Levond. You not taking the family down with you either.” He slowly turned, an obviously disgusted look on his face as he began to push back through the bushes, towards the wooden fence.

I tried frantically to stop him, and explain in a way so he would understand, but he just continued walking. I couldn’t come up with anything that would mean anything to him. He would never have any of that. He’d never understand it. See, Shawn grew up in a rich family, how could he possibly begin to understand the problems a normal family has.

A few seconds later, Shawn was gone. Disappeared into the woods.

Ever since that day, I’ve been selling. Once I got into the action, I couldn’t get back out. The drug itself was addicting enough for me, but the thrill of working my way up to becoming a big time seller? That was what truly got me hooked.

Sure, over the years I’ve made a lot of money off of my adventures with marijuana. I’ve got cops in three states on my payroll, and I’ve got total immunity in 5 other states. Great things to have in this business when you’re like me, and you go against everything the law says. Was it worth it though? Worth it to lose a friend over such a stupid way to make money?

…A friend?
