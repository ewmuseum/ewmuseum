Part I; Phase I - Protocol; The Road To Nazareth - II - In Which The Grinding Stone Turns
Starring: Rejection

She sat him down on the couch. Truth be told, he hated to sit on the thing. At the angle you'd normally sit at on the couch, it felt uncomfortable, and certain parts where the stitching came together were replaced by pleat. They weren't soft, like most commonly found, but were hard, and jut into your back. It was only good for lying down really, which he already was before she interrupted him.

He knew what she was going to say. So he didn't mind at least giving her the respect to listen to what she had to say.

But he did know what she was going to say...

...After she rambled off her "list", she gave him the best good news he could've gotten. Throughout the day, she had been removing his things. Most of it was already packed, as he didn't have a ton of personal items that needed countless boxes. He did, however, appreciate that she was doing all this already. And yet lingering in his head, was the fact that she may have done it just so that she could get out of feeling guilty about it. He didn't know why though.

This was her apartment.

That was the second bit of information she gave him. She had found him an apartment. This angered him just a little bit, as she decided herself where he wanted to live, in what kind of apartment he wanted. Yet, she knew none of those pre-requisites.

So that pissed him off. But he couldn't let her know. She had made the down payment on it, and packed most of his things. So not only was she sure of her decision, but she was also making sure he couldn't get angry with her.

She was doing too much for him to be angry with her. He knew she was evil, but he didn't realize how evil until that day.

He would need to move out in five days, Saturday. That, he didn't like too much. He'd almost need to take the red-eye to Vegas. But he decided that he'd just leave tomorrow instead. It'd be easier, and more comfortable for everyone. They talked for another hour or so, mostly about the planning he had to make on how to get his stuff there. He had no vehicle, so he needed to arrange everything around her schedule. That meant that he'd have to move out the next morning, getting all his things stored by afternoon. He hoped.

And then they hugged. She did at least. He sort of let his arms hang there. She understood why. So she didn't get mad at him, and she didn't hate him for all the problems they had had. No one was at fault she had said. They were just at the end of the road.

He'd never let anyone know, but he hated it when the pavement stopped.
