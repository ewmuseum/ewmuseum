I Get By With A Little Help From My Friend (Wednesday) [2]
Starring: Chris "Neverwhere" McCluskey

Chris “Neverwhere” McCluskey sat alone in his hotel room, after a night of juicy house show action. The curtains were drawn, the TV was off, and an empty food tray was at his side. It seemed that poor old Chris just didn’t have that many friends. Whenever he tried to eat in a restaurant; unless he was incognito, he often got slapped around the face by waiters (who were obvious wrestling fans). The bartender wouldn’t sell him drinks, and the maid wouldn’t change his bed sheets. What a lousy country America really was. Now Chris knew what the Dixie Chicks felt like.

Chris was preparing to get into bed whenever he suddenly started giggling like a school girl and grasped his groin area. The reason for Chris’ glee was soon obvious – his mobile phone was ringing. Well, it was vibrating. Nokia 3330s don’t exactly have the nicest of ring tones, and despite hours or attempting to make “Don’t You Forget About Me” using the built-in composer, he had given up and decided to use the vibrating function. And now it was really paying off! His first call!

“Hello?”

“Chris? Is that you? Guess who!”

“Mum?”

“No! Guess again!”

Chris paused for a moment. Nobody ever called him. The last person to call him was Colin Gear, three months ago in IWA, in a different roleplay. He wasn’t too good at the guessing games, so he gave in very easily.

“I dunno, who is it?”

“I’ll give you a hint: It’s Joe!”

“JOE!”

Suddenly all the memories came flooding back. The first time Chris had met Joe in IWC all those years ago. The C-Team. The Barney lunchbox. LOSER. And of course, the farcical cWo.

“Chris, I don’t have much time. Can you come down here to the police station in Ohio?”

“OHIO! But I’m not even in Cleveland!”

“Get a plane down, and I’ll owe you the money!”

“But you already owe me money Joe. I’m never going to see it again, am I? Okay, what station are you in?”

The voice at the other end went quiet, and soon Joe returned with all the details for Chris. He told Joe he’d be there as quick as he could, and Joe told him that he wouldn’t move an inch until Chris arrived.

After a quick fumble in the dark (to get dressed), Chris was at the airport, via taxi cab, and was boarding a flight to Ohio. It would take three hours to fly from whatever state Chris was in to Cleveland, so Chris had a very long, tense, uneasy and unbearable wait ahead of him.

Three Hours Later…

After finding the police station, Chris enquired at the desk about just what was going on. Joe hadn’t told him over the just why he was currently incarcerated. It turned out Joe had tried stealing the security camera inside a small convenience store, and had electrocuted himself in the process. Despite how seriously unlikely it is, he was fined five-hundred dollars. Not able to pay the fine, he was put in jail until he could. Which is even further unlikely, but fortunately, it fits in nicely with this story.

Chris paid the fine for his dear old buddy, and their first course of action was to go to a bar to catch up on old times. Sitting at the back at 9 a.m. in the morning, they quietly discussed their history. How Chris had let Joe down time and time again. But good old Joe was the forgiving type, and seemingly always forgave his pal. But then Joe got down to serious business.

“So, what’s with this ‘Neverwhere’ stuff? What happened to ‘The Cluck’?”

A very good point indeed, Joseph.

“Jeepers I don’t know Joe. It seemed like a good idea at the time. But I’m sure that’s what Hugh Grant, Bill Clinton and Eric Cantona all said. I thought ‘The Cluck’ was a bit of a goofy name, but ‘Neverwhere’ is absolutely meaningless.”

“Then can I still call you Cluck?”

“Of course you can Joe. But only if I can call you Joe.”

What a touching moment.

“So, how is that International title tournament whatsit going?”

“Well, I’m somehow in the final, which is this Saturday night? Wanna come? I’ve got front row tickets!”

“Do you? Do you really?!”

“No.”

After several more rounds of drinks, Chris decided it was time to go. He did after all have a championship match to prepare for. He contemplated giving Joe some money to get him back onto his feet, but he knew what Joe really wanted. And as Chris finished his last beer and stood up from the table, Joe gazed up at him with puppy-dog eyes as an old Neil Diamond classic, “Hello Again”, played on the radio…

Hello, again, hello
Just called to say hello
I couldn’t sleep at all tonight
I know it’s late
But I couldn’t wait
Hello my friend, hello
Just called to let you know
I think about you every night
But I’m here alone
And you’re there at home
Hello

As the tears started to roll down Chris’ face, he held out his hand and Joe knew what the gesture meant. They left the bar together. However, they didn’t return to a hotel room and make sweet sweet love, or elope. They rented a tour bus, at Joe’s request, and the rest is history.

History that will be told in the up-coming days, preferably before Saturday evening Eastern Standard Time.
