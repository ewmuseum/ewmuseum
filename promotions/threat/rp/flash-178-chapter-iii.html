Chapter III
Starring: Ø

The plush red carpeting in the Belfonte mansion reminded Wolfgang Churchill of the cinemas he attended as a child. Santino and Wolfgang strolled down the corridor menacingly, just as they had a few years earlier. They passed portraits, paintings, and prized family documents before finally arriving at the two large doors that lead to Salvatore's office. Two goons, stationed on opposite sides of the doors, observed the two men wishing to enter. Familiar with their identities, they gave them inaudible permission to enter by way of a head nod. Santino opened the door for Wolfgang and they found themselves in the heart of the office a short time later.

Both pairs of eyes scanned the room with complete awe and admiration. The room was bright, thanks to a gigantic glass chandelier that was stationed above Sal's desk. The room was also richly furnished and Wolfgang immediately collapsed on a soft, leather loveseat. They waited a few moments, but Sal wasn't one to keep his sons waiting. They were in the room for no longer than 30 seconds before he emerged.

An ethereal glow seemed to form around his being, and Churchill immediately stood up and approached him. He extended a hand with every hope that the great man would shake it. Instead, he was surprised to see Sal reach out and give him an affectionate, fatherly hug. The embrace brought out the incredible size difference between the two men. Wolfgang was about 6 inches taller and a hundred pounds heavier than Salvatore Belfonte. However, Churchill could not deny that Sal was still the bigger man; a bias established by the fact that Belfonte was his personal mentor, associate, and friend.

"My, this kid knows how to clean up!" Sal said with an aged chuckle to his son, "I wish I could say the same for you."

Santino's head lowered in shame, even though he knew his father was only kidding.

"Have a seat, my sons." He said proudly to Wolfgang and Santino. Both pulled out chairs at Sal's desk and took seats across the table. Before taking a seat himself, Sal wanted to assure his guest's comfort.

"What can I get you, Wolfgang? Cuban? Scotch? Blowjob?"

Santino laughed maniacally, but Wolfgang could only manage an amused smirk.

"No thanks, Mr. Belfonte."

"Call me Sal! After all, you are a part of the family now."

Churchill's eyes narrowed and moved towards Santino. "Your son tells me this, but I'm having a little trouble understanding what that means exactly. So, what does it mean, Sal?" His words were skeptically spoken, although he didn't mean to be so shrewd. Sal threw a stern glare in Wolfgang's direction, but he quickly lightened up.

"I'm not one to pull wool over your eyes," Sal remarked, "It means exactly how it sounds. When my boy found you, you were out of work, out of clothes, out of a place to stay. Out of society, you were. They turned their backs on you, but we won't. That's what I mean. We will give you what society wouldn't: A second chance. Joining the Belfonte unit is a step in the right direction."

"Wow. You never told me you knew so much about society," Wolfgang hissed with feigned admiration.

"I don't claim to. But this I do know: Society doesn't know what's what, so they grip their people with fear. Fear of difference. Fear of nature. Fear of weapons. They want people to fear flying, flowers, fish ... anything. They even instill a fear of the mob, which they claim to have under control. " Sal stopped to chuckle at his last example. His face then sobered and he continued with all the seriousness he could muster.

"Let them think that. It only makes families like ours stronger. Strength is the reason I have brought you here tonight. Giving you clothes, a place to stay, and some walking around money is not generosity. I see it as a father's responsibility to his son and I don't find it to be as important at this point. No, I think strength is the most pressing issue right now. Let us not kid ourselves, you are not a weak man. Our old strongman and guard, Fredo Brasi, was taken from us a few months ago. Do you remember Fredo, Wolfgang?"

"I remember kicking his ass before, yes." Wolfgang said plainly. Salvatore snickered and continued.

"For one reason or another, his death brought a blow to the Belfonte Family that we never could have predicted. For some odd reason, other families view us as weak. That is why I wanted to speak with you months ago. We courted out possible replacements, but none of them could measure up to you. We've requested your help many times before and you've always graciously supplied it, no matter what other ventures you were tending to at the time. We've yet to be disappointed with the outcomes of your work, or the way you conduct yourself in action."

Sal made the sign of the cross before lowering his head and whispering to Wolfgang, "I hate to desecrate the deceased, but you were even better than Brasi... the man that aroused such fear among the families."

Wolfgang saw Sal's request coming ever since the conversation began and the words he spoke then were initially received with confusion.

"I know what you want, Sal." Wolfgang said, his head falling before darting back up to a rigid position "And I accept. I don't consider this a job or responsibility, but one of the finest honors that anyone could have bestowed on me."

Sal sat back, entangled in deep thought. His fingers crossed, he contemplated what Churchill had just said. He soon realized that they were both on the same page when it came to business and family. Sal was successful, as he had always been, in alluring Wolfgang to the New York underworld.

The two parties stood up and shook hands firmly. Santino rushed to a mini-bar located at the side of the office and immediately started pouring drinks. It was a time for celebration.

The Belfonte Family had acquired itself not only a new strongman but a lifetime member.
