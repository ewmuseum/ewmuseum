Chapter II
Starring: Ø

"Story of a Strongman"
Page Two (Of Five)

The streets aren't one of the most coziest places to live, but Wolfgang Churchill learned quickly to make them his home. After leaving aWc, he found himself without a job; not to mention money, which he had inappropriately squandered. He tried to attract federations to take him under their employ, but he couldn't come to an agreement with any of them. His stubborn personality in the ring was admired by many, but his inability to cooperate in the front offices proved to be a stigma he couldn't break.

A superstar wrestler was homeless as a result. He was sure he could have found a job doing something different when he first took to the streets, but as his physical appearance depreciated with dirt and scraggly facial hair, he found out that no one would hire him. It didn't really bother him because his calling was to be a wrestler, and he would wait as long as it took for the day he could answer that calling.

He found a home in an alleyway near the intersection which split Parker and Flarsheim Streets. His makeshift nest, constructed of cardboard and rags, was located near the back door of a ritzy European restaurant. The owner of the restaurant, a man by the name of Amerigo Saviano, allowed him to stay on his property because he recognized Wolfgang from his days as a Belfonte henchman. The last thing Amerigo needed was trouble with the Family.

The afternoon sky was dark and was filled with even darker clouds. The world around Wolfgang was waiting to rain, so he made preparations in that little alleyway nook he called home. He masterfully constructed a cardboard overhang so that he could remain dry in the upcoming storm, and laid out a grease-stained tablecloth to serve as a mat to pad the hard cement.

As he was putting together his protection, his ears caught a disgruntled customer storming out of the European restaurant. The man was expressing his utmost displeasure at the service and portion size. Amerigo Saviano tried to soothe the customer, with apologies and a request that he be granted a second chance to satisfy the angry eater. Rational people would hop on the offer of a free meal at one of New York's most expensive restaurants, but the wealthy customer seemed more interested in letting off his kettle of emotional steam than to compromise.

Wolfgang got up from his crouched position and investigated what was going on. He walked out onto the sidewalk and noticed a familiar face barking at Amerigo. The countenance belonged to Santino Belfonte, one of Churchill's old business acquaintances. Santino took a respite from his verbal onslaught and glanced up to lock eyes with a very grizzly looking Wolfgang Churchill. Wolfgang darted towards the safety of shadows, but Santino recognized Churchill and trailed him soon thereafter.

"Wait!" Santino shouted, and Wolfgang complied. Out of either embarassment or irritation, Churchill took his time turning around. His hair was long and tangled, and the long beard he wore on his chin was found in a similar condition. His appearance had changed drastically, but Santino still recognized one of his unforgettable aspects: his gigantic eyes. The two, piercing orbs that Wolfgang posessed aroused both fear and loathing in everything they crossed.

"Wolfgang, is that you?" Santino enquired as he inched closer to get a better look. His closer inspection only lead him to believe that he was correct. Churchill confirmed it with a timid head nod.

"Wow," Santino said with heavy sigh, "You look like hell."

"I'll take that as a compliment," Churchill returned wryly. Santino was unable to translate that Wolfgang was being serious in spite of his dry delivery. Belfonte was not properly acquainted with the stenches of the streets and his nostrils began to flare with every menacing scent he had the displeasure of experiencing. He quickly covered his mouth and nose with a hankerchief as he began a conversation that would change Wolfgang's life.

"You live out here?" Santino asked nasally, his eyes transfixed on Churchill's fortress of cardboard and cloth. Once again, Wolfgang gave him a regretful nod. Belfonte shook his head in disbelief.

"You never thought to call the Family? You know we'd be happy to help you."

"I thought about it," Wolfgang replied, "but I thought better. You're family owes me and I'm about to waste my favor on something like this," he explained as he extended a displaying palm to his home, "This... this I can deal with."

"I know you're shitting me, but there's no need, my friend. You're already my favorite turd!" Santino joked with a jovial slap on Churchill. "But, let's be serious now. Anybody who wants to live in the gutter is crazy."

"I blame it on being exposed to the sun too long." Wolfgang said with a sneer.

"Speaking of sons," Santino said quickly to try and switch subjects, "Salvatore has been talking pretty highly of you lately. He tried to get in touch with you, but now I can see why the man was unable to do so. You know I'm not going to leave without you now that I've found you, right?"

Churchill was expressionless, but his body language seemed disinterested for the most part. Santino acted as if he'd ignored it and headed towards a black sedan that was parked awkwardly on top of the sidewalk.

"Come. We'll clean you up and put some real food in that stomach."

"I don't--"

"It's my obligation," Santino said gravely, "To make sure a friend of the Family is well off. You're acting as if our relationship will end because the Belfonte's absolved our debts to you. You need to wake up. You're a part of the family now. None of us can change that. I'm not going to watch a brother of mine live on the streets. It's bad for my reputation...and my conscience."

Santino said this with such stern sentimentality that he was moments away from crying. Churchill appeared intensely affected, as well, by Belfonte's flattering words. Santino wrapped his arm around Wolfgang Churchill's meaty shoulders and tightened his grip.

"The Family isn't a fucking genie that forgets you when you spend your wishes. If we have our way, life in the Belfonte family will be all you've ever wished for. Now, get in the car. We'll head over to the estate later after we get the bare essentials taken care of. Where will we start? There's your hair, a shave, clothes, food...Ah, screw it. I'll let you make the list on the way."

Wolfgang did something then that was painful, not to mention rare on his part: he smiled. He followed Santino to the car and plunged into the leather back seat. His door thundered shut and the car rolled away just moments later.
