-- Part 2 -- Army Days
Starring: Lawrence Neumeyer

Lawrence was lying on a cot in his tent. He was looking up just thinking, his with hands folded on his waste. He found himself doing this a lot. He was quiet during the nights. He was startled by the sound of the Commander yelling outside.

“MEN! GET OUT HERE ON THE DOUBLE!” He demanded at the top of his lungs.

Quickly, Lawrence stumbled out of his tent and lined up in military stance outside in front of him. A couple stragglers ended up finishing the line, as the Commander walked back and forth with a serious look on his face.

“Gentlemen, somewhere in the center of North Korea, there is a United States Embassy. At that Embassy,” he said, while walking up and down the line, “several riots have taken place. No one knows how they have started, or why. The only thing we know, is if someone doesn’t get down their soon, that place is going to be taken over. And that someone, is us. Out of the sixteen of you, eight will be selected. Eight will be selected from other squadrons as well. This is a mission where no killing is needed.

“Going in there and intimidating the rioters should make them pull back. If not, you will use force. The eight of you will be equipped with rubber bullets in your firearms. They are only to be used in extreme circumstances. Once you are finished with this mission, you will fly back to either here, or your homes. In doing this mission, you will have that choice. For those who fly home, I thank you for your time and effort at this campsite. We will be leaving in 700 hours, which will be 4:00AM.

“To those who want to go, be suited up and packed for tomorrow morning. I am not joining you in this mission, I will simply be the one that selects the men that want to go. If not enough want to go, I will hand-select them. So get back to you tents and be prepared. Dismissed.”

The soldiers went back to their tents, all taking in the breaking news. This would be their first combat mission. Some of the men crowded around others’ tents, talking about this. Around David Sanders’ tent were a couple guys all talking.

“I don’t know...” Sanders said. “It’s like god damn suicide. They’re not going to give a shit about us when we go there, and if they try to kill us, we’ve got rubber bullets?”

“Yeah,” one of his friends agreed, “that’s true. I’ve heard about what kind of stuff can happen when these people riot. It’s like their entire country against twenty soldiers. Plus, what if somehow they have firearms? Then we’re royally screwed.”

The talk continued on contemplating thoughts. No one could decide what they wanted to do.

Back in his tent, Lawrence Neumeyer sat on his cot. He wasn’t thinking about if he wanted to go on the mission or not. He knew that he would be the last man asked to go on the mission, judging by the order that the tents were set up. If he needed to go, he’d go. He wasn’t worried about the situation at hand. He could overhear the hollering outside his tent. Everyone wasn’t indecisive about the situation. But whatever they had to worry about, it was tomorrow morning.

Lawrence needed sleep. Because if he did go on the mission, it was sure to be a long-ass flight.

The sweet sound of the Commander woke up the men just like he said, at 4:00AM.

“ATTENTION!!!” He screamed, getting every man out of bed and ready in front of their tents in under a minute. In the slightly cold, misty morning, the men stood in the clothes they slept in. A white tee shirt for most, with a pair of shorts.

“Gentlemen, I will start down on the left side, and go through all of you. You know what my question will be. And answer with a simple ‘yes’ or ‘no.’”

The commander walked down to the very left, on the opposite side of Neumeyer, as he had expected.

“Kershner.” Said the Commander. “Do you wish to accompany us on this mission?”

“No, Sir.”

“Alright.”

He continued down the line.

“Bradshaw. Do you wish to accompany us on this mission?”

“...No, sir...” answered Bradshaw hesitantly.

He continued down.

“Anderson. Do you wish to accompany us on this mission?”

“Sir, yes, sir.”

“Suit up, son.”

The Commander continued down the line. A lot of the men answered with no, but others said yes. Now, he was right before David Sanders’ tent.

“Gallagher. Do you wish to accompany the United States in this mission?”

“Yes, Sir!”

“Thank you. Get ready, private.”

He continued down.

“Sanders. Do you wish to accompany the United States in this mission?”

Sanders stood there, thinking of what he should say. He wanted combat, and here it was. But this could be like suicide, if the events unfolded a certain way.

“Sanders! Do you wish to accompany us in this mission?!”

He choked up. “N – No, sir.”

Next to Sanders was Lawrence Neumeyer, surprised at his answer. He thought for sure he’d go.

“Alright. We’ve got seven. Mr. Neumeyer... Do YOU wish to accompany us on this mission?”

Neumeyer looked over at Sanders.

“You’re damn right I do.”

Neumeyer answered firmly. He would not have had to go if Sanders answered ‘Yes.’ But it was too late. In the back of his mind, Lawrence was worried that he might get injured or something bad would happen. But so was every other man, he thought. He would just try to fulfill his mission without any injuries to show it. After all, this was...

The United States Army.
