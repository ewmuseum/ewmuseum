Of One Such Time - Fourth
Starring: He who once saw

He swung the door open quickly, leaving Paul no time to say anything or warn him to get the fuck out of the car, and sat down in the passenger seat. He then looked straight into his bloodshot eyes, which filled with a mixture of rage and fear.

“What the fuck do you want, asshole?!” he yelled as this stranger wearing a trench coat sat down in his beautiful car. Paul immediately put the money into his pocket, and turned to give this guy some more heat.

But he didn’t answer, nor did he budge in the slightest. He only stared down, with no emotion or response that would give any hint. The thoughts of what this man was here for ran a plenty inside of Paul’s mind, yet none of them made him feel any calmer. Or any safer.

“Mother fucker, you better get the fuck out of my car before I…”

“What’s your name?” he suddenly asked, breaking his silence. He then gave the most serious stare that Paul had ever seen on a man. Never before, and ironically never again, would Paul see that look in the eyes of another human being.

“What the hell do you want?”

“What’s your name?” he repeated, same somber stare. Same glaring eyes.

“It’s Paul, all right. Paul. Now what is it that you…”

“What do you do for a living?”

“What? Who the hell are you anyway?”

“What do you, Paul, do for a living?”

Paul could see patience wearing thin in this man. It was then and only then that fear completely took over. He was now afraid of this stranger, willing to understand what he needed and just what it was he wanted.

“I… I work for a… a firm.”

“Law firm?”

“Yeah it is.”

“Now let me ask you. Is what you just did considered lawful?”

“What did I… Wait, with that kid?”

He only stared, anger building behind his eyes and into his hand.

“All I did was give that kid what he wanted. He wanted some of that shit, and I gave him what he fucking asked me to provide. What’s wrong with…?”

Immediately, reaching across from his seat, he grabbed Paul by the neck, his left hand chocking the breath out of him. His eyes now had the stare of a deer caught in headlights or a sheep caught in a fence, realizing that his death was mere seconds away. No one walked passed the car, since it was so early in the morning and since Paul had made so sure that no one saw his little transaction. No one would see this. No one would care.

“What’s wrong with that?” he said in the calmest of voices. “What’s wrong with that? You make me fucking sick. You have no idea what a fly you are, living off the shit of others in order to survive. Why the fuck would you ever exist if it weren’t for them? What purpose do you serve besides the shit you dish out?

“What the fuck is wrong with you?”

With all of Paul’s breath almost depleted, and his life slowly slipping away, he let go of his grip, and instantly brought a piece of glass from the bottle across his neck. The taste of death filled his mouth, yet he resisted the urge to

“Listen and listen good.

“If I ever see you dealing that shit again – and I will be watching you – I will fucking kill you. If you ever sin again, I will take your life with no hesitation. I will end you, and send you to Hell.

“Do you fucking understand me?”

“Yes.” Paul said, struggling over his coughing and his inability to breathe.

With his left hand holding the piece of glass to Paul throat, blood slowly leaking from a single hole the size of a pore, he reached into his trench coat and pulled out the camera held safely inside. Paul, confused and frightened, stared into the camera as the flash went off and the buzzing sound of his eternal image came out. The man pocketed the picture, put the camera back, and finally took the piece of glass off his neck.

“Take that money out of your pocket and get the fuck out of the car. You don’t disserve any of it.”

Paul pushed open the driver’s side door and ran into the street, away from his beautiful Mercedes and from the money he had stolen only seconds ago from an innocent, naïve young man. All he did after Paul was too far to see was step out of the car, lit the rag that he carefully placed in the gas tank with one of his matches, and walked away from the car.

He was only a few feet away when the car blew into a million pieces, sending shards of metal into the streets and green bills flaming into the evening air.

And, with his hands in his pockets, touching the shards of glass and the picture, he never looked back.
