History [2]
Starring: Chris 'Neverwhere' McCluskey

For a long time I chose to play the nice guy. The role model. I wanted to be the kind of wrestler that the kids watching in the front row with their families, kids like I once was, would look up to and to be there hero. For a while I was. They would come up to me in the street wanting an autograph, a picture with me or even just the chance to shake my hand and exchange some pleasantries. But somewhere along the line something changed, and it wasn’t me.

Nobody approached me in the street. Instead, adolescent boys shouted obscenities at me from across the street. They threw things at me. One time they even let the air out of the tyres of my rental car. Sure, it’s not like I couldn’t refill them at the nearest gas station, but that’s not the point. Where had the young children gone who had loved watching me take on my enemies and win?

Maybe they had just got tired of seeing the good guy win. But hey, is it really a crime to prevail? Just because I was a nice guy didn’t mean I was boring or uninteresting to watch. But like everything that stands still, people passed me by. Soon I no longer got cheers. Sometimes I even got booed. Most of the time, the fans couldn’t have cared less if I was in the ring or in my bed at home. I no longer mattered to them. I still can’t figure out why.

Television had changed their views of good and evil. Of black and white. One huge shade of grey was now the standard. It didn’t matter who won ultimately, so long as somebody got hurt. The day I realised that, I looked back to the young boy I once was; who lost every day of his life. Winning did matter, being the good guy was important. At least to me.

They just cared about men pounding on each other so hard and so much that they would be in wheelchairs by the time they were in their forties. That wasn’t me. That wasn’t what I was about. Yes, this business involves men beating other men until they give up. But I never wanted to really hurt anybody. I knew I could out-wrestle anybody when I was on top of my game; I didn’t need to pummel any person into a bloody heap to pick up the win. But that’s not what the fans wanted. That didn’t make for good viewing.

That’s when I decided to get out of this industry. I wasn’t in it for the money, but at the end of the day, everybody has to make a living, and this was mine. But due to my new unpopular standpoint among the fans, the cheques each month were getting less and less. I decided to bow out at the top, or at least as high as possible, considering if things had of continued like that, I would have been in the gutter; on the bottom rung of the ladder, in no time.

I enjoyed my send-off. My real fans turned up that night and cheered me all the way home. I even had a tear in my eye as I left ring-side that night. There are tears there now, but for different reasons.

I thought I could be happy in my retirement, but no. I couldn’t sit back and watch as the legacy I had tried to hard to make was ignored as if I had never existed. It was then that I realised that my book in this profession hadn’t be completed. I had merely written the prologue.
