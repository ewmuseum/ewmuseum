A Saturday Afternoon Many Years Ago (1)
Starring: Bobby Knickerson

It was a typical Saturday afternoon. Robert Knickerz was eight years of age, and had just finished his homework. Now, the time to relax and slack beckoned. Of course, most kids his age had been enjoying their Saturdays from very early on but Bob was different. See, he wasn't academically excellent and thus, lagged behind in his studies. The parents? Disappointed but determined to help their son to get through the rough times.

As he lay on the bed in his room, Bob began to think about which comic he would buy next. Tired, he very much was. However, there was absolutely no chance of him going to sleep now. It would have resulted in his dad going bonkers if he did find out. They hated their son taking naps during the afternoon.

Instead, they rather he played with his toys or whatever, before the evening tuition.

Robert sighed and got all teary-eyed. This wasn't the life he quite expected. He expected siblings to interact with, the freedom to go out and make friends to play with. He expected to have a skateboard. A father who'd play catch with him. A mother who would dote on him endlessly.

Bob expected a lot of things from his life so far, but alas, none of them had come to materialise. And deep inside, that made him very angry. At the age he was at, however, anger was masked in depression. All bottled up inside of him, stored deep under the surface. Subject to more years of heartache, until one day, it would simply explode.

And then, the true anger would be seen. But by then, it would no longer be mere anger. It would be absolute and uncontrollable blinding rage.

Bobby rolled out of bed and walked over to his bookshelf, where a stack of comics lay invitingly. He smacked his lips and grabbed about six of them, before returning to his bed, smiling. Reading comics always made him feel better. The concept of good versus evil, and good winning; it made him feel all warmish inside. This greatly influenced his thinking, as he believed all that was good would ALWAYS overcome all that was bad.

And we know that's not always true, yes?

Of course, Bob's favourite comics were Archie comics. The dynamics of a group of friends always appealed to him. Even though he couldn't claim to understand what it meant, for one simple reason. He didn't have many friends. There was Andrew, a classmate, who usually helped him with his studies. Then there was Hendrix, another comic collector in the neighbourhood.

And that was about it.

Robert Knickerz threw the comics onto the bed and he himself jumped onto the bed, ready to engage himself in the reading of his favourite comics. This was the most enjoyable thing he was doing on the day. One thing, however. He'd read each and every one of the comics on the bed a million times before.

How sad.
