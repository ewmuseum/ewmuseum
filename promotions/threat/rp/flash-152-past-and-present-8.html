Past and Present [8]
Starring: The Cursed One

“Did you ever notice how the past is so much like the present?”

He stood in the middle of the street, silently staring down into the pages of the novel he had found in the middle of the sidewalk, as ignored as an autumn leaf dancing in the breeze of the impending winter. His eyes, locked behind glass of passed memories, never looked up towards the stranger in the bathrobe. He only opened his ears, and waited for a response, the soft voice of the wind whispering to him.

The man, standing the alcove and tightening his blue bathrobe, looked up at him and grunted a sigh of drowsiness. His eyebrow raised up, his mind obviously deep in thought yet not in the mood for any thinking. He was quite tired, and yet the night was slowly evaporating into time.

“Maybe that’s because it was all the shit the happened in our past that judges what will happen in our present, and into our future. All of that crap was once our present, and, although it’s all the way behind us, we’ll never forget it. On the contrary, we look at the lessons that past taught us and must realize what they meant before we can go any farther into our future.

“Well, something like that.”

The man closed his eyes slowly and rubbed his temple in a circular motion. He looked up from the pages of the vacant book and saw this. For a moment, he almost imagined that the man has just fallen asleep like that, standing impassively and sternly in the doorway of the apartment building with the early dawn blue shinning on his face. The wind picked up a little, tossing the man’s short hair around. He looked up at a street lamp as he reached into his jacket to pull out his camera. He figured they would be going out any minute now, with the approaching sun preparing to fulfill its day to day duty.

He pulled out the camera, aimed for the exhausted man standing in the doorway, and snapped a picture to life. The light and small zap of the camera awoke him. The man looked confused, but didn’t seem to car about the picture he had just taken.

“Why did you come back here anyway? You never really told me.”

He looked down at the fresh picture, still coming to life slowly in the palm of his hand, and looked up to see that the man in the bathrobe was quite awake now, and a bit suspicious.

“I once helped a young woman sitting on that rooftop, the same way you found me. She was looking over the edge, and no one was talking to her. So I just went up there and calmed her down.”

“Wait a second. You’re the one that helped Nicole?”

“Yeah, that was her name.”

“Holy shit.” he said, now truly awake and wide eyed, like his favorite celebrity had just walked by. “She told me about you and how you made her feel better, but I never got the chance to meet you cause I was visiting my relatives in New York for Christmas.”

The man suddenly smiled, for the first time since the two had met on the rooftop a little while ago. He shot out his hand, giving the universal gesture for a handshake and waiting for him to accept the invitation. He simply brought forth his right hand and shook it, feeling the man in the bathrobe shake vigorously.

“I wanted to thank you for helping her, but they never knew who you were or where you went.”

“It wasn’t a problem. I would have done the same for anyone.” he responded, not really sure if he was telling the truth or making conversation. Then a question came to mind. “Hey, does she still live here with David? Do they still live here?”

“No, she’s not anymore. She moved away awhile ago. David got a promotion, and he became some writer for the news on channel five. Nicole was able to get a better job with make up on the set. Didn’t take long for them to earn enough money to buy a house in Aurora. David and Nicole moved there with their two children, Alicia and Adam. Last I heard they were doing just fine.”

A smile went across his face, and his eyes looked down at the book in his left hand. He didn’t know what the novel was without the cover, but he knew that it didn’t matter. He was going to read it anyway. He was going to read it, and then carefully place it under the floorboards, to collect dust in the box with everything else.

They both stood there, silent for a moment, waiting for the other to do something, with the dawn reaching new life before their very eyes. It had already been a long night, but they both knew that there were many possibilities lying behind the events of that night. For them, if only for a moment, the future was unknown.

“That’s good to hear. I have to get going, I have plane to catch in a few hours. For Las Vegas, where my big matches will take place.”

“Big matches? Are you still in the tournament?”

“Yeah. And it’s the finals. One last step. One more match. One last man to get through. But if I know him, Chris isn’t going to go down easily.”

“Well good luck.” the man in the bathrobe said, taking his hand and shaking it one last time.
“Good luck… say.

“What’s your name?”

“Does it matter?”

The man laughed, and gave him a smirk that he wouldn’t be forgetting anytime soon.

“No. No, I suppose it doesn’t.”

The man tossed his hands into his pockets, and looked into his eyes with nothing but admiration and respect – something that he wasn’t use to seeing from strangers. He placed the picture and the book away, inside his jacket, without giving it a second glance to see what had developed or the words locked in ink on the pieces of paper. He only returned the smile as genuinely as he could, turned to the side as the street lamps above his head turned off for the day, and began walking towards the rising sun.

The man went inside, but he never turned around to see him leave.

He never liked good byes anyway.

“Did you ever notice how the past is so much like the present?”

With her snow caked hair tingling the skin of his fingers, he listened to her speak through the sobs of her melancholy crying. For a few minutes that could have been hours, he stood there next to the edge of the building, feeling her breath warming his chest as she breathed deeply. Nothing else mattered at the moment, despite what any of the others thought as they watched them standing there. It was just them. The world melted away through the tears and the emotions.

“I guess I never really thought of that, although at the same time I suppose it was always on my mind. I know that if I was ever confused on what I should do with my life that I could remember what I’ve gone through and use it. I just only wish that I could actually go back and visit my past. Just stop everything and step right back to where it all took place and see if I could make sense of it before I could move on into my future.

“Well, something like that.”

Her grip got harder, as if to thank him for being there for her. A smile crossed his frozen lips, and, from the corner of his eye, he felt a chill slice across his face. A single tear fell from his eye, and froze into the snow trapped in her red hair. She never felt it drop, but he had known that it was there. Once apart of him, and now apart of her

He gathered all his strength into his arms, and hoisted her light body over the ledge and back to the safety of the rooftop. She looked up into his eyes, solid happiness resting in her pupils behind what was once tears of despair.

“Why did you come up here to help me?”

“Because I thought that I could understand who you were. Something like this happened to me before in a place like this, and there was no one there for me. I don’t want to see anyone go through that again.”

He smiled, thoughts of love and compassion running through his mind and lifting his heart into the sky of the gray day. He knew that she felt the same, and that neither of them knew where it could go and what possibilities laid behind those emotions. For them, if only for a moment, the future was unknown.

“Nicole?” spoke a voice through the snow, in the direction of the door. Both he and Nicole turned to see a man standing there, wearing a black jacket and looking at her with joyful yet fearful eyes.

Neither of them spoke just then, but both knew who he was.

She looked at into his eyes once again, but tried to hide her feelings away. He, regrettably, did the same. She pulled him in for one last embrace, squeezing him and shaking him side to side. He simply waited for the moment to end, as he knew it would.

“Thank you.” she spoke softly, her voice just loud enough to break through the falling snow. “Thank you for everything.”

She squeezed one last time before letting him go. He looked down at her, grinning and nodding, and waiting for her to leave him standing alone on the rooftop. But she had one last thing to ask.

“What’s your name?”

“Does it matter?”

She smiled, her eyes glowing beautifully in the winter air, before turning away and looking at her husband. Without looking up at him again, she walked over to him, trudging through the collected snow and hugging her one and only. In the distance he could hear her crying with him, but this time he wasn’t there to wipe the tears away.

He felt the cold wind suddenly, and tossed his hands into his pockets for worth. He then looked at the edge, and decided to sit in the same spot she sat, since no one was left below to watch him. Right leg first, he hoisted himself on the ledge, and sat there, snow falling around him silently and his breath rising in faint mist from his mouth.

The couple went inside, but he never turned around to see them leave.

He never liked good byes anyway.
