Of One Such Time - Third
Starring: He who once saw

The wind had picked up since he stepped inside the nightclub in Baltimore. It was now harsher, ripping through the streets and tossing papers and bags in the roads and in the sky. People zippered and buttoned up their jackets. Couples huddled together. The breeze never died down and never resigned. The breeze just was.

And so was he, following the man named Paul as he stepped a few feet down with an ignorant young man by his side.

Paul walked over to a car that was parked on the curb, a red Mercedes. Nice car for a guy who looked like a complete dick, he thought to himself as he walked, step by step, behind them. His feet tapped lightly against the concrete sidewalk, cracked and dirtied from feet walking God knows how long. His eyes never left the back of Paul’s head, his long brown hair a nuisance and only drawing more revulsion from his follower.

And all this time, his right hand caressed the neck of the beer bottle in his pocket, and felt his heart beat against the camera he kept inside the breast pocket of his trench coat.

When Paul reached his beautiful car, an eyesore to all passersby and begging for someone with a key or knife to walk passed with sudden jealousy in his heart, he reached into his pocket and pulled out the keys. He walked passed Paul and the young man in time to see them both climb into the front seat and sit back. Paul turned on the radio for that little extra bit of security, as if it really mattered.

He walked a few more feet before stopping next to an alley, and leaning up against the wall. Now he could watch.

He could now wait for the right moment.

Minutes passed. The wind’s voice made a somewhat pleasing melody, and his ears embraced it as much as he would allow them to. But all the time he made sure that he watched Paul in the car, and never let him stray too far from his attention. His thoughts went beyond the plagued mind of a man whose past was shadowy. He was now a mere puppet in the scheme of some greater being.

No more.

No less.

Eyes of a mimed reality.

It was early now. Sometime around one or two in the morning. The streets weren’t empty, however. The scum that fills the night crept along, their bloodshot eyes and their aching scars daily reminders to their sins. If the world was a body and its cities the organs, they would be the cancer. They would be the cancerous cells eating away, leaving nothing but waste.

And he figured, if there would ever be a cure, why couldn’t he be that cure.

Inside the car, the young man gave Paul a handshake with one hand, and pocketed something with the other. He pulled the door open, and stepped out, his short, black hair dancing in the breeze that suddenly forced through. He stepped away, tightening his leather jacket around his upper body and leaving Paul to his car. Paul sat in the driver’s side, counting the money he had just earned for himself. Standing in the alley, despite the wind and the radio, he thought he could hear Paul laugh.

Another sin fulfilled.

It was time.
