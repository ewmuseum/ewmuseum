Part I; Phase II - Screaming; Compulsory - II - The Revelation

Part I; Phase II - Screaming; Compulsory - II - The Revelation
Starring: Rejection

He had spent the last few nights couch-surfing with her. He had nowhere to go, or rather nowhere to be. He was out of work for a while, in which time he could only work on his right side. He had started getting some feeling in his fingers, but not enough to hold something like a spoon. The rate at which his paralysis was going away seemed like it would bring him just a few days early of the six week period they had predicted.

Six weeks.

Six weeks of waiting, training with what parts of his body were up to it, and watching as everyone else kept rising. He had a shot at the International title, and it was gone as quick as it had come. Instead he had to watch the likes of some anti-social pawn compete for it. "Beyond lies innocence, not death". What a lie.

And as he lay there, listening to the hum of electrical appliances, he started thinking more about it. More about the man who called himself blind. In the descriptions he could give of him, nearly half of it would contain the same facts that could make up his profile. And in that moment of realization, one more thing fell into place that angered him. This same man that he was mentally degrading was smaller and weaker than he was. And yet, this man was healthy, and well on his way to winning a championship.

He remembered the brief abyss that was the birthing of Threat. They treated him there like they did when he first signed on with Universal Wrestling. Everyone was amazed at the new big, bad guy. Then it closed, and he signed with Threat. The new big, bad guy ended someone's career, and won a title. All in just two weeks. Then they decided to have him help represent half of the company.

Now, he was at home suffering temporary paralysis due to extreme shock and nerve damage. All this was because he was beaten. He lost his match. How could he return now? He labeled himself as unbeatable, and then when he finally was beaten, it sent him to the hospital and out of action for a month and a half. How could something so unbeatable be so feeble? He needed to win back that title. If he was Aggression Champion again, there was a chance he could get back the steam.

"The first two-time Aggression champion, winning the title after coming back from a long injury. What promise this Threat regular was beginning to show."

Pipe dreams.

"Hey, ready to eat?"

From his lying position, he tilted his head back, looking at the world upside down. He saw her standing in the kitchen. Food was on plates, the composition of machinery was now gone, and she stood smiling.

Everything looked the same.

He slid off the couch, rolling over onto his hands and knees. He placed his right arm onto the couch, and lifted up his entire body so that he was standing. He was wearing a knee brace now that the hospital had issued him. They thought it might help, at least with walking around until his leg was fully healed. He slipped his arm back in his sling.

"Looks great. Didn't we eat this last night?"

"It's the only good thing I know how to cook. Everything else is all boxed stuff and oven pizzas. This way you can eat something close to healthy."

"I don't care. It's not my diet I'm concerned about."

"I didn't want to bring this up until later, but now seems like as good a time as any."

She had a way of doing that. Everything she said was always calm and smooth. Even the most extraordinary things were downplayed with her. It helped sometimes.

The past few nights he had spent listening to her. He knew a lot about her. She didn't know that much about him. Most of what he knew were mundane things. Like how she prefers not to watch the trailers at movies, the fact that anything with an expiration date less than six months away was too high a potential to kill you, and that she had a brother. Trivial, yet interesting information. Most of what she knew about him was one thing. His history with his family. The relatives that helped him along the way, especially the ones who set everything into motion. He didn't much talk about himself, just the past. There was no one to reminisce with, so she substituted, trying to be compassionate and understanding.

If bullshitting were a class, then she got a bachelor's in it, and later would go on to teach at the university. But the one thing she didn't tell him, was anything about her current state of affairs. Her job, education, and income were nothing but mere formalities that were never brought up. Well, he had brought them up, but were never discussed. Not seriously at least. Despite her continual ideas that she was one day going to go into space with the mars crew, she was not employed by NASA. He didn't quite know if there even was an employer to speak of. So he kept his mouth shut about him.

"Remember my cop friend? The one who came over after the 'incident'."

"Vaguely."

"Well, I decided to have some fun, and had him run your name through their records. I had him build me a family tree on you. You never mentioned you had a sister."

"That's because I don't."

"No, you do. It's all in the folder. Your parents address, their new names and documents legalizing it. And a copy of the birth certificiate on your sister. All kinds of junk. Read it later, I'll go through it with you in case there's anything you don't understand."

She was the one who didn't understand. He sat there, eyeing her, having not touched any of his food. Yet there she sat, eating away. She was too casual for anyone else's safety or well-being. He knew what she was talking about when she said folder. It was sitting underneath a magazine on the coffee table. It had been there for two days.

For a while, he thought about burning it.
