Homesick
Starring: Randall Alder-Tangk

RING! RING!

No matter when you hear the phone ring, it's always exciting. There is always something about it that made you get that butterfly feeling in your stomach.

RING! RING!

It could be someone ringing you up to tell you that you've won the sweepstakes or something that'll make you richer than you could ever imagine. It might be the guy from the phone company telling you how you can 13 cents a month.

RING! RING!

Or a long lost friend. It was always interesting to find out who was at the other end. WAS being the operative word. Until in came that new caller I.D. crap. It ruined some of the surprise, you didn't get the surprise of someone's voice. Unless you didn't know the number.

RING! RING!

Randall Alder-Tangk sat up from the armchair of his hotel room, looking up at the wall. He'd thought he'd heard the phone, but wasn't sure because he wasn't really sleeping. He was still buzzing from the day's events.

He leapt up from the chair, bouncing up onto his feet and trotting across to the wall. He pulled the phone off of the wall. He went to answer.

"Yo!" he said into the phone.

RING! RING!

He'd answered the wrong phone. He heard at the other end...

"Room service. How may I help you?"

Randall freaked for a second.

"Uh... shit, sorry. Wrong phone."

He placed it back in the cradle and looked at the phone beside it.

RING! RING!

He pulled the phone off the cradle, quickly trying to make it before the person on the other end got tired of ringing and hung up on him.

"Hello?"

"Randy?" the voice at the other end said.

"Dad? Dad! How are you, man?"

"Man, that's how you greet your old man?" he chuckled back at his teenage son.

Randall just laughed in response. His old man continued on talking into the phone.

"Your mother and I went to Pedro's daughter's house today, he bought the Payback Pay-Per-View. You said you'd be on it."

Randall nodded as if his father could see what he was doing from the phone.

"Did you see me?"

"Sure did, son."

"How'd I look, dad? Did I look like a real pro?" he eagerly asked his father.

He heard a chuckle at the end of the line.

"You looked like Andre the Giant on the TV, kid-O!"

"Andre the Giant? Pull out the encyclopaedia to find the founder of wrestling, dad. That guy's older than you, if he hasn't croaked. What'd mum think?" he asked.

He stared vacantly into the wall, trying to picture what his father looked like at the other end of the line.

"Randy, you're mum and I are real proud of you. You looked fantastic. A regular..."

He heard someone call out a name in the background, it was the voice of his mother.

"...a regular Rey Mysterio Jnr!" his father said proudly.

Randall laughed out loud accidentally. It was sweet how his father tried to humour him, bringing up the name of his idol.

"Thanks dad. Did Pedro see?" he asked.

"He wouldn't have missed it for the world. We're all real proud, kid-O."

Randall just smiled, resting on the laurels of his family being proud. The support they'd given him over his short career was amazing. Here he was, a sixteen year-old young man in a promotion like thReat. Being seen on television every week. Hopefully.

"We're real proud, Randall. You should be proud of yourself."

Randall, R.A.T. as he called himself in thReat, just let it sink in. He was going to be a big star with these guys behind him. That's his dream and he was living it. Not quite yet, but he had started the journey to becoming a big star in the world of wrestling.

Maybe one day, some kid would be ringing their father who was miles away and their father would be comforting them saying they "looked like a regular R.A.T."

Maybe one day.

But right now, this was Randall's dream.

The dream of being a professional wrestler.

"Thanks, dad."

His father probably didn't get the last words that Randall said into the phone, it sounded like the generator died on them. It was on a timer. And it would waste too much fuel to crank it up for two more minutes of conversation.

Randall gently hung the phone back up in it's cradle.

He was feeling a little homesick. He missed his mum and dad. He missed the kind words of Pedro as he flipped around on the deck of the boat.

He missed the river.

But in time the sickness would go away. Right now he was on tour with thReat. This was the chance to grab his dream by the throat and shake it for all it's worth.

Homesickness was not an option.
