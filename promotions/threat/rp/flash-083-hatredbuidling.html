Starring: From Another Time - Reflection

It didn’t take too long for the police the show up on the scene. Red and blue mixed with the gluey darkness of the park, and sirens blurred off little spurts of sound every so often, sending a ripple of resonance through the evening air. Voices murmured delicately in the court, mostly police questions witnesses and getting an idea of what happened.

Out of the corner of his eye, he watched these people carrying out their duty from the safety of the park bench. The time had passed by quickly, leaving the past to itself, locked away and dormant, never to be disturbed again. He had taken a picture of a lonely man, sitting by himself, waiting for fate to hurry up and give him what he was waiting for. That had been a little more then half an hour ago now.

He hadn’t really pictured the calm after the incident. Instead, what he waited for was the event itself. He could still hear the clicking of the gun distinctly in his ear, and the fear locked behind the anger in the young man’s voice. But that was all part of the anticipation.

The worst part of that evening.

Now that was buried. Nothing left to do now but stare at the results, which was by far more then he wanted to do. The crowd, scared earlier by the bullets, had now calmed. The scene was marked off with that God damn yellow tape. The blood dried on the concrete of the cement court. And the body of the young man, quiet and still, was packed into a body bag and carried away by an ambulance by two paramedics who could give two shits about the life this body had lived.

The thought occurred to him that he was the only one to see this young man in his true state. Under a glowing street lamp, shining down and watching closely like a guardian angel, he had been sitting on the park bench, tears filling his eyes. With the snap of a camera and the imprinting of film, a moment was now locked in his mind and in his pocket. The one moment no other human being would ever see. A moment that could only be shared between him, the young man, and the eyes that remained unseen in the great beyond.

The thought was the truest emotion he could have after such an awful experience.

The sirens stopped echoing. The lights stopped shinning. The voices silenced and the cars pulled away and, little by little, the park returned to normal. Crickets sang. Trees rustled. The wind blew.

And, as a man, who had been sitting alone on a park bench with his own thoughts and emotions, stepped up and began to walk into the summer night, the low, barely audible crinkling of a newborn picture emanated from his pocket.

Just another picture.

Just another time.
