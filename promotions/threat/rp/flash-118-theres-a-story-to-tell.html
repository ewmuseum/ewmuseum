There's a story to tell..
Starring: T.H.W.

If it’s not to hard to believe, I wrote my senior project on Darnell Corteia. Well not just Darnell, but on young athletes that forego education in terms of trying to make a living a professional athlete. I could’ve gone a completely different route, I could’ve focused on the Europeans who were in poverty, who hung to their hopes, but I focused on Darnell Corteia. In the library we had a few books on him, and tons of periodical material, I never watched him play in my life, I was too young, he declared before I was even in Kindergarten. I’ve seen pictures, I’ve seen some shoddy video in the WCHS video archives. And from what I’ve seen, I know that Darnell could’ve been the greatest to ever play the game.

I got an “A” on that report, but the writing of it just opened up an unquenchable thirst. And that was two years ago. Not many things could capture my attention, to be honest, my family all thought I should be in college, using my writing to become a sports journalist. As much as I would enjoy writing about how the Eagles, Sixers, Phillies, and Flyers are going to win their respective titles, that’s not exactly what I was born to pen. I’ve had questions my whole life, mostly the past handful of years, and I can’t stop what I’m doing and focus on them until I have answers.

Why is Richard “Rip” Hamilton’s jersey hanging on the wall in the Intermediate School’s gym and not Darnell Corteia’s? I don’t get it, sure Rip broke the scoring record, but Darnell isn’t even acknowledged anywhere in the High School. Is it because his “innocence” was lost when he was caught under the influence of illegal narcotics? Is it because he ruined his shot at making the NBA? Your jersey shouldn’t be retired based on what you do after you leave the school, it should be what you do when there.

Now Coatesville is referred by many as the “Rip City,” it’s sad but true, I heard about how it was so alive years ago, and now thanks to drugs, this place is just an empty husk, most of it’s population living in the suburbs, no one cares about Coatesville anymore, not even me, really. Drive down the stretch of Lincoln Highway right through the middle of town after seven, you’ll see homeless walking around, girls who are too young trying to act old walking and going on like cackling hens. A bunch of men standing in front of Dave’s Corner, like we don’t know they’re there to deal drugs. It’s like that on 3rd Avenue too, that’s where they deal crack. Some on 7th as well. The cops know it, but you know that some of these cops are dirty as the streets themselves, you throw the guppy back in hoping that it’ll lead you to something else.

It’s not a big city, I’m surprised they even claim it as a city anymore to be honest, Downingtown’s bigger, West Chester’s cleaner. It’s life’s version of Neverland, a place where people think they never have to grow up. I’m glad I don’t have to focus on that. I’m glad my parents kept me away from a life like that. Eventually, I’m going to get out of Coatesville, and I’m taking my family with me, I’ll never look back. Never.

The way I’m going to get out of here is easy, all I have to do is this, write a history on the Corteia family. The true first family of this city. For some reason, they aren’t written about, some reason their great actions in the Coatesville Sports isn’t remembered. And if the city itself tries to shake it’s head at them, I’ll find a story to tell, that people will read. I don’t have a choice.

-T.H.W.
