Of One Such Time - Preface
Starring: He who can see

With his eyes wide open, he stepped inside. The sunlight streaked through the windows, catching the drifting dust in the room’s heavy air. The aged and tired white carpet reflected a dim light of its own thanks to the sun, but it didn’t really matter. The room had nothing impressive to see, and thus had no reason for good lighting. It was a small, tight little apartment, no noteworthy presence within.

Three rooms to be precise – his “bedroom”, his kitchen, his bathroom. A plain table in the corner, with a lone chair standing next to it and a tarnished stove made up the kitchen. A toilet usually filled with some sort of brown liquid passed off as water making up his bathroom. A mattress, covered with a sheet used especially for those chilly evenings making up the bedroom.

It was hard to believe that a soul existed in this world who would not hate living in such a place.

Not him.

He had no reason to give two shits about his life, and so he didn’t care at all.

Slamming the door as hard as he could, the man who called this place home made sure that no one witnessed him step inside. He then turned and locked the door. With the locking of one chain and the turning of two bolts, the door was secure. He wanted to make sure, maybe for his own reasons or for others, that he was alone. None were worthy, he figured.

With a leather coat and a pair of jeans on, he walked over to his mattress, staring down only at one corner of it. The upper left one against the corner of the wall. The wall that was covered with old wallpaper, carrying a pattern of small, pointless flowers of no importance to anyone who laid eyes on them.

He lifted the mattress, peeking down at the carpet, stained with some sort of brown shit that didn’t give him any interest at the moment. From the tip of the fabric, he lifted the carpet off the ground. A brown wooden floor lay underneath, haven for a cover of dust and other nifty little pieces of crumbs or sand. But, one portion of the dust was missing; finger marks were clearly visible at one end of a board.

Grabbing the edge with his fingertips, he lifted that board out of place, revealing the darkened nook he made so long ago. Darkness invoked his eyes, as he tried to adjust them to see the treasure buried within. Slowly, he made out the silhouette of a black shoe box, resting in the sanctuary he gave it the day he realized he could. He reached in, half expecting to feel the legs of some insect tickle his arm, and grabbed the box. Lifting it into the light of day, it glowed, glimmering through him like a diamond. If there was but one thing in the world he wanted to see, it was this box.

This box and what it held inside.

He walked over to the table and sat down in the chair. He placed the box down on the table the one would place a diary or an antique down carefully. With his dirty fingers, he lifted the lid off the box.

And…

With his eyes wide open…
