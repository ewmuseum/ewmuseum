Part I; Phase I - Protocol; The Road To Nazareth - III - In Which The Scar Burns

Part I; Phase I - Protocol; The Road To Nazareth - III - In Which The Scar Burns
Starring: Rejection

Fresh in his new apartment, the Rejected one began the unpacking of his things. The majority of what was in the old place he shared with his now stagnant mistress belonged to him, yet much of it remained in its same place. Instead, only the most desired and personal objects were brought with him. And yet, his increasing ego had also increased his collection of clothing. Such wastes of material he would never wear were the bulk of his packaging. Now though, he couldn't be concerned with it, as he began to rip the tape off of his newly purchased holding areas.

No sleep, with fatigue still not rearing it's ugly head, he continued to build his new life, assembling pieces of a bookshelf, a nightstand, and a simple side table used for the rare occasions when he would dine in. Working on a diet of caffeine and vitamins his inner stomach lining was slipping into a dangerous state, as his bodies ache for nourishment was not met with the admiration needed from the mind. His focus was not met with his bodies needs or vital desires. He instead focused his work only on leaving and making sure that there was nothing left to keep itself standing on.

This was no longer a simple thing, but something more extravagant. Something more foolish in ways that not even he could comprehend. Yet, he pushed onward...

...into the night, where he would soon realize that things would always be different. Things would always be out of place.

The pale crescent failed in comparison to the possible fluorescent that could aid him, yet his back maintained it's aligned state against the wall. He had opted to go out and consume alcohol, or to start smoking. Yet neither appealed to him. He never could keep it down. And his anger had slowly reclaimed itself as he stared at his born pillars.

All of them had been packed by her. His simple outfits still were still awaiting their opportunity to shine. His regulars had been buried at the bottom, he knew this. He also knew of it's purposeful intent. And that only angered him. It was meant to inspire, or to regulate. Yet neither mattered to him in the end. He counted them, the black words changing into tally marks on his mental chalkboard. With his acquired number of slashes, he raised his body up. Standing once again, he stalked his way to the boxes.

Ripping the tape off of them in a violent motion, they were quickly opening, as he shoved each violated box onto it's side, spilling a few of it's articles onto the hardwood floor. He now looked at his latest and most expensive garments. Once neatly folded, they had become intrepid collections waste. He started moving them out of the way, digging to the bottom of every box, removing the clothes from whence he started. Old pairs of jeans, tight t-shirts that didn't grow as his mass did, and tied at the laces were his only pair of sneakers. As his fingers grazed slowly over the texture of them, he slowly slid them back into the box with the other mundane objects.

Having released his valued articles, he began re-packaging the other clothes, using fewer boxes with the spared room he now had. Using only three of the cardboard containers, he stacked them on top of eachother, moving them to the doorway. Opening his drawer, with its newly archived items, he removed a long lighter. Next to it was the drinking container it so often recquired with use, and he put them both into his back pockets. His arms wrapped around the boxes again, as he pressed them against the door, turning the handle with his momentarily free hand.

He felt his way down the staircase on the side of the apartment building. Carefully placing each foot down onto the next wooden step, he made his way to the ground. Following the sidewalk back, and behind the building, he came to the firepit of his downstairs neighbor. He set down the boxes next it, removing the boxes from their stack. He pulled each piece of clothing up, spraying a little of the fluid on each one before dousing the outside of the box in the clear firestarter. The box tumbled into the stone enclosed circle, as he started to click the black button of the lighter. His small, vibrant flame finally emitted, as he moved it closer to the garments and their container. It slowly ignited into a beautiful display of burning orange, slowly begining to consume the cardboard, as it began the long battle of digesting the clothing.

It was at this time that his neighbor opened his sliding glass door, yelling at the mysterious man who was using his property. Said man turned around, the fire outlining most of his body in the orange flame. But his eyes remained cold, distant, yet focused on eliminating distractions. This look was all the neighbor needed before gulping, and sliding back his door, the noise of mechanics locking into place as he walked out of sight. Rejection turned his stare back to the other boxes of clothing, and began to dampen them again with the lighter fluid, what remained of it.

As his hands grasped at one of the fancier coats in his soon-to-be gone collection, he felt something he hadn't earlier. Inside the pocket he could make out the shape of the object: a celtic cross.

The kind that Lucretia Shepard had worn for the past five years, fastened around her neck by a black chord.

Removing it from the pocket, he closed his hand, making a fist around the pewter symbol. The soft metal crushed just enough to let him know it happened, but it's appearence would still maintain mis-leading. It slid into his pocket, as he continued to work away at the clothing, faster, sloppier. He threw the boxes on lazily, as a slow burst occured after each one entered its burning state. Until there was nothing left to do but watch the fire, which didn't appear to be dying down soon.

He remained standing, his hand fingering the necklace inside his pocket. The coolness of the metal enticing to his hands. He realized that things would always be out of place.

The cold, distant look in his eyes no longer remained. And his sorrow replaced his focus, as he slowly broke down, the cackle of the fire laughing at him, as the scar etched into his soul glowed with the blaze before him.
