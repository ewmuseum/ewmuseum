Past and Present
Starring:

A hollow wind embraced the sky, reaching across the city skyline like the neon orange of a sunset over the horizon. He sat on the rooftop, listening to the blissful humming of life in his ear, waiting for the night to give way to gray dawn. His eyes, all time, staring down at the glib glowing of streetlamps and dreary voices of men drifting through the streets.

And imagining invisible eyes from above staring down, looking over his shoulder like angels of the heavens.

The present was alive. And the past slumbered, deeply and quietly. If you closed your eyes and touched the world, you could feel it breathing in the palm of your hand.

His mind wandered boldly through the catacombs of his memories, cutting his feet on the shards of the shattered moments he wished he could forget. Through these web filled patches of darkness, he traveled, trying to grip what little bit of understanding he could from what had come to pass. He could see so many pieced together, spanning along a canvas hanging a million feet in the air like a landscape of pain and misery. Each one was just as important and as irrelevant as the next.

The moon, serenely shinning from the coal-colored sky, came out from behind the pale clouds crawling through the air. The light filled his iris, and slapped the surface of the picture he held in his hand, revealing both the sweat marks of his fingers upon the image and, more importantly, the stillness of a second, now buried in the darkness.

Yet another time from which to dwell.

-----

A few years before, there had been Atlanta. Time passed by, slowly but surely, and before long the dreary months of winter rested assuredly in the air of Chicago. His eyes scanned the water as he stood on the shoreline of a beautiful lake. A lake far more magnificent and lively then any he could remember seeing.

Minute waves drifted peacefully in opulent patterns, glimmering in the grayness of the December sky. His eyes gazed, lost in the rhythm of beauty and awe filling his soul. Very few things could make a man such as himself stare placidly at life and make him realize his insignificance. Or maybe there were few men who could do such a thing.

The sun had been setting, but lackluster blankets of clouds filled the sky, hiding away all hope of ever seeing that sight. But, with the last showing of light in the sky, soft snowflakes fell from the bleak gray. They salted the water, melting quickly and joining the entirety that was the sea.

He stood, watching the spectacle with invisible eyes. He imagined that he, standing there at that very moment, had been the only man to ever partake in such a divine happening. Part of him wished this to be the truth. But, before long, that wish vanished under manifestation of life that he watched and cherished.

But before long, the sun has disappeared. The water now only existed in a slight hum that filled the air, banking his ear like waves along the shoreline. He stood for a few moments longer before turning around, and throwing his hands into the pockets of his trench coat.

Now in front of him stood a few apartment buildings, warming those within as he stepped through the powdering streets of Chicago. In the distance, the city stood like mountains, scraping the night sky and singing voicelessly.

He carefully stepped through the world, paying attention to almost nothing except the snow falling from the sky before him. Time had passed, and many moments had been saved safely in his pocket, but he had no prospects of life. Nothing to care about anymore. Not even a past he cared to dwell on.

Little did he know what the present had in store for him.
