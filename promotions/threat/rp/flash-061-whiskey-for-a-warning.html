Whiskey For A Warning
Starring: Generative Clash -- Stephen Martin

"Don't you think I'm about to react now?!"
~ The White Stripes, "Black Math"

The honesty of midnight is a struggle to comprehend. Especially in a city of simple silence and old fables. A city built from the ruins of itself, again and again. The smoke of a few fire-brung explosions tear through the Lower East Side, where two cars have collided. A mess. No deaths, but inconvenience all around.

Within this, there's only time for the lazy-eyed drunks of the world to throw themselves onto a barstool seat or into a thick-padded, torn-velvet booth, where the world will slip into the drinks, where it will be swallowed, and where it will eventually be ejected onto itself.

So Stephen Martin notes, quiet, peculiar. Alone. An unused ashtray, crummy and claiming "place your butt here" in glaring white under smokey black, sits in the middle of this table.

Tennessee whiskey is in front of him. A full bottle, untouched. He hates whiskey. But like a proverbial woman in a proverbial U2 song about proverbial hell, where one will "Stay" one night... he bought it for the mere feeling of owning something. It is enjoyable to spend money. Thus the creation of credit cards.

None of which Stephen carries. His lifestyle is that of a nomad, like many others of his kind; floating like a fat mastermind in a novel about a desert planet, going from place to place and leaving a mark.

So it goes.

Stephen isn't a fan of Vonnegaut; his tag team partner, his best friend, and -- yes, a lady -- Zoe Antoinette is perhaps his biggest.

Stephen prefers the darkness and edginess of Stephen King, the layers of problematic life that John Grisham provides. He likes Dickens, parlays him as the greatest author ever.

Nothing is worth capitalizing.

The swing of a metal-and-glass door -- the openable window of six foot high change -- alerts him to something. He swings his head to his right, looking over the top of the booth, seeing a weasely drunk stumble to his stool.

Stephen turns back. He had gotten a page -- and he never got pages -- that essentially told him to come here. To wait a while. Order some whiskey. Get a glass, one glass, and wait some more.

Stephen's being a good little boy while doing this. He let Zoe go off with the rental car, let her go shopping for some shoes or who knows what. Women always have to buy something, they've always got to be peering into shop windows at things they never could have, even in dreams.

Zoe tells him its the female addiction -- instead of drugs, many women turn to shopping. It's just as expensive, Stephen notes to himself. Just as draining on the 'ol bank account.

The door opens again. And Stephen swings his head around again. A man in what appears to be the cheapest sports coat known to man comes around the booth, slides across from him.

"Hi," Stephen says.

The man in the sports coat takes the whiskey. He slides it into an interior pocket, which creates the ghastly look of what could be a dildo underneath the man's coat, from how the coat itself falls against the bottle. And the man gets up, leaves.

Stephen almost swears aloud. But he changes his mind. Instead, he gets up. The guy deserves a rather brunt ass-kicking, and it's up to Stephen to give it to him. Nobody does a Martin the wrong way. If you're gonna screw him over... screw him over the right way, without him knowing.

Stephen looks down at the other end of the bar. The back exit. If Stephen went through there... there would be an element of surprise. The guy would be walking, and -- whop-bam-boom -- Stephen would slap him silly with his sneakers.

Then he sees the folded note on the torn booth's fabric. He leans over the table, shaking the ashtray in doing so, and picks it up.

He unfolds it. Reads it.

"Zoe..." he whispers.

He leaves the bar. Not quite sure of whether he's still going to kick the whiskey-lover's ass or not, but at a hurried pace as if he were still going to do so.

He leaves the drunks to flush away their hopes and dreams along with their failures and nightmares.

So he can maybe help Zoe keep her dream alive before it even begins.
