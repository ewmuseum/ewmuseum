A Saturday Afternoon Many Years Ago (2)
Starring: Bobby Knickerson

Thirty minutes to 6. Robert groaned as he looked up at the clock; there was only so little time left before his evening tuition began. It would drag on for two whole hours, before dinner, and then, sleep. His parents INSISTED that their son sleep early every night. They didn't want Bobby to become a zombie. Staying up late, doing whatever he pleased. Getting into trouble. Making his own body-clock go awry.

Deep inside, Robert's anger just grew. His life was being constricted. HIS life. Yes, his mother might have given him life and both his mother & father were supporting him in any way possible. But it was still HIS life. Bob couldn't understand why he couldn't be like Archie or Jughead.

Or Batman, even. A superhero of the night, prowling around Gotham City, saving innocent victims from the clutches & influences of evil such as The Joker, Penguin, and Catwoman.

He finished reading the last comic of the stack he'd brought out earlier, and just as he was about to return them to the bookshelf, the door to his room opened. It was Mommy Dearest, looking as tired as ever. Robert knew exactly what she was going to say. She'd said the same thing for the last nine weeks straight.

"Bobby, get ready your material for tuition, it's 5:30 already."

The young Knickerz child sighed silently and nodded his head, indicating to his mother that he was well aware of the time. But just as she smiled and began to walk away, the door closing behind her, Robert decided it was time.

It was time to stand up for what he believed in.

It was time to take control of his life, even at the young & tender age of 8.

It was time for Batman to stand up to the twin forces of evil that were curbing the natural way of living.

"MOM!"

"Yes, Bobby?"

This was it, Bob thought. He stood there, fists clenched tightly, his emotions now running wildly in his bloodstream. The comics had been put away. Now, in a strange sense, Robert felt like he was going to put away his mother. But really, all he wanted was to be rid of the silly apron strings holding him back.

"C-Can I have some orange juice?"

Momma smiled again, after a moment of concern at the tone her son had employed whilst calling her.

"Sure, honey. Just be sure to clear your table up and have your books all ready by the time I come back, okay?"

His shoulders sagged as he nodded again, before falling onto his bed, feeling weak. Bob had chickened out at the last minute. Why? Fear, most likely. Fear of how his mother would react. And most definitely, his father would come into the fray, which would mean even more trouble. Robert just couldn't deal with that.

Now, he has to deal with Maths tuition.

"I hate Maths."
