Restless Soul I
Starring: Grieve

Chapter One: The Journey Begins

As the ocean rustled beneath him, the fresh air whispered in the ears of John Edward Grieve. The freelancer advance through the water as water plunged upwards, damping John’s maroon shirt and a slight chill aroused. The weather was neither cold nor warm but the water was freezing. But John’s face showed no emotions as his eyes gazed at the never-ending ocean.

He wasn’t bothered by the chill he had at his spine nor the water on his shirt. His mind was set on something else, something disrupting. His eyes were squinted as the wind continued to polish his face. He looked up into the blue sky spotted by white clouds as a flock of seagulls hovered over him.

John had been hiding from America for nearly four years with only a diary in his grasp. He wanted time to himself, space, a change more the less but with guilt concealed within his soul he had to turn back, a proceed for his father and maybe his grief would fly within the blue skies above and he could live without hatred and despair.

Maybe he could pray to God as he once did.

Maybe he could regain his faith as before.

John scowled at the thoughts.

He had never been in a professional ring before, just a small college mat. He didn’t have the slightest clue of to what the basics were. All he clutched was the look of a monster, his big knuckles, and astonishing death holds. He wasn’t a wrestler, he was a brawler, but wrestling was his goal. He had to be known in order to ease his soul.

So he did it, several nights before he had sign the contract. He had found a place to start and it wouldn’t be easy. John knew that but nothing had been easy for him since 1999.

An hour past and John could finally see the docks up ahead. The sun began to set and the skies rotated colors. As the boat anchored, night awakened. The whispers of the wind had stopped and the night had grown colder.

John seized his bag and set foot on the wooden dock; each step taken sounded as if the dock was going to crumble. John was exhausted and tired. A small lodge sat in the middle of the town. John needed a place to rest but was clueless of where to go. He entered through the door, using his foot. Small conversation were being held by old citizens but as the monster entered through the door, all conversations stopped and all eyes shifted to the man whose chest was about to bulge out from underneath his shirt. They watched him walk from the door, to the reception desk. It was as if they had never seen a man of his magnitude.

“Where can I get a room?” his depressing voice leaped at the male figure behind the desk.

“Here…” the man’s voice trembled with fear. Sweat drizzled down his chubby cheeks as he retained the necessary papers.

John took the pen next to the thin sheet of paper and signed his name on the dotted line, leaving a hundred dollar bill. He was handed the key and he walked away, not asking where the room was to be. He’d find it on his own.

As he walked back through the entrance, he looked down at the key. “Room 4” it stated in black. He looked around the small town and seen a logged cabin with the number 4 carved into the door and his face showed a sign of relief.

He lie in bed, his arms hugged the diary to his chest as if he was holding on to it with his dear life. John laid there, listening to the screams of 1999 and the gunshots that changed his life. His red eyes, glossed and a tear scampered down the side of his face.

John opened the diary up, only to read what he had last written. The only thing he had written and it had been written on the boat ride home. He read the words and closed his eyes. The scream of his mother scarred his mind, making it unbearable to rest. He reached into his bag, pulled out a bottle of some sort, and opened it up. Inside were small tablets of some sort, white tablets. Two poured from the bottle and onto his head. Without the use of a liquid substance, he swallowed them and lied back down.

And minutes past before his mind was at ease.

He could now rest till morning.
