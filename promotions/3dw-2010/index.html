A hardcore-leaning independent fantasy promotion staged out of Sacramento by a returning handler, opening with a fifteen-man debut taping billed as free of authority-figure storylines. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

3DW's own application call, posted 14 September 2010, opens simply: “I'm back after six years away and starting up a new fed.” A debut-card announcement eight days later filled the promotion out through its own commentator, a character introducing himself as Brandon Spears — “formerly known as Jeremy Locke, Flash Andrews, The Hardcore Pop Star” among other gimmicks — who promised a fed built against the grain: “No commissioner, president, board member, CEO angles. No sports entertainment. No old-style grappling,” matches decided by pin, submission or knockout with disqualifications and countouts barely used, and a masked, seven-foot house enforcer billed as Project X to handle anyone who stepped out of line.

That announcement previewed fifteen wrestlers across a five-match card set for the Sacramento Memorial Auditorium on 6 October 2010. The taping that actually aired, 3DTV, opened differently than advertised: a backstage dispute scratched the planned opener, and the show instead led with a tag match, “Deathdealer” Tommy Ganz and “The New Legend” Kenneth Morlock falling to Rob Nolan and Larry Gionet. The wrestler left without an opponent, Na Neun Park, was given a new one, Harrison Butler, on the spot, and won his own in-story debut with a springboard moonsault followed by a driver.

A three-way among Loco Fausto, “The Immaculate Conception” Sebastian Madison and Jaime Roberts went to Fausto as advertised, while a previewed two-team tag match grew a third team, Team 'Merica, added to the mix against Menagerie du Diablerie and The Disciple & Randal Bishop; Menagerie du Diablerie came out on top of the resulting triple threat.

The main event pitted “The Celtic Crippler” Caleb Foley, billed as the promotion's own biggest name, against debuting “Shadow Stalker” Chance Holiday, who won with a Vertebreaker onto a loose ring bell to hand Foley his first loss. A veteran “Death Match Legend,” Christian Copeland, watched the finish silently from ringside, setting up a future program with Foley the surviving record never reaches.

No further document survives past that debut taping's own script. Whether 3DW ran a second show at all isn't established here.
