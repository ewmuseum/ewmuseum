Tag Team Supplement WHAT IS AWA? The Arizona Wrestling Alliance is an e-wrestling federation. If you didn’t realize that by now, it’s probably best that you click on that nifty little back-button above, and forget you ever saw this page.
With that being said, there really isn’t much else to say. If you’re here, your probably know the basics of e-wrestling. AWA doesn’t stray too far away from those basics. We like to keep things simple, following a formula that has worked many, many times before us. While there are no big gimmicks in AWA, we do strive, however, to do the best with that we have, and produce quality shows and flashes.
AWA is a regional federation, meaning everything pretty much stays within the state boundaries of Arizona. But, simply because this is fantasy, and well, we can basically do what we please, AWA will have all the perks of a national or worldwide federation. We’re going to house cards in big arenas. We’re going to have the gargantuan megatron above the entrance ramp. We’re going to have all the high-end production stuff. Let’s have some fun with this.
The overtone of AWA is a classic one. AWA is where the fans will come to see, good, quality wrestling. That doesn’t mean that every match is going to be a straight, one fall, 20 minute time limit, no stipulations match, though. There will be gimmick matches. There just won’t be massive chair-swinging, garbage-can-slamming, brawls flooding every card.
WHO RUNS AWA? 2a. OUT OF CHARACTER AWA is run by [name withheld] and [name withheld]. Neither one calls themselves the president or vice president. They simply are, well, to put it as technically as possible, the two dudes who run this thing.
They will write matches, book cards, and piece the cards and flash shows together. However, these two won’t do it alone... hopefully. AWA will also need the assistance of handlers and even any outside viewers. It will need handlers to write matches mostly, and offer whatever help they can. It’s a team effort.
2b. IN CHARCTER "Bronco" Bud Davis is the AWA Commissioner. He built the federation from the ground up, starting June of 2001. He’s an extremely wealthy Texan wrestling fanatic, on a bit of a power trip. This is his opportunity to actually be a part of the show.
WHAT SHOWS DOES AWA AIR? 3a. GRAND CANYON GRAPPLING This is the main AWA show. It will mostly occur on Sundays should the card be on time (knock on wood). All the matches on the show will be written out in play-by-play form--what we feel is the most exciting form. All major angles will be played out on this show. It will be host to approximately five matches, which is subject to change once we start to get rolling.
3b. AWA REWIND This is the main flash show. All those flashes you write will find their way onto this show. This show will also recap the previous weeks show and preview the next. This is the show to watch if you didn’t have enough time to read all of GCG, which we hope won’t be the case.
3c. The Flash Show This is just a plain flash show. There is no commentary on this, just whatever flashes we received placed one after the other. One of these will be sent out if for some reason, we feel more than one RP a week would be beneficial for AWA.
3d. The AWA Update This isn’t a show, really. It’s just a weekly mailing. On this, there will be OOC notes, bulleted card results, upcoming lineups, the contact list, updated rankings, and more. This pretty much tells you what is going on, and what will be going on.
3e. SUPERCARDS AND PPV’S There will probably be four unnamed pay-per-views per year. Build up to them will be great. However, in between these PPV’s there will be smaller, but important supercards. As of now, there will be one supercard a month. There are currently no names for these events.
3f. WEEKLY SCHEDULE Sunday: GRAND CANYON GRAPPLING Monday: THE AWA UPDATE Tuesday: Nothing Wednesday: Nothing Thursday: AWA REWIND Friday: Nothing Saturday: Nothing HOW ARE MATCH WINNERS DECIDED? 4a. FLASHES I’m sure I don’t need to explain what a flash is to you. So, let me use this space to explain what we look for. We want to be entertained. If you’re going to go with the talk-to-the-camera approach, make sure your character’s personality exudes, and what he or she says is interesting. If you’re going to use the commonly called, slice-of-life approach, make sure the situation is intriguing.
There are also a few basic guidelines. No racial slurs, no derogatory comments based on sex, religion, race, sexual orientation, creed, whatever. Use common sense. As for cursing, use this general rule, if they can say it in real life on cable TV, you can say it here. If not,
[BLEEP], F[BEEP], ####, it out, or whatever. Just make sure the entire word isn’t visible. You may not use other wrestlers in your flashes without their handler’s permission. If you want to use
AWA personnel, okay it with us first. There is also the issue of kayfabe. Do not refer to real wrestling. That’s a no-no.
4b. ANGLES/ON-CARD PARTICIPATION This will make or break AWA. Angles are what make the difference between an okay federation, and a great one. It’s what makes handling fun. It’s what makes reading cards interesting.
Basically, what I’m trying to get across is that we need angles. We need a lot of them. We need short term ones. We need long term ones. We need good ones. Heck, we could even use bad ones (though not preferred). Angles are what will make our fed, and if you’re not in an angle, it will be hard to climb the ladder in AWA.
4c. HEAT This is the accumulation of your flashing and angles. If you’re producing quality flashes and exciting angles, your character has heat. He’s "over" with the fans, so to speak. The fans buy tickets to see your man wrestle. Consequently, if your character has a lot of heat, and some hot shot rookie comes into the fed and challenges your character to a match, even if that rookie out
RP’s your character, the character with more heat will most likely win. However, as a result, the rookie will gain more heat, and maybe the next time the two meet, the rookie will win. Then again, that meeting probably wouldn’t have occurred because those characters with heat will be at the top of the card, and those without will be at the bottom.
4d. STRATEGIES Sorry for the scare. Actually, these aren’t really that important at all. They’re optional. They won’t have any bearing on the match whatsoever. However, if you want your wrestler to be portrayed a certain way in the ring, or you want to see some specific spots performed, this is where you’ll tell us.
There’s no due date on this, and everything in it will be left to our discretion. WHO IS ON THE AWA STAFF? 5a. SUITS "Bronco" Bud Davis: This is the AWA Commissioner. He runs the show in every aspect. He’s a wealthy Texan with a hearty personality.
You can see it in his big gut, rosy cheeks, and championship-belt-sized belt buckle. 5b. ANNOUNCERS Walt Hofstadter: Walt calls everything right down the middle. He’s a fan of clean, technical wrestling. He’ll applaud anyone for a superb executed maneuver, whether the wrestler who performed it is a lowdown scumbag, or an upright goodie-two-shoes.
Lyle Wallop: He’s a flea on every dirty wrestler’s back. If he thinks something in the ring isn’t right, he’s every vocal about it, unlike Walt. His mouth can sometimes get him in the doghouse with heel wrestlers, but his only response to that is "bring it on."
Bill Gardner: He’s a fan of blood, carnage, and mayhem. He absolutely loves to see that dirty chair shot when the ref isn’t looking. Cheating excites him, and the only wrestler’s worthy to be in the ring, are those that use a Machiavellian approach.
5c. INTERVIEWERS/RING ANNOUNCERS Karen Katel: She’s a professional, business-like woman. She dressed to the nines at all times. The only problem (for her, not us)
is that she’s extremely hot as usual. What did you expect? Unfortunately for her, she isn’t taken as seriously as a journalist as she would like. Dick Kruger: He’s the ring announcer. Before your wrestler walks out into the ring, he’ll say his name. Simple enough, eh?
WHAT TITLES DOES AWA RECOGNIZE? 6a. AWA HEAVYWEIGHT TITLE As if you haven’t heard this enough times in FAQ’s, this is what everyone shoots for. If your wrestler has this around his waist, every other wrestler will want to me him, or simply want him in the ring. This title has to be defended once a month at least. If it isn’t, the title can and will be stripped from the holder.
It’s encouraged that the title be defended more than once a month though. It’s alright if it isn’t though. It’s called having a prestigious title or something like that.
6b. AWA GRAND CANYON TITLE This is the secondary title. Most likely, holding this will not make you the number one contender to the big gold. The holder though, is probably right on the fringe of being a true main eventer. This title also must be defended at least once a month.
6c. AWA SKY WEIGHT TITLE This is the title for the little guys. To be eligible for this, your wrestler must be either 235 lbs. or below. This title is for those high-flying, exciting matches we all know and love. It’s not a main event title, but is nonetheless still respected. There is no requirement to how many times this title must be defended, but it will most likely be defended every two or three weeks just to keep things interesting.
6d. AWA TAG TEAM TITLES These go to AWA’s top tag team. This what every tag team shoots for. Heck, there’s nothing else to shoot for. These are to be defended at least once a month also.
WHAT IS THE BOOKING POLICY? We book matches based on angles and heat. Those around the same heat level will be booked together. Those in angles with each other will be booked accordingly.
There will be no undefeated wrestlers in AWA. Losing is not the end of the world. If you’re worried about losing because of title contention, don’t. Title contention will not be based upon records, but on heat. If you’re working on angles and flashing consistently, you’re doing well. Just because you don’t have a winning record, doesn’t mean you’re not a top tier guy.
Another thing we request of you, is that if you have a problem with booking or who won or lost a match, to approach us reasonably with an open mind. Please, don’t whine, moan, throw a tantrum, throw things, curse at us, call our mother’s names, mail bomb us, hack our computers, steal our identities over the internet and ruin of lives, etc. That will not be appreciated, nor tolerated. Especially the last one.
