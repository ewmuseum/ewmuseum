A one-man promotion whose founding FAQ, weekly show, and a later change of hands survive from an April-to-June 2001 run. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The North-American Wrestling Alliance opened after its founder had already passed through other federations that “either folded or gotten stagnant,” and its own FAQ of 15 April 2001 frames the new promotion as an attempt to recapture “the magic of good feds like ECW and the glory days of gimmick wrestling.” It ran one show a week so its own founder “can write the show by hand,” a Monday-night program titled NWA Prime Time, with a second weekly card promised only once he judged himself able to handle it.

In place of pay-per-views, which the FAQ dismissed as something “purely presented for revenue” and unnecessary for a one-man promotion, the NWA ran monthly SuperCards under the banner “NWA Main Event,” each one defending every title on the roster and settling any outstanding feud. Its own guidance for role-plays pushed past the obvious settings — “not... from the ring, arena, backstage, etc.” — toward invented scenarios like an at-home interview or a camping trip interrupted by fight news, alongside a plain house rule against racism, hate, or attacking another wrestler without that wrestler's own consent.

A separate post nine days earlier, on 6 April 2001, had already opened sign-ups for the promotion's debut supercard at a site hosted on Sphosting, ahead of the FAQ itself.

By 21 June 2001 the promotion's site had moved to Angelfire and its flagship show carried a new name, the nWa MeLtDoWn Card, in a post from its own president linking to what it called the last one under that name and pointing to a roleplay-packets page alongside it. The federation's own record shows a change in leadership by this point, without saying more about how or when it happened.

That June post is the last document of the North-American Wrestling Alliance's own that survives. Whether it continued running MeLtDoWn cards after it, and how or when it closed, aren't in the surviving record.
