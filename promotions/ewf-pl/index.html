Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Extreme Wrestling Federation ran the whole of its documented life in Polish, from its own site, ewf.freehost.pl. Its first show, Wrestlepalooza I, aired 11 April 2001 and crowned Kraven its first World Champion out of a battle royal; its own title-history pages trace two of its championships further back still, to a 1999 predecessor it calls PCW, whose belts EWF describes itself as continuing rather than replacing.

Its own broadcasts ran on a self-aware, comedic commentary voice, carried for years by the pairing of Tony “Toster” Hogański and Jan Kowalski, trading jabs at each other and at the promotion's own history in front of a crowd whose signs, read out card after card, needled absent wrestlers and the fed's own long-dead predecessor by name. Its on-screen president, Dagmara Zielińska, appeared in her own comic backstage vignettes alongside the roster she nominally ran.

Six championships ran across its life: the World and Tag Team titles from the start, a hardcore-styled belt it named outright the Fuck the World Championship, an Evolution Championship billed as the spiritual successor to a defunct “Konstantynopol” title, a Daemusin Championship revived in 2009 as a continuation of one of its predecessor's own belts, and an EWF/Polish Championship carried forward from that same predecessor. The World title alone changed hands sixteen times on the surviving record, from Kraven's opening win through a three-way rights transfer settled at its fourth pay-per-view in 2005 and back to Kraven for a third reign in 2007.

Its own last surviving full broadcast, EWF HD “Aborcja,” aired 26 and 27 July 2009 from Lublin, launching a new HD-branded series built to lead into a hundredth Wrestlepalooza. That night crowned Jimmy Hendrixon its first Daemusin Champion, closing him into a straitjacket for the pin after outside interference; saw Y2J take the Evolution Championship from Izzy Nilsen; and closed with Vaclav beating a masked mystery opponent in one main event while, in the other, Nas Jazzowski took the Fuck the World Championship from Tool after the guest referee turned on the champion with his own finisher mid-match.

No further full broadcast survives past that night. Its own site was still being updated as late as June 2013, but no further show, title change, or closing statement of its own survives to say how much longer Extreme Wrestling Federation carried on, or how it ended.
