The wrestling board of a dial-up service that had a fantasy-wrestling scene running on it before the web arrived, and a member-page ecosystem afterwards — fifteen leagues on one register in July 1997, a cross-league Unified Championship with a committee behind it, and a board nobody archived. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The wrestling talk started in the wrong place. PRODIGY sorted its bulletin boards by subject, and wrestling was a topic under “Other” in the Sports BB — which is where the CSWA found it in 1992, along with “a slew of ‘fantasy sports’” and, in its own founding memoir's words, “a few ‘fantasy leagues’ on the board” already running: “Many were fashioned around ‘being’ real wrestlers, while others talked about stats or ‘roleplay’.” Wrestling got a board of its own afterwards, the WRESTLING BB, and the play got a topic of its own on it, FANTASY-PRODIGY.

By 9 July 1997 the board's own register listed fifteen leagues at once, each typed twice over — Real, Mixed or Generated for whose names it booked, Roleplay or Simulator for how it settled a match — with a president, a next card and a paragraph of its own news beside it. Above them sat a Unified World Heavyweight Championship that ran across league lines, with a standing committee of league presidents to sanction it and a published top twenty-five. The board kept a page for itself on the web from 20 February 1996 — “Your place for Fantasy Wrestling on the Web” — and a weekly news desk called FW Central beside it.

The other half of the community lived at pages.prodigy.com, the member-page hosting the service stood up as the web arrived: an address per member, filed by state and member ID. What survives of it here is three pages by three members — a member whose whole page says the wrestling pages are why they like the service, a links wall pointing off to the wrestling web of 1995, and a reviewer working through the leagues one at a time and grading them out of five stars, inviting presidents to write in. Federations kept their own addresses there too, and the biggest of them kept the community's own pages at its own.

The boards themselves do not survive. They were inside the PRODIGY Classic client rather than on the web, and no archive ever reached them; what is left of the community is its member pages and the histories its own federations wrote afterwards.

The community did not end so much as move. The CSWA bought fantasywrestling.com in early March 1998 and posted a notice on its PRODIGY page — stamped 29 March 1998 — and the hub that page became outlived the service under a second name. Wrestling member pages went on being captured at the service for another four years, and the directory the community's own pages sat in was still standing, untouched since 1997, when a crawler last looked in October 2001.

Affiliations

ChampionShip Wrestling Alliance (CSWA)

Present in WRESTLING BB (1992) (1992–1998)
The fed’s own founding memoir records it arriving on the Prodigy service in 1992, mailing seventy prospective members off the board and running ULTRATITLE 1992 there; its own site sat at pages.prodigy.com/cswa/ and its own League Info register of 9 July 1997 lists it among the board’s leagues under its own name. A message it posted to the board survives verbatim inside a served card, header and all — “Board: WRESTLING BB / Topic: FANTASY-PRODIGY.” Its own move notice off the service is stamped 29 March 1998.

Ultimate Wrestling Alliance (UWA, 1992–97)

Present in WRESTLING BB (1992) (1992–1997)
The board's own League Info register of 9 July 1997 lists the UWA among the leagues operating on PRODIGY's Wrestling BB, typed Generated / Roleplay, with a president and an empty news slot; the wing's own placard already records the fed as running on the service's WRESTLING BB under the topic FANTASY-PRODIGY, 1992–1997.

All Action Wrestling Conference (AAWC, All Action, 1993)

Present in WRESTLING BB (1992) (1996–1997)
The board’s own League Info register of 9 July 1997 lists the AAWC with its own World War Games 3 card and its own named roster; its one surviving night, Oktoberfest ’96, was posted to the board on 25 October 1996 in twenty-nine numbered parts.

FantasyWrestling.com / FWrestling.com

Successor to WRESTLING BB (1992)
The federation that anchored the PRODIGY WRESTLING BB's own League Info register moved to fantasywrestling.com in March 1998, and the new domain's own FAQ names that arrival as the reason the site exists at all. The new hub grew a forum system it called 'FW Central' -- the identical name the PRODIGY board's own League Info register and weekly news edition had already coined for the same multi-federation paperwork role. One continuing community institution under a new address, not two unrelated hubs that happen to share a name.
