JAPAN PURORESU ASSOCIATION FLASH BULLETIN 2001.3.24 1) MASSIVE Akita 2) "Nai no Kami" Naoto Kangyo 3) Rick Taylor 4) Kenji Okano 5) Corey Hart 6) Tasogare no Prince 7) Iron Chef Keiji Kobe 8) Shinji Nakata 9) Yuzo Moriboshi ---- [SCENE: A bathhouse. The traditional kind. Calm, relaxing, soothing... [Stepping from the water (at a conspicuous camera angle, keeping above the waist) stands a large man, some would guess a rikishi. And they would be partially right. He used to be one, in his younger days. Now, as he has for over 20 years, he is a pro wrestler. And not that this is anything to be trifled with, oh no, far from it. [He walks to the ledge where a towel and his robe lays. He dries himself off. Moving his hair back with his hands, he then dons his robe, a kimono more like. He slips his feet into sandals, and walks out into another room. Tea and noodles await him at a table. He sits...kneels, more like, and takes a drink.] There are many things to be sorry about. [We know the face, and the voice. It is that of the J1 Rikidozan/Neo Japan Pro Wrestling Unified Heavyweight Champion. This is Masashi Akita. MASSIVE Akita...] I am sorry for the injury that has sidelined my ancient rival...and friend, Jack Shaw. I am sorry that because of it, we cannot fight one more time. That it will be delayed. That it may not be for the Double Titles when we do finally clash. I am sorry for the shame my friend, Tokaio, must feel, tho it was no more intentional as spilling a glass of water. Surely, Tokaio's intention was to win. Not to destroy. And I am sorry for the strife that my newest challenger, Rick Taylor, is suffering at the moment. The choices one must make in order to attain glory....or happiness. I am sorry for the fact that three different persons have been chosen to face me in the Royal Series, and the circumstances behind those choices. Ryo Inoue, he challenged me like a man, a true fighter...and retracted the challenge in a press conference. Fine. In his time, he may choose to fight me. Call it not cowardice, call it "picking his spot." I care not. If I am to face him for the Double Titles, then I shall. If it is to be a mere match, so be it. Jack Shaw, my friend and longtime rival, he is then chosen, to great fanfare. Then...he is injured, and cannot fight. I'm sure Shaw would have chosen to fight, but he instead listened to the doctors. Wise, at his age. Wisdom of one who has seen much in his time, and knows "the score" when it comes to being hurt...or worse. I am sure we shall fight again. But in the present, I am to fight Rick Taylor. The questions arise. Is he ready? Is he worthy? Has he come so far too soon? Allow me to answer as it pertains to MASSIVE Akita... [A quick bite of noodles and a quick swig of tea, a wipe of the mouth...] Is Taylor ready? I do not know. He claims to be ready, sacrificing his family relations to be ready. Fine, if that is his choice. Akita is ready. Akita has been ready since the time Ryo Inoue, and Jack Shaw, were the challengers to the titles. I am the champion. It is my duty to be prepared to face a challenge at all times. No matter who it is that is challenging. In times of being both champion and challenger, I have never been unprepared, not in 25 years. I do not plan to start being unprepared at Ryogoku. Is Taylor worthy? Yamato-san thinks so, if he has placed him in this match. Inoue-san thinks otherwise. The only true test of worthiness in Akita's eyes, is how well he does against me. That is the only way we can see. Has he come so far too soon? I will not dignify this with a response. My views would be biased - having been in the ring for so long, it is not my place to say, without sounding as if I am a bitter or grumpy veteran. Everything comes in its proper time. However, it is the combination of these three questions, that will provide the total answer. Akita is ready. I am ready to defend my titles, risking injury and my career every time I step between the ropes. I am ready to get past the prior challenges of Ryo Inoue, and the misfortunes of Jack Shaw, in order to face you, Taylor. You have one victory against me, and it was some time ago. Since that time, you have worked yourself ragged. Sheer brutality against Ryo Inoue. The Alliance, with yourself in the role that perhaps Shaw was to fill, whether he wanted to or not, symbolically. You marriage, which is not something that is my place to comment about. However, surely these will all factor in to how you perform against me, Taylor. To think otherwise is foolish; to deny that these factors weigh down your mind and body is naļve. If you think this...prepare for some lessons. Lessons in what it means to be a champion. In what it means to be a MAN. [More tea.] I will leave with this, Taylor. Be Rick Taylor. Fight for Jack Shaw, but do not *be* Jack Shaw. For I know what that is better than you could possibly ever know. And I will be Akita, for that is all that I am. Beyond the Double Titles champion, beyond the accolades, I am merely Masashi Akita. And I have no aspirations otherwise. The time for those...has past. You have to remember that my sensei when I was but a young boy was the gaijin Rick Williams, whom I'm sure you all remember from his days of touring Japan. Naoto Kangyo: Matsuoka-san is, needless to say, going to be a great champion. He was his first time when he held the title, and he has already started down that path now for the second time. [Kangyo lets out another small laugh, which is quickly joined in by the press at ringside.] Jinsei Yamaguchi: Mr. Kangyo, any comment on the recent article by Hiroki Hyodo where he accuses you of selling out the mixed martial arts/no holds barred style in favor of Puroresu? Naoto Kangyo: I was sorry to see that article, to be honest. And not just because it involved dragging my name through the mud. I was sorry to see that Hyodo-san was so quick to make a judgment on my reasoning for leaving the MMA/NHB world for JPA. I had always considered him to be a fair and objective journalist, but that piece proved otherwise. Hyodo-san took a completely unjust approach to professional wrestling, condemning it without really having much knowledge of it in the first place. And, to imply that the athletes of Puroresu are not what the athletes of pancrase are is just absurd. These men whom I will soon call my brothers are on the road for nearly half the year, risking their bodies and even lives everyday. As you can witness by Jack Shaw's unfortunate injury, or Ryo Inoue and Rick Taylor's respective conditions following their contest, what happens here in the JPA is just as "real" as what happens anywhere else in the athletic world. Jinsei Yamaguchi: And what about his theory that you left Fight League following your loss to Matsuoka because you were a coward, unable to face your own failure? Naoto Kangyo: As I said before, Mr. Hyodo really should have done more research on his behalf. I was taught in the ways of catch-wrestling - a style that evolved into Puroresu - by a Puroresu combatant. It was only a matter of time, I've always felt, before I ended up in the world of Puroresu as an active wrestler myself. I guess you could say that my career has evolved along the same lines as my fighting style. Hyodo-san was to quick to judge and, in my opinion, his article was founded more on bitterness than it was on facts. While I may not hate my opponents, to say that this isn't something I will fight for as fiercely as ever would be wrong.
