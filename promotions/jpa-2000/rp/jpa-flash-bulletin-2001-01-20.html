JPA FLASH BULLETIN 2001.1.20

20 January 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
JAPAN PURORESU ASSOCIATION FLASH BULLETIN 2001.1.20 1) Corey Hart 2) Jeremy Knyte 3) Iron Chef Keiji Kobe 4) Rick Taylor 5) XYZ 6) MASSIVE Akita 7) Kenji Okano 8) Shinji Nakata ---- (The scene opens backstage at The Kawasaki Gymnasium. More specifically, we find ourselves in the dressing room of "Captivating" Corey Hart, following his tag match with We Hyun Bae against the team of XYZ and EIYUU. Corey is freshly showered and changed, clad in a white, "JPA" T-shirt and blue, nylon, sweat pants. The handsome youth completes the outfit with Nike, cross trainers. He quickly ties his shoe laces before grabbing his nearby gym bag and exiting the room. Just as he closes the door behind him, he is greeted by a JPA reporter and camera crew.) Reporter: Hart-san, we would like to have a few words with you about your performances on the Coronation Tour. Corey: (grins) Sure. That would be cool. Reporter: So far, the results of your matches have been quite mixed. You managed to defeat Shiro Oodora in singles competition and helped win a six man tag with your partners and stablemates, We Hyun Bae and Keiji Kobe. Yet, you lost a match to Billy Fairchild, a few days ago, and dropped a tag match against the duo of EIYUU and XYZ. What is your take on all of this? Corey: Well, despite the two recent losses to my record, I am quite pleased with the progress that I am making here in the JPA. I'm still a rookie in this sport and look at every win and loss as a learning experience. True, I would prefer to win. Let's face it, nobody wants to lose but I realize that it is inevitable. The most important thing is that I have tried my best in each encounter and worked as hard as possible. One of the things that I'm most proud of is the fact that I have been able to adapt so well to the JPA style. Things are much more strenuous here than in the States. When I wrestle in the states, it may only be once or twice a week and the matches usually run between 5 and 8 minutes long, just enough for a few high spots and what not. Here, we wrestle almost five days straight and you seldom see a match that goes below 15 minutes. Plus, there is a great amount of emphasis on what we do in the ring as opposed to a great number of skits or comedy routines, as is frequent in many American feds. I certainly welcome the difference and the challenge. Reporter: Since you have now faced both Japanese and gaijin wrestlers in the JPA, do you feel that you work better with one over the other? Corey: Actually, I find that I work well with both. One of the things that I've noticed while wrestling for the JPA is that the American wrestlers are just as serious and dedicated as the Japanese wrestlers. If we weren't, we wouldn't be in the JPA in the first place. I learned as much from Billy Fairchild as I did EIYUU or Oodora. The big difference isn't between the American and Japanese wrestlers in the JPA but rather between the American wrestlers here and the American wrestlers competing in the states. Since I wrestle in the states part time, I can tell you that a wrestler like Billy Fairchild has more to offer this profession that many of the "superstars" in those big, American feds. Reporter: You seem to be a big fan of Fairchild-san. Corey: (nods) Yeah, I think Billy is really cool. Besides We Hyun and Keiji, I would say that he is one of the guys that I enjoy working with the most. Maybe it's the fact that we're both rookies, both Americans, or around the same age, although he has me by one year. Either way, he and I get along pretty well with one another. I personally think that he's going to be one of the greats in this sport. (grins) Plus, I like working with him because he's one of the few people on the roster that I can lift without much effort. Us small guys gotta stick together. Reporter: Speaking of Billy Fairchild, you'll be facing his friend and some time tag partner, Jason Ramsey, at the next JPA Television taping. Do you have any words for Ramsey-san? Corey: Man, I think Jason is a phenomenal athlete! I haven't had much opportunity to work with him but I am a big fan of his. In fact, I often watch tapes of his matches, so I am already quite familiar with his style. I knew that he and I would eventually face one another but I didn't figure that it would be so early on the tour. Reporter: You mention watching tapes of his work. Has that helped you in performing a strategy? Corey: No doubt. Since I'm the smallest wrestler on the roster, I usually stick to an aerial assault, trying to keep away from my larger opponents. But with Ramsey, I'll also have to be on the look out for his technical skill and stamina. Man, I've seen him go twenty-four minute, wrestling bouts without even breaking a sweat! So, I will definitely have to pace myself. I predict that this will be one of my harder matches but I'm confident that I'll be able to hold my own. I can't assure a win but I can definitely try my hardest. I know Jason will be doing the same and I wish him nothing but luck. Reporter: What about the rest of this tour? Are there any wrestlers that you are looking forward to facing, in particular? Corey: All of them! (smiles) Actually, I can't wait to face XYZ in single's competition. He pinned me in my tag match tonight and I would love to return the favor. I think he's a great wrestler but his attitude just plain sucks. He's just like EIYUU. I just can't stand to hear their prejudiced views on how all American wrestlers are untalented and beneath them. Well, they may have won the match tonight but I certainly showed them that I'm no one's pushover or no-talent. True, XYZ pinned me but he had a heck of a hard time doing it. I heard his last interview and it made my blood boil. I would love to be the one to finally teach him a lesson in humility. Reporter: Are there any last words, Hart-san? Corey: Well, I would like to take the time to thank all of the fans for their support. Since arriving in Japan, everyone has been really welcoming and accommodating. They've truly made this place feel like a second home. (looks into the camera and flashes a thumbs up) And Keiji, keep up the good work. You're going to win the big one, man. I can feel it. (Fade.) ---- {Red Flash.} Voice: Pain means nothing to me. {Black.} Voice: Loses mean little to me. {White Flash.} Voice: Titles mean nothing. {Black.} Voice: For I am a GOD! {Blue Flash.} Voice: As I am Jeremy Knyte! {Fade in.} {We are in a darkened room. A floodlight hangs from above. It provides barely enough light to show the well muscled chest of Jeremy Knyte. He chuckles for a few moments and then he begins to speak in a quiet yet disturbing tone.] Knyte: Praise be to your gods, for some of you have gotten the better of me. Times are about to change children, and the idol of American men, and the wet dream of American women is about to change all that. I am about to change my luck and do what I do best, break people. You may all think you are stars, but Jeremy Knyte has seen many things and it takes more then a few loses to de rail this train. JPA is soon to discover what an American can do when motivated. Your petty honor and morals only inhibit your abilities to become great wrestlers, like myself. Not only do I have power and skill, but I am also skilled in many other areas and my mind, isn't always here. It's a shame too, cause your only going to get hurt. {Knyte again chuckles.} Knyte: You see children, class is in session. I am going to teach you, that wrestling isn't your thing. Your legs will be broken. Your arms will be crushed. Your neck snapped, and your hearts broken. Then after you have suffered so much, you will go home and see me partaking of your significant others womanhood. Defiling your flowers and doing the thing you could never do... Please them. {Knyte begins to laugh like a comic book villain. Only to stop dead. He leans to the camera, his handsome face distorted by the lack of light and the demonic glare in his eyes. He speaks in a higher tone filled with a combination of ecstasy and rage.} Knyte: Oh the horror. The American scum deflowers your women, and cums and cums again as she moans in the passion you could not provide. Sick isn't it. You thought you were in it for the sport, you though you were in it for the pleasure of the victory, but I will destroy you. And honor will demand for you to commit suicide. I will kill you, I will massacre the young put forth before me. I am like the A-Bomb, I am an American evil let loose to teach you your place. That place is serving the more powerful and intelligent Americans. {Knyte turns and we hear a click the lights turn on and Knyte runs his hands through his short and clean cut hair. He wears black sweats and white sneakers. The room is a locker room, with red blood like writing on the wall. It reads ANARCHY! Knyte begins to laugh as he smashes his hand into the lockers. The harder he hits the louder he laughs. The sickening thud finally stops and he turns back to the camera. He stares at it for a few moments before continuing.} Knyte: But their is another American I wish to speak of. Jason Ramsey. {Long pause.} Knyte: Hopefully we will meet in the ring again. The last time in the Pacific Northwest was a wash out. And I would like to show the Japanese fans of the old DWA why I will always be superior to you. Though you have come far from being Taco Bell's whipping boy. This time though you won't have any excuses, this time it would stay in the ring. Of course that is if you want to risk your career in the Knytemare. Or you can just admit me as the better. I always was, and always will be. {Knyte laughs as the scene fades out.} ---- (The camera opens up onto the Iron Chef, Keiji Kobe, dressed in nice slacks and a fairly nice JPA polo shirt. He is in his trademark mask, even though it is clear he's in a religious shrine of some sort. He places a offering of food before a shrine of well known family. However, they is not the Kobe family that the food is set before. It seems the offering has been made to the Inoue family shrine.) Kobe: I'm making this offering for you, Ryo, because I know you forget the most important things in life. I make this offering in the hopes your family will care for you at Shizuoka Shin Messe, Ryo, because you lack the ability to keep yourself safe. Hopefully your ancestors do not want to see the family line die out with you, and will watch over you, because I will destroy you if they don't. I know, you must think I'm mad now, after my lack luster performance as of late, and my unorthodox warning to you. You, why you must be feeling pretty good about yourself, Ryo. After all, in 30 minutes I failed to beat you. And you know full well that I am at my best at the twenty minute mark. On the 16th I had my first, and best, chance of beating you, but I failed, so what hope do I have? You're the great shooter, and I'm just a joke from Mexico, right? Wrong, I am no joke, you are. You style is a crock. You teacher, a moron, with less intelligence than a cow in the field, for he failed to teach you the most important thing in fighting. And you... You're the poorest excuse for a fighter I've ever seen. As a tactician, you're without equal. You not only know thousands of take downs, submissions, and strikes, but your execution of them is without equal. Physically, you're incredible. You've got size, strength, and a surprising amount of speed. Still, you aren't even worthy of being a fighter. Your flaws are many, I don't think I have the time to list them all, but I can tell you your chief flaw. You lack a mind. You've dedicated your life to the practice of martial arts. You've learned all kinds of secret techniques and ploys. You've sculpted your body to peak physical conditioning. You've prepared you body to endure massive amounts of pain. You're able lift an incredible amount of weight. But in all your years of study, all your years of training you forgot to develop something. You forgot to develop the most important weapon of any warrior, not his sword, staff, or body. You forgot to develop your mind. You're grown man in body, but mentally you're an infant. Perhaps it is because I'm a father that I pity you, so I will not only give you some intellectual food I will try to grind it into a form even your underdeveloped Pro Magnum mind can comprehend. You're fighting is like a craftsman who is given a set of tools of excellent quality, plenty of materials, but no blue print. Left to your own devices you'll create a work of stunning beauty, no doubt, but will it be what is needed? Will the work your craft be what is needed to complete the task, given your very limited knowledge of me and my style? You don't know, Ryo, you don't know because you are a mental midget. You don't know because you're a moron. But I do, Ryo... I DO! I know exactly what it takes to get the job done, because I not only have the blue print, Ryo, I WROTE THE DAMN THING! You're a simple craftsman, skilled yes, but at the end of the day you're just a labor. I'm an architect. I'm a designer. I'm a visionary You spend your lifetime thinking about assembling boxes. I work outside the box. Do you understand, yet, you Ox brained imbecile? No, you moron? Well let me try to explain it to you again. You are like that snaggle toothed old man I got to play your master. You a blind, deaf, and crippled man I put on screen last segment who swings blindly and madly hopping that his foes will walk into harms way. Any man with a ounce of common sense could avoid your attacks, and a genius like myself will have no trouble countering, reversing it, and subduing you. Yes, I'll freely admit I didn't beat you the first time we met, but you couldn't beat me either. Oh, I know what the media is saying, that I am destine to lose to you. They point to the fact you held me in your reverse STF for the last two minutes of our match. They all say the bell saved me from the humiliation of loosing to a dunderheaded fool. The critics will say I knew the end of the match was coming like there was a clock in front of me and I just tried to hold out for the last few seconds. I put those critics on the same level of development as imbeciles like you, Ryo, and your average sea invertebrate. I didn't hold on for nearly three minutes of gut wrenching agony in the hopes of a draw. If a draw was the best I though I could manage I would of tapped and saved my energy for another day. I didn't tap because I knew that given thirty more seconds I would of reversed your damn hold and had you crying like a little school girl. While the record books might have it recorded that our match was a draw, I gained a great victory. I might of only held my own against you in our match, but I beat you like a lame mule in the aftermath of our epic struggle. While you offered me your hand as a sportsman in acknowledgment of my effort, I offered you five more minutes to finish our battle so the fans in attendance could see the finish they deserved. I accepted your hand, but you ran away from my challenge like a scared puppy. I shouldn't fault you, why should I expect a man who allows his headband to do the majority of his thinking for him to understand the concept of honor? But even you, a man who couldn't figure out how to open a soda can without a diagram, could see you were outclassed that night and so you knew fear. Despite all your training in the pure fight, your true fighting grace fell short against my joke of a style Lucha and you got scared. Your skill of technique in fighting had been successful in almost every other outing against "real" wrestlers, but you couldn't put me, the joke Luchadore away. You couldn't beat me, the small fly on the wall. Still, you're not quite sure why are you? You have no idea why you couldn't put me down do you? And that scares you, Ryo. You're begin to lose confidence in your abilities. You're going over that entire match looking for the flaw in your technique, and you can't find it. You're pulling your hair out at the ends because you should of won by everyone's count but you lost. I'll tell you why you lost, rather failed to win, as if there was a difference. You didn't win because you thought the difference between our style was only Shooto vs Lucha. You're trying to win by points when this isn't a amateur event. This isn't your high school gym, this isn't a mixed martial arts tournament, this isn't boxing, we don't go to score cards after ten rounds. This is professional wrestling. You don't win by points here, you win by pinning the man's shoulders to the mat, or by getting a submission. You couldn't recognize the goal, so how could you achieve it. You might pay lip service to the fact you know you're after a win over me, or a chance to win the twin title belts, but you forget that once the bell rings. You mind is that of a beast once the bell ring, and I can see that in your eyes, your passion is great, but the instinct you use is no substitute for intelligence. What I'm getting at here, Ryo, is the real difference between us. The difference isn't the fact you're a shooter and I'm Luchadore. The difference isn't that you're a Heavy weight and I'm a middle weight. The difference is you're a tactician, a simple grunt, and I'm a strategist, a general. You'll always win on points, but I'll always win the end, because I understand strategy. I've mastered the orthodox and the unorthodox. I see the near and the far. I am capable to traveling a thousand li with out growing tired, because I know the path to victory. Technique is but one small leaf in the forest that is fighting. I've lived in this forest all my life, I know this world like the inside of my mask, and all you know is a single leaf. My understanding of strategy was enough to counter all your strength, all your tactics, and you know that to be the truth. You know I can, and will beat you given the time to due it in. You saw and felt my presence in the ring that night at Kawasaki Gymnasium. I wasn't growing weaker as the match progressed, I got stronger. The more you fought, the more I understood you. The more I understood you, the less capable you became. This pattern will continue into Shizuoka Shin Messe. It will continue until you reach a point where you are as physically helpless as you are mentally. In that moment, I will win, and I will move on to the finals. That I am sure of. Do you know the funniest part of the joke that is your life, Ryo? After all my examples, all my stories, you still don't get it. You still don't understand the difference between tactics and strategy. And you'll be come to our match at Shizuoka Shin Messe, swinging your arms around swatting at flies like that old man I brought out as your "teacher," and you'll lose. But as I counter your every move. As I break you down and tear you apart. Slowly, you'll come to understand what I've said. You'll see I was right, and you'll finally know that I am the Invincible man of culinary skill, I am the Iron Chef, and you'll never break me. (The shot fades to white, with the phrase "True Flavor" written in Japanese appearing on the screen.) ---- [Night. Rick Taylor is standing outside the Shizuoka Shin Messe. He is wearing blue jeans, boots, a JPA t-shirt, and a leather jacket. He looks at the camera and he begins to speak.] Rick: First and foremost, I wish to extend my congratulations to my oppoent in the Coronation tournament; Inoue-san, you beat me, not an easy task, and I think you will agree that we had a hell of a match and not one of the fans in the Differ Ariake were disappointed, nor will the thousands more who will see it on Samurai TV. I have no shame in my defeat, because you just happened to be the better man that night, Inoue-san. Congratulations, and best of luck from here on out in the tournament. [Rick bows in a gesture of respect to Inoue, should he be watching.] Rick: Now you have to contend with my friend Kobe, and he is no pushover either. Good luck to both of you. Rick: Well, I am no longer in the Coronation tournament. I am a little disappointed, but I have no doubt that I left more than a significant impression on our promoters, and I'm sure that I can earn a title shot after the tournament is over in the near future. But the task at hand is this: Tora Ryoku and I team against an occasional partner of mine, Jack Shaw and the Great Tokaio. Ryoku, I have never partnered with you before, but I have wrestled you before. In fact, I pinned Massive Akita in a tag match with Shaw just a few days ago. Actually, I've won all of my matches since my loss to Inoue. They were tag and 6 man matches, but I got the pin. Ryoku, I hold no ill will to you. Shaw is a partner of mine, but he will be our opponent this night. I have intesified in my desire to win and keep winning here in the JPA and this will not be any less significant now. JAck, you and I are acquaintances and pretty good tag team partners, but I will hold nothing back against you this night, just as I expect you to hold nothing back from me. I'm going for the win, whether it be myself or Ryoku that scores the fall! Great Tokaio, you were an opponent in that 6 man, so I know your style a bit. You and I have different styles, so when you and I are in the ring, it will be interesting, but Jack and I wrestle similarly, so he and I know what to expect from each other more or less, and it will be some brutal wrestling at its best. Ryoku, I will work with you as best as I can. I look forward to this match. Let's prove that our losses were not typical, let's prove that we can step it up to the next level. I'm willing and ready. Let's go into this match in this building, and tear the roof off this bastard! Let's give those fans a match that they'll talk about for months, maybe even years! I'm willing and ready to just that! All of the rest of you should be as well. There's no animosity, here, just a special challenge, so let's just have the best match possible! If we steal the show, well, that's just another ace up our sleeves. See you all soon. [Fade to black.] ---- [Open camera shot to a simple locker room. You turn your attention away from the lockers, away from the sweaty, small Japanese men changing and follows the scene, wondering where it is taking you. As the camera shifts position in seek of another, you hear the familiar voice of XYZ.] Voice of XYZ: Justin Hart…. [A clip flashes by of XYZ pinning Public Access Wrestling's, an American promotion in which XYZ starred, Justin Hart to the mat for the victory. The clip ends as the camera is meandering back through various hallways and doors as the voice is heard again.] Voice of XYZ: We Hyun Bae…. [A clip flashes by of XYZ laying out We Hyun Bae on January 13th with a Death Valley Bomb.] Voice of XYZ: Cory Hart…. [A clip flashes by of XYZ executing a cradle pile driver on Corey Hart just last Thursday night.] Voice of XYZ: The next one shall be Billy Fairchild…. [A clip flashes by of XYZ hitting a punching bag with various rights, lefts, and kicks. The bag is swaying hard left and right as the camera cuts back from the footage and to a room. Where you see XYZ standing in an empty room, except for training tools all around. He is standing next to a light with a string coming off it. His one hand rests on the string as he smiles scornfully.] XYZ: Or perhaps, even…. [A clip flashes by of XYZ standing over a fallen wrestler as he laughs, the scorn leaving his face.] XYZ: Shira Oodora! But no matter which one of you falls, it'll still mean [XYZ pulls the string and the room falls into utter darkness.] LIGHTS OUT! [Fade out.] ---- [A standard Japanese wrestling type interview. Taken before a show, or before a match to be exact. Masashi "MASSIVE" Akita, hair pulled back in the sumo style, bare chested under his large robe, sits patiently, quietly, reflecting on his past match with Jack Shaw…and his upcoming one against SHOICHI 13…] Ah, hai. I'm still feeling the effects of my battle with Shaw-san last week in Tokyo. When you are one such as my age, still getting into the ring to fight, you tend to feel pain quite a bit. Shaw and I have fought constantly, persistently, ever since the old days, in United Japan Pro Wrestling, and it seems we will continue to do so here in Japan Puroresu Association. [Sits silent, as the interviewer asks his question, which we can barely hear] No, I don't regret being Shaw's nemesis through the years. I do regret any pain I've caused him that he still feels today, however such is the nature of battle. And it is the real fighters who continue to do so, fight the glorious fight, working through the pain with each rising of the sun… [Question again] Hai. SHOICHI….SHOICHI is a fine competitor. I feel honored to having to face him. And I should hope he does as well. He is one of those.. I feel can carry the mantle that myself, Shaw, and all the others through the years have carried all this time, when we are done. However, if Shaw is to be believed, his time is not quite done yet. And neither is mine. [Another one] No, no I don't feel I'm disadvantaged by SHOICHI's…advancedness. At all. With all his skill, he still has a deficit of something I have an abundance in, and that is experience. Firsthand knowledge of fights, for … over 20 years now. There are things I can teach him, just as he will teach me this Sunday night. I am not one to dote on what my disadvantages are, merely one to work through them, and somehow, still come out as the victor. Just as I did with Shaw, and hopefully, if the powers are behind me, hopefully do so against either Kobe or Inoue for the Double Titles. Mark my words, Akita will be in the final fight for the Double Titles on the third of February. I have every faith. [Interviewer says quick thanks, and Akita bows in his seat, as we fade….] ---- [scene: a wide shot of a desolate alley somewhere in Tokyo. The overall feeling is dark and ominous. Suddenly, we find Kenji Okano, dressed in training/warm-up gear and wearing headphones, jogging towards the camera.] Okano [slightly winded]: Hey... [at this point the production assistant notices a bruise on Okano's cheek.] Prod. asst: Mr. Okano, what happened? Okano [pointing to the contusion]: This? [smiles] A group of punks tried to attack me. I took them out with ease, just like I've been doing with all my opponents so far in the JPA. Prod. asst: But... [Okano takes a drink from a water bottle, then calmly looks towards the production w/an arrogant smile.] Okano: But? Sure, I may have lost in that tag match, but that was obviously not my fault. Was I pinned? No! So, I'm still undefeated. Anyway, I had to knock some sense into Kobe. Maybe now he'll realize that being a part of the Stupid Gaijin Squad is a waste of time! [Okano pauses to look at his watch.] Okano: Speaking of which, this has wasted enough of mine. [Okano smiles and flashes a peace sign, turns it into the finger, and resumes jogging, shoving the cameraman out of the way as he departs.] ---- [scene: a wrestling ring in the center of a well furnished gymnasium. Shinji Nakata is sitting on the top rope, wearing his familiar ring robe. The camera cuts to a closeup of his face, with footage of his debut matches in the JPA interspersed.] Nakata: As much as the fighting spirit burns within, now I feel a sense of calm content. [footage of Naoki Nakata in action is shown] Nakata: I knew right away, as soon as I stepped into the JPA rings...this is where I belong. Carrying on the glorious tradition of puroresu.
