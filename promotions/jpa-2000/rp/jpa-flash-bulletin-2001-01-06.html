JPA FLASH BULLETIN 2001.1.6

6 January 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
JAPAN PURORESU ASSOCIATION FLASH BULLETIN 2001.1.6 1) Iron Chef Keiji Kobe 2) Rick Taylor 3) XYZ 4) Ryo Inoue --- (The camera opens up onto the outside of a public bath house late at night in a some what run down part of Tokyo on the outskirts of the city. The lights of neon that seem to be everywhere else in the city are absent here, and only a simple flood light illuminates the sign telling of the bath house. A man, large for Japanese, but small for a wrestler, climbs up the three steps to the bath house, carrying an invisible burden. The man is instantly recognizable because his disguise is his identity in most circles. The man is the Iron Chef, and the burden he carries can not be seen because it resides in his mind. Upon entering the bath house, Kobe signs in and then heads to the men's lockers. There behind a half wall, Kobe strips, head still visible, until the sound of running water can be heard and Kobe squats down. Seconds later the mask is seen being laid upon the half wall as Kobe goes about cleaning himself. Steam fills the room and clouds the lens of the camera for a second. When the steam is wiped off the viewer can now see there was a change in time. Kobe is once again in his mask, and is laying rather comfortably inside a public bath tube. The room is clean, but the color scheme is out of date, since this bath house was built in the 1970's. There are a few cracked tiles among the mosaic the covers the back wall. Viewers can see Kobe's chest is bright red, evidence of his having wrestled despite lack of cards in the JPA. And though his body seems to be more comfortable you can look into his eyes and see something is on his mind.) Kobe: There is an old proverb, so worn and tired it lost all meaning to me until this night, the proverb is: "Be careful of what you wish for, you just might get it." I asked, no begged, to be allowed into the tournament for the JPA Heavy-weight crown. I asked our beloved president to give me a shot of a life time, put me in this tournament so I my show my worth to this company, and he in his infinite benevolence has granted my request. Now I stand the smallest man in a tournament composed of some of the world's top athletes and wrestlers. SHOICHI 13, the former Neo-J Heavy-weight champion. Jack Shaw, a living legend, stronger than an ox, wiser than a monk. Rick Taylor, my old friend and rival from Royal Japan, a champion the world over. Ryo Inoue, surely the greatest pure fighter alive today. And yet this is only half of this incredible tournament. How then can I, a simple boy from Ebetsu, hope to compete with such men? Why would I want to face such long odds? I am told the book makers on the streets say I shall not last five minutes with Ryoku. The why is not so hard to figure out, the how is still a mystery. The reason why I sought out this battle is because I seek perfection. The battles I will endure will serve to shape my soul and sharpen my inter blade. By facing these, admittedly impossible, odds I will teach my body new limits, and expand my mind as to new possibilities and approaches. But the perfection I seek is not limited to myself. I seek the prefect company as well. Our company has seen wrestlers come and go, and has experienced already highs and lows. I seek to ensure there will always be more highs than lows. I told our beloved president that if I was allowed into this tournament I would not only show case myself at it's best, but bring out the best in my foes as well. I'm sure Ryoku and myself will have a spirit contest, and the best man will go on to the next round. As for the how I plan to win this tournament, well that is still a mystery, I have a vague recipe in my mind, but there is long road ahead of me before I'm ready to serve. To be sure, I began planning for this tournament before I was ever in it. During our hiatus I traveled to my adopted homeland, Mexico, once again, and tried my hardest to involve myself in as many matches as humanly possible knowing that one minute in a real match is worth a day in the gym. I traveled to America and competed there in a tour expand my knowledge of other styles and test my skills against the world. One of my stops was in Texas, where I met XYZ, who I like to think I influenced slightly to come to this great company and show the same skill he displayed in Texas when I was there. And since returning with Hart-san to my home land I've been working non-stop in the gym with him and We Hyun Bae to reach yet hirer levels of training. Still, no amount of training could allow my small frame to match strength with a Jack Shaw or a MASSIVE Akita and my technique is not as refined as a Ryo Inoue or a Tora Ryoku. To succeed I must bring something to the game other than size or skill, and that something is spirit. Tora Ryoku, your head dropping style is feared by many, your skill in submissions allow you to squeeze harder than a boa constrictor, but I am not out of the game. Though small in body, I am a giant in terms of fighting spirit. And while you may break my body, my spirit is forged of iron, not even your deadly back drop driver can break it. Skilled as you are at attacking my body, there is no way you can attack my spirit. And it is not as if I shall be alone in my battle against you. Already, my comrades in Kichigai no Gaikokujin, have promised to second me in this, the biggest match of my life, and the loyal fans the JPA will serve to strengthen my resolve. But there is one friend and mentor who will follow me into battle against you into the very ring itself. It is this (pointing to his head), my mask. Just as my ancestors centuries ago carried swords as a tangible artifact of their soul, so I carry mine soul on my head. This mask has seen me into and out of my battles. Some were losses, many were victories, but in all the time I had it not once did it fail me. This mask has taken every bump I ever did. This mask has been there to soak up my blood and my sweat, and provided me with a clear window into my match. You do a great disservice to yourself when you remove yours before battle, Ryoku. My mask will be ready to aid me in our coming battle against you, Ryoku. When you drop me on my head, my mask will be there first to catch me and soften the blow. When you kick me in the face you'll see it has no effect, because despite all appearances of being cloth, my mask is a hardened iron shell which protects me. When you put me on the mat my mask will be there in my ear telling me get up, urging me to continue. You see, Ryoku, I wear a mask that is already a champion. I must merely show myself worthy of it's aid in our battle, and you will be defeated. My mask knows nothing of defeat, nothing of surrender, nothing of pain. All it knows is victory, all it seeks is battle. This is not a question of can you match it's power, but rather can I used it's full potential. January 13th, Tokyo Differ Ariake, we find out. Good luck, you shall need it. (As Kobe finishes his speech, the invisible burden seems to have lifted. His mind is now at ease. The shot fades to white, and black Japanese characters meaning "True Flavor" appear on the screen.) ---- [Night. Rick Taylor is standing outside the Tokyo Differ Ariake. He is wearing blue jeans, boots, a JPA t-shirt, and a leather jacket. He looks at the camera and he begins to speak.] Taylor: Finally, the JPA has announced the dates for the long-awaited CORONATION tour, but more importantly, the brackets for the J1 Rikidozan/Neo Japan Unified Heavyweight title tournament. I had a friend, a man by the name of Toshiaki Takada, who wrestled for Neo Japan at the same time that our own Keiji Kobe, another friend of mine, wrestled for them. I was fullfilling some of my final commitments in the States before coming to Japan for one last tour with Royal Japan, but I spoke with Toshiaki and he was one of the top contenders for those belts. To my understanding, the J1 Rikidozan and Neo Japan Heavyweight title both have some storied histories, both together and as seperate entities. It is a great honor to be chosen to compete in this exclusive tournament, and possibly to represent the JPA as the first to wear the Unified titles under the JPA banner. In this tournament I may face some rivals, as well as some friends. However, friendships and rivalries are thrown out the window when it comes to this tournament. Should I get to the finals, I might have to fight Kobe and Jack Shaw on my way to becoming the champion. Or I might have to fight Tora Ryoku and MASSIVE Akita to become the champion. I do not care one way or the other who I may have to face down to road. Right now, I am enjoying knowing that I have been regarded as being valuable enough, after only two shows to this company to be chosen to possibly represent it as our first champion. Which brings me to my first opponent in this tournament: RYO INOUE. Inoue, you are perhaps the finest shoot fighter we have in the JPA, I will not deny that, nor will anyone else. However, when we step into that ring, here at the Differ Ariake, which we are getting to know quite well by now, you and I will kick off the tournament with a bang. You with your pure shootfighting skills will try and defuse my strong style attack, and vice versa. Two very different styles, but I am no submissions slouch. I am no expert, but I am not as clueless as some of my contemporaries back home in the States. Inoue, you and I will no doubt have an excellent match, one certainly for the history books. I know that you will hold nothing back, but neither will I. When I get the adrenaline pumping, the groove in gear and zone is dangerously close, there is nothing you can do to hold me down. I am not an especially powerful man, but I am more than willing and certainluy able to take you one in the ring for as long as it takes to seize victory from you. If you want to try and wear me down, wear me out by slapping on the submissions and dragging the match out so I get tired, you'd better find another gameplan, Inoue. The fact is, I maybe in the best cardiovascular shape of anyone here in the JPA, and I can go as long as you want to go, my friend. You want to go 20 minutes? 30? 45 or 50? An HOUR, maybe? Bring it on, I say! This is no disrespect to you, Inoue, but I am no easy customer. This will be your most difficult, strenuous match so far here in the JPA. I am the first hurdle you must face in your quest for the Unified titles, but you are also my first challenge. I am more than prepared to answer this challenge and be successful in order to prove myself worthy of my place here in JPA. Inoue, I know that you will be no push over. But don't think that I am, either. I don't believe in fate, but I have a feeling about this match, Inoue, and it doesn't look good for you. In Royal Japan, we had a number of shooters just like you, Inoue. When I held the Pacific and Nippon titles, I never turned down a challenge, not even from them. I'm no submissions expert like you, Inoue, but you had better understand that I scored an impressive number of victories over those guys. Sure, they had be hurting at times, but when you have lived the life that I have, physical pain doesn't mean as much as it does to some people. And that's one advantage that I have over you, Inoue: I don't care about pain. You see these scars all over my chest and back? If look really close sometime at my arms, you can see raised scars that I had inked over because they screwed up my tattoos. Look at my forehead--it doesn't look like Shoji Nakamaki's, Dusty Rhodes's or Trazan Gotos's, but I've got some scars there. There was a time a couple of years ago when I was largely a garbage wrestler... I think we all go through PHASES. I don't wrestle in garbage matches anymore, but when I did, the physical pain didn't matter as much as it did to my opponents. But that's a whole different story that I mention to illustrate a point, Inoue; I'm not going to say that you can't hurt me, that's absurd. But even though you can hurt me, make me feel pain, I don't care about it. Oh I feel it, and there is no way that I in some sick fashion LIKE it, but I simpluy do not care. You can slap a step over toehold on me and I'll ride it out until you release it in frsutration or I pass out. I don't give people the satsifaction of making me quit. I do not quit. Yopu'll have to knock me out, Inoue before you can put me out. That said, I look forward to our match. And who knows? Maybe I might walk away from this tournament as the champion. But we'll leave the predictions to the psychics. [Fade out.] ---- [Click…] [As the camera fades in and the picture becomes clearer, you see a rough looking Japanese man sitting down at an airport terminal in Austin, Texas, the capital of the second largest state in the United States of America. A black leather suitcase sits next to him on the floor and another black carry on bag sits next to him on the seat. The man is sitting down to the windows, which allow you to view the runway and the giant jets as the take off/come in.] [The man is wearing a black leather jacket with a plain white shirt, along with a pair of tight blue jeans that most men in there forties enjoy wearing here in the states. The man is sitting back in his hard, plastic airport seat but properly. His elbows are relaxed on the armrests and his hands crossed in his lap. The man is moving his head slowly, taking in his surroundings. He shakes his head in pity for the Americans and then begins to speak in a deep, strong, forceful tone.] THE MYSTERIOUS MAN - Pathetic American airports, can never get a flight out on time, can never deliver ANYTHING on time so it seems…. So glad that I'll be returning to my home in a few days, so glad that I'll be treated with respect and wrestle some of the world's best. My stay in America has been nothing shorter than disappointing but, in turn, enlightening for it really opened my eyes and made me realize that America is not that great and that my country, Japan, is better educated and has more morals, ethics and manners than that of these lazy, egotistical Americans who pride themselves of winning and wrestling to only further themselves and their two-bit lackluster careers. The great sport of wrestling diminished to nothing here in the States, such a shame. But I guess in Japan things will change. [A smile comes over the face of this still unknown wrestler.] In Japan I will go back to true wrestling and true talent. I will be able to showcase my wrestling skills in front of the many, many wonderful Japanese fans expecting nothing shorter than the best. I am so grateful that Mister Genji Yamato-san is allowing me to return to such a prestigious wrestling promotion in my home land and that he is going to place before me only the best possible talent in Japan. I love the idea of having a decent opponent for you people do not know how hard it is to find one here in America. In my first match back from the States to the " Land of the Rising Sun" I get to face a man too big to be a great lightweight like myself but a man also too small to be a great heavyweight. I get to face a technical wizard with an aerial ability in We Hyun Bae, a great Japanese wrestler and a man that should give me… How do I say it…a great opportunity to showcase my talent here in Japan, I just hope the man is ready for such a challenge for I am not one to back down from a fight or give up when the going gets tough. [XYZ, the man facing We Hyun Bae, lifts his right arm to his chin and appears to be in deep thought as he continues.] I've done some research on you young Bae-san and I've discovered that you lack knowledge on how to wrestle and that you depend on the American style of using such things to ease a victory… called weapons. I'm disappointed that such a fine young wrestler with a great heritage of excellent superstars and heroes known all around the world that you resort to that level, to relying on other things to win a match. However, I must ask you Bae-san… Do you enjoy your scars more than the man that gave them to you? Do you pride yourself on having scars more than giving them out? You see Bae-san, you can be a tough, Japanese wrestler that has a lot of potential but if you lack actual knowledge of the sport you participate in, you'll go nowhere in such a decorated and beloved sport as wrestling. I wish you luck in our match Bae-san, I wish you luck down the road of your career and I do hope that you come to wrestle next Saturday Night and that you prepare for a great battle between two Japanese elite my friend or else you will suffer a bitter defeat at my hands and you will realize that we on the Eastern side of the world "kick" it a different way. [Sighs.] This America is changing my speech patterns, thank the dear Lord that I'm leaving in just a few hours if my flight is actually on time, which would be a giant shocker since I'm stuck in this rat invested, backcountry red neck ran airport with inadequate staff and rude customers! [Just then a voice is heard on the PA system, "Red-eye flight to Tokyo, Japan is now in. Please report to Gate C, loading dock 12." A smile comes to the face of XYZ as he gathers his stuff together and the camera fades out.] ---- [Open camera shot. The image on screen bleeds in, the camera is shooting from behind a man. The lighting is such that we only see the drakened back of the man before us as he stands backstage, behind the curtain leading to the runway] Voice of Ryo Inoue: People ask me questions all the time. When they discover that I make living through the art of the fight I'm always asked "Do you ever get scared?" [The footage continues ot roll, the man stands there] Voice of Ryo Inoue: I always respond with a smile "Do I look stupid? Everytime I get out there I'm terrified!" [The overdub is drowned out but the background noise can be heard as ring introductions are made and fans cheer. Still the man stands backstage] Voice of Ryo Inoue: Yes, I Ryo Inoue get scared. I stand behind the curtain before every fight wondering if I am the best. I stand there scared, knowing that I am going out there with the sole purpose of striking a man and using my body as weapon. [The footage still rolls. It is becoming apparent this is the Tokyo Dome and that by the markings on the ring robe the man standing is ryo Inoue] Voice of Ryo Inoue: I'm scared that I will embarass my family, my sensei and my school. I'm scared that maybe my father was right, maybe I was born to be nothing great and should be a salaryman. I'm scared that I will end up behind the desk in some office dreading my existence. [The footage still rolls] Voice of Ryo Inoue: I'm scared that the goal I have set for myself is too lofty. I get scared I will never have the J1 Rikidozan Double championship strapped around my waist. I'm scared that one day, without warning... the cheers will stop... [The footage rools, the curatin opens, Ryo Inoue walks into the spotlight and the fans erupt] Voice of Ryo Inoue: But once the curtain is parted and I'm on that runway looking to the ring... there is nothing that scares me. I know no fear, I cower before no one. I am Ryo inoue, I will be J1 Rikidozan champion. I am Ryo Inoue, I am scared of no man. I am Ryo Inoue... Master of The Pure Fight, I dare you to beat me...
