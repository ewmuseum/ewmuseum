JPA FLASH BULLETIN 2001.3.22

22 March 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
JAPAN PURORESU ASSOCIATION FLASH BULLETIN 2001.3.22 1) "Nai no Kami" Naoto Kangyo 2) Eiji Yatsu & Ryo Inoue 3) Jason Ramsey ---- "THE CASE FOR NO HOLDS BARRED FIGHTING IN JAPAN." [A lone figure sits before his desk; darkness abounds with the exception of the glow from a computer monitor and a few dim ceiling lights in the background. A few lines of text scroll across the computer screen.] "There was a time in Japan when 'No Holds Barred' fighting or, as it has later come to be known, 'Mixed Martial Arts,' was popularized throughout the country as a legitimate and brutal sport, filled with only the most physical of men, only the most dedicated of warriors." [The words come, one letter at a time, as the silhouetted figure slowly bangs them out from his keyboard. Every so often, another figure can be seen in the distance walking along or in front of the writer's desk, quite often holding a briefcase or other paraphernalia. The man, alone otherwise, rubs his eyes. The state of darkness in the room can only be assumed to imply that closing time has already passed, and this man - one of the few stragglers from a long workday - is now engaged in the enjoyable task known as overtime.] "Organizations such as 'RINGS', 'Kajiwara Gumi', 'UWH', and 'Fighting Pancrase Network' thrived in the Land of the Rising Sun, attracting crowds of hundreds, and often thousands, of people, each of which came to see these combatants fight for honor, dignity, glory, and most of all... respect." [A hand reaches back and runs through dark hair, as if reaching for the next words to put down. The only sounds audible, other than the occasional squeaking door or "cling"-ing elevator, remain this man and his words, coming still slowly, one at a time.] "But, as any modern day fan will tell you, those times have past. Then again, perhaps fan is not the correct word to use in this situation. Perhaps, and to say this brings to heart great sorrow... 'Historian' would be the superior choice of words to use. For, certainly, anyone who remembers those days of MMA and NHB fighting could be classified as one." [A nearby clock reads 11:38. The typing continues.] "Is the state of no holds barred or mixed martial arts fighting really that bad in Japan today? A dissertation could be written on that topic, and needless to say, the room for that is not permitted here. Instead, I offer you an example. I will let you, the reader, decide whether or not NHB/MMA fighting, once a dominating aspect of Japanese sport culture, is truly in such a state of decay as I describe it to you. Rather than tell you, I will show you." [One of the individuals proceeding past the writer taps his desk. As the newcomer departs, the writer takes a brief second to wave a hand in his direction.] "I offer you 'Nai no Kami' Naoto Kangyo. Some of my readers may have heard this name in the past. Then again, many of you have not, the reason for the most part being that even with only a few NHB/MMA groups remaining in this nation, they are still small, territorial, and quite often destined for failure within a few scant years. Naoto Kangyo had learned to call one of the larger ones out there, 'Japan Fight League', his home in the last dozen months. He had risen through the ranks, as any true star does, slowly but surely. He had suffered his fair share of losses, no doubt can be raised about that, yet, at the same time, he had tallied up a great amount of wins." [A cough. The author runs his hand over his mouth, letting out a yawn as he stretches his arms over his head. The clock now reads 11:48. Shaking his head, he continues.] "I myself remember reviewing him for this very same newspaper upon his arrival to 'Fight League.' My old readers will remember that I was not so pessimistic about the fate of my beloved sport then, for young stars like Kangyo gave me hope. Hope that this Japanese past time, the descendent of the Greeks, would continue to live on in the bosom of our land, despite the growing concern for its future." [The man darts his head in several directions, as if looking for something. Finally, with a quick jab of his left arm, he retrieves a paper, beaten and worn. Silently reading to himself, he continues.] "'Nai no Kami' Naoto Kangyo's arrival to 'Japan Fight League,' one of the prominent MMA organizations left in this country today, is a sign to me that the days of this sport are far from numbered. Naoto is everything that will help maintain the tradition and legacy of MMA fighting into the next millennium. Naoto is an athlete, that much is true. But, so are many other young men out there. What makes Naoto different? Why is Naoto, and the others like him I have seen since my arrival at the 'Fight League' training center, special compared to the other fighters or, dare I say, 'wrestlers' I have come across? He has a certain quality. One which, I frown to say, is not typically found in those who dominate this sport in today's age. Kangyo and his fellows have Heart. They have desire, pride, a hunger. They have honor. All of which are direct derivatives of Heart. I remember once when a gaijin trainer related to me that 'Heart is all that counts in a fighter. If a man has Heart, everything else will fall into place.'" "He is right of course. When you happen upon a find such as Naoto Kangyo, a young, fresh upstart in the world of MMA, you can't help but think to yourself of where he will be in a few scant years. He has Heart now. He only need perfect his skills. But, as that gaijin said to me, if he has Heart, he can accomplish anything." [The journalist casts the newspaper to the side, shaking his head as he does so.] "I wrote that over a year ago. Naoto represented the salvation of MMA/NHB fighting. Him, and a few others like him, would lead this sport to greatness." "But what happened?" [A deep breath, followed by a methodical tapping of his wooden desk are the next actions of the writer. With almost a feeling of disdain, he continues his article.] "Naoto is a living analogy to the crumbling of Japanese sport. He is a monument to the 'What Went Wrong' in society. But I am getting ahead of myself..." "As I predicted him to, Kangyo worked his way up the ranks of 'Fight League,' fighting consistently in each and every rally the group held. While he did not have a golden record, to call it sterling would not be far-fetched. Within a year after his arrival, Kangyo stood a chance to become Japan Fight League's Gounomono Hasha, which, as I now reflect, would have been the country's top MMA prize, excluding the yearly tournaments held." "It was October 19th, 2000. Naoto Kangyo had but to defeat the infamous Ryshido Matsuoka, a former champion himself. It was a classic case of the young versus the old, the upstart versus the veteran. The winner was assured a number one contender position, and most likely would have received a championship bout as early as December. That day came, and, being relatively close to Tokyo - which is a rare area for 'Fight League' to hold a show in - I myself attended. Unfortunately, the man whom I had almost deemed a journalistic project of mine lost that night. I would like to say that it was a bout that will go down in legend, but it wasn't. To put it bluntly, Kangyo was defeated with what can only be described as relative ease by Matsuoka. Matsuoka would win his second Gounomono Hasha later that year." "I was saddened by Kangyo's lose, but felt confident that the fighter would still one day contend for the highest honor in the NHB/MMA Japanese circuit. How wrong I was." [12:07.] "To make an already long story just a bit shorter, 'Nai no Kami' Naoto Kangyo, the man - no, the boy - who I thought represented what would save MMA/NHB fighting, instead represented what has, in the end, killed it. I was horrified to discover Kangyo appearing on broadcasts of the Japanese Puroresu Association's 'BATTLE START!' television program earlier this month." "Kangyo, it seems, had decided not to bother going after what he had earlier professed to me as his dream. While it's true I noticed his disappearance from 'Fight League' following his loss, I never once dreamed he had sunken so low. I thought, 'He must be recuperating. On vacation. Or simply healing his soul.' But, as it turns out, none of these were to be true. Kangyo had run." "I will be the first to admit that the world of MMA/NHB fighting can be a tough climate to exist in. A man must be steadfast, sound, and never yielding. I would once have thought that Naoto was all of these things. I was proven wrong. Kangyo, like so many others of the young crop of NHB fighters, has discovered that the world of Puroresu, where athleticism is negligible, and where competition is a word given to backstage political squabbles, where the victors and the conquered are chosen by a single man, is easier to endure than that of the real physical world, that of the MMA/NHB world." "The JPA is a haven for those whom, like Kangyo, can simply not cut it in MMA/NHB. It offers safety to those who are scared. Scared of the real world. I point to Kangyo as a symbol. He is symbolic of why the culture of MMA/NHB is being destroyed with each passing day in Japan." "And so I offer you the case of Naoto Kangyo - whom was given his own moniker 'Nai no Kami', which he now bears in JPA, from his performance in 'Fight League' - as the case for all of NHB/MMA fighting in our nation today. Why am I so forlorn over the future of the sport I hold so dear, you may ask? Because as long as there are organizations offering charades, such as JPA, no one will ever take the path of a real fighter, a real warrior. It is the equivalent of a great Samurai fighting with a broomstick, or a great Sumo merely pretending to push his opponent out of the circle. JPA offers men an easy road to stardom and respect. In MMA/NHB fighting, it is a road that must be paved with the sweat, tears, and blood of its traveler." [12:15.] "Naoto Kangyo has killed MMA/NHB. When it is all said and done, he - and others like him - will be the cause of its death. I pity Japan when this day arrives. But most of all I pity Kangyo, for some day he will realize the error and cowardice of his ways." [The typing ceases. The author of this piece examines the article, but doesn't move. He stares at it, basking in the monitor's glow. Then, slowly but surely, he reaches up to the keyboard for one final time.] "Quite possibly the most ironic thing, though, is the fact that Kangyo is currently masquerading about the JPA, pretending to be a part of an elite group of shooto masters dedicated to preserving the sport he has already struck a death blow to." "- Hiroki Hyodo Sports Editor The Tokyo Sun" ---- [One by one the silver and red letters appear onsreen...] T. A. P. Voice over: Total Attack Puroresu, The War For Respect Is ON. Just Give Us A Reason... [fade out] [Slow fade in. We are backstage at Kumamoto Gymnasium. The locker room is packed as Ryo Inoue and his partner and fellow T.A.P. member Eiji Yatsu sit on the bench looking absolutely drained following their tag team victory over MASSIVE Akita and "Iron Chef" Keiji Kobe Sitting nearby is Riki Fudo, formerly of PNM] Ryo: [taking in some breaths. He sits on the locker room bench, sweating dripping off his body] Can we make this quick please? I want to shower and get to my hotel room. Riki Fudo: Oi Ryo, you look especially tired tonight. How are you feeling? Is the strain of this tour getting to you? Ryo Inoue: [shaking his head slowly] No, but no one else has had the kind of tour I have. Between a savage meeting with Rick Taylor at the Chugoku Okayama Budokan and just as brutal fight with Jack Shaw and all my matches for the rest of the tour, I can honestly say that no one has had my kind of schedule this month. Riki Fudo: Yatsu-san, how does it feel to be teaming Ryo and participating in the "Shooto War"? Eiji Yatsu: I believe I've made myself clear on my support of shooto. Judo and Pure Fight may not be the exact art of the samurai, as Kobe has said before, but I believe we showed tonight that shooto is superior to both lucha and strong-style. Riki Fudo: So this teaming maybe a regular thing then? Ryo Inoue: I like teaming Eiji-kun. He is a quick learner and hard striker. I'll tell you up front I've learned some things from him as he is much more skilled in Judo than I. I feel I can trust Eiji Yatsu to watch my back and represent T.A.P. as best as possible. Riki Fudo: Genji Yamato has announced the Tour Dates for for the Junior Struggle Tour next month any thoughts on that Yatsu-san? Eiji Yatsu: In my first showing against Tasogare no Prince, I failed, but now at Junior Struggle, I will be able to redeem myself, and prove that Pure Fight is above all in the junior heavyweight divison. Ryo Inoue: [patting Eiji on the back reassuringly] Mark my words, this man is the break out fighter in the pack of Juniors. He will take this tournament and prove that the Pure Fighters of T.A.P. are he best. He can and WILL bring the Pan Asian Championship to T.A.P. I have every ounce of faith that he will. In fact I will be at every match of his, sitting ringside to cheer him on. Eiji Yatsu: I greatly appreciate Ryo. As much as he says he has learned from me, I have learned greatly from him. No more will it be the contaminated style, but unpolluted fighting skills. Riki Fudo: What about the six-man match at Tokyo Ryogoku Sumo Hall? Ryo Inoue: Shinji Nakata has a mouth larger than his head. Let him meet the challenge of T.A.P. and then watch him cry when he taps out to Naoto Kangyo. Laugh at him when he cried for mercy from Eiji-kun [slaps Yatsu on Eiji Yatsu: Very true. My arsenal of moves has at the same time been reduced of the lucha and strong-style, but increased with more unadulterated submission moves. Many of the fans blinded by the likes of Akita, Togawa, and Kobe may be upset with my somewhat slower style, but the true fans will thoroughly support my new found style and all of T.A.P. At the Sumo Hall, it will be the first great show of power of T.A.P. Tonight was a great victory, but very few were able to view it. Saturday will be different. All the world will see Inoue, Kangyo, and I defeat the so called young guns in devastating fashion. [The camera fades out.] ---- (Scene opens at a special press conference being held at the headquarters of JPA in Tokyo. Several of the JPA stars are in attendance to speak about their respective matches coming at the end-of-tour show at the Ryogoku Sumo Hall on the 24th. The gaijin are first to speak. Jason Ramsey is seated at the table in a perfectly tailored grey suit and is flanked by his Alliance partners, Rick Taylor and Billy Fairchild. All of the gaijin that are participating are at the table but the Alliance is bombarded with questions from the outset.) Reporter 1: Ramsey-san, you will be facing one of JPA's top natives on the 24th. In addition to his prestige, the Great Tokaio is the man who injured Jack Shaw, who has openly stated that he is an ally of the Alliance. Will this be a chance for retribution for you and your partners? Ramsey: In this business, injuries happen. That's a simple fact. Jack Shaw knew it, I know it, Rick and Billy know it, everyone at this table knows it. The day after Jack was hurt, I went to the hospital to visit him. Tokaio happened to be there for the same reason. He seemed genuinely sorry about what happened. We had tea together after we left the hospital and let me tell you this, I have nothing but respect for Tokaio. He is a worthy opponent and will be a great challenge. I harbor no ill will towards Tokaio. Report 2: How will you approach your match with him seeing as how he has just proven how dangerous he can be? Ramsey: Well, first off all when he kicks, I'll duck! (This draws laughter from the press and assembled gaijin.) Seriously, I will obviously have to be wary of his kicking attack. His feet are lethal weapons as is his fighting spirit. I will have to compensate for both. Tokaio will have to be at the top of his game as well because I plan on bring everything I have to Ryogoku and I will prove once again that I posses the heart of a true warrior. Regardless of the outcome it will truly be a great contest between two great athletes. Reporter 3: With your partner Rick Taylor quickly becoming a serious enemy of Inoue ad his T.A.P, will you be joining him in the strong style/shoot style conflict? Ramsey: How did I know that Inoue would come up today? Inoue has blasted strong style, Rick Taylor, and even the JPA who pays him a check every week. If Inoue thinks that strong style is so inferior to shoot, why doesn't he just GET OUT? I'm sick of hearing his name. If he truly wants to practice the ORIGINAL fighting art, I'll challenge him to a Greco-Roman match up. Striking during a ground fight is, well, not the way the ORIGINAL fighters did it. I am the best ground technician in JPA. I learned the purest form of the mat game. My record proves it. Look back on shoot fights from the last ten years. The ground technician wins 9 out of 10 times. What Inoue has done is soil the honor of the TRUE fight. He is a bully, an opportunist, and worst of all a traitor. He is truly a man with no honor. I guess what I'm saying is, if Inoue wants Taylor, he gets Ramsey too. That's what the Alliance is all about. Reporter 1: Billy Fairchild must also rise to a challenge on the 24th. He will be teaming with an enemy to face two of the top juniors in the world. Ramsey: Now trust me, I know that Bill can speak for himself, but I wanted to say a couple of words. Fairchild-san has overcome tremendous hardships to get to where he is. In my opinion and in the opinion of many of the sports writers in this room, Fairchild is the best American junior there is. And contrary to the racist rhetoric of the Iron Chef, that makes him a match for any junior on the planet, bar none. Reporter 2: One more question Ramsey-san. Would you like to give us a prediction on the Taylor/Akita match up for the Double Titles? Ramsey: It will be a contest of epic proportions. Rick Taylor is the toughest man in sports today. Not just puroresu, all of sports. Akita is a legend and a perfect champion, but I feel that Taylor is a force of nature that will be ultimately triumphant. I am proud to be his partner, his friend, and if he'll have me, his second on the 24th at Ryogoku. Now ask someone else a question or I'll get a bad rep among these guys as a showboat! (All of the wrestlers at the table erupt into laughter as the scene fades....) ---- http://jpapuroresu.hypermart.net/ --- [name withheld] - Formerly of The Westsiders [#28, RSPWF 2000 Year End Awards Top 50 Tag Teams] - Japan Puroresu Association - http://jpapuroresu.hypermart.net/
