JPA FLASH BULLETIN 2001.1.29

29 January 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
JAPAN PURORESU ASSOCIATION FLASH BULLETIN 2001.1.29 1) XYZ 2) Christopher Kojima 3) Jinshi Togawa 4) Gustav Jaeger 5) Eiji Yatsu ---- [Open camera shot to a simple JPA logo on a black banner with the familiar face of XYZ sitting down in a gray steel chair. XYZ is wearing his wrestling tights, with a white towel hung over his shoulders. He leans back in the chair, takes a deep breath and then begins to speak..] XYZ: The “CORONATION” tour comes to a close, sadness fills my heart. After numerous epic battles and amazing wins on this tour, after showing my talent to the wrestlers and fans an alike, it comes time for it all to end. I’ve put together a magnificent string of wins here in the JPA and I’m a better wrestler for coming here. But the final week of this tour marks my biggest challenge to date. The Great Tokaio-san, an amazing wrestler with a great deal of talent. A man who upholds the respect and honor of Japanese wrestler, a man who I have come to respect and idolize, in one way or another. Tokaio-san, you’ve proven yourself worthy here in the JPA, despite a lose to a fellow Japanese. You are a great challenge for me. You will test my maturity, my strength and my ability to over come challenge. I have fared quite well before our meeting Tokaio-san and I do hope to fare quite well in our epic battle. But if I do not have the… How do I say it… talent to overcome you Tokaio-san, I at least know I faced a worthy opponent! [Takes a breath.] It is hard for a man like me to admit my shortcomings, when and where I might fail to be victorious in the ring. It is a grave challenge for me to admit when someone is better and you Tokaio-san are. You are wiser and have more knowledge. You are the better wrestler. However, being the better wrestler does not mean you can defeat me! [Smiles.] You see Tokaio-san, I’m a worth opponent for you to face. Not because I necessarily have the talent but more or less because I can step it up when the time comes. Like the domesticated dog Tokaio-san, when cornered… I bite! When my back is up against the wall and there seems to no escape, I somehow find the hole. I find the way out and I find a way to win. Where there is a will, there is a way Tokaio-san. It is like my grandfather once told me. He who paddles the boat, cannot rock it. But he, who paddles hardest, makes waves! [Fade out.] ---- [Fade into a busy airport. As we pan around, we can see a sign that says LAX. As we pan down, we see a athletic-looking young Japanese man, dressed in baggy blue jeans and a Los Angeles Laker's jersey. At the moment, he looks more annoyed than anything.] MAN: Jessica? I told you to shoot around the area, not the whole damn airport. [We hear the voice of a young girl, about ten or eleven.] JE: But Chris. Mom's getting your ticket and probably five hundred things at some gift shop. I was bored. CH: Of course you're bored. If something lasts for more than five minutes, you're bored. JE: Me impatient? Mr. Flip through the channels five-hundred times during the span of four commercials? [The rising insult fight is stopped by the appearance of a young, very pretty Hispanic woman who looks to be in her early 20's. She is dressed in a red sundress. She is trying to hold up several large bags.] HW: Chris, Jess, can you stop fighting, and help me with these bags. I swear, you act like siblings and not future stepfather & daughter. CH: Maria, may I ask you where in the hell did you go to get all these clothes? MA: They have an Old Navy store in the airport. [Both Chris & Jessica, who we can see is a young light-skinned Hispanic child wearing shorts and a Mickey Mouse t-shirt groan.] CH: Well, I didn't really want to eat in Japan the first month anyways... [Maria laughs, then...] MA: It's on my card bozo. Look, Jess, go to the snack bar and get something to eat. JE: OK, Mom. [Jessica scampers off.] CH: You're sure she's safe like that. MA: I taught her self-defense. CH: Ah yes, I remember. Greco-Roman kick to the balls. MA: Hey, it worked. Now, Chris, you sure this has to be done? There's that fed here in LA, or you could work indies even - CH: I explained this to you already babe. I want to work in Japan & prove myself. If I'm successful there, then I can command a price here in the States. Listen, in a few months, you & Jess can come over for Spring Break, then Summer Vacation. It won't be so bad. MA: I know. It's just, the wedding's in August. How are you gonna' come back and help me plan it. CH: First, I'm the groom. My job is too get a lap dance from a stripper - hey, don't look at me like that, find a best man, and make sure no one gets too drunk at the reception. As long as the day ends with me and you exchanging 'I do's,' I could give a damn about the rest Maria. I love you. That's all that matters. [Jessica comes back, with fries and a burger.] MA: OK, I understand. I don't like, but I understand. Now, boarding's beginning. Get your Japanese ass on that plane, Chris. CH: OK, Maria. First, Jessica. [Jessica looks and smiles at Chris.] CH: Be good. If you go spazoid on your Mom, she knows where the duct tape is. Do I need to say more? [All three laugh.] CH: I'll call as much as I can. Now, Maria, avoid the passes from my loser brothers, and set up a damn good wedding. Bye, my darlin's. I gotta' a homeland to go to. [Fade to Black as we see Chris give Jess a peck on the lips, give a quite longer one to Maria, then pick up his bags, and head toward a concourse.] ---- VO: "During the moments in which I stepped out of the 747 Eastern Bargain Aeroplane - carefully onto each of the wobbly discount foldout stairsteps - and scoped out the familiar Tokyo atmosphere, I knew things were going to change." [Fade up on a shirtless Jinshi Togawa, who stands facing away from the camera, arms folded behind his back, and looks out of a rather large window at the orange morning sky which lights up the skyscrapers of New York City. After a moment, he turns, nodding to himself and smiling, then reaches down to the few bags of luggage beside him.] VO: "I'd distanced myself from all forms of puroresu for a few years when I'd lost motivation as a result of the sudden decline of my old promotion, and I wasn't expecting any organizations to regain my interest any time soon, so I'd begun to settle down, doing financial admnistration in New York City. When I received the letter to come participate with several names I'd heard mentioned when I was in Tokyo, however, I found myself filled again with boyish hope and flattery, so I made some phone calls, and it was agreed that in only a few months time I would be wrestling in a ten-man elimination-style Battle Royal as a member of the ranks of the Japanese Puroresu Association." [Switch Fade to a shot of Jinshi Togawa, dressed in baby-blue fullbody sweats, jogging the perimeter of Central Park on a New York morning. As he passes by various people - some who notice him, some who don't - we see his face, covered in sweat, wearing an adamant look of determination.] VO: "Suddenly, I found myself training excessively. Every day, I would wake earlier and jog longer, and every night, I would recall my old days in Tokyo and the competition I had once faced. I was losing precious sleep, but it didn't matter, because I was gaining emotional and physical drive. I began to daydream at work, then forget about certain quota deadlines, and eventually, I began skipping work altogether." [Another switch fade, this time we see Jinshi stretched about the backseat of a cab, a small smile on his face, his hands folded behind his head.] VO: "Eventually, my boss summoned me from his office and I jogged in, casually late, wearing my flashy baby-blue windbreaker. Panting, I sat down in a chair before him and pressed my sweaty neck against the cool, clingy leather surface. 'Hai?', I said, and his right eye gave a quick half-twitch, then he yelled at me, telling me I was fired and calling me an 'unfruitful, lethargic psychopath' - apparently it was my fault the company went spiralling into debt - and while he kept screaming and pointing his short, chubby finger at me I was reminded of how I used to end matches - by grabbing the opponent's misplaced arm, yanking them down to the mat, and applying one of my favorite signature moves, the Diamond Choke, until they went unconscious - so, after smiling at my boss and jogging back to my apartment, I inadvertantly tore my practice dummy's arm off with the move." [Switchfade to a shot from within the dark bathroom of Togawa's apartment. Other than a faint portion of the white counter, all we see is Jinshi's ripped physique hunched over the sink, water dripping from his chin, his nose, his eyebrows, as he stares into his own reflection.] VO: "I think it was that night that I started to suspect that I was redeveloping an unhealthy obsession, so what did I do? I exercised even more: every day, all day, every night, all night, until the morning of my departure. On that morning, I bid farewell to the capital of New York, grabbed a few bags of luggage, and boarded the cheapest flight to Tokyo I could find." [Switchfade to the shot of the airplane we saw earlier flying over the ocean.] VO: "And so I flew across the world, my head racing with anxiety..." [Switchfade again to the plane landed and Togawa grinning as he steps onto the runway.] VO: "...and when I arrived, - When I walked across the warm concrete and thought about my former casual puroresu days; When I looked into the airport-bathroom's mirror at my sleep-deprived, fiery eyes; When I walked up to meet Genji Yamato, to shake his hand, and I looked down and my sweaty palms and watched my fingers tremble - I knew things were going to be different; Things were going to be anything but casual." [Switchfade to a shot of Jinshi Togawa's back as he walks away from the bustling airport, then fade out completely.] VO: "This time, things are going to explode." ---- (The camera fades in to the parking lot outside Tokyo Ryogoku Sumo Hall, the site of the finals of the Coronation Tournament for the JPA. There are a few people outside the Hall at the moment, several of whom are sumo wrestlers who are apparently heading home after today's matches. Talking with the sumo wrestlers is a large gaijin man who speaks Japanese but with a thick German accent. He is wearing a flannel shirt, jeans and work boots. Seeing the camera he waves goodbye to the sumo wrestlers, apparently they're friends of his and walks over to the camera.) Man: Guten Tag. My name is Gustav Jaeger und I have just been hired by Japan Puroresu Association. (Gustav grins, obviously being quite happy to have been hired. Now that the camera is closer it's quite apparent just how huge Gustav is, he easily dwarfs most of the Japanese people around him.) GJ: Very soon I am to be making my debut here in the JPA. I will be competing in an 10-man Battle Royale were the only way to eliminate someone is to pin them or make them submit. Perhaps I am odd but to me this sounds like much fun! I get to square off against nine of the competitors here in this fine association and test myself against them, I cannot wait! (Gustav begins walking down the street, apparently heading back to his hotel. The camera follows him as he walks and he continues to talk.) GJ: In this battle royale, besides myself of course, are Jinshi Togawa, Yuzo Moriboshi, Eiji Yatsu, Inochi Ichihara, Kathakano, Jeremy Knyte, Sameul Otomo und Christopher Kojima. Some of them are new to the JPA, as I myself am but others have been here since the federation opened. All seem to be rather impressive individuals. Yuzo Moriboshi for instance is one of those shoot fighters and almost as strong as I am, or so I hear. Herr Togawa is also said to be quite strong and one who never gives up. Then you have wrestlers like Herr Ichihara who uses a pure technical approach or Herr Otomo who is an expert in judo. Then there are high flyers like Kathkano and the ones who are strong and rely on their power like Goshin Star or Herr Kojima. Worthy opponents all and ones who I am looking forward to facing. Und lest we forget, there is Jeremy Knyte, who has both a traditional approach und can also do the hardcore wrestling thing. He should be interesting to fight. (Gustav grins once more and flexes his arms, showing off his large muscles which practically burst out of the sleeves of his shirt. He's obviously enjoying the prospect of fighting this battle royale.) GJ: Worhty opponents yes but they can be defeated. Defeated by the skill and strength of Gustav Jaeger. For I am well prepared to wrestle here in Japan. You see, unlike many of the other large wrestlers, I am not just a man who is big and strong, oh no. I also have spent much time developing a technical arsenal und working on my stamina. So when the small wrestlers think I will run out of breath, I will still be going strong and when the technical wrestlers try to outwrestle me, I will be able to counter it. And they will all fall to the Freiburg Fury! (With a large grin Gustav waves goodbye to the camera person and enters his hotel.) ---- [The camera fades into Eiji Yatsu. The shot is simple, only the upper-half of Eiji’s body is shown, with a large JPA logo painted on the wall behind him. The sounds of clanking weights and smacks from hitting a mat come in from off screen, leading to the assumption that this spot is coming from one of JPA’s training dojos. Eiji is wearing a blue, full-length t-shirt that has a white JPA logo on the chest, along with sweat stains around the neck and armpits. The shirt is tight against is compact, luchadore-esque build. His black hair is done up in dreadlocks with the last third dyed bright green and is pulled back into a green band. His bright green eyes stare straight into the camera, not showing the sight exhaustion that is apparent on the rest of his body.] Yatsu: The Japan Puroresu Association. How many dojo’s does that make in my career now? Five? Six? There was the dojo I was trained in my youth in the art of judo, back when I was a superstar…well, to the other kids and parents that watched the national tournaments for children under 16. Prodigy, yes, I was called that. I won the Kai Nippon title three times before I was 16. Some people took notice, but not many since none of the big businessmen could promote a wee 15 year old against a seasoned veteran in any of various combat sport competitions. Maybe if I didn’t slide, and was able to stay ahead of the learning curve I could have easily made my way into the world of shoot-fighting. Instead when I did reach the rightful age, I was labeled as too small. Everyone wants to see the super heavyweights smash each other up, not of lightweights grappling. That’s why I moved to the dojo of a little, Mexico-base lucha libre promotion that was looking for a young, spunky domestic to market on their little tours. El Lobo Blanco and I faired decently well, however not necessarily here. Our biggest success was touring in the states, making our biggest stop in a little group called the MCWF…never could quite remember what those letters meant. Then I heard of Super Hyper Visual Fighting, the greatest thing to come around in years. They were selling out of the domes and were putting pressure on Super Japan. Even better, they’d take me. They were looking to open a middleweight division, plus the Ultraclash Tag Tournament was only a month or two away and they needed some guys that were neutral in all the fighting between the heavyweights. So I went back to the dojo, this time their dojo, to brush up on my strong-style skills, and that’s where I stayed. For some reason the fans left, hell, us middleweights only had one match and they were already leaving. SHVF was forced to close down, and I returned to obscurity. Then came my next shot. The one that would take me to the big time. Dream Factory K, formed by some of the same guys that promoted the SHVF, plus they were fashioned to give younger guys from the G-Pro dojos a chance to step up and show them something before coming into the big time. It was there in DFK that I struck up some kind of a friendship with Shoji Suzuki. We were on the same side on issues and we were scheduled to team up. I was amazed, this was original Jet Jaguar, one of the greatest junior heavyweights in our sport, the man I learned to idolize when I doing lucha libre. “Five Star” was his nickname, but I doubt our match reached that nirvana, but after that match I was asked if I’d like to have a try out match in G-Pro. I was star struck. A possible partnership with Suzuki plus a chance at going after the International Super Middleweight title one day in G-Pro. Then, as always, it happened, G-Pro closed its doors and I was left with nothing. What have I been doing since then? Bouncing around the dark depths of puroresu. Club and parking lot shows have been my MO, but now, I thank all seven of the gods of Good Fortune for yet another chance at making it in the sport I love. I only pray that Daikoku blesses JPA with good wealth, and I the hard-working spirit from Ebisu. On the third I will be given my first opportunity to show all those in the JPA what my abilities. Battle royals have never been my strong suit, but I will do my best to face all nine other men sharing the ring with me. It seems that all of those participating are without a place yet within the Japan Puroresu Association, so all will be doubly tough in our attempt to become what we pray for. I will not claim that I am the best fighter in the company, but I want to illustrate my abilities, and as soon as Junior Struggle comes around, my strength in going after the crown. My reach for the brass ring will not be confined to that of the junior division, but also to that of the tag-team. My search continues for a partner willing to join me in my quest, but one will come, eventually. My future is haunted be a dark past, but in this cloudy present I hope to show that I can reach above the clouds.
