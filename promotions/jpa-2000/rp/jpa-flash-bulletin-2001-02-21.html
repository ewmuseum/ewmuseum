JPA FLASH BULLETIN 2001.2.21

21 February 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
JAPAN PURORESU ASSOCIATION FLASH BULLETIN 2001.2.21 1) Jason Ramsey 2) Billy Fairchild 3) Rick Taylor 4) Iron Chef Keiji Kobe 5) Eiji Yatsu 6) Jinshi Togawa ---- (Scene opens in a conference room in a large Tokyo area hotel. A group of sports press reporters have gathered and are awaiting the arrival of Jason Ramsey as he has sent word that he has a big announcement pertaining to the gaijin workers in JPA. A representative of the hotel steps to the podium and directs the reporters attention to a big screen t.v. that has been wheeled into the room. He states that part of the presentation will be made there. With that the lights lower and a video starts to play. A searing guitar track is playing as highlights of several matches begin to flash on the screen. The first few are of Jason Ramsey himself, performing several of his trademark moves, including his Superdriver and Tiger Suplex '85 finishers, the next series of rapid fire scenes involve Ramsey's frequent tag team partner Billy Fairchild. Clips of Fairchilds amazing aerial ability along with some shots of he and Ramsey teaming up make up his montage which is finished off with a slow motion view of his death defying air raid crush. The last scene draws many appreciative "ooh"s and "ahhh"s from the assembled press. The video quickly moves to a highlight reel of Rick Taylor. This section of the tapes shows several of Taylors high impact moves and closes with a slow motion flash of Taylor blasting Ryo Inoue with an elbow smash. The press seeing that the scene is clipped from the Taylor/Inoue match-up for last tours Coronation tournament once again vocalize their appreciation as if they were re-living the brutal match in their minds eye. The video comes to an end and the screen fades to black before a stylized logo covered in an animated green flame fills the screen. ALLIANCE is what the logo reads and the press starts to buzz as they begin to figure out what the video means. The lights come up and the crowd of reporters look back to the podium. The manager of the hotel tells the assemblage that Ramsey is going to read from a prepared statement and will not be taking any questions at this time. With that, he introduces Jason Ramsey who comes to the podium dressed to the nines in a Hugo Boss three piece, his long dark hair pulled neatly back in a pony tail. He steps to the mike and begions to read.) "Gaijin have been a part of the Japanese wrestling scene since the early 50's. Many of the greatest American wrestlers of all time have toured Japan. Harley Race, Ric Flair, Bruiser Brody, the Funks, the list goes on and on. Men like Stan Hansen and the legendary Destroyer came to the orient and never went back to the states. The gaiin worker in Japan has always had to work extra hard to shake the "lazy American" stigma. From the days of Rikidozan, through the glory days of United Japan Pro Wrestling and Neo-Japan, up to the modern day JPA, the gaijin has been a staple to the Japanese wrestling industry. Today, I am proud to announce that, in my opinion, and the opinion of many Japanese wrestling fans, three of the best wrestlers the United States have to offer, have banded together to form a faction. An "Alliance" if you will. Myself, Rick Taylor and Billy Fairchild, have formed a union to better represent gaijin interest in JPA. I want it to be known that we also offered membership in the Alliance to Jack Shaw, but he declined. I am here to say that the Alliance wishes Jack the best of luck in his bid for the Double titles and his invitation stands indefinitely. This is not a "stable" as is seen in the "sports entertainment" of the US. This is three athletes, banded together by common goals and mutual respect. This Saturday, Rick Taylor and I will will begin to work toward that goal by facing Samuel Otomo and Kentaro Ono. Mr. Taylor and I both feel that our skill and fighting spirit will carry us to victory. We welcome Ono and Otomo to the JPA and look forward to a great contest. It is a contest in which we feel we will triumph. The Alliance will be a force in the JPA during the Royal Series and many tours to come. Thank you." (With that, Ramsey takes a step back and picks up a black and green ring jacket with the Alliance logo across the back and poses for the photographers for two or three minutes before bowing and leaving the room.) ---- [Backstage at the last JPA TV taping, reporters are waiting for Billy Fairchild to come back to the locker room after his match for an interview as the camera points out toward the arena. Here he comes. Sweaty and exhausted after the match. As he reaches the reporters they begin to ask him a question...] Reporter: Fairchild-san, ... [Fairchild just walks right past them and into the locker room. The reporters are confused, he has always answered their questions before. He simply walks into the locker room and shuts the door behind him.] [Cut to the JPA dojo where a camera is focused on a car pulling into the parking lot. Billy Fairchild gets out of the car, dressed in upscale attire with black sunglasses and walks right past the camera again, into the dojo and shuts the door behind him. The camera peers in through the window as Fairchild approaches his instructor. The instructor stops class and the other trainee's look on intently at the looming confrontation. Fairchild steps up to his instructor and holds out a key. The instructor doesn't look like he wants to take it. Fairchild thrusts it out a little again, inviting him to take the key. The instructor gets angry at Fairchild and yells at him. Fairchild drops the key on the ground and walks back out of the building, a slight tear showing on his cheek under the rim of the sunglasses. He gets back in his car, and drives away.] [Cut to a shot of Fairchild on a plane, staring out the window at the beatiful landscape below, his face still somewhat empty and lonely. He arrives in America with tears in his eyes as his family greets him at the gate. There is an emotional reunion and lots of hugging. The camera closes in as he approaches his mother and hugs her in his arms.] Fairchild: ... I'm sorry. Mother: It's okay. You tried your best. [The camera is closed in on Fairchild's face as he virtually breaks down on his mother's shoulder.] [Cut to a shot of a beatiful clearing at lakeside. Fairchild is sitting on a pile of rocks, his knees pulled up with his arms crossed over them. His chin rests on his arms as he gazes down into the water. He is reflecting on his life. Focusing himself. The camera zooms in slowly to a close up of his face. He is obviously in deep thought. He lifts his head up, looks around at the trees, the sky, the horizon. Fairchild gets up off the rocks and walks off camera as the camera zooms in on the reflection of the sunset in the lake.] [Cut to a shot of Billy Fairchild at the gym, lifting weights. He lifts with fire and passion, exerting himself to his last ounce of strength as the camera closes in on his reflection in the mirror. The weight begins to get heavy, and Fairchild's face begins to show weakness. Fairchild struggles to lift the weights one last time, but can't do it. Fairchild collapses down to his knees, putting the weights on the floor and holding his head in his hands. Behind Fairchild a pair of legs walks up and a female hand is placed on his shoulder. He looks up and one of the female trainers is standing there.] Trainer: Are you okay? Fairchild: ... fine. Trainer: Okay, you looked hurt. I was just checking. Don't over exert yourself, okay? Fairchild: ... okay. Trainer: ... Is your name Fairchild? Fairchild: ... Yeah ... How'd you know my name? Trainer: I think I saw a segment on the news about you, or at least someone who looked like you. He was from here in Portland and is a wrestler in Japan. Are you a wrestler? Fairchild: ... um ... I don't know anymore. Trainer: You don't know? Fairchild: I mean, I was. But now ... I don't know anymore. Trainer: Oh ... that's too bad. From the clips they showed you looked pretty good. Why'd you give it up? Fairchild: ... [Fairchild tries to find the words as he looks up into this beautiful woman's face and she smiles at him.] Fairchild: ... I don't know. [Fairchild smiles back.] Trainer: Well, that's no reason to quit is it? Fairchild: I guess not. [The two now smile at each other. A new life burns in Fairchild's face. He appears reborn almost.] [Cut to a shot of Fairchild back on a plane, reading a letter signed by Jason Ramsey. Cut to a shot of Fairchild walking up to a house and knocking on the door. From inside a man asks...] Man: Who is it? Fairchild: It's Billy Fairchild. [The door opens and it's Jason Ramsey.] Fairchild: I'd like to take you up on your offer, if it's still open. Ramsey: Of course it is. Come inside. [Fairchild steps into the home and Ramsey shuts the door behind him.] ---- [Interior of a small room. Medium shot of JPA ring announcer and Public Relations Director, Seiji Oyuki, in a black business suit, seated in a swivel chair.] Oyuki: Good evening, JPA fans. Oyuki Seiji here, you may know me as your trusty JPA ring announcer, but I also serve as director of public relations. Tonight, I am going to sit and talk with one of JPA's promising gaijin heavyweights, Rick Taylor. [Camera switches shots to Taylor, sitting in a swivel chair as well, dressed in a blue and white JPA training suit, bearing the familiar JPA star logo. He nods in acknowledgement to Oyuki.] Oyuki: Rick, first, it is well known that you were conspicuously absent from JPA proceedings at the end of the Coronation tour save for your matches, which were commitments you did meet. Why were you absent so often? Taylor: Well, it was irresponsibility on my part. I had some issues that just happened to pop up at about the same time that the Coronation tour was wrapping up. It was a small miracle I was able to make my last two or three matches of the tour, but I have built a reputation on being dependable, and I was at least going to make sure I could be counted on to make the shows. I failed to be dependable in other aspects, and for that I feel like I let the JPA down. I have spoken with Yamamoto-san, and I have apoligized to him, but I would like to apologize to the rest of the JPA staff and all the JPA fans. I shirked my duties as a JPA wrestler, and I am going to make up for my failure on these subsequent tours. I am dedicated to the JPA now, it is my primary professional commitment now. It was highly unprofessional of me to miss commitments in the last few weeks. So, I apologize first of all to the JPA staff for whom I made professional life difficult in the last few weeks. Secondly, I apologize to the JPA fans for my irresponsibility. I will make up for it from this point on. Oyuki: Understood, and accepted. On our first Royal Series card at the Differ Ariake in Tokyo, you will team with Jason Ramsey to take on two of our recent signees, Kentaro Ono and Samuel Otomo. How will you and Ramsey handle these newcomers? Taylor: Well, from what I have heard around our locker rooms and from the tapes I have seen of these two, they are definitely talented and more than have the potential to go VERY far here in the JPA if they have the dedication and drive to succeed. However, Jason and I are determined to make sure that they get no free ride here in the JPA. Oyuki: Interestingly enough, there are rumors that you, Ramsey and Billy Fairchild have been meeting and speaking amidst the recent happenings here. What, if any, substance is there to these rumors? Taylor: Well, it's true that the three of us have spoken a number of times, and it has concerned the recent goiings on in the company. Particularly, the tension that had been building between Inoue and Akita, but even that situation has gotten bizarre as well. I am not at liberty to speak too freely about what the three of us have been talking about, but I can say that the three of us have identical agendas and there will be MUCH more to talk about soon. Oyuki: Would it be safe to say that we should be expecting big things from the three of you soon? Taylor: I would say so, Oyuki-san. Oyuki: Excellent! Now, how do you feel about Jack Shaw being chosen to replace Ryo Inoue to face Masashi "MASSIVE" Akita for the Double Titles at the 2001.3.24 Tokyo Ryogoku Sumo Hall show? Taylor: I think Jack is an excellent choice. I've teamed with him a number of times and I know just how tough that dude is. I've also faced Akita a number of times, I've even pinned him a couple of times. I know how tough he is. Neither of them are all that young any more, but they both have the desire, drive, and heart of lions. There are a loty of young lions here in this company, but I believe that it is the veterans like these two men that truly show us younger guys how to be truly great. I'm not rookie, having been a pro for almost 10 years, and I've spent about half of those years here in Japan, but I'm only 27 years old, and even I, with almost a decade of experience, and number of championships, to my credit, I still watch, wrestle, or team with these guys and I always learn or experience something new. I will watch there championship match with great enthusiasm. Oyuki: What do you think about the actions of Inoue as of late? Taylor: My personal and professional opinions of Inoue are less than sterling. He is an amazing fighter. He beat me in the first round of the Coronation tournament, but he almost didn't. I had a lot of professional respect for him as a fighter at that point, but since the tournament, he has been behaving rather oddly and I would have to say, unprofessionally. When he issued the challenge to Akita, I saw nothing wrong with that. However, when he retracted it and started talking about how the JPA wasn't his top priority anymore, how "some pieces of metal on leather straps" were less imortant than other things he's doing, I just lost a LOT of professional respect for him. I also think he's rather arrogant and extremely conceited as well. We had an excellent match, hell, he broke my nose! I think that match may have been one of my better matches in the last year, but that doesn't mean I like him much. I think he has done more harm to the JPA than good in our short history, but that's just my opnion. Yamato-san continually expresses frustration with him, but he seems to feel that Inoue is a major draw, so he wants to keep him on contract. That is a position I respect, because if we do not draw fans we do not run cards, so I understand the business end of the decisions, but I still think that Inoue has shown the JPA staff and roster a lot of disrespect and I just think he needs to be called on that fact. Inoue and I have a match on 2001.3.10 at the Chugoku Okayama Budokan show, and I intend to do nothing short of defeat him solidly and teach him that strong-style, the style to which I dedicate myself, is not inferior to shooto. Oyuki: It will be interesting to see how that pans out. You say that you will watch the Akita vs. Shaw match with great enthusiasm. Will you be doing some sizing up of both of these men for the possibility of a future match for the Double Titles yourself? Taylor: I would love to wrestle either man for the Double Titles, or whoever the champion is if I gfet a shot. However, I am out to earn a shot. I do not want anything handed to me. I feel I have the potential to ber a big name here in the JPA. I was the top gaijin in Royal Japan, but this is a different company, and I want to build a new legacy here on my accomplishments here. Interestingly enough, I will have a match with Akita-san on 2001.3.13 at the Yamaguchi Yanai Gymnasium show. I do not think it is for the Double Titles, as it's not being promoted as such, but I am looking forward to my match with the champion. He has pinned me before, and I've pinned him, so it'll be a good match. Overall, I am not aware of who my opponent will be at the big tour ending show at the Tokyo Ryogoku Sumo Hall, but whatever it ends up being, I will do my utmost to make it a great match, befitting this tour, which has the potential to be great. Oyuki: Thank you for your time, Taylor-san. Taylor: You're very welcome. Oyuki: See you next time, JPA fans! [Fade out.] ---- (The shot opens up onto a studio setting where Mituso Masanobu, vice president and former interviewer for NJPW, dressed in a subdued suit sits across from "Iron Chef" Keiji Kobe. Kobe has on his trade mark mask as always but instead of his ring gear he has on black slacks and a orange polo.) Mituso Masanobu: I am glad we got the opportunity to sit down and talk about a few matters before the start of the Royal series tour, Iron Chef. First before we discuss this new tour I would like to congratulate you on a most successful run during the Coronation tour. Kobe: I thank you. It was a thrill for me to get to compete all over Japan, a true missionary of the pure Lucha style. The tour was very successful as a whole for the company, and I hope I aided in some small way to that success. Masanobu: Yes, well going more in depth on the subject of the Coronation tour, I would like to hear your feeling on competing in the Heavy weight tournament. Kobe: That tournament had some of the greatest talents from around the world. Men like Jack Shaw, Tora Ryoku, Rick Taylor, and our most worthy champion, Massive Akita. Personal problems prevented me from congratulating Akita-san personally earlier in his win, but I would like to publicly state I believe there is no one more worthy of holding wrestling's highest honor. Masanobu: I noticed you left a name off your list of worthy competitors in the Coronation tournament. Kobe: (Kobe's reply is a bit bashful, if it was not for his mask he would be blushing on camera) My own, yes, I know. I do believe I made a strong showing in the tournament proving myself worthy of the titles, but I left myself off that list because I am choosing to focus more on the middle weight ranks this tour. No disrespect to the larger wrestlers in our ranks, but I believe that the middle weight division is where the real action takes place. Middle weights are full of young fresh ideas, more athleticism, and greater enthusiasm by and large. Masanobu: I thank you for your insights, but the name I was referring to was that of Ryo Inoue, not your own. Why did you leave his name off. Kobe: (Kobe seems a bit preterb now. But tries to hide it) A better question might be why did I leave the name of The Great Tokaio or SHOICHI 13. Masanobu: That may be, but the question is why did you leave off Inoue's name. Kobe: (With almost robotic response that seems to have been prepared by a legal team comes the reply) In the interest of company unity I have kept silent on this subject for as long as possible. I tried to avoid this controversy, but it seems you will not allow it. I left Ryo Inoue's name of the list of men I consider worthy competitors because I do not believe he is a worthy competitor. Masanobu: Inoue did make it to the finals of the tournament. Farther than you got. Kobe: (His pride wounded, Kobe let's all impartiality fly out the window and the volume and rate of his speech goes up a notch or two) But what path did he take to get to it? A path of trickery. The path of deceit. The path of shame, but certainly not the path of wrestler. The man hit me countless times with a closed fist, that isn't wrestling, Masanobu! The savage used illegal choke holds to gain his wins, that isn't wrestling! If Ryo Inoue got his wins by throwing salt in his foes eyes or hitting his opponents with steel chairs would you hold him up as an example of fighting spirit??? Never, but Ryo, he chokes people, hits them with closed fists, ect., and he is defended by you!!! Masanobu: I never defended him. And I would hardly call Mr. Inoue a savage. Kobe: (Claiming down, as if listening to the voice of reason.) Perhaps you are right, perhaps, Inoue is not a savage. (Once again the anger begins to build in Kobe's voice.) After all a savage is ignorant of the right way to behave, where as Inoue, the vaulted whore of fighting arts has deemed himself above the rules normal wrestlers must concern themselves with! But you've heard this much before from me, you know I deem Ryo's sportsmanship as falling some where between Denis Rodman and Attila the Hun. Why do you bring this up? Masanobu: Well I was hopping to get you comments on this so called T.A.P. Kobe: I was hopping not to have to address this vain self gratifying lung at fame by Inoue (spits to the side) with a response, but it seems this exercise in self promotion has captured the eyes of some in the media. The entire premise of T.A.P. is based on some assine garbage that Ryo (spits) has concocted about how the powers that be are out to get him. Inoue (spits) is with out a doubt the whiniest piece of garbage I've ever seen. The man cheats his way through the tournament with his underhanded tricks, and then when he fails against a man twice his age with a bad hip who didn't cheat he cries discrimination! The man has been coddled by management and he has the audacity to cry foul? This is a desperate cry of attention by a fraud who was exposed. The worst part is he is trying to bring in the funeral parlor dubbed a fighting school called Steel Wind. No one remembers these guys, Ryo (spits), because most of the world wasn't born when the name Steel Wind ment something in the fighting world, and those that were around are senile now. Masanobu: Kobe-san, please stop this slander and please refrain from defiling our studio any more. Kobe: My defiling of the studio is nothing compared to how that dog who's name I will not mention defiles the sport of puroresu with his garbage wrestling. I tried to avoid this subject, but you pressed me for my opinion, now you can't stomach it. Well consider this medicine, it may go down hard, but it will help you in the long run. Hopefully this medicine will rid us of the cancer that is that shooto dog. And as far as slander goes, I believe charges should be brought upon that criminal who's name I will not mention and his T.A.P. for the slander and lies he has spouted out against the JPA and Genji Yamato. Our president has used considerable restraint thus far in dealing with the coward Inoue, but that fighting whore should remember the old saying, the nail that sticks up will get hammered down. Masanobu: Do you believe President Yamato should censor Inoue some way, perhaps suspend him or even fire Ryo Inoue for his actions. Kobe: I believe the traitor should be branded a coward for his refusal to face Akita with a glowing ember. Then I would hang the dog up from the highest tree in the land like the traitor he is for forming the outlaw group T.A.P. and then I would feed his body to dogs. Unfortunately, our modern legal code system does not allow such actions, so instead, I'd offer the wretched beast one of two options, retract his statement, shave his head, and agree to serve on ring crew for the length of one tour or leave the JPA and die in obscurity. Enough time has been spent on the traitorous dog Ryo Inoue, I'd like to finish by addressing my opponent at the start Royal tour. Masanobu: Yes, but it would seem hard to top the controversy caused by your last statements, but tell us of Kathakano. Kobe: Well, I have not seen much of Kathakano, to be sure, and that is the way he likes it. Kathakano likes to hide in the shadows and talk of destiny, just like another man new to this federation. Both of them seem to think that if they hide and wait good things will come to them. Well that may be, maybe you are destine for greatness, but while you are waiting for destiny I'm making history. I'm running the place while you cower in the twilight's shadows. Masanobu: It seems once again you speak of someone besides Kathakano. Kobe: Is that what you think? Why, does the cowardly man in the shadows I describe remind you of any one else? The simple fact is Kathakano, I know more about you than you think. I know all about your kind, El Chupacabras, goat sucker. Every Mexican rancher has come across your handy work from time to time, be it a dead sheep, a bloodless goat, maybe even a small calf. You draw you power from others, you take life so that you may live. The stronger your victim, the stronger you become, and you have now named me your next target. But you seem to forget even with all your powers you're not invincible. Masanobu: Are you suggesting Kathakano stalks farm animals. Kobe: No, I'm suggesting that El Chupacabras, the beast who calls himself Kathakano, is in for a rude awakeing. El Chupacabras, they are the lowest of the low. Vial beasts who attack the weak to gain strength. Kathakano is a particularly stupid Chupacabras however, for he has made it his mission to draw blood from iron. Go ahead and try, Chupacabras, do you worst. I won't need any garlic or crosses to defeat, just the strength of Lucha Liebre. I promise you, El Chupacabras, in all your travels you've never met a competitor of my caliber. You are looking at the only middle weight to not only enter, but advance in the tournament to crown a double title champion. I am the only man in the JPA with enough skill to practice pure Lucha Liebre, I am pound for pound the best wrestler in the JPA. You can ask the Twilight prince, creatures of the night do not intimidate me. (Exposing his neck) Go ahead, El Chupacabras, have a little snack, but don't be surprised when you teeth shatter when you try and bite into solid Iron, and your courage leaves you like your soul did long ago. You go on and talk of your darkness and your mystery, there is not mystery in me. I am the Iron Chef, I get paid to win, and that is just what I will do when I face you, Chupacabras. (The shot fades to white.) ---- [The camera fades into the simple scene of Eiji Yatsu standing in front of a large JPA logo. All of his dreadlocks are pulled back into a ponytail, and he's wearing a blue JPA wind suit.] Yatsu: Destiny… Destiny… Now there is a paradox. I myself am a firm believer in destiny. I pray at my family's shrine here in Fujiwara to help me in my upcoming contests, but I know that what is going to happen will happen, there is no fighting it. Then there is Tasogare no Prince, a man believes he knows what his destiny is. Tasogare, you must believe your destiny here in the Japan Puroresu Association is to defeat Kobe in your rematch and come out victorious at JUNIOR STRUGGLE. But we all know that the assumption that you actually know this is completely false. Your destiny cannot be known, Tasogare, but only what your next reality is. The next tour will see you compete against nearly all those who will make of the Junior Heavyweight division. EIYUU, Corey Hart, XYZ, We Hyun Bae, Kenji Okano, and Billy Fairchild, and then there's your first opposition, me. You may not believe that any of us will challenge you on your way to glory, but I beg to differ. I had a decent showing in the battle royal. No, I didn't win, but I was never good in chaotic situations. Finally I will be able to show my one-on-one combat skills to the people of Dai Nippon. While you have been looking forward, I've been looking directly at you. Facing a wrestler of your caliber is a dream come true, let alone the stage that is the JPA. Failure has been a big part of my recent life, but it is finally time for to step up. Defeating you, Tasogare, will be major proof to all my doubters out there that I can make it on a high level. I am ready to meet my destiny, but are you, Tasogare, ready to realize yours is clouded? [The camera fades out.] ---- [Fade in on the crowded noon-hour of a nameless sushi bar in Tokyo. Amidst today's bustling clamor of the prosaic, antsy lunch rush, Jinshi Togawa chews his eel makimono rhytmically, with an eccentric, almost hypnotic tranquility. As he swallows, his eyes - fixated on an appealingly intangible mental image - glaze over slightly and a small, euphoric half-smirk stretches across his face. For a moment, time seemingly stops altogether, even with the ever-meandering crowd still present; For a moment, Jinshi's smile urges his complacence around the restaurant in a contagious manner, but the fatuous zombies that surround him prove too preoccupied with hyperactive sushi-respiration to notice, so Jinshi's smile ducks back inside, and he continues eating.] Jinshi's Voice Over: "That night - the night I won the Coronation Royal - I slept like a baby. After tossing combatant after combatant overhead, including the likes of one rather collosal German, I stumbled into bed, still sweating moderately, still filled with adrenaline, and closed my eyes, and for the first time in incalculable months time, my entire body exhaled, and the next thing I knew, it was a beautiful morning, and I was born again." [Cut to a febrile Jinshi squatting a large amount of weight, his face quite contorted with strain as he thrusts his body up repeatedly.] JVO: "Of course, It soon sank in that things were far from over; things were just beggining, in fact. So, again I found the my brain fidgeting nightly and again I found myself training excessively. It suddenly became apparent that I would be required to choose a style to align with, and when I didn't choose shoot, it became apparent that I had spawned a few adversaries." [Cut to Jinshi sitting pensively cross-legged by a large window, overlooking the sunrise's beautious projection on the face of Tokyo.] JVO: "Team Tiger Claw - whilst not in favor of the shoot-style, may hold an amount of respect for Yuzo and I, which I do honor... - will be treated as I would treat these adversaries, not only because my partner and I wish to make our faces known, but because again, when I'm in the ring, I find myself losing control: shouting, taunting, hunting for an arm to wrench, looking for a head to drop... It's nothing personal, I swear." [Cut video.] JVO: "I'm just a little volatile, that's all." ---- http://jpapuroresu.hypermart.net/ --- [name withheld] - Formerly of The Westsiders [#28, RSPWF 2000 Year End Awards Top 50 Tag Teams] - Japan Puroresu Association - http://jpapuroresu.hypermart.net/
