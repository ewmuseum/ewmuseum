JPA FLASH BULLETIN 2001.1.9

9 January 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
JAPAN PURORESU ASSOCIATION FLASH BULLETIN 2001.1.9 1) Iron Chef Keiji Kobe 2) Billy Fairchild 3) Jason Ramsey 4) Tora Ryoku --- (The shot opens up onto a black screen with the following written on it in blue letters.) "In antiquity those that excelled in warfare first made themselves unconquerable in order to await the moment when the enemy could be conquered. Being unconquerable lies with yourself; being conquerable lies with the enemy." Sun-Tze, The Art of War. (The shot now fades in onto the Iron Chef, Keiji Kobe, standing against a black back drop. The only lighting for him comes from a blue tinted up light, and much of his face and mask remain in shadow.) Kobe: Those words were true in Sun-Tze's time, they are true today, and they will still be true when we face off, Ryoku. I want you to here those words as you prepare for battle, Tora. I want you know what you are up against. You're entering a battle that you can not win, because I will not lose. You can drop me on my head, you can break my arm, you can do what ever you want, but I will not lose because of it. You won't win unless I let you, and don't hold your breath waiting for that. To win over me, Ryoku, you've got to get me to give in some way, and you've got a better chance of getting struck by lighting than that. I know your style, Ryoku, and I know your reputation. You dump men on their heads till they can't get back up, but if you think that is going to work on me, well let us then say you don't no jack about my style. I practice something called Lucha Liebre... I've spent the last five years refining my craft. And to do so, I've dumped myself on my head more times than I care to remember. I've taken 10, 15, 20 foot dives and landed on my head, Ryoku, but I always got up. I've fallen from greater hights than you can throw me, and I'm still here. Did it hurt? Damn right it hurt! Was I ever injured by it? With Lucha Liebre it's not a question of if you get hurt, but when and how bad. In my case the when is constantly, and the how bad doesn't matter. I'm a Luchador, Ryoku, if I wasn't able to deal with the pain I'd be in another business. I don't fear getting dropped on my head, and I don't fear the pain because it's not in my job description. This tournament has a number of different people in it, there are genetically modified warriors, Shoot fighters, living legends, and me, I'm a simple salaryman. Ryo says he didn't want to be a salary man, so you turned to Puroresu. Well let me share something with all of you at home, I wanted to be salary man, so did my parents, in fact they insisted I become one, so here I am. I'm a wrestler, I work for the same company Inoue does, and I am every bit a salary man. I pledged my services to this company. No, I pledged more than that. I pledged my heart, my soul, my body to this company. I gave my word, my sacred pledge, that every time I walk out under this hood I will give it a hundred percent. I didn't pledge I'll win every match, or I'll never get hurt. I pledged to give my all in every match I'm in. I pledged to make making this company a success my number one priority. And when I say I, I'm not talking about Keiji Kobe, father of two, husband of Marie Kobe. When I say I, I speak of the Iron Chef. The Iron Chef has no family, no home, no other commitments other than to his company. I'm not loosing sleep over the fact I'm more than likely going to lose to you, Ryoku, the Iron Chef has no family to bring shame on by loosing. I'm not worried about what injuries I'll sustain in our battle, the Iron Chef has no children or wife to take care of. All I care about is whether I'll live up to my promise, because my word is the only thing to my name. So I will take the risks others cringe at, I will push my body to it's limits. I'll take my game to who new level. But what is this skill and determination going to amount to in the face our your offense, Ryoku? Plenty... My friend, Hart-san, has quoted to me a favorite American wrestler of his by the name of Rick Flair. Flair-san says, "If you want to be the man, you've got to beat the man." Well I am the man, the salary man. The man who loves his company more than anything, and the only man who is going to beat me is some one who loves this company as much. The only way you are going to get a win over me is to prove you want it as bad as me. No, you're going to have to show you want the win more than me. You're going to not only have to go as far as me, but farther, and I don't think you can do that, Ryoku. I don't think you are willing to dive face first into me from the top rope to the floor. I don't think you're willing to get up over and over again after I knock you down. I don't think you'll be willing to fight through the pain of a La Tapatía paseada. More importantly, I don't think you can. I don't think you can fight through the pain I'll put you through. I don't think you can leap the way I do, but we'll see. We'll see how well you can handle the true flavor of the Iron Chef. We'll see if you can absorb the punishment I'll dish out. I'm not sure what you are capable of, but I do know what I'm capable of. I'm the man who is capable of going from rookie to top contender for the middle weight crown in a matter to three weeks. I'm the man who is capable of going undefeated in no less three Japanese federations. I'm the man who is capable of becoming a superstar on two continents, Asia and North America. I'm capable of getting dropped on my head and then getting back up, again, and again, and again. I'm capable of stepping up to the plate on any given night, and beating any one in the federation of any size, style, and shape. I'm the only Middle weight to qualify for the shot at J1 Rikidozan/NJPW Unified Heavyweight Titles. And I didn't get here because Genji Yamato wanted a good laugh. I didn't get here because I was only as good as any heavy weight in the federation. There isn't a single middle weight in this federation that doesn't have as much skill as the heavys. I got here because I'm not only just as good as any heavy, but because I'm better than you, Tora. I do things you can only dream about. In this small frame I pack more heart than your entire family posses. There is no question in my mind that with out your size advantage I'd crush you like a grape. I've shown to President Yamato that I've got enough skill to stay competitive with you. This isn't a battle of skills, Ryoku, because I've already won such a battle. This is high stakes staring contest, the first one to blink will lose. I've got the tools hurt you, the will to keep fighting in the face of your attacks, and the dream need to stay focused. You can't beat me, Ryoku. I am the invincible man of culinary skill, I am the Iron Chef!!! (The shot fades to white.) ---- [Scene fades in with the camera entering a doorway into the JPA dojo. A reporter and translator are following the cameraman. As it comes closer we can make out Billy Fairchild sparring against another trainee. Fairchild is totally controlling this wrestler-wanna-be as the camera arrives at ringside.] Reporter: Fairchild-san! ...may I have a word with ... [Fairchild glances at the reporter and the trainee quickly reverses on Fairchild and takes him down. The instructor gets very angry and steps in as the trainee gets up off of Fairchild. Fairchild rises to his feet in shame as the instructor verbally berates him.] Instructor: You must NEVER lose concentration like that! This is puroresu, gaijin! You are JPA! You are not allowed to be weak!! I will not allow my students to be weak! FOCUS! [He slaps Fairchild, who had his head down in shame the whole time. The rest of the Japanese trainees are getting a bit of a kick out of witnessing this brow-beating. Fairchild doesn't understand much Japanese but he knows he has upset his teacher very much. The instructor sees the camera and reporters and turns back to Billy. Instructor: I knew I never should have agreed to train you. Get out of my ring. Go talk to reporters you can't even understand. [He turns his back on Billy and begins class again with the Japanese trainees as Fairchild exits the ring.] Reporter: ...Fairchild-san? ... [Billy picks his lowered, contemplating head up to look at the reporters and camera.] Reporter (via the translator): May we speak with you for a moment about the upcoming tour? Fairchild (Translated into Japanese subtitles.): ... yeah, sure. Reporter (via translator): First, may I ask about what just happened in the ring there? [Fairchild turns his head back towards the ring as the camera shoots it over his shoulder. Another trainee has made a mistake applying a Fujiwara armbar but the instructor is politely smiling and then demonstrates the proper technique to him. No browbeating like Billy.] Fairchild: Um ... [he turns back to the camera again, his head down in small thought then looks up] Sensei doesn't want me to disgrace him, being the only gaikokojin student he's ever trained, so I guess he puts more pressure on me to succeed. Reporter (via translator): Well, it certainly seems as if he holds you to a different standard than the rest of his students. Do you get taken to task often, as you did when we arrived? Fairchild: Oh yeah, tons of times. Sometimes I feel like he doesn't want me here and wants me to quit. But I can't give up my dream. I love pro wrestling too much. And even though I acheived my first big win [he smiles] last month, I still have much improvement to make. So I'll stay in the dojo as long as Sensei allows it or until I feel confident enough with my skills to leave. Reporter (via translator): You spoke of your match last month. Tell us about your first victory. Fairchild: It was awesome. I mean, I knew very little about my partner, Jason Ramsey, going into the event. We hooked up backstage before the matches and went over some strategy and stuff. And when match-time came we gelled really well together. Alot of the credit goes to him carrying me through that match. I'm still only a rookie and Ryoku and Oodora are lethal. Reporter (via translator): You are teaming with Ramsey-san again on the opening night of the Coronation Tour tomorrow night. You will be facing Kenji Okano and EIYUU. Your thoughts on your opponents. Fairchild: Well, I was just starting at the dojo here when Okano was about ready to graduate. The guy has always been a total prick, quite honestly. Being a greenhorn, I had no clue how to properly defend myself in a wrestling ring and Okano took advantage of that at every turn. He really seemed to enjoy torturing me. Sensei actually had to step in a few times to get him off me. Reporter (via translator): He protected you? Fairchild: Well, I got reemed out afterwards ... as usual (said under his breath). One of those occasions actually led to Okano quitting the dojo I think. Sensei called him off me I think but Okano refused so Sensei physically stepped in and pulled him off me. They got into an argument and Okano stormed off. Last time I saw him. Reporter (via translator): So you have history with Okano? What about EIYUU? Fairchild: Nope. Never ran into EIYUU before. But from what I've seen, he and Okano are a perfect fit. They're both disrespectful assholes and I'd love nothing more than to shut them up at Coronation. Instructor: Gaijin! Get back in the ring! You need to train more if you hope to survive against Okano tmorrow. Fairchild: I think he wants me back in the ring [he points back at the ring as if to ask the reporters if he can go.] Instructor: NOW! Reporter (via translator): Yes, you best be going now. Thank you for the interview. (all said very hurredly) Instructor: Move your lazy ass Gaijin!!! [Fairchild gets back in the ring and his Sensei motions for him to lock up with one of the other trainees. He does and the trainee takes him down with the Fujiwara armbar the Sensei showed him earlier. He doesn't have it hooked quite right, so Sensei tells him to release the hold. Before Billy can get up, Sensei is on him, wrenching back hard with the Fujiwara armbar. Billy begins to scream as we see a small grin on the face of the Sensei. Fade out.] ---- (The scene opens in a busy Tokyo coffee shop. The Starbucks logo is plainly visible through the window. Jason Ramsey of the JPA is seated with Toru Nakanashi of Weekly Gong wrestling press. Ramsey is dressed in a black Armani and sips a cup of special roast as the interview gets underway. Nakanashi greets Ramsey in english and Ramsey responds in Japanese. The journalists face takes on a look of relief as he realizes that Ramsey is fluent in his native tongue and that makes the interview much easier. Nakanashi's coffee is delivered to the table and the questions begin....) Nakanashi: Thank you for joining me today. Ramsey: I am always honored when the sports press of Japan wants to speak to me. the pleasure is all mine. Nakanashi: With the announcement of the coronation tour you must be thrilled with the opportunities to showcase your skill. Ramsey: I have stated many times times before that I love this country. Since my first tour over five years ago, I have been enchanted with Japan. With it's people, it's culture, and most importantly, it's wrestling. I am very proud to be part of a fine organization such as JPA. Nakanashi: You and your partner, Billy Fairchild, face EIYUU and Kenji Okano at the opening contest of the tour. How will you prepare for this challenge? Ramsey: As I always prepare. Cardio-vascular training along with hours of tape on both men. Billy and I will also work on team work and flowing seamlessly in the ring. Nakanashi: Fairchild is a newcomer to Japan but seems to be just as excited to be here as you are. Do you see yourself as a mentor to him? Ramsey: I would never be so forward. Billy Fairchild is his own man but but I offer him my experience as he needs it. I am very selective with who I take on as my partner, and usually some one as young as Fairchild wouldn't stand up to my standards. Billy is a diamond in the rough and has an amazing fighting spirit. He is more than worthy to stand with me in the ring. Nakanashi: How do you see yourself and Fairchild standing up to EIYUU and Okano? Ramsey: Okano is a powerful and skilled fighter. He is easily my equal in the ring and he has my respect. His ability is something to train for. EIYUU is a different story... Nakanashi: How so?? Ramsey: He is reckless. EIYUU is far more Americanized than he should be for his sake. He lacks respect for anyone he faces or the tradition that JPA stands for. He is skilled, for that I respect him, but I plan on taming him and teaching him the respect he needs in this business. Nakanashi: One more question Mr. Ramsey, do you have any predictions for the Coronation tournament? Ramsey: I can't pretend to be able to pick a winner. The talent in the tourney is just too evenly matched. I will, however, tell you this. When this tournament is over and the double crown is awarded, the best wrestler in the WORLD will be holding those belts. JPA is the home of the most talented fighters on Earth. The wrestling world WILL take notice. (With that, the interview ends. Ramsey leaves a generous tip on the table, thanks Nakanashi for the chance to be heard and steps out into the busy Tokyo streets.) ---- [We open to a video montage of Tora Ryoku in training for the opening tour of the Japanese Puroresu Association. The first of a series of clip shows Ryoku jogging in a gymnasium, then to him to doing various cardiovascular exercises starting with pushups, and situps and squats. We go to another set of clips of Tora doing laps in a pool and then some weight training. Finally we find Ryoku in a ring with a sparring partner, his own partner Shiro Oodora. After several clips pass of both men perfecting their techniques we find Tora Ryoku sitting alone on the on the apron of the ring as he is talking with a news reporter who is not seen on camera. Ryoku is now wearing a JPA t-shirt and a towel over his shoulders as well as his usual ring attire.] REPORTER : Thank you Mr. Ryoku for taking the time away from your training to answer some questions. RYOKU : You are very welcome. REPORTER : The announcement of the particpants for the JPA's first official tour "Coronation" will feature the crowning of the first heavyweight champion. What are you thoughts of being included in the group of eight men who will vie for the unified J1 Rikidozan and Neo Japan heavyweight championships? [Tora sits back against the ring ropes in silence for a moment or two contemplating his thoughts before he leans forward and speaks.] RYOKU : I feel very much excited because I have been chosen to be one of the eight who will be showcasing their talents for the entire country to see. That I have the opportunity to immediately rise above the great competition, to be the standard barer and champion. I am also deeply honored to have been chosen to represent this young company in it's quest to crown it's champion. [Pausing for a second as he smiles.] RYOKU : I am quite overwhelmed by it all, I hope to make a good showing for myself and my company win or lose. REPORTER : In the first round your opponent is the "Iron Chef" Keiji Kobe a world reknowned lucha star. What are your thoughts on him? RYOKU : Kobe is the flip side of my style. Kobe is more flash and flare, high risk as opposed to myself, I am more methodical. I expect of him to represent himself, the company and his style to the absolute highest of his abilities as I will undoubtably do the same. To do anything less is a disgrace to the tradition of our sport and those who we're our predecessors. REPORTER : One final question Mr. Ryoku. Keiji Kobe claims that his mask is his ally, his protector and most of all his spirit. That it will aid him against your attack, what do you have to say to these claims? [Ryoku leans back once again in silence as he ponders his the thoughts that are wandering around in his mind. Tora now looks to the man sitting in front of him.] RYOKU : I myself know what he speaks of I wear my own mask, but unlike Kobe the spirit and power of the great and mighty tiger stays with me with or without a mask adorned to my head and face. If I must conquer both Keiji Kobe and his spirit than that is what I shall do, because as the saying the goes to "Kill a snake, you must take it's head!" Keiji Kobe bring your best fight and may your spirit be there for you when you will need it the most. REPORTER : Mr. Ryoku thank you again for the time out of your day. [With that darkness fills our scene as the roar of a tiger bellows.] ---- http://jpapuroresu.hypermart.net/ --- [name withheld] - Formerly of The Westsiders [#28, RSPWF 2000 Year End Awards Top 50 Tag Teams] - Japan Puroresu Association - http://jpapuroresu.hypermart.net/
