Thwack! Thwack! (What is that sound, you ask? Why, it's none other than "Captivating" Corey Hart, kicking the crap out of a padded bag. Today, we find the blond youngster at a small gym in Osaka. Corey wears a white, tank top, emblazoned with the "GLCW" logo, and blue, sweat shorts. He completes the look with Nike, cross trainers. Oblivious to the other patrons, Corey's focus remains on the bag, sweat glistening off of his body.) Corey: (through clenched teeth) Kenji Okano! (He unleashes a wicked, spinning heel kick.) Corey: Tasogare no Prince! (Another kick.) Corey: Christopher Kojima! (With that last name, Corey unleashes a thrust kick that sends the bag almost swinging from its bearings. Placing his hands on his hips, Corey stands back and smiles, surveying the violently rocking bag.) Corey: Not bad. Not bad at all. Now, if only I can do that to KAYO's face. I would be a happy man. (He snatches up a nearby bottle of spring water and takes a huge swig, wiping his brow. He exhales sharply before catching sight of an approaching Minako Yamaguchi, known in most wrestling circles as Princess Yamaguchi. The lithe beauty wears a white, "BLAZE" T-shirt and black, nylon, track pants. Her long, black hair falls straight down her back, tennis shoes resting on her feet.) Corey: (grins) Hey, Mina! Mina: (waves) Hi, Corey! This is the third time I've run into you this week at the gym. (smiles) Trying to kill yourself before you turn 21? Corey: (laughs) Not exactly. I've got a big match against Christopher Kojima coming up and I want to be prepared. Mina: Is this a part of that Junior Struggle that Ken was telling me about? Corey: Nah, this is just a regular match but I approach all of my matches as if they were for a title. After all, no victory is more important than another. I just hope that Kojima is ready because I'm going to come at him with everything that I've got. I know that he doesn't like me much but I personally don't have a problem with him. I think he's a pretty okay guy even if he is a tad bit too full of himself. See, that's where I'll hold the advantage. I'll be going into this thing like a normal, wrestling match while Kojima will be looking to avenge some warped vendetta. (shakes his head) Whatever. He should really watch out for that temper of his. A hot head has been the cause of more than one wrestler losing a match. Heck, I should know. I lost my temper against Kenji Okano and it cost me big time. I still have to put up with his big mouth and constant bragging! (Corey takes a seat at a nearby bench and Mina joins him.) Mina: So, how has this infamous Junior Struggle been? My brother says that it was stressing you out. Corey: (laughs) Stressing me out? Yeah, it was but I feel a lot better now. I may have lost my first two matches to no Prince and Eiji Yatsu but I'm not upset. I feel that I did my best and gave 110% in each performance. It was just a case of my opponents being a little more prepared than I was but that's going to happen in the ring. No one can win all of the time. The important thing is that I learned from the mistakes that I made, in those matches, and I'll use that knowledge to step up my game a few notches. I have faith in myself and I know that I can bounce back and win this thing. (smiles) Even some of my fellow wrestlers have commended me on my performances. Jason Ramsey said a lot of really nice things about me which was really cool. Praise is always nice but, when it comes from a wrestler that I respect as much as Ramsey, it's even better. Jason, Rick Taylor, and Billy Fairchild have done some tremendous things in the JPA. I just hope that I can live up to the expectations that he and many of the others have of me. Mina: You'll do fine, Corey. You've got a bunch of people that believe in you. Corey: Thanks. That means a lot to me and certainly helps when I'm facing wrestlers the caliber of a Billy Fairchild or Keiji Kobe. Knowing that I've got people out there supporting me and cheering me on really inspires me to go out there and give my all. Honestly, I don't know how wrestlers like Okano and his ilk do it. How can you be at the top of your game when a crowd is basically screaming for your blood and praying that you lose? Mina: (with a playful smile) Well, you could always ask him. Corey: (makes a face) Me? Ask Okano something? Ha! The only time you'll see me talking to that putz is when Satan starts peddling snow cones! (Mina giggles as Corey stands to his feet.) Anyway, I had better get back to work. I've still got the Stairmaster to tackle. Mina: (rises to her feet) Hey, I'll join you. (she links her arm in his) We can exchange hot date stories along the way. Corey: (laughs) What? I assumed Ken would have already told you how everything went. Mina: Hardly! My brother never tells me the juicy stuff. That's why I'm coming to you. So, spill and leave nothing out! A badly-hewn dummy of the JPA wrestler Super Genki Man lies on a concrete slab, a number of wires leading from his body to the jury-rigged machinery surrounding it. In the background, a hunched over Chinese man labours, going about his business, though he knows not what he is doing, or why. VIOLENT Tsuchida enters, and the aura of the room changes immediately. He strikes a noble figure, albeit one slightly damaged by years of ring wars, fighting the most unbelievable monsters the world could throw at him. Every one defeated (well, almost every one - that's a story for another time), he has now turned his attention to the JPA, and their roster of "humans". Humans, bah! He has no power over them. The ancient Chinese man - Tsuchida's manservant, Wong - turns to his master with an inquisitive look on his weathered face. Sensing his friend's puzzlement, Tsuchida speaks. "You are wondering just what we are doing, aren't you, my friend?" Wong nods in affirmation. "You remember when we fought Blob Marley, the ectoplasmic rasta voodoo man, eager to avenge the destruction of Jamaica's marijuana crops by over-zealous government officials?" Wong remembers it well, and nods once more. "I took the liberty of confiscating his maracas - not only used to pound out a thumping reggae beat, but also imbued with the power of the Loa, the ancient voodoo gods." Wong is puzzled once again. Though his aims were perhaps noble, Blob Marley had used his powers for evil. His voodoo juju had been purely malevolent. Surelt, Tsuchida with his. ". with my oath to only use my abilities for good, Wong?" interrupted the master. "You are right, my friend. I have sworn never to step over to the dark side, and that explains the machine you are operating." Tsuchida gestures to the pile of wires & transistors that sit behind Wong. "By hooking Super Genki Man - or a reasonable facsimile thereof - to my mach ine, I hope to distill the non-evil energies still contained in the misguided maracas, and imbue the Genki Man's body with them." Tsuchida's mind drifts at this point - he remembers back to his debut in the JPA, just 6 short days ago. He was defeated then, and on each subsequent appearance for the group (though he has yet to be pinned), and each time it has been Super Genki Man and Takeshi Sugiyama standing alongside him. Sugiyama is a non-factor. Fate has intervened and their paths have diverged. However, Tsuchida worries about Genki Man - a young, but talented warrior - and will do anything he can to help them succeed. Standing in their way are Jason Ramsey & Rick Taylor. The world expects defeat for the newcomers, but Tsuchida has an ace up his sleeve - voodoo! Wong signals to his master, the operation is almost complete. Now making a name for himself in the rings of the Japan Puroresu Association, an upstart puroresu promotion headed by former Neo Japan Pro Wrestling star Genji Yamato, Okano has become perhaps a bigger star now than he was at the height of his controversy-filled soccer career. Earlier this week, I spoke with Okano about his role in the JPA's current Junior Struggle tour, and unsurprisingly, he proved to be an entertaining and outspoken interview. Atheltic Magazine Nihon Weekly: Firstly, I want to thank you for allowing us to interview you in the midst of your preparation for the rigors of the Junior Struggle League. Kenji Okano (smiling): Well, I needed a bit of a break, and since you showed up on my doorstep all dressed-up, I couldn't just throw you out. AMNW: The last time Athletic Magazine Nihon Weekly ran an article on you, you had just signed a contract with the JPA. Many in the press criticized you at the time, speculating that you were simply trying to cash in on the notoriety gained by your sensational dismissal from your days on the soccer field. What do you have to say now to those who doubted you? KO: Y'know, a lot of those jealous punks were just jealous that someone as talented and charismatic like myself wasn't just going to disappear and stop reminding them of how things could have been for them if they weren't sports-geek reporters. Most of those dweebs didn't even check into the fact that I was a star amateur-wrestler in school before I signed a contract for [Nagoya] Grampus [Eight] when I turned 18. But yeah, I'm having the last laugh! AMNW: You wasted little time upon your debut in the JPA before inviting controversy once more with your provocative interviews and insulting comments about the other wrestlers. Why? KO: Everyone who knows me knows that I've never been one to hold back my feelings. That's thought to be still fairly taboo in our culture, but it just seems to me the way more people ought to be. If you think somebody's a jerk, just say it. That's my philosophy. AMNW: As I mentioned earlier, you're competing in several important Junior Struggle Tour matches in the days to come. Namely against Eiji Yatsu of the Total Attack Puroresu faction and Corey Hart. What are your thoughts on each of these competitors? KO: Ah yes, Eiji Yatsu. This guy is so insecure with himself that he feels the need to tell everyone how tough he is all the time and how he's a 20th degree blackbelt in judo or whatever. Have you seen his interviews on JPA TV? They're hysterical, I tell you. It's like, "LOOK AT ME I AM A TOUGH GUY" or something. And yeah, he is a good fighter, but so what? If he's such a tough guy, then why does he have to ride Ryo Inoue's coattails? He probably thinks I'm a joke or something because I don't roll around on mats all day while wearing a white bathrobe and pajamas. Whatever. We'll see if he's so tough at Korakuen Hall. As for Corey Hart...man, what more do I need to say? We feuded over the most inane garbage, mainly because he was all offended that I called him a no-talent gaijin. He tried to say that was racist, can you believe it? Anyway, I've beaten him in the past, and I'm sure I'll do so again. Next question. AMNW: Recently you teamed with "The Iron Chef" Keiji Kobe in a match, a man you've competed both with and against during your tenure in the JPA. What are your thoughts on possibly meeting him in the finals of the Junior Struggle League? KO: Eh...I guess that would be alright. I don't really care who I meet in the finals I just hope to prove what I already know, that I'm the best junior heavyweight in the world. I think Kobe is on pretty good terms with me now, since he ditched the goofy mask to fight Yatsu. I told him that would be a good idea a LONG time ago, when he was lowering himself by teaming with Hart and that loser We Hyun Bae, but he didn't believe me. So it looks like he's finally come around. AMNW: And how do you feel about his rival, Tasogare no Prince? KO: ...he's good. He rambles on about destiny like an escapee from an insane asylum, but he's definitely one of the best (along with myself). But as skilled as he may be, I know that I'm better. I wouldn't mind meeting him in the finals, because a win over him would let everyone know that I'm a best without a doubt. And even if I don't beat him when I win the Pan Asia Junior Heavyweight Championship, I know that I'll get a chance to do so later on. AMNW: Best of luck to you, Okano-san, and again I thank you for your time. KO: Not a problem.
