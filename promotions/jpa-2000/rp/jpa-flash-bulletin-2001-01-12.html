JPA FLASH BULLETIN 2001.1.12

12 January 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
JAPAN PURORESU ASSOCIATION FLASH BULLETIN 2001.1.12 1) Jack Shaw 2) Corey Hart 3) Iron Chef Keiji Kobe 4) Kenji Okano 5) Shinji Nakata 6) MASSIVE Akita ---- (A dark gray locker room, with Jack Shaw sitting down on a brown bench. Towel wrapped around his neck, wrestling gear on, and his infamous orange and brown Grain Grower's cap. Subtitles appear on the bottom of the screen.) JS: Well if it ain't one of these conum-condun- ah hell, one of these funny situations. I went and thought that the JPA had gone an' died. I thought I'd never get my chance at that old codger Akita again... then that old lady luck went and gave me a present. (Spitting out a mouthful of tobacco spit, Shaw shifts his weight on the bench.) JS: This match is the only thing I give a crap about. That title, well y'all in Japan know I don't give two cowpies about no titles. I want my chance at Akita, for what is this, the fifthteenth time? I dunno anymore, all the tag matches, trios, and singles matches just seem like a blur now. (Standing to his full 6'10", Shaw bends backwards, loud pops emitting from his back, prior to continuing his speech.) JS: What does matter is right here, right now. I dunno how many more chances at Akita I have left in these old bones, and I don't plan on screwing up this one. Far as I'm concerned, this one is for all the marbles. So this Saturday, the 13th of January 2001, I'll lift up these old bones outta this locker room, walk down towards that ring, and lock horns with an old rival. Something tells me there ain't too much time to savor this match, but I am for damn sure gonna enjoy it as much as I can, and that's a promise. (Shaw, never one for overly long speeches, simple stands up, and walks towards the locker room's exit.) ---- (The scene opens to a hotel in downtown Tokyo. More specifically, we find ourselves in the room occupied by "Captivating" Corey Hart. Suddenly, the front door swings open and Corey enters, a suitcase in hand. The sinewy youth wears a baby blue, turtleneck sweater and matching, blue, denim jeans. He completes the ensemble with brown boots and a book bag slung across his shoulders. His platinum blond hair is short and curled, framing his handsome face. He shuts the door behind him and places the suitcase aside, flashing the camera a warm smile.) Corey: Hey guys! As some of you may know, I just returned from an appearance in PAW. PAW is an independent wrestling promotion in the United States. My friend, Keiji Kobe, and I worked an angle for their PAW Vs Japan card. He and I even got the chance to wrestle one another for a brief moment. It was loads of fun and we both really enjoyed ourselves. Plus, it was really nice getting to spend a little time in the states. While I welcomed the chance to return to my native land and visit my family and friends, I am just as excited to be back in Japan and back with the JPA. And not a moment too soon, it would appear. Looks like I have a match scheduled against Shiro Oodora. I don't know much about Shiro, considering he's a newcomer, but he must be quite good. After all, if he wasn't a talent, he wouldn't be in the JPA. One of the things that I do know about him is that he outweighs me by quite a bit. That doesn't surprise or intimidate me. I am the smallest wrestler on the roster and I am quite accustomed to wrestling those that are larger than me. Besides, we all know that size may give someone a slight advantage but it doesn't guarantee a win. What wins matches are a wrestler's skill, determination, abilities, and, most importantly, heart. And I think that most of you can agree that the size of my heart more than makes up for what I may lack in physical size. I never give up and I never back down from a challenge. I won't stop until I've either earned a victory or given my opponent a match that he won't soon forget. Shiro, I hope that you keep that in mind for our match and I hope that you don't underestimate me. There have been others that have done the same and they all regretted it. So, I suggest that you be at your best because I promise to be on mine. I assure you that, come January 13th, whether win or lose, you will be left Captivated! (grins) Good luck, Shiro, and may the best man win. (He folds his arms across his chest.) Corey: Before I head out to the dojo, I wanted to say a couple of words about what's going on in the JPA. Billy Fairchild, I read the recent article about you and I wanted to thank you for the kind words. I think that you're a great competitor and we have a lot in common. We're both gaijin and two of the JPA's smallest grapplers. Plus, I like your style. You fight with honor, integrity, and dignity. The wrestling world would be a whole heck of a lot better if there were more people like you. If you ever need anything, feel free to let me know. It would be my pleasure to help. By the way, watch out for Okano and EIYUU. I've had experience wrestling both of those guys and they can't be trusted under any and all circumstances. Hope you and Jason Ramsey keep your eyes on those two backstabbers and give them a butt kicking for me! I know that the big news around the JPA is the big Coronation tournament. Everyone is buzzing, wondering just who's going to take the big prize. Well, it should be no surprise to know that I completely support my stable mate, Keiji Kobe. While the tourney is stacked with talent, from Ryo Inoue to Jack Shaw, I don't think any of them can compare to Keiji. He's in a league of his own. My official prediction is that Keiji beats the opposition and takes it all, starting with Tora Ryoku. Just watch and see! (He flashes a smile) I also wanted to send good luck to my other stable mate, We Hyun Bae, in his match against XYZ. You might be talented, XYZ, but your JPA debut is going to be a loss, courtesy of We Hyun. That's not a threat or promise. That's a guarantee. (looking at his watch) Well, I had better get going. I've got training to do and matches to win. See ya later! (He waves to the camera as the scene fades.) ---- (The camera opens up onto a crowded little comic book store in the heart of Tokyo. Children and teens crowd around a table at which sits the "Iron Chef" Keiji Kobe, signing autographs, posing for photos, and talking with his young fans. Over this scene is a voice over.) Reporter: What has brought these children into this shop today? What has caused them to stand in line for hours. It is not the newest video game system. It's not the latest song from the recording artiest Corn. These young children and teens are lined up to get the latest issue of their favorite wrestler/superhero action book signed by the hero himself. But which wrestler has brought this on? It's not Tiger Mask or Zebra Mask like you all may be thinking, rather it is the Mexican import, "Iron Chef!" Not to be confused with the wildly successful television program, Iron Chef deals with the fictious exploits of the Japanese Lucha star the Iron Chef. The Iron Chef comic was wildly successful in Mexico as a weekly series, when the Japan Puroresu Association brought "the Iron Chef" Keiji Kobe into their organization, the comic soon followed and began building a following immediately. We asked Kiyoshi Kariya, VP of Marketing for Battlestar comics, the Japanese publisher of Iron Chef, about the success of the Iron Chef comic. (The shot now cuts to a man in his late forties in a blue suit with a white shirt and sensible tie and thick glasses.) Kariya: It wasn't as simple as translating the Chef comics from Spanish to Japanese. The Mexican comic has to many references and characters unfamiliar to the average Japanese boy. The art work of the comic is simplistic, fitting due to the weekly publishing schedule and low pay given to the Mexican staff. We really built the comic from the ground up. Well, the Iron Chef's ring work helped spur on demand, to be sure, but the stories, the villains, the art work is all new. Reporter: So then there is little in common between the Japanese and Mexican publications. Kariya: Yes, our comic is done in the Manga style by one of the top artists in the industry, Tsutumi Sakamoto, he pencils the comic. Many buy the comic just for his artwork. We've also given more time for production, as this only is a monthly publication, so the stories are better, the colors are richer, the art is crisper thanks to our talented inkier. I think all parties involved are happy with the product. (The shot cuts to the offices of the JPA, where Genji Yamato has a big smile on his face.) Genji Yamato: Undoubtedly we've been most pleased with Kobe's success in the comic book world. For one, we don't have to pay him as much for him to make a decent living. Ha, ha, ha! No just kidding, Kobe is well paid for his work. But the exposure has been great for the JPA, we are a young company, but we'll grow into a strong one thanks to all the young viewers this comic pulls in. The comic helped to keep our name out there among the fans during some tough times, and now it's going to pay off as we head to our first major tour. (The shot shifts to the offices of Fuji TV, where Iron Chef the television series was produced, and a stern looking man in his late fifties with gray balding hair sits in front of the camera.) Reporter: But not every one seems to be as happy about the success of the Iron Chef comic. Here, the head of Fuji TV's legal Department, Chizuo Matsumoto. Matsumoto: While it's clear Battlestar comics, the JPA, and the wrestler Keiji Kobe all benefit from the use of the Iron Chef name. They have all failed to pay what we would consider a reasonable amount for use of our property. While we hope only the best for these men, we wish they would either pay us a fair sum or discontinue the use of our copyrighted name and try flying on their own wings instead of stolen ones. (The shot now cuts to the Iron Chef, decked out in a chef coat bearing the Battlestar and JPA logos on either sleeve and his trademark mask.) Kobe: I've really enjoyed the opportunity to connect with some of my younger fans. Seeing there smiling faces tells me I've reached some one with my craft. They also serve to remind me of my own children in Mexico who I miss quite dearly. Reporter: Yes, and what about your legal troubles? Kobe: Legal troubles? Reporter: It seems that Fuji TV does not think they are getting paid a fair amount for the use of their name. They say the hights of fame to which you have soared were on borrowed wings. Kobe: Borrowed wings? Borrowed wings!?! I got to where I am not because of any TV show, but because I'm a hard worker. I've always strove to be the best, that's why I've gotten where I am. As for the compensation, well Battlestar has assured me they have handsomely compensated Fuji TV. Reporter: And have you and the JPA compensated Fuji TV? Kobe: Do Vader and All Japan compensate George Lucus? No. Did Rey Mysterio compensate Marvel? No. Do Red and Blue Power Ranger compensate who ever made that abomination of a television show? No. Why then should I? Besides, myself and the JPA gave rights to produce my likeness in a comic book with the understanding they would pay Fuji TV a fair share. Reporter: One last question do you think you stand a chance of beating out the great Tiger Mask? Kobe: You mean the masked Tora Ryoku? Yes, I do, for he plans to battle a snake on his belly, not an eagle in the air, so his strategy is flawed. He plans to attack the unattackable, my spirit. It is if he planned to empty the ocean with a clam shell, simply put he does not have tools to get the job done against me. So yes, I will beat him. Reporter: No I was referring to the Tiger Mask comic book, do you think you'll ever out sell it? Kobe: If I do, I must create a legend even grander than the great Tiger Mask's. The first step will be to beat Tora Ryoku, because defeating him leads to the J1 Rikidozan/NJPW Unified Heavyweight Titles. If I win those, I have a good shot of matching Tiger Mask's success. So far I have some what of a undefeated streak myself. Let us pray it can continue. (The shot cuts to the reporter standing with a microphone in front of him. He has a pencil mustache and dark hair slicked back.) Reporter: I just wanted to show you the cover of this comic, it does show the Chef battling a man in a tiger mask. I'm not sure if this is directed at Tiger mask from Battlestar Comics or Tora Ryoku from the Iron Chef, but watch out, because both of them are ready for a war. The comic is in stores now, and you can see the Iron Chef as well as other stars of the JPA in action this weekend as they kick off the 13th at Tokyo Differ Ariake for the CORONATION Tournament. This has been Hiro Ashara, back to you in the studio. ---- SOCCER: OKANO DISRUPTS NATIONAL TEAM AWARDS CEREMONY Tokyo Sports Wire TOKYO - Japanese soccer player turned wrestler Kenji "KAYO" Okano was once again the center of controversy on Wednesday, when he intruded on an invitation-only awards banquet honoring Japan's national soccer team. Calling the players "a team of losers" and their French coach Philppe Bergeroo 'a gaijin meddler' before being removed by security, Okano further worsened his chances of getting his lifetime suspension from the game. The former J-league young player of year was banned for knocking a referee out after a disputed call. He is now a wrestler for the new Japan Puroresu Association, where he competes as a middleweight. ---- [scene: an aerial view of a coastal area. Shinji Nakata stands atop a cluster of rocks, his eyes focused upon the watery expanse. Without diverting his gaze, he speaks.] Nakata: My name...is Shinji Nakata. Son of the late champion Naoki. Some of you might remember me from my brief stint as a light-heavyweight in Neo Japan. [he turns to look at the camera, the fighting spirit evident in his eyes.] I returned to puroresu for one reason: to continue my family's legacy of warriors. Until I become a champion like my father, I will not rest. Prepare yourselves, Japan Puroresu Association, because my chapter in the Nakata legacy begins anew here. [with that, Nakata bows and the camera pans across the coast before fading out...] ---- [The scene we come upon is a normal one for this part of the world, in this part of the business. Sitting in a Tokyo restaurant, empty bowls for rice and trays for sushi sit on a table. Another one, half full, sits in front of a very large man, hair pulled back like a samurai warrior, or sumo 'rikishi.' He is also dressed very well, in a custom tailored suit to fit his large frame. His face is older, somewhat beaten, as if he has seen many many battles. And so he should have, for this is the legend…Masashi Akita. [Sitting across from him, with a mere cup of coffee to refresh him, sits a typical looking reporter for the Weekly GONG. He adjusts his glasses, and switches on his pocket microcassette recorder….] Weekly GONG Reporter (WGR): Akita-sama… despite the problems that Japan Puroresu Association have had, they are going through with the CORONATION tour beginning this weekend. MASSIVE Akita: Ee, so desu. WGR: Yamato-san has chosen to place you in the tournament for the Double Titles…any comment? Akita: Of course. I am honored that a man of Yamato-san's stature, and his place in this business, still considers me to be a worthy contender. The fans, they still chant my name. Elsewhere, in America, there are those that feel past a certain point you are "too old." I wonder if I am almost there. I am not who I once was… WGR: Aie, Akita-sama, that's not true! Akita: Please. Your flattery is most enjoyable. But the reality settles in. Myself, and my opponent, we stretch back to the days of the old United Japan Pro Wrestling…are you too young to remember that? WGR: Almost but not quite. Of course I've seen videos… Akita: Hai. Then you have seen myself, and Shaw, in our younger, more glorious days… and I do not know if I….*we* can do it again for the crowd at Differ Ariake. Does MASSIVE Akita have what it takes to be a reigning champion once again, in this day and age? Shaw, powerful tho he is, still feels the pain of battle throughout the years. I…am beginning to. This may be my last chance to be a champion in a serious setting. WGR: I can quote you on that, yes? Akita; Yes, of course. I have wondered if myself or Shaw are worthy, are deserving even, of competing for the Double Titles. Of having the chance. All that I know is….Shaw and I are old foes, yet we are brothers in some respects. We know what we could do once before…now it is up to us, in front of this new generation, to see if we can do it still. [Akita pops an ebi (shrimp) sushi into his mouth, and swallows] Akita: Time moves like that. You are content, you are in your place….but sooner or later it swallows you whole, at once. Jack Shaw, and MASSIVE Akita, are almost at that point. However, I know I have many battles left to fight. And Shaw is but the first. WGR: IS that all? Akita: Save to say…Shaw is but the first. And I will be here, in the ring, as long as the JPA needs me…. WGR: I see. Thank you Akita-sama! Akita: Hai! [Click. The reporter stops his tape.
