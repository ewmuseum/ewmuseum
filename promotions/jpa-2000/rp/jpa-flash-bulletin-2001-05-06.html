JPA FLASH BULLETIN 2001.5.6

6 May 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
JAPAN PURORESU ASSOCIATION FLASH BULLETIN 2001.5.6 1) Iron Chef Keiji Kobe 2) Cyrus Williams 3) Eiji Yatsu 4) Billy Fairchild 5) Kenji Okano 6) Corey Hart 7) Shinji Nakata 8) MASSIVE Akita 9) Kathakano ---- (The camera opens up onto the JPA junior star and Lucha Leibre legend, the Iron Chef, kneeling in the middle of a room in traditional Japanese decor, tatami covers the floor, and rice paper screens cover the walls. The Iron Chef has on a full length orange polo shirt and black slacks in addition to his trademark orange and black mask. Kobe begins to address the camera looking into the camera with his unblinking black fabric covered eye. As he speaks into the camera the shot slowly zooms into one point on his face.) Kobe: Inoue! I have stood your insults you shouted at me from your tabloid MMA magazines. I have suffered through your long winded triads. I've heard your cries about how your "sport" is being strangled by the oppressive rules of the JPA. I have been forced to listen you whine that Japanese were not allowed shots at the Double titles while you run from Akita. (At this point the camera has zoomed into the blackness of the fabric that covers his right eye. But the camera can not see through the fabric, all it can see is unending night. As Kobe speaks the darkness begins to lighten.) I have been force fed so much of your crap I've almost come to enjoy it from the humor perspective. But just as your lies begin to amuse me, you begin to perpetuate one about how your boryokudan called T.A.P. are the only warriors in the JPA, and I find myself ready to hate you all over again. (Finally darkness has become a shadowy picture of two men struggling, vying from position. One slams the other, and the cycle repeats itself as Kobe continues to speak.) I pour all my energy, all my life force, my very essence into each and every match, and you call it a shame, a fake. You aren't just questioning our leader any more, you challenge the work, the blood, and sweat of every man in this company. You and that kobun whore Yatsu have the nerve to claim that you are greater than every one else in this company! You have the unfettered hubris to claim you are the only true athletes among us! (The shadow picture morphs into an explosion of color showing a clip of the Iron Chef squaring off against Tasogare no Prince) You seem to think your kobun's dedication to the craft is greater than my own. (The clip morphs into a still of Jack-saw being carried after his injury.) You believe your pain is more legitimate than any one else's, and you then mock the misfortune of Shaw-san. (The still wipes into a shot of Masashi "MASSIVE" Akita winning the Double titles from Inoue) You speak of a skill greater than Akita's, but you scurry from him like the cockroach you are. (The shot changes to a shot of Kobe's head in darkened room of the establishing shot) In silence, I have suffered the lies you spread about me in your tabloids, but I will not stand for the blasphemy you've brought against my company . Do you truly want a real fight? In your heart of hearts do you want to do away all pretenses of sportsmanship? Yes? Well that suits me just fine. When I meet in Tokyo Differ Ariake with your worthless kobun, it won't be a match, it won't be a fight, it will be a duel. Two men will enter, and only one shall leave. (As Kobe continues to address his opponent the shot dollies past the Iron Chef's head over his shoulder, and out the sliding door behind him into a small garden pond with a fountain. The music of water flowing can be heard over the Iron Chef's voice which remains the dominate sound.) You must be over joyied hearing this, eh, Yatsu. After all I'm a Luchador, a dancer, a fake while you are the shooter. Remember this though, Inoue can't teach you how to fight. Yatsu, you seem to think if you just learn a stance, practice you kata, and be a good boy you'll be a great fighter, isn't that right? You've no doubt have a good technique, maybe even great style, but you know nothing of a warrior no michi. Even with all your training, all your practice, you've never fought a man that could hurt you, and will if given the chance. Study fighting all you like, Yatsu, but until you've been in one, you'll never understand it. Learning to fight in a Dojo is like a man trying to know water by having some one explain the concept to you. No matter how detailed the description you'll never know water. A description won't quench a man's thirst. Descriptions aren't something you can wash your face or feet with, only real water will do. I know the waters of a real fight, Yatsu, far better than you do. While I may not understand all it's chemical properties, but I know water, for I've tasted it. And I know what it is to fight, for I've been fighting all my life. (The camera whips around in a quick 180 degrees to show the face of Kobe speaking again.) I know what it's like to want to win, not for points, or prizes, or pride, but for survival. I've faced men who've wanted nothing more than to cripple. "Who are these men," you ask, "more masked Mexican clowns?" Yes, I've had my share struggles with other Luchadors like Diablo Verde, Super Víbora, and Loco Muchacho, but I've squared off in battles with men the caliber of Rick Taylor, too. I've even faced you great master in a in a battle where he saw fit to pull no punches or honor any rules in his pursuit to cripple me. Can you say you've fought at that level, Yatsu? Can you say you've taken the very best your oyabun had to offer? Can you say you stood up to all his rage, killer instinct, and fury and gotten up the next day to continue on with your work? I thought not. I know the passion of a fight. I know what it is to struggle for a win. I've been in matches where I'd rather lose the use of my leg rather than admit defeat. I've fought matches where both sides would not be happy until the other was carried out on a stretcher or in a body bag. That spirit, that passion, is the heart and the soul of a fight, and you can't learn that in any text book, class room, or Dojo. You've got to experience it first hand and you will, Yatsu, very soon, when you face me. Make no mistake, I hate you, Yatsu. I hate your whore oyabun, and I hate the boryokudan you stand with, and when we square off I will bring the passion I spoke of. I'll bring all that passion, rage, and fury I have down on your head, Yatsu. I promise you this, Yatsu, you'll learn more about the true nature of a fight in five minutes with me in a match than you did in five years of training. When I am finished schooling you, Yatsu, you will know water, and you will know what the true flavor of defeat is. And Inoue, when you come to second that sheep kobun or yours, be sure to bring some aspirin, not for him, but for your. Because when you see that this masked clown you mocked so well truly is as good as he says.. When you see all your work, tactics, and training is for naught against real fighting spirit... You'll know I am right, you'll be in more pain than you've ever had to deal with in your life. (The shot fades to white.) ---- *Warning, this following flash contains strong language.* [The scene fades into the local Tokyo (see, us ignorant Americans really only know one of three cities. Tokyo for our products, Nagasaki for the Bomb, and Hiroshima for the Bomb. Gaijin is Ichiban!) Radisson Hotel. We zoom into a top room where a dirty looking man in lounging in a bed, drinking some Old Crow whiskey.] V/O: We here at the JPA do not condone nor authorize whatever words come out of Cyrus Williams' mouth. [The man, now identified as new signee Cyrus Williams perks up when the camera is near him.] Cyrus Williams: Well, hello hello hello~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~!~! I've seen and done it all. Wrestled in Europe, Puerto Rico, America, Thailand, Australia, Mexico, and even China, but, there is a time in a wrestler's life when you escape to the country where wrestling is regarded as the top profession, Japan. When I first started wrasslin', about 8 years ago, I came here, because I knew my s**t. I knew that coming here would automatically give me a boost in ability and namefactor. And when I started training, I got my ass kicked. Many many many times. Everyone from Akira Taue to Giant Baba to Liger had seen me in their schools or toiling in the slums of their federations for 3 long years. And when I came back to my native United States, I came back with a vengeance, and ability that has yet to be matched. [Cyrus gets up from the bed, wearing a pair of workout shorts and a Dead Kennedys t-shirt.] Cyrus: For the past 3 years, I've won many many titles, wrestling all over the world, but have yet to maintain a steady career in Japan. That's why, with the right connections, I have signed on the dotted line. And I couldn't be happier. My first match in JPA though, brings a lot of history with it. [A screen flashes with the likenesses of Cyrus, Taylor Threat, and Christopher Kojima, the six man team.] Cyrus: My partner, Taylor Threat and I go back a long way. Both enemies, and friends. For you see, in a federation called TCW, he and I did not like each other. But, hey, since then, I've grown to respect the f**ker. The only man that I know, at the age of 18 (or something like that), has won multiple world heavyweight titles. [The screen shows a montage of clips, most notably Taylor Threat flanked in gold title belts.] Cyrus: As for Christopher Kojima, he does not have my respect. I neither know, nor care to know why he's teamed with us. But, oh f**king well. I'll tell him this though. Cross us, and you f**king die. Its simple as that. [The screen fades back to Cyrus.] Cyrus: As for our opponents, I'll make sure that I don't permanently cripple them, seeing as they are new here as well. JPA, I'm looking foward to running roughshod in here. Soon enough, that Doubles Title is coming around this waist. Good f**king bye. ---- [The screen fades into a black screen. The letters "T.A.P." appear.] Voice Over: The war for respect is on. Victory will be ours. [It then changes into the scene of Yatsu and Inoue running in the hills around the Steel Wind Dojo. After a few seconds it cuts to scenes of Inoue and Yatsu laying in strikes to each other and Eiji and Kangyo grappling on the mat. Finally it comes to Yatsu and Riki Fudo sitting inside the Steel Wind Dojo. Fudo is wearing a dark blazer, while Yatsu is in work out gear consisting of a white, long-sleeve T.A.P. t-shirt and red adidas pants.] Fudo: Hello, I'm Riki Fudo here with Eiji Yatsu who is in final training sessions before he begins is quest for the Pan Asian Jr. Heavyweight title. Yatsu, what have you done in these sessions to ready yourself for the Junior Struggle League? Yatsu: As Inoue has reported before, he and Kangyo have been working with me to finally rid myself of the lucharesu impurities that have hurt me in the past. While I am not completely cleansed of them, I have come very close to the Pure Fight I so desire. Inoue has been trying in particular to improve my striking skills, while Kangyo has helped me keep my grappling skills up. Fudo: Tell the JPA fans, who may not have heard the first time, again why you decided to face off against Shoji Suzuki only about a week before the start of the Junior Struggle League. Yatsu: I was approached by TOP officials to originally join the company, but my commits to T.A.P. prevented me to enter, but I jumped at the chance to fight Suzuki. As you may already know, I was a Suzuki fanatic when I first entered puroresu, but that fetish died when I met Inoue. However, the want to face off against my former idol was too resisting. In addition, Inoue and I believed that wrestling Suzuki would be a good warm up for the JSL and would show how much I really have progressed. Fudo: TOP has mentioned how they'd like to run a rematch, what do you think of this? Yatsu: Suzuki is a great competitor and I'd be honored to face him again. However, it will probably have to wait until after the JSL. By the time we fight again, it may be for the Pan Asian Jr. Heavyweight crown. Fudo: Do you have any intentions to wrestling for any other companies beyond that of T.A.P., TOP, and JPA? Yatsu: There is always the possibility. There are several men I'd like to face in Oita Pro, but for right now the JSL, JPA, and T.A.P. will be my priorities. Oita Pro and TOP will have to wait, possibly forever, until I wrestle for them again. Fudo: Hai. Let's move onto the Junior Struggle League. What are your predictions for the round robin tournament? Yatsu: First and foremost, I have full intentions to not only advance to the finals, but to win the Pan Asian Jr. Heavyweight title and not lose a match on the way. Everyone is already talking about the face off between Tasogare no Prince and Kenji Kobe [Inoue can be heard scoffing in the background.], and young Billy Fairchild playing a possible dark horse. Even though I was considered to be one of the breakout wrestlers of last tour, I don't believe people are giving me enough credit. Victory will be mine. Fudo: You mentioned the "Iron Chef." What comments do you have to say about your up coming match up at the tour opener? Yatsu: I don't know what else I can say at this fool. He's notorious for not being able to back up his mouth. Many believe the JSL was built to set up around the thought that Kobe and Tasogare no Prince would be the best. I challenge that. I don't believe Kobe will reach the final contest. He will fall to me, as he has twice to Inoue already, and he will struggle against all he faces. Lucharesu seems to be the popular trend in the junior division, even more popular than strong-style. Pure Fight will triumph here and lucharesu will drop to the bottom. Fudo: So you're saying that Kobe will be completely unsuccessful in the JSL? Yatsu: He will win some matches, but in the end only one man will be successful. Only the wrestler that is able to capture the Pan Asian crown won't be branded a loser. I will be that man. Defeating Kobe will only be the start. Victory will be mine. [The camera fades out.] ---- [Fade in from black to a shot from inside an airport looking out at the runway with a plane landing. A graphic comes up on the bottom of the screen that says it is May 5th. Cut to a shot of the gate inside the terminal where Billy fairchild is making his way through. He is wearing a black leather sports coat, a green silky shirt opened over a black tank top, black jeans, and black Magnum boots. Other accesories such as a silver chain of beads around his neck, studded belt, and black shades complete the ensemble of this confident young man who appears to be coming into his own. He stops to talk with the JPA camera crew and reporter a bit:] Reporter: Fairchild-San, may we have a word with you? Fairchild: Absolutely. Reporter: First of all, how was your trip? Fairchild: All things considered, it went very well. I took some time to go home and rest up after the last tour, came back to train for the Junior Struggle tour with Jason Ramsey, then it got postponed so I flew back to the states to try and center myself. This tour is a big event for me. All my time as a fan was spent dreaming about this moment. The moment when I'd be able to compete in an event like this, like all my idols before me have. Reporter: So have you been training at home in Oregon as well? Fairchild: Absolutely. I've always tried to keep my body in peak physical shape, but going home to Oregon allowed me to get my mind and spirit in shape as well. Which is a necessity going into a tour like this with so many demanding matches. I'll say it right now; the winner of this tournament will NOT be the smartest wrestler, the most in-shape wrestler, or the wrestler with the most heart. It's going to be the wrestler that can tie all three together more than the other guys. Mind, body, and soul; that combination is going to win this tournament. Reporter: How do you feel about your opponents in the league specifically? One by one. Fairchild: Well, the first person that springs to mind is Iron Chef. That guy has to be considered top of the heap right now as far as the JPA jr's go. I mean, he was the only jr. allowed into the Twin Titles tournament, and there has to be a reason why. His bread and butter is lucha libre, I can go there. When provoked, he can pull out some strong style offense, I can go there as well. His main edge over me is his experience. Mine over him is his unfamiliarity with me and his own ego. I like Chef, but his ego often gets the better of him I've noticed. Next, let's talk about Iron Chef's rival, Tasogare no Prince. He's going to be my first opponent in the league and I'm looking forward to it. Tasogare is so incredibly fluid in his style. The main key to overcoming Tasogare is mental. Staying one step ahead of him, feeling where he's going to be before he gets there. I've been told I have a natural talent for this. Counter wrestling is my strong suit. So this should be an incredible battle of wits between myself and Tasogare. An epic battle fought on the stage of the mind. Now, my rival, Kenji Okano. We have alot of similarities, Okano and I. I've heard us referred to as mirror images on a couple occasions but the more we compete, the more we differentiate from each other I think. Okano is going with what brought him to the dance, his lethal kicks and striking power. While I'm progressing more towards a well-rounded technical style with emphasis on submissions. All I'm going to say is it's easier to counter a wild kick into a tight submission than it is to counter a good submission hold with a kick that is anything more than a minor annoyance. Speaking of submissions, let's talk about Eiji Yatsu. Yatsu's judo background used to scare me. I freely admit that my weakness is shoot style offense. But I've been busting my ass to get better at defending myself and figuring out counters to shoot style moves. This is evident in my first singles encounter with Yatsu. I know it was a time limit draw, but when you really analyze it, he lost. He was so dissappointed with himself that he failed to beat me while I was estatic about just hanging in there with him. I consider that a moral victory on my part. Next time we meet, I'll be even better than I was last time. I'll have some confidence this time. I'll be willing to take a chance and hit a big match-ending move instead of just playing defense all night. Add to that all the heat between Inoue's T.A.P. group that Yatsu is a part of and The Alliance, and you have a nitro .v. glycerine like match-up. I get along well with Corey Hart and am even tagging with him on multiple occasions this tour, but suffice it to say, I'm very confident about my chances against him. I won our first singles meeting and I've only gotten better since then, by leaps and bounds some would say. Corey on the other hand, I don't know how much Iron Chef has been able to teach him since Chef's always going off on a tangent by himslef it seems. He also seems a little too brash in the ring; too risky. I understand that is his style, being a smaller guy, but I think he would improve his game greatly if someone would teach him how to slow it down a bit and use some varrying techniques instead of always having to rely on the high-flying. So again I'll say I like Corey, but I think I'm just a tad better than him in the ring right now. And Lastly, Kathakano. I admit I know very little about him. And since he's my second opponent in the league tournament, I'm going to be keeping a close eye on his matches this week. Hopefully I'll be able to find a chink in the armor that will allow me to go into our match with some sort of gameplan. Reporter: Well, I think that about wraps it up ... wait, what about your match tomorrow? You are facing a man by the name of Red Tiger. Fairchild: [Fairchild takes a deep breath] Well, I know absolutely nothing about Red Tiger. Um, I have no idea what I'm up against so I'll just have to come in at the top of my game tomorrow night and be prepared for absolutely anything he could throw at me. I feel better than I ever have right now about my skills. I'm physically fit, mentally prepared, and spiritually focused. This tour is my moment to shine. I don't plan on messing that up with a loss the first night in. Reporter: Thank you for your time Fairchild-San. Good luck to you. Fairchild: [Walking off] Sayonara. ---- [Scene: The camera opens up to a wide angle shot of an outdoor jacuzzi. We see Kenji "KAYO" Okano and an attractive young woman drinking champagne and conversing while sitting in the jacuzzi, appearing to get comfortable with each other. Okano is wearing a pair of black swimming trunks, while his female companion sports a dark blue two-piece bikini. Okano leans over to whisper something in the lady's ear, and after he does so she abruptly slaps him and storms out angrily, leaving Okano with an obviously confused look on his face.] Okano (shouting): What's so wrong with that?!? Your sister didn't seem to have a problem with it! [At this moment, Okano notices the camera's presence and motions for it to come over. Getting a close look at KAYO, it is fairly apparent that he is slightly inebriated. He smiles, seemingly in good humor about what has just transpired.] Okano: Women...what can I say? [Okano then takes another sip from his champagne glass, and stares at the sky for a moment.] Cameraman: Er, Mr. Okano? Okano (nodding): Yeah, that's me. [an awkward silence passes before the cameraman clears his throat and makes another attempt.] Cameraman: Mr. Okano, I'm from the JPA, and I'm here to get your thoughts about your upcoming match with The Great Tokaio against Ryo Inoue and Naoto Kangyo of T.A.P. as well as your involvement in the Junior Struggle League. Okano: Ohhhhh, right, like you used to do every week? Yeah, I'm just a little out of it right now. So, uh...where do I start? Cameraman (sounding somewhat frustrated): Wherever you'd like, Mr. Okano. [Okano smiles to himself and nods at the cameraman, stroking his goatee.] Okano: That's right, anywhere I'd like...because I am the former J-League Young Player of the year, you know. Ooookay, um, so I'm partnered with Tokaio now? Are you sure that's right? That's weird. But, y'know, he's pretty good for what he is...even if he is sort of ridiculous from time to time, unlike me. And then about T.A.P. or whatever, yeah...Inoue and the Kangyo guy are tough shooters and all, but I'm the best junior in the world! I don't really care about Inoue and his way-too-serious "shooto is better than puro" junk. If I have to sit through another interview of him saying the exact same thing, I think I'll shooto myself....get it? [Okano chuckles to himself while the cameraman groans.] Okano: And now, about the Super J Cup... Cameraman: Junior Struggle League. Okano: Whatever...well, it seems to me as though they could have just called it "KAYO embarrasses a group of overrated losers to win a championship", but nevermind that. Anyway...okay, who else is participating, again? Cameraman: Iron Chef Keiji Kobe, Tasogare no Prince, Kathakano, Eiji Yatsu, Billy Fairchild, and Corey Hart. Okano: Geez, where do I begin? Okay, there's Kobe, and it looks like he's going to be busy with his rivals no Prince and Yatsu. I still kind of like his style, though, but I'll beat him anyway. Tasogare no Prince...I can't even come up with words to describe him. He's not bad in the ring, but outside he's a total goofball. And then we have Billy Fairchild, or as I like to call him, "the idiot responsible for me getting pinned in a tag match against Kobe and no Prince because he sucks as a tag partner". I haven't forgotten that, buddy. Oh, it also looks like I'll get to face off against MY BEST FRIEND IN THE WORLD Corey Hart again! Yatsu...Kathakano, they're just unfortunate victims of the fact that I'm the best junior in the world and that there's absolutely NOBODY who can beat me fair and square. Yeah...okay, I'm done. Cameraman: Aren't you forgetting something? Okano: Whoa, you're right... [Okano smiles and flashes a peace sign, then flips off the camera. The scene fades to black.] ---- (The scene opens to the streets of Tokyo on a dark, starry night, the moon peeking from behind the clouds. The camera catches the flashing lights and neon signs of some of the various shops and establishments before landing on two young men walking along the street, a light drizzle falling from the heavens. Upon closer inspection, the men are revealed as JPA wrestler "Captivating" Corey Hart and his friend, Ken Yamaguchi. Corey wears a blue, Polo, windbreaker, open to reveal a white T-shirt emblazoned with the "Sailor Moon" logo across the chest. He completes the look with baggy, blue denim jeans and Nike cross trainers. His platinum blond hair is short and spiky, framing his handsome face. The equally attractive Ken wears an oversized, red, Mecca, polo shirt and baggy, blue jeans. He completes the look with brown boots and his black hair is fashioned in stylish cornrows. The duo seems oblivious to the sights and sounds around them as they maneuver through the busy streets.) Ken: So, Mina said that I could throw the party at her place since she'll be so busy with her BLAZE work. Should be interesting to see who shows up, don't you think? (Corey, staring into the distance, just nods his head.) Ken: Well, I know Midori will be there and Ryo also said that he could make it if I don't hold it too early. He has to work that morning. If you have any friends that you want to invite, feel free. The more people, the merrier. (Ken tosses another look towards Corey who responds with the familiar vacant look and nod.) Ken: (frowns) Well, I was also thinking of inviting a bunch of aliens from the planet Neptune. I figure they could come and vaporize us all with their proton blasts. (Corey again nods, his thoughts millions of miles away. Suddenly, Ken stops and shakes his head, folding his arms across his chest.) Ken: Okay! That's it! Earth to Corey! Come in, Corey! (As if awakened from some dream, Corey stops to face Ken.) Corey: What? Ken: You haven't been listening to a word that I've said. Hell, you haven't been paying attention since dinner. It's obvious that you've either got something major on your mind or something is really bothering you. Either way, it's time that you stopped zoning out and told me what's going on. Corey: (with a sheepish grin) Sorry about that. I guess I have been a little distant tonight, huh? Ken: Distant? That's an understatement. Try remote. So, what's up? Corey: (sighs) It's the JPA. They're starting the Junior Struggle this week. Ken: (smiles) Ah, that explains everything. For a second, I thought I had done something to upset you. Corey: Nah, you didn't do anything. I've been thinking about this Junior Struggle for weeks now, since the JPA first announced it. Ken: Yeah, I remember you mentioning it a while ago. This thing must really be important to you. (The two men resume their walk.) Corey: (nods) This is my big chance to finally prove to the critics that I've got what it takes to make it with the other cruiserweights. When I first started wrestling in the States, I wasn't exactly winning raves or setting the world on fire. In fact, I was losing. Big time. The critics and insiders basically ripped me apart. They said that cruisers my size seldom made it in professional wrestling. They said that there was no way that I would ever make a name for myself or succeed. For a while, all of that got to me and I even thought about quitting. That is, until the JPA came calling. They were looking for promising cruiserweights and they saw the potential that even I couldn't see. So, they worked with me in the dojo, training me and teaching me to use my weight as a strength and not a weakness. So, this is the big test, facing some of the best cruisers that the world has to offer. It's time to show those so-called insiders that I've got the goods. Don't get me wrong. Winning the title would be great too but my main goal is putting 110% in each of my matches and proving that I'm not the "no talent" gaijin people would like to believe. Ken: Now I see why this thing has occupied your mind so much. This isn't just about some belt. This is about proving something to the doubters out there. Corey: Exactly! I'm nervous but also eager. I know that this will be difficult for me but I'm looking forward to showing everyone what I can do. And It seems my first opponent will be a definite challenge. I'll be facing Tasogare no Prince. I saw his latest promo and heard how he was writing me off as a stepping stone to the title. (shakes his head) When will these arrogant shmucks learn? Underestimating your opponent is a wrestler's worst enemy. Tasogare should know that better than anyone. He underestimated myself and "The Iron Chef" in a previous tag match and it cost him a victory. If he isn't careful, our Junior Struggle match will end with the same result. You see, I'm not coming into this match thinking that it's going to be a cakewalk. I know that no Prince is a talent. Heck, there are few men that can take Chef to the limit but he was one of them. I know what I'm in for and I'm going to prepare myself accordingly. But no Prince can't say the same thing because he's too damn arrogant. If he truly thinks that I'm just going to lay down and let him pin me, he's got another thing coming! Believing that foolishness, he'll end up with the taste smacked out of his mouth. Ken: Well, from what I can see, you hold all of the advantages. I'm sure that you'll win. Corey: (smiles) Thanks, Ken. I'll try my best and that's all that I can do. As long as I give that 110%, I'm happy. I almost feel sorry for no Prince. The poor guy has no idea what he's in for! (The two friends laugh as the scene fades.) ---- [Scene: A well-lit indoor studio. Shinji Nakata stands in front of a white background and assumes a fighting pose for a magazine photograph. The flash bulb goes off, for a split-second encompassing everything in white light. The photographer gives Shinji a thumbs-up, indicating that picture will be the last one, and the young second-generation star relaxes his stance and walks over to a nearby table.
