JPA FLASH BULLETIN 2001.2.2

2 February 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
JAPAN PURORESU ASSOCIATION FLASH BULLETIN 2001.2.2 1) MASSIVE Akita 2) Jason Ramsey 3) Press Release: Ryo Inoue 4) Yuzo Moriboshi 5) XYZ 6) The Great Tokaio 7) Corey Hart 8) Jack Shaw ---- "Watashi wa….Akita Masashi desu…." [Fade up. Night. IN a large, flowing overcoat, hair not quite tied back as usual….we see, amidst the sodium lights of the streetlamps outside Ryogoku Kokukigan in Tokyo, the contender for the Double Crown, MASSIVE Akita, walking slowly, pensively, thoughtfully, around the structure of the arena. [Occassionally he pauses, to look up, and think. His aging face, his experienced eyes….gazing out at the hall. He stops fully now, noticing the camera. He turns, and faces it directly.] My name is Masashi Akita. For most of the past 20 years, I have been known as MASSIVE Akita. For the past 30 years, I have been a wrestler, in one form or another. Before becoming a professional wrestler, I was 'rikishi'…sumo. Trained in a fighting art, the ancient one, Japan's *national sport*…. Ryo Inoue. Tomorrow…amongst scores of fans….the two of us will fight one another, for the honored accolade of being the J1 Rikidozan/Neo Japan Pro Wrestling Unified Heavyweight champion. To earn the right to be decorated with the Double Titles. Inoue, as one who was once part of the very promotion that unified those championships, I expected better of you. To proclaim, you are the ONLY one deserving, EVER of holding these belts. In the long, storied history of the J1 titles, you do every man who has worked, fought, bled, toiled for them a dishonor. Inoue…some time ago, I praised you, and praised your shooto style. However, I also said this: "It is not Akita's way." And when I said this, Inoue, it was not meant as a disservice to your "shooto" way of fighting. Merely, that I did not feel it was right, or natural, for me, after lo these many years. And it comes down to more than technique, Inoue. It also comes down to experience. Inoue….as I said, before coming to puroresu, bck in the *1970s*, I trained and practiced in sumo wrestling. An art that is far and away older, and more developed, than your shooto. My number of years in the puroresu rings dwarfs yours. Indeed, I daresay you grew up watching those such as myself, Akazi, Shaw… You portend to tell me, now, you are the only one deserving of these titles? That is your view. Here is mine. [Clears his throat] You say it matters not that I beat you in team competition. Fine. I will have to beat you in singles. You say your way is the only way, the superior way. Fine. I will have to show you where your shooto fails, as well as succeeds. You say you are the only one deserving of holding the Double Titles. Fine… I will have to show you the error in that way of thinking. Until tomorrow, Inoue-san, soo da sayonara. And may your style serve you well…and may you hope it overcomes me. [Akita walks away, and we fade….] ---- [Scene opens in the gated off parking area of the Ryogoku Sumo Hall in Tokyo. Jason Ramsey climbs out of the JPA shuttle van that transported he and his wife to the end of tour show. Ramsey kisses his Mrs. and gives her a light pat on the backside as she is escorted inside to her seat for the show by a couple of the younger "dojo boys". Ramsey proceeds to the locker room area entrance and is met at the door by Yuzio Tomizawa, the talent relations officer for the JPA. ] Tomizawa: I'm glad to see you've brought your wife tonight. It promises to be a fantastic card. Ramsey: I had to drag her out tonight. My matches in Japan scare her..... Tomizawa: SCARE HER? WHY? Ramsey: Well, I don't have to tell you that the style here is way more intense than in the US. I mean, when was the last time you saw a back drop driver on American cable? Tomizawa: I understand, she doesn't want to see you get hurt. Ramsey: Oh she's seen me get hurt dozens of times! She doesn't want to see me get crippled! Tomizawa: She likes the matches? Ramsey: She loves the matches, she always has liked puroresu. It's more honest, you know? Tomizawa: That's why I'm here! By the way, the reason I met you here is to ask you if we should expect any problems between you and Jeremy Knyte tonight. I saw hs little "skit" and and wondered how you felt about it. Ramsey: The only history that Knyte and I share is ancient history as far as I'm concerned. While I was getting World title shots and beating the top names, he was busy jobbing to everyone with a pair of boots. He briefly belonged to the DWA stable that I was a founding member of. The DWA at one point was the most powerful group in American wrestling. He joined as the group was in it's final decline. He simply wants to try to make a name for himself by picking a fight. I'm not going to jump at what little bait he's offering. I'm better than he is and he knows it. If there is any trouble tonight, he will have to come find it. Tomizawa: I'm glad to hear that as will be Yamoto-san. Speaking of which, he and I would like to get with you Monday morning to sign your contract for the next tour. Ramsey: Consider it done. By the way, has Fairchild signed on for the next tour yet? Tomizawa: I believe he has. Why? Ramsey: Well, he seems a little down lately and I was hoping we'd get another tour to work together. He seems to do pretty well when we work together and I want to see him get his confidence back. A win tonight will do him alot of good. Tomizawa: SHOICHI and Kataka tonight. It should be a good match. Ramsey: It always is in JPA, that's why I'M here. I'm going to be extra focused tonight. We have a big crowd in front of t.v. cameras so I'm going to make sure that Bill and I end up with a mark in the win column. Tomizawa looks at his watch and makes a mental note then looks back up to Ramsey. Tomizawa: I have to get to the broadcast table. 15 minutes until we go on. Good luck tonight, Jason. Ramsey: Thank you Tomizawa-san, I'll see you monday morning. [With that, Ramsey steps into the gaijin locker room and the scene fades. ] ---- To: Genji Yamato, President of Japan Puroresu Association From: Ryo Inoue cc: Baseball Magazine Sha, Weekly Gong, PNM I feel that the statements I made by my trainers were misinterpreted by JPA President Genji Yamato. My goal, the driving force in my career has been to win, hold and successfully defend the J1 Rikidozan Double Championship. Yamato-san has taken offense to my comments, misinterpreting my desire and comments. I did say that I will be the first person to hold the J1 Rikidozan belts that truly "deserves it". I make no apologies for that statement. However there have been those holders of the Double Titles that were indeed worthy champions. But none were true "fighters". None knew the tre spirit of the "fight". I do, it has been my overriding goal to perfect my understanding and mastery of the "fight", to bring people what they really want. What these "wrestling" fans want is "shooto". I respect Massive Akita but I intend to make his "Strong Style" look weak. If Yamato-san has a problem with that then he can try to take away the J1 Rikidozan titles from me in a title match. I am saying plainly that I will win the Double Titles Yamato-san and if you question my fighting spirit then please feel free to be the man I make my first successful defense against. I will not be beaten , I will bow to no one. Ryo Inoue Steel Wind Dojo 2/2/2001 ---- [Start.] [The muscular body of Yuzo Moriboshi sits on a long, wooden bench inside the abandoned Sentaro dojo. His shoulders lean back and rest against the cold, white metal of the long row of lockers once used frequently. He's barefoot, his abdomen flexed naturally as he's somewhat arched. Yuzo begins to undo the tape wrapped around his hands, his nimble fingers picking it apart.] [After successfully getting all the tape off of his right hand, he throws it to the ground in front of his business suit which is slung over a coat hanger. He doesn't bother with the left hand, right away at least. He slides forward, his palms pressing into the oak under his weight. Sweat lines his forehead and jaw, even more slowly travelling down his chest and rock hard biceps.] [His thin lips press together, his blue eyes a picture of immortality. His gaze is unwavering. He smiles dramatically, crossing his arms over his chest.] "Welcome to Sentaro." [His lips curve unevenly, a strange smirk for no apparent reason.] "It's a shame, as I was getting rather good in Neo-Japan. Not anything on no Prince's level, but I was coming around. [He shrugs slowly.] "It's here, in this dojo, where I was first trained. It's a lengthy story and I've decided I should just get it done and over with now. If not, it'll remain a focal point, something I don't wish for it to be." [He unfolds his arms, turning the inside of his elbow to the camera. The word "Sentaro" is scrawled in lettering across his flesh. Scarring around the edges gives this portion of his skin a purple tinge.] "At the age of fifteen I was brought to this same spot. I was told to sit down and listen. I was so young, and so naďve. In front of me stood four suit-wearing men. They all had the smell of liquor and cigar smoke on their bodies. I was interested, as anyone would be when they were promised the greatest training facility in Tokyo. I immediately wanted more information." "And so they told me their story." " Not long before then they invested in a corporation known as Sentaro. It was built from the ground up by some of Japan's most prominent figureheads, mostly stockholders with nothing better to do with their money, but to invest it into puroresu. This entire situation began with one man and his dream of taking puroresu to America and transforming Japan into the world headquarters for a continental wrestling organization. To do so he needed funding, support, and most of all a pawn." "Someone good enough to go to America and defeat everyone. Someone good enough to destroy all competition in Japan. That's where I came in. They didn't want an already successful fighter because someone like that would be smart enough to decline such an offer, or to demand more money. They weren't stupid men, not at all. So you see why they contacted me. I was the big time in few local dojos, fighting matches here and there. They didn't need anyone good, just someone with the basic knowledge needed. So they scouted me, and informed me." "Back to the promise." "I was promised the greatest sensei money could buy. They even told me they had a super facility where I could train for as long as I liked. A teacher of the caliber they could 'buy' would make me stronger, more agile, and a solid competitor in no time." [Yuzo wipes his brow with his forearm.] "All they wanted in return was a slice of my future profit, and to sign me as an official representative of Sentaro. I'd wear Sentaro tights. Sentaro shoot-fighting gloves. Sentaro boots. Sentaro shirts. Sentaro suits. I would represent them until I retired. It didn't sound bad. It didn't sound corrupt. It didn't sound uncomfortable or unnerving. I agreed and began my training right away." "Eventually, as time passed, I became aware of their strict nature, and their disturbing interest in everything I did. They re-explained every time, and I listened. After schooling, and after being prepared for so long, they released me from the dojo. I was an instant success. Neo Japan picked me up." "Sentaro had a problem however. In order for everything to work out in their favor. In order to sap money through me. they needed more than connections. They invested in Neo Japan, watched it thrive. they were overjoyed with both my success and the money they were receiving all the time on my behalf, even if I didn't know. Unfortunately for them, I broke my arm during a routine exercise, and was forced to let it heal. While I was away Neo Japan crumbled and finally died out. I was just leaving my mark, and it left. just like that." "Needless to say, Sentaro was crushed. The decline and fall was so unexpected. Their corporate throat's were slit. Gagging on the way down, Sentaro and it's mysterious creator were forced into the shadows. I can only assume they disbanded, as I was granted to do whatever I like. I made sure to keep the dojo, keep some money, and keep my respect." "Yamato became one of my friends quickly, in Neo Japan. An amazing talent who used his hard work and determination to capture win after win as well as the J1 title. An extremely honest man, he was. After hearing he'd opened up the Japan Puroresu Association, I quickly got in touch. I was accepted." [Yuzo rubs his fingers once again across the purple scratches and lettering on the inside of his arm. He turns his head to the side, smiling.] "Now you know. I am here because I was chosen to lead a renegade corporation. I'm stronger than ever. I know more than ever. At the Tokyo Ryogoku Sumo Hall I make my grand debut in a Battle Royale, pinfall elimination." "Like Neo Japan, I can assume right away my opponents are only of the highest quality, as Yamato himself picked them from the hordes of fighters." "Jinshi Togawa, an honored man who left Tokyo, but missed it in the end. His life was empty without the spectacle of performing inside the ring. Every day he'd wake up sooner, jog longer, train harder. want to be back where he was, more and more. I respect him for doing what it is he willed. I can't promise a win over him, but I can earn one." "Christopher Kojima, another man who left Tokyo, to America. He's using the JPA as a stepping stone, however. Drawn back to Japan, his homeland, he'll try and tackle nine other strong men. I can't promise a win over him, but I can earn one." "Gustav Jaeger. A monstrous man, as big as myself. Strong and seemingly wise. He proclaims his speed, and the fact that it's often underestimated. He tells us of his greatness. Gaijin, maybe, but also a force. I can't promise a win over him, but I can earn one." "Eiji Yatsu. A man who knows the sport. A man who has been around and is familiar with what's happening. He's strong, and talented, and worth worrying over. He has his priorities in line, and he expects he'll win the Battle Royale as most of us do. I can't promise a win over him, but I can earn one." "For all the rest. All strong. All smart. All worthy of the spot they have been given. Knyte, Star, and more. I can't promise a win over these men either." "But with Sentaro off my back. With a clear head and pure spirit. With only the best of intentions. I can earn my win at the Tokyo Ryogoku Sumo Hall." "I can take one step closer to becoming something I've always wanted to be." "A champion without help. A champion without an anchor." [He stands from the bench.] [We end.] ---- [There the camera is. Turned on, filming some various scene. But what scene is it? A typical Japanese household, with a wooden desk sitting in front of you. On the desk sits a piece of paper, with a pen laid over it. Just then the small, Japanese man known as XYZ walks up to the desk wearing a red and black kimono, picks up the parchment and begins to read it aloud, allowing you into the mind of XYZ for the first time all tour.] XYZ: The road has been very tough and very treacherous. I've paid my dues ma, I really have. I've finally made the big time. A few months ago I was just a shell of a man with a broken spirit and no will to live. Then I went to America and things changed. I realized that I did not have the worst life imaginable, that I did not have it rough. So ma, I came home. I've now toured with the Japanese Puroresu Association for roughly a month. I've gained many victories mixed in with few losses. I've learned the tricks of the trade and seen such man as MASSIVE Akita and the Iron Chef perform and I've learned what it takes. Just to think a year ago I thought I reached the top with JU but now, my life is forever changing. Though this week provides one of the biggest challenges so far on tour, I have no fear of losing. My opponent is good, yes but I have learned so much about myself that I have full confidence in winning. It is like you once told me ma, I must understand myself first before entering battle. I do hope you saw me on the first Battle Start ma. I won my fight that night against We Hyun Bae and proved to the Japanese fans I'm for real. I do hope you received your tickets for the big show ma, I want to make it up to you on this night. The match against Tokaio-san is dedicated to you because I feel the pain you undertook over the past years. Don't worry ma, I'll be fine. Don't' worry ma, I'll be okay. Do not shed those tears ma; your boy has come back home! [Fade out.] ---- [Four fingers wrap themselves around the black handle of the sword, as the thumb comes around and locks itself in place. A second hand grasps the soft, yet sturdy, leather just below the first. The long metal blade is a Western broadsword, although it is uncertain particularly which country could have produced such a blade. The reflective metal of the sword cuts the air as it whirs around. The holder, satisfied with it's performance, holds up to his face. With a flick of a switch-] * * * S N A P ! ! ! * * * [A pair of prongs erupt from the hilt and around the eyes of the wielder of the sword. A pair of blank eyes suddenly move and reveal a set of dark irises that lower from the back of the man's head. The wielder lowers the sword, sheathing it in a leather case. There stands a man with a look of death on his mind. The red and white paint that adorns his face simply reveal the identity of this man who we already know: the Great Tokaio.] Tokaio: [Holds sword in one hand.] Sometimes, our inspiration comes from that which is not real. Old stories of how ancient bushi defended themselves against hordes of evil demons with nothing but the sword in their hand. Stories of how a gaijin takes up a fallen foe's katana to defend the remnants of a village that he did not belong to. Stories of heroism. Stories of courage. Stories of honor. And we do our best to live up to those stories, no matter how high the ideals are. [Tokaio reveals a simple cloth and cleans the blade in his hand.] Tokaio: It is from these stories that our idea of the ancient bushi is born of. The Western precepts of knights and chivalry were created by a bunch of lowly minstrels, and thousands of men threw themselves into battle, upholding a way of life that was merely "fashionable" in their day. But honor has a deeper meaning than that... [It is soon revealed that Tokaio is standing in the middle of a forest clearing. Bamboo rises high into the air and the grass is short, but wild, and the mist hanging in the air brings about a sense of cautious quiet. Wearing a simple kimono, Tokaio stands next to a fencing dummy.] Tokaio: You have displayed honor in all that you do, XYZ. I have nothing but respect for you, one who accomplishes all he believes in with honor. The admission of one's faults requires a strong heart, and you have such a heart. [With a flick of the wrist, the sword in the Great Tokaio's hand is now shown from it's profile.] Tokaio: I, however, have lost most of my matches on the Coronation tour. I have spent far too long meditating on too many things, stretching myself too far. Now, my focus has become you, XYZ. You are an honorable man, and so you will be a worthy opponent. But I will not be denied this day. [The Tokaio suddenly jumps into the air, bring the sword up with him. He brings the sword down towards the dummy. Landing on his feet, Tokaio sheaths the sword in it's scabbard.] Tokaio: Makeru wake ni wa ikanaii!!! [The Great Tokaio turns around to leave... A better glimpse of the dummy can be seen. Untouched, the dummy stands strong and does not falter in the cool breeze... But with another small gust, the two halves of a single piece of cloth float down to the ground, settling on the grass to be lost in the leaves...] ---- (It's another cool afternoon in downtown Tokyo. We find "Captivating" Corey Hart walking along a crowded street with his best friend, Lolita Love. Weaving their way through the crowd, the two manage to get in their fair share of window shopping. Corey wears a form-fitting, black, turtleneck sweater and baggy, blue jeans. He completes the look with black, leather boots, a black, book bag slung over his shoulders. His platinum blond hair is short and curled, accentuating his boyish, good looks. Lolita wears a pink, cardigan sweater and blue jeans. She also wears a pair of pink and white, Nike, tennis shoes. Her long, blonde hair falls straight down her back, bangs resting above her blue eyes. Corey points out a few of the sights and sounds before the duo disappears inside of a small restaurant. Moments later, they are seated across from one another at a small table, the restaurant filled with patrons.) Corey: Lolita, I'm really glad that you could come visit. You don't know how good it is to see a familiar face around here. Don't get me wrong. I love most of the guys at the JPA and most have been really friendly and accepting but nothing beats spending the day with your best friend. Lolita: (grins) Well, I'm glad that I was able to find the time to come for a visit. I've never been to Japan before and I'm really looking forward to seeing everything that the country has to offer. After all, I'm only going to be here for a couple of days so I want to get in all of the sight seeing that I can. Corey: Don't worry. I'll make sure that you get to see all of the important stuff. I'll even get to introduce you to some of the JPA wrestlers, if you want. Lolita: That would be cool! Are there any cute ones? Corey: (laughs) Yeah, there's some cute ones. Lolita: Are they single? Corey: (grins) Some of them. Why? Are you looking for a boyfriend? Lolita: (feigning innocence) No! I'm just curious. I ask those questions about everybody. Corey: (rolls his eyes) Suuuuure! (the two friends share a laugh) You know, it's really good to see you again. Lolita: Likewise. When I found out that I was going to have some free time to visit with you, there was no way that I could turn it down. How have you been enjoying things here in Japan? Corey: It's cool. I haven't had too many bad experiences and I do have a few friends that I hang out with but I still get homesick every once in a while. (sighs) I miss you and my family so much. I can only imagine what my poor phone bill looks like. Lolita: (nods her head) We all miss you too. But we understand that this is something that you've always wanted to do. This is your dream and we support you one hundred percent. (smiles) So, tell me all about this upcoming JPA show. Who are you taking on? Corey: (frowns) I'll be tagging with my friend, We Hyun Bae, against these two guys that I absolutely can't stand, Kenji Okano and EIYUU. Those two are racist pricks! They hate all of us American grapplers, saying that we don't even belong in the ring with them! They think we're all talentless losers! Can you believe that? Lolita: (shaking her head) That sucks. Corey: (he folds his arms across his chest, his blue eyes blazing with anger) Well, We Hyun and I have faced them before, trading victories back and forth, but this time will be different. I think it's high time that those morons learned some humility and respect and I'm just the person to teach it to them. I am going to enjoy crushing them into the mat! Lolita: Good luck, Corey. Corey: (snorts) Luck? They're the ones that are going to need that! We Hyun and I are going to absolutely destroy them! They think that we kicked their butts last time? Well, they ain't seen nothing yet. We Hyun and I plan on showing them that their racist attitudes won't be tolerated in the JPA! Lolita: Hmm, I can see that you're fired up about this. I've never seen you so angry before. Corey: You would be angry too if you had to continually put up with their constant snide remarks and putdowns. It's irked me to the point where I don't just want to win the match. I want to hurt them.....badly. Lolita: Just don't let the anger overwhelm you. It's too easy to slip up and make a mistake. Corey: (nods) I know. (smiles) You sound like my buddy, Keiji Kobe. He always gives me great advice. He's the main reason EIYUU and Okano dislike us. They don't think Keiji should be associating with We Hyun and I. They think we aren't worthy of his friendship because we aren't Japanese. (shakes his head) They fail to realize that Keiji likes us because of who we are, not our nationalities. If they think he would ever associate with immoral losers like them then they are living in a dream world! He can't even stomach their attitudes! Lolita: I'm sure that you and your partner will win this one, Corey. After all, the good guys always win, right? Corey: (smiles) We'll see, Lolita. We'll see. Whether win or lose, Okano and EIYUU won't forget this match for some time. That's not a promise or a threat. It's a damn guarantee! (Fade to black.) ---- (Standing in a very cramped locker room is none other than the oldest man in the JPA, Jack Shaw. Wearing his wrestling gear, brown duster, and orange and brown Grain Grower's cap, Shaw looks uncomfortable standing in front of the camera, as shown by his shifting of his weight back and forth) I ain't got too much to say, as words don't do much 'round these parts. I know a ton of y'all seem to gab and gab about this an' that, but that ain't what this here place is all about. Ya see, I prefer to do my talking in the ring, with these here two fists. I don't have much to say about nothing or nobody, because it ain't never personal around here, just fightin' for fightin's sake. (The giant gaijin rubs his hands together, subtitles still on the bottom of the screen.) I ain't going to say much more, as I am gonna be taking on two guys who seem to enjoy mayham and brutality a bit more than most, even me. Plus I'm paired up with a youngster, who if I do say so, looks to be someone I'd feel comfortable passing the torch to... not before I kick ole Taylor's hide a few times though. Yes sir, I'm gonna be doing what I do best... beating the living {TV EDIT} outta someone.
