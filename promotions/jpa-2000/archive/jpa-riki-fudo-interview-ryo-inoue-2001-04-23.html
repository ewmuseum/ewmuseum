JPA: The Riki Fudo Interview: Ryo Inoue

23 April 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Ryo Inoue has Vic Garibaldi's arm trapped in a jujigatame. Inoue already has survived some damaging power blows from Garibaldi in this match but Inoue is a fighter and has toughed it out to the point that he is dominant in this match once more. Garibaldi eventually escapes the jujigatame but then finds himself trapped in Inoue's guard. It's here that Garibaldi picked Inoue up by his wrestling trunks and dropped him almost on his head, making everyone in Osaka Castle Hall think that the young Japan Puroresu Association star was out. But this is Ryo Inoue and Ryo Inoue doesn't let being dropped stop him as he uses the almost powerbomb-like maneuver to his advantage by slipping in the sankujime [triangle choke]. That is where the match ended a few seconds later as the time expired. Inoue, who had promised a 5-minute victory, was taken to the limit by the unproven Garibaldi. It came down to the judges. In fact, it is the judges that Ryo Inoue has a problem with, or perhaps I should says it is one particuliar judge. "I have always respected Keisuke Oyanami but making Genji Yamato a judge in my fight automatically brings the result into question" Inoue says as the masseuse rubs him down almost 3 after the fight. Inoue was one of the only fighters to skip the post TATAMI press conference and go home. It was 35 minutes into the press conference when Junji Shirow approached me and said that I was being granted an exclusive interview with Inoue. I was thrilled, I was getting a scoop, I was scared out of my mind. Ryo Inoue has been very tightlipped in the past. He makes statements to the press that usually end with a direct challenge to JPA president Genji Yamato. Yamato always tries to keep his dignity but Inoue's latest accusation really does bring up a good point. Why was Yamato, Inoue's chief adversary, allowed to judge Inoue's match? Surely Keisuke Oyanami has better judgement than that. "I was the winner of that fight but Yamato let his personal feelings for me cloud his judgement," Inoue states adamantly, "His verdict nearly led to another round that Garibladi couldn't endure. I didn't want to be responsible for a man dying in the ring and luckily it didn't happen but Yamato was anything but unbiased*." There are wads of tissue jammed up Inoue's nose. Early in the fight it looked as if Garibaldi actually broke it. After the match Inoue headed to the back and discovered it wasn't broken. "I am not Rick Taylor. My nose doesn't shatter so easily. I give Vic Garibaldi credit. I underestimated him and paid for it. He really is an excellent fighter. He tested me. He earned my respect." So now it is back to the JPA for Inoue, where it seems he is headed or a showdown with Masashi "MASSIVE" Akita at the end of the Junior Struggle tour. Or is he? "Akita is waiting for me. I know he is. But this fight proved that I am not ready... yet." Inoue groans in discomfort as the hands of his masseuse dig into his back. This is the same man who a year ago entered Neo Japan Puroresu as the protege of Keiji Asamiya, the famed sensei of Steel Wind Dojo. This was a kid who was so poor when he broke into puroresu that Neo-J headman Genichiro Matsumura gave him a small room stay in his own home. This is Ryo Inoue, the boy who just a month after debuting won the J1 Real Tag League Tournament with Jinsei ASAKA. This is Ryo Inoue, the boy who became a man at the hands of "This Is... TENZAKI METAL!", being beaten down by the Asian Assasination Squad, Alex RAPTOR and Hiryoshi Tenzaki himself. This is the Ryo Inoue, the man who came back for vengeance, destroying the A.A.S. and RAPTOR before finally extracting his pound of flesh from Tenzaki. That used to be Ryo Inoue, a man who at one time was even an ally of Genji Yamato. This is now, Ryo Inoue, leader of Total Attack Puroresu, an outlaw group that claims its members have been held back because of their fighting style. At first the press laughed. Inoue was an army of one for close to 2 months. That changed at Korakuen Hall as after Inoue beat Jinshi Togawa. Just as it looked like Inoue's big mouth was going to get him beaten down by Togawa, Yuzo Moriboshi and American Superstar Rick Taylor, out came the silver and red rescue squad of Eiji Yatsu and former NHB/MMA star Naoto Kangyo. T.A.P. was an army ready to challenge the world in order to prove Shoot Style was the force Inoue was claiming it was. T.A.P. met the team of Moriboshi, Togawa and Shinji Nakata at Ryogoku Sumo Hall on 3.24. Inoue and company came out on top as Togawa gave into Kangyo's Butterfly Stretch. "Was there any question we would win?" Inoue asks with a raised brow. "Kangyo is a true fighter, a machine designed to beat the crap out of the Strong Style boys. I love working and training with him". What about Eiji Yatsu, the young junior, a strong contender and the spoiler during the Junior Struggle tour? "Look at the field. I mean some of those guys in there are going to get beat bad," Inoue says with a smile, "Billy Fairchild is decent but Kangyo and I have been working Eiji over and training him. He has to unlearn that lucharesu crap that they taught him at the JPA Dojo. Tasogare No Prince is solid but again Yatsu can take a Strong Style "Boy" like TnP." Of course Ryo leaves out the name that people love to joke about in MMA/NHB circles, "Iron Chef" Keiji Kobe. I don't bother asking Inoue about Kobe for the simple fact that Kobe is considered the biggest joke amongst competitors in TATAMI. Inoue relates a talk he had with Vic Garibaldi while both men were changing after their fight. "Garibaldi said what made him want to try and fight me was the way I humiliated "Iron Chump." It seems young Keiji rubs him the wrong way." Inoue nods slowly as he says this. Kobe made the mistake of making Ryo Inoue very angry back in February, as he insulted not only Inoue but the Steel Wind Dojo and its founder Keiji Asamiya. Inoue worships Asamiya and has taken his sensei's counsel on all matters pertaining to T.A.P. In fact it was Asamiya who convinced Inoue to let T.A.P. have its own show. "June is going to be great" says Junji Shirow, who I talked to on the way to the interview,. "T.A.P. will debut its first card. We're already working on a line-up to blitz the fans". But can T.A.P. book J.P.A. talent? Word is Genji Yamato is furious that Inoue is hosting his own show. Phone calls were made and lawsuits discussed. Steel Wind dojo evicted the 12 JPA students they were training in response. Keiji Asamiya released the simple "Dojos are for Fighters not Dancers" response. Shirow already confirmed that Tetsuo Matsumoto has been booked for a tag match. I know Genji Yamato must be absolutely livid as he and Matsumoto have been trying to arrange the "Final Fight 5" retirement countdown (apparently the issue has been how much of the gate will Matsumoto recieve. Leave it to Tetsuo, the world's biggest mark for himself to stall over money). Inoue rolls onto his back and sits up. He seems more relaxed now, less tense and stressed. He is already talking about the next TATAMI card. He says there only 2 names on his list of opponents, "Stephen Bishop** needs to be taught some... humility. I am no phony as he claims I am." The other opponent I don't even ask about because it is evident by the t-shirt Inoue slips on. The black shirt has Genji Yamato's name dripping in red kanji. The reverse says it even worse as the kanji and english word for "COWARD" are sprayed in yellow. The War For Respect just got a little too personal... *[editor's note: Genji Yamato was on the TATAMI judging panel for all five fights. **[sic: David] --- [name withheld] - Formerly of The Westsiders [#28, RSPWF 2000 Year End Awards Top 50 Tag Teams] - Japan Puroresu Association - http://jpapuroresu.hypermart.net/
