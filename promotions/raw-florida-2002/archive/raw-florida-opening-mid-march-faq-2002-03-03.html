Website 9. Application 10. Contact Information *********************** 1. What is this thing? RAW-Florida is one of the growing number of "baby RAWs". What that means is this: We are a part of the RAW "universe", only we compete at a much smaller, regional level. RAW-Florida is a great place for newcomers to the game to cut their teeth, and a great place for veteran handlers to revisit mothballed characters or fine-tune a new character before breaking out for the big leagues. It's a symbiotic relationship, as the new handlers can look to the veterans for guidance and instruction, and the "newbies" allow the veterans to develop new ideas at a slower pace.
Whenever RAW has roster openings, it will look to "promote from within" and grab a promising character from RAW-F. Much like real-life regional feds, I expect the turnover rate to be high, with wrestlers coming and going with regularity, often to bigger and better things.
And, of course, like Goldilocks, anyone who feels that the pacing and structure of RAW-Florida is juuuuuuuust right is free to stay as long as they wish, and become a "Southern independent legend", or something.
1.1 What can we offer? - One of the finest legacies in e-wrestling, RAW (and before that, WAR!). - The finest staff around - Me!! - A solid regional fed, built around storytelling and character development, which always take precedence over titles and winning matches. - Cards written entirely in report form, to take the load off of writers (read: Me.) and give everyone ample opportunity to devote their full attention to the story they are telling with their wrestlers and their angles.
- Fictional wrestlers only, and no full-time wrestlers from other RAW promotions (guest shots, however, are not only allowed, they're encouraged!!) - Personal rapport with everyone involved, thanks to the nifty raw-ooc yahoogroup. - Strictness yet fairness. - A FAQ pretty much lifted from whoever. *********************** 2. *********************** 4. A little bit of Championships There are three titles up for grabs in RAW-Florida: 4.1 Sunshine State Heavyweight Championship The cream of the crop of the cream of the crop hold and fight for this belt. It's the premier title in the fed, and possibley in all of wrestling. It's defended on a regular basis, and is applicable to wrestlers of all sizes and shapes, including junior-heavyweights, women, etc.
4.2 Sunshine State Tag Team Championship The title for the tag teams of the fed. This title is defended regularly between teams of two competitors, and there are no weight restrictions on this champ- ionship.
4.3 Sunshine State Silver Heavyweight Championship This belt is generally held by young up-and-comers, looking to make their mark but not quite ready for the main event.
*********************** 5. Events 5.1 Big Events We will have one "big event" every 7-14 days, with house shows sprinkled in between. The big events will have names to increase the hype, while the house shows will only increase the hype but just a tab.
Certain big events will be bigger than others, like the Miami Bash Tournament, Winter Warzone, Tampa Bay Tap Out, and the crown jewel of the RAW-Florida wrestling year, Fight Fest, held every year in April in Miami.
5.2 House Shows A la WWF and all the other federations RAW-Florida will go down and have House Shows. These events are never scheduled and just to move the storylines along and for your entertainment.
5.3 Pay Per Veiws Basically the supercards mentioned above will hosted on PPV. ********************** 6. Best ways to succeed around here 6.1 Roleplaying The first being roleplays. This is a must for a handler. It's the only thing that the federation is going to see from you, and gets the idea's of your wrestler(s) across to the rest of the federation, and is a HUGE part in deciding winners, and instrumental in success. The person with a better flash usually wins, but sometimes, even though you have a better flash than your opponent, it doesn't always mean you're going to win.
6.2 Strats The last factor is strategies, or strats. This is more of a "tiebreaker" item, but it is _greatly_ appreciated by the writers and front office of RAW-Florida. A strategy will explain to us what exactly you want your wrestler to do during their match and other parts of the show, as well as suggesting spots and such. It makes the writers' jobs a
_great_ deal easier while writing your match. An ideal RAW-Florida strategy will include the following:
Ring Entrance (How you want your character to come to the ring): Match Strategy (What you want your character to do during their match): Suggested Spots (Some ideas for moves and such during the match): Winning Situation (What they'd do if they won): Losing Situation: (What they'd do if they lost): Other (Other things you want your wrestler to do during the show, match, etc.): 6.3 Angles The third may be the biggest factor. That being angles. Even though your roleplay was slightly better than your opponent, if the bookers have an angle planned for your opponent, and it can be furthered somehow during that match, they may go with that rather than totally choosing a winner based on roleplaying.
********************** 7. Fictional Personalities 7.1 The Owner 7.1.1 [name withheld] [name withheld] is the business man. He walks around with money in his pockets and doesn't let the money run to his head. He walks around with strut but not with a smile or frown on his face. He makes it as if decision were final, as it is. He deals with situations unexpectably and delivers more than you can chew. He tries to stay away from the scene and leave the wrestling to the wrestlers but can step up to the call whenever a challenge is posted. Teague is a straight shooter; not exactly a heel, and not exactly a face. His relationships with the wrestlers are on a strict individual basis.
There are some faces he loves, and some he can't stand - same way with the heels. Don't like his style? Who cares?
7.2 The Announcers 7.2.1 Thomas Reid Thomas is the typical unbiased commentator. He works hard at his play-by-play commentary, and he also has to work hard in just working with his partner,
Kenneth Seger, who enjoys driving Nightrain up the wall. He is the overall neutral character commentator in wrestling today.
7.2.2 Sean Allen Another neutral guy. He helps hype the show up and has a bunch of catchy lines to light up the show when needed. The first of the two color commentators behind the mics.
7.2.3 Kenneth Seger Former Heavyweight Champion in another federation, and former WWC and NCF color commentary man, Kenneth Seger is one of the most intelligent guys you could meet, although sometimes... well, most of the time, he doesn't show it. He always has something smart to say to someone, and usually roots for the "bad guys." Commentates with Reid and Allen on anything worthy of his attention.
7.3 Backstage Interviewers 7.3.1 Adam Smart The young former cruserweight who was injured in a wrestling match in Eastern Japan Tour. Decided to retire at his young age and go on to the only other thing he is good at, interviewing.
7.3.2 DJ Jamez No Former DJ found at nightclub in Miami. He Loves to talk and ask questions that'll suit your fancy. A medium built character who can take care of business if needed. Most wrestlers just like to call on
Jamez to just sit around and chill and talk about the happenings of the federation.
7.4 Referees 7.4.1 Comet Rick Rick worked as head ref in WWC for ages, and has now brought his skill and stiffness for the rules to RAW-Florida to act as head official there.
7.4.2 Richard Roseberry Former wrestler who just quit because of the lack of enjoyment but then wrestling discovered a new taste that brought Richard back to the sport. Even though retired as a wrestler, he still makes the decisions on the match and makes sure the rules are enforced.
7.4.3 Curt Thompson One of RAW-Florida's senior referees has been keeping the peace in wrestling matches for over 20 years. He oversees most of the big matches and Pay Per View main events. It's hard to get things past
Thompson.
7.4.4 Andrew Smith Smith has been called by the wrestler's "the best damn referee there ever was." This may, or may not be true, but most believe the reason that the wrestler's like Smith so much is because he lets a lot of things slide in matches, and doesn't call for too many disqualification's.
