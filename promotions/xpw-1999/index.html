A short-lived, deliberately outrageous Usenet e-fed that ran weekly "War of the Worlds" broadcasts and one pay-per-view before its AOL-hosted website was reported and removed. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

XPW pitched itself on 8 July 1999 as the newsgroup's rowdiest option: “We at XPW pride ourselves on being the most offensive, and adult themed e-fed on the net. Our matches are usually outrageous, and our themes drug induced.” Its first pay-per-view, already announced, was titled Harrison Ford's Birthday, promising to be “goofy” with “several well known Porn stars” appearing, and its own site billed itself “XPW......X-rated pro wrestling.”

A newsletter that survives from 2 August 1999 catches the promotion at its fullest: Guru had just taken the XPW Extreme Championship from Phantom in what the newsletter itself calls “a very bizarre cage match,” a special guest referee appearing to favor Phantom throughout before turning to count the fastest pinfall “in wrestling history” against him, and Guru was booked to defend the title again immediately, against a former XPW World Champion. “Flamethrower” Rob Dalton, the reigning US Champion, was paired against the promotion's tag champions in pursuit of a second title, and its own president had just gone to London to needle a rival newsgroup promotion, WET, on its own turf, challenging it to “send 6 of their best.”

The same newsletter carried the promotion's own backstage churn in the open: a memo from its president over roleplay quality that one wrestler argued heavily against, a legendary wrestler who arrived and then vanished with his own nephew covering his dates, and an open search for a vice president still unresolved among a shortlist of five candidates.

No further document survives past that August newsletter; how the Harrison Ford's Birthday pay-per-view played out, or how the promotion itself ended, isn't in the surviving record.
