> In the T.V world of wrestling... > Do you ever see anyone like Bob Holly or anyone else doing talky > bits? No. Or very rarely. And why not? Because the fed has decided > not to give them the air time to give them the hype and push > necessary. Conversely, those they want to push, they give more air > time too, and more victories, to hype them. In TV world, it is the > feds responsibility. I personally feel in booked leagues, it should also be the fed's responsibility as well. The fed head or committee for a really big league needs to look out amongst their wrestlers and see who has the highest skills, and ask them to contribute. > But in e-wrestling... > It is up to the players to create their own hype, which is why those > that flash the best/most, and get involved in the best angles tend to > and should do better than those who sit around doing nothing. That definitely applies to simmed feds, but again I think for a truly booked fed the promoters should decide who should be pushed and who shouldn't. If you're going to do modern-day pro-wrestling, do it all the way, IMO. > And also, by doing good flashes, by getting involved in good angles, > you are adding to the entertainment and drama value of the fed, and thus > improving the rep of the fed, because, after all, e-wrestling is > about entertainment and drama. I agree wholeheartedly. But I would point out that angles and drama are only part of the equation. The Undertaker and Psycho Sid are two good interviewers, but their match skills are pathetic. Taken another way, a car's chassis (wrestler's personality) can be as flashy as possible, but if the engine (wrestling skills) aren't up to snuff, the entertainment value goes out the window. I enjoy both men's interviews, but I keep wondering why they are headliners. > Therefore, by proving you are a worthwhile asset > > (a) > You will be > rewarded for making the effort by being given victories Here I disagree. In my league (WCA), you have to earn your victories. The best thing about TNM is its push attribute, which is the number one factor in determining who wins. I don't give my handlers control over that; I use it to adjust their push based on the amount of roleplaying done. Can a wreslter win with a low push score? Yes, but those are rather rare and tend to be exciting. In fact, such a situation happened with our Can-Am title. A wrestler who barely talks captured it from one of our top stars, and we called it an upset victory. Nobody complained, and the star went on to recapture the belt from the very same person. That's the entertainment value. > and > > (b) > The > fedhead will see that it is necessary to keep you, and the best way > to do that is to have you high in the rankings, which can only be > done by giving them a push or title. Like I said, I think a wrestler needs to earn their victories. It makes the reward much, much sweeter, IMO. > The better you are, the better your reward and placing. Here's a question, though. The better you are in what? Interview skills? Generating heat? Hulk Hogan proved that. According to some feds (not even all booked feds), Hulk "I know two moves!" Hogan should be the World champion because he talks so much. Yes, rewarding roleplayers by giving them victories mirrors real-life, but is this really something that needs to be followed so exactly? I mean, shouldn't we be rewarding both good interview skills and good wrestling skills? > Theoretically. Thus, conversely, if you do nothing, don`t expect to > get anywhere. This I agree with, to an extent. Some people are like me, who are juggling school, work, and e-wrestling. They may not have time, but that shouldn't penalize them for doing what they can. I don't punish the inactive people, I reward the roleplayers. You might say, 'well, if they're so busy they shouldn't join.' My response would be, 'I don't have any right to tell someone how to have fun.' > That, my friends, is the way the world of booked e-wrestling works. > And it is also why booked feds are the most fun. Hey, it's all apples and oranges to me. If you like it, fine. If you don't, God bless ya.
