[detail withheld] wrote in article < 338B1E...@plinet.com >... > . . . while it started off as a pure sim, lately I've > started changing that. Now, the players who put time and thought into > their roleplays get a bit of a push as opposed to those players who send > in a flash along the lines of "You suck, I'm gonna beat you on Tuesday, > the end." This is almost exactly how the WCA started out; it began using WLS 5.1 (I think that's the right version), and within a month several handlers convinced me that the heavy role-players deserve at least a chance to win over non-role-players. So I've been using that system ever since, and it's worked out tremendously well, IMO. > At the same time, this system doesn't > guarantee either wrestler a victory, and lends itself more to the > "upsets" that happen on "real" wrestling. True, although you appropriately quoted 'upsets' regarding real pro-wrestling. I should point out, though, that a true *angle-driven* fully booked league (not a *roleplay-driven* booked league, aka a writing contest) often does the same thing, with only the league staff knowing what's going to happen. The biggest advantage, IMO, to having upsets in a partially- or fully-simmed league is that even the fed-head is surprised. > By using this system, . . . it also allows wrestlers who WISH to put the > effort in to advance more quickly than those who don' want to be > bothered participating. And the bottom line is that people aren't handed a victory, and it also allows wrestlers to have different levels of determination to compete and get to the top. That's my perspective, at least. > B.Bedlam > peon guerrilla -- [name withheld] [detail withheld] "Make it idiot-proof, and someone will make a better idiot."
