A ProBoards message-board fed whose weekly Impact Xtreme show and eight-part December pay-per-view crowned new champions in four of its five divisions, one title changing hands on a disputed double pinfall its own referees never resolved. Five title lineages, ten shows and 59 wrestlers survive. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

IPW-XFWL ran on its own ProBoards message board into its own third year by January 2004, and its recruitment post makes its priorities plain: “We're a mostly storyline based fed, where the most important things are telling good stories and having fun... If you're more about winning titles then making friends, IPW-XFWL isn't the place for you.” No fixed roleplay quota was enforced; a handler who wrote got more television time because their storylines earned it, not because a deadline demanded it.

Its championship picture ran a World Heavyweight, Tag Team, Women's, E-Championship and Junior Heavyweight title across a weekly show, Impact Xtreme, and periodic pay-per-views. Its October 2003 Funhouse II turned on a mask-versus-career stipulation: “Lions” Ben North, ordered by his own promoter to unmask if he lost to Spindle, seemed to have the match won until his own agent, Bob Munroe, ran in and chaired Spindle by mistake for the fans and on purpose for Lions, costing him the match on a disqualification. Lions fled through the crowd rather than remove the mask, leaving the promotion's own broadcast team arguing over whether a disqualification loss still bound him to the stipulation.

Its title picture turned over almost entirely on one card, Hardcore Holiday, 14 December 2003. Bouncer won the E-Championship in a three-way Triangle Turbulence match with its own unusual pinning rules; Venom took the Junior Heavyweight title from Mark Prohibition in the deciding fall of a two-out-of-three-falls match; Amanda Clark won the Women's Championship from Peaches in a ladder match; the Tag Team Championship was stripped from The Future outright after their chain match against The Russians drew two referees to two different pinfall calls, with a tournament promised for January; and the World Heavyweight Championship changed hands when 411 pinned reigning champion Tyrant in a triple-threat Bring Your Own Weapon match that also involved JuggaO, a former owner of the promotion's own predecessor federation.

No further title change survives past that card. Weekly Impact Xtreme episodes and a January 2004 pay-per-view, New Year's Nightmare, continue the promotion's own record into the new year, but no further document carries it past a 28 January 2004 update bulletin; how much longer IPW-XFWL itself continued isn't in the surviving record.
