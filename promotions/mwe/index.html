Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Mayhem Wrestling Entertainment ran on message boards — first on InvisionFree from 2007, then on Zetaboards from the summer of 2008. Its founder, Tim Fitzgerald, opened it in May 2007 by the fed's own retrospective account, which says it “kicked off on May Sixteenth”; its own later billing disagrees, putting the anniversary in August 2007 in one place and in 2006 in another, and all three readings stand. It closed on 20 March 2008, the day Fitzgerald died, and its roster reopened it four months later under the name they used for the rest of its life: nu-MWE.

The relaunch built out quickly. Monday Night Mayhem ran weekly through the autumn of 2008 and fortnightly after it; a draft special that November split the roster into Mayhem and Genesis brands with a Friday show each, Friday Night Genesis and then Friday Night Overdrive; and a pay-per-view calendar ran behind them — Crowning Glory, Turmoil, Wrestle Wars, Adrenaline Rush, Highway to Hell, Tribulation, Independence Day. The two world titles the split had produced were unified into one MWE Undisputed Championship at Adrenaline Rush on 28 December 2008. Richard Covell owned and managed the company on screen, Ryan Adams and David Ferrera called the shows, Fred Carry announced the ring, and a development territory, ICW, was staged as the way up: win two matches there and continue to MWE.

What its record shows MWE cared about most is writing. It gave out weekly awards for the best wrestler, the best roleplay and the best-written match, added a Feedbacker of the Week in August 2009 for the quality of a member's critique of other people's promos, and gave each award its own escalating prestige level, raised whenever the staff judged a win had earned it. Its shows were co-written — several staff members each posting the segments they wrote, in show order — and the fed credited its own writers at the end of each one. Twice it staged a full year-end ceremony, the Fitzgerald Awards, voting on everything from Match of the Year to Gimmick of the Year.

It kept a Hall of Fame from the start. The first class went in at Crowning Glory II in August 2008 — a posthumous plaque in Fitzgerald's name, then Katherine Tenaud as the first MWE Women's Champion and David Whitman as its first World Champion — and a second ceremony followed in August 2009 for Paige, Sonny Williams, Alejandro Rodriguez and Brian Michaels. A memorial show ran that March, on the first anniversary of Fitzgerald's death, with talent from ICW, WcE and WHQ on the card.

Activity thinned through 2010 and stopped that September. A staff-led relaunch in September 2011 posted a new structure, ran two more Mondays and stopped again in November; a reunion attempt in 2014 never staged a show, and the last staff post, on 3 June 2015, called it “one final hoorah before the lights go out.” What survives is the board itself, captured in 2009 and again in 2018 — its show and card threads, its roleplay archives, its own championship ledger, its roster cards and its weekly award posts. Nothing from the original run survives in it: that board was captured only as a homepage. Its roleplays are served whole at Roleplays.

Storylines

Sinc Mercier and Mason Resoan, and a belt that kept changing hands Written by AI, 2026

The fed's own first Fitzgerald Awards, in April 2009, named this its Feud of the Year, and its own ballot says how it ended: “settled by a ladder match that returned the United States Championship to Sinc Mercier.” The belt had been moving for a year by then. Alejandro Rodriguez took it off both Mercier and Mason Resoan in a Wrestle Wars Threeway Dance that October, before the title was stripped from him and reviewed; Mercier and Resoan settled the rematch between themselves, Resoan winning a straight singles match on the 1 December Monday Night Mayhem and then beating Mercier outright for the vacant title at Adrenaline Rush II that December, with Aiden Copane as special guest referee.

The two men were not straightforwardly enemies throughout it. On the 26 January Mayhem, booked on opposite sides of a tag match, Resoan blindsided his own partner Joey Dallas to finish Mercier off — and then pulled Mercier clear of the ring afterward to save him from a follow-up attack by Ace Cannon, the same night the two of them were meant to be rivals. The belt itself changed hands one more time before the story closed: Mercier won the ladder match the ceremony credits, on the 13 February Friday Night Overdrive, and the two shared a ring again five months later in a mixed tag with the Grand Mystique and Kevin Hardaway — on the 20 July Mayhem, ruled a no-contest after Zak Warner ran in on Hardaway.

D.C. and the Grand Mystique Written by AI, 2026

The fed's second Fitzgerald Awards, that September, gave its own Feud of the Year to D.C. and the Grand Mystique, tracing it “from a return beating through the world title,” with Windy Bridges' own involvement named alongside it. What the shows themselves hold is a hardcore-styled program that never resolved cleanly either way. The two fought a guitar-and-ladder brawl with no formal stipulation named on the 5 July Independence Day card — the Grand Mystique cut open with a wooden splinter, D.C. winning by pinfall off a ladder — and shared the ring again on the 22 June Mayhem, paired against Bridges and Paige in a mixed tag the fed ruled a no-contest. By September the two men were, on paper, tag partners rather than rivals: the 15 September Mayhem billed them together in a late-substitution main event against Zack Crash and Eric Derf, repeatedly tagging each other in and out mid-match before their opponents caught them both with simultaneous finishers for a double, simultaneous three-count.

Affiliations

Illinois Championship Wrestling

Academy — feeds into MWE (2007)
Staged directly as MWE’s own development territory: “Welcome to ICW, win two matches there and continue to MWE.” ICW’s own roster is also staged standing alongside MWE’s at the Tim Fitzgerald Memorial Show, where MWE’s own toast specifically thanks “the development talents in Illinois Championship Wrestling.”

WrestleCrazy Entertainment (WcE)

Crossover booking with MWE (2007)
The Memorial Show’s own ring announcer names WcE talent standing in the ring for its in-ring tribute segment: “Representing Wrestle Crazy Entertainment, James Whitman, Lennox Armstrong, Mark Marvin, Adam Belmont and Ken Forte” — a genuine staged joint appearance, not merely a background mention.

Wrestling's Highest Quality (WHQ)

Crossover booking with MWE (2007)
The Memorial Show's own ring announcer names named WHQ talent standing in the ring for the show's own in-ring tribute segment: "representing Wrestling's Highest Quality, Jesse 'Semi' Anderson, Jack Savage, Dylan Duncan, and Krauser" — a genuine staged joint appearance at MWE's own show.

Shared ownership

WrestleCrazy Entertainment (WcE), Wrestling's Highest Quality (WHQ)
One founder created both federations, and MWE's first roster was seeded from WcE as it declined — "he said he was starting up a new e-fed, he already saw WcE was going down, so he 'saved' me and CJ if you will and brought us over." The founder is named in this record because he is a welcomed memorial subject (Tim Fitzgerald, d. 20 March 2008), not a private operator.
