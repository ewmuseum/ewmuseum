Good Old Windy Bridges

Jun 5 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A beautiful western Canadian day. Yes, even Canada has beautiful days. It may not be florida or some tropical island, but it can get damn hot. And although the prairies haven’t had the best of luck with weather so far in 2009, this day is a clear exception to what has become the weather rule in the province of Alberta. The birds are tweeting. Summertime insects are buzzing. Dogs yapping at cats. Humans yapping with each other about anything and everything. And, of course, on this busy Canadian boulevard, cars are zooming by, of all sizes and colours, makes and models, years and conditions. The sun is shining, barely a clowd in the sky, and the trees are rustling in the light wind. A perfect balance between nature, and between man-made machines and civilization. Amongst this entire scene, two young men wander in the streets. Casually dressed, shorts and t-shirts, backwards ball caps showing their favourite hockey teams (in this city, you don’t even have to ask, you know it’s the Flames). The two men walk down the sidewalk, checking out any beautiful women they can as they do so. Suddenly, one of the two young men stops dead in his tracks. He turns 90 degrees away from his friend and faces a sign on a wall. His friend, a few steps ahead, turns around to realize his friend is no longer following him. “Chuck, what are you stopping for? We gotta get to the park before those college chicks stop washing cars in their bikinis!” Chuck realizes this clear dilemma, but he still continues to read the sign, now paraphrasing its contents aloud. “Jimmy I know, but check this shit out. Windy Bridges and the Simple Men will be performing right here at the Rocky Mountain Way tonight!” Jimmy scratches his chin. “The Rocky Mountain Way huh? Doesn’t he own this place?” “Ya, he bough it a couple months ago after selling that old piece of crap dive bar he had.” Jimmy approaches the sign and starts reading it as well. “The Swamp, right? Didn’t that place burn down?” “It’s a damn shame. That place may have been a dump but it was a part of this town. Anyway, dude, it’s Windy Bridges! I didn’t even know the Simple Men were still together! We gotta check this out tonight!” “Sure thing Chuck, but right now, we gotta catch those college chicks!” The two men begin running down the street towards a nearby park. The camera swing towards the sky as the wing continues to rustle the leaves on trees, and between the sunshine. As the breeze rolls through, a copy of the current day’s newspaper swings through the air, eventually getting caught on a tree branch. The camera zooms in close enough to read the headline on the front page, interestling accompanied by a picture of D.C. “MWE WRESTLER D.C. WANTED FOR QUESTIONING IN RELATION TO SUSPECTED ARSON.” After a moment, the breeze quickly takes the newspaper page away, as the scene fades. --------------------------------- It’s now a few hours later, and we find ourselves only meters from where we just found ourselves. Only this time, we find ourselves within the confines of brick walls, in what seems to be the backstage area of a bar/club. In fact, this is indeed backstage at the Rocky Mountain Way in Calgary. People are hurriedly buzzing by, preparing themselves for the comeback performance of the Simple Men. As stage hands and other important staffers walk or run by, the camera focuses in on a lone man. This man, sitting down on a stool in a separate room, sits quietly, disturbing no one. His face, covered by the shadow of a cowboy hat, portrays mystery, even through the layers of material that cover it. His body portrays a tension that can be felt through the chequered shirt, through the dirty work jeans, and even through the dirty working boots that this man is wearing. Yes, this man, although dressed as if he’s about to go on stage, doesn’t seem like he’s completely ready to take a stage and entertain a crowd through song and dance. Nevertheless, he finds himself here, sitting quietly in a room filled with props, clothes, and what-have-yous. And he sits alone, until, all of a sudden, a man bursts through the room. This man, on the other hand, looks like he’s full of life and totally ready to hit the stage. Moving quickly, we only see his shadow as he enters the room and approaches the sombre man. He is dressed almost exactly like his apparent doppelganger, his jeans, boots, chequered shirt and cowboy hat only deferring slightly in size and colour. “Spud!” The man sitting down lifts his head, almost on command, to reveal none other than, as the other man pronounced, Spud, lead singer of the Simple Men! He smiles, but it’s a fake smile. The despair on his face is evident, even through the now angled shape of his lips. He addresses his addressor. “Windy!” Windy? The other man is Windy Bridges? “How ya doin’ partner?” Yes, in fact, it is Windy Bridges! The camera swings several degrees, almost startled by this sudden revelation, to catch a profile of the standing man, and it is indeed the MWE superstar. Smiling ear-to-ear, in what seems to be, yes, indeed a sincere smile, Windy removes his cowboy hat and places it on a nearby table. He rustles his hair with his hands. “Boy, that thing’s been chafin’ me like crazy! I haven’t worn one o’ those in months.” Spud’s despair continues to be evident to everyone watching through the camera, but seemingly not to Windy. Regardless, he responds to Windy’s comment. “Ya, they do take a while to get used to sometimes.” Finally, Windy catches on. He raises his eyebrow at his “friend”, somehow perplexed by this mood that Spud finds himself in. “Is there somethin’ wrong son?” “No, Windy, everything’s fine. I’m just a little surprised to be here, is all.” “Hell brother, I’m yer best friend, a lie like that ain’t gonna get past me.” Windy grabs a chair, swings it around and sits backwards on it. He gets in really close, inches away from Spud, which startles him and send him back a few inches on his chair. “Com’on son, tell yer good ‘ole Uncle Windy what’s ailin’ ya!” Spud sighs. He might as well tell him, he’s come this far. “Well, Windy, forgive me if I’m a little uncertain about this sudden mood change from you.” Windy seems genuinely surprised by this comment. To him it seems offhand and completely out of the blue. As if he himself either doesn’t remember the last few months of his own life, his own mood change, and the actions that he has taken in order to portray himself as more “serious” or “competitive”, or what have you. “Whatever do ya mean? I’m good ‘ole Windy Bridges! Yer best friend! This here’s how I’ve always been, Spud!” Spud gets up from his stool and he walks over to the other side of the room. Maybe for dramatic effect… maybe because he really doesn’t feel comfortable being next to Windy after the last few months… “Windy, come on, don’t bullshit me. The last time I saw you, you fired me and everyone else from the Simple Men, disbanding the entire band. You screwed me out of several thousand dollars you owed me in bookings. And, interestingly enough that you don’t remember, but you beat the CRAP out of me and left me for dead on the side of the road in a town in the middle of nowhere in Nevada. Hell, I almost didn’t make it! So forgive me if I don’t feel too comfortable right now, here, next to you. The Windy Bridges that I see before me now, is not the Windy Bridges that I saw before me that night in Nevada…. And I’m seriously questioning which one is the real one.” Windy is even further taken aback. Complete and utter surprise. Is it all just an act? Or has this man gone through some severe emotional trauma since we last saw him. Regardless, Windy gets up from his chair and pushes it back in its place, under the nearby table. He approaches Spud, gets in real close. He walks up to him, and looks him right in the eyes, slightly squinting. A real Clint Eastwood kind of look. It’s bizarre, really. “Spud, I’m real sorry. I don’t know what else to tell ya. The man that did that to ya in Nevada, that wasn’t Windy Bridges. I don’t even know who that was. In fact, I don’t know who I’ve been at all these last few months…” Windy chuckles. Amazing how a man who has claimed to be completely serious, completely changed, can find amusement in this situation. “But I can assure ya, that the man ya see before ya, is your best friend. It’s the man ya grew up with right here in this here province. It’s the man who you made loads ‘o money with as the Simple Man. It’s even the man that gotcha laid by tons o’ beautiful women, dozens ‘o times on the road just last year!” Windy laughs, reminiscing on what seem to be some good old times. Spud finally cracks a sincere smile himself. Windy’s speeh has seemingly gotten to him. But he’s still hesitant and removed. “Com’on man! Ya know me! I went through some tough times. But I’m over all that crap now! I’m back, baby! Good ‘ole Windy Montgomery Bridges!” Spud finally lets loose and crack a full smile. He believes his old friend. And once again, we have to wonder if this has been one hell of an act, of if the old Windy Bridges that millions have come to know and love has really come back out of his shell. “Alright, Windy, I believe you… I mean, how could I stay mad at that face?” Windy smiles once again himself. “Well I told ya it was me! But hey, I don’t blame ya for bein’ pissed. I was a real prick back in Nevada. And once this show’s over, I’ma pay ya everythin’ I owe ya in full, with interest. Now let’s go out there and bring this here house down! These people haven’t seen the simple men in ages, and I reckon they’re gonna start ta get rowdy if we keep ‘em waiting any longer!” Windy extends his hand as a gesture of friendship to Spud. The singer hestitates for a second, but he finally gives in to Windy’s big blue eyes and big smile and shakes his best friend’s hand. Windy brings it in for a hug, and the two embrace, in a real hallmark moment. The proceed to exit the room together, encountering the other members of the band just outside the dressing room, as Windy greets them all, excitedly. “Let’s go boys, I have a hankerin for some rock music! WOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOWEEEEEEEEEEEEEE!!!” The scene fades. ------------------------------------- “WINDY!” “WINDY!” “WINDY!” “WINDY!” The crowd exhales their anticipation for their fortunate son. A purely Canadian crowd waiting for a purely Canadian band to bring the house down. Their voices are getting more and more restless, and, of course, louder and louder. They suddenly cheer even louder, as a man appears on stage and approaches the microphone. He adjusts it to his height as the light finally focuses on his shadow, to reveal none other than former WAR, MWE and REV~Pro announcer, interviewer and employee, Smitty Johnson! This night has a real reunion type feel to it. And it begs the question of whether certain people orchestrated it in such a fashion. Smitty stands on stage, casually dressed himself in a buttoned up shirt and a leather jacket with stylish jeans, as well as his trademark sunglasses, greeting the crowd’s recognizing cheers. “Alright people, enough! I ain’t what you all came here to see! Ya know, when Windy invited me out here to introduce his band, I was surprised. We hadn’t talked in a while, and frankly, I really didn’t like what I saw of him while we were out of touch. But the man assures me that he’s changed in the last few weeks, and he really wants to redeem himself. With me, with his band, his friends, and especially, all of you!” The crowd of maybe 150 or 200 rowdy and drunken Canadian people cheers loudly. Certainly excited to see their prodigal son, this speech is only making them more anxious. “Well, it looks like he’s serious, and it looks like he’s back. I ain’t gonna waist any more of your time. Without any further adieu, here he is, along with his best friend Spud, and the rest of the great band, the Simple Man, Windy Bridges!!!!” The stage goes dark. Shadows of men approaching their instruments, as the crowd goes wild. All the men are finally settled in on their instruments, lights and lasers hit the stage as a familiar guitar riff begins to play. Spud and the Simple Men begin playing Windy’s old theme song, Call me the Breeze! The familiar intro plays as a flurry of people dresses as cowboys and farm girls poor onto the stage and begin line dancing. After a moment, Windy Bridges appears amongst the line dancers and begins dancing with them, to a large ovation from the crowd. Spud sings the lyrics, the band plays the instruments, and Windy dances, just like he used to, to the severe delight of the crowd. The music continues until we approach a guitar solo. Windy breaks away from the line dancers and grabs one of the guitars lying around the stage and begins playing the guitar solo from the song as everyone claps along. Windy rocks the hell out of this solo, just as he used to. His solo finally ends and Spud continues singing the lyrics as Windy makes his way, dancing, over to the pianist. He asks permission to take over and then begins playing the piano solo to the song! His second solo ends and he makes his way, once again, dancing, back towards the middle of the stage, putting his guitar back over his shoulder, and continuing to play to the delight of the crowd. The song finally ends, the crowd applauds loudly, and Spuds gets back on the mic. “SATURDAY NIGHT’S ALRIGHT FOR FIGHTING!!!” The band begins to play the classic Sir Elton John song, with Windy’s support on guitar, as the scene once again fades. ------------------------------- “WOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO WEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEE!” The scream is loud and unmistakeable. Amongst the laughs and the cheers of the crowd in the Rocky Mountain Way bar, Windy stands up tall on the bar and yells out, one again, just like he used to. He lifts a beer high above the head and the crowd forming around him cheers. He tilts it slightly towards his mouth and begins chugging it to the delight of his supporters. He cleanly finishes the entire bottle and smashes it right on the ground in front of him. “NEEEEEHAAAAAWWW! “ The sound of the scream is all too familiar. Sounds that we had become accustom to hearing Windy make seem distant, but are quick to come back to memory, especially after all the forgettable things that we had seen him do lately. “You guys wanna see the old Windy, well here he is partners! I tell ya, I cannot believe I stopped actin’ like this, darn this was fun.” Neither can anyone else, the people at this bar included, it seems. The man they came to love for being such a nice guy is making his long awaited return, and they can’t help but love it. “This is what people wanna see. The beer drinkin’! Bar blazin’! Line dancin’! Skynyrd singin’!! WOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO! SON OF A GUN!” Windy grabs another beer and chugs that one too. He finishes the bottle and looks through it looking for another drop, but doesn’t find one. He shrugs and throws the bottle behind him. A waitress passes by with several beers on a platter. Windy grabs two bottles and begins chugging them simultaneously. Beer pours through his mouth and down his shirt, but he continues to drink. He finishes both bottles as the crowd in the bar cheers his pure binge on. He breaks one of the bottles on his head and the barhounds laugh. Windy begins bleeding from his forehead, but it doesn’t seem like he cares. “Is that what you wanna see folks? Is dis here kind of attitude whatcha wanna see from good ole Windy Bridges? WOOOOOOOOO! Feels good to bleed ma own blood darn it! Feels good to let loose! To drink! To have fun! HAHAHAHAHA!! And I guess this is what the good fan of the MWE wanna see! What they wanna see! The fun lovin’ Windy Bridges! The guy who doesn’t care whether he wins or loses! The guy who doesn’t care whether he gets his face caved in, so long as he entertains the fans in the process! The guy who’d rather put on a show than win a championship!” Windy laughs, Windy smiles. The crowd seems a little confused, but the cheer regardless. They certainly like what they see. He even does a little jig. Have we really seen another transformation from this man? Is this really the good, old, fun loving Windy Bridges that everyone came to know and love over the years? Or is it an even darker, even more sadistic Winston Bridges that we have seen this man transform into, right in front of our eyes, over the last few months? It’s hard to tell. “Well lemme tell ya somethin’ son! I missed actin’ like a goodball! I missed bein’ able just to go out and have some gosh darn, clean ole fun! I missed being the party animal that I used to be! But lemme tell ya somethin’ else!” Windy jumps down from high atop the bar, and lands on the floor of his own establishment. He approaches his friend Spud, who’s quietly standing in the corner, smiling, but clearly not certain what to make of all this. Confused, just like the rest of us, he isn’t certain whether to believe this act or not. It certainly doesn’t strike him as genuine. But Windy gets up really close to Spud. Looks him right in the eyes, just waiting to finish his though that he started high atop the bar. Itching to say what he has to say. Oozing to say it. All of sudden, beyond all the fun, beyond all the partying and all the drinking that we have just witnessed… a man appears. A real man. THE real man. THE real Windy Bridges. A sombre, gloomy look appears on Windy’s face, almost out of nowhere. The man transforms from fun and loving to deathly serious in a matter of milliseconds. “I am NOT the Windy Bridges that everyone came to know and love!” Windy Snaps! He takes the beer bottle in his hand and breaks it over Spud’s head! Screams of terror and horror pronounce their way through the bar crowd, but it doesn’t affect Windy, nor does it stop him from continuing his assault. Windy lifts the poor man from off the ground. Spud is now bleeding, and bleeding fairly profusely. He clearly can’t withstand the assault on his skin like windy has come to be used to over his years as a pro wrestler, especially one that has taken as much abuse as he has in and around the ring. The blood oozes down Spud’s forehead and down his cheeks, just as Windy from his forehead and his cheeks, only faster. Only thicker. Windy props him up against the wall of the bar. Pictures and display cases fall the to ground around him, scattering, glass shattering, and Windy rattles who we thought was his best friend violently, bumping him against the same wall. “I am NOT fun-loving!” Windy punches the already injured Spud violently. “I am NOT care-free!!” Windy pummels the man right between the eyes, once again. The fash on Spud’s forehead grows larger. The blood rains thicker, longer, and whatever other adjective you can use to describe this gruesome sight. “AND I AM CERTAINLY NOT A GOODY-TWO SHOES!!!” Windy continues to pummel the helpless man, punch, after punch, after punch, until he’s finally stopped by the bouncer and the bartender. Who grab at him and finally stop him from continuing his assault. Windy regains his cool and takes a step back, ignoring the shocked looks on the faces of the people around him. He takes a seat at a nearby table and starts drinking another beer. He frowns, and throws his cowboy hat clear across the room. He then wipes the blood off his forehead and Spud’s blood off his hand, before looking back towards the crowd. A crowd that has quickly grown deadly silent, and deadly motionless. “What are you all looking at?” The crowd does not respond. “WHAT THE HELL ARE YOU ALL LOOKING AT!!?!?!” Windy gets up from his seat, and the people in the bar take a step back. Some flee the scene completely, exiting the bar and into the streets, in a panic. Others, stay behind, frozen in fear. “GET OUT OF MY BAR!!! ALL OF YOU!!!” Most of the crowd is quick to respond, dispersing in a terror through the doors and into the streets of Calgary. The bartender and bouncer that separated Windy from a helpless Spud approach the fallen man, and prop him up, slowly moving him out of the bar, following the startled crowd. “What the hell do you two think you’re doing?” The men stop dead in their tracks and look back at Windy. Wielding a broken bottle in his hand, neither know what this man, a man with a crazed look on his face, a bloody face, and a weapon his bloody hand, is going to do, or what he’s capable of doing next. “I didn’t say to take him…” The men continue to stand there, in a scared awe. “I said, I DIDN’T SAY TO TAKE HIM! LEAVE HIM HERE AND GO!!!” This time, the men are quick to act, setting Spud back down on the floor, covered in broken glass and beer. They exit the bar, leaving only Windy and Spud behind. The bar has now fallen deadly silent. Windy looks down at his former chum, stopping dead in his tracks for a second, looking at what used to be a man full of life. He suddenly raises his head and looks at the main doors of the bar. He approaches them, pulls a key out of his pocket, and locks them. He walks back over to Spud. An allure of calm and silence has suddenly grown over the man that only moments ago, completely snapped, yelling at a crowd of hundreds and beating what used to be a lifelong friend to a pulp… to near death. He looks down at Spud, but addresses the camera. “I’m confused.” Ya think? “Is that what you people want to see? Do you want to see a bloody… violent… careless Winston Bridges? Do you want to see someone who will do whatever it takes to make a name for himself and to win? Do you want to see the Winston Bridges that is willing to do something like this to get what he wants? That’s willing to break some skulls… to cause a little action… a little destruction?” Windy is motionless. Not a tremor, barely even a breath, as he continues to speak and look down at the lifeless and unconscious body of his former lead singer. “Or do you people want to see the Windy Bridges that drinks. The Windy Bridges that dances… that plays guitar and piano in a cover band… that speaks with a Southern American accent… that drives a pick up truck and wears clothes that you’re more likely to find in a dumpster than on an actual person?” Windy still doesn’t move. The queries, the thoughts, the questions that must be going through his head at he stares a hole through Spud, and threw his own life. “Because at this very moment, there are two forces raging inside me. The good. And the very bad. And both want one thing more than anything in the world. One simple little word called respect… The good… well he would rather get said respect through hard work, through endearing himself to people and through making people laugh and entertaining them. The bad… well that’s a totally different story.” Windy’s own blood drips down from his forehead onto Spud’s shirt. Kind of symbolic of the bond that existed between these two men for years, in an eerie way. “The bad version of Winston Bridges, well he’s simply willing to do whatever the hell is takes for people to respect him… for people to fear him… to drop down to their knees when he walks into a room. To kiss his feet when he walks up to them. And to receive everything that he deserves… and right now, it seems like the bad version of Windy Bridges is the one that wants to manifest itself.” Windy finally raises his head. The blood is starting to dry, but is still gently and slowly sliding down his forehead, beyond his eyes and cheeks. “And you may ask yourself… what exactly does this version of Windy Bridges think he deserves? Well it’s pretty simple. Beyond respect… he deserves to be at the top of MWE. He deserves to be MWE Champion. He deserves to face who ever the hell he wants. Not rookies, not has-been female wrestlers that are clearly well over the hill. Not children playing games for championships… and certainly not the guy who’s been on Jerry Springer more times than he’s contracted a venereal disease!” Normally a comment that stings that much would leave an imprint of remorse on the man who made it. Even the baddest people will feel a glimmer of regret in saying something like that to their worst enemies. But this man, Windy Bridges, standing tall, looking straight into a camera, blood dripping down his face and eyes glaring, motionless, displaces no remorse. He displays no regret, no emotion at all, other than intensity. “Yes, here I find myself once again. Preparing to wrestler once again, one of the biggest jokes in the history of professional wrestling. After being screwed out of a championship in a damn game by one joke, I already get screwed by another joke only hours later. So why don’t we cut the crap D.C., huh? For once in your life, be honest. For once in your life, stop swimming in bullshit and be honest to the people.” Windy snickers. He knows what he’s about to say won’t please too many people, but he’s certain that he wants to say it. Until, of course, you saw even bigger dollar signs in your eyes. And you threw me off a damn balcony! But hey, how can I REALLY blame you for that? I mean, it was front page wrestling news no matter where you went. And I really would have been fine with it, in the end… if you manned up, and confronted me to my face about it.” Windy begins walking, slowly, towards the bar. “But you ran away, like the little girl that you are. You ran away, right back into Brian Michaels’ loving arms. You ran out on me, and out on George Prax, and our of REV~Pro, and back to the MWE. You ran away from your problems and you ran away from your responsibilies.” Windy continues his slow pace towards the bar that he owns. The camera follows, uncertain about whether he should even be here anymore. “Well guess what bitch? Those responsibilities… those problems… they’ve come back to haunt you. They’ve come back to make you pay. They’ve come back to make you FINALLY look like the fool that you really are! I’ve already made you look like a fool once, by pinning your shoulders to the mat right in the middle of the MWE ring.” Windy laughs once again. “But that wasn’t enough to shut your big fat mouth, was it? You had to book yourself in another match against me. And you had to run your mouth, and you had to claim that I only beat you… because you didn’t care… because you were pre-occupied that night… because I wasn’t important enough for you to make an honest effort to beat me.” Windy stops laughing. He stops smiling. And that serious looks re-appears on his face. The blood that once dripped down his face, down onto his long forgotten bandmate’s lifeless body, has now dried and stained windy’s face, but it doesn’t stop him. “Well that’s complete and utter bullshit, and you know it!” Windy finally reaches the bar, slowly making way around to the back of it, not hesitating in his thoughts, not in the slightest. “You’re a professional D.C.. Using carelessness as an excuse? That doesn’t even make any sense! If you didn’t care, then you wouldn’t have teamed with me all those months. If you didn’t care, you wouldn’t have tossed me off that balcony. If you didn’t care, you wouldn’t have ran away out of REV~Pro and back to the MWE. If you didn’t care, you wouldn’t have booked yourself in a match against me to begin with! And if you didn’t care, you certainly wouldn’t have booked yourself into ANOTHER match with me, AFTER I wiped the floor with you, and you certainly wouldn’t have flown out here to Calgary to burn down a bar that I don’t even own anymore!” Windy slams his hands on the table. Dust, beer, and glass radiates into the air, jumping a few inches before slamming back down onto the bar. “And your bullshit, your lying through your teeth, your politics, well it’s really starting to piss me off! You can make whatever claims you want, but D.C., I know why you booked yourself in a match against me. And it wasn’t to make amends for running away from me all those months ago. It wasn’t to physically share your displeasure with the months we shared as best friend. It wasn’t to make fun of me and tell people that you named all the crap that I pawned off on people that last two years. All the products, the stupid band, that old bar… And it certainly wasn’t so that you can find an excuse to commit arson.” Windy’s gaze is as intense as it has ever been. In his mind, what he’s saying it’s totally righteous. I’ve seen it in your eyes, D.C.. I’ve seen it countless times. When we were tagging, you knew what I was capable of, and you knew that it was best to be on my side than against me, because you were afraid. When you threw me off that balcony and proceeded to run away in REV~Pro, you knew that you needed to get rid of me and make sure I didn’t come after you, because you were afraid. And when we faced off against each other only weeks ago on MNM, I could see it in your eyes. I could feel the fear pushing down through your very soul. And I could feel that you wanted to prove to yourself that you weren’t afraid of me, and what I had become.” Windy now laughs out loud, an utterly savage laugh. “But in the end, you dropped to your damn shoulders and I pinned you, because you didn’t care? HA! You were afraid, and you KNEW that you couldn’t beat me! But if that’s the case, then why would you go up against me once again? Because you still think you have something to prove? Because you think you’re over the fear? Or is it because your little friend, Brian Michaels, is forcing you to go up against me, is scheming for you to go up against me, in order to hold me back? In order to keep me away from the US title? From the World title? From beating all your little golden boys, that you’ve conveniently propped up against each other in the main event for that same world title?” Windy shakes his head. He moves over to a nearby sink and wipes some of the blood off his face. He doesn’t even bother to scrub too much, he knows it isn’t going to come off. It’s more of a reflex to try to clean it off than anything. He rips off the ugly chequered shirt he was still wearing, stained with beer and blood, and wipes his face dry with whatever clean section he can still find. He throws it across the room before looking back over to his fallen comrade, Spud. Now semi-conscious, Spud is attempting to crawl across the bar towards the doors, obviously unaware that they are locked, and obviously unaware of Windy’s hateful gaze. “Well I’ve had enough of being held back. I’ve had enough of being treated like a joke, like a side-show, like a mid carder. I’ve had enough of you, and I’ve had enough of your bullshit. And your little plan, well, it’s going to back… fire…” Windy grins as he finishes his sentence. “And just like good ‘ole spud over there, you’re going to be crawling, fearing for your life, uncertain what your faith holds, this coming Monday night. And just like Spud over there, you’re going to get what’s coming to ya.” Windy disappears underneath the bar. A second later, he reappears, and nothing has changed. He still looks as intense, as serious, and as purely crazy as he’s ever been. With one slight difference… the contents of his right hand. A container of gasoline. And the contents of his left hand… a pack of matches. “Because you see, D.C., you can spew out whatever insults that you want. You can call me whatever names you want, claim that I’ll just leave, and run away just like you did. You can claim that your lawyers hold contracts over my head that entitle you to whatever money I make from Windy-Os, from Oh Canada Lager, from the Simple Men, from my bars. Well, D.C., I’m here tonight to tell you that none of that matters. Because the only way I’m going away is if I’m dragged out of MWE. And all those insults, they mean nothing to me, coming from a pussy such as yourself.” Windy now smiles the widest we’ve seen so far. “And all those contracts… all that money… all those profits and all those products… well… simply put, you can have him.” Windy begins pouring the gasoline onto the bar. Over all the (flammable) alcohols, and all over the floor beneath him. “Because after what I’m about to do, D.C., all those contracts, they’ll all be worthless. All those products, the cereals and the beers, I’m going to make sure no one buys even a single box, or a single bottle, ever again. I’m going to make sure that the Simple Men, well, that they never produce an album ever again. And I’m going to make sure that my bar, the new one, it never serves another drop of alcohol, ever again.” Windy jumps over the bar and continues to spill the smelly, tinted liquid across tables. Over chairs. Onto fixtures and all over the floor. “Because D.C., you may like to brag that you stake a claim to all of it. But you deserve none of it. Not a penny. And frankly, I’m sick of being associated to all of that crap. So there’s only one solution. And it involved a lot of gasoline… a few matches, and one hell of an escape plan. So I’ll see you on Monday D.C. But until then…” Windy finishes pouring all the gasoline. He picks up the matches, and without regard for life, limb, or any of the expensive things that furnish this structure, nor poor Spud, Windy lights a match. And uses that match to light the rest of the pack. Holding the blazing pack of matches high above his head, Windy takes one final look into the camera. “Until then D.C…. enjoy the fireworks!” Windy finally lets go of the matches. And the result is catastrophic. The room lights up like a Christmas tree. Flames spread out throughout the room, around and all over furniture and fixtures, and quickly spreads to the bar. Windy calmly walks down a path he left open for himself, and walks over to Spud, who’s still lying, barely moving, close to the door. “I almost forgot, I owe you some money.” You almost have to appreciate his thoughtfulness… Windy takes out his wallet, completely nonchalant about the fire blazing behind him, and pulls a stack of bills out of his wallet. He carelessly drops the money onto Spud before putting his wallet back into his pocket. Continuing his march out the room and out of site, the fire finally spreads the walls and eventually the ceiling. The structure is burning quickly, and it’s burning hard. Wooden beams, holding up the ceiling, begin to collapse under the heat and pressure, dropping to the floor and smashing half-burnt tables underneath. The bar area is not completely engulfed in flames. An explosion, presumably that of the alcohol catching fire, soon follows, sending shards of glass, wood, and anything else you can think of, flying all over the room, but the debris meets nothing but more flames. A fiery yellow and reddish glow now completely envelops our view through what seems to be a stationary camera. Unbeknownst to us, it seems like the cameraman has managed to make his escape allowing the camera to catch as much footage as he, and hopefully Spud, make their escape down the same route Windy just did, away from the flames, away from this catastrophe. This clearly symbolic burning ritual continues. Representing everything Windy Bridges wish he could do to D.C., there is no escape for anything or anyone still left in this room surrounded by fire. Through the sound of the flames, we hear sirens, as official vehicles begin to make their way towards the burning building. The camera proceeds to collapse onto it’s side, but now we can’t see anything other than flames. The end is coming, for this camera, and for this scene. But slowly, and agonizingly, we continue to witness this scene, in an incomprehensible haze, uncertain of anything going on, other than the fact that this place is burning, and very close to being simply burnt. Finally, the camera switches to static. It’s over. The damage is done. Or is it just beginning?
