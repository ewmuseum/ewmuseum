RIP Joey Maz

Sep 25 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Isn’t it funny how one moment you can be on top of the world, and with one simple phone call it can all come crashing down? I’m not talking about my suspension a few months back, or even my loss to CSB. This has to do with a phone call I got right after my match, that blew my entire world up. I walked into the lockerroom after that match, with a smile on my face. I had lost, yes, but it was to someone worthy. Nothing could touch me, or at least that is what I thought until my cell phone flashed that I had a voicemail. It was an unfamiliar number, and normally I wouldn’t have even listened but something told me I needed to. The voice on the other ended was one I hadn’t heard in years, since I left high school. It was my ex girlfriend Leah, who wanted nothing to do with me since some bullshit back in the day. I won’t go into it, but it involved her lacrosse teammate getting caught with her legs over her head. Needless to say, when I heard her speak, I was more than surprised. Hey Zak…listen, I’m going to keep this short and sweet…this is leah...my dad is dead. I tried getting a hold of you when it happened, but I couldn’t get your number. The funeral is tomorrow, back in Syracuse, and I know he would want you there. I was speechless. Her father Joe was the only reason I was still alive and on this earth. The man was like a father to me, and I couldn’t believe he was gone. Normally I was the last to leave the arena, but as soon as that phone was hung up I was on my way to the airport. I didn’t care how much I had to pay, I wasn’t going to miss this for the world. Luckily, there was a flight that would get me to Syracuse by six in the morning, so it worked out. To those of you who have never been famous, let me tell you one thing. Being in a crowded airport sucks. People wouldn’t leave me alone, and it got to be bad enough that I had to duck inside the security office to get a moment to myself. They snuck me out a back door, and onto the plane where once again, I was bombarded by people. Thank god that Steve Jobs founded Apple, cause my Ipod saved me. I passed out pretty quickly, and I was lucky enough to have a smoking hot flight attendant wake me up. Had the circumstances been different, I woulda nailed her in the bathroom right then and there but I wasn’t feelin it. I hopped off the plane, where a rental car was already waiting for me. Being a hometown legend had it’s benefits, including free rental cars. I had a bit of time to kill before the service, so I stopped by my parent’s house. Yea, I was still a momma’s boy at heart, and right about now I needed some family. Nobody was home though, but they left the key in the same spot even after all these years. They still had a stash of clothes that somehow fit me, which was always nice when I was in town. There was a suit that I hadn’t worn in years, and after a nice warm shower I was dressed up and ready to go. The church where the funeral was being held was only a couple blocks away, and I knew traffic was going to be a bitch so I ended up footing it. I got there a bit early which was a good thing, because it was already almost filled to capacity. As soon as I walked in I saw her standing there, and there was no need for words. She was sobbing as we embraced, and it was the most awkward feeling that I have had in years. When she finally pulled away, looking her in the eyes was hard. I know this is short notice, but my family and I would really appreciate it if you could say a few words for my dad. She knew I couldn’t say no, so I just nodded my head. Making up a speech for someone this important to me wasn’t going to be easy, but I had shot promos on the spot before so I wasn’t exactly an amateur. The service started soon after, and before I knew it I was standing next to the casket, without any clue what to say. As most of you know, Joe Mazella shaped the lives of countless young men and women, and I was one of those kids. While it is true that now in life I have pretty much everything I could ever want, it wasn’t always like that. In fact, had it not been for Mr. Mazella, I wouldn’t be standing here before all of you. Before I was known as Ivory, before I was a wrestling superstar, I was just another high school stoner who was skating by on the bare minimum. I would show up to school baked, go to one class, and leave to get stoned again. It got to the point where I would black out for days, and not know what happened. One day, I stumbled into school on some mix of god knows what, and I thought that it would be the last day anyone would see me. Yet as if by some sort of divine intervention, the first person I stumbled into was none other than Maz. He was the cool administrator, always joking around with us kids and acting as if he were really one of us. That day was different though. He pulled me aside and was genuinely concerned. He knew that I had been going through a tough time, and he just wanted to make sure I was doin ok. He knew I was messed up, but he didn’t bring it up once. He just wanted to make sure I was ok, and he wanted me to know that there was at least one person that cared. I don’t know if he ever knew it, but that day he saved my life. That day he showed me that life was worth living, and that I actually mattered to someone. I never got the chance to tell him what an impact he had on my life, and now I never will. So to you, Joseph Mazella…Rest in Piece, I hope that I can grow up to be half the man that you were and to this day, I will ALWAYS bleed Black and Gold for you Coach Maz...for you. I’ll admit it, I broke down at that point. I hadn’t talked to Joe often, but it didn’t matter. Every time I did, he knew just what to say to make things ok, and now I didn’t have that anymore. I know it sounds cliché, but I lost more than a friend when Joe died, I lost a family member. I couldn’t even stay in the church any longer, it hurt too much. There was a bench right outside that I sat on, and I couldn’t do anything but cry. Sure enough, as soon as the tears began to flow there was some two bit asshole with a camera right in my face, asking how I felt, and even about my match with D.C. That pushed me over the edge, and one right hook later I had a fresh new camera for my collection. Since my emotions were already at an alltime high, I figured a promo was due. I let the camera linger on the paparazzi who was now laying in a small pool of blood, before turning it on myself. A church wasn’t the best spot for this though, so I started to walk aimlessly around the neighborhood I once called home. Isn’t it a shame how people wait until a man is at his very lowest point to pounce? Take that cameraman for example…he saw me crying, which I NEVER do, and decided that it was time to try and make a name for himself. Instead of becoming some famous journalist, known for his edgy approach, he is just another victim. I made him famous, but for all the wrong reasons. But that’s not what this is about, that isn’t why I jacked his camera. I have a match coming up, and it can either make or break me. I know that most people will agree with me when I say that my match with Kevin was the biggest in my career, but I don’t know if it was as major as this one. I get to face one of the MWE Origianls, a man who was one of the reasons I chose MWE when GWC folded. I get to face Mr. Controversy himself, D fucking C. Yet as sure as I stand before you all right now, I don’t know what to expect. I don’t really know what D.C. I’m going to get. Will I get the future hall of famer, or will I get the ego-maniacal prick who bitches about money. Will I get the man who has put on some of the greatest matches in Professional Wrestling history, or will I get a man who flat out lies about wanting to face people. Yeah, I said it, you are a bold-faced liar D.C. Up until your little shoot, I had no idea you wanted to face me. Did you ever once come to me and see if I wanted it? No. Hell, I don’t even believe when you say that you were turned down by the board, because they knew damn well I wanted to face anyone and everyone. This was all a play to get more money, and it worked. But in the process, you pissed some people off and one of them was me. I will thank you though, because this time I am where I belong on the card. I’m not curtain-jerkin, and people might actually tune in to my match. So for that D.C., I thank you. Just don’t mistake my gratitude as some sort of defeat because then you would be sorely mistaken. Yeah, I lost last week but it wasn’t a surprise to me. I had said it before, and I’ll say it again…I DON’T WANT THE WORLD FUCKING TITLE. It’s just a piece of metal, that makes people think they are better than they really are. Take Sinc for example. He was good, he was World Title material, but he let it get to his head and what happened? BOOM it was over, and Grand Mystique is now holding the belt. Who knows though, CsB or Jesus Cruz might come out of nowhere and take that title. Lord knows both of them have the talent…I should know. Enough about that though, because regardless of how this match turns out for me, a title isn’t in my future plans. Hell, other than making it week to week, I have no plans. I’m not like Mr Revolution DC and his little crusade, although I know where he is coming from quite well. You see, DC and I are quite similar, which is scary as fuck. We are both overlooked and underrated, the only difference is that he gets paid 7 mil for it while I get a fair bit less than that. Competitivly, most would say he has the edge on me and I would be hardpressed to deny that. He has done so much, that I can’t really compare in terms of accomplishments. Yet he still chose to call ME out, instead of someone else. I can’t figure out why, and it irks me. Maybe he sees me as sort of a little Mini-D.C. Maybe I should be parading around, talking about a revolution. Or maybe, instead of TALKING about it, and jockeying for money, I should do something about it. You can sell your shirts, make all that revenue without stepping into the ring, but I’ll do it MY way. I’ll step into that ring every match, and try to knock people the fuck out. I don’t care who it is, from David Renshaw to Grand Mistique, to Sonny, Paige, and David. If that bell rings, and you are standing across from me, I’m gunnin for you. The MWE Board wants to overlook me, and only change it when their precious little D.C. threatens to walk? I wonder how happy they will be when I break his face. I wonder if they will overlook me when I have him gasping for air as the life is choked out of him. Now don’t get it twisted, I have nothing against D.C. as a person. He is a chill dude, who I don’t mind crackin a beer with after a show. As a professional though…let’s keep it simple and say that we have some creative differences. So D.C., when you feel my foot hit your face, and your eyes water up…don’t take it personally. When you scream in pain and have to tap out, it’s not personal. I’m just doin what I can to get noticed. This funeral has been sort of a rebirth for me, in a number of ways. I will still be the same bong tokin, racist jokin Warner you all know and love, but things are changing. First it is Jesus Cruz, next….who knows. Just know that nothing is impossible, especially for me. Shoot up my Addicts, withdrawal is a bitch. You want a witty ending to all of this?
