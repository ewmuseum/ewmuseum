Just a position of Juxtaposition; A David Whitman RP

Jun 5 2009

David Whitman

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What is it that makes a face-off epic? That makes a struggle, a historic one? What EXACTLY is it? Sometimes, it is the thrill of trying to find out which of similar entities is the most intense. Which powerful monster has the most power, which will is truly the strongest, which resilience can topple all others… Other times, it is the excitement of pitting completely contrasting elements against each other and watching which will prevail. Darkness, or light… Heat, or cold… And on a special, very rare occasion, we are treated to a battle of undreamt of proportions. That is when both types of struggles are fused. When frustration, cockiness, passion, fear, anticipation, technicality, brutality… all collide. When three different generations of sorts clash. The match becomes even better when it is realized that each component is not what it seems, but rather, a surprising contradiction. The freshest face being the one that has the most experience. The comedic loudmouth being the angriest, most fed-up one. And the champion, the one who should feel the best, feeling like nothing, worthless. None of it takes away from the levels of intensity of the matchup, though. Perfected technical prowess, fine-tuned hardcore brutality and polished high-risk intensity. And all with the highest and most coveted of trophies on the line; the Mayhem Wrestling Entertainment World Heavyweight Championship. This juxtaposition of three different styles, three different mindsets, three different and yet similarly skilled athletes, will prove to be explosive, exciting, and undeniable… The Dominican Republic. Santo Domingo. Two unlikely friends. One very likely and in fact quite predictable spot. The two buddies of sorts are chilling at the Boca Chica beach, which, aside from a bar or airport, would be the most likely place you’d find these two. If you were to look for them, that is. They sit, each one on their own beach chair on the pristine white sand, and between them another beach chair, this one covered with twenty or so unopened beer cans, some folders, loose papers, a briefcase and a backpack. Tammy and David are standing on a balcony outside of a hotel room in Brooklyn’s Lincoln Plaza.
