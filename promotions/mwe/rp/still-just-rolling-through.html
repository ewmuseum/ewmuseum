Still Just Rolling Through; Semi RP: Part 2

Mar 14 2009

Jesse Anderson

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The scene fades in at the front of a western style police station that says Dallas Sheriff's office. There's a big Texas star stamped over the door. From the right side of the view, a white and blue police cruiser pulls in front of the camera and stops. The driver's door opens and out steps a deputy, clad in a brown shirt, brown slacks, and a brown ten-gallon hat. He has tufts of blonde hair sticking out from under his hat and a long blonde mustache that curls down towards his cowboy boots. A shiny golden badge in the shape of a star supplements his chest pocket and a six-shooter supplements his hip. The deputy steps around the car and opens the door to the back seat on the other side. A large head pokes up from behind the car; the head of the Semi. Deputy: Just watch y'er head now. Le's go, we aint got all day! The deputy begins leading Semi towards the front door of the sheriff's office. As Semi steps further from the cruiser, you see that his hands are cuffed behind his back. The two make their way to the door and the deputy opens it with one hand, and impatiently puts the other hand on Semi's back, leading him through. The camera cuts to inside the office now. The bright light shining through the blinds leaves only bars of light across the floor. There are desks scattered about with heaps of paperwork and knick knacks piling on most of them. Only one desk is occupied, by a skinney man with messy brown hair. There is a tall cactus in the corner beside a water cooler, and above the water cooler is a large painting of an old man with gray hair that reads "Sheriff Tex" at the bottom. In the opposite corner of the room is a jail cell with thick steel bars in front of a toilet and a torn up mattress. Semi glances at the cell coldly for a moment. As Semi and the deputy walk in the door, the man at the one occupied desk looks up from the desk and watches them enter. Semi has a more than smug look on his face. He looks angry enough that you could cook an egg on his forehead. As they step inside, the deputy with Semi calls to the other deputy at his desk. Deputy: Get the sheriff for me, would'ja, Jeff'ry? Jeffery nods his head and scoots his chair back a little to get out. He turns his head slightly, then puts his hand to the side of his mouth and hollers. Jeffery: Hey, Tex! Get on out here! Jeffery returns his look back to Semi for a moment then back down to his desk before scooting his chair back in and clearing his throat. The deputy following Semi looks annoyed and pulls Semi further into the center of the room from the door. After a short while, a wooden door in the back wall opens, and the gray haired man in the painting steps through. He has cold blue eyes and patches of gray stubble around his chin. He, too, is wearing a brown uniform a large hat, a badge, and a gun. He darts his eyes around the room, from Jeffery, to Semi's captor to Semi himself. The old man sticks out a bony finger and beckons them forward. Semi is pushed along and they step over to Tex, closer to his door, but still in the middle of the main room. Tex: What do we have here, Bill? Bill mumbles for a moment and starts checking his pockets hastily. Bill: We have here a Mister Anderson who is charged with... He stops and begins checking his pockets more furiously. Bill: There it is! Bill pulls a notepad from his pants pocket and begins reading it quickly to Tex. Bill: Evading police, grand theft auto, grand theft clothing, speeding, failure to yield, failure to stop at a stop sign, driving a vehicle without the proper license... He flips the page and continues on. Bill: Driving down the center of the road, failure to stop at another stop sign, failure to use a turn signal and... Bill turns his head slowly from the paper to give Semi a very cold and menacing look as he finishes the list through clinched teeth. Bill: Making fun of my mustache. The notepad is slipped back into Bill's pocket, but his intense eyes are still trained on Semi. Tex looks between Bill and Semi with a thoughtful look in his eyes. He turns his attention to Semi. Tex: Well, what do you have to say for yourself? Semi returns Tex's thoughtful look and says nothing at first. He stares into Tex's eyes long and hard before beginning to speak. Semi: Do you know who I am? Tex looks annoyed and answers. Tex: Yeah, I know who you are. You're that TV wrastler. So? Semi's look turns to surprise. Semi: So? I'll tell you so! Semi drops his voice very low and speaks slowly. Semi: I was a friend of Tim Fitzgerald. Tex opens his mouth to reply but stops. He turns white as a ghost and his eyes drop to the floor. Bill's eyes grow wide and he takes off his hat and places it over his heart. His eyes too drop to the floor. Tex speaks up, in a small voice. Tex: Tim Fit... Fitzgerald, huh? Semi: Yeah, that's right, Tim Fitzgerald. Tex crosses his arms and looks down at his feet. He sighs deep. Tex: God rest his soul. Bill: He was too good for this world. Semi looks between them with a look saying they should be ashamed. Semi: Exactly. What would Tim say about all of this if he were here right now? The sheriff and his deputy glance at each other momentarily before both return their looks to the floor. Semi: I'll tell you what he would say. He would say that we should let bygones be bygones; live and let live. Tex sighs, but doesn't budge. Semi: You know I'm right. Look... my car broke down in the desert and I needed to get here to wonderful, beautiful Dallas, Texas, so I borrowed some transportation to get the rest of the way. I'm here now and the truck can be returned to it's owner. I'll even put in a full tank of gas. No one was hurt. The corner of Semi's mouth pulls into a mischievous grin. Semi: And come ooooon; who doesn't break a couple silly traffic laws when they're in the middle of the desert? I just didn't realize I was being pursued. Tex, still with his eyes to the floor, grins an agreeable grin and nods his head. A tear comes to Bill's eye and he sniffles. Semi: So are you really going to go to all the trouble and heartache of convicting a man of something he needed to do just to get to his job to feed and clothe himself? Or are you going to do like one of the greatest men to ever come from this state would want you to and be compassionate and understanding towards your fellow man? Bill breaks down and begins sobbing like a baby. He excuses himself and walks away, with his hat over his face. Tex watches him leave with a look of worry; Semi watches him leave with a look of amusement. Tex looks from Bill back to the floor, then finally up to Semi before he speaks, softly and soothingly. Tex: Sir, I do believe you're right. Tex walks around to behind Semi and grabs a ring of keys from his back pocket. Tex unlocks the handcuffs from Semi's wrists and stuffs them, into his pocket, along with the keys. Tex turns Semi around and puts his hands on Semi's broad shoulders. Tex: Tim certainly was that kind of man and you're right; we should all be more like him sometimes. You're free to go; just don't do it again. Semi: You're doing the right thing. I guarantee Tim is looking down on you with grace as we speak. Tex's eyes brighten, and he smiles wide with honor. Tex: You're a good man for saying so. Now get on out of here and good luck in Terror Dome. Tex winks at Semi and motions to the door. Semi: Thank you. Semi turns and begins heading towards the camera and towards the front door. As he turns away from the other men in the room, Semi lets out a silent sigh of relief and rolls his eyes high into his head for a moment. He grins slightly and mumbles under his breath. Semi: Idiots. Semi then walks to the front door, pushes it open, and disappears outside, letting the door slam behind him. All three officers jump at the loud bang of the door. The officer at the desk just watches as the scene progresses while Tex stands over Bill for a long while and tries to calm him as he continues to ball. Bill grabs a handkerchief from a desk drawer and blows his nose loudly. The sound is then heard of the same door being opened again. All three pairs of eyes raise to see what's going on. The door then opens back and Semi steps back inside with a face that, for possibly the first time in Semi's life, looks humbled. He looks just passed the camera and speaks up softly. Semi: You know what else Tim would do? He would give me a ride to the nearest bus station. The scene cuts out. OOC This was my tribute to Tim and attempt at a humorous RP. I didn't know Tim real well, but I was part of WCE (but not the fed) when Tim was in charge and knew him from there, and then fedded as Semi with Tim (as John Austin) in WHQ. He was always a great guy and a lot of fun to have around and I surely do miss him.
