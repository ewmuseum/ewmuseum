Zak Warner Ivory Cory Bouncer Bartender Mika Skye - Part IV - Fall, 2006 Cory had quietly hoped that the constant stream of activity the city held would help distract the two of them from the morning’s happenings, but it didn’t seem to. It was if nothing could distract Zak, he even ate with lost eyes, as if he didn’t have to look at the food he put in his mouth. “He’ll eat the fork if he keeps that up.” Cory thought as he watched. He sighed as gently as he could, not wanting to disturb the tension, and went back to his breakfast, trying to figure out what he could possibly say in this situation. “So… what you wanna do today?” Zak looked up from the table, his eyes finally actually locking on Cory. Two words had immediately came to mind, two words above all others. “The Lost.” “The Lost? Alright man, sounds good to me. You wanna fill the day first though?” Too late, Zak had already went back to his own reality and his meal, leaving Cory to dine alone. “Don’t worry, I’ll think of something for us to do.” he said, a faint feeling of victory in his voice. The day faded into the memories of the civilization, the night becoming the new now, the signal for Zak and Cory to head to The Lost. The Lost was just a runaway escape from life and the harshness of reality for some, but for a few, it was life. Their existence was based around the flashing lights and the dimmed sense of the world. For them, the bands and the alcohol and the store-bought smoke was all. Their livelihood, the families, everything they knew, was built on the distorted view of life they offered for a few hours everyday. It also held some of the answers Zak sought after. At least, he hoped it did. That’s what ran through his body along side his blood as he made his way through the city, a dimmed sense of hope. Could The Lost hold a life for him? It would have to tell him more then he already knew, wouldn’t it? After all, all he knew about himself so far was his name, an address and the year. Hardly enough to build any knowledge of self on. “That woman, she knows me. She knows things, things she can tell me. The woman in The Lost…” he thought as he made his way blindly through the city, taking care to watch for signs so he could make his way back to the apartment. That was far in the back of his mind though, as Miami flowed past him, a slideshow of disjointed pictures. Every image he saw stirred something deep within him, something from long ago, a trace of a reality he didn’t know he existed. “There it is my friend, The Lost, one of the most happening places in all of Mi-am-i. Just ask the owner, he’ll tell ya.” Cory said as the two got in line. “Which is weird, because the line is never long.” “Good doormen to thank for that.” said a bouncer as he browsed their ID before brushing them into the club, a shove for Cory. “So Zak…” Cory’s voice was cut off by the blasting music Disar creates. Adjusting, he continues. “… What you wanna come here for?” Zak continued his path, cutting through the crowd, brushing Cory’s question with the turns of his head as he surveyed the crowd. “Where is she…?” he thought, the old cliché of not being able to hear himself think brand new in his mind. “She’s got to be here.” He slowly absorbed the entire room, letting the décor and people sink in. Everything from the jukebox nobody really used to the expansive stage with speakers to match its extravagance, the pulsating dance floor and the platforms around it to the long bar with it’s soft lighting. The air was tainted with the smell of alcohol, sweat and the subtlety of sin. He quickly spotted a familiar face. The barmen. He was soon up close. “What can I get ya?” “A person.” “Oh? Never heard of it, how about you order something I can dish out.” “I’m looking for someone.” “Sorry buddy, I aint missing persons. Go ask security.” The barman looked towards someone else, a young lady. Following his eyes, Zak saw her mouth begin to create sounds. “Can’t help me, you saw her. She ordered a drink for me.” “Tonight?” “No.” Visibly angry now, the tender pressed his palms onto the bar, pushing himself slightly higher, an effort to intimidate. “You expect me to remember a single person who ordered drinks, and not even from tonight?” A quick silence. “Yes.” The barman shook his head and began to walk off, the annoyance almost flying from his skull. “Don’t waste my time, alright? I’ve got work to do.” He showed Zak a profile view as he restarted his service of the woman. Zak watched for a moment, gathering his thoughts and whatever hope he could salvage. With a sigh, he returned to cutting through the crowd in search when he was halted by a sudden hand on his shoulder. Turning, the face of a man filled his vision, so close the stubble of his thin beard could be individually counted. “Couldn’t help but overhear, ‘cause I was standing behind you in the queue. You see, I’m a regular round ‘ere, so I might know who it is you’re looking for.” Zak wasn’t sure what to say to the man’s words that were being shouted in his ear. “Now, what she look like?” An image immediately burst into mind with vivid accuracy. “A bit shorter then me, long light brown hair, a hint of black. She was wearing tight gray jeans, a faded pink top. A bright voice.” The man paused for a moment, considering the verbal portrayal. “Now, although that’s a pretty common image, if I had to guess I’d say you were talking about Skye. She’s a friend of mine round here. If I remember right, she was wearing that outfit the other night. I was checking her out, ya know?” He laughed at his own joke with a pride that dumbfounded Zak. He ended his chuckle with a nod towards the back. “Come with me mate, she’s sitting up back. Away from all the noise, well, as far from it as ya can get in here.” With each followed step, hope grew in Zak’s heart. Beyond a sea of glow-sticks and alcohol and drug fueled dancing, a glimpse of an answer supposedly sat. He prayed that this Skye was the right woman, he begged whatever force that dictated his life for this one, brief enlightening. “Here she is my friend.” The man gestured to a table in a corner of the club, with three figures sitting around it. She stopped breathing. As did the man behind them. Almost in unison, they mouthed the same words. “Bullshit…” “Zak, you can’t be serious.” His cold, fogged eyes calmed all doubt. “Can you help me?” She didn’t move, betraying her mind that moved faster then any time before. Out front, tomorrow, 1pm, ok?” He nodded eagerly. “Ok then, see you tomorrow Zak.” She clutched her head as she turned to Samantha and Kristine, the man’s seat empty. “Look girls, sorry but I gotta go… where’d Mika go?” They both shrugged and pointed towards the door before waving. “Ok, if ya have to go. But why so suddenly?” “Yeah! Stay and have fuuuun! Your friend can join us! He’s a hottie!” They laughed as Skye shook her head and began to walk away, looking back to Zak to find he had already vanished into the crowd to find Cory. He was standing near the bar, on his toes, scanning the crowd for any sign of his friend, spotting him emerging from the field of the eerie green glow, creating a smile and wave. “Hey Zak! Where’d you go off to? Is that a hint of a smile I see? I guess you found what you came here for then?” Looking towards the door, Zak saw Skye walk out, a stumble in her step. “Yes, I did.” Outside, Skye walked in her own world, almost oblivious to everything in her path. “Have a safe walk home Skye.” said the doorman, his words only getting a slight hand gesture in response. “Bitch must be fuuucked up.” he said to one of his co-workers. His name’s Zak. He’s… lets just say he’s an old friend.” “Old friend as in old flame? Or old friend as in friend?” She didn’t answer, instead she looked to the stars. “Oh, ok then.” As silence fell over them, Mika casually looked around, searching for something. He stopped on a dumpster next to a small closed restaurant, his legs then copying his eyes. “Look Skye, I’m really sorry about this.” She stopped to face him. “What’re you talking abo.” his fist finished her sentence. Before she could scream for help, his boot was across her throat, allowing him to drag her with all his strength across the hard ground, leaving a trail of flesh and hair and blood, her wails of pain muffled by her own lack of oxygen. He laid her next to the big wheel of the dumpster and took a step back, avoiding her tear filled eyes and her gasps for breath in between struggled pleas. Blocking it all out, including his own feelings of remorse and pure self-hatred, he delivered a hard kick to her face, driving her skull into the tire, cueing her unconsciousness. “I fucking love this girl… why did it have to be her… why? But it has to be done… I guess…” Mika lifted the lid of the dumpster before raising her to her feet. He gave her one last look into the now glazed over eyes that he held open.
