Gimme back my Bullets

Mar 15 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
OOC: The deadline on the show says Sunday, March 14th, and there hasn't been an RP posted today, so I hope I'm not late :S Anyway, here it is... edit: fixed a slight coding error. -------------------------------- ”Redemption.” The voice is somber, yet confident. Loud, yet calm. The sound of a man who has been through a lot, both good and bad, yet has managed to take it all in stride. To step aside from himself, and realize that that what has been happening to him, and around him, may not have been completely at the disposal of his will. ”Redemption is what this has been all about. From my return at Friday Night Overdrive. To my match against Zak Warner at the last episode of Monday Night Mayhem. For some reason, I felt the need to redeem myself. In the eyes of my opponents. Of my co-workers. Of my loved wons… of my fans… and most importantly, in my own eyes.” It Is the voice of a man uncertain of his future, but sure of where he’s been and what he’s taken from it. Windy Bridges. How the mighty have fallen. The camera pans back slightly to reveal the face of a man who, as said, has been through a lot in his life, yet alone in the last few months. A pensive man, one pondering his past, as well as his present and his future. A man, whilst enjoying his life and all the good fortunes that have been brought forth to him, has stepped into a lull, and is not enjoying it. Windy is sitting on a bench. No, not in front of some baseball hall of fame (ya, how Canadian is that, eh?), but in a park. The grass is green, the birds are chirping, the kids are playing, running, carefree. Leaves are growing on the trees and the sun is shining. This certainly isn’t Canada in mid March. Nevertheless, we are here, an interesting scene for a man that still doesn’t seem to have completely found himself. ”So now I sit here, in this park in God-knows where, and I ask myself that same question. Have I redeemed myself in front of anymore? Moreso, have I ever even needed to redeem myself in anyone’s eyes? Or is it some weird transposition of what I was told by Koala and the Grand Mystique back in January? Was what they told me on that faithful night where GM crashed my car, and beyond, what people thought of me? Or was it what I had come to expect of people?” Windy shifts his weight and crosses his legs. He takes a deep breathe of fresh air and takes in his surroundings. As if he just awoke in this place, on this bench. As if he got here at the same time as everyone else watching this scene along with him. ”You know, sometimes I don’t know why I continue to do this. I don’t even know why I even came back at all. When I first did, it was a spur of the moment, rash decision. I was in no physical or mental condition to face a specimen like the Grand Mystique. He was on a roll, he was beating everybody, and most people will tell you that they expected him to make quick work of me. But you know what? He didn’t make quick work of me. I took him to the brink. I almost beat him. And even Grand Mystique will tell you that I was more of a challenge than he expected of a man who had claimed to be broken, who had claimed to not know his direction in life, in this industry. But bringing GM to the bring was not good enough. And that brought me down. And that downward spiral continued to the next Monday Night Mayhem, where Zak Warner seemingly made quick work of me. And you know what? A lot of people seem to expect it to continue into Tim’s tribute show this coming Monday, and I can’t blame them. The Windy Bridges they see here, is not the Windy Bridges that went 8 and 1 in the old MWE. He’s not the Windy Bridges that went undefeated, 6-0, in EW, and he’s not the Windy Bridges who helped make REV~Pro as big as it became in such little time. No, this Windy Bridges is a man that’s grown, that’s evolved. This Windy Bridges is a man that needs to get out of a funk.” Windy stands up and watches some more kids run by, running, laughing, playing. He looks at them as they go by and smiles. He then begins walking westward down a path. Does he know where he’s going? Is there a destination to this determined march? Or, just like before, is Windy searching for something at random, hoping to stumble upon some sort of redemption? ”So I could stand around and talk a little more about what I’ve accomplished, about my legacy, about my record. But all of that is in the past. The fact remains… Windy Bridges is 0 and 2 in the new MWE, and, if popular demand decided match, would be 0 and 3 after this Monday. The fact remains, I am 6 months older then when I was at the top of my game. I have gone through several injuries since then. And something is holding me down. Ever since my losses to the Grand Mystique, and to Zak Warner, I have searched long and hard for what it is that my performances have been lacking… for what certain something that was holding me down… and then I realized…” Windy cocks his head away from the camera. The camera pans to follow his sight, to discover a large building. Tall structure, gray in color, kind of bland and reeking of death. Beneath the several floors of gray, a parking lot buzzes with cars, in and out, and even more distinguishing… ambulances. Yes, this is the scene of a hospital. ”Well, it seems like my journeys have finally brought me somewhere. But now, I’m not sure if it’s something that’s been holding me back… no, no, no… it’s clear to me now… that ever since REV~Pro, it’s been someone. Or several people. And today. I cast those demons. Today…. I confront those people that have turned me into a joke. And today, I become a new man." Windy has arrived at his destination. The big, gray building is intimidating in front of him. Demons was right. Windy stares up the building, his head arching up higher than one could think possible. He gulps, swallows his pride, and steps towards the building, stopping only for the sliding doors to swing open. And then he disappears inside. Deep within the paper walls and blue-washed scrubs. Amongst the diseased and the recovering, Windy is now in a place he, for better or for worse, knows very well. Determined, he walks by rooms and rooms of the sickly and injured, looking for one in particular. Room after room, one can only imagine what diseases and what injuries plague each person. And how many of those injuries Windy has endured, has suffered, in hospitals such as this one? But Windy isn’t here on some sort of charity work. No, this time, it’s all about Windy. Reaching his destination, Windy freezes at the door. He stops, and ponders what he’s about to do. He looks into the room, and the camera follows his action. Peering into the room, a man, lying on a bed. Broken, covered from head to toe in casts. Not moving. The only sign of life, a soft, calm, continuous beeping sound. Windy enters the room. He approaches the bed, and whatever is left of this man, and he sizes him up. It’s odd… almost as if Windy feels superior to the man lying on the hospital bed. ”Since as long as I can remember…. We’ve been working together. As long as I can remember, you’ve been my best friend in professional wrestling. You’ve been my tag team partner. And you’ve been the one man who’s understood. Since as long as I can remember, you were there for me when I was down, and you were there to lift me up whenever I needed it. In MWE, you took me out, got me drunk, took me to parties. You were right there when I received the key to Canada. You were right there when I needed to tag out during a match. Hell, if it weren’t for you, I would have never grown the balls to talk to Debbie. You, were my closest friend, and one of the best things to ever happen to me.” Windy sighs. He paces around the room. He glances around, sizes up the room. He walks over to the window and gazes out, as he continues to talk. ”But then last fall… something happened, and you changed. It must have been this business with Brian Michaels in MWE. Maybe it was something with Lindsey. But hey, what would I know? You never came to talk to me! I was great for a good time out on the town, I was great for showing you a good dive bar and singin’ a tune… but when it came to anything serious, you brushed me aside.” Windy turns back towards the bed, even more determined. However, the man lying there, has not moved an inch. Still, the calming, continuous sound of the machinery beeping, representing the sound of this man’s life. ”D.C…. to you… I was nothing but a joke…. A clown… I was a Canadian in a cowboy hat, carrying around a guitar, that got you some good press and gave you some fun out on the town, but I was nothing more to you than that. And when you were done with me, you threw me off a balcony and pretended like I never existed. You threw me out like a toy you didn’t want anymore, you spoiled brat! And I took it! I didn’t care. I assumed you had some troubles and that, somehow, was fine with me. Windy gets really close to the man now. Inches away from the cast that separates him from what lies beneath. ”But that isn’t fair to me, now is it? I’m used to be the punching back. I’m used to being everyone’s tampon. Use me as you need to, sully me with your sweat, with your blood, and then toss me away whenever I cease to amuse you. It’s been the case everywhere I go, and with anyone I meet. Well, D, now, I’m a changed man. I’m no longer the punching bag you grew to love, and then to hate. This is the new Windy Bridges, and like it or not, I’m going to be up in yours, and everyone else’s face, from now on. And being here… in this hospital room… getting this off my chest… and in your face… if the first step to discovering the winner in me that’s been lost, ever since you tossed me off that balcony and into the trash. D.C., you might be hanging by a thread on this machinery, but to me… you’re dead.” Suddenly, Windy is startled by the sound of a woman’s voice. ”HEY! What the hell do you think you’re doing!?!” Windy jumps up. He turns to see a women dressed in hospital scrubs, arms crossed, tapping her foot, not looking too happy. ”Why… I was just visiting with my friend here…” ”Friend!? Son, this man was found in the street last night after being hit by a bus. By all accounts, he’s a street bum. He ain’t got no friends!” ”Street bum? Nah, I think you’re making a mistake… this is D.C.’s room, the professional wrestler.” ”D.C.!? That guy was discharged two weeks ago! Ain’t he wrestlin’ at that show on Monday!?” Windy’s eyes widen and his face reddens in embarrassment. He had been mulling over coming here and confronting what he called his demons for weeks. And now, it was too late. D.C. was out of his hospital bed and Windy was standing over the bed of some homeless person who was in a bus accident, looking like a jackass. He begins walking towards the door and scurries passed the pissed off, overworked and underslept hospital nurse, and slowly begins making his way down the corridor. ”Oh… well… then, I’m so sorry…” ”Ya, you better be!” Once again, Windy disappears amongst victims of disease, crime and accidents, no more redeemed then when he arrived… ------------------------------------ ”So you’re on you’re way?.... good… I’m here, ya. I’ll be waiting… alright…. See you soon…” Sitting on the steps of the hospital he just vacated, Windy doesn’t feel healed, or renewed, as a man usually does when he leaves the hot stench of death and disease that succumbs the other side of these walls. No. He feels like a jerk. He wanted to redeem himself, instead, made himself look like an idiot. He wanted to look like some hotshot. Instead, he looked like a noob. ”You know, I didn’t want it to come to this. As you can tell, I had trouble even getting to this point. Now, my hand is forced, and I have to do something drastic. I’m about to meet with a man who will change the very nature of who Windy Bridges is. I’m about to meet with a man who is infamous for the things that he’s done, yet revered for the success he’s had. And hopefully, some of that success can rub off on me and carry me to my own. Unfortunately, it might not be soon enough to get me a win tomorrow night on Tim’s Tribute Show against Mason Resoan. I’m going into a match with a man on a relative role. A man who’s been wrestling on the cusp of the top of the card for months. A man just as determined if not moreso than myself, but a man who’s also in peak physical condition. I have to say, things don’t look very good for me.” Windy stands up on the steps and slowly begins walking down. Step by step, he gets closer to the concrete ground below. ”But that’s not going to stop me from going into that match with as much determination and drive as I possibly can. Resoan, you may be in a better spot for this match than I am, you may be more confident in yourself and you may have the odds on your side, but this wouldn’t be the first time I’d have gone into a match as the underdog. And I’ll tell you. Rut or no rut. Bad string of matches or not… that’s not going to stop me from doing some damage. Tomorrow night is the beginning of a new era for Windy Bridges, and you, and the fans alike, will hopefully get to witness the beginning of a transformation. Now I’m not going to lie, it’s going to be a long and difficult journey. But in the end, people won’t believe what hit them. In the end, I will become a new man, and I will become an MWE Champion. And guys like Mason Resoan, are the ones who stand in my way.” Windy reaches the ground below and begins walking towards the parking lot. His eyes focused in front of him, the camera turns to discover what he was looking at… a black, stretch limousine, parked silently but calmly on the edge of a parking lot, waiting. Windy reaches the limousine, but turns back to the camera. ”Mason, you stand around and you run down the MWE roster like it’s cool. You stand around and talk me down for not beating GM just because he burned a Canadian flag, an act, might I add, is actually legal in our country. You stand around and preach your own accomplishments as if it means something to me. Well, let me tell you something Mason. You mean nothing to me. Your accomplishments, and what you think of Ace Cannon or Joey Dallas, that means nothing to me. I’m no longer a man that cares about the person he’s facing. From now on, I’m out for #1, and only #1. And it’s too bad if you have to stand in my way. You see, I’m about to step into a limousine with a man I never thought I’d directly associate myself with. And once I step in, there’s no turning back. And this man, will make Windy Bridges a star once again. And hopefully, he can do it quickly enough to make Mason Resoan an example of what happens, when you mess with the new and improved Windy Bridges!” Windy turns around and opens the door to the limousine. ”You got here faster than I thought.” A voice emanates from inside the limousine. ”Well apparently too fast for you. Get in Windy, we have a lot of work to do and not a lot of time to do it.” Windy turns back towards the camera. He takes one final look into it, as if looking directly at his fans. As if looking at them for the last time. As if it were an end of an era. The end of Windy Bridges as we know him, before he steps out into a great unknown, as a knew man, as a different man. Finally, Windy turns back towards the limousine and takes a step inside. He shuts the door behind him, as the limousine quickly pulls away, outside of the parking lot and into the streets beyond. Into the new, great, unknown, only the people inside knowing what awaits.
