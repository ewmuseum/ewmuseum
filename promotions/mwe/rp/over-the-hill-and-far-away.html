Over the Hill and Far Away

Feb 12 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It’s bitterly cold. The kind of cold that turns your fingers as purple as a beet and your face as red as a tomato. Which is kind of ironic because those vegetables would never survive in this kind of weather. If Hell froze over, this would be it. This must be Canada. And with further inspection of the surroundings of this scene, it’s clear that it is. The camera pans from what seems to be darkness to discover a fairly busy street. Bars with cowboys and bulls on the banners. Restaurants claiming to have the “World’s best Beaver tails”. Canadian flags on poles hanging from every business. Yup, this is Canada. Calgary, Alberta, Canada, to be exact. And it’s pretty lively for a briskly cold winter evening. But people are nice and snug in their brightly colored puffy parkas, walking down the street, merrily, looking for nightlife, looking for excitement. In a few words, being Canadian. Pretty lively for a frozen over version of Hell. At least, it’s a frozen over version of Hell for one person. One person, walking amongst many, but walking alone. And you can tell that he’s alone. You can tell that he’s been alone for a while. One person, one man, that simply appears so physically and emotionally spent that the world could be bursting with laughter and joy around him and it wouldn’t matter. The world could be blessed with like around the clock, and this man would still be on the dark side of the moon. For him, Hell exists everywhere he goes. Everywhere he is. This man IS Hell. Nevertheless, he walks down the busy Calgarian street with a distinct limp. He’s wearing a pair of old, faded jeans, and not the kind you buy faded, the ones that you know have been through the trenches, have been through the fields, through mud and dirt, through wash after wash, through dust and through stains. Just like the man wearing them, in fact. He’s wearing a gray parka. Indistinct in color, but, unlike his jeans, fairly new. The hood covers his face as he continues his long march down to the street, through the masses of people and against the wind current in this brisk and awful western Canadian winter. Whether he is wearing that hood in order to protect himself from the cold and from the wind… whether he is wearing it to hide his shame… or whether he is wearing it to protect himself from the people around him, or better yet, protect the people around him from him, in this broken down and beaten state, only he knows. But the people around him don’t notice him. It’s as if this gray-wearing jean-faded creature is a grey ghost fading into the night’s sky. The man’s pace slows and eventually comes to a halt. He looks across his street, and realizes he has just about arrived at his destination. Even though that destination might really be nowhere he wants to be. Even though, really, he might not want to be anywhere right now. Even though, no one may want to be where he currently is. He looks up and across the street, and the camera follows his incoherent and invisible stare, as if in some sort of trance, discovering for itself the destination this man has brought it to. Clam’s Restaurant, Bar & Grill Interesting name for a restaurant that claims to be a grill in the middle of a prairie, surrounded by oil and wheat fields and mountains, in the middle of winter, nowhere near anywhere where you might find a clam. And at the risk of sounding like a broken record, nowhere near where you’d expect this one man, in his present condition, to be. The place itself is warm and inviting. From the outside, it looks like a large log cabin. A wooden exterior frame goes up ten feet, and 25 feet on either side of a large oak door right in the middle. Large windows intrude the passage of these wooden vertical logs, but from this distance you can’t see anything inside other than blurry figures. Ten feet up, the large logs move inward and away from the perspective of the camera before ending another 10 feet up, meeting the night’s sky and a few nearby stars. Breaking this uninteresting union between logs and sky is a large neon sign with the words uttered above that make this structure’s name flashing in red and green, along with, again, strangely, a cowboy lit up and tipping his hat to passersby. Something you’d see in the movies, not the real, almost unreal, life we’re witnessing here. Each side of the restaurant is barren. No other buildings within an alley’s distance on either side, and, uniquely, considering the inviting nature of this structure, nothing inviting down those alleyways. You’d almost expect that this man’s destination would be the sides of this building. You’d almost expect him to keep on walking, instead of entering this structure and meeting whatever new Hell waits for him inside. Nevertheless, he is here, despite the fact that he looks worlds away. And in a moment, he has crossed the street and is finally there. The man steps up to the window and looks at a menu posted outside the business, as if it would affect his decision to enter this place. More than likely, delaying his entrance into this place, and delaying facing whatever may face him once he steps through the intimidating door made of wood and glass. You can almost feel his hesitation. After a moment, he finally pulls the door open briskly and enters the restaurant. The inside is just as inviting as the outise. Bursting with laughter and joyous screaming. Bursting with movement and activity. Eating. Drinking. Socialization. Once again, the anti-thesis of where you’d expect this man, in his present condition, to be. The man’s head, comfortably tucked away inside the hood of his parka, turns slightly and examines his surroundings. Wooden walls that rise up into the air and meet halfway from each other. A bar on one side of the restaurant, connected to the kitchen. People walking, whether it be waiters, waitresses, bartenders, patrons, managers. All with a purpose. Respectively, delivering food, serving drinks, searching for the facilities, making sure everything is running smoothly. What is this man doing here? A fireplace crackling loadly in the middle of it all, and it catches the man’s attention. He turns his body, still covered, and begins walking towards the fire. Through people, through employees, in between tables and chairs. Almost like an obstacle course. The man finally reaches his new destination and extends is glove-covered hands in front of the fire, warming them up. But he quickly moves on, turning his head, and once again, examining his surroundings and searching for a new destination. And he finally finds it. A table in the corner. A table in the dark reaches of this restaurant. Possibly the only dark reaches of this restaurant. At least, the poorly lighted reaches of this restaurant. Perfect. Well… not really. Nothing about this even comes close to being in the same utterance as perfect. But the man, once again, treks to his destination. Limping, slightly. Sulking, heavily. He reaches the table and pulls the chair out. He unzips his coat. Every interlocked spike slowly coming undone, slowly revealing a plain t-shirt underneath. Slowly revealing this man to the world. He finally reaches the bottom. He then proceeds to remove a sleeve, revealing an arm. Then the other sleeve. And then, finally, he removes the parka. Revealing the man underneath. Windy Bridges. But it’s a far cry from being the Windy Bridges the world has come to know and love. This is a different man. A darker man. A broken man. Windy doesn’t attract much attention. Whether it’s the fact that he know has a pretty distinct light brown beard, or whether it’s the fact that he is not the chipper, lively, human being most people are used to recognizing is unknown, but this man is certainly a shell of the confident man that became a US Champion in MWE and never lost the title. A man who won the same title in EW and never lost it. A man who has an exceptional record in professional wrestling, and a man who is known to have an exceptional attitude. The man you see before you, shows no distinct qualities that tell you that he is, or ever was, any of those things. This man is not Windy Bridges. This man is barely even a man. Windy sits down and raises a finger to a waitress. This is the kind of place that offers one kind of beer. The waitress acknowledges the hand gesture and quickly scurries over to the bar. She returns a few moments later with a beer on her tray, asking for five dollars. As Windy fishes through his pocket for funds, the woman’s face lights up, recognizing Windy. ”HEY! Aren’t you—“ ”What did you say? Five dollars? Here’s five and a twoonie.” Windy cuts the waitress off. He’s not interested in anything she has to say. He’s not interested in being recognized. And he’s certainly not interesting in having to sign anything or interact with anyone, not tonight. He doesn’t even raise his eyes to look at it. He just takes the beer. He just takes a large swig. He just sulks. Then, she appears. As if by magic. Her magnetic glow turns every head, male, and even female, in the entire place. Even Windy wasn’t able to accomplish that. Her beauty radiates across the restaurant as she appears through the doorway. Her cheeks are red from the cold. Her luscious blond hair is perfectly styled, despite the wind. And the rest of her body looks pretty good as well. Wearing tight jeans, a white parka, hands in her pocket, she walks into the restaurant and looks around. Intently. Looking for something… someone… And finally, she finds him, sitting in the corner… sulking… She walks over…. Through people… between tables… between chairs, and finally reaches him. She looks at him as he sulks, as he doesn’t even raise his face from his drink. And she frowns, putting her arms to her hips. “Who do you think you are?’ Windy looks up from his drink, somewhat perplexed at the question. As if there was an obvious answer. Most people would consider Windy a “Simple Man” (cue rimshot), but lately, that question has seemed more and more difficult to answer. Nevertheless, there it is, out in the open. A question that would offend most seems to intrigue Windy more than bother him. As if it was a constructive one, and not a rhetorical question. Windy’s eyebrow raises to the sky as he ponders how to put this question into words. As if he has the answer but can’t fathom that he would need to answer this, especially to someone as close as this person…. As the girl who, just a few weeks ago, was the love of his life… as Debbie Dallas.* “Who do I think I am?” Windy’s whisper slowly raises to a normal tone and finally a shout before he can complete his sentence “Who do I think I am!? I know what I used to be! I used to be a professional wrestler! I used to be a professional bull rider! I used to be a movie star! I used to be a business man. And now? I’m nothing! NOTHING! My reputation is shot. My body is broken down. No one can look into these eyes and takes me seriously. Either because I was crippled by a damn circus act or because I’m a comedy act myself. I’m not too sure which one it is Debbie! One thing I’m sure of is the person that I think…. That I KNOW I am… and that’s a nobody. A man who’s lost everything. Even you…” A distinct change from the thousands of words we haven’t heard thus far this even. It’s as if this woman suddenly set off a spark in an otherwise cold and unlit man. But it was just that… a spark. Windy calms down. And returns his attention, and his eyes, to his beer. Debbie sighs. She understands. She hurts. But she’s also angry for what Windy has become. But she’s uncertain of whether to be angry at the Grand Mystique or at the wrestler formerly known as Windy Bridges. There’s only one person who she could get mad at here, at this moment, in this restaurant, and she’s certain that this person won’t try to kill her. At least, she hopes. This is a changed man, and even she can see it through her blind love for the man. Between her thoughts and ponders, Debbie removes his snug parka and lays it on a chair like a coat hanger. She slides into a chair across from Windy. She’s dressed elegantly and beautifully. A red sweater. Designer jeans. She isn’t showing any skin, but she’s showing her heart and sharing her thoughts with the man in front of her. A man that ignores her. A man that is too obsessed with his own gloom to notice the people around him. Nevetheless, she grabs his cold and distant hand. She almost as to reach for it. Almost as if releasing the stress in her shoulders, and slowly transferring it to the carcass on the other side of the table, she slumps her shoulders and gives Windy a smile. One of desperation, and slight relief. “Windy… you haven’t lost me… I’m here. I’m always here. I always was here… and I always will be. Your family is there for you… and so are your friends. Do you have any idea how much we’ve been trying to reach you? How long is it since you’ve gone home?’ Windy shakes his head. He’s unsure. His thoughts are kind of fuzzy, but he attempts to answer. “I don’t know Debbie… I’ve been staying at a hotel… I just… I just had to get away from everything for a few days.” Windy smiles. But it’s forced. He knows Debbie isn’t here to yell at him. At least not through a hateful type of anger – the anger that he feels towards just about anything right now. But he’s also not in the mood to put on a smiling face just for the sake of satisfying Debbie. As he comes to this realization, the smile fades. His hand retracts from the touch of Debbie’s. And he slips back into the bowels of the bottle of beer in his hand. “The truth is that I don’t want to be around anyone right now. To tell you the complete truth… I’ve considered dropping out of the match in New York…” Debbie’s eyes widen. She can’t believe her ears! Windy asked for this match specifically, and now he’s considering forfeiting it! In any condition, she’ll be damned if she lets him walk away from his troubles that easily. “Debbie: Windy, you can’t be serious… Windy snarls and raises his eyes briskly to Debbie, also raising his voice. “Windy: What, so now you think I can’t be serious? Because of a few comedy gimmicks, because of a few jokes, a few pranks, I can’t be serious?” Windy voice gets louder. A near yell. Debbie looks around, slightly embarrassed, slightly regretting the comment she made in jest to a match who has it in every warped mental interest to twist and turn until it molds his ungodly satisfactions of confrontation. “Windy, relax… that’s NOT what I meant! You asked for this match! You asked for GM! You asked to put this to rest. And I know your medical condition. I know how you’re feeling right now. But I also know you’ve had much worse done to you, mostly at the hands of this… this monster! But at the same time… I know that you’re not right… I know that you shouldn’t go through with this. Windy… I don’t know what you should do, but I know I can’t sit here and watch you drive your life into a hole for no reason! This isn’t the Windy I fell in love with. And I’ll be DAMNED if I let you do this to yourself!! ” Windy can’t believe it! He doesn’t think he’s heard Debbie ever talk to him like that, yet alone at this tone. He’s almost happy that she did. But, just as he was as nearly as a few minutes ago… he’s angry. Not at Debbie, but this is the first real human interaction he’s had in days, and unfortunately for Debbie, he decides that he will unleash all his anger, here, tonight. “The Grand Mystiques says that I turned him into the most hated and most feared man in the wrestling world. He may as well like that. But he’s not the only one that this whole ordeal has affected, Debbie.” Debbie lowers her eyes. She knows what’s coming. Windy has been through a lot, mostly at the hands of GM. In the last five months, Windy has been at both the highest point of his career, as well as the lowest point of his career, and considering some of the successes he’s had, that a damn long drop. Despite having faced adversity in his life, Windy didn’t know how to deal with this much going against it. And Debbie was one of the only people who understood that. Unfortunately for her, Windy didn’t know this. Windy was too wound up in his own sorrow that he could have been talking to a complete stranger right now and it didn’t make a difference. Nevertheless, Debbie was going to get this speech. Again. And she was going to sit there and take it, because she didn’t know what else to do. “I’ve lost everything. I’ve lost my acting career. My investments are down the toilet. And I’ve severely risking my health and even my life getting in the ring again with this maniac. Especially in a match with no rules!” Debbie shakes her head in disagreement as windy speaks before slamming her arms on the table and cuts Windy off. “Then why the HELL are you doing this, Windy?” Debbie’s voice is loud. A near yell. Enough to get the attention of a few people around her and Windy. Not many, one or two, but enough to considered a “scene” for most people. And not that it’s not warranted. Windy stares at Debbie in disbelief. He’s never heard Debbie speak this loudly or angry to him, at least not under negative circumstances, wink wink, nudge nudge. Debbie’s hand trembles, whether in anger, fear, or nervousness, only she knows. Her eye is twitching. But neither says another word. They just sit there staring at each other. Debbie in a mix of anger and fear, windy in simple disbelief. But it’s suddenly clear to her. It’s clear what she wants to say. And it’s clear how she should say it.Finally, Debbie decides to take a deep breath and say her piece. “Why are you putting your life at risk? Why are you scaring me half to death with all this SHIT and scaring your own parents… your own family half to by not returning their calls? Why are you cutting everyone off in a time when you probably need them the most!? Debbie can’t believe it herself. She’s never been this angry before. She seldom ever raises her voice, like ever, not in private… yet alone in a public place, in a busy city, sitting next to a public figure who’s already attracting a fair bit of negative attention. And attention they are certainly getting. If the crowd at this tavern was being modestly Canadian before, this was enough to put them over their guilt of staring at any person, celebrity or not, and they’re staring alright. It’s almost as if life and movement in this place has stopped. People have stopped chatting. The bartender has stopped serving drinks. The band in the corner has stopped playing. Even the fire seems like it’s stopped crackling. Everyone, and that means everyone, has dropped what they were doing, dropped their evening of enjoyment, their drinks, their food, their music, and they are now looking at Debbie Dallas and Windy Bridges, as if they were in a little box, on network TV, playing roles in a soap opera. And they may as well be. But Windy’s not going to sit there and take it. He knows that his life has been reduced to drastic words and drastic actions. But he has enough common sense left in him to know that it isn’t going to help anyone if he sits there dishing out and taking punishment from Debbie Dallas. And while he doesn’t care how far down his hole stretches, he knows he doesn’t want to drag Debbie down that hole with him. In fact, it’s probably his own clear thought at the moment. Clear enough to allow him to get up from the table he and Debbie are sitting at. Clear enough to make him pick up and put on his parka, as Debbie, shocked, and a bunch of restaurant clients and employees, shocked, look on. Clear enough to allow him to maneuver past human life, past tables, past chairs, through the restaurant. Clear enough to take him out those restaurant doors that brought him into this situation to begin with, and clear enough to allow him to begin walking down the cold Calgarian street. In which direction, he doesn’t know. Towards which destination, he doesn’t know. He hasn’t known for weeks. All he does know, is that he’s lost. But soon… He is found… Debbie comes rushing out the restaurant herself. Her purse in one hand, her parka in another. Probably not the smartest idea not to be wearing that, but she doesn’t care. She has one thing… one person… on her mind, and she can’t see him continuing like this. She won’t allow it. Dressed in all but a sweater, she rushes down the street. She rushes after Windy. Desperately. “WINDY!’ She yells. She screams. But no answer. Windy continues. He’s had enough. He just wants to escape. But she follows. And she gains ground on the limping man. She’s within a few feet…. She’s almost there… But then… It happens…. A man appears through a dark alley. A man dressed all in black. A man with a knife. A man with no purpose other than greed. A man with no care other than money. And he grabs Debbie. And he shakes her. And twists. She welps. She screams. She struggles. She punches and kicks. But the man has her. The man eyes her purse. He grabs her purse, but she won’t let go. But the man shoves her down and pulls away the purse. The man smiles. And he turns. But he is not met with the brisk wind of a Calgarian night, instead, he is met with the brisk fist of Windy Bridges! Windy punches the mugger in the face, and he drops, down to the ground, down next to windy. For a second, the despair in windy’s body and eyes is replaced by blind anger. Blind love for the woman he just walked out on. And it’s possessing him. He mounts the man and begins wailing. Lefts, rights, and lefts again. Punch after punch, taking out his anger. He splits the man over on his forehead, he bruises him as he hits him, but he continues. It’s been a long time. It’s been a lot of things brooding. It’s been a lot of things festering, building up, growing. And now, he gets his chance to release it. Fist after fist into the man’s face, uninterrupted fury. But finally, it is interrupted. “Windy! Stop! Debbie jumps on Windy. Debbie pleads desperately, physically, with Windy, to let up and let go. But instead, he pushes her away and to the ground! Debbie falls, it seems, for ages, as Windy finally lets up and finishes. He has destroyed himself. He has destroyed the people around him. All of them. And the streets of Calgary are calm. He releases the man. He gets up off him and approaches Windy, as the mugger holds his face, and runs off. Windy can’t believe himself. The one person he had no intention of hurting, is now lying on the floor beneath him. Windy pulls his hair. He rubs his eyes and face. He doesn’t believe it. He can’t. He drops to his knees and lifts Debbie up, slowly, cautiously, into his arms. “Debbie… I’m sorry… I never wanted this to happen to you. Debbie moans in pain as she holds her head. She finally regains her balance, and gets to her feet. Dazed, she barely knows where she is, who she’s with. “Debbie… I never wanted you to get hurt…” “I… mm… I never wanted you to get hurt either… Windy…” “I know Deb… I know…” “No, Windy… I have to… I have to say it… you do… whatever you have to do… and I will support you. But know that I will be with you. Every step of the way.” Windy lets out a smile. Somewhat forced. The smile of a man with lot of pressure on his shoulders. The smile of a man who realizes how ridiculous his situation and his actions have become and have been degraded to. “I know Deb… I’m sorry” Windy gets to his feet, and helps Debbie to hers. But he doesn’t let her go. He looks her in the eyes… and they embrace…………. ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- “They say that whatever doesn’t kill you will certainly make you stronger. Up to last night, I thought that was some sort of cruel joke.” Windy’s eyes come into focus, but not much else. For the first time in a long time, they’re focused. “Grand Mystique, you damn near DID kill me. Not once… not twice… not even three times. Hell not even four times! Honestly, I’m not even sure if I have enough brain cells left in my head to remember the number of times my life was endangered when you were around.” .” For the first time in a long time, Windy’s eyes give off an aura of determination. “And none were worst than last month’s fiasco in New Hampshire. I’m not going to sit here and relive every damn moment of pain, both emotional and physical, I felt that night, and for many nights after that, but I will tell you that it was extreme.” .” The camera pans out slightly, until we see the bridge of Windy’s nose, and continues to zoom out, until we get a full head shot of Windy. Windy’s face is slightly more mangled than it was the night before. A few more gashes and scraped, probably from the fight the night before. Still, it doesn’t seem to affect Windy’s mood like his prior injuries did. For the first time in a long time, it looks like Windy Bridges is back. “ “I was thinking about that very sentence just the other day while I was sulking in my own guilt. ‘Whatever doesn’t kill me, makes me stronger’. I was thinking how silly that sounded. Broken bones, concussions… those things don’t make you stronger.” The camera pans out a little further. Now we have a view of Windy from the chest-up, in front of a colorful red wall. A wall that looks full of life. A painting of a park sits in the background, with a bunch of people circa 1850 looking like they’re enjoying themselves. For the first time in a long time, Windy, cracks a slight smile. “ “But then I finally got it. It’s not about what makes your bones, or your brains, or your muscles stronger. It’s about in here. .” As the camera pans out even further to view the length of Windy’s arms and torso, and finally, his entire body in what seems to be a hotel room set-up,Windy clenches his fist and knocks softly on the side of his head. “ “GM, you may have killed off a few brain cells when you dropped me inside a cold, steel structure, down 15 feet onto uninviting concrete. You may have broken my bones, you may have hurt me physically. But you didn’t hurt my will. You didn’t hurt my spirit. You may like to tout that you did, and I will admit that you came DAMN close, but Mystique, I’m still here. Windy Bridges is still here. .” For the first time in long time, Windy’s eyes rage with fire. “ “So here we are. Am I stronger? We’ll find out on February 13th. Am I more determined? I think I am. After what you did to me, I was a broken and fragile man. But I’ve come to the realization that I can’t live my life feeling sorry for myself. I can’t live my life regretting that I came to New Hampshire that night. And I can’t live my life hating you. .” Windy is breathing heavily with intensity. So much so that the gash on his head he received from GM a few weeks ago begins bleeding. A thin stream of blood runs down his forehead and down his cheek. Windy senses it and wipes some of it off with his finger. He grabs a tissue and wipes off the rest. He continues, as if nothing happened. For the first time in a long time, Windy Bridges is back. “ Friday Night OverDrive. It ends. After six months… it finally ends. No matter what, one of us doesn’t come out of that match. It’s as plain and simple as that. GM, you claim that this started off as a matter of respect… and slowly built itself and boiled over into a matter of some sort of epic crusade? Spare me your gibberish, GM. I know what this is truly about. I know what this has always been about. I can see it in your eyes. I can hear it in your words. I can feel it in your actions and I can even sense it the way you walk into a room. This isn’t about respect. This isn’t some crusade…. This is a personal battle. This is you versus me. And I’ll be DAMNED if I let you take any last ounce of spirit I still have left in me. GM, you’ve awoken a monster. And you’ll live to regret it. At OverDrive… simply put….” Windy walks up closer to the camera. Until once again, we can only see his eyes. “ It’s over.” And with that, it is. Windy walks away, leaving nothing but of red wall. Leaving nothing but the emptiness we started with. But this time. It’s full of life. End Scene. *fixed a slight coding error.
