Mar 28 2009

David Whitman

Quote: Life is, ironically and yet not so much, peppered by death. Streaks of little wins peppered by big losses. And long-standing joy peppered by sadness. But somehow, we get through it. Somehow. I was on an amazing high. Beating some of the best in the world, one by one. Champion once again. Undisputed, at that. We thank you that he is in your gentle and loving hands, far from the cruelty, violence and pain of our world. Then it all came crashing down. When the trouble was near we could not understand how you seemed to remain far away. Facing Cannon, I wasn’t sure I would win, but I know for certain that had he beaten me cleanly, I would be alright. Of course, Resoan saw to it that that didn’t happen. And so, to me, it felt like a loss. A clean loss. Even though it wasn’t. But we always see the losses as clean losses, no matter how dirty they may be. Even though we might not admit it, and kick and scream and blame and point fingers, our ego takes a big hit. For just like any win feels like a win, any loss feels like a loss. But we know that, if life is soured by bitterness, an unforgiving spirit brings no peace. What do I do now? Do I continue with the plans I’ve made? Are they even going to turn out right? Can I let go of everything that’s happened, and just move on? …is freed from the past with all its hurt, and rests for ever in the calm security of your love… Where am I going? Amen. ___________________________________________________________________________________________ It is the date of the Pay-Per-View. Highway to Hell is mere minutes from starting, according to the schedule, and the place is already packed to maximum capacity. Actually, Wembley Stadium is a bit OVER-crowded, as some successfully scalped ticket and friend-of-a-worker type deals have apparently attributed to more fans than the seating can hold. Which is fine, because by the end of the show, nobody will be sitting down anyway. Through the ocean of criss-crossing voices and clanking, clicking and clattering sounds, it is almost impossible to notice anything of importance happening within the massive arena unless the event in question were highlighted by a theme song of some sort. And even though that is not the case as David Whitman, dressed in his same old pants and the MWE “Three Words” shirt and title on shoulder walks down the ramp, a loud response from the crowd grows out of one or two fans noticing it. David, as if unfazed by anything happening around him or otherwise, makes his way to the ring and rolls inside. Most of the fans are excited, however, most of the workers are confused, and some even disgruntled. As two or three cameramen struggle to set up their cameras hurriedly, David beckons towards the announcers table, where most of the on-screen MWE crew is situated. Obviously asking for a mic, he gets it as Fred Carry walks over and hands one to him. Reaching down from the other side of the ring ropes, David flashes an uncomfortable smile at Carry, who has his own worried expression going on. David walks around the ring in a circle, almost seeming as if he has to adjust his mind to where he is. He stops in the middle of the ring as he places the mic to his mouth, without actually looking up. The cheering and slight jeering of the crowd stops, and the arena becomes almost silent if not for the occasional whispering, electrical hum and the odd loud yell. David: I have something to say. More murmuring starts, but inexplicably at a softer level. None of this seems to affect David as he continues talking. David: Mere hours from now, I am going to face off in this very ring against Mason Resoan. Some inevitable crowd response occurs; loud cheers and jeers. Nothing like what you’d see on TV, but still loud to merit a response from the person speaking in the ring… though it doesn’t get one. David: Resoan…. A man who has always been attacked for his nationality. He starts to take a deep breath, but stops in the middle of it, almost as if trying to speak quickly before he derails his train of thought. David: Which is probably how he became so successful in this sport in the first place. The crowd’s response is still moving along at an irregular pace but at this point it’s become clear that none of it matters to David as he continues talking as if to himself. David: I mean, it might get irritating, but at the same time, you can’t take anyone seriously when they try to verbally abuse you because of where you were born. David moves along slightly, with those little ticks everyone has when they’re least thinking about it; slowing down his winking, rubbing his nose slightly, his chin, the back of his neck. David: Which is also probably how he built up his arrogance. Or, his overflowing self-confidence, if you will. Never having to take any opponent seriously will get you a long way in wrestling. But hey. I digress. The arena light effects, still being adjusted, scatter around while surrounded by the more commonplace lighting. David: This guy is up there with… well. A pause. David: He’s in a league all his own. A millisecond of a contemplative smirk. David: I mean, he’s a lot like Dallas, in a way. The crowd response at Dallas’ name is much like any of the other responses; a mixture, love-hate; 50-50 at best. Though at a loud volume. David: His complex is a bit less excessively grand in nature, but they’re close. David is still seemingly ignoring the crowd, though obviously not by intention. David: Biggest difference between them, or actually, between Mason and MOST wrestlers… is perseverance. A spotlight glistens off the MWE title for a second, blinding an unwitting fan in the seas of fans. David: Because Mason believes he is perfect, he is constantly unknowingly striving for perfection. He misses a move; he remembers that so that next time he doesn’t miss. He gets caught in a tricky situation; he tries to store it in his memory for the next time it happens. A section of the crowd is illuminated by a sequence of red and blue beams of light for a while. David: Mason is like the rest of us, but in a way, by NOT acknowledging that and even going so far as to DEFY that, he has put himself in a constant state of improvement. So in other words, he’s not right about being perfect. But because he thinks he is, he is continually as close as possible to being it. David pauses. David: That’s the best way to be wrong, is what that is. The crowd’s contemplative murmurs reach a high for the night. David: How else would you explain the ability to hold one of the longest undefeated streaks in a place crammed with the cream of the combat crop? How else, if at all, would you explain the fact that a pupil could defeat his teacher? How else, and please tell me if you can, could you show me a reason Resoan’s career is considered a fantastic feat, by crowds of fans to critics alike? Another pause. David: You can’t. The champion finally looks up. David: You can’t, you couldn’t, and you shouldn’t want to even attempt to. He looks around, almost as if acknowledging the crowd for the first time all night. David: Despite the despicable way he talks to the fans and his occasionally askew view of situations and mentalities that surround him, when Mason Resoan is wrong, he is wrong in the best type of way. He chuckles, slightly opaquely. David: Me? To tell you the truth, since I’ve become the so-called “New David”, and yes I realize not many people call me that but they THINK it… More mixed reactions, this time seemingly fazing David slightly as he restarts his sentence. David: Since I’ve become the New David… I’ve been an odd mix. One of small kernels of the old me, with huge big bits of things I’ve never felt or been. And in a way, I embody imperfectness. David looks at the title on the shoulder and doesn’t remove his stare as he continues speaking. David: In the span of a couple of weeks I lost two people who have defined who I am, broke a short-lived undefeated streak with a vengeance by officially starting a defeated streak, turned a quarter of my fans against me and become the laughing stock of the smark community. He looks around, and, for the first time tonight, it seems, he actually LOOKS at the crowd. David: Yeah, I said it. The crowd pops loudly, or at least 3/4ths of the crowd does. David shows a genuine smile, which slowly melts away into a thinking expression as he puts the mic back to his mouth. David: Thing is… I kind of like it. The crowd pops again. He cocks his head as if trying to recall something. David: When I beat a bunch of athletes for my first ever world title run, I was at an unbelievable disadvantage. Same thing second time. And when I beat D.C. and Alejandro in one of the biggest matches in my career, I was coming in with the disadvantage. And, it’s funny. Because I never really thought about it. I never really thought about any of it, to be honest. Am I who I am because I let myself be that person, or because I strive to be someone else? He stops. Five seconds pass, uninterrupted by sound or motion… he continues. David: I don’t know. He looks up, trying to figure out the answer. David: Mason is coming into this match confident. I know that deep down, he has his doubts, but after what happened to me these past few weeks and this being his big break, he’s gonna be oozing confidence for the most part. And I am going into this match unsure of myself. With the thought of Mason’s solid record, and my contrasting spotty and spastic record. Not to mention by past mental issues. So, obviously, we’re going into this match with the outcome looking complacent for Mason and unhappy for me. David stops, lets his hand drop to his side as he takes a very deep breath, and slowly puts the mic back to his mouth as the crowd await in a silent hush. David: But THAT… THAT is the WONDERFUL thing. No response from the fans. David: Because it doesn’t matter HOW we go into this match. Still nothing. David: All that will matter is the moment we’re standing in that ring, and that bell goes off. Some “YEAH!”s and “WOOOO!”s are heard, scattered. David: Because then, I know I will fight for my LIFE. I will exhaust my body to the point of crumbling and the risk of death. The crowd becomes more animated. David: The fact that I walk into it unprepared is the catalyst. David closes his eyes as he continues, fired up. David: Everything is on the line; my belt, my mental well-being, my future in this business as well as in life… And I am going... to go… all out. The crowd erupts at levels unheard of all night. David: Highway to Hell will be HELL for Mason and me. It will be HEAVEN for every wrestling fan around the world. And for wrestling itself? I can’t say yet. But I WILL be able to say by the end of the night. Because that is when it will be DEFINED. David drops the mic and raises the belt high in the air. He rolls out of the ring, and, though he seems to ignore the near-deafening cheers from the audience, he walks up the ramp and towards the back with a satisfied look on his face.
