OOC: Purple=Warner, Dark Blue=Ivory ____________________________________________________ - Part II - Fall, 2006 Zak sat watching the sun being eclipsed by the Earth with dim eyes. He had done that a lot lately. He watched nothing in particular, just whatever crossed his sight. A voyeur of society, each scene a combination of warm familiarity and the chilling unknown. His every thought fell back to the club known as The Lost and the woman that had found him there. Zak had no idea who she was, but still found himself captivated by her nameless silhouette she had cast over his mind. With his unconvinced efforts to remove her from his conscious thought failing, he stood, and began down the beach on which he sat, his path leading towards the city. As he walked, thoughts assaulted his mind with the force of a seasoned army. "Where am I? Who was that woman? How did she know me? Why does she know me? Who am I? Who is this Zak? And why am I in his body? Who am I?" "Someone," he thought, "will be able to answer this. Someone always can." Finding himself once more in the city was a mind altering experience. Basic navigation was hindered by the usual array of distracters. Streams of people following the example set by ants, darting to and fro with more reason then one would expect and cars acting like territorial beasts, roaring, threatening and nudging to gain any amount of land they can muster. Civilization seemed to take a backseat to humanity in this city. As he walked, Zak found himself looking at the faces of almost everyone who passed him. He soon found almost all them to be identical in expression. Stoic, disenchanted and detached. As if none cared, as if none were alive. Of course, there was the brief exception. People walking with friends, mouths moving in unison with their feet, laughter and joy abound. Children happily gazing, their eyes occasionally meeting Zak’s similarly journeying eyes. As to be expected, mothers and fathers quickly saw this and pulled their child hurriedly along, but no before looking at Zak with fear and contempt, the words ’Pervert’ and ‘Freak’ carved into their tongue but never released. With aimless intention, Zak wandered until a flash of red on top of the worn concrete. Tender drops of blood, still moist and new, drying in the sun and leading into an alleyway filled with air that rots lungs. With a dart, Zak lifted his sight and down between the tarnished walls to see the brake lights of a car fade into the black and the quiet sound of wheels fresh in motion. Unconcerned, he continued down his stone paved road. In time Zak grew tired of his goalless search for nothing. Recalling the night before, he reached into his back pocket and withdrew the slim black wallet. An investigation of its interior discovered a driver’s license, a key and one-hundred dollars cash, nothing more. Reading over the details written on the license not only provided Zak with a name, but also an age, a photo and an address. Still no certain of his own identity, he turned to a store window and looked at his own dim image. After a breath of two, he raised the ID next to his face. The pictures matched. “Well, at least I know I’m me…” He released a sigh that was part hope and part hopelessness before returning his attention to the address. 6868 Lincoln Rd. Miami Beach, FL 33139 He read it over again and again, each time it showed the same information and each time his knowledge of its location remained at nothing. A sense of defeat began to sink into his mindset as he began to search around him, hoping blindly that he was already on Lincoln road, of can at least find any indication of where it was. He circled for no reason at all, but it was all he could think to do. “Hey buddy, you look lost.” came a voice from behind. Turning to face the mysterious vocal sound, Zak found a man. Seemingly in his twenties, and looked strikingly normal, but unlike the rest of the cities population he came across as genuine, in spite of the smile where the frown should have been. “Where ya looking to get?” Despite still taken aback by this unprovoked generosity, Zak managed to splutter a response. “6868 Lincoln Road…” “Lincoln Road? Easy done.” With that, the man began to walk off, slicing through the crowd with what one could call his aura. Realizing he had no choice, Zak hesitantly followed. As they walked, Zak looked, slowly absorbing his surroundings and not caring for what he saw all too much. “Quite a sad lookin’ town, aint it?” asked the stranger, “Didn’t mean to interrupt your bit of sight seeing, but the sour taste on your face made me ask. Plus, can’t say I’m too much of a fan of silences.” He talked constantly through up-turned lips. “Feels like the only natural thing in this city is the sky.” Zak’s remained largely motionless, still unsure on who this man was, or what he wanted. “Might have to agree with ya one that one, but you get used to it.” Zak’s necked pushed his head upwards as his feet followed his guide’s. Seemingly getting lost in the red clouds and dreary demeanor of nature, he quickly found himself brushing against other people, inciting strings of profanity, verbal and physical abuse and a quick return to reality. This brought about a slight laugh from his leader through the city. “Haven’t been here long, have ya?” the nameless man asked, the same grin plastered on his face. Zak's silence was answer enough for him. Sometimes silence could say more than any amount of words. “You move here, or a tourist?” “I’m not sure, if I can be perfectly honest.” “Ok… still haven’t made up your mind then? May not seem like it, but Miami can be a great place to live, once ya get used to it that is. I moved here not long ago actually…” His voice seemed to trail back into his memory. Neither man spoke for some time, their silence lost among the abrasive noises of those around them. A few blocks past and the stranger spoke once again. “Oh, by the way, my name’s Cory. Can’t believe I forgot to introduce myself. What’s yours?” Zak hesitated, still unsure if he was able to trust this man. After all, it was only a few moments ago that Zak was just finding out his own name, so he wasn’t sure he wanted some stranger sharing the same amount of knowledge. “Ok, don’t have to tell…” “Zak. My name’s Zak.” “Zak? Ok then, Zak it is.” After time and extended quiet, Cory stopped walking and gestured towards a tall building. Zak’s eyes followed Cory’s body language and saw a pile of concrete and glass constructed to resemble class. “There ya go Zak, 6868 Lincoln Road. A whole heap of apartments, I take it yours is somewhere in there?” Zak timidly began walking towards the complex. His face had a confused look etched on, as he was still uncertain as to what, or who he was looking for. “Someone’s is…” “Oh?” His inquisition ignored, Cory followed. Once again, Zak’s feet lead the way despite his lack of knowledge or destination. An expired sense of familiarity flooded his thoughts, getting more aggressive with every step and every second guess. A short elevator ride followed, Zak using to time to fumble for and look at the key mysteriously in his wallet. The third floor seemed to be the end point as Zak and Cory found themselves standing in front of a door, Zak with key in hand and traceable fear and apprehension in mind. A click later, the door swung open, making way for surprise. The pair walked in, the tone of their footsteps a stunning contrast, Cory’s bright and casual, if a little forced, while Zak’s were meek and racked with concern and confusion. “Man, this place could use some major cleaning.” Cory said as he gazed around in the shadows. Zak said nothing in response, instead did the same as Cory, quickly finding that he was right, not that vision was entirely necessary to reach that conclusion, the dominating smell of an abundance of dust was all that was really needed. Suddenly, faded light poured into the room as Cory opened the blinds that hid the balcony, following the action up with a flick of a light switch. “Nice view!” Once more, Zak spoke in silence. Instead of admiring the freshly awoken view, he attended to what the illumination revealed. There wasn’t too much to see, simply cobwebs and white sheets covering what one would assume was furniture, a nearby kitchen along with a few closed doors. Curiosity soon discovered two bedrooms, a bathroom and still more white sheets. Zak returned to the main room to find Cory sitting on a couch, sheet tucked safely underneath his feet. “Doubt anyway will care, by the looks of things no one’s been here in a loooong time.” Zak tossed a protective covering aside, freeing a table and chairs from its grasp. Upon it, the first thing he saw was a photo frame with no memory to be proud of. He stared at it deeply, something stinging in his mind and a chill in his heart. Quickly tearing his eyes from it to avoid confounding himself further, he found a newspaper, surprisingly crisp and without signs of damaging age. He inspected it with scrutiny. “What’s today’s date?” Cory looked partially shocked by the voice. “Uh… the 6th. Why?” “Of what?” “That would be September my friend. Why?” Zak looked up from the paper and towards his answers. “Whoever lives here was here today then.” “No way show.” Struggling to believe the claim, Cory hurried over to the table and the newspaper it held. He almost laughed when he read it. “Good one Zak, it’s today’s date alright, but in 2001. The things five years old! Gotta read the year too mate.” he said before heading back over to the couch and laying himself down, as if getting ready for a prolonged stay.
