MWE 24/7 — 09-10 PPV RP Archives

Confined

Aug 20 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
OOC: It's a shame that Cannon probably isn't going to RP... I honestly wanted to take this much further, and while I'm pretty happy with the promo part and even some of the stuff I set up in the future, the end kinda falls apart and I apologize for that. I was really hoping we could make this an epic MWE moment... maybe in the future. In any case I still tried to do a lot more than just RPing to have something up, so I hope you enjoy. ---------------- “It seems like we’ve been here before.” Other than this one rugged, raspy voice, the scene is quiet. “It seems like we’ve been here way too often, in fact.” It is so dark that it’s actually deafening. While a faint light creeps in on the edges of the screen, the rest is purely black. “It seems like every time I attempt a comeback, an appearance, someone has something to say about it.” Obviously, we’re hearing the distinguished and unique voice of Windy Bridges. After so many matches, so many interviews and promos, the man formerly known as Winston Montgomery Bridges on his very Canadian birth certificate, fans all across the world have dedicated a special place in their hearts for this Canadian. “It doesn’t matter what I have to say. Why I decide to make the trip out to whatever event we’re talking about. Someone always has something to say. A statement to make.” The light begins to immerse the rest of the screen, slowly, but surely. “I’m not going to sit here and tell you that I’m tired of it. I’m not going to whine and complain that what Ace Cannon did to me was unfair, unwarranted, and un-anything else. Even though that it was. I have but one, simple message for Ace Cannon.” We now begin to see Windy Bridges. He is sitting calmly, not moving a single inch. The light is not enough to make out any of his features, but the intensity and determination that is oozing out of him is evident, even in this bad lighting. “Ace Cannon, I’m coming for you. I’m coming for revenge, for vengeance, and to make a statement of my own. Nearly a month ago on Monday Night Mayhem, you viciously attacked me. You smashed my face against a piano, you threw my head into a bass drum. You punted me, kicking me off a 10 foot stage. And then you took that same piano, and you turned it over onto me, nearly ten feet below.” Windy is seething. An expression on his face that we’ve never seen before, and yes, we are now starting to see it as light fills the scene. “And you know what Ace Cannon? To tell you the truth, that didn’t bother me all that much. Over the years, I’ve broken bones. I’ve cracked ribs. I’ve been tossed off stages, I’ve been kicked, punched, bruised and battered. Hell, I’ve even been blown up, on numerous occasions. So what you did to me, honestly, I can live with. So I feel a little physical pain, so what.” Windy lowers his head. We can now see the bruises on his face. Blue and black marks cover the right side of his face, even a month after the attack. “They say whatever doesn’t kill you makes you stronger. And in the amount of times I’ve been attacked, battered, blown up, well brother, I’m living proof of that. I’m here, I’m as good as I ever was and I’m as determined as ever. Unfortunately, the same saying, as cliché as it is, does not hold true for another person that was brutally attacked on that one night in Chicago.” Windy raises his head yet again, still seething. Now folly lit, we can see that windy is sitting on a plain wooden chair, in a plain, non-descript room. Cement floors, cement walls, it almost feels like a prison. A single light bulb hangs above Windy’s head, swinging slightly, back and forth, disorienting the light source for the room. Windy is wearing dark blue jeans, a plain grey t-shirt, and a leather jacket. Since we last saw him, he has shaved his head, to the size of a 2-3 day non-shave stubble beard. Not exactly the flashy self-promoting Windy that we all remember. “You see, that night on Mayhem, Ace Cannon not only attacked me. He attacked a dear friend of mine. He attacked the lead singer of the Simple Men, and my best friend. He attacked Spud McKnenzie.” The seething look on Windy’s face has now blended with one of disdain. “If beating him to a pulp and smashing a guitar over his face wasn’t enough, Cannon kicked him in the face, much like you did with me. But your punt had an effect that even you couldn’t have possibly wanted.” He sighs, almost as if he was still in disbelief of what he is about to say. “Cannon, you kicked Spud right in the throat. The force of your foot against his neck crushed his larynx and ripped his vocal chords to shreds. Spud McKenzie will never sing again, and probably never even talk again, at least not under his own command. Moreover, the pain from the injury was so severe that doctors had to put him in an induced coma, one that he’s been in since the attack, for an entire month.” Windy finally shows signs of life, standing from his seat. He looks up at the bouncing light, lifts his arm, and stops the waving. It’s now motionless, still, and shining directly above and down to Windy. “There’s really not much more I can say Cannon. I know I’m not going to intimidate you with my words. I know I’m not going to scare you, or affect anything you do – or attempt to do – this Sunday at Crowning Glory. All I can say is that this Sunday, my actions will speak much louder than my words. And the pain that I felt. That Spud felt. That we all felt for Spud. Cannon, you’re going to be introduced to its world.” With that said, Windy turns to his right and walks out of the room. Slowly, but again, surely, the light begins to fade, and as quick, if not quicker, than we were exposed to this room, it fades away, into nothingness. -------------------------------------- Not minutes later, we find ourselves in what’s clearly the halls of a correctional facility. The walls are clear in describing the location of this prison, as the camera pans in and focuses on the spray painted writing. OSWALD CORRECTIONAL FACILITY STATE OF MASSACHUSETTS The camera pans down the hall, to the lobby and down to the administrative wing of the prison, all the way to the Warden’s office. Once again, the label on the door is quite clear in describing who this office belongs to. ARNOLD GLYNN WARDEN The door slowly opens and we enter a lavishly decorated, however somber office. Leather chairs and sofas, bookcases and desks made out of expensive wood and filled with leather-bound books that have probably never been read. In the center of it all, two figures. One that we’ve already seen, Windy Bridges. Looking slightly more relaxed, albeit just as complacent, Windy sits, slouched in one of the leather chairs facing the warden’s desk. On the other side of the big, intimidating desk, is a big, intimidating figure, the person that can only be Warden Arnold Glynn. A tall, clean cut black man, Glynn has an aura about him that portrays authority. The prisoners that frequent the inner parts of these walls certainly respect and fear the caretaker of this facility. Maybe the perfect man to be speaking to Windy heading into his match. “I want to make one thing perfectly clear” Windy sits up straight, intrigued and interested in what Glynn might have to say. “I’m all ears Warden” “While the writing on the wall might say that we are a correctional facility, this place is not meant to be anything more than a state penitentiary. The people that are brought here to me, that are held within these walls, they’re not supposed to come out. Ever.” Glynn’s facial expression remains constant as he slowly gets up from his own, superior leather chair (it’s even got wheels!) and walks over to a window, covered by steel bars, of course. “And your friend, even with all the good things you have to say about him, did some very bad things Mr. Bridges. He messed with some very powerful people, and believe you me. From the stories that I’ve heard, George Prax is lucky to be alive, yet alone getting three square meals a day and a bed to sleep in.” Windy turns to Glynn as the Warden turns his attention back to the wrestler. And yes, THAT George Prax. You didn’t think he would go away that easily, did you? “Warden, I’m well aware of some of the things he did. But he is a friend, and a big reason I am the man I am today, believe it or not. I have a few things to say to him, and I would appreciate it if you could let me speak to him… in private.” Glynn sighs. “Mr. Bridges, I have trouble giving that privilege to the petty thieves that come through here, yet alone someone like George Prax.” “What can I do to change your mind? You want some free tickets to Crowning Glory? An autographed Simple Men CD? Some free Windy-Os?” Glynn smiles, slowly moving closer to Windy, and finally taking a seat next to him in the adjacent leather chair. “Well, Mr. Bridges, I AM running for office at the next electio—“ “Consider it done, Warden. Need a contribution or appearance, I’m there.” Both men smile – even though it’s a little forced on Windy’s part – as they get up and shake hands and the scene fades.” ------------------------------- Once again, we find ourselves in the halls of this prison – not far from the office of the Warden. This time, we enter a small, private room, with chairs and a small table with a few old magazines scattered across it. The only semblance of decoration is the un-watered fern in the corner next to the window – obviously fitted with steel bars, just like the rest. After a moment, the door swings open and Windy Bridges enters the room, accompanied by a security guard, who holds the door open, hand firmly planted on his weapon, and Windy steps into the room and sits down in one of the chairs. A few seconds later, the sound of chains clanging together can be heard in the distance, as a second security guard walks in the room and takes a spot on the other side of the door. The clanging gets louder, filling the air as it gets closer. Finally a man wearing prison garments, with his legs tied together with his hands in chains, stumbles into the room, with another security guard, this one holding an assault rifle, standing behind him. “Come on guys, is all this really necessary? Where the hell am I gonna go” The voice, once again, is undistinguishable. It’s none other than former REV~Pro owner and MWE employee and investor, George Prax! “Ya, George, when you escape – twice – and make it once to the Amazon, and the second time to Burma, ya, it’s pretty necessary.” Arnold Glynn enters the room along with the security guards, arms crossed and looking rather displeased with these entire proceedings. George Prax sits down, rather relieved to be off his feet. The guards and Glynn aren’t moving. Windy looks at them, annoyed. “Arnold, I thought we agreed that this meeting would be private?” Glynn grunts. “Fine. Guys, lets go. But two of you stay in front of the door, and if you see or hear anything out of the ordinary, I want you in here and I want you in here fast.” The security guards nod in agreement as they leave the room with Glynn, shutting the door behind them. “What the hell did you do?” Prax smiles as Windy looks at him, confused. “Well, let’s see. Ten counts of embezzlement. Twenty counts of misappropriation of funds… Perjury… what else. How much time you got anyway? Prax laughs, nearly maniacally, as Windy just sits there, sort of uncomfortably. “The guys around here, they call me the original Bernie Madoff. Without all the press, of course. Glad I managed to contain that. Add to all that the fact that I escaped the tightest prison in the country twice, skipped the country and made it halfway across the world, and let’s just say I have sort of a reputation in the prison system.” “Well Glynn obviously seems burdened to have you around here. Are you getting out anytime soon?” Once again, Prax laughs. “Windy, guys that do things like I do don’t get out. Ever. I’m serving 356 consecutive years with all those convictions, so unless I manage to sneak out – and stay out this time – this is going to be my home for a long, long time.” “They’re really taking these white collar crimes seriously, aren’t they?” “It’s like how every night you turn the on the news and they tell you your favourite food causes cancer. Catching people who steal money from other rich people, it’s all the rage right now, but it’s a phase. But the damage is done. I got caught. It is what it is. Windy sits up straight, looking right at Prax as he finishes his sentences. The man he sees before him is a shell of the man that took a wrestling company, ran it like an independent company and put no money in it and turned it into a multi-million dollar business. Certainly not the same man that was the reason for Windy Bridges as we know him. “Listen George. I’m sorry I didn’t come to visit you, or frankly even called. I think you know why—“ “Don’t worry Windy. Not many people want to be associated with someone who did the things that I did. Frankly I’m surprised you’re even here now.” Windy shifts in his seat a little uncomfortably. “Well George, I didn’t just come here out of the blue, just to visit you.” Prax smiles and laughs. “Windy, don’t worry, I figured as much. And you don’t have to act like I’m going to jump up and bite you. I might be criminally insane, but I’m not Hannibal Lecter. I’m a white collar criminal for crying out loud.” Windy lets loose, somewhat. “I know George. It’s not even that really, it about what I came here to tell you. I know they haven’t let you watch any TV or read the news in a while.” Prax raises his eyebrow curiously. “I’m here to tell you that on Sunday, I’m going to be wrestling Ace Cannon.” Prax’s other eyebrow joins the right one firmly implanted 3 inches up his forehead. “A month ago, I was doing an appearance on Monday Night Mayhem, and, well, Cannon unexplainably attacked both myself and Spud. Spud’s in a coma, and he’s probably never going to speak or sing again.” “Wow. Now that’s a mouthful to take in. Windy, you realize you and Cannon and the only two originals that haven’t fought each other yet?” “That’s why I’m here man. To tell you the truth, I’m determined, I’m ready for this, but I don’t know what to expect. Cannon, he’s been in a weird place lately. Ever since the last time MWE went under and we all stopped talking, he lost touch with reality. Up to a little while ago he was living in a trailer in the middle of a desert with a couple of dogs. And even after he attacked me, he disappeared, making only one appearance at the next Mayhem. No one’s even heard from him since.” Prax finally relaxes and smiles at Windy. “Cannon’s a special one. Ever since I took him under my wing when we left WZCW, he’s shown both brilliance in and out of the ring, and I’ve seen him completely removed and complacent. Best way to describe him would be an enigma.” Windy stands up. He begins pacing back and forth around the small room. “So how do I approach this? This isn’t a typical match, and frankly, I don’t know what I’m going to do to him with no rules. If I can’t control myself, and if I get into a precarious position, there’s no telling what measures he might take.” “The Cannon you’ve been talking about here, I’ve seen this before. He’s not into this. I can’t tell you whether he feels remorse or whether he’s so emotionless about the whole matter that he’s lost touch with reality. If I were a betting man – and I am – I’d tell you to go out there and beat the shit out of him. Maybe you’ll beat some sense into him.” Windy stops and looks at Prax. Whether the look in his eyes is that of relief, worry, or motivation is hard to tell. But clearly, Prax has told him something that has changed his opinion. “Well one thing’s for sure. I’m going to get revenge on Cannon for what he did. The trail of destruction that he’s left over the years, it’s gone unnoticed and unpunished for way too long. And the group of us that started this whole thing, this revolution… well, it’s about time it all ended. This Sunday Ace Cannon is finally going to get what’s coming to him. Thanks Prax.” Prax slowly gets up, smiling at his former protégé. “Honestly, Windy, it was my pleasure. And it was great to see you. Which is what makes this so hard. Windy, confused, doesn’t say a word. Before he can even react, Prax lunges towards him and pushes him down, along with the camera man. The clang of his chains alerts the guards, and they swing open the door, but it’s too late. Somehow, in all the confusion, and while the camera was down, Prax found a way out of the room! Arnold Glynn bursts into the room, fuming. “Damnit, not again!” The camera pans away from him and towards Windy, who is left smiling and as determined as ever, as we fade to black.
