A crow left of the murder

May 23 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
We find ourselves in a luxurious skyline office. Plants, paintings and ornaments are visible at every corner available corner. Floors draped in expensive Persian carpets. A brightly lit room to properly highlight all the other expensive things in this room. And a widely viewable window overlooking the beautiful skyline of Boston, Massachusetts. Yes, this is the office of a very rich and powerful man. And needless to say, that rich and powerful man is here to flaunt and taunt about all his hard earned fixtures. Sitting behind a luxurious oak desk, a desk bigger than any one man would ever need, is George Prax, former wrestling owner and enemy to many. Sitting at his desk, wearing his reading glasses and working on, most likely, his next big scheme, Prax seems unfazed by the cameras and their presence, as he continues to work for a minute. A man that has always been hard to read, in terms of emotions, he never let other people really get into his mind, and what he was thinking, and plotting, a quality that has made him very rich and successful over the years. What he used that quality for, and what he did exactly to acquire that power and those riches, however, are things that he probably wouldn’t want very many people to know, especially not in a public forum such as this. As Prax continues to work, the camera zooms in on the walls, examining Prax’s many accolades, degrees, and pictures with celebrities and powerful people, which include a degree from Harvard, one from Oxford, and many honorary degrees from across the country. Included in the pictures are ones with him shaking hands with men such as Donald Trump, former US president Ronald Regan, even Mikhail Gorbachev and Prince Charles. This man has certainly made his way across the dining rooms of very many rich and powerful people. But right now, he is here, in his office, and he has summoned a camera crew, presumably to say something. And he finally puts down his pen, and takes off his reading glasses. He looks at, directly into the camera, and smiles, in a way only he could. “It took me a long time to get an office up here, on the top floor of the tallest building in Boston. As a former professional fighter all over the world… MMA… boxing… amateur wrestling… as well as a well known announcer in professional wrestling and other sports, not very many people took me or my ideas seriously. And it didn’t matter how much I flashed my degrees… my well drawn out business ideas and plans… I was still the guy who used to punch people’s brains out for a living. It didn’t matter how smart I was, I was always going to be typecast as nothing more than a go-getting athlete.” Prax gets up from his desk, probably tired of looking at legal and business-type documents. He takes a step over to the window beside him and looks out at the city of Boston. One of the oldest cities this side of the Atlantic, Boston has this unique historical charm, mixed with a modern touch that you can’t find many other places. And George Prax has made his business right here, in the heart of it all. “I went to Harvard for my Bachelor of Commerce. And then I went to Oxford for a PhD in marketing. I had an incredible dissertation that had professors raving. Straight A students. But school wasn’t paying for itself. You see, I didn’t have rich and powerful parents to pay my way. I couldn’t charm my way into these schools, and through school bills. I had to pay my way into and through everything. I had to live in one room apartments below one Chinese restaurant… and above another Indian restaurant. Let me tell you, the girls didn’t like the smell of one or the other, yet alone the smell of both!” Prax continues to keep his back turned to the camera and out towards the city. But slowly, as if becoming progressively more and more pensive, Prax raises his eyes to the sky. “So I did the only thing I knew how to do, beyond my business skills. I started fighting. The money was good, and, just like any other normal, red-blooded American, it got me excited. It made me feel big, and important. At first, it was underground. Real Fight Club kind of stuff. And let me tell you, once you’ve been in a real fight club, you don’t want to go back. But I had to go back. Every week, sometimes several times a week. Until my face was full of lumps and the rest of my body black with bruises. Until I had lost piles of blood. And then I’d fight again. Because it was the only way.” Prax drifts off for a moment, reminiscing on old times, bloodying and bruising relative strangers in the slums of Boston and then in England, and being bloodied and bruised himself. And whether these memories of beatings and triumphs are good or bad ones, only Prax knows. “And then something interesting happened. I finished my dissertation. I got my second diploma. And people could hereon-in call me doctor. And I had a choice. Go work at some university, write a few papers, do a few television appearances here and there when my expertise were required for some low watched news program… or continue fighting… make the big times… and make something of my self… or die in the process. And once again, I did the only thing a real, red-blooded American COULD do in such an instance. I continued fighting. And I continued winning, and making good money.” Prax’s view finally returns to within his office. Still standing by his window, the look on his face becomes rather glum, a rare feature in his facial expressions. “But after a while, my body started to break down. I was fighting too much, and not taking enough breaks in between. Before you knew it I couldn’t fight anymore, my career ended by a knee injury. So I went into broadcasting. But I didn’t limit myself to fighting. Boxing, wrestling, even mainstream sports, I did it all. And I made a name for myself beyond the fighting world. But it still wasn’t enough. I still knew that I could do better. So I started working for wrestling promotions. Promoting events, representing owners and wrestlers, even as low as building rings at one point. But once again, no one wanted to take me seriously. In anything I did. No one wanted to listen to my ideas. No one wanted to go into business with me. Nothing.” All of a sudden, Prax smiles. A sincere smile. A thoughtful smile. An odd expression for such a man. “But after years of toiling, flashing my accomplishments, my degrees… after years of being brushed aside as an ex-fighter with a meaningless degree and a head that’s been bashed in, one man… one man FINALLY took some confidence in me. One man finally decided that I was a good investment. And as it turns out, it was one of the biggest mistakes this man ever made. It almost killed his promotion. One man funded me, allowed me to do what I knew I could do best, and start my own promotion. And that man… was Richard Covell! Richard Covell gave me the money I needed to start WAR. I had the brains, the know-how and the connections, and he had the money. But he got paranoid, and he reeled us in. He brought us into MWE because he thought that we would overtake him and his company. He brought us into MWE because, unlike a lot of people before him, he saw what I was capable of, and he got scared.” Prax walks back in towards his desk, and takes a seat atop it as he looks back up towards nothingness, reminiscing on memories that are a little more consistent than the last. Once again, some good ones, some bad ones, but which are good or bad to him is, once again, unclear. “So we came in… and we made an impact. And I took that opportunity that I was given, and I pounced on it. WAR made me a lot of money. It made me a lot of powerful friends, and it opened a lot of doors. And although I was run out of the old MWE, I had made enough of a name for myself and accumulated enough funds to start my various businesses… and to eventually open up Revolution Pro. And even though that latest venture failed…. I am back… and I am certainly back… with a vengeance.” Prax smiles. He smiles wide, and he smiles hard. “And that plan will be revealed to the public in due time. When the time is right, that is. But what is this speech all about? Why am I telling you all, all about my life and my history? Why should I assume that you care, and what does it have to do with Winston’s match this Sunday against Sinc Mercier? If you can even call that debacle a match… Well, folks, it’s simple. Windy Bridges was the one who convinced me to come back, and, more importantly, convinced me to come back… by his side. Windy Bridges convinced me that of all the wrestlers, of all the snakes that had turned their backs to me, of all the crows from our band that had come and gone, he was the one crow left of the murder.” Prax gets up off his desk and begins walking across the large room, over to a book case. “And don’t get either of us wrong. Windy can certainly take care of Sinc Mercier on his own, I think that should be a given to everyone. And he could certainly handle any other superstar here in the MWE on his own. But he needs me, in order to make his impact. And over the coming months, an impact, we will make. Step one is complete. I have made my return. Step two, is for Windy to win those championships, which he should easily do. And step 3… well… you guys will just have to wait and see. But this Sunday, unfortunatlely, MWE fans in Rome will not get to see George Prax. Windy sent me home, and I had no problem with that. Because Windy needs to fight his own fight this time, and wants me nowhere near that fiasco of a match, if you can even call it that. And that is why I am here, in Boston, while Windy is in Rome, preparing for his match. But before I left him in Europe, I had him film this announcement to before the match, when the time was right. Because Windy did not want to waste more energy talking about this match. Windy wanted to let his fists do the talking this Sunday.” Prax pulls out a DVD case and opens it. Inside, surely enough, is a shiny new DVD. Prax removes it from its case and inserts it into a nearby DVD player, next to a big screen plasma screen. “I think everyone has heard me speak enough. The fact remains that I will not be at Tribulation, but this man will. And he will win. So without further adieu, here he is, Mr. Winston Bridges!” Prax picks up a remote from his desk and presses play. Quickly enough, Windy’s face appears on the big screen. Sitting behind a desk, with a glass of water and some papers on it, Windy is wearing a black suit with a purple tie. A simple but expensive suit. The setting is simple. Other than the table and it’s contents, Windy is surrounded by blue paint on the walls behind him. A plant sits in the corner, one of those neat spider plants. The lighting shining down on Windy’s face is artificial. In fact, if we didn’t know where Windy actually was, we could certainly assume that this video was taken almost anywhere in the world. “So this Sunday, I enter the ring with Sinc Mercier… and I quickly exit it in order to find two championships…. To actually FIND two championships. Wait, wait, wait. I don’t think you understand me. I have to FIND two championships. Like… ACTUALLY search for them… not wrestle for them. Not fight for them. Not compete for them in any sort of semblance of the word. I actually have to run around like an idiot looking for the belts. What a joke!” Windy shakes his head disapprovingly. He’s certainly been one to wear his emotions on his sleeve in recent times, and the anger and disgust that he’s felt have certainly been two emotions on display in recent times. “And from what I understand, there are some people that aren’t too please with this match at all, other than myself. Zak Warner never truly got his title rematch, since I retired Paige. Paige herself is upset, since she feels that she should be the one who picks the new champion, or at least the new contenders. Joseph Sharpman is upset because he was supposed to be that hand picked champion. And really I could care less about what other people think of my matches, but two things are certain. For starters, albeit for different reasons, I agree with them. This match disturb me. Hell, I don’t even know why I’m calling it a match. It’s nothing more than a joke, and I’m embarrassed to have my name associated with it. And the other thing is certain, is that I find it amusing that Sinc Mercier has managed to piss off so many people simply by getting a match booked at a pay-per-view. Simply by wanting to please the most people possible and entertain them.” Windy lets out a disdainful smile. “And instead of pleasing people… instead of entertaining them and showing them what all this fun is all about… he’s pissed off his co-workers. And he’s pissed me off to the point where I’m going to want to tear his little carcass apart come Tribulation. And I’m sure he’s going to spend the nights leading up to our match crying about how all these people don’t like him, which is something that I find incredibly ironic. Because Sinc Mercier, a few months ago, that would have been ME!” The smile once again dissipates from Windy’s face. Memories of when he was a carefree, fun-loving good guy, who cares more about pleasing the fans then winning and making a name for himself. An attitude that cost him a lot in the end. Years off his career. His movie and record deals. But now, the roles are reversed. “A few months ago, it would have been ME going to the boss with the idea of a Scavenger Hunt Match for a championship. It would have been ME pissing a bunch of people off by getting that booked. And it would have been ME crying myself to sleep simply because I’m not the most popular man in the world. And I know I must be sounding like a broken record. A record that’s been shattered into dozens of pieces. But I will continue to say it, until you finally get it, Sinclair. The reason I’ve taken all this time to go against you, is not because I hate you, or because I want you to suffer.” Windy takes a sip from his glass of water. Trying to muster the words that make the most sense, the words that describe exactly what he feels, what he thinks. “Son, I want you to know how it feels. I want you to be able to tell me how it feels. I want you to let me know what you think of ALL these people grinding their teeth, waiting for an opportunity to punch you and cave your face in, simply because you put the fans interest ahead of your own, in a business that thrives on wrestlers doing whatever they can to get ahead. Does it feel bad? Does it hurt? Let it all out man. You’ll feel better. Instead of bottling it up inside. Instead of continuously attempting to redeem yourself towards the fans… towards your co-workers… towards everyone else around you... Instead if continuing to dig your hole… deeper… and deeper… and deeper… until you’re eventually six feet under.” Windy doesn’t flinch. He’s barely even moving anymore. Staring right into the camera. Staring a hole through the equipment, through all the cables and airwaves cutting him off between standing face-to-face with Sinc right now. Because that’s what he’s doing. To him, there is no camera. There are no walls or airwaves separating him from Sinc. There are no people standing in his way. In his mind, he’s addressing Sinc directly, and no one around him matters. “Because as I told you. I’ve been in your position. I’ve seen the things you’ve see. I’ve felt the things you feel. I’ve wanted the exact same things you seek out right now. You see, I was always the good guy. The fan favourite. The babyface. But deep down inside, I knew I was resented. Not only by a lot of fans. More importantly, by a lot of my co-workers and fellow wrestlers. I knew that they resented not only my success, but the rapport I had with the fans. And that I knew that no matter what I did, that resentment would continue to grow. It would continue to stifle until one day, one of them snapped and did me more harm than good.” Windy’s expression becomes cold and resentful. “And then, one of them did snap…” A sickened look appears on Windy’s face. Memories he does not want to relive. Feelings he does not want to feel. Pain he has kept hidden away for what seems to be a long while now. “Finally, someone took the actions that a lot of people around the industry were feeling. Finally, someone stood up to my carelessness. And that person, was the Grand Mystique. The Grand Mystique snapped. And the Grand Mystique took actions that would set off a series of events that would lead to one of the bloodiest and brutal matches this business has ever seen, and one of the most intense, soul-grinding feuds in wrestling history. And despite all the crap that I took from him, all the beatings and all the times I came near death, I am grateful to GM, for showing me that I no longer needed to be a little douchebag in order to achieve success. That I no longer needed to act like a fool to receive the respect I deserved. That respect is earned through hard work in the ring, not tomfoolery and lollygagging. Windy’s eyes are firmly set in a gaze right through the camera in front of him. Right at this moment, nothing else exists. Not the camera, not this room, not anything between him and Sinc. To Windy, he is talking directly to Sinc Mercier. “And trust me Sinc, there could have been many other people who decided that I needed to learn a lesson. Many people on this earn wanted me dead, or at the very least wounded. But I came to my senses. I saw the resentment in my fellow wrestlers’ eyes. And I realized I couldn’t continue on the path that I was on. A path of self-destruction. A path of building anger and resentment, that would destroy, not only me, and those around me, but everything that I even came into contact with.” The look on Windy’s face, once again, begins to express the confidence that we have come to know him for. Both as a face and as a heel. Over the years, he has been known as one to exuberate such a confidence, such a carelessness about what people think about him. And it’s funny, how after such a change in mood, in attitude, he still exhibits that same over-confidence, just on the difference side of the coin. “Sinc Mercier, I am your Grand Mystique. I will take you down to the hell that I’ve lived. I will show you things you will regret for the rest of your life. I will make you suffer. But not because I hate you. Because I feel sorry for you. I feel sorry that you act like such an idiot, and you think that people like it. I feel sorry that you think this path that you are on will lead you to success. I feel sorry that you believe your own crap. And for that, just like a stern but loving father, I will beat some sense into you. I know you booked this match so you wouldn’t have to fight me, but I WILL fight you. And after I beat you from one end of Rome to the other, I’m going to take the only things that could mean anything to you. I will take away your will to please the fans, and I will take away both the United States and the Intercontinental championships. And I will leave you to rot in that ring.” Windy finally gets up from behind the table as the camera begins to pan out, revealing him fully. He steps around the table and stands, in front of camera, eyes still completely fixated on what’s looking from beyond. “Let me make myself perfectly clear. I want those championships. And I want to make you suffer. But moreso, I want to make a man out of a child. I want Sinc Mercier to come to his senses, and I want him to grow up. This is a man’s world, and you’re nothing but a child playing with fire trucks and making jokes. But in this man’s world, actions have consequences. I learned that the hard way, and I will expect nothing less from you. At Tribulation, you will pay for your sins. And if I win those two championships in the process, then so be it. But your blood will be spilled, and your lesson, will be learned. You can count on that. Windy continues to stand in front of the camera, eyes gazing into what is beyond, into his sole viewer’s eyes. Gleaming with confidence, windy stands proud. In his head, he has already become the US Champion, and the Intercontinental Champion, and he has already taught his lesson to Sinc Mercier. But he has a ways to go until he can say these things for certain. Standing confidently in front of the camera, Windy ponders what actions he might take in only a few days, as the scene fades.
