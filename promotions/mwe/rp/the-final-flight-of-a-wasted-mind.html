QUOTE When it comes to interesting careers, few wrestlers reach the level of MWE’s first world heavyweight champion, David Whitman. Capturing the title at Crowning Glory by defeating 9 other men in May of 2007, his first title run ended at the hands of a man who would soon become a long-time rival of his; Brian Michaels. “Whitman was a decent wrestler back then. Of course, I was countless times better. He never defeated me after I took his belt. I’d love to kick hi- I mean, face him again sometime.” After losing the title at MWE’s Turmoil Pay-per-view, David would not hold that gold again until roughly a year later, when he regained the title at MWE’s second Crowning Glory. “I was stunned,” says David Whitman. “It was like taking a trip back in time. So many moments during that battle royal had me asking myself; ‘why does this feel so familiar??’ and winning, that just took the cake.” But that would only be the beginning of the Déjà-vu-fest for the native Arizonian. Just one month later, history repeated itself yet again in its worst fashion for David, as he lost the title once more, at Turmoil; the same pay-per-view he had lost it at, little more than a year before. That night, the title went to a man who once walked side-by-side with David as part of a “Family”, the man known as none other than Sonny Williams. Billy “Sonny” Williams, who at one time was one of David’s best friends, had lost his love for the fans and become despised by many since his so-called turn to the dark side. With the unexpected help of the insane clown known only as Jekyl, Sonny managed to beat David Whitman into a bloody pulp and become part of the cause of the then-champ’s bout of temporary insanity. About four weeks later after that incident, Sonny would go on to best David and Jekyl in a three-way match to become the new MWE champion. “I think what hurt the most was realizing I lost the title without being pinned. That’s one of the reasons I can’t stand triple threat matches. It’s like a tag team match where if your partner is pinned, you lose the match too; but in this case your partner is trying to beat you as well!” David Whitman after Sonny and Jekyl’s brutal beating on MWE2’s first Monday Night Mayhem. During 2007, Whitman and Williams were part of the faction known as the Mayhem Mafia, along with fellow MWE superstar Pala. This group became a huge success with the MWE audience, as many found that they had created a perfect balance between funny and serious that led them straight to the top. Their rivalry with Rated-X went down in the books as one of the most heated faction wars in wrestling history. During all of this Whitman and Williams became close friends; basing promos on real life situations, and soon the Mayhem Mafia became a household name. “Mayhem Mafia! Man, that was so much fun,” recalls David. “It bothers me. How someone like Sonny could go from that guy to- to what he is now. It’s very, very unsettling. I mean, I knew him very well, and knowing that he could change like that is just so scary because- well, what’s to say that it won’t happen to me? But hey. All I know is, I’m glad that I am able to say that I’ve been a part of the Mayhem Mafia. I learned a lot, and above all, it was fun as hell. David Whitman during the “Mayhem Mafia” era. The good times would soon come to an end, however, as David began having trouble backstage. “Yeah, he’d come over to just rant about how unfair it all was; all that crap with Michaels and whatever, he’d just go on and on,” says David’s cousin, James Whitman. “David is usually a pretty quiet guy, and it doesn’t take a lot to make him happy. He’ll put up with a lot of sh*t, too. But stuff was going on backstage, mostly with the guys who were running the show; stuff that David just couldn’t handle. He wanted to wipe his hands clean of the whole thing, and that’s exactly what he did.” During David’s hiatus from MWE, he wrestled for Wrestling’s Highest Quality with a lighter work schedule. He still managed to win the US title and hold onto it for a while. Around that time, David’s cousin James had also taken a vacation from wrestling and opened a bar; the Top Rope Tavern. David decided to make a series about that bar with the help of James and James’ stable mates, and the show became an instant success. “Of course. It was a match- no pun intended- made in heaven,” recalls James’ wrestling partner and David’s trainer, Thomas Williams. “Wrestling. Drinking. Joking around and having fun. It was basically all we ever did. So it was a logical next step, and when James and David both had light workloads, they came up with the idea. All of us chipped in. The fans loved it, and that was that. The series became even more popular than our movie, Bulletproof JEST- which, by the way, is out on DVD!” Not long after the first few episodes of the show were aired though, David and company began having trouble yet again; the people in charge of the network (and the wrestling federation) where TRT was aired started having doubts about the show due to its raucous nature. This in turn led to a production slowdown, which led to a screeching halt just before post-production of their seventh episode. David had once again run into a roadblock; the show’s future was uncertain, as was his own future as a wrestler for that company. That’s when the call came. “I remember exactly where I was; my little niece’s school play. She’s James’ sister Jessica’s kid, but she’s never met James since James and Jess haven’t been on speaking terms for the longest while. I’d been over there a couple of times though, and we were quite close… before I had to stop talking to them. It was the one and only thing James had ever asked of me in his life, so uh... yeah. Anyway, I was at the play. For some reason, the little scamp created a character that’s peculiarly reminiscent of me.” David laughs. David Whitman, with his little niece, at her school play. “I even had to lend her my ‘Awesome Style’ hoodie so she would look just like me. Anyway, we were just posing for a picture after the play was over, when my phone started ringing. She’d change the ring tone to some weird Lavigne song that we always made fun of, and she started to laugh. As soon as the picture was taken I took my cell phone out and answered it. It was a weird voice I had never heard before. They asked if I wanted to come back to Mayhem Wrestling Entertainment. I thought it was some sort of prank call, and hung up.” But it was the real deal. “Later that night when I got home there were some cars parked outside my house, and people in suits waiting at the door. It was eerie, like some sort of movie. We went inside and they told me all about the return of MWE, and how they would love to see the first ever MWE champion return along with the company, sort of like, a symbolic kinda thing. I asked them, ‘What if I win the title, just like last time?’ And they said; ‘That would be astonishing. But the main thing is to have you there. The chances of you repeating history are slim, but you can try.’ And well, yeah.” David chuckles. “I also remember the shock of hearing about Sonny for the first time, though, and that’s one of the hardest things I’ve ever had to digest; I had never had a friend turn into a completely different person like that before.” David even goes so far as to compare the procedure of getting through the ordeal to the well-known Kübler-Ross model of the stages of grief. “The whole process has truly been like losing a friend, you know, having a close friend die. I didn’t even realize that until I was talking to my therapist about it, and she brought up the five stages. I was like… ‘Well, wait a minute, that actually makes sense!’ I mean first, there was denial; after the initial shock, I definitely remember saying to myself, over and over; ‘This isn’t happening. I can still change him. Sonny’s still Sonny on the inside, right?’ Then I felt the anger; when I got beat down by Sonny and Jekyl, I felt furious- for the few minutes I was conscious. After that, I completely lost it- I was ready to just break him in half. Bargaining came soon after that as I started telling myself that maybe I could still change him; maybe all I had to do was change MYSELF a little. Become a little looser with the rules? Turn my back on a few fans? As I realized I couldn’t do that, I went through the depression stage. I lost sight of it all. I had lost one of my closest friends, and was basically alone in the federation. What was the point of it all? Acceptance was the toughest stage, but once it came, it came in a burst- I was completely over it. ‘If this is what he wants, then so be it.’ I mean, all that really mattered once I accepted it, was the title shot. And that’s what made it all easier. I have a title shot now, and I’ve got nothing standing in the way of me giving it my all. Not friendship, not anything.” Despite all of this, Whitman still holds Williams in high regard as a wrestler. “I respect him a ton. I still say that Sonny can beat anyone he wants to in the ring if he puts his mind to it. That’s why I’m not just letting the rage steer me. I’ve been practicing and will continue training because I know that at one point, this match is going to be about sheer skill versus sheer skill.” That match, the MWE World Heavyweight Championship match at Wrestle-Wars II, is going to be, in David’s own words, “the biggest match of his career” yet. “Yeah, it sounds cliché as hell, but this match is going to be huge. Epic, even. I know we’re both going to go to the extreme in this match; dip into that reservoir of untapped energy and just go nuts. All I can say is, when the smoke clears and the dust settles, when that bell rings and those speakers start blasting loudly, when all is said and done… one thing will be certain- MWE will be changed. In more ways than one.” Indeed. All signs indicate that the first ever Whitman-Williams Pay-Per-View main event will indeed be one of the biggest matches in MWE history. And regardless of the outcome, it seems that most everyone can agree that this match will definitely be insane. Dude, have you read this article? As they hit the ground, David wakes up, sweating, his heart racing at an incredible rate. He is in his hotel room bed, which is completely drenched in his sweat. He turns to look at the alarm clock, and sees the time. Hours; 10. Minutes; 27. Seconds; 08. He takes a deep breath as he lies back down. He looks up at the ceiling. David’s thoughts drift away to an unknown destination… As he just lays there. Eyes wide open.
