Hell In A hand Basket; Zack Crash RP

Aug 10 2009

Zack Crash

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A pop. A lurch. A tingling sensation. All of these could be used to describe what just happened inside the mind of Zack Crash. It came so suddenly it’s like it didn’t happen at all. Yet it did happen. And its power was enough to freeze him in mid-motion. A memory that has risen to the top of his consciousness with such randomness it’s almost scary. And what a memory it is. Our scene opens up to the familiar view of Zack’s humble apartment in Charleston. We find Zack sitting on the couch, frozen with a black controller held in his hands. Next to him, Kent presses furiously on his own control as flashes lights and sound emit for the television screen. From the automated grunts and moans coming from the screen, we can assume they are playing some form of video game. Maybe it’s Tekken or something similar. Kent’s body is fully committed to defeating his opponent and friend. But right now, Zack’s blank expression tells us his mind is anywhere but here. He stares absently at the screen. Kent: Oh yeah! Take that! Man I’m SOOOOO kicking your ass right now! Come on, you’re not even trying anymore! Zack doesn’t even hear his words. The rush of thought going on inside his head swiftly and powerfully drowns them out. He never was one to give too much stock into nostalgia. So why does he find himself now thinking about that event with such vividness? The game’s soundtrack signals the end of the fight as Kent throws up a fist in triumph. Zack remains still, the controller still held firmly in his hands. Kent: Who’s the man? I’m the man! Kent turns to face Zack, ready to gloat till he’s blue in the face, but stops abruptly. He finally notices Zack’s rather out of place expression. And it worries him. Kent’s face falters a bit as he leans in closer to Zack. Kent: Hey? You ok buddy? Hellooooooooo? He waves a palm in Zack’s face, bringing him out of his revere with a twitch. His eyes focus again, and he remembers where he is. He looks up and gives Kent a reassuring smile. Zack: Oh, sorry man. Guess I spaced out for a moment there. The response comes a little too quickly and shakily, as if he already had it planned. Kent eyes him with an arched eyebrow. Clearly he knows something is bothering him. Trying to divert him, Zack gets up and crawls over to a neat pile of PS2 games. An outdated system perhaps, but Zack enjoys it well enough. Zack: So, what do you wanna play next? I got Smackdown vs. Raw, Guitar Hero, Twisted Metal? OH, I got it! Zack reaches into the pile and pulls out and brandish a copy of Star Wars: Battlefront 2. Zack: I got this! Now I know you hate Star Wars, but trust me, this game kicks major butt on multiplayer mode. You’ll like it, promise. Zack goes to put the game in, waiting for Kent to offer his usual groans of protest. But there is only silence. Feeling his gaze on his back, Zack turns to find Kent looking at him with a most meaningful expression. Rather uncharacteristic of him. Zack maintains his smile. Zack: What? Zack says to break the uncomfortable silence. Kent rolls is eyes in irritation. Kent: Don’t give me that ‘what’ crap! Lets hear it. Zack: Lets hear what? Kent sits up straighter and crosses his arms to show Zack he is not backing down that easily. But Zack continues to feint ignorance. Kent: I know something is bothering you. So out with it. Zack turns away, resuming his set up of the game. He opens removes the disk and places it in the slot as the systems gives its usual start up chorus. Zack: I don’t know what your talking about. Zack said with a laugh, trying to sound as if he found the question humorous. But Kent keeps on him. Kent: I can see it in your eyes. You were just spacing out like you were strung out on drugs a second ago. So don’t tell me nothing is wrong. Zack goes to sit back down on the couch next to Kent, picking up his controller and keeping his voice and features expressive and happy, trying to hide that his body is shaking slightly. Zack: Seriously man, nothing’s wrong. Kent eyes him admonishingly for a moment before finally releasing a long, heavy, exasperated sigh. Kent: Whatever, have it your way. But just know it isn’t good to keep stuff bottled up inside. Zack: Dually noted. Kent surrenders to not push the conversation further, knowing full well he won’t get any different a response. But his constant looks over at Zack tell us he’s still visibly worried. The night continues on as usual. Zack and Kent continue to shoot the breeze. But there is still a notable change in Zack. What could it be? Whatever it is, if must be bad if it makes Kent Bishop worry about him. Finally, the night comes to an end as Zack begins to escort Kent to the door. Zack: Thanks for coming over. Kent: You sure that nothing’s wrong? Zack: Yeah. Absolutely 100 percent here. Zack flexes his muscles as if this will showcase the fact that he’s fit, chuckling to himself as he does. Kent merely frowns. But it isn’t the usual stiff, humorless, ill-tempered frown we see him normally wearing. It looks almost…sad. Kent just shrugs. Kent: Ok man. If you insist. Zack: See ya. Kent merely waves with his back to him as he makes his way down the hall. Zack waits until Kent is out of sight before his face becomes blank and expressionless once more. He shuts the door quietly, though his senses for some reason magnify the sound inside his head. Pacing inside his apartment, he goes to turn off the TV, then sits back down at the kitchen table in silence. A pregnant, depressing silence. The kind where one unwittingly becomes introspective. Without knowing why, Zack found himself standing up and proceeding over to the part of the fridge where he hung up a calendar with a magnet. With his finger as a guide, he searched the month of August until he found today’s date. Then, he understood. The sudden wave of memory. It was an unconscious reminder. How could he have forgotten it was today? Had it really been that long? The scene dissolves, as time and space take us on a virtual ride into the past. Soon, we find ourselves in a different location. Up and down the hall his father paced, hands clasped behind his back, his brow heavy with fresh sweat. Against the wall, on a cold, wooden, sat a much smaller version of Zack Crash. Looking at him, he appears to be no older than eleven, his features familiar, only younger. The hallway is bright with florescent lights that cast an almost angelic glow. The light is almost magnified by the equally white tile floors. The whole place smells sterile and so clean in makes the head spin. From the looks of it, it’s a hospital. Zack, even as a boy, always hated places like this. Apart from a bad experience he had with getting a shot earlier, he thought they were unnaturally clean. This one was no exception. From a door opposite where Zack is sitting, a man dressed in a long white coat steps out with a clipboard under his arm. He’s a young, impressive looking man. Everything about him looks like how doctor should appear. Before he can even shut the door behind him, Zack’s father rushes toward the man while Zack remains seated. He looks pale and worried. Mr. Crash: How’s my wife? The doctor’s young, handsome features look grave. Clearly, this can’t mean good news. Doctor: Perhaps we should talk in my office. The doctor leads Mr. Crash away toward his office with a gesture, Zack following closely on their heels. Later, they enter the doctor’s simply furbished office, both Zack and his father taking chairs on the either side of each other in front of the man’s desk. He sits down, leaning on his elbows with his hands cupped together. Doctor: There’s no real easy way to tell you this, so I’ll spare you the drama of beating around the bush and get right to the point. Mr. Crash: Why? What is it? What’s wrong? The doctor leans back in his chair and straightens his black tie, which offsets the whiteness of his coat. Zack only hangs his head quietly, helpless to the inevitable news that is slowly coming. Doctor: I’m afraid your wife’s breast cancer has come back. And it’s rather aggressive. There’s a moment of deadly silence before Mr. Crash speaks up, his voice cracking under strain and despair. Mr. Crash: Wait a minute! I thought you said her cancer was in remission! That’s what you told us a year and a half ago isn’t it? Doctor: Mr. Crash, I told you before, remission doesn’t mean your wife’s cancer has just magically disappeared. Mr. Crash: Then what DOES it mean? Mr. Crash tone is almost accusatory as he glares at the doctor, angry at his incompetence. The doctor sighs, looking at Mr. Crash with a pitying look. He turns his gaze to Zack, equally pitying before turning back to his father, whose arms and legs are shaking like noodles. Doctor: Remission means that it’s gone into a sort of…hibernation you could say. Its usual affects aren’t noticeable, and you can’t even tell it’s still inside you. But, unfortunately, it almost always comes back. Mr. Crash: But I thought she was getting better? Zack’s father looks like he’s about to break down into tears while Zack sits idly beside him. In truth, he doesn’t know what’s going on inside him. Being so young, he hasn’t yet dealt with grief or loose. Now that he’s faced with it, he doesn’t know how to confront it. Mr. Crash: How long? He asks humorlessly. The doctor sighs. Doctor: Six months I’d say. Maybe more if she’s strong. Like I said, it’s unusually aggressive. A silence falls between them so thick it threatens to choke out the air in the room. The doctor slowly rises from his chair, walking over to put a comforting hand on Mr. Crash’s shoulder. Doctor: I just want you to know I’m very sorry about all this. Then he turns to face Zack. Doctor: Are you ok son? What? The question is so asinine it forces Zack to react. He merely looks up into the doctor’s face as he looks down on him with pity. Zack hates the expression he’s giving him. Zack: Uh huh. Is all he’s able to muster. He doesn’t know how he feels. This is the woman who’s always been so protective of him. In fact, some might say overly protective. So much so that she barely let Zack get his own bike when he was younger. And Zack would be lying if he said her overbearing, motherly protection didn’t get on his nerves every so often, not to mention being the target of many bullies at school. But still, it’s his mother. And now…she’s going to die? How are you supposed to feel at a time like this? The doctor gives Mr. Crash a light nod, then leaves the office to give Mr. Crash and his son some time alone. Again…silence. Mr. Crash: What do we do now? The question came at a whisper, barely audible. Zack looked up at his father, his face scrunched up with overwhelming sadness. Zack could see a small glisten of something pass down his cheek, and only slowly did he come to realize this was a tear. Without warning, Mr. Crash turned and threw his arms around his son, burying him in a tight embrace. Mr. Crash: What do we do now son? And Zack didn’t know. And as he returned his father’s hug, he too felt a small drop of moisture crawl down his cheek. Mr. Rash stood up, using the sleeve of his shirt to whip away his tears. He holds out a hand for Zack to take, pulling him out of the chair and onto his feet. When he speaks, it is with forced cheer Mr. Crash: Come on son. Let’s go see mom. The scene dissolves again, and we find ourselves returned to the present. Zack is back at the kitchen table, leaning over with his arms propping himself on the table. His body looks heavy, reflecting the bitter memories of his mind he can’t stop. Getting a sudden surge of an idea, he glances over toward the clock mounted on his wall. It isn’t that late. Visitors might still be allowed. Steeling himself with a tight locked lip, he sits up, gathers his keys, and strolls out the door. Several minutes later, we found ourselves now in the darkness of an open space. Outside, guessing from the rushes of wind the camera picks up. All over, we see tiny, neatly cut marble stones, all placed in precise locations in measured rows. Standing over one grave, a fresh, newly bought bouquet of flowers in his hands is Zack Crash. He looks down on it with a sad smile, placing the flowers down at its feet. He stays kneeling, rubbing the small, humble grave with one hand, speaking so low we cannot hear what he is saying. It’s hard to read, but the camera is able to pick up the small lettering on the grave. Julie Marie Crash Devoted mother. Loving wife. May God unfold unto her his loving arms and guide her to the kingdom of eternal light. It’s a simple inscription. But oh so beautiful. In Zack’s eyes, we see a type of inner reflection. The type that comes when one examines a defining moment. And this was one such moment. When his mother’s death, ultimately transformed him into the thrill seeker you see before you. How so, you ask? Well, that’s a story for another day. I have to use something to keep you coming back don’t I? Zack: You know… He speaks. So softly and so quietly, it’s unsure whether he is speaking to us or himself. I don’t really know what to say when I come here. I always try to think of something on the way over, but then just end up forgetting it. I could talk about my career, but I know you wouldn’t like that huh mom? You never did much care for it when I was younger. I remember how you used to scoff when you caught me watching it when I wasn’t supposed to. Zack chuckles weakly at the fond memory as it floats into his consciousness. That uncharacteristic frown we have seen for most of this day steadily brightens into the familiar smile we are used to seeing. But there is still a lack of energy behind them. But you’ll have to forgive me then mom, because there’s nothing else I can really think of that’s worth mentioning. Besides, I have business to take care of. Zack turns to face the camera, and there is no humor in his eyes this time as with his usual promos. They are narrow, peering down at the audience beyond the lens with stunning viciousness. He considers his words carefully before speaking. For the first time in my career, I’m not particularly looking forward to this tag match this week. Now just for the record, let me dispel what are bound to come up as reasons for this lack of my usual enthusiasm. It’s not because I lost my last match. Why should I care about that? Scotty McLoser and The Sickness both proved CSB and me are better then them in the way they conducted themselves during the actual match. Simple as that. Enough said. It’s also not due to a lack of a good partner. Far from it. I like Eric Derf, but we’ll get back to him later. The reason I’m not looking forward to this match is for one reason, and one reason only. My opponents. Kevin Hardaway and Anthony Clark. Both are great athletes. Both are former champions. Both hail from the glorious federation that was once known as the Global Wrestling Coalition. Both are now his opponents. But previous championships and glory days long dead, these things mean nothing to Zack Crash in regards to his opponents. They will not win the match for you. No. All that matters is the match at hand. Now that isn’t to say I’m scared of these men. Again, far from it. This is more a matter of morals than it is of difference in skill. Which K-Hard falls into this category most spectacularly. He offends my personal morals more than any other man I’ve had the misfortune to share a locker room with. Clark’s more “business as usual” for me. So really, the reason I’m not looking forward to this match falls on one person’s shoulders. And guess who that is? A pause. Long and thick. Zack begins pacing through the aisles of grave, each step very meticulous and measured so as not to disturb those resting beneath him. So good job Hardaway. You’ve spoiled my fun. Give yourself a much-deserved pat on the back. You did one thing I thought nobody could do. Unfortunately, that’s about all you ever achieve here in MWE. Now considering the fact that I’ve never faced you, let alone speak to you, you and the others watching must be wondering why I’m so upset with you. What is it about you that so offends my personal morals? It’s so simple I’ll be surprised if anyone doesn’t see this coming. It’s because you’re a traitor. He speaks the last word with a special emphasis of malice. Traitor. A very strong word to say the least. And to Zack, it’s the most offensive thing anyone could be called. Now I know it’s probably known of my business to begin with, but I don’t care. If there’s one thing I live by, it’s that you don’t turn your back on your friends. So naturally, you can see why your actions of late have tickled me the wrong way. How long has Zak Warner been your friend? How much history do you two have together? Years of history and friendship, and in a single instant, you destroyed it all. It surprises me how easily things can be destroyed or broken. And now, just looking at you or listening to you disgust me. Lil’ Hitler? More like Lil’ Judas if you ask me. Though is words are harsh, he speaks softly, almost like a whisper. With the darkness slowly surrounding him, his presence there in the graveyard appears almost ghostly. Step by step, he continues to walk down the path framed with headstones. The whole scene has a very haunting look, made more so by the deep furrow of Zack’s lips and eyes. And what for? Nothing! Zip! Nada! Zilch! Oh, you run around with this crazy notion in your head that Zak betrayed you first, but I don’t buy that for a second. You know what I think? I think you’re jealous of Warner. Of what exactly? Hell if I know what goes on in that twisted little brain of yours. But just like every scumbag, you have the nerve to blame your screwed up nature on other people. News flash Hardaway, it isn’t Warner’s fault, or Angie’s fault, or Sarah’s, or anybody else’s that you’re a sick, twisted, disturbed little pansy. You had a rough childhood you say? Please! Don’t give me that trade excuse. It’s all you. Only you. And this isn’t going to help your career like you think it will. It’ll end up destroying it. Symbols of destruction encompass him on both sides. Many of the graves, though difficult to see in this lack of light, are old and moth-eaten. They look to be several years old, their markers stained with black as if those for whom they were made have been long forgotten by time and nature. Their stone is rough, and no longer smooth like the newer ones. Zack finds them a clever symbolism to K-Hard’s career. And speaking of careers let me ask you something. Every time you mention your accolades, you always mention GWC. But I wonder, what about all the other stuff you did huh? Where’s XHF in your bragging huh? You remember that place right? The place where you were suspended for stealing another person’s promos. Or what about those other second hand promotions in HWF or USXF? Face it. GWC was your one, and only day in the sun. But your sun has burnt out. POOF! It’s gone and it’ll never come back. You may have had a chance to rekindle it here, but now, you destroyed it just like you destroy everything else in your life. So go back home, take a few anti-depressants, and shut the hell up. And I know what you’ll say to this. I’m the rookie, you’re the veteran. Blah blah blah blah! Maybe you are. Maybe you are better than me. I won’t deny how good you are. But the thing is, I’m not looking to be better than you in this match. I’m looking to make sure you go into Crowning Glory with as many bruises as I can give. To me, this isn’t about winning or loosing. It’s about defending what’s right. It’s about showing the fans that amoral pieces of crap like you always get their just desserts. In the end, it may not come from me, but dammit I’m going to try. So you better get ready this Monday, because it won’t be the last time this month you get beat by some guy named Zack. In the background, a small rumble can be heard. A quick flash of light shrouded by the clouds above signal the coming of an approaching storm. The night will be loud and wet. Zack always liked the rain. He always found it comforting. When he was younger, his mother used to tell him that God and the angels were crying whenever it rained. But there will be no comfort in this rain. And now we move onto Anthony Clark. Sorry to say Clark, but you’ve caught me in a bad mood today. So while your not nearly as much an a-hole as Hardaway, I’m not going to let up on you either. Cause while your “business as usual” to me, there is something I’m sick of. Why is it that every pretty boy out there seems to think this qualifies him as a good wrestler? Seriously, I wanna know! Clark, just because you may THINK you’re God’s gift to women doesn’t A.) make it true, and B.) make you a good wrestler. Look at your win/lose rate. If they’re any indication of your looks, then might I suggest rhino-plasty. Clark, you seriously need to grow up and do it now. This isn’t some junior high locker room. Just because you may have one pretty girlfriend, who more then likely has the crabs, doesn’t make you a tough guy. And hell, maybe all the girls do want you for all I know. But that still doesn’t change the fact that you haven’t done anything in MWE. Hell, I’ve had less time here than you, and whom do you think people talk about more? I’ll give you a hint. Zack points a stiff index finger toward his chest, jabbing his finger several times in the sternum, indicating himself. But I think I know why you haven’t done anything. Look back at your time in GWC. What was your greatest achievement there? The GWC Tag Team Championship. Longest reigning too if I’m not mistaken. A very impressive accomplishment. But therein lies the problem. That requires a partner. And that’s it isn’t it? You can’t do anything without someone else behind your back looking out for you. On your own, you’re NOTHING! But when you’re riding the coattails of someone else, then you’re king of the world. Which is probably why you’re so happy about this match. Hoping that K-Hard wins this for you huh? That’s why you’ll never go anywhere here. Because the only way you’ll ever be successful is if you have someone else do it for you. Zack pauses for a moment, standing still in a pool of light from a nearby post lamp. Another flash of thunder. He gazes up at it, almost as if he is gazing up at heaven above, praying for a form of salvation. But Zack needs no salvation. It is his opponents who need to do the praying. Unlike you Clark, I don’t need a tag team partner to carry me through to the end of a match. Now that isn’t to say I’m not grateful when a good partner comes along. Which brings me back to Derf. Now Derf is my kinda guy. The best word that I can think of that best describes him is upstanding. He respects others in the locker room, and he respects the fans. Which why he’ll go farther then K-Hard or Clark. Because Derf understands something they don’t. That sometimes, those who make it in this business aren’t always the fastest, the strongest, or the best. Sometimes, it’s the ones the fans are drawn to. And what do fans react to? Wrestlers who respect them equally as much as the fans respect the wrestlers. K-Hard lost that, and Clark never had it to begin with. Which is why me and Derf will be a future Hall Of Famers, and K-Hard and Clark will be left to fester in obscurity. Zack’s face has suddenly grown weary. Such a long stay in such a depressing area, along with his passionate ranting has finally taken its toll on him. Putting his hands deep into his coat pockets, he strolls between the graves, heading for the entrance where is car is currently parked. He reaches the rot iron gates leading to the parking spaces only to stop, turning to face the camera once more. All the critics have been saying they think I’m not vicious enough. They tell me I need more edge. Well I must ask…is this vicious enough for you? What’s that old saying, “be careful what you wish for because you just might get it.” Oh, and just because I find it to be appropriate. Hardaway, did you know that they say the deepest circle of Hell is reserved specifically traitors? Interesting huh? So you better start saying your Hail Mary’s and Our Father’s, because this Monday, I plan on sending you to Hell in a hand basket. And Clark, sorry, but you’ll just have to go along for the ride. With that done, Zack turns his back and slowly walks off into the darkness beyond. As he disappears and we hear the revving of an engine and the squeal of tires pulling away, a small sprinkle of rain slowly begins to fall before it becomes a down pour.
