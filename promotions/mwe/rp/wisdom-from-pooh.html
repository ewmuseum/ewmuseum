Wisdom from Pooh

Jul 18 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“You can always count on Americans to do the right thing - after they’ve tried everything else.” - Winston Churchill “David will be back, be patient.” - Eddie Sanchez “All good things come to he who is patient, and waits.” “Waiting is a trap. There will always be reasons to wait. The truth is, there are only two things in life, reasons and results, and reasons simply don't count.” - Dr. Robert Anthony “You can't stay in your corner of the Forest waiting for others to come to you. You have to go to them sometimes.” - Winnie the Pooh I’m not waiting… Thanks Pooh… The scene opens in a darkened room, illuminated only by candles, the room has a cold and dark feel about it. It seems a place that a man would go to grieve. But there is only one man in this room, sat on a stone bench pressed against the wall, and he is not grieving, not moving infact, he is contemplating… thinking. He looks up and his head moves into the candlelight, his face covered in his mask, hiding his identity… Although if you look into those eyes that are now transfixed on the camera, you would discover a lot about this man that his face wouldn’t tell you. It would tell you that although still a young man, he is a warrior, a warrior who has been through a lot of triumphs and tragedies throughout his life. But as the warrior looks ever deeper into the camera, he begins to move, and the sound of his breathing echoes off the walls, and when he speaks, his voice sounds powerful and you catch every single word. Alejandro: The words “patience” and “wisdom” are often linked together, and I often ask myself, is it a wise person who sits around and watches the world go past them, a person who does nothing except wait and not act? To me, that is a lazy person, a coward. A wise person chases down all there is to discover, then they are prepared for the questions a wise person must so easily and “wisely” answer, with what exactly? Experience. And to have experience, you must have once done something, so now I’m going to do something. You just wait, you haven’t seen nothing yet. The scene fades into another, in contrast to the last, this scene is of a bright day in Atlanta, Georgia. The camera pans around the scene, showing Alejandro Rodriguez, in white shorts and a blue t-shirt, and his manager Darrel Jones, wearing a white t-shirt and black shorts, jogging along a path through Piedmont Park. Alejandro seems to be jogging with ease, but Darrel is huffing and puffing and he is a little red in the face. Alejandro begins to slow down into a walk, and to his great relief, Darrel does the same. Darrel talks through heavy breaths. Darrel: Explain to me… why… we need to run… through this cursed park… every morning since we got here… Alejandro: We’re training Darrel, since Billy Joe returned to Texas, I have no training buddy. And have you seen the shape you’re in? Do you want to die? Darrel: There’s nothing wrong with my body. I do plenty of sports. Alejandro: Golf on a Sunday doesn’t count. Darrel: I do other things, like tennis. Alejandro laughs to himself, and begins walking on, Darrel catches up to him and pulls out his wallet. Darrel: A hundred dollars says I beat you at tennis. Alejandro smiles wide. Alejandro: You’ve got a deal. Alejandro shakes his hand, and they walk for about five minutes to the tennis courts, Alejandro and Darrel each pay for rackets and tennis balls, then they make their way over to the court, where they face each other. Darrel: Do you know the rules? Alejandro: Of course, I watch it all the time. Darrel: Do you actually play tennis? Alejandro shrugs as Darrel gets in a serving position. Alejandro: How hard can it be. Darrel smirks, tosses the ball into the air and slams it right past Alejandro with an ace. Darrel: That hard. Alejandro: Yeah, I wasn’t ready… Darrel laughs and serves again, Alejandro gets his racket to it but he aims wrong and it hit’s the net. Alejandro: There’s something wrong with this racket. Alejandro takes his turn to serve, it skims over the net and Darrel returns with a slice, which Alejandro just gets back over the net. Darrel rushes forward to get it and lobs it over the net but Alejandro is there and he hits it across court away from Darrel, where it bounces against the board. Darrel: Not bad. So what d’you think about facing Michaels this week? Alejandro hesitates to answer and instead serves again, with Darrel smashing it across court to take the point. Alejandro: Well besides D.C. and Whitman, I’ve probably faced him more times than anyone, he’s awesome in the ring. And the haters out there who think otherwise are dumb, he’s one of the best in the business. He’s beaten me, I’ve beaten him, he’s been the champ, I’ve been the champ, he’s beaten the best and so have I. We’re both veterans of the business, and we don’t give a damn about people not liking us, we share a lot in common. Darrel serves and Alejandro sends it to the back of the court, Darrel back-hands it just over the net and Alejandro smashes it out of the line. Alejandro: Michaels is one of the very few people in the MWE I respect, I have respect for all the MWE Originals, the ones who have paved the way for the younger generation and yet still they’re here, competing, fighting, winning. And you get the disrespectful ones, the ones who have no idea how to look at the bigger picture, and understand that for MWE to turn out the way it has, and how to continue developing, you have to show some level of respect to your peers, to your superiors. Darrel serves another ace and Alejandro wipes his head. Darrel wipes his forehead and takes a seat, Alejandro does the same. Alejandro: And yeah, we were all punks once, me and Michaels, we were in the same boat. We came to the MWE boasting about being the biggest talents, the future, and we were, but we had no respect. We were young and less mature than we are now. But through paving the way, through teachings from our superiors, we learnt the respect that we needed to move on. The respect that some, do not have yet. Darrel: What do you think about what his opponents usually say to him? Alejandro: Some argue that Michaels is past his prime, and that may be true, but why should I turn around like most people and make cheap comments, untrue comments? Why shouldn’t I respect a man who has won the biggest title three times? He has proven time and time again that he is worthy of being one of MWE’s greatest wrestlers, one of MWE’s greatest characters. He has done things, that only people like Nic Kraven could dream about, punks like Kraven, who insult Michaels, who attack him when he was going through a hellacious match. Darrel: What if Kraven attacks Michaels in your match? Alejandro: I can assure you Darrel, Michaels will not be alone if Kraven interfere’s in our match. If Kraven had any balls at all he would face Michaels when not fatigued or injured, and see what happens then. He has no respect, he is a coward, or at least, he acts like one. I never even heard of this guy before I went away, and now he has the audacity to attack one of MWE’s legends? I don’t think so. Alejandro stands up and throws his racket aside. Darrel stands up too. Alejandro: Lets get out of here, tennis sucks. Darrel laughs and places his racket down before walking after Alejandro. They walk out of the tennis courts and begin walking down a path to the lake. Darrel: What happened to your whole searching for Whitman plan? Alejandro: Well Eli called me into his office the other day, and he said if I want to get booked on cards I should leave Whitman be until Crowning Glory. I agree with him, so after this match with Michael’s I’m going to take another little break… Darrel: WHAT? You’ve only just come back… Alejandro: Yes, and what I want isn’t here… Look, I don’t expect you to understand Darrel, but just please, go with it, trust me for once… Darrel says nothing but gives a little nod of the head. Alejandro: … and when I find him, he’ll know, and so will you. Darrel looks puzzled at this and he stops in his tracks, but Alejandro keeps on walking. Darrel: Hey... Alejandro! Alejandro turns around. Darrel: You owe me a hundred dollars! The scene changes once again back to the dark and cold room, the candlelight is still illuminating the very gaunt scene with Alejandro, now with a cynical look in his eyes, which are facing the camera. He begins to speak again. Alejandro: I will go to the ends of the Earth, from the USA to Australia, from Africa to Aruba, from Russia to the Netherlands… I will go back to wherever I know David Whitman has ever set foot and I will track him down. I will do whatever it takes to do so and I will harm any of his friends or anyone else who gets in my way, I hope he understands that. I hope he understands and comes back, because we need him. And my hunger for competition is wearing thin, and Michaels cannot fulfil that hunger, however much of a legend he is. Watch your back Whitman. The scene fades to black.
