I outperform and I don't let things bug me

Sep 22 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
OOC: The intro calls back to the RP I did in the CD section a couple weeks ago, so if you feel lost/feel you need more background--go look there. (It's been almost a month since Madison Seton begin the newest adventure of her life: college. As we saw previously—Madison hasn't let the experiences of her new surroundings change her at all. She's still the same crazy kid she had been her entire life. Even though she's about 700 miles from her Wisconsin home, she needn't worry much about home sickness. After going around the country this past summer with older sister Laura, which and whom again have been brought up before in these parts—Madison had been “dropped off” at Kansas State University when school began. Little did she know there was to be yet another surprise for herself. Just the previous week, Laura's rookie season in the WNBA came to an unfortunate close, her team missing the playoffs by two games. Being the caring and understanding sister she is, Laura had decided in advance to rent out an apartment in Manhattan,KS, and this is where she went to once said season ended. She knows with Madison's childish [in a good way!] mind that Maddie would be quick to get home sick, and moving out there so Madison could have a familiar face around was her way of helping solve that. As many friends as one can make in a new surrounding, you still want to be home those first few weeks/months. Needless to say, it's worked as Madison's had a handful of bouts with it and yes, it's worked out well with Laura there. Plus, as a “cool” side effect, Madison's new women's basketball friends get meet the celebrity that is Laura Seton. Women's basketball? Well, you knew Madison had a scholarship for the KSU team, right? You already knew that she was a star high school player, right? I'll take your word for it you already did... We fade in and find ourselves in a grassy field with a few fallen leaves about it, having blown over from a few nearby trees. The smell of fall is in the air and with fall comes football. A handful of girls are tossing a ball around—these girls being Madison's new friends and teammates; there are five of them here. Across a narrow road from here are three residence halls—these known as the “Strong Complex,” the more upscale halls. We see a basketball court straight ahead and just to the right of it, next to a pathway leading to the road from the main residence hall of this complex, is Madison. She holds a rather tanned complexion and wears a red t-shirt with her high school logo on it, jean shorts and white Nike hightops with black anklet socks, her hair worn down but clipped back away from her face—a clip on both her right and left side of her head. She looks around and sees a number of students milling about—whether it be just taking a simple walk on this early fall evening or heading off to a night class, and gives a grin. She cups her hands to her mouth.) Madison: Attention fans! Please direct your attention to the southwest Strong Complex lot! (A number of people look towards her with “WTF” expressions. The yelling gets her friends' attention as well and they look to her and either roll their eyes or give a facepalm as it's Madison being Madison.) Madison: Now entering the game....number 45....MADISONNNNN SETONNNNN!! (Madison then dashes as fast as she possibly can from her spot to the middle of the field where her friends are, nearly crashing into a couple students in the process—lucky for her she has great agility. Her friends can only chuckle and shake their heads. Madison turns to her right to face a Caucasian dirty blonde haired girl, in a blue t-shirt and jean shorts with black Adidas hightops. This being her roommate Emily.) Madison: We ready now? (Emily nods and the girls split into teams of three and usual stuff with a pickup touch football game goes on. You know—a bunch of trickery, a few hits just for the fun of it and since it's girls playing, plenty of hair pulling. We get to point to where Madison's team has the ball, the three girls in a huddle. The quarterback is a Caucasian, about 5'10”, obviously in good shape, with red-tinted hair. She wears a yellow shirt and white shorts that acquired a couple grass stains, to go with low-cut Nikes. Her name is Annie.) Annie: You say you have good hands, Madison? Madison: In it to win it. Emily: Better to see now than having to find out in a conference game. Annie: Good point. Okay, Emily, you're setting a screen on whomever is matched up on Maddie. Maddie, you just run your butt off down field. (Madison looks overly eager, looking forward to this play. They break and Annie takes the ball. She lifts it with an appropriate “HIKE!” and steps back. Emily does her part and Madison for a quick second is open. Annie heaves the ball as far as she can. Madison has to step up a bit to get to it, but she reaches it and has a smooth catch. After running to the left a bit, she turns upfield and has daylight to the makeshift end zone. The other players can't catch her.) Madison: (over-excited) Fifteen, 10, five...TOUCHDOWNNNNN....GREEN BAY PACKERS!! (Touchdown Green Bay Packers? You know what that means, right? Madison gives a spike of the ball and begins moving her head, searching for something, anything—as in a group of people. Unfortunately she sees no one but her friends, so she takes off towards them—and it's “Lambeau Leap” time. Sort of. She jumps towards her friends, expecting them to be able to catch her, but the last-second leap catches them off-guard as they just figured she'd be high-fiving or whatever. Instead it turns in to a pseudo-car wreck. As Madison comes down, she crashes right on top of her roommate, catching a couple teammates in the process and the three crash to the ground. The other three standing players help their friends up, and Emily gives Madison a playful smack on the back of the head amidst a swarm of light laughter. It is at this point that the girls decide to break the game up and head back to their rooms. It's obvious they have more fun planned for the evening, but right now it's time for some studying or perhaps TV watching. Madison and Emily get to their half of their suite in their residence hall and Madison quickly gets rid of her shoes and socks and slides on a pair of purple flip-flops. Emily and her sit at their respective desks in the room and Madison begins messing around with her computer as Emily begins reading. Madison begins blasting rock music.) Emily: Hey, Maddie, can you turn that down? Madison: Sure. (Yet the music keeps going for another minute.) Emily: Can you turn it down right now? Madison: OH! Sure...haha. (She turns it off completely and the two girls get back to what they were doing for a couple more minutes before Emily speaks.) Emily: We still meeting Laura for dinner? (Madison turns around in her chair as Emily does the same, putting her book down.) Madison: Yeah. Emily: An idea where? Madison: Sonic. (Emily gets an astonished look.) Emily: Are you KIDDING!? You HAVE to be joking. She has all this money, has taken us out before, and now SONIC!? (Madison busts out laughing at the reaction.) Madison: Hahaha—no, not Sonic! Hahahaha—I think it's Old Chicago again. Emily: I thought you said Laura hates that place? Madison: She doesn't hate it. She just, like, hates most of the menu is fatty, greasy, unhealthy stuff and she, like, hates seeing us down all that food. Emily: So why is she doing this? Madison: Gives me and her, you know, time together and stuff and like, lets you all hang out with us. Emily: (chuckling) Sonic. You idiot. Madison: (to no one in particular) EM CALLED ME AN IDIOT!! (There's a sound of steps coming from the other half of the suite and through the connecting bathroom. The door opens and we see a Caucasian girl, in a very short-sleeved blue top and matching shorts with white socks and her brown hair down—her name is Stephanie.) Stephanie: Good. (Madison looks playfully shocked at Stephanie's dry humor.) Madison: I see how it is. No one likes me. I get it. Stephanie: So go back to Minnesota or Canada. Madison: I'm from Wisconsin! Stephanie: Go back to that cow you have to milk. Madison: (pouting) You're not coming to dinner with us then. (They both stick a tongue out at each other.) Stephanie: It's 6:30 for that, right? Emily: Yeah. Stephanie: Rock on! (We fade from this to after dinner. Madison showed her large appetite again having an appetizer all for herself before having a huge bowl of fettuccine alfredo. It is now the setting of Laura's apartment, which is just across the street from the campus—just a walk on a long pathway from Madison's residence hall to a sidewalk and another short walk on that before Laura's place. We are in the living room of it. There is a large white sofa against the far wall from our viewpoint, facing a 40” LCD TV as well as coffee table with a couple textbooks on it. Just to the left of the sofa is a nightstand with a lamp on it. To our left is a door that leads to a porch/patio area with two chairs on it. A few feet in front of this door is a brown recliner and to the left of that a second white couch. On our right is the kitchen area of this one-bedroom apartment, a counter closest to us and a fridge on the against the right wall and a sink with accompanying dishwasher against the back wall with a cupboards surrounding the sink area. We see Laura herself for the first time. She has light brown hair which is tied in a small ponytail and has on a white t-shirt featuring a bald eagle on it, blue jeans and black sneakers. She, like her sister, is in pretty good shape and stands six feet tall. Laura walks to her sofa against the back wall and sits down. Madison, still in her same clothes as before, slips off her flip-flops and heads for the other sofa and lies out on it face up, feet closest to her sister.) Laura: I still don't understand how you can eat all that disgusting stuff. Madison: I thought you just against massive burgers? I thought you liked pasta! Laura: Well, I prefer that over a hamburger, but not in that large quantity! At least eating all that at one sitting. Madison: I'm sorry I get really, really hungry? Laura: I don't think I've ever known anyone with an appetite like yours. Madison: (smiling, almost proud of herself) What can I say, I love eating! Laura: (chuckling) Yes, you do. You love to eat. (Madison's eyes light up as she gazes up in imagination.) Madison: Especially a big hot fudge sundae, like a foot tall, hot fudge all over the place! Sprinkles raining down on it! With bits of Oreo cookie through it and small bits of cookie dough too! Topped with a cherry and garnished with a yummy fresh-baked chocolate chip cookie on the side! (She snaps out of her dream state and looks at her sister excitedly, like saying “Huh? Huh? Yeah?” Laura smiles and shakes her head.) Laura: You're such a dork. (There's a quiet moment before Laura continues.) Laura: So how are you? Madison: Great! Laura: How've things been going for you? I know you've made some close friends. How about everything else? Madison: Well, you know... (Madison sighs. This question seems to set off a light bulb in her head. While it's been a month since; it seems to just sink in now that the summer is over. Her fun vacation is but history now. A slight look of sadness is apparent.) Madison: Summer was SO awesome—and, like, this seems to be a downer. Like, I enjoy it here and yeah, I've got a bunch of cool friends but, you know, I almost wish summer could have lasted and stuff. Laura: It was really cool with you. I missed having you around those last five weeks before I got here—and I'm going to miss having you tag along until spring. What about Dave and Jenny back home? Still feel some regret about not being able to hang out with them all summer? Madison: Some, yeah. But there's twitter and facebook—I can keep up with them and vice versa. I mean, like, how many people my age can say they literally traveled, you know, across the entire country in one summer? That is SO cool! Like, having all that awesome fish in Connecticut, being able to have an awesome deep dish pizza in Chicago—well, me anyways, hehe—having a chance to rollerblade across the Riverwalk in San Antonio, and stuff. So many sights seen and, of course, it was cool spending time with you. It's not that I didn't want to spend time with my friends, but I at least got two weeks with them. Laura: What was your favorite part? Madison: (with a big grin) Wrestling! Laura: I still wish you hadn't started that. (chuckling) Sunny Rayne. Hahaha...how the heck did you come up with that idea for that first night? Madison: I don't know. Just sound like a cool, you know, oxymoron, and stuff. I always wanted to try face paint—I had fun. Laura: So what are you in now? That you just signed with? (Madison sits up and rearranges herself so now her head is closest to Laura. She puts her hands on the armrest of the sofa and her head on top of it.) Madison: MWE. Mayhem Wrestling... (Laura cuts her off, remembering now.) Laura: Entertainment, right, right. You said shows are Monday nights. You going to be able to make those? Make them with out interfering with classes and games? Madison: Classes won't be a problem, I'm done before noon every day anyways. Games though might cause a problem once the season starts since there's a handful of Monday nighters. Laura: And what takes priority? (Madison gets an expression that reads “You already know this.”) Madison: Games. Duhhh. Laura: You say you can make it for classes—maybe so but the first show is the toughest to rebound from since you aren't in a routine. Madison: Don't worry! The first one is in Oklahoma City. I can make that trip no problem. If I was in Wisconsin, that'd be a different story. Besides, it's bi-weekly. It's not like I'm missing a ton of shows if there's conflict. Laura: How about your match? (Madison gets an excited smile on her face again, being able to talk about her second-favorite sport, after basketball, of course.) Madison: Tag team effort! I get to team with a cool guy named Eric Derf. Laura: Who do you face? Madison: A guy named Oblivion and The Great Milenko. Laura: Most important thing; can you trust your partner? What have you seen from him? Madison: If you're wondering about, like, if he's any good and stuff... (As Madison gives a head toss, we can see Laura get a bit agitated. I'm sure you've noticed this as well, Madison seems to have a tendency to use “like,” “you know,” and “and stuff” more often than anything. This drives Laura up a wall.) Laura: WILL YOU STOP TALKING LIKE THAT!? I thought that speech class was going to help. (Madison is taken aback at the 'scolding.') Madison: It's only been one month. I'm not going to be able to change my 18 years of speech pattern overnight, like, you know. (Madison cringes after those last few words, after of another yelling at. Laura just lets it go this time as she lightly shakes her head. Like Madison said, she's only 18. She's not exactly going to have the same elocution as Laura.) Laura: Just....keep going. Madison: Okay, so, like... (A minor facepalm by Laura but no words are spoken.) Madison: Eric's won the X-Television Title once and he seems to be one the bigger stars there, or at least a highly-regarded “ready to breakthrough” type. He's been in a couple of main events. Laura: That's your partner!? Madison: Yeah....? (Madison seems confused at Laura's response, but there's reason. Laura's watched Madison's progression over the past few months and, with how far Madison got in her last federation, has became quietly jealous. She still gives as much help as possible, but Madison has achieved, so far, much more much faster than her rather successful sister. At this point in her career—3 months—sure Laura had taken home a title, as Madison did in her other fed—neither were World Titles but still, but Madison's was of a higher 'rank' than Laura's. Plus, within a month and a half, Madison was main-eventing. It took Laura literally years before she was able to do that, and even today still has trouble pulling off a main event match. So it's no surprise Laura is a bit upset hearing Madison's teaming with a potential superstar. She does a good enough job to keep this hidden though.) Laura: Do you think that means something? That you're teaming with that type of person? Madison: Not necessarily. I mean, for all I know, MWE just put me with Eric because they wanted me to make my debut—hmm—I don't want to say “easy,” but maybe they just didn't want me to feel over pressured. You know? Like, I know I still have to be awesome and still have to work hard and win and all that and stuff—but...(getting a thought in her head)...it's kind of like a rookie on a sports team. You don't necessarily start the person right away. You ease them in to the swing of things, have them sit the bench and play sporadically and once they've proven themselves or the light goes on that it's obvious “it's time,” the person gets to play. Maybe MWE just wants to make sure I CAN compete but don't want me in a single's match. (Laura lifts her legs onto the sofa and curls them behind her.) Laura: What about the quality of the person though that you're with? Madison: They probably want to make sure I can function at a high level. Laura: Do you think they have high thoughts for you? Madison: (another look of “You know this) Duh. Otherwise they wouldn't have signed me. Laura: I mean putting you this high right now? Madison: If they think I'm ready to be in the same area as Eric Derf; I think that's awesome! If this was a matter of “we want Madison on the card but don't know where to put her” and that's how I ended up here—then so be it. I'll be in this area soon enough anyways. Laura: What about your opponents? (Madison squirms a bit to readjust herself on the sofa but maintains her same position in the end.) Madison: Oblivion is someone making himself known as well, like Eric. The Great Milenko, from what I've heard, is a legend. I've seen some stuff of, you know, Oblivion and he sure looks tough. (There's another subtle hint of jealousy on Laura's face and in her actions—small but there. She could have only dreamed of such a match for a debut. Even if it's not where Madison will be all the time—this IS quite a starting point for the kid.) Laura: You going to win? Madison: (smiling) Of course! I'm Madison Seton. I'm in it to win it. Laura: Tell me why you'll win. Madison: Because I'm better. I'm with a top-caliber teammate and I'm a team player. It's not just me that's better—but US. WE'RE better. It's about team play, just like basketball. Yes, I've been a solo star in b-ball and so far in wrestling, but b-ball is a team game and I've been able to keep that in mind. That's why we went unbeaten last year—I didn't make every game “The Madison Seton Show.” I kept my teammates involved and even though I set scoring records I made sure to put the team first. It's about trust and being able to work together. Eric seems like a good guy so I don't see those being a problem. (Laura nods in approval at this, rather impressed Madison was able to make a great analogy.) Laura: Not a bad line of thinking. While you may be able to handle teamwork, what of your opponents? Madison: (almost brattily) What about them? Laura: Is there anything about them you don't like? Anything you see as weak? Madison: No. Laura: Are you serious? Madison: (chuckling) If they weren't any good they wouldn't be in MWE. This is the bigs! (Laura gets an expression that seems to say, “You just made my point for me.”) Laura: Exactly. This isn't the small summer stuff you had going on. This is going to be top-tier competition all around. Can you handle that? Madison: In it to win it. If I wasn't, I wouldn't have bothered trying for MWE. Laura: But can you handle it? Madison: It won't be easy, if that's what you want to know. I know it won't be a cakewalk like what I was in a couple months ago. I'll have to work hard. It's like what I'm going through now with basketball. I ripped it up in high school and now I'm going to even harder level, college. It's tough to succeed in basketball—just like wrestling, but I did on a lower level. Now I come to a bigger level and now it's time to work harder. Laura: You still haven't talked about your opponents much. (There's another bit of silence before it looks like a thought passes through Madison's head. She gets a slightly puzzled look.) Madison: You mean like trash talk? (Laura gets a look that SCREAMS “Duh!!”) Madison: I told you, like, before and I'll tell you again; I think that's stupid to do. Tell me this, when you've gone off on an opponent and then you lose—how did you feel? Like a hypocrite almost when you have to come back the next week and say, “Oh, I was wrong?” You feel like you're lower than what you were calling whomever you were facing? Suddenly you call them trash or whatever funny insult you can think of...and now they've beaten you. (sarcastically) Yeah, that's worth it. That always looks good. (in her regular tone) That just makes you look dumb. Laura: But it's how you get an edge. Madison: What about, you know—(mildly getting upset, a rarity for her) wrestling!? Laura: Obviously you have to be able to outdo the person with athletic skill—but demeaning them with words goes a long way. It's playing with their heads and a lot of these guys can't take it. Madison: First off, that's pretty low if you have to resort to name calling in order to win. If that's someone's key to winning, call a person a nasty name, then they have a problem themselves. Secondly, if someone is losing a match or struggling because they were, like, called a “loser” or something lower on an intelligence scale than an amoeba, that person has a LOT of growing up to do. If they can't take a verbal assault, I don't know what in the world they're doing in wrestling. Wrestling is sport and thought. Sport is won via athleticism. Thought is maintained by being strong-willed. That's how I win matches. I outperform and I don't let things bug me. If someone wants to call me a name, let them! If Oblivion wants to make a derogatory remark about me, an 18 year old, let him—and let him see how adult he looks when he sees how people react to him insulting a kid. The way to beat me is to outperform me. Not talk to me like a dirtbag. If they choose to go that route, I just won't listen. I'll be working out and readying myself to fight and stuff and thus, be more prepared. Why talk? Just wrestle. Laura: I still don't understand how you can hold to that theory after actually being in the ring. I figured it was just you being naïve at first. (Laura shakes her head but then realizes how she sounds. She takes a deep breath before continuing.) Laura: I'm just saying that I figured you'd understand why these guys talk like this. (Madison nods in full agreement.) Madison: Oh, I do. It's tough in there! Talking is a way to take out extra aggression you may have, I get that. But it's also very stupid—because as I said, you lose and then you wind up looking like an idiot. I mean...(she takes a deep sigh)...if someone were to hurt me in a cheap way or picked a fight with me and hurt me—yeah, then I'd get angry. Then I'd probably talk at them—but, you know? (she shrugs as she sits up) I don't know these guys besides a little bit of film and paperwork. Why should I try trashing someone I've never met in my life and won't be meeting until Monday night? It sounds immature. For all I know, these opponents of mine are nice guys who just happen to be on the other side of the ring, not jerks. Laura: You have your way, I guess. Madison: In it to win it. That's all there is, it's that simple. You have that need like I do and you're all set. Laura: How about homework? Madison: I'm in that to win that too! Laura: No...haha...I mean, have you finished it? Madison: I have a chapter of psychology that I'm supposed to read... (Laura gets a slightly disappointed look and speaks in a similar tone.) Laura: Madison... Madison: ...but we still have a bit to go on the last chapter and I doubt we'll get to the whole.. Laura: MADISON! (Madison gives a huff and her playful pout again.) Madison: Fine, be that way. Laura: (playing along) Okay, I will. Madison: FINE! Laura: Double fine! Madison: Triple fine with five scoops of chocolate ice cream and gummy bears on top! Laura: Ewwww! (Madison gets up at this point and as she walks over past Laura...) Madison: Nah, nah, nah-nah, nah! (Again Madison sticks out her tongue, which Laura retaliates with by smacking Madison with one of the textbooks; doing so “steel chair” style against Madison's back as she slides her feet back into her flip-flops. Madison turns back and feigns pain.) Madison: OUCH! I'M TELLING!! Laura: Who you gonna tell? (The cue is just too easy for Madison to pass up, she replies in the same way as is said in the song.) Madison: Ghost Busters!! Laura: They don't exist! Madison: (as if whining) Then I'm telling my mommy! Laura: She's my mom too! Madison: (with a sly grin) But she likes me more. (Obviously that's not true. Laura grabs the other book off the table and speaks playfully as she holds it up, threatening to throw it.) Laura: Will you just get out of here? (Madison knows it's time to go, and she does so. The door opens and closes as Laura tosses the book on to the sofa she had been on. All she can do is just chuckle to herself.) Laura: Yup, that's my sister. (It's safe to say that Madison Seton is a bit of an oddball. That's who she is. She loves life and all the fun that goes with it. At the same time, she enjoys a new challenge and she's at the crossroads of new challenges for all this next year. New environment, new level of basketball and new level of wrestling—as she stated. It's not very hard to understand why she's been a fan favorite during her short career with her attitude, and it probably hasn't hurt either that Maddie has showed a lot of promise in the ring. The question is if she can continue her past success or if she's hit a plateau? Not that she can't improve and I'm sure she will no matter what happens this coming Monday, but is where she's at right now enough to gain a victory? Even with the star Eric Derf for her partner, is what she can currently bring to the table going to be enough? We fade out.)
