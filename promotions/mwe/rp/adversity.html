MWE 24/7 — 08-09 PPV PR Archives

Adversity

Mar 25 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A scene quickly opens up, with several people walking down a random sidewalk in London, England. A large poster is seen on a wall. All of a sudden, loud techno music is heard throughout a room, its beat thumping against the bodies of those inside it. The rhythm of the music bounces off the pulses of all the young-bloods having a good time. There is a lot of movement, as flashing lights above the room easily spot the more vibrant of the young people. As the music comes to a halt by the disk jockey, who smoothly transitions from the extremely synthetic “Poker Face” by Lady GaGa to the mesmerizing flow of “Go Go Gadget Flow” by Lupe Fiasco. The cameras pan over to show Brian Michaels now sitting on a party chair. He is seemingly intoxicated, with his shoulders in a slumped position and his green eyes narrowed at the bartender. He speaks with a slur, the effect of the alcohol in his system. He is wearing a green high school Letterman’s Jacket, blue pants, and the not-yet-released “Air Yeezy’s” on his feet. The Air Yeezy’s are dangling as the large chair keep Michaels’ feet from hitting the ground. The camera fades back to his show his heavily-chiseled torso, before revealing a stubble of blonde hair under his chin. His eyes lazily look around, obviously angered at the bartender. He begins to walk sloppily, before tripping on a can of Bud Light. As the scene surveys Michaels, it is almost like he has hit rock-bottom. Here is the former MWE Champion, former Rated-X member, Brian Michaels. He has gone from limousines and company jets, to lying in a puddle of beer, not only dirtying his letterman’s jacket, but his jeans and shoes as well. He transforms from the drunk townsman of the club to the laughingstock. People of all ages and races stop and point at Michaels, laughing at him when he is at his lowest point, and kicking him when he is down. Others, a bit more compassionate, simply shake their head as they think back to the old Brian Michaels. The good Brian Michaels, they think. Meanwhile, he attempts to get up from his current state of being. His outfit is now drenched in stale, warm beer, as he goes to the bathroom to take off the jacket. The stench and must of the beer repel people away from anywhere near him, as they hurriedly hold their noses and walk quickly to the dance floor. He sighs, and bows his head in embarrassment. Michaels: F*** , MAN! With near perfect timing, Michaels uses his blurry vision to see a familiar face out of the corner of his eye. At first, he witnesses the hazel eyes he used to look into every night. Following the eyes, he sees the beautiful and fragrant blonde hair. He finally turns a 180 to see the former love of his life, Christina Darby. She dons a pink skirt that hits only to the top of her thighs. Her bodacious body is complimented by a tight, white shirt that reveals plenty of her filled-up cleavage. She immediately makes eye contact with Brian Michaels, and attempts to turn away, but it is too late. Michaels:…You’re the prettiest girl I’ve ever seen. LOOP-DE-DOO. Can I marry you? Christina looks to her left at a small crowd that is gathered. She hides her face in embarrassment, trying not to confront her drunken ex-boyfriend, who appears to be closing in on her. She can literally taste the, what appears to be, gallons of alcohol Michaels has chugged down so far. Michaels: Wait a sec…is that you? It IS YOU! BECKY FROM THE FIFTH GRADE! Do you remember when me and Mike used to make fun of you and pretend to like, shove those blocks up your ass? The fact that Michaels is so buzzed that he doesn’t even recognize her is the tipping point, and she sees his random pause as a sign that this is the time to leave. She begins to walk towards the dance floor, thinking that he is too drunk to follow her. However, much to her chagrin, he does follow. He nearly stumbles onto it, missing the step-up to the floor. Some of the partiers try their best to ignore the bumbling, drunk wrestler, as he attempts to recognize who this blonde girl really is. Christina, thinking quickly, grabs a single guy and begins to dance with him. As the camera closes in on the man…well, ****…pictures are worth 1,000 words. (OOC NOTE: EPIC) Christina: Ugh… She tries to find another man, but Michaels is closing in on her now. She tries not to turn around, but he forcefully does it for her in a violent manner. Michaels: Sha-nay-nay…where you goin’ giz-irl?! I CAME HERE TO PART-AY! He is quickly distracted, however, by the game of Beer Pong going on by the front door. He walks away, nearly mesmerized by the beer version of the famous Ping-Pong game. There are only four men at the table, all seemingly in their mid-20’s, like Michaels. There are two teams, with one hitting the white ping-pong ball into a cup. The losing team shrugs, and chugs down all the beer at the table. Michaels: I WANNA PLAY! I WANNA PLAY, I WANNA PLAY-AY-AY-AY! The guys in the group look at him as if he were buzzed out of his mind, which he is, and reluctantly give him a paddle. He looks at the paddle, admiring its features, as if he’s never seen one before. He shrugs his shoulders, before being given the ball and some quick instructions on how to aim it. They set up the six cups as Michaels nods his head as if to brush their advice off, and he hits the ball as hard as he can. The ball bounces against the wall, while the four young men laugh Michaels off as a joke. The ball comes back with the same speed and power, however, and lands right in the middle cup. They all look astonished as he throws his arms up in a slight celebration. Michaels: LOOK MOMMY, I’M A BEAST AT THIS ONE! The men see him as a fun drunk, until Christina tries to slide out the front door. He again, barely sees her out of the corner of his eye, and follows her like a fat kid follows cake. Before that however, he grabs a cup of the potentially disease-filled beer, and chugs it down in one swallow, kind of like people drink the David Whitman kool-aid. He is obviously intrigued with her impressive specimen, as he looks her up and down. As he studies her facial features, his memory slowly comes back to him. Michaels: Are you…are you, Christy… Christina: No. She answers this with a sharp no, as if to put an abrupt end to the subject. Michaels finds a near-broken stool laying on the sidewalk of the busy English street, and sits to ponder. He scratches his head with a befuddled look in his green eyes, not comprehending the situation in front of him. He continues to look at her with a questioning look, and she rolls her eyes, obviously wanting to leave into the night. She even attempts to speak in an English accent, trying to throw off Michaels. Christina: Bloody Americans…you never know what the hell they’re talking about… Michaels walks to her, not as wobbly as before. His eyes gleaming, he stares deep into her eyes, as if he were looking into her soul. His once drunken-demeanor begins to wash away like rain on a sunny day, and he begins to grasp the fact that the woman in front of him is his ex-girlfriend, Christina Darby. Obviously still feeling the effects of being drunk, he speaks with a bit of a slur, but attempts to speak as clearly as possible. Michaels: You left me…why? The once-peaceful look on Christina’s face, evaporates, and she all of a sudden lashes out at the drunk wrestler, causing a scene. Christina: Because everything in your life, had to be about you…it was YOUR house. It was YOUR money. You never gave me a say in decisions…it was always about man-power. I got sick of taking a backseat and having to be home to please you after being out all night. He goes from looking at her in a questioning way, to having a purple-ish color on his face. It is apparent that he is fired up and is about to respond, similar to how a violent drunk would. He throws his hands up in the air, seemingly sick of the situation in its entirety. Michaels: Last I checked, I didn’t ask you to cry me a river…Do you know what I’m sick of? I’m tired of hearing about girl power. I’m sick of women going around talking about how hard they have it. Let me ask YOU something, ya crazy bitch, how well are you living right now? Christina seems intent on continuing her argument, and thinks of an excuse off the top of her head. Christina: Don’t make this about me, I’m living perfectly fine right now…besides, YOU’RE drunk, you have no father, no mother, and I’m the one you’re looking out for? I think you should be a little bit more worried about yourself and how YOU’RE going to make it. She says “you’re” with an emphasis, and a cold sharp tone. The reality of the fact hits Michaels hard, and he swallows his pride, much like he swallowed the cups of beer earlier in the night. Michaels: Babe, come on…I lub you…Don’t be like this. The alcohol seems to have an effect on the consistency of Michaels’ words, as he was just ranting and raving on Christina, and now he is asking for her forgiveness. She gives him a peculiar look and puts her hands on her small hips, pouting. Christina: I need help…but not yours. I’m really in a rut, financially… Michaels again, goes from humble to act all I-told-you-so. He gives a cocky smirk before beginning to speak again. There is a certain fire in his eyes, that depicts his thoughts on the inside. Michaels: So, Mrs. “So Fly”…has crash landed, in my arms, once again. I knew you’d come back. I’ll walk you home. He extends his hand out, almost as if he predicted this situation would occur, and waits for her hand. She studies it, his clean-cut fingernails, his palms, which are as hard as the cement they stand on. She gleams her eyes before leaning in, speaking in a very low voice. Christina: No. With that, Christina walks away. Michaels, on the other hand, is enraged, and the violent drunk in him is unleashed. He follows her, nearly running, and grabs her by the hand forcefully. Michaels: I SAID…You’re coming, with me. She growls, then thinks of the one thing that pisses Michaels off above everything else. She gives a smirk, then looks at him dead in his eyes, her hazels meeting his deep forest green. Christina: Politics. Suddenly, he stops. He looks aroused, and cocks an eyebrow up, letting go of her for a second. Michaels: What? Self-assurance arises once again in not only her tone, but her posture, as she is not standing up straight. He grabs her hand once more as her single word now becomes something of a chant. Christina: Politics, politics, politics. You never earned ANYTHING in life, and now you want me back. It’s driving you crazy because you know you can’t cheat to HAVE me…goodbye, Brian. They say there’s a moment in every man’s time, where he must realize what he wants to do with it. In this situation, Brian could kill a bitch, and the judicial give him would give twenty-five years to life. Or, he can admit to his wrongdoings and let the woman of his life leave him, in a pool of his own beer. Slowly pondering which of these situations he’d rather have, he chooses the latter. Christina walks off, oh-so-slowly, as if every step of her boots hitting the cement is like another piece of Michaels’ heart breaking. He puts his head down, knowing that not even drinking the night away will relieve him of the past. While he walks to the side of the wall where the ‘’CAN I HAZ BAILOUT?” poster was placed, he sees another poster… Michaels: Adversity…adversity. Almost stumbling once more, he begins to walk towards the local bookstore, and nearly falls down from trying to push the doors open. He picks up the pace, going towards the Reference Section. An old Irishman who migrated to England apparently, walks up to him wearing the store’s uniform. Irishman: Excuse me, sire, can I help you today? Michaels facepalms the clerk of the store, and pushes him towards the DVD section. He finally reaches the reference section and picks up a Dictionary. Heh, ****-tionary. Anyway, he raises the dictionary and feverishly flips through the “A” section. He knows what the word means, however, he wants to read it again by its definition. He reads to himself: NOUN: pl. ad•ver•si•ties 1. A state of hardship or affliction; misfortune. 2. A calamitous event. Michaels says out loud “Use it in a sentence” before realizing he is not at a spelling bee, but at a library. The other librarian, a woman in her mid-forties, walks towards Michaels with a “shh” finger gesture. Michaels promptly responds with the middle finger. He speaks even louder now, as he has been provoked. Michaels: No, why don’t YOU shut the **** up? I’m reading my goddamn book and here you are, trying to tell me to be quiet...Please…make like Maxxamillion Mustafa and get the **** out. The librarian rolls her eyes, before getting back to her work. Michaels continues to taunt her before getting back to the dictionary. For a moment, he attempts to connect this word to his life, and to his upcoming match at Highway to Hell. He pauses, realizing he has a match against GM. “Aw shit” he thinks, knowing he is exhausted, not to mention he is in the process of sobering up. He wants to look good for the camera, obviously, so he begins to walk his way to the bathroom. On his way there, he sees ANOTHER poster…this time, depicting Grand Mystique. Michaels: Damn straight. Michaels arrives at the bathroom and feels like he needs to “unload his tank”, so he goes inside the dirty, musty bathroom. He sees a piece of **** left unflushed. Michaels: COME ON! FLUSH THE PIECE OF ****, I AIN’T TOUCHING IT! He curses, before backing out and going to the next stall. He walks around and damn-near slips at all the wetness on the ground. Michaels: CAN SOMEBODY HIT THE ****ING TARGET?! He sighs and walks to the third stall. He unzips his pants as the camera pans out to avoid a wardrobe…well, function. There is an abrupt disturbance, however, as a kid runs in, happily and knocks on the door as the “splash” from Michaels pee hits the urinal. Michaels: Goddamnit, what is it now? I can’t take a piss in the library because the splashing noises are too loud? The child does not allow Michaels’ anger to affect him, though. In fact, the child swings open the stall door that Michaels forgot to lock, and Michaels looks on with embarrassment and fright. Michaels: Holy **** kid, close the door! The 10-year-old patiently closes the door as the camera closes in on his deep face. A zipping sound is heard as Michaels flushes and comes out the stall. He washes his hands before staring at the child with an awkward face. Michaels: What is so important, that you had to come in to the bathroom and open my stall to tell me? The young boy, puts on a frown, feeling he has disappointed his hero. He shrugs it off quickly, trying to tell Brian the important news. Young Kid: GM…kidnapped, Randy. That is all Michaels needs to hear. He looks around, his humor once again transitioning into a more serious, and violent outlook. Michaels: Thanks, kid. He ruffles the child’s brown hair, before walking confidently out of the bathroom, and out to the main library area. Michaels walks towards the area where there rows of tables lined up against each other and he rises to the top of the middle table, almost breaking it in half. He clears his throat and begins to speak, much to the chagrin of the librarians. Michaels: Grand, Mystique. The definition of grand is noble or admirable in conception, or intent. The definition of Mystique is an aura of heightened value or interest. He smirks at the arrogance in GM’s name alone; he knows he used to be in that same position. Meanwhile, the librarians are huddled up in the corner, plotting of a way to dethrone Michaels from the top of the table. Michaels: GM thinks, that I’m supposed to be emotionally disturbed, or affected, by him stealing my little brother. He shows a bit of his signature smirk before shaking his head, telling the halfway-full library that Mystique is wrong. Michaels: No. GM, In case you have yet to notice, I don’t have a mother. I don’t have a father, and I DAMN sure don’t consider Ace Cannon OR Randy Covell my brothers. There isn’t a “loved family member” thing going on in MY family. See, in the Covell family… He rubs his chin, thinking of the memories at the Covell kitchen table. Terrible times, he thinks, and begins to speak once more. Michaels: Dad would be in the back with Ace, continuing to work on the “skills” he never learned. Randy was the varsity quarterback for four years…Mom was the housewife. What was I? The oldest kid, who Randy hated talking to. He shakes his head again, laughing off the suggestion that he and Randy were close in any way. Michaels: No, GM, you got it all wrong…I LIKE the fact that you’re taking the kid who was constantly baby’d around by my dad. It's about damn TIME that runt got toughened up. So, GM, for that, I thank you. Michaels does a fake, sarcastic applause, with a steady motion. Michaels: But enough of baby brother...let's make like Dr.Phil, and talk about you... Michaels: Grand Mystique, ain't shit. Okay, so I wanted to get that outta the way first. Let's look at GM, the beginning. From what I remember in watching him at EW, he always seemed like a second-tier kind of guy. Like, in EW, you knew he could get the job done, but he was too busy on Maxxamillion Mustafa's rocks, to really get anywhere. So what did he do? He got rid of the problem. After thinking about it for a second, he realizes that GM's situation is much like D.C.'s. He continues to compare these two in his mind, before he actually speaking of it. Michaels: Come to think of it, you're a lot like D.C. You both had someone in your ways...and so you took them out of the equation. You both have succeeded since then. I congratulate you for that...MWE US Champion...EW Internet Champion. Wait a darn minute! He puts his hand on his chin, sarcastically mocking GM's lack of quality title wins. Michaels: Now, don't get me wrong, the US Title is a fine belt...but where's the World Championships? Are you ****ing SERIOUS? I'm supposed to talk about your accomplishments, and you've never won a World Title? **** the script, I'm going freelance. Michaels walks towards the media center, and pushes an attractive young blonde woman out of her seat, and signs on to MWEWrestling.com. He sees a topic that is titled "HtH PPV predictions" filled with fans opinions of who's going to win. He scrolls down and reads the predictions for his match. QUOTE Brian Michaels vs Grand Mystique Outcome: GM Reason: Upset of the night QUOTE Brian Michaels vs Grand Mystique Outcome: GM Reason: Not sure, he just will QUOTE Brian Michaels vs Grand Mystique Outcome: GM Reason: Rooting for the upset here, I believe he can do it QUOTE Brian Michaels vs Grand Mystique Outcome: Grand Mystique Reason: This is a close one...but I think GM is going to pull it out here Michaels: Here's a promo for your ass... He logs off the MWE site, and turns off the computer. Rising now, Michaels speaks with full confidence, with a sprinkle of ego on top. Michaels: Adversity. The will, to win. There comes a time when the Main Event scene, seperates the weak from the strong. The good, from the bad. The workers, from the slackers. Grand Mystique, for the past two years, I have been... He uses his hands to count, as he tries to think of all the memories he has accumulated in a two-year span at MWE. Michaels: Run over by my best friend, I've had two women leave me, my mother has passed, my brother's been kidnapped, my other brother thinks I've cheated to earn my victories, and I've had my balls busted by Covell because I'm not his real kid. Michaels: Do you HONESTLY, honestly, believe, that I don't know what it takes to win on Sunday night? I know how big of a match this is for you. It's even bigger for me. My first match back...I lost to Ace Cannon. I let myself down, and I let my fans down. This is the match that will define my career. Michaels suddenly remembers his last loss as he tries to build some more hype for the match. He uses his left hand to signify GM, and his right to signify Michaels, with the left hand moving upward and the right hand moving downward. Meanwhile, he walks towards the "S" section to find a book that he'd like to point out. Michaels: You've got the rising heel in Grand Mystique...quite possibly the future of MWE...who believes that arrogance and having confidence in your abilities is the key element for winning. He uses his philosophical words to try and confuse his opponents. He takes out the Sharon Draper book "Forged by Fire". The book deals with a young man who deals with adversity throughout his life. From his little sister being sexually assaulted, to his best friend being killed in a car accident. Michaels: On the other side of the ring, you've got me. Battle-tested, no family to guide me, no friend to stand beside me. I've looked in the mirror, and I realize, that yes, my time may be passing. I realize that I'm the underdog. But that, Grand Mystique, is the reason I will come out victorious on Sunday night. The feeling that, no one thinks I can do it, ironically enough, gives me a feeling of self-empowerment that I can't even understand. It's almost as if Michaels' entire history is flashing in front of his very eyes. He truly believes that this match will be the match that his legacy will depend on. He thinks about the main character's legacy being the person that saved his little sister from the fire and a potential death. He feels like he controls his future, too. Michaels: It's about overcoming the odds at Highway to Hell. It's about proving to the younger fans of MWE that I can still get it done. It's about not knowing whether you even have the confidence in yourself to get it done, and getting that job done. GM, I'm gonna tell ya like it is...things don't look good right now. My life is in the ****er, but that ring...is my haven. I know it sounds cheesy, but it feels like I can go out there, and feel the warmth of the crowd, and just embrace it. I don't know if they're going to back me up Sunday...but to myself...I have to prove that I can still come from the bottom of the ladder and prove everyone wrong. Michaels begins to flip through the book, reading about the main character's ups and downs. He faces adversity, dealing with a drunk and violent father. Michaels knows that he faces these very challenges today. Michaels: I ain't going down like this, man. I'm the underdog because people think you're better than me. No matter what I've done in the past two years, it doesn't matter. It will all be over for me, if I cannot beat you on Sunday. Michaels looks on with a determined, yet confident look in his green eyes. His re-assuring way of telling the fans how big the match is to him, shows just how much he knows it will effect his life and career's outcome. The book concludes with an epic battle, much like the one that will occur sunday night, between the main character and his father. The main character, after taking **** from the old man for seventeen years, finally beat his ass. Triumph. Michaels: I was released...I gave this promotion my all, and it's never been enough. To think...guys like you, GM, have accomplished half the stuff I have...and you're still the favorite. Michaels: But I'm not going to harp on that anymore. Sunday night...it stops. The bitching, the moaning, the complaining, and I, am going to love it. Highway to Hell will be an amazing show, and I guarentee you that GM vs Michaels will be an amazing match. Michaels: I proved Whitman wrong two years ago...I will do it again this year. I have gone from egotistical, to humble, to doubtful...and on sunday night...triumphant. He closes the book with a thud, as he speaks over the camera with his "thoughts". My dad taught me that to be successful, you had to be perfect. I am not perfect, but sunday night, I will do my best to BE perfect.
