The Grand Mystique

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The scene opens to the Chicago Ridge – based facility that was the training ground for MWE. ICW had already seen several successful graduates move up to the flagship Monday Night Mayhem broadcast. In the ring of this empty arena was another hopeful. This guy was different, not because of his clear ability between the ropes. He was special because he is Randy Covell, the son of MWE owner, Richard. Randy shows particular skill as he runs the ropes, guided by a grey haired, wrinkled and tanned old man who watches his every move from ringside. The junior Covell’s agility is obvious and he capably changes tasks with comfortable speed as the silent ringside observer barks out commands at him during the routine. Covell appears to be totally at home in the squared circle. The silver fox barks at him again and Randy flips over the top rope and lands with aplomb on the apron to end the exercise. The youngster is thrown a clean white towel and he wipes his brow with it, popping it around his neck when he is done. “You do well young Randy.” The accented English indicates that he is probably Japanese. “After everything, I am glad that you are able to well and focus on your training.” His golden skin wrinkles around his eyes and cheeks as a muted smile glides by his features. “Thanks Jose.” A tired and breathless response. His mentor is concerned about how much this is really affecting Randy, despite the brave and neutral reply. “Go....rest.” “Huh?” “Home, R ‘n’ R, Forty-eight hours, no arguments.” He walks off, leaving the youngster bemused by the immediacy of his comments. Nonetheless Randy makes for the changing room, hoping that a long shower would help pass the nagging stresses of the past few days. “Excuse me, Mr Covell?” Mr Covell.....That can’t be good. A tall man approaches Randy, who turns to face the stranger. Dressed in a smart but casual suit, with an open necked blue shirt, the size of the guy surprises Randy. He is tall and broad shouldered. Randy surmised that he must be a failed athlete of some sort. So many find their way into journalism if they are smart enough. “I was wondering if you could spare some time for some questions?” “I believe my fathers’ press statement covered everything.” The prepared response flowed off the tongue in exactly the way that Richard had drilled into Randy’s head during their last phone call before training yesterday. It was a tired response to something that he didn’t want to talk about yet again. “Actually this is about you, your career, the MWE etc?” “Oh OK.” Randy gestures to the raised seating structure to his right. He is happy to be talking about anything but Brian. “Thanks for your time....I appreciate it’s been a difficult few days.” “You have no idea.” They sit down and share a chuckle. The guy takes out a notepad and places a dictaphone on the seat in between them. “I’m Alex by the way, from MWE-zine.” They shake hands affably with good humoured grins on their faces. “So big question out of the way first, what are the ambitions of Randy Covell” Randy: “I want to do everything that I can in MWE. Get through the main roster, wins titles and prove to people that I am worthy.” Alex: “Is the Covell name a hindrance?” Randy: “I haven’t decided yet. The way I see it I could hide under a mask, abandon my heritage and some people will still hate on me. I think by being honest about my family allows me to be seen at face value.” Randy scratches his chin. He seems to be relaxed. Alex “As Ace Cannon’s brother, and the son of Richard Covell, surely that gives you a bit of stroke, a head start?” Randy: “What are you saying?” Randy doesn’t seem so relaxed now. The questions’ tone is suddenly direct and aggressive. Alex: “Well Ace and Brian are main eventers in MWE but they have as much talent as two snails. They got where they are through - ” Randy: “Natural ability and being the best at what they do!” Randy protests furiously. Alex: “Do you really believe that?” Randy almost snaps back at the arrogance of the guy and the sheer accusive nature of the question. He knows his dad would go mental at him as he has directly disobeyed one order; no interviews. Randy: “Look bud, this is over!” Randy is defensive but attempts to cover himself with his inherited self confidence. He gets up, turns around. Seething inside but desperately trying to keep calm, he doesn’t look back, walking away towards the showers calmly and assured, determined to salvage a drop of credibility from what could be his part in the recent family disaster. Alex places his dictaphone inside his jacket and goes to leave. He adjusts his jacket and goes to leave. He has had enough of the insight that he was after. * Randy emerges from the changing room, suited and booted, ready to leave for the day. He heads to his locker to fetch some things before he leaves for the day. He is just about to open his locker when something catches his eye. Blue and reflecting brightly in the harsh lights of the room, he walks over to the object which is out of place on the cream tiling and pine bench layout of the changing area. He kneels down to pick it up. He is confused. His furrowed brow indicates that Randy is sure that he recognises it but dismisses its origin as impossible. And with that, Randy throws it in the bin that is in the far corner. He looks at his wristwatch and realises that he has to get some shopping before his cinema date tonight. He opens the locker and fires his deodorant – two short and sharp bursts underneath each arm pit and carelessly slams the door shut with loud impact. The door doesn’t shut. Randy has already turned away and without looking back, he presses his palm against the metal lock, but the door doesn’t shut all the way. Must be the deodorant, thought Randy, so he puts his arm inside to just shove everything back so he can get going. He had already spent much longer than he wanted to here. After the events of the past fortnight, Randy needed to blow off some steam and had no doubt that Toni; the cute leggy blonde that he had arranged to meet tonight could help. As he pushed around inside the locker, he felt an obstruction. He opened the door and saw what had caused the blockage, yet he was bemused by its’ presence. As he opened up the door, the light showed a crystal ball, a perfect sphere that revealed itself in a gleaming glory. Randy reached inside. As he touched it, the realisation of what he found hit him. That mask and the crystal ball was no coincidence. A flurry of thoughts washes over him – his “brother”, Brian Michaels – the man he idolised for the last twenty years – was fighting the Grand Mystique thanks to his father at MWE pay-per-view Highway to Hell. GM wore a blue mask and his weapon of choice was a...... It was exactly what Randy held in his hands. Randy leans against the metal frame of the locker, wondering exactly what it all meant, who he could talk to and tell. Maybe Brian? Dad? Father or brother; Randy had never considered them much different until a few days ago. Then......Randy was unable to think about anything. The open locker door slams shut, trapping Randy like a vice with tremendous force. It was ever so brief but the impact is enough to knock Randy senseless. He crumbles, to his knees but almost in slow motion. He is barely conscious, unable to do much beyond survive from one breath to the next. His eyes are shut but the eyelids flicker and flinch. It is a sign of life as his brains attempts a reboot. He has landed with his left cheek buried on the tiling. Blood gushes from two but deep cuts on both sides of his head. Within seconds blood is flowing like a stream in the straight plastered edges of the tiles. It has quickly formed a small pool, which stains his face and the floor. As the camera follows this, we see the edge of a shoe. The assailant? Blood reaches the rubble sole and it does, it steps through it to Randy’s feet. The legs are picked up by the scruff of his smart black trousers and we follow the lifeless shape of Randy Covell as he is dragged to the end of the room. It leaves a slurred trail of blood, like that of a slug or snail, shed from Randy’s fresh crimson mask. “Oh Randy.” A voice terribly familiar, yet not identified by the camera has a playfully ashamed tone. As if this is a game, as if he could control the trail of lost blood. Randy is propped upright in a corner of the exit to the communal shower room. His assailant observes this form; pathetic and weak, for only a second before he turns away. He walks through the red trail, past the puddle that marks its origin. He reaches the trash can that Randy has used only minutes earlier. He pulls it out, straightening it carefully with his hands before he places it carefully on his head. We now see the face for the first time. It is somewhat inevitable that it is the Grand Mystique yet as he looks over the bloody mess of his actions with no emotion coming from those dark eyes, only hatred and anger comes through. This is GM at his best, at his worst. He doesn’t care. This is all preparation for the main event, this is the Grand Mystique getting ready to face Brian Michaels at Highway to Hell. But let’s go back for a second. * Following his non event of a match with Zak Warner at Monday Night Mayhem, GM was still fuming as he walked backstage after the show had finished. He had changed into a slightly more casual outfit with an open collar shirt and black jeans, yet he still wore his mask as a habit. He rushed through the gorilla area, not talking to anyone and went into the emptying arena. His annoyance was crystal clear. He dropped down the steel ramp and headed to the banked seats at the side. Hopping over the barrier, he climbs a few rows before taking a random seat in the middle. As he was surveying the area, which was so recently populated by thousands, a sea of humanity who were enjoying the chance to boo GM at every turn. An ocean of faces, a living, breathing wall of noise. Yet as negative as the reaction was, GM was now so used to it that it had got to the point that he revelled in it. He remembers seeing Paige come to the ring in the corner of his eye. A pretty girl but clearly focussed on something else. And then it was over. Zak had been made to look like a chump. I was standing there with no one to fight and she was ogling his title. That was the match over and done with. Was I not right to be angry? He had let himself and his fans down. And he let me down too. The whole match was his idea and he can’t even finish it. GM’s fist pounds down on the plastic seat in front of him. Zak Warner ended up unconscious as a result of what GM had done to him. The Big Bang had claimed another victim. Just like it would need to at Highway to Hell. But now Zak was in his past. He would not be facing Warner at the pay-per-view for the Intercontinental title. As such, GM allowed his thoughts to wander on to the next target, victim. The name resonated within his skull – the man, the legend, the Career Stopper – Brian Michaels. Brian had made a career of being “the man”. GM had seen it happen in EW at close quarters. GM had enjoyed his work, though it showed Michaels’ personality perfectly. He went from mourning, broody emo to DC’s drunken best mate within days. It was frustrating yet typical Rated X – no consistency but still as entertaining as anything on your TV service of choice. GM could move past that. In his eyes, in EW at least, he could see how Brian Michaels had earned his title honours. It appeared genuine; being the offspring of the legendary Richard Covell had no effect. Then something changed, GM turned up in MWE but everyone was talking about Brian Michaels, such a great psychotic heel. GM destroyed Maxx but everyone was talking about what a career defining match the Three Stages of Hell at Adrenaline Rush was. He made me sick. Something about it still does. *See, I’ve gone on to better things, I’ve “beaten” Antonio DeMarco – I gave David Whitman a match to remember the week after – and then I gave Windy Bridges a beating to within a centimetre of his existence! But I never get the credit for it.* And so with Highway to Hell approaching quicker with every second. GM knows he had to beat Brian Michaels, something to put him over, onto the next level. GM was on fire, he knew his heat would continue, regardless of the result again Zak Warner. But GM felt he needed more than that against Michaels. Something special, someone unique. Someone was the key, the key to Brian Michaels, the Career Stopper and Brian Michaels the man, the simple soul, the “never was” son of the boss, the “politicker” that everyone thought was the measuring stick but nothing more than a name that people wanted to get past. Everyone thought that the “relationship” with Richard Covell was the reason – it seemed legit for so long and they were so wrong. Everyone from Cannon, the “bastard” to Whitman to DC, it all came back to Richard Covell Senior; the pretence, the .......nothingness – all it was, was sympathy, pity. GM knew that the effects of ending the relationship were still fresh and raw, it would be the easy and obvious target, but he couldn’t care any less. GM felt this was logical – expose the emotional upheaval in his personal life and attack someone that Brian Michaels would be foolish to deny any feelings for. And then there were going to be consequences. GM was never going to be intimidated or scared by Brian Michaels. But by implementing this plan, GM would be subject to the effects of stoking Brian’s personal fires. Everything that Michaels had suffered in recent weeks – his mum, the funeral, being disowned by Richard Covell, the Tim show, losing versus his “brother” Ace Cannon and being handed his release from MWE – could come to the fore. It could all come back to bite him in the backside at Highway to Hell, because doing this would really be asking for it. Coulda, woulda, shoulda – GM refused to ever allow consequence to dictate his actions. Why start now? It was then that GM realised that he had to do this. He wanted everything from Brian Michaels. If he couldn’t take his best and emerge with his hand raised, then GM didn’t deserve to be in MWE. * The scene cuts back to the ICW changing area. GM is looking at the blood stained and prone form of Randy Covell in front of him. As he comes to, Randy flails around in an attempt to defend himself. GM ends that with a stiff kick to the ribs. GM stands over Randy as the young victim drifts in and out of consciousness. It was all too brief but when he fought for that second, GM saw a defiant soul through wide and desperate eyes. “So, was it worth it? To be the next “Career Stopper”? Randy, you had everything going for you. The son of the owner, the brother of Ace Cannon and Brian Michaels – legends and icons of MWE, but you were most like the Career Stopper.” GM knelt down to be closer to Randy’s face for a second. “The never say die, guts ‘n all attitude, the agility, the bravado. But where has it got you?” GM moves Randy’s face towards him so he faces his eyes. The wounds on the side of his head have bled profusely, covering his face completely. GM has blood on his hand now and wipes it on the wall. When it doesn’t come off easily, GM is visibly unimpressed. “You know, your career doesn’t need to follow Brian’s – the out of fashion accessory to DC in Rated X. It doesn’t even need to go down the same drain as Ace Cannon’s. Because if it does, then you’ll never fill your “potential”!” GM even does the cheesy speech marks. He kicks out at Randy, but gets no physical reaction beyond a defiant stare that told GM everything that he already knew. Randy had been compared to Brian Michaels all of his life. GM knew that they were so similar. All promised word but no end product. GM could respect the Brian Michaels who took no shit, the guy who would superkick the head off of his own mother if he saw the benefit. That was a guy that GM felt that he could work with. But that guy – he was dead, buried at Adrenaline Rush. And while he looked and sounded like Brian Michaels was real convincing, GM knew that the soul, the spirit and the heart of that guy had died a long time ago. “Randy, Randy, Randy.” GM grabbed him by his wet, red collar. RC’s eyes are lifeless with not even enough energy to keep them focussed on him. GM knew that even if Randy could fight him, he wouldn’t just out of personal safety. “Your family won’t stay down will they?” GM cackles quietly to himself. “Just stay down. Ace didn’t learn his lesson and he refuses to accept that Joey Dallas is just superior. Brian Michaels will learn to accept it as a simple fact. He can’t help it. Really it is just life coming back to bite him.” GM drops Randy and gets on his hands and knees to go eye to eye with him. The young Covell lies crumpled on the cold tiling. He tries to lift his head up enough to make proper eye contact. “Brian Michaels needs to accept that he isn’t good enough anymore. If his knees don’t let him down then his back will. He is a walking example of what an ego can do to you. Misplaced self-belief and a short sighted confidence. He could have gone out as the legend, on top of the business and riding the crest of a wave of critical acclaim and professional respect.” Randy has forced himself into a sitting position. He now sees GM properly and is not able to look him in the eyes for the first time. “What? You refuse to dismiss Brian kid?” Randy is breathing heavily, almost panting and although he opens his dry lips and tries, he cannot speak, before GM cuts him off. “I’m pretty sure that Brian Michaels is going to try and show everyone that this won’t affect him, his life and his career. But we all know deep down that it isn’t true.” GM rubs his sticky hands on the arms of Randy’s new suit. A lot of blood sticks to GM’s palms and his fingers trail a fresh stain down the material. “See, Brian Michaels is an unstable being, almost drowning in a personality dichotomy. He is frail and weak yet wants to be the disturbed bad ass that haunts his nightmares.” GM places his hands on Randy’s dried cheeks. “Brian Michaels will have to acknowledge your suffering as it mirrors his own future and past. Tricked by Daddy, abandoned by Rated X, left alone by his mum and most recently, left in a heap in the ring like a sack of shit by his “brother” Ace Cannon.” GM places his thumbs in the open wounds that have been opened by GM’s initial assault. “My job will be to end your brothers suffering Randy.” GM uses his thumbs and forefingers on the wounds. Randy’s eyes are wide open but GM’s are shut with a calm focus. “The MWE has caused him so much pain – emotional anguish, physical agony.” Randy struggles but GM is able to restrict his head movements with only four fingers. “And it is down to me – to remove Brian Michaels from MWE. I can’t extract his influence from history but I can, I will end his present and future.” Randy begins to flail again. A quick knee to the ribs causes Randy to curse in pain, through gritted, blood-stained-teeth. “Come on Randy! Give me an example of that true Covell spirit – give me a hint of what the Career Stopper is going to feel at Highway to Hell because all he knows is how to be just another Covell” Randy is defiant. He says nothing. GM looks deep into his eyes; he can see the anger, the grit and determination that GM had seen in Richard Covell and in the past, Brian Michaels. But beyond that front, that facade of desperate self presentation, GM saw a crack, the splintered glass that sat above a cauldron of simmering frustration and pain. GM moves his thumb above the two centimetre long wounds. He presses them ever so gently. Crack. He moves fingernail over it and presses again. Crack. Crack. SMASH! GM digs the fingernail of his thumbs into the wound. Blood spits out and flesh is ripped. Randy screams loudly, a high pitched yelp that needs no second guesses that he was in a serious amount of agony. GM thrusts Randy’s head away. He instantly recoils, defensively and instinctively as fresh blood appears. GM stands over him once again Randy attempts to crawl away from the perpetrator of this hellish torture. “Randy, I frankly don’t care if you tell him or not. It changes nothing.” GM again wipes his hands on the white tiling. It marks it faintly but not a lot is removed. GM examines his palms closely. The lines on his hand show darker trails of red among the fainter, already dried blood. “It doesn’t matter.” He speaks with a wandering and vacant tone. “Next Sunday at Highway to Hell, Brian Michael’s blood will decorate the canvas and stain my hands, just like this.” GM looks up from his hands. He doesn’t look at Randy, only looking at the blood stained wall. “His legend and his top level status get destroyed. The glass ceiling above which he has spent his entire career is going to be smashed into a million pieces by the man who has surpassed him in every department, not least skill and ability to go all out, no matter who he faces.” GM realises that he had not thought that to himself, but he had said it aloud. Resoundingly so, that it echoes a little. He turns a little and for the first time, he directly acknowledges the camera. “The Grand Mystique is going to meet Brian Michaels at the end of the Highway to Hell. The end of it, where he will be left lying in a puddle of his own blood.” GM leaves as the camera moves back to Randy, who is surrounded on all sides by blood that has again puddle around his head and stained the tiling from the assault. Randy knew that GM had changed his like but it just added itself to a list of events that had forced him to reconsider his position on life, on being a wrestler, the bosses’ son, on being a Covell. But as he passed in and out of consciousness, with more fresh blood trickling slowly down his temple, Randy began to accept that no longer being a Covell would have no affect on Brian’s match at Highway to Hell. Whether he had been abandoned by Richard or not, if he was Randy’s brother anymore or not, the outcome would still be the same.
